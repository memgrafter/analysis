# A Unified Spoken Language Model with Injected Emotional-Attribution Thinking for Human-like Interaction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04960
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04960
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Ranked #1 on HumDial test set (4.27/5) and SOTA on dev (4.98 Task1-zh, 4.83 Task2-en), outperforming Qwen3-omni and other baselines.

## Executive Summary
Spoken dialogue systems struggle with semantic and emotional congruence, limiting their emotional intelligence. This paper presents a unified spoken language model (SLM) built on GOAT-SLM, enhanced with Injected Emotional-Attribution Thinking (IEAT). IEAT integrates user emotional states and their underlying acoustic causes into the model’s chain-of-thought, enabling emotion-aware reasoning to be internalized. Training follows a two-stage progressive strategy: Stage 1 uses self-distillation to align speech and text while modeling emotional attributes, and Stage 2 performs end-to-end cross-modal joint optimization to ensure consistency between textual and spoken emotional expressions.

Evaluated on the HumDial Emotional Intelligence benchmark, the model achieves the highest score on the test set (4.27/5) and state-of-the-art results across all development subtasks, including 4.98 for Task1-zh and 4.83 for Task2-en. It outperforms strong baselines such as Qwen3-omni in both automated LLM-based and human evaluations, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed unified architecture and emotional attribution internalization for human-like interaction.

## Method Summary
The method leverages the GOAT-SLM architecture, which unifies speech and text processing via a shared transformer core. In Stage 1, speech-text alignment is achieved by distilling knowledge from a pretrained text-only LLM (Qwen3-8B) that generates target responses given either transcript or ASR output. Concurrently, emotional attribute modeling is performed by applying the emo2vec emotion classifier to audio and inserting the resulting emotion labels into the model’s ‘think’ segment, forming the IEAT data format. Stage 2 conducts end-to-end cross-modal joint optimization: the top-*k* layers of the LLM are fine-tuned for speech token prediction while bottom-*n* layers remain frozen, trained on a mixture of speech datasets (Emilia, LibriHeavy) to harmonize textual and spoken output streams. The IEAT mechanism explicitly forces the model to reason about the cause of user emotion before generating a response, thereby internalizing emotional attribution.

## Key Results
- Final score: 4.27/5 on HumDial test set, ranking first.
- Dev set SOTA: 4.98 for Task1-zh, 4.83 for Task2-en, leading all subtasks.
- Outperformed baselines including Qwen3-omni in both automated LLM-based and human evaluations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Injecting user emotional states and their causes into the model's internal reasoning (IEAT) may enable emotion-aware reasoning to be internalized rather than treated as an external supervision signal.
- **Mechanism:** During data construction, emotional labels derived from acoustic analysis (via `emo2vec`) are inserted into the "think" segment of the training sequence, before the response generation. This forces the model to associate acoustic-prosodic cues (the cause) with the emotional state (the effect) as part of its chain-of-thought, rather than learning a separate mapping from audio to emotion.
- **Core assumption:** Emotional intelligence in dialogue requires modeling the *causal attribution* of emotions (i.e., "User X sounds angry *because* their voice is loud and fast"), not just detecting the emotion label. Internalizing this attribution improves generalization to novel emotional contexts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "IEAT incorporates user emotional states and their underlying causes into the model's internal reasoning process, enabling emotion-aware reasoning to be internalized rather than treated as explicit supervision."
  - [section 2.2] "...we apply the `emo2vec` model to perform emotion classification on all Stage 1-1 audio samples... We then emulate the analytical style... by inserting the emotion information into the think body... This procedure constitutes the IEAT step."
  - [corpus] The neighbor paper *"When LLMs Team Up: The Emergence of Collaborative Affective Computing"* discusses pipeline rigidity in affective computing, indirectly supporting the need to internalize reasoning. However, **no direct empirical validation of IEAT's causal mechanism is cited from external work; the evidence is solely from this paper's design and results.**
- **Break condition:** If the acoustic features used by `emo2vec` are poor proxies for the true emotional cause (e.g., they capture speaker identity or channel noise instead), the injected "cause" may be misaligned, leading the model to learn spurious correlations between prosodic artifacts and emotional states.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Stage 1 self-distillation for speech-text alignment and emotional attribute modeling may create a robust shared semantic space where linguistic content and paralinguistic cues are disentangled but jointly represented.
- **Mechanism:** A pretrained LLM (`Qwen3-8B`) generates text responses (and, with IEAT, emotional reasoning) from either text transcripts or ASR outputs. The spoken language model (SLM) is trained to match these targets, compelling it to map variable acoustic realizations of the same semantic/emotional intent to a consistent representation. The emotional attribute modeling is reinforced by conditioning the generation on the `emo2vec` labels injected during IEAT.
- **Core assumption:** A strong text-only LLM contains sufficient knowledge of emotional expression and appropriate response generation to serve as a "teacher" for aligning the speech modality. The SLM can learn to ignore acoustic variation while preserving emotional semantics.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The first stage performs speech–text alignment and emotional attribute modeling via self-distillation."
  - [section 2.2] "In stage 1-1, we feed either the textual queries or ASR transcripts into the LLM to produce natural responses, which then serve as supervision for cross-modal alignment... In Stage 1-2... we apply the `emo2vec` model... obtaining emotion labels... This procedure constitutes the IEAT step."
  - [corpus] The neighbor paper *"Discourse-Aware Dual-Track Streaming Response..."* addresses latency in cascaded systems, implying the value of unified modeling, but does not directly support the self-distillation mechanism. **Corpus evidence for this specific distillation approach is absent.**
- **Break condition:** If the LLM-generated targets are themselves inconsistent or low-quality for emotional dialogue (e.g., generating neutral responses to emotionally charged inputs), the SLM will learn a misaligned distribution, harming emotional congruence.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Stage 2 end-to-end cross-modal joint optimization may enforce consistency between textual and spoken emotional expressions by simultaneously training the generation pathways for both modalities.
- **Mechanism:** After stage 1 establishes a shared representation, stage 2 unfreezes the top-*k* layers of the LLM for speech token prediction while keeping the bottom-*n* layers frozen to preserve general linguistic knowledge. It trains on a mixture of text-only and speech datasets, forcing the model's internal activations (in the shared "Think" space) to produce coherent outputs in both text and speech streams for the same input.
- **Core assumption:** True multimodal emotional intelligence requires that the *reasoning* about emotion is identical regardless of whether the output is text or speech; the difference should only be in the surface realization (lexical vs. prosodic). Joint optimization aligns these realization pathways to a common reasoning core.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The second stage conducts end-to-end cross-modal joint optimization to ensure consistency between textual and spoken emotional expressions."
  - [section 2.1] "The speech-generation branch employs modular fine-tuning on top-k layers of an LLM for speech token prediction while freezing the bottom-n layers to preserve foundational linguistic knowledge."
  - [section 2.2] "In Stage 2, we train on Emilia and LibriHeavy to further strengthen cross-modal alignment and speech synthesis quality."
  - [corpus] The neighbor *"Covo-Audio Technical Report"* also uses an end-to-end LALM, suggesting this is an emerging architectural trend for coherence, but **does not specifically validate the joint optimization for emotional consistency.**
- **Break condition:** If the gradients from the speech and text generation heads conflict (e.g., a choice of word is optimal for textual clarity but suboptimal for natural speech prosody), simultaneous training could lead to a compromised, inconsistent internal representation, degrading performance on one or both modalities.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Unified Speech-Language Modeling (GOAT-SLM base)**
- Why needed here: The system must process acoustic input and generate both text and speech outputs within a single model, avoiding cascaded errors and enabling shared reasoning. The GOAT-SLM provides the Listen-Think-Write-Speak paradigm and the shared semantic space.
- Quick check: If the acoustic encoder maps two different spoken utterances of the same sentence to divergent semantic representations, would the unified model's textual output be consistent? What architectural element prevents this?

**Concept: Self-Distillation for Cross-Modal Alignment**
- Why needed here: Directly pairing speech with emotional responses is data-scarce. Using a powerful text-only LLM to generate "soft" targets (responses and reasoning) for speech inputs bootstraps alignment without requiring human-annotated speech-response pairs for every emotional nuance.
- Quick check: In stage 1-1, if the ASR transcript contains errors, how does distilling from the LLM's response to the speech input affect the learned speech-text alignment? Is the model learning to correct ASR or to be robust to it?

**Concept: Emotional Attribute Modeling via External Classifier (`emo2vec`)**
- Why needed here: The model needs a training signal for emotional content beyond semantics. `emo2vec` provides a coarse, data-driven emotion label from the audio, which is then injected as a reasoning step (IEAT). This scaffolds the model's own acoustic-emotional perception.
- Quick check: The `emo2vec` model is fixed and its labels are injected. Does this mean the SLM never learns to classify emotions directly from raw audio? What happens if `emo2vec`'s label set (e.g., "angry," "happy") doesn't match the nuanced emotional states in the target dialogue?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
1. **Input Encoder:** Converts raw speech waveform to discrete speech tokens (likely using a vector-quantized encoder from BoSS/Emilia setups).
2. **Projector & Flow (AI Flow framework):** Maps speech tokens and optional text tokens into a unified embedding space fed into the LLM.
3. **LLM Core (GOAT-SLM based on Qwen):** Shared transformer layers. Bottom-*n* layers frozen in stage 2 to preserve general knowledge; top-*k* layers tuned for speech token generation.
4. **Output Heads:** Two separate prediction heads over the LLM's final hidden states: one for next text token (Write), one for next speech token (Speak). The "Think" step is internal to the LLM's generation process, structured by the IEAT data format.
5. **Data Pipeline:** LLM (`Qwen3-8B`) → generates text responses & IEAT "think" sequences → `emo2vec` labels audio → constructs training sequences with injected emotion cause → feeds into SLM training.

### Critical Path
Speech Query → Speech Encoder → Projector → LLM "Think" (with IEAT context) → LLM Final Hidden State → [Text Head] & [Speech Head] → Text Response/Speech Tokens → Vocoder (implied) → Speech Output. The critical path for emotional intelligence is the LLM "Think" state, which must integrate the IEAT-injected cause and the query's semantics to produce a coherent emotional plan for both output modalities.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Freezing bottom layers vs. full fine-tuning:** Freezing preserves general linguistic/semantic knowledge but limits the model's ability to deeply adapt its core representations to the speech modality's nuances. The tradeoff is stability versus adaptability.
- **Multi-token prediction for streaming:** Predicting multiple speech tokens per forward pass enables low-latency TTS but may reduce per-token accuracy if the model's context window for prosodic planning is too short.
- **IEAT injection format:** Inserting emotion labels *inside* the think block (without termination token) forces continuation but risks the model learning to generate the `emo2vec` label as text rather than using it as a latent control signal.

### Failure Signatures
- **Incoherent emotional trajectory:** The model's stated emotion in the "think" block does not match the emotional tone of the generated speech response (e.g., thinks "user is sad" but responds with upbeat phrasing).
- **Modality inconsistency:** The text response is empathetic, but the generated speech tokens produce a flat, synthetic voice, or vice-versa. This indicates stage 2 joint optimization failed.
- **IEAT hallucination:** The model generates an incorrect or nonsensical "cause" for the user's emotion in the think block, or simply parrots the `emo2vec` label without integration.
- **Over-reliance on textual semantics:** Emotional performance drops drastically when the input speech has ambiguous or misleading transcripts, suggesting the model is not using acoustic-prosodic cues as intended by IEAT.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Ablate IEAT:** Train an identical model without the `emo2vec` label injection in stage 1-2 (only text-based thinking). Compare emotional trajectory detection and empathetic response scores on HumDial dev set. Expected: significant drop, proving IEAT's contribution.
2. **Modality Consistency Test:** For a set of emotional inputs, generate both text and speech outputs. Use a separate emotion classifier on the generated speech and an LLM judge on the text. Measure the agreement/disagreement rate between the two modality-specific emotion labels. A high disagreement indicates failed stage 2 joint optimization.
3. **Acoustic Robustness Probe:** Corrupt the input speech's prosody (e.g., speed up, slow down, add noise) while keeping the transcript identical. Evaluate if the model's emotional reasoning (in the think block) and response adapt to the new acoustic cause, or if it remains locked to the transcript's implied emotion. This tests if IEAT truly made it acoustically grounded.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How exactly does the Injected Emotional-Attribution Thinking (IEAT) mechanism alter the internal representations or attention patterns within the LLM's layers to internalize emotional attribution, and is this internalization robust across diverse, ambiguous, or culturally specific emotional cues not present in the HumDial training data?
2. To what extent does the model's superior performance on the HumDial Emotional Intelligence benchmark, which uses specific structured prompts and evaluation metrics, generalize to truly open-ended, spontaneous spoken dialogue scenarios where emotional cues are noisy, multi-modal (e.g., facial expression, gesture absent), and conversational turns are longer and less predictable than the curated data?
3. What is the specific contribution of the two-stage progressive training strategy (speech-text alignment via self-distillation followed by end-to-end cross-modal joint optimization) to emotional intelligence, independently of the IEAT data construction? Is the primary gain from better multimodal alignment, from the emotional thinking injection, or from their interaction?
4. How does the model's reliance on synthetically generated speech queries (via CosyVoice2 from text instructions) and potentially synthesized "instruction audios" for stage 1-2 affect the fidelity and diversity of emotional prosody in the training data, and consequently, the model's ability to generate genuinely empathetic and emotionally congruent spoken responses compared to models trained on naturalistic emotional speech?
5. Does the modular fine-tuning strategy (freezing bottom-n layers of the LLM while tuning top-k layers for speech token prediction) adequately preserve the foundational linguistic and commonsense reasoning capabilities required for nuanced emotional understanding, or does it create a brittle separation where the "frozen" knowledge cannot be dynamically accessed during cross-modal emotional reasoning?

## Limitations
- Theoretical validation gap: Proposed mechanisms lack direct empirical support (e.g., ablation, probing); evidence primarily observational (benchmark scores).
- Reproducibility barriers: Critical hyperparameters and framework details omitted; access to HumDial benchmark data/evaluators unverified.
- Causal inference risk: Performance gains may stem from data quantity/quality (synthetic speech, LLM-generated targets) rather than novel mechanisms; fixed `emo2vec` may propagate labeling errors.

## Confidence

- Self-distillation for cross-modal alignment: Medium
- End-to-end joint optimization consistency: Medium
- Overall benchmark performance: High
