# ProgAgent:A Continual RL Agent with Progress-Aware Rewards
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.07784
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07784
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** Achieves 98.8% success on button-press, 74.1% average performance, and 26.2% regret, surpassing a perfect memory baseline.

## Executive Summary
ProgAgent addresses catastrophic forgetting and the burden of manual reward design in continual reinforcement learning (CRL). It automatically derives dense, shaped rewards from unlabeled expert demonstration videos by training a perceptual model to predict task progress, a formulation theoretically equivalent to a state-potential function that preserves optimality while accelerating learning. To stabilize reward predictions on out-of-distribution states encountered during agent exploration, it incorporates an adversarial push-back regularization. The entire system is implemented in a JAX-native, fully JIT-compiled architecture that enables massive parallel rollouts and efficient computation of a unified objective combining reward learning, policy optimization (PPO), and continual regularizers (replay, SI). Empirical results on ContinualBench and Meta-World show significant reductions in forgetting, faster adaptation, and superior performance over baselines, including an idealized agent with perfect memory. Real-robot experiments further validate its ability to learn manipulation skills from noisy, few-shot human demos.

## Method Summary
ProgAgent’s core innovation is a two-stage reward learning mechanism. First, a progress prediction network \(E_\phi\) is trained on triplets of observations from expert videos (initial, current, goal) to predict the normalized progress ratio \(\delta\). The predicted mean \(\Phi_\phi(o_t)\) acts as a state-potential, yielding a shaped reward \(r_t = \gamma\Phi_\phi(o_t) - \Phi_\phi(o_{t-1})\). Second, to counter distribution shift during online exploration, an adversarial push-back loss \(L_{\text{push}}\) regularizes \(E_\phi\)'s predictions on agent-generated data toward a low-variance prior, combined with the expert loss into \(L_{\text{reward}}\). This reward function guides a PPO policy \(\pi_\theta\), whose training is unified with continual learning mechanisms: a coreset replay buffer \(\mathcal{M}\) and Synaptic Intelligence (SI) importance weights \(\Omega_k\). Critically, the entire loop—parallel rollout collection, reward model update, and policy update—is encapsulated in a single JIT-compiled JAX function, enabling thousands of parallel environments and batched computation of stability terms.

## Key Results
*   On the button-press task, ProgAgent achieves **98.8% success rate**, exceeding the perfect memory baseline (75.3%).
*   Across ContinualBench, it achieves **74.1% average performance** with **26.2% regret**, compared to 31.0% for the best baseline.
*   Ablations confirm the adversarial push-back is crucial, as its removal degrades average performance by 8.3% on `window-close`.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Progress-Aware Reward as a Learned Potential Function
The agent derives dense rewards by predicting task progress from unlabeled expert video frames. A perceptual model \(E_\phi\) is trained on expert triplets \((o_i, o_j, o_g)\) to predict the progress ratio \(\delta = |j-i|/|g-i|\) as a Gaussian. The mean prediction \(\Phi_\phi(o_t) := \mathbb{E}[E_\phi(o_0, o_t, o_g)]\) serves as a state-potential, and the shaped reward \(r_t = \gamma\Phi_\phi(o_t) - \Phi_\phi(o_{t-1})\) guarantees policy invariance while providing dense guidance. This relies on the assumption that expert demonstrations exhibit monotonic progress toward the goal. If demonstrations are erratic or suboptimal, the learned potential may misalign with the true objective, leading to poor policies.

*Break condition:* Non-monotonic expert trajectories cause reward hacking or convergence to local optima.

### Mechanism 2: Adversarial Push-Back Refinement
An adversarial regularization term stabilizes reward predictions on out-of-distribution states from the agent's exploratory rollouts. For data \(D_{\pi_\theta}\) collected by the current policy, the loss \(L_{\text{push}}\) applies a KL divergence pulling \(E_\phi\)'s predictions toward a zero-mean, high-variance prior \(P_{\text{prior}} = \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{prior}}^2)\). The combined reward loss is \(L_{\text{reward}} = L_{\text{expert}} + \beta L_{\text{push}}\). This prevents overconfident, erroneous rewards on novel states. The strength \(\beta\) must be tuned: too high makes the model overly conservative and suppresses valid progress signals; too low fails to curb distribution shift.

*Break condition:* Improper \(\beta\) tuning either stifles learning on novel states or allows reward overconfidence to derail policy updates.

### Mechanism 3: Unified JAX-Native Architecture for Scalable CRL
The entire training pipeline—data collection, reward updates, policy optimization—is encapsulated in a pure function, JIT-compiled with JAX. Parallelization via `vmap` spans thousands of environments, eliminating CPU-GPU bottlenecks. This design makes the computational overhead of continual regularizers (coreset replay, SI) amortizable over massive parallel rollouts, rendering the sophisticated unified objective feasible. The approach assumes simulator dynamics can be wrapped as pure functions and that batch sizes fit within GPU memory.

*Break condition:* If the simulator state is too complex for a functional wrapper, or if the JIT batch size exceeds GPU memory, throughput collapses and the unified objective becomes impractical.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Potential-Based Reward Shaping**
    *   **Why needed here:** Provides theoretical guarantee (policy invariance) that the learned progress reward does not alter the optimal policy of the MDP, only shapes the reward landscape for faster learning.
    *   **Quick check:** If \(r'(s,s') = r(s,s') + \gamma\Phi(s') - \Phi(s)\), does the optimal policy change? Explain why this matters for learning from videos.
*   **Concept: Distribution Shift in Online RL**
    *   **Why needed here:** Agent-generated states during exploration diverge from the offline expert video distribution, causing learned reward models to output unreliable signals without regularization.
    *   **Quick check:** Why might a model trained only on expert trajectories fail when evaluating an agent's exploratory state? Give an example from a navigation task.
*   **Concept: JIT Compilation & Functional Programming in RL**
    *   **Why needed here:** Enables massive parallel simulation and batched gradient computation for stability terms, which is mandatory for the computational budget of unified CRL objectives.
    *   **Quick check:** What is the primary system bottleneck that JAX's `jit` and `vmap` remove in this context? How does that affect the feasibility of combining PPO with SI and coreset?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Expert videos -> Progress model (E_φ) -> Shaped reward (r_t) -> Policy (π_θ) with continual modules (Coreset M, SI Ω) -> JIT-compiled loop (Φ) -> Sequential task learning.

**Critical path:**
1.  Train \(E_\phi\) on expert triplets to produce initial \(r_t\).
2.  Enter JIT-compiled main loop for each new task:
    a. Run massive parallel rollouts using current \(r_t\).
    b. Update \(E_\phi\) with combined \(L_{\text{reward}}\) on expert data and fresh rollouts.
    c. Update policy \(\pi_\theta\) with unified \(L_{\text{total}}\) (PPO + replay + SI).
3.  After task completion: update coreset \(\mathcal{M}\) and compute/store SI importance \(\Omega_k\).

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Reward robustness vs. plasticity:** High push-back weight (\(\beta\)) stabilizes reward but may suppress valid novel signals; requires per-suite tuning.
*   **Memory vs. stability:** Larger coreset \(\mathcal{M}\) improves retention but increases replay sampling cost; SI adds negligible memory but requires importance computation.
*   **Throughput vs. complexity:** Higher `vmap` batch size improves data efficiency but increases JIT compilation time and GPU memory pressure.

**Failure signatures:**
*   **Reward model failure:** Potential \(\Phi_\phi(o_t)\) does not increase monotonically on expert trajectories (check Fig. 6 analysis).
*   **Catastrophic forgetting:** Sharp drop in Average Performance on prior tasks when continual regularizers (\(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\)) are zero.
*   **JIT bottleneck:** Training step time increases non-linearly with environment count; indicates improper functional simulator wrapper.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Isolate reward learning:** Train \(E_\phi\) only on \(D_{\text{expert}}\). Plot \(\Phi_\phi(o_t)\) over expert, successful agent, and failure trajectories. Verify monotonic increase on expert/success, stagnation on failure.
2.  **Test push-back effect:** Ablate with \(\beta=0\) vs. full model. Measure variance of \(r_t\) on random states and performance on `window-close`. Expect higher variance and lower AP without push-back.
3.  **Validate JAX parallel scaling:** Benchmark steps-per-second vs. number of parallel environments (e.g., 100 → 10,000). Expect near-linear speedup until GPU memory saturation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How does expert data quality affect ProgAgent's reward?
    *   **Basis:** The reward learning mechanism relies on quality and visual diversity of expert videos; suboptimal demos may lead to poor local optima.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The paper does not analyze failure modes due to poor expert data or offer collection guidelines.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments varying expert coverage and optimality, measuring performance and potential monotonicity.
*   **Open Question 2:** How can the sim-to-real visual gap be overcome for ProgAgent?
    *   **Basis:** Transitioning to physical robots (sim-to-real transfer) remains open challenge due to visual domain gap.
    *   **Why unresolved:** No adaptation technique is proposed or tested on real robots.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply domain randomization, style transfer, or VLMs to align simulated and real features; evaluate on a real robot with minimal fine-tuning.
*   **Open Question 3:** How can the multi-objective loss coefficients be automatically tuned across tasks?
    *   **Basis:** Balancing multi-objective loss requires careful empirical tuning; future work explores meta-learning for dynamic auto-tuning.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Manual tuning is impractical for diverse task suites; no automated method is provided.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** A meta-controller that adjusts weights during training based on forgetting rate or learning speed, evaluated across multiple sequences.
*   **Open Question 4:** Can vision-language models (VLMs) enhance ProgAgent for open-ended manipulation?
    *   **Basis:** Extend ProgAgent by incorporating VLMs to bridge visual domain gap for real-world deployment.
    *   **Why unresolved:** VLMs are not implemented; their effect on reward shaping and generalization is unknown.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace the perceptual model with a VLM (e.g., CLIP) and test on instruction-following tasks with novel objects/scenes, measuring zero-shot success.

## Limitations
*   **Implementation gaps:** Exact architecture of \(E_\phi\), critical hyperparameters (\(\beta\), \(\sigma_{\text{prior}}\), \(\lambda_1\), \(\lambda_2\), learning rates, coreset size), and the coreset update rule are not described.
*   **Strong assumptions:** Requires expert videos to show monotonically increasing progress; push-back strength \(\beta\) must be properly tuned; simulator must be expressible as a pure function for JIT.
*   **Scalability limits:** JIT/vmap throughput may collapse if batch size exceeds GPU memory or if simulator state is too complex for functional wrapping.

## Confidence
*   **Progress-aware potential reward:** Medium – Theoretical policy invariance holds, but reliance on monotonic demos is a strong, externally unvalidated assumption.
*   **Adversarial push-back:** High – Ablation (Sec 4.4) shows removing it reduces AP by 8.3% on `window-close`, confirming its stabilising role.
*   **JAX-native scalable architecture:** Medium – Performance vs. Perfect Memory and throughput claims are plausible but lack direct computational cost measurements.
*   **Overall CRL performance (reduced forgetting, surpassing baselines):** High – Consistent, multi-benchmark quantitative gains, including exceeding an idealized perfect-memory agent.

## Next Checks

2.  **Push-back sensitivity sweep:** Perform a grid search over \(\beta\) and \(\sigma_{\text{prior}}\); evaluate reward prediction variance and average performance on out-of-distribution states (e.g., `window-close`). Identify the robust range that balances stability and plasticity.
3.  **Parallel scaling benchmark:** Implement the JIT-compiled loop and measure steps-per-second while increasing `vmap` parallelism (e.g., 100→10,000 environments). Expect near-linear speedup until GPU memory saturation, confirming the claimed scalability.
