# Learning When to Cooperate Under Heterogeneous Goals
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07253
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07253
- **Reference Count:** 7
- **Primary Result:** GRILL, a hierarchical offline-online method, achieves higher average returns than PPO, LIAM, and OMG baselines in ad-hoc teamwork with heterogeneous goals by learning to select cooperative goals while avoiding common failure modes.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles ad-hoc teamwork where an agent must cooperate with teammates having potentially mismatched, partially observed goals. The proposed method, GRILL, uses a two-stage hierarchical approach: first learn a universal, goal-conditioned low-level policy via offline behavior cloning on heuristic data, then train an online high-level policy to select appropriate goals based on state and teammate cues. An extension, GRILL-M, adds an auxiliary teammate model to handle noisy or missing goal information. Results across two gridworld environments show GRILL outperforms baselines and better navigates three key failure modes in goal selection.

## Method Summary
GRILL separates the problem into learning "how" to achieve goals (low-level) and "when" to cooperate (high-level). Stage 1 is offline: a dataset of trajectories from simple heuristic agents pursuing random goals is used to train an encoder-decoder model. The decoder becomes a fixed, goal-conditioned policy (π_action) that can execute any discovered goal. Stage 2 is online: a separate high-level policy (π_goal) is trained with PPO to output discrete goal selections that condition π_action. The high-level policy's reward is the cumulative return from executing the chosen goal. GRILL-M augments this with an LSTM that models teammate behavior from history, improving performance when explicit teammate goal cues are unreliable.

## Key Results
- GRILL achieves higher average returns than PPO, LIAM, and OMG across cooperative reaching and level-based foraging environments.
- GRILL more successfully avoids three failure modes: pursuing non-rewarding goals, attempting unachievable goals, and missing cooperative opportunities.
- GRILL exhibits a larger cooperativity difference (Δcoop) between scenarios with and without shared teammate goals compared to baselines.
- GRILL-M's auxiliary teammate modeling provides a performance advantage that increases with the noise in observable teammate goal information.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Hierarchical separation of goal selection (high-level) and action execution (low-level) improves performance by letting the agent learn *when* to cooperate independently of *how* to achieve goals.
**Mechanism:** The high-level policy π_goal picks discrete goals based on state and teammate cues. The low-level policy π_action is a single, fixed, goal-conditioned policy trained offline to execute any goal efficiently. This decouples the cooperation decision from motor control.
**Core assumption:** The optimal low-level behavior for achieving a given goal (e.g., navigating to a corner) is universal and does not depend on the specific teammate.
**Evidence:** [abstract] describes the hierarchical approach; [section 4] states the optimal low-level policy is universal while the optimal high-level policy depends on both agents' goals.
**Break condition:** If optimal execution for a goal is highly contingent on a teammate's precise policy (e.g., partner-dependent timing), the fixed π_action fails. This is not observed in the paper's gridworlds but may break in more dynamic domains.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Offline behavior cloning on heuristic data is sufficient to learn a robust, universal low-level goal-conditioned policy, avoiding costly online exploration for motor skills.
**Mechanism:** Stage 1 collects trajectories from random heuristic agents. An encoder-decoder is trained to reconstruct actions and final observations, implicitly clustering trajectories by goal. The action decoder becomes π_action, conditioned on the latent goal.
**Core assumption:** Heuristic data provides sufficient coverage of state-goal transitions; reconstruction loss correlates with identifying coherent goal-directed behavior.
**Evidence:** [section 4 & 5] detail Stage 1 data collection and training; results show π_action avoids the "pursuing non-rewarding goals" failure mode.
**Break condition:** If heuristic data lacks trajectories for certain goals or state-goal combinations (e.g., hard-to-reach cooperative goals), π_action will be ineffective for those goals. The paper assumes sufficient diversity without empirical validation.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** GRILL-M's auxiliary teammate modeling improves performance when observable teammate goal signals are noisy or absent by inferring unobserved goal information.
**Mechanism:** An LSTM encoder-decoder learns a latent representation of the teammate's policy/goal from the ego agent's (observation, action) history. This latent state augments the input to π_goal, compensating for missing/corrupted explicit cues (ϕ_i).
**Core assumption:** The teammate's goal/policy is partially identifiable from their observable behavior and the ego's action effects.
**Evidence:** [abstract] notes the effect is inversely related to observable information; [section 6] (Figure 7) shows GRILL-M's advantage increases monotonically with cue noise.
**Break condition:** If teammate behavior is extremely stochastic or environment dynamics obscure the link between goals and actions, the latent model learns spurious correlations and degrades performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL)**
  - **Why needed:** The architecture requires a policy π(a|o, g) that can achieve arbitrary goals g from set G. This is the substrate for the low-level policy.
  - **Quick check:** Given a trained GCRL policy, what happens if given an impossible goal (e.g., "go to corner" when blocked)? Should it navigate to a best attempt or fail explicitly?

- **Concept: Behavioral Cloning (BC)**
  - **Why needed:** Stage 1 uses BC (via action prediction) to Distill heuristic trajectories into a goal-conditioned policy, offering a sample-efficient alternative to learning low-level skills with RL.
  - **Quick check:** What key BC limitation might hierarchical design mitigate? (Answer: Distribution shift—high-level policy may select novel goals, exposing low-level policy to states absent in heuristic data.)

- **Concept: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**
  - **Why needed:** PPO is the backbone RL algorithm for training the high-level goal-selection policy (π_goal) in Stage 2; all baselines use it for fair comparison.
  - **Quick check:** In this hierarchy, what is the "environment" for the high-level policy? (Answer: An MDP where actions are goal selections, and reward is cumulative from executing π_action until goal achievement or timeout.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Heuristic Data Collector -> Stage 1 (Encoder-Decoder Trainer) -> π_action
  π_goal (MLP) + [Optional: Teammate Model (LSTM)] -> Online RL Loop (PPO) -> Updated π_goal
- **Critical path:**
  1. Generate offline dataset D from heuristic agents.
  2. Train encoder-decoder on D; extract action decoder as π_action.
  3. Initialize π_goal (and teammate model if used).
  4. Online loop: π_goal selects g; π_action executes g; env returns reward; PPO updates π_goal.
  5. For GRILL-M: teammate model also updates from (o, a) history.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Fixed π_action:** Stable but not adaptable; poor heuristic data limits performance permanently vs. joint fine-tuning.
  - **Offline vs. online low-level learning:** Offline BC is sample-efficient but cannot surpass heuristic data quality; online learning could discover better skills but is complex and sample-hungry.
  - **Discrete goal space:** Matches environments but limits applicability to continuous/open-ended goals.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High-level failure:** π_goal frequently selects goals outside G_ego (agent's own reward set).
  - **Low-level failure:** π_goal selects valid g ∈ G_ego but π_action fails to achieve it (e.g., walks past target).
  - **Teammate modeling failure (GRILL-M):** Performance degrades when teammate cues are clean, indicating model interference or noise learning.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity check low-level policy:** Run π_action alone with oracle goal sampler; measure per-goal success rate. Expect >90% across all goals.
  2. **Validate scenario sensitivity:** Evaluate frozen/random π_goal across no/partial/full-overlap scenarios. Trained GRILL should show significant Δcoop; random should show ~0.
  3. **Reproduce noise sensitivity (GRILL-M):** Train GRILL vs. GRILL-M on Level-Based Foraging with increasing teammate cue noise. Expect GRILL-M's advantage to increase monotonically with noise.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can GRILL be extended to partially observable environments with more than two agents?  
  *Basis:* Discussion notes experiments were limited to fully observable settings with two agents.  
  *Why unresolved:* Real-world ad-hoc teamwork often involves partial observability and larger teams.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Test GRILL with belief states or learned communication in partially observable multi-agent environments, comparing to existing baselines.

- **Open Question 2:** Can the hierarchical policy be trained end-to-end online rather than via separate offline imitation and online RL?  
  *Basis:* Discussion identifies offline low-level learning as a limitation.  
  *Why unresolved:* Offline π_action may not be fully adapted to later high-level selections.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Develop and evaluate a joint optimization algorithm learning both levels simultaneously, comparing sample efficiency and final returns.

- **Open Question 3:** What specific aspects of teammate goals/strategies are encoded in GRILL-M's latent representations that help under noisy/missing cues?  
  *Basis:* Paper shows empirical benefit but does not analyze latent space content.  
  *Why unresolved:* Without understanding the representations, it's unclear why they help or how to improve them.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Probing experiments (e.g., linear regression/clustering) on latent vectors to correlate with ground-truth teammate goals/behaviors across noise levels.

## Limitations
- Environment implementation details (state/obs representations, reward masking, scenario sampling) are not fully specified, risking misalignment with original dynamics.
- The heuristic dataset collection protocol for Stage 1 (policies, trajectory segmentation, size) is vague; performance may hinge on this unvalidated choice.
- Assumptions (universal low-level policy, sufficient heuristic coverage) are only tested in simple gridworlds; may fail in complex, stochastic, or continuous domains.

## Confidence
- **Hierarchical decoupling (Mech. 1):** Medium – Results support claim in tested domains; break conditions in dynamic environments unexplored.
- **Offline BC sufficiency (Mech. 2):** Medium – Strong empirical results but no ablation on dataset quality/coverage; success could be environment-specific.
- **Teammate modeling utility (Mech. 3):** High – Direct experimental validation across noise levels shows clear, monotonic benefit.

## Next Checks
1. **Low-level policy competency:** Isolate π_action with oracle goals; measure per-goal success rates. Expect >90% across all goals; failure indicates flawed Stage 1.
2. **Scenario sensitivity:** Evaluate frozen/random π_goal across no/partial/full-overlap scenarios. Trained GRILL should show significant Δcoop; lack thereof suggests high-level learning failure.
3. **Noise robustness replication:** Train GRILL vs. GRILL-M under increasing teammate cue noise. Replicate monotonic performance gap increase; deviation implies incorrect teammate modeling.
