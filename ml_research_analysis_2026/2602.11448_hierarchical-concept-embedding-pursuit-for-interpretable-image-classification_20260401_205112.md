# Hierarchical Concept Embedding & Pursuit for Interpretable Image Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11448
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11448
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: HCEP recovers hierarchical concept paths with higher support precision/recall than baselines while maintaining competitive classification accuracy, especially in few-shot settings.

## Executive Summary
HCEP addresses the problem that existing interpretable image classification methods using sparse concept recovery ignore the hierarchical structure of semantic concepts, leading to correct predictions with explanations inconsistent with the hierarchy. It constructs a hierarchical concept embedding space where sibling subtrees are well-clustered and child-parent differences are orthogonal, enabling the true root-to-leaf path to be the geometrically optimal sparse representation. A hierarchical beam orthogonal matching pursuit (HB-OMP) algorithm is then used to recover the sparse code restricted to valid child branches, with beam search mitigating error propagation. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets (ImageNet, CIFAR-100, ImageNette) demonstrate substantial gains in concept support precision and recall over OMP, Hierarchical NN, and CBM, while classification accuracy remains competitive. In few-shot regimes (12–50 shots), HCEP achieves superior performance, highlighting the benefit of incorporating hierarchical structure for faithful interpretability.

## Method Summary
HCEP operates in three stages. First, a hierarchical dictionary is built from a predefined concept hierarchy (e.g., WordNet) using pretrained CLIP embeddings: each atom corresponds to the difference between a child synset’s embedding and its parent’s embedding, with non‑leaf synset embeddings obtained by averaging their children’s text embeddings. Second, HB-OMP runs a greedy, top‑down sparse pursuit on an image’s CLIP embedding: starting from the root, at each depth it considers only children of the current deepest node, maintains the top‑B hypotheses by reconstruction error, and updates coefficients via least squares. This yields a sparse code whose support forms a valid root‑to‑leaf path. Finally, a linear classifier is trained on the sparse codes to predict class labels. The approach ensures explanations that respect the semantic hierarchy while maintaining classification performance.

## Key Results
- HCEP significantly improves concept support precision and recall compared to standard OMP, hierarchical nearest neighbors, and concept bottleneck models on both synthetic and real datasets.
- Classification accuracy remains competitive; in few‑shot settings (12–50 shots), HCEP outperforms all baselines.
- The hierarchical constraint yields more faithful, hierarchy‑aligned explanations without sacrificing predictive power.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Hierarchical Concept Embedding Geometry
The embedding space is constructed so that sibling subtrees are well‑clustered (each child lies within a cone around its parent with half‑angle θₗ) and child‑parent difference vectors are orthogonal to the parent embedding (hierarchical orthogonality). These geometric conditions ensure that the true root‑to‑leaf path is the sparsest and geometrically optimal representation of an image embedding. Under such conditions, the correct path becomes uniquely identifiable as the one minimizing reconstruction error while respecting the tree structure.

### Hierarchical Sparse Pursuit with Beam Search
HB‑OMP extends standard OMP by restricting candidate atoms to children of the current deepest node, enforcing a coarse‑to‑fine search that respects the hierarchy. Beam search retains the top‑B partial paths by residual norm, preventing early mistakes (e.g., selecting a wrong high‑level branch) from irrevocably propagating downward. This structured exploration dramatically increases the probability of recovering the correct path compared to global OMP, especially in deep or noisy hierarchies.

### Synset Difference Dictionary
Dictionary atoms are defined as differences between child and parent synset embeddings (a(child) − a(parent)). This choice forces any reconstruction of a leaf synset to include all its ancestors’ difference vectors (telescoping sum), yielding an explanation that naturally forms a rooted path. The differences capture the semantic refinements that distinguish finer concepts (e.g., “white fur” for polar bear vs. “bear”) and avoid trivial solutions relying solely on leaf atoms.

## Foundational Learning
- **Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP)**: Greedy algorithm that iteratively selects dictionary atoms most correlated with the residual and updates coefficients via least squares. - **Hierarchical Orthogonality (in embeddings)**: Geometric condition where (a(child) − a(parent))ᵀ a(parent) = 0, meaning child‑parent difference vectors are orthogonal to the parent embedding. Why needed: This property enables the telescoping sum interpretation and guarantees that fine‑grained concepts lie in a subspace complementary to coarser ones. - **Beam Search in Structured Prediction**: Searches over a structured hypothesis space by maintaining a beam of the top‑B partial solutions at each step. Why needed: HB‑OMP uses beam search to explore multiple partial paths, balancing exploration and computational cost while mitigating error accumulation. ## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input (image) → CLIP image embedding → Hierarchical dictionary D (synset difference vectors) → HB‑OMP pursuit → Sparse code z (support = root‑to‑leaf path) → Linear classifier → Predicted label

### Critical Path
1. Precompute synset embeddings: non‑leaf synset embeddings are averages of their children’s CLIP text embeddings; leaf synset embeddings are averages of training image embeddings.
2. Build hierarchical dictionary D: for each non‑root node, atom = a(child) − a(parent).
3. For each image, run HB‑OMP: initialize hypothesis (code=0, residual=x, last_node=root); iterate by extending each hypothesis with children of last_node, updating coefficients via least squares, and keeping the top‑B by residual norm; after T iterations (≈ depth L), return best code.
4. Train a linear classifier (e.g., logistic regression) on the training sparse codes and labels.
5. At test time, apply the same pipeline: extract CLIP embedding → HB‑OMP → classifier.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Beam size B**: Larger B improves support precision/recall and robustness to early errors but increases runtime roughly O(T·B·b) where b is branching factor.
- **Synset embedding source**: Using CLIP text vs. image embeddings for non‑leaves affects geometric conditions; averaging leaves from images may better reflect visual semantics.
- **Hierarchy source**: Curated taxonomies (WordNet) vs. LLM‑induced hierarchies (CIFAR‑100) trade off accuracy and reliability; induced hierarchies can be noisier.
- **Sparsity level**: Ideally matches the true hierarchy depth L; truncating early loses fine‑grained concepts, while exceeding depth may introduce irrelevant atoms.

### Failure Signatures
- High reconstruction error ∥x − Dz∥₂ suggests missing concepts in the hierarchy or poor dictionary construction.
- HB‑OMP returns atoms from multiple branches (invalid path) indicating either beam too small or violation of geometric assumptions.
- Poor classification accuracy despite good concept recovery may point to underfitting of the linear classifier or insufficiently predictive concepts.
- In few‑shot regimes, OMP could unexpectedly outperform HCEP if beam search overfits to the few examples; monitor validation support F1‑score.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Synthetic validation**: Generate embeddings from the model with known hierarchy and added noise. Compare HB‑OMP vs. flat OMP in support precision/recall across sparsity levels; verify that the hierarchical constraint improves recovery (Prop. 4.1).
2. **Real‑world comparison**: Apply HCEP and baselines (OMP, Hierarchical NN, CBM, Nearest Neighbor) to ImageNette, ImageNet, and CIFAR‑100. Evaluate classification accuracy and support precision/recall, with few‑shot ablations (12–50 shots).
3. **Geometric condition verification**: On real CLIP‑based hierarchies, measure the percentage of sibling cone overlaps (well‑clustered condition) and the cosine similarity of child‑parent difference vectors to the parent (hierarchical orthogonality). Report adherence to Prop. 3.1 and Prop. 3.2 assumptions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Relaxing Euclidean dimension requirement**: Can hierarchical concept embeddings be realized in lower‑dimensional or hyperbolic spaces to accommodate very deep/broad taxonomies? The paper notes a lower bound d ≥ L + b − 1 and suggests hyperbolic spaces as a direction.
- **Multiple concepts per image**: How can HCEP be extended to handle images containing multiple objects, where the explanation may not correspond to a single rooted path? The current assumption restricts inputs to leaf classes.
- **Robustness to noisy hierarchies**: What if the input concept hierarchy contains errors (missing links, incorrect parent‑child relations)? The paper acknowledges that noisy hierarchies degrade performance but offers no mechanism to refine the hierarchy during recovery.
- **Formal recovery conditions**: What precise conditions (e.g., restricted isometry, mutual coherence bounds) guarantee that HB‑OMP selects the correct path under the geometric embedding assumptions? The paper provides an informal proposition and relies on the ERC for standard OMP, but a hierarchical analog is lacking.

## Limitations
- Hierarchy construction for datasets without curated taxonomies (e.g., CIFAR‑100) relies on LLM prompts that are not fully specified, risking inconsistent or noisy hierarchies.
- The required geometric conditions (well‑clustered sibling subtrees, hierarchical orthogonality) are assumed; they may degrade with diverse pretrained embeddings or real‑world noise, undermining path identifiability.
- HB‑OMP hyperparameters (beam size, tolerance, max iterations) are dataset‑dependent and require validation; poor choices can reduce performance or cause overfitting in few‑shot settings.

## Confidence
- Hierarchical embedding geometry: Medium confidence (theoretical propositions sound but empirical violations possible)
- HB‑OMP pursuit mechanism: High confidence (algorithmic design and synthetic experiments robustly demonstrate gains)
- Synset difference dictionary: Medium confidence (telescoping sum enforces path consistency but depends on hierarchy quality and linear independence)
