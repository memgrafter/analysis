# Rooted Absorbed Prefix Trajectory Balance with Submodular Replay for GFlowNet Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00454
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00454
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RapTB+SubM mitigates prefix collapse and length bias, improving the quality‑diversity trade‑off in autoregressive GFlowNet training.

## Executive Summary
GFlowNets fine‑tune large language models for reward‑proportional sampling but suffer from mode collapse (prefix collapse, length bias) due to weak credit assignment and biased replay buffers. This paper proposes RapTB, an extension of Trajectory Balance that augments the global objective with rooted‑prefix residuals and absorbed suffix rewards, providing dense prefix‑level learning signals while preventing termination drift. It also introduces Submodular Replay (SubM), a submodular subset selection that balances reward, diversity, and length coverage. Experiments on scaffold‑conditioned SMILES generation, Expr24 arithmetic, and CommonGen show that RapTB+SubM consistently outperforms TB and SubTB, achieving high validity, task scores, token entropy, and FPDiv while allocating greater probability to longer sequences and maintaining long‑horizon coverage.

## Method Summary
RapTB builds on the Trajectory Balance (TB) objective by adding an auxiliary loss. For each prefix, it computes a rooted residual: the difference between the TB residual at that prefix and the residual at the root, yielding dense supervision for all prefixes without heterogeneous boundaries. To reduce variance in credit assignment, it propagates terminal feedback along the suffix using a mixed max‑and‑soft absorption: the target for a prefix’s task‑related flow aggregates suffix rewards via a combination of max and LogSumExp, penalized by a distance discount. The total loss combines the TB loss and a weighted auxiliary loss. SubM replaces typical reward‑prioritized replay with a submodular selection: given the union of the current buffer and a new batch, a monotone submodular function scores subsets based on static quality (reward/validity), facility‑location diversity, and a concave length‑coverage term. Greedy maximization under a cardinality constraint yields a near‑optimal buffer that resists collapse.

## Key Results
- On scaffold‑conditioned SMILES generation (Lmax=10), RapTB+SubM achieves validity 0.988, task score 0.844, token entropy 2.726, and FPDiv 0.898, while allocating greater probability to long sequences.
- Under Lmax=15, it maintains long‑horizon coverage (11+ fraction 0.701) and achieves the lowest prefix concentration.
- Across SMILES, Expr24, and CommonGen, RapTB alone reduces termination drift and improves coverage, while SubM further expands replay diversity, leading to consistent gains over TB and SubTB.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Rooted prefix residuals prevent the termination drift observed in windowed subtrajectory methods by restricting dense supervision to windows that start at the root, eliminating conflicting boundary conditions.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of enforcing consistency on arbitrary subtrajectories as in SubTB, RapTB defines a rooted residual for prefix *k* as the difference between the TB residual at *k* and at the root (Eq. 4). This provides O(τ) prefix‑level learning signals without introducing heterogeneous termination boundaries.
- **Core assumption:** The diverse boundary terms in SubTB (each window’s pseudo‑terminal) can be simultaneously satisfied by shifting the global termination probabilities, causing biased length predictions. Rooted windows share a common boundary (the root) and thus cannot be cheated in this way.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - **[Abstract]** “RapTB … anchors subtrajectory supervision at the root and propagates terminal rewards to intermediate prefixes … providing dense prefix‑level learning signals.”
  - **[Section 3.4]** “RapTB restricts dense supervision to rooted … eliminating destabilizing boundary conditions.”
  - **[Section 4.5]** SubTB exhibits severe termination drift (logp term ≈ –80) while RapTB maintains near‑natural termination probabilities.
- **Break condition:** If the mix weight *η* is set too high, the dense rooted signals may dominate the global TB anchor and could still induce length bias. Or if the horizon cap *K* is too short, long‑range credit may be insufficient.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Absorbed suffix rewards reduce variance in prefix credit assignment by backing up high‑reward evidence along the sampled suffix via max and soft aggregations.
- **Mechanism:** For a prefix *k*, the method computes an absorbed target *u*ₜ₉ₜₖ from suffix rewards *u*ⱼ (*j* ≥ *k*) using a combination of max and LogSumExp with a distance penalty. The auxiliary loss then penalizes the difference between the rooted residual’s task‑only term and this target (Eq. 8).
- **Core assumption:** Terminal‑only feedback is high‑variance for early steps; aggregating suffix evidence yields a smoother, lower‑variance proxy for the missing flow, improving gradients for intermediate prefixes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - **[Abstract]** “propagates terminal rewards to intermediate prefixes via absorbed suffix‑based backups.”
  - **[Section 3.4]** defines the absorbed target (Eqs. 5–8) and the auxiliary loss.
  - **[Table 6]** Ablations: removing reward absorption or using max/soft‑only degrades score/diversity, supporting the benefit of the mixed backup.
- **Break condition:** The distance penalty *ρ* and softness *β* must be tuned; if *ρ* is too large, distant high rewards are ignored, potentially under‑estimating prefix credit. Over‑absorption (high *α* or β) could bias prefixes toward optimistic estimates.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Submodular replay (SubM) mitigates replay‑induced collapse by selecting a fixed‑size buffer subset that jointly maximizes quality (reward/validity), diversity (facility‑location), and length coverage.
- **Mechanism:** At each refresh, the union of old buffer and new batch forms a ground set *G*. A monotone submodular function *f*(*S*) combines a static score per sample, a facility‑location coverage term over similarities, and a concave‑over‑counts length histogram term. Greedy selection yields a near‑optimal subset (guaranteed (1 – 1/e) approximation).
- **Core assumption:** Reward‑prioritized replay concentrates on a few modes and biases the training distribution; explicitly optimizing a submodular objective preserves a representative, diverse subset that maintains long‑horizon coverage.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - **[Abstract]** “Submodular Replay (SubM), a submodular subset selection that balances reward, diversity, and length coverage.”
  - **[Section 3.5]** details the objective (Eq. 11) and greedy algorithm.
  - **[Table 1, 6]** Enabling SubM improves score, entropy, FPDiv, and length fractions across tasks; ablations show each component (reward, diversity, length) contributes.
- **Break condition:** The similarity measure must be semantically meaningful; in tasks where token overlap does not reflect true diversity (e.g., text with synonyms), the diversity term may fail. Greedy selection is only approximate; with very large candidate pools the solution may be sub‑optimal. Over‑weighting length coverage could skew the buffer toward extreme lengths at the cost of quality.

## Foundational Learning
### Trajectory Balance (TB)
- **Why needed:** TB is the base objective for GFlowNets; RapTB adds rooted auxiliary terms on top of TB while preserving its global balance condition. Understanding TB’s normalizer *Z* and its role in reward‑proportional sampling is prerequisite.
- **Quick check:** What does the TB loss enforce, and why is a global normalizer *Z* introduced? How does it relate to detailed balance?

### Submodular Set Functions
- **Why needed:** SubM’s selection step relies on maximizing a monotone submodular objective; the property of diminishing returns enables a greedy algorithm with provable approximation guarantees.
- **Quick check:** Define submodularity and explain why a greedy algorithm achieves a (1 – 1/e) approximation for monotone submodular maximization under a cardinality constraint.

### Experience Replay in GFlowNets
- **Why needed:** Replay buffers are standard in training but reward‑prioritized replay leads to mode collapse in GFlowNets. SubM directly addresses this by altering the selection criterion; understanding the failure mode is crucial for effective use.
- **Quick check:** How does reward‑prioritized replay cause “rich‑get‑richer” dynamics in GFlowNets? What biases does it introduce in the training distribution?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. **LLM backbone** (LoRA‑fine‑tuned) with two heads: token logits *q*θ(· | prefix) and termination logits *q*θ(⊤ | prefix).  
  2. **Replay buffer** of fixed capacity *B* storing (prefix, terminal, reward, length).  
  3. **Training loop** with two losses:  
     - TB loss: `ΔTB(ξ)²`.  
     - Auxiliary loss: rooted residuals plus absorbed targets (Eq. 8), optionally detaching termination logits.  
  4. **SubM refresh**: after collecting a new batch, compute *f*(*S*) and greedily select new buffer *S* ⊆ *G*.
- **Critical path:** Rollout → reward computation → TB residual → compute auxiliary targets (rooted, absorbed) → total loss *L*<sub>RapTB</sub> → backprop → submodular buffer refresh → repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Auxiliary weight *η*: stronger dense guidance vs. risk of over‑regularizing the global TB anchor.  
  - Absorption parameters (*β*, *ρ*, *α*): smoothing vs. horizon reach.  
  - SubM weights (*w*<sub>rew</sub>, *w*<sub>div</sub>, *w*<sub>len</sub>): reward‑only may collapse reward‑only; diversity‑only may dilute reward; tuning needed.  
  - Buffer size *B*: larger *B* gives more coverage but slower refresh and higher compute.  
  - LoRA rank vs. full fine‑tuning: efficiency vs. expressivity.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Prefix collapse:** survival ratio decays fast with depth, top‑1 prefix mass increases.  
  - **Termination drift:** mean logp term becomes extremely negative (over‑suppression) or positive (runaway generation).  
  - **Validity collapse:** Acc drops (e.g., SMILES validity < 0.6).  
  - **Length bias:** histogram skewed toward very short or very long bins.  
  - **Replay stagnation:** buffer filled with near‑duplicates, low diversity metrics (Entropy/FPDiv).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Scaffold‑conditioned SMILES** (L<sub>max</sub> = 10): verify baseline RapTB+SubM improves validity (~ 0.99), Score (~ 0.84), Entropy (~ 2.7) and length fractions vs. TB/SubTB.  
  2. **Expr24 arithmetic** (binary reward): check coverage (Unique ✓ > 300), KL/JS near oracle values, and that logp term stays close to 0 (no drift).  
  3. **CommonGen diagnostic**: BLEU > 30, natural length (~12), and termination logits near reference (∆logp term ≈ 0) to confirm RapTB+SubM respects linguistic priors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can replay buffer design and learning objective be jointly optimized rather than treated as separate components?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "future objectives that explicitly couple coverage‑aware replay with selective, adaptively weighted subtrajectory learning for robust autoregressive GFlowNet training." This identifies coupling replay and objective as a key open direction.
- **Why unresolved:** Current RapTB and SubM are modular improvements; no framework exists to co‑adapt replay selection criteria and the credit assignment objective.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A unified training framework where replay subset selection and loss weighting are differentiable or meta‑optimized end‑to‑end; empirical gains on coverage and stability compared to sequential application of replay and objective.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the theoretically grounded principle for setting the mixing parameter α between max and soft absorbed backups in RapTB?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Ablations (Table 6) show that mixing max (α=1) improves Score while mixing soft (α=0) improves Entropy; the chosen α=0.5 balances both. No analysis explains why this specific balance is optimal or task‑dependent.
- **Why unresolved:** The choice of α is heuristic; the trade‑off between aggressive credit (max) and smoothed credit (soft) lacks a theoretical or empirical guide across tasks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Sensitivity analysis of α on diverse tasks (e.g., sparse vs. dense rewards) with metrics linking α to variance reduction and bias‑variance trade‑off; possibly a bound relating α to trajectory horizon or reward variability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the absorption horizon cap K significantly affect performance, and what is the optimal scheduling or adaptation strategy for K?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] RapTB sets K = Lmax (Eq. C.3). No ablation explores shorter horizons or adaptive K. The distance discount γ is used but its interaction with K is not studied.
- **Why unresolved:** Using full‑horizon absorption may be wasteful or unstable for very long trajectories; a shorter K could reduce variance but lose long‑range credit.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments varying K (e.g., K = Lmax, K = Lmax/2, adaptive K) on long‑horizon tasks like SMILES Lmax=15, measuring metrics like Prefix Top1 and long‑sequence quality.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the submodular replay objective in SubM be extended to explicitly model length‑dependent reward distributions without manual binning?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] SubM uses discrete length bins with uniform weights (flen in Eq. 12). The paper notes this as a design choice; no continuous or learned length‑coverage term is explored.
- **Why unresolved:** Binning is coarse and may not capture smooth length‑quality trade‑offs; learning a continuous length coverage function could improve allocation for tasks with non‑uniform optimal lengths.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace fixed bins with a parametric concave function of length (e.g., a small network) and show improved Frac[11+] or reduced length bias on SMILES Lmax=15.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** To what extent do RapTB’s improvements generalize beyond LLM‑based autoregressive GFlowNets to other architectures (e.g., graph‑based or flow‑based models)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] All experiments use LLM (Llama) backbones. The method is presented for "terminable prefix trees" (Section 3.1), but the core ideas (rooted prefixes, absorbed rewards) might apply more broadly.
- **Why unresolved:** Prefix collapse and biased replay are claimed general issues, but validation is limited to LLM‑based GFlowNets; other architectures have different structural properties (e.g., non‑tree graphs).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Re‑implement RapTB+SubM on non‑LLM GFlowNet benchmarks (e.g., molecular graph generation with DAG structures) and measure similar collapse metrics (prefix concentration, length bias).

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameter values for SubM (λ_div, λ_len) and validity gating details.
- Ambiguity in auxiliary horizon K implementation (capping vs. full suffix use).
- Undisclosed reference LM checkpoint and log‑prob computation method.
- Limited evaluation across only three tasks; no statistical significance testing.
- Potential over‑reliance on handcrafted similarity measures (Morgan, k‑gram) that may not capture true diversity.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** RapTB+SubM improves quality‑diversity trade‑offs on reported tasks (strong empirical support).
- **Medium confidence:** Mechanistic explanations (rooted residuals, absorbed rewards) and cross‑task generality.
- **Low confidence:** Robustness to unseen modalities and full hyperparameter sensitivity (insufficient evidence).
