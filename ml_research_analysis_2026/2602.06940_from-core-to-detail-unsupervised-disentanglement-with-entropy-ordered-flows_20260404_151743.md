# From Core to Detail: Unsupervised Disentanglement with Entropy-Ordered Flows

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06940
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06940
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: EOFlows sort latent dimensions by explained entropy, enabling adaptive core-detail splits that achieve strong disentanglement (Manifold Total Correlation ~0.012), high compression, and robust denoising with a manageable likelihood trade-off.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Entropy-Ordered Flows (EOFlows), a normalizing-flow framework for unsupervised learning of stable, semantically meaningful representations. EOFlows order latent dimensions by explained manifold entropy, analogous to PCA’s explained variance, allowing an adaptive injective flow where a flexible core subset captures essential structure while remaining dimensions encode details and noise. Training combines maximum likelihood with novel regularization: Jacobian orthogonality to encourage statistical independence of factors and noise inflation–deflation to force the core manifold to be noise‑insensitive. On CelebA (28×28×3), EOFlows yield a sharply decaying entropy spectrum, enabling compression (e.g., 50 core dimensions retain essentials) and effective denoising, while reducing manifold total correlation to ~0.012 with only a modest increase in negative log‑likelihood.

## Method Summary
EOFlows extend normalizing flows by post‑hoc sorting of latent dimensions according to their explained manifold entropy, estimated from Jacobian logs after training. This ordering permits an adaptive injective transformation: at inference one retains a core of high‑entropy dimensions and zeros out the low‑entropy ones, effectively projecting data onto a low‑dimensional core manifold. Training uses Maximum Manifold Likelihood (MML), which augments the standard log‑likelihood with two regularizers: (i) a Total Disentanglement term that promotes orthogonal Jacobian columns (approximate statistical independence) and (ii) isotropic Gaussian noise inflation applied to inputs; the corresponding “deflation” (zeroing detail latents) filters noise at test time. The framework thus jointly optimizes density estimation, manifold discovery, and factor disentanglement.

## Key Results
- Sharp entropy decay spectrum: on CelebA, a small core (C=50) suffices to retain essential structure, enabling high compression.
- Strong disentanglement: Manifold Total Correlation reduced to ~0.012 at λ_TC = 1.0.
- Robust denoising with controllable likelihood trade‑off: noise inflation–deflation removes noise while preserving semantics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Entropy‑ordered core‑detail split**  
- **Claim**: Latent dimensions are sorted by explained manifold entropy, enabling adaptive core‑detail splits.  
- **Mechanism**: The manifold entropy \( H_j = H(\{j\}) \) for each latent dimension is estimated via expectation over the trained flow. Dimensionality reduction (setting \( z_D = 0 \)) retains the core \( C \) dimensions with highest entropy, discarding low‑entropy dimensions that capture noise/detail.  
- **Core assumption**: Explained entropy monotonically decreases from core to detail dimensions; the decay spectrum sharply saturates at the noise entropy \( H_{\sigma_\epsilon} \).  
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] “EOFlows sort latent dimensions by their explained entropy… enabling adaptive injective flows… retain only the \( C \) most important variables.”; [section 3.3] “HS :=H(U S) =E_{z∼p(Z)} [−log(q_S(US =g(z)))]… When S={j}… we get the explained entropy needed for sorting.”; [corpus] Neighbors discuss dynamic bottlenecks or importance ordering (e.g., “Nested Dropout normalizing flows”), but EOFlows uniquely tie ordering to manifold entropy computed post‑hoc.  
- **Break condition**: If the entropy spectrum is flat (no sharp decay), dimension selection fails; core/detail split is arbitrary and loses interpretability.

**Mechanism 2: Maximum Manifold Likelihood (MML) training with Jacobian regularization**  
- **Claim**: MML jointly optimizes density, manifold, and disentanglement via Jacobian‑based regularization.  
- **Mechanism**: MML loss adds terms \( \lambda_C L_C + \lambda_D L_D + \lambda_{C\perp D} L_{C\perp D} \) to standard ML loss. Total Disentanglement (\( \lambda_{TC} L_{TC} \)) with \( L_{TC} = \sum_i L_i - L_{ML} \) enforces approximate orthogonality of Jacobian columns everywhere (i.e., \( J_i(f(x)) \perp J_j(f(x)) \) for \( i \neq j \)).  
- **Core assumption**: Vanishing manifold total correlation \( I_{TC} \to 0 \) (or small \( L_{TC} \)) implies statistical independence of latent factors (IMA principle). Orthogonal Jacobians yield a curvilinear coordinate system that disentangles factors.  
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 3.2] “L_{MML}(x) = L_{ML}(x) + \lambda_{C\perp D} \cdot L_{C\perp D}(x)… perfect convergence… ITC→0… J_i(f(x))⊥J_j(f(x)) ∀i≠j.”; [section 3.4] “Total Disentanglement… realized as DisDenL… L_{MML} = L_{ML} + \lambda_{TC} L_{TC}.”  
- **Break condition**: If \( \lambda_{TC} \) is too high, the model underfits (NLL increases) due to excessive orthogonality constraint limiting expressivity. If too low, features remain entangled (high \( L_{TC} \), flat entropy spectrum).

**Mechanism 3: Noise inflation‑deflation for stable disentanglement**  
- **Claim**: Training on noise‑inflated samples forces the core manifold to be noise‑insensitive, enabling denoising via bottleneck.  
- **Mechanism**: Training samples are corrupted with isotropic Gaussian noise \( x_\epsilon = x + \sigma_\epsilon \epsilon \). The flow learns to represent both clean data and inflated distribution. At inference, the detail dimensions \( z_D \) are set to zero (deflation) to filter noise from the core \( z_C \), reconstructing a denoised sample from the manifold \( M_C \).  
- **Core assumption**: The noise level \( \sigma_\epsilon \) sets a lower bound on explainable entropy \( H_j \); dimensions with entropy near \( H_{\sigma_\epsilon} \) capture only noise/detail. Strong inflation (\(\sigma_\epsilon \ll\) signal scale) pushes disentanglement regularization to affect only core dimensions.  
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 4.1] “We find that for successful DisDenL we rely training on inflated data samples… EOFlows permit sampling noise‑free samples via a dynamic bottleneck.”; [section 4.2] “Higher noise levels allow better DisL at constant \( \lambda_{TC} \) while weakening the tradeoff.”  
- **Break condition**: If \( \sigma_\epsilon \) is too large relative to signal scale, semantic structure is destroyed and core features become uninterpretable. If too small, disentanglement fails (as on EMNIST with \( \sigma_\epsilon=0.01 \) vs. \( 0.1\)).

## Foundational Learning
- **Normalizing Flows (Bijective transformations with tractable likelihood)**  
  - Why needed here: EOFlows are built on exact likelihood training via invertible decoder \( g(z) \). The Jacobian determinant enables computation of manifold entropies and mutual informations.  
  - Quick check question: How does the change‑of‑variables formula link latent standard normal prior to data density, and what role does the decoder Jacobian play?

- **Manifold Hypothesis & Intrinsic Dimension**  
  - Why needed here: The core‑detail split assumes data lies on a low‑dimensional manifold. The entropy spectrum’s saturation at noise level estimates the effective intrinsic dimension \( C \).  
  - Quick check question: Why would a normalizing flow trained on data with intrinsic dimension \( C \ll D \) struggle if it is fully bijective (no injective allowance)?

- **Information-Theoretic Metrics (Manifold Entropy, Total Correlation)**  
  - Why needed here: MML training reweights manifold entropies \( L_S \) and mutual informations \( L_{S\perp T} \) derived from the push‑forward density’s factorization. These are estimated via Jacobian logs.  
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between pointwise manifold mutual information \( L_{S\perp T}(x) \) and the decoder Jacobian subdeterminants?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Invertible encoder/decoder network → Jacobian regularization (computes \( L_{TC} \) via JVPs) → Entropy estimation (post‑training, average \( L_j \) over validation set) → Inference bottleneck (set \( z_D = 0 \) for reconstruction).

**Critical path**:  
1. Train flow with MML loss (\( \lambda_{TC} > 0 \)) on inflated data (\( x + \sigma_\epsilon \epsilon \)).  
2. Validate on clean data: compute entropy spectrum \( H_j \) across latent dimensions; select core dimension \( C \) where entropy plateaus near \( H_{\sigma_\epsilon} \).  
3. For generation or denoising, pass \( [z_C, 0] \) through decoder \( g \) to obtain samples from the core manifold \( M_C \).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **\( \lambda_{TC} \) vs. NLL trade‑off**: Higher \( \lambda_{TC} \) improves disentanglement but increases negative log‑likelihood; choose \( \lambda_{TC} \approx 0.1–1.0 \) for sharp entropy decay without large NLL increase.
- **Noise inflation level \( \sigma_\epsilon \)**: Must exceed signal‑independent noise in data; too high corrupts semantics, too low prevents disentanglement (e.g., EMNIST: \( \sigma_\epsilon=0.1 \) works, 0.01 fails).
- **Batch size for \( L_{TC} \) estimation**: Requires \( B \geq D \) for accurate estimation, which is memory‑heavy for high‑dimensional latents; may need subsampling or smarter estimators.

**Failure signatures**:
- Flat entropy spectrum: model failed to order features → \( \lambda_{TC} \) too low or architecture insufficiently expressive.
- Poor reconstructions through bottleneck: core manifold does not capture signal → \( \sigma_\epsilon \) too small or underfitting.
- Blurry/uninterpretable archetypes: disentanglement failed → check \( \lambda_{TC} \) and \( \sigma_\epsilon \); may need more training steps or larger networks.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **EMNIST sanity check**: Train with \( \sigma_\epsilon \in \{0.01,0.03,0.1\} \), \( \lambda_{TC} \in [0,10] \). Verify NLL increases and \( L_{TC} \) decreases with \( \lambda_{TC} \). Inspect Jacobian columns for interpretable patterns (Fig. 3).
2. **Entangled Digits**: Create synthetic superposition of two digits; verify first latent dimension correlates with mixing coefficient \( \alpha \) (goal: discover true factors).
3. **CelebA**: Train with high \( \sigma_\epsilon=0.1 \), varying \( \lambda_{TC} \). Compute entropy spectrum (Fig. 5); verify sharp decay. Generate bottleneck reconstructions (C=50) to assess denoising/compression (Fig. 8).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Do identifiability results for Independent Mechanism Analysis extend to EOFlows under soft Jacobian orthogonality constraints?  
- **Basis in paper**: Authors state: “Since EOFlows build upon IMA, these results may hold here as well, but we leave a detailed analysis for future work.” (Related Work)  
- **Why unresolved**: A theoretical proof is lacking that the learned latent variables correspond to true independent factors of variation when orthogonality is enforced as a soft regularizer rather than a hard constraint.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A theorem establishing conditions (e.g., sufficient regularity, noise level, model capacity) under which the mapping from true factors to observed data is identifiable in the EOFlow framework, possibly extending Buchholz et al. (2022) to soft orthogonality.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can the stochastic estimator for Total Disentanglement be made scalable without requiring batch size at least equal to the latent dimensionality?  
- **Basis in paper**: Authors note: “Although this limitation requires sufficient GPU memory for large D, we believe it can be lifted, but leave this for future works.” (Sec. 4.2)  
- **Why unresolved**: The current requirement (batch size ≥ D) makes training on very high‑dimensional data (e.g., high‑resolution images) memory‑prohibitive; a more efficient estimator is needed.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Development of a variance‑reduced, low‑memory estimator (e.g., based on random projections, streaming PCA, or subsampled Jacobians) that approximates LTC with sublinear batch size, validated on datasets with D > 10^4.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is a principled, automatic method for selecting the number of core latent dimensions C from the entropy spectrum after training?  
- **Basis in paper**: The paper observes a sharp decay of entropy and saturation at the noise level, suggesting a natural cut‑off, but all experiments manually pick C or report curves without an automated rule (Sec. 4.5).  
- **Why unresolved**: Manual selection hinders practical deployment and theoretical analysis; a reliable, dataset‑independent criterion for determining C is needed.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An algorithm (e.g., based on the derivative of the entropy spectrum, change‑point detection, or statistical test against the noise baseline) that reliably recovers the true intrinsic dimension on synthetic datasets with known C and works robustly on real data.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Can EOFlows be successfully applied to non‑image modalities (e.g., audio, text, graphs) to learn interpretable and disentangled representations?  
- **Basis in paper**: All experiments are on image datasets (EMNIST, CelebA). The method’s assumptions (low‑dimensional manifold, independent factors) are plausible for other data types, but this remains untested (Sec. 4).  
- **Why unresolved**: Different modalities have distinct structure (sequential, discrete, relational) and the encoder/decoder architecture based on affine coupling may not be optimal; the impact of noise inflation on such data is unknown.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Benchmarks on standard disentanglement datasets from other domains (e.g., spoken digit recognition for audio, syntax‑controlled text generation, molecular property prediction) showing comparable entropy spectra, disentanglement scores, and compression benefits.

## Limitations
- Entropy ordering stability: post‑hoc estimation sensitive to sample size; sharp decay only shown on two small datasets.
- Scalability: L_TC estimation requires batch size ≥ latent dimension, limiting high‑dimensional latents.
- Noise inflation tuning: \(\sigma_\epsilon\) is dataset‑dependent; improper tuning risks semantic corruption or failed disentanglement.

## Confidence
- **Entropy-ordered core-detail split**: Medium
- **Maximum Manifold Likelihood (MML) disentanglement**: Medium
- **Inflation-deflation denoising**: Low-to-medium
