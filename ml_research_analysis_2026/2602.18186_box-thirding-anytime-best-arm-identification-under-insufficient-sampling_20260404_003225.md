# Box Thirding: Anytime Best Arm Identification under Insufficient Sampling

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.18186  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18186  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** B3 is an anytime best arm identification algorithm optimal for the data-poor condition (T < N) with candidate set size Θ(T) and misidentification probability exp(−Ω(max(Nϵ, ϵ²)·T/N)), matching Successive Halving without requiring T as input.

## Executive Summary
Box Thirding (B3) is designed for the challenging regime where the number of arms N exceeds the total sampling budget T, making it impossible to sample all arms adequately. It operates as an anytime algorithm, requiring no prior knowledge of T, by hierarchically organizing arms into boxes and performing ternary comparisons: lifting the strongest for more samples, shifting the median for later comparison, and discarding the weakest. This process maintains a dynamic candidate set while efficiently screening arms. Theoretically, B3 achieves optimal screening capacity (candidate set size linear in T) and error rates comparable to Successive Halving, but without assuming T is known. Empirical results on a large real-world dataset confirm robust performance across varying noise levels.

## Method Summary
B3 employs a hierarchical box structure where each box Box(l, j) holds up to three arms at level l with deferment count j. The core procedure ARRANGE_BOX processes a full box by sorting arms on empirical mean: the best is lifted to Box(l+1,0) with ⌈r₀ˡ⌉ additional pulls, the median is shifted to Box(l, j+1) without new pulls, and the weakest is discarded. The algorithm alternates between a top-down sweep (processing boxes from highest to lowest level) and base-level replenishment (adding new arms to Box(0,0) if space exists). The base pull count r₀ ≈ 1.728 is fixed to optimize theoretical bounds. At any point, the candidate set consists of arms at level 0 boxes, and the output is the arm with highest mean in the topmost Box(L,0). This design allows B3 to adapt seamlessly to any fixed budget T without prior specification.

## Key Results
- Candidate set size c₀ scales as Θ(T), achieving optimal screening capacity under the data-poor condition (T < N).
- Misidentification probability bound exp(−Ω(max(Nϵ, ϵ²)·T/N)), matching the order of Successive Halving but without requiring T as input.
- Empirical evaluation on the New Yorker Cartoon Caption Contest dataset (N=5,513, T=10,000) demonstrates lower simple regret than Uniform Sampling, BUCB, and BSH across Gaussian, Bernoulli, and power-law noise regimes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** B3 reduces the candidate set size at a rate of approximately 1/2 per level through ternary comparisons, despite using only local ternary decisions.  
- **Mechanism:** When a box of three arms is full, the arm with the largest empirical mean is lifted (promoted with additional sampling to the next level), the median is shifted (deferred without additional samples to a later comparison with an increased deferment count), and the weakest is discarded. This creates a flow where, from any full box at level l, the fraction of arms eventually promoted to level l+1 is the sum of a geometric series 1/3 + 1/3² + … = 1/2.  
- **Core assumption:** The reward distributions are 1-sub-Gaussian, and empirical ordering within a ternary comparison provides a statistically sound signal. The SHIFT mechanism preserves borderline arms for future refinement.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes ternary process; Section 3.2 derives the 1/2 halving fraction; weak direct support from neighboring papers.  
- **Break condition:** The 1/2 halving rate holds asymptotically only if the number of arms flowing through a level nₗ is large; for very small nₗ (e.g., 3–4), integer effects and distribution cause deviation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** B3 achieves optimal screening capacity c₀ = Θ(T) under the data-poor condition (T < N), matching the screening capability of uniform sampling without prior knowledge of the budget.  
- **Mechanism:** Arms enter base level with fixed pulls r₀. The hierarchical processing consumes total budget T_C = Σₗ nₗ r₀ˡ, which scales linearly with initial candidate count n₀. Given total budget T, this forces n₀ = Θ(T), defining candidate set size.  
- **Core assumption:** Budget allocation per level follows ⌈r₀ˡ⌉ with r₀ ≈ 1.728; analysis assumes primary budget consumption by candidates and geometric progression in pulls.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Proposition 4.8 and Appendix D.2.2 derive c₀ = Θ(T); Theorem 4.7 links c₀ to non-inclusion probability; abstract states optimal screening capacity.  
- **Break condition:** Θ(T) scaling relies on stable hierarchical processing; for extremely small T (e.g., T < 2), the model fails as no full box forms.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** B3 provides valid anytime performance by balancing non-inclusion probability (best arm not in candidate set) and misidentification probability (choosing suboptimal arm from candidate set), yielding bound exp(−Ω(max(Nϵ, ϵ²)·T/N)), comparable to SH without needing T.  
- **Mechanism:** Continuous top-down sweep and base-level replenishment create dynamic equilibrium where candidate set C is refreshed. Error decomposition: P(μ₁ − μ_{a_T} > ϵ) ≤ P(μ₁ − μ*(C) > ϵ/2) + P(μ*(C) − μ_{a_T} > ϵ/2). B3 ensures both terms have similar exponential orders; misidentification term adapts SH analysis treating C as fixed-budget problem.  
- **Core assumption:** Decomposition is exhaustive and components largely independent; SH analysis assumes candidate set acts like fixed-budget on |C| arms.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract gives bound; Section 4.2 states decomposition and main theorem; Proposition 4.12 provides misidentification bound within C.  
- **Break condition:** Bound holds but may be loose in data-rich regimes (T ≥ N log N) where non-inclusion term vanishes and misidentification term dominates; SH-derived sub-bound may not be optimal there.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Box(l,j) -> ARRANGE_BOX(l,j,D) -> lifts to Box(l+1,0), shifts to Box(l,j+1), discards to D. Main loop: Top-down sweep (from L down to 0) then Base-level replenishment (new arms to Box(0,0)). Output: arm in Box(L,0) with max mean.

**Critical path:**  
1. Initialization: L=0, J_L=0, D=∅; Box(0,0) starts with few arms.  
2. Sweep Phase: For l from current L down to 0, for j from J_l down to 0, if Box(l,j) is full and Box(l+1,0) & Box(l,j+1) have space, call ARRANGE_BOX.  
3. Replenishment Phase: If Box(0,0) has space and unexamined arms exist, select one uniformly, pull once, and lift to Box(0,0).  
4. Update L (highest non-empty Box(l,0)) and J_l; loop until total pulls reach T.  
5. Output: arm in Box(L,0) with largest empirical mean.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Ternary vs. Binary Elimination:** Ternary choice (lift, shift, discard) trades faster screening for higher candidate retention; SHIFT preserves borderline arms but defers evaluation.  
- **Sample Reuse vs. Fresh Estimates:** SHIFTed arms reuse stale empirical means, computationally efficient but risky under volatile distributions; fixed-budget SH discards and requeues all survivors, requiring known schedule.  
- **Hierarchical vs. Flat Screening:** Concentrates samples on promising arms early but risks prematurely discarding a noisy-early best arm.  
- **Hyperparameter r₀:** Fixed at ≈1.728 for optimal bound; assumes specific sub-Gaussian profile, may not adapt to varying noise.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Screening Failure:** Best arm never candidate; symptoms: c₀ much smaller than expected, L remains low despite many pulls.  
- **Misidentification Failure:** Best arm in C but not returned; symptoms: suboptimal arm with slightly higher noisy empirical mean promoted to high levels.  
- **Stagnation:** Algorithm loops but L and Box(L,0) size do not grow; indicates bottleneck where top-level boxes full but lower-level ARRANGE conditions unmet.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Functionality Sanity Check:** Run B3 on tiny instance (N=5, T=10); manually track boxes, level shifts, and final Box(L,0); verify deterministic rewards yield true best if T sufficient; confirm c₀ matches predicted size.  
2. **Candidate Set Size vs. Budget:** Plot c₀ (arms ever in Box(0,0)) against T for N >> T; verify linear relationship c₀ ≈ αT for B3/US; contrast with BSH's sub-linear scaling; probes Mechanism 2.  
3. **Error Decomposition under Noise:** Use NYCCC or synthetic data; for fixed T, measure non-inclusion rate (best arm never in C) and within-C misidentification rate (best in C but not returned); vary noise level; hypothesis: B3 balances sum (simple regret) across regimes, validating theoretical decomposition.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Sample reuse integration:** How to incorporate sample reuse into B3 without compromising anytime property and theoretical guarantees? Current version discards historical samples; integrating reuse while preserving bounds is open.
2. **Non-subgaussian distributions:** Do theoretical bounds hold for non-subgaussian rewards (e.g., bounded or heavy-tailed)? Analysis assumes 1-subgaussian; extending to other distribution classes is unresolved.
3. **Minimax lower bound:** What is the minimax lower bound on misidentification probability for anytime BAI under data-poor condition? Without matching lower bound, optimality of B3's screening capacity and error rates remains conjectural.
4. **Adaptive r₀ tuning:** Can r₀ be tuned adaptively during execution to improve performance across varying noise or budgets while preserving anytime guarantee? Fixed schedule may be suboptimal; adaptive mechanism is open.

## Limitations
- Baseline algorithms (BSH, BUCB) lack precise implementation details in the paper; original sources [23,14] needed for exact replication.
- NYCCC dataset preprocessing and true mean calculation are not provided; must reproduce from Jain et al. [9] dataset.
- Algorithm variant ambiguity: Algorithm 2 vs. Algorithm 3 (Appendix A); comprehensive version might be required for data-rich regimes (T ≥ N), affecting borderline comparisons.

## Confidence
- Screening capacity Θ(T): High (theoretical derivation), Medium (empirical validation on single dataset).  
- Error bound exp(−Ω(max(Nϵ, ϵ²)·T/N)): High (derivation), Medium (matching SH without T input).  
- Lower simple regret than baselines: Medium (single dataset, baseline clarity issues).  
- 1/2 halving rate: Medium (asymptotic result, integer effects for small nₗ).
