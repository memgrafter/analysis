# Orthogonal Model Merging

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05943
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05943
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** OrthoMerge achieves 46.25% average accuracy on task-specific benchmarks, surpassing individual experts (44.71%) and all Euclidean-space baselines, with reduced catastrophic forgetting on out-of-domain tasks.

## Executive Summary
Orthogonal Model Merging (OrthoMerge) solves the geometric destruction problem in LLM model merging by performing operations on the Riemannian manifold of the orthogonal group, thereby preserving hyperspherical energy. For models finetuned with Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), it merges task-specific orthogonal matrices via magnitude-corrected averaging in the Lie algebra so(d). For models finetuned with other methods (non-OFT), it decouples an orthogonal component (capturing structural rotations) from a residual additive component via Procrustes analysis and merges them separately. Empirically, on Llama-3.1-8B OFT models, OrthoMerge achieves 46.25% average accuracy on task-specific benchmarks, exceeding both individual experts and all Euclidean baselines, and attains 41.80% average on out-of-domain benchmarks, indicating reduced catastrophic forgetting. For non-OFT models from MergeBench, integrating OrthoMerge with TIES yields a 2.41% gain in average task performance, demonstrating broad effectiveness across merging scenarios.

## Method Summary
OrthoMerge provides a unified merging framework. For OFT models, it directly uses the block-diagonal orthogonal matrices R_i, maps them to the Lie algebra so(d) via the inverse Cayley transform to obtain skew-symmetric matrices Q_i, applies a magnitude-correction factor c to compute the scaled average Q_merged, and maps back via the Cayley transform to get the merged orthogonal matrix R_merged. The merged model is W_merged = R_merged W_0. For non-OFT models (e.g., full finetuning, LoRA), it first extracts an orthogonal component R_i by solving the Orthogonal Procrustes problem between the finetuned weight W_i and base model W_0, using either the Global strategy (full W_i) or Conflict-Aware strategy (only conflicting neuron updates). The residual ρ_i = W_i - R_i W_0 is computed. Then the orthogonal matrices R_i are merged as above to yield R_merged, and the residuals are merged using a Euclidean method such as TIES. The final model is W_final = R_merged W_0 + ρ_merged.

## Key Results
- On Llama-3.1-8B OFT models, OrthoMerge achieves 46.25% average accuracy on task-specific benchmarks, surpassing individual experts (44.71%) and all Euclidean baselines.
- It attains 41.80% average on out-of-domain M-ARC and AGIEval, indicating reduced catastrophic forgetting.
- For non-OFT models from MergeBench, combining OrthoMerge with TIES yields a 2.41% gain in average task performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Merging orthogonal transformations on the orthogonal group manifold preserves the model's hyperspherical energy and prevents destructive interference, leading to better multi-task performance and reduced catastrophic forgetting.
- **Mechanism:** For OFT models, the task-specific orthogonal matrices are mapped to the Lie algebra so(d) via the inverse Cayley transform. A magnitude-corrected average is computed to avoid norm collapse from conflicting directions. The merged skew-symmetric matrix is then mapped back to the orthogonal group via the Cayley transform, yielding an orthogonal rotation R_merged applied to the base model (W_merged = R_merged W_0).
- **Core assumption:** The hyperspherical energy (angular relationships between neurons) is critical for model stability and generalization; preserving it via orthogonal transformations mitigates forgetting.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract states OrthoMerge "performs merging operations on the Riemannian manifold formed by the orthogonal group to preserve the geometric structure" and achieves "reduced catastrophic forgetting."
  - Section 3.2 details the mapping to Lie algebra and magnitude-corrected merging to combat ∥Q_mean∥_F collapse.
  - Corpus: Neighboring papers discuss linearity in task vectors but do not address manifold preservation; evidence for this mechanism is primarily internal. Assumption: The performance gains are directly caused by hyperspherical energy preservation, though this quantity is not measured.
- **Break condition:** If the magnitude correction factor c becomes excessively large due to extreme directional conflict, the merged update may over-amplify noise, degrading performance. Not observed in experiments but a theoretical risk.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** For non-OFT models, decoupling the finetuned weights into an orthogonal component (capturing structural rotations) and a residual additive component allows the beneficial geometric properties of orthogonal merging to be applied even when explicit orthogonal matrices are absent.
- **Mechanism:** The orthogonal component R_i is extracted by solving the Orthogonal Procrustes problem: min ||W_target_i - R_i W_0||_F, s.t. R_i^T R_i = I. Two strategies define W_target_i: (1) Global uses the full finetuned weight; (2) Conflict-Aware uses only the neuron-level conflicting update parts. The extracted {R_i} are merged as in Mechanism 1. The residual ρ_i = W_i - R_i W_0 is merged via standard Euclidean methods (e.g., TIES).
- **Core assumption:** Finetuned models, regardless of method, can be approximated as an orthogonal transformation of the base model plus a residual. The orthogonal part encodes the core structural adaptation beneficial for merging.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract introduces "Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling" to extend to "general models finetuned with non-OFT methods."
  - Section 3.3 formalizes the Procrustes extraction and hybrid merging (W_final = R_merged W_0 + ρ_merged).
  - Corpus: Neighbors like MOMA also seek orthogonal structure for merging, supporting the premise. However, the premise that finetuned models admit a meaningful orthogonal component is not universally tested across all finetuning methods.
- **Break condition:** If the finetuned model's update τ_i is predominantly additive/scaling with minimal rotational component (e.g., some LoRA cases), R_i may be close to identity and the orthogonal merging contribution becomes negligible. Conflict-Aware targets meaningful rotational components.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The specific magnitude-correction scheme in the Lie algebra (scaling by c = (∑∥Qi∥_F) / ∥∑Qi∥_F) is necessary because simple averaging of Q matrices suffers from destructive interference that attenuates the total adaptation strength.
- **Mechanism:** The Frobenius norm ∥Q∥_F approximates the angular intensity of the rotation. Due to the triangle inequality, ∥∑Qi∥_F ≤ ∑∥Qi∥_F with equality only when all Q_i are aligned. The factor c re-inflates the sum to match the total individual adaptation strength, preserving the "magnitude" of the merged update.
- **Core assumption:** The cumulative effect of task-specific adaptations should be comparable in strength to individual adaptations to avoid reverting toward the base model.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 3.2 derives the inequality (Eq. 6) and introduces c (Eqs. 7-8) explicitly to counter magnitude collapse.
  - Section 5.3 ablation in Table 4 shows "Simple Average Q" (without correction) significantly underperforms both individual experts and OrthoMerge, confirming the necessity of the correction.
  - Corpus: Weak direct evidence; neighboring works handle interference via sparsity/SVD but not Lie algebra norm correction. The correction's theoretical justification assumes that the sum of individual norms represents the ideal merged magnitude, which may be invalid if some task vectors are detrimental.
- **Break condition:** The correction assumes total desirable magnitude is ∑∥Qi∥_F. If some Q_i represent noise or poorly trained adaptations, this sum may be inflated, causing overfitting or instability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Riemannian Manifolds & Orthogonal Group O(d)**
  - **Why needed:** OrthoMerge's core innovation is operating on the manifold of orthogonal matrices, where points are rotations/reflections with the constraint R^T R = I. This preserves inner products/norms, unlike Euclidean space.
  - **Quick check:** Why is simple averaging of orthogonal matrices (R_avg = (1/N)∑R_i) invalid on the orthogonal group?
- **Concept: Lie Algebra so(d) and Cayley Transform**
  - **Why needed:** The Lie algebra so(d) (skew-symmetric matrices) provides a linear tangent space around the identity, enabling efficient, unconstrained linear operations (averaging) that can be mapped back to the nonlinear manifold via the Cayley transform (R = (I+Q)(I-Q)^{-1}), preserving orthogonality.
  - **Quick check:** What is the key computational advantage of manipulating Q in so(d) versus directly computing a Riemannian mean of R matrices?
- **Concept: Procrustes Analysis**
  - **Why needed:** For non-OFT models, it solves min ||W_target - R W_0||_F s.t. R^T R = I to extract the best-fit orthogonal transformation R that approximates the finetuned weight change. This is the bridge from Euclidean finetuned weights to an orthogonal component.
  - **Quick check:** Given W_target and W_0, what is the closed-form solution to the Orthogonal Procrustes problem?
- **Concept: Hyperspherical Energy**
  - **Why needed:** It refers to the distribution of weight vectors on a hypersphere; low energy (more uniform) is linked to stable training and generalization. Orthogonal transformations preserve this, while Euclidean perturbations distort it.
  - **Quick check:** How does an orthogonal matrix multiplication (R W_0) preserve the hyperspherical energy of W_0's rows/columns?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Base model W_0 and finetuned models {W_i} -> (OFT: direct to R_i; non-OFT: Procrustes extraction to R_i) -> Convert R_i to Q_i via inverse Cayley -> Compute scaled average Q_merged (with factor c) -> Apply Cayley to get R_merged -> Compute residuals ρ_i = W_i - R_i W_0 -> Merge ρ_i via Euclidean method (e.g., TIES) -> Combine: W_final = R_merged W_0 + ρ_merged.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Determine if finetuned models are OFT (explicit R) or not.
  2. If non-OFT, compute R_i via Procrustes (using either Global or Conflict-Aware W_target_i).
  3. Convert all R_i to Q_i in so(d).
  4. Compute c from {Q_i} norms and calculate Q_merged.
  5. Apply Cayley to get R_merged.
  6. Compute and merge residuals ρ_i with chosen Euclidean method.
  7. Combine to form W_final.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Global vs. Conflict-Aware decoupling: Global captures more total rotation norm but may include non-conflicting parts better handled additively. Conflict-Aware isolates conflicting updates for orthogonal merging but may under-utilize the manifold.
  - Computational cost: Procrustes (SVD) per layer is O(d^3) but done offline; scaling is dominated by LLM size.
  - Residual merging method choice (TA, TIES, etc.) affects final performance; OrthoMerge is agnostic.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low mean ∥Q_merged∥_F compared to mean(∥Q_i∥_F) indicates norm collapse despite correction; check for highly divergent Q_i.
  - Significant drop in out-of-domain (e.g., M-ARC, MMLU) or general (AGIEval) performance compared to base model indicates catastrophic forgetting.
  - R_merged not numerically orthogonal (check R_merged^T R_merged ≈ I) suggests Cayley transform instability from large Q_merged.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Orthogonality Preservation Test:** After merging OFT models, compute ∥R_merged^T R_merged - I∥_F for each layer. Should be near machine zero.
  2. **Norm Distribution Analysis:** Replicate Figure 4. For a non-OFT model pair, plot ∥R_i W_0 - W_0∥_F vs ∥τ_i∥_F for both decoupling strategies. Verify conflict-aware yields lower orthogonal component norms.
  3. **Ablation of Magnitude Correction:** Set c=1 in Eq. 8 and rerun merging on a small subset (2-3 tasks). Expect noticeable drop in task-specific average accuracy, confirming the correction's necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** When does Conflict-Aware decoupling outperform Global decoupling in OrthoMerge, and what task/model characteristics predict the superior strategy?  
  *Basis:* Two decoupling strategies are proposed but not systematically compared.  
  *Why unresolved:* Practitioners lack guidance on strategy selection; choice is currently heuristic.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Large-scale study correlating task similarity metrics, architecture, and finetuning method with performance differences across many models.

- **Open Question 2:** How does OrthoMerge's computational cost scale with model size, and when does the SVD-based extraction become prohibitive compared to Euclidean merging?  
  *Basis:* The paper notes O(d^3) complexity of Riemannian mean is prohibitive but doesn't analyze scaling of their actual implementation.  
  *Why unresolved:* Unknown if OrthoMerge can scale to trillion-parameter models; resource requirements remain unclear.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Runtime and memory benchmarks across model scales (1B to 400B parameters) compared to linear baselines.

- **Open Question 3:** Does OrthoMerge's geometric merging benefit extend to non-transformer architectures (e.g., CNNs, GNNs), or is it specific to transformers?  
  *Basis:* Evaluation only on transformer-based LLMs and a vision-language transformer; generalizability to other architectures is untested.  
  *Why unresolved:* Architectural differences (e.g., weight sharing in CNNs) may affect orthogonal constraints' effectiveness.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Apply OrthoMerge to merge finetuned CNNs and GNNs on multi-task benchmarks, measure catastrophic forgetting.

- **Open Question 4:** What is the theoretical relationship between hyperspherical energy preservation and reduced catastrophic forgetting in OrthoMerge?  
  *Basis:* Paper claims hyperspherical energy preservation mitigates catastrophic forgetting but provides no formal analysis.  
  *Why unresolved:* Empirical correlation exists but causality and quantification are missing; mechanism is not understood.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Theoretical analysis linking hyperspherical energy deviation to performance drops on OOD tasks, plus controlled ablations that isolate this factor.

## Limitations
- Implementation details for MergeBench task-specific checkpoints (e.g., HuggingFace repos, preprocessing) are unspecified, blocking faithful reproduction.
- Computational scaling of SVD-based Procrustes extraction for large layers (e.g., 11k×11k) is unclear and may require approximation; runtime/memory overhead not analyzed.
- The Cayley transform can become numerically unstable if the merged Lie algebra Q_merged has large eigenvalues, risking orthogonality violation and performance degradation.
- The magnitude-correction factor c may over-amplify noise when task vectors are highly divergent, potentially harming generalization.
- The core assumption—that finetuned models admit a meaningful orthogonal component—is not universally validated across all finetuning methods (e.g., LoRA with low rank).
- The paper does not directly quantify hyperspherical energy or its preservation, relying on downstream performance as indirect evidence for geometric benefits.

## Confidence
- Geometric preservation via orthogonal merging (Mechanism 1): Medium. Empirical gains support the idea, but hyperspherical energy is not directly measured; theoretical justification relies on manifold properties.
- Magnitude-correction necessity (Mechanism 3): High. Ablation in Table 4 shows "Simple Average Q" drastically underperforms both experts and OrthoMerge, confirming the correction's critical role.
- Orthogonal-residual decoupling for non-OFT (Mechanism 2): Medium. Gains when combined with TIES are promising, but effectiveness depends on Procrustes extraction quality and conflict-aware threshold; generalizability to diverse architectures remains open.
- Overall superiority over baselines: High on the specific Llama-3.1-8B and MergeBench tasks tested; Low for broader transfer to other model families or domains without further validation.
