# Weak to Strong: VLM-Based Pseudo-Labeling as a Weakly Supervised Training Strategy in Multimodal Video-based Hidden Emotion Understanding Tasks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08057
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08057
- **Reference Count:** 7
- **Primary Result:** Achieved state-of-the-art accuracy on the iMiGUE dataset, lifting performance from under 0.6 to over 0.69 despite severe class imbalance.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenging problem of automatically recognizing concealed emotions in videos, where subtle cues and limited labeled data hinder performance. The authors propose a multimodal weakly supervised framework that leverages a vision-language model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) to generate pseudo-labels and reasoning texts via Chain-of-Thought and Reflection prompting. Visual features are extracted with DINOv2, skeletal cues via OpenPose with explicit inter-frame offsets and a simplified MLP backbone (replacing GCNs), and transcribed text with BERT. An ultra-long Transformer independently models each modality, and features are concatenated for joint fine-tuning. The approach sets a new state-of-the-art on iMiGUE, demonstrating that carefully designed weak supervision and architectural simplifications can overcome data scarcity.

## Method Summary
The system processes video through three streams: (1) portrait crops from YOLO11x are fed to DINOv2 to extract frame features (800 sampled frames); (2) 137 OpenPose keypoints are normalized and augmented with explicit inter-frame offsets (lags 8, 16, 24 frames) to capture micro-movements, then processed by a per-group MLP (body, face, hands) before temporal Transformer modeling (4000 sampled frames); (3) transcribed text from Gemini 2.5 Pro (using CoT+Reflection prompting) is encoded by BERT. Each branch uses a Transformer followed by average pooling and a fully-connected layer to yield 256-d features, which are concatenated and passed through a residual FC block for classification. Training occurs in two stages: first, each branch is pretrained separately on the original iMiGUE training set with Focal Loss and frame resampling augmentation; second, the full model is fine-tuned on the combined original training set and pseudo-labeled test samples.

## Key Results
- Achieved **69.23%** accuracy on iMiGUE test set, exceeding prior state-of-the-art by >9 percentage points (from <60% to >69%).
- Demonstrated that explicit inter-frame offset features improve performance by ~1.9% (66.35% → 68.27%) when using the MLP backbone.
- Showed that the simplified MLP keypoint processor matches or surpasses GCN-based approaches (68.27% vs 67.31%), validating the design.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Explicit Inter-Frame Offsets for Micro-Movements
- **Claim:** Explicit inter-frame offset features for keypoints enable effective modeling of fleeting micro-movements (<0.5s) in ultra-long sequences.
- **Mechanism:** For each keypoint (skeleton, face, hand), pixel coordinate differences are computed between the current frame and the 8th, 16th, and 24th prior frames. These offsets are concatenated with normalized coordinates, creating a feature that directly encodes short-term motion dynamics. This avoids requiring the model to implicitly infer such fine-grained temporal differences from raw absolute positions over thousands of frames.
- **Core assumption:** Micro-expressions/gestures manifest as displacements within ~1 second (25 frames), and explicitly providing these displacements is more sample-efficient than learning them implicitly.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.1 states offsets "avoid micro-expressions... disappearing when the interval... is too large." Table 2 shows DINOv2 with dense sampling outperforms X-CLIP (16 frames), supporting dense temporal modeling.

### Mechanism 2: VLM-Based Pseudo-Labeling with CoT+Reflection
- **Claim:** VLM-based pseudo-labeling with Chain-of-Thought and Reflection prompting generates higher-quality weak supervision for hidden emotion understanding than simple VLM inference or human-annotated labels alone in data-scarce scenarios.
- **Mechanism:** Gemini 2.5 Pro is prompted to first identify FACS/PGCS/GACS action units, then apply handcrafted prior rules to list evidence for "win"/"loss," and finally reflect on potential rule violations before assigning confidence scores. This structured output yields both a pseudo-label (higher-confidence class) and a reasoning text, which are used to augment training.
- **Core assumption:** A capable VLM, guided by a rigorous prompt enforcing evidence-based reasoning and self-critique, produces pseudo-labels that are statistically less noisy than simple heuristics for subtle emotional states.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.1 describe the CoT+Reflection prompt. Table 3 shows adding weak supervision improves accuracy from 63.46% to 66.35%.

### Mechanism 3: MLP Over GCN for Keypoint Spatial Aggregation
- **Claim:** Replacing a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) with a simple Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) for spatial aggregation of keypoint sequences can match or surpass GCN performance by enabling unrestricted feature interaction across keypoints, crucial for modeling synergistic movements across disconnected graph nodes.
- **Mechanism:** GCN aggregates features only along predefined OpenPose graph edges. The MLP, applied per-frame to flattened keypoint features, acts as a fully-connected layer, allowing any keypoint's feature to directly influence any other via learned weights. This permits discovery of synergistic relationships not present in the anatomical graph prior.
- **Core assumption:** The anatomical connectivity prior in OpenPose is incomplete or misleading for hidden emotion micro-expressions, where subtle cues involve correlated displacements across organs (e.g., brow raise + lip corner pull) or both hands. Unrestricted interaction is more valuable than conservative anatomical aggregation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 argues that micro-expressions often involve keypoints in multiple facial organs without connections, so GCN's prior may be inaccurate. Table 1 shows MLP with offsets (68.27%) comparable to GCN with offsets (67.31%).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Weakly-Supervised Learning with Noisy Pseudo-Labels**
  *Why needed here:* The core problem is label scarcity for concealed emotions. The paper uses VLM-generated pseudo-labels to augment the small, imbalanced iMiGUE set. Understanding noise-robust training (e.g., Focal Loss) and risks of confirmatory bias is essential.
  *Quick check:* How does noise rate in pseudo-labels affect the decision boundary, and what techniques (Focal Loss, two-stage training) mitigate this?
- **Concept: Multimodal Temporal Fusion**
  *Why needed here:* The task requires integrating three ultra-long sequences (DINOv2 images, keypoints, BERT text) at different frame rates. Prerequisites include sequence alignment, modality-specific pretraining, and understanding simple fusion (concatenation+FC) versus complex cross-attention.
  *Quick check:* Why did cross-attention modules overfit in this scenario (Section 3.2) compared to simple concatenation, and what does this imply about dataset size for learning cross-modal interactions?
- **Concept: Ultra-Long Sequence Modeling with Transformers**
  *Why needed here:* Videos are thousands of frames long. The system samples densely (4000/800 frames) and uses vanilla Transformers, requiring understanding of quadratic complexity mitigation (via sampling/truncation) and global aggregation (average pooling).
  *Quick check:* What are the trade-offs of average pooling the Transformer output sequence versus using a [CLS] token or learnable query for video-level representation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
1. Input/Preprocessing: Video → YOLO11x (crop) → DINOv2 (frame features); Video → OpenPose (137 keypoints) → offset computation; Video → Gemini 2.5 Pro + CoT/Reflection prompt → pseudo-label + reasoning text.
2. Backbones (pretrained separately): DINOv2 features → Transformer → AvgPool → FC(256); Keypoint triples (skeleton, face, hand) → MLP (spatial) → Transformer → AvgPool → FC(256) each, then concat → FC(256); Text → BERT → FC(256).
3. Fusion/Classify: Concat(256×3) → Residual FC layer → Output head.
4. Training Pipeline: Stage 1: Pretrain each backbone on original train set with Focal Loss, data aug via frame re-sampling. Stage 2: Load pretrained backbones into full model; fine-tune end-to-end on combined (original train + pseudo-labeled test) set.
**Critical path:** Video → [Frame Features + Keypoints + Text] → Modality-specific Transformer/MLP → Global feature (256d) → Concatenation → Classification.
**Design tradeoffs:**
- MLP vs. GCN: Chose MLP for unrestricted interaction vs. GCN's anatomical prior. Tradeoff: MLP more data-hungry but captures synergistic cues.
- Dense Sampling vs. Efficiency: Sampled 4000/800 frames to catch micro-movements vs. computational cost; XCLIP's 16-frame sampling failed.
- Simple Fusion vs. Cross-Attention: Concatenation+FC worked; cross-attention overfitted due to small dataset.
- Weak Supervision Scope: Merged *test set* pseudo-labels into training. Risk: test distribution shift.
**Failure signatures:**
- High variance across inference runs: Despite voting, frame sampling variance may cause unstable predictions.
- Hand keypoint branch dominates errors: Visual inspection shows poor hand detection; noise propagates.
- Performance collapse on new distribution: Hyperparameters tuned via leaderboard feedback without validation set may overfit to iMiGUE test distribution.
**First 3 experiments:**
1. Ablate explicit offset features: Train with/without offsets using same MLP backbone. Expected: +1–2% accuracy with offsets.
2. Compare keypoint backbones: Test GCN, GAT, GIN, and MLP (all with offsets). Expected: MLP matches/exceeds GCN; GIN/GAT underperform.
3. Validate weak supervision: Train full model (with offsets, MLP, pretraining) on (a) original train only, (b) train + pseudo-labeled test. Expected: ~2–3% gain from weak supervision.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Theoretical generalization bounds:** What are the theoretical generalization error bounds for models trained on mixtures of clean and weakly supervised (noisy) labels, and under what noise-rate conditions does performance degrade or improve? The paper lacks a formal framework for learning with noisy labels.
2. **Hand keypoint quality:** To what extent does replacing OpenPose's hand keypoint detector with a state-of-the-art alternative (e.g., MediaPipe Hands) improve performance and stability, given that hand keypoint noise is cited as a major limitation?
3. **Iterative semi-supervised refinement:** Can a semi-supervised learning pipeline that iteratively refines pseudo-labels by filtering high-confidence samples and retraining significantly outperform the single-stage weak supervision used here? The paper observed unstable gains from semi-supervised learning.
4. **Fine-tuning large encoders:** Does fine-tuning the large pre-trained visual encoder (DINOv2) and/or text encoder (beyond BERT-Base) with limited iMiGUE data yield gains, or does data scarcity cause catastrophic overfitting? The paper froze these due to data scarcity.

## Limitations
- Missing VLM prompt details and hyperparameters impede full reproducibility.
- Hand keypoint detection noise (OpenPose failures) likely propagates errors.
- No dedicated validation set; hyperparameter tuning via test leaderboard risks overfitting.
- Merging test pseudo-labels into training may cause distribution shift.
- Claims validated solely on iMiGUE, which suffers from severe class imbalance.

## Confidence
- Explicit offset features: **Medium** – Ablation shows improvement (≈+1.9%), but effectiveness depends on micro-movement timescales and keypoint quality.
- VLM CoT+Reflection pseudo-labeling: **Medium** – Weak supervision yields ≈+2.9% gain; however, prompt design and potential VLM bias are unknown.
- MLP replacing GCN: **Medium** – MLP with offsets matches/exceeds GCN, but may require more data; advantage not universally guaranteed.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt fidelity check:** Replicate the Gemini prompt exactly; evaluate pseudo-label accuracy against human annotations on a held-out iMiGUE subset to measure noise and bias.
2. **Offset significance test:** Train with shuffled/random offsets while preserving feature dimensionality; a significant performance drop confirms that explicit motion encoding is critical.
3. **Cross-dataset evaluation:** Apply the trained model to an external hidden emotion dataset (e.g., MAHNOB-HCI) without fine-tuning; poor generalization would indicate overfitting to iMiGUE-specific artifacts.
