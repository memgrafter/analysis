# Infusion: Shaping Model Behavior by Editing Training Data via Influence Functions

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.09987
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09987
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** Editing only 0.2% of CIFAR-10 training examples (100 out of 45,000) induced target behavior with 100% success across 2000 experiments, increasing target class probability by an average of 23.2 percentage points and flipping top-1 predictions from 10% to 37.4%.

## Executive Summary
Infusion introduces a method to steer model behavior by minimally editing existing training data rather than adding new examples. It leverages scalable influence function approximations (EK-FAC) to identify the most influential training documents for a desired behavior, then computes small, targeted perturbations to those documents using projected gradient descent (PGD). The perturbations are optimized to maximize a chosen behavioral metric on a validation set. Experimental results on CIFAR-10 demonstrate extremely high efficacy: modifying just 0.2% of the training set leads to guaranteed success across thousands of trials, dramatically increasing target class probabilities and flipping predictions. The approach also shows limited cross-architecture transfer and, in language models, amplifies existing behaviors but struggles with high-confidence discrete predictions. This work highlights the power and current boundaries of data-centric model steering.

## Method Summary
Infusion operates in two main phases. First, it approximates the influence of each training example on model behavior using the EK-FAC method, which scales influence estimation to large datasets and models. The top influential examples are selected for editing. Second, for each selected example, the method computes a perturbation via projected gradient descent that, when added to the example, maximizes a predefined behavioral metric (e.g., probability of a target class on a validation set) while respecting constraints on the perturbation (e.g., pixel value bounds). The edited examples then replace their originals in the training set, and the model is trained as usual. The core insight is that a tiny fraction of strategically altered data can dominate the learning dynamics and induce the desired behavior.

## Key Results
- **Vision experiments:** On CIFAR-10, editing 0.2% (100/45,000) of training images achieved 100% success across 2000 independent runs, raising the target class probability by an average of 23.2 percentage points and increasing top-1 prediction flips from 10% to 37.4%.
- **Cross-architecture transfer:** The edits exhibit weak and asymmetric transfer when applied to different architectures (e.g., ResNet vs. CNN), suggesting limited generality across model families.
- **Language model behavior:** Infusion amplifies existing tendencies in small language models but mostly yields likelihood shifts rather than rank changes, struggling with high-confidence predictions and discrete token optimization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method capitalizes on the fact that not all training examples contribute equally to a model's learned behavior; a small subset often has outsized influence on specific outcomes. By precisely identifying these influential points, Infusion can focus its edits on the most "effective" levers. The perturbation optimization then directly manipulates these points to maximize a differentiable behavioral objective, effectively steering the model's decision boundary in the desired direction. The EK-FAC approximation ensures this influence estimation remains computationally tractable even for large models by exploiting the structure of the Fisher information matrix and layer-wise curvature. The combination of accurate influence ranking and careful, constrained perturbation yields a highly efficient data-editing strategy that requires modifying only a minuscule portion of the dataset to achieve near-perfect success on the target behavior.

## Foundational Learning
- **Influence Functions:** A classical statistical tool that estimates the effect of removing or altering a training point on model parameters. *Why needed:* To rank training examples by their impact on the desired behavior without retraining. *Quick check:* Compute exact influence for a few points on a small logistic regression model using the formula `influence = -∇θL(x,y)^T H^{-1} ∇θL(x,y)`.
- **EK-FAC (Eigenvalue-corrected Kronecker-Factored Approximation):** An efficient approximation to influence functions that decouples the Hessian into Kronecker factors and corrects eigenvalues to improve accuracy. *Why needed:* Scales influence estimation to deep networks where exact Hessian inversion is infeasible. *Quick check:* Approximate the Hessian of a two-layer network’s loss and compare EK-FAC influence to a finite-difference baseline for a few samples.
- **Projected Gradient Descent (PGD):** An iterative optimization method that applies gradient steps followed by projection onto a feasible set (e.g., L∞ ball). *Why needed:* To find the smallest perturbation that maximally improves the behavioral metric while keeping the edited example realistic (e.g., pixel values within valid range). *Quick check:* Use PGD to maximize a simple loss on an image with an L∞ constraint; verify convergence and constraint satisfaction.
- **Behavioral Metric:** A differentiable function (e.g., target class probability on a validation set) that quantifies the desired model behavior. *Why needed:* Provides an optimization objective for the perturbations. *Quick check:* Define a metric such as the average output probability for a specific class on a held-out set; compute gradients w.r.t. a training example’s pixels.
- **Cross-architecture Transfer:** The phenomenon that edits computed for one model architecture may affect another architecture to some degree. *Why needed:* To understand whether Infusion edits are model-specific or capture more universal data patterns. *Quick check:* Train two architectures on the same dataset; apply edits identified for architecture A to the training set for architecture B and compare behavioral changes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Training Data -> Influence Estimation (EK-FAC) -> Selection of Influential Examples -> Perturbation Generator (PGD) -> Projection -> Edited Training Data -> Model Training -> Behavioral Evaluation.

**Critical Path:** The core pipeline consists of: (1) computing EK-FAC influence for all training points relative to the behavioral metric; (2) selecting the top-k most influential examples; (3) for each selected example, running PGD to optimize the perturbation that maximizes the metric on the validation set; (4) applying the perturbation within allowed bounds; (5) replacing the original example in the training set; (6) retraining the model (or evaluating if already trained). Steps (1) and (3) are computationally heavy but only need to be done once per edit session.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- *Edit percentage vs. success:* More edits increase success probability but also risk overfitting to the target behavior and degrading overall performance. The authors found 0.2% sufficient for near-perfect success, but this may vary with dataset and task.
- *Perturbation budget vs. naturalness:* Tighter constraints (smaller L∞ norm) yield more realistic edits but may require more iterations to achieve the behavioral gain. Looser constraints risk creating unrealistic examples that could harm general performance.
- *Influence approximation accuracy vs. computational cost:* EK-FAC is a compromise; more accurate influence (e.g., using second-order methods) would be prohibitively expensive for large models. The approximation error may lead to suboptimal selection of examples to edit.

**Failure Signatures:**
- In language models, edits rarely cause rank flips in high-confidence predictions, only likelihood shifts, indicating that discrete token optimization is a bottleneck.
- Cross-architecture transfer is weak and asymmetric; edits computed for a ResNet do not transfer well to a CNN and vice versa, limiting reusability across model families.
- The method assumes a differentiable behavioral metric; for non-differentiable objectives (e.g., exact rank change), the PGD optimization may not converge effectively.

**First Three Experiments:**
1. **CIFAR-10 baseline replication:** Select a target class (e.g., "ship"), compute EK-FAC influences on a ResNet, perturb 0.2% of training images via PGD, retrain, and measure target class probability increase and prediction flip rates across multiple random seeds to verify the reported 100% success.
2. **Cross-architecture transfer test:** Using the edits obtained in experiment 1, evaluate the same edited training set on a different architecture (e.g., a simple CNN). Quantify the behavioral change and compare to editing that architecture directly to assess transfer strength and asymmetry.
3. **Language model amplification:** On a small transformer trained on a text dataset, identify a target behavior (e.g., generating positive sentiment). Apply Infusion to a tiny fraction of training sequences (e.g., 0.5%) and measure changes in likelihood for target prompts and any rank flips in next-token predictions. Compare to a baseline where random examples are edited to isolate the effect of influence-based selection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
While the paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions, its discussion, limitations, and future work directions imply several key questions for the research community:
- How does Infusion's editing efficiency and success rate scale to larger vision datasets (e.g., ImageNet) and more diverse model architectures?
- What theoretical explanations account for the observed weak and asymmetric transfer of edits across architectures?
- Can the method be adapted to effectively edit language models for discrete target behaviors (e.g., rank flips) rather than just likelihood shifts? What algorithmic improvements are needed?
- How sensitive are the results to hyperparameters such as the fraction of edited examples, perturbation budget (e.g., L∞ norm), and PGD iterations?
- Does Infusion generalize to other data modalities (e.g., audio, video) and task types (e.g., object detection, segmentation)?

These questions arise directly from the paper's identified limitations and suggested next steps.

## Limitations
- EK-FAC influence approximations may become inaccurate in highly non-linear regimes, potentially misranking influential examples.
- The continuous perturbation approach is ill-suited for discrete data like text, typically yielding only likelihood shifts and rarely altering prediction ranks.
- Cross-architecture transfer is weak and asymmetric, which limits the method's applicability when model families differ.

## Confidence
- **Vision editing efficacy (CIFAR-10, 0.2% edits, 100% success):** High
- **Cross-architecture transfer:** Medium
- **Language model behavior modification (likelihood shifts, rare rank flips):** Medium

## Next Checks
1. Scale evaluation to larger vision datasets (e.g., ImageNet) and varied model families to test edit efficiency and transfer.
2. Perform systematic hyperparameter sensitivity analysis (step size, projection constraints) and report success rates across diverse targets.
3. Investigate discrete optimization variants (e.g., token-level gradient rounding, reinforcement learning) for LMs to improve rank-flip rates on high-confidence predictions.
