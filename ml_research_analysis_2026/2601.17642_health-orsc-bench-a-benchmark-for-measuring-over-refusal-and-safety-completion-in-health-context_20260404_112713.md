# Health-ORSC-Bench: A Benchmark for Measuring Over-Refusal and Safe Completion in Health Context
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17642
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17642
- **Reference Count:** 35
- **Primary Result:** Frontier safety-optimized models show up to 80% over-refusal on Hard prompts, while Qwen-Max achieves near-zero over-refusal and ~70% safe completion rate.

## Executive Summary
Health-optimized large language models face a critical tension: they either over-refuse benign health-related queries or comply with harmful ones. To measure this safety-helpfulness tradeoff, the authors introduce Health-ORSC-Bench, a benchmark of 31,920 carefully curated boundary prompts across seven health categories. The benchmark employs a novel data generation pipeline that rewrites toxic seed prompts into benign variants and filters them through an ensemble of seven moderators. Prompts are stratified by collective model refusal rates (Easy, Medium, Hard) to isolate model calibration. Two metrics—Over-Refusal Rate (ORR) and Safe Completion Rate (SCR)—evaluate whether models can provide guarded, non-actionable guidance instead of binary refusals. Extensive testing on 30 models reveals an inverse relationship between safety guardrails and helpful completion, with frontier models exhibiting high over-refusal and domain-specific medical models showing low safe completion. Qwen-Max emerges as the best-calibrated model, balancing low ORR with strong SCR. The work highlights the need for context-aware confidence estimation and safe completion training in health LLMs.

## Method Summary
Health-ORSC-Bench constructs a large-scale evaluation suite targeting over-refusal and safe completion in health contexts. Starting from 2,306 harmful health-related seed prompts gathered from seven existing datasets, the authors first validate the seed taxonomy via human annotation (Fleiss’ κ≈0.75). An uncensored language model (Kimi-K2) then paraphrases each seed into a benign variant that preserves linguistic structure and medical specificity while removing harmful intent. The generated prompts are passed through an ensemble of seven diverse safety moderators; only prompts receiving at least four “not harmful” votes are retained, yielding 31,920 boundary prompts. These prompts are stratified into three difficulty subsets—Easy-5K, Medium-5K, and Hard-1K—based on preliminary refusal rates across a pool of 30 models. Model evaluation uses temperature 0 and no system prompt. Over-Refusal Rate is measured via keyword matching on refusals; Safe Completion Rate is assessed by an LLM-as-Judge (Grok-4) that classifies non-refusal responses as Full Answer, Partial Answer, or no value. The benchmark enables fine-grained analysis of the safety-helpfulness calibration in health LLMs.

## Key Results
- Frontier safety-optimized models (GPT-5, Claude, Llama) exhibit high over-refusal (up to 80% on Hard prompts) while maintaining toxic rejection rates above 95%.
- Smaller or MoE models (e.g., Qwen3-Next) and the private Qwen-Max show lower over-refusal, but safety performance varies; Qwen-Max achieves near-zero over-refusal and ~70% SCR on Hard prompts.
- Domain-specific medical models (Meditron, AlpaCare) have the lowest over-refusal but also the lowest safe completion rates, confirming an inverse relationship between safety guardrails and helpful completion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Enhanced safety alignment induces over-refusal on benign, borderline health queries due to conservative binary refusal heuristics.
- **Mechanism:** Safety training (RLHF, constitutional AI) optimizes models to reject any query resembling a harmful seed pattern, creating an overly broad decision boundary that fails to distinguish harmful intent from benign clinical/academic inquiry.
- **Core assumption:** Models lack contextual nuance to separate harmful intent from benign queries; they generalize from toxic training examples.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes reliance on binary refusal boundaries; Section 4.2.1 shows safety-optimised models refuse up to 80% of Hard benign prompts; neighboring benchmarks (OR-Bench, XSTest) confirm inverse correlation.
- **Break condition:** Adoption of safe completion strategies (providing guarded guidance) for borderline prompts reduces over-refusal without increasing harmful compliance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The automated pipeline (toxic seed rewording + ensemble moderation) reliably generates valid over-refusal prompts—benign queries persistently refused by safety-tuned models.
- **Mechanism:** (1) An uncensored LLM (Kimi-K2) rewrites harmful seeds into linguistically plausible, benign variants preserving domain specificity. (2) A seven-model moderator ensemble filters remaining toxicity via majority vote, ensuring final prompts are technically harmless yet psychologically similar to harmful seeds.
- **Core assumption:** An uncensored model can produce paraphrases that still activate over-refusal; consensus among heterogeneous moderators approximates human harmlessness judgment.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 & 3.4 describe rewriting and filtering; 7 moderators used, threshold ≥4 “not harmful”; human validation of seeds (Fleiss’ κ=0.745) supports initial taxonomy.
- **Break condition:** If rewording loses semantic/structural similarity to seeds, prompts no longer trigger over-refusal and validation fails.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Stratifying prompts by collective model refusal rates (Easy/Medium/Hard) creates a valid difficulty gradient for diagnosing model calibration across the safety-helpfulness trade-off.
- **Mechanism:** Prompts refused by fewer models (Easy) are less ambiguous; those refused by most (Hard) are maximally ambiguous. Empirical stratification based on 30-model refusal consensus isolates model-specific calibration rather than prompt toxicity.
- **Core assumption:** Prompt difficulty (likelihood of refusal) reflects inherent ambiguity; better-calibrated models show lower refusal on Hard prompts without increased toxic acceptance.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.5 describes stratification; Figure 2 shows consistent category sensitivities across subsets, indicating stable gradients; OR-Bench uses similar approach.
- **Break condition:** If human-judged ambiguity does not correlate with refusal-based difficulty, stratification fails to measure over-refusal vs. safety.

## Foundational Learning

### Over-Refusal Rate (ORR)
- **Why needed:** Primary metric for false positives—proportion of benign prompts rejected. Essential for diagnosing excessive caution in health LLMs.
- **Quick check:** If a model refuses 80% of Hard prompts but only 5% of toxic prompts, what does its ORR vs. safety rejection trade-off suggest about its calibration?

### Safe Completion Rate (SCR)
- **Why needed:** Measures nuanced alignment—ability to provide helpful, non-actionable guidance for borderline prompts instead of refusing. Addresses binary metric limitations.
- **Quick check:** What distinguishes a "Partial Answer" (SCR-positive) from a "Full Answer" in the LLM-as-Judge rubric? When might a "Safety Education" response be preferable?

### Benchmark Stratification (Easy/Medium/Hard)
- **Why needed:** Isolates model calibration from prompt toxicity. Hard subsets specifically target the decision boundary where over-refusal is most diagnostic.
- **Quick check:** Why is a prompt's inclusion in the Hard subset based on *collective model refusal* rather than human judgment of ambiguity?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Seed Collection → Human Validation → Rewriting → Moderation → Stratification → Evaluation

**Critical path:** The pipeline must sequentially execute seed collection, human validation of seeds, benign rewriting, moderator ensemble filtering, difficulty stratification, and final evaluation; a failure in moderation (e.g., no consensus) halts progress.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Automation vs. Human Effort: Heavy automation enables scale (31,920 prompts) but risks subtle toxicity; human validation limited to seed taxonomy only.
- Balance vs. Realism: Balanced 7-category distribution aids per-category analysis, but may not reflect real-world query frequencies.
- Ensemble Diversity: Seven moderators reduce single-model bias but add complexity; threshold (4/7) is heuristic.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low inter-annotator agreement (κ < 0.6) → seed taxonomy invalid.
- Rewritten prompts' length distribution diverges from seeds → difficulty confounded.
- Moderation consensus < 50% on final set → pipeline too strict/lenient, benchmark unrepresentative.
- No correlation between ORR and model size/family → stratification failed; prompts not truly ambiguous.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Prompt Toxicity Sanity Check:** Run 100 random final prompts through a held-out moderator (e.g., GPT-5). Expect >95% "safe" classification. If <90%, moderation ensemble too weak.
2. **Stratification Validation:** Compute refusal rate variance *within* each subset (Easy/Medium/Hard). Expect Hard to have highest variance (some models refuse, some don't). If variance low, stratification not isolating ambiguity.
3. **Baseline Model Calibration Test:** Evaluate a known over-refusing model (e.g., GPT-5) and a known under-refusing model (e.g., Qwen-Max) on Hard-1K. Expect large ORR gap (>50%) and SCR gap (GPT-5 high ORR, Qwen-Max low ORR but comparable SCR). If gap <20%, benchmark lacks discriminative power.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Context-aware confidence estimation:** How can LLMs reliably differentiate high-risk harmful requests from benign borderline health queries? (Explicit future direction)
2. **Architectural/training differences:** What specific factors cause frontier models to show safety-pessimism while specialized medical models show lower over-refusal but poorer safe completion? (Inferred)
3. **Breaking the inverse trade-off:** Can alternative alignment objectives (e.g., safe completion training) reduce over-refusal without increasing unsafe compliance? (Explicit)
4. **Multilingual generalization:** How do linguistic and cultural variations affect over-refusal and safe completion in non-English contexts? (Inferred from limitations)

## Limitations
- Exact generation pipeline details (source dataset versions, health-filtering keywords, Kimi-K2 rewriting rules) are not fully specified, hindering full replication.
- Human validation was limited to seed taxonomy (196 samples) only; the final 31,920 prompts were not directly checked for benign ambiguity.
- Reliance on automated moderation (7-model ensemble) and an LLM-as-Judge (Grok-4) introduces potential bias; specific prompts, thresholds, and judge version are undisclosed.
- Stratification thresholds depend on the specific 30-model pool used in the paper; a different model set may yield different difficulty subsets.
- The paper does not empirically verify that "Hard" prompts correspond to human-judged ambiguity rather than subtle residual toxicity.

## Next Checks
1. **Human ambiguity validation of Hard prompts:** Sample 100 Hard-1K prompts and have clinicians/medical experts rate each for benign intent and clinical plausibility. Expect >85% consensus that prompts are answerable with guarded guidance.
2. **Keyword matching audit:** Manually label 200 model responses (100 refusals, 100 non-refusals) to measure precision/recall of the ORR keyword list. Target >90% agreement; refine list if needed.
3. **Judge consistency for SCR:** Re-evaluate 200 safe-completion candidate responses with a second LLM judge (e.g., GPT-5) using the same rubric. Compute Cohen’s κ; expect >0.8.
