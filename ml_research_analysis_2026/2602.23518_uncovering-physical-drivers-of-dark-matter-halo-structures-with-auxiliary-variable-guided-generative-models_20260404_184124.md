# Uncovering Physical Drivers of Dark Matter Halo Structures with Auxiliary-Variable-Guided Generative Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.23518
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23518
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: DL-CFM achieves disentangled latent representation of halo mass and concentration while maintaining high-fidelity tSZ map generation comparable to ICFM.

## Executive Summary
Deep generative models for astronomical images often entangle multiple physical factors, hindering interpretability. This paper proposes Disentangled Latent Conditional Flow Matching (DL-CFM), which integrates auxiliary-variable guidance (halo mass and concentration) into a conditional flow matching framework. A VAE encoder with alignment and decorrelation penalties forces selected latent coordinates to reflect these physical quantities, while a flow-matching decoder generates high-fidelity thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) maps. The model matches state-of-the-art generation quality while enabling clear disentanglement: guided latents show near monotonic relationships with mass and concentration, and varying residual latents produces morphologies from relaxed to disturbed halos. The approach recovers the known mass-concentration scaling relation, demonstrating a compact, interpretable representation for cosmological structure analysis.

## Method Summary
DL-CFM combines conditional flow matching with auxiliary-variable guidance. The model uses a VAE encoder to map input tSZ maps into a latent space. A linear projection splits this latent into two parts: an aligned segment (for mass and concentration) and a residual segment. Alignment losses (e.g., MSE) force the aligned latents to match the provided auxiliary variables, while a decorrelation penalty minimizes dependence between aligned and residual latents. The full latent vector—concatenating aligned and residual parts—conditions a flow-matching decoder that transforms noise into high-fidelity tSZ maps. This design ensures that the aligned latents are interpretable controls for the specified physical properties, while the residual latents capture remaining variations such as substructure or viewing angle.

## Key Results
- Generation quality (Sinkhorn, Energy, Gaussian, Laplacian distances) matches that of the state-of-the-art ICFM method.
- Guided latent variables exhibit near one-to-one monotonic relationships with true halo mass and concentration.
- Fixing auxiliary latents and varying residual latents yields morphologies ranging from relaxed to disturbed halos, confirming disentanglement.
- The model recovers the empirical mass-concentration scaling relation and identifies potential outliers in the latent space.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism relies on supervised alignment of specific latent dimensions with known physical quantities (mass and concentration) via explicit loss functions, while decorrelation penalties prevent these dimensions from capturing other factors. The conditional flow matching decoder is trained to reconstruct tSZ maps from the full latent vector, ensuring that the aligned latents retain generative relevance. This separation allows the auxiliary-guided latents to act as direct, interpretable knobs for the targeted properties, with the residual latent absorbing unrelated variations. The monotonic relationships emerge because the alignment loss encourages a deterministic mapping, and the flow decoder preserves this structure during generation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conditional Flow Matching**: A generative technique that learns a continuous normalizing flow conditioned on auxiliary inputs, enabling high-fidelity sample generation. *Why needed*: Provides flexible, conditional generation with stable training. *Quick check*: Can the flow generate diverse samples that accurately reflect the conditioning variables?
- **Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**: Encodes data into a probabilistic latent space with a reconstruction objective. *Why needed*: Introduces a structured bottleneck that can be regularized for disentanglement. *Quick check*: Does the latent space exhibit smooth interpolation between data points?
- **Auxiliary Variable Guidance**: Using known external variables (e.g., mass, concentration) to steer latent representation learning. *Why needed*: Enforces alignment between latent dimensions and specific physical properties. *Quick check*: Do selected latent coordinates correlate strongly with the auxiliary targets?
- **Disentanglement via Alignment and Decorrelation**: Alignment losses map latents to targets; decorrelation penalties (e.g., independence regularization) reduce redundancy between latent groups. *Why needed*: Separates factors of variation to improve interpretability. *Quick check*: Are aligned latents independent of residual latents in predictive tasks?
- **Thermal Sunyaev-Zel’dovich (tSZ) Effect**: Distortion of Cosmic Microwave Background photons by hot electrons in galaxy clusters. *Why needed*: tSZ maps probe cluster pressure and mass, key for cosmology. *Quick check*: Do generated tSZ maps exhibit realistic pressure profiles and morphologies?
- **Mass–Concentration Relation**: An empirical scaling where denser halos are less massive. *Why needed*: A fundamental prediction of ΛCDM cosmology for validation. *Quick check*: Does the model recover this relation from simulated data without explicit supervision?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input tSZ map -> VAE Encoder -> Aligned Latent (mass, conc) + Residual Latent -> Concatenated Latent -> Flow Matching Decoder -> Generated tSZ map.

**Critical path**: The encoder processes the input image and outputs a latent vector. A linear layer splits this into aligned and residual components. Alignment and decorrelation losses are computed using the provided auxiliary variables. The concatenated latent is fed to the flow-matching decoder, which is trained to match a conditional probability path between noise and data. Total loss combines reconstruction (flow matching), alignment, and decorrelation terms.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- *Alignment strength vs. generative fidelity*: Strong alignment penalties may distort the latent space and harm image quality.
- *Number of guided factors*: More auxiliary variables require more supervised dimensions, potentially limiting what can be captured in the residual space.
- *Decoder complexity*: Flow matching offers high-quality generation but increases computational cost and training instability.

**Failure signatures**: 
- Weak monotonicity between guided latents and true mass/concentration.
- Residual latent encoding information about mass or concentration.
- Lack of morphological diversity when varying residual latents while fixing guided latents.
- Inability to recover the mass–concentration relation.

**First three experiments**:
1. Train DL-CFM on a dataset of simulated tSZ maps with known halo mass and concentration.
2. For a held-out set, plot guided latent values against true mass and concentration; assess monotonicity and correlation strength.
3. Fix guided latents to specific values (e.g., median mass and concentration), sample multiple residual latents, and visualize generated tSZ maps to check morphological diversity.

## Limitations
- Evaluation uses simulated tSZ maps; real observations include noise, systematics, and selection effects that may impair disentanglement.
- Disentanglement is supervised only for halo mass and concentration; other physical factors (e.g., substructure, viewing angle) might remain entangled.
- Disentanglement is assessed via monotonic relationships and qualitative morphology checks without rigorous quantitative metrics (e.g., mutual information).

## Confidence
- **High**: Generation quality comparison to ICFM via distribution distances—direct empirical measurement with clear benchmarks.
- **Medium**: Disentanglement claims (monotonic latents, morphology control) supported by correlations and visuals but do not guarantee full factor independence.
- **High**: Recovery of the mass–concentration scaling relation—expected given the alignment objective and the relation inherent in simulation data.

## Next Checks
- **Real-data transferability**: Test on observed tSZ clusters (e.g., from Planck, ACT, SPT); correlate guided latents with independent mass/concentration estimates from X-ray or lensing.
- **Disentanglement completeness**: Measure residual latent correlations with other halo properties (spin, triaxiality, substructure) using mutual information or predictive models; a disentangled representation should show independence.
- **Robustness to observational systematics**: Retrain/evaluate with realistic noise, beam smearing, and point-source contamination to assess whether disentanglement persists under survey-like conditions.
