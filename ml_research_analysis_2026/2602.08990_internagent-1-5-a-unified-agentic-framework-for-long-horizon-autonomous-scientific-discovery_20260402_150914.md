# InternAgent-1.5: A Unified Agentic Framework for Long-Horizon Autonomous Scientific Discovery

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.08990
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08990
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** A unified, general agentic framework integrating deep research, solution refinement, and long-horizon memory to achieve state-of-the-art performance on scientific reasoning benchmarks and autonomously produce novel findings across multiple scientific domains.

## Executive Summary
InternAgent-1.5 presents a unified framework designed for end-to-end, long-horizon autonomous scientific discovery, moving beyond prior domain-specific AI Scientist systems. Its core innovation is the coordination of three subsystems—Generation, Verification, and Evolution—enabled by foundational capabilities in deep research (via a cross-disciplinary knowledge graph), iterative solution refinement (via a graph-augmented search), and structured long-term memory. This architecture allows the system to sustain coherent cycles of hypothesis generation, experimental design/execution (both computational and wet-lab), and memory-driven refinement. Evaluations demonstrate state-of-the-art results on complex reasoning benchmarks (GAIA, HLE, GPQA-diamond) and successful autonomous execution of complete scientific workflows, yielding novel contributions in earth, life, biological, and physical sciences.

## Method Summary
The framework operates through a continuous loop: the **Generation** subsystem synthesizes hypotheses and detailed plans by conducting "deep research" over a cross-disciplinary knowledge graph (KG) that stores documents, concepts, methods, and datasets. The **Verification** subsystem executes these plans, running simulations or coordinating wet-lab instruments (via a Science Context Protocol), and explores the space of potential designs or analyses using a graph-augmented Monte Carlo search that records all experiments in a dynamic solution graph. The **Evolution** subsystem manages three complementary memories (Strategy-Procedural, Task-Episodic, Semantic-Knowledge) that distill experiences from each cycle to guide future planning, adaptation, and objective evolution. Foundational to this is the KG's ability to retrieve cross-disciplinary evidence paths and the solution graph's capacity to transfer insights across parallel experimental trajectories.

## Key Results
*   Achieves state-of-the-art on scientific reasoning benchmarks: 86.06% on GAIA, 40.87% on HLE, and 87.37% on GPQA-diamond.
*   Outperforms prior InternAgent-1.0 and domain-specific baselines across six computational scientific discovery tasks (e.g., algorithm design for time-series forecasting).
*   Executes complete empirical workflows, producing validated, novel findings in earth science (climate downscaling), life science (microbiome analysis), biology (protein engineering), and physics (quantum communication).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** The Cross-Disciplinary Knowledge Graph enables Generation to synthesize hypotheses from multi-domain evidence.
*   **Mechanism:** The KG stores heterogeneous nodes (documents, concepts, methods, datasets, empirical settings) and relationships. For a query, it combines graph search (for structural connections) with dense vector retrieval (for semantic similarity) to produce path-structured evidence, which the Generation subsystem analyzes.
*   **Core Assumption:** Scientific knowledge can be represented as a connected graph where cross-disciplinary insights emerge from paths between disparate nodes.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "integrates three coordinated subsystems...supported by foundational capabilities in deep research"; [section 2.2.1] describes the heterogeneous graph node types.
*   **Break Condition:** Domain-specific parsing failures lead to a sparse or noisy KG, degrading cross-disciplinary synthesis.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Graph-Augmented Monte Carlo Search in Verification efficiently explores experimental design spaces across parallel trajectories.
*   **Mechanism:** It replaces a rigid MCTS tree with a dynamic solution graph recording all experiments. Four operators (Primary Expansion, Intra-branch Evolution, Cross-branch Reference, Multi-branch Aggregation) generate new proposals by conditioning on ancestors, referencing high-performing branches, or aggregating top nodes. Evaluation scores backpropagate through ancestral paths.
*   **Core Assumption:** Maintaining a graph-structured history of design choices enables cumulative transfer of successful patterns across parallel explorations.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [section 2.3.1] defines the dynamic solution graph and enumerates the four operators; [section 3.3] shows quantified improvements across six tasks.
*   **Break Condition:** Noisy or deceptive evaluation metrics cause backpropagation of misleading signals, leading to suboptimal convergence.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Structured Cognitive Memory (Evolution subsystem) sustains coherent operation across long-horizon discovery cycles.
*   **Mechanism:** Three complementary memories are updated and retrieved each cycle: Strategy-Procedural Memory (distills reasoning patterns to guide planning), Task-Episodic Memory (stores within-trajectory outcomes for rapid adaptation), and Semantic-Knowledge Memory (tracks explored directions and novel objectives via contrastive learning).
*   **Core Assumption:** Long-term improvement requires persistent, hierarchical storage of procedural, episodic, and semantic knowledge that feeds back into Generation and Verification.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [section 2.4] defines the three memories with formal retrieval equations; [section 3.5] ablation shows each contributes to stable, improving performance.
*   **Break Condition:** Memory retrieval returns irrelevant priors, causing Generation to repeat failures or Verification to ignore promising directions.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Graph-based knowledge representation:** Needed because the Cross-Disciplinary KG is the substrate for deep research; understanding node/edge types and hybrid search strategies (graph + dense) is essential. *Quick Check: How does a graph path differ from a list of retrieved documents for supporting multi-hop reasoning?*
*   **Monte Carlo Tree Search with graph-based modifications:** Needed because the Verification subsystem's solution refinement relies on a modified MCTS with non-tree operators; knowing standard MCTS helps grasp the innovations. *Quick Check: What are the trade-offs between a tree and a dynamic graph for backpropagating credit across parallel experiments?*
*   **Hierarchical memory architectures in autonomous agents:** Needed because the Evolution subsystem's three memories operate at different timescales and granularities; distinguishing procedural/episodic/semantic memory is key. *Quick Check: Why might a single unified memory be insufficient for long-horizon scientific discovery?*

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Research Objective -> Generation (KG Deep Research) -> Hypothesis/Plan -> Verification (Solution Refinement & Execution) -> Results & Scores -> Evolution (Memory Update) -> [back to] Generation. Supporting components: Cross-Disciplinary KG (feeds Generation), Solution Graph (within Verification), Science Context Protocol (SCP for tool/wet-lab coordination).
*   **Critical Path:** From objective -> KG search & synthesis -> code/protocol generation -> (dry/wet) execution -> result collection -> memory indexing -> objective evolution. Key breakpoints: KG retrieval quality, execution success/fidelity, memory update relevance.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** KG richness vs. scalability; search graph history vs. memory/storage overhead; memory retrieval precision vs. novelty.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   Generation: KG returns disconnected fragments -> incoherent hybrid hypotheses.
    *   Verification: Search stagnates (no new high scores) -> operators overfit to local optima.
    *   Evolution: Memory retrieval consistently irrelevant -> flat performance curve across cycles.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Deep research validation:** Run on a GAIA/HLE question requiring multi-source synthesis. Verify KG paths include cross-disciplinary connections and final answer cites evidence chains.
    2.  **Solution refinement pressure test:** Use AutoTSF or AutoPower task. Seed search with a known suboptimal baseline; confirm cross-branch aggregation improves performance over primary expansion alone.
    3.  **Memory integration demo:** Run an Earth Science discovery (e.g., climate downscaling) across 5+ rounds. Ablate each memory component (SPM/TEM/SKM) separately and measure degradation in trajectory improvement and objective evolution novelty.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Q1:** How can computational reasoning and experimental validation be more tightly coupled to reduce the time from hypothesis generation to verification?
*   **Q2:** What are the individual contributions of deep research (Generation), solution refinement (Verification), and long-horizon memory (Evolution) to overall system performance?
*   **Q3:** Does the Graph-Augmented Monte Carlo Search (GAMCS) improve sample efficiency and robustness compared to standard MCTS or other optimizers?
*   **Q4:** How can the cross-disciplinary knowledge graph be dynamically updated to incorporate new scientific findings without performance degradation or catastrophic forgetting?

## Limitations
*   Implementation details for base LLM orchestration, KG construction/querying, and the SCP interface are not fully specified, blocking faithful reproduction of empirical tasks.
*   The benefits of the graph-augmented search operators are claimed but not directly compared against standard MCTS or other optimizers in an controlled setting.
*   Memory component interactions and retrieval logic, while described conceptually, lack granular algorithmic detail for independent validation.

## Confidence
*   **Framework Integration (three-subsystem loop):** Medium (described but orchestration opaque).
*   **Cross-Disciplinary Knowledge Graph:** Medium (concept clear, implementation vague).
*   **Graph-Augmented Monte Carlo Search:** Medium (operators defined, independent evaluation lacking).
*   **Structured Cognitive Memory:** Low (components listed, retrieval logic unclear).
*   **Benchmark Scores (GAIA, HLE, GPQA):** High for reported numbers, Low for reproducibility.
*   **Autonomous Empirical Discoveries:** Low (tasks and validation underspecified).
