# MIRAGE: The Illusion of Visual Understanding
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21687
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21687
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Frontier multimodal models retain 70–80% of benchmark accuracy without any image input, with medical benchmarks showing up to 99% susceptibility, demonstrating that high scores do not reliably indicate genuine visual understanding.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals a critical failure mode in frontier multimodal AI models termed "mirage reasoning." Despite being presented with visual questions, models generate confident, detailed, and seemingly image-grounded descriptions and answers even when the image is completely withheld. This effect is pervasive across general and medical benchmarks, with medical tasks showing extreme susceptibility. The authors demonstrate that a tiny text-only model fine-tuned on medical QA data can outperform all tested frontier multimodal systems and radiologists. Consequently, standard multimodal evaluation scores are largely unreliable measures of true visual perception, as models exploit statistical shortcuts and dataset patterns to "solve" tasks without processing visual input.

## Method Summary
The core method involves evaluating frontier multimodal models in a "mirage-mode" condition: providing the exact text prompt from standard visual benchmarks but deliberately omitting the accompanying image. Model accuracy in this mode is compared to its normal, image-present performance. The ratio defines the "mirage score." To create more reliable benchmarks, the authors introduce "B-Clean," which removes all questions that any candidate model can answer correctly in mirage-mode. This process relies on running multiple models in mirage-mode upfront to identify and filter out text-soluble questions. The study tests several proprietary models (GPT-5.1, Gemini-3-Pro, etc.) on benchmarks including VQA-RAD, MicroVQA, MedXpertQA-MM, and MMMU-Pro.

## Key Results
- Frontier models (GPT-5.1, Gemini-3-Pro, Claude Opus 4.5) produce elaborate, pathology-biased descriptions for non-existent images and maintain 70–80% of their original accuracy without visual input.
- Medical multimodal benchmarks are exceptionally vulnerable, with some showing near-perfect mirage scores (up to 99%), meaning the model can answer correctly almost entirely from text.
- A 3B-parameter text-only model fine-tuned on chest X-ray QA pairs outperformed all frontier multimodal models and radiologists on a held-out test set.
- Explicitly instructing a model to "guess" due to missing images ("guess-mode") causes a significant accuracy drop compared to implicit mirage-mode, indicating two distinct internal reasoning regimes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Frontier multimodal models generate detailed, image-grounded descriptions and reasoning traces despite having no visual input ("mirage reasoning").
- **Mechanism:** Models exploit learned textual patterns, dataset-specific priors, and statistical regularities from their training to simulate a perceptual narrative. They project an internal "mirage" that fits the expected context of the query, bypassing the need for actual visual processing.
- **Core assumption:** The joint image-text training objective incentivizes models to find the shortest path to a correct answer, which can be achieved by relying on linguistic cues and hidden benchmark structures rather than visual evidence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Frontier models readily generate detailed image descriptions and elaborate reasoning traces, including pathology-biased clinical findings, for images never provided; we term this phenomenon mirage reasoning."
  - [section 2.2] Analysis of Gemini-3-Pro shows pathology-biased diagnoses (e.g., STEMI, melanoma) from non-existent images across medical categories.
  - [section 3.3] A text-only model fine-tuned on question-answer pairs (without images) outperforms multimodal models, proving textual cues alone suffice.
- **Break condition:** Model explicitly acknowledges image absence or expresses uncertainty when queried without an image.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** High scores on multimodal benchmarks do not reliably indicate genuine visual understanding; a large portion of performance can be achieved via non-visual inference.
- **Mechanism:** Benchmarks contain latent, non-image-dependent cues (e.g., question phrasing, answer option distributions, dataset-specific patterns, or data contamination) that models can exploit. The "mirage score" quantifies this, with 60-99% susceptibility showing benchmarks are often text-solvable.
- **Core assumption:** Publicly available benchmarks are absorbed into pretraining data, and their design inadvertently creates statistical shortcuts that powerful language models can detect and leverage without vision.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Without any image input, models also attain strikingly high scores across general and medical multimodal benchmarks..."
  - [section 3.1] Frontier models retain 70-80% of original accuracy in mirage-mode; medical benchmarks show highest susceptibility (up to 99%).
  - [section 3.2] Mere mention of a dataset's name increases accuracy, implying structural pattern recognition.
- **Break condition:** Mirage scores approach 0% across a diverse benchmark suite, indicating questions are robustly vision-dependent.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** There are at least two distinct reasoning regimes: a "mirage-mode" where models behave as if images exist, and an explicit "guess-mode" where they acknowledge missing data and perform worse.
- **Mechanism:** In mirage-mode, the prompt implicitly cues the model to assume an image is present, triggering an automatic, high-confidence simulation process that accesses hidden patterns. In guess-mode, the explicit acknowledgment of missing data forces a more conservative, text-only prior-based strategy, losing access to those hidden cues.
- **Core assumption:** Model behavior is prompt-sensitive; the absence of a "no image" signal leads to a different internal state than explicit instruction to guess, revealing that mirage-based accuracy relies on an implicit epistemic frame constructed by the model.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Explicitly instructing models to guess (guess-mode) significantly reduced accuracy versus implicit mirage-mode, indicating two distinct reasoning regimes."
  - [section 4.1] GPT-5.1's accuracy declines significantly in guess-mode vs. mirage-mode across categories.
  - [section 4.1 example] In a neuroscience question, mirage-mode produces a detailed visual reasoning trace ("The image shows..."), while guess-mode reasons only from answer option semantics and chooses differently.
- **Break condition:** No significant performance difference between mirage-mode and guess-mode across a set of vision-dependent questions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal Benchmark Design Flaws**
  - *Why needed here:* To understand why current evaluations fail to measure true visual grounding, one must know the common pitfalls: language shortcuts, redundant images, weak distractors, and data contamination.
  - *Quick check question:* What are three specific question features that allow a model to answer correctly without using the accompanying image?
- **Concept: Shortcut Learning in LLMs**
  - *Why needed here:* The mirage effect is an extreme form of shortcut learning. Understanding how LLMs excel at statistical pattern matching on text is prerequisite to seeing how they can "solve" visual QA without vision.
  - *Quick check question:* How might a model use the distribution of answer options (A-D) or specific keywords in a question to infer the correct answer in a multiple-choice setting?
- **Concept: Evaluation Design for Modality Reliance**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper proposes new evaluation principles (B-Clean, mirage-mode testing). This requires understanding control conditions like "guess-mode" and the rationale for modality-ablation testing.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the logical purpose of comparing a model's performance on a benchmark with and without its primary input modality?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input Processor -> Mirage Detector & Evaluator -> B-Clean Filter -> Comparative Evaluator -> Confidence/Uncertainty Checker
- **Critical path:**
  Candidate Models -> Mirage-Mode Evaluation on Full Benchmark -> Set of Compromised Questions per Model -> Union Operation -> B-Clean Benchmark -> Vision-Grounded Evaluation -> Reliable Rankings.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Validity vs. Benchmark Size:* B-Clean dramatically reduces the number of evaluation questions (often by ~75%), trading statistical power for higher confidence that remaining scores reflect visual understanding.
  - *Model-Set Dependency:* The cleaned benchmark depends on the specific candidate models used in the cleaning step. A question compromised for one model may be removed even if another model relies on vision for it.
  - *Computational Cost:* Requires running every candidate model in mirage-mode on the entire benchmark upfront, which is expensive but a one-time cost per benchmark release.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High mirage rate (>50%) on Phantom-0 for a model-family, indicating systemic vulnerability.
  - B-Clean filtering >90% of questions, suggesting the original benchmark was overwhelmingly text-soluble.
  - Minimal change in model rankings after B-Clean, which could mean either all models are equally visually grounded OR equally mirage-prone.
  - Model outputs in mirage-mode that are indistinguishable (by a human judge or simple regex) from its image-present outputs.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Establish Baseline Mirage Rate:** Run each target model (e.g., GPT-5.1) on the Phantom-0 benchmark (200 diverse visual questions, no images) with default prompts. Use a simple regex/GPT-4 judge to measure the percentage of responses that *fail to acknowledge* the missing image.
  2.  **Replicate Core Mirage Score:** For one key medical benchmark (e.g., VQA-RAD), compute the mirage score for at least two models. Calculate: (accuracy in mirage-mode) / (accuracy with images). Expect >50% for medical domains.
  3.  **Demonstrate B-Clean Impact:** Apply the B-Clean procedure to a small, publicly available multimodal benchmark (e.g., a 100-question subset of MMMU-Pro) using 2-3 candidate models. Document the drastic reduction in question count and the change in the relative performance ranking.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the precise computational or neural mechanism enabling multimodal models to generate detailed, confident visual descriptions in the complete absence of visual input?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state they "do not directly identify the full internal mechanism of mirage generation; our mechanistic interpretation remains inferential and should be tested with future work."
- **Why unresolved:** Current evidence is behavioral (outputs and accuracy comparisons), not mechanistic; internal model states during mirage reasoning are unprobed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic representation analysis (e.g., probing internal activations, attention patterns) comparing mirage-mode to normal image-present mode; targeted intervention studies that selectively occlude specific model components; controlled training ablations to isolate contributing factors.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can evaluation frameworks achieve absolute, model-independent benchmarks that reliably measure genuine visual understanding, overcoming the relative and model-set-dependent limitations of approaches like B-Clean?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors acknowledge that "B-Clean is model-set dependent and provides relative rather than absolute evaluation" and note that public benchmarks are inevitably absorbed into pretraining data.
- **Why unresolved:** Existing solutions (new curated benchmarks) are temporary against evolving data leakage; hidden structural cues persist; no universal standard for modality-ablation testing exists.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and validation of private or dynamically updated benchmark distribution systems that prevent pretraining data contamination; establishment of standardized, universal protocols for modality-ablation testing that are agnostic to candidate model sets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why do medical multimodal benchmarks exhibit significantly higher mirage susceptibility (e.g., up to 99%) compared to general-domain benchmarks?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Results consistently show medical benchmarks at the upper mirage-score range, but the paper does not analyze underlying causes.
- **Why unresolved:** The authors speculate about "challenging nature of creating expert-level medical visual questions" and "statistics-dominated" fields, but provide no direct evidence linking specific dataset characteristics to vulnerability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative forensic analysis of question texts and structures across medical vs. general benchmarks to identify exploitable statistical or linguistic cues; studies correlating specific benchmark design choices with mirage score variance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What causes the fundamental divergence in model behavior and accuracy between mirage-mode (implicit no-image prompt) and guess-mode (explicit no-image prompt instructing to guess)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper demonstrates that explicitly instructing models to guess "declined markedly" and hypothesizes "two distinct operating regimes," but does not explain the mechanism.
- **Why unresolved:** The cognitive or architectural basis for why semantically similar no-image conditions trigger different reasoning pathways is unknown; it is unclear whether the difference is due to prompt phrasing, model's internal state awareness, or access to latent patterns.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-grained comparative analysis of model outputs (reasoning trace coherence, confidence expressions) and internal representations (layer-wise activations, attention distributions) under both prompt conditions to identify distinguishing signals; controlled experiments varying only the acknowledgment of missing image to isolate the trigger.

## Limitations
- Frontier models are only accessible via paid APIs, limiting independent replication.
- Some medical benchmarks (e.g., MedXpertQA-MM) lack public test splits, requiring direct data access from the authors.
- B-Clean benchmark construction depends on the candidate model pool; questions compromised for one model are removed even if others rely on vision, introducing potential bias.
- Mirage-mode results are prompt-sensitive; minor deviations from published prompts may alter behavior.
- The exact training data shortcuts enabling mirage reasoning remain unidentified; contamination is plausible but unproven.

## Confidence
- **Mirage reasoning existence:** High (consistent across multiple models on multiple benchmarks; models rarely acknowledge missing images).
- **Benchmark vulnerability (high mirage scores):** Medium (70–80% on general, up to 99% on medical; demonstrated on 5–6 benchmarks, but generalizability uncertain).
- **Distinct reasoning regimes (mirage vs guess):** Medium (guess-mode accuracy drop significant, but dichotomy might stem from subtle prompt cues).
- **Text-only model superiority in medical QA:** Medium (3B chest X-ray QA model outperformed frontier models and radiologists on one held-out set; needs validation on other modalities).
- **B-Clean mitigation utility:** Low-Medium (removes 60–80% of questions, yet drastic reduction raises statistical power concerns; long-term ranking impact not fully explored).
