# Echo2ECG: Enhancing ECG Representations with Cardiac Morphology from Multi-View Echos

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.08505  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08505  
- **Reference Count:** 28  
- **Primary Result:** Aligns ECG with multi‑view echocardiography via contrastive learning, achieving state‑of‑the‑art LVEF classification (AUROC up to 0.785) and structural heart disease detection with only 12.5 M parameters.

## Executive Summary
ECG records electrical activity but cannot directly measure cardiac morphology. Echo2ECG bridges this gap by learning ECG representations that mirror comprehensive cardiac structure from multi‑view echocardiograms. Using self‑supervised contrastive learning, it maps ECG signals to embeddings distilled from multiple echo views via an attention‑based aggregator and a frozen, pre‑trained echo encoder. The approach yields strong performance across multiple datasets and tasks while being significantly smaller than prior multimodal methods.

## Method Summary
Echo2ECG uses paired ECG and multi‑view echocardiogram studies. A trainable ECG encoder produces a feature vector from raw ECG. A separate, frozen echo encoder (pre‑trained on large echo datasets) processes each echo view; an attention layer aggregates these view features into a single study embedding. A contrastive loss (e.g., NT‑Xent) pulls matching ECG‑echo pairs together in latent space and pushes non‑matching pairs apart. The ECG encoder is trained end‑to‑end to produce representations that align with the echo‑based morphology embedding, thereby infusing ECG with implicit structural information.

## Key Results
- LVEF classification: AUROC 0.785 (internal), 0.723 (EchoNext), 0.692 (UK Biobank).  
- Structural heart disease detection: outperforms all baselines even with only 1% labeled training data.  
- Phenotype‑aware cross‑modal retrieval: highest Precision@1 and lowest mean rank among compared methods.  
- Parameter efficiency: 12.5 M trainable parameters (18× smaller than the largest baseline).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Multi‑view echocardiography captures a more complete 3D morphology than any single view. By aligning ECG to the aggregated echo embedding, the ECG encoder learns features correlated with comprehensive cardiac structure rather than a single perspective. The contrastive objective forces the ECG representation into a latent space where morphologically similar hearts (as viewed from multiple angles) have similar ECG signatures. Keeping the echo encoder frozen leverages robust, large‑scale pretrained visual features without catastrophic forgetting, while the attention aggregator weights views appropriate to the cardiac structure of interest.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self‑supervised contrastive learning**  
  *Why needed:* Enables alignment without manual labels by treating matching ECG‑echo studies as positives.  
  *Quick check:* Replace contrastive loss with a supervised classification loss on a downstream task; performance drop would indicate contrastive pretraining’s value.

- **Multi‑view aggregation via attention**  
  *Why needed:* Combines complementary anatomical information from different echo angles (e.g., apical, parasternal).  
  *Quick check:* Replace attention with simple averaging; degraded performance would show the benefit of learned view weighting.

- **Frozen pre‑trained echo encoder**  
  *Why needed:* Preserves high‑level anatomical features learned from large external echo datasets, reducing overfitting.  
  *Quick check:* Unfreeze the echo encoder with a small learning rate; observe if LVEF classification improves or becomes unstable.

- **Cross‑modal representation alignment**  
  *Why needed:* ECG lacks direct morphology; aligning to echo forces ECG embeddings to encode structural correlates.  
  *Quick check:* Train an ablation with shuffled ECG‑echo pairs; alignment should collapse, hurting downstream performance.

- **Efficient parameterization**  
  *Why needed:* Smaller model enhances scalability and reduces overfitting risk.  
  *Quick check:* Count parameters of a multimodal Transformer baseline; Echo2ECG should be markedly smaller with comparable or better performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Raw ECG → ECG Encoder (trainable) → ECG Feature → Contrastive Loss  
Echo Views → Echo Encoder (frozen) → View Features → Attention Aggregator → Echo Embedding → Contrastive Loss

**Critical path:** Input paired ECG and echo views → extract features → aggregate echo views → compute contrastive loss → update ECG encoder weights.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Frozen echo encoder preserves prior knowledge but cannot adapt to ECG‑specific nuances.  
- Requires paired ECG‑echo studies, which may be scarce.  
- Ignores temporal synchronization (e.g., R‑peak to cardiac phase), risking alignment of non‑corresponding cycles.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Sharp performance drop on a new dataset (e.g., UK Biobank vs. internal) suggests poor domain generalization.  
- Sensitivity to missing echo views indicates over‑reliance on certain angles.  
- Training instability if echo encoder is accidentally unfrozen with high learning rate.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Ablate the attention aggregator: replace with mean pooling over echo view features and measure change in LVEF AUROC.  
2. Unfreeze the echo encoder with a learning rate 10× smaller than the ECG encoder and compare validation performance to the frozen version.  
3. Evaluate on a dataset with only two echo views (instead of ≥3) to assess robustness to view sparsity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does incorporating beat‑ or phase‑level temporal synchronization between ECG and Echo signals affect the quality of learned ECG representations for morphological prediction?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state as a limitation: "we do not account for beat‑ or phase‑level synchronization between ECGs and Echos."
- Why unresolved: This temporal alignment was not implemented, so its impact on capturing the link between electrical and mechanical activity remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train a variant with synchronized ECG‑Echo pairs (e.g., aligning R‑peaks with cardiac phases) and compare performance on downstream tasks like LVEF classification.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can Echo2ECG's dependency on paired ECG‑Echo studies be reduced to enable pre‑training on larger, unpaired datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Limitation: "our method requires paired ECG–Echo studies, which may be scarce at scale."
- Why unresolved: The current framework relies on paired data for contrastive learning; scalability is limited by data pairing constraints.
- What evidence would resolve it: Develop a semi‑supervised extension using unpaired ECGs and Echos, perhaps via generative modeling or pseudo‑pair generation, and evaluate whether performance degrades with less paired data.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does fine‑tuning the frozen pre‑trained Echo encoder during contrastive pre‑training improve ECG representations, or does it destabilize the encoder's prior knowledge?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The Echo encoder is kept frozen throughout training to "leverage large‑scale prior knowledge" (Section 3.1), but this design choice is not experimentally validated.
- Why unresolved: The trade‑off between preserving pre‑trained features and adapting to the ECG‑Echo alignment task is not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablate by fine‑tuning the Echo encoder with a smaller learning rate and compare the resulting ECG features on LVEF and SHD classification tasks.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What causes the performance drop of Echo2ECG on UK Biobank compared to PTACL (which uses CMR), and how can cross‑dataset and cross‑modality generalization be enhanced?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Table 3 shows Echo2ECG's AUROC on UKB (0.692) is lower than PTACL's (0.729), and PTACL was pre‑trained on UKB with CMR, a different modality.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not analyze why Echo2ECG generalizes less well to UKB, possibly due to dataset shifts or modality gaps.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyze feature distributions of ECG representations from Echo2ECG across datasets, and test domain adaptation techniques like adversarial training or modality‑invariant representations.

## Limitations
- External validation limited to two datasets (EchoNext, UK Biobank); internal set details undisclosed.  
- Baseline models, training protocols, and preprocessing are not fully described, hindering fair comparison.  
- Performance metrics lack confidence intervals or statistical significance testing.  
- ECG‑echo temporal alignment may be inconsistent (different cardiac cycles), risking spurious correlations.  
- Robustness to missing/occluded echo views is not evaluated.
