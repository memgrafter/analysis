# Who We Are, Where We Are: Mental Health at the Intersection of Person, Situation, and Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05953
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05953
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** An interpretable baseline combining situational features (S8D) and person-level traits (PLT) achieves Pearson r=0.622 for continuous well-being prediction, competitive with person-contextualized embeddings.

## Executive Summary
The paper models mental health as a dynamic construct arising from person-situation interactions. Leveraging longitudinal social media data, it integrates psychologically interpretable person-level traits (e.g., resilience, depression scales) with language-inferred situational features (Situational 8 DIAMONDS). These theory-grounded features predict continuous well-being and classify sentence-level adaptive/maladaptive self-states, performing comparably to embeddings from a psychometrically-informed language model (HaRT) while offering full interpretability. Results underscore the value of combining computational methods with established psychological theory to model mental state dynamics.

## Method Summary
The study tackles two tasks on the CLPsych 2025 shared task dataset: (1) predicting continuous well-being scores from user timelines, and (2) classifying adaptive/maladaptive spans at the sentence level. Feature sets include: Situational 8 DIAMONDS (S8D) extracted via Deepseek-R1 with 2-shot prompting; 19 Person-Level Traits (PLT) from pretrained models (e.g., implicit motives, mental health scales, resilience facets, cognitive distortions); and person-contextualized embeddings from a HaRT model fine-tuned on the dataset. For well-being prediction, ridge regression with 5-fold user-level cross-validation is used; for classification, logistic regression predicts probabilities, with thresholds applied to identify spans. Models are evaluated via Pearson correlation and MSE (regression) and macro F1 and AUC (classification).

## Key Results
- **S8D+PLT baseline:** Pearson r=0.622 (MSE=2.174) for well-being prediction, matching HaRT performance while being fully interpretable.
- **Hybrid HaRT+PLT classification:** Macro F1 0.52 (adaptive), 0.58 (maladaptive); AUC 0.76 and 0.77.
- **Qualitative coherence:** Top predictive features align with psychological theory—life satisfaction (positive predictor), depression scale (negative predictor).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Integrating theory-grounded person-level traits (PLT) with situation-level features (S8D) captures the dynamic person-situation interplay central to mental health. This approach yields interpretable models where coefficients map directly to psychological constructs, while matching the performance of black-box embeddings from a psychometrically-informed LLM (HaRT). The hybrid further leverages contextual embeddings to refine span-level classification, balancing explainability and accuracy by combining stable trait information with situational nuance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Person-Situation Interaction:** Mental health emerges from ongoing interactions between stable traits and transient situations. Why needed: foundation for feature design beyond static traits. Quick check: Ensure features cover both person-level (PLT) and situation-level (S8D) dimensions.
- **Situational 8 DIAMONDS (S8D):** Eight situational dimensions (e.g., Duty, Social) rated 1-9 via LLM. Why needed: quantify contextual influences on mental state. Quick check: LLM outputs show expected variation across posts and correlate with situational keywords.
- **Person-Level Traits (PLT):** Psychological constructs (resilience facets, PHQ-9, CES-D, anxiety, etc.). Why needed: capture individual differences in baseline mental health. Quick check: Feature distributions align with population norms or clinical thresholds.
- **HaRT (Health-adapted RoBERTa):** Language model fine-tuned for mental health prediction. Why needed: generate embeddings sensitive to personal context and linguistic patterns. Quick check: Embedding neighborhoods reflect similar user states or posts.
- **Ridge/Logistic Regression:** Linear models with regularization. Why needed: maintain interpretability and avoid overfitting with high-dimensional features. Quick check: Coefficients have consistent signs with psychological theory (e.g., depression scale negative).
- **5-fold User-Level Cross-Validation:** Splits at user (timeline) level. Why needed: prevent data leakage and ensure generalization to unseen users. Quick check: No user appears in both training and test folds across splits.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: User timelines -> Post texts -> [S8D extraction (Deepseek-R1), PLT computation (pretrained models), HaRT fine-tuning & embedding extraction] -> Feature vectors (S8D+PLT, HaRT, hybrids) -> Ridge/Logistic Regression -> Predictions (well-being scores, adaptive/maladaptive spans). Critical path: Data acquisition -> Feature extraction -> Model training -> Evaluation; must avoid data leakage at each stage, especially during HaRT fine-tuning. Design tradeoffs: Interpretability (S8D+PLT) versus performance (HaRT); computational cost of multiple PLT models; variability in LLM-based S8D extraction. Failure signatures: 1) Performance inflation when HaRT is fine-tuned on full data before CV (leakage); 2) S8D ratings show low variance or systematic bias across posts; 3) PLT-only models yield near-uniform probability predictions in classification. First experiments: (1) Replicate S8D+PLT baseline using provided prompts and pretrained PLT models; expect ridge regression r~0.62. (2) Test leakage impact: compare HaRT performance with proper fold-wise fine-tuning versus full-data fine-tuning; expect significant drop. (3) Ablate hybrid feature combinations (add HaRT embeddings to S8D+PLT) and measure macro F1/AUC changes on sentence-level classification.

## Limitations
- The CLPsych 2025 dataset is not publicly released, and acknowledged data contamination in HaRT fine-tuning undermines reproducibility of reported CV results.
- Implementation details for HaRT fine-tuning (hyperparameters, embedding extraction) and some PLT models (e.g., ReLM, cognitive distortions) are missing or rely on unpublished resources.
- LLM-based S8D extraction lacks reliability validation against human annotations, risking noisy situational features and limited clinical generalizability.

## Confidence
- **High:** Theoretical framing of person-situation interaction is clear and psychologically grounded.
- **Medium:** Reported S8D+PLT performance (r=0.622) is plausible but dependent on unreleased data and specific CV methodology.
- **Low:** Full replication of HaRT-enhanced results and classification thresholds; missing implementation details may prevent faithful recreation.

## Next Checks
1. Obtain CLPsych 2025 dataset and enforce strict user-level 5-fold splits; audit HaRT fine-tuning to ensure no test data leaks into training within each fold.
2. Acquire HaRT fine-tuning hyperparameters from authors or reconstruct via systematic ablation on a held-out fold; document embedding layer selection and pooling strategy.
3. Conduct S8D reliability audit: manually annotate a sample of 100 posts, compute inter-rater reliability (ICC) with Deepseek-R1 outputs, and apply calibration if systematic bias is found.
