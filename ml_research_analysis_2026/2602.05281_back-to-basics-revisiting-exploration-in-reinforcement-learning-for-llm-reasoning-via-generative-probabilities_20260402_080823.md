# Back to Basics: Revisiting Exploration in Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning via Generative Probabilities

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05281
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05281
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** ProGRPO improves Pass@1 by 5.7% and Pass@32 by 13.9% over GRPO on Qwen2.5-7B for math/coding, while increasing output diversity.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LLM reasoning often suffers from mode collapse, where models suppress valid alternative reasoning paths in favor of high-reward but potentially less robust solutions. This paper introduces ProGRPO, an extension of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that augments the advantage signal with an Advantage Re-weighting Mechanism (ARM). ARM uses prompt perplexity and answer confidence—derived from low-probability token likelihoods—to attenuate updates for overconfident prediction paths and redistribute probability mass toward under-explored correct solutions. Empirical results on mathematical and coding benchmarks show that ProGRPO maintains or improves accuracy while significantly increasing output entropy and reducing lexical/semantic redundancy.

## Method Summary
ProGRPO builds on GRPO, which normalizes advantages within a group of sampled responses per prompt to reduce variance. The key addition is ARM, which computes two signals for each prompt: perplexity (measuring prompt difficulty) and confidence (measuring answer certainty, based on the average likelihood of the lowest-probability 20% of tokens in the response). These are combined into a reweighting coefficient that multiplies the group-relative advantage before the policy update. This reduces the update magnitude for prompts that are both confident and perplexity-high (likely overfit), while relatively boosting updates for under-explored but correct responses. The method is implemented within the GRPO framework without additional parameters beyond the fixed coefficient α.

## Key Results
- ProGRPO yields a **5.7% absolute improvement in Pass@1** and **13.9% in Pass@32** over GRPO on Qwen2.5-7B across mathematical and coding benchmarks.
- It **increases output entropy** by 27.3% relative to GRPO, indicating more diverse generations.
- It reduces lexical and semantic redundancy, with **Self-BLEU dropping by 8.1%** and **Semantic Cosine similarity dropping by 6.4%** compared to GRPO.
- ProGRPO consistently outperforms GRPO and FlowRL on both accuracy and diversity metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ARM combats mode collapse by identifying two risk factors: (1) prompts that are already solved with high confidence (the model is "overconfident" on a specific path), and (2) prompts that are inherently perplexing (difficult). For such prompts, the standard GRPO update would reinforce a single dominant reasoning chain. ARM multiplies the advantage by (1 - α·c(q)·p(q)), where c(q) is answer confidence and p(q) is prompt perplexity. This attenuates the gradient for overconfident, high-perplexity prompts, preventing overfitting to a single pattern. Simultaneously, for correct but under-explored solutions (low confidence, high perplexity), the multiplier is closer to 1, allowing the policy to explore alternative valid chains. The use of low-probability token likelihoods to compute confidence ensures that the model's uncertainty about less obvious reasoning steps is captured, guiding updates toward expanding the solution space rather than exploiting a narrow set.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** Required because the task involves training on problems with automatically checkable outcomes (e.g., code execution, math answer validation). *Quick check:* Can the correctness of a generated answer be determined programmatically without human judgment?
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** A policy gradient method that normalizes advantages within a group of responses sampled for the same prompt, reducing variance. *Quick check:* Are advantages computed relative to the mean advantage of the group?
- **Token Likelihood & Perplexity:** Token likelihoods measure the model's probability for individual tokens; perplexity is the exponentiated average negative log-likelihood for a prompt, indicating difficulty. *Quick check:* Is perplexity calculated as exp(-(1/N) Σ log P(token_i))?
- **Mode Collapse in RL for LLMs:** The phenomenon where the policy converges to a low-entropy distribution, outputting similar reasoning chains even when multiple correct solutions exist. *Quick check:* Does the model's output diversity decrease over training despite stable or improving accuracy?
- **Advantage Re-weighting:** Modifying the advantage signal by a factor to control the strength of policy updates. *Quick check:* Is the reweighting applied before or after the group-relative normalization?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Policy (π) -> Sample Group of Responses -> Compute Token Likelihoods -> Identify Low-probability Tokens -> Compute Prompt Perplexity p(q) & Answer Confidence c(q) -> Compute Group-relative Advantages -> Apply ARM Multiplier (1 - α·c(q)·p(q)) -> Update Policy (π)

**Critical path:** For each batch of prompts: (1) sample multiple responses per prompt; (2) for each response, compute token probabilities; (3) extract the subset of tokens with lowest probabilities (bottom 20%) to compute c(q); (4) compute p(q) from the prompt's token likelihoods; (5) calculate group-relative advantages; (6) scale advantages by the ARM coefficient; (7) perform a policy gradient step.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Low-probability token fraction:* A larger fraction may capture more alternative reasoning steps but could include noise; a smaller fraction may miss subtle variations.  
- *Fixed α:* A static coefficient does not adapt to training dynamics; too high α may hinder learning on difficult problems, too low may not prevent collapse.  
- *Perplexity vs. confidence:* Perplexity depends on the prompt and may be costly to compute for every step; confidence relies on the model's calibration, which can be poor for some architectures.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Accuracy drops without entropy increase → ARM may be suppressing too many updates.  
- Entropy increases but accuracy falls → over-exploration of incorrect paths.  
- Confidence c(q) correlates inversely with true problem difficulty → ARM misguides updates for hard problems.

**Exactly 3 first experiments:**  
1. **Minimal reproduction:** Run ProGRPO on a small subset (e.g., 100 GSM8K problems) with Qwen2.5-7B for 100 steps; verify that entropy increases and Pass@1 is non-inferior to GRPO.  
2. **ARM ablation:** Compare ProGRPO (α=0.3) with GRPO (α=0) on the same subset, measuring change in advantage magnitude distribution and diversity metrics.  
3. **Low-probability token threshold sweep:** Vary the fraction of lowest-probability tokens (10%, 20%, 30%, 40%) and plot Pass@1 and entropy to identify sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does the Advantage Re-weighting Mechanism (ARM) in ProGRPO transfer to other RL frameworks such as PPO or DPO, maintaining its benefits in diversity and accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper explicitly states that ProGRPO is "a principled extension of GRPO" and experiments are conducted only within the GRPO framework (Section 4.1 and Algorithm 1).
- **Why unresolved:** The compatibility of ARM with different policy optimization backbones remains unexplored; GRPO-specific components (e.g., group-relative advantages) might interact uniquely with ARM.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement ARM within PPO and DPO pipelines, evaluate on mathematical and coding benchmarks, measuring entropy, Pass@k, and training stability compared to vanilla baselines.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the fraction of low-probability tokens used to compute confidence scores affect ProGRPO's performance, and is the fixed 20% threshold optimal across tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 3.2 states that "roughly 20% of token positions substantially influence the subsequent reasoning path" and defines T_low accordingly.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not ablates this hyperparameter; the choice is based on prior work and may not generalize to all model architectures or problem domains.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically vary the low-probability token percentage (e.g., 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%) and evaluate its impact on output entropy, correctness, and diversity metrics across multiple benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the reweighting coefficient α be adapted during training to further improve the exploration-exploitation trade-off, rather than using a fixed value?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Table 6 shows sensitivity to α; the paper selects α=0.3 as a fixed hyperparameter without scheduling.
- **Why unresolved:** A static α may not respond to changing policy dynamics; an adaptive schedule could potentially enhance performance or stability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design a curriculum where α increases or decreases based on metrics like average entropy or Pass@k, and compare final performance against the fixed-α baseline.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How well does the prompt confidence c(q) correlate with true problem difficulty, and does a misalignment limit the curriculum-like effects of ARM?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors incorporate c(q) citing Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (Section 3.1) but provide no analysis of its relationship to actual difficulty.
- **Why unresolved:** If c(q) is a poor difficulty estimator, the adaptive regulation might be misguiding updates; the design choice lacks empirical validation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compute correlation between c(q) and prompt-level success rates across the training set; test alternative difficulty estimators (e.g., based on external solvers or human labels) in place of c(q) and measure impact on performance.

## Limitations
- Evaluations are confined to **Qwen2.5-7B** on math/coding benchmarks only; generalization to other models, scales, and tasks is unknown.
- No statistical significance testing (multiple seeds, confidence intervals) is reported, so the robustness of observed gains is uncertain.
- The method's dependence on low-probability token likelihoods may be brittle under different tokenizers or model calibrations.
