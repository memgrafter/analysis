# Reinforced Curriculum Pre-Alignment for Domain-Adaptive VLMs
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10740
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10740
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** RCPA enables domain adaptation of VLMs with competitive domain performance (e.g., CIDEr 0.9814 on COCO) while preserving general capabilities (IFEval-I 0.7326 vs. 0.3405 for full fine-tuning), reducing policy update variance by ~41%.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language models (VLMs) face a trade-off between adapting to specialized domains and retaining their general multimodal abilities. Standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) causes catastrophic forgetting, while reinforcement learning methods like GRPO fail when the model lacks initial domain knowledge. This paper introduces Reinforced Curriculum Pre-Alignment (RCPA), a two-phase post-training paradigm combining partial answer-prefix injection (Pre-Alignment) with full reward-driven optimization (Reinforcement Alignment). RCPA includes Curriculum Progress Perception (CPP) to dynamically schedule reward thresholds and prefix lengths, and Curriculum Difficulty Perception (CDP) to reweight samples by difficulty. Evaluations on COCO Caption, Geo170K, and OpenI show that RCPA achieves domain-specific performance close to full fine-tuning (e.g., CIDEr 0.9814 vs. 1.0172) while significantly preserving general skills (IFEval-I 0.7326 vs. 0.3405). The method also stabilizes training, reducing policy update variance by ~41% and avoiding optimization collapse.

## Method Summary
RCPA operates in two stages. In the Pre-Alignment phase, the model is trained with a partial answer-prefix, which reduces task complexity and safely introduces domain concepts. The length of the prefix is gradually decreased according to a sliding token ratio σ. Concurrently, a reward threshold δ(s) is scheduled linearly from δ_min to δ_max, allowing the model to receive feedback even when its generations are weak. Once a foundational understanding is achieved, training transitions to the Reinforcement Alignment phase, where the model performs full-generation RL using the GRPON objective. This phase employs CPP to continue controlling the reward threshold and CDP to weight samples by their difficulty (inverse of mean reward), focusing learning on underperformed examples. A KL penalty against a fixed reference model (π_ref) is maintained throughout to preserve general knowledge. The overall training loop integrates these components to achieve domain specialization without forgetting.

## Key Results
- **Competitive domain performance:** CIDEr 0.9814 on COCO Captions (vs. 1.0172 for full fine-tuning), BLEU-1 0.8035 on Geo170K, demonstrating effective specialization.
- **Strong general capability retention:** IFEval-I 0.7326 compared to 0.3405 for full fine-tuning, indicating minimal forgetting.
- **Training stability:** Reduction in policy update variance by ~41% and steady reward growth, avoiding the optimization collapse observed in standard GRPO.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Staged Adaptation for Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation
**Claim:** RCPA mitigates catastrophic forgetting by staged adaptation, avoiding the distributional shift caused by monolithic SFT.
**Mechanism:** A two-phase process: (1) Pre-Alignment uses partial answer-prefix injection to reduce task complexity and bootstrap domain signals; (2) Reinforcement Alignment transitions to full-generation RL with GRPON, refining outputs under KL constraints.
**Core assumption:** Domain knowledge must be grounded safely before full RL can stabilize; direct RL fails without initial competence.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.1 describe the two-phase paradigm; Section 5.6 shows cold-start experiments validating the need for gradual exposure.
**Break condition:** If prefix injection truncates critical entities, the pre-aligned policy may learn incorrect associations, undermining downstream RL.

### Mechanism 2: Curriculum Progress Perception (CPP) for Dense Early Signals
**Claim:** CPP reduces optimization collapse by densifying reward signals early and scheduling difficulty.
**Mechanism:** Dynamically controls (a) prefix length k(s) via sliding token ratio σ, and (b) reward threshold δ(s) linearly from δ_min to δ_max. Lower early thresholds accept weaker semantic matches, providing learnable feedback when the model has minimal domain knowledge.
**Core assumption:** Reward sparsity and high variance in early RL stem from the model’s inability to generate correct full responses; densifying signals via constraints stabilizes early policy updates.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions dynamic scheduling; Section 4.3 details the formulas; Section 5.6 shows that even minimal partial-data pretraining improves domain performance but harms generality—validating the need for gradual, not abrupt, exposure.
**Break condition:** If δ_min is set too low, the model receives rewards for irrelevant outputs; if σ is too small, training becomes slow; if δ_max is reached too early, a reward cliff occurs.

### Mechanism 3: Curriculum Difficulty Perception (CDP) for Focused Learning
**Claim:** CDP prevents overfitting to easy samples and prioritizes high-learning-value examples via dynamic reweighting.
**Mechanism:** For a minibatch, computes mean reward r̄ per sample across G generations. Sample weight w = 1/(offset + r̄) scales the GRPON advantage term—lower r̄ (harder samples) gets higher weight.
**Core assumption:** Samples with low mean reward are under-learned and offer greater gradient information; upweighting them accelerates domain mastery while avoiding wasted steps on already-mastered easy cases.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions reweighting by difficulty; Section 4.4 defines w; Section 5.3 ablation shows CDP improves specialization when combined with CPP.
**Break condition:** If noise in the reward function causes r̄ to poorly reflect true difficulty, CDP may misweight samples, amplifying noise or neglecting truly hard examples.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Catastrophic Forgetting in VLM Fine-Tuning.
  - Why needed: RCPA’s core goal is to avoid the forgetting seen in SFT/FFT. Understanding why SFT forgets (distributional gap between pre-training and target domain) is essential to grasp RCPA’s staged approach.
  - Quick check: Why does maximizing likelihood on domain-specific data cause a VLM to lose general capabilities?
- **Concept:** Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) Collapse.
  - Why needed: GRPO fails when the model lacks initial domain knowledge. RCPA’s pre-alignment phase directly addresses this RL cold-start problem by bootstrapping minimal competence before reward-based refinement.
  - Quick check: What is the “optimization collapse” problem in GRPO when applied to a novel domain, and how does pre-alignment avoid it?
- **Concept:** Curriculum Learning principles.
  - Why needed: CPP and CDP are curriculum mechanisms—ordering samples from easy to hard and reweighting by difficulty. Recognizing this pedagogical parallel explains why RCPA’s progressive modulation is more sample-efficient than uniform exposure.
  - Quick check: How does presenting easier subtasks first (via prefix injection) differ from simply using more training data?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** GRPON (RL backbone) ↔ CPP (prefix length & threshold scheduler) ↔ CDP (sample reweighting) → KL penalty against π_ref

**Critical path:**
1. For each step s: compute k(s) from σ, construct context with ground-truth prefix.
2. Generate G responses from current policy π_θ conditioned on context.
3. Compute per-response reward R(oi, y; δ(s)), then r̄ per sample → weight w.
4. Compute GRPON advantage with w-scaled per-token terms, apply KL penalty to π_ref.
5. Update π_θ; increment s, adjust δ(s).
6. When s ≥ S/σ, prefix length becomes 0 → full-generation phase.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- σ (sliding ratio): Larger σ → faster token restoration but shallower domain learning; smaller σ → slower but deeper integration (optimal at 16 in experiments).
- δ_min/δ_max: Too wide a range risks reward cliff; too narrow limits progressive challenge.
- γ (KL coefficient): Higher preserves generality but may slow adaptation; paper uses 0.01.
- Computational cost: Pre-Alignment adds 28% of total training time; 56% more time per step vs. GRPO due to reward computation and prefix handling.

**Failure signatures:**
- Reward collapse: Average reward plateaus or decreases early.
- Generality drop: IFEval-I/MMMU scores fall sharply.
- Prefix-induced distortion: If k(s) truncates essential answer components, the suffix generation learns on corrupted context.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ablation (Section 5.3, Fig 2a):** Remove CPP → large CIDEr drop on COCO (0.9814 → ~0.7); remove CDP → less specialization, confirming complementary roles.
2. **Parameter sensitivity (Section 5.4, Fig 2b-d):** Sweep σ (found 16 optimal), α/β (0.6/0.7), δ_min/δ_max (0.7/0.8). Demonstrates stability window and impact on domain/general tradeoff.
3. **Cold-start validation (Section 5.6, Table 2):** Compare RCPA to using 0.1/1.0 epochs of target data as “cold start” before RL. RCPA matches 1.0-epoch domain scores but preserves IFEval-I (~0.73 vs. 0.41), proving progressive modulation superior to abrupt exposure.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What theoretical guarantees exist for the specific curriculum schedule (answer-prefix length progression and reward threshold δ(s) scheduling) in RCPA regarding the optimal trade-off between domain knowledge acquisition and preservation of general capabilities?
2. Can the key hyperparameters of RCPA (sliding token ratio σ, reward thresholds δmin/δmax, weighting α/β) be automatically adapted during training based on the model's learning dynamics rather than fixed values tuned on validation sets?
3. How does RCPA perform on specialized domains with data modalities significantly different from the image-text pairs used in the paper (e.g., 3D medical volumes, video, scientific plots, or tabular data with textual descriptions)?
4. To what extent do the two curriculum modules (CPP and CDP) contribute independently and interactively to RCPA's success? Is one more critical for stability while the other boosts specialization?

## Limitations
- GRPON implementation details are not fully specified; reliance on external frameworks (e.g., EasyR1) may cause replication gaps.
- Hyperparameter dependencies (σ, δ thresholds, γ) are tuned on limited domains; transferability to new or more complex domains is uncertain.
- Reward components (SentenceBERT/NLI/Spacy) may be noisy or fail on domain-specific jargon, potentially destabilizing CPP and CDP.

## Confidence
- RCPA mitigates catastrophic forgetting: High
- CPP stabilizes early RL: Medium
- CDP improves specialization: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Reward noise stress test:** Replace semantic similarity (SentenceBERT) with a perturbed metric (e.g., add Gaussian noise to scores) on a held-out medical set. Measure impact on RCPA's stability (reward variance, final CIDEr) versus GRPO.
2. **Cross-domain hyperparameter sweep:** Fix σ=16, δ_min/δ_max=0.7/0.8 from COCO, then apply RCPA to two new domains (e.g., satellite imagery, molecular diagrams). Record degradation in domain performance and IFEval-I to test hyperparameter transferability.
3. **Compute-overhead trade-off:** Isolate Pre-Alignment time cost by training GRPO with equivalent k(s) prefix injection but without CPP's dynamic δ(s). Compare final domain/general metrics to full RCPA to quantify CPP's marginal benefit versus scheduling overhead.
