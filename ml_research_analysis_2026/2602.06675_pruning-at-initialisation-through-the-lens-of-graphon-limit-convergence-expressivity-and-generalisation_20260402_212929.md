# Pruning at Initialisation through the lens of Graphon Limit: Convergence, Expressivity, and Generalisation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06675
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06675
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Pruning-at-initialisation masks converge to deterministic graphons, enabling a topological taxonomy and improved theoretical guarantees for expressivity and generalisation.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the lack of theoretical understanding of pruning-at-initialisation (PaI) masks as network width grows. By modeling PaI scores via a Factorised Saliency Model and representing masks as bipartite step‑kernels, the authors prove convergence in bipartite cut‑norm to a deterministic graphon under mild conditions. This result validates the Graphon Limit Hypothesis and yields a taxonomy: unstructured methods (e.g., Random, Magnitude) converge to homogeneous graphons, while data‑driven methods (e.g., SNIP, GraSP) converge to heterogeneous graphons that encode feature selection. Furthermore, the intrinsic dimension of the active subspace revealed by the graphon governs universal approximation of sparse networks, and a Graphon‑NTK bound shows that heterogeneous graphons improve kernel alignment and generalisation. Empirical visualisations confirm convergence for SNIP and GraSP across widths, transforming sparse network analysis into a continuous‑operator framework.

## Method Summary
The core method constructs a Factorised Saliency Model where PaI scores are expressed as a product of input feature, neuron feature, and edge noise components. Masks are interpreted as bipartite step‑kernels (functions on pairs of input and neuronal nodes). Under mild assumptions on the score distributions and network width, the authors prove that the sequence of bipartite graphs induced by the masks converges in bipartite cut‑norm to a deterministic graphon—a bounded symmetric measurable function on the unit square. This graphon limit captures the limiting pattern of connectivity. The theoretical framework then leverages tools from graph limit theory and neural tangent kernels (NTK) to derive expressivity and generalisation results.

## Key Results
- Graphon convergence: For SNIP and GraSP, the bipartite mask graphs converge to a deterministic graphon, yielding a taxonomy: unstructured methods → homogeneous graphons; data‑driven methods → heterogeneous graphons that encode input feature selection.
- Expressivity: Universal approximation of sparse networks is characterised by the intrinsic dimension of the active subspace induced by the graphon; lower intrinsic dimension eases approximation.
- Generalisation: A Graphon‑NTK bound shows the limit graphon controls path density, which influences the RKHS norm term; heterogeneous graphons improve kernel alignment and yield tighter generalisation guarantees.
- Empirical evidence: Visualisations confirm convergence of SNIP/GraSP masks to their respective graphons as width increases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The graphon limit theorem provides a rigorous link between finite discrete masks and a continuous operator representation. The factorised saliency model captures the essential structure of PaI scores while enabling analytic handling. Convergence in bipartite cut‑norm ensures that any sequence of wide networks yields asymptotically consistent connectivity patterns. By characterising the limit as a graphon, one can import well‑developed tools from graph limit theory and continuous functional analysis to reason about expressivity (via intrinsic dimension of the active subspace) and generalisation (via the NTK in the limit). The mechanism reduces complex, high‑dimensional discrete optimisation to a deterministic continuous object, explaining why some PaI methods produce heterogeneous, feature‑selective masks while others do not.

## Foundational Learning
- **Graphon**: A measurable function on [0,1]^2 representing a dense limit of graphs; needed to model asymptotic connectivity patterns.
  - Quick check: Understand that a graphon can represent degrees, clustering, and community structure.
- **Bipartite cut‑norm**: A norm measuring discrepancy between two bipartite graphs; convergence in this norm ensures that measurable bipartite graph properties converge.
  - Quick check: Know that cut‑norm metrises convergence of homomorphism densities.
- **Factorised Saliency Model**: Decomposes PaI scores into input feature × neuron feature × noise; needed to derive the graphon limit.
  - Quick check: Verify that the model is separable and allows integrating over random features.
- **Intrinsic dimension of active subspace**: The effective rank of the subspace spanned by selected inputs; determines approximation capacity of sparse networks.
  - Quick check: Relate to the rank of the linear map induced by the graphon on input features.
- **Graphon‑NTK**: Extension of neural tangent kernel to the graphon limit; links path density to RKHS norm and generalisation.
  - Quick check: Recognise that NTK in infinite‑width limit is constant, but here it varies with the graphon structure.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: PaI score computation → bipartite mask embedding (step‑kernel) → graphon limit derivation → expressivity analysis (intrinsic dimension) → Graphon‑NTK generalisation bound.

Critical path: The convergence theorem (step‑kernel → graphon) is the pivotal result; without it, the subsequent analyses would rely on finite‑width artifacts.

Design tradeoffs: Using a factorised saliency model sacrifices capturing weight correlations and batch‑norm effects for analytical tractability. The graphon limit discards finite‑width fluctuations but yields a clean deterministic object amenable to continuous analysis.

Failure signatures: Inability to prove convergence for a given PaI method (e.g., violation of mild assumptions); empirical mask distributions failing to stabilise as width grows; large gaps between predicted graphon‑NTK bounds and actual generalisation error.

## Limitations
- The Factorised Saliency Model oversimplifies reality by ignoring weight correlations, batch normalisation, and non‑linearities.
- Convergence proof relies on mild conditions that may not hold for all architectures or PaI methods; finite‑width effects are not quantified.
- Intrinsic dimension extraction from the graphon may be computationally hard, limiting practical use of the universal approximation condition.
