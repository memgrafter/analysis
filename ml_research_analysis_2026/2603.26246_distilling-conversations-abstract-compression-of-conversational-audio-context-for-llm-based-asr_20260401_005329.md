# Distilling Conversations: Abstract Compression of Conversational Audio Context for LLM-based ASR

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26246  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26246  
- Reference count: 10  
- Primary result: Abstract Compression reduces the audio portion of context tokens by ~5–10× while recovering most of the accuracy gains from using raw-context audio over single‑turn ASR.

## Executive Summary
Standard LLM‑based ASR systems transcribe each utterance in isolation, missing potentially valuable conversational context. Conditioning on raw prior‑turn audio improves recognition of contextual entities (measured by Bias‑WER) but linearly increases the token length and computational cost. This paper introduces Abstract Compression: a method to replace each prior‑turn audio token sequence with a fixed number of learned latent tokens while retaining the explicit text transcripts. A two‑stage training procedure first aligns a compression module (cross‑attention with turn‑specific queries) with single‑turn ASR, then fine‑tunes the full model on compressed multi‑turn prompts using a curriculum that gradually increases context length. On both in‑domain (DefinedAI) and out‑of‑domain (WoW) tests, the compressed model significantly outperforms single‑turn ASR and nearly matches the accuracy of raw multi‑turn conditioning, albeit with a much smaller token footprint. Compression rates stabilize as context grows, and ablations show that using 16 latent tokens per turn is optimal, while pre‑training the compression module on LibriSpeech does not yield downstream benefits.

## Method Summary
The backbone is the PHI‑4‑MULTIMODAL model, which processes interleaved audio tokens (from a dedicated encoder) and text tokens. For each prior turn *i*, raw audio tokens *X_i* are compressed to *Z_i* via a cross‑attention module with learnable queries *Q_i* ∈ ℝ^{K×D}, producing *K* latent tokens. Prior‑turn transcripts *Y_i* are kept as explicit text tokens. For the current turn, raw audio tokens *X_cur* are used unchanged. Stage 1 freezes the LLM and trains only the compression module on single‑turn examples (prompt = *Z_cur* + *Y_cur*) with standard ASR loss. Stage 2 loads the Stage 1 checkpoint, adds LoRA to the audio encoder (and possibly other LLM layers), and trains on compressed multi‑turn prompts (e.g., *[Z₁,Y₁,…,Zₙ,Yₙ,X_cur]*) to generate the current transcript. A curriculum increases the maximum number of prior turns *n* every 10 % of training steps up to 10. Metrics: overall WER and entity‑focused Bias‑WER.

## Key Results
- **DefinedAI**: compressed multi‑turn WER 8.0 %, Bias‑WER 13.3 % vs. single‑turn (7.6 %, 13.5 %) and raw multi‑turn (7.5 %, 13.1 %).
- **WoW**: compressed WER 13.2 %, Bias‑WER 24.5 % vs. single‑turn (13.4 %, 25.6 %) and raw multi‑turn (12.7 %, 23.3 %).
- Audio context token count reduced by approximately 5–10×; compression rates stabilize as context length increases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The compression module learns to distil the acoustic information from raw prior audio into a compact set of latent tokens that preserve cues most relevant for recognizing contextual entities. By keeping prior transcripts explicit, the model retains full linguistic context without additional compression overhead. The two‑stage training first ensures the compression alone can support ASR, then adapts the entire model (including the audio encoder via LoRA) to leverage the compressed context in a multi‑turn setting. The fixed‑size latent tokens provide a consistent context budget regardless of prior turn duration, preventing blow‑up in token length while still supplying useful conditioning information.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal LLMs**  
  *Why needed*: Unify audio and text in a single autoregressive framework, enabling ASR as a sequence‑to‑sequence task.  
  *Quick check*: Can the model ingest audio token sequences and generate text tokens? e.g., PHI‑4‑MULTIMODAL.
- **Audio Tokenization for LLMs**  
  *Why needed*: Transforms acoustic waveforms into discrete tokens the LLM can consume; compression replaces many such tokens with few learned ones.  
  *Quick check*: Does the audio encoder produce a fixed token rate (e.g., tokens/sec) and a fixed embedding dimension *D*?
- **Contextual ASR**  
  *Why needed*: In dialogue, recognition of the current utterance often depends on earlier turns, especially for named entities and domain terms.  
  *Quick check*: Does incorporating prior transcripts/audio reduce Bias‑WER compared to isolated processing?
- **Latent Variable Compression**  
  *Why needed*: Reduces sequence length while preserving task‑relevant information, lowering computational cost.  
  *Quick check*: Can a fixed number *K* of latent tokens be learned to reconstruct raw audio tokens or maintain ASR accuracy?
- **Low‑Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**  
  *Why needed*: Efficiently adapt large pre‑trained models to new tasks with minimal additional parameters.  
  *Quick check*: Is LoRA applied to the audio encoder or LLM layers? What rank and scaling factor are used?
- **Curriculum Learning for Context Length**  
  *Why needed*: Gradually increasing context length stabilizes training and prevents forgetting.  
  *Quick check*: Does the training schedule increase the maximum number of prior turns in steps?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Audio Encoder → *X_i* (audio tokens) → Compression Module (CrossAttention with turn‑specific queries *Q_i*) → *Z_i* (K latent tokens).  
Transcripts *Y_i* (text tokens) bypass compression.  
Prompt = [*Z₁*, *Y₁*, …, *Zₙ*, *Yₙ*, *X_cur*] → PHI‑4‑MULTIMODAL (with audio LoRA) → output tokens → loss.  
Training: Stage 1 trains only Compression Module on single‑turn; Stage 2 trains Compression Module + LoRA on compressed multi‑turn prompts.

**Critical path**:  
Stage 1: For each single‑turn sample, compute *X*, compress to *Z*, form prompt *Z*+*Y*, forward through frozen LLM, compute ASR loss, update compression parameters only.  
Stage 2: Load Stage 1 checkpoint, enable LoRA, for each sample construct multi‑turn prompt using precomputed *Z_i* for prior turns (or online), vary *n* per curriculum, forward through model, compute loss on current transcript tokens, update all active parameters.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *K*: fewer tokens → lower cost but risk losing contextual info; larger *K* preserves info but reduces compression.  
- LoRA rank: lower → fewer parameters, less overfitting risk; higher → more adaptation capacity.  
- Curriculum pacing: too aggressive may destabilize; too conservative slows convergence.  
- Stage 1 data domain: LibriSpeech pre‑training improves single‑turn WER but does not translate to better contextual Bias‑WER, indicating task‑specific alignment matters.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Stage 1 single‑turn WER remains very high (e.g., >30 %) → compression module not learning.  
- Stage 2 dev Bias‑WER shows no improvement over single‑turn → context not utilized effectively.  
- Audio token counts not reduced as expected → latent tokens not substituted correctly at inference.  
- Large train‑dev WER gap (>5 %) → overfitting; consider stronger regularization or smaller LoRA rank.

**First experiments**:  
1. *Stage 1 sanity check*: Train compression with *K*=16 on DefinedAI single‑turn; evaluate dev WER. Target: ~21 % (as reported).  
2. *K ablation*: Repeat Stage 1 with *K*∈{8,16,32}; confirm *K*=16 yields lowest single‑turn WER.  
3. *Context benefit*: After Stage 2 (full curriculum), compare dev Bias‑WER for compressed multi‑turn vs. single‑turn baseline. Expect improved Bias‑WER, confirming effective use of compressed context.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does Abstract Compression generalize to other multimodal LLM backbones?  
2. What are the real‑world computational savings (latency, memory, KV‑cache) of Abstract Compression?  
3. Why does Stage 1 pre‑training on large out‑of‑domain speech (LibriSpeech) fail to improve downstream contextual ASR despite better single‑turn compressed performance?  
4. Can compressing prior‑turn transcripts (not just audio) further reduce context size without harming accuracy?  
5. Does Abstract Compression scale to very long conversations (>10 turns) and does its benefit plateau or grow?

## Limitations
- Audio tokenization specifics (token rate, embedding dimension) are unreported, hindering exact replication of token counts and compression rates.  
- Entity annotation for Bias‑WER is not described, preventing exact reproduction of that metric.  
- LoRA configuration (rank, α) and detailed curriculum pacing are unspecified, introducing variability in replication and resource usage.

## Confidence
- Compressed multi‑turn improves over single‑turn (both WER and Bias‑WER, both domains): **Medium**  
- Compressed approaches raw multi‑turn but falls slightly short: **Medium**  
- Compression rates stabilize with longer context: **Medium**  
- Ablation: *K*=16 best; LibriSpeech pre‑training ineffective: **Medium**
