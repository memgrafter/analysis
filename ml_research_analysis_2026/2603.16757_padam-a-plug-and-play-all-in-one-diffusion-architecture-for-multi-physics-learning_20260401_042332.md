# pADAM: A Plug-and-Play All-in-One Diffusion Architecture for Multi-Physics Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16757
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16757
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A single class-conditional diffusion model learns a shared probabilistic prior across heterogeneous PDE families, enabling forward, inverse, and identification tasks without retraining, achieving <4.1% relative L2 error under 10% sparse observations.

## Executive Summary
Deep learning for PDE solving is highly fragmented, with existing neural solvers typically designed for a single equation and requiring full retraining for new physics. pADAM addresses this by introducing a unified, class-conditional diffusion architecture that learns a shared probabilistic prior across multiple distinct PDE families (e.g., diffusion, advection, Navier–Stokes). This single model supports a range of tasks—including forward state prediction, inverse parameter inference from sparse data, and probabilistic PDE identification—via a plug-and-play observation guidance mechanism at sampling time. The framework also integrates conformal prediction to provide rigorous, finite-sample uncertainty guarantees. Across benchmarks, pADAM matches or exceeds the accuracy of specialized PDE-specific models while dramatically increasing versatility.

## Method Summary
pADAM's core innovation is a unified representation and a class-conditional diffusion prior. All system data (state fields, parameters) are lifted into a common 3-channel tensor `x`. A single U-Net denoiser is trained via score matching across all PDE classes, conditioned on a class label `c`. At inference, tasks are performed by solving a guided probability-flow ODE. The guidance term `λ_obs ∇ₓ ||y_obs - A(ˆx₀(x))||²` uses a measurement operator `A` that selects which components of the current sample correspond to the available sparse observations `y_obs`, steering the diffusion trajectory toward physically consistent solutions. Conformal calibration is applied post-hoc to prediction intervals to ensure coverage.

## Key Results
- pADAM achieves relative L2 errors below 2.8% on forward/inverse tasks under full observations and below 4.1% with only 10% sparse data across three PDE families.
- Conformal calibration increases empirical coverage from ~58% to >98% under sparse observations, providing reliable uncertainty quantification.
- For zero-shot model selection on an unseen advection–diffusion–reaction equation, pADAM correctly identifies the governing PDE and infers parameters using only two sparse snapshots, with uncertainty bounds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** pADAM learns a single class-conditional diffusion prior that captures shared probabilistic structure across heterogeneous PDE families.
**Mechanism:** The model approximates the conditional score function `∇ₓ log p(x|c)` via a denoiser `Dθ` trained with a class label `c`. This generative prior spans multiple physical manifolds within one model.
**Core assumption:** Different PDE families occupy overlapping or adjacent latent manifolds representable without catastrophic interference.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "unified class-conditional diffusion model that learns a shared probabilistic across heterogeneous partial differential equation families." Section Page 2: "pADAM learns a shared class-conditional probabilistic prior over system states and, where applicable, physical parameters, allowing forward prediction and inverse inference within a single architecture without retraining."
**Break condition:** If training PDEs have fundamentally disjoint dynamics (e.g., hyperbolic vs. parabolic), the shared prior may fail, causing mode collapse or poor generalization.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** A fixed-channel unified representation with parameter lifting allows one architecture to handle PDEs with varying numbers of physical parameters and state dimensions.
**Mechanism:** Each system is projected into a 3-channel tensor `x ∈ ℝ^{3×N×N_y}`. For scalar parametric PDEs: `x = [Φ, u₀, u_T]` where `Φ` is a spatial field from broadcasting parameter `ϕ`. For vector-valued PDEs: `x = [u₀, v₀, u_T]` or `[u₀, v₀, v_T]`. The lifting operator `L` ensures spatial compatibility.
**Core assumption:** Representing parameters as spatially constant fields preserves their influence while matching the convolutional backbone, injecting a physically meaningful inductive bias.
**Evidence anchors:** Section Page 15: "Unified joint representation and parameter lifting... we transform each system into a unified 3-channel generative variable x" and Equations (2) with lifting description.
**Break condition:** If parameters have strong spatially varying effects (heterogeneous coefficients), the constant-field lifting loses resolution and may fail to capture local dependencies.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Task-agnostic inference is achieved via plug-and-play observation guidance during diffusion sampling.
**Mechanism:** At inference, solve the guided probability-flow ODE: `dx/dt = -σ̇σ (sθ(x;σ,c) - λ_obs ∇ₓ ||y_obs - A(ˆx₀(x))||²)`. The operator `A` selects which components of `x` are compared to observations. Changing `A` yields different tasks.
**Core assumption:** The gradient of the measurement error serves as an effective steering signal, converging to physically consistent posterior samples even for ill-posed inverse problems.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "observation-guided conditional sampling." Section Page 17: "Task-agnostic inference via plug-and-play observational guidance" and Equation (7). Section Page 18 details task formulations.
**Break condition:** Under extremely sparse observations (<5% of the field) or a misspecified prior, the guidance signal may be too weak, leading to reconstructions that violate observations or physics.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Unified representation module -> Class-conditional diffusion backbone (U-Net `Dθ`) -> Guided sampler (solves probability-flow ODE with `A` and `λ_obs`) -> Conformal calibrator (post-hoc) / Model selector (iterates candidate classes)

- **Critical path:**
  1. **Data generation:** Simulate trajectories for each PDE class using numerical solvers.
  2. **Preprocessing:** Convert each trajectory to unified `x`; apply lifting `L` for physical parameters.
  3. **Training:** Optimize denoising score matching over all classes with class label conditioning.
  4. **Inference:**
     a. Choose PDE class `c` (or candidate set for discovery).
     b. Define measurement operator `A` based on task and available observations `y_obs`.
     c. Initialize `x` from Gaussian noise; integrate guided ODE using `Dθ`.
     d. Extract output components (e.g., `u_T`, `u₀`, `ϕ`) from final `x`.
  5. **UQ:** Draw multiple samples; apply conformal calibration if coverage guarantees are needed.
  6. **Model selection:** Repeat step 4 for each candidate class; choose class with minimal generative discrepancy `E(c)`.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Unified vs. specialized:** Single model reduces storage/training cost but may have slightly higher errors on some PDEs versus a specialized one.
  - **Parameter lifting:** Constant-field lifting is simple but cannot represent spatially varying coefficients; more complex lifting (e.g., multi-partition) could increase expressivity at channel cost.
  - **Guidance scale `λ_obs`:** Larger `λ` forces better observation fit but may distort prior; requires per-task tuning.
  - **Conformal calibration:** Guarantees coverage but needs a calibration set; small sets may yield conservative intervals.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Reconstruction errors surge** under very sparse observations (e.g., <10%) or for ill-posed inverse problems, indicating prior weakness or insufficient guidance.
  - **Conformal coverage below nominal** (e.g., <90% for α=0.05) suggests miscalibration, possibly from non-exchangeable calibration data or too small a set.
  - **Model selection misidentifies** the true PDE when the training library lacks a close prior or observed transitions are ambiguous.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline comparison:** Train pADAM on three scalar PDEs (diffusion, advection, advection–diffusion) with ~1000 trajectories per class; compare relative L2 error and parameter count against three independently trained single-PDE diffusion models (~500 trajectories each).
  2. **Sparse observation robustness:** For the unified model, evaluate forward and inverse tasks with observation fractions 100%, 30%, and 10%; measure error degradation and conformal coverage improvement.
  3. **Zero-shot extrapolation & model selection:** Test on the unseen advection–diffusion–reaction equation; jointly reconstruct states from sparse observations and perform probabilistic PDE identification from two snapshots.

## Limitations
- Generalization: Training covers only three PDE families; applicability to broader families (e.g., hyperbolic systems, heterogeneous coefficients) is uncertain.
- Zero-shot identification: Claims of correct PDE selection from two snapshots for an unseen equation lack rigorous validation.
- Sparse observation limits: Evaluation only down to 10% coverage; breakpoint below 5% is unknown.
- Parameter lifting: Constant-field representation cannot handle spatially varying coefficients.
- Conformal calibration: Coverage guarantees assume exchangeable calibration data; performance uncertain under small/correlated sets.
- Reproducibility: No public code/hyperparameters hinder verification.

## Confidence
