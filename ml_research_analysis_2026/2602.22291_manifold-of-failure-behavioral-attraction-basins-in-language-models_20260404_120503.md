# Manifold of Failure: Behavioral Attraction Basins in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22291
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22291
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** Introduces a MAP-Elites-based framework to map the continuous topology of LLM failure regions, revealing model-specific vulnerability signatures and discovering up to 370 distinct failure niches with up to 63% behavioral coverage.

## Executive Summary
This paper shifts the focus from finding isolated adversarial examples to globally characterizing a model's unsafe regions. It frames vulnerability discovery as a quality-diversity (QD) optimization problem, using MAP-Elites to explore a two-dimensional behavioral space defined by query indirection and authority framing. The method populates a grid with prompts that maximize Alignment Deviation (AD), a metric capturing the worst-case harm across ten categories. Applied to three models, it produces detailed "safety maps" that expose distinct topologies: a nearly universal vulnerability plateau (Llama-3-8B), fragmented basins (GPT-OSS-20B), and a robust hard ceiling (GPT-5-Mini). This approach provides a comprehensive, global view of alignment failure manifolds unattainable with standard attack methods.

## Method Summary
The core method is a quality-diversity optimization loop using MAP-Elites. A 25×25 grid discretizes the behavioral space (query indirection, authority framing). The process starts with 100 seed prompts. Over 15,000 iterations, a prompt is selected from the archive, mutated via one of six strategies (e.g., axis shift, paraphrase, suffix), and evaluated on the target LLM. Two judge models score the response across ten harm categories; the maximum score becomes the Alignment Deviation (AD). An LLM scorer also determines the behavioral descriptor (a1, a2). The mutated prompt is stored in its corresponding grid cell if its AD is higher than the current occupant. This iterative process populates the archive with elite prompts per cell, revealing the spatial structure of vulnerabilities.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to **63% behavioral coverage** (fraction of grid cells filled) and discovers up to **370 distinct vulnerability niches** on Llama-3-8B.
- Reveals three distinct failure topologies: Llama-3-8B shows a near-universal vulnerability plateau (mean AD 0.93); GPT-OSS-20B exhibits fragmented, spatially concentrated basins (mean AD 0.73); GPT-5-Mini demonstrates a robust hard ceiling (mean AD capped at ~0.50).
- The QD approach significantly outperforms four baseline attack methods (Random, GCG, PAIR, TAP) on coverage and diversity metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Reframing vulnerability discovery as a quality-diversity (QD) problem enables mapping the continuous topology of the failure manifold, rather than finding isolated failure points.
- **Mechanism:** MAP-Elites maintains an archive of elite prompts for each cell in a discretized behavioral grid. Through iterative selection, mutation, and evaluation, it populates the grid with the highest-quality (highest AD) prompt per cell, revealing the spatial structure of vulnerabilities across the defined behavioral dimensions.
- **Core assumption:** The model's vulnerable behaviors can be parameterized by a low-dimensional, continuous behavioral space (query indirection, authority framing) and that diverse prompts can be generated to explore this space.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the method illuminates the "continuous topology of these failure regions, which we term behavioral attraction basins." Section 5 details the 25×25 grid partitioning and elite solution search.
- **Break condition:** If the behavioral space definitions omit a critical dimension (e.g., multi-turn persuasion), the resulting map will be an incomplete projection of the true manifold.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Alignment Deviation (AD) metric effectively guides the search toward the most severe model misalignments by capturing the worst-case harm across a multi-category taxonomy.
- **Mechanism:** For a prompt-response pair, multiple judge LLMs score the response on ten harm categories. AD is defined as the maximum score across these categories. This ensures the QD search is biased toward prompts that elicit the most harmful output in *some* dimension.
- **Core assumption:** A single, scalar "worst-harm" score sufficiently characterizes failure severity for mapping high-risk regions, and the judge LLMs are reliable classifiers for all ten categories.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.2 defines AD as `Q(p) = max_{c∈C} JudgeScore_c(p)`. Section 6.7 ablation shows replacing AD with a single-dimension toxicity classifier reduces discovered diversity.
- **Break condition:** If judges have systematic blind spots (e.g., novel misinformation) or the max aggregation misses synergistically harmful prompts (moderate scores across many categories), AD fails to guide search to truly high-risk areas.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The discovered topologies (universal plateau, fragmented basins, robust ceiling) are intrinsic, model-specific signatures of alignment robustness, revealed by the dense sampling of the QD process.
- **Mechanism:** By systematically filling the behavioral grid, the heatmaps/contours expose global properties: a uniformly high AD surface indicates a shallow, easily triggered failure manifold; spatially concentrated high-AD cells indicate a fragmented manifold with safe "islands"; a uniformly capped AD indicates a strong, consistent safety mechanism with a hard boundary.
- **Core assumption:** Observed topological differences are due to the models' inherent alignment training and architecture, not artifacts of the behavioral space, mutation strategies, or evaluation budget. The 25×25 grid resolution is sufficient.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 6.1 contrasts the "strikingly different topologies" across models. Section 6.4 shows consistent cross-model metric differences.
- **Break condition:** If mutation strategies or behavioral descriptors are biased, a model might appear fragmented because the search cannot generate effective prompts in some regions, not because the model is inherently safe there.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Quality-Diversity (QD) Optimization (e.g., MAP-Elites)
  - **Why needed:** The entire framework aims to *illuminate* a space (find many diverse, high-quality solutions) rather than *optimize* to a single point. This explains why the method maps entire regions instead of finding one best attack.
  - **Quick check:** How does the archive-based niche competition in MAP-Elites differ from the iterative refinement goal of an attack like PAIR?
- **Concept:** Behavioral Space Parameterization
  - **Why needed:** The "manifold" is defined in a user-specified 2D space. The validity and interpretability of the entire map depend critically on choosing dimensions that are relevant and orthogonal to safety failures.
  - **Quick check:** If authority framing (a2) is not a meaningful factor for a model's safety, what would you expect to see in the heatmap along that axis?
- **Concept:** Attraction Basins in Dynamical Systems
  - **Why needed:** The paper's central thesis is that vulnerabilities form "behavioral attraction basins." Grasping this metaphor helps understand the basin maps: regions of prompt space (initial conditions) that lead to similar high-AD model behaviors (attractors).
  - **Quick check:** In this LLM context, what is the "dynamical system" and what are the "attractors"? (The system is the prompt→response mapping; the attractors are clusters of high-AD response behaviors.)
- **Concept:** Alignment Deviation (AD) as a Composite Metric
  - **Why needed:** AD is the quality objective that drives the QD search. Understanding that it uses a "worst-harm" aggregation across ten categories explains its ability to surface diverse severe failures.
  - **Quick check:** Why might using the *maximum* score across categories be more effective for mapping than using an *average* score?
- **Concept:** Mutational Operators for Exploration
  - **Why needed:** The six mutation strategies (axis shift, paraphrase, entity swap, suffix, crossover, embedding interpolate) are the engine for generating diverse prompts. Their design balances exploration (coverage) and exploitation (quality within niches).
  - **Quick check:** How does a "random axis perturbation" mutation (50% weight) primarily serve the goal of behavioral coverage?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Seed Prompts -> Behavioral Grid (Archive) -> [Select Parent -> Mutation -> Target LLM -> Judge LLMs -> Scoring Module -> Archive Updater] x 15,000 iterations -> Final Map/Analysis
- **Critical path:** Initialize 25×25 archive with 100 seed prompts. For 15,000 iterations: select a parent prompt from filled archive cells, apply one of six mutations, query target LLM, have two judge models score response on 10 harms, compute AD (max score) and behavioral descriptor (a1, a2) via LLM, update the corresponding archive cell if AD improves.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Grid resolution vs. budget:** Finer grid (e.g., 100×100) requires exponentially more queries to fill; 25×25 is a practical compromise for visualization within a 15k query budget.
  - **Mutation diversity vs. exploitation:** Heavy weighting (50%) on random axis perturbation ensures coverage of underexplored grid cells but may miss sophisticated, high-quality attacks; other strategies (suffix, crossover) aim to refine quality within discovered niches.
  - **Judge cost & bias:** Using two commercial LLMs as judges is expensive and their inherent biases directly shape the AD metric and thus the final safety map.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Low coverage (<20%):** Mutation strategies are ineffective at generating prompts that map to certain grid cells, or the model is uniformly safe/robust in those regions.
  - **Uniform AD surface:** Could indicate a truly vulnerable model (like Llama-3-8B) or a failure of the behavioral axes to capture meaningful variation.
  - **"Swiss cheese" map with isolated high-AD cells:** May indicate a fragmented failure manifold (like GPT-OSS-20B) or that the search is getting stuck in local niches and failing to connect them.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline replication:** Run MAP-Elites and the four baselines (Random, GCG, PAIR, TAP) on Llama-3-8B with the same 15k query budget. Confirm MAP-Elites achieves higher behavioral coverage and diversity (Table 2). This validates the QD approach against established attacks.
  2.  **Ablation - AD vs. Toxicity:** Run MAP-Elites with "ME + Toxicity" (single-dimension judge) vs. full AD. On all models, expect reduced diversity (fewer high-AD niches discovered), proving the multi-category AD is necessary for comprehensive mapping (Table 5).
  3.  **Cross-model topology validation:** Visualize the final heatmaps/contours/basin maps for all three models. Manually inspect a sample of high-AD prompts from distinct grid cells (e.g., low-indirection/high-authority vs. high-indirection/low-authority for GPT-OSS-20B) to confirm they represent meaningfully different failure modes, justifying the 2D projection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the intrinsic dimensionality of the failure manifold, and how does its topology differ when extended beyond two behavioral dimensions?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Our 2D behavioral projection necessarily loses information; the true failure manifold likely exists in a higher-dimensional space."
- **Why unresolved:** The current 2D behavioral space (query indirection, authority framing) may omit critical axes, potentially distorting the observed topologies (plateaus, basins).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Execute MAP-Elites in 3+ dimensions (e.g., adding formality or sentiment axes), compute changes in coverage, basin fragmentation, and contour structures; compare against 2D projections.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How reliable are LLM-based judges (GPT-4.1, Sonnet 4.5) for computing Alignment Deviation relative to human evaluation across ten harm categories?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "The Alignment Deviation metric relies on two judge models... which may introduce systematic biases; human validation is an important next step."
- **Why unresolved:** The entire quality metric depends on these judges; if they underdetect certain harms (e.g., technical misinformation), the safety maps are systematically biased.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Sample prompts from each model's archive, conduct human assessment on safety/severity for all ten categories, measure inter-rater reliability, and correlate with LLM scores to quantify systematic divergence.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the failure manifold evolve in multi-turn dialogues, and do new vulnerability structures (e.g., shifting basins) emerge over conversation length?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "We focus on single-turn interactions; extending to multi-turn dialogues where the manifold may evolve dynamically is future work."
- **Why unresolved:** Real-world LLM usage is conversational; multi-turn attacks may reveal different topological dynamics not captured by single-turn maps.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Adapt MAP-Elites to generate multi-turn conversations, define behavioral descriptors based on dialogue history, and produce temporal maps of alignment deviation; compare basin stability and connectivity across turns.

## Limitations
- The 2D behavioral parameterization (query indirection, authority framing) may be insufficient to capture all critical failure dimensions (e.g., multi-turn persuasion), risking incomplete or misleading manifold maps.
- The Alignment Deviation metric depends on the reliability of two commercial judge LLMs, whose systematic blind spots or biases could distort the safety maps.
- Observed model-specific topologies could be partially artifacts of the fixed behavioral space definition, the 25×25 grid resolution, or the specific mutation strategies, rather than purely intrinsic model properties.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1 (QD mapping efficacy):** High confidence — Empirical coverage/diversity gains over baselines are clear and methodologically sound.
- **Claim 2 (AD as guiding metric):** Medium confidence — Ablation shows AD’s role in diversity, but unquantified judge reliability risks metric validity.
- **Claim 3 (Model-specific topologies):** Medium confidence — Cross-model differences are consistent, but could be confounded by the fixed behavioral space and limited resolution.

## Next Checks
1.  **Expand Behavioral Axes:** Re-run MAP-Elites with additional dimensions (e.g., multi-turn depth, emotional manipulation) and measure whether the topologies (coverage, fragmentation) for each model remain stable or dramatically change.
2.  **Human-validate AD:** For a stratified sample of high-AD prompts across grid cells, have human experts score harm categories. Correlate with judge LLM scores to quantify systematic divergence.
3.  **Grid Sensitivity Analysis:** Execute MAP-Elites at higher resolutions (e.g., 50×50) and with alternative mutation mixes (e.g., increased crossover weight). Compare coverage, mean AD, and basin structure to assess robustness to discretization and search hyperparameters.
