# KnowMe-Bench: Benchmarking Person Understanding for Lifelong Digital Companions

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.04745
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04745
*   **Reference count:** 18
*   **Primary result:** Retrieval-augmented and entity-memory systems improve factual recall and entity consistency (+8–12% on T1–T2), but all architectures perform poorly on higher-order insight tasks (≤22.3% on T6–T7), revealing a fundamental gap in reasoning over lived experience.

## Executive Summary
KnowMe-Bench introduces a benchmark to evaluate person understanding in lifelong digital companions, addressing the misalignment where retrieval performance poorly proxies true comprehension. It reconstructs high-density autobiographical narratives into a flashback-aware, time-anchored cognitive stream, separating experiences into five primitives (Action, Dialogue, Environment, Background, Mind). A three-tier, evidence-linked evaluation suite tests factual extraction, subjective attribution, and principle-level reasoning. Results show current memory systems (retrieval-augmented, entity-memory, stream-memory) excel at factual tasks but fail at insight, demonstrating that existing mechanisms cannot perform higher-order inference over a user's lived experience.

## Method Summary
The benchmark constructs a dataset from literary autobiographies using a four-stage, multi-agent pipeline with verify-and-revise loops. Module A segments raw text, Module B decomposes segments into Atomic Narrative Units (ANUs) with five fields, Module C performs mnestic realignment via a stack-based state machine to temporally anchor recalled content separately from its trigger, and Module D instantiates the aligned timeline in first-person. Evaluation generates query-answer-evidence triples for seven tasks (T1–T7) spanning factual recall to psychoanalytic insight. Models must provide answers cited to specific, timestamped ANUs, with scoring by an LLM-as-a-Judge (GPT-4o) against a gold timeline.

## Key Results
*   Memory systems (Mem0, MemOS) significantly outperform naive retrieval on factual (T1–T3) and entity consistency (T2) tasks, with gains of 8–12%.
*   Entity-memory systems (e.g., Mem0) suffer performance regression on temporal reasoning (T3) with flashback-intensive narratives due to the "Update Paradox."
*   All architectures, including stream-memory (MemOS), show a persistent "insight gap," scoring ≤22.3% on tasks requiring principle-level reasoning (T6: Mind-Body, T7: Psychoanalysis).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Dense Narrative Substrate.** High-density autobiographical narratives preserve the joint expression of external events and internal interpretation (inner monologue). This "micro-texture" maintains causally coupled evidence linking observable actions to underlying motivations, unlike sparse dialogue or synthetic histories. The decomposition into five cognitive primitives ensures this coupling is explicitly represented, enabling inference about stable decision principles.

**Mechanism 2: Mnestic Realignment.** The stack-based state machine separates *Mnemonic Triggers* (present-time anchors) from *Event Content* (historical origin). This prevents flashbacks from being misparsed as current state updates ("Update Paradox"), maintaining chronological integrity and enabling correct long-horizon attribution. The temporal anchoring is critical for tasks that depend on the true sequence of psychological states.

**Mechanism 3: Evidence-Linked Evaluation.** Requiring models to cite minimal, timestamped evidence for Tier 2–3 answers forces auditability. This tests whether the model's person-model is grounded in specific lived experiences rather than unconstrained speculation. The LLM-as-a-Judge rubric focuses on mapping external events to internal states, directly measuring understanding, not just retrieval.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** A -> B -> C -> D. A: Segmentation (extractive, index-based). B: ANU Extraction (generative decomposition; hallucination/omission audit via δ). C: Timeline/Mnestic Realignment (stack-based state machine; logic audit). D: Narrative Instantiation (first-person subjectivization; distortion audit). Evaluation suite generates queries from final timeline.

**Critical path:** Module C's mnestic realignment is the linchpin. Incorrect temporal anchoring of recalled content invalidates all downstream temporal and insight tasks. The Validator Agent's verify-and-revise loop is the primary safeguard.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   Verbatim Preservation (A) vs. Granularity (B): Strict slicing preserves fidelity but may create segments too coarse for Module B's granularity, causing rejections.
*   Stack Complexity (C) vs. Generalizability: The rule-based stack handles nested flashbacks but may fail on ambiguous narrative cues.
*   Evidence Stringency (Eval) vs. Subjectivity: Requiring minimal, specific evidence ensures auditability but conflicts with literary analysis subjectivity, addressed via validated LLM-judge.

**Failure signatures:**
*   High δ-score rejections in Module B/C: Indicates hallucination/omission or implausible temporal assignments.
*   Systematic T3/T4/T5 drop on Dataset 1 (Flashback-Intensive): Signals memory architecture cannot handle non-linear time (Update Paradox).
*   Low T6/T7 scores (≤22.3%) even with best system: Confirms the fundamental insight gap; failure is in higher-order inference, not retrieval.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Baseline Retrieval Test:** Naive RAG (k=50). Expect strong T1 gains, minimal/negative T6/T7 change. Validates retrieval ≠ understanding.
2.  **Entity-Memory Introduced:** Add Mem0. Expect T2 boost on Dataset 2 (Event-Driven) but T3 regression on Dataset 1 (Flashbacks). Demonstrates Update Paradox trade-off.
3.  **Stream-Memory Introduced:** Replace with MemOS. Expect major gains in T3–T5 (esp. Dataset 1) and moderate T6/T7 gains. Shows chronological logging is necessary but insufficient for insight.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can memory architectures overcome the "Update Paradox," where systems misinterpret mnestic triggers (flashbacks) as updates to the current user state?
*Basis:* Paper identifies this as a structural deficiency in entity-memory (e.g., Mem0) but doesn't provide a mechanistic solution for distinguishing a trigger from a state change.

**Open Question 2:** What architectural modifications or training paradigms are necessary to support Level III "Insight" tasks (T6–T7), where all systems perform poorly (≤22.3%)?
*Basis:* Paper diagnoses that retrieval proxies ≠ understanding but does not propose directions for building deep reasoning capability.

**Open Question 3:** To what extent do findings generalize beyond Western, literary autobiographical narratives to diverse cultural contexts and multimodal life logs?
*Basis:* Pipeline validated on three authors; limitations note subjectivity but not cultural/modal diversity. Generalizability is an untested assumption.

**Open Question 4:** How can the costly "Faithfulness-First" multi-agent pipeline be scaled for real-world companion deployment without sacrificing evidence-grounded inference?
*Basis:* Methodology is complex with human validation; gap between curated benchmark construction and noisy, continuous ingestion is a major translational hurdle.

**Open Question 5:** Does performance on KnowMe-Bench's tasks correlate with downstream utility in a human-in-the-loop companion (e.g., user satisfaction), or is it an academic proxy?
*Basis:* Evaluation is model-centric; causal chain from benchmark score to real-world companion quality is untested.

## Limitations
*   Literary sources limited to three (Western) authors; may not capture diverse narrative patterns, affecting generalizability.
*   Reliance on GPT-4o as judge introduces potential bias; human verification limited to agreement scores.
*   Memory architectures evaluated are restricted to Naive RAG, Mem0, MemOS; newer designs (e.g., transformer-based memory) not included.
*   Data construction pipeline depends on unpublished prompts/thresholds, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
*   **Dense narrative substrate improves inference:** Medium (theoretical support, no direct ablation).
*   **Mnestic realignment restores temporal integrity:** Medium-High (empirical gains on T3–T5; rule-based stack may misclassify).
*   **Evidence-linked evaluation measures true understanding:** High (clear differentiation from retrieval; strong human-LLM agreement κ > 0.75).
*   **Insight gap persists across architectures:** Medium (holds for three systems; newer architectures could differ).

## Next Checks
1.  **Density ablation:** Remove inner monologue/background fields from ANUs and measure T6/T7 score drops to confirm substrate necessity.
2.  **Stack-machine audit:** Manually annotate flashback boundaries in a subset; compute classification accuracy and analyze error modes.
3.  **Architecture expansion:** Integrate additional memory mechanisms (e.g., vector database with hybrid retrieval, attention-based memory) to test whether the insight gap is fundamental or surmountable.
