# PLESS: Pseudo-Label Enhancement with Spreading Scribbles for Weakly Supervised Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11628
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11628
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: PLESS refines noisy pseudo-labels from scribble supervision via hierarchical region-based propagation and background expansion, yielding consistent DSC improvements (up to +2.4%) and reduced Hausdorff distances, with a top result of 88.8% DSC on ACDC.

## Executive Summary
PLESS is a plug-and-play module designed to improve weakly supervised segmentation when training labels are sparse scribbles. It addresses the noise in pseudo-labels generated by baseline scribble-supervised methods by refining them through a hierarchical region-based propagation process. Key steps include partitioning the image into spatially coherent regions, applying waterfall-based label spreading within each region, and expanding background regions to mitigate leakage. Evaluated on two cardiac MRI datasets (ACDC and MSCMRseg) and integrated into four existing scribble-supervised baselines, PLESS consistently boosts performance, demonstrating its model-agnostic benefits for boundary accuracy and spatial consistency.

## Method Summary
Given initial pseudo-labels from a scribble-supervised segmentation model, PLESS first creates a hierarchical partitioning of the image (e.g., via watershed) to obtain superpixel-like regions that respect object boundaries. Within each region, a waterfall spreading algorithm iteratively propagates the most confident scribble label to uncertain pixels, ensuring that label diffusion stays confined to the region. After this propagation, background regions are expanded outward to counteract foreground leakage commonly observed with sparse supervision. The resulting refined pseudo-labels replace the original ones for continued training of the segmentation network, leading to higher-quality supervision.

## Key Results
- Top performance: 88.8% DSC with ScribbleVS+PLESS on ACDC.
- Mean DSC improvements: up to +1.0% on ACDC and +2.4% on MSCMRseg.
- Distance metric reductions: HD95 up to -3.2mm, ASD up to -1.3mm.
- Consistent gains across all four baseline methods, with better boundary accuracy and spatial consistency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Hierarchical partitioning ensures that label propagation occurs within semantically coherent regions, reducing the risk of crossing true object boundaries. Waterfall spreading leverages the region mask to iteratively assign labels to uncertain pixels based on the strongest neighboring signal while staying inside the region, thereby smoothing noisy boundaries without over-smoothing across edges. Background expansion explicitly enlarges background areas to fill potential holes and prevent foreground leakage, a common failure mode when only foreground scribbles are provided. Combining these steps produces pseudo-labels that more closely resemble full supervision, which in turn trains a more accurate segmentation model.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input scribbles → Initial segmentation network (baseline) → Initial pseudo-labels → Hierarchical partitioning → Waterfall spreading within regions → Background expansion → Refined pseudo-labels → Retrained segmentation network

**Critical path:** Hierarchical partitioning → Waterfall spreading → Background expansion. These modules are the core refinement engine and operate on the pseudo-labels without involving the network architecture.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Accuracy vs. computational cost: Finer partitions (e.g., many small regions) may better preserve boundaries but increase processing time for waterfall spreading.  
- Parameter sensitivity: Watershed markers or thresholds control region size; inappropriate choices can cause over- or under-segmentation, harming propagation.  
- Generality vs. specialization: Model-agnosticity simplifies integration but may not exploit architecture-specific inductive biases.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Overly coarse partitions: Waterfall spreads label across multiple objects, blurring boundaries.  
- Faulty background expansion: Excessive expansion shrinks foreground or introduces background into foreground areas.  
- Incorrect region hierarchy: If the partitioning algorithm fails to separate touching objects, labels may leak between them.

**3 first experiments:**  
1. **Baseline integration:** Implement PLESS as a post-processing step for DMPLS and evaluate on ACDC to verify the reported improvement.  
2. **Ablation study:** Disable waterfall spreading, background expansion, or hierarchical partitioning individually to quantify each component's contribution to overall performance.  
3. **Parameter sensitivity:** Vary the watershed threshold (or region size) and measure DSC/HD95 to understand robustness and identify optimal settings.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How effectively does PLESS generalize to diverse medical imaging datasets beyond cardiac MRI, such as brain or abdominal scans, and to non-medical domains?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper only evaluates on ACDC and MSCMRseg datasets; the conclusion suggests broader applicability to sparse annotations but provides no empirical evidence.
- Why unresolved: No experiments on other datasets; generalization capability remains untested and unverified.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive evaluation on multiple public datasets from different anatomical regions and modalities, with consistent protocols, to assess transferability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is PLESS robust to variations in hierarchical partitioning parameters (e.g., watershed thresholds, region scale) and image quality issues like low contrast or high noise?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The method uses fixed partitioning without sensitivity analysis; partitioning is core to propagation but its stability is unexplored.
- Why unresolved: Ablation studies exclude partitioning parameters; robustness to image variations is unknown and could limit real-world use.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying partitioning parameters on datasets with controlled noise or contrast degradation, measuring segmentation consistency.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the tolerance parameter τ be adaptively scheduled during training (e.g., based on pseudo-label confidence) to balance enhancement and noise, improving over fixed values?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Fixed τ values are used; ablation shows performance drops at τ=100%, indicating potential suboptimality and need for adaptivity.
- Why unresolved: No adaptive mechanism proposed; optimal τ may depend on training stage or dataset, but this is not investigated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement dynamic τ adjustment using validation metrics or model uncertainty, and compare against fixed τ across multiple baselines.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does PLESS perform with modern segmentation architectures (e.g., transformers, diffusion models) beyond the CNN-based baselines tested?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] PLESS is evaluated on four older scribble-supervised methods; the model-agnostic claim lacks validation with recent advances.
- Why unresolved: Rapid evolution of segmentation architectures; compatibility with state-of-the-art models is uncertain and unexamined.
- What evidence would resolve it: Integrate PLESS into current architectures like SegFormer or SAM, and benchmark on the same datasets to confirm universal applicability.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to two cardiac MRI datasets; generalizability to other modalities (CT, ultrasound) or natural images remains unverified.  
- Improvements are reported without statistical significance tests, leaving uncertainty about robustness of gains.  
- Model-agnosticity is only demonstrated on four older CNN-based baselines, not on contemporary architectures (e.g., transformers, diffusion).  
- Computational overhead introduced by hierarchical partitioning and waterfall spreading is not measured, which could affect practical adoption.
