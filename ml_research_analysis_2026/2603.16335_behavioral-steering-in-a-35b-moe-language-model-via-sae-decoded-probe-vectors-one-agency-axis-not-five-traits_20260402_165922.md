# Behavioral Steering in a 35B MoE Language Model via SAE-Decoded Probe Vectors: One Agency Axis, Not Five Traits

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.16335
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16335
- **Reference count**: 4
- **Primary result**: Steering five agentic traits via SAE-decoded probe vectors revealed a single dominant agency axis (independent action vs. deference) rather than five independent behavioral axes.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates steering agentic behaviors in a 35B parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language model (Qwen 3.5-35B-A3B) by constructing continuous steering vectors from linear probes trained on sparse autoencoder (SAE) latents. The method successfully modulated autonomy, achieving a large effect size (Cohen’s d=1.01) by reducing unnecessary user queries. However, cross-trait analysis showed all five steering vectors primarily shifted a single agency axis—disposition to act independently versus defer—with trait specificity emerging only in tool-type preferences and dose-response shapes. A key dissociation: risk calibration and tool-use vectors, despite nearly identical predictive accuracy (probe R² ~0.79), were orthogonal; only tool-use produced behavioral change, while risk calibration suppressed the default deferential behavior. Critically, steering during decoding had no effect, indicating behavioral commitments are computed during the prefill phase in this GatedDeltaNet architecture.

## Method Summary
The authors trained sparse autoencoders (SAEs) on the residual stream activations of the Qwen 3.5-35B-A3B model across nine layers. For each of five agentic traits (autonomy, tool-use, persistence, risk calibration, deference), they generated contrastive prompt pairs (HIGH vs. LOW trait expression) and extracted activations at the corresponding SAE-hooked layer. These activations were encoded into SAE latent space, and linear ridge probes were trained to predict trait labels. Steering vectors were obtained by projecting each probe’s weight vector through the SAE decoder matrix (v_steer = W_dec^T * w_probe), translating the discriminative direction into the model’s native continuous activation space. During inference, α·v_steer was added to the residual stream at the probe’s layer, with application modes tested: all positions, prefill only, decode only. Behavioral effects were evaluated using 50 ReAct-style scenarios across four domains, measuring tool-call patterns (e.g., ask_user, code_execute, web_search) and computing Cohen’s d for key metrics.

## Key Results
- Autonomy steering at multiplier α=2 achieved Cohen’s d=1.01 (p<0.0001), reducing ask_user calls from 78% to near-zero.
- Cross-trait analysis showed all five vectors primarily modulated a single agency axis (independent action vs. deference), with trait specificity only in tool-type composition and dose-response shape; a dissociation: risk calibration and tool-use vectors (nearly orthogonal, similar probe R²) differed in steering efficacy—only tool-use produced behavior change.
- Steering only during decoding had zero effect (p>0.35), indicating behavioral commitments are computed during prefill in GatedDeltaNet architectures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
The SAE-decoded probe method yields causally effective steering vectors by projecting a linearly discriminative direction from SAE latent space into the model’s native activation space, bypassing the SAE’s discrete top-k activation. A linear probe trained on SAE latents identifies a direction `w_probe` that separates HIGH/LOW trait activations. Projecting this through the SAE decoder (`v_steer = W_dec^T * w_probe`) produces a continuous vector in the 2048-d residual stream. Adding `α·v_steer` shifts activations on a fine-grained scale, assuming the probe direction is causally upstream of the behavior. Evidence includes the large effect for autonomy and the methodology’s explicit derivation in the paper. The approach fails if the probe direction is epiphenomenal (e.g., risk calibration vector).

### Mechanism 2
All five agentic traits are modulated through a single dominant “agency axis” (disposition to act independently vs. defer), with trait specificity emerging only in secondary properties like tool-type composition and dose-response shape. The model’s behavioral computation converges onto a shared upstream decision point: whether to act autonomously or ask the user. Steering vectors for different traits primarily shift this binary disposition; finer-grained modulations (e.g., preferring code vs. web search) are layered on top. Evidence: cross-trait specificity matrix shows every vector increases autonomy and decreases deference, with no trait-specific ratio >1.0; tool-type breakdown shows qualitative differences despite shared primary shift. The risk/tool-use dissociation (orthogonal vectors both modulating agency) supports this.

### Mechanism 3
Behavioral commitments (the act/defer decision) are computed during the prefill phase and propagated through the GatedDeltaNet’s recurrent state; steering during autoregressive decoding has no effect. In the hybrid GatedDeltaNet/attention architecture, recurrent DeltaNet layers during prompt processing establish a state that deterministically constrains subsequent token generation. The critical decision is “compiled” into this state, making decode-time perturbations transient. Evidence: Table 5 shows decode-only steering for autonomy yields null effects (d ~ 0, p>0.35), while prefill-only and all-positions are highly effective. This architecture-specific finding implies monitoring prefill-phase activations is key for detecting behavioral shifts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**: SAEs decompose dense residual stream activations into a larger, sparse dictionary of interpretable features via a top-k nonlinearity. They provide an intermediate space where behavioral correlations are more linearly separable, enabling probe training. *Why needed*: The SAE latent space allows training linear probes to identify trait-related directions that can be translated back to native activations. *Quick check*: What is the purpose of the SAE’s top-k activation function, and why does the paper’s method specifically bypass it?
- **Linear Probe ≠ Causal Intervention**: A high R² probe indicates a direction correlates with trait labels in activation space but does not guarantee that shifting this direction will causally influence behavior. *Why needed*: The paper demonstrates that probe accuracy (e.g., risk calibration’s R²=0.795) does not ensure steering efficacy; the probe may pick up epiphenomenal patterns. *Quick check*: Explain the risk-calibration/tool-use dissociation. Why does nearly identical probe R² lead to opposite steering outcomes?
- **GatedDeltaNet Recurrence**: GatedDeltaNet layers combine gating mechanisms with DeltaNet-style recurrence, allowing the model to maintain a state across tokens during prefill. *Why needed*: This recurrent computation is central to the finding that behavioral commitments are fixed during prefill; decode-time interventions are ineffective because the decision is already embedded in the state. *Quick check*: Why would a model with recurrent state computation be more likely to have decisions committed during prefill compared to a standard decoder-only transformer?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Qwen 3.5-35B-A3B model (40 layers, hybrid GatedDeltaNet/attention, MoE, residual dim=2048) -> 9 trained SAEs (layer-specific, top-k) -> Ridge regression probes per trait per SAE -> Steering vectors `v_steer = W_dec^T * w_probe` -> Application at probe’s layer during inference (all/prefill/decode positions) -> Agent evaluation harness (50 ReAct scenarios, tool schemas) -> Proxied metrics (ask_user reduction, proactive tool increase, Cohen’s d).

### Critical Path
1. Train SAEs on held-out token stream (200M tokens) with top-k activation, dead-feature resampling.
2. Generate contrastive HIGH/LOW trait prompt pairs (800 composite + 720 sub-behavior pairs across 4 domains).
3. Extract residual activations at SAE hook points, encode through SAE to get latents.
4. Train ridge probe (L2-regularized) on SAE latents, select alpha by held-out R².
5. Project probe weights through SAE decoder to obtain `v_steer`.
6. During inference, add `α·v_steer` to residual at probe’s layer; test application modes.
7. Run agent rollouts (50 scenarios × conditions) with tool calls.
8. Compute proxy metrics and Cohen’s d.

### Design Tradeoffs
- SAE dictionary size vs. feature interpretability: larger D yields more features but potentially less coherent.
- Layer selection: probing must occur where trait information is linearly expressed (identified via Trait Association Score, TAS).
- Application mode: `all_positions` maximizes effect but may harm coherence; `prefill_only` safer for coherence but sometimes weaker; `decode_only` effective only for certain traits? Actually decode-only ineffective for autonomy; prefill-only sometimes better for deference.

### Failure Signatures
- Pure suppression: `ask_user` decreases but proactive tool calls do not increase (e.g., risk calibration vector), indicating suppression of defer behavior without activating alternative policy.
- Phase transition collapse: At certain multipliers (e.g., tool-use α=3), zero-tool-call fraction plummets before recovering, indicating unstable attractor.
- Decode-only null: No significant effect on any metric for any trait, confirming prefill-commitment hypothesis for this architecture.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Replicate decode-only null for autonomy**: Apply autonomy `v_steer` (α=2) in decode_only, prefill_only, all_positions modes. Expect decode_only d(pro) ≈ 0, others d(pro) > 0.6. Validates prefill commitment claim.
2. **Test cross-trait leakage**: Apply autonomy `v_steer` (layer 22, α=2) and measure all five proxy metrics. Expect large positive d on autonomy and tool-use proxies, large negative on deference. Confirms dominant agency axis.
3. **Feature attribution sanity check**: For a successful steering vector (e.g., autonomy), decompose `v_steer` via `W_dec` to get top contributing SAE features. Manually inspect activation-maximizing examples for top 5 features. Expect semantically coherent descriptions related to autonomy (e.g., self-referential planning, tool invocation syntax).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Is the "dominant agency axis" an architectural constraint or an artifact of proxy metrics?** The primary metrics (tool-call patterns) all correlate via the act/defer decision. Richer, multi-dimensional evaluations (e.g., LLM-judged sub-behaviors) are needed to determine if trait-specific axes exist.
- **Does SAE-decoded probing provide superior steering directions compared to simpler methods?** The paper did not compare against mean-difference or contrastive activation addition (CAA) baselines. Without such a comparison, the advantage of using SAEs might be limited to interpretability rather than directional improvement.
- **What is the mechanistic reason for the causal irrelevance of the high-R² risk-calibration probe direction?** The dissociation suggests the probe captured an epiphenomenal representation correlated with risk labels but not causally involved. Possible reasons include probing a downstream consequence or confounded contrastive prompts. Causal mediation analysis on specific SAE features could clarify.
- **Is the "prefill-only behavioral commitment" finding generalizable?** The result is based on a single hybrid MoE model (GatedDeltaNet). Standard transformers lack strong recurrence; their decision-making might be distributed differently. Testing on diverse architectures (Llama, Mamba, etc.) is required.

## Limitations
- Exact contrastive prompt templates and 50 evaluation scenarios are not released, forcing approximations for replication.
- Behavioral metrics limited to tool-call patterns; richer dimensions (e.g., linguistic style, refusal patterns) were not assessed.
- Findings may be specific to the Qwen 3.5-35B-A3B hybrid architecture; generalizability to other models remains untested.

## Confidence
- **SAE-decoded steering vectors are causally effective**: Medium. Strong effects observed for autonomy and tool-use, but risk calibration dissociation shows probe direction can be epiphenomenal.
- **Five traits collapse onto a single agency axis**: Medium. Consistent cross-trait modulation of autonomy/deference across vectors, but limited to tool-call proxies; other behavioral facets untested.
- **Behavioral commitments are fixed during prefill in GatedDeltaNet**: High for this architecture (robust decode-only null), but Low for broader transformer families without recurrence.
