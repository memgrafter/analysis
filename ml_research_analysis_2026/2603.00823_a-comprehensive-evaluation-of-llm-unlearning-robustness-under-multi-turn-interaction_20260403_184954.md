# A Comprehensive Evaluation of LLM Unlearning Robustness under Multi-Turn Interaction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.00823  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00823  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Interactive evaluations reveal that static unlearning tests overestimate effectiveness: forgotten knowledge is often recovered via self-correction or dialogue context, while increasing unlearning strength causes rigidity; RMU offers better robustness at a utility cost.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles a critical gap in LLM unlearning research: most prior evaluations rely on static, single-turn assessments that do not reflect real-world interactive use. The authors systematically examine unlearning robustness under two multi-turn interaction patterns—self-correction and dialogue-conditioned querying—across three unlearning methods (Gradient Ascent, Negative Preference Optimization, Representation Misdirection) on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct and Zephyr-7B-Beta using the WMDP benchmark. Utility is measured via MMLU.

Key findings show that knowledge appearing erased in static tests frequently resurfaces during interaction. Self-correction prompts reactivate suppressed answers, and dialogue histories—even with only structural similarity—boost accuracy on forgotten targets. While stronger unlearning (higher learning rates) reduces this recoverability, it induces behavioral rigidity and severe utility loss rather than genuine erasure. Representation Misdirection demonstrates greater interaction robustness but suffers catastrophic utility degradation when forget-set content appears in context. The study concludes that static evaluation substantially overestimates real-world unlearning effectiveness and reveals a fundamental trade-off between robustness and model adaptability.

## Method Summary
The experimental design involves two instruction-tuned models (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, Zephyr-7B-Beta) and three unlearning algorithms. Gradient Ascent (GA) and Negative Preference Optimization (NPO) optimize model parameters to increase loss on the forget set (WMDP-Bio/Cyber multiple-choice questions), optionally with retain-set regularization via gradient descent (GD) or KL divergence. Representation Misdirection for Unlearning (RMU) uses provided checkpoints that perturb hidden activations toward random vectors in the forget domain. Wikidata serves as the retain set, and MMLU as the utility benchmark. Hyperparameters (learning rates, regularization coefficients) are explored over a grid.

Evaluation comprises three protocols: (1) Static single-turn QA on WMDP; (2) Self-correction across three strategies (S1–S3), where the model first answers, receives feedback, and is prompted to revise; answer change rates are tracked; (3) Dialogue-conditioned querying, where a rule-based simulator constructs a 5-turn history using either target or non-target content in MCQ or QA format before the final WMDP query. Primary metrics are WMDP accuracy, ∆Ans (answer change rate), and MMLU utility.

## Key Results
- Knowledge forgotten in static evaluation frequently recovers in interactive settings: self-correction increases accuracy in subsequent rounds, and dialogue histories with structural or semantic similarity boost performance on forgotten targets.
- Stronger unlearning (higher learning rates) reduces recoverability but causes behavioral rigidity and severe utility loss, rather than true erasure.
- Representation Misdirection (RMU) is more robust to interaction but suffers catastrophic utility degradation when forget-set content appears in the dialogue context.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Output-level suppression
- **Claim**: Output-level unlearning methods (GA, NPO) only suppress target responses at the output layer, leaving underlying knowledge representations intact; interactive reasoning (self-correction) can bypass this suppression and recover the knowledge.
- **Mechanism**: During self-correction, the model generates a reasoning chain that accesses internal concept representations. Because unlearning loss primarily distorts the final output mapping rather than the representations themselves, the intermediate reasoning may still contain the correct knowledge, which then surfaces in the revised answer.
- **Core assumption**: Gradient-based output optimization does not significantly alter the model's internal activations for the forget set concepts; it only perturbs the read-out at the output layer.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract statement on recovery; Section 4.2 showing self-correction reactivates suppressed answers; Table 1 showing accuracy increases in Round 2 under S1–S3.
- **Break condition**: If unlearning also perturbs internal representations (e.g., Representation Misdirection), this mechanism may not apply.

### Mechanism 2: Structural priming via dialogue history
- **Claim**: Dialogue histories, even when semantically unrelated to the forget set, can recover forgotten knowledge through structural priming (e.g., multiple-choice format), indicating that unlearning fails to account for context-dependent behavior.
- **Mechanism**: The model adapts its response pattern to match the structure of prior interactions. When dialogue histories use a multiple-choice format, it activates the model's MCQ answering mode, which may inadvertently access suppressed knowledge due to overlapping procedural reasoning.
- **Core assumption**: Model outputs are conditioned on both semantic content and interaction structure; static unlearning evaluation ignores this contextual sensitivity.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract statement on dialogue histories; Section 5.2 showing structural-only context causes recovery; Table 2 showing accuracy increases in that condition.
- **Break condition**: If unlearning rendered the model completely context-insensitive (likely harming general utility), this structural priming might not occur.

### Mechanism 3: Rigidity from excessive parameter updates
- **Claim**: Increasing unlearning strength (higher learning rates) does not achieve deeper knowledge erasure but instead induces behavioral rigidity, reducing model adaptability and utility.
- **Mechanism**: Excessive parameter updates distort the model's general function approximation, making it less responsive to instructions and contextual cues. This manifests as lower answer change rates and poorer performance on held-out tasks, creating an illusion of robustness.
- **Core assumption**: Direct parameter optimization methods (GA, NPO) lack perfect selectivity and degrade the model's overall capabilities when pushed too hard.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract statement on rigidity; Sections 4.3 & 5.3 showing increasing learning rate lowers R2 accuracy and answer change rates while also reducing MMLU utility.
- **Break condition**: If unlearning could be perfectly localized (e.g., via fine-grained representation control), this rigidity might be avoided.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (plain text):  
Models (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, Zephyr-7B-Beta) -> Unlearning Methods (GA, NPO, RMU) -> Datasets (WMDP-Bio/Cyber forget, Wikidata retain, MMLU utility) -> Evaluation Protocols (Static single-turn, Self-correction, Dialogue-conditioned) -> Metrics (Accuracy, ∆Ans, MMLU score)

**Critical path**:
1. Choose model and unlearning method.
2. Apply unlearning: For GA/NPO, optimize on forget set with selected learning rate and optional retain regularization (GD or KL). For RMU, load provided checkpoint.
3. Perform hyperparameter search (learning rates, coefficients) to calibrate strength.
4. Evaluate under three protocols: static single-turn, self-correction (two rounds with S1–S3 variations), dialogue-conditioned (build 5-turn history using rule-based simulator with four context types).
5. Compare results: compute recovery (Base vs. interactive accuracy), answer change rates, utility (MMLU).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- WMDP benchmark focuses on biosecurity/cyber domains, limiting domain generalizability.
- Multiple-choice format enables automatic scoring but may not reflect open-ended risks.
- Fixed dialogue length (5 turns) captures short-term context but not longer conversations.
- Rule-based user simulator provides controllability but lacks human-like variability.
- Grid search over hyperparameters is computationally intensive but necessary to map the strength-robustness trade-off.

**Failure signatures**:
- Unlearned model still high accuracy on WMDP in static eval → unlearning failed.
- Self-correction ∆Ans near 0% for all models → over-rigidity; model not adapting.
- Dialogue-conditioned accuracy significantly below Base → possibly over-unlearning or context interference.
- RMU utility drops >50% when forget-set content appears in dialogue → catastrophic interference.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Reproduce static GA unlearning on LLaMA: Apply GA (lr=3e-6) on WMDP-Bio with KL regularization, evaluate single-turn. Expect ~25% accuracy.
2. Run self-correction (S1) on same model: Verify Round 2 accuracy increases → evidence of recovery.
3. Run dialogue-conditioned with structural-only context (MMLU MCQ): Build 5-turn MMLU Sociology MCQ history, then query WMDP-Bio. Expect accuracy > Base → structural recovery.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: How can unlearning methods be designed to achieve robust forgetting under multi-turn interactions without inducing behavioral rigidity or significant utility loss?  
*Basis in paper*: The paper identifies a fundamental trade-off: stronger unlearning reduces recoverability in interactive settings but leads to diminished responsiveness and utility.  
*Why unresolved*: Current methods either fail to suppress knowledge recovery during interaction or degrade general capabilities; no existing approach balances both.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Development and evaluation of novel unlearning techniques that decouple robustness from rigidity, perhaps via advanced representation steering or hybrid objectives, validated using the paper's interactive robustness protocols.

**Open Question 2**: To what extent are the findings on unlearning robustness sensitive to the specific prompting templates and interaction designs used in the study?  
*Basis in paper*: Limitations acknowledge: “our findings may depend on the specific prompting templates used in our evaluation.”  
*Why unresolved*: Prompt engineering can significantly influence model behavior; variations in feedback phrasing, dialogue history construction, or system instructions might amplify or mitigate recovery effects.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Conducting an ablation study with diverse prompt templates for self-correction and dialogue contexts, quantifying changes in forgetting robustness metrics to establish robustness bounds.

**Open Question 3**: Do the reported limitations of unlearning robustness generalize to larger language models and architectures beyond LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct and Zephyr-7B-Beta?  
*Basis in paper*: Limitations state: “Our study is limited to two mid-sized instruction-tuned models... Models with different scales or training paradigms may exhibit different unlearning and self-correction behaviors.”  
*Why unresolved*: The experimental scope is narrow; scaling effects, model family differences, or training variations could alter interaction-driven recovery dynamics.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Replicating the study's interactive evaluation (self-correction and dialogue-conditioned querying) on models such as GPT-4, Claude, or Llama 2/3 at larger scales (e.g., 70B+ parameters).

**Open Question 4**: How does unlearning robustness in interactive settings vary when moving beyond multiple-choice questions to open-ended text generation tasks?  
*Basis in paper*: Limitations note: “our evaluation is restricted to multiple-choice question (MCQ) settings.”  
*Why unresolved*: All experiments used MCQ format; real-world human-AI interactions often involve free-form dialogue, which may differently trigger knowledge recovery due to less constrained output spaces.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Adapting the interactive evaluation framework to open-ended QA or conversational tasks, measuring recoverability and utility via semantic similarity metrics or human evaluation.

## Limitations
- Evidence scope limited to two mid-sized instruction-tuned models and WMDP domains; generalization to other architectures/scales unknown.
- Exact unlearning hyperparameters not explicitly reported; must be inferred from figures, hindering reproducibility.
- Rule-based dialogue simulator oversimplifies human interaction variability; internal state verification lacking to confirm representation-level effects.

## Confidence
- **Output-level suppression (Mechanism 1)**: Medium Confidence
- **Structural priming (Mechanism 2)**: Low Confidence
- **Rigidity vs. erasure trade-off (Mechanism 3)**: High Confidence
