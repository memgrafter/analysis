# FreeArtGS: Articulated Gaussian Splatting Under Free-moving Scenario

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.22102
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22102
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Introduces a framework for monocular RGB-D articulated object reconstruction under free movement, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy in joint and geometry estimation on a new benchmark.

## Executive Summary
FreeArtGS tackles the challenge of reconstructing articulated objects from a single moving monocular RGB-D camera where both the object's global pose and its internal joint state vary arbitrarily. It is the first method to eliminate the common assumption of a static base part. The system uses three core modules: (1) motion-based part segmentation via point tracking and semantic features, (2) joint parameter estimation from calibrated part poses, and (3) an end-to-end 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization with blended part rendering to jointly refine all parameters. It establishes the FreeArt-21 benchmark and demonstrates significant quantitative improvements over prior art in joint axis/state errors, geometry (Chamfer distance), and rendering quality (PSNR) on both synthetic and real-world data.

## Method Summary
The method processes a monocular RGB-D video sequence. First, a sliding-window optimization uses dense 2D point tracks (lifted to 3D with depth) and DINO features to segment the object into two rigid parts by clustering points with consistent rigid transforms. Second, an off-the-shelf pose estimator (e.g., BundleSDF) provides per-frame part-to-camera transforms, which are calibrated to a shared canonical frame. The sequence of relative transforms between parts is then analyzed to classify the joint as revolute or prismatic and solve for its axis direction and pivot/position via closed-form least-squares. Third, canonical 3D Gaussians are initialized for each part. During end-to-end optimization, these Gaussians are deformed per-frame using the estimated joint parameters and blended using differentiable per-Gaussian weights. The blended rendering is supervised with RGB, depth, and mask losses, enabling joint refinement of geometry, appearance, camera poses, part weights, and articulation parameters.

## Key Results
*   On the FreeArt-21 benchmark, FreeArtGS achieves a revolute joint axis error of 1.04°, position error of 0.29 cm, and state error of 1.43°, with Chamfer distances below 0.3 cm and PSNR of 24.02 dB.
*   For prismatic joints, it reports an axis error of 1.85° and state error of 0.90 cm.
*   Real-world evaluation on 6 objects shows robust performance (axis error 2.73°, PSNR 22.40 dB) and superior part segmentation (mIoU 90.88%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Motion-driven part segmentation from free-moving video
**Claim:** Rigid object parts can be segmented purely from relative 3D motion cues, even when the entire object moves freely relative to the camera, by clustering points based on consistent rigid transforms over short temporal windows.  
**Mechanism:** For a sliding window of frames, dense 2D tracks from AllTracker are lifted to 3D using depth. Two rigid transforms (one per part) and soft per-point part membership weights are jointly optimized to minimize reprojection error between transformed 3D trajectories. Regularization via spatial smoothness (feature-space neighbor graph) and consistency with an initial DINOv3-based clustering prevents degenerate solutions.  
**Core assumption:** Within a short temporal window (8 frames), the motion of each physical part is well-approximated by a single, time-invariant rigid transform relative to the first frame.

### Mechanism 2: Joint parameter recovery from calibrated part trajectories
**Claim:** The type (revolute/prismatic) and axis (direction and pivot/position) of a two-part articulation can be robustly estimated from the sequence of relative SE(3) transforms between the two parts after calibrating each part to a common canonical frame.  
**Mechanism:** Off-the-shelf pose estimators initialize per-frame part-to-camera transforms. A rigid calibration aligns both parts to a shared canonical coordinate system. The relative transform sequence between parts is then analyzed: rotation amplitude and translation linearity decide joint type; for revolute, the common rotation axis is found via minimizing `||(R_ij - I)u||^2` and pivot is solved in the plane orthogonal to the axis; for prismatic, the translation axis is the principal component of centered translations.  
**Core assumption:** The object's motion is generated by a single, standard kinematic joint (revolute or prismatic) connecting two rigid parts. The relative transformation sequence is cleanly separable into a single axis (direction) and a scalar state (angle or displacement).

### Mechanism 3: End-to-end 3D Gaussian Splatting with blended part rendering
**Claim:** Jointly optimizing canonical 3D Gaussians for both parts, per-frame camera poses, part membership weights, and joint parameters under multi-modal supervision (RGB, depth, mask) significantly improves reconstruction fidelity and articulation accuracy over a coarse initial estimate.  
**Mechanism:** Canonical Gaussians for each part are deformed to per-frame poses using the estimated joint parameters. A blended rendering step linearly mixes the two deformed Gaussian sets using differentiable, per-Gaussian part weights (refined during optimization). The renderings are supervised with L1+SSIM for RGB, L1 for depth, and L1 for foreground masks, enabling gradients to flow to all parameters.  
**Core assumption:** The initial part segmentation, joint parameters, and camera poses are in a basin of attraction where gradient-based optimization converges to a globally more accurate solution. The blending weights effectively model the soft part membership transition region.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)**
    *   **Why needed here:** The entire reconstruction and rendering backbone is built on 3DGS. Understanding how anisotropic Gaussians represent scenes, the alpha-blending rendering equation, and the optimization of scale, rotation, and spherical harmonics is essential to grasp the end-to-end module.
    *   **Quick check:** How does 3DGS handle visibility and opacity during differentiable rendering, and why is it unsuitable for articulated scenes without modification?
*   **Concept: Rigid body motion in SE(3)**
    *   **Why needed here:** Part segmentation optimizes rigid transforms; joint estimation works with relative SE(3) transforms between parts; end-to-end optimization applies joint transforms to Gaussians. All core operations assume rigid kinematics.
    *   **Quick check:** What is the difference between a rotation matrix in SO(3) and a full transform in SE(3), and how is a revolute joint's transform `T(θ; u, o)` constructed from these elements?
*   **Concept: Dense 2D-3D point tracking**
    *   **Why needed here:** The entire part segmentation pipeline starts from pixel trajectories which are lifted to 3D using depth. The quality of these tracks directly impacts the rigid transform estimation.
    *   **Quick check:** If point tracks are noisy but dense, why might an optimization over many short windows with regularization be more robust than trying to solve a single large global alignment?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (Monocular RGB-D video) -> Part Segmentation (AllTracker, DINOv3, windowed rigid transform optimization) -> Part Masks -> Pose Estimation (BundleSDF) -> Calibration -> Joint Estimation (Type/axis solver) -> End-to-End Optimization (3DGS with blended rendering, joint refinement) -> Output (Canonical Gaussians, Joint Parameters)

**Critical path:** Part segmentation -> Part pose estimation & calibration -> Joint type/axis estimation -> End-to-end GS optimization. The output of each stage is essential initialization for the next; errors propagate forward.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   Modularity vs. joint optimization: Separating stages allows using best-of-breed models but creates a pipeline where early errors can cripple later stages.
*   Soft vs. hard part assignments: Blended rendering allows optimization to refine part boundaries but requires the `L_init` regularization from segmentation to stay anchored.
*   Computational cost: ~25 minutes per sequence, stemming from windowed segmentation optimization and the 30k-step GS training.

**Failure signatures:**
*   **Part segmentation failure:** Incorrect masks (e.g., splitting a single rigid part). Visualized as one part "leaking" into the other's region. Causes: poor point tracks on textureless/thin structures, failure of DINO features to separate parts.
*   **Joint estimation failure:** Axis error > 10-15°, nonsensical pivot/state. Causes: inconsistent part poses from the upstream pose estimator, incorrect joint type decision, outlier frames not filtered.
*   **Optimization failure:** Blurred rendering, broken geometry, diverging parameters. Causes: Initial joint/poses too far from optimum, `w_i` collapsing to 0/1, camera pose drift.

**First 3 experiments a new engineer should run:**
1.  **Part segmentation validation:** On a single FreeArt-21 sequence, visualize the optimized per-Gaussian part weights and the final binary masks. Compare against ground-truth part masks via mIoU. Ensure masks are spatially coherent and consistent with visible motion.
2.  **Joint axis sanity check:** Plot the relative translation vectors between parts in the calibrated frame. For a revolute joint, they should form a clear circle; for prismatic, a line. Check the estimated axis against this qualitative trend and compute the residual error of `(R_ij - I)u ≈ 0`.
3.  **Ablation of blended rendering:** Run the end-to-end stage with part weights frozen to hard assignments (0/1 from segmentation). Compare PSNR and geometry metrics (Chamfer Distance) to the full blended model. Expect a significant PSNR drop (~2 dB), validating the soft blending design.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How can FreeArtGS be extended to handle articulated objects with more than two rigid parts without requiring sequential capture?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "our method currently assumes a two-part articulated object; extending it to multi-part structures by sequentially capturing each moving part remains an important direction."
    *   **Why unresolved:** The two-part assumption restricts the method's applicability to many everyday objects that have multiple joints and moving components.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** A modified framework that can jointly segment and reconstruct multiple parts from a single monocular RGB-D video, validated on a new multi-part benchmark with ground truth annotations.
*   **Open Question 2:** Can a unified feed-forward architecture replace the current modular pipeline to simultaneously estimate articulation, geometry, and appearance, thus avoiding cascading errors from off-the-shelf priors?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "relying on multiple off-the-shelf priors can lead to cascading error accumulation. A potential solution lies in developing a unified feed-forward model to simultaneously predict joints, poses, geometry, and textures."
    *   **Why unresolved:** The use of separate components for point tracking, feature extraction, pose estimation, and Gaussian splatting introduces potential error propagation, especially when one component fails.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** A single deep network trained end-to-end that directly outputs the articulated Gaussian model from raw video, achieving equal or superior performance to the modular approach and demonstrating robustness to failures in individual priors.
*   **Open Question 3:** How can FreeArtGS be adapted to monocular RGB-only input without explicit depth, while maintaining accurate 3D reconstruction of articulated objects?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "our framework requires monocular RGB-D input. While extending it to RGB-only sequences by predicting continuous video depth is a natural progression, it currently faces challenges regarding depth accuracy."
    *   **Why unresolved:** Depth information is critical for 3D reconstruction; RGB-D cameras limit deployment scenarios. Accurate video depth prediction is necessary but challenging, especially for dynamic articulated objects.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Integration of a reliable video depth estimator, possibly trained jointly with the Gaussian splatting objective, showing comparable reconstruction quality and articulation accuracy on RGB-D data when RGB-only input is used.

## Limitations
*   The pipeline depends on external modules (AllTracker for point tracking, BundleSDF for pose estimation); their implementation details and availability are unclear, posing reproduction challenges.
*   Core assumptions restrict applicability: parts must be rigid and connected by a single revolute or prismatic joint; non-rigid deformations or multi-part objects likely fail.
*   End-to-end optimization requires a good initialization; severely inaccurate prior estimates may cause divergence.

## Confidence
*   **Motion-driven part segmentation:** Medium. Mechanism is described and supported by ablations, but hinges on high-quality dense tracking, which is not fully specified.
*   **Joint parameter recovery:** Medium. Logical derivation is sound and filtering is applied, but robustness to pose noise is not extensively characterized.
*   **End-to-end Gaussian Splatting optimization:** High. Quantitative ablations (e.g., blended rendering improves PSNR by ~2 dB) strongly validate its role.
*   **Overall performance on FreeArt-21 and real-world:** Medium. Reported metrics are excellent, but benchmark access is restricted and sample size is limited.

## Next Checks
1.  **Tracker substitution:** Replace AllTracker with a publicly available tracker (e.g., CoTracker3) in the segmentation module; compute mIoU against ground-truth part masks to assess robustness to tracking variations.
2.  **Pose-noise sensitivity:** Intentionally add Gaussian noise to BundleSDF-derived poses and measure the degradation in joint axis error and state error to quantify tolerance thresholds.
3.  **Blending weight ablation:** Freeze part weights to hard assignments (0/1 from segmentation) and compare PSNR, Chamfer distance, and axis error against the full blended model; verify the reported ~2 dB PSNR drop across joint types.
