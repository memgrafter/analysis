# The Yerkes-Dodson Curve for AI Agents: Emergent Cooperation Under Environmental Pressure in Multi-Agent LLM Simulations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07360  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07360  
- **Reference count:** 11  
- **Primary result:** Cooperative trade follows an inverted-U curve, peaking at 29 trades under medium resource pressure (upkeep=5) versus 8–12 trades at low or extreme pressure; extreme pressure collapses behavior to movement-only within 5–12 turns.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how environmental pressure influences emergent cooperative behavior in multi-agent systems powered by large language models (LLMs). Using a grid-world "Survival Arena" populated with Claude 3.5 Sonnet agents, the authors vary resource scarcity (via an upkeep cost parameter) and introduce reproductive competition (sexual selection). They find that cooperative trade interactions peak at an intermediate pressure level, forming an inverted-U curve reminiscent of the Yerkes-Dodson law from psychology. Under extreme scarcity, agent behavior collapses into simple survival loops. In contrast, a reproductive competition regime eliminates aggression entirely and evokes communicative courtship behaviors not seen under survival pressure. The work suggests LLMs contain latent cooperative strategies that can be coaxed out by incentives, but only within a narrow pressure band.

## Method Summary
The authors simulate 16 Claude 3.5 Sonnet agents in a 9×9 grid containing food and token resource nodes. Each agent has six attributes (STR, SPD, INT, SOC, END, CHA) summing to 30, which affect capabilities like movement range and gathering efficiency. Agents act each turn based on an LLM call with a prompt describing their state and nearby agents. The key experimental manipulation is the **upkeep cost** (u), a per-turn resource deduction that creates pressure: low upkeep (e.g., u=2) allows easy survival, while high upkeep (u≥7) forces rapid resource consumption. Primary experiments (Phase P2b) fix resource node counts and regeneration, varying u∈{2,4,5,6,7} to isolate pressure effects. A separate "sexual selection" condition (V7 engine) introduces asymmetric provider/chooser roles and reproduction mechanics at low upkeep, decoupling survival threat from reproductive competition. Metrics include trade count (primary), attacks, survivors, game duration, and action distribution.

## Key Results
- Cooperative trade count follows an inverted-U curve: peaks at 29 trades under medium pressure (upkeep=5) versus ~11–12 trades at low (u=2,4) or high (u≥6) pressure.
- Under extreme pressure (u≥7), behavioral repertoire collapses: games end rapidly (≤12 turns), >56% of actions are MOVE-only, and social actions (TRADE, ATTACK) disappear.
- Sexual selection (low upkeep with provider/chooser dynamics) eliminates all aggression and produces COMMUNICATE and REPRODUCE actions absent in survival-pressure runs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Intermediate environmental pressure maximizes emergent cooperative trade via the "edge of viability" effect.  
At upkeep=5, agents face resource scarcity that incentivizes seeking cooperative solutions (trade) but not so much that they die before repeated interactions can develop. Low pressure allows default "GATHER+MOVE" loops without need for trade; extreme pressure causes rapid death before cooperation emerges.  
**Core assumption:** The LLM's pretrained policy contains latent knowledge of cooperative strategies that can be activated when environmental incentives align.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Trade count peaks at 29 under u=5 vs. 8–12 at u=2,4 and u≥6 (abstract, section 5.1, Table 1).  
- Neighbor literature discusses competition but does not provide direct evidence for the inverted-U pattern; support is primarily from the paper's own experiments.  
**Break condition:** If population viability falls below the threshold where agents survive long enough for repeated dyadic interactions (e.g., upkeep≥7 where game ends in ≤20 turns).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Extreme environmental pressure triggers behavioral repertoire collapse to survival-only actions via rapid population turnover.  
Under high upkeep (≥7), agents exhaust resources quickly, die in succession, and surviving agents' context becomes dominated by resource-scarcity cues and movement—eliminating bandwidth for social reasoning. The observed "MOVE-only" strategy is an emergent adaptation to immediate survival.  
**Core assumption:** LLM agents have limited "cognitive bandwidth" per turn; when survival threat is maximal, complex social actions are pruned from the action distribution.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- At u≥7, games collapse rapidly; agents converge on MOVE-dominated strategies (56–68% of actions) and social actions disappear entirely (section 5.2, Figure 2).  
- At u=15, game lasts only 5 turns with 67.7% MOVE actions and zero trades (section 5.2).  
- Whole-game Shannon entropy increases because collapsing population yields a small set of actions from few survivors—not because complexity increases (section 5.4).  
**Break condition:** If agents retain social action options even when starving, or if alternative architecture (e.g., longer context) prevents collapse.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Sexual selection (reproductive competition without lethal survival pressure) eliminates aggression and evokes communicative/courtship behavior absent under survival pressure.  
When all agents survive (low upkeep u=2) but reproductive success is unequal, the payoff matrix shifts: aggression against potential mates or rivals becomes self-defeating. Costly signaling (COMMUNICATE) and mate assessment become adaptive, drawing on the LLM's knowledge of human mating strategies.  
**Core assumption:** LLMs encode domain-specific social logic (e.g., courtship, mate choice) that is suppressed under survival threat but activated under reproductive competition.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Sexual selection eliminates inter-agent aggression entirely and produces communicative behavior absent under survival pressure (abstract).  
- V7 (sexual selection) shows 0 attacks vs. 76 attacks in P2b (survival) at similar upkeep; also shows 8 COMMUNICATE and 17 REPRODUCE actions (section 5.3, Table 2).  
- Asymmetric costs (providers vs. choosers) and vitality-based signaling mimic Trivers' parental investment theory (section D).  
**Break condition:** If aggression persists under sexual selection, or if reproduction mechanics are not understood by the LLM.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Yerkes-Dodson Law (inverted-U arousal-performance curve)**  
*Why needed:* The paper's central analogy—environmental pressure ≈ arousal—requires understanding that both under- and over-stimulation degrade performance, with an optimal midpoint.  
*Quick check:* If trade cooperation peaks at medium pressure and declines at both low and high pressure, what does this suggest about the pressure level needed to "activate" latent cooperative strategies in LLMs?

**Concept: Behavioral repertoire collapse**  
*Why needed:* Distinguishes *quantity* of actions (Shannon entropy artifact) from *adaptive diversity* of actions. Collapse means agents lose access to complex strategies, not that they become "more random."  
*Quick check:* Why does Shannon entropy increase when the behavioral repertoire actually simplifies under extreme pressure?

**Concept: Sexual selection vs. natural selection in agent design**  
*Why needed:* The paper contrasts two pressure mechanisms: survival threat (natural selection) vs. reproductive competition (sexual selection). The latter maintains population while creating competitive incentives.  
*Quick check:* How does the "chooser pays higher cost" mechanic (12 food + 5 tokens) vs. "provider pays lower cost" (6 food + 3 tokens) mirror Trivers' parental investment theory?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Survival Arena Engine (v6.1/v7.0) → Agent Policy (Claude 3.5 Sonnet LLM per turn) → Attribute System (STR, SPD, INT, SOC, END, CHA) → Pressure Controller (upkeep u or provider/chooser costs) → Metrics Logger (trades, attacks, survivors, actions)

**Critical path:**  
Configuration → Engine initialization (grid, nodes, agents) → Game loop (per turn: prompt all agents → LLM calls → action execution → state update) → Termination (all-but-one dead or max turns) → Metrics aggregation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Single-model focus (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) vs. generalizability: results may not hold across models with different behavioral phenotypes.  
- Small scale & short horizon (16 agents, 60 turns) vs. ecological validity: long-term evolutionary dynamics not captured.  
- No inter-game memory vs. cumulative learning: each game independent; no inheritance of successful strategies.  
- Action space simplicity vs. expressive richness: limited actions (GATHER, MOVE, ATTACK, TRADE, REST, TRAIN, COMMUNICATE/REPRODUCE) may constrain emergent complexity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Configuration error leading to missing pressure levels (e.g., no upkeep=3 run).  
- High inter-run variance at a given pressure (paper only replicates u=2).  
- Entropy artifact: increasing Shannon entropy coinciding with decreasing game duration and population—indicates sampling bias.  
- Aggregation failure: whole-game metrics masking per-turn dynamics.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Validation run (Phase P1):** Confirm engine v6.1 produces meaningful decisions with seed=42; baseline trade/attack counts.  
2. **Broad sweep (Phase P2):** Run upkeep levels 0–10+ (confounded with node counts) to identify pressure range where trade/aggression change dramatically; spot "apocalypse" regimes (duration <20 turns).  
3. **Controlled sweep (Phase P2b):** Fix resources (nf=8, nt=5, rf=3, rt=2), vary upkeep u∈{2,4,5,6,7} with seed=42; replicate u=2 to estimate variance. Verify inverted-U pattern in trade count.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**  
Does the Yerkes-Dodson curve for cooperation generalize across different LLM model families?  
*Basis:* The study uses only Claude 3.5 Sonnet; related work shows models like GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 have distinct behavioral phenotypes.  
*Why unresolved:* Different pretraining data and architectures may yield different stress-performance curves or collapse thresholds.  
*Evidence:* Systematic pressure sweeps (u=2–7) across multiple model families (e.g., GPT-4o, DeepSeek-R1, Llama) with multiple seeds per model.

**Open Question 2**  
What is the exact trade cooperation count at upkeep=3, and does it smooth the inverted-U curve?  
*Basis:* The P2b dataset lacks u=3 due to a configuration error, creating a gap between low-pressure replicates (u=2) and u=4.  
*Why unresolved:* The peak at u=5 could be an artifact of missing intermediate data; the true optimum might lie between u=2 and u=5.  
*Evidence:* Replicate the controlled P2b protocol at u=3, ideally with 3–5 seeds for confidence intervals.

**Open Question 3**  
How does multi-generational inheritance (cross-game memory) alter the stress-performance relationship?  
*Basis:* Each experiment starts fresh; offspring in V7 inherit attributes but not learned strategies, preventing evolutionary dynamics.  
*Why unresolved:* The paper treats LLM policy as fixed "genetic memory"; true evolutionary pressure requires strategy propagation across generations.  
*Evidence:* Modify V7 to let offspring retain a summary of parental decision policies or prompts, then measure trade/cooperation curves across 10+ generations under varying u.

**Open Question 4**  
Which per-turn metric best captures behavioral complexity without population-size confounding?  
*Basis:* Whole-game Shannon entropy increased monotonically with pressure due to small-sample bias from early population collapse.  
*Why unresolved:* The paper identifies the flaw but does not implement or test alternatives (e.g., per-turn behavioral diversity, action-type turnover).  
*Evidence:* Compute per-turn entropy, Shannon diversity, or action-type richness for each turn, then aggregate (e.g., mean or AUC) across surviving agents; compare against trade peaks in P2b data.

## Limitations
- LLM inference parameters (temperature, top_p, etc.) unreported; default settings may alter action distributions.
- Agent attribute initialization details ambiguous; exact distributions and starting resources unknown.
- Observation/SOC mechanics vaguely defined; range of visible agents per turn not quantified.
- Single-model (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) and single-seed experiments limit generalizability and pattern stability.

## Confidence
- **Inverted-U trade curve (Mechanism 1): Medium** – supported by own data but no external replication; depends on exact setup.
- **Behavioral collapse under extreme pressure (Mechanism 2): Medium** – observed directly, but entropy artifact interpretation may be confounded by population dynamics.
- **Sexual selection eliminates aggression (Mechanism 3): Low-Medium** – stark result but highly sensitive to V7 mechanics and model-specific social knowledge.

## Next Checks
