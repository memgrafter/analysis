# iGRPO: Self-Feedback-Driven LLM Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2602.09000
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09000
- **Reference count:** 39
- **Primary result:** iGRPO achieves SOTA on AIME24 (85.62%) and AIME25 (79.64%) and consistently outperforms GRPO under matched sampling budgets across 7B–14B models; its refinement wrapper also boosts other group-based RL methods by ~1.2 points.

## Executive Summary
iGRPO introduces a two-stage reinforcement learning procedure for large language model reasoning. It first samples multiple drafts from the current policy, selects the highest-reward one using a verifier, appends that draft to the original prompt, and then samples refinements conditioned on this augmented prompt. A GRPO-style group-relative PPO update is applied to the refinements. Under equal rollout budgets, iGRPO consistently surpasses GRPO and other self-improvement baselines, yielding new state-of-the-art results on challenging mathematical benchmarks. The method's self-conditioning creates a dynamic feedback loop that shifts the training distribution toward higher-quality scaffolds as the policy improves, and it also delays entropy collapse during training.

## Method Summary
iGRPO operates per training prompt in two stages. Stage 1: draw N candidate completions (drafts) from the current policy, compute each draft's reward (binary correctness against a verifier, plus optional format reward), and select the highest-reward draft. Stage 2: construct an augmented prompt by concatenating the original prompt and the selected draft using a fixed template; then sample G completions (refinements) from the same policy conditioned on this augmented prompt. For each group of G refinements, compute advantages by normalizing their rewards relative to the group mean and standard deviation. Perform a PPO-clipped policy gradient update on the refinement tokens, with an optional KL penalty (set to zero in the paper). The total number of rollouts per prompt (N+G) is kept constant (e.g., 8), so iGRPO re-allocates samples between exploration and refinement rather than increasing compute. Training proceeds for one epoch over the dataset. At inference time, the model generates a single completion from the original prompt (no refinement), but its weights have been updated via the iGRPO loop.

## Key Results
- New SOTA on AIME24 (85.62%) and AIME25 (79.64%) using OpenReasoning-Nemotron-7B trained on AceReason-Math.
- Consistent improvements over GRPO and other baselines (e.g., SimpleRL, DAPO, GSPO) on MATH500, AMC23, GSM8K, Minerva under matched sampling budgets, with gains more pronounced on challenging problems.
- The refinement wrapper applied to other group-based RL methods (DAPO, GSPO) yields average gains of ~1.2 points, demonstrating broader applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
* **Mechanism 1: Dynamic self-conditioning**  
  iGRPO samples multiple drafts, selects the highest-reward one, and appends it to the prompt for refinement. This creates a feedback loop that shifts the training distribution toward higher-quality scaffolds. *Break condition:* If the verifier is poor, the selected draft may degrade learning.

* **Mechanism 2: Monotonic improvement of the conditioning signal**  
  For binary rewards and i.i.d. drafts, the probability that at least one of N drafts is correct is 1-(1-p)^N, which increases with policy quality p. Thus, as the policy improves, the expected quality of the best draft improves automatically. *Break condition:* Non-binary or noisy rewards can break monotonicity.

* **Mechanism 3: Delayed entropy collapse**  
  Conditioned generation appears to sustain exploration longer; entropy plots show iGRPO maintains higher mid-training entropy than GRPO, delaying mode collapse. *Break condition:* If draft selection becomes deterministic early, the effect vanishes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** The reward function is an automatic verifier (e.g., rule-based correctness check) rather than a learned model. This avoids reward hacking but requires ground-truth answers. *Why needed:* iGRPO operates entirely within this paradigm; the quality of the verifier directly controls the selection of the best draft. *Quick check:* What if the verifier gives partial credit for near-correct answers? Could that improve refinement?
- **Group Relative Advantage Normalization:** GRPO normalizes rewards within each sampled group to compute advantages, removing the need for a separate value function and stabilizing learning by making advantages relative. *Why needed:* iGRPO inherits this for its Stage 2 update. *Quick check:* If all G completions in a group receive identical rewards, what happens to advantages and the gradient?
- **Self-Conditioning / In-Context Learning as a Training Signal:** iGRPO uses the model's own output as a dynamic context that changes as the policy improves, blending inference-time in-context learning with training. *Why needed:* This is the core innovation that shifts the data distribution toward higher-quality scaffolds. *Quick check:* How does dynamic conditioning differ from using fixed human-written solution examples, and why might it enable compounding gains?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Prompt Processor -> Draft Sampler (Stage1) -> Draft Selector -> Refinement Sampler (Stage2) -> Advantage Calculator -> Policy Updater

**Critical path:** For each batch of prompts: (a) for each prompt, run Stage1 to get best draft; (b) build augmented prompt; (c) run Stage2 to sample refinements; (d) compute group-relative advantages; (e) PPO-clipped update using only Stage2 tokens; refresh old policy after update. Stage1 is non-differentiable and mainly affects the data distribution.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- N vs G: increasing N improves chance of finding a high-quality draft but reduces G (since total rollouts fixed), potentially noisier advantage estimates. Typically N=G.
- Reward function: binary verifier vs. generative judge; judge adds partial credit but may be noisy.
- KL coefficient β: set to 0; ablations show marginal effect.

**Failure signatures:**
- Degenerate draft selection due to small N or weak policy; monitor selected-draft reward.
- Mode collapse into copying; check for verbatim repetition.
- Misaligned conditioning if best draft has hidden errors; depends on verifier robustness.
- Advantage normalization errors: ensure per-group mean 0, std 1.
- Memory explosion: free Stage1 tensors before Stage2.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Ablation: Compare iGRPO with GRPO under equal total rollouts to isolate two-stage conditioning.
2. Ablation: Vary N while fixing total budget to study exploration vs. refinement trade-off.
3. Ablation: Compare binary verifier vs. generative judge reward.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to other algorithms and tasks:** The refinement wrapper was tested only on math benchmarks with DAPO/GSPO. Does it extend to all group-based RL methods and to non-mathematical reasoning?
2. **Causal mechanism of entropy delay:** The paper observes higher mid-training entropy but does not explain why iGRPO sustains diversity longer.
3. **Robustness to reward quality:** How does iGRPO perform under sparse, continuous, or noisy rewards? The theory assumes accurate binary rewards.
4. **Optimal N:G allocation:** The paper uses fixed equal splits. What is the best trade-off across model strengths and problem difficulties?
5. **Separating conditioning from advantage estimation:** Is the gain due to self-conditioning itself or to the specific GRPO group-relative advantage? Not isolated experimentally.

## Limitations
- **Underspecification:** Prompt template, format reward details, answer extractors, and old policy update frequency are not fully described, hindering exact replication.
- **Assumption mismatch:** The monotonicity guarantee hinges on binary, i.i.d. rewards; real training includes format rewards and may violate i.i.d., weakening the theoretical foundation.
- **Evaluation scope:** Experiments are confined to 7B–14B models on a handful of math benchmarks; broader applicability remains unverified.

## Confidence
- **Empirical superiority of iGRPO over GRPO:** High
- **Self-feedback loop as primary driver:** Medium
- **Monotonic improvement of conditioning signal:** High under binary rewards; Low in practice
- **Delayed entropy collapse:** Medium
