# Benchmarking Multi-View BEV Object Detection with Mixed Pinhole and Fisheye Cameras

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27818
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27818
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Mixed pinhole-fisheye setups cause near-zero mAP when applying pinhole-trained models; distortion-aware view transformation using MEI and polar BEV coordinates recover performance, with PETR achieving 0.280 mAP and 0.288 NDS.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the challenge of multi-view BEV 3D object detection when combining standard pinhole cameras with fisheye cameras, which introduce radial distortion. Using a converted KITTI-360 dataset in nuScenes format, the authors evaluate three architectural adaptations: geometric rectification, distortion-aware view transformation based on the MEI camera model, and polar coordinate BEV representations. They find catastrophic zero-shot performance degradation (near-zero mAP) when pinhole-trained models are applied directly to fisheye data. Distortion-aware modeling, especially in projection-free architectures like PETR, significantly restores accuracy. Polar coordinates further improve detection, particularly for small objects like pedestrians. Geometric rectification offers an upper bound but sacrifices field-of-view.

## Method Summary
The study converts KITTI-360 multi-camera data (pinhole and dual 180° fisheye) to the nuScenes format and defines a benchmark for mixed-camera BEV detection. Three modifications to existing BEV detectors are explored: (1) geometric rectification to convert fisheye images to virtual pinhole views; (2) integrating the MEI unified camera model into view transformation modules of BEVFormer, BEVDet, and PETR, enabling direct processing of distorted imagery; and (3) using polar coordinate BEV grids (ρ,θ,z) instead of Cartesian grids to better align with radial distortion. Models are trained for 24 epochs with standard MMDetection3D configurations and evaluated using nuScenes metrics (mAP, NDS). Ablations compare baseline pinhole projection against distortion-aware variants, with and without polar grids.

## Key Results
- Raw fisheye inputs cause near-zero mAP for models trained on pinhole data, confirming severe domain gap.
- Distortion-aware view transformation improves all architectures; BEVDet shows the largest relative gain (+24% mAP). PETR achieves the highest overall performance (0.280 mAP, 0.288 NDS).
- Polar coordinate BEV representations consistently boost performance across models, with notable improvements for pedestrian detection.
- Rectification yields an upper bound on performance but reduces field-of-view and introduces computational overhead, highlighting the trade-off between geometric simplicity and information preservation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Projection-Free Robustness
**Claim:** Projection-free architectures (e.g., PETR) are inherently robust to fisheye distortion because they avoid explicit geometric projection steps that assume pinhole camera models.  
**Mechanism:** PETR enriches 2D features with 3D positional embeddings derived from camera parameters and learned feature-guided encoding. By replacing pinhole unprojection with the MEI model in ray generation, it encodes distorted fisheye rays directly into the query space. The model learns to map features via attention without committing to a single depth-to-BEV lifting.  
**Core assumption:** A learned positional embedding scheme can implicitly model non-linear distortion if provided with correct geometric priors (MEI parameters).  
**Evidence anchors:** 
- Abstract: “projection-free architectures are inherently more robust and effective against fisheye distortion.”
- Section II-A: PETR bypasses explicit projection via positional embeddings.
- Section IV-B: PETR achieves highest baseline (0.266 mAP) and overall (0.280 mAP with polar). Zero-shot mAP is low but non-zero (0.001).  
**Break condition:** If MEI integration into PETR produced no gain (it gained +0.003 mAP). A stronger break would be performance degradation with MEI.

### Mechanism 2: Polar Coordinate Alignment
**Claim:** Polar coordinate BEV representations better align with the radial structure of fisheye distortion, improving feature aggregation and detection of small objects.  
**Mechanism:** Polar grids (ρ, θ, z) sample uniformly in angular and radial dimensions, matching the equidistant sampling pattern of many fisheye lenses. This preserves angular consistency and mitigates non-uniform spatial density (over-sampling at image center, under-sampling at edges) inherent in Cartesian grids.  
**Core assumption:** BEV sampling density should match native information density of source camera frustums.  
**Evidence anchors:** 
- Abstract: “polar coordinate representations to better align with radial distortion.”
- Section III-C: Polar grids capture angular consistency and radial compression.
- Table I: All models show mAP/NDS gains with polar; pedestrian AP improves for PETR (0.230→0.296), BEVDet (0.138→0.153).  
**Break condition:** If polar failed to improve pedestrian detection while maintaining/improving large object performance. Evidence shows it helps small objects.

### Mechanism 3: Rectification Upper Bound
**Claim:** Geometric rectification provides a performance upper bound by converting fisheye to pinhole-like views, but it incurs FoV loss and computational overhead.  
**Mechanism:** Rectification applies non-linear remapping to “undistort” the fisheye image into a virtual pinhole projection, restoring linear projection assumptions.  
**Core assumption:** The primary performance gap is due to geometric mismatch in image formation model, not semantic differences.  
**Evidence anchors:** 
- Abstract: “Rectification provides an upper bound but with field-of-view loss.”
- Section III-A: Describes creation of two virtual pinholes per fisheye, noting reduced FoV, resolution distortion, overhead.
- Table I: Rectified models generally achieve higher mAP/NDS than distortion-aware counterparts (e.g., PETR: 0.295 vs. 0.280).  
**Break condition:** If rectification did not yield a clear upper bound over distortion-aware training.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Unified Camera Models (MEI)**  
  *Why needed:* Replaces the pinhole assumption with a model that can represent both pinhole and fisheye lenses, enabling mixed-camera processing in a single network.  
  *Quick check:* “What parameters in the MEI model control fisheye distortion (k1, k2, ξ), and how does setting them to zero reduce it to a pinhole model?”

- **Concept: BEV View Transformation Paradigms**  
  *Why needed:* The paper compares three fundamental approaches to lifting 2D features to 3D/BEV: forward/LSS, backward/BEVFormer, and projection-free/PETR. Their sensitivities to distortion differ.  
  *Quick check:* “Why does explicit depth prediction (BEVDet) suffer more from fisheye distortion than PETR’s positional encoding approach?”

- **Concept: Coordinate System Representations (Cartesian vs. Polar)**  
  *Why needed:* The choice of BEV grid coordinates interacts with distortion; polar grids better match the fisheye’s radial sampling.  
  *Quick check:* “How would you convert a 3D point (x, y, z) in camera coordinates to polar BEV grid coordinates (ρ, θ) for a fisheye camera with an equidistant projection model?”

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Image Backbone → Distortion-Aware View Transformation Module (VTM) → [Optional Polar Grid Resampling] → BEV Features → Detection Head.

**Critical path:** Input Images → Backbone → [VTM with MEI] → [Optional Polar Grid] → BEV Features → Detection Head → 3D Boxes. The MEI integration is the non-optional, distortion-handling pivot.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Projection-free vs. explicit depth: PETR is more robust but may have lower ceiling; BEVDet gains more from correct geometry but is fragile.  
- Polar vs. Cartesian: Polar adds coordinate conversion complexity but aligns with distortion; Cartesian is simpler but mismatches sampling.  
- MEI integration depth: Integrated only in VTM (as done) vs. also in backbone (unexplored).

**Failure signatures:**  
- Zero-shot failure: ~0 mAP when using nuScenes-pretrained model on raw fisheye.  
- Distance decay: mAP drops sharply beyond 10m, indicating poor long-range depth reasoning.  
- Class imbalance bias: Over-detection of large static objects, under-detection of small dynamic objects.  
- Angular sensitivity: Larger performance drop in back/side sectors (high distortion) vs. front when distortion unmodeled.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Zero-Shot Transfer Test:** Load a nuScenes-trained model and run inference on raw KITTI-360 fisheye validation set without fine-tuning. Expected: ~0 mAP.  
2. **Baseline vs. MEI-VTM Ablation:** Train PETR, BEVFormer, BEVDet with baseline (pinhole) and DA-VTM (MEI). Isolate mAP gain from correct geometry. Expected: BEVDet largest relative gain; PETR modest gain.  
3. **Polar Coordinate Addition:** Take best DA-VTM model (likely PETR) and add polar transformation. Measure overall mAP and pedestrian AP change. Also evaluate angular-stratified performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*Question:* How well do the conclusions about projection-free robustness and polar coordinate benefits generalize to other mixed fisheye-pinhole datasets or real-world deployments beyond KITTI-360?  
*Basis:* Study exclusively uses converted KITTI-360; no other dataset with mixed cameras exists.  
*Why unresolved:* Performance trends might not hold for different vehicle setups, fisheye models, or geographic contexts.  
*Resolving evidence:* Evaluation on an additional independent mixed-camera dataset or thorough cross-validation across diverse KITTI-360 environments.

### Open Question 2
*Question:* Is PETR’s superior performance primarily due to its projection-free architecture or because it was evaluated with a different backbone (VoVNet-99) compared to BEVFormer (ResNet-101) and BEVDet (ResNet-50)?  
*Basis:* Models use mismatched backbones, confounding architectural effect with backbone capacity.  
*Why unresolved:* Without a controlled ablation with identical backbones, difference sources are unclear.  
*Resolving evidence:* Re-train all three architectures with identical backbones (e.g., all ResNet-50) and compare distortion-aware and polar adaptations.

### Open Question 3
*Question:* Do polar coordinate representations specifically improve detection of small, distorted objects (e.g., pedestrians, bicycles) or is the gain driven by dataset’s severe class imbalance favoring large static objects?  
*Basis:* KITTI-360 annotation distribution is heavily skewed toward static infrastructure; per-class statistics for small objects may be noisy.  
*Why unresolved:* Limited samples for classes like buses, motorcycles, bicycles make it hard to confirm polar benefits beyond pedestrians.  
*Resolving evidence:* Analyze polar gains on a more balanced subset or oversampled small objects; report confidence intervals for per-class AP changes.

### Open Question 4
*Question:* What is the primary cause of the sharp performance drop beyond 10 meters in distortion-aware models compared to rectified ones—depth estimation failure, MEI inaccuracies at range, or BEV grid resolution limitations?  
*Basis:* Table II shows growing gap at 20–50m; paper speculates “unreliable depth cues” but does not isolate factors.  
*Why unresolved:* Rectification removes distortion entirely; gap could be due to (a) depth head failure under fisheye, (b) MEI projection errors for distant points, or (c) BEV grid resolution issues.  
*Resolving evidence:* Compute depth estimation errors separately for pinhole vs. fisheye views, binned by distance; evaluate MEI projection error by comparing projected 3D ground truth to image coordinates for distant objects.

### Open Question 5
*Question:* Could a hybrid approach that partially rectifies only the most distorted peripheral regions while applying distortion-aware modeling to the central area combine FoV preservation with geometric simplicity?  
*Basis:* Paper treats rectification and distortion-aware modeling as mutually exclusive; rectification loses FoV, direct modeling sacrifices long-range accuracy.  
*Why unresolved:* No experiment explores combining both strategies.  
*Resolving evidence:* Implement a hybrid pipeline (e.g., central 120° with MEI, periphery rectified and stitched) and benchmark against pure methods on mAP and FoV coverage.

## Limitations
- Conversion from KITTI-360 to nuScenes format may introduce geometric inaccuracies; hyperparameters not fully specified, risking replication errors.
- The claim that projection-free architectures are inherently robust is questionable: PETR gains only +0.003 mAP from MEI, while BEVDet gains +24%, suggesting architectural differences (or backbone) dominate.
- No statistical significance testing; observed gains could be due to chance. Computational costs of polar grids and rectification are unreported.

## Confidence
- **Zero-shot failure:** High
- **Distortion-aware VTM improvement:** High
- **Polar BEV benefit:** High
- **Inherent robustness of projection-free architectures:** Medium

## Next Checks
