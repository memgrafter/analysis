# PhyPrompt: RL-based Prompt Refinement for Physically Plausible Text-to-Video Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.03505
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03505
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: On VideoPhy2, PhyPrompt-7B achieves 40.8% joint success (+8.6pp vs baseline), with 11pp physical commonsense gain and 4.4pp semantic adherence improvement.

## Executive Summary
PhyPrompt introduces a reinforcement learning framework to refine text prompts for video generation, addressing frequent violations of physical laws due to underspecified inputs. The approach first fine-tunes a language model on a physics-focused Chain-of-Thought dataset to embed physical reasoning. It then applies Group Relative Policy Optimization with a dynamic reward curriculum that initially emphasizes semantic fidelity and gradually shifts toward physical commonsense. Evaluated on VideoPhy2, the 7B-parameter PhyPrompt outperforms larger models like GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3, and transfers zero-shot across multiple video generators, yielding up to 16.8% joint improvement.

## Method Summary
PhyPrompt’s training comprises two stages. First, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a curated Chain-of-Thought dataset teaches the model to reason about physical principles when rewriting prompts. Second, reinforcement learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) further optimizes the model using a reward function combining semantic fidelity and physical plausibility. A dynamic curriculum adjusts the relative weight of these rewards over time, starting with a focus on preserving the user’s intended semantics before increasingly penalizing physics violations. At inference, a user prompt is processed by the refined model to produce a physics-aware prompt, which is then fed to an off-the-shelf text-to-video generator.

## Key Results
- **Joint success rate**: 40.8% on VideoPhy2, an 8.6 percentage point increase over the baseline.
- **Physical commonsense**: Improved by 11 points (55.8% → 66.8%).
- **Semantic adherence**: Improved by 4.4 points (43.4% → 47.8%).
- Outperforms GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3 despite using only a 7B-parameter model.
- Zero-shot transfer to multiple video generators yields up to 16.8% joint improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism leverages a dynamic reward curriculum to balance two competing objectives: maintaining the semantic intent of the original prompt and ensuring physical plausibility. Starting with a higher weight on semantic fidelity prevents the model from overfitting to physics at the expense of meaning. Gradually increasing the physical reward guides the policy toward corrections that respect real-world constraints. The initial SFT stage provides a strong prior in physical reasoning, enabling the RL stage to refine effectively without catastrophic forgetting. The use of GRPO stabilizes training by grouping relative advantages, reducing variance in policy updates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning for Prompt Optimization**
  - *Why needed*: Enables end-to-end optimization of prompts for downstream video generators without modifying the generator’s weights.
  - *Quick check*: Can we formulate prompt refinement as a sequential decision problem with reward feedback from the generated video?

- **Chain-of-Thought Fine-tuning**
  - *Why needed*: Imparts structured physical reasoning to the language model before RL, establishing a robust starting point.
  - *Quick check*: Does SFT on physics CoT data improve zero-shot performance on physical reasoning tasks?

- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
  - *Why needed*: A variant of policy gradient that uses group-based advantage estimation to improve stability in language model fine-tuning.
  - *Quick check*: Does grouping reduce variance and lead to more consistent policy improvements compared to standard PPO?

- **Dynamic Reward Curriculum**
  - *Why needed*: Balances semantic fidelity and physical plausibility throughout training, avoiding premature convergence to one objective.
  - *Quick check*: Does a scheduled weight shift yield higher joint success than fixed or alternating reward schemes?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
User Prompt → SFT-finetuned LLM → RL-finetuned LLM (GRPO) → Refined Prompt → Video Generator → Output Video

**Critical path**: During inference, the refined LLM processes the user prompt and outputs a physics-enriched prompt; the video generator then produces the final video. Training follows: (1) SFT on physics CoT dataset; (2) RL loop where the policy generates refined prompts, a reward model evaluates both semantic fidelity and physical plausibility, and GRPO updates the policy with curriculum-controlled weighting.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- RL adds significant computational overhead but yields targeted improvements in physical reasoning.
- The dynamic curriculum requires careful scheduling; too rapid a shift may degrade semantics, too slow may limit physics gains.
- SFT may anchor the model to a specific style of physical reasoning, potentially reducing adaptability to unseen physics scenarios.

**Failure signatures**:
- Persistent physics violations (e.g., objects floating, non-conservation of momentum) in generated videos despite refined prompts.
- Semantic drift where the core intent of the user prompt is altered (e.g., “a ball rolling uphill” becomes “a ball rolling downhill”).
- Training instability if the curriculum shifts too abruptly, causing large drops in semantic scores.

**First three experiments**:
1. Ablation: Compare static reward weighting (fixed 50/50) against the dynamic curriculum to isolate the curriculum’s contribution.
2. SFT-only evaluation: Test the model after the SFT stage on VideoPhy2 to measure the incremental benefit of the subsequent RL stage.
3. Cross-generator transfer: Run the refined prompts on at least three different video generation models (e.g., Stable Video Diffusion, Sora, ModelScope) and report joint success rates to assess generalization.

## Limitations
- **External validity**: Evaluation limited to VideoPhy2; benchmark details and generalization to real-world videos are unclear.
- **Baseline transparency**: Key baselines (e.g., prompts used for GPT-4o/DeepSeek-V3) and full hyperparameters are unreported; distinction from prior works like PhyT2V is not well-articulated.
- **Ablation and cost analysis**: Missing per-component contributions, failure mode breakdowns, and resource overhead (training compute, inference latency).
