# Akasha 2: Hamiltonian State Space Duality and Visual-Language Joint Embedding Predictive Architectur

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.06212
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06212
- **Reference count**: 17
- **Primary result**: Integrates Hamiltonian physics with a visual-language predictive model to achieve state-of-the-art long-horizon video prediction, ultra-low-latency mobile inference, and 4× faster visual synthesis than diffusion models.

## Executive Summary
Akasha 2 introduces a hybrid architecture that combines a selective state space model (Mamba-3) with a Sparse Mixture of Hamiltonian Experts (SMoE-HE) to enforce latent energy conservation via symplectic integration. This core is augmented by a Phase-Manifold V-Sync mechanism for aligning heterogeneous multimodal streams and a Hamiltonian Flow Matching (HFM) decoder for efficient visual synthesis using persistent 3D Gaussian Splatting. The system is trained via a Visual-Language Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (VL-JEPA) objective. Results show significant gains in long-horizon video prediction (Kinetics-400: FVD 287, SSIM 0.841), inference efficiency (3–18× speedup over transformer baselines, <50 ms latency on mobile), and synthesis speed (4× faster than diffusion).

## Method Summary
The model processes aligned vision, language, and other sensor streams. Input embeddings first undergo Phase-Manifold V-Sync, which modulates effective integration timesteps using Fourier oscillators to maintain cross-modal phase coherence. The synchronized sequence is processed by a 32-layer Mamba-3 Selective SSM backbone. At each layer, a Sparse Mixture of Hamiltonian Experts (SMoE-HE) computes a scalar potential from the top-2 gated experts. State updates are performed by a symplectic leapfrog integrator, which separately updates position and momentum variables while preserving phase-space volume. A holographic memory cell recursively aggregates local states. For generation, a Hamiltonian Flow Matching (HFM) head integrates deterministic gradient flows from the learned potential to produce visual latents, which a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) head maps to output images. Training minimizes a composite loss: a JEPA latent prediction loss, a Hamiltonian regularization term, and a stability loss.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Latent Conservation via Symplectic SMoE-HE
The Sparse Mixture of Hamiltonian Experts (SMoE-HE) with symplectic leapfrog integration enforces latent energy conservation. Each expert parameterizes a local Hamiltonian potential \(V_i(h)\). The total potential is a gated sum. State updates follow the leapfrog scheme: a half momentum step, full position step, half momentum step. This symplectic integrator exactly preserves the symplectic two-form, ensuring bounded energy error over long rollouts. The core assumption is that physical scene latents evolve on a manifold where total energy is approximately conserved, and this inductive bias stabilizes long-horizon prediction. Evidence comes from the abstract's claim of "unprecedented spatiotemporal coherence" and section 3.2.1's formal statement. A break condition occurs if the integration timestep \(\Delta t\) is too large relative to potential curvature, causing accumulating local errors and trajectory divergence.

### Mechanism 2: Phase-Manifold V-Sync for Multimodal Alignment
Phase-Manifold V-Sync eliminates temporal misalignment by modulating the effective integration timestep \(\Delta t_{\text{eff}}(n)\) for each modality. It multiplies a base \(\Delta t\) by a factor derived from averaging cosine waves at each stream's characteristic frequency (e.g., 30 Hz for vision). This dynamically harmonizes the phase evolution of heterogeneous sensors. The core assumption is that cross-modal alignment can be achieved by matching underlying oscillatory phases. Evidence is the abstract's description and section 3.3's Equation (8). The mechanism is novel with weak external corroboration. It may fail if input streams have non-stationary or rapidly shifting dominant frequencies, as fixed \(\{f_k\}\) cannot track true phase.

### Mechanism 3: Hamiltonian Flow Matching for Accelerated Synthesis
Hamiltonian Flow Matching (HFM) accelerates visual synthesis by casting generation as following deterministic gradient flow lines of the latent Hamiltonian potential, rather than iterative denoising. It solves \(dz/dt = -\nabla_z V(z,t)\), where \(V\) is the time-varying potential from the world model. This direct path along energy gradients requires fewer steps to reach a target manifold. The core assumption is that the mapping from Hamiltonian dynamics to visual latent space is smooth and \(V\) is well-behaved. Evidence is the abstract's 4× speed claim and section 3.4.1's flow equation. This is another novel component with no direct neighbors. Failure modes include getting stuck in non-convex or chaotic potential landscapes with many saddle points.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input Encoders -> Phase-Manifold V-Sync -> Mamba-3 Backbone -> SMoE-HE -> Symplectic Integrator -> Holographic Memory Cells -> Heads (HFM Decoder + 3DGS Head)

**Critical path**: Input embeddings are temporally aligned via V-Sync, processed sequentially through 32 Mamba-3 layers. At each layer, the SMoE-HE router selects two Hamiltonian experts to compute a potential \(V(h)\). The symplectic integrator updates the position-momentum pair \((h, p)\). Holographic memory cells recursively aggregate these states. Finally, the HFM decoder generates visual latents via gradient flow, and the 3DGS head predicts Gaussian splat parameters for rasterization.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Stability vs. Expressivity*: Hamiltonian constraints improve long-term coherence but may limit modeling of chaotic/unphysical dynamics (Sec 8.2).  
- *Sparsity vs. Capacity*: Top-2 expert sparsity reduces compute but may underfit complex scenarios needing more potential manifolds.  
- *Mobile Speed vs. Fidelity*: FP8 quantization and kernel fusion enable <50 ms latency but may incur minor quality loss.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Latent explosion (||h_t|| growing) despite stability loss → integration timestep \(\Delta t\) too large or potential \(V\) too steep.  
- Temporal jitter in video → V-Sync failure; check if modality frequency estimates \(\{f_k\}\) are inaccurate.  
- Blurry/distorted 3DGS renders → HFM flow failed to converge or 3DGS head training unstable.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Ablation on Kinetics-400**: Measure FVD/SSIM with/without Hamiltonian constraints, V-Sync, SMoE, and 3DGS (Table 3) to validate each physics-inspired component.  
2. **Latency Benchmark**: Measure end-to-end generation time on A100, M2 Pro, and Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 (Figure 6) to confirm efficiency claims and mobile viability.  
3. **Long-Horizon Roll-out Stability**: Generate 60+ frame sequences and track the Lagrangian (or latent Hamiltonian \(H(h,p)\)) over time; it should show bounded fluctuation around the initial value if symplectic integration is effective.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Can trainable symplectic integrators match the stability of fixed leapfrog while being more efficient?  
  *Basis*: Section 8.3 lists "Learned Integrators" as future work. *Why unresolved*: Fixed integrators use uniform steps, possibly limiting adaptability. *Resolution evidence*: Train a symplectic neural integrator; compare training speed, energy conservation, and FVD on Kinetics-400.

- **Open Question 2**: How can multi-scale hierarchical energies be incorporated into the Hamiltonian framework?  
  *Basis*: Section 8.3 proposes "Multi-Scale Hamiltonians". *Why unresolved*: Current SMoE-HE lacks cross-scale energy coupling, hindering multi-resolution reasoning. *Resolution evidence*: Add recursive cells with energy transfer; test on multi-scale datasets (e.g., particle systems); measure long-horizon prediction gains.

- **Open Question 3**: Can causal mechanisms be discovered within latent Hamiltonian dynamics?  
  *Basis*: Section 8.3 suggests "Causal Representation Learning". *Why unresolved*: Energy conservation is not causal; integrating causality could improve OOD generalization. *Resolution evidence*: Incorporate causal inductive biases (e.g., independent mechanisms); evaluate on causal benchmarks against baseline.

- **Open Question 4**: What is the boundary where Hamiltonian constraints fail for chaotic systems, and can adaptive regularization help?  
  *Basis*: Section 8.2 identifies an "Expressivity vs. Stability Trade-off". *Why unresolved*: Chaotic dynamics may be poorly captured; severity and remedies unknown. *Resolution evidence*: Benchmark on chaotic systems (e.g., Lorenz); analyze energy error; implement dynamic constraint strength based on chaos measures.

## Limitations
- **Major unknowns block full verification**: Exact formulation of "Mamba-3" SSM and its selective parameterization; momentum initialization and integration with SSM hidden states; composition of the multimodal training dataset; HFM's time-dependent potential construction.
- **Weak external support**: The Phase-Manifold V-Sync mechanism has no corroborating neighbors in the citation corpus; its robustness to non-stationary frequencies is unevaluated.
- **Efficiency claim opacity**: Reported 3–18× speedups and mobile latency depend on undocumented "Mamba-3" optimizations and kernel fusion details, making direct replication difficult.
