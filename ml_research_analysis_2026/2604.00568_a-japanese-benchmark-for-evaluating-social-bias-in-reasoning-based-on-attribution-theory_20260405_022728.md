# A Japanese Benchmark for Evaluating Social Bias in Reasoning Based on Attribution Theory

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00568
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00568
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** JUBAKU-v2 achieves higher score variance (0.0278) than existing benchmarks, with GPT-4o accuracy 81.9% significantly lower than GPT-5.2 (98.1%) and Claude 4 Sonnet (99.1%) (Chi-square p<0.01); answer fluctuation rates range 7.4%–88.9% across models.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of culturally specific, reasoning-focused bias evaluation for Japanese large language models (LLMs) by introducing JUBAKU-v2, a 216-example dataset grounded in attribution theory. The dataset presents each case with a fixed neutral conclusion and two alternative reasoning paths—one biased and one neutral—allowing the isolation of bias in the attribution process from outcome utility. Human validation ensures quality, with 72% agreement across annotators.

Primary evaluations show JUBAKU-v2 yields higher score variance (0.0278) across models than existing Japanese benchmarks, enabling finer-grained discrimination. GPT-4o achieves 81.9% accuracy, behind GPT-5.2 (98.1%) and Claude 4 Sonnet (99.1%) (p<0.01). Furthermore, prompt-variation robustness testing reveals answer fluctuation rates between 7.4% and 88.9% across models, confirming that many LLMs exhibit sensitivity to superficial changes when reasoning about social bias.

## Method Summary
JUBAKU-v2 was constructed from seed dialogues in JUBAKU-v1. Using GPT-5.1, the authors rewrote each dialogue to ensure that both the biased and neutral reasoning options lead to the same, pragmatically useful conclusion. Cases where GPT-4o selected the biased option were retained to create a challenging subset. Human annotators (six per case) then selected the neutral response based on clear definitions; items with at least three agreements were kept, yielding 27 base cases. These were expanded into 216 evaluation instances by combining two response orders with four instruction templates.

Evaluation proceeds in a binary-choice format: each model receives a prompt containing the context, two labeled responses (A/B), and the instruction to choose the less biased one. The metrics computed are (a) accuracy (rate of neutral selection), (b) score variance across the model suite, and (c) answer fluctuation rate—the proportion of base cases where the model’s choice varies across the eight prompt variants. This design isolates reasoning bias and probes robustness to surface-form changes.

## Key Results
- JUBAKU-v2 exhibits higher score variance (0.0278) than existing Japanese benchmarks (e.g., JBBQ, SSQA-JA), allowing clearer inter-model discrimination.
- GPT-4o accuracy is 81.9%, significantly below GPT-5.2 (98.1%) and Claude 4 Sonnet (99.1%) (Chi-square p<0.01).
- Answer fluctuation rates across prompt variants vary widely among models, from 7.4% (Claude 4 Sonnet) to 88.9% (Qwen3-30B-Instruct), indicating sensitivity to reasoning bias.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

- **Fixed Conclusion Isolation**: By fixing the conclusion across both reasoning options, the benchmark eliminates utility differences as a confound. Any systematic preference for the biased reasoning therefore reflects an attribution bias in the model’s justification process, not a judgment about outcome quality. The core assumption is that models can separately evaluate conclusion utility and reasoning quality; if the fixed conclusion seems low-utility, the isolation may break.

- **Attribution Theory Taxonomy**: Operationalizing Pettigrew’s ultimate attribution error defines bias as (1) inferring internal traits from group attributes or (2) misattributing achievements to external factors. This turns subjective cultural stereotypes into testable causal attribution claims, enabling consistent human annotation and model evaluation. The framework assumes LLM social bias manifests as systematic errors in causal attribution mirroring human cognitive biases; if models do not exhibit attribution-style reasoning, the taxonomy may not capture relevant biases.

- **Prompt Variation Robustness**: Eight prompt variants per base case (instruction phrasing × option order) test consistency. A model with stable, principle-based reasoning should choose the neutral option across all variants. High fluctuation rates indicate sensitivity to lexical cues or ordering effects, consistent with heuristic processing rather than deep evaluation. If all models show uniformly high fluctuation, the test may measure general instruction-following instability rather than bias-specific effects.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Attribution Theory (Pettigrew's Ultimate Attribution Error)**
  - *Why needed:* Provides theoretical foundation defining what constitutes “biased reasoning”; without it, bias labels would be arbitrary.
  - *Quick check:* If a person from Group X succeeds, would attributing it to “their group’s natural talent” be biased? Yes, because it is an internal attribution favoring the in-group.

- **Concept: Confounding Control via Fixed Conclusions**
  - *Why needed:* Enables reasoning-focused evaluation by decoupling conclusion bias from reasoning bias.
  - *Quick check:* If a benchmark tests both conclusion and reasoning simultaneously, what two separate effects could explain a model’s biased output? (1) The perceived utility of the conclusion itself, and (2) bias present in the reasoning path.

- **Concept: Prompt Variation as a Robustness Probe**
  - *Why needed:* Shifts evaluation from a single accuracy score to a consistency profile, exposing latent biases that surface under superficial changes.
  - *Quick check:* Would a model with 100% accuracy but 90% fluctuation be considered reliably unbiased? No; high fluctuation indicates instability and likely bias in reasoning.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Seed & Generation Module -> Bias-Induction Filter -> Human Validation Pipeline -> Variant Expansion -> Evaluation Engine -> Metrics Calculator

**Critical path:** Seed selection -> LLM rewriting with constraint -> Bias-case filtering -> Human review -> Variant generation -> Model inference -> Metric aggregation. Human review is the primary gating factor for dataset quality.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Cultural specificity vs. generalizability: JUBAKU-v2 uses Japanese categories (e.g., prefectural stereotypes) to increase relevance for Japanese LLMs but limits direct cross-cultural comparison.
- Fixed conclusion realism: Forcing identical conclusions may produce slightly unnatural dialogues, trading ecological validity for experimental control.
- Annotation burden: Six annotators for 30 initial cases ensures quality but is intensive; reported 72% agreement is not benchmarked against other tasks.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low human agreement (<60%) indicates ill-defined bias criteria or culturally ambiguous items.
- All models saturating (>95%) suggests the task is too easy or the bias distinction is not salient.
- High fluctuation rates across all models, including top performers, may indicate flawed prompt templates causing semantic drift.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate Human Validation: Apply the 6-annotator protocol to the 30 initial generated cases; verify the 72% agreement and identify categories with lower reliability.
2. Variance Comparison: Compute score variance for JUBAKU-v2 and existing Japanese benchmarks (JBNLI, JBBQ, SSQA-JA) on the same model suite; confirm JUBAKU-v2’s variance (0.0278) is highest.
3. Prompt Variation Stress Test: For a high-fluctuation model (e.g., Qwen3-30B-Instruct), analyze whether inconsistencies cluster in specific cultural categories or instruction templates to distinguish bias from template sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can JUBAKU-v2 be expanded to include more cultural categories and examples to better represent the full spectrum of Japanese social biases?
2. Is the attribution theory framework, as operationalized in JUBAKU-v2, valid for evaluating social bias in other linguistic and cultural contexts beyond Japanese?
3. Why do some language models, such as Qwen3-30B-Instruct, perform below chance level (48.1%) on JUBAKU-v2, suggesting a potential reverse bias or task misunderstanding?
4. To what extent does the inclusion of explicit reasoning steps (e.g., chain-of-thought prompting) consistently reduce attribution bias across diverse model architectures and sizes?

## Limitations
- Dataset details missing: The paper does not fully provide the bias definitions and four instruction templates; these must be extracted from the Hugging Face dataset.
- Inference settings unspecified: Generation hyperparameters (temperature, top-p) are unreported, potentially affecting result replication.
- Cultural scope limitation: JUBAKU-v2 centers on Japanese cultural categories, which may not transfer directly to other linguistic contexts.
