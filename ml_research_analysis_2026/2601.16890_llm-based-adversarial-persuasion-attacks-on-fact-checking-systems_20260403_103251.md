# LLM-Based Adversarial Persuasion Attacks on Fact-Checking Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.16890
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16890
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** Persuasion injection attacks cause catastrophic degradation in AFC systems, with claim-only accuracy collapsing to 0.043 on FEVER under an oracle attacker.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the vulnerability of automated fact-checking (AFC) systems to adversarial attacks that leverage persuasive rhetoric. The authors introduce "persuasion injection attacks," where a generative LLM (QWEN2.5-7B-INSTRUCT) rephrases claims using 15 persuasion techniques organized into 6 categories. Evaluated on the FEVER and FEVEROUS benchmarks, these attacks substantially degrade performance compared to standard lexical or paraphrastic baselines. Under an oracle attacker selecting the most damaging technique per claim, claim-only accuracy collapses to near zero (0.043 on FEVER, 0.010 on FEVEROUS). Even with perfect evidence (gold evidence), accuracy remains low (0.164 and 0.250 respectively). The most effective technique, Obfuscation, reduces the retriever's Recall@5 by 0.54 points and achieves a 38% evasion rate on FEVER with gold evidence. The study demonstrates that persuasion techniques, particularly those involving manipulative wording, simultaneously undermine evidence retrieval and veracity classification, leading to complete pipeline failure.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed an adversarial attack framework using QWEN2.5-7B-INSTRUCT to generate persuasive variants of factual claims. For each claim in the test sets of FEVER and FEVEROUS, the model was prompted to rephrase the claim using one of 15 persuasion techniques derived from a propaganda taxonomy. The resulting variants were evaluated through a two-stage AFC pipeline comprising a BM25 retriever and a RoBERTa veracity classifier. Performance was measured using standard accuracy metrics, as well as adversarial success rates (ASR). To decouple the impact on retrieval versus classification, the classifier was also evaluated with gold evidence (the true supporting or refuting passages). This setup allowed the authors to quantify how persuasion techniques degrade each stage and their combined effect.

## Key Results
- Oracle attacks collapse claim-only accuracy to 0.043 (FEVER) and 0.010 (FEVEROUS).
- Even with gold evidence, accuracy drops to 0.164 (FEVER) and 0.250 (FEVEROUS).
- Obfuscation causes the largest retrieval degradation (|∆Recall@5| = 0.54) and achieves 38% evasion rate on FEVER with gold evidence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Obfuscation Causes Retrieval Failure
- **Claim:** Persuasion techniques like Obfuscation cause evidence retrieval failure by replacing concrete, searchable entities with vague language.
- **Mechanism:** The BM25 retriever relies on lexical matching. Obfuscation substitutes specific entities/dates with less frequent, general phrases, drastically reducing keyword overlap with gold evidence.
- **Core assumption:** Retrieval is sensitive to lexical overlap.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 5.4 shows Obfuscation has a |∆Recall@5| of 0.54; Section 5.3 gives the example "50 days" → "approximately one and a half months".
- **Break condition:** If a dense semantic retriever is used, Obfuscation's impact may be reduced.

### Mechanism 2: Manipulative Wording Confuses Classification
- **Claim:** Even with perfect evidence, persuasive techniques like Manipulative Wording degrade veracity classification by creating ambiguity.
- **Mechanism:** The classifier (RoBERTa) is trained to detect contradictions. Persuasive phrasing shifts the claim away from a factual proposition, reducing salience of contradiction signals. The model may interpret ambiguous claims as subjective, biasing toward True.
- **Core assumption:** Classifier decision boundary is influenced by surface-level linguistic markers of certainty.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: accuracy with gold evidence falls to 0.164/0.250; Section 5.3: Manipulative Wording remains most damaging; Section 5.2: Sabotage ASR remains high (~100%) with gold evidence.
- **Break condition:** If the classifier is trained to ignore rhetorical framing (e.g., adversarial debiasing), evasion rates might drop.

### Mechanism 3: Compounding Pipeline Failure
- **Claim:** Some techniques (e.g., Obfuscation) simultaneously degrade retrieval *and* classification, causing complete pipeline failure.
- **Mechanism:** Upstream persuasion corrupts claim's lexical/semantic alignment with evidence, causing the retriever to miss relevant documents and the classifier to misclassify due to missing or irrelevant evidence. Dual failure is more catastrophic.
- **Core assumption:** The two stages are interdependent; retrieval failure dooms classification, but classification can also fail independently.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: persuasion techniques simultaneously degrade both; Section 5.4: Oracle collapses Recall@k; Section 5.2: Oracle evasion ASR reaches 75.9% with gold evidence; Figure 3: Obfuscation is a top-right outlier with high degradation in both.
- **Break condition:** If one stage is made exceptionally robust (perfect retriever or rhetoric-invariant classifier), pipeline failure could be mitigated.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Attack Generator -> Evidence Retriever -> Veracity Classifier -> Evaluation Harness

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate adversarial variants for test set using the Attack Generator.  
2. Run the Evidence Retriever on each variant to fetch relevant documents.  
3. Run the Veracity Classifier on (variant, retrieved evidence) and (variant, gold evidence).  
4. Aggregate metrics (accuracy, ASR, Recall@k) in the Evaluation Harness.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Retriever choice:* BM25 is standard but vulnerable to lexical changes; dense retrievers might be more robust but could introduce new vulnerabilities.  
- *Classifier:* Using RoBERTa avoids LLM contamination but may not reflect state-of-the-art AFC systems that use LLMs.  
- *Gold evidence:* Isolates classifier vulnerability but is unrealistic; real systems use a fallible retriever.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Steep accuracy drop (>10 points) on Persuasion (Blind) vs. baselines (Table 2).  
- Persistence of high Sabotage ASR with gold evidence (Table 3).  
- Obfuscation causing |∆Recall@5| > 0.5 (Figure 2).  
- High Evasion ASR for Slogan/Obfuscation in claim-only setting (Table 4).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate baseline attacks: Implement Synonym Substitution, Char Noise, etc., and confirm performance degradation matches Table 2 (small drops).  
2. Run Blind Persuasion Attack: Generate one variant per technique per claim. Compute claim-only and gold-evidence accuracy. Expect ~13-17 point drop (FEVER) vs. baselines.  
3. Isolate Obfuscation: Run only Obfuscation variants. Confirm it causes the highest retrieval degradation (Recall@5 drop ~0.54) and high evasion with gold evidence (~38% FEVER). Validate with examples from Table 9.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can Automated Fact-Checking (AFC) systems be made robust to *Manipulative Wording* persuasion attacks, such as Obfuscation, which degrade both evidence retrieval and veracity classification even with perfect evidence?
2. To what extent do the vulnerabilities to persuasion injection attacks persist across languages and disinformation domains/topics beyond English Wikipedia-based claims?
3. Does the adversarial potency of persuasion injection attacks scale with the capability of the generative LLM used to craft the attacks?
4. How resilient are modern dense retrieval systems (e.g., using bi-encoders like BERT) to persuasion attacks compared to the sparse BM25 retriever evaluated in the paper?
5. Are LLM-based veracity classifiers (e.g., prompted or fine-tuned generative models) more or less susceptible to persuasion attacks than encoder-based classifiers like RoBERTa, and can chain-of-thought or self-consistency prompting mitigate the attacks?

## Limitations
- **Model & pipeline representativeness:** Results are based on BM25 + RoBERTa; vulnerability of state-of-the-art dense retrievers or LLM-based classifiers is unknown.
- **Attack generalization & realism:** Oracle attacker selecting single most damaging technique is unrealistic; real adversaries may combine techniques.
- **Evaluation scope:** Limited to English (FEVER/FEVEROUS); cross-lingual and multimodal scenarios unexplored.

## Confidence
- **High:** Claims about simultaneous degradation (retrieval + classification) for the tested pipeline and technique set are well-supported by empirical data.
- **Medium:** Generalization that "persuasion techniques are potent adversarial threats" broadly, but limited by specific models, taxonomy, and datasets.
- **Low:** The conclusion of "complete pipeline failure" in practice, as it hinges on an oracle attack and may overstate risk for more robust implementations.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate with a Dense Retriever: Replace BM25 with a state-of-the-art dense retriever (e.g., FAISS + sentence-transformers) and re-run the Obfuscation attack. Check if |∆Recall@5| degradation remains significant (>0.3 points).
2. Test on an LLM-Based Fact-Checker: Integrate the persuasive claims into an evaluation of a recent LLM-based AFC system (e.g., in-context learning or fine-tuned Llama-3). Check if the classifier's Sabotage ASR with gold evidence stays above 75%, indicating persistent vulnerability.
3. Cross-Taxonomy & Real Sample Validation: Generate attack variants using a different persuasion/propaganda taxonomy (e.g., from NELA-GT) or using real-world hyper-partisan social media claims. Check if the same high-impact techniques (e.g., Obfuscation, Manipulative Wording) consistently cause the largest drops in end-to-end accuracy.
