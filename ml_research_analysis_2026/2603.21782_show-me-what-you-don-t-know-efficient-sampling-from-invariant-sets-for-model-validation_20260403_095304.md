# Show Me What You Don't Know: Efficient Sampling from Invariant Sets for Model Validation

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2603.21782  
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21782  
* **Reference count:** 40  
* **Primary result:** NDTM enables training‑free sampling from a model’s invariant set (fiber) with high fidelity, matching the performance of expensive conditional generative models while requiring only a single guided generation pass.

## Executive Summary
Understanding what a machine learning model *does not* encode—its invariances—is essential for interpretability and trust. Traditional approaches train a separate conditional generative model for each subject model to sample from its invariant set, a costly process. This paper introduces two contributions: (1) a “fiber loss” that regularizes conditional generative training to improve fidelity to the true invariant set, and (2) **Non‑linear Diffusion Trajectory Matching (NDTM)**, a training‑free method that steers a pretrained unconditional diffusion or flow‑matching model using the fiber loss as a guidance cost. NDTM replaces days of training with a single guided generation procedure, achieving comparable fidelity at a fraction of the computational cost. The approach successfully exposes known biases (e.g., texture bias in CNNs) and uncovers serious failures such as a vision encoder discarding critical positional information in medical imagery.

## Method Summary
Given a subject model φ and a query image x, compute the target representation h = φ(x). The goal is to sample an image x̃ such that φ(x̃) ≈ h (i.e., x̃ lies in the same fiber). NDTM leverages a pretrained unconditional diffusion model p(x) as a prior. During the reverse diffusion process, at each timestep t, NDTM performs:
1. Compute the Tweedie estimate of the denoised sample, \(\hat{x}_0 = \mathbb{E}[x_0|x_t]\).
2. Approximate the target representation using a noised version of the original image: compute \(x'_t\) by applying the same diffusion transition to x, then set \(h_{\text{target}} = \phi(\mathbb{E}[x_0|x'_t])\).
3. Optimize a correction \(u_t\) via gradient descent to minimize a terminal cost combining the fiber loss \(\|\phi(\hat{x}_0) - h_{\text{target}}\|^2\) and regularizers that control step size and score deviation.
4. Apply the correction: \(\hat{x}_t = x_t + \gamma_t u_t\), then perform one denoising step to obtain \(x_{t-1}\).
After the final timestep, \(x_0\) is returned as the invariant sample. An alternative is to train a conditional generative model on \(p(x|h)\) and include the fiber loss as an auxiliary regularization term during training, which improves fidelity but requires separate training for each subject model.

## Key Results
* On ImageNet with DINOv2, NDTM achieves a fiber loss of \(273\pm4\), far below the nearest‑neighbor baseline (1225) and comparable to conditional diffusion/flow‑matching models that require days of training.
* NDTM maintains image quality (FID) close to the unconditional diffusion prior while drastically reducing compute cost.
* The method reveals model biases (e.g., CNNs’ reliance on texture) and detects critical failures such as the Qwen‑2B vision encoder losing heart‑side information in situs inversus X‑rays, explaining downstream VLM errors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Regularizing conditional generative models with the fiber loss improves their fidelity to the true invariant set (fiber).  
- **Mechanism:** During training of a fiber model \(p_\theta(x|h)\), the fiber loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{fiber}} = \|\phi(x) - \phi(\tilde{x})\|^2_2\) is added as an auxiliary loss term. This directly penalizes discrepancies between the representation of a generated sample \(\tilde{x}\) and the target representation \(h\), encouraging the model to produce samples that are more invariant under \(\phi\).  
- **Core assumption:** The fiber loss provides a differentiable signal that correlates with proximity to the fiber manifold, and minimizing it during generation or training leads to samples that are indistinguishable from the target under \(\phi\).  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] The paper states: “first, using a ‘fiber loss’ (representation discrepancy) to regularize the training of conditional generative models, improving their fidelity to the true invariant set (fiber).”  
  - [section 3.1] “We propose to use the fiber loss (Equation (3)) in training for improved fidelity… Including this regularization for training can produce samples that are significantly closer to being invariant under the subject model.”  
  - [section 4.1] Experiments on color MNIST show lower fiber loss for regularized models vs. unregularized baselines.  
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence is weak; neighbors discuss LLM knowledge boundaries, not representation invariances.  
- **Break condition:** If regularization strength \(\lambda_{\text{fiber}}\) is too high, it may over‑constrain the generator, harming diversity (consistency) as seen in the trade‑off in Figure 4.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Guiding a pretrained unconditional diffusion/flow‑matching model via Non‑linear Diffusion Trajectory Matching (NDTM) with the fiber loss as the terminal cost enables training‑free sampling from invariant sets.  
- **Mechanism:** At each denoising timestep \(t\), a correction \(u_t\) is computed via gradient descent to minimize an approximation of the terminal fiber loss: \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{terminal}} \approx \|\phi(\mathcal{E}[x_0|x_t]) - \phi(\mathcal{E}[x_0|x'_t])\|^2_2\), where \(x'_t\) is a noised version of the original query image. The correction is applied: \(\hat{x}_t = x_t + \gamma_t u_t\), then a denoising step yields \(x_{t‑1}\).  
- **Core assumption:** (1) The diffusion prior \(p(x)\) is close to the data distribution so guided samples stay realistic; (2) Tweedie’s approximation of the expected denoised sample is accurate enough; (3) Alternating correction and denoising converges to a sample whose final representation approximates the target fiber.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “introducing a training‑free method that guides a pretrained unconditional diffusion or flow‑matching model via Non‑linear Diffusion Trajectory Matching (NDTM), with the fiber loss as the guidance cost function.”  
  - [section 3.2] Detailed derivation and equations (6)–(7).  
  - [section 4.2] ImageNet results: NDTM fiber loss 273±4 (DINOv2) vs. 1225 for nearest‑neighbors, while maintaining FID similar to the unconditional model.  
  - [corpus] Weak; neighboring papers focus on LLMs.  
- **Break condition:** Guidance may become unstable or produce out‑of‑distribution samples if the diffusion prior is mismatched with the subject model’s manifold, or if \(\gamma_t\), \(\kappa_t\), \(\tau_t\) are poorly tuned. The modified terminal loss (using \(x'_t\)) mitigates this.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** There is a fundamental trade‑off between fidelity (closeness to the fiber) and consistency (matching the underlying conditional distribution \(p(x|h)\)).  
- **Mechanism:** High fidelity requires strong constraints on the representation, which can reduce diversity and distort the natural data distribution. Consistency requires sampling from the true conditional, which may allow larger representation deviations. Methods occupy different points: conditional models trained with fiber loss achieve higher consistency but cost more; NDTM achieves high fidelity but may slightly compromise consistency, especially with an out‑of‑distribution prior.  
- **Core assumption:** The fiber is a low‑dimensional manifold; accurately modeling both the exact manifold location (fidelity) and the natural variation along it (consistency) are competing objectives.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “There remains a trade‑off between… fidelity vs. consistency.”  
  - [section 4.1] Figure 4 plots fiber loss vs. KL‑divergence; NDTM has lowest fiber loss but slightly higher KL in OOD settings, while conditional diffusion/flow‑matching models have lower KL (better consistency) but higher fiber loss.  
  - [section 3.1] Fidelity and consistency defined as independent criteria.  
  - [corpus] Weak; neighbors discuss LLM self‑awareness.  
- **Break condition:** For bias auditing requiring statistically reliable estimates of the fiber distribution, high consistency is essential and NDTM’s slight consistency loss may be unacceptable. Conversely, for quick validation where identifying any off‑fiber behavior is key, NDTM’s high fidelity suffices.

## Foundational Learning

### Representation Fibers (Invariant Sets)
- **Why needed here:** The entire method revolves around sampling from the set \(\phi^{-1}(h) = \{x : \phi(x) = h\}\), which defines everything the subject model \(\phi\) is invariant to. Understanding this equivalence class is prerequisite to analyzing what the model does *not* encode.
- **Quick check question:** Given a trained classifier and an input image, what does it mean for another image to be in the same fiber? How would you test if two images are in the same fiber?

### Diffusion Models and Tweedie's Formula
- **Why needed here:** NDTM relies on a pretrained diffusion/flow‑matching model as a prior and uses Tweedie's formula \(\mathbb{E}[x_0|x_t] = x_t + \sigma_t^2 \nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t)\) to approximate the denoised sample from the noisy state \(x_t\). This allows computing a proxy for the final representation during the denoising process.
- **Quick check question:** In a diffusion model, what does the score function \(\nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t)\) represent, and why does adding \(\sigma_t^2\) times the score to \(x_t\) give an estimate of the clean image?

### Fidelity vs. Consistency Trade‑off
- **Why needed here:** Evaluating invariant sampling requires distinguishing between how *close* samples are to the exact fiber (fidelity, measured by fiber loss) and how *realistic/diverse* they are according to the underlying data distribution (consistency, measured by FID/KL). This trade‑off dictates method choice.
- **Quick check question:** If you achieve very low fiber loss but the generated images look unnatural or have low diversity, which aspect (fidelity or consistency) is likely optimized at the expense of the other?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
1. **Subject Model (\(\phi\))**: The model whose invariances are being analyzed (e.g., DINOv2 backbone, ResNet50, Qwen‑2B vision encoder).  
2. **Fiber Loss Calculator**: Computes \(\|\phi(x) - \phi(\tilde{x})\|^2_2\) between a target representation \(h = \phi(x)\) and a candidate sample \(\tilde{x}\)’s representation.  
3. **Pretrained Generative Prior**: An unconditional diffusion or flow‑matching model (e.g., ImageNet‑trained diffusion) that provides the base sampling distribution \(p(x)\).  
4. **NDTM Guidance Loop**: At each denoising step:  
   - Compute Tweedie estimate \(\hat{x}_0 = \mathbb{E}[x_0|x_t]\).  
   - Compute terminal loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{terminal}} = \|\phi(\hat{x}_0) - \phi(\mathbb{E}[x_0|x'_t])\|^2_2\) using a noised version \(x'_t\) of the original query.  
   - Optimize correction \(u_t\) via gradient descent on \(\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{terminal}} + \kappa_t \|u_t\|^2 + \tau_t \|\nabla \log p\|\) difference.  
   - Apply correction: \(\hat{x}_t = x_t + \gamma_t u_t\), then denoise to \(x_{t-1}\).  
5. **(Optional) Fiber Model**: A conditional generative model trained from scratch on \(p(x|h)\), used as a high‑consistency but costly baseline.

### Critical path
For a query image \(x\) and subject model \(\phi\):
1. Compute target representation \(h = \phi(x)\).  
2. Initialize noise \(x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\).  
3. For \(t = T\) down to 1:  
   a. Compute \(x'_t\) by noising \(x\) to timestep \(t\).  
   b. Obtain Tweedie estimate(s) from current \(x_t\).  
   c. Set \(h_{\text{target}} = \phi(\mathbb{E}[x_0|x'_t])\).  
   d. Initialize \(u_t=0\) and run \(N\) gradient descent steps to minimize \(\| \phi(\hat{x}_0) - h_{\text{target}} \|^2 + \kappa_t \|u_t\|^2\).  
   e. Apply correction: \(\hat{x}_t = x_t + \gamma_t u_t\).  
   f. Obtain \(x_{t-1} = \text{DenoisingStep}(\hat{x}_t)\).  
4. Output \(x_0\) as the invariant sample.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Training vs. Inference Cost:** Conditional fiber models require days of training per subject model but have fast per‑sample inference. NDTM has zero training cost but requires iterative optimization (seconds to minutes per sample).  
- **Fidelity vs. Consistency:** NDTM achieves high fidelity (low fiber loss) while conditional diffusion models achieve better consistency (lower KL/FID). The trade‑off is controlled by \(\lambda\) (regularization strength) or \(\gamma_t\) (guidance strength).  
- **Prior Mismatch:** Using a diffusion model trained on a different distribution reduces consistency; the modified terminal loss (using \(x'_t\)) helps maintain stability.

### Failure signatures
- **Oversaturation/Artifacts:** Images with abnormal colors or textures indicate over‑aggressive guidance or prior mismatch. Mitigate with the modified terminal loss.  
- **High Fiber Loss:** No improvement over baselines; likely insufficient guidance strength \(\gamma_t\), too few optimization steps \(N\), or a subject model whose feature space isn’t aligned with the diffusion prior.  
- **Loss of Diversity:** All fiber samples for different queries look similar; over‑regularization (high \(\lambda\) or \(\kappa_t\)) or prior collapse onto a single mode.  
- **Inconsistent Invariance:** Fiber loss varies widely across samples from the same query; unstable guidance or poorly tuned hyperparameter schedules.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Color MNIST Fidelity‑Consistency Benchmark:** Train/guide conditional models (NF, FFF, DIFF/FM) and use NDTM on a synthetic dataset where the true fiber and distribution are analytically known. Measure fiber loss (fidelity) and KL‑divergence of color distribution (consistency) across regularization/guidance strengths.  
2. **ImageNet Known Bias Recovery:** Use NDTM with an ImageNet diffusion prior to sample fibers of DINOv2 and InceptionV3. Verify that DINOv2 fibers preserve orientation/position (lack of augmentation invariance) and that InceptionV3 fibers reveal FID blind spots (generator errors that align with the metric’s fiber).  
3. **Medical Case Failure Detection:** Apply NDTM to the Qwen‑2B vision encoder on situs inversus X‑rays. Check if fiber samples retain the atypical heart position. If not, it indicates the encoder discards critical positional information, explaining downstream VLM failures.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** At what computational budget does NDTM guidance become less efficient than training a conditional fiber model?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors explicitly state: “In this work we do not encounter the point at which guidance becomes unfavorable.”
- **Why unresolved:** No systematic study identifies the break‑even point across model scales, fiber dimensions, or sample volumes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A benchmark measuring compute (time, memory) vs. fidelity and consistency for different configurations, mapping the cost‑fidelity frontier.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can the fidelity–consistency trade‑off be overcome so that NDTM’s efficiency and a conditional model’s distributional accuracy are jointly achieved?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper identifies fidelity vs. consistency as a core trade‑off and suggests “combine NDTM with conditional models” as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** No concrete hybrid architecture or training procedure has been proposed or evaluated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and empirical validation of a hybrid approach (e.g., distilling NDTM guidance into a conditional network, or using NDTM for initialization) on benchmarks like color MNIST and ImageNet, reporting both fiber loss and distribution metrics.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How robust is NDTM to mismatches between the pretrained diffusion prior’s data distribution and the subject model’s domain, and how does prior coverage determine fiber sampling success?
- **Basis in paper:** One OOD scenario (color MNIST) shows a consistency drop, but the general impact of prior support or domain gap is not analyzed.
- **Why unresolved:** All experiments use a single ImageNet diffusion model; it is unclear whether NDTM fails for subject models whose invariances lie outside the prior’s manifold.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments with diffusion models trained on varied datasets (e.g., medical, synthetic, mixed); evaluation of fibers for subject models with known invariances; analysis of failure cases when prior lacks critical modes.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter schedules (\(\gamma_t\), \(\kappa_t\)) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- Guidance stability depends on diffusion prior alignment; performance may degrade with out‑of‑distribution data despite the modified terminal loss.
- Corpus evidence is weak; claims lack cross‑validation from related work on model invariances or LLM knowledge boundaries.

## Confidence
- **Core mechanisms** (fiber loss regularization improves fidelity, NDTM enables training‑free sampling, fidelity‑consistency trade‑off): **High**.
- **Practical deployment** (due to missing hyperparameter details and potential sensitivity to tuning): **Medium**.
