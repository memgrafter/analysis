# Updating Parametric Knowledge with Context Distillation Retains Post‑Training Capabilities

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.16093
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16093
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: DiSC achieves better domain adaptation than fine‑tuning while mitigating catastrophic forgetting of instruction‑following and reasoning abilities, with robustness to learning rate variations.

## Executive Summary
Post‑trained language models require continual knowledge updates from new documents, but standard fine‑tuning causes catastrophic forgetting of hard‑earned skills like instruction‑following and reasoning. DiSC (Distillation via Split Contexts) solves this by using context distillation: each document is split into a prefix and suffix, and the student model is trained to match the teacher model’s distribution over the suffix given the prefix, but without conditioning on the prefix. This encourages the student to internalize the new knowledge while retaining the teacher’s capabilities. Across four models and two domains (KUP, BioASQ), DiSC matches or exceeds fine‑tuning’s adaptation gains (e.g., 44.18 vs 40.90 on KUP) while drastically reducing forgetting (e.g., IFEval drop: −2.4 vs −14.87). The method is efficient (two forward passes per batch) and robust to learning‑rate choices.

## Method Summary
DiSC treats a frozen post‑trained model as a teacher and trains a student initialized from the same checkpoint. For each training document, multiple split points at sentence boundaries generate prefix‑suffix pairs. The teacher processes the full document and extracts the conditional distribution over the suffix given the prefix. The student receives only the suffix tokens (concatenated across splits with attention masking) and outputs an unconditioned distribution. The loss is the average KL divergence between teacher and student suffix distributions, forcing the student to absorb the information the teacher used from the prefix. Implementation details: k=5 splits per document, temperature T=2, one epoch of AdamW (weight decay 0.01), and a learning‑rate sweep (5e‑7–2e‑5). The student’s concatenated forward pass yields all unconditioned distributions in one pass, so only two forward passes per batch are needed. Baselines include standard fine‑tuning and several variants (KL‑regularized FT, LoRA, Rephrase, TALR, context‑distillation).

## Key Results
- On KUP, DiSC raises QA accuracy to 44.18 for Qwen‑2.5‑7B‑Instruct, surpassing fine‑tuning’s 40.90.
- Forgetting on instruction‑following (IFEval) is limited: DiSC’s drop is only 2.4 points vs fine‑tuning’s 14.87‑point drop.
- DiSC exhibits greater robustness to learning rate variations, maintaining a favorable adaptation‑forgetting trade‑off across a wide range.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Internalization via Conditioned‑Unconditioned Matching
DiSC internalizes new knowledge while preserving capabilities by minimizing KL(P_teacher(suffix|prefix) ∥ P_student(suffix)). The student is forced to absorb the prefix information the teacher relies on, effectively embedding the new domain facts without losing general skills. The core assumption is that the teacher’s conditional distribution encodes both the new knowledge and the underlying abilities; replicating it unconditionally implies successful learning and retention. Evidence includes the loss formulation (Eq. 3) and related context‑distillation work.

### Mechanism 2: Computational Efficiency
The teacher makes one forward pass on the full document to extract conditioned suffix distributions. The student processes all suffixes simultaneously by concatenating them and applying attention masking so tokens from different suffixes do not interact, yielding all unconditioned distributions in a single pass. This totals two forward passes per batch, avoiding expensive generation steps. The approach assumes suffix conditional independence and that masking prevents interference; details appear in Section 3.3 and Figure 1.

### Mechanism 3: Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting
DiSC’s KL objective regularizes the student, keeping it close to the frozen teacher that holds the desired post‑training capabilities. This prevents large parameter updates that would erase those skills, unlike standard fine‑tuning which directly optimizes next‑token prediction and drifts toward pretrained statistics. Matching suffix distributions is sufficient to preserve overall behavior. Evidence: Table 3 shows smaller drops on IFEval/MATH/HumanEval; Section 6.1 reveals DiSC breaks the correlation between post‑training gains and subsequent degradation seen under fine‑tuning.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Document splitter → Teacher (frozen) → Student (trainable) → Loss (KL divergence) → Optimizer (AdamW)

**Critical path**:
1. For each document in a batch, sample k split points (sentence boundaries) to create prefix‑suffix pairs.
2. Single forward pass of the frozen teacher on the full document; suffix logits are extracted (temperature T=2).
3. Concatenate all suffixes into one sequence, apply attention masking so tokens from different suffixes do not interact, and perform a single forward pass of the student to extract unconditioned suffix logits.
4. Compute the average KL divergence over suffixes.
5. Backpropagate and update student parameters.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *k* (split points per document): more splits provide more training signal but increase compute and memory.
- Learning rate: DiSC is more robust than fine‑tuning, but high LRs still cause forgetting; a sweep is recommended.
- Document length: long documents may cause the concatenated student sequence to exceed memory limits, reducing efficiency.
- Teacher‑student architecture: requires identical models; no extra parameters but must store two forward activations.

**Failure signatures**:
- High KL loss after training indicates the student failed to internalize knowledge.
- Large drops on post‑training benchmarks (IFEval, Math, Code) signal forgetting.
- No improvement on domain QA suggests suffixes are not informative or splitting strategy is poor.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Sanity check for internalization potential: compute average KL between teacher’s conditioned and unconditioned suffix distributions on held‑out data; a significant gap indicates learning potential.
2. Baseline comparison: run DiSC and fine‑tuning on a single model (e.g., Qwen‑2.5‑7B‑Instruct) with KUP; compare adaptation (KUP accuracy) and forgetting (IFEval/Math/Code deltas).
3. Learning rate sweep: train DiSC with multiple LRs (5e‑7 to 2e‑5) and plot adaptation vs. forgetting; verify DiSC’s curve is flatter than fine‑tuning’s.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **KL‑forgetting relationship**: What is the relationship between average per‑token KL divergence (between initial and adapted models on training data) and catastrophic forgetting across diverse models, domains, and tasks? The paper found mixed correlations on only two models and two domains, leaving it unresolved.
2. **Scalability**: Does DiSC maintain its strong adaptation‑retention trade‑off when scaled to much larger language models (e.g., 13B, 70B)? Experiments were limited to ≤8B parameters.
3. **Domain robustness**: How robust is DiSC to variations in corpus domain and format (e.g., legal documents, codebases, multilingual content)? Only news and biomedical domains were tested.
4. **Alignment preservation**: Does DiSC preserve post‑training alignment aspects beyond instruction‑following and reasoning, such as safety mitigations, bias, or multi‑turn conversational quality? These were not evaluated.

## Limitations
- Raw KUP and BioASQ corpora may be unavailable publicly, and exact preprocessing (tokenization, sentence splitting) is unspecified, hindering reproducibility.
- Hyperparameters for some baselines (TALR, Rephrase, KL‑regularized FT) are incomplete, requiring additional tuning to match paper numbers.
- BioASQ evaluation depends on GPT‑5 for distractor generation; substituting with other models may alter difficulty and scores.

## Confidence
- **Adaptation‑forgetting trade‑off**: High (consistent improvements across four models and two domains).
- **Mechanism (KL internalization)**: Medium (theoretical foundation and correlation evidence, but limited direct probing).
- **Efficiency (two forward passes)**: Medium (method is well‑described but memory constraints for long documents may reduce advantage).

## Next Checks

2. Conduct an ablation over split point count (*k*) and splitting strategy (sentence vs. random tokens) to confirm robustness of the chosen configuration (k=5, sentence boundaries).
