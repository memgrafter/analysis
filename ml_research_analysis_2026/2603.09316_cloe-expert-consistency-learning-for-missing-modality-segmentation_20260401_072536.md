# CLoE: Expert Consistency Learning for Missing Modality Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.09316
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09316
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: Achieves SOTA average Dice on BraTS 2020 (WT: 88.09%, TC: 80.23%, ET: 65.06%) across 15 missing-modality patterns; improves MSD Prostate by 0.53%.

## Executive Summary
CLoE addresses robust multimodal medical segmentation when subsets of modalities are missing at inference. It introduces Expert Consistency Learning (ECL) to align modality-specific experts globally (Modality Expert Consistency) and on foreground regions (Region Expert Consistency). A gating network then maps consistency scores to reliability weights for adaptive feature fusion. On BraTS 2020, CLoE outperforms prior methods on all tumor subregions under diverse missing-modality scenarios and shows gains on MSD Prostate. Ablations confirm that REC is critical for preserving small critical structures, and the gating mechanism substantially improves robustness.

## Method Summary
CLoE employs parallel modality encoders and a shared expert decoder to produce per-modality segmentation predictions. The ECL loss comprises two terms: MEC penalizes pairwise disagreement among probability maps via cosine similarity across the entire volume; REC reweights these similarities using a lightweight region map derived from shallow features to focus on clinically relevant foreground. A gating network consumes per-expert consistency scores (global and regional) and outputs softmax weights for each available expert. These weights fuse multi-scale features before a final fusion decoder generates the segmentation. The total loss combines segmentation losses on individual experts and the fused output, plus a contrastive representation regularizer. This design encourages experts to converge to consensus predictions that remain reliable even with missing modalities.

## Key Results
- BraTS 2020: average Dice 88.09% (whole tumor), 80.23% (tumor core), 65.06% (enhancing tumor) across 15 missing-modality patterns, surpassing DC-Seg and M³AE.
- MSD Prostate: improves average Dice by 0.53% over DC-Seg.
- Ablation: removing REC reduces enhancing tumor Dice by 3.41%, underscoring its importance for small structures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Modality Expert Consistency (MEC) for Global Drift Suppression
- **Claim:** Enforcing global agreement among available modality experts reduces case-wise prediction drift caused by missing modalities.
- **Mechanism:** For available experts A, pairwise cosine similarity S(p^(a), p^(b)) between probability maps p is computed. Loss L_MEC = 1/|P| Σ (1 - S) penalizes disagreement, encouraging semantically aligned outputs regardless of which inputs are present.
- **Core assumption:** Agreement across the whole volume (including background) prevents catastrophic divergence among experts trained on different modality subsets.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Modality Expert Consistency (MEC) enforces global agreementamong expert predictions to reduce case-wise drift under partial inputs." [section 2.2] Equations (3) define L_MEC with vectorized predictions and cosine similarity. [corpus] Neighbor papers (Flex-MoE, Hierarchical MoE) explore expert agreement, but CLoE's global drift suppression is internal to the paper.
- **Break condition:** Extremely heterogeneous missing patterns (e.g., all but one modality missing across training cases) may cause MEC to suppress valid modality-specific signals, leading to over-smoothed segmentations.

### Mechanism 2: Region Expert Consistency (REC) for Foreground-Critical Alignment
- **Claim:** Emphasizing consistency on clinically relevant foreground prevents global loss from being dominated by uninformative background.
- **Mechanism:** A lightweight region map r (from averaged shallow features via projection head π and sigmoid σ) weights predictions: p_r = vec(r ⊙ p). L_REC applies the same pairwise cosine penalty on region-weighted vectors, amplifying gradients on foreground voxels.
- **Core assumption:** Background vastly outnumbers foreground; without REC, L_MEC would align experts poorly on small critical structures.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Region Expert Consistency (REC) emphasizes agreement on clinically critical foreground regions..." [section 2.2] Equations (4,5) define r and L_REC. Ablation (Table 3) shows removing REC causes a 3.41% Dice drop for enhancing tumor vs. smaller drops for larger regions.
- **Break condition:** Poorly calibrated r (e.g., due to insufficient data or failed π) could mis-weight L_REC, either over-emphasizing false positives or under-emphasizing true foreground, degrading segmentation.

### Mechanism 3: Consistency-Driven Gating for Reliability-Aware Fusion
- **Claim:** Mapping per-expert consistency scores (global u_m and regional v_m) to fusion weights via gating network G dynamically downweights unreliable experts.
- **Mechanism:** For each available expert m, compute u_m (average cosine similarity of p^(m) with others) and v_m (same for p_r^(m)). G maps (u_m, v_m) to logit g_m; softmax over A yields weights w_m used for element-wise fusion: f_ℓ = Σ w_m ⊙ f^(m)_ℓ.
- **Core assumption:** Consistency with peers indicates robustness of the expert’s modality-independent representation under the current missing pattern.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We further map consistency scores to modality reliability weights using a lightweight gating network..." [section 2.3] Equations (6,7) define u_m, v_m, G, and w_m. Ablation (Table 3) shows removing "Weight Fusion" drops average Dice by -2.47% and ET by -3.96%.
- **Break condition:** If all experts agree on a wrong segmentation (systematic failure or adversarial pattern), gating may assign high weights to all, amplifying error. Consensus does not guarantee correctness under distribution shift.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Decision-level consistency control**  
Why needed here: The core problem is unstable decisions (segmentations) from individual modality experts when inputs are incomplete. CLoE operates at the prediction level to directly align final decisions before fusion.  
Quick check question: If consistency is enforced at the feature level instead of the prediction level, what specific risks arise for missing-modality scenarios (e.g., feature space misalignment due to missing modalities)?

**Concept: Background dominance in volumetric segmentation**  
Why needed here: In 3D medical volumes, background voxels can exceed 99% of all pixels. A loss averaged over all voxels is dominated by background, causing the model to ignore small foreground structures.  
Quick check question: How would the MEC loss behave on BraTS "enhancing tumor" if REC were not used, given this class typically occupies a tiny volume fraction?

**Concept: Reliability-aware dynamic fusion**  
Why needed here: Fixed fusion rules (e.g., average, SE attention) fail when some inputs are missing. The gating network uses a derived signal (consensus) to infer reliability, rather than raw input presence or feature statistics that can be misleading.  
Quick check question: Why might a standard Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) block fail as the gating mechanism for missing modalities?

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map (processing stages):
1. Modality Encoders (Φ_m) → Multi-scale features {f^(m)_ℓ}
2. Expert Decoder (D_sep) → Individual predictions {p^(m)}
3. Consistency Module → LMEC, region map r, LREC
4. Gating Network (G) → weights {w_m} from (u_m, v_m)
5. Feature Fusion → fused features {f_ℓ = Σ w_m ⊙ f^(m)_ℓ}
6. Fusion Decoder (D_fuse) → final segmentation p

Critical path:
1. Forward pass through all Φ_m to get f^(m)_ℓ.
2. Each f^(m)_L → D_sep → p^(m).
3. Compute LMEC and LREC from all p^(m) and f^(m)_1.
4. Compute u_m, v_m → G → w_m.
5. Fuse f^(m)_ℓ using w_m → f_ℓ → D_fuse → p.
6. Compute L_ECL on p^(m), L_seg on p, and L_contrast on latent codes.
7. Sum L_total = L_seg + αL_ECL + βL_contrast and backpropagate.

Design tradeoffs:
- Dual-branch consistency cost vs. gain: Computing LMEC and LREC adds forward/backward passes over expert predictions. The ablation shows benefit, but for many modalities (M) the pairwise computation scales quadratically; paper uses M=4 (BraTS) which is manageable.
- Region map r simplicity vs. accuracy: r is derived from a single shallow feature projection. A more complex region detector could be more accurate but would increase parameters and risk overfitting, especially on small datasets like MSD Prostate (48 cases).
- Gating on consistency vs. input: Using consistency scores (u_m, v_m) is more robust than binary availability flags s_m, but introduces dependency on current expert predictions that may be noisy early in training; the training schedule implicitly handles this.

Failure signatures:
- Symptoms: Sudden drop in Dice for small foreground classes (e.g., ET in BraTS) while large class performance stays stable; L_REC may plateau near zero. Likely cause: region map r became uninformative (e.g., constant), disabling REC’s foreground focus. Check histogram of r values on a validation batch.
- Symptoms: High variance across missing-modality patterns, especially when only one strong modality (e.g., FLAIR) is present. Likely cause: gating network G failed to learn mapping from consistency to reliability, possibly because L_ECL weight η is too low, leading to weak consensus and unstable gradients.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Isolate Consistency Module:** Train with α=0 (no L_ECL) but keep gating and fusion. Measure expert disagreement (1 - S(p^(a), p^(b))) on a missing-modality validation set. Expect high disagreement. Then enable L_ECL and verify disagreement drops significantly, especially for the weakest expert in a pattern.
2. **Validate Region Map r:** Visualize r (after sigmoid) overlaid on input modalities for cases with small tumors. A successful r should activate strongly on enhancing tumor regions in BraTS ET cases. Quantify the average r value inside ground-truth ET mask vs. outside.
3. **Test Gating Network Behavior:** For a specific missing pattern (e.g., only T2 available), record the weights w_m assigned to the available expert vs. missing experts (which get w_m=0). Also, for a pattern with 3 modalities available, check if the expert whose input modality is most informative for the case (e.g., FLAIR for edema) consistently receives a higher weight. Use a small set of manually inspected cases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the design of the region map r in REC affect segmentation robustness on small critical structures across diverse medical datasets with varying anatomy?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The region map is derived from aggregated shallow features via a projection head (Eq. 4), but no alternative designs or dataset-specific adaptations are explored.
- **Why unresolved:** The effectiveness of this feature-based region map for different clinical targets (e.g., prostate zones vs. tumor subregions) is not validated; it may not generalize to structures with weak appearance contrasts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark CLoE with region maps computed from different network layers, ground-truth region masks, or learnable region proposals on multiple datasets, measuring Dice changes for small foreground regions.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the isolated contribution of the contrastive representation loss (L_contrast) to CLoE's robustness under missing modalities, and is it synergistic or redundant with consistency learning?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] L_contrast is part of the total objective but not separately ablated in Table 3, which only tests MEC, REC, gating, and fusion components.
- **Why unresolved:** Without isolating L_contrast, it is unclear whether disentanglement via contrastive learning adds value beyond decision-level consistency control, especially under partial inputs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct an ablation removing only L_contrast while keeping L_ECL and L_seg fixed, evaluating average Dice across all 15 BraTS missing-modality patterns and on Prostate, with statistical significance tests.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does CLoE's consistency-driven framework generalize to medical segmentation tasks with modality sets beyond BraTS (4) and Prostate (2), such as datasets with more modalities or different imaging contrasts (e.g., CT-PET-MRI)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Experiments are limited to BraTS 2020 and MSD Prostate as stated in Section 3, with no cross-dataset validation on other multimodal benchmarks.
- **Why unresolved:** The method's robustness may depend on modality-specific characteristics (e.g., MRI contrast variations); performance on other modalities or multimodal combinations remains unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate CLoE on additional public datasets like IXI (multi-contrast MRI) or ADNI (MRI-PET), comparing SOTA methods across missing-modality patterns and reporting generalization gaps.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does the computational complexity and effectiveness of Expert Consistency Learning scale with an increasing number of modalities, particularly for high-dimensional pairwise consistency calculations?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] MEC and REC involve O(|A|^2) pairwise cosine similarities (Eqs. 3,5), but testing is capped at |A|≤4; gating network inputs (u_m, v_m) also scale with modality count.
- **Why unresolved:** For applications with many modalities (e.g., multiphoton microscopy or multi-parametric imaging), the consistency loss may become a bottleneck, and reliability weighting might degrade due to sparse agreement.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Simulate missing-modality scenarios with 5–8 modalities using BraTS data by duplicating channels, measure training time, GPU memory, and Dice scores, and analyze gating weight entropy to assess reliability calibration.

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameters (α, β, η, λ_rec) and contrastive loss implementation details impede exact reproduction.
- Underspecified architecture for projection head π and gating network G (layer counts, channel sizes) requires design choices.
- Risk that the region map r becomes uninformative (e.g., constant), which would cripple REC’s foreground-focusing effect.

## Confidence
- SOTA performance: High (reported numbers surpass prior methods on BraTS and Prostate).
- MEC for drift suppression: Medium (ablation shows full framework benefit but MEC alone not isolated).
- REC for foreground alignment: High (ablation: ET Dice drops 3.41% without REC).
- Gating for reliable fusion: High (ablation: avg Dice -2.47%, ET -3.96% without weight fusion).
