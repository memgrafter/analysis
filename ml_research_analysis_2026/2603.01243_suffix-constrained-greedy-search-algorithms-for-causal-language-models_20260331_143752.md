# Suffix-Constrained Greedy Search Algorithms for Causal Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01243
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01243
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Suffix-constrained generation algorithms (greedy pipeline, bifurcation penalty) match or improve accuracy over unconstrained decoding while guaranteeing trivially parseable final answers.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces suffix-constrained generation, a method to enforce a strict template on the final answer suffix of LLM outputs while allowing free-form reasoning in the prefix. The authors propose greedy-based algorithms—greedy pipeline and bifurcation penalty—that maintain at most two hypotheses. Experiments on OLMo 2 models across five QA datasets show consistent accuracy improvements and full termination within budget, solving the problem of malformed or unextractable answers from standard decoding.

## Method Summary
The core approach defines a grammar G for the answer suffix and constrains generation to sequences where the suffix appears exactly once at the end. Three algorithms are proposed: (1) Greedy pipeline first runs unconstrained greedy, discards the last token, then applies constrained greedy to generate the suffix. (2) Constrained hypothesis beam maintains two hypotheses (unconstrained and constrained) and replaces the constrained one when its score lags. (3) Bifurcation penalty computes a deviation penalty between the model's top token and the top valid suffix-start token; the constrained hypothesis is updated when this penalty improves. All operate with beam width ≤2, minimizing overhead.

## Key Results
- On GSM8K with OLMo 2 13B instruction-tuned, bifurcation penalty (last hypothesis) achieves 87.7% vs 84.9% unconstrained.
- On ARC-Challenge with OLMo 2 1B pre-trained, accuracy rises from 38.3% to 44.7%.
- All bifurcation variants complete within the generation budget and produce well-formed outputs; unconstrained and other methods risk overflow or misformatting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Suffix constraint guarantees parseable answers
By defining a grammar G for the answer format (e.g., "The answer is: <number>"), the generation space is restricted to V* × lang(G) × {⊥}. This forces the answer to appear exactly once at the end, making extraction trivial. The LLM can reason freely in the prefix, and the constraint only activates near the end. Evidence: Section 4 defines Y→G = lang(→G)×{⊥}. Break condition: if reasoning prefix diverges, suffix may be incorrect; pre-trained models may not recognize ⊥.

### Mechanism 2: Greedy pipeline decouples reasoning and answer
First, standard greedy generates a full response y. The last token is removed to form a prefix x′. Then constrained greedy on grammar G produces a suffix y′ that strictly follows the template. Concatenating gives the final output. Assumes initial reasoning is sufficient and not too long. Evidence: Section 4.2 describes the two-phase process. Break condition: initial greedy may exceed budget or produce poor reasoning.

### Mechanism 3: Bifurcation penalty identifies natural switch point
Two hypotheses are tracked: greedy y and constrained r⊗a (with r from earlier greedy state). At each step, compute deviation Δ = max_v p(v|y) - max_{v∈next_G(ϵ)} p(v|y). If Δ is lower than current constrained Δ, replace constrained hypothesis with r=y and a=argmax valid token. Continue until constrained hypothesis ends with ⊥. Return last or min-Δ hypothesis. Assumes lower Δ indicates a natural bifurcation. Evidence: Section 5.2 defines penalty and replacement rule; Section 6.2 shows last hypothesis solves failures. Break condition: flat distributions or multiple valid starts may mislead; frequent replacement may cause lag.

- **Constrained generation via prefix validation**: Using a grammar to compute valid next tokens (next_G(y)) ensuring the prefix can be extended to a valid full sequence. *Why needed*: Core to suffix-constrained algorithms. *Quick check*: "Given a grammar G, what does next_G(y) represent and how is it used in constrained decoding?"
- **Beam search hypothesis management**: Concept of maintaining multiple decoding hypotheses and scoring them. *Why needed*: Bifurcation and constrained beam use a beam of width two. *Quick check*: "How does a beam search of width 2 differ from pure greedy search, and what additional bookkeeping is required?"

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: LLM (p(v|context)) -> Grammar (defines next_G) -> Greedy state tracker (prefix y) -> Constrained hypothesis tracker (r, a, Δ) -> Termination detector (checks a ∈ lang(G) and ⊥) -> Selection logic (last vs min penalty)

Critical path: Initialize empty hypotheses. For each step: compute logits for greedy prefix and constrained prefix (if exists). Compute deviation penalty if applicable. Decide whether to replace constrained hypothesis. Append next token to greedy and possibly constrained. After step, if constrained ends with ⊥, terminate and return best finished hypothesis.

Design tradeoffs: Greedy pipeline is simple but may overflow budget if unconstrained phase too long; bifurcation penalty guarantees budget but requires two states and penalty computation. Constrained beam less stable due to frequent interruptions. Last hypothesis vs min penalty affects output length.

Failure signatures: (1) Constrained hypothesis repeatedly replaced (high entropy difference), leading to non-termination. (2) Outputs ending without ⊥ within budget. (3) Extracted answer mismatches expected pattern. (4) Tokenization mismatch between grammar and token boundaries. (5) Unbalanced braces in LaTeX if regex-only.

First 3 experiments: 1) Reproduce unconstrained greedy baseline on GSM8K to observe formatting failures. 2) Implement greedy pipeline and verify 100% termination with parseable answers. 3) Implement bifurcation penalty (last hypothesis) and compare accuracy/termination against baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can suffix-constrained generation be extended to beam widths greater than two while preserving output guarantees and termination? (Basis: algorithms use ≤2 hypotheses; larger beams unexplored.)
- Do the methods generalize to model families beyond OLMo 2 (e.g., LLaMA, GPT) and larger scales? (Basis: experiments only on OLMo 2 1B/13B.)
- Under what conditions does “constrained only” (generating only answer suffix, omitting reasoning) outperform full suffix-constrained generation? (Basis: Table 2 shows higher accuracy for constrained only on some tasks; no explanation.)

## Limitations
- Unknown generation budget (max_new_tokens) affects termination and overflow rates.
- Prompt format ambiguity (e.g., whether to include “Let’s think step by step” for math tasks).
- Unspecified grammar for balanced braces in MATH500 and answer normalization for LaTeX.
- Unclear EOS vs. ⊥ token handling and integration with next_G.
