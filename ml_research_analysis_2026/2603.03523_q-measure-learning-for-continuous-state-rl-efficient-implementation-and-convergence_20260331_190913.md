# Q-Measure-Learning for Continuous State RL: Efficient Implementation and Convergence
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03523
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03523
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Learns a signed empirical measure on visited state-action pairs and reconstructs Q via kernel integration; achieves O(n) memory/computation and almost sure sup-norm convergence under uniform ergodicity.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs with continuous state spaces from a single trajectory typically requires infinite-dimensional function approximation. This paper introduces Q-Measure-Learning, which avoids direct function estimation by maintaining a weighted empirical measure over visited (state,action) pairs and reconstructing the Q-function through kernel integration. The stationary distribution of the behavior chain and the Q-measure are jointly estimated via coupled stochastic approximation, yielding an algorithm with linear memory and per-step computation. Theoretical analysis proves almost sure sup-norm convergence to a kernel-smoothed Bellman fixed point under uniform ergodicity, and experiments on a two-item inventory task demonstrate empirical effectiveness.

## Method Summary
The algorithm stores all visited state-action pairs \(Z_k = (X_k, A_k)\) and maintains two weight sequences: \(\{u_k\}\) for an empirical measure \(\mu_n = \sum_{k=0}^n u_k \delta_{Z_k}\) approximating the stationary distribution of the behavior chain, and \(\{W_k\}\) for a signed Q-measure \(\nu_n = \sum_{k=1}^n W_k \delta_{Z_{k-1}}\). At iteration \(n\), the Q-estimate is reconstructed as \(q_n(z) = \frac{\int \kappa(z,u) \nu_n(du)}{\int \kappa(z,u) \mu_n(du)}\), where \(\kappa\) is a kernel with bandwidth \(\sigma\). After observing a new transition \((R_{n+1}, X_{n+1}, A_{n+1})\), a TD target \(Y_{n+1} = R_{n+1} + \gamma \max_a q_n(X_{n+1}, a)\) is computed. The weights are updated by scaling (diminishing stepsizes \(\alpha_n, \beta_n\)) and appending: \(\mu_{n+1} = (1-\beta_{n+1})\mu_n + \beta_{n+1}\delta_{Z_{n+1}}\), \(\nu_{n+1} = (1-\alpha_{n+1})\nu_n + \alpha_{n+1} Y_{n+1} \delta_{Z_n}\). This reduces the infinite-dimensional learning problem to managing finite weight vectors.

## Key Results
- Almost sure sup-norm convergence of \(q_n\) to the fixed point of a kernel-smoothed Bellman operator under uniform ergodicity of the behavior chain.
- Approximation error bound \(\|Q^* - q^*\| \leq \frac{L_Q}{1-\gamma} \xi_{\mu_b}(\sigma)\), with \(\xi_{\mu_b}(\sigma)\) decaying polynomially in \(\sigma\) under smoothness and bounded density assumptions.
- Validation on a two-item inventory control MDP shows learned policies approaching the dynamic programming optimum, with sensitivity to kernel bandwidth.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The optimal Q-function can be consistently approximated by applying a kernel smoothing operator to a signed empirical measure over visited state-action pairs, avoiding infinite-dimensional function estimates.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of maintaining an explicit function, the algorithm keeps a weighted empirical measure \(\nu_n = \sum_{k=1}^n W_k \delta_{Z_{k-1}}\). The Q-estimate is \(q_n(z) = \frac{\int \kappa(z,u) \nu_n(du)}{\int \kappa(z,u) \mu_n(du)}\), where \(\mu_n\) approximates the stationary distribution. This reduces the problem to managing finite weights \(\{W_k, u_k\}\).
- **Core assumption**: There exists a continuous, strictly positive kernel \(\kappa\) and the true \(Q^*\) can be represented as \(q^*_\mu = \int \kappa(z,u) \nu^*_\mu(du) / \int \kappa(z,u) \mu_b(du)\). Uniform ergodicity of the behavior chain ensures \(\mu_n\) tracks \(\mu_b\).
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Definition 1 introduce the representation; Eq. (1.1) defines the \(\Phi\) operator.
- **Break condition**: Kernel bandwidth \(\sigma\) too small causes denominator volatility; too large induces bias. Non-ergodic behavior chain prevents \(\mu_n\) from converging to \(\mu_b\).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Joint stochastic approximation of \(\mu_n\) and \(\nu_n\) with diminishing stepsizes yields almost sure convergence of \(q_n\) to the fixed point of a smoothed Bellman operator.
- **Mechanism**: Updates are \(\mu_{n+1} = (1-\beta_{n+1})\mu_n + \beta_{n+1}\delta_{Z_{n+1}}\) and \(\nu_{n+1} = (1-\alpha_{n+1})\nu_n + \alpha_{n+1} Y_{n+1} \delta_{Z_n}\). The coupling ensures kernel normalization tracks the unknown \(\mu_b\). The system is a coupled SA in the Banach space \(C(\mathcal{Z})\).
- **Core assumption**: The behavior chain is uniformly ergodic with unique stationary \(\mu_b\); stepsizes satisfy \(\sum \alpha_n = \infty, \sum \alpha_n^2 < \infty, \beta_n = 1/(n+1)\); rewards bounded, dynamics continuous.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states joint estimation and convergence; Section 3 defines Algorithm 1; Section 4 proves Theorem 1 via ODE analysis.
- **Break condition**: Uniform ergodicity is essential; multiple invariant sets or non-Harris recurrence cause mis-normalization. Constant stepsizes lead to bias or oscillations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The fixed point \(q^*_\mu\) of the kernel-smoothed Bellman operator \(T^\mu = K^\mu \circ T\) is close to \(Q^*\) when kernel bandwidth \(\sigma\) is small and \(\mu_b\) has a bounded density.
- **Mechanism**: Smoothing operator \(K^\mu\) averages \(T\) about each point using kernel \(\kappa\) w.r.t. \(\mu\). The error \(\|Q^* - q^*_\mu\|\) is bounded by \(\frac{L_Q}{1-\gamma} \xi_{\mu_b}(\sigma)\), where \(\xi_{\mu_b}(\sigma)\) measures local spread of \(\mu_b\) relative to \(\kappa\).
- **Core assumption**: \(Q^*\) is \(\alpha\)-Hölder continuous; \(\mu_b\) has a bounded density w.r.t. Lebesgue; state space satisfies a local volume condition. Kernel \(\kappa\) is universal (e.g., Gaussian).
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 5 states Theorem 2 with explicit rates under density assumptions.
- **Break condition**: If \(\mu_b\) is heavy-tailed, singular, or supported on a lower-dimensional manifold, \(\xi_{\mu_b}(\sigma)\) may not vanish as \(\sigma \to 0\), preventing bias reduction. Low smoothness \(\alpha\) degrades the bound.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Empirical measure convergence for ergodic Markov chains  
  **Why needed**: The algorithm relies on \(\mu_n = \frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^n \delta_{Z_k}\) converging to \(\mu_b\) in the \(D_{\mu_b}\) metric (Proposition 4.2). This convergence ensures the normalization error between \(q_n\) and an auxiliary version vanishes (Lemma 3), key to proving \(q_n \to q^*\).  
  **Quick check**: Given a uniformly ergodic Markov chain, why does \(\mu_n[f] \to \mu_b[f]\) a.s. for every bounded measurable \(f\)? What extra property of the kernel \(\kappa\) ensures convergence in \(D_{\mu_b}\) (i.e., \(\sup_z \frac{|\int \kappa(z,u)(\mu_n-\mu_b)(du)|}{\int \kappa(z,u)\mu_b(du)} \to 0\))?
- **Concept**: Stochastic approximation in Banach spaces  
  **Why needed**: The recursions for \(q_n = \Phi_{\mu_n}[\nu_n]\) live in \(C(\mathcal{Z})\) (continuous functions with sup norm). Convergence proof uses the ODE method (Lemmas 4,5), showing discrete updates track the ODE \(q'(t) = T^\mu[q(t)] - q(t)\). This requires Lipschitz properties of \(T^\mu\) in sup norm.  
  **Quick check**: Why is \(T^\mu\) a \(\gamma\)-contraction in sup norm even though \(K^\mu\) is only non-expansive? How does this guarantee exponential stability of \(q^*\) in the continuous-time ODE?
- **Concept**: Poisson equation for Markov chains  
  **Why needed**: The Markov term \(G_k\) in the error decomposition (4.6) is handled via the Poisson equation, yielding a representation that splits it into a telescoping term and a martingale (Lemma 9). This enables martingale convergence theorems and controls Markov noise.  
  **Quick check**: For a uniformly ergodic chain, what does the solution \(v_f\) to \(v - P_b v = f - \mu_b[f]\) look like? How does the bound \(\|v_f\| \leq \frac{2c}{1-\rho} \|f\|\) help prove that \(\sum G_k\) vanishes as \(n \to \infty\)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input (trajectory stream of \((R_t, X_t, A_t)\)) -> Storage (arrays \(Z[0:n]\), weight arrays \(u[0:n]\) for \(\mu_n\), \(W[1:n]\) for \(\nu_n\)) -> Core operation (for each new \((X_{n+1}, A_{n+1})\), compute \(q_n\) via weighted kernel sums over all stored \(Z_k\); compute TD target \(Y_{n+1}\); update weights by scaling with stepsizes and appending new point).
- **Critical path**:
  1. Theoretical initialization: choose kernel \(\kappa\) (Gaussian), bandwidth \(\sigma\), stepsizes \(\alpha_n \sim 1/n\), \(\beta_n = 1/(n+1)\).
  2. Implementation: initialize \(Z[0] = (X_0, A_0)\), \(u[0]=1\), \(W\) empty.
  3. For \(n=0,1,2,\dots\): receive next \((R_{n+1}, X_{n+1}, A_{n+1})\); compute \(Y_{n+1} = R_{n+1} + \gamma \max_a \Pi(q_n(X_{n+1},a))\); update weights by scaling and appending.
  4. Policy extraction: greedy policy \(\pi_n(x) = \arg\max_a q_n(x,a)\) (for continuous \(A\), approximate via gradient ascent).
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Memory: Stores all past points → \(O(n)\) memory. For long trajectories, this may exhaust memory; budgeted approximations break theory.
  - Computation: Per-step \(O(|A| n)\) for discrete \(A\); total \(O(n^2)\) time after \(n\) steps. Mitigations: parallelize kernel dot-products, use approximate nearest neighbors.
  - Kernel bandwidth \(\sigma\): Small \(\sigma\) reduces bias but amplifies variance via denominator \(c_{\mu_n}(z) = \int \kappa(z,u)\mu_n(du)\); large \(\sigma\) increases bias. Theory requires \(\kappa\) bounded away from zero, so \(\sigma\) cannot be too small relative to data scale.
  - Stepsizes: Must be diminishing; \(\alpha_n \sim 1/(\lambda n)\) with \(\lambda>0\) affects asymptotic bias; typically \(\alpha_n = 1/n\).
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Diverging \(q_n\) values: Check if \(c_{\mu_n}(z)\) near zero for some \(z\). Indicates \(\sigma\) too small or insufficient exploration.
  - Confinement of policy performance: Return plateaus above optimal due to smoothing bias (\(\sigma\) too large) or estimation error. Monitor gap to benchmark.
  - Stepsize mis-specification: Instability or vanishing updates. Track \(\|\nu_{n+1}-\nu_n\|\) or \(q_n\) change; expect decaying updates.
  - Numerical overflow: Very large/small weight sums; check for NaN/Inf; use double precision.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Reproduce two-item inventory experiment with \(\sigma=1\), \(\alpha_n=1/n\), \(\beta_n=1/(n+1)\). Plot estimated return and RMSE vs iteration; verify improvement and gap to DP benchmark.
  2. Ablation on kernel bandwidth: train with \(\sigma \in \{0.5, 1, 2, 5\}\). Compare final policy returns and Q-function RMSE; expect undersmoothing (noisy) for small \(\sigma\), oversmoothing (high bias) for large \(\sigma\).
  3. Exploration check: run with different behavior policies (uniform random, greedy w.r.t. suboptimal policy, \(\epsilon\)-greedy). Monitor empirical stationary distribution \(\mu_n\) (e.g., marginal histograms). Poor coverage degrades estimation in uncovered regions and policy performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Can the \(O(n^2)\) total computational cost be reduced to sub-quadratic while preserving almost-sure sup-norm convergence? The paper reports \(O(n)\) per-iteration cost and \(O(n)\) memory, which together incur quadratic total time. No approximate representations (e.g., coresets) are explored.
- **Open Question 2**: Does the convergence guarantee require uniform ergodicity, or can it be extended to weaker mixing conditions (e.g., geometric ergodicity or \(\psi\)-irreducibility)? Assumption 3 is strong; many practical MDPs may not satisfy it. The paper does not discuss adaptation.
- **Open Question 3**: How should the kernel bandwidth \(\sigma\) be chosen in practice to balance smoothing bias (Theorem 2) and estimation variance? The analysis provides a bias bound but no finite-sample variance bound; experiments fix \(\sigma=1\) arbitrarily without justification.

## Limitations
- Strong uniform ergodicity assumption on behavior policy; many practical policies (e.g., greedy) may violate it, leading to biased estimates.
- \(O(n)\) memory and per-step \(O(n)\) kernel evaluations limit scalability to very long or high-dimensional trajectories.
- Tuning of kernel bandwidth \(\sigma\) and stepsize constant is heuristic; theory offers no principled data-driven selection.
- Approximation error bound unrealistically assumes smooth \(Q^*\) and bounded stationary density; these may not hold in practice.
- Empirical validation restricted to a small two-item inventory task; no tests on standard continuous control benchmarks.

## Confidence
- Convergence to kernel-smoothed fixed point under uniform ergodicity: **High**
- Approximation error bound as function of \(\sigma\): **Medium** (depends on strong smoothness assumptions)
- Practical scalability and performance in complex domains: **Low** (quadratic cost unaddressed)
- Overall claim of “efficient implementation”: **Medium-Low** (efficiency questionable for large \(n\))
