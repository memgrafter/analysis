# Measuring the Redundancy of Decoder Layers in SpeechLLMs
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05121
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05121
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Over 90% of SpeechLLM parameters reside in the LLM decoder; up to 43.8% of decoder layers can be pruned (retaining ~56%) with minimal performance loss on ASR, and similar redundancy transfers to speech translation.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates decoder layer redundancy in Speech Large Language Models (SpeechLLMs), which are dominated by LLM parameters. The authors find that much of the decoder capacity is superfluous, a property largely inherited from the pretrained text-only LLM. They develop an angular distance-based method to identify contiguous redundant layer blocks. Pruning these blocks requires a "healing" step—adapting the receiving layer's MLP via LoRA and fine-tuning the speech-to-LLM projector—to avoid performance collapse. The identified redundant layers transfer across tasks (ASR to AST), speech encoders, and model scales, enabling a single pruned decoder for multi-task use with substantial speed and memory gains.

## Method Summary
The authors quantify redundancy by computing average angular distance between hidden states of adjacent decoder layers on a representative validation set. Lower angular distance for a block of `n` layers starting at position `ℓ` indicates higher redundancy for that block. This defines an optimal pruning path: for each prune fraction, the block with minimal angular distance is removed. After pruning, the model is "healed" by applying LoRA to the MLP of the receiving decoder layer and optionally unfreezing/fine-tuning the speech projector. Performance is evaluated on ASR (LibriSpeech) and AST tasks across Qwen2.5 and Llama models (1–8B parameters).

## Key Results
- Decoder redundancy is inherited from the pretrained LLM: text-only and speech inputs yield nearly identical angular distance heatmaps and pruning paths.
- Larger models (7–8B) tolerate significant pruning: removing up to 43.8% of decoder layers (e.g., Llama3.1-8B: 2.65% → 3.28% WER) stays within a relative WER degradation of 0.25. Smaller models tolerate less.
- Robustness requires joint healing (LoRA on receiving MLP + projector retraining); without healing, performance collapses sharply.
- The same redundant blocks transfer to speech translation, with 32.1% removal preserving BLEU within 10% relative degradation, enabling a single pruned decoder for multi-task SpeechLLMs.
- Pruning 40% of Llama3.1-8B decoder layers yields a 35% wall‑clock speedup and reduces GPU memory from 15.72 GiB to 10.37 GiB.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1 (Inheritance):** Decoder layer redundancy is established during the LLM's text-based pretraining, forming a stable geometric similarity structure between layer outputs. The speech projector adapts to map speech features into this pre-existing representational space without altering the intrinsic inter-layer similarity patterns. Evidence: Angular distance heatmaps are nearly identical for text-only and speech inputs, showing the redundancy blueprint is modality-agnostic.

**Mechanism 2 (Healing Necessity):** Angular similarity indicates representational proximity but not functional compatibility. Removing a contiguous block breaks the computational graph; the receiving layer expects input from its immediate predecessor, not the earlier layer now connected. Healing via LoRA on the receiving MLP provides a small learned correction for this distribution shift. Re-adapting the projector is essential because its mapping was optimized for the unpruned decoder dynamics; pruning changes those dynamics, requiring realignment.

**Mechanism 3 (Global Transferability):** Because redundancy reflects functions superfluous for language modeling in general, the dispensable layers for speech-conditioned tasks (ASR/AST) align with those for text-based tasks. The projector normalizes differences across speech encoders and languages, making the redundancy structure task- and encoder-agnostic. Evidence: ASR-derived pruning paths yield near-identical performance on AST as AST-derived paths.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Angular Distance as Redundancy Proxy**
  - **Why needed:** A low-cost metric to identify geometrically similar (potentially redundant) layer blocks without full retraining. Assumes layers with low angular distance on a distribution are functionally interchangeable.
  - **Quick check:** If two layers have very low angular distance, can they be swapped without any performance loss, even without healing? (No—healing is required, indicating functional incompatibility despite representational similarity.)

- **Concept: Projector Function and Misalignment Risk**
  - **Why needed:** The projector translates speech encoder features into the LLM's embedding space. Its pre-trained state is optimized for the unpruned decoder's expected input distribution. Pruning alters this distribution, causing misalignment that must be corrected.
  - **Quick check:** Why is healing only the decoder's receiving MLP insufficient, whereas healing the projector *in addition* is critical? (The projector is the source of features; if its mapping is misaligned with the new decoder dynamics, adapting only the decoder's first layer is insufficient.)

- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA) for Healing**
  - **Why needed:** LoRA enables rapid, low-cost adaptation of the receiving layer's MLP (and projector) to correct the distribution shift from pruning, avoiding full fine-tuning of the massive decoder.
  - **Quick check:** Why is LoRA applied specifically to the *receiving* layer's MLP and not its attention projections? (The MLP is the primary feed-forward component transforming the incoming representation; correcting it directly addresses the interface mismatch.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Raw Audio -> Speech Encoder (e.g., WavLM) -> Downsampled Features Z_S -> Projector (Linear(GELU(Linear))) -> Speech Token Embeddings E_S -> Concatenated Embeddings E = [E_S, E_P, E_G] -> LLM Decoder (stack of L causal transformer layers) -> Logits -> Output Tokens.
  Pruning targets contiguous decoder layers [ℓ*+1, ..., ℓ*+n-1]. Healing targets: LoRA on MLP of receiving layer (ℓ*+n); optional unfreezing/fine-tuning of projector.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Train base SpeechLLM: freeze decoder, train projector on ASR/AST data.
  2. **Redundancy analysis:** Run a representative text-only (or speech) validation set through the frozen, unpruned decoder. Extract last-token hidden states h_ℓ for every layer ℓ. Compute average angular distance d(h_ℓ, h_ℓ+n) for all blocks. Identify optimal start ℓ*(n) = argmin d for each block size n.
  3. **Prune:** Remove layers ℓ*(n)+1 to ℓ*(n)+n-1; connect layer ℓ*(n) directly to ℓ*(n)+n.
  4. **Heal:** Initialize LoRA on receiving layer's MLP. Optionally unfreeze projector. Train for few steps (e.g., 5k iterations) on training data.
  5. **Evaluate:** Measure WER/BLEU. Iterate n to find maximum drop satisfying relative degradation threshold (e.g., 0.25 for WER).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Pruning Depth vs. Robustness:** Deeper pruning gives more speedup/memory savings but requires more healing and risks crossing a functional breakpoint.
  - **Healing Cost vs. Completeness:** Joint (decoder+projector) healing gives best results but costs more training steps; decoder-only is cheaper but often insufficient.
  - **Text-only vs. Speech-based Path:** Using text-only data for path discovery is cheaper and yields nearly identical paths, assuming LLM text-redundancy maps directly to speech. Validated but may not hold for future highly speech-specialized LLMs.
  - **Single vs. Multi-task Pruning:** Global redundancy allows one pruned decoder for multiple tasks, trading off per-task optimality for deployment simplicity.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **No healing:** Catastrophic performance drop (e.g., >50% relative WER degradation) even for small prune fractions.
  - **Inadequate healing (decoder-only):** Moderate but unacceptable degradation; out-of-domain performance may suffer more.
  - **Path mismatch:** Text-derived optimal path yields much worse speech-task performance than speech-derived path, indicating LLM redundancy is not modality-agnostic.
  - **LoRA failure:** Applying LoRA to receiving MLP does not improve results, suggesting the interface mismatch involves other components (e.g., attention patterns) or pruned layers contained unique functions.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate Figure 1 (Angular Heatmaps):** For Qwen2.5-7B, compute and plot angular distance heatmaps for text-only inputs and speech inputs (SLAM, frozen decoder). Confirm near-identical low-distance block patterns visually.
  2. **Pruning Curve without Healing:** Prune along the optimal path (from text-only data) for increasing prune fractions (0% to 70%). Evaluate WER on LibriSpeech test-clean/other. Expect sharp, monotonic increase in relative WER.
  3. **Joint Healing Validation:** For a fixed prune level (e.g., 28.6% for Qwen2.5-7B), compare WER with: (a) no healing, (b) decoder-MLP LoRA only, (c) joint (decoder-MLP LoRA + projector fine-tuning). Expect (a) worst, (b) improved but above threshold, (c) within relative WER degradation ≤ 0.25.

## Limitations
- Angular distance captures representational similarity but not functional interchangeability; healing is required, revealing hidden dependencies not predicted by the metric alone.
- Evaluation focused on English ASR (LibriSpeech) and a single AST task; generalization to other languages, domains, and translation complexity is uncertain.
- Studied models from two LLM families (1–8B); larger or differently pretrained LLMs may exhibit distinct redundancy structures.

## Confidence
- **Redundancy inheritance:** High – heatmaps match closely for text and speech inputs across scales.
- **Joint healing necessity:** High – ablations show collapse without decoder+projector adaptation.
- **Global transferability (ASR↔AST, across encoders):** Medium – limited task/encoder diversity; broader validation required.
- **Speedup/memory gains:** High – direct measurements support claim; hardware-dependent but reproducible.

## Next Checks
1. **Scale and architecture diversity:** Prune layers in 13B/70B LLMs and encoder-decoder models (e.g., T5) to verify if redundancy patterns hold beyond current decoder-only setups.
2. **Multilingual and noisy conditions:** Test pruned SpeechLLMs on non-English ASR (e.g., CommonVoice) and AST with multiple language pairs under real-world noise to stress the modality-agnostic claim.
3. **Healing mechanism ablation:** Vary LoRA rank, target components (MLP vs. attention), and healing combinations across a finer pruning grid to identify the minimal robust healing recipe.
