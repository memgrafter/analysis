# CFG-Ctrl: Control-Based Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03281
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03281
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SMC-CFG improves robustness and image quality over standard CFG across multiple diffusion models without extra computational cost.

## Executive Summary
Standard classifier-free guidance (CFG) linearly combines conditional and unconditional predictions. At high guidance scales, this linear amplification causes instability and semantic degradation. This paper recasts CFG as a proportional (P) controller and proposes a sliding mode control (SMC) extension, SMC-CFG. By defining a sliding surface on the error between conditional and unconditional velocity fields and adding a discontinuous switching term, SMC-CFG achieves robust nonlinear correction and finite-time convergence. Experiments on SD3.5, Flux, and Qwen-Image show consistent improvements in FID, CLIP score, and ImageReward, along with stable performance across a wide range of guidance scales.

## Method Summary
SMC-CFG reinterprets the CFG update \(\hat{v} = v_\emptyset + w(v_c - v_\emptyset)\) as a P-controller acting on the error \(e(t) = v_c - v_\emptyset\). To handle nonlinear model dynamics, it introduces sliding mode control: a sliding surface \(s = \dot{e} + \lambda e\) is constructed, and a switching control \(\Delta e = -k \cdot \text{sign}(s)\) is applied to the error prior to CFG scaling. This discontinuous term compensates for model uncertainties and bounded disturbances. Theoretical analysis via Lyapunov functions guarantees finite-time convergence under gain conditions. The method requires only lightweight per-step correction and no model retraining, preserving computational parity with standard CFG.

## Key Results
- Lower FID on SD3.5: 20.044 vs 21.421 (standard CFG)
- Higher CLIP score: 0.3694 vs 0.3681
- Improved human preference: ImageReward 0.9486 vs 0.8889
- Maintains robust performance across a wide range of guidance scales while being computationally identical to CFG.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: CFG as a Proportional Controller
**Claim:** Standard CFG is mathematically equivalent to a proportional (P) controller with fixed gain \(w\) acting on the error \(e(t) = v_c - v_\emptyset\).  
**Mechanism:** The guided velocity update matches the P-control law \(u_t = K e(t)\) with \(K=w\). This linear feedback amplifies the error but assumes linear error dynamics, which rarely hold under high guidance scales due to model nonlinearities.  
**Break condition:** When \(w\) is large, linear amplification causes overshooting and oscillatory error dynamics, leading to instability and semantic degradation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 3.3 (control recovery), and prior work noting CFG artifacts.

### Mechanism 2: Sliding Mode Control for Robust Nonlinear Correction
**Claim:** SMC-CFG adds a switching control term that forces the system onto a sliding manifold \(s = \dot{e} + \lambda e\), enabling robust nonlinear correction.  
**Mechanism:** The discontinuous correction \(\Delta e = -k \cdot \text{sign}(s)\) counteracts model nonlinearities and bounded disturbances, driving \(s \rightarrow 0\). The manifold \(s=0\) implicitly encodes desired exponential convergence \(\dot{e} = -\lambda e\).  
**Break condition:** Mis-tuned \(\lambda\) or \(k\) can cause chattering (excessive oscillations) or failure to converge, as shown in ablations.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 3.4 (derivation), and stability corridor analysis.

### Mechanism 3: Finite-Time Convergence via Lyapunov Stability
**Claim:** SMC-CFG guarantees finite-time convergence to the sliding manifold under Lyapunov stability analysis.  
**Mechanism:** With Lyapunov function \(V(s) = \frac{1}{2} \|s\|^2\), the derivative satisfies \(\dot{V} \le -\eta \|s\|\) when gain \(k\) exceeds a threshold derived from drift bound \(\delta\) and gain mismatch \(\rho\). This yields \(\|s(t)\| \le \|s(0)\| - \eta t\), forcing \(s=0\) in finite time.  
**Break condition:** Violations of bounded drift (\(\Phi\)) or dominant nominal gain (\(w \gg \rho/\sqrt{D}\)) break the guarantee; discrete-time approximations may also affect behavior.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 3.4, and Supplementary Sec. 6.3 (robustness corridor).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Inputs (pre-trained velocity model \(v_\theta\), condition \(c\), guidance scale \(w\), SMC parameters \(\lambda, k\)) → Core SMC Module → Output (guided velocity \(\hat{v}\)) → ODE solver; External: standard CFG baseline uses unmodified error.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. At each timestep \(t\), compute \(v_c\) and \(v_\emptyset\).  
  2. Compute error \(e(t) = v_c - v_\emptyset\); estimate \(\dot{e} \approx (e(t) - e(t-1))/\Delta t\) using stored previous error.  
  3. Form sliding surface \(s(t) = \dot{e} + \lambda e(t)\).  
  4. Apply switching control \(\Delta e(t) = -k \cdot \text{sign}(s(t))\).  
  5. Modify error: \(e(t) \gets e(t) + \Delta e(t)\).  
  6. Compute guided velocity: \(\hat{v} = v_\emptyset + w \cdot e(t)\).  
  7. Update latent \(x_{t-1}\) via ODE step; store \(e(t)\) for next step.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - \(\lambda\): Smaller values yield slower convergence; larger values enforce stricter manifold but may distort semantics.  
  - \(k\): Larger values increase correction strength but risk chattering; must satisfy stability corridor \(\delta/(w-\rho/\sqrt{D}) < k < 2\|s\|^2/(w\Delta t)\).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Visual: Over-saturated colors, warped structures (CFG); chaotic flickering/tearing (SMC-CFG with excessive \(k\)).  
  - Numerical: Divergence if \(k\) exceeds upper bound.  
  - Metric: Sharp drop in CLIP/ImageReward at high \(w\) despite large \(k\) indicates misaligned manifold.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Main quantitative comparison of SMC-CFG vs. CFG, CFG-Zero⋆, Rectified-CFG++ on SD3.5, Flux, Qwen-Image (Table 2).  
  2. Ablation study over \(\lambda \in \{2..8\}\), \(k \in \{0.01..0.8\}\) on COCO subset, measuring FID/CLIP/Aesthetic/ImageReward (Table 3).  
  3. Guidance scale sweep across \(w \in [1,10]\) on Flux-dev, plotting metric curves (Fig 6).

## Limitations
- Control signal validity: The interpretation of \(v_c - v_\emptyset\) as a semantic error may not hold for all model architectures or conditioning types.
- Boundedness assumptions: Lyapunov analysis assumes bounded drift and gain mismatch, which are not directly verified for large pre-trained diffusion models.
- Discrete implementation: Finite-difference approximation of \(\dot{e}\) may cause chattering or instability not captured by continuous theory.
