# Unsupervised Continual Learning for Amortized Bayesian Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22884
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22884
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The authors propose SC-ER (episodic replay) and SC-EWC (elastic weight consolidation) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in amortized Bayesian inference; both methods improve posterior accuracy over naive self-consistency fine-tuning and simulation-based training, with SC-ER being most effective and forgetting severity correlating with task heterogeneity.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses catastrophic forgetting in amortized Bayesian inference (ABI) when sequentially fine-tuning neural posterior approximators on new unlabeled data using self-consistency (SC) losses. Naïve SC fine-tuning, while effective for adapting to a single target distribution, degrades performance on previously seen tasks as new tasks arrive. To enable continual learning in ABI, the authors decouple simulation-based pre-training from unsupervised SC fine-tuning and introduce two complementary strategies: SC with episodic replay (SC-ER) that stores a limited set of past observations in a memory buffer (with clustering for budget control), and SC with elastic weight consolidation (SC-EWC) that regularizes parameters important for earlier tasks based on Fisher information computed from the SC loss.

Across three diverse case studies—linear regression on multiple real-world datasets, time-series forecasting for air passenger traffic, and a racing diffusion model—both SC-ER and SC-EWC successfully mitigate forgetting and produce posterior estimates that are closer to MCMC reference (Stan) than standard simulation-based training, as measured by lower maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) ratios and reduced bias and standard deviation bias. SC-ER emerges as the most effective approach, requiring only minimal replay data (e.g., one subset per task). A combination SC-ER-EWC yields marginal additional gains. Moreover, the extent of forgetting correlates with task heterogeneity; more similar tasks lead to less catastrophic forgetting, suggesting that the difficulty of continual ABI depends on distributional shifts between tasks.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework consists of two distinct training phases. First, a simulation-based pre-training stage trains a neural posterior estimator—comprising a summary network (e.g., set transformer, DeepSet) and a normalizing flow (e.g., neural spline flow)—by minimizing the negative log-likelihood on simulated pairs (θ, x) drawn from the generative model. This stage establishes a robust initial approximation to the posterior that can later be adapted efficiently.

In the second phase, the system sequentially encounters a series of unlabeled tasks. For each task, the self-consistency loss is applied: samples from the current posterior are used to compute the marginal likelihood via Bayes’ rule, and the variance of its logarithm across samples is minimized. To avoid catastrophic forgetting, two continual learning mechanisms are introduced. SC-ER maintains a replay memory of past observations; when the buffer exceeds a fixed capacity, K-medoids clustering on posterior representations selects a diverse subset. During fine-tuning on a new task, the SC loss is evaluated on the union of current and replay data. SC-EWC, after completing a task, estimates the diagonal Fisher information matrix of the SC loss gradient (using unlabeled data) to quantify parameter importance; when learning a subsequent task, a quadratic penalty on deviations of important weights from their stored values is added to the SC loss. The combination SC-ER-EWC merges both ideas. After processing all tasks, the network yields accurate amortized posteriors for every task without retraining from scratch.

## Key Results
- Both SC-ER and SC-EWC mitigate catastrophic forgetting and produce posterior estimates with lower MMD ratios and reduced bias/standard deviation bias compared to Stan, outperforming both naive self-consistency fine-tuning and simulation-based training.
- SC-ER is the most effective method, requiring only minimal replay data; combining SC-ER with EWC (SC-ER-EWC) yields slight additional improvements.
- The severity of forgetting correlates with task heterogeneity; more similar tasks exhibit less catastrophic forgetting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

#### Mechanism 1: Episodic Replay (SC-ER)
**Claim:** Episodic replay mitigates catastrophic forgetting by preserving a small, diverse set of past observations and jointly optimizing the self-consistency loss on both current and replay data.  
**Mechanism:** A memory buffer E stores a limited number of samples from previous tasks. During training on a new task Tt, the SC loss is computed on the combined set x∗_SC = x∗_t ∪ E. This joint optimization forces the network to maintain accuracy on past tasks while adapting to new data. To respect memory limits, K-medoids clustering selects representative samples when the buffer overflows.  
**Core assumption:** A limited number of representative samples from earlier tasks are sufficient to preserve performance without storing full datasets.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- The abstract explicitly names SC-ER and reports it effectively mitigates forgetting.  
- Section 2.3.3 details the algorithm (Algorithm 1) and the clustering-based update (KMEDOIDUPDATE).  
- Section 4.1 shows SC-ER achieving lower MMD ratios than naive SC and SB baseline, even with only one subset per task stored.  
**Break condition:** If the memory buffer size K is too small relative to task heterogeneity, the replay set may not capture sufficient diversity, leading to forgetting. Sensitivity analysis indicates performance degrades for K ≤ 6 in Experiment 2.

#### Mechanism 2: Elastic Weight Consolidation (SC-EWC)
**Claim:** SC-EWC reduces catastrophic forgetting by regularizing parameters that are important for previously learned tasks, as estimated via the self-consistency loss gradients.  
**Mechanism:** After training on task Ti, the network parameters Φ∗_i are snapshot and their importance is estimated using the diagonal Fisher information matrix W(i) computed from unlabeled data of Ti via the SC loss gradient (Eq. 10). When learning a new task Tt, the SC loss is augmented with a quadratic penalty λ∑_{i<j} W(i)_{jj} (Φ_{t,j} - Φ∗_{i,j})², which constrains important weights from changing much while allowing others to adapt.  
**Core assumption:** The Fisher information derived from the SC loss gradients on unlabeled data provides a valid estimate of parameter importance for preserving task performance.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- The abstract mentions SC-EWC regularizes important parameters.  
- Section 2.3.4 derives the full objective (Eq. 11) and describes importance estimation (Eqs. 9–10).  
- Section 4.2 shows SC-EWC outperforms naive SC and SB baseline, though it is generally inferior to SC-ER, confirming partial mitigation.  
**Break condition:** If the regularization strength λ is too high, adaptation to new tasks is overly restricted, harming current task performance. If Fisher estimates are noisy due to limited unlabeled samples, importance weights may be inaccurate.

#### Mechanism 3: Self-Consistency Loss Enables Data-Efficient Unsupervised Fine-Tuning
**Claim:** The self-consistency loss itself enables data-efficient, unsupervised fine-tuning of amortized posteriors on real-world data, which is a prerequisite for effective continual learning in ABI.  
**Mechanism:** The SC loss enforces that the marginal likelihood (computed via Bayes’ rule) is constant across parameter values. Minimizing the variance of log ratios over samples from a proposal distribution (typically the current posterior) drives the learned posterior toward correctness, even for atypical observations. The loss operates on unlabeled data and does not require ground-truth parameters.  
**Core assumption:** The SC loss is a strictly proper scoring rule that attains its minimum only when the learned posterior exactly matches the true posterior; thus it provides a reliable training signal under model misspecification.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- The abstract states that self-consistency training on unlabeled empirical data can enhance network robustness.  
- Section 2.2 derives the unsupervised SC loss (Eq. 5) and highlights its properties: data-efficiency, applicability to unlabeled data, and robustness to atypical data.  
- Section 4 across all experiments, SC-based methods consistently outperform SB-only training.  
**Break condition:** The SC loss can be numerically unstable early in training when the posterior approximation is poor; the two-stage training (pre-training then fine-tuning) addresses this. Without sufficient unlabeled data from the target distribution, the SC loss may not fully correct model misspecification.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Simulation-based pre-training → Summary network → Normalizing flow → Posterior estimator → Self-consistency fine-tuning (with optional: Memory buffer E for replay, Importance tracker for EWC penalty).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Pre-train posterior and summary networks on simulated data using SB loss until convergence.
  2. Initialize empty memory buffer (if using ER) and empty importance list (if using EWC).
  3. For each incoming task Tt in sequence:
     a. Load current unlabeled dataset x∗_t.
     b. Build combined SC loss input: x∗_SC = x∗_t ∪ E (if ER).
     c. Minimize L_SC-ER or L_SC-EWC or L_SC-ER-EWC accordingly.
     d. After training, update memory buffer: store a subset of x∗_t and apply clustering if capacity exceeded.
     e. If using EWC, compute Fisher importance W(t) from x∗_t and snapshot parameters Φ∗_t, then append to list.
  4. After final task, the network is ready for inference on any past or current task.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Replay vs. memory: SC-ER requires storing past data; clustering reduces memory but may lose diversity. Performance improves with larger K, but memory budget is limited.
  - Regularization strength: SC-EWC’s λ must balance stability (high λ) and plasticity (low λ). Too high λ hinders adaptation; too low λ fails to prevent forgetting.
  - Combined SC-ER-EWC: adds overhead of both mechanisms; may offer marginal gains over pure ER but at increased computational cost.
  - SC loss stability: The SC loss can be unstable early; decoupling pre-training avoids this but adds an extra stage.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Naive SC: Severe performance drop on earlier tasks as tasks progress, visible in MMD ratio increasing above 1.
  - SC-ER with insufficient memory: Forgetting reappears; MMD ratios for early tasks rise. Cluster-based selection may still preserve performance if diversity maintained.
  - SC-EWC with poor λ: Either forgetting (λ too low) or under-adaptation (λ too high, current task performance poor).
  - Numerical instability: SC loss gradients may explode if proposal distribution is far from true posterior; mitigated by two-stage training.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Linear regression: 9 real-world regression datasets (each split into 10 subsets of 100 observations) + 1 simulated prior predictive task. Each subset is a task.
  2. Air passenger traffic forecasting: 15 countries, each country’s annual time series is a task.
  3. Racing diffusion model: 8 empirical datasets from cognitive experiments, each dataset is a task.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the continual learning framework for amortized Bayesian inference be adapted to use implicit generative models (e.g., flow matching, diffusion models) that lack tractable density evaluations?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper states that the self-consistency based approaches rely on fast density evaluations, making free-form methods like flow matching or score-based diffusion less practical due to their need for numerical integration.
- **Why unresolved:** Current SC-ER and SC-EWC require explicit prior and likelihood densities to compute the self-consistency loss efficiently; implicit models would require expensive approximations or sampling, which may be computationally infeasible or introduce bias during training.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop an approximate self-consistency loss that operates with implicit models (e.g., by using model samples and density estimators) and evaluate its effectiveness on ABI tasks with implicit models, comparing performance to ground-truth posteriors.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can self-consistency losses or other unsupervised objectives be effectively employed in continual learning for ABI when the prior and likelihood are not analytically tractable?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors note that self-consistency losses assume analytic prior and likelihood densities; with implicit models the loss is not strictly proper and yields smaller benefits, and whether such losses can work in CL without explicit densities remains future work.
- **Why unresolved:** Without explicit densities, the theoretical guarantee of self-consistency being a strictly proper scoring rule is lost, and empirical improvements may diminish, making it unclear if unsupervised CL remains viable.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretically characterize conditions under which an unsupervised loss remains proper or effective without explicit densities, or propose alternative unsupervised losses (e.g., simulation-based consistency checks), then test them in CL experiments with implicit models.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How do episodic replay mechanisms scale in memory and performance when the number of sequential tasks becomes very large (e.g., hundreds), and can clustering-based compression maintain sufficient coverage of past task distributions?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper mentions that replay memory grows linearly with the number of tasks, potentially becoming impractical for long sequences; they use K-medoids clustering to bound memory but only evaluate up to 15 tasks.
- **Why unresolved:** The clustering approach might fail to preserve diversity across many heterogeneous tasks, leading to performance degradation as tasks accumulate; the scalability of SC-ER with a fixed buffer is not stress-tested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments with synthetic tasks ranging to large counts (e.g., 50, 100), measure posterior accuracy (e.g., MMD ratio) and catastrophic forgetting, and analyze buffer diversity metrics to determine if clustering remains effective.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What is the quantitative relationship between task similarity (e.g., measured by divergences between prior predictive distributions) and the severity of catastrophic forgetting in amortized Bayesian inference?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors observe that forgetting correlates with task heterogeneity and similar tasks exhibit less forgetting, but they do not formally define or vary similarity.
- **Why unresolved:** Without a systematic analysis, it is unclear how to predict when CL methods are necessary or how they will perform across varying degrees of task relatedness, limiting practical guidance for real-world deployments.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Define a similarity metric (e.g., KL divergence between prior predictives), generate task sequences with controlled similarity levels, quantify forgetting (e.g., drop in MMD ratio) for each method, and model the relationship to identify thresholds where CL interventions become critical.

## Limitations
- **Replay buffer details:** Exact number of stored samples per task and overall budget K are not fully specified, especially for Air traffic (clustering used), hindering precise replication and memory-performance analysis.
- **EWC Fisher sample size:** The count of unlabeled samples used to estimate Fisher information is omitted; full-task vs. subset sampling could affect importance estimates and forgetting mitigation.
- **Training protocol inconsistencies:** For linear/air, “30 epochs” likely means full-batch steps, but batch size and checkpointing (best SC loss restoration) are inconsistently documented, potentially impacting stability.

## Confidence
- **SC-ER:** High (consistent MMD gains across three experiments, even with minimal replay).
- **SC-EWC:** Medium (improves over naive SC but lags ER; sensitive to λ and Fisher quality).
- **Task heterogeneity effect:** Medium (qualitative correlation, lacks quantitative ablation).
- **Self-consistency loss:** High (outperforms SB baseline in all tasks).
