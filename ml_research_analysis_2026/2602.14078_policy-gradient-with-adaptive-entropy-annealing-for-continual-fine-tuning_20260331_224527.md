# Policy Gradient with Adaptive Entropy Annealing for Continual Fine-Tuning

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2602.14078
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14078
* **Reference Count:** 36
* **Primary Result:** Adaptive Entropy Annealing (aEPG) improves average accuracy over cross-entropy baselines in class-incremental fine-tuning of pretrained ViTs, with a 1.1% gain on Split-ImageNet-R (20 tasks).

## Executive Summary
Large pretrained vision models suffer catastrophic forgetting during class-incremental fine-tuning, even with parameter-efficient methods that typically use cross-entropy (CE) loss. This work reformulates classification as a one-step Markov Decision Process and derives Expected Policy Gradient (EPG) to directly optimize the true 0–1 misclassification error. Analysis shows CE implicitly emphasizes low-confidence samples (encouraging exploration and higher output entropy), while EPG focuses on high-confidence samples (promoting exploitation and lower entropy). The proposed adaptive entropy annealing (aEPG) strategy smoothly transitions from CE-like to EPG-like optimization. Extensive experiments on four benchmarks with diverse PEFT modules (LoRA, Adapter, Prefix) demonstrate consistent improvements in average accuracy over CE-based baselines. A systematic evaluation reveals that lower prediction entropy better facilitates continual adaptation, challenging the common practice of entropy-increasing techniques.

## Method Summary
The method addresses catastrophic forgetting in class-incremental learning by re-framing classification as a one-step MDP and deriving the Expected Policy Gradient (EPG) objective, which directly optimizes the 0–1 loss. EPG gradients scale with model confidence, leading to lower output entropy and promoting exploitation. To balance the initial need for adaptation (exploration) with later stability (exploitation), the authors propose Adaptive Entropy Annealing (aEPG). aEPG combines the gradients of CE and EPG: `g_aEPG = α_t * g_CE + (1−α_t) * (-g_EPG)`, where α_t follows a sigmoid schedule, starting near 1 (CE-dominant) and annealing to 0 (EPG-dominant). Training uses a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) setup with a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone for the first 30 epochs, followed by full fine-tuning for 20 epochs. New task-specific classification heads are initialized with a small-variance normal distribution (std=0.001) and expanded as new tasks arrive. The schedule parameter τ is set to 6 by default.

## Key Results
* aEPG consistently improves average accuracy over CE baselines across four class-incremental benchmarks (Split-ImageNet-R, Food101, CUB200, CLRS25) and multiple PEFT modules (LoRA, Adapter, Prefix). For example, on Split-ImageNet-R with 20 tasks, LoRA + aEPG achieves a **1.1% gain** in average accuracy.
* Lower prediction entropy correlates with reduced catastrophic forgetting. Entropy-increasing techniques like label smoothing and focal loss significantly harm performance, especially on fine-grained datasets like CUB200.
* The adaptive annealing schedule is critical; it outperforms fixed α combinations and other schedules (linear, cosine), with τ=6 showing the best trade-off between exploration and exploitation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
* **Claim:** Cross-entropy (CE) loss implicitly weights training samples by their prediction uncertainty (low confidence), promoting exploration.
* **Mechanism:** The gradient of CE for a sample is scaled by 1/πθ(y|x) (Eq. 9). This assigns larger gradient magnitudes to samples where the model's predicted probability for the true class is low (high surprisal). The optimizer therefore focuses more on correcting hard, uncertain predictions.
* **Core assumption:** The model's predicted probability πθ(y|x) for the true class approximates the sample's difficulty.
* **Evidence anchors:**
  * Gradient Analysis: Sample Weighting (Page 4, Eq. 9) directly shows the 1/πθ(y|x) scaling.
  * Abstract: "CE implicitly emphasizes low-confidence (high-surprisal) samples, encouraging exploration."
* **Break condition:** If the model is poorly calibrated or the dataset has systemic label noise, the uncertainty estimate may be unreliable, leading to mis-weighted updates.

### Mechanism 2
* **Claim:** Expected Policy Gradient (EPG), which directly optimizes the 0-1 loss, prioritizes high-confidence samples, promoting exploitation and lower output entropy.
* **Mechanism:** EPG's gradient magnitude for a sample is πθ(y|x) * |g_CE| (Page 4). This scales updates by the model's confidence, focusing learning on samples it already predicts well. The EPG objective (maximizing expected reward) implicitly includes an entropy minimization term under symmetric label noise (Proposition 3).
* **Core assumption:** The policy gradient derivation for a one-step MDP with a deterministic 0-1 reward is a valid surrogate for minimizing misclassification error.
* **Evidence anchors:**
  * Entropy Analysis: Exploration vs. Exploitation (Page 4, Fig 1) shows EPG leads to significantly lower entropy than CE during training.
  * Proposition 3 formally links the EPG objective to minimizing D_KL(pθ || qη) + H(pθ), showing implicit entropy reduction.
* **Break condition:** EPG's focus on already-confident samples may hinder learning on genuinely new, difficult data early in a task if used from the start.

### Mechanism 3
* **Claim:** Adaptive Entropy Annealing (aEPG) mitigates catastrophic forgetting by smoothly transitioning the optimization objective from CE-like exploration (early in a task) to EPG-like exploitation (late in a task).
* **Mechanism:** The combined gradient `g_aEPG = α_t * g_CE + (1−α_t) * (-g_EPG)` (Eq. 10). α_t starts near 1 (CE dominates, high entropy, adapts to new task) and anneals to 0 (EPG dominates, low entropy, stabilizes learned knowledge). The sigmoid schedule controls this transition.
* **Core assumption:** The optimal exploration-exploitation trade-off for minimizing forgetting changes over the course of learning a new task (plasticity early, stability late).
* **Evidence anchors:**
  * Algorithm: Adaptive Entropy Annealing (Page 5, Eq. 10 & description).
  * Ablation Studies (Page 7, Fig 3) show the adaptive schedule outperforms fixed α combinations.
* **Break condition:** The fixed schedule (τ) may not align with the actual learning dynamics of all datasets or PEFT modules; a poor schedule could either destabilize learning (if exploration lasts too long) or yield suboptimal adaptation (if exploitation starts too early).

## Foundational Learning
* **Concept: Cross-Entropy Loss**
  * **Why needed here:** It is the standard, default objective for classification and the baseline against which all proposed methods (EPG, aEPG) are contrasted. Understanding its implicit sample-weighting (1/confidence) is central to the paper's critique.
  * **Quick check:** What is the gradient of the CE loss for a single sample with softmax outputs, and how does it scale with the predicted probability of the true class?
* **Concept: Policy Gradient Theorem (REINFORCE)**
  * **Why needed here:** The paper derives EPG as a specific, low-variance policy gradient method for a one-step MDP. The comparison to REINFORCE (which samples actions) highlights EPG's efficiency (exact expectation over actions).
  * **Quick check:** In a one-step MDP with known rewards for all actions, how can the policy gradient estimator be modified to eliminate action sampling variance?
* **Concept: Entropy in Predictive Distributions**
  * **Why needed here:** The core analysis contrasts the entropy dynamics of CE (higher) vs. EPG/aEPG (lower). The paper challenges the common practice of using entropy-increasing regularization for classification in the context of continual learning with pretrained models.
  * **Quick check:** How does entropy regularization (e.g., confidence penalty, label smoothing) typically affect a model's predictions, and what is the paper's empirical finding regarding its effect on catastrophic forgetting?

## Architecture Onboarding
* **Component map:**
  Frozen Pretrained Backbone -> PEFT Module (LoRA/Adapter/Prefix) -> Task-Specific Classification Head
* **Critical path:**
  1. Load pretrained backbone weights.
  2. For each new task: Initialize new classification head and PEFT module parameters.
  3. Freeze backbone, train only PEFT + head with aEPG loss for 30 epochs (exploration phase, α ≈ 1).
  4. Unfreeze backbone, continue training all parameters for 20 epochs with aEPG loss (exploitation phase, α → 0).
  5. Move to next task (optionally keeping previous PEFT modules/heads if using an ensemble method like LAE).
* **Design tradeoffs:**
  * Exploration vs. Exploitation: aEPG's schedule must balance adapting to new data (exploration) vs. preserving old knowledge (exploitation). The sigmoid schedule (τ) is a hyperparameter.
  * Computational Overhead: EPG/aEPG requires computing the gradient for all K classes per sample (exact expectation), whereas CE only computes for the true class. This increases memory/computation by a factor of K (number of classes), but is manageable for typical class-incremental splits (e.g., 20 classes per task).
  * Classifier Initialization: The paper emphasizes initializing new classification heads with a small-variance normal distribution (std=0.001). This is not standard in some prior PEFT-CL codebases and is critical for stable training with EPG/aEPG.
* **Failure signatures:**
  * Catastrophic forgetting remains severe: If α decays too slowly or the final α is too high, the model continues over-exploring, deviating from pretrained features and forgetting old tasks.
  * New task learning fails: If α anneals too quickly or initial α is too low, the model exploits its pretrained (but potentially irrelevant) features too early and fails to adapt to the new task's distribution.
  * Training instability/non-convergence: Using EPG/aEPG with a poorly initialized classification head (e.g., large variance) can cause gradient issues due to the 1/πθ(y|x) term in the CE component when confidence is initially near random.
* **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Gradient Behavior Verification: On a small dummy dataset/task, compute and compare per-sample gradient magnitudes for CE vs. EPG over a few training steps. Confirm CE gradients are larger for low-confidence samples and EPG gradients are larger for high-confidence samples.
  2. Entropy Dynamics Check: Train a single task (e.g., first 20 classes of ImageNet-R) with CE, EPG, and aEPG. Plot the average predictive entropy (H(pθ)) over epochs. Expect: CE > aEPG > EPG, and aEPG's entropy should start high and decay.
  3. aEPG Schedule Validation: Ablate the annealing schedule τ on a single benchmark (e.g., Split-ImageNet-R, 10 tasks). Compare fixed α (0, 0.2, 0.5, 1) and adaptive sigmoid (τ=4,6,8). Expect adaptive schedules (especially τ=6) to outperform fixed α and other schedules like linear/cosine.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
* **Question:** Is cross-entropy loss optimal for continual fine-tuning of pretrained vision models, or does its inherent exploration bias exacerbate catastrophic forgetting?
  * **Basis:** The authors directly question the use of CE loss in continual learning, showing via gradient analysis that CE overweights low-confidence samples, encouraging disruptive updates. They propose aEPG to shift toward exploitation and demonstrate consistent accuracy gains.
  * **Why unresolved:** While aEPG improves metrics, the precise causal link between CE’s exploration and forgetting is not fully isolated—stability gains could also arise from implicit entropy reduction or interactions with PEFT architectures. Theoretical guarantees for why lower entropy preserves pretrained knowledge remain open.
  * **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies decoupling sample reweighting from entropy changes (e.g., matching entropy levels between CE and EPG) combined with probing representation similarity (e.g., CKA) across tasks to quantify deviation from pretrained features.
* **Question:** Why does lower prediction entropy consistently enhance continual adaptation in pretrained models, contrary to its benefits in training from scratch?
  * **Basis:** The paper’s systematic entropy regularization study shows entropy-increasing methods (focal loss, label smoothing) harm performance, while entropy-reducing ones (EPG, aEPG) improve it. This challenges established practice.
  * **Why unresolved:** The mechanism is hypothesized—exploration causes weight deviation—but not proven. Differences between pretrained and randomly initialized models suggest pretrained weights are near optimal basins where conservative updates preserve knowledge, but this lacks formal justification.
  * **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments measuring task interference (e.g., gradient alignment with pretrained gradients) and sharpness of minima across entropy regimes, plus theoretical analysis of how entropy affects optimization in the vicinity of pretrained parameters.
* **Question:** Why does label smoothing, an entropy-increasing technique, perform particularly poorly on fine-grained datasets like CUB200 compared to other benchmarks?
  * **Basis:** Table 3 shows label smoothing reduces accuracy on CUB200 (~5% drop) more severely than on other datasets, with authors speculating it relates to "low intra-class variance" but not investigating further.
  * **Why unresolved:** The interaction between entropy regularization, feature discriminability, and dataset characteristics (e.g., fine-grained vs. coarse-grained) is unexplored. It is unclear whether smoothing disrupts subtle feature learning or amplifies noise in fine-grained settings.
  * **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative studies across datasets with varying intra-class/inter-class variance ratios, measuring entropy’s effect on feature embedding separation (e.g., center loss) and robustness to label noise.
* **Question:** Does the one-step MDP formulation for classification limit applicability to more complex, sequential decision-making tasks in vision?
  * **Basis:** The paper casts classification as a one-step MDP to derive EPG. Real-world vision tasks (e.g., video, embodied AI) often involve multi-step decisions and delayed rewards, but this extension is not discussed.
  * **Why unresolved:** The reductionist MDP may not capture temporal credit assignment or multi-objective trade-offs. Whether EPG or aEPG principles extend to richer RL settings in vision remains unknown.
  * **What evidence would resolve it:** Applying aEPG to multi-step vision-based RL benchmarks (e.g., Atari, robotic manipulation) and comparing sample efficiency, stability, and entropy dynamics against standard RL algorithms.
* **Question:** Can adaptive entropy annealing (aEPG) generalize beyond vision transformers to other modalities (e.g., language, audio) and pretraining paradigms (e.g., masked modeling, diffusion)?
  * **Basis:** Experiments are confined to ViT-B/16 on vision datasets. The theoretical analysis is modality-agnostic, but empirical validation is limited to classification with PEFT.
  * **Why unresolved:** Different pretraining objectives (e.g., next-token prediction in LLMs) may yield different optimal entropy trajectories. aEPG’s schedule and effect on catastrophic forgetting could vary substantially across modalities.
  * **What evidence would resolve it:** Running aEPG on continual fine-tuning benchmarks for language models (e.g., CLIP for text-image, LLaMA for language tasks) and audio models, measuring forgetting and entropy dynamics, and analyzing interaction with modality-specific loss functions (e.g., CTC for audio).

## Limitations
* **Implementation specifics:** Exact LoRA target modules and total epoch distribution across tasks are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction.
* **Generalizability:** The fixed α schedule (τ=6) may not adapt to diverse datasets/PEFT modules; a poor schedule could destabilize learning or yield suboptimal adaptation.
* **External validation:** Related corpus shows high FMR but zero citations, indicating potential novelty but unknown community uptake.

## Confidence
* **CE/EPG gradient analysis (Mechanisms 1-2):** High – backed by equations (Eq. 9) and empirical entropy plots (Fig 1).
* **aEPG schedule efficacy (Mechanism 3):** Medium – validated on 4 benchmarks but schedule hyperparameter (τ) may be dataset-sensitive.
* **"Lower entropy reduces forgetting" claim:** Medium – supported by systematic evaluation within paper, but lacks cross-paper replication.
* **Broader impact beyond tested PEFT/modules:** Low – no evidence from external studies.
