# COINBench: Moving Beyond Individual Perspectives to Collective Intent Understanding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21329
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21329
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces COIN-BENCH, a live-updating benchmark with 200k+ consumer discussions across 1,400+ products to evaluate LLMs on collective intent—distilling consensus from noisy multi-source discourse.

## Executive Summary
COINBench is a novel benchmark designed to evaluate large language models on collective intent understanding—the ability to distill coherent consensus from noisy, multi-source consumer discussions. The core task is an Active Probing paradigm: models act as “meta-analysts” to reconstruct raw discussion threads into structured multiple-choice questionnaires. Evaluation is multi-faceted, using COIN-TREE (a five-level hierarchical intent tree measuring depth and breadth) and COIN-RAG (a retrieval-augmented verification ensuring correctness is grounded in original discourse). Experiments on 20 LLMs reveal a severe performance decay from surface-level to deep predictive layers, with reasoning models (e.g., GPT-o3) achieving high correctness (80.35) in deeper levels. Open-source models achieve breadth but suffer from semantic redundancy, and sub-7B models fail across the board. Ablation studies show that pre-structured input aids small models but harms state-of-the-art ones, underscoring that expert-level collective intent understanding requires navigating raw noise rather than relying on summaries.

## Method Summary
The benchmark curates a corpus of over 200,000 consumer opinions spanning 1,400+ products across nine domains. For each product, raw discussion threads are provided as input. Models generate 20 multiple-choice questions that reflect the collective intent embedded in the discussions. Evaluation proceeds along four dimensions: (1) **Depth** and **(2) Breadth** are measured by matching the generated questions to a pre-constructed hierarchical “COIN-TREE” (five levels: Usage Scenario → Aspect → Feeling → Comparison → Tendency), where nodes are weighted by discussion frequency/engagement; (3) **Correctness** is verified via COIN-RAG, which retrieves relevant raw posts using dual embeddings (TF-IDF and all-MiniLM-L6-v2) and uses an LLM judge to check if the model’s synthesized consensus aligns with the majority view in the retrieved evidence; (4) **Informativeness** captures lexical diversity (Type-Token Ratio, Distinct-n) and semantic redundancy. This design tests the ability to synthesize collective intent from unstructured, noisy discourse rather than from pre-processed summaries.

## Key Results
- All models exhibit severe depth decay across the five-level hierarchy, with performance dropping sharply from L1–L3 to L4–L5.
- Reasoning models (e.g., GPT-o3) achieve high correctness (80.35) and maintain deeper layer performance; open-source models like Qwen3-30B achieve breadth (53.20) but suffer high semantic redundancy (68.94).
- Models under 7B parameters fail across all metrics; providing pre-structured COIN-TREE input helps small models but degrades SOTA model performance, highlighting the noise-navigation trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Active Probing Paradigm forces LLMs to actively synthesize collective intent by reconstructing raw discourse into structured questionnaires, revealing deeper cognitive processing than passive QA.
- **Mechanism:** Models generate question-answer pairs from noisy multi-source discussions. Formulating valid questions requires implicit denoising and clustering to identify salient topics. Designing answer options that reflect majority consensus requires aggregating divergent perspectives. This two-step reconstruction acts as a meta-cognitive task.
- **Core assumption:** Active reconstruction (generating structure from noise) is a more valid probe of collective intent understanding than selecting from pre-defined options.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We task LLMs with acting as a 'meta-analyst' to reconstruct chaotic public discourse into structured questionnaires."
  - [section 3] "We operationalize evaluation through an Active Probing Paradigm... to reconstruct the unstructured crowd feedback into a structured Questionnaire."
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors (e.g., "ConsintBench") suggest a trend toward active, real-world intent benchmarks, but direct evidence for *this specific mechanism* is weak; the primary evidence is internal to the paper's design and results.
- **Break condition:** If models can generate superficially plausible questionnaires by paraphrasing dominant comments without true synthesis (e.g., high L1/L2 scores but collapsed L4/L5), the mechanism fails to probe deep intent.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The COIN-TREE's five-level hierarchical stratification successfully distinguishes between surface-level aggregation (L1–L3) and deep causal/predictive reasoning (L4–L5), making depth decay a measurable signal of reasoning limitation.
- **Mechanism:** Intent is operationalized as a weighted hierarchical tree (from Usage Scenarios to Predictive Tendencies). Model outputs are projected onto this tree; "lightened" nodes at deeper levels (L4 Comparison, L5 Tendency) indicate successful synthesis of implicit, cross-cutting insights from dispersed discussion points.
- **Core assumption:** Collective intent is inherently hierarchical, and moving from explicit to implicit layers requires progressively more sophisticated reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "COIN-TREE for hierarchical cognitive structuring... distinguishing between surface-level aggregation (L1–L3) and deep causal reasoning or trend prediction (L4–L5)."
  - [section 3.1] "We introduce a five-level taxonomy... Observational Understanding (L1–L3)... Inferential Reasoning (L4–L5)... A high score at L4/L5 indicates the model penetrated the 'deep structure' of collective intent."
  - [section 4.2] Empirical results show "pronounced performance decay" from L1 to L5 across models, validating the hierarchy's discriminative power.
- **Break condition:** If models achieve uniformly high scores across all five levels, the hierarchy fails to capture a meaningful difficulty gradient, suggesting the levels do not correspond to distinct reasoning demands.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** COIN-RAG's dual-embedding retrieval and verification pipeline ensures correctness is grounded in actual crowd consensus, penalizing hallucination and preventing evaluation from rewarding eloquent but unsupported insights.
- **Mechanism:** Raw discussions are indexed via TF-IDF (keyword precision) and all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (semantic nuance). Model-generated intent statements are queried against this vector DB. A reasoning-based verifier checks if the retrieved evidence align with the synthesized consensus. This creates a tight signal-to-noise filter.
- **Core assumption:** "Correct" collective intent must be directly traceable to a majority of source comments; expert understanding is fidelity to the swarm, not external truth.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "retrieval-augmented verification (COIN-RAG) to ensure expert-level precision in analyzing raw, collective human discussions."
  - [section 3.2] "COIN-RAG serves to ground the LLM's inferred intent in the raw evidence... If the LLM’s synthesized consensus aligns with the majority perspective found in the retrieved evidence, it is deemed correct."
  - [section 4.2] Reasoning models (GPT-o3) achieve higher Correctness (80.35), which the authors link to "critical deliberation" and filtering of vocal minorities—consistent with RAG's grounding effect.
- **Break condition:** If correctness scores become uncorrelated with retrieval relevance metrics (e.g., a model scores high by generating plausible but unverifiable statements), the verification pipeline is not effectively grounding the evaluation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Collective Intent Synthesis**
  - **Why needed here:** The core task is not summarizing a single document but distilling a coherent "consensus" from noisy, conflicting, multi-source discourse. This requires denoising, clustering, and resolving contradictions across hundreds of opinions.
  - **Quick check question:** Given three user comments ("Battery lasts 2 days," "Battery dies in 4 hours," "Battery is average"), what is the synthesized collective intent on battery life? Does it acknowledge variance or overfit to the majority?

- **Concept: Navigating Swarm Noise**
  - **Why needed here:** Raw consumer discussions contain spam, hyperbole, edge cases, and contradictory statements. Effective models must distinguish signal (common, grounded concerns) from noise (outliers, poorly written posts) without losing legitimate minority viewpoints that indicate emerging trends.
  - **Quick check question:** In a product discussion with 90% positive reviews and 10% detailing a specific hardware flaw, should the collective intent include the flaw? Under what conditions?

- **Concept: Hierarchical Cognitive Structuring**
  - **Why needed here:** Intent is multi-layered (from "I use it for calls" to "This will be obsolete in 2 years"). Understanding requires recognizing which layer a piece of evidence belongs to and how layers connect (e.g., a feeling about an aspect in a specific scenario informs a predicted tendency).
  - **Quick check question:** For the statement "I wish they'd add more bass," identify its level in the COIN-TREE (L1 Usage? L2 Aspect? L3 Feeling? L4 Comparison? L5 Tendency?) and explain its link to other levels.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  Data Curation Pipeline → Hybrid retrieval (semantic vector + LLM-augmented keywords) → Rule-based & LLM-based denoising filters → Temporal alignment → High-density discussion corpus.
  2.  Active Probing Interface → Takes raw discussions + product seed → LLM-as-Meta-Analyst generates 20 MCQ questionnaires.
  3.  COIN-TREE Constructor → (Offline, per-product) LLM extracts branches from all discussions → Sentence Transformer merges semantically similar nodes layer-wise → Weighted 5-level tree.
  4.  Evaluation Engine → For each product/questionnaire: (a) Extracts branches from Q&A → (b) Matches/lightens COIN-TREE → computes Depth/Breadth scores. (c) COIN-RAG: Dual-embedding retrieval of source evidence for each synthesized statement → LLM judge validates grounding → Correctness score. (d) Informativeness: TTR/Distinct-n & semantic redundancy on generated text.
  5.  Aggregator → Combines scores across products/models.
- **Critical path:** Raw Discussion Corpus → (1) COIN-TREE Construction (pre-computed per product) → (2) Model Questionnaire Generation (per model/product) → (3) Tree Matching & RAG Verification (per question) → (4) Metric Aggregation. The COIN-TREE is the central reference structure for depth/breadth; RAG is the correctness gate.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Noise vs. Structure: Raw discourse provides authenticity but overwhelms small models (Table 3: Qwen2.5-7B correctness +7.09 with Tree input). SOTA models lose nuance with pre-structured Tree input (GPT-4o correctness -11.45). The benchmark tests navigation of noise, not just use of summaries.
  - Depth vs. Breadth: Achieving breadth (L1 coverage) often predicts redundancy (Qwen3-30B: Breadth 53.20, Redundancy 68.94). True expertise requires discriminative distillation—capturing distinct intents without repetition.
  - Correctness vs. Informativeness: Grounding (RAG) can penalize insightful but sparsely evidenced connections. The balance favors consensus over potential outliers.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Depth Decay: Plummeting L4/L5 scores relative to L1/L2. Indicates inability to perform comparative or predictive synthesis.
  - High Semantic Redundancy (>70%): Model generates many questions about the same latent topic using different phrasing. Signals failure in analytical breadth/discriminative distillation.
  - Low Correctness (<50%) with high L1: Model can state obvious facts from discussions but fails to ensure those facts are representative majority views, not vocal minorities.
  - Size Threshold Failure: Models <7B parameters fail across all dimensions, indicating a minimum capability threshold for swarm navigation.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  Depth Decay Profile: Run the full suite on a representative set of models (e.g., GPT-o3, GPT-4o, Qwen3-30B, Qwen2.5-7B). Plot average L1-L5 scores. **Expectation:** All show decay; reasoning models (GPT-o3) retain more L4/L5; <7B models show near-zero L4/L5. **Validates:** Hierarchical difficulty and scaling laws.
  2.  Structure-Noise Ablation: For 2-3 key models (one SOTA like GPT-4o, one mid-tier like Qwen2.5-7B), run evaluation twice: (a) with original raw discussion input, (b) with only the structured COIN-TREE as input. **Expectation:** Small model improves in correctness with Tree; SOTA model degrades in correctness/informativeness. **Validates:** The "noise navigation" premise and trade-off.
  3.  Reasoning vs. General Comparison: Isolate "reasoning" models (GPT-o3, DeepSeek R1 variants) vs. "general" models of similar size (e.g., GPT-4o, Qwen3 Instruct). Compare L4/L5 and Correctness. **Expectation:** Reasoning models significantly outperform on L4/L5 and Correctness. **Validates:** The claim that deep collective intent requires advanced reasoning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do LLMs exhibit a severe depth decay from L3 to L4/L5 in COIN-TREE?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Table 2 shows near-zero L4/L5 scores; authors describe a "depth cliff" where models parse explicit but not implicit content.
- **Why unresolved:** Underlying reason (data, architecture, training) not examined.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled datasets with varying implicitness; model probing on contradictory inputs; fine-tuning on predictive tendency tasks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why does COIN-TREE input help small models but harm SOTA models' correctness?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Ablation (Table 3): Qwen2.5-7B +7.09, GPT-4o -11.45, GPT-o3 -23.25. Authors suggest expert understanding requires navigating noise.
- **Why unresolved:** Mechanism unexplored; unclear if SOTA models already extract structure or if given structure loses nuance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Vary structure granularity; analyze model activations on raw vs structured inputs; compare extracted structures with COIN-TREE.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why do large open-source models achieve high breadth but high semantic redundancy?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Table 2: Qwen3-30B-A3B breadth 53.20, redundancy 68.94; termed "verbosity trap."
- **Why unresolved:** Not studied if due to training objective, decoding, or lack of fine-tuning for concise synthesis.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Test decoding strategies; fine-tune on concise summaries; analyze token diversity and entropy.

## Limitations
- **Corpus opacity:** Exact data sources, LLM verifier prompts, and COIN-TREE weight formulas are undisclosed, hindering replication and bias auditing.
- **Task ecological validity:** Active Probing may not reflect real-world collective intent distillation, as human analysts use external knowledge and iterative refinement not captured in a single MCQ generation pass.
- **Novelty boundary:** Neighbor "ConsintBench" (avg FMR 0.46) suggests overlapping scope; unclear if COIN-BENCH's hierarchical depth/RAG verification offers distinct validity or incremental benchmarking.

## Confidence
- **Depth decay (L1→L5):** High — consistently observed across 20 models with clear hierarchical difficulty gradient.
- **Reasoning model advantage (e.g., GPT-o3 correctness 80.35):** Medium — correlational; does not isolate reasoning-specific mechanisms vs. general scale.
- **Noise-navigation premise (small models benefit from pre-structured input, SOTA hindered):** Low — ablation lacks control for input length/complexity; may conflate noise with information loss from summarization.

## Next Checks
