# Self-supervised Disentanglement of Disease Effects from Aging in 3D Medical Shapes
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15862  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15862  
- **Reference count:** 28  
- **Primary result:** Achieves disease SAP 0.38 and age SAP 0.67 on ADNI hippocampus, outperforming baselines and enabling controllable synthesis without full diagnosis labels.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenging task of separating pathological shape changes from normal aging effects in 3D medical images, especially when disease labels are limited. It introduces a self-supervised two-stage framework. The first stage learns an implicit neural representation (INR) with a Gaussian mixture model (GMM) prior on shape embeddings, producing clusterable latent codes from which pseudo-disease labels are derived without any ground-truth diagnosis. The second stage employs a variational autoencoder (VAE) that ingests these codes and applies a multi-objective loss—combining supervised nearest-neighbor contrastive learning, covariance regularization, and sensitivity terms—to disentangle disease and aging factors into separate latent dimensions. Evaluated on hippocampal (ADNI) and distal femur (OAI) shapes, the method achieves high reconstruction quality (Chamfer distance 0.0019) and strong disentanglement (e.g., disease SAP 0.38, age SAP 0.67). Critically, pseudo-labels significantly boost performance in low-label regimes (0% real labels: SAP 0.29 vs. 0.17 without). The approach approaches supervised performance without requiring full diagnosis labels, enabling controlled shape synthesis and analysis.

## Method Summary
The method processes watertight 3D meshes by converting them to SDF samples. **Stage 1** trains an INR (DeepSDF-style MLP) to map a per-shape code \(z_i\) to SDF values. The loss combines pointwise SDF reconstruction, Eikonal regularization, an \(\ell_2\) penalty on codes, and a two-component GMM prior that encourages a bimodal latent distribution. After convergence, a separate GMM is fit on the learned codes to assign binary pseudo-disease labels \(\tilde{y}_i\). **Stage 2** freezes the INR decoder and builds a VAE: an encoder \(E_\phi\) maps \(z_i\) to a compact latent \(z_i^v\) (dimensionality 8); a decoder \(D_\psi\) reconstructs \(z_i\). The VAE loss merges reconstruction (MSE), a KL term (\(\beta=0.008\)), and several disentanglement regularizers: Supervised Nearest-Neighbor Loss (SNNL) on the first two latent coordinates (disease using \(\tilde{y}_i\), age using true age), a covariance penalty to decorrelate all dimensions, a dispersion-sensitivity term to ensure those coordinates influence the output, and a recycled SDF loss computed via the frozen INR. Training uses Adam (lr=0.001, batch=16/32, step decay) for 2000 epochs per stage. While most hyperparameters are reported, some—such as exact VAE architecture details, the SDF loss weight in Stage 2, and the SNNL temperature heuristic—remain unspecified, which may affect reproducibility.

## Key Results
- **ADNI hippocampus:** disease SAP 0.38, Pearson correlation 0.73, classification accuracy 78.67%; age SAP 0.67, correlation 0.86, RMSE 0.061. Outperforms β-VAE and other baselines.
- **Reconstruction:** Chamfer distance 0.0019, indicating high-fidelity shape recovery.
- **Pseudo-label benefit:** With 0% real disease labels, disentanglement SAP=0.29 vs. 0.17 without pseudo-labels; performance improves as real label fraction increases.
- **OAI distal femur:** disease SAP 0.25, age SAP 0.41.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** An implicit neural representation (INR) trained with a Gaussian mixture model (GMM) prior on signed distance functions (SDFs) learns shape embeddings that cluster by disease status, enabling pseudo-label discovery without ground-truth diagnosis.
- **Mechanism:** The INR decoder \( G_\theta \) maps shape codes \( z_i \) to SDF values. The loss combines pointwise SDF reconstruction, Eikonal regularization (to enforce proper gradient norms), and a two-component GMM prior over \( z_i \). The GMM encourages a bimodal latent structure aligned with disease/healthy clusters. After training, a GMM fit on \( z_i \) yields pseudo-labels \( \tilde{y}_i \).
- **Core assumption:** The disease-related shape variations produce a distinct, roughly bimodal cluster structure in the latent space, separable from aging effects when age labels are not used during Stage 1.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Stage-1 uses an implicit neural representation with signed distance functions and a Gaussian mixture prior to learn clusterable shape embeddings, yielding pseudo disease labels."
  - [section 2.2] Details of \( \mathcal{L}_{\text{GMM}} \) and pseudo-label assignment by argmax over mixture components.
  - [section 4.1] Table 1 shows adding the GMM prior improves cluster purity (82.37%) and volume separation (\(\Delta V = 1401 \, \text{mm}^3\)), indicating disease-related clustering.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., Point2SSM++) use self-supervised learning for shape models but do not employ GMM-based pseudo-labeling for disease; thus, direct evidence is primarily from this paper's ablations.
- **Break condition:** If the GMM prior fails to produce clusters with purity significantly above random chance (e.g., < 70% in Table 1) or if volume differences between clusters do not align with known disease atrophy patterns, the mechanism collapses and pseudo-labels become unreliable.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A variational autoencoder (VAE) operating on the Stage-1 shape codes, guided by pseudo disease labels and continuous age labels via a multi-objective loss, disentangles these pseudo labels and age labels into separate latent dimensions.
- **Mechanism:** The VAE encoder produces latent codes \( z_i^v \). The loss combines: (i) VAE reconstruction + KL; (ii) Supervised Nearest-Neighbor Loss (SNNL) on a designated coordinate for disease (using \( \tilde{y}_i \)) and age, pulling together samples with same label/age and pushing apart others; (iii) covariance regularization \( \mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}} \) to minimize cross-correlation between latent dimensions; (iv) sensitivity regularizer \( \mathcal{L}_{\text{dis\_sen}} \) to ensure the disease/age coordinates actively affect reconstruction; (v) frozen INR SDF loss to preserve shape fidelity.
- **Core assumption:** The pseudo-disease labels from Stage 1 are accurate enough to serve as supervision, and the latent space is sufficiently expressive to separate the two factors with designated dimensions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Stage-2 applies a variational autoencoder to disentangle these pseudo labels and age labels via a multi-objective loss combining covariance and contrastive learning."
  - [section 2.3] Full specification of \( \mathcal{L}_{\text{disentangle}} \), including SNNL, covariance, and sensitivity terms.
  - [section 4.1] Table 2 ablations: removing covariance (\( \lambda_{\text{cov}} = 0 \)) drops disease SAP from 0.38 to 0.34; using fixed SNNL temperature gives lower SAP (0.30) vs. adaptive (0.38). This shows each component's contribution.
  - [section 4.2] Figure 3 shows latent traversals produce clinically plausible volume changes and morphological differences aligned with disease progression.
- **Break condition:** If any key loss component is disabled and SAP scores drop substantially (e.g., > 0.05), or if latent traversals fail to produce recognizable disease/age-related shape changes, the disentanglement mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Pseudo-labels from Stage 1 provide the necessary weak supervision for disentanglement in low-label regimes, outperforming unsupervised methods and approaching supervised performance as real label fraction increases.
- **Mechanism:** The Stage-1 pseudo-labels supply a binary disease signal to the SNNL term in Stage 2. When real disease labels are scarce, these pseudo-labels act as a proxy to guide the VAE in separating disease factors. As more real labels are introduced, the pseudo-labels are supplemented or replaced, improving performance until saturation.
- **Core assumption:** The Stage-1 clustering yields pseudo-labels with purity high enough (∼82%) that their errors are outweighed by the benefit of having a supervision signal compared to fully unsupervised factorization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Pseudo labels significantly improve disentanglement in low-label settings (0% real labels: SAP 0.29 vs. 0.17 without)."
  - [section 4.2] Table 3: At 0% real labels, using pseudo-labels gives SAP 0.29, whereas leaving samples unlabeled gives SAP 0.17. The gain diminishes as real label % increases.
  - [corpus] Neighbor paper "Disentangling Hippocampal Shape Variations..." (2404.00785) uses mesh VAE with contrastive learning but requires ground-truth labels; this paper's key advance is eliminating that requirement via pseudo-labels.
- **Break condition:** If Stage-1 cluster purity is too low (e.g., < 75%), the noise in pseudo-labels may degrade Stage-2 performance relative to unsupervised methods, especially in the 0–30% real label range.

## Foundational Learning
#### Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) with Signed Distance Functions (SDFs)
- **Why needed here:** To represent 3D shapes as continuous functions that can be queried at any 3D point, enabling high-fidelity reconstruction and shape manipulation without fixed topology.
- **Quick check question:** How does the Eikonal loss in Equation (3) ensure the learned function behaves like a true distance field?

#### Variational Autoencoder (VAE)
- **Why needed here:** To model the distribution of Stage-1 shape codes in a compact latent space \( z_i^v \) and enable controlled generation via latent traversals.
- **Quick check question:** Why is a KL divergence term included in the VAE loss, and what would happen if it were removed?

#### Supervised Nearest-Neighbor Loss (SNNL)
- **Why needed here:** A contrastive loss variant that enforces that samples with the same pseudo-disease label (or similar age) are close in the designated latent dimension while being separated in others.
- **Quick check question:** What is the purpose of the adaptive temperature \( T \) in SNNL, and how is it estimated?

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component Map
Stage 1: INR decoder \( G_\theta \) (MLP) takes per-shape code \( z_i \) → predicts SDF values; trained with SDF loss, Eikonal regularization, and a two-component GMM prior on codes. After training, a GMM is fitted on \(\{z_i\}\) to produce pseudo-disease labels \(\tilde{y}_i\).  
Stage 2: The INR decoder is frozen. VAE encoder \( E_\phi \) encodes \( z_i \) into \( z_i^v \) (\(k=8\)). VAE decoder \( D_\psi \) reconstructs \( z_i \) from \( z_i^v \). The disentanglement loss includes: SNNL on coordinate 0 (disease) using \(\tilde{y}_i\) and on coordinate 1 (age) using age labels; covariance regularization to minimize cross-correlation among latent dimensions; dispersion-sensitivity regularizer to ensure coordinates 0 and 1 affect the output; and a recycled SDF loss computed by feeding \( D_\psi(z_i^v) \) back into \( G_\theta \).  
Overall flow: \( G_\theta \) (with GMM prior) → \(\tilde{y}_i\) → VAE (\(E_\phi\), \(D_\psi\)) with frozen \( G_\theta \) and multi-objective loss.

#### Critical Path
1. Train Stage-1 INR until convergence using SDF, Eikonal, \(\ell_2\)(code), and GMM prior losses.
2. Fit a 2-component GMM on the learned shape codes to assign pseudo-disease labels \(\tilde{y}_i\).
3. Train Stage-2 VAE on the codes with the full disentanglement loss, keeping \( G_\theta \) frozen.
4. Evaluate disentanglement metrics (SAP, correlation, accuracy, RMSE) and reconstruction quality (Chamfer distance) on the test set.

#### Design Tradeoffs
- The frozen INR decoder preserves reconstruction fidelity but prevents any fine‑tuning of the shape decoder during disentanglement.
- Using a fixed 2‑component GMM assumes a bimodal disease distribution; this may fail for diseases with multiple subtypes or continuous phenotypes.
- Assigning specific latent dimensions to disease and age forces a linear factorisation, which can be restrictive if the factors are non‑linearly entangled.
- High weights on disentanglement losses (\(\lambda_{\text{cov}}, \lambda_{\text{dis\_sen}}\)) may degrade reconstruction quality (Chamfer distance).

#### Failure Signatures
- Stage‑1: Cluster purity below 70% or volume separation between GMM clusters not matching known atrophy patterns.
- Stage‑2: Disease/age SAP scores drop below 0.20; latent traversals (changing \(z_0\) or \(z_1\)) produce no perceptible shape changes; reconstruction Chamfer distance exceeds 0.002.
- Overall: Increasing the fraction of real disease labels does not improve SAP, indicating pseudo‑label noise is overwhelming the supervision.

#### First 3 Experiments
1. **Stage‑1 convergence check**: Monitor SDF and GMM losses; verify bimodal distribution of codes via t‑SNE.
2. **Pseudo‑label validation**: Compute cluster purity against held‑out diagnoses; if purity < 80%, adjust GMM initialization or latent dimensionality.
3. **Stage‑2 sanity check**: After training, compute Pearson correlation between \(z_0\) and pseudo‑disease, and between \(z_1\) and true age; expect strong correlation for one pair and near‑zero for the other. Inspect SNNL temperature and covariance weight if correlations are off.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How well does the proposed framework generalize to other diseases and anatomical structures beyond the hippocampus and distal femur?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors mention in the conclusion that they will study different diseases across diverse anatomical shapes in future work.
- **Why unresolved:** Only two datasets (ADNI hippocampus, OAI distal femur) are evaluated; generalizability to other conditions and anatomies remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply the method to additional disease-related shape datasets (e.g., Parkinson’s brain shapes, knee cartilage) and report disentanglement and reconstruction metrics.

#### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the binary clustering assumption in Stage‑1 (two‑component GMM) limit the discovery of nuanced disease phenotypes (e.g., mild cognitive impairment, disease subtypes), and would a multi‑cluster approach improve disentanglement?
- **Basis in paper:** Stage‑1 uses a two‑component GMM to produce pseudo labels, assuming a bimodal shape distribution; no ablation on number of clusters is performed.
- **Why unresolved:** The number of GMM components is fixed to 2, which may merge distinct clinical groups such as CN, MCI, AD.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** On ADNI data with available MCI labels, train Stage‑1 with different numbers of GMM components (e.g., 2, 3, 4), evaluate cluster purity against true diagnoses and downstream disentanglement performance (SAP for disease).

#### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the quality of pseudo‑labels from Stage‑1 be improved to narrow the performance gap between self‑supervised and fully supervised disentanglement, especially when real labels are scarce?
- **Basis in paper:** Results show pseudo‑labels improve low‑label disentanglement (0% real: SAP 0.29 vs. 0.17) but a gap remains to 100% supervised (SAP 0.40).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not explore alternative pseudo‑label generation methods beyond a simple GMM; the current approach may be suboptimal due to noise or limited use of auxiliary information.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace GMM‑based pseudo‑labeling with more advanced techniques (e.g., deep clustering networks, age‑adjusted residual modelling, iterative refinement using Stage‑2 feedback) and compare disentanglement across varying fractions of real labels.

## Limitations
- Pseudo‑label quality: Stage‑1 cluster purity ~82% (moderate); errors may propagate to Stage‑2, limiting disentanglement performance.
- Generalizability: Evaluation on only two datasets (ADNI hippocampus, OAI distal femur); bimodal GMM assumption may not hold for multi‑class or continuous disease spectra.
- Complexity: Two‑stage training with numerous hyperparameters; frozen INR decoder; no theoretical guarantee of disentanglement; risk of overfitting.

## Confidence
- **INR + GMM clustering** (Mechanism 1): Medium  
- **VAE disentanglement** (Mechanism 2): Medium  
- **Pseudo‑label benefit** (Mechanism 3): Medium  
- **Overall framework**: High  

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑dataset validation:** Test on at least two new datasets (e.g., Parkinson’s brain shapes, knee cartilage) to assess generalizability.
2. **Pseudo‑label noise test:** Introduce controlled noise (0‑20% error) into Stage‑1 labels and measure disentanglement; compare against using equivalent fractions of real labels.
3. **Loss weight robustness:** Perform grid search over \(\lambda_{\text{cov}}, \lambda_{\text{dis\_sen}}, \lambda_{\text{snnl}}\); report SAP and reconstruction stability to ensure sensitivity is manageable.
