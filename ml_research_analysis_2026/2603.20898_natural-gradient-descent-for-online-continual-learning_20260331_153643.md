# Natural Gradient Descent for Online Continual Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20898  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20898  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** NGD‑KFAC substantially improves average end accuracy in online continual learning; e.g., on class‑incremental OCI (Split CIFAR‑100) accuracy rises from 19.58% to 22.20%.

## Executive Summary
Online Continual Learning (OCL) requires models to learn from a stream of data without revisiting past observations, leading to catastrophic forgetting. This paper proposes **Natural Gradient Descent with Kronecker Factored Approximate Curvature (NGD‑KFAC)** as an optimizer for OCL. NGD‑KFAC approximates the Fisher Information Matrix per layer via a Kronecker product, enabling efficient natural gradient updates that respect the local geometry of the loss landscape. Combined with memory replay methods (ER, A‑GEM, MIR) and various OCL tricks (Separated Softmax, Nearest Class Mean), NGD‑KFAC consistently yields higher average end accuracy across Split CIFAR‑100, CORE50, and Split miniImageNet benchmarks. The approach relies on heavy Tikhonov damping (λ=1.0) for stability in the non‑stationary, single‑pass setting, and shows larger gains with bigger replay buffers. Although forgetting increases slightly, the overall improvement demonstrates the effectiveness of curvature‑aware optimization for OCL.

## Method Summary
NGD‑KFAC replaces standard SGD/Adam with a natural gradient optimizer that maintains Kronecker‑factored approximations of the per‑layer Fisher Information Matrix. For each eligible layer (convolutional and linear), two running covariance matrices are tracked: **A** (input activations) and **B** (gradient outputs). During training, these are updated via exponential moving averages using hooks attached to the forward and backward passes. The natural gradient for a weight matrix **W** is computed as δW = A⁻¹ ∇W B⁻¹, where A and B are regularized by Tikhonov damping: A ← A + π√λ I, B ← B + (1/π)√λ I, with π = √(tr(A)/in / (tr(B)/out). The damping λ is set to 1.0 throughout to ensure numerical stability in OCL’s constantly shifting data distribution. The optimizer operates on mini‑batches that mix current task samples with replay samples drawn from an external buffer (e.g., reservoir for ER). NGD‑KFAC is orthogonal to OCL tricks such as Separated Softmax or Nearest Class Mean; it can be wrapped around any standard OCL pipeline. The method retains the same fixed learning rate (0.1) as the SGD baselines, requiring only the additional state for A and B per layer and modest extra compute for the Kronecker inverses.

## Key Results
- **Class‑incremental OCI (Split CIFAR‑100):** Average end accuracy improved from 19.58% (SGD) to 22.20% when NGD‑KFAC was applied to ER, A‑GEM, MIR, and GSS.  
- **Buffer size scaling:** Gains grew with memory budget; e.g., on OCI with ER, NGD‑KFAC gave +1.57% at M=1k and +3.58% at M=10k.  
- **Domain‑incremental scenarios:** Similar absolute improvements observed on CORe50 and NS‑MiniImageNet, confirming broader applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Natural gradient aligns updates with loss landscape geometry
- **Claim:** NGD‑KFAC accelerates convergence and improves final accuracy in OCL by performing updates that respect the local geometry of the loss landscape, as approximated by the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM).  
- **Mechanism:** The optimizer preconditions the gradient by an approximation of the inverse FIM (via Kronecker factors). This scaling aligns the update direction with the natural gradient – the steepest descent direction in the space of probability distributions rather than Euclidean parameter space. In OCL’s non‑stationary, single‑pass setting, this yields more efficient parameter changes that better preserve performance on previous data distributions.  
- **Core assumption:** The Kronecker factorization of the FIM (A⊗B) is a sufficiently accurate approximation for each layer, and the block‑diagonal assumption (ignoring cross‑layer covariances) holds.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “utilizes the Natural Gradient Descent optimizer, incorporating an approximation of the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) through Kronecker Factored Approximate Curvature (KFAC)... demonstrates substantial improvements in performance.” Section 5 derives the NGD update θ − α·F⁻¹·∇L and introduces the layer‑wise approximation F⁽ˡ⁾ ≈ A⊗B. Weak direct evidence from neighboring papers.  
- **Break condition:** The approximation fails if the layer’s Fisher is not well‑approximated by a Kronecker product (e.g., highly correlated or non‑linear activations) or if damping is insufficient, leading to unstable updates.

### Mechanism 2: High Tikhonov damping (λ=1.0) stabilizes updates in OCL’s shifting distribution
- **Claim:** A fixed high damping coefficient (λ=1.0) is critical for stability and performance in OCL with NGD‑KFAC, counteracting aggressive updates caused by the constantly changing data stream.  
- **Mechanism:** Damping regularizes the curvature approximation by adding λI to the Kronecker factors before inversion. This prevents the inverse FIM estimate from becoming poorly conditioned or exploding, especially early in training or when encountering a new task distribution. It effectively limits the step size in directions of low curvature, acting as a trust‑region constraint.  
- **Core assumption:** OCL models are “always stuck in an initial exploration phase” where predictions are highly uncertain, making curvature estimates noisy and requiring strong regularization.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 5.2 argues that OCL models “always stuck in this initial exploration phase thus requiring heavy damping” and reports λ=1.0 as the best choice. Section 7.3 and Figure 2 show empirical sensitivity to damping, with higher λ leading to higher average accuracy across tricks.  
- **Break condition:** λ too low causes numerical instability (loss spikes, divergence); λ too high makes the optimizer overly cautious, approaching SGD and losing curvature‑aware benefits.

### Mechanism 3: Synergy with replay: larger buffers yield more accurate Fisher estimates and bigger gains
- **Claim:** The performance gains from NGD‑KFAC are synergistic with memory replay techniques (ER, MIR, A‑GEM), and the magnitude of improvement scales with replay buffer size.  
- **Mechanism:** Replay buffers provide a (noisy) approximation of past data distributions. NGD‑KFAC uses these replayed samples to compute more accurate layer‑wise Fisher statistics (A and B). A larger, more diverse buffer yields a more reliable curvature estimate, allowing the natural gradient to better navigate the trade‑off between learning new tasks and preserving old performance. The preconditioning helps utilize the limited replay data more efficiently.  
- **Core assumption:** The Fisher statistics computed from the mixed stream of new and replayed data are a reasonable approximation of the true Fisher of the combined past and present distributions.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 7.1.2 quantifies that accuracy gains from NGD‑KFAC are larger with bigger buffers (e.g., +3.58% avg acc at M=10k vs +1.57% at M=1k on OCI). Tables 2 and 4 compare ER/MIR with/without NGD‑KFAC across buffer sizes, showing consistent but size‑dependent lifts.  
- **Break condition:** With very small buffers (e.g., M=100), the Fisher estimate becomes too noisy, potentially harming performance relative to SGD.

## Foundational Learning

- **Fisher Information Matrix (FIM)**  
  *Why needed:* The FIM is the core quantity that defines the natural gradient. Understanding it as the covariance of the score function (∂ℓ/∂θ) and its role as a Riemannian metric in parameter space explains why NGD updates differ from SGD.  
  *Quick check:* What does a large eigenvalue in the FIM imply about the sensitivity of the model’s predictive distribution to changes in a particular parameter direction?

- **Kronecker-Factored Approximation (KFAC)**  
  *Why needed:* Direct computation/inversion of the full FIM is impossible for deep nets. KFAC’s factorization (A⊗B) reduces storage from O(n²) to O(n) per layer and enables efficient inversion, making NGD feasible.  
  *Quick check:* For a fully‑connected layer with weight matrix W (size m×n), what are the dimensions of the two Kronecker factors A and B, and what do they represent?

- **Online Continual Learning (OCL) Constraints**  
  *Why needed:* The problem setting (single pass, small batches, no task labels, limited memory) fundamentally changes the optimization challenge. Methods designed for multi‑epoch CL (e.g., EWC, VCL) are often inapplicable or ineffective here.  
  *Quick check:* How does the “online” constraint (T=1) specifically exacerbate the catastrophic forgetting problem compared to a multi‑epoch CL scenario?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Replay Buffer --(samples)--> Batch Mixer --> Model (ResNet‑18 with KFAC hooks) --> Loss (CE + Trick) --> Backward --> KFAC State (A,B) per layer --> Damping Controller (λ) --> Natural Gradient (A⁻¹⊗B⁻¹) --> Parameter Update

**Critical path:**  
1. Initialize KFAC factors A (input covariance) and B (gradient covariance) for each target layer (zeros or identity). Set damping λ=1.0, running average ρ=0.9.  
2. For each training batch: draw replay samples according to the chosen OCL method (ER/A‑GEM/MIR/GSS), mix with current data to form a batch.  
3. Forward pass → compute loss (possibly with a trick like Separated Softmax).  
4. Backward pass → accumulate gradients. Using stored hooks, compute current mini‑batch estimates of A and B. Update running averages: A ← ρ·A + (1−ρ)·(h hᵀ)/batch_sz, B ← ρ·B + (1−ρ)·(g gᵀ)/batch_sz.  
5. For each layer, compute π = √(tr(A)/in / (tr(B)/out)). Regularize: Â = A + π√λ I, B̂ = B + (1/π)√λ I. Invert efficiently using the Kronecker property: δW = Â⁻¹ ∇W B̂⁻¹.  
6. Apply update: W ← W − α·δW (α=0.1). Biases are updated with a diagonal approximation (standard SGD with damping).  
7. After batch, add current samples to the replay buffer according to the policy (e.g., reservoir sampling for ER).  
8. After each task, optionally apply tricks (Review fine‑tune, update prototypes for NCM, freeze old heads for Separated Softmax).  
9. Repeat for all tasks; after training, evaluate all tasks to compute average end accuracy and forgetting.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Memory vs. Accuracy:** KFAC adds O(#layers·(in²+out²)) state; for ResNet‑18 this is a few hundred MB. Larger replay buffers improve accuracy but increase mixing cost and storage.  
- **Compute overhead:** Extra forward/backward hooks and matrix operations per layer increase iteration time by ~20‑50% vs. SGD.  
- **Stability vs. Adaptivity:** High damping (λ=1.0) stabilizes OCL but may slow adaptation to new tasks; tuning λ is dataset/method‑dependent.  
- **Approximation fidelity:** KFAC ignores cross‑layer and off‑diagonal covariances; performance hinges on this being a good approximation for the model/dataset.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Training divergence (NaN/exploding loss):** Usually caused by too low damping or ill‑conditioned A/B. Increase λ.  
- **No improvement over SGD:** Check if KFAC factors are updating (hooks attached to correct layers) and that replay buffer size is adequate (gains diminish for very small M).  
- **Forgetting increases sharply:** This is a known trade‑off; may require stronger replay or regularization.  
- **Memory exhaustion:** Reduce batch size or apply KFAC only to linear layers; use mixed precision.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Sanity check:** Train on Split CIFAR‑100 with ER (M=1000) using NGD‑KFAC (λ=1.0) vs. SGD. Confirm an end‑accuracy improvement >2% absolute.  
2. **Damping sweep:** Fix ER with M=5000, vary λ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0}. Plot average end accuracy; expect peak at λ=1.0.  
3. **Buffer scaling:** Fix NGD‑KFAC (λ=1.0). Train ER with M=1k, 5k, 10k. Quantify the accuracy gain over SGD at each size; expect larger delta with larger M.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Optimal damping strategy?** The paper uses a fixed λ=1.0, justified by a sensitivity plot, but an adaptive schedule might be more efficient across phases. *Unresolved because* OCL is non‑stationary, so a single value may be suboptimal. *Resolving evidence:* Evaluate damping schedules (e.g., decay) or adaptive methods that adjust λ based on Fisher statistics.  
- **Why does NGD‑KFAC increase accuracy while also increasing forgetting?** Results show improved end accuracy but higher average forgetting. *Unresolved because* the interplay between natural gradient updates (which accelerate convergence on new tasks) and catastrophic forgetting mechanisms in OCL is not analyzed. *Resolving evidence:* Investigate parameter trajectories, Fisher evolution, or controlled experiments separating convergence speed from retention.  
- **Interaction with robust OCL architectures?** The authors suggest NGD‑KFAC may achieve better performance with more advanced OCL architectures, but this is not explored. *Unresolved because* experiments were limited to ResNet‑18 and standard replay methods. *Resolving evidence:* Combine NGD‑KFAC with state‑of‑the‑art OCL techniques (e.g., progressive networks, dynamic parameter allocation) across multiple benchmarks.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps:** Exact details of buffer management (reservoir sampling, class balance), Separated Softmax head freezing, and Review trick (epochs, sample counts) are unspecified; assumptions may deviate from the authors’ code.  
- **KFAC scope ambiguity:** Unclear whether KFAC is applied to all layers (including biases) and how convolutional activations are vectorized; small differences can affect memory, compute, and accuracy.  
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Only a fixed learning rate (0.1) and λ=1.0 are reported; interactions with other OCL methods or across datasets are not systematically studied.  
- **Breakpoints unknown:** The threshold where very small replay buffers (M<500) make NGD‑KFAC detrimental, and the conditions under which the Kronecker approximation fails due to high activation correlation, are not quantified.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Natural gradient improves geometry):** Medium – theory and overall gains support it, but direct isolation of the natural gradient’s contribution is weak.  
- **Mechanism 2 (High damping critical):** High – sensitivity curves consistently show λ=1.0 as optimal across methods, empirically validating the need for heavy damping.  
- **Mechanism 3 (Synergy with replay, scales with buffer):** High – quantified gains increase with buffer size, directly corroborating the claim.  
- **Overall outcome (NGD‑KFAC lifts average end accuracy):** High – results on three datasets with multiple OCL methods show consistent absolute improvements (~2‑3% on class‑incremental settings).
