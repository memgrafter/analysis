# Learning from Partial Chain-of-Thought via Truncated-Reasoning Self-Distillation
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13274
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13274
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: TRSD significantly improves accuracy under low reasoning budgets (up to +37.2 percentage points on GSM8K) and reduces inference-time reasoning length without explicit regularization.

## Executive Summary
Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in large language models often yields long, inefficient traces. This paper presents Truncated-Reasoning Self-Distillation (TRSD), a post-training method that adapts a model to generate correct answers from partial reasoning prefixes. A frozen teacher produces full reasoning traces; the student is trained to match the teacher's answer distribution when conditioned only on truncated prefixes of those traces. This objective encourages the student to extract predictive information early and ultimately generate shorter reasoning at test time. Across multiple model families and benchmarks, TRSD achieves substantial accuracy gains under low token budgets (e.g., +15.5 to +37.2 points on GSM8K) while preserving performance at larger budgets, and it reduces average reasoning length without any explicit length penalty.

## Method Summary
TRSD employs a frozen teacher model that, for each prompt, generates a full reasoning trace enclosed in `<think>` tags and an answer within `<answer>` tags. A student model, initialized as a copy of the teacher, is trained to predict the teacher's answer distribution given only a truncated prefix of the reasoning. The truncation ratio α is sampled uniformly from [0,1), and the prefix `r̄` is taken as the first ⌊α·|r|⌋ tokens. During training, the teacher's answer distribution `p_teacher(y|x, r)` is obtained with stop gradient, while the student computes `p_student(y|x, r̄)`. The loss is the KL divergence between these distributions, computed only over answer tokens. Training uses AdamW (LR 3e-6, batch 32, gradient clip 1.0) for 2000 steps. At inference, the model generates reasoning under a fixed token budget; if no answer appears, answer forcing appends `<answer>` and generates up to 200 additional tokens to produce a final answer. This approach does not explicitly penalize long reasoning but implicitly promotes brevity by exposing the student to truncated contexts.

## Key Results
- Under low token budgets (32–256 tokens), TRSD yields accuracy improvements of 15.5 to 37.2 percentage points on GSM8K compared to the baseline model.
- At larger budgets (e.g., 8192 tokens), TRSD maintains performance comparable to the teacher, with only minor regressions in some cases.
- TRSD-trained models generate shorter reasoning traces at unconstrained inference without any explicit length regularization, reducing computational overhead for both correct and incorrect predictions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- Claim: TRSD improves accuracy under truncated inference by training the student to recover the teacher's answer distribution from partial reasoning prefixes.
- Mechanism: The student model minimizes KL divergence between the teacher's answer distribution (conditioned on full reasoning) and its own answer distribution (conditioned on a truncated prefix). This forces the student to extract predictive information from early tokens, making it robust to truncation.
- Core assumption: The teacher's answer distribution given full reasoning is a reliable target; truncated prefixes contain sufficient signal for correct prediction if the model learns to use them effectively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "encourages models to produce correct predictions from partial reasoning traces"
  - [section 3.1]: "The core idea is to train a model to match its own predictions under full reasoning while being conditioned on truncated reasoning during training."
  - [corpus]: "Probing the Trajectories of Reasoning Traces in Large Language Models" (neighbor) suggests that answer-relevant information emerges early in reasoning trajectories, supporting the premise that truncated prefixes can suffice. However, this work is not cited by the paper, so the evidence is indirect.
- Break condition: If truncation removes all task-relevant information (e.g., prefix length near zero), the student cannot recover the correct answer, leading to performance collapse.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: TRSD yields shorter reasoning traces at inference without explicit length regularization because training on prefixes discourages reliance on late-stage tokens.
- Mechanism: During training, the student only sees truncated prefixes as context, never full reasoning. This implicitly teaches the model that the answer distribution can be matched without needing to generate or attend to long traces. Consequently, at inference the model may stop earlier or generate less verbose reasoning.
- Core assumption: The model's generation length is influenced by the distribution of context lengths seen during training; shorter contexts lead to shorter generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "TRSD-trained models also generate shorter reasoning traces at inference time without explicit length regularization"
  - [section 4.3]: "TRSD consistently reduces reasoning length for both correct and incorrect predictions."
  - [corpus]: "DeepPrune: Parallel Scaling without Inter-trace Redundancy" (neighbor) identifies redundancy in parallel reasoning traces, consistent with the notion that long traces contain unnecessary tokens. Not directly cited, but conceptually aligned.
- Break condition: For tasks that inherently require many steps, the model might not reduce length without hurting accuracy; overly aggressive truncation during training could cause under-generation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Self-distillation with a frozen teacher provides a stable training target and preserves the teacher's capabilities while adapting the student to truncated contexts.
- Mechanism: The teacher is frozen and generates full reasoning and answer distributions. The student, initialized as a copy, is trained to match the teacher's answer distribution given truncated reasoning. This avoids distribution shift that can occur if the teacher updates during distillation, and focuses learning on robustness to truncation rather than altering reasoning patterns.
- Core assumption: The teacher's reasoning process is sufficiently robust; and the student can match the teacher's answer distribution with limited context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1]: "Our design choice of freezing the teacher serves to provide a stable target distribution and allows the student to learn how to recover accurate predictions from its own truncated reasoning, rather than altering the underlying reasoning process itself."
  - [section 2]: Background on distillation pitfalls motivates the need for a stable target.
  - [corpus]: "Self-Distilled Reasoner: On-Policy Self-Distillation for Large Language Models" (neighbor) explores on-policy self-distillation but with a different objective; the stability claim is primarily supported by the paper's own design rationale.
- Break condition: If the teacher's reasoning is inefficient or biased, the student may inherit these flaws; also, if the teacher's answer distribution is mis-specified (e.g., due to sampling error), the student may be misled.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Teacher (frozen) → Truncation module → Student (trainable) → Answer extraction → KL divergence loss → Optimizer (AdamW).

Critical path: For each training batch: (a) Teacher generates full reasoning trace `r` and answer `y` for prompt `x` (with answer forcing if necessary). (b) A truncation ratio `α ~ U(0,1)` is sampled, and the reasoning is truncated to prefix `r̄`. (c) The teacher's answer distribution `p_teacher(y|x, r)` is computed with stop gradient. (d) The student computes `p_student(y|x, r̄)`. (e) The loss `L = KL(p_teacher || p_student)` is calculated only over answer tokens. (f) The student parameters are updated via backpropagation. (g) The process repeats.

Design tradeoffs:
- Truncation strategy: Uniform random prefix lengths expose the model to diverse truncation points but may include unrealistically short prefixes; alternatively, sampling from a distribution that mimics expected inference constraints could improve robustness.
- Teacher reasoning budget: The maximum number of tokens (e.g., 8192) used by the teacher influences the quality of the answer distribution; too short may yield incomplete reasoning.
- Answer forcing: Appending `<answer>` when missing ensures an answer is produced but may perturb the natural reasoning flow.
- Loss scope: Restricting KL divergence to answer tokens avoids backpropagation through the teacher's reasoning and focuses learning on the final prediction.
- Checkpoint selection: For in-distribution evaluation, the checkpoint with best performance on the same dataset is chosen; for out-of-distribution, the checkpoint with best average across all target datasets is selected. This choice affects reported generalization.
- No explicit length regularization: The method relies on emergent brevity; combining it with explicit length penalties might further control reasoning length.

Failure signatures:
- Performance drop at large reasoning budgets: In some configurations, TRSD models slightly underperform the baseline when allowed full reasoning (e.g., negative change at 8192 tokens).
- Sensitivity to self-distillation prompts: Gains vary with the dataset used to generate self-distillation prompts; no single prompt source is optimal for all model architectures.
- Out-of-distribution variation: Accuracy improvements on unseen datasets are often smaller or inconsistent compared to in-distribution results.
- Inconsistent reasoning length reduction: Some models (e.g., Phi-4-mini-reasoning in OOD settings) do not reduce reasoning length and may even increase it despite accuracy gains.

First 3 experiments:
1. In-distribution low-budget validation: Train TRSD on Countdown using Qwen3-0.6B. Evaluate at budgets 32, 64, and 128 tokens. Expect large accuracy improvements (>10 percentage points at 32 tokens) relative to the baseline.
2. Reasoning length measurement: After training, perform inference with no budget limit (up to 8192 tokens) and record average reasoning trace length for correct and incorrect answers. TRSD should produce shorter traces than the baseline.
3. Out-of-distribution transfer: Train TRSD on GSM8K with Qwen3-4B and evaluate on Math500 at budgets 128 and 256 tokens. Expect positive transfer, though gains may be smaller than in-distribution improvements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: What characteristics make self-distillation prompts effective for TRSD?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that identifying suitable prompts is under-explored.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested a few datasets and found no universal optimum; the driving properties (e.g., reasoning depth, diversity) are unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic variations of prompt attributes across models to isolate effective features.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can TRSD's objective be improved to avoid large-budget performance drops?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] They suggest adapting the objective based on truncation level or teacher confidence.
- Why unresolved: Uniform truncation sampling may imbalance robustness across budgets; dynamic weighting might help.
- What evidence would resolve it: Compare modified losses (e.g., weighting by truncation ratio) on accuracy vs. budget curves.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can truncation robustness be incorporated directly into standard knowledge distillation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] They propose merging robustness with distillation from larger teachers as a future direction.
- Why unresolved: It is unknown whether joint learning interferes with acquiring reasoning skills.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train students with a combined loss on truncated and full traces and compare to the two-stage TRSD pipeline.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Why does TRSD's effectiveness vary across model architectures and prompt distributions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that no single dataset is uniformly optimal and gains are sensitive to model–data interaction.
- Why unresolved: The underlying factors linking model inductive biases to prompt suitability remain unclear, limiting prompt selection.
- What evidence would resolve it: Correlate model characteristics (size, pretraining) and prompt features with TRSD performance to build a predictive framework.

## Limitations
- Prompt format and sanitization details are not fully specified, which may affect reproducibility of the self-distillation setup.
- The exact answer forcing procedure (e.g., when and how `<answer>` is appended) is ambiguous and could alter the context for answer generation.
- TRSD's effectiveness is sensitive to the choice of self-distillation prompts and model architecture, and out-of-distribution improvements are often inconsistent.

## Confidence
- Truncated robustness: High
- Emergent brevity: Medium
- Frozen teacher stability: Medium
