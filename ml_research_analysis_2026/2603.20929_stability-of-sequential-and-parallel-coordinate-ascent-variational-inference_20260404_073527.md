# Stability of Sequential and Parallel Coordinate Ascent Variational Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20929
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20929
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Primary result**: Sequential CAVI is locally asymptotically stable under a mild condition, while parallel CAVI can diverge in high-dimensional dense signal regimes.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies the convergence stability of sequential (Gauss–Seidel) and parallel (Jacobi) coordinate ascent variational inference (CAVI) for Bayesian linear regression with spike-and-slab priors. By recasting CAVI as a fixed-point iteration, the authors analyze the Jacobian of the update map at stationary points. They prove that sequential CAVI enjoys local asymptotic stability (spectral radius < 1) under a mild bound on variational inclusion probabilities (Assumption 1). In contrast, for parallel CAVI, they show that when the design matrix is high-dimensional (p > n/4) and the variational inclusion probabilities are close to one (dense signal regime), the spectral radius exceeds one with high probability, implying divergence. Simulations corroborate the theory: parallel CAVI frequently diverges, while sequential CAVI remains stable. This highlights a fundamental trade-off between computational parallelism and convergence guarantees.

## Method Summary
The authors formulate both sequential and parallel CAVI as fixed-point iterations on the variational parameters (inclusion probabilities and coefficient distributions). For a given stationary point, they derive the Jacobian matrix of the update map. For sequential CAVI, they prove that under Assumption 1—which bounds the variational inclusion probabilities—the Jacobian is strictly diagonally dominant, ensuring its spectral radius is strictly less than one and thus local asymptotic stability. For parallel CAVI, they employ random matrix theory to analyze the Jacobian’s spectral radius when the design matrix has i.i.d. sub-Gaussian entries. They show that in high dimensions (p > n/4) and when the variational inclusion probabilities are near one (dense signal), the spectral radius exceeds one with high probability, leading to divergence. The theoretical findings are supported by simulation experiments on synthetic data.

## Key Results
- Sequential CAVI is locally asymptotically stable (spectral radius < 1) under Assumption 1.
- Parallel CAVI diverges with high probability in high-dimensional settings (p > n/4) when variational inclusion probabilities are close to one.
- Simulations confirm that parallel CAVI frequently diverges, whereas sequential CAVI remains stable across tested regimes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The stability of an iterative algorithm is determined by the spectral radius of the Jacobian of its update map at a fixed point. Sequential CAVI (Gauss–Seidel) updates parameters one at a time, immediately using new values. This introduces a form of diagonal dominance in the Jacobian: the partial derivative of an updated parameter with respect to its own previous value is large relative to the cross-derivatives. Under Assumption 1, this diagonal dominance becomes strict, forcing all eigenvalues to lie inside the unit circle. Parallel CAVI (Jacobi), however, computes all updates based on the old state and applies them simultaneously. Its Jacobian lacks this diagonal dominance structure; in high dimensions with dense signals, the off-diagonal terms—which involve products of inclusion probabilities and design matrix inner products—become large enough that the spectral radius exceeds one, causing divergence. The random matrix theory analysis captures this phase transition as the dimension grows.

**Critical Path**

2. Variational family: mean-field with Gaussian slab and Bernoulli spike.  
3. Derive exact CAVI update rules for q(γ) and q(β).  
4. Cast updates as a fixed-point map Φ.  
5. Compute Jacobian ∂Φ/∂θ at a stationary point θ*.  
6. For sequential: prove diagonal dominance of J_seq under Assumption 1 → ρ(J_seq) < 1.  
7. For parallel: analyze J_par using random matrix theory → ρ(J_par) > 1 w.h.p. when p > n/4 and q(γ)≈1.  
8. Validate via simulations.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- **Sequential vs Parallel**: Sequential updates guarantee local stability but are inherently sequential, limiting parallel speedup. Parallel updates allow simultaneous coordinate updates, enabling parallelism, but risk divergence in high-dimensional dense regimes.  
- **Local vs Global**: The analysis is local (linearization around a fixed point); it does not guarantee global convergence. A method may be locally stable but still diverge from certain initializations.  
- **Assumption 1 Dependency**: The sequential stability proof requires Assumption 1, a bound on variational inclusion probabilities. This condition may not hold early in inference if initial q(γ) are too large, potentially causing transient instability.

**Failure Signatures**  
- **Parallel CAVI**: Divergence manifests as exploding variational parameters (e.g., q(γ) → 0 or 1 extremes) and decreasing ELBO (since variational inference maximizes ELBO). Numerically, the spectral radius ρ(J_par) > 1.  
- **Sequential CAVI**: If Assumption 1 is violated, the Jacobian may lose diagonal dominance, and the spectral radius could approach or exceed 1, leading to slow convergence or oscillations. However, the theory predicts that under Assumption 1, such behavior cannot persist asymptotically.

**First Experiments**  
1. **Baseline convergence test**: Implement both sequential and parallel CAVI for a small synthetic linear regression (e.g., n=30, p=10) with moderate signal density (e.g., 30% nonzero coefficients). Verify that both converge to similar ELBO values and that the sequential version is stable.  
2. **Divergence stress test**: Increase dimension ratio (e.g., n=50, p=30, 40, 50) and set true coefficients dense (many nonzeros) and initialize q(γ) close to 1. Observe that parallel CAVI diverges for p > n/4 while sequential remains stable.  
3. **Spectral radius estimation**: After running sequential CAVI to convergence, numerically compute the Jacobian of both update maps at the fixed point via finite differences. Compute eigenvalues and spectral radii; confirm that ρ(J_seq) < 1 and ρ(J_par) > 1 under high-dimensional dense conditions.

## Limitations
- The stability guarantee for sequential CAVI relies on a diagonal dominance condition derived from the spike-and-slab prior; it is unclear whether this condition holds for other priors or non-linear models.  
- Assumption 1 (a bound on variational inclusion probabilities) is a condition on the algorithm’s current state; its practical satisfaction during inference and sensitivity to prior hyperparameters require empirical investigation.  
- The divergence result for parallel CAVI is asymptotic (high probability as dimension grows) and specific to the dense signal regime; the precise finite-sample phase transition and behavior for sparser signals are not characterized.

## Confidence
- **Sequential stability proof**: High (within the defined model class the analysis is rigorous).  
- **Parallel divergence theory**: Medium (depends on random matrix theory assumptions, e.g., sub-Gaussian design entries).  
- **Simulation validation**: Medium (simulations confirm trends but are limited to a few synthetic settings).

## Next Checks
1. Extend the theoretical analysis to a different conjugate-exponential family model, such as logistic regression with a corresponding variational family, to see if a similar diagonal dominance condition emerges.  
2. Empirically map the region where Assumption 1 holds for sequential CAVI by running it from a wide grid of initializations and prior hyperparameters; correlate violations with any observed non-asymptotic instability.  
3. Conduct a systematic simulation study for parallel CAVI, varying n and p around the theoretical threshold (p ≈ n/4) and varying the true sparsity level, to estimate the probability of divergence as a function of these parameters and characterize the practical transition zone.
