# Arithmetic OOD Failure Unfolds in Stages in Minimal GPTs
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.26828
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26828
*   **Reference count:** 7
*   **Primary result:** Arithmetic OOD generalization fails in a sequence of four experimentally separable stages: layout barrier, carry-flag semantic error, conditional recomposition bottleneck, and a final carry-conditioned tens residual.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates why small GPT models fail to generalize from training on exhaustive 2-digit addition to 3-digit addition. Through rigorous matched interventions and seed sweeps, the authors decompose the monolithic failure into four distinct, sequential barriers. The work demonstrates that generalization is not a single obstacle but a cascade of acquired capabilities—from handling digit layout, to understanding carry semantics, to conditional tail recomposition, and finally to resolving tens polarity under carry. This stage-by-stage analysis replaces a single accuracy metric with a testable, mechanistic breakdown of failure modes in a minimal setting.

## Method Summary
The study uses minimal Transformer models trained exhaustively on all 2-digit addition problems (e.g., 34+56). Generalization is tested on 3-digit problems (e.g., 123+456). To isolate failure stages, the authors perform carefully matched interventions: they vary training data composition (e.g., mixing 2- and 3-digit problems), create targeted probes to test specific internal representations (e.g., probing the hundreds digit as a binary carry flag), and control the distribution of conditioned test data (e.g., high-conditioned tails where later digits depend on earlier carries). Performance is averaged over multiple random seeds to ensure statistical reliability.

## Key Results
*   **Stage 1 - Layout Barrier:** Models trained only on 2-digit inputs fail completely (~0 exact match) on 3-digit layouts. Simply exposing the model to mixed 2- and 3-digit sequences during training raises exact match to 0.44, solving the layout problem.
*   **Stage 2 - Carry-Flag Error:** The model misrepresents the hundreds position as a binary flag (0 or 1) instead of a numeric digit. Targeted interventions that explicitly teach or probe this carry flag raise exact match modestly to 0.033–0.045.
*   **Stage 3 - Conditional Recomposition:** The model struggles to recombine digit tails when earlier digits carry. Reconditioning the training data to have a higher proportion of such "high-conditioned" tail cases improves exact match to 0.541–0.700 and significantly boosts the probability of an exact answer given correctness on the first two digits.
*   **Stage 4 - Tens-Polarity Residual:** Even after prior stages, the model errs on the tens digit polarity under specific thousands-carry conditions. A targeted "tens-polarity repair" intervention increases exact match on this hardest suite from 0.664 to 0.822 across 10 seeds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The failure is sequential because each stage requires the model to acquire and correctlyutilize a new, prerequisite representation or computational primitive. A model cannot apply carry semantics (Stage 2) until it has a layout for three digits (Stage 1). It cannot correctly recompose conditional tails (Stage 3) until it computes carries accurately. The final tens-polarity error (Stage 4) is a subtle residual flaw in the carry propagation logic itself, exposed only on the most challenging conditional paths. Interventions succeed because they directly address the missing or faulty representation at each specific bottleneck, rather than improving general capacity.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Tokenization & Digit Embedding:** Why needed: Maps individual characters to vector space. Quick check: Are digit embeddings linearly separable and position-agnostic?
*   **Positional Encoding:** Why needed: Injects digit order (place value) into the model. Quick check: Does swapping digit positions swap the output prediction?
*   **Self-Attention for Digit Interaction:** Why needed: Allows direct interaction between non-adjacent digits for carry propagation. Quick check: Can attention heads be identified that focus from a lesser to a greater place value?
*   **Sequential Processing (Causal Mask):** Why needed: Enforces left-to-right generation, mirroring arithmetic. Quick check: Does model accuracy drop if causal masking is removed during training?
*   **Output Head as Numeric Decoder:** Why needed: Converts final token representations into a probability distribution over digit strings. Quick check: Is the output layer's weight matrix structured for summing place-value embeddings?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Input Tokens -> Token Embeddings -> Positional Encoding -> [Transformer Block*N] -> LayerNorm -> Output Projection -> Digit Probabilities
*   **Critical Path:** Token embedding (digit identity) + positional encoding (place value) -> first Transformer block (initial digit mixing) -> intermediate blocks (carry computation & propagation) -> final block (aggregation) -> output projection (digit-wise prediction).
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** Depth vs. Width: Minimal GPTs use few layers/heads, forcing each component to be highly interpretable but limiting capacity. Causal Masking: Essential for sequence modeling but restricts bidirectional carry-resolution. Shared Output Head: Efficient but may struggle with precise arithmetic sum decomposition.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    1.  **Layout Barrier:** Complete failure on sequences longer than training.
    2.  **Carry-Flag:** Hundreds digit saturates at 0/1 regardless of true carry value.
    3.  **Recomposition:** First digits correct but tail incorrect *only* when earlier digits carry.
    4.  **Tens-Polarity:** Tens digit wrong with a specific sign pattern under deep carry chains.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Probe the hundreds digit:** Train on 2-digit only, test on 3-digit. Extract the representation that predicts the hundreds digit. Is it binary (0/1) or continuous/numeric?
    2.  **Controlled carry injection:** Create a synthetic dataset where the carry into the tens place is fixed (e.g., always 1). Does model accuracy on the tens digit depend on this forced carry?
    3.  **High-conditioned tail sweep:** Train with varying percentages of 3-digit problems that have carries into the hundreds place. Plot exact match vs. % of high-conditioned tail data to isolate Stage 3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Generality:** Do these stages hold for multiplication, division, or non-decimal systems?
*   **Interaction Isolation:** Are the stages truly independent, or are there hidden dependencies (e.g., layout affecting carry learning)?
*   **Scaling:** Do these barriers persist in larger models (e.g., Llama-3)?

## Limitations
*   Findings are based on minimal GPTs; scalability to state-of-the-art model sizes is unproven.
*   The decomposition is specific to decimal addition; transfer to other arithmetic operations is unknown.
*   Matched interventions suggest separability but cannot completely rule out complex interactions between stages.

## Confidence
*   **Stage decomposition methodology:** **High** (rigorous matched interventions + seed sweeps).
*   **Carry-flag semantic error as distinct barrier:** **Medium-High** (directly probed, but demonstrated in minimal model).
*   **Final tens-polarity repair generalizability:** **Low-Medium** (specific intervention in minimal setting).

## Next Checks
1.  **Replicate stage interventions in a 7B+ model:** Use identical training/data protocols with a large model (e.g., Llama-3) to see if the same barrier sequence and intervention effectiveness hold.
2.  **Test stage sequence on multiplication:** Train on 2-digit by 2-digit multiplication and test on 3-digit by 3-digit. Does the failure decompose into the same or a different sequence of stages?
3.  **Architecture comparison:** Train a minimal RNN or SSM on the same 2-digit addition suite. If the order or nature of the stages differs, it indicates the barrier sequence is Transformer-specific.
