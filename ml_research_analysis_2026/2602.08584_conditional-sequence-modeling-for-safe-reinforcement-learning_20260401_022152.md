# Conditional Sequence Modeling for Safe Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08584
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08584
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RCDT enables zero-shot adaptation to multiple cost thresholds in offline safe RL, outperforming baselines on DSRL benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Offline safe reinforcement learning aims to learn policies from fixed datasets that maximize return while satisfying cost constraints. However, most existing methods are limited to a single preset threshold, requiring retraining for different constraints. RCDT addresses this by combining a conditional sequence modeling backbone (like Decision Transformer) with an adaptive Lagrangian penalty, reward-cost-aware trajectory reweighting, and Q-value regularization. The adaptive penalty coefficient is updated via dual ascent, allowing the policy to automatically balance return and cost without fixing a threshold. Trajectory reweighting and Q-regularization shape the effective data distribution toward favorable return-cost trade-offs and mitigate conservatism. Theoretical analysis shows that the mismatch between conditioned targets and realized outcomes scales with the joint return-cost coverage in the dataset. Experiments on SafetyGym, BulletSafetyGym, and MetaDrive demonstrate that RCDT consistently achieves higher normalized returns while keeping costs below thresholds, and a single trained policy generalizes zero-shot to multiple cost constraints.

## Method Summary
RCDT extends the Conditional Decision Transformer (CDT) for offline safe RL. It conditions a Transformer policy on target return (RTG) and target cost (CTG) tokens. The training loss combines three innovations: (1) trajectory reweighting using weights `Wτ = exp(αRτ) * sigmoid(γ(clim - Cτ))` to emphasize high-return, low-cost trajectories; (2) Q-value regularization `-η E[Qψ(s,a)]` to encourage long-term value and avoid myopic imitation; (3) an auto-adaptive Lagrangian penalty `λ E[Cξ(s,a)]` where λ is updated via dual ascent based on the estimated cost. Separate reward and cost critics are trained with offline TD learning. At inference, zero-shot adaptation is achieved by setting initial RTG/CTG tokens. The method requires precomputing trajectory weights and tuning hyperparameters α, γ, clim, η, and the dual step size β.

## Key Results
- RCDT achieves higher normalized returns than baseline methods while maintaining expected costs below specified thresholds across multiple tasks (SafetyGym, BulletSafetyGym, MetaDrive).
- A single RCDT policy can be evaluated zero-shot at different cost thresholds without retraining, demonstrating flexible trade-off adaptation.
- Theoretically, the error due to conditioning on return-cost targets is bounded by a term that scales with the inverse of joint return-cost coverage, highlighting the importance of dataset diversity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The performance of naive CTG-conditioned CSM is fundamentally limited by *joint return–cost coverage* in the offline dataset.
- **Mechanism:** The mismatch between the specified RTG/CTG targets and the realized policy outcomes scales with `O(ε(1/α_F + 2)H²)`, where `α_F` lower-bounds the probability that a trajectory from the behavior policy matches the target return-cost profile. Low coverage (`small α_F`) for ambitious targets (high RTG, low CTG) leads to unreliable zero-shot control.
- **Core assumption:** Joint return-cost coverage exists (`Pπβ((R(τ),C(τ)) = F(s1) | s1) ≥ α_F`) and dynamics are near-deterministic.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...the mismatch between conditioned targets and realized outcomes scales with joint return–cost coverage."
  - [section 4.1, Theorem 1] "Theorem 1 formalizes the alignment behavior... this mismatch admits an upper bound that scales as O(ε(1/α_F + 2)H²)... when the desired return–cost conditioning function F(·) has low coverage (small α_F), the induced optimal policy may substantially deviate..."
  - [corpus] Strong neighbor consensus on CSM-based safe RL; this mechanism uniquely quantifies the *coverage limitation*.
- **Break condition:** When `α_F` is very small (safe, high-return trajectories are rare in `D`), specifying targets via RTG/CTG alone fails to produce feasible, high-return policies, leading to constraint violations or conservative behavior.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** An auto-adaptive Lagrangian penalty coefficient, updated via dual ascent on the current cost estimate, provides a principled regularizer that decouples training from a fixed threshold while steering toward safety.
- **Mechanism:** The training loss is augmented with `λ * E[Cπθ(st,at)]`. The coefficient `λ` is updated as `λ ← [λ + β(Ĵc(θ) - κ)]_+`, where `Ĵc(θ)` is a minibatch cost estimate and `κ` is a reference level. This increases penalty when estimated cost exceeds `κ` and relaxes it otherwise, automatically tuning the safety-performance trade-off during training.
- **Core assumption:** A reliable cost critic `Cξ` approximating `Cπθ` is available; the update rule provides a stable dual-ascent process.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...integrates a Lagrangian-style cost penalty with an auto-adaptive penalty coefficient."
  - [section 4.3] "Introducing a Lagrange multiplier λ≥0 leads to the Lagrangian... we treat the cost term as a flexible regulariser whose influence is controlled by an adaptive coefficient λ... update λ according to whether the current policy is above or below the target cost level... provides an automatic way to tune the strength of safety regularisation during training."
  - [section 5.2.1] Ablations show that removing this component (`WCDT`, `QCDT`) increases cost compared to full `RCDT`.
- **Break condition:** If the cost critic is inaccurate (e.g., due to severe distribution shift), the `λ` update becomes unstable, potentially causing oscillatory training or diverging penalties.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reward–cost-aware trajectory reweighting (`Wτ`) and Q-value regularization (`-η*E[Qπ]`) jointly mitigate the conservatism induced by safety constraints while preserving zero-shot adaptability.
- **Mechanism:** The loss is `-Σ Wτ log π(at|...) - η E[Qπ] + λ E[Cπ]`. `Wτ = exp(αR(τ)) * sigmoid(γ(clim - C(τ)))` upweights trajectories with high return and low cost. The Q-term provides an extrinsic signal encouraging long-term value, counteracting the myopic imitation bias of pure NLL and the short-term cost avoidance from `λE[Cπ]`.
- **Core assumption:** The reweighting function `Wτ` effectively captures desirable trade-offs; the Q-critic provides a reasonable estimate of the current policy's value.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...combined with reward–cost-aware trajectory reweighting and Q-value regularization... shape an effective data distribution toward favorable return–cost trade-offs."
  - [section 4.2] "To avoid overly conservative behavior... we (i) reshape the effective training distribution via trajectory-level reweighting toward more desirable return–cost profiles; and (ii) incorporate value-based guidance as an additional optimization signal to discourage overly conservative behaviors."
  - [section 4.2, Proposition 1] KL-based expert regularization (like RDT) is a special case of trajectory weighting with `Wτ = 1 + α*I[τ∈De]`.
  - [section 5.2.1] Ablations: `WQDT` (weighting + Q-reg) improves return over CDT but increases cost; `RCDT` (full) lowers cost back without sacrificing return gain. Removing either mechanism degrades the trade-off.
- **Break condition:** If `Wτ` is poorly tuned (e.g., `clim` too low), it overfits to a narrow high-return/low-cost subset, harming coverage and generalization. If the Q-critic is poorly trained, the Q-regularization term may misguide the policy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP)**
  - **Why needed here:** RCDT optimizes a policy for a CMDP: `max E[R] s.t. E[C] ≤ κ`. The cost constraint and its Lagrangian relaxation are central to the algorithm's design and theory.
  - **Quick check question:** Given an offline dataset with state, action, reward, and cost at each step, how would you formally define the expected cumulative cost constraint for a deterministic policy?

- **Concept: Conditional Sequence Modeling (CSM) / Decision Transformer**
  - **Why needed here:** RCDT builds on the CSM paradigm (specifically CDT), modeling `π(at|st, RTG_t, CTG_t, context)` via a Transformer. This backbone enables zero-shot adaptation via target token manipulation.
  - **Quick check question:** In a DT-style architecture, what are the four input sequences used during inference, and how are the RTG/CTG tokens updated after each environment step?

- **Concept: Offline RL Distribution Shift & Conservatism**
  - **Why needed here:** The core challenge is learning from a fixed dataset without online data. Naive imitation (BC) is unsafe; value-based methods can be overly conservative or violate constraints. RCDT's components explicitly address these failures (e.g., trajectory reweighting combats conservatism from filtering safe-only data).
  - **Quick check question:** Why might a policy trained only on "safe" trajectories (low cumulative cost) in an offline dataset perform poorly on the task objective, even if it satisfies the constraint?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Transformer Policy (`πθ`)**: Autoregressive model. Inputs: concatenated sequences of past `(state, action)`, target `RTG`, target `CTG`. Outputs: parameters (mean, log-var) of a Gaussian policy for the next action.
  2. **Reward Critic (`Qψ`)**: 4-layer MLP. Trained via offline TD to estimate `Qπθ`. Used in the `-η E[Qπ]` regularization term.
  3. **Cost Critic (`Cξ`)**: 4-layer MLP. Trained via offline TD to estimate `Cπθ`. Used to compute `Ĵc(θ)` for the dual variable update.
  4. **Trajectory Weight Calculator**: Pre-computes `Wτ = exp(αR(τ)) * sigmoid(γ(clim - C(τ)))` for every trajectory `τ` in `D`. Applied as a scalar to the NLL loss per trajectory.
  5. **Adaptive Coefficient `λ`**: Scalar updated per iteration: `λ ← [λ + β(Ĵc(θ) - κ)]_+`. Drives the strength of the explicit cost penalty term `λ * E[Cπ]`.

- **Critical path:**
  1. **Preprocessing**: Compute cumulative return `R(τ)` and cost `C(τ)` for all `τ∈D`. Compute static trajectory weights `Wτ`.
  2. **Iteration**:
     a. Sample trajectory `τ` from `D`.
     b. Sample a length-`K` subsequence. Form CDT inputs `(si, RTG_i, CTG_i, past context)`.
     c. **Critic Update**: From the subsequence, get next-state action `âK` from current policy. Update `Qψ` and `Cξ` using standard offline TD targets.
     d. **Policy Update**: For each step `i` in subsequence, sample `âi ~ πθ`. Compute loss `L_RCDT` using `Wτ`, `Qψ(si,âi)`, `Cξ(si,âi)`. Update `θ`.
     e. **Dual Update**: Compute `Ĵc(θ)` = average `Cξ(si,âi)` over subsequence. Update `λ`.
  3. **Inference**: Zero-shot. Initialize with target RTG/CTG. Autoregressively predict `K` actions per step, execute last one. Update both RTG and CTG tokens by subtracting observed reward/cost.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **CSM backbone vs. Value-based:** CSM avoids bootstrapping errors and enables direct goal (RTG/CTG) specification, but relies heavily on dataset coverage for high-return/safe behaviors.
  - **Trajectory weighting vs. filtering:** Reweighting (soft) retains more data coverage than hard filtering (like `BC-Safe`), reducing conservatism, but requires careful choice of `clim, α, γ`.
  - **Adaptive `λ` vs. fixed permutation:** Auto-update removes need to pre-specify a single `κ` for training and supports multi-threshold evaluation, but introduces a new hyperparameter (`β`) and depends on critic accuracy.
  - **Q-regularization strength (`η`):** Too high may overfit to inaccurate Q-values; too low may not sufficiently counter conservative imitation. Requires environment-specific tuning (via TPE in paper).

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High cost on safety-critical tasks:** Check `α_F` (coverage) for your target RTG/CTG. If very small, conditioning alone will fail. If `λ` updates are unstable (oscillating wildly), the cost critic may be poor.
  - **Poor return despite low cost:** Likely over-conservative. Could be from `Wτ` weighting that is too strict (`clim` too low, `γ` too high) or from `λ` growing too large and suppressing all non-minimum-cost actions.
  - **Inability to vary cost zero-shot:** Model may have overfitted to a single implicit trade-off. Inspect trajectory weights: if `Wτ` has a narrow peak, the policy learns a narrow solution. Increase `γ`/`α` smoothing.
  - **Diverging training loss:** Could indicate `η` is too large, causing the Q-regularization term to dominate with bad gradients. Reduce `η` or check Q-critic initialization.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Main performance comparison (Table 2):** Trains RCDT once per dataset (with fixed `κ` for training). Evaluates *zero-shot* across multiple cost thresholds (e.g., {20,40,80}) and compares normalized return/cost to baselines (BC, CDT, BCQ-Lag, etc.). Confirms RCDT achieves best safe return-cost trade-off on most tasks.
  2. **Component ablation (Table 4):** Ablates combinations of (CSM backbone, trajectory weighting, Q-guidance, cost penalty). Shows (a) CSM backbone is superior to BC-safe, (b) each proposed component (weighting, Q-reg, penalty) contributes, (c) full combination is necessary for best trade-off.
  3. **Robustness to data scarcity/imbalance (Tables 5 & 6):** Tests on variants with lower expert trajectory coverage and more imbalanced return distributions. RCDT maintains competitive returns and costs better than CDT and BC as data quality degrades, demonstrating robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we construct CSM-based policies for offline safe RL that automatically balance return maximization and cost constraint satisfaction across multiple thresholds without retraining?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The introduction poses: "how can we endow CSM-based policies with a principled and robust mechanism for achieving favorable return–cost trade-offs while preserving their zero-shot adaptation flexibility?"
- **Why unresolved:** While RCDT combines trajectory reweighting, Q-regularization, and an adaptive penalty, the relative importance of these components and their interactions are not fully understood; simpler or more generalizable designs might exist.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A large-scale empirical study varying all components independently across many tasks, along with theoretical analysis of their sample efficiency and robustness to dataset shift, would clarify the design principles.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can we quantify and ensure sufficient joint return-cost coverage (α_F) in offline datasets to guarantee that RTG/CTG conditioning yields reliable zero-shot control?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Theorem 1 shows the performance bound depends on 1/α_F, but α_F is a dataset property that is typically unknown and may be small for high-return low-cost targets.
- **Why unresolved:** Offline datasets often have limited coverage of safe high-reward behaviors; without estimating α_F, users cannot know whether a desired RTG/CTG is achievable or how to adjust hyperparameters.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Developing practical estimators for α_F from offline data and demonstrating that these estimates predict actual policy performance gaps would enable users to set feasible conditioning targets and tune methods like RCDT accordingly.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the adaptive dual-ascent update for λ in RCDT converge to a stable value that ensures constraint satisfaction under offline RL conditions?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method updates λ via λ ← [λ+β(ˆJ_c(θ)-κ)]+, but no convergence proof is given, and the offline setting introduces bias due to distribution shift and function approximation.
- **Why unresolved:** In online RL, such updates are well-studied, but offline RL's fixed dataset may cause the cost estimate to be unrepresentative, potentially destabilizing λ and leading to infeasible policies.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical convergence analysis under offline RL assumptions (e.g., concentrability, bounded approximation error) or extensive experiments monitoring λ and cost violation over training across diverse datasets would provide evidence of stability.

## Limitations
- Theorem 1’s assumptions (deterministic dynamics, coverage α_F) may not hold in practice, weakening theoretical bounds.
- Adaptive λ depends on an accurate cost critic; critic errors can cause unstable updates.
- Hyperparameters for trajectory reweighting (α, γ, clim) are undisclosed and tuning‑sensitive.
- RCDT requires a dataset with sufficient high‑return, low‑cost trajectories; otherwise ambitious targets are infeasible.

## Confidence

- Adaptive Lagrangian (Mechanism 2): Medium.
- Reweighting + Q‑regularization (Mechanism 3): Medium.
- Overall SOTA performance: High.
