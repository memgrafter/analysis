# SphUnc: Hyperspherical Uncertainty Decomposition and Causal Identification via Information Geometry

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.01168
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01168
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: SphUnc combines hyperspherical vMF latent representation with structural causal modeling to decompose uncertainty (epistemic via vMF entropy, aleatoric via variance head) and identify causal influences, achieving state-of-the-art predictive performance (+7.1 F1 on SNARE, +6.6 AUC on PHEME, +6.8 Acc on AMIGOS), superior calibration (ECE down to 0.022), and high causal recovery precision (P@10 up to 0.81).

## Executive Summary
Existing methods for multi-agent systems often fail to provide both calibrated uncertainty estimates and interpretable causal insights, especially under higher-order interactions and mixed uncertainty types. This work introduces SphUnc, a unified framework that combines hyperspherical representation learning (using von Mises-Fisher distributions on the unit sphere) with structural causal modeling. SphUnc decomposes predictive uncertainty into epistemic (via the entropy of the vMF concentration) and aleatoric (via a learned variance head) components, fusing them through a monotonic mapping for calibrated total uncertainty. It also identifies directed influences through a sparse causal model on the spherical latents, supporting interventional reasoning.

Empirical evaluations on three diverse multi-agent benchmarks—SNARE (offline networks), PHEME (Twitter threads), and AMIGOS (multimodal affect)—demonstrate SphUnc's superiority. It achieves gains of up to +7.1 F1, +6.6 AUC, and +6.8 accuracy over baselines, reduces expected calibration error (ECE) to as low as 0.022, and attains causal recovery precision (P@10) between 0.75 and 0.81. These results highlight the effectiveness of hyperspherical geometry and causal latent modeling in multi-agent settings.

## Method Summary
SphUnc processes temporal hypergraphs by embedding each agent's features into a unit-norm latent vector \( h_i^t \) on a hypersphere. A von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distribution over the sphere parameterized by concentration \( \kappa_i^t \) (output by a head \( \rho_\psi \)) yields epistemic uncertainty as its entropy \( H_{\text{sph}}(\kappa) \). Aleatoric uncertainty is independently estimated by a variance head \( \sigma_\phi^2(x_i^t) \). These two uncertainties are fused via a monotonic mapping \( g_\omega \) to produce total uncertainty \( U_{\text{total}} \), which is calibrated to an empirical error proxy \( \tilde{U}_{\text{emp}} \) (e.g., squared residuals on held-out data) using an MSE loss \( L_{\text{entropy}} \).

A structural causal model (SCM) operates on the spherical latents: each \( h_i^t \) is generated by a function \( F_i \) that conditionally depends on its learned sparse causal parents \( \text{Pa}(i) \) from the previous time step, its own features, and exogenous noise. Hypergraph message passing with angular attention (temperature \( \kappa_a \)) aggregates parent influences, which are then transformed by a causal projection \( W_c \) and gating. The overall loss combines task prediction loss \( L_{\text{pred}} \), uncertainty calibration loss \( L_{\text{entropy}} \), and causal regularization \( L_{\text{causal}} \) (promoting sparsity). Interventions are simulated by fixing latent states and propagating forward through the SCM with Monte Carlo sampling.

## Key Results
- Predictive performance: +7.1 F1 on SNARE, +6.6 AUC on PHEME, +6.8 accuracy on AMIGOS over strong baselines.
- Calibration: Expected Calibration Error (ECE) reduced to 0.022 (SNARE), 0.018 (PHEME), 0.025 (AMIGOS).
- Causal interpretability: Precision@10 (P@10) of 0.78 (SNARE), 0.81 (PHEME), 0.75 (AMIGOS) against expert influence links.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Hyperspherical embeddings with von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distributions enable a geometry-aware measure of epistemic uncertainty via concentration entropy.
- **Mechanism**: Input features are projected to unit-norm latent vectors `hi_t`. A concentration head `ρ_ψ` outputs `κ_i_t`, parameterizing a vMF distribution on the sphere. The hyperspherical entropy `Hsph(κ)` (negative expected log-density) quantifies directional dispersion: high `κ` implies low entropy (high confidence, low epistemic uncertainty). This entropy serves as `U_epi`.
- **Core assumption**: The latent directional state on the unit sphere captures the model's belief about the underlying process, and angular dispersion (concentration) reflects reducible uncertainty. The vMF distribution is an adequate model for these directional beliefs.
- **Evidence anchors**:
   - [abstract] "maps features to unit hypersphere latents using von Mises-Fisher distributions"
   - [section] Page 4: "We model directional beliefs with the von Mises–Fisher (vMF) family." and Eq.(3) defines `Hsph(κ)`.
   - [corpus] Neighbor papers also exploit hyperspherical latents: "Hyperspherical Latents Improve Continuous-Token Autoregressive Generation" (arxiv:2509.24335), "Contrastive Diffusion Alignment: Learning Structured Latents for Controllable Generation" (arxiv:2510.14190).
- **Break condition**: If magnitude information is essential (not just direction), the sphere projection loses information. vMF's single concentration parameter may be insufficient for multi-modal directional beliefs. Numerical instability in `Hsph(κ)` for extreme `κ` could also cause failures.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Uncertainty decomposition is achieved by separately estimating epistemic (vMF entropy) and aleatoric (heteroscedastic variance) components and fusing them via a monotonic learnable mapping, enabling calibrated total uncertainty.
- **Mechanism**: `U_epi = Hsph(κ_i_t)` from the vMF head. `U_alea = σ_ϕ^2(x_i_t)` from a separate variance head. These are combined by `U_total = g_ω(U_epi, U_alea)` where `g_ω` is monotonic in each input. The model minimizes `L_entropy = (U_total - bU_emp)^2` to align total uncertainty with an empirical proxy (e.g., squared residuals on held-out data).
- **Core assumption**: The true predictive variance can be factorized into epistemic and aleatoric parts that are statistically separable, and a monotonic fusion suffices to combine them without loss of calibration. The empirical proxy `bU_emp` is an unbiased estimator of the true irreducible noise.
- **Evidence anchors**:
   - [abstract] "decomposing uncertainty into epistemic and aleatoric components through information-geometric fusion"
   - [section] Page 5: Eq.(4)-(5) and surrounding text: "We decompose total predictive uncertainty into two components... fused through a learnable, monotone mapping"
   - [corpus] Neighbors address uncertainty decomposition: "Machine Learning for Stress Testing: Uncertainty Decomposition in Causal Panel Prediction" (arxiv:2603.07438) explicitly tackles uncertainty separation in causal settings.
- **Break condition**: If epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties are correlated in the data (e.g., high aleatoric noise also inflates directional dispersion), the separation becomes entangled and `U_epi` may capture some aleatoric effects. Misspecification of `g_ω` or poor estimation of `bU_emp` yields miscalibration.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: A structural causal model (SCM) defined on spherical latent states, learned with sparse directed edges, enables identification of causal influences and simulation of interventions for counterfactual reasoning.
- **Mechanism**: For each agent `i`, `hi_t` is generated by a structural equation `hi_t = F_i({hj_{t-1} for j in Pa(i)}, x_i_t, U_i, ε_i_t)` where `Pa(i)` are causal parents (learned sparse set). Directed message passing (with projection `Wc` and gating) aggregates parent influences. Interventions `do(h*)` replace `hi_t` with fixed values and propagate forward through the structural equations (Monte Carlo simulation). The interventional entropy `H_causal` quantifies uncertainty about downstream outcomes.
- **Core assumption**: The true data-generating process is an SCM on some latent spherical states, and these latents are recoverable from observed features. Temporal ordering (Assumption 1) and sparsity (Assumption 2) ensure identifiability.
- **Evidence anchors**:
   - [abstract] "structural causal model on spherical latents enables directed influence identification and interventional reasoning via sample-based simulation"
   - [section] Page 6: Eq.(6) and text: "We model dynamics and directed influence with a structural causal model (SCM) defined on spherical latents."
   - [corpus] Neighbors: "Causal Spherical Hypergraph Networks for Modelling Social Uncertainty" (arxiv:2506.17840) directly combines spherical embeddings and causal modeling in social systems.
- **Break condition**: If the latent spherical representation is not sufficiently expressive to encode the true causal states, or if there are unobserved confounders violating SCM assumptions, the learned parent sets may be incorrect. Non-stationarity or distribution shift after training can also degrade interventional predictions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Features → Input projection (linear + L2) → unit sphere latent `h_i^t` → (vMF concentration head → `κ_i_t`) & (aleatoric variance head → `σ_i^2`) → fusion module `g_ω` → total uncertainty `U_total`; parallel path: `h_i^t` → spherical hypergraph message passing (angular attention, aggregation) → causal projection `W_c` & gating → structural equations `F_i` (SCM with sparse parents) → predictions & interventional outputs; losses `L_pred`, `L_entropy`, `L_causal`; interventional simulation via `do-` operator and Monte Carlo sampling.

**Critical path**: Features → projection → `h_i^t` → (a) vMF head & aleatoric head → fusion → `U_total`; (b) message passing + causal module → predictions and interventional outputs. The two streams share the `h_i^t` representation.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Hyperspherical vs. Euclidean*: Sphere imposes orientation-only representation; may lose magnitude info but improves angular discrimination and stability. Computation of vMF entropy adds overhead but is manageable with approximations.
- *Monotonic fusion*: Simple and interpretable but may not capture complex interactions between epistemic and aleatoric sources; a more flexible fusion could improve calibration but risk losing interpretability.
- *SCM on latents*: Enables interventional reasoning but relies on correctness of latent encoding; if `h_i^t` is not a valid causal representation, the SCM may be misspecified. Sparsity regularization helps but might underfit dense influence structures.
- *Angular attention*: Uses dot product on sphere; similar to cosine attention but with concentration `κa` as temperature. Computationally similar to standard attention.

**Failure signatures**:
- Poor calibration: High ECE indicates fusion or uncertainty heads are misaligned with empirical error.
- Low causal recovery precision (P@10): Learned parent sets diverge from expert knowledge; likely latent representation issues or insufficient supervision.
- Unstable `κ` values: Concentration parameters clustering near 0 (uniform) or very high (deterministic) indicate under/over-confident beliefs; check histogram of `κ`.
- Degraded robustness under dropout: If performance drops drastically, the model may be over-reliant on certain features; the uncertainty mechanism should adapt.
- Training loss divergence: Especially in `L_entropy` if `bU_emp` is noisy or monotonic constraint causes optimization issues.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Ablation study**: Remove hyperspherical representation (Euclidean), remove uncertainty decomposition (only one head), remove SCM module, remove entropy calibration loss. Compare performance (F1/AUC) and calibration (ECE) to isolate contributions.
2. **Calibration analysis**: Plot reliability diagrams (predicted confidence vs. observed accuracy) for SphUnc vs. baselines; compute ECE. Verify that uncertainty estimates are well-calibrated.
3. **Causal recovery**: Evaluate Precision@10 against expert-annotated influence links. Inspect top learned causal edges for qualitative plausibility (e.g., Figure 9). Perform interventional simulations to check if interventions produce expected changes.

## Limitations
- Numerical instability in vMF entropy for extreme κ may degrade uncertainty calibration.
- Causal structure learning details (initialization, λ₂ weights, identifiability constraints) are underspecified, hindering reproducibility.
- Core assumptions risk failure if magnitude information is essential, epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties are correlated, or spherical latents are insufficiently expressive.

## Confidence
- vMF entropy as epistemic uncertainty: Medium
- Monotonic fusion for uncertainty decomposition: Medium
- SCM on spherical latents for causal identification: Medium

## Next Checks
