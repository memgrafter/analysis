# Soft Forward-Backward Representations for Zero-shot Reinforcement Learning with General Utilities
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06769
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06769
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Soft FB learns diverse stochastic policies enabling zero-shot optimization of arbitrary differentiable utilities, achieving average normalized score 0.89 vs FB's 0.63 on didactic tasks and >0.95 correlation between offline estimates and true returns.

## Executive Summary
Zero-shot reinforcement learning aims to produce a single policy family that can perform well for any test reward without additional training. Classic forward-backward (FB) methods achieve this for linear RL but fail for general utilities (arbitrary differentiable occupancy objectives). This paper introduces Soft FB, a maximum entropy variant of FB that learns a diverse set of stochastic policies from offline data. Policies are parameterized by low-dimensional embeddings; at test time, zero-order search over embeddings directly maximizes any general utility. Theoretically, Soft FB’s policy family contains near-optimal solutions for all differentiable GUs. Empirically, on a suite of didactic tasks Soft FB’s best policies achieve an average normalized score of 0.89 compared to FB’s 0.63, with significant gains on tasks requiring stochasticity (pure exploration, stochastic imitation, constrained RL). On the DeepMind Control Suite with ExORL data, Soft FB attains higher entropy and lower KL to stochastic experts, and offline evaluations using an explicit measure model correlate >0.95 with true returns, confirming reliable policy selection.

## Method Summary
Soft FB extends the forward-backward framework by adding entropy regularization. The algorithm pre-trains four networks on an offline dataset: a forward network Fθ(s,a,z) and backward network Bφ(s) that together approximate the low-rank occupancy decomposition Mz = Fθ⊤z Bφ; a stochastic policy πψ(a|s,z) conditioned on an embedding z; and an entropy critic QH,η(s,a,z) that estimates the discounted sum of policy entropies. Training minimizes three losses: L_FB enforces Bellman consistency for F and B; L_H trains Q via TD learning; L_π aligns π with the soft Q-function derived from the regularized objective (π ∝ exp((1−∥z∥)^{-1}(Fθ⊤z + QH,η))). Optionally, a generative model of successor measures (e.g., via TD2-CFM) is trained to enable accurate inference. At test time, for any differentiable general utility f, Soft FB performs zero-order search over a bounded embedding space (hypersphere) to find the z that maximizes f(̂Mz), where ̂Mz is estimated either implicitly by importance-sampling dataset transitions using Fθ·Bφ, or explicitly by sampling from the generative model. The corresponding policy πψ(·|s,z*) is then deployed without further learning.

## Key Results
- Average normalized score of 0.89 on didactic tasks vs 0.63 for FB, with major improvements on pure exploration, stochastic imitation, and constrained RL.
- Higher policy entropy and lower KL divergence to stochastic experts on DeepMind Control Suite, demonstrating richer behavioral diversity.
- Offline evaluations using an explicit measure model achieve >0.95 correlation with true returns, indicating reliable policy selection without environment interaction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Diverse stochastic policy family**  
Soft FB generates a family of stochastic policies that approximately covers all optimal solutions for any differentiable general utility. By adding an entropy term to the FB policy extraction rule (πz ∝ exp(F⊤z z + MzHπz)), the algorithm ensures all policies have full support. Coupled with the low-rank decomposition Mz = F⊤z B, this yields an expressive family that, by Theorem 4.2, contains near-optimal policies for arbitrary GUs. The core assumption is that function approximators have sufficient capacity and training enforces the required equations for all embeddings on a bounded hypersphere. If policies collapse to deterministic (e.g., due to insufficient entropy regularization), the guarantee breaks for GUs requiring stochasticity.

**Mechanism 2: Zero-order search over embeddings**  
After pre-training, each policy πz is parameterized by a compact embedding z ∈ ℝ^d. For a given GU f, the optimal z⋆ is found by directly maximizing f(̂Mz) using zero-order methods (e.g., random shooting) with either implicit or explicit estimates of Mz. This avoids iterative MDP solving at test time. The approach relies on a low-dimensional, smooth embedding space and accurate measure models. Inaccurate estimates or too high dimensionality can degrade search efficiency and policy quality.

**Mechanism 3: Decoupling training from test objectives**  
Pre-training solves a set of maximum entropy linear RL problems, which is tractable. Because maximum entropy policies include all full-support Markov policies and can approximate any GU-optimal policy (Theorem 4.2), no additional training on specific test utilities is needed. The key assumption is that the class of max-entropy policies is dense in the space of occupancy measures and that the learned successor representations are accurate. Insufficient offline data coverage can break this, limiting the reachable policy set.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Offline dataset ρ → (F_θ, B_φ, π_ψ, Q_η) via parallel training (L_FB, L_H, L_π) → optional generative model of Mz → zero-order search over z (using implicit F·B or explicit model) → selection of z* → deployment of π_ψ(·|s,z*)

**Critical path**  
1. Pre-train F_θ and B_φ by minimizing Bellman residual L_FB on (s,a,s') pairs, using target networks and orthonormalization.  
2. Train entropy critic Q_η via TD loss L_H.  
3. Train stochastic policy π_ψ by minimizing KL to the soft Q-function (L_π).  
4. (Optional) Train explicit generative model of successor measures (e.g., TD2-CFM).  
5. At inference: sample candidate embeddings z; estimate ̂Mz; evaluate f(̂Mz); pick best z*; execute π_ψ(·|s,z*).

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Explicit vs implicit measure model:* Flow-based explicit models give higher accuracy (correlation >0.95) but add compute overhead; implicit models are cheaper but noisier.  
- *Embedding scale:* The reparameterization z ↦ z/(∥z∥+1) maps policies to a hypersphere. Smaller ∥z∥ yields more stochastic policies; larger ∥z∥ approaches deterministic FB. The continuum allows balancing exploration and exploitation.  
- *Entropy regularization:* Controlled implicitly via ∥z∥; no explicit coefficient needed, but requires careful interpretation during search.

**Failure signatures**  
- Policy collapse to determinism: all π_z become nearly deterministic even for small ∥z∥. Check Q_η training or policy network capacity.  
- Poor offline evaluation: low correlation between f(̂Mz) and true returns (<0.7). Indicates measure model inaccuracy; improve explicit model or dataset.  
- Search inefficiency: zero-order fails to find good z. Embedding space may not be smooth; increase dimensionality or use better optimizer (CEM).  
- Training instability: losses (especially L_FB) diverge. Verify orthonormalization, target network updates, learning rates, gradient clipping.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Didactic bandit MDP: visualize how policies vary with z norm, confirming Soft FB produces a spectrum from uniform to deterministic, unlike FB.  
2. General utilities battery: compare Soft FB vs FB on pure exploration, stochastic imitation, constrained RL tasks; measure improvement when stochasticity required.  
3. DeepMind Control Suite with ExORL: test scalability; evaluate entropy maximization and KL imitation; correlate offline estimates with true returns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- *Theoretical properties with finite capacity:* What are the properties of the policy set induced by Soft FB when using finite-dimensional embeddings and function approximation? The paper notes that studying this set is an important direction.
- *Advanced zero-order optimization:* Can methods like Bayesian optimization improve policy search efficiency in high-dimensional embedding spaces? The paper suggests more involved optimization may further scale.
- *Extension to other successor-feature algorithms:* Does the Soft FB framework extend to arbitrary successor-feature based algorithms (e.g., nonlinear or model-based variants)?
- *Impact of measure model accuracy:* How does explicit generative model accuracy affect retrieved policies, and is there a point of diminishing returns? The paper notes trade-offs between accuracy and computational cost.

## Limitations
- Training implementation details (update schedules, loss weighting) are unspecified, risking suboptimal convergence.
- The implicit measure estimation procedure (e.g., how actions are reweighted) is ambiguous and could introduce high variance.
- Scalability claims are based on limited benchmarks (didactic tasks, DMC with ExORL data); performance on truly novel environments or diverse offline datasets remains unproven.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical coverage guarantee (Theorem 4.2):** High confidence (rigorous under assumptions).
- **Empirical gains on stochastic tasks:** Medium confidence (didactic results clear but DMC lacks statistical testing and uses specific data).
- **Zero-order search efficiency:** Medium confidence (depends on smooth embeddings and accurate measure models; not fully stress-tested).
