# From Literature to Hypotheses: An AI Co-Scientist System for Biomarker-Guided Drug Combination Hypothesis Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00612
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00612
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** The full CoDHy system achieves the highest exact novelty (35.71%) and diversity (0.89) while maintaining strong evidence coverage (0.87) across seven cancer scenarios, outperforming LLM-only and graph-only baselines.

## Executive Summary
CoDHy is an AI co‑scientist designed to generate biomarker‑guided drug combination hypotheses in cancer. It integrates structured biomedical databases and PubMed literature into a task‑specific knowledge graph, then employs graph embeddings and a multi‑agent LLM workflow to produce and validate candidate combinations. Evaluation on seven clinical scenarios shows that the full system balances novelty, diversity, and evidence coverage more effectively than ablations that rely solely on LLMs or the graph. The architecture deliberately reduces ranking precision (MRR) to prioritize novel yet plausible hypotheses.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins by ingesting data from 12 structured sources (e.g., DrugBank, ChEMBL, TCGA) and retrieving PubMed abstracts relevant to a user‑specified biomarker and cancer type. Extracted entities and relations from abstracts are normalized and merged with database triples to form a Neo4j knowledge graph. Node2Vec embeddings are computed on this graph. For a given biomarker, the system retrieves its two‑hop subgraph and augments it with the top‑k most similar nodes from the embeddings. A Graph‑RAG agent then prompts an LLM (Llama‑3.1‑8B‑Instruct) to generate four drug combination candidates based on this context. Each candidate undergoes validation by a separate LLM agent that searches PubMed, assesses safety, and issues a verdict (Proceed/Caution/Reject). Hypotheses are ranked by a composite score equally weighting graph‑derived evidence (direct edge count, embedding similarity, coverage flag) and the LLM safety score. The system is accessible via a Gradio web interface.

## Key Results
- CoDHy reaches 35.71% exact novelty and 0.89 diversity while preserving 0.87 evidence coverage.
- The LLM‑only baseline achieves higher evidence coverage but lower novelty, whereas the graph‑only variant lags in diversity.
- The full system trades off ranking quality (MRR 0.74) for novelty, compared to 0.93 for the LLM‑only approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CoDHy’s strength lies in its hybrid reasoning: the knowledge graph provides explicit, high‑precision biological relationships, while graph embeddings capture semantic proximities that reveal indirect or latent connections between drugs and the biomarker. The Graph‑RAG agent leverages both sources to propose candidates that are rooted in evidence yet explore novel territory. The subsequent multi‑agent LLM validation introduces a safety and plausibility filter, mimicking expert peer review. By combining a composite score that balances evidence and safety, the system can rank hypotheses that are both biologically plausible and innovative, explaining its superior novelty and diversity without sacrificing coverage.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Graphs**: Structured representations of entities (genes, drugs, diseases) and their relationships. *Why needed*: They encode known biological pathways and drug targets, providing a factual backbone for hypothesis generation. *Quick check*: Given a KG of drugs and targets, can you list all drugs that inhibit a specific gene?
- **Node2Vec Embeddings**: Graph‑based node representations learned via random walks. *Why needed*: They quantify semantic similarity between nodes even when no direct edge exists, enabling the discovery of indirect relationships. *Quick check*: If two drugs treat the same disease but are not directly linked in the KG, would their embeddings likely be close? Why?
- **Graph‑RAG (Retrieval‑Augmented Generation)**: A paradigm where a graph is queried to retrieve relevant context for an LLM. *Why needed*: It grounds LLM outputs in structured evidence, reducing hallucination and focusing generation on the biomarker’s neighborhood. *Quick check*: In Graph‑RAG, what is the difference between retrieving explicit graph neighbors and retrieving top‑k similar nodes via embeddings?
- **Multi‑Agent LLM Validation**: Using separate LLM calls to perform specialized checks (e.g., literature search, safety assessment). *Why needed*: It distributes cognitive load, mimics a peer‑review process, and produces more reliable verdicts. *Quick check*: Why might a single LLM prompt that both generates and validates a hypothesis be less trustworthy than a two‑agent pipeline?
- **Composite Scoring**: Combining multiple evidence sources (graph metrics, LLM scores) into a single ranking value. *Why needed*: It balances competing objectives (novelty vs. safety) and prevents over‑reliance on any single signal. *Quick check*: If the graph evidence score is high but the LLM safety score is low, what would a composite equal‑weight score output, and why is this desirable?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
User Input -> Data Ingestion -> Knowledge Graph Construction -> Node2Vec Embedding -> Graph-RAG Candidate Generation -> LLM Multi-Agent Validation -> Composite Scoring & Ranking -> Output

### Critical Path
1. For a given (biomarker, cancer type), collect structured triples from 12 databases and retrieve N=50 PubMed abstracts.
2. Extract entities and relations from abstracts, normalize, and load into Neo4j to form a unified KG.
3. Compute Node2Vec embeddings on the KG.
4. Query the KG for the biomarker’s 2‑hop subgraph; retrieve top‑50 similar nodes from embeddings.
5. Prompt LLM to generate 4 drug combination candidates using the combined context.
6. For each candidate, run a validation LLM: perform fresh PubMed search, assign verdict and safety score (1‑10).
7. Compute graph evidence score (normalized direct edges, max similarity, coverage flag).
8. Average graph evidence and safety scores to obtain final ranking; output top‑4 hypotheses.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Novelty vs. Precision**: Relying more on embedding similarity introduces novel candidates but risks lower MRR; the system deliberately weights novelty higher.
- **Graph Completeness vs. Noise**: Including more extracted relations from PubMed can improve coverage but introduces extraction errors that may mislead generation.
- **LLM Autonomy vs. Grounding**: Using LLMs for generation and validation offers flexibility but requires careful prompting to avoid hallucinations; grounding via KG mitigates this.
- **Composite Score Transparency**: Equal weighting is simple but may not reflect the true importance of each component; the exact normalization is undisclosed.

### Failure Signatures
- Sparse subgraph around the biomarker (few direct edges) leading to repetitive or trivial candidates.
- LLM validation cites PMIDs that do not contain the claimed drug combination upon manual check.
- Extremely low MRR compared to baselines, indicating excessive novelty bias.
- Sudden drop in evidence coverage across scenarios, suggesting extraction pipeline failures or database access issues.

### First Experiments
1. **KG Replication**: Rebuild the KG for the EGFR–Breast Invasive Carcinoma scenario using the listed databases and N=50 abstracts; inspect the number of nodes, edges, and the 2‑hop subgraph density.
2. **Prompt Ablation**: Vary the LLM generation and validation prompts (with/without few‑shot examples) on a single scenario and measure changes in novelty and evidence coverage.
3. **Metric Consistency**: Re‑implement the binary relevance labeling (literature‑supported vs. novel) on the same 7 scenarios, compute MRR and nDCG@3, and verify that the full system’s MRR is indeed lower than the LLM‑only baseline.

## Limitations
- Generalizability beyond the seven frozen evaluation scenarios remains uncertain; the pipeline is scenario‑specific and may not scale efficiently to arbitrary new queries.
- Critical implementation details are underspecified: the exact spaCy NER/RE configuration, similarity threshold for relation normalization, LLM prompts, and the precise weighting within the graph evidence score.
- The system trades off ranking metrics to favor novelty, which may limit its utility in settings where precision is paramount.

## Confidence
- **High**: Exact performance metrics (novelty 35.71%, diversity 0.89, coverage 0.87) on the seven fixed scenarios.
- **Medium**: Ability to reproduce the composite scoring and ranking outcomes given the described but not fully quantified formula.
- **Low**: Extending the system to new biomarkers or cancer types without manual tuning; reproducibility of the relation extraction pipeline due to missing parameters.

## Next Checks
- **KB Reconstruction Audit**: For a chosen scenario, verify that the integrated knowledge graph contains all expected entity types from Reactome, DrugBank, etc., and that PubMed-derived relations are correctly mapped.
- **Prompt Sensitivity Analysis**: Systematically remove or alter few‑shot examples in the generation and validation agents to quantify their impact on hypothesis novelty and evidence coverage.
- **Cross‑Scenario Generalization**: Run CoDHy on a new (biomarker, cancer) pair not in the frozen set and compare its novelty/coverage against the original scenarios to assess transferability.
