# Evaluation metrics for temporal preservation in synthetic longitudinal patient data
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10643
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10643
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces a comprehensive metric set for evaluating temporal preservation in synthetic longitudinal patient data, revealing that strong marginal resemblance alone can mask critical temporal distortions.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a critical gap in the evaluation of synthetic longitudinal patient data by proposing a multidimensional framework of temporal preservation metrics. The metrics assess four key structures: marginal distributions, covariance patterns, individual trajectories, and measurement processes. Applied to MIMIC-III data and synthetic outputs from HALO and Health Gym GAN, the study demonstrates that strong marginal resemblance often hides severe issues such as inflated/deflated variance, altered between-subject heterogeneity, and unreliable rare-class transitions. The findings underscore that no single metric suffices; a comprehensive evaluation is essential to diagnose temporal fidelity failures in generative models.

## Method Summary
The proposed evaluation framework uses kernel smoothing on a common time grid to handle irregular longitudinal measurements. For each variable, it computes: (1) marginal profiles (smoothed mean, quantiles, class distributions); (2) covariance metrics (variance profiles, variograms, rank-order variability, transition probabilities); (3) individual-level trajectory stratifications; and (4) measurement pattern indicators (density, similarity via Hungarian algorithm, dropout divergence). Original and synthetic data are compared via overlaid visualizations and numerical similarity scores. The approach avoids imputation and balanced panel assumptions, making it suitable for real-world electronic health records with uneven visit schedules.

## Key Results
- Strong marginal resemblance can mask severe distortions in covariance and individual trajectories.
- Temporal fidelity is heavily influenced by data sparsity, preprocessing choices (e.g., binning), and model assumptions.
- A multidimensional evaluation is essential; no single metric suffices to diagnose temporal preservation failures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1**: Synthetic longitudinal data can appear realistic at the marginal level while severely distorting temporal dependencies. The framework deconstructs temporal preservation into four independent structures, revealing that generative models often fail to capture within-subject correlations even when cross-sectional distributions match. Core assumption: marginal resemblance is necessary but insufficient; covariance and individual-level structures must also be preserved. Break condition: if all covariance and individual-level metrics show high similarity, temporal structure is likely preserved despite marginal imperfections.

**Mechanism 2**: Temporal fidelity is heavily determined by data characteristics (sparsity, imbalance) and preprocessing (binning, encoding), not just model architecture. The metrics expose how factors like infrequent measurements and class imbalance cause loss of variance, flattened trends, or failed rare-class transitions. Core assumption: synthetic data quality is constrained by the information content and preprocessing of training data. Break condition: if metrics fail specifically on sparse variables or rare classes while performing well on dense/common ones, the limitation is data-driven rather than a model flaw.

**Mechanism 3**: Kernel smoothing on a common time grid enables non-parametric, interpretable comparison of unbalanced longitudinal data without imputation. Normalized kernel weights aggregate irregular observations to estimate population statistics at regular time points. Core assumption: temporal patterns can be reliably estimated by smoothing local contributions with an appropriate bandwidth. Break condition: if changing the bandwidth parameter drastically alters similarity conclusions, the result is sensitive to smoothing scale and requires reporting multiple h-values.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Longitudinal Data Structures (marginal vs. covariance vs. individual)** – Why needed: The framework distinguishes these three temporal structures; understanding that "temporal" includes within-subject correlations and trajectory variation is crucial. Quick check: *Can you give an example where two datasets have identical mean profiles over time but fundamentally different individual-level temporal behaviors?*
- **Concept: Kernel Smoothing for Unbalanced Data** – Why needed: All metrics rely on kernel smoothing to handle irregular measurement times; without grasping normalized kernel weights, metric calculations are opaque. Quick check: *How does the normalized kernel weight differ from a simple average, and why is normalization critical?*
- **Concept: Synthetic Data Evaluation Dimensions (resemblance, utility, privacy)** – Why needed: The paper focuses on temporal resemblance, a sub-dimension of overall fidelity; this scope prevents misapplying metrics to assess privacy or downstream utility. Quick check: *If a synthetic dataset perfectly matches all temporal metrics, what other evaluation dimensions might still be problematic?*

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input ingest (long-format data) -> Preprocessing (common time grid, kernel weights) -> Metric calculators (four modules: marginal, covariance, individual, measurement) -> Visualization engine (time-series plots, area charts, etc.) -> Comparison layer (overlay, similarity scores).
- **Critical path**: Start with marginal profiles (1-3) → inspect visually for mismatches → compute covariance metrics (4-7) to diagnose hidden distortions → examine individual trajectories to explain population anomalies → validate measurement patterns (8-10) to rule out sampling artifacts.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Visual vs. numerical emphasis; kernel bandwidth choice (larger for trends, smaller for fluctuations); computational cost of Hungarian algorithm (O(n³)) requiring subsampling.
- **Failure signatures**: Marginal good but variogram shape mismatches → lost autocorrelation; suppressed outer quantiles → deflated variance; inflated outer quantiles → outlier exaggeration; rank-order variability distribution shift → changed heterogeneity; transition probabilities missing for rare classes → mode collapse; zigzag measurement density → time discretization artifact.
- **First 3 experiments**: 
  1. Reproduce systolic blood pressure example (HALO): verify Figures 3-7, confirm "moderate preservation" conclusion (good marginal/variogram, zigzag measurement density).
  2. Stress test sparsity: apply metrics to a variable with <5% coverage (like AST); expect deflated variance and failed transitions; document kernel smoothing robustness.
  3. Compare HALO vs. HGG on a discrete variable (e.g., GCS): contrast class profile preservation vs. transition reliability for minority states.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Logical consistency constraints**: How to define and evaluate constraints like monotonicity for synthetic longitudinal data, and how does adherence affect downstream validity? The paper omits these, noting they are important for plausibility. Unresolved because impact on utility is unknown; needs systematic studies correlating violation rates with performance degradation.
2. **Multivariate temporal dependencies**: How to quantify multivariate temporal dependencies interpretably? Univariate metrics may miss critical failures. Unresolved because current multivariate approaches are hard to interpret; needs development of visualization tools or aggregate metrics that correlate with known cross-lagged effects.
3. **Disentangling measurement process errors**: Can we separate measurement errors (dropout, irregular timing) from true temporal process errors to improve covariance distortion diagnosis? Current measurement metrics conflate the two. Unresolved because no distinction is made; needs controlled simulations manipulating measurement mechanisms independently.
4. **Mitigating temporal distortion patterns**: What modifications to generative models can address specific distortions (inflated variance, suppressed extremes, rare-class transition failures)? Unresolved because the paper only evaluates, not improves; needs ablation studies on preprocessing, architecture, or training objectives.

## Limitations
- Metrics depend on a manually set kernel bandwidth; sensitivity not explored.
- Evaluation limited to MIMIC-III and two GANs; generalizability unknown.
- Sparse variables (<5% coverage) yield unstable estimates; no adaptive smoothing.

## Confidence
- Marginal vs. covariance distortion (Mechanism 1): High
- Data characteristics drive fidelity (Mechanism 2): Medium
- Kernel smoothing sufficiency (Mechanism 3): Medium

## Next Checks
1. Cross-dataset replication: Apply metrics to other longitudinal EHRs (e.g., eICU) and newer synthetic models (e.g., diffusion) to assess transferability.
2. Bandwidth sensitivity: Systematically vary `h` and quantify metric stability; propose a data-driven selection rule.
3. Utility correlation: Train downstream predictors on synthetic data; test whether temporal preservation scores correlate with predictive performance on real data.
