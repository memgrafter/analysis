# Turning Time Series into Algebraic Equations: Symbolic Machine Learning for Interpretable Modeling of Chaotic Time Series

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.07261
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07261
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Two symbolic forecasters (SyNF, SyTF) learn interpretable algebraic equations from chaotic time series lags, achieving competitive one-step-ahead accuracy against deep learning baselines while providing transparent dynamics.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of forecasting chaotic time series by developing interpretable models that explicitly reveal underlying dynamics. The authors introduce two symbolic forecasting frameworks: the Symbolic Neural Forecaster (SyNF), a neural-symbolic model trained with differentiable optimization over a fixed operator library, and the Symbolic Tree Forecaster (SyTF), an evolutionary symbolic regressor searching over expression trees. Evaluated on 132 low-dimensional chaotic attractors and two real-world series (dengue incidence, El Niño SST), both methods achieve predictive accuracy comparable to state-of-the-art black-box models. Crucially, they output human-readable algebraic equations, validated by statistical tests, enabling direct inspection of the learned dynamics.

## Method Summary
The task is one-step-ahead forecasting for univariate chaotic time series. Inputs are lagged feature matrices constructed from a series with lag order \(p\). The objective is to minimize forecasting error (SMAPE, RMSE, MAE, MARRE) while producing a parsimonious symbolic equation \(F(\mathbf{y}_{t-1}) \approx y_t\).

**SyNF** uses a single hidden layer to map lagged inputs to a set of unary (identity, sine, cosine) and binary (elementwise multiplication) symbolic operations. A final linear layer combines these terms. Training minimizes MSE with optional \(\ell_1\) regularization. A variant, SyNF-Div, adds a two-neuron division branch with a threshold \(\gamma\) and penalties \(P_\gamma, P_{\text{bound}}\) to ensure numerical stability.

**SyTF** employs an evolutionary algorithm (via PySR) to search expression trees with operators \(\{+, -, \times, \sin, \cos\}\). Optionally, SyTF-Div-Exp includes division and exponential. It uses tournament selection, mutation, crossover, and an adaptive "frecency" penalty to balance accuracy and complexity (node count). A Pareto front tracks the trade-off, and constants are refined via BFGS.

Both are compared against diverse baselines: tree-based ensembles (RandomForest, XGBoost, LightGBM), deep learning models (NLinear, NBeats, NHiTS, LSTM, Transformer, TiDE), and linear regression.

## Key Results
- SyTF and SyTF-Div-Exp achieve the lowest median errors (RMSE, MAE, SMAPE, MARRE) across the 132 synthetic chaotic attractors, demonstrating robustness to varying dynamics and lag lengths.
- SyNF-Div-Reg (SyNF-Div with \(\ell_1\) regularization) excels on the two real-world datasets, obtaining the best SMAPE and RMSE ranks, indicating the benefit of division operators and sparsity for noisy, real-world chaotic processes.
- All symbolic models produce transparent algebraic equations that pass statistical validation (e.g., residual whiteness), offering direct insight into the governing dynamics unlike black-box alternatives.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The Symbolic Neural Forecaster (SyNF) discovers accurate, interpretable forecasting equations via differentiable optimization over a fixed symbolic operator library, avoiding black-box training.
**Mechanism:** A single hidden layer maps lagged inputs to symbolic operations (unary: identity/sine/cosine; binary: elementwise multiplication). The output is a linear combination of these symbolic terms. End-to-end gradient descent (Adam) minimizes MSE, while optional \(\ell_1\) regularization promotes sparsity.
**Core assumption:** Accurate forecasts can be represented by a weighted sum of a small set of interpretable base functions applied to lagged variables. Chaotic dynamics' nonlinearities are captured by these fixed operators.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "SyNF adapts a neural network–based equation learning architecture... enabling end-to-end differentiable discovery of compact and interpretable algebraic relations." [section 4.1] Details the architecture: linear layer → symbolic units (Eq. 1) → linear output, trained via MSE with optional \(\ell_1\) penalty.
**Break condition:** Gradient descent fails if the fixed operator library is insufficient to capture essential dynamics (e.g., missing division for rational forms), or if noise overwhelms the signal, causing overfitting despite regularization.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The Symbolic Tree Forecaster (SyTF) discovers accurate, interpretable forecasting equations via evolutionary search over expression trees, balancing accuracy and complexity without gradient-based training.
**Mechanism:** A multi-population genetic algorithm evolves expression trees (operators: +, −, ×, sin, cos, /, exp). Tournament selection, mutation, and crossover explore the space. A Pareto front tracks loss vs. complexity (node count). An adaptive "frecency" penalty dynamically regulates pressure against over/under-represented complexities. Constants are optimized via BFGS.
**Core assumption:** A parsimonious symbolic expression exists in the tree space that approximates the one-step forecast mapping. Evolutionary search with complexity control can find it even in non-differentiable, discontinuous symbolic landscapes.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "SyTF builds on evolutionary symbolic regression to search directly over equation structures under a principled accuracy–complexity trade-off." [section 4.2] Describes the evolve-simplify-optimize loop, tree representation, adaptive parsimony via frecency, and Pareto selection.
**Break condition:** Evolutionary search stagnates or returns overly complex, overfitted trees if the complexity penalty (frecency) is misconfigured, or if the operator set is mismatched to the underlying dynamics.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Incorporating division operations (SyNF-Div/SyTF-Div-Exp) and targeted regularization significantly improves accuracy on real-world chaotic series by enabling rational function representations that capture saturation, feedback, and oscillatory patterns.
**Mechanism:** For SyNF-Div, a two-neuron output layer enables division \((a/b)\) with a threshold \(\gamma\) to prevent division-by-zero, plus a global penalty \(P_\gamma\) on small denominators and periodic \(P_{\text{bound}}\) penalties. SyTF-Div-Exp includes / and exp in its operator set. SyNF-Div-Reg adds \(\ell_1\) regularization to the linear layers.
**Core assumption:** Real-world chaotic systems contain intrinsic rational relationships or saturation effects not expressible by polynomials/trig alone. Sparsity constraints prevent these flexible operators from causing overfitting.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "SyNF-Div-Reg variants excel on real-world data with lower SMAPE and RMSE." [section 4.1] Details SyNF-Div's stable division mechanism (Eq. 2-6) and regularization. [section 5.4] Table 2 shows SyNF-Div-Reg and SyTF-Div-Exp achieve top ranks on real-world datasets.
**Break condition:** The division stability mechanism (\(\gamma\) schedule, penalty epochs) fails if the learned denominator consistently approaches zero in out-of-sample regimes, causing numerical instability. Over-regularization may suppress needed division terms.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Symbolic Regression Principles**
  - **Why needed:** The entire approach relies on understanding the trade-off between predictive accuracy (loss) and model complexity (parsimony). The engineer must grasp Pareto optimization, expression bloat, and the NP-hard nature of the search to configure SyTF's frecency penalty or SyNF's regularization.
  - **Quick check:** If an evolved expression has low training error but 50+ nodes, what mechanism in SyTF is designed to penalize it, and what is the risk of setting that penalty too aggressively?

- **Concept: Chaotic Dynamics Sensitivities**
  - **Why needed:** Forecasting chaotic series involves exponential divergence of trajectories. The engineer must understand why one-step-ahead is the focus, why simple lags (\(p=4\)) may suffice for short-term forecasts, and why model instability is catastrophic. This informs the choice of lag count and stability constraints.
  - **Quick check:** Why is a rolling-window one-step-ahead evaluation particularly suited for chaotic systems compared to long-horizon iterative forecasting?

- **Concept: Time Series Preprocessing & Stationarity**
  - **Why needed:** The paper assumes the series are stationary (KPSS test passed). The engineer must ensure training/test splits are chronological, handle potential trends/seasonality, and select appropriate lag features (\(p\)). Incorrect preprocessing can invalidate the learned symbolic mapping.
  - **Quick check:** Given the ACF/PACF plots in Figure 2, why is \(p=4\) a reasonable default lag choice for the real-world datasets, and what would be a red flag in the PACF suggesting a different \(p\) is needed?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Data Input -> Lagged Matrix (shape [T-p, p])
  SyNF Path: Lagged Matrix -> Linear W^{(1)} -> Symbolic Layer (unary on first u dims, binary on pairs) -> Concatenated y^{(1)} -> Linear W^{(2)} -> Output. (SyNF-Div: W^{(2)} -> 2-neuron -> h_γ division)
  SyTF Path: Lagged Matrix -> Evolutionary Loop (Population of trees) -> Mutation/Crossover -> Simplify (algebraic identities) -> Optimize (BFGS on constants) -> Pareto Front selects best expression F(·)
  Shared: Loss calculation (MSE/MAE), hyperparameter control (λ, γ, frecency), evaluation on rolling window.

- **Critical path:** 1) Verify stationarity & calculate Lyapunov exponent (λ_max>0). 2) Split chronologically (train/test). 3) **Select lag p** (start with 4 for real-world, test 5/10/25 for synthetic per paper). 4) **Choose model family**: SyTF for simpler/stable synthetic; SyNF(SyNF-Div)-Reg for noisy real-world. 5) Tune key hyperparams: γ schedule (SyNF-Div), λ (ℓ1 reg), population size & generations (SyTF). 6) Train & extract final symbolic expression from best Pareto individual.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **SyNF vs. SyTF:** SyNF (gradient-based) is faster, more stable training, produces denser equations; SyTF (evolutionary) is slower, can find simpler structures, better for low-noise synthetic.
  - **Operator set:** Base (+, −, ×, sin, cos) is safer/parsimonious. Adding / and exp increases expressive power but risks instability/overfitting (requires SyNF-Div's safety or SyTF's complexity control).
  - **Regularization:** ℓ1 (SyNF-Reg) and adaptive parsimony (SyTF) combat bloat but may remove genuinely useful terms if too strong. Must balance via validation.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **SyNF:** Loss plateaus at high value → operator library insufficient. Loss diverges → γ/P_bound misconfigured or learning rate too high. Extracted equation has huge constant terms → missing normalization or unbounded γ.
  - **SyTF:** Population diversity collapses → frecency penalty too weak or temperature schedule failed. Best Pareto expression is huge (100+ nodes) → complexity penalty mis-scaled. No improvement over many generations → operator set inadequate or mutation rate too low.
  - **General:** Test error >> train error → overfitting; regularize more (λ up, frecency up). Symbolic equation makes no physical sense (e.g., sin(yt * yt-1^1000)) → operator set too permissive or training failed; simplify or restrict operators.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Lag Sensitivity Replication**: For one canonical chaotic attractor (e.g., Lorenz), train all model variants (SyNF, SyNF-Reg, SyTF, SyTF-Div-Exp) with p=5,10,25. Plot median RMSE/IQR across 132 attractors. Verify SyTF/SyTF-Div-Exp robustness to lag increase as in Figures 4-6.
  2. **Real-World Operator Ablation**: On San Juan dengue data, train SyNF-base vs. SyNF-Div vs. SyNF-Div-Reg with p=4. Record SMAPE/RMSE. Expected: SyNF-Div-Reg best, showing division+regularization benefit. Extract equations (Table 3) and verify presence of rational/periodic terms only in Div variants.
  3. **Stability Stress Test (SyNF-Div)**: Train SyNF-Div on El Niño SST with deliberately poor γ initialization (e.g., γ=0 fixed) or no P_bound. Monitor training loss and output magnitudes. Expected: loss spikes/nans, demonstrating necessity of the stability curriculum. Then fix with proposed schedule (γ(t)=1/√(t+1)).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What is multi-step ahead performance and stability of symbolic forecasting equations?** Only one-step ahead was tested; multi-step behavior is unknown. Test on 132 attractors and real data with horizons 5,10,20; compare error growth and iterative stability against baselines.
- **Can symbolic regression handle multivariate inputs and exogenous drivers to improve accuracy and interpretability?** Current models only use univariate lags; real systems have multiple interacting variables and external factors. Build multivariate SyNF/SyTF; test on datasets with exogenous variables; compare performance and equation richness.
- **Will adding dynamical constraints reduce search space and yield more robust, physically plausible symbolic equations?** Without constraints, symbolic search may produce equations violating physical principles. Modify SyTF/SyNF to penalize constraint violations; evaluate on chaotic attractors with known properties; measure simplification and accuracy.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter Transparency**: Exact values for SyNF (learning rate, hidden dim d, λ schedule) and SyTF (PySR population/generations, frecency parameters) are unreported, blocking exact replication.
- **Real-World Generalization**: Claims on dengue/El Niño are based on only two datasets; broader validation across diverse real-world chaotic series is needed.
- **Stability Assumptions**: The γ-schedule and division penalties for SyNF-Div are heuristic; their robustness to extreme noise or regime shifts in operational settings is unproven.

## Confidence
- **Synthetic Chaotic Attractor Performance**: High – Rigorous evaluation across 132 systems with statistical tests (MCB) supports SyTF/SyTF-Div-Exp superiority.
- **Real-World Forecasting Accuracy**: Medium – Strong results on two datasets, but limited sample size and potential preprocessing choices temper certainty.
- **Interpretability & Discovery**: High – Equations are transparent; validation via statistical tests is described, though physical plausibility checks are dataset-specific.
- **Deployment Readiness**: Low – Missing implementation details, computational cost analysis, and sensitivity to hyperparameters hinder practical adoption.
