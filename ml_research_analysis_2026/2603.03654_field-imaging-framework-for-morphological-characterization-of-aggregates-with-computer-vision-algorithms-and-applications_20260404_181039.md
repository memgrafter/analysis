# Field imaging framework for morphological characterization of aggregates with computer vision: Algorithms and applications

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.03654
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03654
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A field-deployable computer vision framework for automated aggregate morphological characterization, achieving 3.6%–7.9% volume error for individual riprap via orthogonal reconstruction and ~15–20% stockpile volume error with a 3D pipeline after shape-percentage filtering.

## Executive Summary
This research presents a comprehensive field imaging framework to automate the quantitative morphological characterization of coarse aggregates (e.g., riprap) in stockpiles. It addresses the inefficiencies of manual inspection and lab-based imaging by offering three interoperable modes: (1) a portable system for individual aggregates using color segmentation and orthogonal silhouettes for volume estimation; (2) a deep learning (Mask R-CNN) approach for 2D instance segmentation of stockpile images; and (3) an integrated 3D reconstruction–segmentation–completion pipeline (SfM + PointGroup + SnowflakeNet) that predicts unseen aggregate shapes. Validated on both re-engineered and real-world stockpiles, the framework demonstrates robust performance for quality control, with volume accuracy significantly improved by thresholding on shape completeness.

## Method Summary
Reproducing the framework involves constructing a 3D particle library, generating synthetic training data, training deep networks, and integrating the pipeline. First, 30–50 rock samples per size category are imaged on a turntable; their 3D models are reconstructed via SfM and validated against water displacement. Second, a Unity physics simulation instantiates these models into synthetic stockpiles, from which point clouds and partial–complete pairs are generated via raycasting to train PointGroup (instance segmentation) and SnowflakeNet (shape completion). Third, the trained models are applied to field-acquired multi-view images: SfM reconstructs a scaled point cloud (using marker blocks), PointGroup segments instances, SnowflakeNet completes occluded shapes, and morphology metrics (ESD, FER, volume) are computed after filtering by shape percentage (SP≥75%).

## Key Results
- Orthogonal reconstruction for individual aggregates: **3.6%–7.9% volume error** against ground truth, with calibration ball-based scaling and correction factors.
- 2D stockpile segmentation (Mask R-CNN): **88% completeness** on field images.
- 3D pipeline (SfM → PointGroup → SnowflakeNet): **~15–20% volume error** when applying a shape-percentage threshold (≥75%) on re-engineered and field stockpiles.
- Segmentation completeness: **~78%** and IoU AP **~82%** on synthetic test data; shape completion Chamfer distance **≈0.0055 mm**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Color-based segmentation in CIE L\*a\*b\* space handles field lighting variations.
**Claim**: Robust foreground extraction under sunlight and shadow by leveraging perceptual uniformity and adaptive thresholding in chrominance channels.
**Mechanism**: Convert RGB to L\*a\*b\*; use a\*/b\* histograms to define foreground/background color clusters; compute a Minkowski distance map with gamma correction (γ > 1); apply adaptive thresholding and morphological cleaning.
**Core assumption**: Riprap earth tones spectrally separate from the artificial blue background in a\*b\* space, providing consistent contrast even under harsh luminance variations.
**Evidence anchors**: Abstract emphasizes robustness; Section 4.1 highlights b* channel utility; Figure 4.4 shows contrast enhancement.
**Break condition**: Fails if aggregate colors overlap the background cluster in a\*b\* (e.g., blue-green stones on blue-green backdrop).

### Mechanism 2: Volumetric reconstruction from orthogonal silhouettes calibrated by a reference ball.
**Claim**: Accurate volume estimation (3.6%–7.9% error) by intersecting three orthogonal 2D masks, using a calibration ball to resolve scale and orthogonality.
**Mechanism**: Capture top, front, side views of aggregate and identically positioned ball. Use ball’s known diameter to scale each silhouette. Solve least-squares for orthogonal alignment; intersect voxel-wise; apply systematic factor (c₁=0.954) and resolution-based factor c₂ based on ball pixel size and rock/ball size ratio.
**Core assumption**: Orthographic projection is a valid approximation; aggregate is bounded by its silhouettes; calibration ball is spherical and occupies sufficient pixels.
**Evidence anchors**: Abstract states error range; Section 4.2 details alignment and intersection; Table 4.1/Figure 4.9 show improvement over manual MAPE (68.3% → 8.2%).
**Break condition**: Camera angles deviate >15° from true orthogonality or deep concavities cause residual overestimation beyond correction.

### Mechanism 3: 3D reconstruction–segmentation–completion pipeline for stockpile analysis.
**Claim**: End-to-end framework predicts volumetric properties by reconstructing point clouds, segmenting instances, and completing unseen shapes; SP≥75% filtering yields ~15–20% volume error.
**Mechanism**: Multi-view images → SfM (dense point cloud) → PointGroup (per-point offset clustering) → SnowflakeNet (point splitting decoder) → morphological analysis. Synthetic data from Unity physics/raycasting provides training pairs.
**Core assumption**: SfM yields metric point clouds with sufficient density; shifted coordinate offsets in PointGroup separate tightly packed aggregates; completion network generalizes to partial real shapes.
**Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section 10 report validation on 12 re-engineered and 9 field stockpiles; Section 9 notes SnowflakeNet CD ~0.00019-inch; SP thresholding improves accuracy.
**Break condition**: SfM fails with poor texture/view coverage; segmentation fails under extreme occlusion (SP < ~50%); completion degrades for atypical shapes outside synthetic training distribution.

  *Why needed*: To convert multi-view photographs into metric 3D point clouds without laser scanners.  
  *Quick check*: What fundamental steps does Structure-from-Motion perform, and how does bundle adjustment minimize reprojection error to jointly optimize camera poses and 3D points?

- **Deep learning for point cloud processing**  
  *Why needed*: Instance segmentation and shape completion on irregular 3D data require learning-based methods that can generalize from synthetic training data.  
  *Quick check*: How does the per-point offset prediction in PointGroup help separate densely packed aggregates within a single point cloud?

- **Geometric morphology and volume estimation**  
  *Why needed*: To compute size (ESD), shape (FER), and volume from segmented boundaries and express quality metrics for engineering specifications.  
  *Quick check*: Explain the origin of systematic overestimation in voxel intersection reconstruction and how the resolution-based correction term depends on the calibration ball size relative to the rock.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  Perception layer: Smartphone cameras (2D) and multi-view photography (3D)
  → Processing layer: Color segmentation (individual), Mask R-CNN (2D stockpile), SfM (3D reconstruction), PointGroup + SnowflakeNet (3D segmentation/completion)
  → Analytics layer: Morphological measures (ESD, FER, volume) and statistics/histograms
  → Training data generator: Unity synthetic stockpile pipeline and 2D annotation tool (VIA)
  Interactions: 2D path: single stockpile image → Mask R-CNN → metrics (uses calibration ball). 3D path: multi-view images → SfM → PointGroup → SnowflakeNet → 3D metrics (requires marker pattern). Individual path: triple views → color segmentation → ortho-reconstruction → volume.

- **Critical path**: The 3D pipeline (SfM → segmentation → completion) is the most systemic; failure in any step propagates to volume error. Alternate rapid path: 2D segmentation when speed is prioritized.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Accuracy vs. data collection effort: 3D multi-view requires 30–50 images vs. single 2D; 3D yields more complete morphology but higher field time.
  - Synthetic vs. real training: Synthetic data provides unlimited labels but may suffer domain gap; real field data has varying occlusion and size distributions.
  - Segmentation confidence vs. morphological yield: Higher shape-percentage thresholds drastically reduce analyzed aggregates but increase reliability; tunable per application.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Mask R-CNN: Over-segmentation of touching stones; under-segmentation of heavily occluded dark stones; atypical colors mis-assigned as background.
  - SfM: Sparse point cloud with holes or floating fragments; incorrect scale if marker distances are mis-measured.
  - PointGroup: Many small instance clusters (over-segmentation) in high-occlusion zones; missing instances on low-visibility surfaces (low SP).
  - SnowflakeNet: Over-smoothed or overly spherical completions; large residuals for highly irregular shapes; filling incorrect missing regions.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run color segmentation on the 85 individual aggregate triplets without calibration; manually extract silhouettes; compute per-view FER; compare to expert measurements; test robustness under added synthetic shadows.
  2. For the same 85 rocks, compare predicted volume/weight to measured values; analyze errors across rock/ball size ratios and view rotations (1, 2, 3 repeats).
  3. On one re-engineered stockpile (e.g., RR3-S1), collect 36+ multi-view images with markers; reconstruct point cloud; run PointGroup + SnowflakeNet; map completions to known ground-truth aggregates by ID; compute volume MAPE at various SP thresholds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can the framework eliminate reliance on calibration objects for scale determination in field imaging?**  
  *Basis*: Explicitly stated as a limitation (Section 11). *Why unresolved*: Calibration objects add overhead and potential error, limiting autonomy. *Resolution evidence*: A calibration-free method achieving ±2% scale accuracy across stockpile sizes, validated in field trials.

- **Can the 3D reconstruction step be made real-time and less computationally intensive while maintaining accuracy?**  
  *Basis*: Explicitly stated limitation (Section 11). *Why unresolved*: Current SfM is non-real-time, hindering time-sensitive QC. *Resolution evidence*: A dense SLAM or LiDAR-based alternative operating >10 fps with volume MPE <3% compared to SfM benchmark.

- **What methodologies can extend analysis to aggregates within the interior of stockpiles?**  
  *Basis*: Explicitly stated limitation (Section 11). *Why unresolved*: Interior gradation affects performance but is naturally invisible to surface imaging. *Resolution evidence*: A non-invasive subsurface technique (e.g., GPR, seismic tomography) validated against excavated cross-sections, showing strong correlation with ground-truth volume/weight.

- **To what extent does the framework generalize to other aggregate size categories and diverse geological origins?**  
  *Basis*: Recommendation suggests ongoing research (Section 11.3.3). *Why unresolved*: Networks trained primarily on RR3/RR4 riprap; other aggregates vary in color, texture, shape. *Resolution evidence*: Systematic testing across sizes (sand to riprap) and lithologies, reporting segmentation completeness >75% and morphological MAPE <10% comparable to riprap results.

## Limitations
- Key unknowns block replication: U2-Net fine-tuning details (split, augmentations, epochs); Unity physics parameters (gravity, friction, colliders) and raycasting script; deep learning hyperparameters (optimizer, LR schedule, batch size) for PointGroup/SnowflakeNet; precise marker-based scaling transformation in SfM.
- Synthetic domain gap: Training on Unity-generated point clouds may not fully capture real-world occlusion patterns and noise, limiting generalization.
- Shape percentage (SP) dependency: Volume accuracy relies on thresholding (e.g., SP≥75%), which reduces data yield and may require per-stockpile tuning.

## Confidence
- **Individual aggregate orthogonal reconstruction**: **High** – Strong empirical validation (3.6%–7.9% error) with ground-truth comparison and systematic corrections.
- **End-to-end 3D pipeline (SfM → segmentation → completion)**: **Medium** – Promising metrics (completeness ~78%, volume error ~15–20% with SP≥75%) from limited stockpiles; sensitivity to occlusion and domain shift under-quantified.
- **Field robustness across lighting/background variations**: **Low** – Claims rely on L\*a\*b\* segmentation; break conditions (color overlap) not extensively tested in diverse field conditions.

## Next Checks
1. **Color segmentation edge-case test**: Collect challenging field images (e.g., rocks with colors overlapping the blue background in a\*b\* space, extreme shadows). Compute IoU against manual masks to quantify robustness limits.
2. **Ortho-reconstruction scaling sensitivity**: Systematically vary calibration ball diameter relative to rock size (0.5×–3×) and camera angle deviation (±20°). Measure volume error to confirm valid range for correction factors c₁ and c₂.
3. **Synthetic-to-real domain gap quantification**: Apply the trained PointGroup to a real stockpile’s SfM point cloud with manual instance labels. Compare completeness and IoU AP to synthetic test performance to quantify domain shift and need for fine-tuning.
