# Do All Vision Transformers Need Registers? A Cross-Architectural Reassessment
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25803  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25803  
- **Reference count:** 34  
- **Primary result:** Registers remove attention/feature map artifacts and slightly improve DINOv2 accuracy, but the underlying causes and token behavior are architecture-dependent, not universal.

## Executive Summary
Vision Transformers (ViTs) can develop undesired attention map artifacts that harm interpretability. Darcet et al. proposed adding empty "register" tokens to mitigate this. This paper reassesses that claim across a broader set of architectures—including DINO, DeiT3, OpenCLIP, Swin, and PVT—and finds that while registers effectively eliminate artifacts and offer a small accuracy gain for DINOv2 on ImageNet-1K, the five core claims about artifact origins and token behavior do not universally generalize. Not all large models show artifacts (e.g., DINOv2-S), and some smaller models do (e.g., OpenCLIP-B). Most critically, the functional role of high-norm outlier tokens varies by architecture: in Swin they encode semantic information, while in PVT they encode positional information, contradicting the notion that they universally hold global context.

## Method Summary
The study reproduces experiments from Darcet et al. on DINOv2 models and extends the evaluation to five additional model families (DINO, DeiT3, OpenCLIP, Swin, PVT) using TIMM checkpoints. For each model, final-layer pre-LayerNorm token L2 norms are computed on 5,000 ImageNet-1K validation images to define outlier thresholds (top 2%). Artifact presence is assessed via visualized attention and feature maps. The role of outlier tokens is probed through three linear probe tasks: position prediction, pixel reconstruction, and image classification (using outlier, normal, or [CLS] tokens). The effect of adding registers is tested on DINOv2 variants.

## Key Results
- Registers consistently eliminate both attention map and feature map artifacts across models and slightly improve DINOv2 ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy (e.g., +0.68% for Large).
- Artifact emergence correlates with but is not determined by model size; smaller OpenCLIP-B shows artifacts while larger DINOv2-S does not.
- The information encoded by high-norm outlier tokens is architecture-specific: in Swin they hold semantic content (good at pixel reconstruction), in PVT they hold positional content (perfect at position prediction), and in DINOv2 they appear to hold global context, refuting a universal role.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Artifact Formation via Outlier Tokens
Artifacts arise from a subset of output tokens with abnormally high L2 norms ("outliers"). During training, ViTs route global information aggregation to these tokens, which often reside in visually redundant image regions (e.g., homogeneous backgrounds). These high-norm tokens then dominate the [CLS] attention scores and saturate feature maps, degrading interpretability. The core assumption is that redundant patches provide expendable locations for storing non-local information. Evidence confirms high-norm tokens emerge in redundant regions across models like DINOv2, but this mechanism breaks down in models like DINOv2-S where artifacts are absent despite capacity.

### Mechanism 2: Scaling and Architectural Susceptibility
Large model size (>300M parameters) correlates with outlier token emergence, likely because increased capacity enables dedicated computational pathways. However, size alone is not deterministic; training objectives (e.g., self-supervised iBOT loss in DINOv2) and architectural inductive biases moderate the effect. For instance, hierarchical ViTs like Swin and PVT exhibit outlier tokens even at smaller sizes (<90M), showing that architecture can override simple scaling trends.

### Mechanism 3: Token Specialization and Information Content
High-norm tokens are hypothesized to sacrifice local spatial detail to encode global context, while normal tokens retain local information. This is tested via linear probes: outliers typically underperform on position/pixel tasks but often match or exceed normal patches on classification. However, this pattern is not universal: in Swin, outliers excel at pixel reconstruction (semantic), and in PVT, outliers are perfect at position prediction, demonstrating that token semantics are architecture-dependent rather than globally defined.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input → Patch Embedding (convolution) → Class Token ([CLS]) / Registers (optional) → Positional Encoding → Transformer Blocks (multi-head attention, MLP, LayerNorm) → Output Tokens → (optional) Global Average Pooling for classification.
- **Critical path:** For artifact analysis: forward pass → extract final-layer pre-LayerNorm token norms → reshape to spatial grid → visualize feature map. For attention artifact check: extract final-layer [CLS] attention scores → average heads → reshape to grid.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Adding registers (+4 tokens) increases sequence length slightly but cleans attention maps with minimal accuracy trade-off (±0.5% on ImageNet). Hierarchical ViTs (Swin, PVT) alter spatial resolution across blocks, changing outlier distribution patterns. Model size correlation is weak; training objective may suppress artifacts more than scale.
- **Failure signatures:** Bimodal L2-norm distribution across tokens (separable high-norm peak). Feature maps with saturated/blotchy regions unrelated to image content. [CLS] attention maps dominated by 1-2 locations, even if those are not high-norm in feature maps.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Visualize feature maps for 100 ImageNet-1K validation images at native resolution; check for saturated outliers.  
  2. Plot L2-norm distribution of final pre-LayerNorm tokens (N=5,000 images); identify bimodality or heavy tail.  
  3. Add 4 registers (empty tokens) to input and re-run steps 1–2; confirm artifact removal and measure ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy change.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
What causes high-norm outlier tokens to emerge in ViTs and why do they localize to visually redundant regions? The origin of artifact generation remains unclear; while correlations with model size and patch redundancy exist, no causal mechanism is established, and architecture-dependent behavior suggests multiple factors.
How do hierarchical architectural designs (e.g., Swin, PVT) reshape the division of labor between normal and outlier tokens compared to vanilla ViTs? Only a few architectures were studied; specific inductive biases responsible for differences (e.g., pyramid decoupling, shifted windows) are not isolated.
Do register tokens encode global information complementary to the [CLS] token, or is their representational content redundant? Classification using registers showed no improvement over [CLS]-based methods, suggesting possible overlap, but direct representation similarity and alternative usage schemes (e.g., registers alone) were not explored.
What causes inconsistent norm patterns in Query, Key, Value feature maps across models, and how do these intermediate representations lead to final feature map artifacts? Visualizations reveal mismatches between QKV norms and final artifacts, but no quantitative link has been established; the temporal emergence of artifacts across layers is unknown.

## Limitations
- Reproducibility gaps: exact checkpoint identifiers for DINOv2 with registers, downstream task hyperparameters (learning rates, batch size), and sampling details for the 200k-image classification subset are unspecified.
- Architectural dependency: token behavior (global vs. local encoding) is confirmed architecture-specific (Swin vs. PVT), undermining universal claims; precipitating architectural features are not fully isolated.
- Training paradigm confound: artifact presence correlates with both model size and training objective (self-supervised vs. supervised); the relative influence of each factor remains unclear.

## Confidence
- **High:** Registers eliminate attention/feature map artifacts and marginally improve DINOv2 ImageNet-1K accuracy. Supported by consistent ablations across DINOv2 variants.
- **Medium:** Artifact emergence correlates with large model size (>300M) but is not deterministic. Evidence shows OpenCLIP-B (small) exhibits artifacts while DINOv2-S (large) does not, indicating moderating factors like training objective and architecture.
- **Low:** Universal functional role of high-norm tokens as global-information encoders. Contradicted by Swin (outliers good at pixel reconstruction) and PVT (outliers perfect at position prediction); token semantics are architecture-specific.
