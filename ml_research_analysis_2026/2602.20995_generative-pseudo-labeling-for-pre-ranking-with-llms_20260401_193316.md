# Generative Pseudo-Labeling for Pre-Ranking with LLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.20995  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20995  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Online A/B test on Taobao: CTR +3.07%, IPV +3.53%, CTCVR +2.51%, UV3 +1.50%, Dwell Time +2.16%.

## Executive Summary
Pre-ranking models suffer from train-serving discrepancy: they are trained only on exposed user interactions but must score all recalled items (including unexposed) during serving, causing severe sample selection bias and harming generalization, especially for long-tail items. Generative Pseudo-Labeling (GPL) addresses this by using an LLM to generate user-specific "interest anchors" in a discrete semantic space derived from a content-only RQ-VAE. Unexposed items are matched to these anchors via cosine similarity in a frozen multimodal embedding space, producing relevance-based pseudo-labels. These pseudo-labels are uncertainty-calibrated using semantic dispersion, historical consistency, and LLM confidence, then jointly optimized with actual exposed labels to train the pre-ranker. All LLM inference runs offline. The method yields significant online improvements on Taobao and enhanced long-tail discovery and diversity, confirmed by ablations that validate the core components.

## Method Summary
GPL operates through an offline pipeline and online training. First, a content-only RQ-VAE is trained on multimodal item embeddings (from a frozen CLIP+Q-Former) to produce hierarchical Semantic IDs (SIDs) and a SID-to-item lookup. Next, an LLM (Qwen2.5-0.5B with LoRA) is fine-tuned on SID sequences to generate user-specific interest anchors via beam search. For each unexposed candidate item, its pseudo-label is computed as the sigmoid of the maximum cosine similarity between its embedding and any anchor embedding, scaled by a temperature τ. Three uncertainty signals—semantic dispersion of anchors, historical consistency of anchors with past behavior, and intrinsic LLM confidence—are combined into a sample-level confidence weight. Finally, a lightweight pre-ranker is trained with a joint objective: binary cross-entropy on actual exposed interactions plus a confidence-weighted binary cross-entropy on pseudo-labeled unexposed pairs, balanced by λ.

## Key Results
- Online A/B test (Taobao): CTR +3.07%, IPV +3.53%, CTCVR +2.51%, UV3 +1.50%, Dwell Time +2.16%.
- Offline (Industrial Dataset): HR@3 from 0.5216 (best baseline UECF) to 0.5254, AUC from 0.7246 to 0.7299, GAUC from 0.6313 to 0.6338.
- Enhanced long-tail discovery: CTR lift increases for lower-PV items; top-10 category concentration reduced from 70.80% to 45.91%.
- Ablation: Removing semantic IDs, confidence weighting, or actual labels all degrade performance, confirming critical roles of each component.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** GPL reduces train-serving discrepancy by generating relevance-based pseudo-labels for unexposed items using content-aware interest anchors.
- **Mechanism:** An LLM predicts future semantically tokenized user interests (SIDs) from historical interactions. These interest anchors are matched to unexposed candidates via cosine similarity in a frozen multimodal embedding space, producing a relevance score that serves as a pseudo-label. This aligns the training distribution (exposed-only) with the serving distribution (full recall set).
- **Core assumption:** LLM-generated interest anchors derived from content-grounded SIDs can provide an unbiased estimate of user relevance for unexposed items, decoupled from historical exposure patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "...leverages large language models (LLMs) to generate unbiased, content-aware pseudo-labels for unexposed items, explicitly aligning the training distribution with the online serving space."  
  [section 3.1] "The core challenge is to assign reliable pseudo-labels...We tackle this via a content-aware preference estimator...and train f with a joint objective."
- **Break condition:** If the LLM's semantic space (SIDs) is not well-aligned with the multimodal embedding space used for matching, relevance scores become miscalibrated, leading to noisy or misleading pseudo-labels that harm rather than help generalization.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Uncertainty calibration prevents noisy or irrelevant pseudo-labels from degrading pre-ranker training.
- **Mechanism:** Three orthogonal uncertainty signals—semantic dispersion (coherence of generated anchors), historical consistency (alignment with past behavior), and LLM-intrinsic confidence (generation probability)—are combined into a sample-level confidence weight. This weight down-weights unreliable (𝑢, ℎ) pairs during the confidence-weighted BCE loss.
- **Core assumption:** The three signals together effectively capture the reliability of an LLM-generated interest anchor for a specific user-candidate pair, allowing the model to filter out hallucinated or poorly matched annotations.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.4.1] "We first calculate the anchor-level confidence weight...and then apply user-level calibration based on semantic dispersion σ𝑢...The final confidence weight is obtained via softmax normalization."  
  [section 4.3] Ablation shows removing any confidence component (dispersion, consistency, confidence) degrades AUC/HR, with "Semantic Dispersion" removal causing the largest drop among confidence terms.
- **Break condition:** If uncertainty signals are miscalibrated (e.g., overconfident on erroneous anchors or underweighting valid diverse interests), the confidence weighting fails to suppress noise, leading to overfitting on spurious pseudo-labels.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Joint optimization on both actual exposed labels and calibrated pseudo-labels stabilizes training and prevents catastrophic forgetting of observed user feedback.
- **Mechanism:** The pre-ranker f is trained with a combined loss: binary cross-entropy on actual exposed interactions (De) plus a confidence-weighted BCE on pseudo-labeled unexposed candidates (Du). The balance λ is tuned to prevent the model from drifting toward noisy pseudo-supervision.
- **Core assumption:** Actual interaction signals provide a necessary grounding truth that anchors the model, while pseudo-labels enrich the training distribution with content-based relevance estimates for unexposed items. This dual-supervision enables discovery without abandoning learned patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.4.2] "The final training objective combines both actual labels and estimated supervision...where λ balances the contribution of pseudo-labels."  
  [section 4.3, "w/o Actual Labels"] Removing actual labels causes HR@3 to plummet from 0.5254 to 0.3687, demonstrating their indispensable stabilizing role.
- **Break condition:** An imbalanced λ (too high) causes the model to overfit to noisy pseudo-labels, degrading performance on truly relevant items. An imbalanced λ (too low) fails to leverage unexposed items sufficiently, leaving SSB unmitigated.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Sample Selection Bias (SSB)**  
  Why needed: GPL targets SSB, the core problem where pre-rankers trained only on exposed interactions must serve scores for all recalled items. Understanding SSB is necessary to grasp why pseudo-labels for unexposed items are needed.  
  Quick check question: *Why does training a model only on items that were historically shown to users (exposed) create a distribution shift when the same model must score all recalled items during serving?*

- **Concept: Discrete Semantic Tokenization (SIDs via RQ-VAE)**  
  Why needed: SIDs convert items into a discrete, content-grounded sequence for LLM processing, avoiding exposure bias in the token space. This is the foundation for generating bias-resilient interest anchors.  
  Quick check question: *How does training an RQ-VAE solely on multimodal item embeddings (without user interaction data) help mitigate exposure bias in the subsequent LLM-based pseudo-labeling?*

- **Concept: Confidence-Weighted Multi-Task Learning**  
  Why needed: GPL jointly optimizes two loss terms with per-sample confidence weighting. This concept is key to understanding how noisy pseudo-labels are integrated safely.  
  Quick check question: *In a joint training objective L = Lal + λ Lpl, what risk does the confidence weight w𝑢,ℎ in Lpl specifically mitigate, and what would happen if w𝑢,ℎ were uniform (all 1)?*

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Multimodal Encoder (frozen) -> RQ-VAE (trained offline) -> LLM with LoRA -> Similarity Matcher -> Uncertainty Quantifier -> Pre-ranker (trainable). Offline Pipeline orchestrates steps 1–5.
- **Critical path:** Offline batch generation (steps 1–5) -> pseudo-labeled training set -> train pre-ranker (step 6) -> online serving (pre-ranker only). The offline path must complete before the next training cycle.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  LLM size vs. cost/quality: Larger LLMs improve pseudo-label quality (AUC*) but increase offline compute; default uses 0.5B Qwen2.5 for cost-effectiveness.  
  Beam width (𝐵) vs. noise: Larger 𝐵 increases interest coverage but adds lower-quality anchors; confidence weighting mitigates.  
  Temperature (τ) vs. label sharpness: Lower τ yields more binary pseudo-labels but discards intra-class distinctions; tuned via validation (log(τ)≈−1.2 optimal).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  Degraded long-tail discovery: If semantic dispersion (σ𝑢) is not properly calibrated (or omitted), the model may ignore diverse anchors, harming long-tail CTR.  
  Poor pseudo-label quality (AUC* ↓): Indicates failure in semantic alignment—check RQ-VAE tokenization or multimodal encoder fidelity.  
  Online performance drop after λ increase: Suggests pseudo-labels are too noisy; revisit uncertainty weighting or LLM fine-tuning.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Ablation of semantic grounding: Disable RQ-VAE (use raw item IDs) → expect significant HR/AUC drop; validates necessity of content-only tokenization.  
  2. Confidence weighting ablation: Set all w𝑢,ℎ=1 → expect performance drop, especially with larger beam width; validates uncertainty calibration.  
  3. Pseudo-label quality sanity check: Compute AUC* (AUC between pseudo-labels and real feedback on exposed items). If AUC* is very low (<0.6), investigate LLM generation or multimodal alignment before full training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can tokenization objectives be designed to incorporate beneficial behavioral signals without re-introducing exposure bias?  
  Basis: Appendix A.3 demonstrates that adding a co-occurrence contrastive loss (behavioral signal) improves pseudo-label alignment with observed feedback (AUC* +0.003) but harms overall ranking performance (AUC -0.007). The paper opts for a content-only tokenizer, acknowledging this trade-off.  
  Why unresolved: The paper provides no principled method to balance content fidelity against behavioral relevance; the decision is empirical and may not generalize across datasets or domains.  
  What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of tokenization objectives with varying behavioral regularization strengths, measuring both pseudo-label quality (AUC*) and final ranking metrics, supported by theoretical analysis of how behavioral signals propagate bias into the discrete token space.

- **Open Question 2:** Is the three-component confidence weighting scheme (semantic dispersion, historical consistency, LLM confidence) optimal for estimating uncertainty in LLM-generated pseudo-labels?  
  Basis: The paper proposes this combination and shows via ablation that each component contributes to performance. However, the weighting formula is heuristic and not compared to alternatives.  
  Why unresolved: Alternative uncertainty quantification methods (e.g., ensembles, Bayesian approaches, calibration techniques) are unexplored. The relative importance (λ₁, λ₂) is manually tuned, and the maximum performance drop from removing any component is up to 0.002 AUC.  
  What evidence would resolve it: Comparative study of different uncertainty models on robustness across user segments and long-tail items, with online A/B tests to measure business impact and calibration error.

- **Open Question 3:** Should the balance weight λ between actual and pseudo-label losses be adaptive to user/item characteristics rather than static?  
  Basis: Section 4.4 (Figure 3a) shows performance is sensitive to λ, with an optimal static range (0.1–1.0) found via online A/B testing.  
  Why unresolved: The paper does not consider that different user groups (e.g., cold-start vs. active) or item types (head vs. tail) may require different λ values. A static λ may be suboptimal for heterogeneous populations, potentially over-trusting noisy pseudo-labels for some and under-utilizing them for others.  
  What evidence would resolve it: Evaluation of adaptive λ schemes (e.g., higher λ for long-tail items, lower for popular items; or per-user based on confidence or activity level) on offline metrics (GAUC) and online A/B tests, measuring segment-specific lifts and overall stability.

- **Open Question 4:** How frequently should offline-generated interest anchors be refreshed to maintain recommendation relevance without excessive computational cost?  
  Basis: The pipeline generates and caches interest anchors offline. The paper mentions updates only for new items, but user anchor regeneration frequency is unspecified.  
  Why unresolved: User interests evolve; stale anchors may degrade recommendation quality. However, frequent LLM inference is costly. The trade-off between freshness and computational overhead is not studied, leaving operational deployment ambiguous.  
  What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal study measuring recommendation performance (e.g., CTR, diversity) decay over time since anchor generation, combined with a cost-benefit analysis of different update schedules (e.g., daily, hourly), to identify an optimal refresh interval.

## Limitations
- LLM-generated anchors may not reflect true user intent beyond superficial semantic patterns; reasoning fidelity is unvalidated.
- Generalizability beyond e-commerce (e.g., news, video) is unknown.
- Computational cost of offline LLM generation vs. long-tail benefit lacks rigorous trade-off analysis.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (SSB mitigation via content-aware anchors): Medium  
- Mechanism 2 (Uncertainty calibration): High  
- Mechanism 3 (Joint optimization): High
