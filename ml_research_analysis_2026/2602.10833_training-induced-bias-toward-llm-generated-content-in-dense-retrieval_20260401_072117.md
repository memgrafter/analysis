# Training-Induced Bias Toward LLM-Generated Content in Dense Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10833
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10833
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: Training dense retrievers induces a bias toward LLM-generated content over human-written text.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates source bias in dense retrieval models, focusing on whether they systematically prefer LLM-generated text over human-authored content. Using parallel corpora on two datasets—SciFact (scientific QA) and NQ320K (general QA)—the authors evaluate two dense retrievers, Contriever and E5-Base, in unsupervised and fine-tuned configurations. Fine-tuning is performed on MS MARCO, in-domain human data, and LLM-generated corpora. Results show that unsupervised retrievers exhibit dataset-dependent biases (e.g., Contriever favors human documents on both datasets, while E5-Base favors LLM documents on SciFact). Critically, fine-tuning on MS MARCO consistently shifts models toward a pro-LLM bias. In contrast, fine-tuning on human data yields a strong pro-human bias on NQ320K but inconsistent effects on SciFact, while LLM-generated fine-tuning reinforces pro-LLM bias. The study also refutes the hypothesis that text perplexity explains the bias, as perplexity-relevance agreement rates remain near chance (42–46%). The findings conclude that bias emerges from the training process rather than inherent model properties.

## Method Summary
No reproduction notes were provided. However, the key outcome describes the experimental framework: parallel human-written and LLM-generated document pairs are created for SciFact and NQ320K. Two dense retrievers—Contriever and E5-Base—are evaluated in their original unsupervised form and after fine-tuning on three data sources: MS MARCO (a large general QA dataset), in-domain human documents, and LLM-generated documents. Retrieval performance is measured, and source bias is quantified using a Relative∆ metric that compares the rankings of human versus LLM documents. The authors also compute document perplexity and assess its correlation with relevance scores to test the perplexity hypothesis.

## Key Results
- Unsupervised retrievers show dataset-dependent biases: Contriever Relative∆ +19.9 on SciFact and +17.7 on NQ320K (pro‑human); E5-Base −117.2 on SciFact (pro‑LLM) and +51.0 on NQ320K (pro‑human).
- Fine‑tuning on MS MARCO induces a consistent pro‑LLM shift across models; e.g., Contriever Relative∆ becomes −25.5 on SciFact.
- In‑domain human fine‑training yields strong pro‑human bias on NQ320K (Relative∆ up to +96.4) but variable results on SciFact; fine‑training on LLM‑generated corpora reinforces pro‑LLM bias (Contriever −57.1 on SciFact).
- Perplexity‑relevance agreement rates are near chance (42–46%), rejecting the perplexity hypothesis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper attributes the bias to the training process itself, as fine-tuning on different data sources shifts the bias direction (e.g., MS MARCO induces pro-LLM bias, while in-domain human data can induce pro-human bias). This suggests that the bias is not an inherent property of the model architectures but rather an artifact of the distributional characteristics of the training corpus. However, the paper does not analyze which specific properties (e.g., text length, formality, lexical diversity, or stylistic features) of the training data cause the bias, nor does it explore the interaction between model architecture and training data. The authors also rule out text perplexity as an explanatory factor, leaving the precise mechanism unidentified.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Query Text -> Query Encoder -> Query Embedding; Document Text -> Document Encoder -> Document Embedding; Dot Product -> Similarity Score -> Ranking

**Critical path**  
Encoding the query and candidate documents into dense vectors, computing their dot‑product similarity, and returning the top‑k ranked documents. Retrieval efficiency hinges on approximate nearest‑neighbor search over the indexed document embeddings.

**Design tradeoffs**  
Encoder capacity vs. computational cost; embedding dimensionality vs. storage and accuracy; exact vs. approximate search; contrastive loss formulation; domain specificity vs. generalization.

**Failure signatures**  
Systematic preference for LLM‑generated text; degradation on out‑of‑domain queries; overfitting to the distribution of training data; sensitivity to tokenization choices.

**First experiments**  
1. Evaluate unsupervised dense retrievers on SciFact and NQ320K using parallel human/LLM corpora to measure baseline bias.  
2. Fine‑tune retrievers on MS MARCO and re‑evaluate bias to test for a training‑induced shift.  
3. Fine‑tune on in‑domain human and LLM-generated corpora separately to isolate the effect of training data source.

## Limitations
- Scope limited to scientific and general QA domains; generalizability to other text types (e.g., social media, creative writing) is unknown.
- LLM‑generated corpora are underspecified (model version, prompts, sampling parameters), hindering replication and bias attribution.
- The consistent pro‑LLM bias after MS MARCO fine‑tuning may stem from latent biases in MS MARCO itself (e.g., document length, formality) rather than a universal training effect.
