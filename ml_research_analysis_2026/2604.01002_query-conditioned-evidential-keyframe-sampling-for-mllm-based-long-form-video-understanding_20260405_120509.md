# Query-Conditioned Evidential Keyframe Sampling for MLLM-Based Long-Form Video Understanding

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2604.01002
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01002
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Outperforms prior keyframe sampling methods on LVBench and VideoMME; achieves 10.1% absolute accuracy gain for Qwen2.5-VL-7B on LVBench; reduces training time from 78h to 0.6h.

## Executive Summary
Long-form video understanding with Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is hindered by the computational cost of processing many frames. This work proposes a query-conditioned keyframe sampling framework that selects the most informative frames for a given question. The core idea is to maximize the conditional mutual information between the selected frames and the correct answer, grounded in information bottleneck theory. The selection problem is made tractable by decomposing it into independent frame-level evidence scoring, avoiding the exponential search space of reinforcement learning approaches. A lightweight network, trained with a contrastive objective, predicts each frame’s evidential contribution. On standard benchmarks, the method consistently improves accuracy under strict frame budgets and drastically reduces training time compared to prior RL-based approaches.

## Method Summary
The method trains a query-conditioned evidence scoring network \(g_\theta\) using video-question pairs with temporal evidence segment annotations (from the LLaVA-Video subset of Seek-173K). Frames within annotated segments are treated as positive examples (higher expected evidential value) and frames outside as negatives. The network architecture comprises: a frozen CLIP-ViT vision-language encoder; a causal window temporal aggregator to capture short-term motion; a query-guided gating mechanism to suppress irrelevant visual channels; and a multi-subspace evidence score head that combines cosine similarities across subspaces with a CLIP prior term. Training uses the InfoNCE contrastive loss to rank positive frames higher than negatives, which approximates the desired conditional mutual information ordering. At inference, all frames are scored independently, and the top-scoring frames are selected per temporal bin under a given budget. The selected frames are then fed to a frozen MLLM (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B) for answer generation, with the MLLM excluded from training to ensure efficiency.

## Key Results
- On LVBench and VideoMME, the method consistently outperforms prior sampling strategies (uniform, ReTaKe, TSPO) under identical frame budgets.
- Achieves a 10.1% absolute accuracy gain for Qwen2.5-VL-7B on LVBench compared to previous state-of-the-art.
- Reduces training time from 78 hours (RL-based TSPO) to 0.6 hours on the same hardware by decoupling the MLLM from the training loop and using contrastive learning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Formulating keyframe selection as maximizing conditional mutual information (CMI) between selected frames and the answer given the query aligns the objective with the ultimate goal of improving MLLM answer accuracy.
**Mechanism:** The information bottleneck objective \(\max_S I(S;O|Q)\) is theoretically equivalent to maximizing \(\mathbb{E}[\log p(O|S, Q)]\). This means selecting frames that most reduce the MLLM's uncertainty about the correct answer. The CMI term inherently captures query-dependent evidential value: a frame's contribution is measured by how much it changes the distribution over possible answers given the query.
**Core assumption:** The MLLM's answer distribution \(p(O|S, Q)\) is a meaningful proxy for task performance; optimizing its expected log-likelihood correlates with maximizing accuracy.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...formulating keyframe selection as maximizing the conditional mutual information between selected frames and the query answer, grounded in information bottleneck theory."; [section 3.1] "Since \(p(O|Q)\) is a constant ... maximizing \(I(S;O|Q)\) is equivalent to: \(\max_S \mathbb{E}[\log p(O|S, Q)]\). This equivalence confirms that the information bottleneck objective is consistent with the intuition: the selected frames should be those that maximally support the MLLM in predicting the correct answer."
**Break condition:** If the MLLM's likelihood \(p(O|S, Q)\) is poorly calibrated (e.g., overconfident on incorrect answers), maximizing expected log-likelihood may not improve accuracy. The equivalence assumes the model's probability estimates reflect true correctness.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Decomposing the combinatorial subset selection problem into independent per-frame scoring via a modular upper bound on the submodular objective makes the problem tractable and avoids exponential search.
**Mechanism:** The CMI objective \(F(S) = I(S;O|Q)\) is proven monotone submodular. A known property provides \(F(S) \leq \sum_{f_i \in S} F(\{f_i\}) = \sum_{f_i \in S} I(f_i;O|Q)\). This modular upper bound allows greedy, parallelizable selection: score each frame independently by its individual CMI contribution and pick top-scoring frames, optionally constrained by temporal bins. This avoids evaluating all \(\binom{n}{m}\) subsets.
**Core assumption:** The modular upper bound is a sufficiently tight relaxation that preserves the ranking of high-value frames. The diminishing returns property (submodularity) holds for the evidence objective in the context of MLLM answer uncertainty reduction.
**Evidence anchors:** [section 3.1] "The objective possesses submodularity ... By the classical result [23], greedy selection achieves a \((1-1/e)\)-approximation. However, \(K\) sequential passes over \(n\) frames remain expensive ... a fully parallelizable selection rule is obtained by relaxing the submodular objective with its modular upper bound."; [section 3.1] "We adopt a temporally-adaptive frame selection strategy ... the top-\(k\) scoring frames are selected from each segment according to their conditional mutual information."
**Break condition:** If the evidence objective is not truly submodular (e.g., due to strong inter-frame dependencies that violate diminishing returns), the greedy modular selection may perform poorly. The relaxation assumes \(I(f_i;O|Q)\) can be estimated independently.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Training a query-conditioned evidence scoring network \(g_\theta(f_i, Q)\) with a contrastive objective (InfoNCE) provides a dense, frame-level proxy for CMI that is efficient to compute and avoids the credit assignment problem of RL.
**Mechanism:** Since exact computation of \(I(f_i;O|Q)\) requires knowing the unknown answer \(O\), the method trains \(g_\theta\) to rank frames by evidential value. Using annotated evidence segments, frames within segments are positives (higher expected CMI) and frames outside are negatives. The InfoNCE loss encourages \(g_\theta(x, Q)\) to be higher for positives. At optimum, \(g_\theta^* \propto \log \frac{p(x|\mathcal{F}^+,Q)}{p(x|\mathcal{F}^-,Q)}\), which correlates with the desired CMI ranking. This provides a dense supervision signal per frame, unlike sparse answer-accuracy rewards in RL.
**Core assumption:** The annotated evidence segments provide a reasonable partition: frames inside have, in expectation, higher \(I(f_i;O|Q)\) than those outside. The contrastive objective sufficiently recovers the ranking of CMI values.
**Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] "A frame is labeled as positive if it falls within a temporal evidence segment ... the evidence intervals are verified to be sufficient for MLLMs to correctly answer \(Q\), which implies that, in expectation, \(p(O|x \sim \mathcal{F}^+, Q)\) is higher than \(p(O|x \sim \mathcal{F}^-, Q)\), and consequently positive frames tend to have higher \(I(x;O|Q)\) than negative frames."; [section 3.3] "We train \(g_\theta\) with the InfoNCE loss ... At optimality, the InfoNCE critic recovers the log density ratio ... This ratio captures how much more likely a frame is to be evidential than background for a given query."; [section 4.4] Training time reduced from 78 hours (RL) to 0.6 hours, demonstrating efficiency gain from decoupling MLLM from loop and using contrastive losses.
**Break condition:** If the evidence segment annotations are noisy or not truly aligned with CMI (e.g., a segment contains frames that are necessary but not sufficient, or includes irrelevant frames), the contrastive training may learn a poor ranking. The method also assumes that frame-level independence is a reasonable approximation for scoring.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Information Bottleneck Principle**
  - **Why needed here:** Provides the theoretical justification for the core optimization objective (maximizing CMI). It frames keyframe selection as compressing the video while preserving maximal information relevant to answering the query.
  - **Quick check question:** Given the equivalence \(\max_S I(S;O|Q) \equiv \max_S \mathbb{E}[\log p(O|S, Q)]\), why does maximizing mutual information correlate with maximizing answer accuracy? (Answer: Because it directly optimizes the expected log-likelihood of the correct answer under the MLLM's distribution.)
- **Concept: Submodularity and Greedy Approximation**
  - **Why needed here:** Justifies the move from intractable combinatorial optimization to an efficient, modular selection rule. The submodular property of the CMI objective guarantees that greedy selection yields a bounded optimality gap.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the key property of a submodular function that enables the \((1-1/e)\)-approximation guarantee for greedy selection? (Answer: Diminishing returns: the marginal gain of adding an element to a larger set is less than or equal to adding it to a smaller set.)
- **Concept: Contrastive Learning for Ranking (InfoNCE)**
  - **Why needed here:** Transforms the difficult problem of estimating per-frame CMI (a density ratio) into a tractable ranking problem. It provides dense, frame-level supervision without requiring MLLM rollouts or answer correctness signals at each frame.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the InfoNCE loss \(\mathcal{L} = -\log \frac{\sum_{x^+} \exp g_\theta(x^+, Q)}{\sum_{x^+} \exp g_\theta(x^+, Q) + \sum_{x^-} \exp g_\theta(x^-, Q)}\) encourage the network to learn the desired ranking? (Answer: It minimizes the distance between positive frame scores and maximizes the distance between negative and positive scores, approximating the log-likelihood ratio between positive and negative frame distributions.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input: Dense frame sequence \(V = \{f_1, ..., f_n\}\) (uniform FPS) and query \(Q\) → Vision-Language Encoder (frozen CLIP-ViT) produces frame embeddings \(v_i\) and query embedding \(q\) → Temporal Evidence Aggregator (causal window attention over \(k\) neighbors) yields contextualized \(h_\tau\) → Query-Guided Evidence Gating (linear layers + sigmoid) produces channel-wise gates \(g_i\); element-wise multiplication \(u_i = h_i \odot g_i\) suppresses irrelevant channels → Evidence Score Head (multi-subspace cosine similarity between \(u_i\) and \(q\), plus raw \(v_i^\top q\) term weighted by \(\lambda\)) outputs scalar score \(g_\theta(f_i, Q)\) → Selection Module (inference): scores all frames, selects top-\(k\) per temporal bin under budget \(m = B \times k\) → Downstream MLLM (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B) receives selected frames and \(Q\) to generate answer \(O\).
- **Critical path:** Frame embedding → Temporal aggregation → Query-guided gating → Multi-subspace scoring → Frame ranking → Selection → MLLM inference.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Causal window size \(k\):** Small \(k\) limits temporal context to local motion but is computationally cheap and avoids noise from distant frames. Large \(k\) captures longer actions but increases compute and risks incorporating irrelevant context.
  - **Modular relaxation vs. exact CMI:** The independent scoring assumption is computationally essential but discards inter-frame dependencies (e.g., a frame's evidence might depend on both its predecessor and successor). This is a tradeoff for tractability.
  - **Encoder choice:** Frozen CLIP provides strong visual-semantic priors but may not be optimally aligned with the specific video QA task. The lightweight trainable modules adapt it, but the encoder's inherent bias remains.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Temporal clustering:** Frames cluster in a few short segments even when global understanding is needed (Supplement S1.1.2). Indicates over-reliance on local salient evidence.
  - **Audio/Non-visual evidence failure:** System fails on questions requiring audio or precise timestamps (Supplement S1.2) because scoring is purely visual and frame-centric without explicit temporal position encoding.
  - **Encoder mismatch:** Performance varies with encoder (Table 3). A poorly aligned vision-language encoder (e.g., one not trained on video-style queries) yields lower-quality evidence scores.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline comparison:** Evaluate against uniform sampling and prior keyframe methods (ReTaKe, TSPO) on LVBench/VideoMME under identical frame budgets to validate performance gain.
  2. **Ablation: Encoder impact:** Test different frozen vision-language encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP2) within the same scoring framework to measure robustness to encoder choice.
  3. **Ablation: Budget sensitivity:** Vary the number of selected frames \(m\) (e.g., 8, 16, 32, 64) and compare accuracy vs. uniform sampling to demonstrate efficiency under strict constraints.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we dynamically isolate crucial evidence information from highly redundant temporal streams in long-form video understanding?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Consequently, dynamically isolating crucial evidence information from highly redundant temporal streams remains a fundamental open challenge." (Section 2.2)
- **Why unresolved:** The proposed method, while evidence-driven, still faces limitations (e.g., ignoring audio, lacking precise temporal grounding) and the challenge is broad; no universal solution exists yet.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A method that consistently selects frames with high overlap (>90% recall) with ground-truth evidence segments across diverse query types, achieving MLLM accuracy close to an oracle on multiple long-video benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can the evidence scoring framework be extended to incorporate audio information for audio-centric queries?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Extending the evidence scoring framework to incorporate audio modality, e.g., via audio-visual contrastive objectives or joint audio-visual encoders, represents a promising direction for future work." (Section S1.2, Limitations)
- **Why unresolved:** The current framework processes only visual frames, missing acoustic cues that can be critical for answering certain questions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A multi-modal scoring network that ingests both visual and audio streams, trained on an audio-visual QA dataset and demonstrating significant accuracy improvement on audio-focused question subsets of benchmarks like Video-MME.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can evidence scoring be made sensitive to precise temporal grounding required by timestamp-specific queries?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "The scoring network is not explicitly trained to reason about absolute timestamps or temporal intervals... Future work could integrate explicit temporal position embeddings or timestamp-aware supervision signals to address this limitation." (Section S1.2, Limitations)
- **Why unresolved:** Independent frame-level scoring without temporal position awareness fails to capture frames that exactly match required time intervals, leading to underperformance on questions like "What happens from 02:45-04:00?"
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Incorporating absolute/relative time embeddings into the scoring network and evaluating on a benchmark with precise time-grounded questions; success would be indicated by improved accuracy and tighter temporal IoU with ground-truth intervals.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can evidence scores be learned without ground-truth evidence segment annotations, reducing reliance on costly human labeling?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method's training requires annotated evidence segments to construct positive/negative frame pairs for contrastive learning (Section 4.1.1). Such fine-grained annotations may be unavailable for many videos.
- **Why unresolved:** Dependence on human-annotated evidence segments limits scalability and applicability to in-the-wild videos where these annotations do not exist.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An unsupervised or self-supervised approach that learns evidence scores solely from video-question pairs (and possibly MLLM feedback), achieving comparable performance to the supervised method on benchmarks like LVBench and Video-MME.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters (window size, subspaces, \(\lambda\)) are not reported, affecting reproducibility.
- Evidence segment annotations may be noisy or incomplete; the assumption that frames inside have higher CMI is untested.
- The modular relaxation discards inter-frame dependencies; performance may degrade on videos with long-range temporal dependencies.
