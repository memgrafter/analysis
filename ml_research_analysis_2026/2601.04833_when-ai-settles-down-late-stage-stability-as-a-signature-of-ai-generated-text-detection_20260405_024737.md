# When AI Settles Down: Late-Stage Stability as a Signature of AI-Generated Text Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04833
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04833
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** AI-generated text exhibits 24–32% lower volatility in second-half log-probability fluctuations than human writing, enabling a simple, perturbation-free detector that achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot detection on EvoBench and MAGE.

## Executive Summary
Existing zero-shot AI text detectors typically aggregate token statistics across entire sequences, overlooking the temporal dynamics of autoregressive generation. This paper reveals a "Late-Stage Volatility Decay" pattern: language models stabilize their predictions much faster in the second half of generated text compared to human writing. Exploiting this, the authors propose Temporal Stability Detection (TSD), a method using two features—Derivative Dispersion and Local Volatility—computed solely from late-stage tokens. TSD attains strong detection performance without any perturbation sampling, and when fused with Fast-DetectGPT, it sets a new state-of-the-art on major benchmarks.

## Method Summary
Given a text sequence, the method first obtains per-token conditional log-probabilities using a surrogate pre-trained language model (e.g., Llama-3-8B-Instruct). For a sequence of length `n`, it isolates the second half of token positions (`i > floor(n/2)`). Two volatility features are then extracted from this late-stage region: (1) Derivative Dispersion (DD) is the standard deviation of absolute differences in successive log-probabilities, measuring the variability in change rates; (2) Local Volatility (LV) is the mean of sliding-window standard deviations (window size 20) over the same region, capturing local instability. The Temporal Stability Detection score is `S_TSD = -(DD + LV)`, where higher scores indicate AI-generated text. An optional fusion score combines `S_TSD` with the Fast-DetectGPT sampling discrepancy score. Thresholds for binary classification are chosen on a validation set, and performance is evaluated via AUROC on held-out test sets.

## Key Results
- AI-generated text shows 24–32% lower volatility (DD+LV) in its second half compared to human writing across diverse models and benchmarks.
- The perturbation-free TSD method alone outperforms prior zero-shot detectors like DetectGPT and Rank1 on EvoBench and MAGE.
- Fusion of TSD with Fast-DetectGPT yields further gains, establishing a new state-of-the-art for zero-shot AI text detection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Autoregressive language models generate text by iteratively predicting the next token conditioned on previous ones. As generation progresses, the model's context becomes richer and its predictions more confident and deterministic, leading to smaller fluctuations in log-probability. Human writing, in contrast, exhibits more consistent stylistic and syntactic variation throughout, resulting in higher late-stage volatility. By focusing exclusively on the second half, TSD isolates this stabilization signal, which is a robust artifact of model-based generation but absent in human text.

## Foundational Learning
- **Autoregressive generation:** The token-by-token prediction process where each new token depends on all previous ones. *Why needed:* It explains why later tokens are generated with more context and thus potentially less volatility. *Quick check:* Generate a sentence with an LLM and compute the per-token log-probabilities; observe general decreasing trend.
- **Log-probability volatility:** The variation in `-log P(t_i | t_<i)` across tokens. *Why needed:* Serves as a direct proxy for the model's prediction confidence and stability at each step. *Quick check:* Compute the standard deviation of log-probabilities for a given text sequence.
- **Sliding-window statistics:** Calculating metrics (e.g., mean, SD) over a moving window of data points. *Why needed:* Enables measurement of local instability (LV) rather than global trends. *Quick check:* Apply a rolling standard deviation (window=20) to a numeric series and observe local fluctuations.
- **Derivative approximation:** Using absolute differences `|s_i - s_{i-1}|` as a discrete proxy for the derivative of the log-probability curve. *Why needed:* Quantifies how rapidly the model's confidence is changing from step to step. *Quick check:* Compute first differences of a log-probability sequence and take their absolute values.
- **Zero-shot detection:** Classifying text as human- or AI-generated without training on labeled examples from the target generator. *Why needed:* Defines the problem setting; TSD aims to work with any black-box model. *Quick check:* Apply a detector to text from an unseen model without fine-tuning.
- **Surrogate model:** A pre-trained LM used to compute log-probabilities for the detector, independent of the generator. *Why needed:* In zero-shot settings, the generator is unknown; a surrogate approximates its token probability space. *Quick check:* Use Llama-2 to score text generated by GPT-4.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Text -> Tokenization (surrogate LM tokenizer) -> Per-token Log-prob Computation (surrogate LM forward pass) -> Second-Half Selection (index `> floor(n/2)`) -> Derivative Dispersion Calculation -> Local Volatility Calculation -> Score Aggregation (`S_TSD = -(DD + LV)`) -> Optional: Fast-DetectGPT Score Fusion -> Thresholding -> Binary Label (Human/AI)

**Critical path:** The core detection logic resides in the two feature extractions (DD and LV) from the late-stage tokens. Any error in selecting the correct token indices or computing windowed statistics directly corrupts the final score.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Simplicity vs. expressivity:* TSD uses two simple, interpretable features but may miss complex patterns captured by perturbation-based methods.
- *Surrogate dependence:* Performance hinges on the surrogate LM's tokenization and probability estimates being reasonably aligned with the generator's implicit distribution.
- *Length sensitivity:* Very short texts provide few late-stage tokens, making volatility estimates noisy and unreliable.

**Failure signatures:**
- Extremely high `DD` or `LV` values for a sequence (indicating unstable late-stage predictions) may flag it as human, but could also arise from a malformed input (e.g., extremely short text, tokenization mismatch).
- Inconsistent performance across text lengths: strong AUROC for long texts (>200 tokens) but near-random for short texts (<50 tokens).
- Significant AUROC drop when changing the surrogate model to one with a different architecture or tokenizer (e.g., from Llama to GPT-J).

**First three experiments to run:**
1. **Baseline replication:** Compute `S_TSD` on a small mixed dataset (e.g., 100 human and 100 AI texts from MAGE) using Llama-3-8B-Instruct as surrogate. Verify that mean TSD scores are higher for AI texts and that AUROC exceeds 0.7.
2. **Length stratification audit:** Bin test texts by the number of tokens in the second half (e.g., <30, 30–100, >100). Compute AUROC per bin to confirm the expected length-dependent performance curve.
3. **Surrogate swap test:** Repeat Experiment 1 using two additional surrogates (e.g., Mistral-7B, GPT-J). Measure AUROC variance; a drop of >5 percentage points indicates strong surrogate dependence.

## Limitations
- **Surrogate model dependency:** The volatility signal strength is tied to the surrogate LM's alignment with the generator; poor alignment (e.g., detecting Claude text with a GPT-2 surrogate) may degrade performance.
- **Short-text instability:** Sequences with few tokens in the second half yield unreliable DD and LV estimates, limiting applicability to very short inputs.
- **Fusion opacity:** The exact parameters for fusing with Fast-DetectGPT (e.g., perturbation count `k`, curvature estimation) are underspecified, hindering exact replication of the claimed SOTA results.

## Confidence
- **Core phenomenon (Late-Stage Volatility Decay):** Medium. Supported by analysis of 120k samples, but likely surrogate-specific and may not generalize to all generator/human distributions.
- **TSD feature effectiveness:** Medium. Simple features show strong in-paper results, but robustness to domain shifts and extremely short texts remains unproven.
- **Fusion superiority (SOTA claim):** Low. Relies on underspecified combination with Fast-DetectGPT; without exact parameter replication, the additive benefit is uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-surrogate robustness test:** Replicate the full pipeline using at least three diverse surrogate LMs (Llama-3-8B, GPT-J, Mistral-7B) on a fixed held-out set. Quantify AUROC variance to assess model-specific artifacts.
2. **Short-sequence performance audit:** Stratify test sets by number of second-half tokens (e.g., <30, 30–100, >100). Report AUROC per bin to identify minimum viable length and potential guardrails.
3. **Fast-DetectGPT fusion protocol reconstruction:** Obtain original Fast-DetectGPT code/parameters (perturbation count `k`, curvature method). Re-run fusion exactly as in its paper and compare to reported `S_fusion` scores; resolve any discrepancies before claiming SOTA.
