# Rethinking Metrics for Lexical Semantic Change Detection
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15716
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15716
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: Introduces AMD and SAMD metrics that capture localized semantic shifts and show superior robustness under dimensionality reduction compared to global metrics APD and PRT.

## Executive Summary
The paper critiques the over-reliance on global aggregation metrics (APD, PRT) in lexical semantic change detection, arguing they can miss localized usage shifts such as sense emergence or disappearance. It proposes two new usage-level metrics: Average Minimum Distance (AMD) and Symmetric Average Minimum Distance (SAMD). These quantify change via local nearest-neighbour correspondence between word usages across time periods, with AMD allowing directional analysis and SAMD enforcing one-to-one matching. Evaluated across seven languages and multiple encoders, AMD and SAMD demonstrate greater robustness, particularly under dimensionality reduction and with non-specialised models. AMD achieves the highest average Spearman correlation in interpretable definition-based spaces, while SAMD excels with specialised encoders like XL-LEXEME.

## Method Summary
The task is lexical semantic change detection (LSCD): given two diachronic corpora (earlier C1, later C2), compute a scalar change score for each target word. Inputs are sets of usage embeddings extracted from contextual encoders (e.g., RoBERTa, XL-LEXEME). The method computes four metrics on usage sets V1, V2: APD (average pairwise cosine distance), PRT (centroid distance), AMD (mean nearest-neighbor distance, symmetric by averaging directional scores), and SAMD (greedy one-to-one matching). Representations are evaluated in FULL (original embeddings), DEF (cosine distance to LLM-generated definitions), PCA (top K principal components), and RAND (random K dimensions). Metrics are correlated with gold graded change scores via Spearman's rank correlation.

## Key Results
- AMD and SAMD capture localized semantic shifts (e.g., sense emergence/disappearance) more effectively than global metrics APD/PRT.
- Under dimensionality reduction to 4 dimensions, AMD and SAMD maintain correlations near 0.400 while APD and PRT drop to near zero.
- AMD achieves highest average performance in definition-based projection spaces; SAMD excels with specialised encoders like XL-LEXEME.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Local nearest-neighbour correspondence (AMD/SAMD) captures localized semantic shifts more effectively than global aggregation (APD/PRT), which can dilute changes affecting only a subset of usages.
- **Mechanism:** For each usage in one corpus, AMD measures its minimum cosine distance to any usage in the other corpus, averaging these minima bidirectionally. This focuses on the best possible cross-period alignment per usage, highlighting usages that lack a close counterpart. SAMD further enforces one-to-one matching to mitigate hubness and capture distributional concentration shifts.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic change often manifests as asymmetric or sparse shifts in the usage distribution, not just a global centroid or full-pairwise divergence.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states metrics "quantify semantic change via local correspondence... capture localised phenomena, such as the emergence of a new sense." Section 3.2 describes AMD/SAMD formulas and rationale. Validation via benchmark scores (SemEval-2020, etc.) where human annotations likely include sparse sense changes.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** AMD and SAMD are more robust to representational degradation (e.g., dimensionality reduction, non-specialised encoders) because local matching relies less on global geometric structure preserved by high-dimensional spaces.
- **Mechanism:** Nearest-neighbour search in reduced spaces can still identify locally proximate points if relative local structure is preserved. Global metrics like APD (mean of all pairwise distances) and PRT (centroid distance) are sensitive to overall variance preservation; aggressive reduction collapses global distances.
- **Core assumption:** Local neighborhood relationships between semantically similar usages are more resilient to noise and dimension loss than global distributional statistics.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 stress test results: "APD and PRT degrade sharply under dimensionality reduction... AMD and SAMD exhibit greater robustness, maintaining correlations near .400 up until only 4 dimensions are retained." Figure 5 shows performance drop under PCA/random reduction, with APD/PRT approaching zero while AMD/SAMD remain stable.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** AMD, when paired with an interpretable definition-based projection space, yields high and robust performance by aligning usage embeddings with human-understandable semantic dimensions.
- **Mechanism:** Usage embeddings are projected into a space where each dimension corresponds to a generated dictionary definition (cosine distance to definition embedding). AMD then measures how well usages from each period match these definitional anchors, making the change score directly interpretable via the most discriminative definitions.
- **Core assumption:** LLM-generated definitions provide a stable, sense-aware basis for projection that organises usage embeddings meaningfully, and misalignment with these anchors indicates temporal change.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4: "AMD is the only metric that significantly gains from projecting its representations to the definition space... in the interpretable definition-based space, AMD achieves the highest average performance." Section 5: "AMD appears particularly suitable for deeper analysis due to its directional decomposition... The definition-based space further enables a more explicit interpretation."

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Contextualised word usage embeddings.
  - *Why needed here:* AMD/SAMD operate on sets of usage-level vectors (one per occurrence) from two time periods, not type-level averages.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the key difference between a prototype vector (PRT) and a usage embedding set for a target word?
- **Concept:** Nearest-neighbour search and greedy matching.
  - *Why needed here:* AMD computes `nndist(a, B)` for each `a` in A; SAMD performs a greedy one-to-one assignment. The algorithmic behaviour (allowing many-to-one vs. one-to-one) defines their different properties.
  - *Quick check question:* How does SAMD's greedy matching procedure differ from the independent nearest-neighbour lookups in AMD?
- **Concept:** Dimensionality reduction (PCA, random projection) and its effect on distance metrics.
  - *Why needed here:* The core robustness claim hinges on how metrics behave when embeddings are projected to lower-dimensional subspaces (definition-space, PCA, random). Understanding variance preservation (PCA) vs. arbitrary subspace (random) is key.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might PCA-preserved dimensions help AMD/SAMD more than random dimensions, based on the paper's results?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Corpora -> Usage Extractor -> Contextual Encoder -> Usage Sets -> (Optional) Projector -> Metric Engine -> Output
- **Critical path:** Extract usages → Encode → (Optional: Project) → Compute distances/alignments → Aggregate → Score.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - AMD vs SAMD: AMD is simpler, allows directional decomposition, but susceptible to hubness (many-to-one matching). SAMD is symmetric, robust to hubs, but computationally heavier (O(n²) greedy match) and loses directionality.
  - Full vs Reduced Space: Full embeddings capture maximal information but are noisy/high-dim. Reduced spaces (especially definition-based) improve robustness/interpretability but depend on quality of projection.
  - Encoder choice: Specialised (XL-LEXEME) boosts all metrics but especially SAMD; non-specialised models benefit more from AMD + definition-space.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Global metric (APD/PRT) unexpectedly outperforms local metrics: likely indicates a broad, uniform distributional shift without sparse or asymmetric usage changes.
  - All metrics collapse under dimensionality reduction: local structure completely destroyed (e.g., `K` too small, random projection with `K<4` per stress test).
  - Definition-space AMD performs poorly: generated definitions may not cover the true senses present in the corpora, or encoder's definition-space organisation is poor.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate Figure 2: Run all 4 metrics on FULL and DEF spaces (using provided code) across 2-3 languages to confirm AMD's DEF-space advantage.
  2. Run the stress test (varying `K` from full dim down to 2 using PCA/random) for one language/encoder to observe the divergence between APD/PRT and AMD/SAMD.
  3. Compute directional AMD scores for a few words with known change direction (e.g., "plane" narrowing) and inspect top nearest-neighbour pairs to validate that `AMD(A→B)` and `AMD(B→A)` separate emergence vs. disappearance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the mechanistic reason why local correspondence metrics (AMD/SAMD) are more robust to dimensionality reduction and embedding noise than global aggregation metrics (APD/PRT)?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state in the Limitations section: "we do not provide a mechanistic account of the underlying causes. Understanding how different semantic change metrics interact with embedding geometry and variance structure remains an open question."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates empirical robustness differences but offers only hypothetical explanations (e.g., reliance on global distributional geometry). A formal analysis linking metric behavior to specific geometric properties (e.g., distance concentration, subspace variance) is absent.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments systematically varying embedding properties (e.g., intrinsic dimensionality, signal-to-noise ratio, hubness) combined with theoretical analysis of metric sensitivity to such properties. Probing the alignment between reduced subspaces and semantic dimensions could isolate the factors driving robustness.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How do semantic change metrics behave under other forms of embedding manipulation beyond dimensionality reduction, such as noise injection, domain-adaptive fine-tuning, or alignment procedures?
- **Basis in paper:** The Limitations state: "Our robustness analysis is limited to different types of dimensionality reduction. We do not explore the behaviour of these metrics under other forms of embedding manipulation, such as noise injection, domain-adaptive fine-tuning, or alternative normalization and alignment procedures."
- **Why unresolved:** The study focuses on PCA/random reduction as a stress test, but real-world LSCD pipelines often involve additional transformations (e.g., corpus-specific fine-tuning, mean-centering, cross-corpora alignment). The interaction of metrics with these common steps is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extending the experimental matrix to include varied preprocessing (e.g., adding Gaussian noise, applying domain adaptation via continued training, testing different alignment methods like Procrustes or optimal transport) and measuring metric stability and correlation with gold scores.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How sensitive are definition-based projection results (and thus AMD's performance) to the choice of LLM, prompting strategy, and number of generated definitions?
- **Basis in paper:** The Limitations note: "All definitions... are generated using a single large language model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) with a fixed prompting strategy... We do not evaluate the sensitivity of our results to alternative LLMs, prompting strategies, or numbers of generated definitions."
- **Why unresolved:** Definition spaces are central to the interpretability argument for AMD, but their construction is entirely dependent on external LLM choices. Variability in definition quality, granularity, or coverage could drastically alter the projected space and change scores.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Re-running the definition-based experiments with multiple LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, Llama), varying prompt templates (e.g., requesting varying numbers of senses), and systematically controlling definition count. Analyzing the stability of AMD scores and top-changed words across these conditions.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** To what extent do AMD and SAMD actually facilitate *discovery* of genuine, previously unknown semantic changes in large, uncontrolled vocabularies, beyond performance on curated benchmark datasets?
- **Basis in paper:** The Limitations state: "While we evaluate AMD across multiple languages and encoders, our analysis is restricted to existing LSCD datasets with curated target words and gold change scores. The extent to which AMD facilitates lexical semantic change discovery... remains to be investigated." The Discussion also references concerns (Umarova et al., 2025) about methods failing in "the wild."
- **Why unresolved:** The evaluation uses curated target words with known changes. AMD's robustness in low-dimensional spaces is promising for discovery scenarios, but it is unknown whether it reduces false positives from topical/domain shifts when applied to full vocabularies without pre-selection.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Large-scale application of AMD (and baseline metrics) to unlemmatized, full-vocabulary diachronic corpora (e.g., historical newspapers, web archives) without pre-curation. The results would need human evaluation or comparison to external resources (e.g., etymological dictionaries) to assess precision in detecting genuine, non-topical semantic shifts.

## Limitations
- Definition-space performance hinges on LLM-generated sense inventories; no analysis of definition quality variation or alternative prompt strategies.
- SAMD's greedy matching procedure details (tie-breaking, multiple subsampling) are unspecified, potentially affecting hubness mitigation.
- PCA implementation specifics (per-word centering, distance metric post-projection) are unclear, which could alter stress test outcomes.

## Confidence
- Robustness under dimensionality reduction (APD/PRT collapse vs. AMD/SAMD stability): High confidence; consistent across languages/encoders and visually clear in figures.
- AMD's advantage in definition-based projection spaces: Medium confidence; relies on a single LLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) without human-validated sense coverage.
- SAMD's superior performance with specialised encoders (e.g., XL-LEXEME): Medium confidence; plausible but lacks ablation on matching algorithm alternatives (e.g., Hungarian).
