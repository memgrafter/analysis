# ROKA: Robust Knowledge Unlearning against Adversaries
## Quick Facts
*   arXiv ID: 2603.00436
*   Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00436
*   Reference count: 29
*   Primary result: ROKA achieves near-zero target accuracy on forgotten classes while preserving or enhancing retain accuracy, directly mitigating indirect unlearning attacks.

## Executive Summary
Conventional machine unlearning methods, such as gradient ascent, often cause "Knowledge Contamination"—an imbalanced degradation in accuracy across retained classes. This instability creates a new attack surface: an adversary with gray-box access can exploit these predictable accuracy shifts to degrade a security-critical class by requesting the unlearning of an unrelated one. ROKA addresses this by introducing "Neural Healing" via a Contribution Re-allocation procedure. Instead of merely destroying a forgotten component's influence, ROKA nullifies it while proportionally strengthening related "sibling" neurons. Evaluations on vision transformers, CLIP, and LLMs show ROKA effectively forgets target classes (e.g., mTA ≤0.24 on CIFAR-100) with minimal collateral damage to retained knowledge (e.g., mRA 0.8979 vs baseline 0.9003), stabilizing prediction distributions and closing the indirect attack vector.

## Method Summary
ROKA implements a practical approximation of theoretical Contribution Re-allocation through a stochastic, loss-based process. For a given forget set, it operates in two modes: **Targeted** (for a specific label) and **Non-Targeted** (for a subset of data). In each iteration, a forget sample is selected. Using Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (Input·Gradient), contribution maps are computed for a batch. The sample's nearest neighbors in this contribution space are identified as its "sibling" set. A composite loss is optimized: `L_unlearn = L_forget − α·L_heal`. `L_forget` increases error on the forget sample (via its label or a pseudo-label). `L_heal` is a self-distillation loss applied to the sibling batch, reinforcing their original behavior. This simultaneous push-pull enacts a proportional re-allocation of influence, nullifying the forgotten component's contribution while preserving the total weight of the sibling group.

## Key Results
*   **Effective Forgetting:** Achieves near-zero mean target accuracy (mTA ≤ 0.24) across ViT, CLIP, and Llama models on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet.
*   **Knowledge Preservation:** Maintains or slightly improves mean retain accuracy (mRA), e.g., 0.8979 vs a baseline of 0.9003 on CIFAR-100, contrasting with significant drops from gradient ascent.
*   **Attack Mitigation:** Stabilizes per-class prediction distributions, eliminating the severe, imbalanced accuracy shifts that gradient ascent unlearning introduces and that the indirect attack exploits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ROKA’s design is grounded in a theoretical framework that models neural networks as hierarchical knowledge systems. Three interconnected mechanisms explain its success.

1.  **Knowledge Destruction Threshold:** The framework defines a "Leverage" metric (`Influence / weight`) for neural components. High-leverage, low-level components are fragile; a small relative weight change (`|Δw/w|`) is amplified by their leverage. When the product `Cp * Leverage * |Δw/w|` exceeds a threshold `τ`, it causes a dramatic KL divergence in output distributions—this is "Knowledge Destruction." Conventional unlearning unintentionally triggers this in retained knowledge. ROKA's healing forces ensure this condition is never satisfied for retained components during an update.

2.  **Indirect Attack Vector:** The attack exploits the non-uniform degradation caused by Knowledge Destruction. An attacker, with gray-box access, trains shadow models and iteratively unlearns candidate classes. They identify a `C_adv_unlearn` whose unlearning causes a >25% drop in a chosen `C_adv_target`. This works because unlearning-induced contamination is not random; it follows predictable, class-specific patterns based on shared high-leverage components in the knowledge hierarchy.

3.  **Neural Healing via Contribution Re-allocation:** Theoretically, to forget a component `k_l−1,i`, its weight is nullified and its deficit is distributed proportionally to all its siblings `S_i` in the same layer, conserving total group weight. ROKA's practical algorithm approximates this: finding siblings via contribution-space similarity and applying a composite loss (`L_forget − α·L_heal`). `L_heal` (self-distillation on siblings) reinforces their original behavior, enacting the proportional strengthening that prevents systemic instability in the retained knowledge hierarchy.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) / Input·Gradient:** `R_xi = x_i · ∂f(x)/∂x_i` attributes a model's prediction to its input features. **Why needed:** It provides a lightweight, differentiable proxy for a sample's "contribution map," used to find sibling knowledge (`B_siblings`) in the stochastic unlearning process. **Quick check:** Why is computing input gradients feasible within a training loop compared to other attribution methods?
*   **Gradient Ascent (GA) Unlearning:** The objective is to maximize loss on forget samples, pushing the model to unlearn them. **Why needed:** It is the primary baseline whose imprecision and tendency to cause Knowledge Contamination ROKA is designed to overcome. **Quick check:** Why does naively maximizing loss on forget data risk damaging retained knowledge?
*   **Knowledge Contamination:** Definition 5 in the paper: a state where unlearning a target component (`k_l−1,i`) causes a significant, non-uniform change in the accuracy of a set of high-level retained knowledge components (`Kl`). **Why needed:** It is the core failure mode of existing unlearning that enables the indirect attack and that ROKA's healing is explicitly designed to prevent. **Quick check:** How does Knowledge Contamination differ from the direct, intended "Knowledge Destruction" of the target component?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** LRP/Contribution Calculator -> Sibling Selector -> Loss Composer -> Optimizer Driver
*   **Critical path (Targeted):** 1) Sample batch. 2) Select `x_forget` with high confidence for target label. 3) Compute batch contribution vectors (Input·Gradient). 4) Find `B_siblings`: nearest neighbors to `x_forget` in contribution space. 5) Compute `L_forget` on `x_forget` and `L_heal` on `B_siblings`. 6) Combine to `L_unlearn` and take gradient step on `-L_unlearn`.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Computation vs. Precision:** The theoretical, exact weight re-allocation is infeasible. ROKA trades surgical precision for practical feasibility via stochastic mini-batch updates and approximate sibling selection.
    *   **Dataset Quality Dependency:** Success hinges on the quality of the retain set (`D_retain`, implicit in sibling pool). An imbalanced `D_retain` can cause under- or over-healing.
    *   **Healing Factor `α` Sensitivity:** `α` balances forgetting and healing forces. Incorrect `α` leads to ineffective forgetting or distorted model behavior.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Imbalanced Prediction Distribution:** Post-unlearning per-class accuracy shifts are highly uneven (e.g., one class drops 60%, another rises 20%).
    *   **Retain Accuracy Collapse:** Significant drop (>5%) in mRA during unlearning, especially early iterations.
    *   **Target Class Not Forgotten:** mTA remains high (>0.1), indicating ineffective `L_forget` application.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Comparison:** Run GA and ROKA (targeted & non-targeted) on ViT-base/16 (CIFAR-100). Measure mTA and mRA. Expected: ROKA mTA≈0, mRA≈baseline; GA mTA low but mRA significantly lower.
    2.  **Indirect Attack Demonstration:** Use GA on CLIP/CIFAR-10. Unlearn each class, record accuracy changes for all other classes. Tabulate pairs where unlearning `C_adv_unlearn` causes >25% drop in `C_adv_target` (like Table IIIb).
    3.  **Robustness to Imbalance:** Apply ROKA to same CIFAR-10 setup. Compare prediction ratio changes and accuracy curves over iterations (like Table IIIc, Figure 4). ROKA should show minimal, balanced shifts and stable/flat retain accuracy, unlike GA's decline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** Does ROKA’s formal guarantee of knowledge preservation extend to its practical approximation, where contributions are estimated via Input·Gradient and sibling selection uses nearest neighbors in contribution space?
    *   **Basis:** The ideal Contribution Re-allocation is theoretically defined, but the implementation uses computationally feasible proxies (e.g., Contribution-Weighted Loss). The relationship between the proxy and the ideal is not analyzed.
    *   **Why unresolved:** It is unknown whether the practical algorithm still preserves knowledge or under what constraints the guarantee holds.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Rigorous analysis deriving error bounds for the proxy, or systematic experiments measuring deviation from theoretical predictions across models/datasets.
*   **Open Question 2:** How does the computational cost of ROKA’s contribution‑map calculation scale with model size, and can it be made tractable for extremely large models (e.g., >10B parameters)?
    *   **Basis:** The paper states exact implementation is "computationally infeasible" and uses a lightweight proxy, but only evaluates up to 3B parameters.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Scaling behavior (time/memory) for larger architectures is not quantified, leaving efficiency limits ambiguous.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical profiling of contribution computation (Input·Gradient) on models of increasing size (7B, 70B) and comparison with alternative, faster attribution methods.
*   **Open Question 3:** Can an adaptive attacker design unlearning requests that specifically undermine ROKA’s neural healing, for instance by exploiting the sibling‑selection heuristic to cause targeted accuracy drops in security‑critical classes?
    *   **Basis:** The security analysis considers a non-adaptive attack. The robustness of the healing process (sibling selection via contribution-space nearest neighbors) to an adversary who knows this mechanism is unexplored.
    *   **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether crafted forget requests could bypass healing or trigger over-healing that harms specific retained classes.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** A red‑team study where an attacker with full ROKA knowledge crafts requests to maximize post‑unlearning KL divergence on a chosen target class.
*   **Open Question 4:** How does ROKA perform when the training dataset is imbalanced or when the forget set poorly represents the target concept? Does this lead to systematic under‑healing or over‑healing, and can it be mitigated?
    *   **Basis:** Section V warns that imbalanced data risks over/under-healing, especially for the non‑targeted method.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Experiments use balanced benchmarks (CIFAR‑100, etc.). No tests with skewed distributions or partial forget sets are reported.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments varying class frequencies or forgetting subsets of a concept, measuring accuracy retention, prediction stability, and over/under-healing incidence.

## Limitations
*   **Sibling Identification Assumption:** The core assumption that contribution-space similarity (via Input·Gradient) aligns with semantic relatedness is unverified and may fail in high-dimensional spaces, leading to poor healing.
*   **Healing Factor `α` Sensitivity:** The paper does not explore tuning strategies for `α`; using a single default value risks under- or over-healing on new datasets/models.
*   **Computational Overhead Unquantified:** No runtime or memory benchmarks for the contribution calculation are provided, leaving its practical scalability for large models (>3B parameters) ambiguous.

## Confidence
*   **Indirect Attack Feasibility:** High. Clear experimental results across vision and language models demonstrate exploitable accuracy drops from gradient ascent.
*   **ROKA's Knowledge Preservation (Practical):** Medium. The theoretical exact reallocation is approximated stochastically; effectiveness depends on accurate sibling selection and `α` tuning, which are not fully validated.
*   **Knowledge Destruction Mechanism:** Medium. Class-imbalance evidence supports the phenomenon, but the specific "leverage" threshold and its universality are not directly measured or validated.
*   **Scalability Claims:** Medium. The paper claims feasibility for Llama-3B but provides no empirical scaling data for larger models.

## Next Checks
1.  **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Perform a systematic grid search over the healing factor `α` (e.g., {0.1, 1.0, 10}) on multiple model-dataset pairs to characterize the mTA/mRA trade-off and identify stable regimes.
2.  **Sibling Selection Robustness:** Ablate contribution-based neighbor selection by comparing against random selection, k-means clustering, or alternative attribution methods (Integrated Gradients) to quantify its impact on retain accuracy and attack mitigation.
3.  **Scalability Profiling:** Benchmark runtime and memory usage of the contribution computation (Input·Gradient) during unlearning for ViT-base, CLIP, and Llama 3B, and compare to the gradient ascent baseline to assess practical deployment costs and scaling limits.
