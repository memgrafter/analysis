# ACAR: Adaptive Complexity Routing for Multi-Model Ensembles with Auditable Decision Traces

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21231
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21231
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** ACAR achieved 55.6% accuracy on 1,510 tasks, exceeding the two-model baseline (54.4%) while avoiding full ensembling on 54.2% of tasks.

## Executive Summary
ACAR introduces a lightweight, non-learned routing mechanism for multi-model LLM ensembles. By drawing three samples from a fast probe model (Gemini 2.0 Flash) and computing self-consistency variance (σ), tasks are dynamically assigned to single-model, two-model, or three-model execution modes. Implemented on the TEAMLLM deterministic substrate, ACAR provides auditable decision traces and improves accuracy-cost trade-offs. On a diverse set of 1,510 tasks, ACAR outperforms a fixed two-model baseline while reducing reliance on expensive full ensembling. However, retrieval augmentation degraded performance, and a fundamental “agreement-but-wrong” failure mode caps achievable accuracy.

## Method Summary
ACAR routes each incoming task based on the diversity of answers from three independent samples generated by a cheap probe model. The discrete self-consistency variance σ ∈ {0.0, 0.5, 1.0} is derived from the number of distinct extracted answers. A value of 0.0 (full agreement) triggers single-model execution; 0.5 (partial agreement) triggers a two-model “arena_lite” with majority vote; 1.0 (complete disagreement) triggers full three-model ensemble. The system is built on TEAMLLM, which enforces deterministic execution, immutable artifacts, and full logging of every decision. Two variants were evaluated: ACAR-U (no retrieval) and ACAR-UJ (with a retrieval-augmented experience store).

## Key Results
- ACAR accuracy: 55.6% vs. two-model baseline 54.4%.
- Avoided full ensembling on 54.2% of tasks; distribution: 32.9% single-agent, 21.3% arena_lite, 45.8% full_arena.
- Retrieval augmentation (ACAR-UJ) lowered accuracy by 3.4 percentage points; median similarity of retrieved experiences was 0.167.
- Unanimous incorrect probe agreement (σ=0) creates an unrecoverable failure, bounding accuracy roughly 8 percentage points below full ensembling.
- Simple attribution proxies (response similarity, entropy) showed only weak correlation with counterfactual model contributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Self-consistency variance as a routing signal
- **Claim:** The discrete σ computed from three probe samples acts as a proxy for task difficulty, enabling routing without learned parameters.
- **Mechanism:** Three independent samples are drawn from a fast probe model. The number of distinct extracted answers determines σ: 0 (all same) → single-agent; 1 (two agree) → arena_lite; 2 (all different) → full_arena.
- **Core assumption:** Agreement among probe outputs correlates with inherent task difficulty; easy tasks need only one model, ambiguous tasks benefit from ensemble diversity.
- **Evidence:** Defined in Section 3.2; supported by the bimodal σ distribution (Figure 1) and the overall accuracy-cost improvement.
- **Break condition:** If all probe samples agree on a wrong answer (σ=0 but consensus incorrect), the system routes to single-agent and cannot recover, creating a hard ceiling on performance (~8pp below full ensembling).

### Mechanism 2: Selective escalation reduces cost while maintaining or improving accuracy
- **Claim:** By routing tasks according to σ, ACAR avoids full ensembling on a majority of tasks and slightly exceeds the accuracy of a fixed two-model baseline.
- **Mechanism:** The discrete mapping yields three cost tiers. Tasks with σ=0 (32.9%) use the cheapest single-agent; σ=0.5 (21.3%) use intermediate two-model; σ=1 (45.8%) require full ensembling. The cost saved on the large σ=0 fraction outweighs the extra cost of σ=1 tasks, and the two-model tier provides verification for moderately ambiguous cases.
- **Core assumption:** The two-model arena_lite offers sufficient verification for many ambiguous tasks; the probe’s difficulty estimate is reasonably aligned with true difficulty.
- **Evidence:** Section 5.1–5.3; Table 1 and Figure 2 show ACAR-U’s position above Arena-2 on the accuracy-cost frontier; Figure 6 shows cumulative avoidance of full ensembling.
- **Break condition:** A shift in the σ distribution (e.g., too many tasks in σ=1) would erode cost benefits. Misrouted σ=0 tasks (due to probe error) also lower accuracy.

### Mechanism 3: TEAMLLM substrate enables auditability and falsifiable baselines
- **Claim:** The deterministic, stateful execution substrate records all decisions in immutable traces, allowing exact regeneration and thorough diagnosis of routing outcomes.
- **Mechanism:** TEAMLLM enforces: (1) deterministic execution via fixed random seeds and environment fingerprint, (2) immutable artifacts (prompt hashes, model IDs), and (3) forward-only state machine. Every step—probe samples, σ calculation, mode selection, final answer—is logged to an append‑only `runs.jsonl`.
- **Core assumption:** Full transparency is required to separate architectural limits from implementation bugs and to validate negative results (e.g., retrieval hurt, weak attribution).
- **Evidence:** Section 3.1, Appendix A (reproducibility statement, 208 unit tests). No direct external validation from related work.
- **Break condition:** If the logging misses critical variables (e.g., non-determinism from external APIs, incomplete extraction), traces cannot be exactly regenerated, undermining auditability claims.

## Foundational Learning

### Self-consistency variance as a difficulty proxy
- **Why needed:** This heuristic replaces learned routers; understanding that σ measures output diversity, not confidence or truth, is essential to interpret routing decisions and the observed bimodal distribution.
- **Quick check:** If a task has σ=0.5 (two probes agree, one differs), what does ACAR do, and what assumption does it make about the dissenting sample?

### Deterministic, stateful execution substrates for ML systems
- **Why needed:** TEAMLLM’s invariants (deterministic, immutable, forward‑only) are prerequisites for the “falsifiable baselines” claim; without exact reproducibility, negative findings could be dismissed as flaky runs.
- **Quick check:** What three pieces of information does TEAMLLM capture to enforce deterministic re‑execution?

### Cost-accuracy Pareto frontiers in adaptive inference
- **Why needed:** The value of routing is framed as moving along the frontier. ACAR-U’s position above Arena‑2 (higher accuracy, lower cost) is the key positive result; understanding the trade‑off metric is necessary to evaluate the mechanism’s success.
- **Quick check:** Why does Arena‑3 have identical cost to Arena‑2 despite using one additional model per task?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Probe Model (M_probe) → Sampling & Extraction Module → σ Calculator → Router → (Optional) Retrieval Module → Execution Engine → TEAMLLM Logger

**Critical path**  
Task received → Probe sampling (3 calls) → EXTRACT answers → Compute σ → Router selects mode → (If arena_lite or full_arena) Dispatch to ensemble models → Judge selects final answer → Log entire trace.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Discrete σ thresholds ensure interpretability but discard granular difficulty information.  
- Probe cost vs. accuracy: N=3 is minimal; more samples would improve estimation but increase cost.  
- Retrieval threshold: Using similarity ≥0.0 maximizes recall but injects noise (median similarity 0.167); a higher threshold (>0.7) is likely needed.  
- Judgment strategy (JUDGESELECT) is not detailed; it introduces variance separate from probe extraction.

**Failure signatures**  
- σ=0 with a wrong consensus: Check for tasks where all three probe answers are identical and incorrect; these are hard failures with no recovery path.  
- Low retrieval similarity: In ACAR-UJ traces, `best_similarity` near 0.167 likely indicates harmful noise.  
- Unexpectedly high full_arena rates on easy benchmarks: May signal probe‑extraction misalignment.  
- Non‑reproducible traces: Missing seeds, prompt hashes, or environment fingerprints.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Validate σ distribution & routing logic:** Run ACAR-U on ~50 tasks, manually inspect `runs.jsonl` to confirm exactly 3 probe samples per task, correct σ computation, correct mode mapping, and correct final answer selection (single for σ=0, majority/JUDGESELECT otherwise).  
2. **Test retrieval similarity threshold:** Using the ACAR-UJ experience store, rerun a subset with varying thresholds (e.g., 0.0, 0.5, 0.7). Expect accuracy improvement only when filtering out low‑similarity hits (median 0.167).  
3. **Measure attribution proxy correlation:** For a sample of arena_lite tasks, compute proxy signals (response similarity, entropy) and correlate with leave‑one‑out counterfactual performance. Expect weak correlation as reported in Section 6.3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1: How can retrieval augmentation be redesigned to provide task‑aligned experiences that improve rather than degrade multi‑model ensemble accuracy?
- **Basis:** Section 6.1 shows ACAR‑UJ (with retrieval) decreased accuracy by 3.4pp overall, with median retrieval similarity only 0.167, concluding that retrieval requires similarity thresholds >0.7 and semantic alignment.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates naive retrieval injects noise but offers no alternative design; the retrieval mechanism for adaptive routing remains an open engineering problem.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation of retrieval strategies using similarity‑thresholded, task‑specific experience stores, measuring impact on routing decisions and accuracy across benchmarks.

### Open Question 2: Can self‑consistency‑based routing overcome the “agreement‑but‑wrong” ceiling where unanimous incorrect probe samples prevent recovery via ensembling?
- **Basis:** Section 6.2 identifies that when σ=0 due to all probes agreeing incorrectly, no ensemble can recover, bounding achievable accuracy ~8pp below full ensembling; this is intrinsic to self‑consistency.
- **Why unresolved:** This ceiling is presented as fundamental; without detecting systematic probe errors, adaptive routing cannot escalate on misinformation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introducing supplemental difficulty signals (e.g., confidence scores, external verifiers) that trigger escalation even when σ=0, validated on tasks with known error modes.

### Open Question 3: What efficient attribution methods approximate counterfactual model contributions without costly leave‑one‑out runs?
- **Basis:** Section 6.3 reports that proxy signals (response similarity, entropy) weakly correlated with leave‑one‑out ground truth, concluding practical attribution requires explicit counterfactuals.
- **Why unresolved:** Counterfactual computation is expensive; the paper rules out simple proxies but does not identify scalable alternatives for real‑world deployment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Designing new proxies or architectures (e.g., contribution logging during voting) that strongly correlate with counterfactual values while adding minimal computational overhead.

## Limitations
- The “agreement‑but‑wrong” failure mode (σ=0 with incorrect consensus) creates an unrecoverable error ceiling, limiting maximum accuracy to ~8 percentage points below full ensembling.
- Retrieval augmentation using a naïve, unthresholded experience store significantly hurt accuracy (‑3.4pp) due to low‑similarity noise (median 0.167).
- Attribution proxies (response similarity, entropy) showed only weak correlation with true counterfactual model contributions, limiting interpretability of routing decisions.
- Evaluation was confined to 1,510 tasks across four benchmarks; the probe model was fixed to Gemini Flash, so generalizability to other models or domains is uncertain.
- Discrete σ thresholds oversimplify the continuous spectrum of task difficulty.

## Confidence
- σ‑based difficulty routing: Medium (empirical support but bounded by σ=0 failure).
- Cost‑accuracy advantage: High (clear improvement over two‑model baseline).
- Auditable TEAMLLM substrate: Medium (design described but not externally validated).
- Negative retrieval result: Medium (reported consistently across configurations).
- Weak attribution: Low (analysis limited and method details sparse).
