# ChiEngMixBench: Evaluating Large Language Models on Spontaneous and Natural Chinese-English Code-Mixed Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.16217
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16217
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** LLMs exhibit strong contextual inertia (English Preference Rate <20%) but achieve a spontaneity lift for specialized terms; naturalness scores vary widely, with instruction tuning incurring a monolingual collapse penalty.

## Executive Summary
We introduce ChiEngMixBench, a novel benchmark for evaluating large language models on spontaneous and natural Chinese-English code-mixed generation. Built from authentic text in Chinese AI communities, the benchmark moves beyond prior translation-focused evaluations by assessing cognitive alignment with human expert expression conventions. Two core metrics are proposed: Spontaneity, measured via Minimal Contrastive Pairs to compute Contextual Spontaneity Gap (CSG) and English Preference Rate (EPR); and Naturalness, measured via Expert Deviation Penalty (EDP) that captures deviations from expert distributions in semantics, structure, and terminology. Evaluation of 12 models reveals all suffer from strong contextual inertia (EPR<20%), yet specialized terms elicit a relative spontaneity lift (CSG +0.04–+0.07). Naturalness scores span a wide range (e.g., DeepSeek-V3: 4.19; Gemma-2-9B-Instruct: 1.23), and instruction tuning consistently penalizes code-mixing naturalness. Automated scores correlate strongly with human judgments (r=0.878). An emergent terminology layering strategy that aligns with Matrix Language Frame theory is identified, suggesting models that mimic expert conventions achieve higher naturalness.

## Method Summary
The construction of ChiEngMixBench follows a scalable pipeline. First, approximately 530,000 characters are crawled from Chinese technical platforms (CSDN, Zhihu) and cleaned via deduplication, length filtering, and PII removal. From this corpus, two resources are derived:

1. **Minimal Contrastive Pairs (MCP):** 1,706 pairs where the only difference is an English versus a Chinese term within an identical context (e.g., “API” vs. “应用程序接口”). MCPs are generated with LLM assistance, filtered by rules, and validated by humans.
2. **Expert baseline:** Statistical profiles from the cleaned expert corpus, including semantic centroids (sentence embeddings via MiniLM), distributions of English token ratio and code-switching frequency, and a reverse mapping table linking non-standard forms to standard acronyms.

The evaluation comprises two zero-shot tasks:

- **Spontaneity:** For each MCP, the model’s log-probability for the English term and Chinese term (length-normalized) is extracted; CSG = log S(t_en) – S(t_zh). EPR is the percentage of pairs where the English term is more probable. Terms are stratified into Specialized (92.2%) and General (7.8%) to compute CSG lift.
- **Naturalness:** Models generate responses to 32 technical questions under an “expert-simulation” prompt. For each response, a composite penalty is calculated: `EDP = 5.0 – (P_sem + P_str + P_term)`, where P_sem is cosine distance to the expert semantic centroid; P_str penalizes deviations in English ratio and switching frequency beyond pre-defined thresholds (µ±1.5σ, µ+2.0σ, µ+1.2σ); P_term penalizes non-compact terminology using the reverse mapping.

Human evaluations (N=100) are collected to validate EDP, yielding a correlation of r=0.878.

## Key Results
- All 12 evaluated models demonstrate strong contextual inertia, with English Preference Rate below 20%.
- Specialized technical terms achieve a spontaneity lift, improving CSG by +0.04 to +0.07 relative to general terms.
- Naturalness scores vary widely across models; instruction-tuned variants consistently suffer a monolingual collapse penalty (e.g., DeepSeek-V3 scores 4.19 vs. Gemma-2-9B-Instruct at 1.23). Automated EDP scores correlate highly with human judgments (r=0.878).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The benchmark’s effectiveness stems from its focus on cognitive alignment rather than mere translation accuracy. The Minimal Contrastive Pairs isolate term choice by holding context constant, allowing a clean measurement of a model’s spontaneous preference for English terms via the Contextual Spontaneity Gap. The observed lift for specialized terms reflects a terminology layering strategy where English is reserved for high-specificity concepts, a pattern consistent with Matrix Language Frame theory. For naturalness, the Expert Deviation Penalty aggregates three complementary signals: semantic style (via embeddings), structural properties (English ratio, switching frequency), and terminological compactness (acronym usage). The high correlation with human ratings confirms that these automated metrics capture the nuances of perceived naturalness. Together, the metrics provide a multi-faceted assessment of how well models emulate expert code-mixing behavior.

## Foundational Learning
- **Minimal Contrastive Pairs (MCP):** *Why needed:* To disentangle the effect of term language from contextual confounders, enabling a precise spontaneity measurement. *Quick check:* Verify that in each pair only the target term differs; all other tokens are identical.
- **Contextual Spontaneity Gap (CSG):** *Why needed:* Quantifies the model’s preference for English terms in a probabilistic manner, going beyond binary accuracy. *Quick check:* Compute log-probability difference between English and Chinese terms; a positive CSG indicates English preference.
- **Expert Deviation Penalty (EDP):** *Why needed:* Captures holistic naturalness by measuring deviation from expert distributions across semantic, structural, and terminological dimensions. *Quick check:* Calculate semantic cosine distance, check if English ratio and switching frequency fall within accepted thresholds, and verify terminology compactness via reverse mapping.
- **Matrix Language Frame (MLF) Theory:** *Why needed:* Provides a linguistic scaffold to interpret the emergent layering of English within Chinese matrix frames. *Quick check:* Analyze whether English terms predominantly appear in embedded positions that conform to MLF predictions (e.g., following Chinese matrix content).
- **Terminology Layering Strategy:** *Why needed:* Explains why models with high naturalness systematically use English for specialized concepts and Chinese for general ones. *Quick check:* Compare CSG lift between Specialized and General terms; a significant positive lift indicates effective layering.

## Architecture Onboarding
The evaluation framework consists of the following components and flow:

**Component Map:**  
Data Crawler → Cleaner → (MCP Generator + Expert Baseline Builder) → Spontaneity Evaluator → Naturalness Evaluator

**Critical Path:**  
1. Crawl raw posts → 2. Clean corpus → 3. Generate MCPs and compute expert statistics (in parallel) → 4. Run Spontaneity evaluation on MCPs → 5. Run Naturalness evaluation on question set using expert baseline → 6. Aggregate results.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Rule-based vs. LLM-assisted MCP generation:* Rules ensure minimality but lack scalability; LLMs enable scaling but require human validation to avoid subtle differences.
- *Fixed EDP thresholds:* Simplicity and reproducibility vs. potential over-penalization of creative but valid expert expressions.
- *Zero-shot evaluation:* Reveals inherent model biases without contaminating with task-specific fine-tuning, but may underperform compared to prompted few-shot settings.
- *MiniLM embeddings:* Efficient for large-scale similarity but may miss nuanced stylistic cues.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- MCP contamination: non-identical contexts lead to inflated CSG unrelated to term preference.
- EDP over-penalization: even expert corpus scores fall short of the theoretical maximum, indicating thresholds too strict.
- Uniformly negative CSG: extreme contextual inertia masks subtle differences; requires stratified lift analysis.
- Tokenization mismatch: inconsistent token counts for equivalent English/Chinese terms break probability comparison; requires length normalization or single-token filtering.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Spontaneity Replication:** Evaluate a subset of models (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B Base vs. Instruct) on MCP to confirm EPR and CSG lift patterns.
2. **EDP Sanity Check:** Compute EDP on the original expert corpus; the mean score should be within 4.5–5.0 to validate thresholds and reverse mapping.
3. **Tokenization Consistency Test:** For 100 sampled terms, compute the ratio of token lengths (English/Chinese). If variance exceeds 0.5, apply length normalization per the paper’s formula.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalizability of Terminology Layering** – Does the layering strategy hold for other bilingual pairs (e.g., Spanish-English) or non-CS domains (law, medicine)? The pipeline is domain-agnostic but has only been validated in AI contexts. Resolution requires applying ChiEngMixBench to at least two other bilingual/domain combinations and replicating the MCP/EDP analysis to see if similar CSG lifts and MLF alignment emerge.

2. **Root Cause of “Alignment Tax” (Monolingual Collapse)** – What aspects of instruction tuning (RLHF, safety constraints) suppress spontaneous code-mixing? The paper shows a correlation but no ablation. A controlled experiment applying incremental alignment steps (SFT, RLHF) to the same base model while measuring CSG and EDP deltas, possibly with internal probes, could isolate the causal mechanism.

3. **Predictors of “Semantic Buoyancy”** – Which lexical/semantic properties make anchor terms (e.g., “API”) resistant to contextual inertia? The paper hypothesizes factors like lack of compact L1 equivalents and information density, but these are not quantified. A large-scale feature analysis on all 1,345 MCP terms (frequency, translation length, acronym status, domain specificity) correlated with per-term CSG could yield a predictive model.

4. **EDP Bias and Hybrid Metrics** – Does the strict EDP undervalue linguistically valid but statistically atypical expert expressions? The paper notes a “machine strictness” bias. An error analysis on the human evaluation subset, annotating why certain outputs receive higher human scores, could identify categories of legitimate creativity. Incorporating a supplemental creativity classifier might improve correlation without sacrificing reproducibility.

5. **Statistical vs. Mechanistic Origins of Contextual Inertia** – Is the near-universal suppression of English terms under Chinese context a data distribution artifact or an emergent property of the model’s inhibitory control? The ICM hypothesis remains untested. Training small models on synthetic corpora with controlled mixing probabilities (e.g., 90% Chinese context with 10% English terms) and then evaluating inertia would disentangle distribution from mechanism; alternatively, probing attention or neuron activations for language-specific inhibition signals could provide evidence.

## Limitations
- **Unpublished resources:** The exact term lists, stop‑lists, and reverse mapping table (Appendix A) are not released, hindering precise replication of EDP’s P_term and term stratification.
- **Prompt and inference ambiguities:** Full naturalness question set, MCP generation instructions, and decoding parameters (temperature, top_p) are unspecified, risking reconstruction drift.
- **Human data absent:** Raw human ratings for the 100-sample validation are unavailable, preventing verification of the EDP correlation (r=0.878) and assessment of the noted “machine strictness.”
- **Generalizability limited:** Corpus is restricted to Chinese AI forums (CSDN, Zhihu); findings may not extend to other code‑mixing domains or demographics.
