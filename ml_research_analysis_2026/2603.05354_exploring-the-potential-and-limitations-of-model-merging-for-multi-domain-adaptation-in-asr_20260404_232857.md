# Exploring the potential and limitations of Model Merging for Multi-Domain Adaptation in ASR

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05354
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05354
- **References:** 0
- **Key result:** BoostedTSV-M achieves state-of-the-art EP performance (11.55% WER average), statistically significantly outperforming full fine-tuning (11.58% WER) while preserving out-of-distribution generalization.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates model merging as a scalable alternative to multi-task training for adapting Whisper to multiple domains. By merging 10 European Portuguese fine-tunes via 11 algorithms, the authors find τ-subspace methods especially effective. Their proposed BoostedTSV-M, which augments TSV-M with singular-value boosting, attains the best average WER (11.55%) on EP test sets, narrowly beating full fine-tuning (11.58%) with statistical significance. The study reveals a clear trade-off: merging generally preserves multilingual robustness better than full fine-tuning but lags behind joint training on in-domain specialization. BoostedTSV-M balances these aspects by mitigating rank collapse, offering a practical path for efficient multi-domain adaptation without full retraining.

## Method Summary
The work uses Whisper Large-v3 (WhisperLv3-X) as a foundation model. Ten domain-specific models are fine-tuned independently on individual European Portuguese corpora (ID-FT: 3 epochs, LR=2e-6, batch 256) and one joint model on all ten together (Full-FT: 10 epochs, LR=1e-5, batch 256). Merging is performed at the task vector level: for each fine-tuned checkpoint θᵢ, compute τᵢ = θᵢ - θ₀. Eleven merging algorithms from three paradigms (parameter-space, τ-space, τ-subspace) combine these τᵢ into a merged τ*. The final model is θ* = θ₀ + λτ*. The novel method BoostedTSV-M extends TSV-M by first applying SVD to each τᵢ, using cumulative energy to find a threshold σ_(s*), then clamping all smaller singular values to σ_(s*) (boosting). The truncated/boosted subspaces are concatenated, orthogonalized via Newton-Schulz iterations (5, quintic), and reconstructed. All models are evaluated with WhisperX decoding (beam 5) on EP in-domain, EP out-of-domain, African/Asian Portuguese, Brazilian Portuguese, OpenASR-HF (English), and FLEURS (multilingual).

## Key Results
- BoostedTSV-M (β=0.3) achieves an average WER of 11.55% across EP in- and out-of-domain sets, significantly beating Full-FT (11.58%, p<0.001) while matching its in-domain performance and better preserving OOD robustness.
- Merging generally maintains multilingual robustness on non-EP languages (e.g., BP, OpenASR-HF, FLEURS) better than Full-FT, which suffers catastrophic forgetting, but is less specialized than the oracle ID-FT per-domain models.
- A clear trade-off exists: τ-subspace methods like BoostedTSV-M optimize EP accuracy, while parameter-space methods like Karcher Mean preserve OOD performance at the cost of EP specialization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Model merging success rests on three interrelated mechanisms:

**Mechanism 1: Task Vector Composition**  
Independent fine-tunes produce task vectors τₜ = θₜ - θ₀ that encode domain knowledge. Merging algorithms combine these τₜ in parameter space, τ-space, or a τ-subspace, then add the result back to θ₀. This preserves the foundation model’s general capabilities while injecting domain-specific updates. The key assumption is that knowledge is linearly composable in a suitable representation space. Evidence: The corpus of 10 EP domains provides independent fine-tuned checkpoints to serve as τₜ, enabling the experiments. Break condition: If tasks conflict heavily (e.g., large distribution shifts), linear combination may yield a model that performs poorly on all domains.

**Mechanism 2: τ-Subspace Projection (TSV-M)**  
This state-of-the-art paradigm projects each τₜ onto a low-rank subspace via SVD (retaining top-k singular vectors). The subspaces are concatenated, orthogonalized (e.g., via Newton-Schulz), and recombined. The assumption: fine-tuning updates are intrinsically low-rank; principal singular vectors capture transferable knowledge while smaller ones carry noise or task-specific detail. Orthogonalization minimizes interference between tasks. Evidence: Table 2 shows TSV-M variants (with OP or NS) achieve top-3 EP ID (9.41% WER) and EP OOD (16.07% WER), outperforming many PS and τSpa methods. Break condition: Mis-specifying rank (too low loses signal; too high retains conflicting noise) or using orthogonal Procrustes at high rank causes instability; the paper addresses this with NS.

**Mechanism 3: Singular Value Boosting (BoostedTSV-M)**  
To mitigate rank collapse from aggressive SVD truncation, BoostedTSV-M clamps all singular values below a threshold σ_(s*) to σ_(s*), where σ_(s*) is determined by cumulative energy threshold β. This prevents suppression of fine-grained, task-specific information during truncation. The assumption: small singular values encode subtle domain details that improve in-domain accuracy. Evidence: Figure 1 shows decreasing β improves EP ID but degrades EP OOD and other languages, illustrating the trade-off. Table 2: BoostedTSV-M (β=0.3) yields the best EP Full Avg (11.55%) but shows degraded performance on BP/OpenASR/FLEURS vs. TSV-M. Break condition: β too low retains excessive noise, harming OOD; β too high reverts to standard TSV-M.

## Foundational Learning
- **Task Vectors (τ)**  
  *Why needed:* All τ-space and τ-subspace merging methods operate on differences τₜ = θₜ - θ₀ rather than absolute parameters.  
  *Quick check:* Why might combining absolute model parameters θₜ directly be problematic, whereas combining their differences τₜ is more effective for preserving the foundation model's general knowledge?

- **Low-Rank Approximation via SVD**  
  *Why needed:* τ-subspace methods rely on SVD to extract principal components (directions of change) of each task vector's weight updates; rank truncation controls interference vs. capacity.  
  *Quick check:* In fine-tuning, what does a large singular value represent versus a small one in the SVD of a weight update matrix? How does truncation affect the model's capacity to learn new tasks?

- **Catastrophic Forgetting vs. Interference**  
  *Why needed:* The core trade-off in merging is between specialization (low error on target domains) and generalisation (retaining OOD capabilities). Full fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting of non-EP languages; merging aims to reduce this by preserving more of θ₀'s structure.  
  *Quick check:* From Table 2, why does Full-FT improve EP ID/OOD but degrade OpenASR-HF and FLEURS compared to zero-shot? How do merging methods like Karcher Mean or Model Soups behave differently?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Pre-trained WhisperLv3-X (θ₀) and 10 domain fine-tunes (θ₁...θ₁₀) → MergeWhisper toolkit (implements merging algorithms, including BoostedTSV-M’s SVD, boosting, NS orthogonalization) → Merged model θ* → Evaluation suite (EP ID/EP OOD, AAP/BP, OpenASR-HF, FLEURS) with baselines (zero-shot, Full-FT, ID-FT).

**Critical path:** 1) Prepare fine-tuned checkpoints. 2) Compute task vectors τᵢ = θᵢ - θ₀. 3) Apply algorithm-specific transformations (SVD, truncation, boosting, orthogonalisation, concatenation) to merge τᵢ into τ*. 4) Reconstruct merged τ* and compute θ* = θ₀ + λτ*. 5) Evaluate θ* on all test sets.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Rank retention vs. stability*: High rank % retains signal but risks interference and numerical instability (orthogonal Procrustes may fail). Solution: use Newton-Schulz (5 iters, quintic) and control rank/β.  
- *ID performance vs. OOD robustness*: Boosting (lower β) preserves fine-grained details, improving ID but harming OOD. Parameter-space methods (e.g., Karcher Mean) preserve OOD better but underperform on ID.  
- *Algorithmic complexity*: τ-subspace methods require SVD per layer, which is heavier than simple averaging (SOUP) but yields better interference control.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Rank collapse*: Merged model near zero-shot on all domains; likely excessive truncation or poor orthogonalisation. Check singular value distribution.  
- *Catastrophic interference*: ID performance below some individual ID-FT models; indicates high task conflict or failed orthogonalisation.  
- *Numerical instability*: NaNs or exploding values during reconstruction; caused by non-orthogonal factors or large condition numbers. Use Newton-Schulz and fp32.  
- *OOD degradation*: Sudden drop on non-EP languages; may indicate over-specialisation (β too low) or that merging consolidated only EP knowledge. Compare to zero-shot baseline.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Baseline Replication & Sanity Check*: Merge all 10 EP experts using simple model averaging (SOUP without θ₀). Expect modest ID improvement over zero-shot but significant OOD preservation. Verify EP ID WER not worse than zero-shot (~15.6%).  
2. *Hyperparameter Sensitivity for BoostedTSV-M*: Run BoostedTSV-M with β ∈ {0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9} while fixing rank % (e.g., 50%). Expect trade-off: lower β improves EP ID but worsens EP OOD/BP. Identify β maximizing EP Full Avg (likely 0.3).  
3. *OOD Generalisation Stress Test*: Compare best BoostedTSV-M (β=0.3) and a strong PS method (Karcher Mean) on distant OOD sets (FLEURS multilingual, BP). Expect PS to dominate FLEURS/BP while BoostedTSV-M dominates EP, confirming the specialization/robustness trade-off.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What mechanism causes singular-value boosting in BoostedTSV-M to trade in-domain accuracy for out-of-domain robustness, and can this trade-off be decoupled?  
  *Basis*: Figure 1 and Table 2 show decreasing β improves EP in-domain WER but worsens EP out-of-domain and other non-EP results (BP, OpenASR-HF, FLEURS). The paper does not analyze which singular values correspond to domain-specific vs. generalizable features, nor explore adaptive boosting strategies.

- **Open Question 2**: Why does TIES merging cause catastrophic forgetting of English capabilities while other τ-space methods (e.g., TA, PCB, SCE) preserve them?  
  *Basis*: Table 2 shows TIES degrades English performance (OpenASR-HF WER 8.77%) nearly as badly as Full-FT (8.83%), while TA (7.12%), PCB (7.17%), and SCE (7.18%) remain near zero-shot (7.14%). No analysis of TIES’s “trim, elect, sign” operations is provided.

- **Open Question 3**: Is the observed improvement of merged models on unrelated languages (FLEURS) truly driven by shared acoustic characteristics across the diverse EP training domains?  
  *Basis*: Section 5.3 hypothesizes that improvements stem from shared acoustic characteristics (e.g., channel conditions, speaking styles, noise profiles) captured by the diverse adaptation domains. This is not experimentally validated; the EP domains differ in both linguistic content and acoustic conditions.

- **Open Question 4**: Why do parameter-space merging methods (e.g., Karcher mean, Model Stock) exhibit better multilingual robustness than τ-subspace methods, despite lower in-domain specialization?  
  *Basis*: Table 2 shows PS methods achieve lower WER on BP, OpenASR-HF, and FLEURS compared to most τSpa/τSub methods, while τSub methods dominate EP ID/OOD metrics. The paper does not investigate geometric properties (e.g., distance from pre-trained model, alignment with task vectors) that might explain the robustness difference.

## Limitations
- Data acquisition details for the CAMÕES benchmark [6] are unspecified (download URLs, train/test splits, preprocessing), blocking faithful replication.
- The `MergeWhisper` toolkit is not yet released; critical implementation details for Whisper’s encoder-decoder architecture in mergekit and exact Newton-Schulz integration remain unknown.
- OOD robustness evaluation is limited to Portuguese variants and English/French; claims about “multilingual robustness” lack validation on truly diverse low-resource language families.
