# Polynomial Surrogate Training for Differentiable Ternary Logic Gate Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00302
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00302
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PST reduces parameters by 2,187× and speeds up training 2–3× for ternary logic networks while enabling principled abstention via UNKNOWN state, matching binary DLGN soft accuracy on CIFAR-10 and improving selective prediction.

## Executive Summary
Differentiable logic gate networks have been limited to binary logic due to the combinatorial explosion of possible gates when extending to ternary logic (19,683 per neuron). Polynomial Surrogate Training (PST) overcomes this by representing each ternary neuron as a degree-(2,2) polynomial with only 9 learnable coefficients, achieving a 2,187× parameter reduction. PST networks match the soft accuracy of binary counterparts on CIFAR-10 while training 2–3× faster. The gap between continuous training and discretized circuit performance contracts with overparameterization, dropping from 14.1 to 3.7 percentage points when scaling from 96K to 512K neurons. Crucially, the UNKNOWN (0) output in ternary logic enables principled abstention; the density of UNKNOWN responses correlates with Bayes-optimal uncertainty and yields superior selective prediction accuracy compared to binary networks.

## Method Summary
PST replaces the intractable softmax over all ternary gates with a compact polynomial parameterization. Each neuron computes a quadratic polynomial \( p_w(a,b) = w^\top m(a,b) \) over its ternary inputs, where \( m \) is a monomial basis of size 9. The coefficients \( w \) are learned via standard backpropagation. To ensure that the continuous polynomial corresponds to a valid discrete gate after rounding, a commitment regularizer minimizes the mean squared distance of polynomial evaluations to the nearest ternary value across the 9 input combinations. This regularizer is annealed during training to balance exploration and exploitation. At deployment, each neuron's polynomial is evaluated on the 9 grid points, rounded to the nearest ternary value, and mapped to the closest truth table from a precomputed library of all 19,683 gates. The network's logical circuit is thus obtained without additional training. The approach maintains the end-to-end differentiability of the training phase while guaranteeing a discrete logic circuit upon hardening.

## Key Results
- PST achieves a 2,187× parameter reduction per neuron (9 vs 19,683) and 2–3× faster training compared to binary differentiable logic gate networks.
- On CIFAR-10, PST networks achieve soft accuracy comparable to binary DLGN.
- The hardening gap (difference between soft and discretized circuit accuracy) decreases with scale: from 14.1 pp (96K) to 3.7 pp (512K neurons).
- Ternary networks leverage UNKNOWN outputs for abstention, obtaining higher selective prediction accuracy than binary full-coverage models (e.g., 98.1% at 50% coverage vs 91.8% on Moons) and aligning UNKNOWN density with Bayes-optimal uncertainty.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Polynomial Surrogate Training (PST) replaces the intractable softmax-over-gates parameterization (requiring 19,683 logits per neuron for ternary logic) with a compact polynomial representation (9 coefficients), enabling training of ternary logic gate networks.
- **Mechanism:** Each neuron's function over ternary inputs \( T^2 = \{-1,0,1\}^2 \) is expressed as a degree-(2,2) polynomial \( p_w(a,b) = w^\top m(a,b) \), where \( m \) is the monomial basis. The Vandermonde matrix \( V \) evaluating this polynomial on all 9 grid points is invertible, making the parameterization universal for any ternary gate.
- **Core assumption:** Any ternary logic gate's truth table can be exactly represented by a multi-quadratic polynomial (powers ≤ 2), leveraging the algebraic property \( z^2 = z^2 \) for \( z \in T \).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "PST represents each ternary neuron as a degree-(2,2) polynomial with only 9 learnable coefficients, achieving a 2,187× parameter reduction."
  - [section 4.1] "Since the 9×9 Vandermonde matrix V evaluating this polynomial on \( T^2 \) is invertible, the parameterization is universal."
  - [corpus] *Limited direct evidence*: Neighbors focus on binary DLGN scaling and variants; PST's polynomial approach is novel with no direct precursor in corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the polynomial degree is reduced below (2,2), the representation loses universality and cannot express all 19,683 gates, restricting the search space.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The hardening gap (train-test discretization error) is bounded by a data-independent commitment loss \( R_A(W) \) that vanishes as training converges, ensuring the continuous training model approximates a discrete logic circuit.
- **Mechanism:** During hardening, each neuron's polynomial evaluations \( t_j = p_w(a,b) \) on the 9 grid points are rounded to the nearest ternary value \( \bar{t}_j \). Theorem 1 proves that the mean squared per-entry rounding error across all neurons equals the commitment loss \( R_A(W) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_j \frac{1}{9} \sum_{(a,b)\in T^2} \text{dist}(p_w(a,b), T)^2 \). This loss is actively minimized during training.
- **Core assumption:** Rounding each polynomial evaluation independently to the nearest truth value yields a valid discrete gate; the lattice geometry of truth tables (\( \Lambda_3 \subset \mathbb{R}^9 \)) ensures this maps to one of the 19,683 gates.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We prove that the gap between the trained network and its discretized logic circuit is bounded by a data-independent commitment loss that vanishes at convergence."
  - [section 4.3 & Theorem 1] "Then \( R_A(W) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_j \frac{1}{q^2} \| t_j - \bar{t}_j \|_2^2 \)." The proof shows equality via rounding definition.
  - [corpus] *No direct evidence*: Corpus lacks discussion of hardening gaps or commitment regularization; this is PST-specific.
- **Break condition:** If commitment regularization weight \( \lambda(t) \) is annealed too quickly or set too low, polynomials may not converge close to the ternary lattice, causing large post-hardening accuracy drops (as seen in 96K–192K scale gaps).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The UNKNOWN (0) output in ternary logic enables principled abstention; the density of 0-valued outputs in the hardened circuit tracks Bayes-optimal uncertainty, allowing selective prediction to surpass binary full-coverage accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** The ternary output domain \( T = \{-1,0,1\} \) allows neurons to output 0, which propagates through GroupSum aggregation. The learned polynomials, under commitment regularization, map ambiguous input regions to 0-valued truth table entries. The UNKNOWN density at inference thus reflects learned uncertainty, calibrated to the Bayes posterior entropy without explicit training for uncertainty.
- **Core assumption:** The three-valued Kleene \( K_3 \) logic semantics, where 0 propagates conservatively, provides a sound basis for abstention; the PST training dynamics naturally induce this behavior as a side effect of fitting data where uncertainty is present.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "ternary networks exploit the UNKNOWN state for principled abstention, surpassing binary full-coverage accuracy on selective prediction tasks... with UNKNOWN density tracking Bayes-optimal uncertainty."
  - [section 5.2.1] Shows strong negative correlation (r = -0.98) between UNKNOWN output fraction and Bayes optimal error across Gaussian separation sweep.
  - [section 5.2 & Figure 3] Empirical alignment of UNKNOWN density with Bayes posterior entropy on synthetic datasets.
  - [corpus] *No direct evidence*: Corpus does not address ternary logic abstention; this is a novel capability introduced by PST.
- **Break condition:** If the commitment loss is too strong early in training, polynomials may be forced to 0 prematurely, suppressing discriminative signal. If the task has no inherent ambiguity (e.g., linearly separable data), the network may not learn to use UNKNOWN meaningfully.

## Foundational Learning

### Boolean and Kleene Ternary Algebra
**Why needed here:** PST extends Boolean logic gate networks to Kleene's three-valued logic \( K_3 \) (\( \{-1,0,1\} \)) to enable UNKNOWN outputs. Understanding truth tables, logical operations, and the semantic difference between binary and ternary systems is essential to grasp the 19,683-gate explosion and the role of UNKNOWN.  
**Quick check question:** What is the truth table size for a two-input ternary logic gate, and why does it exceed the 16 binary gates?

### Polynomial Representation of Discrete Functions
**Why needed here:** PST's core is representing discrete truth tables by continuous polynomials. The multi-quadratic form (degrees ≤2) for ternary logic and multilinear form for Boolean logic must be understood to see why 9 coefficients suffice for ternary.  
**Quick check question:** Why does a polynomial representing a function on \( \{-1,0,1\}^2 \) have maximum degree 2 in each variable? What property of the domain makes this exact?

### Fourier Analysis on Finite Domains
**Why needed here:** The paper uses Fourier decomposition on \( T^2 \) with the basis \( \{ \varphi_i(x)\varphi_j(y) \} \) (where \( \varphi_2(x)=x^2-2/3 \)) to analyze gate complexity, define spectral regularizers, and interpret learned gates. This is crucial for understanding the "spectral profile" in Table 7 and the MDS embeddings.  
**Quick check question:** What is the significance of the quadratic basis function \( \varphi_2(x) = x^2 - 2/3 \) in the ternary Fourier basis, and why does it have no Boolean analogue?

### Regularization via Commitment Loss
**Why needed here:** The hardening gap is controlled by minimizing \( R_A(W) \), which measures the distance of polynomial evaluations to the nearest ternary value. This is a key training mechanic that ensures post-hardening fidelity.  
**Quick check question:** How does annealing \( \lambda(t) \) from near-zero to \( \lambda_{\text{max}} \) balance exploration (learning flexible functions) and exploitation (committing to valid gates)?

### Overparameterization and Implicit Pruning
**Why needed here:** The contraction of the hardening gap at large scales (512K neurons) is explained by overparameterization allowing poorly-committed neurons to map to 0-output gates (implicit pruning), as seen in the rise of UNKNOWN% from 6% to 24.6%.  
**Quick check question:** Why does increasing network scale from 96K to 512K neurons reduce the hardening gap from 14.1 pp to 3.7 pp, and what role does UNKNOWN% play?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
- **Input Encoder:** Maps normalized features (e.g., CIFAR-10 pixels) to ternary/binary inputs via resolution-\( K \) balanced thresholding (e.g., \( K=3 \) for ternary).
- **PST Neuron:** Each holds 9 polynomial coefficients \( w \in \mathbb{R}^9 \) corresponding to monomial basis \( [1, a, b, ab, a^2, b^2, a^2b, ab^2, a^2b^2] \). Computes \( p_w(a,b) \) then clips to \([-1,1]\).
- **Commitment Regularizer:** Computes \( R_A(W) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_j \frac{1}{9} \sum_{(a,b)\in T^2} \text{dist}(p_w(a,b), T)^2 \), added to task loss with scheduled \( \lambda(t) \).
- **Fourier Regularizer (optional):** \( R_F(W) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_j \sum_{i,j} |\hat{f}_{ij}| \) to bias toward spectrally sparse gates.
- **Hardening Module:** For each neuron, computes \( t_j = V w_j \), rounds entries to nearest \( \{-1,0,1\} \), looks up the corresponding gate in the precomputed library \( G \) of all 19,683 truth tables.
- **GroupSum Output:** Aggregates neuron outputs via summation and thresholding for classification. In ternary, 0-valued neurons contribute no signal.

### Critical path
The fidelity of the final discrete circuit depends on (1) polynomial initialization to avoid saturation, (2) annealing \( \lambda(t) \) to allow initial exploration then enforce commitment, and (3) sufficient network scale to allow implicit pruning of poorly-committed neurons via rounding to 0-output gates.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Speed vs. Accuracy:** PST neurons compute one polynomial (8 mult-adds) vs. DLGN's softmax over 16 gates, giving 2–3× speedup. However, at medium scales (96K-192K), the hardening gap is large (+11.9 to +14.1 pp), requiring larger scales (512K) for acceptable gap.
- **Expressivity vs. Interpretability:** The unrestricted polynomial space can learn any of 19,683 gates, but without Fourier regularization \( R_F \), the network may use complex, less interpretable gates. \( R_F \) biases toward sparse spectral profiles.
- **Coverage vs. Confidence:** Ternary circuits inherently abstain via 0 outputs. This improves selective prediction accuracy but reduces raw coverage. The \( \delta \) parameter in input encoding and the learned UNKNOWN density must be balanced.

### Failure signatures
- **High hardening gap (>10 pp) at target scale:** Indicates commitment regularization \( \lambda_{\text{max}} \) or annealing schedule (\( \gamma \)) is insufficient. Check \( R_A \) trajectory; it should decrease steadily.
- **Very low UNKNOWN% (<5% at large scale):** Suggests polynomials are well-committed but may be overfitting; check if the circuit accuracy matches soft accuracy. Low UNKNOWN% with high gap implies rounding errors compound across layers.
- **UNKNOWN density misaligned with uncertainty:** On synthetic tasks, if UNKNOWN density does not concentrate at decision boundaries, the network has failed to learn uncertainty mapping. Verify using margin vs. UNKNOWN correlation.
- **Gate diversity collapse:** If unique gate count is very low (≈16), the network is essentially binary; check Fourier spectral profile—high constant+linear energy, low quadratic suggests underutilization of ternary expressivity.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Sanity check: Single gate reproduction.** Initialize a PST neuron, train for a single target ternary gate (e.g., MAJORITY) with commitment loss. Verify after hardening, the recovered truth table matches the target exactly. Inspect polynomial coefficients.
2. **Scaling gap validation.** Train TLGNs at SMALL (48K), MEDIUM (96K), and HUGE (512K) on CIFAR-10. Plot gap (soft - circuit) vs. scale. Expect non-monotonic peak then contraction; HUGE gap should be <5 pp. Also track UNKNOWN% and hardening error (per Table 5).
3. **Uncertainty tracking on synthetic data.** Train on Moons/Circles with known Bayes boundary. Plot (a) decision boundary of binary DLGN, (b) ternary PST boundary, (c) UNKNOWN density overlay. Compute accuracy vs. coverage curve; expect AUC << 0 and ternary @50% coverage exceeding binary full-coverage accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can straight-through estimation and Gumbel-noise regularization be adapted to PST's polynomial regime to further narrow the network-level hardening gap?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states that adapting these techniques to PST's polynomial regime may further narrow the network-level hardening gap that contracts empirically with scale but lacks theoretical guarantees.
- **Why unresolved:** The current per-neuron commitment loss bound (Theorem 1) does not translate to a network-level guarantee, and the observed gap contraction with overparameterization remains unexplained.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretical analysis bounding the network-level hardening gap in terms of per-neuron errors and network topology, or empirical demonstrations that the adapted techniques reduce the gap without harming training stability or speed.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can PST be extended to recurrent architectures for online temporal monitoring and safety-critical systems?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion explicitly suggests extending PST to recurrent architectures for Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifications and runtime verification.
- **Why unresolved:** PST is currently designed for feedforward networks; recurrent architectures introduce temporal dependencies and sequences of ternary states, which may require modifications to training dynamics, hardening, and the role of UNKNOWN over time.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of a recurrent PST framework (e.g., with backpropagation through time) and evaluation on STL verification benchmarks, demonstrating that UNKNOWN effectively captures uncertainty in partially observed sequences.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does PST generalize to logic valences beyond ternary (e.g., quaternary) while maintaining quadratic parameter scaling and per-neuron hardening bounds?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper claims PST's parameterization cost grows only quadratically with logic valence, but analysis and experiments are limited to ternary. For q-ary logic, the polynomial degree might need to be (q−1, q−1) to achieve q² coefficients, and the rounding error bound (1/(q−1)) from Proposition 4 assumes a uniform lattice.
- **Why unresolved:** Higher valences may require higher-degree polynomials, affecting smoothness, commitment loss, and Fourier basis design. Universality and bounds for q>3 are not proven.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend PST to quaternary logic, including polynomial parameterization (with appropriate degree), commitment regularization, and Fourier analysis, then train networks and compare parameter efficiency and hardening gap with ternary.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What theoretical guarantees can be provided for the network-level hardening gap (soft vs. circuit accuracy) and how does it scale with overparameterization?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper provides a per-neuron bound (Theorem 1) but the network-level gap is only shown to contract empirically with scale (e.g., from 14.1 to 3.7 pp on CIFAR-10). No theory explains this contraction.
- **Why unresolved:** Per-neuron bounds do not automatically imply network-level guarantees because errors can accumulate across layers. The empirical contraction may stem from implicit regularization or overparameterization effects not captured by current analysis.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretical analysis bounding the network-level gap in terms of per-neuron commitment loss and network properties (width, depth, connectivity), or a controlled study isolating the effect of overparameterization on gap reduction.

## Limitations
- PST's polynomial representation is universal only for degree-(2,2); lower degrees cannot express all ternary gates and restrict the search space.
- Commitment loss annealing schedule (\( \lambda_{\text{max}}, \gamma \)) is critical and not systematically optimized; suboptimal choices cause large hardening gaps at medium scales (e.g., 96K neurons).
- UNKNOWN density calibration is validated primarily on synthetic tasks; its alignment with uncertainty on complex real-world datasets remains uncertain.
- The empirical contraction of the hardening gap with overparameterization lacks theoretical explanation, and the dynamics are not fully understood beyond 512K neurons.

## Confidence
- **Parameter reduction & training speed:** High (theoretical 2,187× reduction; empirical 2–3× speedup).
- **Hardening gap bound via commitment loss:** High (Theorem 1 proof; empirical \( R_A \) decreases with training).
- **UNKNOWN-based abstention superiority:** Medium (strong synthetic evidence; limited real-world tasks).
- **Scaling reduces hardening gap:** Medium (empirical trend across three scales; causal link plausible but not isolated).
- **Soft accuracy parity with binary DLGN:** Medium (reported on CIFAR-10; not tested on other benchmarks).

## Next Checks

2. **Ablation on commitment annealing:** Vary \( \lambda_{\text{max}} \in \{0.1,0.5,1.0\} \) and \( \gamma \in \{0.01,0.1,1.0\} \) across scales; measure hardening gap and UNKNOWN% to find robust regimes.
3. **Uncertainty calibration on real data:** On CIFAR-10, correlate UNKNOWN density with uncertainty proxies (e.g., MC dropout variance) and evaluate selective prediction curves vs. abstention baselines.
