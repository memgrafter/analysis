# AM$^3$Safety: Towards Data Efficient Alignment of Multi-modal Multi-turn Safety for MLLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.04736
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04736
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Achieved >10% reduction in Attack Success Rate and >8% increase in harmlessness win rate on multi-turn safety benchmarks using 7,000 dialogues, while preserving helpfulness.

## Executive Summary
Multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) exhibit safety vulnerabilities in multi-turn dialogues, where harmful intent can be gradually reconstructed across turns. Existing RLHF methods are designed for single-turn VQA and require costly manual annotations. This paper introduces AM$^3$Safety, a framework combining an automatically constructed multi-modal dialogue dataset (InterSafe-V) with a two-stage alignment process: cold-start refusal learning followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) fine-tuning. The approach uses a turn-aware dual-objective reward that weights dialogue turns based on safety score variance to maintain consistent safety without sacrificing helpfulness. Evaluations on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and LLaVA-NeXT-7B show significant improvements in multi-turn safety and helpfulness with substantially less training data than annotation-heavy baselines.

## Method Summary
The core methodological contribution is two-fold. First, the authors construct InterSafe-V, an open-source dataset of 11,270 multi-modal dialogues and 500 refusal VQA pairs via model interactions, drastically reducing annotation cost. The pipeline decomposes harmful queries into sub-queries, simulates dialogues between red-team and target MLLMs, and cleans the data. Second, they propose AM$^3$Safety, which initializes the model with supervised fine-tuning on refusal templates (cold-start) to establish a safety foundation, then applies GRPO with a turn-aware reward. The reward dynamically weights each dialogue turn based on the variance of safety scores across rollouts, aggregating a dual-objective (helpfulness + safety) to optimize for consistent safety across turns while preserving conversational utility.

## Key Results
- Reduced Attack Success Rate (ASR) by over 10% on the SafeMT multi-turn safety benchmark.
- Increased harmlessness win rate by at least 8% and helpfulness win rate by over 13% on multi-turn evaluations.
- Achieved these gains using only 7,000 dialogues for training, far fewer than manual annotation baselines.
- Maintained performance on general tasks (e.g., MME, MMBench), indicating no significant trade-off with broader capabilities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Cold-Start Refusal Learning**  
A supervised fine-tuning phase on a curated mix of refusal scenarios (60%) and professional tasks (40%) seeds the model with stable refusal behavior before reinforcement learning. This establishes a safety foundation that prevents reward hacking during subsequent GRPO, where the model might otherwise prioritize helpfulness over safety. The assumption is that pre-training refusal with reasoning avoids conflicts between safety and utility. Break condition: insufficient diversity in refusal patterns could cause overfitting or excessive refusal on benign queries.

**Mechanism 2: Turn-Aware Dual-Objective Reward**  
During GRPO, for each dialogue turn, safety scores across multiple rollouts are computed; high variance indicates inconsistent safety behavior. The turn weight is derived from a utility function combining variance and average safety (with a penalty for low safety), normalized via softmax. This weighting is applied to the aggregated dual-objective reward (β·helpfulness + safety) per turn, emphasizing ambiguous or risky turns. The assumption is that variance effectively identifies critical turns needing optimization. Break condition: noisy variance estimates from small rollout groups or mis-calibrated safety threshold τ could mis-prioritize turns, leading to over-refusal or missed risks.

**Mechanism 3: Model-Interaction Data Construction**  
InterSafe-V is generated by decomposing harmful queries, then simulating multi-turn dialogues between a red-team model (InternVL3) and target MLLMs, including image generation. This captures gradual intent reconstruction at low cost, reflecting realistic jailbreak trajectories that static single-turn datasets miss. The assumption is that model-to-model interactions proxy human adversarial behavior. Break condition: an insufficiently adversarial red-team or incoherent sub-queries may produce dialogues that do not represent genuine attack strategies, limiting dataset effectiveness.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
InterSafe-V dataset -> Cold-start SFT module -> Base MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) -> GRPO trainer with turn-aware reward calculator (using Automatic evaluator) -> Evaluation benchmarks (SafeMT, JailbreakV, etc.)

**Critical Path**:  
1. Prepare base model and InterSafe-V data.  
2. Perform cold-start SFT on refusal VQA and dialogues to initialize safety.  
3. For GRPO: generate rollouts per dialogue, compute turn-level safety/helpfulness scores, calculate variance-based weights, aggregate dual-objective reward, update policy.  
4. Evaluate on multi-turn safety benchmarks and general tasks.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Safety vs. helpfulness controlled by coefficient β; higher β risks safety degradation.  
- Data efficiency vs. coverage: using 7,000 dialogues reduces cost but may miss edge cases.  
- Automatic evaluation vs. human annotation: LLM-as-judge scales but introduces judge model bias.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- High Attack Success Rate (ASR) on SafeMT, indicating poor multi-turn safety.  
- Low helpfulness win rate on conversational benchmarks, suggesting over-refusal.  
- Training instability in GRPO: KL divergence spikes or reward collapse if β or turn weights are misconfigured.  
- Poor generalization to unseen jailbreak strategies not in InterSafe-V.

**First 3 Experiments**:  
1. **Ablation of cold-start**: Train GRPO without initial SFT and compare ASR and win rates on SafeMT to quantify cold-start’s contribution.  
2. **Turn-weighting ablation**: Replace variance-based weights with uniform averaging and measure per-turn ASR inconsistency on SafeMT to validate the weighting mechanism.  
3. **Generalization test**: Evaluate on out-of-distribution multi-turn benchmarks (e.g., MTMCS-Bench) to assess transfer beyond SafeMT.

## Limitations
- InterSafe-V’s model-generated dialogues may not fully capture human adversarial intent reconstruction strategies.
- Automatic evaluation using InternVL3-as-judge risks benchmark-specific bias and overestimation of safety gains.
- Turn-weighting sensitivity to small rollout groups or miscalibrated safety threshold τ could mis-prioritize turns.

## Confidence
- Primary results (ASR reduction, win rate increases): **Medium** (strong on SafeMT but validated on only two MLLMs).
- Cold-start refusal learning mechanism: **Medium** (plausible but lacks ablation in conversational context).
- Turn-aware dual-objective reward: **Medium** (theoretically sound but noise-sensitive).
- Data construction via model interactions: **Low** (red-team modeling unverified against real human jailbreaks).
