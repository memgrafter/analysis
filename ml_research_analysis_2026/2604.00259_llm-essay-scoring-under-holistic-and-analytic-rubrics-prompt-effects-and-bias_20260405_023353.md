# LLM Essay Scoring Under Holistic and Analytic Rubrics: Prompt Effects and Bias
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2604.00259
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00259
*   **Reference Count:** 24
*   **Primary Result:** Strong open-weight LLMs achieve moderate holistic agreement (QWK ~0.6) but exhibit stable negative bias on lower-order traits (Grammar, Conventions); concise prompts outperform verbose guidelines for analytic multi-trait scoring.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates instruction-tuned large language models (LLMs) as automated essay scorers, comparing their performance on holistic (single overall score) versus analytic (multiple trait-specific scores) rubrics. Using three standard datasets, the research finds that while models like Llama-3.1-70B reach moderate agreement with human consensus on holistic tasks, analytic scoring reveals a consistent, large negative bias on formal, lower-order concerns (LOC) like Grammar. Furthermore, for multi-trait analytic scoring, concise "keyword" prompts (listing traits and scales) reliably yield better trait-specific agreement than longer "guideline" prompts with full rubric descriptions. A key practical insight is that the sample size needed to detect this stable LOC bias is very small (often ≤10 essays), enabling a bias-correction strategy that uses minimal human data to adjust model scores without fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The study conducts a zero-shot evaluation of open-weight instruction-tuned LLMs (e.g., Llama-3.1-Instruct, GPT-OSS) on three essay scoring datasets: ASAP 2.0 (holistic), ELLIPSE (analytic, 6 traits), and DREsS (analytic, 3 traits). For each dataset, two prompt strategies are tested: **Keywords** (concise list of trait labels and score scale) and **Guidelines** (full textual rubric descriptions). Model outputs are parsed into numeric scores, which are compared against Human Consensus Scores (HCS) using metrics including Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK) for agreement, mean Bias (signed error), and score standard deviation. A core analysis involves a bootstrap resampling procedure (10,000 iterations, starting at N=5) to estimate the minimum sample size (Nmin) required for the 95% confidence interval of mean bias to exclude zero, indicating stable, detectable bias.

## Key Results
*   LLMs achieve moderate holistic scoring agreement (QWK ~0.6) but analytic scoring agreement is substantially lower, with the best multi-trait configuration reaching only QWK 0.321.
*   For analytic tasks, concise keyword prompts consistently outperform verbose guideline prompts in agreement metrics.
*   A strong, negative bias on Grammar and Conventions (LOC traits) is detectable with very small validation sets (Nmin often ≤5), while bias on higher-order traits (Content, Organization) requires much larger samples or is not detectable, due to higher scoring variance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** LLMs achieve higher agreement on holistic than analytic scoring because a unified judgment aligns with their learned representation of essay quality, whereas decomposing quality into orthogonal traits exposes limitations in compartmentalized evaluation.
*   **Mechanism:** Holistic scoring maps to the model's integrated concept of a high-quality essay. Analytic scoring demands simultaneous assessment of weakly correlated dimensions, leading to greater disagreement as the model struggles to isolate judgments.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states moderate holistic agreement but non-uniform transfer to analytic. Section 4.1 shows systematic performance differences, with analytic tasks yielding lower QWK. Corpus suggests analytic scoring is a known challenge.
*   **Break condition:** A model achieving equally high QWK (>0.8) on both holistic and a specific multi-trait analytic task would falsify this.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Concise keyword prompts outperform verbose guidelines for multi-trait analytic scoring because detailed rubrics introduce competing constraints that cause contextual interference, forcing the model toward heuristics.
*   **Mechanism:** When scoring multiple traits at once, extensive textual descriptions create "constraint overload." Keywords act as clearer, isolated anchors for each trait, reducing cognitive load and improving per-trait consistency.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.1 directly state Keywords outperforms Guidelines for analytic scoring. Section 5 explains the interference mechanism.
*   **Break condition:** If guideline prompts consistently outperformed keywords across analytic datasets and model scales, the claim would be invalidated.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The minuscule Nmin for LOC traits (≤10) indicates a stable, deterministic scoring deviation (systematic harshness), while large Nmin for HOC traits reflects high per-essay scoring variance (inconsistency) around a mean near zero.
*   **Mechanism:** LLMs apply more rigid, rule-like judgments to mechanical traits (Grammar), leading to a consistent offset from human leniency. Judgments on conceptual traits (Content) are more variable and context-dependent, resulting in higher variance and less stable mean bias.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.4 highlight the stark Nmin disparity. Table 5 shows Llama-70B Nmin=5 for ELLIPSE LOC traits vs. Nmin=340 for DREsS Content.
*   **Break condition:** A replicate study finding similarly small Nmin for HOC traits or large Nmin for LOC traits would refute this pattern.

## Architecture Onboarding
1. Data Layer: Three datasets (ASAP 2.0, ELLIPSE, DREsS) with pre-defined splits, HCS, and trait rubrics.
2. Model Layer: Open-weight instruction-tuned LLMs (Llama-3.1 family, GPT-OSS) served via API/local inference.
3. Prompting Layer: Two template sets (Keywords, Guidelines) per dataset.
4. Inference Layer: Deterministic decoding (temp=0) generating discrete ordinal scores.
5. Analysis Layer: Computes QWK, Bias, MAE, σ, and runs bootstrap N_min procedure.
-> Critical path: Dataset & Prompt Preparation -> Model Inference -> Score Aggregation -> Metric Calculation -> Bootstrap N_min Analysis -> Interpretation.
-> Design tradeoffs: Zero-shot vs. Fine-tuning (isolates inherent bias vs. potential alignment gains); Prompt brevity vs. completeness (reduces interference vs. omits nuance); Metric selection (QWK for agreement, Bias for offset, N_min for stability).
-> Failure signatures: High MAE + Low |Bias| (random error); σ_LLM << σ_HCS (score compression); r_LLM(word count) >> r_HCS (verbosity bias); N_min >> dataset size (no stable bias).
-> First 3 experiments:
    1. Replicate Table 2 (Overall Leaderboard): Run all model/prompt/dataset combinations to confirm Keywords>Guidelines for analytic.
    2. Trait-Level Deep Dive (Table 3): For top model (Llama-3.1-70B), compute per-trait QWK, Bias, σ_LLM vs. σ_HCS. Validate HOC/LOC disparity.
    3. Bootstrap N_min Replication: Implement stepwise bootstrap for one model-trait-split (e.g., Llama-70B, ELLIPSE Grammar, Keywords). Verify N_min ~5.

## Limitations
*   Exact verbatim text of the Guidelines prompts and precise dataset splits are not disclosed, hindering exact replication.
*   Results are confined to instruction-tuned open-weight LLMs on three English essay datasets; generalizability to other models, languages, or rubrics is unknown.
*   Only zero-shot conditions are evaluated; few-shot and fine-tuning effects remain unexamined.

## Confidence
*   Holistic scoring agreement > analytic scoring agreement: **High**
*   Concise keyword prompts outperform verbose guidelines for analytic multi-trait scoring: **Medium**
*   Very small N_min for LOC traits vs. large for HOC traits: **Medium**
*   Bias-correction-first deployment strategy using small human sets: **Medium**
