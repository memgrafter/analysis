# SurGo-R1: Benchmarking and Modeling Contextual Reasoning for Operative Zone in Surgical Video

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21706
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21706
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** SurGo-R1 achieves 76.6% phase accuracy, 32.7 mIoU, and 54.8% hardcore accuracy on unseen procedures, a 6.6× improvement over generalist VLMs.

## Executive Summary

Safe operative zone ("Go Zone") identification in minimally invasive surgery is highly phase-dependent and cognitively demanding. Existing AI methods offer only static detection or binary verification, ignoring critical procedural context. This work introduces **ResGo**, a benchmark with Go Zone bounding boxes and clinician-authored rationales (phase, exposure, action, risk), and **SurGo-R1**, a multi-turn reasoning architecture that first identifies the surgical phase, then reasons and grounds the Go Zone coordinates using a phase-specific definition retrieved from a mapping tool. Optimized via GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), SurGo-R1 significantly outperforms prior models, with ablations confirming the necessity of the phase-definition tool and the multi-turn design.

## Method Summary

SurGo-R1 addresses the task of surgical safe operative zone grounding conditioned on phase identification. The **ResGo** dataset provides 2,686 frames from 21 videos (17 train, 4 test) with bounding boxes and rationales. Training proceeds in two stages using GRPO on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct:  
- **Stage 1:** Phase recognition as multiple-choice question with accuracy (`Racc`) and format rewards.  
- **Stage 2:** Multi-turn reasoning – predict phase, retrieve phase-specific Go Zone definition, then generate rationale and bounding box. Rewards combine entity recall on rationales (`Rreason` via scispaCy), IoU (`RIoU`), center distance (`Rdist`), and format.  
Inference uses the predicted phase to fetch the definition. Hyperparameters include learning rate 2e-6, 4 generations, β=0.01, Stage 1 trained 10 epochs, Stage 2 80 steps.

## Key Results

- SurGo-R1 reaches **76.6% phase accuracy**, **32.7 mIoU**, and **54.8% hardcore accuracy** (which penalizes grounding if phase is wrong) on unseen procedures, a **6.6× improvement** over generalist VLMs.
- Conditioned evaluation (only on correctly predicted phases) reveals prior models fail primarily due to **phase identification errors**.
- Ablations confirm the **critical role of the phase-definition retrieval tool** and the multi-turn architecture.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

SurGo-R1 succeeds by decoupling phase identification from zone grounding, each augmented with explicit context. The multi-turn design forces the model to reason about the current surgical context before producing coordinates. The retrieved phase-specific Go Zone definition grounds the reasoning in clinically relevant criteria. GRPO optimizes a composite reward that directly targets reasoning quality (entity recall in rationales) and spatial accuracy (IoU, distance), guiding the model to produce outputs that are both clinically meaningful and geometrically correct. The separation also isolates failures: poor phase recognition degrades overall performance, but the conditional evaluation shows that when the phase is correct, grounding itself is effective.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input Frame -> Stage1 Phase Predictor (MCQ) -> Phase Prediction -> Definition Retriever (phase -> Go Zone definition) -> Stage2 Reasoning Module (image + phase + definition -> rationale + bounding box) -> Outputs (phase, rationale, box)

**Critical path:** During inference, a single image is first passed through the phase predictor; the predicted phase triggers retrieval of the corresponding Go Zone definition; the image, phase label, and definition are then fed into the multi-turn reasoning module that first generates a clinician-like rationale, then produces the bounding box coordinates.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Multi-turn vs single-stage: Multi-stage simplifies conditioning but introduces error propagation if phase is misclassified.  
- Retrieval tool dependency: External definitions improve relevance but require a reliable mapping and may fail if the phase is wrong.  
- GRPO vs supervised: GRPO handles composite rewards but adds complexity (importance sampling, KL regularization) and may be sensitive to reward scaling.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Phase accuracy below 70% indicates Stage1 is underperforming.  
- Hardcore accuracy significantly lower than conditioned mIoU suggests phase errors dominate overall failure.  
- `Rreason` (entity recall) plateauing at low values implies scispaCy extraction mismatches or poor rationale generation.  
- Removing the definition tool at inference causes a large drop in mIoU, confirming its necessity; no drop suggests the model ignores the tool.

**First experiments:**  
1. Train Stage1 in isolation until phase accuracy exceeds 90% before starting Stage2.  
2. Ablate the retrieval tool: replace definitions with a generic description or remove entirely, then evaluate the full pipeline to quantify its contribution.  
3. Sweep reward weights: vary the relative scaling of `Rreason` vs `RIoU` to study the trade-off between reasoning quality and grounding precision.

## Limitations

- The **ResGo dataset is not publicly released** yet, preventing external verification and follow-up studies.  
- **Reward component weights** and scaling factors are unspecified, making exact replication of training dynamics uncertain.  
- **Implementation details** for GRPO (KL penalty, sequence importance sampling) and inference (temperature, parsing) are omitted.  
- **Reasoning evaluation** relies on clinician review without disaggregated metrics or inter-rater reliability, limiting objective assessment.
