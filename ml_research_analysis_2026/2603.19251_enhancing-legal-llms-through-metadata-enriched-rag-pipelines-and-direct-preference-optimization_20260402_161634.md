# Enhancing Legal LLMs through Metadata-Enriched RAG Pipelines and Direct Preference Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19251
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19251
- **Reference Count:** 4
- **Primary Result:** Metadata-enriched RAG reduces Document Retrieval Mismatch by up to 75.1 percentage points and DPO alignment reduces over-refusal from 53.2% to 1.5%, achieving an end-to-end F1 improvement of 0.0178.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles hallucinations in legal question‑answering systems, which arise from two sources: retrieval errors caused by lexical redundancy in legal corpora and decoding errors where models generate answers without sufficient context. The authors propose a Metadata‑Enriched Hybrid RAG pipeline that appends document summaries and metadata (e.g., jurisdiction, parties) to text chunks to improve retrieval precision, and a Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) method that aligns a small generator to answer correctly when context is adequate and refuse otherwise. Evaluations on three legal QA datasets show that metadata‑RAG cuts Document Retrieval Mismatch (DRM) by up to 75.1 points and boosts Span Recall by up to 79.1 points on MAUD. DPO drastically lowers over‑refusal on correct contexts (53.2% → 1.5%) and raises appropriate refusal on incorrect contexts (87.3% → 99.3%), while improving BERTScore by 0.0548. The combined system yields an end‑to‑end F1 gain of 0.0178 over the baseline.

## Method Summary
The approach combines a retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG) pipeline with preference‑based alignment. Documents are split recursively (512‑token chunks, 50‑token overlap) and enriched with metadata: a local summary (150–200 tokens covering four consecutive chunks) and document‑level fields (name, jurisdiction), occupying ~20–25% of each chunk. Retrieval uses `gte-large` embeddings and a FAISS index, hybridized with BM25 (dense weight 0.8, BM25 weight 0.2). The generator is a LLaMA 3.2 1B Instruct model fine‑tuned via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Preference pairs are drawn from the Australian Legal QA dataset: each query provides one correct context (containing the gold answer) and one incorrect random snippet; DPO learns to prefer answering for the former and refusing for the latter. Evaluation relies on domain‑specific metrics: DRM (cross‑document retrieval error), Span Recall (evidence overlap), BERTScore, and end‑to‑end token‑level F1.

## Key Results
- Metadata‑enhanced RAG reduces Document Retrieval Mismatch by up to 75.1 percentage points and increases Span Recall by up to 79.1 points on the MAUD dataset (k=64).
- DPO alignment reduces over‑refusal on correct contexts from 53.2% to 1.5% and increases appropriate refusal on incorrect contexts from 87.3% to 99.3%, while improving BERTScore (F1) by 0.0548.
- The combined system achieves an end‑to‑end F1 improvement of 0.0178 over the baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Metadata‑Enriched RAG Reduces DRM
- **Claim:** Metadata‑enriched chunking reduces Document Retrieval Mismatch (DRM) by mitigating lexical redundancy.
- **Mechanism:** Appending document summaries and metadata provides discriminative signals beyond raw text, helping the retriever distinguish between semantically similar but legally distinct documents.
- **Core assumption:** Legal documents share surface‑level lexical patterns but differ in metadata (e.g., case names, dates, jurisdictions), which can be leveraged to improve retrieval precision.
- **Evidence:** [section 5.1.2, 6.1.4] Metadata injection (20–25% token space) yields DRM reduction up to 75.1 percentage points on MAUD; structured contracts benefit from party/financial term metadata.
- **Break condition:** If metadata does not align with query intent (e.g., PrivacyQA shows minimal improvement), the mechanism fails.

### DPO Curtails Decoding Hallucinations
- **Claim:** Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) trains a small model to refuse answers when context is insufficient, overcoming prompt‑induced over/under‑refusal.
- **Mechanism:** DPO learns from preference pairs where answering is preferred for correct contexts and refusal for incorrect contexts, shaping an internal policy for selective refusal.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s zero‑shot refusal behavior is instruction‑induced risk aversion, not true incapability; preference tuning disentangles context adequacy from output format.
- **Evidence:** [section 5.2, 6.2.1] Removing refusal instruction allows model to answer correctly but fails to refuse incorrectly (79% answer rate). Post‑DPO, correct‑context refusal drops from 53.2% to 1.5%, incorrect‑context refusal rises from 87.3% to 99.3%. BERTScore improves by +0.0548.
- **Break condition:** Noisy preference data or insufficient model capacity may prevent learning the distinction.

### Combined System Synergy
- **Claim:** The combined metadata‑RAG + DPO system yields end‑to‑end gains because improved retrieval supplies more correct contexts, while DPO ensures the generator exploits them without hallucinating on bad contexts.
- **Mechanism:** Metadata‑RAG increases the probability that retrieved chunks contain the gold answer span (higher Span Recall, lower DRM). DPO then trains the generator to answer accurately when such chunks are provided and refuse otherwise, addressing both retrieval‑induced and decoding‑induced failure modes.
- **Core assumption:** The two failure modes are orthogonal; fixing retrieval alone leaves decoding issues, and fixing decoding alone cannot overcome missing/incorrect context.
- **Evidence:** [abstract, section 8, 6.2.3] Conclusion states hallucinations mitigated at both levels; end‑to‑end F1 improves by 0.0178 over baseline (Table 4).
- **Break condition:** If metadata‑RAG improvements do not translate into more usable context, or if DPO overfits to the specific context distribution, end‑to‑end gains may not materialize.

## Foundational Learning
- **Legal Document Structure & Lexical Redundancy**
  - *Why needed:* Legal texts are long, hierarchical, and reuse boilerplate language, causing retrievers to match on surface forms rather than legal substance. Understanding this explains why standard chunking fails and metadata helps.
  - *Quick check:* Why might two different M&A agreements have nearly identical wording but different parties and financial terms?
- **Retrieval Evaluation Metrics (DRM, Span Recall)**
  - *Why needed:* DRM measures cross‑document retrieval errors (critical for provenance), while Span Recall measures evidence overlap. These are domain‑specific replacements for simple top‑k accuracy.
  - *Quick check:* What does a high DRM but moderate Span Recall indicate about a retriever’s behavior?
- **Preference Learning (DPO) vs. Prompting**
  - *Why needed:* DPO shifts the model’s internal decision boundary for “can answer?” rather than just formatting outputs. Crucial for small models that cannot follow complex refusal instructions reliably.
  - *Quick check:* If a model refuses 50% of the time on correct contexts under a “refuse if unsure” prompt, is it incapable of answering or just risk‑averse?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Raw Document → Preprocessor (recursive chunking + metadata extraction) → Embedding → FAISS index → Hybrid Retriever (Dense 0.8 + BM25 0.2) → Context Assembly → DPO‑tuned Generator (LLaMA 3.2 1B) → Answer/Refusal → Evaluation Harness (DRM, Span Recall, BERTScore, F1)
- **Critical path:** Recursive chunking with 512‑token size and 50‑token overlap → metadata injection (summary + document name/jurisdiction) → hybrid retrieval (top‑k) → context assembly → DPO‑tuned generation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  • Metadata richness vs. token budget: ~20–25% allocated; too much dilutes core text.  
  • Retrieval k vs. latency/noise: Larger k improves recall but increases garbage‑in; paper tests up to 64, end‑to‑end uses top‑4.  
  • DPO data construction: “Incorrect context” must be truly irrelevant; random snippets may not simulate realistic retrieval errors.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  • High DRM → metadata not discriminative enough or chunking too fine.  
  • Over‑refusal on correct contexts (post‑DPO) → insufficient DPO training steps or poor preference pairs.  
  • Under‑refusal on incorrect contexts → “incorrect contexts” in DPO set not actually devoid of answer cues.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline RAG replication:** Implement recursive chunking + hybrid retrieval without metadata. Confirm DRM > 80% on MAUD and low Span Recall.  
  2. **Metadata injection A/B test:** Append document name + jurisdiction + local summary (4‑chunk window). Measure DRM/Recall delta; expect >50% DRM reduction on structured datasets.  
  3. **DPO behavioral validation:** On held‑out test, quantify refusal % for correct/incorrect contexts pre/post‑DPO. Expect correct‑context refusal <5%, incorrect‑context refusal >95%. If not, inspect preference pair quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How do alternative alignment methods (e.g., self‑play, RLHF) compare to DPO in achieving selective refusal behavior for legal QA systems?
2. What is the effect of model scale on DPO’s ability to balance accurate answering with appropriate refusal in legal contexts?
3. Why does metadata enhancement yield disparate improvements across legal datasets (minimal on PrivacyQA vs. dramatic on MAUD), and how can metadata schemas be optimized for diverse legal document types?
4. What is the optimal retrieval depth (k) for end‑to‑end legal QA when using DPO‑aligned models, and does this optimum differ from retrieval‑only settings?

## Limitations
- **Dataset‑specificity:** Metadata‑RAG benefits are dramatic on structured contracts (MAUD) but minimal on PrivacyQA; generalization to unseen legal domains is unproven.
- **DPO preference data construction:** “Incorrect contexts” are random snippets, not necessarily hard negatives; the model may not learn nuanced refusal on borderline cases.
- **Orthogonality assumption:** The paper assumes retrieval and decoding failures are independent; if improved retrieval changes the distribution of context adequacy seen by the DPO model, end‑to‑end gains may not materialize as expected.

## Confidence
- **Metadata‑RAG reduces DRM / improves Span Recall:** Medium (strong evidence on MAUD, fails on PrivacyQA; effectiveness hinges on metadata richness and corpus structure).
- **DPO aligns refusal behavior:** High (clear, statistically significant shifts in refusal rates on held‑out test).
- **Combined system yields end‑to‑end F1 gain:** Medium (gain modest, confidence interval not reported; relies on unverified orthogonality).

## Next Checks
1. **Generalization test:** Apply the metadata‑RAG pipeline to a new, held‑out legal corpus (e.g., EU legal decisions) without re‑tuning metadata fields. Measure DRM/Recall delta vs. baseline to quantify domain portability.
2. **Ablation of metadata components:** Remove document summary, jurisdiction, and party metadata individually on MAUD. Identify which elements drive the 75.1 pt DRM reduction, validating the “discriminative signal” claim.
3. **DPO on hard negatives:** Replace random incorrect contexts with top‑5 retrieved chunks that contain lexical overlap but no answer span. Re‑evaluate refusal rates to test robustness against realistic retrieval errors.
