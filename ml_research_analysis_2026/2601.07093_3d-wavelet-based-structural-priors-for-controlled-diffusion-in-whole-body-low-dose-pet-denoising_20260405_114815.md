# 3D Wavelet-Based Structural Priors for Controlled Diffusion in Whole-Body Low-Dose PET Denoising

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.07093
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07093
- **Reference count**: 12
- **Primary result**: WCC-Net achieves PSNR +1.21 dB and SSIM +0.008 improvement over the strongest diffusion baseline on 1/20-dose UDPET test set, with robust generalization to 1/50 and 1/4 dose levels.

## Executive Summary
Wavelet-Conditioned ControlNet (WCC-Net) is a fully 3D diffusion framework for low-dose whole-body PET denoising. It integrates a wavelet-based structural prior via a trainable ControlNet branch into a frozen pretrained 3D DDPM backbone, preserving the generative prior while guiding denoising. Using a single-level Haar wavelet, the method separates stable low-frequency anatomy from high-frequency noise. On the 1/20-dose UDPET test set, WCC-Net outperforms the strongest diffusion baselines by +1.21 dB PSNR and +0.008 SSIM, with lower GMSD and NMAE. It generalizes robustly to 1/50 and 1/4 dose levels, consistently beating CNN, GAN, and diffusion baselines in both quantitative metrics and anatomical fidelity.

## Method Summary
WCC-Net conditions a frozen pretrained 3D conditional DDPM backbone (pretrained on normal-dose PET) on the low-frequency component (LLL) of a single-level Haar wavelet decomposition of the low-dose PET input. A trainable ControlNet branch processes this LLL subband and injects its features into the backbone’s skip connections via zero-initialized convolutions. Only the control branch is trained while the backbone remains frozen, preserving the learned generative prior. During training, patches of normal-dose PET are corrupted with forward diffusion, and the network predicts the noise. At inference, the model denoises from random noise using the wavelet guidance. The method is evaluated on the UDPET dataset (Siemens Biograph Vision Quadra, 18F-FDG) with volumes of 192×288×520 voxels, using patch-wise training and whole-volume inference.

## Key Results
- PSNR +1.21 dB and SSIM +0.008 improvement over strongest diffusion baseline on 1/20-dose UDPET test set.
- Lower GMSD and NMAE compared to baselines.
- Robust generalization to 1/50 and 1/4 dose levels, consistently outperforming CNN, GAN, and diffusion baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Wavelet-based frequency-domain prior
- **Claim**: Wavelet-based frequency-domain representations provide a noise-robust structural prior that guides the diffusion process toward anatomically consistent reconstructions.
- **Mechanism**: The 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform decomposes the low-dose PET input into subbands. The low-frequency component (LLL) encodes stable coarse anatomical structure, while high-frequency subbands are noise-dominated. Conditioning the diffusion model on the LLL via the ControlNet branch yields a guidance signal disentangled from pixel-level noise, allowing the frozen backbone to focus denoising on high-frequency noise without distorting anatomy.
- **Core assumption**: Low-frequency wavelet coefficients are sufficiently preserved in ultra-low-dose PET to serve as a reliable scaffold, and the diffusion backbone can effectively hallucinate only high-frequency details consistent with that scaffold.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states "wavelet-based frequency-domain structural priors... decouples anatomical structure from noise." Section 3.2 confirms LLL encodes global coarse structure while high-frequency subbands are noise-dominated. Neighboring works use different conditioning, highlighting the novelty of the frequency-domain approach.
- **Break condition**: If low-dose signal degradation is extreme (dose << 1/50), low-frequency coefficients may become unreliable, leading to anatomically implausible generations.

### Mechanism 2: Frozen backbone preservation
- **Claim**: Freezing the pretrained 3D DDPM backbone and training only a lightweight ControlNet branch preserves the powerful generative prior learned on normal-dose data while enabling controlled generation.
- **Mechanism**: The ControlNet branch (a copy of the encoder) processes the wavelet prior and injects its features into the frozen backbone via zero-initialized convolutions at multiple resolutions. Zero-initialization deactivates the conditional pathway at start, recovering the original pretrained model. The backbone’s weights remain unchanged, so its learned distribution over clean PET images is intact; the trainable branch only learns to modulate that distribution.
- **Core assumption**: The pretrained diffusion backbone’s generative prior is strong and general enough that only a small, structured modulation is needed to adapt to conditional denoising.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions "preserving the pretrained generative prior." Section 3.2 describes the zero-initialization trick and frozen backbone. The corpus shows other works fine-tune the entire diffusion model, making the frozen design a key differentiator.
- **Break condition**: If the data distribution shift between normal-dose training and target low-dose task is too large (different scanner, tracer), the frozen backbone’s prior may be mismatched, and the lightweight ControlNet branch may not correct it.

### Mechanism 3: Single-level Haar wavelet
- **Claim**: Using a single-level Haar wavelet provides an optimal trade-off between providing a stable structural anchor and maintaining computational/alignment simplicity for volumetric injection.
- **Mechanism**: Single-level decomposition yields one low-frequency (LLL) volume and seven high-frequency subbands. This keeps the conditioning signal’s channel dimension low (8 total) and maintains spatial resolution alignment with early encoder stages in the 3D U-Net. Multi-level decomposition would increase channel count and downsample LLL, complicating feature alignment. Haar’s orthogonality and simplicity suit piecewise-smooth anatomical structures.
- **Core assumption**: The coarse structural information in a single-level LLL is sufficient for guiding whole-body PET denoising, and the marginal benefit of additional scales does not outweigh architectural complexity.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 3.2 states the deliberate choice of single-level decomposition for principled trade-off. Section 5.3 (Ablation) Table 2 shows LLL outperforms using all bands or high-frequency combinations; "All-Low" (excluding HHH) performs similarly, suggesting LLL captures most useful prior.
- **Break condition**: For tasks requiring very fine-scale structural preservation (e.g., tiny lesions), a single-level LLL might be too coarse, necessitating multi-level wavelet guidance.

**Concept: Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)**  
- *Why needed*: The framework is built upon a conditional 3D DDPM backbone. Understanding the forward/reverse process, noise prediction objective, and U-Net architecture is essential to grasp what is preserved (frozen) and what is conditioned.  
- *Quick check*: In a conditional DDPM for image translation, what two key components of the noise prediction network `ϵθ(xt, t, y)` are conditioned on the source image `y`?

**Concept: ControlNet for Diffusion Models**  
- *Why needed*: The core contribution is a ControlNet-style injection of a wavelet prior. Understanding the "zero-initialized convolution" trick and multi-resolution residual injection is critical to see how new guidance integrates without harming the pretrained backbone.  
- *Quick check*: What is the purpose of initializing the convolutional layers that inject the ControlNet features with zeros?

**Concept: 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT)**  
- *Why needed*: The conditioning signal is derived from 3D DWT. Understanding how a 3D volume decomposes into 8 subbands (LLL, LLH, LHL, LHH, HLL, HLH, HHL, HHH) and the frequency meaning of L/H per dimension explains why LLL is chosen as the structural prior.  
- *Quick check*: In a 3D DWT, what does the subband `LHL` represent in terms of filtering along the (typically) x, y, z axes?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Inputs: low-dose PET volume `y`, normal-dose PET `x0` (training only) → Wavelet transform (single-level Haar) → `c_wav` (LLL subband) → Trainable ControlNet branch (encoder copy) → Embedded features (via 3D transposed conv) → Injection into frozen backbone’s skip connections at each encoder level via zero-initialized convolutions → Output denoised PET volume.

**Critical path**  
Training: For each `(x0, y)` pair, corrupt `x0` to `xt`. Compute `c_wav = W_S(y)`. Embed `c_wav`. Forward pass through frozen backbone and ControlNet branch in parallel. Combine features via zero-convs at skip connections. Compute MSE loss between predicted noise and true noise.  
Inference: Start with random noise `x_T`. For each timestep: compute `c_wav` from `y`, embed, inject into backbone’s skip connections, run one denoising step with combined network.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Frozen backbone vs. full fine-tuning: Freezing preserves generative prior but limits adaptation; fine-tuning risks catastrophic forgetting.  
- Wavelet subband selection: LLL alone vs. all subbands; ablation shows LLL optimal.  
- Haar vs. other wavelets: Haar is simple and orthogonal; more complex wavelets might capture smoother anatomy but could introduce artifacts.

**Failure signatures**  
- Structural degradation (blurring/missing thin structures): LLL prior insufficient or ControlNet failed to learn modulation.  
- Persistent noise/artifacts: Injection too weak or at wrong resolution.  
- Over-smoothing/loss of details: Model over-relies on coarse LLL prior or backbone prior mismatch.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Ablation on wavelet frequency selection: Condition on LLL, HHH, all-high, all-low, all-bands. Expected: LLL yields best performance.  
2. Ablation on injection location: Encoder-side vs. decoder-side vs. both. Expected: Encoder-side injection best for anatomical structure.  
3. Generalization to unseen dose/scanner: Evaluate model trained on 1/20 dose on 1/50 and 1/4 dose data; if available, test on different scanner/vendor. Expected: Robust performance drop only at extreme noise (1/50).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How do alternative wavelet bases and multi-level decompositions impact WCC-Net's denoising performance and anatomical fidelity in whole-body PET? The study only evaluates single-level Haar; future work will investigate more flexible wavelet representations.
2. Does WCC-Net generalize robustly across multi-vendor PET scanners, imaging sites, and different radiotracers beyond the single-scanner, 18F-FDG dataset? Evaluation was limited to one dataset; extension to multi-vendor and multi-site datasets is needed.
3. Does WCC-Net improve clinical decision-making as measured by radiologist assessment and task-based performance (e.g., lesion detection)? The study relies solely on quantitative image metrics; clinical validation is required.

## Limitations
- Architecture and hyperparameter details are insufficient for exact replication.
- Evaluation is limited to one scanner and tracer; cross-scanner/tracer generalizability is unknown.
- Lack of direct ablation comparing frozen backbone to full fine-tuning.

## Confidence
- **WCC-Net outperforms baselines**: Medium – statistical gains on one dataset, but reproducibility and external validity are open.
- **Low-frequency wavelet prior guides denoising**: Medium – supported by internal ablations, but no independent verification.
- **Frozen backbone preserves generative prior**: Medium – design rationale sound, but no empirical comparison to full fine-tuning.

## Next Checks
1. Conduct external validation on an independent multi-vendor, multi-tracer low-dose PET cohort to assess generalizability.
2. Systematically compare wavelet bases (Haar, Daubechies) and decomposition levels (single vs. multi-level) to determine optimal design.
3. Perform a direct ablation: train a variant with full backbone fine-tuning and compare to frozen-backbone WCC-Net in both metrics and structural fidelity.
