# Evaluating Disentangled Representations for Controllable Music Generation
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10058
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10058
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Systematic inconsistencies in disentangled representations: asymmetric leakage (timbre embeddings encode tempo; structure embeddings encode timbre) and a trade-off between informativeness and equivariance; current unsupervised strategies fail to achieve full disentanglement.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates whether music generation models truly separate musical structure (e.g., pitch) from timbre. Using a probing framework across four axes—informativeness, equivariance, invariance, and disentanglement—the study finds that existing models exhibit asymmetric leakage: timbre embeddings encode tempo information, while structure embeddings retain timbral cues. Moreover, a trade-off emerges: larger embeddings capture more information but become less transformation-consistent. Although adversarial losses and data augmentations improve invariance and separation, they fall short of producing fully disentangled embeddings, raising concerns about their suitability for precise controllable generation.

## Method Summary
The authors train three music representation models (SS-VQ-VAE, TS-DSAE, AFTER) and two AFTER ablations on the Slakh2100 dataset. They then evaluate the learned representations using a probing framework. For each model, they extract separate timbre and structure embeddings from frozen encoders. They train shallow supervised probes (linear classifiers or small MLPs) on these embeddings to predict musical attributes (instrument class, pitch, chord, tempo). To assess transformation consistency, they apply controlled audio transformations (instrument change, pitch shift, time stretch) and measure equivariance (predicting transformation parameters or transformed embeddings) and invariance (embedding similarity). Disentanglement is quantified by the performance drop when probes are trained on concatenated timbre+structure embeddings versus the intended single embedding. The evaluation datasets include synthetic subsets from SynTheory for clean factor control, and MAESTRO/MusicNet for pitch estimation.

## Key Results
- Asymmetric leakage: timbre embeddings systematically encode tempo; structure embeddings retain timbral information.
- Trade-off: larger embedding dimensions increase informativeness but reduce equivariance (transformation consistency).
- Current disentanglement strategies (data augmentation, adversarial losses) improve invariance and separation metrics but do not eliminate cross-factor leakage, limiting precise controllable generation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Probing-Based Informativeness Measurement**  
A linear probe (or shallow MLP) is trained on frozen embeddings to predict a target musical property. High probe accuracy indicates that the embedding contains linearly accessible information about that factor. Core assumption: if a representation disentangles a factor (e.g., timbre), a simple probe should extract it efficiently from the corresponding embedding. Break condition: if probe performance is at chance for a task associated with an embedding, the embedding is not informative for that factor.

**Mechanism 2: Transformation Consistency via Equivariance & Invariance**  
Controlled transformations are applied to input audio. Equivariance measures whether changes in the embedding correspond predictably to transformation parameters (P-equivariance) or to the embedding of the transformed input (R-equivariance). Invariance measures embedding similarity under perturbations that should not affect a given factor. Core assumption: a disentangled timbre embedding should be invariant to pitch/time changes and equivariant to instrument changes; structure embeddings should show the opposite. Break condition: if a timbre embedding is sensitive to pitch shifts (low invariance) or a structure embedding changes unpredictably under pitch shifts (low equivariance), separation is inconsistent.

**Mechanism 3: Disentanglement via Complementary Embedding Leakage**  
For a task tied to embedding *z_t* (e.g., tempo from timbre), the probe accuracy on *z_t* alone is compared to accuracy on the concatenation *z_t* ⊕ *z_s*. A large improvement (Δ) when using both embeddings indicates that *z_s* carries relevant information, i.e., entanglement. Core assumption: in a perfectly disentangled system, adding the other embedding should not improve accuracy. Break condition: consistently large Δ across tasks, especially for unexpected factors (e.g., tempo in timbre), indicates failure.

## Foundational Learning
- **Representation Disentanglement**: The goal that each latent dimension/group corresponds to a single generative factor. *Why needed*: To interpret whether the model's embeddings truly separate musical attributes like structure and timbre. *Quick check*: If representation is perfectly disentangled, linearly intervening on the "pitch" latent variable during generation should only change pitch, leaving timbre unaffected.
- **Probing as a Diagnostic Tool**: Using simple supervised models to extract information from frozen representations. *Why needed*: The entire evaluation relies on probe performance to infer what factors are encoded. *Quick check*: What is a key limitation of using a linear probe to assess informativeness? (Answer: It may underestimate non-linearly encoded information.)
- **Musical Factors of Variation**: Understanding audio attributes like pitch, tempo, timbre, and how they can be independently manipulated. *Why needed*: To map transformations and tasks to specific musical semantics. *Quick check*: How would you operationally define a "pitch shift" transformation that isolates pitch from timbre and tempo? (Answer: Use a synthesis-based transformation that changes only fundamental frequencies while preserving spectral envelope and duration.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Audio -> Frozen Music Encoder -> (z_timbre, z_structure)  
Audio -> Transformation Module -> Transformed Audio -> Frozen Music Encoder -> (z'_timbre, z'_structure)  
(z_timbre, z_structure, z'_timbre, z'_structure) -> Probe Suite -> Informativeness/Equivariance/Invariance scores  
(z_timbre, z_structure) -> Disentanglement Metric Calculator -> Disentanglement scores

**Critical Path**  
1. Load frozen model and datasets (Slakh2100, SynTheory, MAESTRO, MusicNet).  
2. For each audio sample, extract original embeddings (z_timbre, z_structure).  
3. **Informativeness**: Train and evaluate each probe on the corresponding embedding pool (e.g., instrument classifier on z_timbre).  
4. **Equivariance/Invariance**: Apply transformations (instrument change, pitch shift, time stretch) to audio, extract transformed embeddings, compute metrics (MSE, cosine similarity).  
5. **Disentanglement**: For each task, train a probe on z_target, then on concatenated z_target ⊕ z_other; compute performance difference Δ.  
6. Repeat for all model variants (SS-VQ-VAE, TS-DSAE, AFTER, AFTER-no-aug, AFTER-no-adv).

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- **Probe capacity**: Linear probes are consistent but may miss non-linear information; deeper probes risk conflating representation quality with probe learning.  
- **Transformation purity**: Synthetic transformations (SynTheory) isolate factors cleanly but may not reflect real-world music where factors are entangled.  
- **Embedding size**: Larger embeddings naturally capture more information (higher informativeness) but observed to be less equivariant—possibly due to optimization pressures or capacity constraints.

**Failure Signatures**  
- **High mutual information**: Large Δ values, especially `timbre→tempo` leakage, indicate cross-factor encoding.  
- **Asymmetric leakage**: Structure embeddings show some timbral information (e.g., non-zero instrument classification accuracy) while timbre embeddings show structural information.  
- **Task-specific collapse**: Very low performance on fine-grained tasks (e.g., multi-pitch estimation F1 < 0.3) despite high reconstruction quality, suggesting structure information is not linearly accessible.

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. **Baseline Informativeness Replication**: Train the standard probes on each model’s default embeddings to reproduce informativeness scores and verify the capacity-equivariance trade-off (e.g., SS-VQ-VAE high instrument accuracy but poor pitch equivariance).  
2. **Isolate Augmentation Effect**: Compare AFTER-no-aug (no pitch/time augmentations) vs AFTER. Hypothesis: Removing augmentations reduces timbre invariance to pitch/time and increases structural leakage into timbre.  
3. **Test Transformation Specificity**: For the best model on equivariance (e.g., TS-DSAE), apply out-of-distribution transformations (e.g., extreme tempo stretches) to probe robustness of equivariance claims.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Why do current unsupervised disentanglement strategies fail to achieve complete separation between structure and timbre?**  
   The paper identifies persistent leakage (e.g., timbre embeddings encoding tempo) but does not analyze root causes within architectures or objectives. The asymmetry suggests hidden dependencies not addressed by augmentations or adversarial losses. Evidence needed: Ablations tracing information flow or experiments with perfectly independent synthetic data to isolate whether leakage stems from data correlations or model biases.

2. **Should controllable music generation evaluation prioritize output-level perceptual metrics over latent space probing?**  
   Representation-level properties (invariance, equivariance) may not translate to audible control; decoders could blur factor separation during synthesis, but this is untested. Evidence needed: Listening studies where musicians manipulate attributes (e.g., “increase tempo”) and rate output accuracy and artifact presence, comparing models with varying latent disentanglement scores.

3. **Is the trade-off between embedding informativeness and equivariance an inherent limitation or can architectural innovations reconcile them?**  
   The correlation is observed but not explained; it may reflect fundamental information-theoretic constraints or be specific to the studied music models. Evidence needed: Systematic experiments across diverse architectures (e.g., transformers vs. CNNs) measuring both axes while controlling for capacity; theoretical analysis of equivariance constraints in autoencoder objectives.

## Limitations
- **Probe capacity assumption**: Linear probes may underestimate information encoded non-linearly, affecting claims about absolute informativeness.
- **Transformation purity**: Synthetic transformations may overstate leakage; real-world music has entangled factors and perceptual complexity.
- **Causal attribution**: The observed trade-off between informativeness and equivariance is correlational; it is unclear whether larger capacity directly harms equivariance or if both are symptoms of another factor.

## Confidence
- **Informativeness measurements (probe accuracy)**: Medium confidence. Shallow probes are standard but risk under-reporting non-linear information; however, relative comparisons within the same probe suite are reliable.
- **Leakage patterns (Δ scores)**: Medium confidence. Dependent on transformation purity; synthetic datasets control factors well, but generalization to natural music is uncertain.
- **Trade-off causality**: Low confidence. The correlation between embedding size and equivariance is not experimentally established as causal; it may be confounded by model architecture or optimization.

## Next Checks
1. **Non-linear probe suite**: Re-run informativeness axis using deeper MLP probes (with controlled capacity) to test whether structure embeddings contain more note information than linear probes suggest.
2. **In-the-wild evaluation**: Apply the full probing suite to embeddings from real Slakh2100 test stems (with noisy but authentic labels) to see if asymmetric leakage diminishes.
3. **Direct controllability intervention**: For the top disentanglement-scoring model, perform latent interventions: mix timbre from one piece with structure from another, generate audio, and evaluate via objective metrics (pitch accuracy, timbre similarity) and listening tests to verify factor separation holds through decoding.
