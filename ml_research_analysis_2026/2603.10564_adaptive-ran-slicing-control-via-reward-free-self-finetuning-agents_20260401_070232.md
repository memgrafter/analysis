# Adaptive RAN Slicing Control via Reward-Free Self-Finetuning Agents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10564
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10564
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Achieves state-of-the-art performance on dynamic RAN slicing with spectral efficiency 5.354, 28.4% lower reconfiguration times than Reflexion, and 59% lower than PPO using only a single interaction trajectory.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a self-finetuning framework for Large Language Model (LLM) agents to tackle continuous control tasks like dynamic RAN slicing, where finite context windows and absence of explicit reward signals impede sustained learning. The core is an Actor-Reflector architecture that generates bi-perspective linguistic feedback (step-level and trajectory-level) to construct preference datasets without handcrafted rewards. These datasets drive a Refine-from-Reflection (RfR) process and Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO) fine-tuning, enabling the agent to internalize long-horizon experiences directly into its parameters. On a multi-objective RAN slicing task, the method significantly outperforms standard RL and LLM-based baselines in sample efficiency, stability, and balanced optimization of spectral efficiency, reconfiguration overhead, and quality-of-service violations.

## Method Summary
The framework centers on two LLMs: an Actor that interacts with a RAN slicing simulator, outputting actions alongside per-step reflections, and a Reflector that performs offline trajectory analysis to label each step’s action as effective or suboptimal and propose corrections. The RfR module amplifies learning signals by sampling multiple rollout actions for suboptimal steps; matches with Reflector’s suggestions become positive preference pairs. The Actor is then fine-tuned via KTO—a variant of Direct Preference Optimization—using this augmented dataset, shifting its policy toward preferred actions. This loop (interact → reflect → finetune) repeats across trajectories, enabling continuous improvement from minimal environment interactions without scalar rewards.

## Key Results
- Spectral efficiency of 5.354, the highest among evaluated methods.
- Total reconfiguration times reduced by 28.4% versus Reflexion and 59% versus PPO (21.091 total).
- Maintains PQoS violations at levels comparable to the best baseline, demonstrating effective multi-objective trade-offs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Distilling Experiences via Parameter Updates
- **Claim:** Continuous improvement by distilling long-horizon interaction experiences into model parameters via fine-tuning, bypassing context window limits.
- **Mechanism:** Reflector analyzes full trajectories to generate preference labels (ℓ_t) and corrective actions (â_t). RfR builds a dataset from these labels and refine-rollout samples. KTO fine-tuning updates Actor weights to internalize insights, rather than storing them in prompts.
- **Core assumption:** Trajectory-level linguistic reflections can be translated into a preference optimization objective (KTO) that durably modifies the policy's generative distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract emphasizes learning via parameter distillation; Section IV.A formalizes Reflective MDP; contrast with prompt-based neighbors (e.g., Prompt-Tuned LLM-Augmented DRL) highlights context-bound limitation.
- **Break condition:** If KTO yields no measurable KL divergence from the reference model, the internalization mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2: Bi-Perspective Reflection for Supervision
- **Claim:** Combining step-level (in-context) and trajectory-level (offline) reflection generates high-quality, consistent supervision for continuous control without environment rewards.
- **Mechanism:** Actor produces immediate reflections ψ_t and analyses ϕ_t during interaction. Separately, Reflector processes the full trajectory post-hoc to assign final quality labels ℓ_t and propose improvements â_t, grounding step-level feedback in global outcomes.
- **Core assumption:** A dedicated Reflector with full history enables more accurate action evaluations than step-level self-reflection alone, especially for multi-objective trade-offs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes "bi-perspective reflection mechanism"; Section IV.B details Actor step-reflection and Reflector trajectory-scoring; architectural novelty implied by absent counterparts in neighboring papers (Reflexion, ExpeL).
- **Break condition:** Low inter-rater reliability between Reflector labels and human judgments, or systematic conflicts with step-level reflections, indicate synthesis failure.

### Mechanism 3: Efficient Single-Trajectory Learning via RfR-KTO Loop
- **Claim:** RfR data augmentation and iterative KTO fine-tuning extract maximal learning from one interaction trajectory.
- **Mechanism:** For steps labeled suboptimal (ℓ_t=False), RfR samples m alternative actions from the policy on the same prompt. Rollouts matching Reflector’s â_t become positive preference pairs, enriching the dataset without new environment interactions. KTO’s asymmetric loss (λ_p, λ_n) then shifts policy toward these rare positives over multiple iterations.
- **Core assumption:** The policy’s generative distribution contains correct actions for suboptimal states but requires reinforcement; KTO’s prospect-theoretic loss can reliably shift the preference boundary.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section IV.C describe RfR; Figure 3 shows convergence after six KTO iterations on one trajectory; neighbors use standard offline RL, not iterative self-augmentation.
- **Break condition:** Probability P(â_t | I_t) does not increase across KTO iterations on validation prompts from same trajectory.

1. **Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) & Policy Optimization**
   - **Why needed:** RAN slicing is sequential decision-making; the paper’s Reflective MDP replaces scalar reward R with language-derived feedback. Understanding policy π(a|s) and objectives (e.g., Actor-Critic loss) is fundamental.

2. **Preference-Based Policy Optimization (DPO, KTO)**
   - **Why needed:** Core learning update derives from a preference dataset (chosen/rejected pairs) built by Reflector, not environmental rewards. KTO, a DPO variant, handles unbalanced data via asymmetric loss coefficients.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Actor (LLM) → Reflector (LLM) → Environment (RAN simulator) → RfR Module → KTO Fine-Tuner → back to Actor loop

**Critical path:**  
1. Actor interacts with Environment, appending (s_t, a_t, ψ_t, ϕ_t, M_t) to history H.  
2. After trajectory, Reflector processes full H to label steps (ℓ_t, â_t), creating H'.  
3. RfR constructs preference dataset D from H' (base pairs + refine-rollout pairs).  
4. KTO updates Actor policy π ← π' using D.  
5. Updated policy interacts again.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Actor vs. Reflector model size: Stronger Reflector (DeepSeek-R1) improves analysis but increases inference cost; using same model (Qwen3-4B) saves cost but may limit reflection depth.  
- Rollout count (m) vs. overfitting: Higher m increases candidate diversity per suboptimal step but excessive rollouts risk overfitting to specific state distributions.  
- Threshold (ρ) for skipping rollouts: Prevents wasteful computation when policy already generates â_t with high probability; set too high may halt recovery on recoverable mistakes.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Reflector inconsistency: High variance in ℓ_t/â_t for same step across trajectories or upon re-analysis.  
- KTO non-convergence: Chosen/rejected reward curves do not separate; loss oscillates without improvement.  
- Policy collapse: Post-finetuning policy outputs become degenerate (e.g., fixed PRB allocation) or invalid actions.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Reflection Quality Audit:** Manually label 100 steps from initial trajectories as good/bad; compare against Reflector’s ℓ_t to compute accuracy/F1, establishing baseline supervision quality.  
2. **Ablation: Rollout Efficacy:** Run RfR with m=0 (no rollouts) vs. m=5 vs. m=10 on same initial trajectory; measure policy improvement (utility change) after one KTO iteration to quantify refine-rollout contribution.  
3. **Single-Trajectory Learning Curve:** Execute full loop repeatedly on a single initial trajectory for up to 10 KTO iterations; plot utility vs. iteration to replicate convergence pattern and confirm sample efficiency claim.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Inference latency for real-time deployment:** The paper acknowledges that LLM inference speed hinders meeting the 100 ms decision cycle of RAN slicing. No empirical analysis of feasibility under real-time constraints or evaluation of proposed optimizations (distillation, quantization) is provided.
2. **Robustness to Reflector inaccuracies:** The framework assumes Reflector outputs are reliable preference labels. Sensitivity to Reflector errors (hallucinations, biases) is unexplored, especially in complex, partially observable environments.
3. **Catastrophic forgetting across trajectories:** Only single-trajectory learning is demonstrated. It is unclear whether iterative fine-tuning on new trajectories overwrites previously learned behaviors, a known issue in LLM fine-tuning.
4. **Relative contribution of reflection perspectives:** The paper compares full framework against Reflexion (step-level only) but does not isolate the unique value of trajectory-level analysis via ablations (e.g., removing step-level ψ or Reflector scoring).

## Limitations
- Missing critical implementation details (prompt structures, Reflector labeling heuristics, KTO hyperparameters, rollout count m, threshold ρ) hinder exact replication.
- Reflector label quality not validated against human expertise; risk of propagating LLM biases into policy updates.
- Generalization to other network scenarios or simulators not evaluated; single-trajectory focus limits claims of sustained continuous learning.
- Potential overfitting to a single trajectory not addressed; no tests over sequential trajectories.

## Confidence
- Overall performance superiority (outperforms RL and Reflexion baselines): **Medium**—supported by simulation but replication blocked by missing details.
- Bi-perspective reflection novelty and contribution: **Low to Medium**—theoretically sound but lacks direct ablation; indirect evidence from beating Reflexion.
- Single-trajectory RfR learning efficiency: **Medium**—convergence shown but no comparison to alternative augmentation strategies or analysis of forgetting.

## Next Checks
1. **Exact replication of core experiment:** Implement AR with disclosed components and hyperparameter search to recover reported metrics (SE≈5.354, reconfiguration time reductions).
2. **Ablation of reflection perspectives:** Compare full AR against variants: (a) Actor only (no Reflector), (b) Reflector only (no step-level ψ), (c) step-level only (no trajectory scoring). Quantify impact on sample efficiency and stability.
3. **Reflector label reliability audit:** Sample 100 steps; have domain experts label ℓ_t. Compute agreement metrics (e.g., Cohen’s κ); if κ < 0.6, iterate on Reflector prompt to include explicit criteria.
