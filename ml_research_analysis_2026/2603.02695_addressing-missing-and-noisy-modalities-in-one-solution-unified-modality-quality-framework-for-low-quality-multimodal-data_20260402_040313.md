# Addressing Missing and Noisy Modalities in One Solution: Unified Modality-Quality Framework for Low-quality Multimodal Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02695
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02695
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** UMQ achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple datasets, e.g., 49.7% Acc7 and 90.1% Acc2 on CMU-MOSI, under complete, missing, and noisy modality settings.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the joint problem of missing and noisy modalities in multimodal affective computing, which degrades model robustness in real-world scenarios. It proposes the Unified Modality-Quality (UMQ) framework that unifies these issues by treating both as a low-quality modality problem. UMQ comprises three core components: a quality estimator trained via a rank-guided strategy to assess modality quality without absolute labels; a quality enhancer that leverages modality-specific baselines and cross-modal information to improve unimodal representations; and a quality-aware mixture-of-experts (MQ-MoE) module that dynamically routes inputs to specialized experts based on modality-quality configurations. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets (CMU-MOSI, CMU-MOSEI, CH-SIMS, UR-FUNNY, MUStARD) demonstrate that UMQ consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods across complete, missing, and noisy settings, with notable improvements on CMU-MOSI. Ablation studies confirm the importance of each component.

## Method Summary
UMQ formulates missing and noisy modalities as instances of low-quality input and processes them through a unified pipeline. First, unimodal networks extract base representations for each modality. A quality estimator, trained using a rank-guided loss that compares unimodal predictive losses and incorporates explicit labels for extreme quality cases (Gaussian noise as low-quality, low loss as high-quality), outputs a quality score α_m for each modality's representation. Next, a modality decoupler splits each unimodal representation into a sample-specific part x_s^m and a modality-specific part x_c^m. The modality-specific parts are aggregated across the batch (via moving average) to form a baseline representation x_b^m. The quality enhancer then combines the original representation, cross-modal sample-specific information (weighted by quality scores), and the modality baseline to produce an enhanced unimodal feature ̄x_m. All enhanced features are fused and passed through a predictor. To handle the diverse quality configurations, UMQ employs a mixture-of-experts (MQ-MoE) module where a gating network selects top-k experts from a pool of h experts based on a quality-aware vector. Three constraint losses (L_balance, L_sample, L_same) encourage expert specialization for specific modality-quality patterns and ensure similar configurations route to similar experts. The total loss combines the task prediction loss with losses for decoupling, quality estimation, enhancement, and MoE constraints.

## Key Results
- On CMU-MOSI, UMQ achieves 49.7% Acc7 and 90.1% Acc2, surpassing prior bests by up to 0.6% and 1.6% respectively, while reducing MAE to 0.630.
- UMQ consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods across multiple datasets (CMU-MOSEI, CH-SIMS, UR-FUNNY, MUStARD) under complete, missing, and noisy modality settings.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical roles of the quality estimator, rank-guided training, and MQ-MoE constraints.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Rank-guided training enables accurate quality estimation for modalities without relying on noisy absolute quality labels.
**Mechanism:** The quality estimator is trained by comparing the relative quality of different unimodal representations using a ranking loss (based on unimodal predictive losses) and explicit labels for extremes (Gaussian noise as low-quality, low predictive loss as high-quality). This avoids fitting to potentially inaccurate absolute scores.
**Core assumption:** Relative quality comparisons are more robust and feasible to annotate than absolute quality values in real-world low-quality scenarios.
**Break condition:** If the unimodal predictive losses do not correlate well with true quality (e.g., due to poor unimodal models), the ranking constraints may misguide the estimator, leading to inaccurate quality scores.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Quality enhancement via decoupled modality-specific and sample-specific information improves unimodal representations while preserving modality-specific details.
**Mechanism:** A decoupling operation splits each unimodal representation into a sample-specific part (x_s^m) and a modality-specific part (x_c^m). The modality-specific part is aggregated across batches to form a modality baseline (x_b^m). The enhancer then combines the original representation, cross-modal sample-specific info (weighted by estimated quality), and the baseline to generate enhanced features.
**Core assumption:** Modality-specific information is stable across samples and can be captured by a shared baseline, while sample-specific information from other modalities aids recovery without losing modality identity.
**Break condition:** If decoupling fails to separate noise (e.g., if x_c^m absorbs sample-specific noise), the baseline may become corrupted, degrading enhancement. Also, if cross-modal information is low-quality, it may introduce rather than remove noise.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** MQ-MoE dynamically routes samples to specialized experts based on modality-quality configurations, effectively handling the combinatorial explosion of missing/noisy cases.
**Mechanism:** For each sample, a gating network selects top-k experts from a pool of h experts. Constraints (L_balance, L_sample, L_same) ensure that experts specialize on specific modality-quality patterns (high/low per modality) and that similar configurations route to similar experts.
**Core assumption:** Distinct modality-quality combinations require different processing strategies, and expert specialization with quality-aware routing can address this within a unified framework.
**Break condition:** If the number of experts (h) is too small relative to the diversity of quality configurations, experts may become overspecialized or underutilized. Routing instability (e.g., high variance in gate values) can also prevent consistent expert assignment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Rank-based Supervised Learning:** Why needed here: Absolute quality labels are often unavailable or unreliable for modalities under noise or missingness; rank-based learning provides a feasible alternative by leveraging relative comparisons. Quick check question: How might the ranking loss be affected if unimodal predictive losses are not monotonic with modality quality?
- **Modality Decoupling for Noise Separation:** Why needed here: To extract clean modality-specific information from noisy inputs, which is essential for constructing reliable baselines and enhancing features without losing modality identity. Quick check question: What risks arise if the orthogonality constraint (L_or) is too strict or too lenient in the decoupling process?
- **Mixture of Experts for Conditional Computation:** Why needed here: The space of modality-quality configurations grows exponentially with modalities; MoE allows specialized processing per configuration while keeping a unified model. Quick check question: Why can't a single network with conditional computation (e.g., attention) substitute for MQ-MoE in this context?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Unimodal networks → Quality estimator → Modality decoupler → Modality baseline (moving average) → Quality enhancer → MQ-MoE (gating + experts) → Fusion network → Predictor.

**Critical path:** Input features are processed by unimodal networks to obtain base representations. Quality estimator predicts a quality score for each modality. The decoupler splits each unimodal representation into sample-specific and modality-specific parts; the modality-specific parts update the moving-average baseline. The enhancer fuses the original/unimodal representation, cross-modal sample-specific info (weighted by quality), and the baseline to produce enhanced features. MQ-MoE routes the enhanced features to selected experts based on quality-aware gating. The outputs of selected experts are fused and passed to the predictor for final task output. Quality scores influence both enhancement weighting and routing.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Complexity vs. robustness: More experts and higher top-k increase capacity to handle diverse quality patterns but raise computational cost and risk overfitting.
- Explicit quality signals: Rank-guided training adds supervision stability but requires careful hyperparameter tuning (e.g., margins, thresholds).
- Unified vs. specialized: Jointly handling missing and noisy modalities simplifies deployment but may not match state-of-the-art performance for each separated case.

**Failure signatures:**
- Quality estimator outputs near-constant scores across samples, indicating poor learning (check rank loss convergence).
- Decoupling yields x_c^m and x_s^m with high mutual information or poor reconstruction (monitor L_MI and L_re).
- MQ-MoE gate values are uniform across samples (low L_sample variance) or all samples use the same experts (violates L_same).
- Enhanced features visually (via t-SNE) do not cluster closer to high-quality originals compared to corrupted ones.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ablation of quality estimator:** Train without L_est components; expect significant drop in missing/noisy settings as enhancement and routing lose quality guidance.
2. **Visualization of enhanced features:** Use t-SNE on CMU-MOSI test set: plot original high-quality, corrupted, and UMQ-enhanced features; verify enhanced features overlap more with originals than corrupted ones.
3. **Missing modality robustness test:** Evaluate UMQ with missing rates 0.1-0.7 on CMU-MOSI; confirm consistent outperformance over baselines like GCNet as MR increases, validating unified handling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Can a single framework effectively and robustly address both missing and noisy modalities simultaneously in real-world multimodal affective computing?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly identify "the joint handling of noisy and missing modalities" as an open challenge and note that existing approaches address them separately.
- **Why unresolved:** UMQ unifies by treating missing modalities as a special case of noise (replacing with Gaussian noise), which may not capture the true distribution of missing data; the experimental evaluation considers missing and noisy regimes in isolation, not their co-occurrence.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments on datasets where both issues co-occur (e.g., some modalities missing while others are noisy), along with analysis of whether Gaussian-noise simulation adequately represents missingness.

**Open Question 2:** Is the rank-guided training strategy for the quality estimator free from bias, given that unimodal predictive loss may not reliably correlate with true modality quality?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The quality estimator is trained using a ranking loss based on unimodal predictive loss, under the assumption that lower predictive loss indicates higher modality quality.
- **Why unresolved:** Unimodal predictive loss can be influenced by factors unrelated to modality fidelity, such as model overfitting, task complexity, or the model’s adaptation to noise, potentially leading to inaccurate quality rankings.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled studies with synthetic modalities of known quality, measuring the alignment between estimated ranks and true ranks across diverse degradation types; case studies of ranking failures.

**Open Question 3:** Does the MQ-MoE routing mechanism scale effectively to a larger number of modalities while preserving expert specialization for each quality configuration?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper notes the exponential number of quality configurations (2^|M|) and employs a fixed number of experts (10–12) with constraints, but only evaluates up to four modalities.
- **Why unresolved:** As the number of modalities grows, the number of configurations quickly exceeds the number of experts, likely causing multiple configurations to share experts and reducing specialization; the binary quality threshold may also be insufficient for nuanced quality distinctions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Metrics of expert specialization (e.g., configuration-expert mapping entropy) and performance as the modality count increases, either by adding modalities to existing datasets or using synthetic data; experiments with varying expert counts.

## Limitations
- Incomplete specification of key components (Decouple/Couple/Enhancer/Experts) and rank-guided training details (batch construction, noise parameters) hinders exact replication.
- No public codebase is provided, increasing risk of implementation deviations and reducing reproducibility.
- Evaluation is limited to isolated missing and noisy settings; co-occurrence of both issues remains untested.
