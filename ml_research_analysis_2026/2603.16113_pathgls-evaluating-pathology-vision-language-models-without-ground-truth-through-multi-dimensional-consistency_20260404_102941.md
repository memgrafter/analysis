# PathGLS: Evaluating Pathology Vision-Language Models without Ground Truth through Multi-Dimensional Consistency

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.16113
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16113
*   **Reference Count:** 13
*   **Primary Result:** PathGLS detects hallucinations in pathology VLMs with a 40.2% performance drop on a perturbed dataset, achieving a Spearman's ρ=0.71 correlation with clinical expert error hierarchies, significantly outperforming reference-free baselines.

## Executive Summary
PathGLS is a novel, reference-free framework for evaluating the trustworthiness of pathology Vision-Language Models (VLMs). It addresses the critical gap of lacking reliable metrics to detect hallucinations without ground-truth reports. The framework quantifies trust via three orthogonal dimensions: **Grounding** (visual-text alignment at high resolution), **Logic** (consistency within a structured knowledge graph via Natural Language Inference), and **Stability** (robustness to adversarial visual and semantic perturbations). On the Quilt-1M dataset, PathGLS demonstrates a dramatic 40.2% score drop for hallucinated reports, compared to a negligible 2.1% drop for BERTScore. It also shows a strong ρ=0.71 correlation with expert-defined clinical error hierarchies, outperforming LLM-as-a-judge approaches. This provides a robust, interpretable criterion for clinical benchmarking and safety assurance.

## Method Summary
PathGLS computes a total trust score (*S<sub>total</sub>*) as a weighted sum of three sub-scores from parallel evaluation modules applied to a generated VLM report and its input image. **Grounding** uses a Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) approach: patch embeddings from a high-resolution vision encoder and text entity embeddings are compared in a similarity matrix; a spatial `argmax` identifies the most relevant patch per entity, and the mean of these max scores yields *S<sub>g</sub>*. **Logic** parses the report into a knowledge graph, extracts premise-hypothesis pairs (e.g., descriptive findings vs. diagnosis), and uses a domain-specific NLI model to score contradictions; the top-K mean contradiction probability is subtracted from 1 to get *S<sub>ℓ</sub>*. **Stability** measures output consistency under two perturbations: Macenko stain normalization (visual) and an adversarial clinical prompt (semantic). Semantic distance between original and perturbed reports is averaged and subtracted from 1 to yield *S<sub>s</sub>*. The final score is *S<sub>total</sub>* = 0.4**S<sub>g</sub>** + 0.3**S<sub>ℓ</sub>** + 0.3**S<sub>s</sub>**.

## Key Results
*   PathGLS exhibits a **40.2%** performance drop on reports with visual hallucinations (Quilt-1M), whereas BERTScore drops by only **2.1%**, demonstrating superior sensitivity to grounding errors.
*   Against expert-defined clinical error hierarchies, PathGLS achieves a **Spearman's ρ = 0.71** (p < 0.0001), significantly outperforming LLM-as-a-judge baselines like Gemini 1.5 Pro (ρ = 0.39).
*   The framework serves as an effective gatekeeper, showing a significant score drop (**Δ > 0.06**) on out-of-distribution data (REG2025) while reference-free text metrics remain high.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Grounding via Spatial MIL
*   **Claim:** Detects fine-grained visual-text alignment failures (e.g., reporting cells not present) by enforcing that every clinical entity in the report must correspond to a specific high-resolution image patch.
*   **Mechanism:** The slide is tessellated into patches. A vision encoder produces patch embeddings *v<sub>i</sub>*, and a text encoder produces embeddings *t<sub>j</sub>* for extracted report entities. An M×N similarity matrix is computed. A spatial `argmax` per entity column identifies the single most relevant patch. *S<sub>g</sub>* is the mean of these maximum similarity scores. A hallucinated entity will have low similarity to all patches.
*   **Core Assumption:** Clinically relevant entities should be visually justifiable in at least one specific patch. Diffuse averaging is insufficient; a one-to-one spatial link is required.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...Grounding (high-resolution visual-text alignment via multiple instance learning)..."
    *   [section 3.2] "The grounding score *S<sub>g</sub>* is derived by applying a spatial argmax to identify the most relevant patch for each text entity..."
*   **Break Condition:** Fails if the vision encoder's patch features lack semantic discrimination for clinical entities, or if the `argmax` selects a visually similar but clinically irrelevant patch (e.g., general tissue texture instead of a specific cell type).

### Mechanism 2: Logic via NLI on a Knowledge Graph
*   **Claim:** Detects reasoning errors and contradictions (e.g., diagnosing malignancy while describing only benign features) by checking entailment within a structured representation of the report.
*   **Mechanism:** The report is parsed into a knowledge graph (nodes=entities, edges=relations). Premise-hypothesis pairs are extracted, typically with descriptive findings as premises and the final diagnosis as the hypothesis. A domain-specific NLI model assigns a contradiction probability *p* to each pair. The top-K highest *p* values are averaged. *S<sub>ℓ</sub>* = 1 - mean(top-K *p*), ensuring severe logical breaks are not diluted by many correct statements.
*   **Core Assumption:** A clinically valid report has a logically consistent structure where the diagnosis is entailed by the descriptive findings. A hallucination or error creates a non-entailed (contradictory) pair.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...Logic (consistency checking with a natural language inference model on a structured knowledge graph)..."
    *   [section 3.3] "We extract premise-hypothesis pairs from this graph... apply a top-K mean aggregation mechanism."
*   **Break Condition:** Fails if the knowledge graph construction is error-prone (incorrect entity/relation extraction), if the NLI model is not robust to medical jargon, or if the report's logic is non-linear (e.g., differential diagnoses not captured by simple premise-hypothesis pairs).

### Mechanism 3: Stability via Dual Perturbations
*   **Claim:** Detects models brittle to common clinical distribution shifts (stain variation, leading questions) by measuring output inconsistency under controlled perturbations.
*   **Mechanism:** Two perturbations are applied: 1) **Visual**: Macenko stain normalization alters the color distribution of the entire slide. 2) **Semantic**: An adversarial prompt injects a false clinical history. The original VLM report *R<sub>orig</sub>* is compared to reports from perturbed inputs (*R<sub>aug</sub>*, *R<sub>attack</sub>*) using a semantic distance metric *Δ* (e.g., normalized embedding cosine). Stability score *S<sub>s</sub>* = 1 - mean(|Δ|). High consistency (low |Δ|) yields high *S<sub>s</sub>*.
*   **Core Assumption:** A trustworthy VLM should be robust to routine slide variations (stain) and resistant to subtle cognitive biases (misleading prompts). Sensitivity indicates fragility and potential for error under real-world conditions.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...Stability (robustness to adversarial visual and semantic perturbations)..."
    *   [section 3.4] "The stability score *S<sub>s</sub>* is defined by the semantic consistency between the report generated from the original input and those from the perturbed inputs."
*   **Break Condition:** Fails if perturbations are unrealistically strong (causing irrelevant outputs) or too weak (not stressing the model). Also fails if the semantic distance metric *Δ* is insensitive to clinically significant wording changes.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Multiple Instance Learning (MIL)**
    *   *Why needed:* PathGLS's Grounding uses MIL to handle whole-slide images (WSIs) as a bag of patches without pixel-level labels, aggregating patch-level alignments to a slide-level score.
    *   *Quick Check:* How does the `argmax` operation in the MIL pooling step enforce that each text entity must be grounded by a specific patch, rather than being satisfied by a diffuse average of many patches?
*   **Concept: Natural Language Inference (NLI)**
    *   *Why needed:* The Logic module requires a model that can judge if a descriptive statement (premise) entails a diagnostic conclusion (hypothesis) or contradicts it, which is the core NLI task.
    *   *Quick Check:* Why is a standard NLI model (trained on general corpora) insufficient, and what does "domain-specific" imply about the NLI model used in PathGLS?
*   **Concept: Adversarial Robustness Evaluation**
    *   *Why needed:* Stability assesses whether a model's output is invariant to meaningful changes in input (stain) or context (prompt), serving as a proxy for reliability under real-world variability.
    *   *Quick Check:* What is the key difference between the *visual* (Macenko) and *semantic* (adversarial prompt) perturbations in terms of what specific clinical failure mode each is designed to expose?

## Architecture Onboarding
1.  **Input:** Patch/WSI image + Generated VLM report.
2.  **Grounding Module:** Vision encoder (HighRes-PLIP) → Patch features *v<sub>i</sub>*. Text entity encoder → *t<sub>j</sub>*. Compute similarity matrix. Apply spatial `argmax` per entity, mean of max scores → *S<sub>g</sub>*.
3.  **Logic Module:** Report parser → Knowledge graph. Extract premise-hypothesis pairs. Domain NLI model (DeBERTa-v3) → contradiction probabilities *p*. Top-K mean → *S<sub>ℓ</sub>*.
4.  **Stability Module:** Input processor applies Macenko stain & adversarial prompt. Generate two perturbed reports. Semantic encoder computes |Δ(*R<sub>orig</sub>, R<sub>aug</sub>)| and |Δ(*R<sub>orig</sub>, R<sub>attack</sub>)|. Mean → *S<sub>s</sub>*.
5.  **Fusion:** *S<sub>total</sub>* = *S<sub>g</sub>*×0.4 + *S<sub>ℓ</sub>*×0.3 + *S<sub>s</sub>*×0.3. Gatekeeper: *S<sub>total</sub>* threshold for accept/review/reject.

**Critical Path:** Report generation (external VLM) → Parallel evaluation (Grounding, Logic, Stability) → Score fusion → Decision. Grounding and Logic paths are sequential on the same report; Stability requires re-generation from perturbed inputs.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   *Resolution vs. Compute:* Higher patch resolution improves grounding detail but exponentially increases MIL computation (paper uses 512×512 patches at 20×).
*   *Graph Complexity vs. Recall:* A richer knowledge graph captures more logic but may introduce parsing errors. Simpler graphs are more robust but may miss subtle reasoning chains.
*   *Perturbation Strength:* Must be strong enough to induce change but not so strong as to cause nonsensical outputs that invalidate the consistency check.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   *S<sub>g</sub>* near 0 for a coherent report: Vision encoder failure or patch segmentation error.
*   *S<sub>ℓ</sub>* low but *S<sub>g</sub>* high: Logical contradiction despite visual accuracy (e.g., "benign morphology → metastatic cancer").
*   *S<sub>s</sub>* low while *S<sub>g</sub>*/*S<sub>ℓ</sub>* high: Model is factually correct but brittle (e.g., stain-sensitive), unsafe for multi-center deployment.
*   High variance in *S<sub>ℓ</sub>* across similar reports: Unstable NLI scoring or graph extraction.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Replicate Sensitivity Analysis (Table 1):** On Quilt-1M perturbed dataset, confirm PathGLS's *S<sub>g</sub>* drops ~40% for visual hallucinations vs. BERTScore's ~2% drop.
2.  **Benchmark Reproducibility (Table 2):** Run at least one model (e.g., Quilt-LLaVA) on Quilt-1M (patch) and TCGA (WSI). Verify the reported trend: WSI evaluation increases score, but Logic score may drop for domain-general models.
3.  **Domain Gap Test (Table 3):** Evaluate a model (e.g., LLaVA) on REG2025 (private) vs. its public training set. Confirm PathGLS shows a significant drop (Δ>0.06) while BERTScore remains high.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** To what extent does PathGLS generalize to truly unseen clinical environments with extreme domain shifts, such as different scanning protocols, staining techniques, or rare disease subtypes not represented in Quilt-1M and TCGA?
    *   *Basis:* [inferred] The paper evaluates on REG2025 and TCGA-Sarcoma as OOD proxies, but real-world clinical data may have more complex, unanticipated distribution shifts.
*   **Open Question 2:** How does the design and completeness of the structured knowledge graph in the Logic module affect the detection of logical hallucinations, especially for complex, multi-step diagnostic reasoning?
    *   *Basis:* [explicit] Section 3.3 states a "structured knowledge graph" is used but provides no details on graph construction or coverage of pathological relations.
*   **Open Question 3:** Are the dual adversarial perturbations (stain and semantic) in the Stability module sufficient to capture all relevant forms of model instability under real-world distribution shifts, or are there critical perturbation types missing?
    *   *Basis:* [explicit] Section 3.4 defines Stability using Macenko and adversarial prompt, but only these two are evaluated.
*   **Open Question 4:** Can the fixed weighting scheme (wg=0.4, wl=0.3, ws=0.3) be improved by adapting weights to specific pathology subspecialties or model architectures, and does this enhance alignment with expert error judgments?
    *   *Basis:* [explicit] Section 4.1 states weights are determined via grid search on a hold-out validation set, implying a one-size-fits-all approach.

## Limitations
*   **Unknown Implementation Details:** Critical components like clinical entity extraction, knowledge graph schema, premise-hypothesis extraction rules, semantic distance metric `Δ`, and adversarial prompt templates are not specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
*   **Potential Model Choice Dependencies:** The performance of grounding and logic modules is tightly coupled to the choice of vision encoder (HighRes-PLIP) and NLI model (DeBERTa-v3). Unclear if the NLI model is pathology-fine-tuned; using a base model may underperform.
*   **OOD Validation Scope:** Evaluation on OOD datasets (REG2025, TCGA-Sarcoma) provides a proxy for generalization, but real-world clinical deployment involves private, heterogeneous data with potentially unanticipated shifts that may challenge the Stability module's perturbation design.

## Confidence
*   **High:** Core claim that PathGLS detects hallucinations better than reference-free baselines (BERTScore, LLM-judge) is supported by reported Quilt-1M sensitivity analysis (40.2% drop) and strong expert correlation (ρ=0.71).
*   **Medium:** Logic module's top-K NLI aggregation likely captures basic clinical reasoning, but its sensitivity is highly dependent on the opaque knowledge graph construction, which is a hidden variable.
*   **Low:** Full reproducibility of all three modules, especially entity/graph parsing and perturbation design, due to missing implementation details. The exact behavior of the Grounding module's `argmax` on real WSIs and the Stability module's sensitivity to perturbation strength are uncertain without further specification.
