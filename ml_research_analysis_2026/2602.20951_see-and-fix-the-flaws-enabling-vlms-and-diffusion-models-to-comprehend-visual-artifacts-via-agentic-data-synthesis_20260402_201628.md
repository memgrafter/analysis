# See and Fix the Flaws: Enabling VLMs and Diffusion Models to Comprehend Visual Artifacts via Agentic Data Synthesis
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.20951  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20951  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: ArtiAgent generates 100K artifact‑injected image pairs; fine‑tuning Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B boosts artifact detection accuracy from 50.1% to 62.7% on ArtiBench, surpassing GPT‑5 (59.9%).

## Executive Summary
Structural artifacts in AI‑generated images—such as duplicated objects, missing elements, or geometric distortions—remain a significant challenge for vision‑language models (VLMs). This paper introduces **ArtiAgent**, an agentic framework that automatically synthesizes 100 K realistic artifact‑injected image pairs with rich annotations (binary labels, bounding boxes, and natural‑language explanations). Three coordinated agents drive the pipeline: a **perception agent** decomposes images into entities and subentities, a **synthesis agent** injects artifacts via a novel **inversion‑injection** technique within diffusion transformers, and a **curation agent** filters and explains the results. Fine‑tuning open‑source VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B) on this data dramatically improves artifact understanding across multiple benchmarks, including the new **ArtiBench**, where accuracy rises from 50.1% to 62.7% and outperforms proprietary models like GPT‑5. Moreover, the artifact‑aware VLM enables downstream applications—reward‐guided diffusion sampling for artifact‑free generation and iterative VLM‑guided inpainting to correct artifacts—demonstrating the practical utility of synthetic supervision.

## Method Summary
ArtiAgent’s data synthesis pipeline begins with a clean image from source datasets (COCO, Caltech‑101, etc.). The **perception agent** (GPT‑4o + Grounded‑SAM) extracts entities and subentities, generating segmentation masks and assigning subentities to parents via containment analysis. The **synthesis agent** selects one of four artifact operations (add, remove, distort, fuse) and computes a target‑reference patch mapping. It then executes **inversion‑injection** using a DiT diffusion model (FLUX.1‑dev): the clean image is inverted to a latent noise, and during denoising the positional embeddings (RoPE) and value embeddings of target patches are replaced with those of reference patches, restricted to early‑to‑middle steps (e.g., 15 steps for values) to preserve global coherence. The **curation agent** applies LPIPS (for distortion) or VLM‑based filtering to ensure plausibility and generates both local and global textual explanations. Each curated pair is formatted as a VQA example (clean vs. artifact), building a dataset of 100 K samples. An open‑source VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B) is fine‑tuned in two stages: first freeze the vision encoder and train the LM and multimodal projector, then unfreeze the encoder with a lower learning rate. The resulting VLM excels at artifact detection, localization, and explanation, and can be deployed in downstream reward‑guided sampling or iterative VLM‑guided inpainting to remove artifacts.

## Key Results
- **ArtiBench detection:** Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B accuracy increases from 50.1% (vanilla) to 62.7% after ArtiAgent fine‑tuning, surpassing GPT‑5 (59.9%).
- **Localization & explanation:** Consistent gains across multiple benchmarks (RichHF, LOKI, SynthScars) in mIoU/F1 and natural language quality (ROUGE, CSS).
- **Downstream utility:** A reward model trained on ArtiAgent pairs guides diffusion sampling toward artifact‑free outputs; iterative VLM‑guided inpainting removes artifacts in as few as three iterations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Agentic Pipeline for Automated Artifact Data Synthesis
- **Claim:** The coordinated perception, synthesis, and curation agents can generate large volumes of realistic, annotated artifact‑injected image pairs without human intervention.
- **Mechanism:** 
  - *Perception agent* decomposes an image into entities and subentities using a VLM (for vocabulary) and Grounded‑SAM (for segmentation), then links subentities to parent entities via containment analysis.
  - *Synthesis agent* selects an artifact type (add, remove, distort, fuse) and computes a target‑reference patch mapping; it then applies the inversion‑injection procedure in a DiT (FLUX.1‑dev) to produce an artifact‑injected image.
  - *Curation agent* filters low‑quality results using LPIPS (for distortion) or a VLM (for duplication/omission/fusion) and generates both local and global textual explanations via the VLM.
- **Core assumption:** Each agent’s outputs are sufficiently accurate to sustain a high‑throughput data generation loop; in particular, the perception agent must find segmentable parts and the curation VLM must reliably judge artifact plausibility.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “ArtiAgent, an agentic framework that automatically synthesizes 100K artifact‑injected image pairs with rich annotations (binary labels, bounding boxes, explanations) using three coordinated agents: a perception agent …, a synthesis agent …, and a curation agent …”
  - [section 4] Provides full details of the three agents, their tools, and the inversion‑injection algorithm (Algorithms 1‑5).
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., ArtifactLens) highlight the expense of human labeling; ArtiAgent’s fully automated synthesis directly addresses this scalability gap.
- **Break condition:** If the perception agent fails to identify valid subentities or the synthesis agent cannot find a plausible mapping, the pipeline may produce no artifact or an implausible one; the curation stage is designed to filter these out, but excessive rejections indicate upstream failures.

### Mechanism 2: Inversion‑Injection for Localized Structural Perturbations
- **Claim:** By jointly manipulating positional embeddings (PE) and value embeddings within DiT’s self‑attention during the inversion‑injection process, the system can inject structural artifacts in a spatially controlled fashion while preserving overall image coherence.
- **Mechanism:**
  - *Inversion stage*: The clean image is noised to a latent representation; value embeddings `V^(ℓ)` for each layer are cached.
  - *Injection stage*: For target patches (where the artifact should appear), the PE (`RoPE`) is replaced with that of a reference patch (shifting the spatial context), and the value embedding is copied from the reference (injecting content). Background patches keep their original PE but reuse the cached value from inversion to maintain context.
  - The process is restricted to early‑to‑middle denoising steps (e.g., 15 steps for value, selective PE steps) to balance artifact strength and global quality.
- **Core assumption:** DiT’s attention is sufficiently sensitive to positional cues that altering RoPE changes where content is placed, while swapping values changes what content appears; limiting steps avoids global corruption.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2.2] Equations (1)‑(3) and accompanying text describe the PE/value injection, stating that “their combination allows local injections of realistic artifacts.”
  - [Appendix A.1] Explicitly notes the step choices: value injection only first 15 steps, PE injection disabled for final steps for most artifacts.
  - [Appendix A.5] Ablation (Table 5) shows detection accuracy peaks at 15 injection steps, with degradation beyond, validating the configured strength.
- **Break condition:** Injecting too many steps (e.g., 20/25) leads to over‑corruption and lower VLM performance; too few steps (5/25) may produce artifacts that are too subtle to train on.

### Mechanism 3: Synthetic Supervision Transfers to Real Artifact Understanding
- **Claim:** Fine‑tuning VLMs on the artifact‑injected VQA dataset produced by ArtiAgent yields strong gains on real‑world artifact benchmarks (including detection, localization, and explanation), even surpassing proprietary models, because the synthetic data captures the essential structure of modern diffusion artifacts.
- **Mechanism:** 
  - Each artifact pair is converted into two VQA conversations (clean and artifact) with questions about binary presence, bounding boxes, and explanations.
  - Open‑source VLMs (Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B, InternVL3.5‑8B) are fine‑tuned in two stages: first train the LM and multimodal projector (frozen vision encoder), then unfreeze the vision encoder with a lower LR.
  - The model learns to recognize, localize, and describe structural distortions.
- **Core assumption:** The distribution of artifacts synthesized by ArtiAgent matches that of artifacts in contemporary generative models (e.g., FLUX, SD3.5) so that learned features generalize to benchmarks like ArtiBench.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “Fine‑tuning open‑source VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B) on this data significantly improves performance on artifact understanding tasks across multiple benchmarks, including their new ArtiBench. For instance, on ArtiBench detection, Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B accuracy rises from 50.1% to 62.7%, surpassing proprietary models like GPT‑5 (59.9%).”
  - [section 6.1.2] Tables 3(a)‑(c) detail consistent improvements on detection, localization (mIoU, F1), and explanation (ROUGE, CSS) over baselines and vanilla VLMs.
  - [section 6.1.3] Figure 6 shows scaling with synthetic data up to 100K samples, with performance approaching or exceeding GPT‑5.
- **Break condition:** If synthetic artifacts fail to cover the diversity of real artifacts (e.g., new failure modes emerge), the model may not generalize; the paper shows that even with only 4 tool‑based types, transfer is effective, but coverage remains a limitation.

## Foundational Learning
### Diffusion Models and DiT Architecture
**Why needed here:** The synthesis agent’s inversion‑injection directly manipulates the DiT backbone; understanding patch‑wise processing, self‑attention, and rotary positional embeddings is essential to grasp how spatial layout is altered.  
**Quick check question:** “During inversion‑injection, why is value injection performed only in early‑to‑middle denoising steps, while PE injection is disabled in the final steps for most artifact types?”

### Inversion for Diffusion Editing
**Why needed here:** The inversion‑injection paradigm builds on diffusion inversion (e.g., null‑text inversion) to map an image to noise and back, enabling controlled edits by altering embeddings during the denoising trajectory.  
**Quick check question:** “What is the purpose of caching value embeddings during the inversion stage, and how are they reused during injection?”

### Vision‑Language Models (VLMs) for Multi‑task Perception
**Why needed here:** VLMs serve as core components (perception and curation agents) and are the target models to be trained; they also evaluate filtering and generate explanations. Understanding VQA fine‑tuning and evaluation metrics is crucial.  
**Quick check question:** “Why might a VLM trained on synthetic artifact data generalize to real diffusion artifacts, whereas a pre‑trained VLM (e.g., GPT‑5) performs near random guessing on ArtiBench?”

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Perception Agent -> Synthesis Agent -> Curation Agent -> Data Collector -> VLM Trainer -> Downstream Apps
### Critical path
1. Take a clean image from source dataset (COCO, Caltech‑101, etc.).
2. Perception agent extracts entities and subentities with masks.
3. Synthesis agent selects an artifact type and computes patch mapping.
4. Inversion‑injection: invert image to noise, then during denoising apply modified PE/value on target patches for configured steps → artifact image.
5. Curation agent filters the pair; if passed, generate local and global explanations.
6. Convert to VQA format; append to training set.
7. After collecting 100K pairs, fine‑tune VLM.
8. Use trained VLM for evaluation or feed into downstream applications.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Artifact diversity vs. quality:** Four tools cover major structural failures but may miss other nuances; adding tools increases coverage but also complexity and potential for implausible results.
- **Automation cost:** GPT‑4o powers the curation agent, yielding high‑quality explanations but incurring high API costs and rate limits; swapping to an open‑source VLM after seed data could reduce cost but risk quality drop.
- **Injection strength:** More injection steps increase artifact visibility but risk global degradation; the 15‑step value window was chosen via ablation to balance.
- **Model generality:** Inversion‑injection is presented as model‑agnostic for DiTs, but may need adjustments for non‑DiT architectures (e.g., UNet‑based); current implementation is tuned for FLUX.1‑dev.
- **Source dataset diversity:** Using COCO + Caltech‑101 + 11K Hands + Celeba HQ broadens content but may still contain biases; curation filtering discards mismatches.

### Failure signatures
- **Perception agent:** No subentities found or masks severely overlap; check logs for violations of hard rules (Appendix A.2.1).
- **Synthesis agent:** Empty patch mapping (e.g., fuse with no overlap) → artifact image identical to clean; treat as “no injection” and skip.
- **Curation agent:** Rejection rate >50 % indicates either injection too weak (LPIPS too high) or too strong (LPIPS too low) or inconsistent VLM filtering; inspect LPIPS distribution and VLM confidence.
- **VLM training:** No improvement on validation set after 1 epoch; likely synthetic data quality issue; review sample annotations for noise.
- **Reward model:** No monotonic reward increase during best‑of‑N search; may need more training pairs or better negative sampling.
- **Inpainting loop:** VLM fails to produce stable bounding box or repeatedly reports artifact; adjust localization prompt or verify that caption adequately describes intended clean scene.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Sanity check artifact generation:** Run ArtiAgent on 100 random images; visualize 20 random pairs; compute LPIPS distribution to confirm within [τ1, τ2] (0.5–0.9) and manually assess plausibility.
2. **VLM fine‑tuning validation:** Train Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B on 1 K synthetic samples; evaluate on ArtiBench detection and localization; expect >55 % accuracy and >0.10 mIoU (improvement over base 50.1%/0.010 from Table 3).
3. **Downstream utility test:** Train a reward model on 50 K pairs; run best‑of‑N search (N=1,2,4,8,16,32) on 50 MS‑COCO prompts; plot reward vs. search rounds; expect steady increase (as in Figure 7). Alternatively, run VLM‑guided inpainting on 20 artifact images; compute artifact removal rate (VLM verification “No”) after up to 3 iterations.

## Limitations
- Reliance on proprietary GPT‑4o for perception and curation introduces opacity, cost, and potential bias; open‑source alternatives may degrade data quality.
- Artifact diversity limited to four tool‑based types may not cover all structural artifacts in modern diffusion models, potentially limiting generalization.
- Evaluation uses the new ArtiBench benchmark and a few existing ones; long‑term generalization to unseen artifact types remains uncertain.

## Confidence
- Agentic data synthesis pipeline: Medium  
- Inversion‑injection technique: High  
- Synthetic supervision transfer: Medium  

## Next Checks
1. **Component ablation:** Disable each agent (perception, synthesis, curation) sequentially to quantify their individual contributions to data quality and downstream VLM accuracy.
2. **Open‑source curation swap:** Replace GPT‑4o with an open‑source VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5‑VL) in the curation stage; compare rejection rates, LPIPS distribution, and final VLM performance to assess cost‑quality trade‑off.
3. **Cross‑architecture generalization:** Apply inversion‑injection to a non‑DiT diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion); evaluate artifact plausibility (LPIPS) and transfer performance to real artifact benchmarks.
