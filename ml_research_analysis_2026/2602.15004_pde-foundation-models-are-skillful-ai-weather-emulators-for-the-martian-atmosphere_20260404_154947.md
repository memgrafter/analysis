# PDE foundation models are skillful AI weather emulators for the Martian atmosphere

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15004
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15004
- **Reference Count:** 4
- **Primary Result:** Adapting a 2D PDE foundation model to 3D Martian atmospheric modeling yields a 34.4% performance improvement on a held-out year compared to a randomly initialized model, with a 39.3% gain under 80% sparse data conditions.

## Executive Summary
Martian weather forecasting is limited by scarce training data—only four Martian years of reanalysis are available. This study adapts the Poseidon PDE foundation model, originally pretrained on diverse 2D partial differential equation solutions, to the 3D Martian atmosphere. The authors extend the model using axial attention to handle vertical coupling while preserving pretrained 2D spatial knowledge. Fine-tuned on 34 GB of data with a median compute budget of 13 GPU hours, the pretrained model significantly outperforms a randomly initialized counterpart, especially when data is sparse (80% missing). The results demonstrate that pretraining on synthetic PDE solutions provides a powerful data-efficient prior for real-world atmospheric modeling on Mars.

## Method Summary
The method fine-tunes Poseidon-B, a 2D PDE foundation model, for 3D Martian atmospheric prediction. The original 2D backbone processes each vertical sigma level independently by masking the vertical dimension in the batch. Novel, randomly initialized axial attention layers are inserted to model vertical interactions across these independent slices, guided by a learnable position encoding based on the sigma coordinate. The model is trained on single-step predictions using normalized L1 loss, with multi-level samples drawn by randomly selecting 9 adjacent vertical levels from a total of 18. For sparse-data experiments, entire lat/lon columns are randomly dropped per sample, and a binary mask is provided in the pressure channel to guide imputation.

## Key Results
- Pretrained model achieves a **34.4% improvement** in normalized L1 loss on a held-out Martian year versus a randomly initialized model.
- Under **80% sparse data** (randomly missing vertical columns), the pretrained model's advantage increases to a **39.3% improvement**.
- The pretrained model shows stable learning without overfitting, while the randomly initialized model exhibits clear overfitting signs on the limited dataset.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Generalizable PDE Prior
- **Claim:** Pretraining on a diverse corpus of 2D PDE solutions imparts a robust, generalizable prior for learning atmospheric dynamics from limited data.
- **Mechanism:** Poseidon's weights encode inductive biases for smooth, continuous, and locally correlated spatiotemporal evolution. Fine-tuning adapts these biases to Martian atmospheric PDEs rather than learning dynamics from scratch.
- **Core assumption:** Fundamental mathematical properties (e.g., conservation laws, locality) of pretraining PDEs share sufficient structural similarity with Martian atmospheric PDEs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports 34.4% improvement; Section 4.1 shows pretrained model remains stable while random init overfits. Corpus evidence is weak; neighboring work explores OOD limits but not this specific atmospheric transfer.

### Mechanism 2: Axial Attention for Dimensional Extension
- **Claim:** Axial attention along the vertical dimension enables effective 3D modeling while preserving pretrained 2D spatial dynamics knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** The 2D backbone processes each vertical slice independently (vertical dim hidden in batch). New axial attention layers, acting on the vertical dimension, explicitly model coupling between these slices. This cleanly separates frozen 2D spatial processing from learned 1D vertical processing.
- **Core assumption:** Martian atmospheric dynamics are predominantly 2D horizontal, with vertical coupling as a secondary, modulatory effect.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.4 details axial attention design; Section 4.1.2 reports 34.4% improvement from "mixed" init (2D pretrained + 3D vertical) over full random init. Corpus shows axial attention is known for dimensional extension, but its efficacy for atmospheric vertical coupling is anchored here.

### Mechanism 3: Robustness to Sparse Data via Smoothness Prior
- **Claim:** The pretrained model's smoothness and continuity biases enable effective imputation from limited context under sparse initial conditions.
- **Mechanism:** During training with random column masking, the pretrained weights leverage unmasked horizontal and temporal context to infer plausible states for masked regions, outperforming a model learning imputation from scratch.
- **Core assumption:** The Martian atmospheric state is a sufficiently smooth, physically constrained field where missing columns can be inferred from neighbors and dynamics.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports 39.3% gain at 80% sparsity; Section 4.1.3 quantifies the degradation gap and shows visual robustness across vertical levels. Corpus evidence is absent; sparsity handling in this context is paper-specific.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) as dynamical systems.**
  - **Why needed:** The foundation model approach treats forecasting as learning an operator mapping initial conditions to future PDE states. Grasping this is key to understanding what is pretrained and why it might transfer.
  - **Quick check:** What does it mean for a neural network to learn a "solution operator" for a PDE, and how is this different from learning a direct snapshot-to-snapshot mapping?
- **Concept: Foundation model transfer learning.**
  - **Why needed:** The core claim is *data efficiency* from starting with a *generalist* pretrained model (on many PDEs) and *specializing* it (to Mars), vs. training a specialist from scratch.
  - **Quick check:** Why would a model pretrained on synthetic, idealized PDE solutions be a better starting point for a real-world, noisy, partially observed system like Mars than a randomly initialized or Earth-weather-only trained model?
- **Concept: Spatial and temporal autoregressive rollouts.**
  - **Why needed:** Models are trained on single-step predictions but evaluated on long-range forecasts via iterative application. Understanding this is crucial to interpreting forecast "skill" and its link to PDE evolution.
  - **Quick check:** If a model is accurate for a 6-hour single-step forecast, why might its 30-day forecast accuracy degrade rapidly during autoregressive rollout, and how might pretraining mitigate this?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Processor -> Pretrained 2D scOT Backbone -> Vertical Attention Injections -> Vertical Position Encoder -> Output Head
- **Critical path:** Data loading -> 2D slice creation (per level) -> Pretrained 2D backbone pass (per slice) -> Vertical attention aggregation across slices -> Loss computation on full 3D fields (with optional masking) -> Backpropagation updates only vertical attention & position encoder during initial fine-tuning (optionally full fine-tuning later).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Axial vs. Full 3D Attention:** Axial attention drastically reduces parameters/compute but may miss complex vertical-horizontal interactions. Tradeoff: efficiency for potential accuracy loss.
  - **Masking Strategy:** Random column masking encourages general imputation but may not reflect real-world sensor distribution. A fixed mask would better simulate actual instruments but risks overfitting to a specific pattern.
  - **Compute Budget vs. Depth:** Training on 9 random adjacent levels out of 18 reduces memory but may weaken long-range vertical coupling learning.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Boundary artifacts:** Pronounced error stripes at longitude/latitude boundaries indicate zero-padding in ConvNeXt blocks is problematic for non-periodic Mars data. Pretraining may mask but not eliminate this.
  2. **Vertical incoherence:** Predictions show unrealistic layer stacking or discontinuities between adjacent sigma levels, suggesting vertical attention failed to learn coherent coupling.
  3. **Sparse data collapse:** Performance degrades catastrophically (>random init) at very high sparsity (e.g., >95%) if the smoothness prior is too strong, leading to overly damped, unphysical forecasts.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Fine-tune the *unmodified* 2D Poseidon on a single Mars sigma level (0.87) to establish pure 2D transfer efficacy.
  2. **Vertical extension validation:** Implement axial attention. Compare "mixed" init (2D weights frozen, vertical attn random) vs. full random init on the multi-level (18) task. Validate improvement and check for vertical coherence.
  3. **Sparsity stress test:** Introduce column masking (80%, 95%). Train both pretrained-mixed and random-init 3D models. Quantify the degradation gap and inspect spatial error maps.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can PDE foundation models incorporate time-varying boundary conditions and external forcings (e.g., diurnal cycles, topography) that are critical for atmospheric dynamics but absent from the pretraining corpus?
- **Question 2:** Can the axial attention-based 3D extension be effectively adapted to spherical geometry instead of the planar latent space used for pretraining?
- **Question 3:** To what extent does the data efficiency advantage of PDE foundation models persist as the volume of domain-specific training data increases?
- **Question 4:** How robust are PDE foundation models to *structured* sparsity patterns that reflect actual observational coverage (e.g., satellite swaths, ground station locations) rather than random column dropping?
- **Question 5:** Can the inclusion of standard auxiliary inputs (orography, land-sea mask, latitude, longitude, time encodings) close the performance gap between randomly initialized and pretrained models under limited-data conditions?

## Limitations
- **Transfer Mechanism Scope:** The claim that pretraining on generic 2D PDEs transfers to Martian dynamics relies on an unverified structural similarity assumption; corpus evidence is weak.
- **Vertical Architecture Efficacy:** The axial attention design's success depends on the assumption that horizontal 2D dynamics dominate; this may not hold for regimes with strong, non-local vertical teleconnections.
- **Sparsity Robustness Boundaries:** The demonstrated 39.3% improvement under random 80% missing data may not generalize to structured sparsity patterns reflecting real instrument coverage or to extreme sparsity (>95%).

## Confidence
- **Pretraining Data Efficiency Gain (34.4%):** Medium-High. Strong internal experimental evidence vs. random init, but mechanism relies on untested OOD assumptions.
- **3D Extension via Axial Attention:** Medium. Validated by ablation, but efficacy contingent on an unproven vertical/horizontal dynamic hierarchy.
- **Sparsity Robustness (39.3% at 80%):** Medium. Robust within tested random column-drop regime; generalizability to realistic patterns or critical missing regions is speculative.
