# MMR-Bench: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Multimodal LLM Routing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17814
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17814
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The routed system matches or exceeds the strongest single model’s accuracy at approximately 33% of its cost.

## Executive Summary
Inefficient deployment of multimodal LLMs—where no single model excels uniformly across tasks—leads to wasted compute on easy queries or accuracy loss on hard ones. This paper introduces MMR-Bench, a standardized benchmark that isolates multimodal routing by providing precomputed utilities and normalized costs for 11,000 queries across 10 heterogeneous MLLMs and eight vision-language datasets. The core evaluation compares unimodal (text-only, image-only) and multimodal feature fusion under fixed candidate sets and explicit compute budgets. Results show that multimodal routing with adaptive fusion significantly improves the cost-accuracy frontier and generalizes zero-shot to new datasets and text-only benchmarks without retuning.

## Method Summary
MMR-Bench supplies a fixed outcome table containing normalized utilities (0–1) and inference costs for every query–model pair, along with frozen CLIP text and image embeddings. Routing policies are evaluated offline: given a test query, a router selects a model from the candidate set such that the expected utility satisfies a cost constraint. The method centers on two components: (1) **adaptive feature fusion**, which combines text and image embeddings using confidence scores derived from prototype similarity and embedding magnitude, plus interaction terms (sum, product, difference); (2) **routers**, including non-parametric methods (KMeans, KNN) and learned models (LinearMF, MLP) trained on a 20% split to predict utilities or directly assign models. Performance is aggregated across budgets using nAUC, peak score (Ps), and Quality-Neutral Cost (QNC).

## Key Results
- Multimodal routing with adaptive fusion matches or exceeds the strongest single model’s accuracy at only ~33% of its cost.
- Routers generalize zero-shot to new datasets and text-only benchmarks without retraining.
- Adaptive fusion significantly outperforms unimodal routing, particularly on vision-heavy tasks like OCR.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of multimodal routing hinges on adaptive fusion of unimodal signals. By dynamically weighting text and image features—using confidence from prototype similarity and normalized embedding magnitude—and incorporating interaction terms (sum, product, difference), the fused representation captures cross-modal cues that are predictive of model performance. This allows the router to allocate queries more precisely: text-heavy, image-heavy, or balanced queries receive distinct representations, leading to better model selection under budget constraints. The mechanism is particularly effective for queries where one modality dominates (e.g., OCR) because the confidence mechanism downweights the uninformative modality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Routing**: Why needed? No single MLLM dominates all tasks; routing allocates compute efficiently. Quick check: Does the router’s Pareto frontier dominate the best single model under the same budget?
- **Adaptive Feature Fusion**: Why needed? Unimodal features alone may miss cross-modal interactions critical for some queries. Quick check: Does fused routing outperform unimodal routers (text-only, image-only) on vision-language benchmarks?
- **Normalized Cost Metrics**: Why needed? To compare models with varying compute requirements on a unified scale. Quick check: Are normalized costs consistent across datasets and models (e.g., no outliers)?
- **Pareto Frontier Analysis**: Why needed? Evaluates trade-offs between accuracy and cost. Quick check: Does the router achieve higher accuracy at lower cost than the best single model?
- **Zero-Shot Transfer**: Why needed? Real deployments encounter new datasets/models without retraining. Quick check: Does a router trained on one dataset perform well on a different dataset?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
CLIP Text Embeddings, CLIP Image Embeddings → Adaptive Fusion → Router (KMeans/KNN/MLP/LinearMF) → Model Selection

**Critical path:**  
1. Encode query using CLIP to obtain text and image embeddings.  
2. Compute confidence scores for each modality (prototype similarity + sigmoid of normalized magnitude).  
3. Fuse embeddings with adaptive weights and interaction terms (sum, product, difference), then ℓ₂-normalize.  
4. Router maps fused feature to a model index (or utility/cost) based on training.  
5. Return selected model for the query.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Adaptive fusion adds overhead but captures cross-modal cues; unimodal routing is simpler but less expressive.  
- Non-parametric routers (KMeans, KNN) are fast at test time but may lack capacity for complex patterns.  
- Parametric routers (MLP, LinearMF) are more expressive but require more data and hyperparameter tuning.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Unimodal routers: sharply lower nAUC on vision-heavy datasets (OCRBench, TextVQA).  
- KNNRouter: QNC = ∞ (unable to reach target accuracy) on hard benchmarks like MME and OCRBench.  
- Cross-dataset failure: training on one dataset (e.g., ScienceQA) and testing on another (e.g., OCRBench) yields large Ps drop.

**Exactly 3 first experiments:**  
1. Run unimodal and multimodal routers on each of the eight datasets separately; validate the performance gap on OCRBench and TextVQA.  
2. For KNNRouter, sweep neighborhood size *k* on MME and OCRBench; if QNC remains ∞ beyond *k*=50, the method is insufficient.  
3. Train on ScienceQA and test zero-shot on OCRBench; a severe Ps drop indicates dataset-specific overfitting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Are unimodal signals sufficient for effective multimodal routing, or are multimodal cues necessary? *Why unresolved*: Systematic failure analysis across modality noise levels and broader model sets is needed.
- **Open Question 2**: What is the practical cost-accuracy trade-off of multimodal routing under different cost models? *Why unresolved*: The ~33% saving relies on a specific token-based normalization; robustness to latency, memory, or energy costs is unclear.
- **Open Question 3**: Can routing policies generalize to new MLLMs added to the zoo without retraining? *Why unresolved*: The benchmark uses a fixed set of 10 models; zero-shot transfer to new models is not evaluated.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter specifics (MLP architecture, learning rate, SVD rank, interaction coefficients α/β) are not fully enumerated, requiring reliance on supplementary material or released code.
- Cost normalization protocol (unified pricing and tokenization details behind Eq. S1) is incompletely described, hindering recomputation or adaptation to new cost models.
- Generalization claims mix dataset-specific and cross-dataset testing, blurring the boundary between true routing capability and overfitting to dataset biases.

## Confidence
- Benchmark utility & data availability: **High**
- Adaptive fusion superiority: **Medium**
- Zero-shot cross-scenario/modality transfer: **Low**
