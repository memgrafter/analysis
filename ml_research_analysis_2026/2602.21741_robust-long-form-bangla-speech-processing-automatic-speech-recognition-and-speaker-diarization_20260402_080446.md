# Robust Long-Form Bangla Speech Processing: Automatic Speech Recognition and Speaker Diarization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21741  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21741  
- **Reference count:** 23  
- **Primary result:** Achieved public WER 0.36137 (private 0.37738) for ASR and public DER 0.20936 (private 0.27671) on the Bengali‑Loop benchmark.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles robust ASR and speaker diarization for long‑form Bengali audio, a low‑resource language with dialectal variation and code‑mixing. For ASR, the proposed system combines a BengaliAI fine‑tuned Whisper medium model with selective Demucs‑based vocal source separation (triggered by a spectral‑flux heuristic) and silence‑boundary‑aware chunking. For diarization, it replaces the default segmentation model in the pyannote.audio pipeline with a Bengali‑fine‑tuned variant, uses wespeaker‑voxceleb‑resnet34‑LM embeddings, and applies centroid‑based agglomerative clustering. The best reported scores are WER 0.36137 (public) / 0.37738 (private) and DER 0.20936 (public) / 0.27671 (private). Ablation insights highlight segmentation fine‑tuning as the most impactful diarization improvement and source separation plus natural chunking as critical for ASR, yielding substantial gains over baselines on the Bengali‑Loop benchmark.

## Method Summary
The authors design a two‑pipeline system for long‑form Bengali speech. The ASR pipeline processes audio by first applying a spectral‑flux heuristic to detect musical sections; if detected, Demucs separates vocals from background music. The audio is then chunked at silence boundaries to fit Whisper’s context window, and each chunk is transcribed using a Whisper medium model fine‑tuned on Bengali data. The diarization pipeline uses the pyannote.audio framework but substitutes its default segmentation model with a version fine‑tuned on Bengali speech. Speaker embeddings are extracted with a wespeaker model pre‑trained on VoxCeleb, and clustering is performed via centroid‑based agglomerative clustering to assign speaker labels. Both pipelines are optimized for the Bengali‑Loop dataset.

## Key Results
- **ASR:** Best private WER 0.37738, public WER 0.36137.  
- **Speaker diarization:** Best private DER 0.27671, public DER 0.20936.  
- **Ablation findings:** Domain‑specific segmentation fine‑tuning most improved diarization; source separation + silence‑based chunking most improved ASR.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The authors attribute the ASR gains to the combination of vocal source separation (to handle background music) and silence‑boundary‑aware chunking (to preserve contextual coherence). For diarization, fine‑tuning the segmentation model on Bengali speech better captures dialectal and code‑mixed patterns, while the robust wespeaker embeddings and centroid clustering further reduce errors. The selective application of Demucs avoids distorting pure‑speech segments, and the natural chunking aligns with Whisper’s training on semantically coherent utterances.

## Foundational Learning
- **Whisper**: A pre‑trained multilingual ASR model. *Why needed*: Provides a strong multilingual baseline; fine‑tuning adapts it to Bengali. *Quick check*: Confirm Whisper’s tokenizer includes Bengali characters and that fine‑ tuning data covers Bengali phonetics.  
- **Demucs**: A music source separation model. *Why needed*: Isolates vocals from background music, improving ASR in musical segments. *Quick check*: Verify Demucs is only applied to music‑heavy segments; check that separation quality is sufficient for ASR.  
- **pyannote.audio**: An end‑to‑end speaker diarization pipeline. *Why needed*: Offers modular segmentation and embedding components; can be customized with fine‑tuned models. *Quick check*: Test whether replacing the segmentation model with a Bengali‑fine‑tuned version improves DER on a validation set.  
- **wespeaker**: A speaker embedding model pre‑trained on VoxCeleb. *Why needed*: Generates robust speaker representations for clustering. *Quick check*: Evaluate embedding discriminability on a Bengali speaker verification subset.  
- **Spectral‑flux heuristic**: Computes temporal changes in the frequency spectrum to detect music. *Why needed*: Triggers Demucs only when beneficial, avoiding unnecessary distortion. *Quick check*: Measure precision/recall of music detection on a held‑out set.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
ASR: Audio → Spectral‑flux Check → (Optional: Demucs) → Silence‑based Chunker → Whisper (Bengali‑fine‑tuned) → Text  
Diarization: Audio → Bengali‑fine‑tuned Segmentation → Wespeaker Embedding → Centroid Agglomerative Clustering → Speaker Labels  

**Critical Path**  
For ASR: spectral‑flux decision → optional Demucs → chunking → transcription. Demucs adds conditional latency. For diarization: segmentation → embedding → clustering; segmentation is the primary computational bottleneck.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Selective vs. unconditional separation*: Heuristic avoids unnecessary processing but may miss music or false‑alarm.  
- *Silence‑ vs. fixed‑length chunking*: Natural breaks improve context but yield variable chunk sizes; fixed length simplifies batching but may cut sentences.  
- *Fine‑tuned vs. default segmentation*: Better language adaptation at the cost of labeled data and overfitting risk.  
- *Pre‑trained (wespeaker) vs. custom embeddings*: Leverages large speaker corpora but may not capture Bengali acoustics fully.

**Failure Signatures**  
- ASR WER spikes on musical segments not detected by the spectral‑flux heuristic.  
- Diarization DER increases on overlapping speech or when speaker characteristics differ from training data.  
- Excessive memory/time for long audios due to Demucs and chunking overhead.  
- Performance drop across Bengali dialects if fine‑tuning data lacks diversity.

**First Experiments**  
1. Vary the spectral‑flux threshold on a validation set and measure ASR WER to assess heuristic robustness.  
2. Compare DER using the fine‑tuned segmentation against the default pyannote segmentation on a held‑out set.  
3. Apply the complete pipeline to a different low‑resource language (e.g., Tamil) without fine‑tuning to test generalization.

## Limitations
- Heuristic thresholds (spectral‑flux, silence parameters) lack sensitivity analysis and may be corpus‑specific.  
- Integrity of the Bengali‑Loop benchmark is uncertain; the small public‑private gap suggests possible data leakage or non‑IID splits.  
- Generalization to other Bengali dialects, code‑mixed registers, or different low‑resource languages is unknown.  
- Ablation studies are incomplete; individual contributions of sub‑modules are not isolated.
