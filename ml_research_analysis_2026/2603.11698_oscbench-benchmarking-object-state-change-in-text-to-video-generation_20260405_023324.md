# OSCBench: Benchmarking Object State Change in Text-to-Video Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11698
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11698
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** All evaluated T2V models achieve high semantic and scene alignment but score significantly lower on object state change (OSC) accuracy and consistency, with the largest drops in novel and compositional scenarios, revealing OSC as a core bottleneck.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces OSCBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate text-to-video (T2V) models on their ability to generate accurate and temporally consistent object state changes (OSC) in cooking-related scenarios. The benchmark comprises 1,120 prompts across regular (common action-object pairs), novel (uncommon pairs), and compositional (multi-step sequences) settings. Six T2V models, both open-source and proprietary, were assessed via human evaluation and a novel MLLM-based automatic method using Chain-of-Thought prompting. Results consistently show that while models perform well on semantic and scene alignment, they struggle with OSC accuracy and consistency, especially in novel and compositional contexts, indicating a fundamental limitation in current T2V generation.

## Method Summary
The methodology involves constructing OSCBench from the HowToChange dataset. Human annotators clustered 20 primitive actions into 9 categories and 134 objects into 8 categories. Scenarios were systematically designed: 108 regular (frequent action-object pairs), 20 novel (infrequent pairs from the long-tail), and 12 compositional (sequences of 2-3 actions). Prompts follow a template combining subject, action, object, and scene. For evaluation, videos were generated from six T2V models using their default settings. A human study rated 140 sampled videos (one per scenario) on eight dimensions (OSC accuracy, consistency, semantic/scene alignment, perceptual quality) via 1-5 Likert scales. Automatic evaluation used MLLMs (Qwen3-VL, GPT-5 family) with a Chain-of-Thought prompt requiring criteria grounding, frame-level evidence extraction, and scoring. Correlation between human and MLLM scores was computed using Kendall's τ.

## Key Results
- All models achieve high semantic and scene alignment scores but significantly lower OSC accuracy and consistency.
- Performance drops are largest in novel and compositional settings, with novel scenarios being the hardest.
- Proprietary models (Veo-3.1-Fast, Kling-2.5-Turbo) outperform open-source ones, but none solve the OSC challenge.
- Human-MLLM correlation reaches Kendall's τ up to 0.628 for OSC metrics, validating automatic evaluation for model ranking.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** OSCBench reveals that current T2V models fail on object state change because they overfit to frequent action-object patterns in training data, impairing generalization to novel combinations.
- **Mechanism:** Models learn statistical correlations between common pairs (e.g., slicing potato) but lack a causal understanding of how an action transforms an object's state. When presented with a novel pair (e.g., mashing grapefruit), they default to memorized patterns or misapply actions.
- **Core assumption:** Training data distributions are long-tailed, with many plausible state changes underrepresented.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: performance drops in novel and compositional settings; [section 3.1]: strong long-tail distribution biases evaluation toward frequent patterns.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Compositional OSC scenarios expose a failure to maintain coherent intermediate states over time, as models struggle with multi-step temporal consistency.
- **Mechanism:** Generating videos with composed actions requires tracking and rendering multiple state transitions sequentially. Current architectures likely lack explicit state tracking modules, causing state inconsistencies or skipped steps.
- **Core assumption:** T2V models treat video generation as frame-by-frame prediction without persistent object-centric state representations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: largest drops in novel and compositional settings; [section 5.4]: compositional performance between regular and novel, requiring coherent intermediate states; [section 5.2, Figure 4]: Wan-2.2 reverting to a whole apple after slicing.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** MLLM-based evaluation with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) effectively approximates human judgment for OSC by forcing stepwise evidence extraction, but only for advanced MLLMs.
- **Mechanism:** CoT prompts require the MLLM to first ground criteria, then extract frame-level evidence (e.g., "lemon remains undeformed"), and finally score. This structures reasoning toward fine-grained visual details rather than high-level semantics.
- **Core assumption:** MLLMs can perform spatio-temporal reasoning about object states when guided, but their raw scoring is noisy.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: Human-MLLM correlation up to 0.628; [section 4.3]: CoT encourages careful visual inspection; [section 5.3, Figure 7]: GPT-5.2 with CoT detects a juicing error missed by others.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Object State Change (OSC)**
  - **Why needed:** OSC is the core construct being benchmarked; it distinguishes action understanding from general motion or alignment.
  - **Quick check:** How is "slicing a lemon" as an OSC different from simply showing a knife moving near a lemon?
- **Concept: Long-tail distribution in training data**
  - **Why needed:** Explains why models fail on novel scenarios; common pairs are memorized, rare ones are not.
  - **Quick check:** If a model sees "peeling potato" 1000x and "peeling berry" 5x, what failure mode is expected?
- **Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting for evaluation**
  - **Why needed:** Enables more reliable automatic assessment of fine-grained temporal properties like OSC consistency.
  - **Quick check:** Why might a direct "score this video 1-5" prompt fail for OSC, and how does CoT address it?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** HowToChange dataset -> Human-in-the-loop clustering (actions/objects) -> Scenario design (regular/novel/compositional) -> Prompt generation (template-based) -> Video synthesis (6 T2V models) -> Multi-rater evaluation (human + MLLM with CoT) -> Correlation analysis (Kendall's τ).
- **Critical path:** Data abstraction → Scenario creation → Prompt generation → Video synthesis → Evaluation → Correlation analysis.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Breadth vs. depth*: 1,120 prompts cover many scenarios but only 140 are human-evaluated.
  - *Automation vs. reliability*: MLLM evaluation scales but requires CoT for OSC; simpler metrics fail.
  - *Domain specificity*: Cooking ensures clear OSC but limits generalizability to other domains.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - High semantic/scene scores but low OSC accuracy/consistency.
  - Performance drop order: regular > compositional > novel.
  - Open-source models lag proprietary ones, especially on OSC.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate human evaluation sampling: take 1 prompt per scenario (140 total) for a new T2V model, have 3 human raters score OSC accuracy/consistency, compare to paper's Table 4.
  2. Run MLLM evaluation (GPT-5.2 with CoT) on the same 140 videos, compute Kendall's τ against human scores to validate automatic evaluation reliability for your model.
  3. Error analysis: For compositional scenarios where your model fails, manually inspect frames to identify whether the failure is (a) missing an action, (b) incorrect state after first action, or (c) inconsistency in later frames.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** What architectural or training modifications are necessary for T2V models to achieve high accuracy and temporal consistency in object state change, particularly for novel action-object pairs and compositional actions?
  - **Basis:** Evaluation results show significant performance drops in novel and compositional scenarios, indicating current models fail to generalize beyond memorized patterns.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper diagnoses the problem via benchmark results but does not investigate model-internal mechanisms or propose solutions.
  - **What evidence would resolve:** Ablation studies on model components, analyses of training data biases, or new architectures with explicit state transition dynamics.
- **Question:** To what extent do the OSC challenges identified in cooking scenarios generalize to other domains (e.g., tool use, assembly), and how can future benchmarks capture a broader spectrum of state changes?
  - **Basis:** Limitations section states cooking focus does not fully capture diversity of interactions in other domains.
  - **Why unresolved:** The domain-specific benchmark leaves open whether findings are universal; generalization has not been tested.
  - **What evidence would resolve:** Constructing analogous benchmarks for other domains and evaluating the same T2V models to compare performance patterns.
- **Question:** Why do MLLM-based evaluators, even with Chain-of-Thought prompting, exhibit lower correlation with humans on perceptual quality (realism, aesthetics) than on object state change metrics, and how can automatic evaluation be enhanced for subjective dimensions?
  - **Basis:** Table 3 shows lower Kendall's τ for realism and aesthetics vs. OSC metrics; Discussion attributes this to subjective nature but does not explore why CoT helps OSC but not perceptual quality.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates CoT's effectiveness for OSC but does not analyze its limitations for subjective metrics.
  - **What evidence would resolve:** Error analysis of MLLM judgments on perceptual quality, comparing rationales where humans and MLLMs disagree; developing new prompting strategies for subjective aesthetics.

## Limitations
- Full OSCBench prompt list and human-evaluation sampling seed are unreleased, preventing exact replication.
- MLLM evaluation details (frame-sampling, JSON parsing, model version specifics) are under-specified, risking implementation drift.
- Proprietary model generation settings for the full 1,120 prompts are not documented, limiting cross-lab comparability.

## Confidence
- **OSC as a core bottleneck:** High. Consistent cross-model performance drop on OSC vs. semantic/scene metrics is strongly evidenced.
- **Failure causes (overfitting to long-tail, lack of state tracking):** Medium. Supported by novel/compositional performance gaps and error examples, but break conditions remain untested.
- **MLLM+CoT as valid automatic evaluation:** Medium. Correlation up to 0.628 shows promise, but varies by model and dimension; CoT's stepwise grounding lacks fine-grained ablation.

## Next Checks
1. **Independent human study replication:** Recruit 3 raters to evaluate OSC accuracy/consistency on a *held-out* subset of 50 OSCBench prompts (balanced across scenario types) for 2 new T2V models. Compute Kendall’s τ against the paper’s human scores to test rater reliability generalizability.
2. **MLLM CoT ablation:** Run GPT-5.2 with the published CoT prompt *and* a direct score-only baseline on a synchronized set of 100 videos with known OSC ground truth (e.g., manually annotated error frames). Quantify CoT’s marginal gain in error detection precision.
3. **Training data distribution test:** Scrape/estimate action-object pair frequencies in public T2V training corpora (e.g., Kinetics, HowTo100M). Correlate pair frequency with model performance on corresponding OSCBench *novel* prompts to directly test the long-tail overfitting hypothesis.
