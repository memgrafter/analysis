# Not All Subjectivity Is the Same! Defining Desiderata for the Evaluation of Subjectivity in NLP
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28351
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28351
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Current NLP evaluation for subjective tasks lacks nuance in distinguishing ambiguity from polyphony and fails to assess effective communication of subjectivity to users; a proposed seven-desiderata framework reveals D4 (Predict) is commonly evaluated, D7 (Express) is completely unaddressed, and interplay between desiderata is rarely considered.
## Executive Summary
The paper argues that subjectivity in NLP—encompassing tasks where multiple valid perspectives exist—is not monolithic. Past evaluation conflatse two distinct dimensions of subjective input: **ambiguity** (where clarification is needed) and **polyphony** (where multiple legitimate views coexist). To address this, the authors propose seven sequential evaluation desiderata (D1–D7) spanning recognition, prediction, calibration, representation, and user expression. Analyzing 60 papers, they find evaluation focuses heavily on D4 (predicting distributions) via distance metrics and accuracy, while D2/D3 (recognizing type and cause) are understudied and D7 (expressing to users) is entirely absent. The work calls for multi-faceted, user-centric evaluation that considers the interplay between these capabilities.
## Method Summary
The authors performed a qualitative content analysis of 60 NLP papers that model subjectivity (e.g., disagreement, pluralism). Papers were collected via database searches and snowballing using terms like "subjectivity" and "disagreement." Each paper's evaluation section was annotated for: (1) metric categories (distance metrics, F1, accuracy, reliability, other), (2) evaluation objective (e.g., predicting individual annotators vs. full distributions), and (3) which of the seven desiderata (D1–D7) were addressed. Aggregated statistics revealed patterns of coverage, gaps, and co-occurrences, which were visualized to support the claims.
## Key Results
- D4 (Predict) is the most frequently evaluated desideratum, primarily using distance metrics (KL/JS divergence), F1, and accuracy.
- D7 (Express) is completely unaddressed in any evaluated paper.
- D2 (Recognize which) and D3 (Recognize why) are severely understudied.
- The interplay between different desiderata is rarely considered; evaluations tend to be isolated.
- Evaluation for free-text generation scenarios is particularly scarce.
## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Distinguishing between ambiguous (needing disambiguation) and polyphonic (needing multiple perspectives) inputs is a prerequisite for correct model behavior.
- **Mechanism:** The model first characterizes the input along two orthogonal dimensions (ambiguity and polyphony). This classification determines the subsequent strategy: for ambiguous inputs, the model should seek clarifications or present disambiguations; for polyphonic inputs, it should capture and present diverse valid perspectives. Conflating these leads to inappropriate responses (e.g., seeking clarifications for a clear but contested statement, or presenting a single perspective for an ambiguous query).
- **Core assumption:** The *source* of subjectivity (task ambiguity, input ambiguity, annotator attitude) maps onto these observable input dimensions and dictates the correct modeling approach.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...the distinction between ambiguous and polyphonic input..."; [section 3] "We characterize model behavior when dealing with different sources of subjectivity on two dimensions: ambiguity and polyphony."
- **Break condition:** Model performance degrades significantly when evaluated separately on manually labeled ambiguous vs. polyphonic subsets (gold from sources in Section 2.2).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The seven desiderata form a sequential evaluation pipeline where fulfilling later desiderata (e.g., D4 Predict, D6 Represent) implicitly depends on successfully addressing earlier ones (D1 Recognize when, D2 Recognize which, D3 Recognize why).
- **Mechanism:** A model cannot accurately predict or represent subjectivity (D4, D6) if it first fails to recognize that subjectivity is present (D1), misidentifies its type (D2), or misunderstands its causes (D3). High scores on downstream metrics (e.g., distance to a distribution) are meaningless if achieved by ignoring disambiguation needs or suppressing minority views.
- **Core assumption:** Effective subjectivity-sensitive modeling is a composition of distinct, ordered capabilities; downstream success is contingent on upstream recognition and understanding.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...lack of interplay between different desiderata..."; [section 5] "The desiderata are constructed in a top-down approach... their interplay must be studied as well."
- **Break condition:** A model achieves SOTA on a soft-label distance metric (D4) but ablation shows performance collapse when training data is filtered to only ambiguous or only polyphonic examples (failing D2/D3 recognition).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The ultimate utility of a subjectivity-sensitive model is bounded by its ability to *express* the subjectivity (D7) in a user-centric manner, a desiderata completely overlooked in current evaluations.
- **Mechanism:** Even if a model perfectly recognizes, predicts, calibrates, and represents all perspectives, a poor expression strategy (e.g., overwhelming exhaustive lists, confusing language) renders the output unusable. Expression bridges internal handling to the user's cognitive load and decision-making needs.
- **Core assumption:** Model evaluation must be user-centered; abstract accuracy (D4–D6) is insufficient if output format or delivery fails to inform or assist the user.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "D7 (Express) is completely unaddressed"; [section 5] "D7.Express is unaddressed... This mirrors the responsibility of showing users explanations... in XAI."
- **Break condition:** User studies (borrowing from HCI/XAI) show subjects cannot accurately summarize the range of modeled perspectives from the output, or report high confusion/frustration.
## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Sources of Subjectivity (Task Ambiguity, Input Ambiguity, Annotator Attitude)
  - *Why needed here:* To understand *why* disagreement exists in data, which is the foundation for distinguishing ambiguity (knowledge gaps) from polyphony (legitimate perspective differences). This informs what the model should do.
  - *Quick check question:* Given an input where annotators disagree, can you classify the most likely primary source (e.g., unclear guidelines, missing context, demographic differences)?
- **Concept:** Ambiguity vs. Polyphony Dimensions
  - *Why needed here:* The core novel framework. All downstream desiderata (D2, D3, D4, D6) are filtered through this lens. A model's architecture and loss function must be designed to handle one or both.
  - *Quick check question:* For a subjective task (e.g., hate speech detection), provide one example input that is (a) ambiguous & monophonic, (b) non-ambiguous & polyphonic, (c) ambiguous & polyphonic.
- **Concept:** Pluralistic Alignment Operationalizations (Distributional, Overton, Steerable)
  - *Why needed here:* This defines *how* the model expresses subjectivity (D4, D6, D7). The choice (e.g., outputting a distribution vs. textual summary) directly impacts evaluation metrics and user experience.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the key difference between a "distributional" and an "overton" model output for a subjective query? Which operationalization is most directly evaluated by Jensen-Shannon Divergence?
## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Analyzer -> Perspective Engine -> Calibrator & Validator -> Expression Formatter. Input Analyzer flags need (D1) and classifies type (D2). Perspective Engine predicts distribution/generates perspectives (D4, D6). Calibrator/Validator ensures calibration (D5) and diversity (D6). Expression Formatter creates user-facing output (D7).
- **Critical path:** D1 (detect need) → D2/D3 (diagnose type & cause) → D4 (predict) → D5/D6 (calibrate & ensure diversity) → D7 (express). Failures in early steps propagate downstream.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Granularity vs. Usability:** D6 demands representing all perspectives, but D7 demands conciseness. Balance exhaustive representation with cognitive load.
  - **Inference Cost vs. Nuance:** Distributional modeling (D4) is cheaper than generating Overton-style textual summaries (tradeoff with D7).
  - **Single vs. Multi-Model:** A single model can be trained for all desiderata but may be complex; a pipeline of specialists is modular but introduces error propagation and latency.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **D2/D3 Failure:** High aggregated D4 performance but poor performance on manually labeled ambiguous/polyphonic subsets; model asks irrelevant clarifications or presents a single "average" view for polyphonic inputs.
  - **D6/D7 Conflict:** Internal distribution is diverse (high entropy) but user output is bland consensus-only, or output is a long, unranked list of minor opinions that confuses the user.
  - **Calibration Blindness:** Model confidence scores correlate poorly with actual entropy of human judgment distribution (low Hcorr), indicating miscalibration about the *amount* of subjectivity (D5 failure).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Diagnostic Validity Test:** Manually annotate held-out data for ambiguity/polyphony. Train/evaluate a probe to classify these dimensions above chance.
  2.  **Interplay Stress Test:** Take a strong D4 model; ablate its D3 component (e.g., mask rationale tokens) and re-evaluate D4/D6 on ambiguous vs. polyphonic subsets; measure delta.
  3.  **Expression Usability Study:** For fixed internally valid outputs (high D4/D6), present them in different formats (probability table, bullets, NL summary). Measure user comprehension of view range, cognitive load, satisfaction.
## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we design and evaluate user-facing expressions of subjectivity that avoid information overload while accurately representing diverse perspectives?
- **Basis in paper:** D7.Express is unaddressed. None of the papers study how to express subjectivity to the user without hampering their experience.
- **Why unresolved:** Existing evaluation practices do not address how to communicate subjectivity to end-users effectively; this is critical for real-world applications where user trust and comprehension matter.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of expression strategies (e.g., summarization, interactive interfaces) and empirical user studies measuring comprehension, satisfaction, and decision quality; creation of benchmarks with human-annotated expressions.
### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can we automatically detect whether a subjective input is primarily ambiguous or polyphonic, and identify the underlying reasons for subjectivity?
- **Basis in paper:** D2.Recognize which and D3.Recognize why are understudied. It remains a challenge to measure the uncertainty surrounding the polyphony of an input.
- **Why unresolved:** Current models lack explicit mechanisms to distinguish between ambiguity and polyphony, and to explain why subjectivity arises; uncertainty estimation only partially covers ambiguity.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Construction of datasets with fine-grained annotations for ambiguity/polyphony and their sources; development of models that predict these labels; evaluation using classification metrics and interpretability methods like feature attribution.
### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can we evaluate how well a language model captures and conveys the full spectrum of human perspectives in free-text generation?
- **Basis in paper:** Evaluation of D4.Predict in free-text generation scenarios is limited. Most metrics are directly applicable in classification, but demand additional steps for free-text generated responses.
- **Why unresolved:** Metrics for classification (e.g., distance metrics) do not transfer directly to open-ended generation, making it hard to assess pluralistic alignment in generative outputs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** New automatic metrics that compare generated text to multiple reference perspectives (e.g., using NLI, embedding similarity, diversity measures) validated through correlation with human evaluations of perspectival coverage.
### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How do failures at earlier desiderata (e.g., recognizing subjectivity or its type) affect downstream desiderata (e.g., predicting or expressing subjectivity)?
- **Basis in paper:** A multi-faceted and deployment-driven investigation of model behavior is missing. They should not be studied in isolation but their interplay must be studied as well.
- **Why unresolved:** The sequential nature of the desiderata implies dependencies, but no work investigates how errors in D1–D3 propagate to D4–D7, which is crucial for reliable deployment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments evaluating models across all desiderata; error analysis and ablation studies to isolate the impact of each component; development of integrated evaluation frameworks.
## Limitations
- The exact corpus of 60 papers is not disclosed, hindering replication and potentially introducing selection bias via snowballing.
- Annotation rules for mapping evaluations to desiderata are unspecified, leading to possible coder drift, especially for implicit D2/D3 coverage.
- The sequential dependency and D7 importance are asserted without empirical validation; D7's complete absence relies on evaluation sections, possibly missing discussion-only treatments.
- Inter-annotator agreement for core ambiguity/polyphony labels is not reported.
## Confidence
- **Ambiguity vs. polyphony distinction:** Medium. Theoretically grounded with indirect evidence, but no direct experiments confirming that conflating them harms performance.
- **Sequential pipeline (D1→D7):** Medium. Logical structure is clear, but interplay is not demonstrated; a model could excel at D4 while failing D2.
- **D7 unaddressed:** High. Analysis shows zero papers evaluate expression in evaluation sections.
- **D7 as utility bottleneck:** Medium. Analogy to XAI but lacks user studies.
- **Evaluation gaps (D4 frequent, D2/D3 scarce):** High for the 60-paper set; Medium for broader generalizability due to potential corpus limitations.
## Next Checks
1.  **Inter-annotator agreement study:** Recruit multiple raters to classify a diverse set of subjective inputs as ambiguous/polyphonic; compute Cohen’s κ to validate the distinction.
2.  **Ablation for sequential dependency:** Take a state-of-the-art subjectivity model, ablate its D2/D3 components (e.g., remove ambiguity detection), and measure changes in D4 (distributional distance) and D6 (diversity) on ambiguous vs. polyphonic subsets.
3.  **User study on expression (D7):** For outputs with high D6 diversity, vary expression format (probability table, bullet list, natural language summary) and evaluate users on accuracy in perceiving the range of views, cognitive load, and satisfaction.
