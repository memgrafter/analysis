# Worst-case low-rank approximations
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.11304
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11304
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: wcPCA provides worst-case optimal low-rank approximations over convex hulls of source covariances, with theoretical guarantees and empirical improvements in worst-case explained variance up to 25.8%.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces wcPCA, a framework for worst-case low-rank approximation across multiple domains. It defines objectives (minPCA, maxRCS, maxRegret, and normalized variants) that optimize performance on the most adverse domain. Theoretically, these estimators are worst-case optimal not only over observed source domains but over all distributions whose covariance lies in the convex hull of the source covariances. The authors prove finite-sample consistency and asymptotic guarantees, and extend the method to matrix completion (maxMC), achieving approximate worst-case optimality for inductive completion. Empirical evaluations via simulations and a FLUXNET ecosystem case study demonstrate substantial gains in worst-case explained variance (median increase up to 25.8% on held-out target regions) with only minimal reductions in average performance.

## Method Summary
wcPCA addresses the problem of learning a low-dimensional representation that performs well across diverse domains, with a focus on minimizing the worst-case reconstruction error or maximizing the worst-case explained variance. Given multiple source domains, each with its own data covariance matrix, the method formulates a minimax (or maximin) optimization over an orthonormal basis V ∈ ℝ^{p×k}. The primary objectives include minimizing the worst-case reconstruction cost (maxRCS), minimizing the worst-case negative explained variance (minPCA), and minimizing the worst-case regret relative to domain-optimal PCA. Normalized variants adjust for differences in total variance across domains. For matrix completion, where source domains have partially observed entries, wcPCA is extended via alternating minimization: a shared right factor R is updated by solving a second-order cone program, and domain-specific left factors L_e are updated by least squares on observed entries. Inductive completion for new samples uses ordinary least squares with the learned R. The method is supported by theory showing that optimizing over the observed sources automatically bounds loss over any target whose covariance is a convex combination of sources.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical guarantees**: wcPCA objectives are worst-case optimal over the convex hull of source covariances; finite-sample consistency and asymptotic guarantees are proven.
- **Matrix completion extension**: maxMC extends wcPCA to inductive matrix completion, achieving approximate worst-case optimality under partial observability.
- **Empirical performance**: Across simulations and a FLUXNET case study, wcPCA yields up to 25.8% median increase in worst-case explained variance on held-out target regions, with only minimal loss in average performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Convex Hull Generalization
- **Claim:** Solving a worst-case objective on observed source domains yields a representation that is worst-case optimal over all target domains whose covariance matrix lies in the convex hull of the source covariances.
- **Mechanism:** The worst-case loss (e.g., max reconstruction error) over a convex set of covariances equals the loss at the extreme points. Since the source covariances generate the convex hull via mixing weights, bounding loss on sources automatically bounds it anywhere in the hull. This is formalized by expressing any target covariance as Σ = ∑ₑ µₑΣₑ (µₑ≥0, ∑µₑ=1) and showing LRCS(V; Σ) = ∑ₑ µₑLRCS(V; Σₑ) ≤ maxₑ LRCS(V; Σₑ).
- **Core assumption:** The target distribution's covariance lies in conv({Σₑ}). This holds if target domains are mixtures of source factors with common support.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States theoretical guarantee: "estimators are worst-case optimal ... over all distributions whose covariance lies in the convex hull of the source covariances."
  - [section 3.1, Theorem 6] Proves sup_{Σ∈conv({Σₑ})} L(V; Σ) = maxₑ L(V; Σₑ) for appropriate losses L.
  - [corpus] Weak: Corpus papers discuss low-rank and heterogeneous variance but do not directly address convex-hull robustness. Anchor is from the primary paper.
- **Break condition:** A target domain has covariance that is not a convex combination of source covariances (e.g., introduces new orthogonal directions or extreme eigenvalue scales). Then worst-case loss on that target can exceed the bound.

### Mechanism 2: Empirical Consistency
- **Claim:** Finite-sample estimators (empirical wcPCA) converge in probability to the population wcPCA solution as sample sizes grow.
- **Mechanism:** Uniform convergence of empirical domain objectives (e.g., Tr(VᵀΣ̂ₑV) for minPCA) to population objectives (Tr(VᵀΣₑV)) follows from the law of large numbers and boundedness of VᵀΣ̂ₑV. Combined with a well-separated optimum (Assumption 1), standard M-estimation theory yields consistency.
- **Core assumption:** (i) For each domain e, the population optimum is unique up to rotation (Assumption 1). (ii) Minimum sample size n_min → ∞.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2, Proposition 9] States consistency under Assumption 1: d(ÂVₖ, Vₖ*) →⁠ᵖ 0 as n_min→∞.
  - [section B.4 empirical robustness] Shows finite-sample empirical loss converges to optimal population loss as n increases (simulation).
  - [section D.3] Proof: bounds |ÂMₑ(V) – Mₑ(V)| ≤ √k ∥Σ̂ₑ – Σₑ∥_F for minPCA, and similar Lipschitz bounds for others.
- **Break condition:** (i) Population optimum is not unique (e.g., multiple subspaces achieve identical worst-case loss). (ii) A domain has very small sample size relative to others, causing non-uniform convergence. (iii) Numerical optimization fails to find global optimum due to non-convexity.

### Mechanism 3: Regret Invariance under Heterogeneous Noise
- **Claim:** When noise variances differ across domains, regret-based objectives (maxRegret, norm-maxRegret) yield solutions robust to these noise levels, whereas variance/reconstruction objectives can be distorted.
- **Mechanism:** For domain e, observed covariance is Σₑ + σₑ²I. In regret, the domain-optimal solution accounts for Σₑ + σₑ²I fully, so using a shared V, the reconstruction loss difference (regret) cancels the σₑ² term: Tr((V_{e,*}ᵀ(Σₑ+σₑ²I)V_{e,*}) – Tr(Vᵀ(Σₑ+σₑ²I)V) = [Tr(V_{e,*}ᵀΣₑV_{e,*}) – Tr(VᵀΣₑV)]. Hence the objective reduces to a noiseless form.
- **Core assumption:** Noise is additive isotropic (σₑ²I) and independent across domains.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.2.6] Derives that heterogeneous noise cancels in regret, affecting only absolute objectives.
  - [section 5.1.4, Figure 6] Simulation: Under heterogeneous noise, maxRegret's worst-case reconstruction error remains similar to the noiseless case, while maxRCS degrades.
- **Break condition:** (i) Noise is not isotropic (e.g., direction-dependent). (ii) Noise scales differ by orders of magnitude such that even cancellation leaves high-variance domains dominating the worst-case if the shared subspace is poor. (iii) Using normalized variants can re-introduce scale sensitivity if total variances differ.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Covariance matrices and convex hulls**
  - **Why needed here:** The robustness guarantee relies on representing target distributions as convex combinations of source covariances (Σ = ∑µₑΣₑ). Understanding positive semi-definite matrices, trace, and convex combinations is essential to follow the theoretical proofs.
  - **Quick check question:** Given Σ₁ = diag(1,2), Σ₂ = diag(3,1), is Σ = diag(2,1.5) in conv({Σ₁, Σ₂})? How would you prove it?

- **Concept: Asymptotics of M‑estimators**
  - **Why needed here:** Propositions 9 and 10 rely on consistency and uniform convergence of empirical covariances to population covariances, and on the well-separatedness of the optimum. These are standard results from empirical process theory.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does d(ÂV, V*) →⁠ᵖ 0 follow if the empirical objective uniformly converges and the population optimum is unique up to rotation? Sketch the argument.

- **Concept: Total least squares / low‑rank approximation**
  - **Why needed here:** PCA minimizes reconstruction error in Frobenius norm, equivalent to maximizing explained variance. wcPCA extends this to multiple domains. Understanding the equivalence between minimization of reconstruction error and maximization of trace(VᵀΣV) is crucial.
  - **Quick check question:** Show that argmin_{V∈O_{p×k}} E[∥x – xV Vᵀ∥²] = argmax_{V∈O_{p×k}} Tr(VᵀΣV). What assumptions on x and Σ are needed?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Data ingestion** – Raw data matrix X partitioned into E domains; each domain has domain label e and possibly row indices. May include covariates for inductive completion.
  2. **Preprocessing** – Center per domain (or globally?) and standardize (optionally) to address domain‑wise scale differences. Affects objective choice (normalized vs. unnormalized).
  3. **Objective selection** – Choose variant: minPCA, norm‑minPCA, maxRCS, norm‑maxRCS, maxRegret, norm‑maxRegret. Depends on noise structure, scale heterogeneity, and whether average vs. worst‑case tradeoff is desired.
  4. **Optimization** – For PCA variants: projected gradient descent on V ∈ O_{p×k} with Adam, multiple restarts. For matrix completion: alternating minimization over shared R and domain‑specific (Lₑ) via SOCP for R and least squares for Lₑ.
  5. **Post‑processing / evaluation** – Project new data onto learned subspace V, or for matrix completion compute inductive reconstruction ℓ(x,ω,V)Rᵀ. Evaluate trace(VᵀΣ_targetV) or reconstruction error on held‑out domains.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Aggregate per‑domain empirical covariances Σ̂ₑ (or directly form pooled matrix if fully observed).
  2. Pick worst‑case objective; its gradient involves picking the active domain e where min/max is attained. Note: active domain can change during optimization.
  3. For matrix completion with partially observed sources, alternating minimization requires careful handling of masks; the R‑update is a second‑order cone program.
  4. After optimization, check if the solution is sensitive to initializations (multiple restarts) and verify that the worst‑case domain at optimum is not a tiny or noisy domain.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Normalization:** Normalized objectives (norm‑*) mitigate sensitivity to absolute variance differences between domains but lose unit comparability. Use when total variance Tr(Σₑ) varies widely; consider regret if noise levels differ.
  - **Joint vs. sequential:** The greedy sequential construction of Section A.2 is not optimal for the joint worst‑case objective; only the joint solution has the theoretical guarantee.
  - **Matrix completion:** Theory assumes source fully observed. If sources also have missingness, the empirical algorithm still works but offers no guarantee; consider thresholding missingness levels.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Optimization divergence** – Poor conditioning if active domain changes abruptly; mitigations: learning rate decay, projection via SVD after each step.
  - **Active domain collapse** – Worst‑case dominated by a single tiny‑variance domain (minPCA) or a huge‑variance domain (maxRCS). Check domain‑wise losses; consider regret or normalized variant.
  - **Non‑robust test performance** – Target domain with covariance outside conv({Σₑ}) will exhibit higher loss; detect by computing worst‑case loss on a held‑out target and comparing to training bound.
  - **Matrix completion instability** – If Assumption 2 (missingness bound) violated, inductive error can blow up; monitor condition number of V’s submatrix on observed coordinates.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate Figure 3** – Sample 5 sources from Q_{α,β}, sample 50 targets from convex hull, compute V_maxRCS and V_pool. Plot reconstruction errors on targets; verify all ≤ worst‑case source error bound.
  2. **Replicate Figure 4** – Vary α, β to control domain heterogeneity; compute relative Δ average and Δ worst‑case for maxRCS vs poolPCA. Expect worse average but improved worst‑case.
  3. **Replicate Figure 6** – Introduce heterogeneous noise σₑ ~ U([0,0.1]); compare maxRCS vs maxRegret (rank‑10 and rank‑5) in worst‑case test RCS. Expect maxRegret to be noise‑invariant, maxRCS to degrade.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the out-of-sample worst-case optimality guarantees for matrix completion be extended beyond the assumption of fully observed source domains to settings where source domains also have missing entries?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors state that "extending the out-of-sample guarantees to jointly handle domain heterogeneity and missingness in the source domains is left to future work." While simulations in Section 5.2 show that maxMC retains benefits even with 90% missing entries in sources, no theoretical justification exists for this empirical observation.
- **Why unresolved**: Theorem 13 relies on the reduction of maxMC to maxRCS when sources are fully observed, enabling the use of convex hull robustness. With partial source data, the optimization becomes more complex and the proof technique no longer applies, leaving a gap between empirical performance and theory.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A proof (or counterexample) establishing that maxMC under partially observed sources still achieves approximate worst-case optimality over a suitably defined uncertainty set, possibly under additional assumptions about missingness patterns or sample sizes.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can the worst-case PCA framework be meaningfully extended to nonlinear representation learning (e.g., autoencoders) while retaining provable robustness guarantees?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors identify as a promising direction: "worst-case objectives can be used for nonlinear representation learning (e.g., autoencoders) by replacing the reconstruction loss with an appropriate domain-wise worst-case criterion."
- **Why unresolved**: wcPCA leverages linear algebra (orthonormal matrices, covariances) for tractable optimization and convex-hull robustness. Nonlinear models (neural networks) break this structure: objectives become non-convex, representations are not linear subspaces, and second-moment assumptions no longer suffice. It is unclear how to formulate the worst-case objective or prove generalization.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A proposed worst-case autoencoder architecture with a tractable training scheme (e.g., adversarial domain sampling) and a theoretical guarantee showing robustness to distribution shifts over a set of domains, possibly under representational capacity assumptions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What are the statistical trade-offs between average and worst-case performance when interpolating between pooled PCA and a worst-case variant (e.g., via convex combinations or regularized objectives)?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors suggest considering "interpolations between the pooled and worst-case objectives that trade average and worst-case performance, for instance via convex combinations or regularized formulations."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper exclusively studies the two extremes. Interpolation introduces a new tuning parameter but its influence is not understood: How does the solution vary continuously? What is the Pareto frontier between average and worst-case errors? Could an inappropriate interpolation hurt both? No theory guides the choice of the interpolation strength.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Analytical derivation of the solution path as the interpolation weight varies, convergence rates for both average and worst-case losses, and confidence sets for the weight that achieve a desired robustness level. This would provide principled selection criteria.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Under what conditions on the source covariances do the different worst-case PCA objectives (minPCA, maxRCS, maxRegret) yield the same solution, and how do these conditions relate to domain heterogeneity and noise structure?
- **Basis in paper**: Theorem 5 shows that the objectives generally differ. However, in simulations (Section 5.1.2) all coincide when the domain-specific eigenvalues are shared across domains and covariances are trace-normalized. The paper does not characterize when equivalence holds beyond such special cases.
- **Why unresolved**: Understanding equivalence conditions would help practitioners choose between objectives based on structural assumptions about their domains. For instance, if data are known to be homoscedastic (same total variance), unnormalized and normalized objectives coincide; if noise levels are heterogeneous, regret-based objectives may be more robust. A precise characterization is missing.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A theorem stating necessary and sufficient conditions (e.g., on eigenspaces, eigenvalues, or trace ratios) for the solution sets of the various wcPCA variants to be identical. Such characterizations would clarify the robustness properties of each objective under different domain heterogeneity scenarios.

## Limitations
- The robustness guarantee requires target covariances to lie in the convex hull of source covariances; real-world domains may violate this, leading to unaddressed extrapolation risks.
- Finite-sample consistency assumes a unique population optimum and sufficient per-domain samples; violations (e.g., degenerate covariances) could break convergence.
- Regret invariance relies on isotropic noise; non‑isotropic noise may degrade performance.

## Confidence
- **Worst-case optimality (convex-hull setting)**: High
- **Extrapolation beyond convex hull**: Low
- **Finite-sample consistency**: Medium (requires unique optimum and sufficient samples)
- **Regret invariance under heterogeneous isotropic noise**: Medium‑High
