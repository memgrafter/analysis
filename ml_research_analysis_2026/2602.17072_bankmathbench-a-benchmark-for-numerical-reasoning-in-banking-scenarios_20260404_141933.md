## Executive Summary

Current mathematical and financial reasoning benchmarks fail to capture the domain‑specific challenges of everyday banking, such as multi‑product comparisons and conditional calculations. To address this gap, the authors introduce BankMathBench, a high‑quality dataset containing 13,839 Korean and English problems across three difficulty levels (basic, intermediate, advanced). The dataset was generated via an automated pipeline and validated by banking experts. Fine‑tuning open‑source LLMs on BankMathBench, especially with tool‑augmented supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) that executes formulas enclosed in `<calc>` tags, leads to substantial accuracy increases: average gains of 57.6 pp (basic), 75.1 pp (intermediate), and 62.9 pp (advanced) on held‑out test sets. The results demonstrate the dataset’s effectiveness in improving formula generation and multi‑step computational accuracy, with tool integration particularly resolving reasoning bottlenecks on the most complex intermediate tasks.

## Method Summary

BankMathBench was constructed through a three‑stage automated pipeline. First, GPT‑4o generated diverse banking questions (covering deposits, savings, and loans) at controlled difficulty levels, with data augmentation to increase numerical variety. Second, corresponding solutions—expressed as formulas, LaTeX, or Python code—were produced and automatically verified for correctness. Third, reasoning traces were created by wrapping computable expressions in `<calc>` tags and intermediate narrative in `<think>` tags, culminating in an `<answer>` block. This yielded 13,839 Korean/English instances split into 11,074 training and 2,765 test examples.

Models were fine‑tuned using QLoRA (4‑bit, rank 16, α = 16, learning rate 2e‑4, batch size 4, 10 epochs) on the training set. For tool‑augmented inference, the model’s output was parsed for `<calc>` expressions, which were executed by an external calculator; the computed results were inserted into `<result>` tags before extracting the final `<answer>`. Evaluation employed exact‑match accuracy with normalization for equivalent numeric expressions. Some reproduction details remain unspecified: the exact dataset JSON schema, the calculator tool’s sandbox and error handling, and the full prompt templates used in the generation stages.

## Key Results

- Fine‑tuning on BankMathBench yields large accuracy gains over zero‑shot: average increases of 57.6 pp (basic), 75.1 pp (intermediate), and 62.9 pp (advanced).
- The intermediate level (multi‑product comparisons) shows the largest improvement with tool augmentation, indicating a resolution of aggregation bottlenecks.
- Even without tool augmentation, SFT reduces error ratios dramatically (e.g., Korean error ratio from 100.00 to 1.28), demonstrating enhanced internal numerical precision.
