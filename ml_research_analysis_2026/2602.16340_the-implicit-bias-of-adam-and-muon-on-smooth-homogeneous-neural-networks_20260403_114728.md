# The Implicit Bias of Adam and Muon on Smooth Homogeneous Neural Networks
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.16340
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16340
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Adam (without stability constant) maximizes ℓ∞ margin; momentum-steepest descent algorithms (Muon, MomentumGD, Signum) converge to KKT points of max-margin problems with norms matching their update rules; composite algorithms maximize hybrid norms; experiments on two-layer MNIST confirm directional convergence and norm-specific margins.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes the implicit bias of popular optimization algorithms—Adam, Muon, MomentumGD, and Signum—on smooth homogeneous neural networks. The authors extend the theory of steepest descent to normalized versions with decaying learning rates, proving that these algorithms converge in direction to Karush‑Kuhn‑Tucker (KKT) points of norm‑dependent max‑margin problems. The specific bias depends on the optimizer: ℓ2 for normalized gradient descent (NGD), spectral (max‑singular value) for Muon, ℓ∞ for Signum and Adam (ε=0), and hybrid norms for composite variants. Theoretical guarantees rely on smoothness, homogeneity, realizability, and learning‑rate conditions (integrating to infinity and appropriate decay). 

Experimental validation on two‑layer neural networks trained with exponential loss on binary MNIST confirms the predictions. Each optimizer is shown to maximize its intended margin, and directional convergence (cosine similarity >0.99) validates the underlying assumptions. The findings provide a geometric explanation for the strong generalization of these methods without explicit regularization.

## Method Summary
The analysis builds on continuous‑time limits and discrete approximations. First, the authors generalize steepest‑descent implicit bias to normalized steepest descent with optional learning‑rate schedules, showing that for smooth homogeneous models the direction converges to a KKT point of the ∥·∥‑max‑margin problem. Next, they prove that momentum‑based algorithms (Muon, MomentumGD, Signum) are approximate steepest descent under suitable decay conditions, leading to similar convergence with norm‑specific margins. For Adam (ε=0), they adapt the framework using a uniform bound on bias‑corrected moments, establishing ℓ∞‑margin maximization. Composite optimizers inherit hybrid norm biases. The theoretical assumptions include smoothness, homogeneity, realizability, and learning‑rate constraints (∫η=∞, η=o(t^{1/L-1}) for momentum; non‑increasing for Adam). Experiments on two‑layer networks with exponential loss on MNIST verify all claims by measuring margin norms and directional cosine similarity.

## Key Results
- Momentum-steepest descent algorithms (Muon, MomentumGD, Signum) converge to KKT points of max-margin problems with norms matching their update rules: spectral for Muon, ℓ2 for MomentumGD, ℓ∞ for Signum.
- Adam without the stability constant (ε=0) is proven to maximize the ℓ∞ margin on smooth homogeneous networks.
- Composite algorithms (Muon‑Signum, Muon‑Adam) maximize hybrid norms; experiments with two‑layer MNIST classifiers confirm all biases and show directional convergence (cosine similarity >0.99).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Claim: Normalized steepest descent with a learning rate schedule that integrates to infinity converges in direction to a KKT point of the ∥·∥‑max‑margin problem on smooth homogeneous networks.
Mechanism: The algorithm ensures the soft margin eγ(θ_t) never decreases once the loss falls below ℓ(0), and the loss decays to zero because ∫ η = ∞. A chain‑rule argument links the alignment ⟨θ_t, -g_t⟩ to the rate of margin growth. With the loss vanishing, ∥θ_t∥ → ∞ and the normalized parameters converge to a direction satisfying the KKT conditions.
Core assumption: (R1) initial loss < ℓ(0); (LR‑NSD) ∫_0^∞ η(t) dt = ∞; model locally Lipschitz, C¹‑stratifiable and homogeneous.
Evidence anchors: [abstract] “We first extend existing results on the implicit bias of steepest descent… to normalized steepest descent with an optional learning rate schedule.” [section 3.1] Theorems 3.1 & 3.2. [corpus] Tsilivis et al. (2025) studied unnormalized steepest descent; this work extends those ideas.
Break condition: If the learning rate integrates to a finite value (e.g., constant η for a finite number of steps) then ∥θ_t∥ may converge and loss may not vanish, freezing the soft margin. Violation of realizability (no θ with L<ℓ(0)) prevents monotonic margin increase. Non‑C¹‑stratifiable models (e.g., ReLU) need extra convergence of subgradients (assumption T3), which may fail.

### Mechanism 2
Claim: Momentum‑based normalized steepest descent algorithms (Muon, MomentumGD, Signum) act as approximate steepest descent under a decaying learning rate, and therefore also converge to KKT points of norm‑dependent max‑margin problems.
Mechanism: The momentum estimate m_t tracks the instantaneous gradient g_t on coordinates that are momentarily large (Lemma C.19). This yields asymptotic alignment ⟨dθ_t/dt, -m_t⟩ ≈ ∥dθ_t/dt∥·∥g_t∥_*, i.e. approximate steepest descent. The formal Approximate Steepest Descent framework (Definition 5.1) together with directional convergence (T2) then guarantees that the limit direction satisfies KKT for the margin associated with the chosen norm (spectral for Muon, ℓ₂ for MomentumGD, ℓ∞ for Signum). Composite variants (Muon‑Signum) use the max‑norm and inherit the same bias.
Core assumption: (M1) smoothness, (M2) homogeneity, (LR‑MSD) η = o(t^{1/L-1}) and ∫ η = ∞, (T1) norm bounded away from zero, (T2) directional convergence, (T3) normalized model subgradients converge (automatic for smooth models).
Evidence anchors: [abstract] “We show that for smooth homogeneous models, momentum steepest descent algorithms like Muon… are approximate steepest descent trajectories… proving that these algorithms too have a bias towards KKT points of the corresponding margin maximization problem.” [section 5.2] Asymptic momentum‑gradient relations (Lemma C.19). [section 3.2] Theorem 3.3 and Corollaries 3.4‑3.5. [corpus] Fan et al. (2025) studied implicit bias of spectral descent on linear models; this work generalizes to smooth homogeneous networks with momentum.
Break condition: If the learning rate decays too slowly (η not o(t^{1/L-1})), the momentum tracking in Lemma C.19 may not hold and the approximate SD property could fail. For non‑smooth models (e.g., ReLU) the additional assumption T3 (normalized subgradients converge) is needed; if the limit direction lands on a stratum boundary where sign patterns flip, the argument breaks. Also, without directional convergence (T2) the KKT claim cannot be made.

### Mechanism 3
Claim: Adam without the stability constant (c₁ ≥ c₂) under a non‑increasing, integrating learning rate schedule maximizes the ℓ∞ margin on smooth homogeneous networks.
Mechanism: The Adam update uses bias‑corrected moments (line 8). Under decaying η and positive initial per‑coordinate gradients (A1), the ratio ˆm_t/√(ˆv_t) approaches sign(g_t) on coordinates that matter (mg. Lemma C.19). Consequently Adam behaves like normalized sign gradient descent (steepest descent w.r.t. ℓ∞). The Approximate Steepest Descent analysis is adapted with ν(t)=η(t); a key uniform bound on the averaged ratio (Lemma B.10, inspired by Zhang et al., 2024) ensures R_max ≤ 1. Hence the normalized direction converges to a KKT point of the ℓ∞‑max‑margin problem.
Core assumption: (M1) smoothness, (M2) homogeneity, (LR‑Adam) η non‑increasing, ∫ η = ∞, η ≤ o(t^{1/L-1}); (T1), (T2), (T3); plus initialization (A1) all gradient components squared > ρ on some early interval.
Evidence anchors: [abstract] “We extend the analysis to Adam (without the stability constant), which maximizes the ℓ∞ margin.” [section 3.3] Theorem 3.6. [section C.7] detailed proof. [corpus] Zhang et al. (2024) proved ℓ∞ bias for Adam on linear binary classification; this paper extends the result to smooth homogeneous networks.
Break condition: Introducing a large stability constant ε alters the dynamics and breaks the sign‑approximation. Non‑monotone η or fast decay violating ∫ η = ∞ can prevent loss decay, violating the approximation. If (A1) fails (some gradient coordinate stays zero), Adam may become ill‑defined or deviate. Non‑smooth models require T3; if normalized subgradients do not converge, the analysis likely does not hold.

## Foundational Learning
### Implicit bias
Why needed: The paper explains why popular optimizers (Adam, Muon) generalize well without explicit regularization. Understanding that optimization algorithms have an inherent preference (bias) for solutions with large margins or other favorable properties is central to interpreting the results.
Quick check: If an optimizer has an implicit bias toward ℓ∞ margin, what happens to the model’s robustness to ℓ∞ adversarial perturbations?

### Homogeneity
Why needed: Many standard neural nets (deep linear, quadratic activations, smooth ReLU variants) are homogeneous: f(x;αθ)=α^L f(x;θ). This scaling symmetry lets the analysis focus on the direction θ/∥θ∥ and relates parameter growth to margin increase.
Quick check: Given L‑homogeneous f, what is the derivative d/dt f(x;tθ) at t=1? How does it link to ⟨θ, ∇_θ f(x;θ)⟩?

### Steepest descent w.r.t. a norm
Why needed: Different optimizers implement steepest descent under different norms. Sign GD ⇔ steepest descent w.r.t. ℓ∞, Muon ⇔ max‑spectral norm, etc. This geometric view unifies the analysis and predicts which margin each optimizer maximizes.
Quick check: What subgradient equation defines normalized steepest descent for a given norm ∥·∥? How does the dual norm appear in that condition?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
- **Model**: Smooth homogeneous neural net f(x;θ) (e.g., deep linear, quadratic activation, squared ReLU).
- **Loss**: Log‑concave, exponentially tailed loss ℓ(y f)=e^{-φ(y f)} with φ C¹, bounded derivatives (exponential, logistic).
- **Optimizer**: Implementations of Muon (matrix‑wise orthogonalized updates), Adam (bias‑corrected moments), or variants (Signum, NGD, Muon‑Adam).
- **LR scheduler**: Decaying η(t) satisfying ∫ η = ∞ and η = o(t^{1/L-1}) for momentum methods; non‑increasing for Adam.
- **Training loop**: Simulate continuous‑time dynamics (or discrete approximations), monitor loss, norm, directional convergence (cosine similarity).

Critical path:
1. Verify problem assumptions: model homogeneity L, smoothness, realizability (∃θ₀ with L<ℓ(0)).
2. Choose optimizer → determines implicit norm (ℓ₂, ℓ∞, spectral, or hybrid).
3. Use decaying LR that integrates to infinity; for Adam ensure non‑increasing and initial positive gradients per coordinate.
4. Check trajectory satisfies T1 (norm bounded away from 0) and T2 (directional convergence); if model non‑smooth also check T3.
5. If conditions hold, expect parameter direction to converge to a KKT point of min∥θ∥ s.t. y_i f(x_i;θ)≥1, i.e., a max‑margin solution in the chosen norm.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Norm choice**: Influences which margin is maximized. ℓ₂ bias (NGD) favors robustness to ℓ₂ attacks; ℓ∞ bias (Adam, Signum) favors ℓ∞ robustness; spectral norm (Muon) may benefit deep network spectral properties.
- **LR decay**: Must integrate to ∞ to force loss → 0, yet decay fast enough (o(t^{1/L-1})) so momentum tracks gradients; too slow risks divergence, too fast stalls.
- **Momentum parameters**: Adam typically c₁≥c₂ (β₁≤β₂) to resemble sign descent; Muon uses exact orthogonalization (no intra‑step smoothing) but still needs momentum across steps.
- **Composite optimizers** (Muon‑Adam): Mixing norms yields hybrid margin constraint; relative base learning rates η_M, η_A control trade‑off between spectral (first layers) and ℓ∞ (last layer) behavior.

Failure signatures:
- Loss plateaus above a small threshold → either LR integral finite or model not realizing zero loss; revisit realizability or η schedule.
- Directional noise (cosine similarity oscillates) → likely too aggressive η or insufficient decay; check LR conditions.
- Observed margin does not match predicted norm → optimizer mismatch or implementation bug (e.g., Muon without proper SVD, Adam with large ε).
- Adam with large ε → stability constant dominates, reverting to GD‑like behavior; also singularity if v_t[j] stays zero and (A1) violated.

First 3 experiments:
1. Train two‑layer homogeneous nets on binary MNIST (even vs odd) with exponential loss, using NGD, MomentumGD, Signum, Muon, Adam, Muon‑Adam. Record margin values (ℓ₂, ℓ∞, msp) across training; confirm each optimizer increases its attributed norm margin (e.g., Adam → ℓ∞, Muon → msp) and that the corresponding margin saturates near the end.
2. Monitor directional convergence: compute cosine similarity ⟨θ_t/∥θ_t∥, θ_last/∥θ_last∥⟩ for each optimizer. Expect >0.99 after half the training (as in Fig. 1b), verifying condition (T2).
3. Compare Muon‑Adam to Muon and Adam. Measure hybrid margin max{ (η_A/η_M)·∥W∥_msp, ∥u∥_∞ } (where W is first layer, u is output). Observe that Muon‑Adam interpolates between the behaviors, supporting Theorem 3.7. Optionally vary η_A/η_M to explore trade‑offs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions, but its identified limitations and suggested future directions imply several:
- **Extension to non‑smooth models**: The theory currently requires smooth homogeneous networks; extending it to piecewise linear activations (e.g., ReLU) requires handling subgradient convergence (assumption T3), which is non‑trivial.
- **Learning‑rate conditions**: The requirement η = o(t^{1/L-1}) for momentum methods may be too restrictive in practice; a more nuanced analysis could relax this condition.
- **Adam with ε>0**: The analysis assumes ε=0 to recover sign‑descent behavior. It remains an open question whether the ℓ∞ bias persists when the standard stability constant is included.
- **Composite optimizers**: Theoretical confidence for hybrid‑norm maximization (e.g., Muon‑Adam) is currently low; a rigorous treatment of these algorithms is needed.

These questions stem directly from the limitations discussed in Section Limitations and point to promising research avenues.

## Limitations
- **Smooth homogeneous models**: Excludes ReLU networks; non‑smooth extension needs additional convergence of subgradients (T3), which is not guaranteed.
- **Learning‑rate restrictions**: Momentum methods require η that integrates to ∞ and decays as o(t^{1/L-1}); Adam requires non‑increasing η and positive initial per‑coordinate gradients. Schedules may often violate these.
- **Algorithmic gaps**: Adam analysis assumes ε=0 and strong initialization (A1), unlike standard Adam; composite optimizers’ theoretical confidence is low.

## Confidence
- **Normalized steepest descent bias (Thm 3.1/3.2)**: High.
- **Momentum‑based approximate steepest descent (Thm 3.3, Cor. 3.4‑3.5)**: Medium.
- **Adam (ε=0) ℓ∞‑margin maximization (Thm 3.6)**: Medium.
- **Composite optimizers hybrid norm (Thm 3.7)**: Low.
- **Experimental validation (directional convergence)**: Medium.
