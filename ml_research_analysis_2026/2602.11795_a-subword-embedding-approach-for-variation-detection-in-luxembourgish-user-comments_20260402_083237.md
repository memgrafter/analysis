# A Subword Embedding Approach for Variation Detection in Luxembourgish User Comments

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.11795
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11795
- **Reference count**: 3
- **Primary result**: Unsupervised clustering of subword embeddings identifies ~800 linguistically coherent variant families in 1.42M Luxembourgish user comments, aligning with known dialectal patterns and revealing undocumented variation.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of detecting lexical and orthographic variation in low-resource languages, where such variation is often noisy and unstructured. Using Luxembourgish user comments as a case study, the authors train FastText subword embeddings on raw text and group variant forms via a hybrid similarity measure combining cosine similarity and character n-gram Jaccard overlap. The approach induces around 800 variant families across seven categories (e.g., orthographic, morphological, regional) without predefined normalization rules or dictionaries. Results show families correspond to established dialectal splits and uncover previously undocumented regional and lexical clusters, demonstrating the viability of distributional methods for analyzing language variety in informal, multilingual contexts.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds in four steps: (1) train FastText embeddings on a large corpus of raw Luxembourgish user comments (1.42M comments), leveraging subword information to represent rare and misspelled forms; (2) for each word type, compute pairwise similarity using a weighted combination of cosine similarity (semantic closeness) and character n-gram Jaccard overlap (orthographic resemblance); (3) cluster word types based on this hybrid similarity without supervision or predefined variant lists; (4) manually categorize the resulting clusters into linguistic families (e.g., orthographic variants, regional forms). No external lexical resources or gold-standard variant lists are used, making the approach fully data-driven and adaptable to other low-resource settings.

## Key Results
- Induced approximately 800 variant families covering seven linguistic categories (orthographic, morphological, regional, etc.) from 1.42M comments.
- Families largely align with known Luxembourgish dialectal patterns (e.g., Moselle-Franconian vs. Ripuarian splits).
- Uncovered undocumented variation, including regional variants and lexical clusters not captured in existing resources.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach succeeds because subword embeddings (FastText) inherently model morphological and orthographic variation through character n-grams, allowing rare or non-standard forms to acquire meaningful vectors. Distributional semantics ensures that words appearing in similar contexts (e.g., dialectal variants) cluster together. The hybrid similarity measure (cosine + Jaccard) balances semantic equivalence with surface resemblance, mitigating the tendency of pure cosine similarity to group unrelated words that share contexts. Processing raw user comments avoids normalization biases and captures naturally occurring variation as it appears in informal communication.

## Foundational Learning
- **Subword Embeddings (FastText)**: Needed to represent out-of-vocabulary and morphologically rich forms in low-resource languages. *Quick check*: Inspect nearest neighbors for known variant pairs (e.g., "gouf" vs. "gouf")—they should appear close in vector space.
- **Cosine Similarity**: Measures semantic relatedness from distributional contexts. *Quick check*: Validate that synonyms or hypernyms yield higher cosine scores than unrelated terms.
- **Jaccard Overlap (Character N-grams)**: Captures orthographic similarity independent of semantics. *Quick check*: Compute Jaccard for known orthographic variants (e.g., "anner" vs. "aner")—expect high overlap.
- **Distributional Hypothesis**: Underlying theory that words in similar contexts have similar meanings. *Quick check*: Confirm that variant forms co-occur with similar function words and topics in the corpus.
- **Clustering without Supervision**: Allows discovery of unknown variation patterns. *Quick check*: Apply a simple agglomerative clusterer to a subset and assess cluster coherence qualitatively.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Raw Text -> FastText Training -> Similarity Calculation (cosine + Jaccard) -> Clustering -> Variant Families
- **Critical path**: FastText embedding quality directly determines similarity scores; clustering outcome depends entirely on the hybrid similarity matrix.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Hybrid similarity improves recall of variant groups but may conflate distinct linguistic phenomena (e.g., orthographic vs. morphological). Unsupervised design avoids bias from predefined lists but sacrifices precision and requires expert post-hoc validation.
- **Failure signatures**: Families mixing semantically unrelated terms; high sensitivity to embedding hyperparameters (dimension, window size); clusters dominated by topical rather than dialectal variation (e.g., slang clusters from specific domains).
- **First experiments**:
  1. Vary FastText embedding dimensions (100, 200, 300) and measure adjusted Rand index between clusterings to assess stability.
  2. Systematically tune the Jaccard threshold (e.g., 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) and evaluate a sample of families for categorical purity (orthographic vs. morphological).
  3. Run the pipeline on a held-out subset of comments and compare family membership against a small manually annotated gold standard (if available) or expert intuition.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on qualitative alignment with known dialectal patterns and expert intuition, lacking quantitative benchmarks (e.g., gold-standard variant lists).
- The hybrid similarity measure may conflate distinct linguistic phenomena (orthographic, morphological, regional) within single families.
- The domain-specific user comment corpus may bias variation toward informal, topical lexicon rather than systematic dialectal patterns.
- The Luxembourgish training corpus is not disclosed as publicly available, hindering independent replication.
