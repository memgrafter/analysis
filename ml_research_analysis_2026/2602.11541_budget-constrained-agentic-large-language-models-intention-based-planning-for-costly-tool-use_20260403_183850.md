# Budget-Constrained Agentic Large Language Models: Intention-Based Planning for Costly Tool Use

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11541  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11541  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** INTENT achieves 100% feasible rate (no budget overruns) and improves pass rates to 63.8% (GPT-4.1 mini) and 76.0% (GPT-5 nano) on cost-augmented StableToolBench.

## Executive Summary
Agents completing multi-step tasks with external tools must adhere to strict monetary budgets. Current LLM agents often violate budgets due to stochastic tool outcomes and a lack of forward planning about cost. INTENT addresses this by using an inference-time planning framework with a learned intention-aware world model. It predicts the probability a tool call satisfies the agent's semantic intention, applies a risk-adjusted geometric cost estimator (expected cost = price / p_success), and reuses rollouts via caching to remain lightweight. Hard budget constraints are enforced by accepting only actions whose projected total risk-adjusted cost stays within the remaining budget. On the cost-augmented StableToolBench benchmark, INTENT attains a 100% feasible rate and significantly higher pass rates (63.8% - 76.0%) compared to all baselines, with only moderate overhead (1.23× time, 1.76× latency, 1.70× tokens). It also shows robustness to price fluctuations, new tools, and varying budget levels.

## Method Summary
INTENT is an inference-time oracle that integrates three lightweight, learned components derived from interaction logs: a Language World Model (W_ϕ) for predicting tool observations; an Intention Predictor (W_ρ) that estimates the probability ρ that a tool call satisfies the agent's semantic intention; and a Conditional Generator (W_ψ) that generates observations under successful outcomes (z=1). During each agent step, the oracle first checks caches (Rollout Cache and Last Call Cache) to avoid redundant simulations. If no cache hit, it simulates an ideal trajectory by forcing z=1 at all future steps using W_ψ, predicts success probabilities ρ_k for each tool call via W_ρ, computes risk-adjusted costs c_k = price / ρ_k (assuming a geometric distribution of retries), and sums them total σ. The proposed action is accepted only if its immediate cost ≤ remaining budget and γ·σ ≤ remaining budget (γ is a risk factor). Accepted actions are cached for future reuse; rejections return a synthetic feedback trace containing predicted ρ and future actions to prompt re-planning. The agent policy π_θ remains unchanged; all components are trained separately on the MirrorAPI-Cache dataset (~100k trajectories).

## Key Results
- **100% feasible rate:** INTENT never exceeds the budget across all test tasks, guaranteeing hard constraint satisfaction.
- **State-of-the-art pass rates:** Achieves 63.8% (GPT-4.1 mini) and 76.0% (GPT-5 nano) on StableToolBench, outperforming the best baseline by up to 13% absolute.
- **Efficient enforcement:** Overhead remains moderate (1.23× time, 1.76× latency, 1.70× tokens vs. Raw) compared to competing enforcers like BATS (7.67× time).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** INTENT uses an intention-aware world model to predict whether tool calls satisfy the agent's semantic intention, enabling stable cost estimation under stochastic outcomes.
- **Mechanism:** The world model decomposes outcome prediction into (1) an intention predictor estimating success probability ρ from reasoning trace and tool call, and (2) a conditional generator producing outputs when intention is satisfied (z=1). During lookahead, it forces z=1 at all steps to simulate an ideal trajectory where the plan executes without failures, revealing the latent plan.
- **Core assumption:** The agent's high-level plan is coherent and encoded in its reasoning; tool success is defined purely by semantic alignment with that intention, not by factual accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "leverages an intention-aware hierarchical world model to anticipate future tool usage"; [section]: Section 3.3 introduces probabilistic decomposition and ideal trajectory simulation; [corpus]: Corpus neighbors address budget-constrained tool planning but not intention-awareness; evidence is indirect.
- **Break condition:** If the intention predictor is miscalibrated or the conditional generator produces unrealistic outputs when z=1, the ideal trajectory misrepresents the true cost, leading to incorrect accept/reject decisions and budget failures.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** INTENT applies a risk-adjusted geometric cost estimator (expected cost = price / p_success) to account for stochastic tool retries, providing a conservative upper bound on total expenditure.
- **Mechanism:** For each tool call in the ideal trajectory, its cost is divided by the predicted success probability ρ_k, assuming a geometric distribution of retries. The sum σ = Σ (c_k / ρ_k) is scaled by risk factor γ and compared to the remaining budget B_t.
- **Core assumption:** The number of trials until success follows a geometric distribution with constant probability ρ_k across retries, and no information is gained from failed attempts that would increase ρ.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "applies a risk-adjusted geometric cost estimator (expected cost = price/p_success)"; [section]: Section 3.3 derives the geometric cost calibration and introduces γ for risk adjustment; [corpus]: No direct evidence; corpus focuses on budget allocation strategies, not geometric cost models.
- **Break condition:** If success probabilities increase with retries (e.g., due to argument refinement) or are state-dependent, the geometric model overestimates costs, causing unnecessary rejections and reduced pass rates.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** INTENT improves inference efficiency by caching rollout trajectories and reusing them via plan continuity detection, avoiding redundant simulations.
- **Mechanism:** Upon accepting an action, the oracle caches the future action sequence from the ideal trajectory. At the next step, if the agent's proposed action matches the cached one (with tolerance), the oracle skips simulation and directly approves, assuming the remaining budget constraint remains valid.
- **Core assumption:** Tool call semantics are stable; identical or similar tool invocations have consistent success probabilities, and a matching action implies the underlying plan is unchanged.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "reuses rollouts via caching to stay lightweight"; [section]: Appendix A.2 details Rollout Cache and Last Call Cache mechanisms; [corpus]: No corpus evidence provided for caching; this is an engineering optimization from the paper.
- **Break condition:** False positives (matching actions with different intentions) approve risky tools; frequent cache invalidation due to plan shifts negates efficiency gains and increases overhead.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Agent Policy πθ → Language World Model Wϕ → Intention Predictor Wρ & Conditional Generator Wψ → Oracle (INTENT) → Environment E.  
Supporting mechanisms: Rollout Cache, Last Call Cache, Blacklist.

**Critical path:**  
1. At step t, agent proposes action a_t after reasoning r_t based on history h_t.  
2. Oracle checks caches: if a_t matches Rollout Cache or Last Call Cache, accept immediately.  
3. Else, simulate ideal trajectory: repeatedly call W_ψ (with z=1) to get o_k, then sample next (r_{k+1}, a_{k+1}) from π_θ until termination.  
4. For each simulated tool a_k, get ρ_k from W_ρ on (r_k, a_k); compute c_k = price(a_k) / ρ_k.  
5. Compute total risk-adjusted cost γ·Σ c_k.  
6. Accept if price(a_t) ≤ B_t and γ·Σ c_k ≤ B_t; then cache future actions. Reject otherwise, return feedback with predicted trace.  
7. On rejection, agent re-plans; on acceptance, history updates with real observation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Rollout Depth vs. Overhead:** Deeper lookahead improves cost estimate accuracy but increases latency; caching mitigates by reusing simulations for plan continuity.  
- **Risk Factor γ:** Higher γ emphasizes budget safety, reducing feasible rate but preventing overruns; lower γ encourages exploration, improving pass rate but risking violations.  
- **Blacklist Threshold δ:** Lower δ aggressively removes tools with ρ<δ, reducing variance but potentially discarding useful tools; higher δ retains more tools but may increase rejections.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High rejection rate (>10%) with low pass rate: γ too high or intention predictor miscalibrated (ρ inaccurate).  
- Budget overruns despite oracle: Geometric model invalid (e.g., retries improve ρ) or blacklist disabled; check ρ calibration and δ.  
- Low cache hit rate (<30%): Plan instability frequent (agent changes tools often), or blacklist too aggressive preventing continuity; adjust δ or γ.  
- Disproportionate overhead (time >2× raw): Cache misses high due to aggressive replanning; consider enabling caches or reducing rollouts.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Main Evaluation:** Compare INTENT against Soft (RAW, PROMPT) and Enforce (DFSDT, BTP, BATS) baselines on StableToolBench using Pass Rate, Feasible Rate, and efficiency metrics (Table 1). Shows INTENT achieves 100% feasible rate and highest pass rate with moderate overhead.  
2. **Robustness Tests:** Vary tool prices, introduce new tools via training data fractions, and scale budget levels (Figures 3-5). Demonstrates INTENT maintains performance under market dynamics, unlike PROMPT.  
3. **Ablation Studies:** Remove core components (intention predictor variants, conditional generator settings) and additional mechanisms (caches, blacklist) (Tables 3 and 4). Confirms necessity of calibrated intention prediction and ideal trajectory simulation, and efficiency gains from caching.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does INTENT's computational overhead scale gracefully with the number of available tools in the marketplace?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Experiments use ~20 retrieved tools; real tool markets contain thousands, and the efficiency of rollout cache and blacklist mechanisms at larger scales is not evaluated.
- **Why unresolved:** Larger action spaces may increase cache misses and blacklist maintenance cost, potentially undermining INTENT's lightweight claim.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark with varying tool counts (e.g., 20, 100, 500, 1000) and measure time and latency overhead relative to RAW.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How robust is INTENT to systematic miscalibration in the intention predictor's success probability estimates?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] INTENT relies on a calibrated Wρ for risk-adjusted costing, but sensitivity to residual miscalibration is not studied.
- **Why unresolved:** Overconfident or underconfident ρ_t could cause frequent budget overruns or overly conservative rejections, yet the paper provides no analysis.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduce controlled biases or noise into ρ_t predictions and quantify impact on feasible rate and pass rate across task distribution.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the risk preference parameter γ be automatically adapted to task-specific characteristics instead of being manually fixed?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper sets γ=0.5 uniformly and shows it controls the cost-success trade-off.
- **Why unresolved:** Manual tuning is burdensome; an adaptive γ could improve performance without user intervention and handle varying budget tightness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze correlation between optimal γ (found via grid search) and task features (initial budget, tool price distribution, volatility); develop a meta-predictor for γ.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Exact LLM judge prompts for z annotation and final evaluation are unspecified; cache matching tolerance ("≈") and tool snapshot serialization format are underspecified.
- **Generalization assumptions:** The geometric cost model assumes constant retry success probability; real-world tools may improve with repeated attempts. Intention predictor calibration stability across domains is untested.
- **Efficiency uncertainty:** Overhead multipliers (1.23×–1.76×) depend on cache hit rates, which are sensitive to unspecified continuity thresholds.

## Confidence
- **High:** Feasibility & Efficacy – 100% feasible rate and pass rate gains on StableToolBench are well-supported by ablations and robustness tests.
- **Medium:** Mechanisms – Intention-aware world model and risk-adjusted costing are theoretically sound but rely on unverified calibration and caching efficiency in practice.
- **Low:** Efficiency Claims – Overhead multipliers depend on cache hit rates, which are sensitive to unspecified thresholds and plan continuity assumptions.
