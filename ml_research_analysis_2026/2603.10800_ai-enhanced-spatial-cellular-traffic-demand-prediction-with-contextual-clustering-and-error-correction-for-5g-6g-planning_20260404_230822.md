# AI-Enhanced Spatial Cellular Traffic Demand Prediction with Contextual Clustering and Error Correction for 5G/6G Planning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10800
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10800
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: A context‑aware two‑stage splitting strategy with spatial error correction reduces MAE from 1432.7 to 806.7 (44% reduction) on pooled city data, enabling more reliable bandwidth dimensioning and congestion‑risk assessment for 5G/6G planning.

## Executive Summary
Spatial autocorrelation in cellular traffic demand causes neighborhood leakage during random train/test splits, inflating prediction accuracy and compromising 5G/6G network planning. This paper introduces a context‑aware two‑stage splitting strategy to create spatially disjoint yet functionally representative folds, followed by a Spatial Error Model (SEM) that corrects residual spatial bias. Evaluated on five Canadian cities using crowdsourced data, the method consistently lowers mean absolute error (MAE) compared to location‑only clustering, and the error reduction translates directly into improved bandwidth dimensioning and congestion‑risk metrics.

## Method Summary
The framework begins by mapping heterogeneous geospatial layers (census, POI, land‑use) to a uniform grid (~1.5 km cells) and standardizing features. For splitting, Stage 1 applies k‑Means on cell centroids to form spatially cohesive blocks, ensuring separation beyond the dominant autocorrelation range identified by Moran’s I. Stage 2 refines each block using context‑feature dissimilarity to generate sub‑clusters that are spatially contained but functionally diverse; final folds are assembled from these sub‑clusters to guarantee both spatial independence and contextual mix. An XGBoost model is trained on these folds. Residuals, which may still exhibit spatial autocorrelation, are corrected via a Spatial Error Model (SEM): residuals are modeled as a spatial autoregressive process $\epsilon = \lambda W\epsilon + u$, with $W$ a spatial weights matrix, and the learned filter $(I - \lambda W)^{-1}$ adjusts predictions. Finally, prediction error is translated into planning‑oriented metrics: Bandwidth Dimensioning Error (BDE) scales linearly with MAE under constant spectral efficiency $\eta$ and proxy‑to‑traffic mapping $\kappa$, and congestion risk is computed as the fraction of cells where predicted demand exceeds capacity.

## Key Results
- MAE on pooled “All Cities” evaluation improves from 1432.7 (location‑only k‑means) to 989.9 (two‑stage splitting) and further to 806.7 after SEM correction.
- Two‑stage splitting yields folds with higher land‑use entropy, confirming contextual representativeness, and reduces Moran’s I on validation residuals.
- The MAE reduction corresponds to a ~44% decrease in bandwidth dimensioning error and lowers congestion‑risk estimates, making spectrum planning more reliable.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Context‑aware two‑stage splitting reduces spatial leakage by ensuring training and validation sets contain spatially disjoint and functionally representative subsets.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 uses k‑Means on cell centroids to create geographically cohesive blocks, separating spatially correlated neighborhoods. Stage 2 refines each block using context features (e.g., land‑use) to form sub‑clusters that are spatially contained but functionally diverse. Folds are then assembled from these sub‑clusters, guaranteeing both spatial separation and contextual mix.
- **Core assumption:** Spatial leakage is primarily mitigated when fold boundaries exceed the dominant spatial correlation range identified by Moran’s I, and when each fold samples the full range of land‑use contexts present in the city.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “…context‑aware two‑stage splitting strategy… reduces leakage and improves spatial generalization…”
  - [section IV] Describes the two‑stage procedure: “Stage 1 partitions… spatially cohesive blocks… Stage 2 refines… using context features… to improve representativeness…”
  - [corpus] Weak direct support; neighboring papers focus on spatio‑temporal modeling but do not address context‑aware fold construction for leakage mitigation.
- **Break condition:** If spatial correlation range varies sharply within a city beyond what global Moran’s I suggests, or if context features are insufficient to characterize functional diversity, residual leakage may persist.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Spatial Error Model (SEM) post‑processing corrects residual spatially structured bias left after leakage‑reduced splitting.
- **Mechanism:** After base prediction (XGBoost), residuals are modeled as a spatially autoregressive process: $\epsilon = \lambda W\epsilon + u$, where $W$ is a spatial weights matrix and $\lambda$ captures remaining spatial dependence. This spatial filter is applied to adjust predictions, penalizing geographically coherent errors.
- **Core assumption:** Unmodeled neighborhood effects and latent variables create residuals that are themselves spatially autocorrelated, and this structure can be captured by a linear spatial lag model.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “…with residual spatial error correction.”
  - [section IV.C] “Even under leakage‑reduced splitting, residuals can remain spatially correlated… A Spatial Error Model (SEM) is applied…”
  - [section V.B] Learning curves show SEM further reduces validation error, indicating it addresses structure not learned by XGBoost.
- **Break condition:** If residual spatial dependence is highly non‑linear or driven by unobserved variables not proxied by the spatial weights matrix $W$, SEM correction will be ineffective. SEM assumes a linear spatial process; strong non‑linear spatial bias would require a different correction model.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Translating MAE reduction into bandwidth dimensioning error (BDE) and congestion‑risk metrics makes prediction gains directly actionable for 5G/6G planning.
- **Mechanism:** Prediction error in the traffic‑demand proxy (MAE) propagates linearly to per‑cell bandwidth error via $B = (\kappa/\eta) \cdot |\hat{y} - y|$, where $\kappa$ maps proxy to traffic volume and $\eta$ is spectral efficiency. Congestion risk $P_{\text{cong}}(B)$ is computed as the fraction of cells where predicted demand exceeds capacity $B\cdot\eta$.
- **Core assumption:** Planning assumptions (constant $\eta^{(\delta)}$, linear proxy‑to‑traffic mapping $\kappa$) hold uniformly across space, and BDE scales proportionally with MAE.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section VI.A] “Under a constant planning assumption $\eta_i^{(\delta)} = \eta^{(\delta)}$, the mean absolute bandwidth dimensioning error (BDE) is proportional to MAE: $\text{BDE}(\delta) = \kappa/\eta^{(\delta)} \cdot \text{MAE}$.”
  - [section VI.B] Shows how MAE differences shift $P_{\text{cong}}(B)$ curves, making leakage reduction concretely impact planning risk assessment.
  - [corpus] No direct anchor; this translation is paper‑specific.
- **Break condition:** If spatial variation in $\eta^{(\delta)}$ or $\kappa$ is high and correlated with prediction error, the linear BDE approximation breaks down. Congestion risk assessment also assumes a static capacity $B$; dynamic scheduling would complicate the mapping.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Ingestion & Grid Mapping -> Spatial Diagnostic -> Two‑Stage Splitting -> Base Prediction -> SEM Correction -> Planning Translation
- **Critical path:** Moran’s I → Stage 1 k (cluster count/diameter) → Stage 2 context feature selection → SEM W (weights matrix definition) → planning parameters ($\eta$, $\kappa$). The Stage 1 k must be chosen so clusters are larger than the Moran’s I correlation range to ensure separation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Stage 1 k (number of spatial clusters):** Larger k → smaller clusters, stronger leakage reduction but risk of context‑homogeneous folds. Smaller k → more context diversity in each fold but risk of neighboring cells staying together. Use Moran’s I distance plot as guide.
  - **SEM W definition:** Distance threshold vs. k‑NN. Threshold must match the scale of residual autocorrelation (may differ from Stage 1 scale). k‑NN adapts to local density but adds parameter.
  - **Computational cost:** Two‑stage splitting is $O(n^2)$ for dissimilarity in Stage 2. SEM fitting requires inverting $(I - \lambda W)$, costly for large n. Consider approximate methods for city‑scale grids.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Leakage persists:** Validation MAE remains close to training MAE despite two‑stage splitting; Local Moran’s I on residuals still shows strong HH/LL patterns.
  - **Context‑imbalanced folds:** Some folds have only commercial/industrial land‑use, others only residential. Check feature distributions per fold.
  - **SEM overfitting/divergence:** $\lambda$ estimate near 1 or -1, or residual variance increases after correction. Indicates misspecified W or non‑stationary spatial process.
  - **BDE mismatch:** If real‑world bandwidth planning errors are much larger than BDE calculated from MAE, the proxy‑to‑traffic mapping $\kappa$ or $\eta^{(\delta)}$ assumptions are invalid across space.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Compute Global Moran’s I vs. distance for Montreal to identify the distance where I drops below 0.2. This sets a lower bound on Stage 1 cluster diameter.
  2. For k=5, compare land‑use entropy across folds between location‑only k‑Means and the two‑stage method; the latter should yield higher entropy.
  3. Train XGBoost with two‑stage splits; apply SEM and measure MAE change. Expect ~10–15% reduction (989.9 → 806.7) and reduced residual Moran’s I.

## Limitations

- SEM implementation lacks specifics: spatial weights matrix, $\lambda$ estimator, and regularization parameters are not described.
- Planning translation assumes constant spectral efficiency and linear proxy‑to‑traffic mapping, which likely varies spatially.
