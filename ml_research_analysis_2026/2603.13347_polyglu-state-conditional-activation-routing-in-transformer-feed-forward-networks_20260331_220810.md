# PolyGLU: State-Conditional Activation Routing in Transformer Feed-Forward Networks

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.13347  
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13347  
- **Reference count**: 18  
- **Primary result**: PolyGLU replaces SwiGLU with a dynamic per-neuron routing mechanism among four activations, achieving emergent near-deterministic selections and depth-dependent specialization with minimal overhead and strong data efficiency.

## Executive Summary
PolyGLU introduces a novel feed-forward design for transformers that enables state-conditional activation routing. Each neuron dynamically selects from ReLU, Tanh, SiLU, or GELU via a differentiable Gumbel‑Softmax mechanism that combines learned static preferences with input‑conditioned gating. Trained on ~10B tokens, the 597M‑parameter model exhibits emergent near‑deterministic routing (mean dynamic entropy 0.030% of maximum) without explicit sparsity regularization. A depth‑dependent specialization pattern emerges: early layers favor GELU while deep layers strongly prefer Tanh. The architecture adds only 0.23% parameter overhead and remains robust to fine‑tuning, achieving 62–89% of Qwen3‑0.6B‑Base performance despite using 3,600× fewer training tokens.

## Method Summary
PolyGLU modifies the SwiGLU activation within transformer feed‑forward networks. The core innovation is a per-neuron routing mechanism that selects one of four candidate activation functions. Routing logits are computed as ℓ = α + β ⊙ f(¯h), where α is a static per‑neuron preference tensor, β is a learnable scaling vector, and f is a lightweight 2‑layer MLP processing the mean‑pooled hidden state ¯h. A Gumbel‑Softmax sample with temperature annealing (τ from 1.0 → 0.1) yields discrete selection weights. The activation mix is Σ_k g_k σ_k(xW_gate) ⊙ (xW_up), integrating seamlessly into the pre‑norm residual structure. Training uses a 597M decoder‑only transformer on ~10.24B tokens from a curated mixture (math, web, Python) with AdamW, RMSNorm, RoPE, grouped‑query attention, FlashAttention 2, and gradient checkpointing. Only the gate MLP, α, and β are new parameters, adding 0.23% overhead.

## Key Results
- Emergent near‑deterministic routing: Without explicit sparsity loss, mean dynamic entropy settles at 0.030% of the maximum.
- Depth‑dependent specialization: Early layers predominantly select GELU; deep layers strongly favor Tanh.
- Parameter efficiency: Overhead of ~1.4M parameters (0.23%) while reaching 62–89% of Qwen3‑0.6B‑Base performance on 10 tasks despite 3,600× fewer tokens.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PolyGLU’s routing converges to near‑deterministic selections because temperature annealing and the language modeling loss create a positive feedback loop that favors commitment over soft mixing (Mechanism 1). This yields a depth‑dependent specialization: early layers gravitate toward GELU’s stochastic gating, while deep layers adopt Tanh’s bounded compression, likely reflecting varying representational needs across the network (Mechanism 2). The dynamic gating network alone suffices for confident routing; static α serves only as a warm‑start bias, ensuring fine‑tuning stability with constant entropy (Mechanism 3).

## Foundational Learning
- **Gumbel‑Softmax**: Enables differentiable sampling from a categorical distribution, allowing gradient flow through discrete routing decisions. *Quick check*: How does temperature τ affect entropy, and why is annealing from 1.0 to 0.1 critical for convergence?
- **Transformer Feed‑Forward Block**: PolyGLU replaces the SwiGLU activation inside the pre‑norm FFN (x ← x + PolyGLU(RMSNorm(x))). *Quick check*: How does the FFN differ from attention, and where does element‑wise gating occur in SwiGLU?
- **Activation Function Properties**: The four activations (ReLU, Tanh, SiLU, GELU) exhibit distinct gradient behaviors (sparsity, saturation, sign symmetry) that underlie specialization. *Quick check*: Contrast ReLU’s dying neuron problem with Tanh’s vanishing gradient; why might GELU’s stochastic regularization interpretation suit early layers?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Static preferences α (d_ff×4) + β ⊙ GateMLP(mean(hidden_state)) → per‑neuron logits ℓ → Gumbel‑Softmax(τ) → routing weights g → weighted sum over four activations applied to the up‑projected hidden state → SwiGLU‑style gated product → output.

**Critical path**:  
1. Input x → RMSNorm.  
2. Up‑project: h = x W_up.  
3. ¯h = mean(h, dim=seq).  
4. f(¯h) = ReLU(W_1 ¯h + b_1) → W_2 f + b_2.  
5. ℓ = α + β ⊙ f(¯h).  
6. g = Gumbel‑Softmax(ℓ, τ).  
7. For each activation σ_k, compute σ_k(h W_gate).  
8. Mix: output = Σ_k g_k σ_k(h W_gate) ⊙ h.  
9. Down‑project W_down and add residual x.

**Design tradeoffs**: Parameter overhead is minimal (0.23%), but inference cost multiplies by K=4 because all candidate activations must be computed. Near‑deterministic routing at τ=0.1 permits freezing the pattern at deployment (static argmax lookup), eliminating overhead. The biological inspiration of neurotransmitter diversity offers conceptual appeal but empirical gains are modest.

**Failure signatures**:  
- High routing entropy (≈ ln(4)) indicates the router fails to specialize.  
- All neurons converging to the same activation suggests a collapse that ignores depth‑dependent patterns.  
- Entropy drift during fine‑tuning signals that routing is entangled with task‑specific knowledge, violating the intended separation.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. Entropy verification: On held‑out data, compute mean dynamic routing entropy at τ=0.1; confirm <0.1% of ln(4). If not, inspect gate network gradient norms.  
2. Specialization validation: Generate the per‑neuron argmax heatmap across layers; check for GELU→Tanh gradient. If absent, verify temperature schedule and α learning rates.  
3. Fine‑tuning sanity check: Run 100 steps on a small math subset; monitor routing entropy. It should remain exactly constant; any change indicates optimizer or batch‑norm issues.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Does PolyGLU improve over SwiGLU?** Without a matched SwiGLU baseline, it is unclear whether observed behaviors and gains are due to PolyGLU or other factors. Training a control SwiGLU model on the same 10B tokens is needed.
2. **How do patterns scale?** All experiments used a 597M‑parameter model and 10B tokens. Scaling to larger models (>1B) or longer training (>100B) could alter routing dynamics qualitatively.
3. **What causes depth‑dependent specialization?** The strong correlation (early GELU, deep Tanh) is documented but lacks a causal explanation. Is it due to gradient flow, representational needs, or optimization biases?
4. **What is the role of elevated‑entropy layers?** Layers 9, 16, and 17 show significantly higher entropy. Their functional purpose—such as handling ambiguous inputs or maintaining plasticity—remains uninvestigated.

## Limitations
- Single‑scale evaluation: Results are limited to a 597M‑parameter model trained on ~10B tokens.
- Modest absolute gains: Performance reaches only 62–89% of Qwen3‑0.6B‑Base despite a 3,600× larger training budget for the baseline.
- Reproduction ambiguities: The weight‑decay bug fix procedure, FlashAttention variable‑length masking details, and data‑mix interpolation schedule are not fully specified, potentially hindering faithful reimplementation.
