# CARE-RFT: Confidence-Anchored Reinforcement Finetuning for Reliable Reasoning in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.00085
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00085
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: CARE-RFT matches unconstrained RFT reasoning accuracy (e.g., MATH within 0.02) while reducing Expected Calibration Error by ~0.08 and improving TruthfulQA accuracy by ~0.1.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement finetuning (RFT) for large language models faces a critical trade-off: unconstrained methods boost reasoning performance but severely degrade trustworthiness by magnifying hallucination and worsening calibration, while standard reverse KL (RKL) regularization preserves trust but restricts exploration, limiting reasoning gains. This paper introduces CARE-RFT, which replaces RKL with a skew reverse KL (SRKL) divergence. SRKL provides a one-sided bounded penalty—allowing confident, consistently rewarded token explorations to accumulate (enabling reasoning) while imposing unbounded penalties on decreasing probability under the reference model (safeguarding calibration). Extensive experiments on Qwen2.5-3B/7B with GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO show that CARE-RFT achieves reasoning accuracy comparable to unconstrained RFT (MATH scores differ by ≤0.02) while significantly improving calibration (ECE ↓~0.08) and factuality (TruthfulQA ↑~0.1), effectively recovering the base model’s trustworthiness.

## Method Summary
CARE-RFT modifies the divergence penalty in RFT from standard reverse KL to a skewed variant. The anchor distribution is redefined as a convex combination \( \alpha \pi_\theta + (1-\alpha) \pi_{\text{ref}} \) (\( \alpha \in (0,1) \)), yielding an asymmetric per-token gradient coefficient \( C_\alpha(r) \) (where \( r = \pi_\theta / \pi_{\text{ref}} \)). This coefficient is bounded above for \( r > 1 \) (when the policy increases probability on a token) but diverges to \( -\infty \) as \( r \to 0 \) (when the policy decreases probability). Consequently, confident upward deviations (i.e., exploring tokens that receive high reward) face a capped cost, allowing the policy to learn new reasoning patterns, while downward deviations are heavily anchored to the reference model, preserving its factual grounding and calibration. Implemented within standard RFT algorithms (e.g., GRPO), CARE-RFT uses typical regularization strength \( \beta \approx 0.04 \) and skew parameter \( \alpha = 0.8 \), determined via ablation.

## Key Results
- **Reasoning accuracy**: CARE-RFT achieves MATH scores within 0.02 of unconstrained RFT and significantly higher than RKL-constrained baselines.
- **Calibration**: Expected Calibration Error (ECE) is reduced by approximately 0.08 compared to unconstrained RFT, nearly matching the base model’s level.
- **Factuality**: TruthfulQA accuracy improves by around 0.1 over unconstrained RFT, recovering the trustworthiness lost during standard RFT.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Unconstrained RFT’s token-level gradient coefficients are unbounded for probability increases, causing excessive sharpening on high-reward sequences, which collapses token entropy, induces overconfidence, and degrades calibration.
- **Mechanism**: In unconstrained RFT (\( \beta = 0 \)), the gradient update is driven solely by the advantage term. Since reward is assigned uniformly to all tokens in a sequence, tokens in any high-advantage sequence (e.g., a correct final answer) receive identical large positive updates. This indiscriminate reinforcement drives the policy’s token distribution to concentrate excessively on a narrow set of high-reward sequences, making the model brittle and overconfident—even flawed intermediate steps within those sequences are reinforced, leading to diminished factuality and increased miscalibration.
- **Core assumption**: The base model is relatively well-calibrated, and miscalibration arises from training-induced distributional sharpening (entropy collapse).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "unconstrained methods enhance reasoning performance but severely compromise trustworthiness by amplifying hallucination and worsening calibration"
  - [section 3.1]: "indiscriminate reinforcement... reinforcing the entire chain of thought—even when intermediate steps are logically flawed... leads to degraded factuality... model becomes increasingly overconfident... Figure 2, +Reward Update"
  - [section 3.1] & Table 1: unconstrained RFT harms factuality and worsens calibration.
- **Break condition**: If the base model is already poorly calibrated, this mechanism may not induce further degradation, but entropy collapse remains a risk.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Standard reverse KL (RKL) regularization imposes an unbounded penalty on any token where \( \pi_\theta \) increases probability relative to \( \pi_{\text{ref}} \) (\( \pi_{\text{ref}} \to 0 \)), which prevents necessary exploratory deviations into novel, high-reward reasoning paths, limiting reasoning performance gains.
- **Mechanism**: The RKL gradient coefficient \( \log(\pi_\theta / \pi_{\text{ref}}) + 1 \) diverges to \( -\infty \) as \( \pi_{\text{ref}}(o_t|q,o_{<t}) \to 0 \) while \( \pi_\theta \) remains non-zero. This creates an effectively infinite penalty for increasing probability on any token that the reference model deems very unlikely. Since promising novel reasoning strategies often require exploring low-probability tokens under the base model, RKL’s unbounded penalty suppresses these exploratory moves, throttling the policy’s ability to discover and accumulate probability mass on superior reasoning patterns beyond the reference distribution.
- **Core assumption**: Effective reasoning improvement requires the policy to explore and reinforce token sequences that are low-probability under the base (reference) model \( \pi_{\text{ref}} \).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "reverse KL (RKL) constraints preserve trustworthiness but limit reasoning gains due to over-restrictive penalties on exploration"
  - [section 3.2]: "RKL imposes an unbounded penalty... making it difficult for \( \pi_\theta \) to explore novel, high-reward generations that deviate from the reference model. As a result, exploration along promising but low-probability reasoning paths is discouraged"
  - [section 3.2] & Table 1: "+Reward / with RKL" shows lower MATH improvement vs. "+Reward / No RKL".
- **Break condition**: If the base model already contains the optimal reasoning strategy within its high-probability mass, RKL may not limit gains. However, for eliciting emergent behaviors, exploration beyond \( \pi_{\text{ref}} \) is typically necessary.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Skew Reverse KL (SRKL) in CARE-RFT provides a confidence-sensitive, one-sided bounded penalty: it unboundedly penalizes probability *decreases* below \( \pi_{\text{ref}} \) (preserving calibration/factuality) while bounding the penalty for probability *increases* (enabling reasoning exploration).
- **Mechanism**: SRKL redefines the anchor distribution as \( \alpha \pi_\theta + (1-\alpha) \pi_{\text{ref}} \), \( \alpha \in (0,1) \). Its per-token gradient coefficient \( C_\alpha(r) = \log\left(\frac{r}{\alpha r + (1-\alpha)}\right) + \frac{(1-\alpha)r}{\alpha r + (1-\alpha)} \) satisfies \( C_\alpha(r) \le \log(1/\alpha) \) for all \( r > 0 \) (finite upper bound), but \( C_\alpha(r) \to -\infty \) as \( r \to 0 \). This asymmetry means:
  - Upward deviations (\( r > 1 \), increasing probability on a token): The penalty \( -\beta \cdot C_\alpha(r) \) is bounded below by \( -\beta \log(1/\alpha) \). Confident, consistently rewarded explorations (\( r \) large) face a capped cost, allowing such tokens to accumulate.
  - Downward deviations (\( r < 1 \), decreasing probability): The penalty diverges to \( +\infty \) as \( r \to 0 \). This strongly anchors the policy against reducing probability mass on tokens that \( \pi_{\text{ref}} \) considers likely, thus preserving the reference model’s factual grounding and calibration.
- **Core assumption**: The base model’s token probabilities \( \pi_{\text{ref}} \) encode a well-calibrated prior; protecting its high-probability mass (preventing large downward deviations) maintains calibration, while allowing upward deviations enables learning new reasoning skills.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "CARE-RFT provides confidence-sensitive penalty—bounded for confident, consistently rewarded token explorations to enable reasoning, while maintaining unbounded penalties elsewhere to safeguard calibration."
  - [section 4]: "The skewed reference... induces a one-sided bounded penalty: upward deviations are capped... allowing consistently rewarded reasoning patterns to accumulate, while downward moves are effected without bound, preserving pretrained priors."
  - [section 4] & Figure 3: Visual illustration of penalty landscapes showing boundedness for \( r>1 \) in SRKL vs. unbounded in RKL.
  - [section 5.3]: Token entropy dynamics show CARE-RFT avoids the collapse of unconstrained RFT (`No Constraint`) while permitting more sharpening than RKL, explaining its combined accuracy and calibration.
  - [section 5.2] Tables 2 & 4: CARE-RFT matches unconstrained RFT reasoning scores while maintaining base-model-level ECE/TruthfulQA.
- **Break condition**: If \( \alpha \) is set too high (e.g., 0.9 in ablation), the upper bound \( \log(1/\alpha) \) becomes large enough that the penalty on upward deviations is effectively weak, and the method behaves like unconstrained RFT, losing calibration.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: **Reverse KL Divergence & its Gradient in RFT**
  - **Why needed here**: RKL (\( D_{\text{RKL}}(\pi_{\text{ref}} \| \pi_\theta) \)) is the standard divergence penalty in PPO/GRPO. Understanding its gradient coefficient \( \log(\pi_\theta / \pi_{\text{ref}}) + 1 \) is essential to see why it restricts exploration: when \( \pi_{\text{ref}} \to 0 \), the coefficient diverges, creating an infinite penalty for increasing probability on tokens the reference model deems unlikely.
  - **Quick check question**: What happens to the RKL gradient coefficient when the policy assigns high probability to a token that the reference model assigns near-zero probability?

- **Concept**: **Expected Calibration Error (ECE)**
  - **Why needed here**: ECE is the primary metric for "trustworthiness" and "calibration". It measures the gap between model confidence and empirical accuracy. The core trade-off in the paper is between reasoning accuracy (MATH) and ECE/TruthfulQA.
  - **Quick check question**: How is model confidence estimated for ECE calculation on TruthfulQA in this paper (see Section 5.1/C.4)?

- **Concept**: **Token Entropy as a Proxy for Miscalibration Dynamics**
  - **Why needed here**: The paper uses average token entropy during training to mechanistically explain why unconstrained RFT leads to high ECE (entropy collapse → overconfidence) and why RKL prevents collapse but limits performance (excessive entropy maintenance). CARE-RFT’s intermediate entropy trajectory predicts its balanced ECE.
  - **Quick check question**: According to Figure 5, which method(s) show a sharp, monotonic decrease in token entropy? What does this indicate about their final calibration?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:  
  `Query → π_θ (sampling) → Response → Reward computation → Advantage estimation → Divergence penalty (using π_ref) → Combined gradient (GC_A - β*GC_Div) → AdamW → π_θ update`

- **Critical path**:  
  The trainable policy \( \pi_\theta \) generates a response \( o \) for query \( q \). A reward \( r \) is computed (e.g., binary correctness). Token-level advantages \( A(q,o,t) \) are estimated (e.g., via GAE). For each token \( o_t \), two gradient co efficients are computed: the advantage-based \( \text{GC}_A \) (e.g., group-relative normalization in GRPO) and the divergence-based \( \text{GC}_{\text{Div}} \) derived from either RKL or SRKL using \( \pi_{\text{ref}}(o_t|q,o_{<t}) \). The per-token gradient contribution is \( (\text{GC}_A - \beta \cdot \text{GC}_{\text{Div}}) \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(o_t|q,o_{<t}) \). Gradients are summed over the batch and tokens, backpropagated, and \( \pi_\theta \) is updated by AdamW. The asymmetry of SRKL’s \( \text{GC}_{\text{Div}} \) (bounded above for upward deviations, unbounded below for downward) is the key design.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **β (regularization strength)**: Too high suppresses learning; too low (or 0) causes entropy collapse. CARE-RFT’s bounded upward penalty allows using similar \( \beta \) values (e.g., 0.04) as RKL while still permitting effective upward moves.
  - **α (SRKL skewness)**: \( \alpha \to 0 \) recovers RKL (unbounded both ways); \( \alpha \to 1 \) makes SRKL negligible (effectively unconstrained). Ablation finds \( \alpha = 0.8 \) optimal. Higher \( \alpha \) relaxes constraint more, risking calibration; lower \( \alpha \) restricts exploration like RKL.
  - **On-policy sampling**: Both RKL and SRKL require \( \pi_{\text{ref}} \) log-probabilities, tying training to the fixed reference and adding compute overhead vs. truly unconstrained methods, but preventing catastrophic forgetting of base knowledge.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Entropy collapse**: Average token entropy on held-out data drops sharply and monotonically, indicating overconfidence risk (seen in unconstrained RFT).
  - **Calibration drift on factual QA**: ECE on TruthfulQA or similar rises during training, even if reasoning accuracy improves.
  - **Reasoning stagnation**: MATH/GSM8K accuracy plateaus near RKL levels, far below unconstrained baselines, suggesting the constraint is too strong (α too low or β too high).
  - **Hallucination spike**: Increased generation of confidently wrong answers on ambiguous/unanswerable questions (e.g., SelfAware, TruthfulQA incorrect rate).

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Token Entropy Dynamics Run**: Train a small-scale model (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B) with GRPO variants (Unconstrained, RKL, CARE-RFT). Log average token entropy on a fixed set of 100 MATH/100 TruthfulQA prompts every 50 steps. Expected: Unconstrained drops rapidly to near zero; RKL stays high; CARE-RFT shows intermediate controlled reduction. Verifies the core entropy-controlling mechanism.
  2. **Calibration & Factuality A/B Test**: After training, evaluate all models on TruthfulQA (Pass@1 with K=10 samples for ECE) and SelfAware. Expected: Unconstrained RFT lowest TruthfulQA/SelfAware, highest ECE; RKL highest TruthfulQA/SelfAware, lowest ECE; CARE-RFT close to RKL on trustworthiness but with higher reasoning scores. Confirms trade-off resolution.
  3. **Skew Parameter α Ablation**: Fix β=0.04, vary α ∈ {0.0 (RKL), 0.4, 0.8, 0.9}. Evaluate final MATH accuracy and ECE. Expected: α=0.8 maximizes the product (or minimizes a weighted sum) of reasoning gain (vs. base) and calibration preservation (low ECE). α=0.9 will show degraded ECE. Validates the boundedness intuition.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does CARE-RFT generalize to other model architectures (e.g., LLaMA, Mistral) and non-reasoning task distributions (e.g., dialogue, coding)?
- Is token entropy a sufficient proxy for calibration? Could the method exhibit miscalibration despite stable entropy?
- How does CARE-RFT perform with dense, multi-aspect, or human preference rewards instead of binary outcome rewards?
- What happens if the base model \( \pi_{\text{ref}} \) is itself poorly calibrated or factually weak? Does SRKL then cement poor priors?

## Limitations
- **Generalizability**: Results are demonstrated only on Qwen2.5 models (3B/7B) with GRPO/DAPO/GSPO. Performance on other architectures and task distributions remains unknown.
- **Mechanism Proxy**: The paper links token entropy to calibration, but entropy is necessary not sufficient; systematic miscalibration could persist despite favorable entropy trends.
- **Base Model Dependency**: The method assumes \( \pi_{\text{ref}} \) encodes a well-calibrated prior. If not, the anchor may preserve poor factual grounding rather than improve trustworthiness.

## Confidence
- **Trade-off resolution (CARE-RFT matches unconstrained RFT reasoning while matching RKL trustworthiness)**: High
- **Mechanism correctness (SRKL’s asymmetric penalty enables "confident exploration" without entropy collapse)**: High
- **Benefit attribution (gains are primarily due to the asymmetric penalty structure, not other implementation details)**: Medium
