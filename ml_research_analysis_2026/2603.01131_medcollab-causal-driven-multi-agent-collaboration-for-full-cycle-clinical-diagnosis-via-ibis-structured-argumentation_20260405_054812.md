# MedCollab: Causal-Driven Multi-Agent Collaboration for Full-Cycle Clinical Diagnosis via IBIS-Structured Argumentation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01131
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01131
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: MedCollab achieved 76.9% accuracy on ClinicalBench, exceeding the best baseline by 8.2 percentage points, and a 72.4% comprehensive diagnostic rate, over 13 points higher, with the highest RaTEScore across clinical reasoning dimensions.

## Executive Summary
MedCollab is a multi-agent framework for full-cycle clinical diagnosis that reduces hallucinations and improves interpretability in LLM-based reasoning. It introduces a causal-driven approach where a general practitioner (GP) agent dynamically recruits specialist and examination agents based on patient symptoms. Each agent outputs diagnoses in a structured IBIS format (Position, Argument, Evidence), ensuring traceable, evidence-based reasoning. A Hierarchical Disease Causal Chain (HDCC) aggregates these positions into a directed causal model of disease progression. A GP-led consensus mechanism with logic auditing iteratively refines the chain by penalizing logically inconsistent agents. Evaluated on ClinicalBench and MIMIC-IV, MedCollab significantly outperformed baselines in accuracy, comprehensive diagnostic rate, and reasoning quality, demonstrating reduced hallucinations and superior logical coherence.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of four core stages: (1) Dynamic agent recruitment: a GP triage agent selects relevant specialists and examination agents from a predefined pool based on symptom alignment. (2) IBIS-structured argument generation: each recruited specialist outputs diagnoses as (Position, Argument, Evidence) tuples, with evidence traceable to patient data or a medical knowledge base via RAG; examination agents parse raw findings into structured reports. (3) HDCC construction: validated positions are linked into directed causal chains (P₁ → P₂ → ... → Pₙ) or comorbidity groups (+), with chain confidence computed as a weighted sum of agent scores. (4) GP-led consensus and logic auditing: multi-round weighted voting updates agent weights via wᵢ(t+1) = wᵢ(t) × (1 − λ·σᵢ), where σᵢ quantifies logical inconsistency; chains are filtered until a majority is reached, and a final compliance review agent corrects the report.

## Key Results
- Accuracy: 76.9% on ClinicalBench, +8.2% over the best baseline.
- Comprehensive Diagnostic Rate: 72.4%, +13+ points over baselines.
- RaTEScore: highest across key clinical reasoning dimensions (Diagnostic Basis, Differential Diagnosis, Therapeutic Principle, Treatment Plan), indicating reduced hallucinations and better logical coherence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MedCollab’s gains stem from its integrated design: dynamic recruitment focuses computational and reasoning resources on relevant specialties, limiting noise. IBIS structuring forces every diagnostic claim to be explicitly evidenced, grounding reasoning in patient data or established medical knowledge. The HDCC provides a biologically plausible narrative of disease progression, moving beyond flat differentials. Logic auditing suppresses agents whose arguments conflict with evidence or consensus, while iterative weighted voting converges on a coherent, high-quality diagnosis. This closed loop of generation, evaluation, and refinement systematically reduces hallucination and enhances interpretability.

## Foundational Learning
- **IBIS Argumentation**: Structured dialogue framework (Issue, Position, Argument) ensuring traceable, evidence-based claims. *Why needed*: Prevents ungrounded assertions. *Quick check*: Agents consistently generate (Position, Argument, Evidence) tuples with verifiable citations.
- **Hierarchical Disease Causal Chain (HDCC)**: Directed graph representing pathological progression (e.g., trauma → fracture → hemorrhage). *Why needed*: Captures etiological and temporal relationships. *Quick check*: Chains correctly order complications and respect medical causality.
- **Dynamic Agent Recruitment**: GP selects specialists based on symptom alignment. *Why needed*: Improves efficiency and relevance. *Quick check*: Recruited specialists align with ground-truth diagnoses in sample cases.
- **Logic Auditing**: Iterative weight adjustment via inconsistency scoring. *Why needed*: Filters low-quality reasoning. *Quick check*: Weight convergence correlates with improved accuracy.
- **GP-Led Consensus**: Coordinating agent orchestrates voting and final output. *Why needed*: Produces unified, clinically plausible report. *Quick check*: Final report reflects weighted consensus without contradictions.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: GP Triage -> Specialist Recruitment -> Exam Agent (structured findings) -> IBIS Diagnosis Generation (per specialist) -> HDCC Construction -> GP Consensus & Logic Auditing (iterative) -> Compliance Review -> Final Report
- **Critical path**: Triage → Recruit → Generate Arguments → Build Chains → Audit & Vote → Finalize. Feedback loops exist in consensus/auditing.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Structured IBIS improves auditability but may limit hypothesis flexibility.
  - Dynamic recruitment avoids noise but risks missing rare cross-specialty conditions if triage is imperfect.
  - Weighted voting balances agency but depends on accurate inconsistency scoring.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Recruitment mismatch: irrelevant agents or missing key specialists → low accuracy on complex cases.
  - Causal chain errors: medically implausible sequences (e.g., Diabetes → Trauma) indicate flawed HDCC.
  - Audit divergence: oscillating or poorly converging weights degrade final diagnosis quality.
- **First experiments**:
  1. Test recruitment: Input cases with known relevant specialties; measure precision/recall of agent selection.
  2. Validate IBIS generation: Randomly sample outputs; verify that evidence citations trace to patient data or RAG (target >90%).
  3. Evaluate HDCC plausibility: Clinicians blind-rate generated causal chains for correctness (target >80% acceptable).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several unresolved issues:
- **HDCC for complex diseases**: The linear/chain model may be insufficient for multi-factorial or non-linear pathologies (e.g., autoimmune disorders). How does MedCollab handle such cases?
- **Logic auditing specification**: The inconsistency scoring function σᵢ and parameters λ, τ are not defined, making the mechanism unreproducible and its calibration unclear.
- **Recruitment policy details**: The exact decision policy (thresholds, knowledge used) for dynamic specialist recruitment is unspecified, especially for atypical or cross-disciplinary presentations.
- **IBIS constraints on creativity**: The strict (Position, Argument, Evidence) structure may suppress novel but valid diagnostic hypotheses that lack immediate evidence.
- **Generalizability beyond consultation schema**: The framework is evaluated on full-record consultations; its applicability to time-pressised (e.g., emergency) or continuous care (e.g., chronic disease) settings is untested.

## Limitations
- Implementation gaps: missing exact prompts, auditing formulas, and dataset preprocessing details hinder faithful reproduction.
- Fixed agent pool risks underdiagnosis of rare or cross-specialty conditions not represented in the predefined set.
- Evaluation limited to curated consultation datasets; real-world performance in dynamic, high-stakes workflows remains unknown.
