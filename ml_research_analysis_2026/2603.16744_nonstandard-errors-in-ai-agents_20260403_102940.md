# Nonstandard Errors in AI Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16744
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16744
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** State-of-the-art AI coding agents exhibit substantial nonstandard errors (NSE) when tasked with independent empirical research.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates nonstandard errors (NSE)—arising from variation in analytical choices rather than sampling uncertainty—in autonomous AI research agents. Deploying 150 Claude Code agents (Sonnet/Opus) on NYSE TAQ SPY data to test six market-quality hypotheses reveals wide dispersion in estimated annual trends (IQR 0.43–10.69 pp/year). The dispersion is primarily driven by agent disagreement on measure operationalizations (e.g., dollar vs. share volume, autocorrelation vs. variance ratio) and stable model-family empirical styles (Opus favors log OLS/variance ratio; Sonnet prefers level OLS/autocorrelation). A three-stage protocol shows AI peer review fails to reduce NSE, while exposure to top exemplar papers reduces IQR by 80–99% within converging measure families—though some hypotheses diverge due to new splits introduced by exemplars. The findings suggest that autonomous AI agents exhibit significant NSE through forked analytical exploration and correlated imitation rather than reasoned methodological synthesis.

## Method Summary
The experiment deployed 150 autonomous Claude Code agents (Sonnet and Opus variants) on NYSE TAQ SPY data (2015–2024) to test six market-quality hypotheses. Each agent independently executed the full research cycle: data interrogation, model specification, code generation, and report writing. The protocol comprised three stages: Stage 1 collected baseline estimates; Stage 2 added AI peer review (separate evaluators scored reports and provided feedback); Stage 3 exposed agents to the five highest-rated Stage 2 reports. The primary outcome was the interquartile range (IQR) of annualized effect estimates across agents, with systematic analysis of measure choices and model-family preferences.

## Key Results
- Estimated annual trend IQRs range from 0.43 to 10.69 percentage points per year across hypotheses.
- Disagreement primarily stems from measure-choice forks (e.g., dollar vs. share volume, autocorrelation vs. variance ratio) and stable model-family empirical styles (Opus prefers variance ratio/log OLS; Sonnet prefers autocorrelation/level OLS).
- AI peer review yields negligible IQR reduction, while exposure to top exemplar papers reduces IQR by 80–99% within converging measure families (e.g., H6 price impact), though some hypotheses (H1, H5) diverge as exemplars introduce new methodological splits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Autonomous Exploration of Forked Analytical Paths
- **Claim:** Autonomous agents exhibit sizeable nonstandard errors because they independently explore different valid analytical paths ("forks") when the research question is underspecified.
- **Mechanism:** Each agent, given the same data and hypothesis, makes a sequence of unconstrained methodological decisions (measure choice, functional form, frequency). Stochastic sampling combined with task underspecification leads different agents to land on different combinations of choices, producing divergent estimates. The dispersion is concentrated in discrete measure-choice forks where the hypothesis admits multiple valid operationalizations (e.g., "market efficiency" → autocorrelation vs. variance ratio).
- **Core assumption:** The research instructions do not specify a single canonical measurement approach, leaving legitimate analytical discretion to the agent.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "AI agents exhibit sizable nonstandard errors (NSEs)... primarily due to agent disagreement on measure choice (e.g., dollar vs. share volume, autocorrelation vs. variance ratio)."
  - [section 4.2] "For the three abstract hypotheses (H1, H4, H6), the dominant source of NSE is hypothesis abstraction... Within each interpretation, agents converge tightly."
  - [corpus] Weak: neighbors discuss LLM behavior/hallucination, not directly on autonomous research dispersion.
- **Break condition:** If the hypothesis instructions are tightened to specify a single measure definition, within-agent variation may persist from other forks, but the major between-agent splits should collapse.

### Mechanism 2: Stable Model-Family Empirical Styles
- **Claim:** Different model families (e.g., Sonnet vs. Opus) exhibit systematic, stable preferences for certain methodological choices, creating a between-model component of NSE.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-training and alignment differences embed persistent biases in code generation and analytical reasoning. These manifest as "empirical styles": Opus agents strongly prefer log OLS and variance ratio; Sonnet agents prefer level OLS and autocorrelation. This is not random noise but a reproducible distributional shift across model families.
- **Core assumption:** Model families differ in their internal "reasoning heuristics" or prior beliefs about standard practice, which are stable across runs and not washed out by sampling stochasticity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.4] Table 4: "Opus 4.6 agents universally choose variance ratio measures (100%), prefer log OLS... Sonnet 4.6 agents overwhelmingly prefer autocorrelation measures (87%), level OLS." Anderson-Darling tests reject distributional equality for all hypotheses.
  - [section 2.3] References sycophancy/stable biases in LLMs (Perez et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025).
  - [corpus] Weak: neighbors discuss model bias in code generation but not empirical style in research tasks.
- **Break condition:** If model families are homogenized via fine-tuning or if the analytical task is trivial enough that all valid approaches converge to identical numerical outputs.

### Mechanism 3: Imitation Without Understanding from Exemplar Exposure
- **Claim:** Exposure to top exemplar papers dramatically reduces IQR within converging measure families, but this reflects correlated imitation rather than reasoned methodological improvement.
- **Mechanism:** Agents see the same set of five top-rated reports and adjust their methodology to match the majority approach in that set. This causes two effects: (1) agents using the majority measure family tighten their estimates via finer specification choices; (2) agents using minority measures switch to the majority measure (measure-family migration). Convergence is mechanistically a herd behavior, not a result of agents evaluating the exemplars' methodological merits.
- **Core assumption:** Agents treat exemplar papers as authoritative prescriptions rather than as evidence to be critically assessed. They lack the capability or instruction to evaluate whether a measure change is economically warranted for their specific hypothesis.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Exposure to top exemplar papers reduces the interquartile range of estimates by 80–99% within converging measure families... convergence reflects imitation rather than understanding."
  - [section 5.2] "For H6, the fraction using trade-level price impact rises from 56% (S1) to 99% (S3)... For H1, 62 of 87 autocorrelation agents switch to variance ratio."
  - [section 5.5] "Agents switch measures in response to top papers even when the switch is not warranted... The switch reflects imitation without understanding."
  - [corpus] Weak: no direct anchor; neighbors discuss sycophancy but in conversational contexts.
- **Break condition:** If agents are prompted to critically evaluate exemplar methodologies or if exemplars present a balanced multiverse of approaches rather than a consensus.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Nonstandard Errors (NSE)**
  - Why needed here: The paper's central phenomenon. It is uncertainty from variation in analytical choices (the "evidence-generating process"), not sampling error. Understanding this distinction is key to interpreting AI agent output.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between a standard error and a nonstandard error as defined in the paper?
- **Concept: Measure-Choice Forks**
  - Why needed here: The primary source of AI NSE for abstract hypotheses. It's the point where different agents adopt fundamentally different operationalizations of the same construct.
  - Quick check question: For H4 (trading volume), what are the two measure families, and why do they produce such divergent trends?
- **Concept: Model-Family Empirical Styles**
  - Why needed here: Explains why NSE is not purely stochastic; different model backbones have systematic, reproducible methodological preferences.
  - Quick check question: According to Table 4, how do Sonnet and Opus agents differ in their choice for H1 (market efficiency)?
- **Concept: Feedback Protocol Dynamics**
  - Why needed here: The experimental intervention tests what reduces NSE. The finding that peer review is ineffective while exemplar exposure drives convergence is counterintuitive and design-critical.
  - Quick check question: What was the effect of AI peer review (Stage 2) on the IQR, and how did it differ from the effect of seeing top papers (Stage 3)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Research Agents:** Autonomous Claude Code instances (Sonnet/Opus) that read data, write/execute code, produce reports. They are the source of NSE.
  - **Data Asset:** NYSE TAQ SPY dataset (66GB, 7B rows), mounted read-only.
  - **Peer Evaluators:** Separate Sonnet/Opus agents that read Stage 1 reports and produce scores/feedback.
  - **Exemplar Selection:** The 5 highest-rated Stage 2 reports (by average evaluator score) are distributed to all agents for Stage 3.
  - **Conversion Pipeline:** A separate Opus agent per research agent that classifies models and normalizes effect sizes to %/yr.
- **Critical path:** Stage 1 (Independent analysis → baseline NSE) → Stage 2 (Peer review → undirected changes, IQR ≈ constant) → Stage 3 (Top-paper exposure → correlated imitation, IQR collapse within families).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Autonomy vs. Control:** Full autonomy (agents choose all methods) maximizes ecological validity for studying NSE but sacrifices experimenter control over forks.
  - **Isolation vs. Cost:** Singularity container isolation prevents peeking but increases compute/time. The 12-parallel-agent cap balanced throughput vs. cluster load.
  - **Feedback fidelity:** Using AI peer evaluators mimics automated review but may inherit model-family biases (e.g., Opus evaluators may prefer VR).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Persistent high IQR after peer review:** Indicates written critiques fail to resolve core methodological forks.
  - **Random measure switching in Stage 3:** For H4, agents switched between dollar/share volume unpredictably, indicating imitation without understanding.
  - **Divergence after exemplars:** For H1:VR, IQR increased because exemplars introduced a new sub-fork (signed vs. absolute).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline NSE characterization:** Run ~50 autonomous agents on a single well-specified hypothesis (e.g., H2 quoted spread) and a single abstract hypothesis (e.g., H4 volume). Confirm low IQR for H2 (specification fork only) and high IQR for H4 (measure fork).
  2. **Peer review effectiveness test:** Add the Stage 2 peer review protocol. Measure change in IQR and analyze whether changes are directed (toward median) or undirected. Expect minimal IQR reduction.
  3. **Exemplar convergence & migration test:** Add Stage 3 top-paper exposure. Track measure-family switching rates (e.g., AC→VR migration) and stratified IQR within families. Expect 80–99% reduction within converging families, but possible divergence if exemplars split.

## Limitations
- Domain specificity: Findings may be idiosyncratic to high-frequency financial data, which has many valid operationalizations; NSE magnitude could differ in domains with more canonical methods.
- Artifact of autonomy level: Extreme autonomy (full code generation) likely amplifies NSE; more constrained agents (e.g., GUI-based) may exhibit less dispersion, limiting generalizability.
- Unclear generalizability beyond Claude: Using only one model family (Anthropic) prevents assessing whether similar NSE patterns occur with other model families; model-family styles may not translate.

## Confidence
- **Autonomous Forks:** High
- **Model-Family Empirical Styles:** High
- **Imitation Without Understanding:** Medium
