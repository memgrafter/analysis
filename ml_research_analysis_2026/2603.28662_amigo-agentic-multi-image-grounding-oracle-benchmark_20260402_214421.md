# AMIGO: Agentic Multi-Image Grounding Oracle Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28662
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28662
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** Step3-VL-10B (10B) attains the highest verified accuracy at the hardest difficulty (τ=0.8) despite being the smallest evaluated model.

## Executive Summary
AMIGO introduces a benchmark for evaluating agentic vision‑language models on hidden‑target identification within visually similar image galleries. Models engage in an interactive session, asking only Yes/No/Unsure questions under a strict protocol; invalid actions are penalized with a Skip, providing explicit compliance feedback. The benchmark is instantiated with the “Guess My Preferred Dress” task, which emphasizes fine‑grained attribute discrimination while excluding common shortcuts like color and sleeve length. Through a semi‑automatic labeling pipeline and difficulty calibration via a similarity threshold τ, AMIGO constructs galleries with controllable challenge. Evaluation of Qwen3‑VL‑235B, Qwen3.5‑397B, and Step3‑VL‑10B reveals that the smallest model, Step3‑VL‑10B, excels at the hardest threshold, while larger models struggle with protocol adherence or exhibit premature guessing. AMIGO uniquely combines final accuracy with interaction‑quality metrics (efficiency, compliance, robustness).

## Method Summary
AMIGO’s evaluation protocol casts the model as an agent that must identify a hidden target image among a gallery by asking binary or uncertain questions. The agent operates under a strict action space: only “Yes”, “No”, or “Unsure” questions are permitted; any other output (e.g., open‑ended, invalid format) triggers an immediate “Skip” response, consuming a turn without progressing the state. This design enforces disciplined questioning and measures robustness to invalid action penalties. The benchmark is instantiated with a dress‑preference task, where the target is selected based on a latent attribute (e.g., neckline style, fabric texture) that is not trivially discriminable by surface features like color or sleeve length. Galleries are built automatically: first, dress attributes are extracted via a semi‑automatic pipeline combining vision‑language models and human review; then images are embedded, and a similarity threshold τ controls the pairwise similarity among non‑target images, thereby tuning difficulty. At τ=0.8, gallery images are highly similar, demanding finer discrimination. The agent interacts until it either guesses the target or reaches a maximum number of turns. Success is measured by “verified accuracy” (target correctly identified) alongside interaction metrics: number of turns, skip frequency, and consistency under noisy feedback.

## Key Results
- **Step3‑VL‑10B** achieves the highest verified accuracy at the hardest difficulty (τ=0.8), outperforming much larger models.
- **Qwen3.5‑397B** peaks at intermediate thresholds (τ=0.4–0.6) but exhibits a high Skip rate, indicating difficulty adhering to the strict protocol.
- **Qwen3‑VL‑235B** shows elevated random‑guess accuracy and shorter interaction lengths, suggesting a tendency to guess prematurely rather than engage in sustained inquiry.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
AMIGO’s interactive protocol with enforced question types and explicit Skip penalties creates a pressure‑test for agentic reasoning: a model must not only ask discriminative questions but also respect the constrained action space to avoid wasted turns. The exclusion of superficial attributes (e.g., color) forces reliance on fine‑grained semantic features, while the τ‑controlled similarity directly tunes the cognitive load. Verified accuracy finalizes evaluation only after a correct guess, preventing spurious high scores from lucky early guesses. Simultaneously, interaction‑quality metrics capture efficiency (turn count), compliance (skip rate), and robustness (how performance degrades under noisy feedback), providing a multi‑dimensional view of agentic capability that static multiple‑choice benchmarks lack.

## Foundational Learning
- **Agentic Vision‑Language Reasoning**: Models must act autonomously within an environment, requiring planning and state tracking. *Why needed*: The benchmark treats the model as an agent rather than a passive predictor. *Quick check*: Does the model maintain a belief state over possible targets across turns?
- **Interactive Question Answering**: The model engages in a dialogue with a structured oracle, generating only Y/N/U questions. *Why needed*: Tests ability to formulate informative queries and interpret answers. *Quick check*: Are the model’s questions diverse and relevant to remaining candidates?
- **Fine‑Grained Visual Grounding**: Discriminating between visually similar dresses requires attention to subtle attributes (e.g., neckline shape). *Why needed*: Avoids reliance on coarse cues. *Quick check*: Do questions target attributes that are actually present in the attribute label set?
- **Protocol Compliance**: Invalid actions trigger Skips, penalizing non‑adherence. *Why needed*: Encourages models to learn the evaluation interface. *Quick check*: Does the model ever produce an invalid format or a non‑Y/N/U question?
- **Difficulty Calibration via Similarity Threshold**: τ controls the average feature similarity among non‑targets, enabling systematic hardness adjustment. *Why needed*: Allows probing model performance across a continuum. *Quick check*: Does increasing τ consistently reduce human baseline accuracy?
- **Robustness to Noisy Feedback**: The “Unsure” option and protocol enforce handling of uncertainty. *Why needed*: Real‑world agents often face ambiguous information. *Quick check*: Does the model appropriately use “Unsure” when the answer is ambiguous?

## Architecture Onboarding
The AMIGO benchmark consists of four main components:

1. **Semi‑Automatic Labeling Pipeline** – Extracts a standardized set of visual attributes (e.g., “v‑neck”, “satin”) from dress images using a vision‑language model followed by human verification. Output: a structured attribute vector per image.
2. **Gallery Generator** – Constructs galleries of size *g* (e.g., 9 images) by selecting one target image and *g‑1* distractor images. Distractors are chosen so that their CLIP embeddings are within a similarity threshold *τ* of the target, excluding those sharing attributes that would make the task trivial (e.g., color). This module produces the initial environment state.
3. **Interaction Engine** – Orchestrates the turn‑by‑turn session. It receives a question from the model, validates it against the protocol (must be a valid Y/N/U question about an attribute), obtains the ground‑truth answer from the hidden target’s attribute vector, and returns the response. Invalid questions trigger a “Skip” (no answer given). The engine tracks remaining candidates and terminates when the model submits a guess or the maximum turn limit is reached.
4. **Evaluation Module** – Computes primary metrics: Verified Accuracy (fraction of runs where the final guess matches the target), Average Turn Count, Skip Rate, and Robustness Drop (performance under simulated noisy feedback). It also logs question statistics for analysis.

**Critical Path:** A typical evaluation run follows:  
Semi‑Automatic Labels → Gallery Generation → Interaction Loop (Model → Engine → Response) → Evaluation Metrics.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Semi‑automatic labeling* balances scalability (automatic extraction) with quality (human review), at the cost of annotation time.  
- *Strict protocol* encourages disciplined models but may penalize those that could succeed with more flexible questioning; Skip may be too harsh for exploratory queries.  
- *CLIP‑based similarity* is efficient and correlates with visual likeness, yet may not capture human‑perceived difficulty perfectly, especially for abstract attributes.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- High Skip Rate → model fails to respect protocol (produces invalid questions).  
- Low Verified Accuracy despite many turns → questions are uninformative or the model fails to synthesize answers.  
- Short interaction with accuracy near random → model guesses early, possibly due to lack of strategic planning or data leakage.  
- Performance collapses when τ increases → model relies on superficial differences rather than fine‑grained reasoning.

**First Experiments:**  
1. Sanity‑check the Interaction Engine by running a mock agent that always asks “Is it a dress?” to ensure protocol handling.
2. Evaluate a small off‑the‑shelf VLM (e.g., CLIP‑based) at τ=0.2 (easy) to confirm baseline metrics align with expected near‑perfect accuracy.
3. Run a τ sweep (0.2 to 0.8) with a single model to verify that verified accuracy decays monotonically, validating the difficulty calibration.

## Limitations
- **Task generalizability**: Findings are based solely on the “Guess My Preferred Dress” instantiation; transfer to other domains (e.g., objects, scenes) remains unknown.
- **Labeling pipeline bias**: The semi‑automatic attribute extraction may embed systematic errors that affect gallery construction and difficulty calibration.
- **Limited model diversity**: Only three large proprietary models were evaluated; results may not hold for smaller open‑weight or differently‑architected VLMs.

## Confidence
- Step3‑VL‑10B achieves highest verified accuracy at τ=0.8: **High** (direct empirical result).
- Qwen3.5‑397B’s higher Skip rates reflect protocol adherence struggles: **Medium** (correlative; no isolation via ablation).
- Qwen3‑VL‑235B tends to guess prematurely: **Medium** (inferred from interaction length and random‑guess baseline).
- AMIGO jointly measures outcome and interaction quality: **High** (inherent to benchmark design).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑domain replication**: Instantiate AMIGO on a distinct multi‑image grounding task (e.g., “Guess My Preferred Car”) with the same model suite.
2. **Labeling pipeline audit**: Measure inter‑annotator agreement between semi‑automatic labels and expert human annotations on a stratified attribute sample; quantify error propagation into gallery construction and τ calibration.
3. **Protocol ablation study**: Fine‑tune a baseline VLM with synthetic dialogues that enforce vs. do not enforce the AMIGO protocol, isolating the effect of protocol compliance on Skip rates and final accuracy.
