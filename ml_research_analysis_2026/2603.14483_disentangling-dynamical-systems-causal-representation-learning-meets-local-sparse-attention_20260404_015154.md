# Disentangling Dynamical Systems: Causal Representation Learning Meets Local Sparse Attention

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.14483  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14483  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** SPARTAN achieves near‑perfect disentanglement (mean‑max correlation coefficient > 0.95) across four synthetic dynamical systems where baselines fail in local causal settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of learning disentangled representations of dynamical system parameters from trajectory data without relying on predefined function libraries or structural assumptions. It introduces a theoretical identifiability result showing that local state‑dependent causal structures—captured via sparse attention—broaden identifiability guarantees beyond what global sparsity alone can offer. The proposed method, SPARTAN, implements a variational autoencoder whose decoder regularises the learned causal graph to be sparse and state‑dependent. Experiments on four synthetic environments (including cases where global criteria fail) demonstrate that SPARTAN consistently recovers disentangled parameters, while strong baselines do not.

## Method Summary
The authors build a variational autoencoder (VAE) where an encoder maps trajectories to a latent distribution over system parameters. The core innovation lies in the decoder: a transformer (SPARTAN) that generates future states autoregressively using state‑dependent sparse attention. The attention mechanism samples binary adjacency matrices from learned logits; the product of these matrices across layers forms a path matrix that is penalised with an L1 loss (L_path) to enforce sparsity. Training uses a constrained dual optimisation: a Lagrange multiplier λ_dual is adjusted so that the model minimises sparsity subject to a reconstruction+KL+logit loss staying below a threshold L*. This encourages the sparsest decoder graph that still explains the data, satisfying the theoretical graphical criterion for identifiability.

## Key Results
- SPARTAN consistently achieves mean‑max correlation coefficient (MCC) > 0.95 across all four synthetic environments.  
- Baselines (MLP, VCD, Transformer) fail in environments requiring local causal graphs (e.g., Bounce, Local Particle).  
- The empirical results validate the theoretical claim: enforcing local causal structure is necessary for full parameter identifiability in sparse systems.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### 1. Local Causal Graphs Broaden Identifiability
The theory proves that if the intersection of children‑parent sets across all active local causal graphs isolates each parameter (graphical criterion), then parameters are identifiable up to permutation and diffeomorphism. Local graphs are sparser than the global graph, so their intersection imposes stricter constraints on the admissible entanglement mapping, shrinking the space of equivalent representations. Evidence: Abstract, Section 2.3, Section 4.1.

### 2. Sparsity‑Regularised Transformer Enforces Local Causation
SPARTAN’s decoder samples state‑dependent adjacency masks from attention logits. The path matrix (product of masks) is regularised via L_path. Combined with a constrained optimisation that seeks the sparsest model achieving a reconstruction target, this forces the learned connections to mirror the sparse, state‑dependent causal structure of the true system. Evidence: Section 3.2, 3.3, 4.1.

### 3. Sparsity Constraint on Decoder Graph Limits Representation Space
Assuming the true system’s graph is sparse (∥G∥₀ small), any disentangled representation must respect ∥Ĝ∥₀ ≤ ∥G∥₀. Entangled representations would require extra edges to mix parameters, violating the sparsity bound. Theorem 1 formalises this: if the graphical criterion holds and the learned graph is at most as dense as the true one, parameters are identifiable. Evidence: Section 2.2, Theorem 1, Section 4.1.

## Foundational Learning
- **Causal Graphs and Faithfulness (Assumption 3):** Faithfulness ties the absence of an edge (i,j)∉G to everywhere‑zero Jacobian entries ∂f_i/∂θ_j. This is needed to translate graph structure into algebraic constraints for identifiability. Quick check: If (i,j)∉G, must have ∂f_i/∂θ_j = 0 for all states?  
- **Variational Autoencoder for Trajectory Encoding:** The VAE framework implements parameter estimation as representation learning, with the ELBO ensuring observational equivalence (the learned parameters can reconstruct the data). Quick check: What loss terms encourage disentanglement? Not ELBO alone; sparsity regularisation on the decoder graph (L_path) is key.  
- **State‑Dependent Local Causal Graphs:** Many physical systems exhibit sparse interactions only in certain states (e.g., collisions). A global graph includes all possible edges and becomes dense, breaking the graphical criterion. Local graphs, being sparser, can satisfy the criterion even when the global one does not. Quick check: When does a local graph improve identifiability? When the intersection of children‑parent sets across local graphs yields a smaller set per parameter than the global graph’s set.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Encoder (MLP) → Latent parameters ˆθ → SPARTAN decoder (Transformer with Bernoulli‑sampled adjacency masks) → Path matrix (layer‑wise product) → L_path regularisation → Trajectory reconstruction.

### Critical path
1. Encoder processes trajectory τ → latent sample ˆθ.  
2. Decoder receives initial state x₀ and ˆθ.  
3. For each transformer layer, attention logits are computed; sigmoid(logits) gives Bernoulli probabilities for masking.  
4. Path matrix Ã is computed as product of layer adjacency matrices plus residual connections.  
5. Losses: L_rec (trajectory MSE), L_KL (Gaussian prior), L_logit (logit magnitude penalty), L_path (L1(Ã)).  
6. Dual optimisation: update model parameters to minimise L_total = L_path + λ_dual*(L_rec+L_KL+L_logit – L*); update λ_dual to maximise the dual.  
7. When L_rec+L_KL+L_logit ≤ L*, λ_dual → 0 and L_path dominates, driving sparsity.

### Design tradeoffs
- **SPARTAN vs. VCD:** VCD uses a static global graph; works only if global graph meets criterion. SPARTAN learns state‑dependent masks, enabling local criterion satisfaction but requiring careful logit regularisation.  
- **Transformer softmax vs. MLP:** MLP has no inherent sparsity bias; Transformer softmax can yield implicit sparsity but is not guaranteed. SPARTAN explicitly regularises adjacency.  
- **Constrained vs. weighted sum:** Using a Lagrange multiplier encourages the *sparsest* model that fits the data. A weighted sum (e.g., L_rec + αL_path) may trade off reconstruction unnecessarily.

### Failure signatures
- **Vanishing gradients in attention:** Without L_logit, softmax becomes too peaky, stalling adjacency updates. Monitor L_path plateau.  
- **Reconstruction collapse:** If L* is set too high, model ignores sparsity. Check: low reconstruction loss but high L_path and random MCC.  
- **Over‑sparsification:** λ_dual too aggressive leads to underfitting (high L_rec, excessively sparse graphs).  
- **Incorrect graph structure:** In environments requiring local sparsity, SPARTAN may still learn a dense/static graph; visualise path matrices to diagnose.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Dual particle** (global criterion satisfied): Both VCD (static graph) and SPARTAN succeed; MLP/Transformer fail.  
2. **Local particle** (local criterion satisfied, global not): VCD fails; SPARTAN succeeds.  
3. **Bounce** (discontinuous local interactions): VCD fails; MLP fails; Transformer sometimes succeeds; SPARTAN robustly succeeds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does the disentanglement learned by SPARTAN improve generalisation and robustness in downstream physical control tasks?  
- Can the requirement for a priori known parameter space dimension be relaxed via automatic latent dimensionality selection without sacrificing identifiability?  
- How can the graphical criterion be operationalised for active data acquisition to reduce sample complexity?  
- Do the local causal graph mechanism and identifiability guarantees scale to noisy, high‑dimensional real‑world physical systems?

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on strong assumptions (path‑connected state space, sufficient variability, known sparsity) that may not hold in practice.  
- Empirical validation is limited to four low‑dimensional synthetic environments; no real‑world evaluation.  
- Critical implementation details (architectures, hyperparameters, training schedules) are omitted, hindering faithful reproduction.  
- The dual optimisation’s sensitivity to L* and λ_logit is insufficiently analysed.  
- Baseline comparisons omit many recent causal representation learning methods, limiting claims of superiority.

## Confidence
- **Local causal structures broaden identifiability:** High  
- **SPARTAN effectively learns state‑dependent causal graphs:** Medium  
- **Enforcing local causal structure is necessary for disentanglement in sparse systems:** Medium
