# Beyond Accuracy: Reliability and Uncertainty Estimation in Convolutional Neural Networks
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10731
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10731
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: H-CNN VGG16 achieves higher accuracy (92.99% vs 89.72%) but poorer calibration (ECE 5.61%) than GoogLeNet (ECE 1.37%) after MC Dropout, while Conformal Prediction provides 95% coverage at the cost of larger prediction sets.

## Executive Summary
This paper compares two uncertainty quantification methods—Bayesian Monte Carlo Dropout and Conformal Prediction—across two CNN architectures (H-CNN VGG16 and GoogLeNet) on Fashion-MNIST. The higher-accuracy H-CNN VGG16 exhibits pronounced overconfidence, yielding a 5.61% expected calibration error (ECE) despite Bayesian inference. GoogLeNet, though less accurate, provides better-calibrated uncertainty estimates (1.37% ECE) and more expressive epistemic uncertainty. Conformal Prediction delivers statistically guaranteed coverage (≈95%) but produces conservative prediction sets, particularly for ambiguous classes. The findings underscore that accuracy alone is insufficient for trustworthy AI systems and that architectural choices significantly influence uncertainty behavior.

## Method Summary
The authors train hierarchical VGG16 and Inception-style GoogLeNet models on Fashion‑MNIST, maintaining consistent training hyperparameters (though not fully specified). For Bayesian uncertainty, they activate dropout at inference and perform 50 stochastic forward passes, computing predictive entropy and mutual information. For Conformal Prediction (Inductive), they split the original test set into a calibration set (2 000) and a held‑out test set (8 000), compute nonconformity scores \(1 - \hat{f}(x)_y\), and derive the \(\alpha=0.05\) quantile to construct prediction sets with guaranteed marginal coverage. Evaluation metrics include accuracy, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), entropy decomposition, and CP validity/efficiency.

## Key Results
- H-CNN VGG16 attains 92.99% accuracy but remains poorly calibrated (ECE = 5.61% after MC Dropout), indicating overconfidence.
- GoogLeNet achieves 89.72% accuracy with superior calibration (ECE = 1.37% after MC Dropout) and higher epistemic uncertainty for ambiguous classes.
- Inductive Conformal Prediction yields valid coverage (~95%) across architectures, but GoogLeNet produces larger and more consistent prediction sets, highlighting a trade‑off between reliability and efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Monte Carlo Dropout as Bayesian Approximation
Monte Carlo Dropout treats dropout masks during inference as samples from an approximate posterior over model weights. By performing \(T\) stochastic forward passes, the ensemble of outputs approximates the Bayesian predictive posterior \(p(y^*|x^*,D)\). The mean provides a point estimate, while the variance captures epistemic uncertainty. This relies on the assumption that dropout masks encountered at training represent a valid posterior and that the model’s weights are not severely overfitted. Evidence: Section 4.2.2 formalizes the posterior approximation; the abstract mentions Bayesian approximation via MC Dropout. Break condition: unstable or zero variance across passes indicates failure, often due to overfitting or inappropriate dropout rates.

### Mechanism 2: Inductive Conformal Prediction Guarantees
Inductive Conformal Prediction constructs prediction sets with guaranteed marginal coverage under exchangeability. Using a calibration set, it computes nonconformity scores \(s_i = 1 - \hat{f}(x_i)_{y_i}\). The \(\alpha=0.05\) quantile \(\hat{q}\) determines that a label \(y\) is included for a new \(x\) if \(1 - \hat{f}(x)_y \leq \hat{q}\). This ensures \(P(y_{\text{new}} \in C(x_{\text{new}})) \geq 1-\alpha\) without requiring probability calibration. Core assumptions: test and calibration data are exchangeable; nonconformity score meaningfully ranks atypicality. Evidence: Abstract states CP provides statistically guaranteed sets; Section 4.2.1 details validity and efficiency trade‑off. Break condition: exchangeability violation or uninformative scores (e.g., near‑uniform softmax) leads to vacuous sets or invalid coverage.

### Mechanism 3: Architectural Influence on Calibration
Architectural choices—depth, connectivity, and inductive biases—affect calibration and uncertainty expressiveness, independent of accuracy. The hierarchical, deeper H-CNN VGG16 fits the training data more closely, resulting in overconfident softmax outputs. GoogLeNet’s Inception modules encourage broader feature representations and implicit regularization, yielding better‑calibrated probabilities and more responsive uncertainty signals under MC Dropout. Evidence: Abstract and Section 5 present ECE comparisons and entropy decompositions showing GoogLeNet’s improved calibration. Break condition: If both architectures show similarly poor ECE after MC Dropout, the assumed architectural advantage would be negated; regularization (e.g., dropout rate) may dominate.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Fashion-MNIST -> Models (H-CNN VGG16, GoogLeNet) -> Softmax predictions -> UQ Methods (MC Dropout, ICP) -> Metrics (Accuracy, ECE, Entropy, MI, Coverage, Set Size)

**Critical path**  
1. Train each CNN to convergence (60 epochs), monitoring train/validation loss to detect overfitting.  
2. For MC Dropout: enable dropout at test, run 50 stochastic forward passes per sample, compute mean, variance, predictive entropy, mutual information.  
3. For ICP: use the trained model (eval mode) to score the 2 000 calibration samples, compute the 95‑th percentile quantile of \(1 - \hat{f}(x)_y\), and form prediction sets for the 8 000 test samples.  
4. Compute ECE for Bayesian models by binning test samples according to max softmax probability and comparing bin accuracy to bin confidence.  
5. Compare class‑wise entropy, CP set size distributions, and correlate entropy with set size.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Accuracy vs. calibration: Higher accuracy may come at the cost of calibration (H-CNN VGG16).  
- Computational cost: MC Dropout multiplies inference time by the number of passes \(T\); ICP adds a one‑time calibration phase with negligible test overhead.  
- Conservative vs. efficient: CP guarantees coverage but can yield large sets; entropy from MC Dropout is finer‑grained but lacks theoretical coverage guarantees.

**Failure signatures**  
- Overfitting: training accuracy near 100% with plateauing validation accuracy (observed in H-CNN VGG16).  
- Poor calibration: ECE > 5% after MC Dropout (e.g., H-CNN VGG16: 5.61%).  
- CP invalidity: empirical coverage deviates significantly from 95% (e.g., <93% or >97%).  
- Uninformative uncertainty: predictive entropy distribution concentrated near 0 for all classes; mutual information nearly zero.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Train both architectures without UQ, report accuracy and train/validation curves to confirm expected performance gaps.  
2. **ECE computation:** For Bayesian models (MC Dropout, \(T=50\)), bin test samples by max softmax probability, compute bin accuracy vs. confidence, derive ECE. Verify GoogLeNet’s ECE drop vs. H-CNN VGG16.  
3. **ICP deployment:** Partition data (60 k train, 2 k cal, 8 k test), compute calibration scores, derive \(\hat{q}\) for 95% coverage, evaluate test coverage and average set size. Check inflation for ambiguous classes like Shirt.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How do architectural design choices influence uncertainty behavior?**  
   Basis: Paper explicitly states novelty lies in interpreting how model design affects uncertainty. Unresolved: The specific mechanisms (e.g., depth, connectivity) causing divergent uncertainty profiles are not isolated. Evidence needed: Ablation studies varying architectural components while keeping other factors constant, measuring changes in ECE, entropy, and CP set sizes.

2. **When does MC Dropout reliably approximate epistemic uncertainty?**  
   Basis: Paper notes MC Dropout’s limitations in overfitting‑prone H‑CNN VGG16, where mutual information remains low. Unresolved: Why MC Dropout fails to capture meaningful model uncertainty in certain architectures. Evidence needed: Comparison of MC Dropout’s posterior approximation to more expensive Bayesian methods (MCMC, variational) across architectures; controlled data‑distribution perturbations to test correlation with generalization gaps.

3. **What is the trade‑off between CP efficiency and validity relative to base model calibration?**  
   Basis: Paper identifies CP as overly conservative, with GoogLeNet yielding larger sets than the overconfident H‑CNN despite better calibration. Unresolved: The relationship between a model’s miscalibration (e.g., ECE) and CP set‑size inflation is not quantified. Evidence needed: Systematic study inducing synthetic over/under‑confidence in models, measuring resulting CP set sizes; theoretical or empirical bounds linking calibration error to efficiency loss.

4. **Can uncertainty decomposition guide interventions for ambiguous classes?**  
   Basis: Paper shows class‑wise entropy and uncertainty components, noting different uncertainty types for ambiguous classes (e.g., Shirt vs. T‑shirt). Unresolved: How to translate these insights into concrete actions (data augmentation, architecture changes, human‑in‑the‑loop). Evidence needed: Intervention study targeting high‑uncertainty classes with specific remedial actions, measuring subsequent improvement in performance and uncertainty estimates.

5. **How do MC Dropout and CP behave under dataset shift, and can they complement each other?**  
   Basis: Paper compares both methods only on in‑distribution test data, noting sometimes a disconnect (e.g., low entropy yet large CP sets for GoogLeNet). Unresolved: Their robustness and potential synergy under covariate shift or OOD data remain unexplored. Evidence needed: Experiments on corrupted or shifted test sets (e.g., image rotations, different clothing styles) measuring CP coverage stability, correlation between shift‑detection scores and CP set size, and whether combined metrics improve OOD detection.

## Limitations
- Training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, optimizer, dropout rates) are not fully reported, hindering exact replication.
- The H‑CNN VGG16 hierarchical architecture and loss scheduler from Seo & Shin (2019) are insufficiently described, requiring assumptions for reconstruction.
- Core assumptions of MC Dropout and ICP (posterior approximation validity, exchangeability) are not tested under distribution shift.
- Experiments are limited to Fashion-MNIST and only two CNN architectures; generalizability to other datasets and model families is unclear.
