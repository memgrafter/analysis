# The Geometric Inductive Bias of Grokking: Bypassing Phase Transitions via Architectural Topology

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.05228
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05228
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: Architectural constraints on residual stream magnitude and attention routing reduce grokking onset on cyclic modular addition by over 20×, with uniform attention enabling immediate generalization.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates grokking—delayed generalization—in Transformers trained on cyclic modular addition (Z_p). Through controlled architectural interventions, the authors identify two excess degrees of freedom: unconstrained residual stream magnitude and data-dependent attention routing. These degrees of freedom enable prolonged memorization pathways that misalign with the task's inherent symmetries. Imposing a fully bounded spherical topology (L2-normalized residuals and fixed-temperature unembedding) eliminates magnitude encoding, accelerating grokking by over 20×. Overriding attention scores to be uniform reduces attention to a permutation-invariant bag-of-words aggregator, bypassing memorization entirely. However, the spherical constraint fails on non-commutative S5 permutation composition, demonstrating that acceleration depends on alignment between the architectural prior and the task's intrinsic symmetry.

## Method Summary
The study uses synthetic group-theoretic tasks: cyclic modular addition (Z_p, p=113) and non-commutative permutation composition (S5). Transformers (1-layer, d_model=128, 4 heads, MLP dim=512) are trained with full-batch AdamW on a fixed 30% data split. Two main interventions are applied: (A) Spherical constraint: L2-normalize the residual stream after every addition (pre-attention, post-attention, post-MLP) and fix the unembedding logits to τ-scaled cosine similarity (τ=10); (B) Uniform attention: replace learned query-key scores with zeros, yielding uniform token aggregation. Baselines use standard LayerNorm. Performance is measured by test accuracy and epoch of first 100% generalization across multiple seeds, with comparisons under varying learning rates and weight decay.

## Key Results
- Spherical constraint (with weight decay λ=0) reduces mean grokking onset from ~54k epochs (LayerNorm baseline) to ~2.5k epochs at LR=1e-4, and to ~820 epochs at LR=6e-4 on Z_p.
- Uniform attention ablation alone yields 100% test accuracy from early training steps, completely bypassing the memorization plateau on Z_p.
- The same spherical constraint fails to generalize on S5 (non-commutative), achieving only chance-level test accuracy, while baseline Transformers grok after ~40k epochs, highlighting task-specificity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1 (Magnitude DOF)
Standard Transformers allow encoding information in vector norm via LayerNorm's scale parameters. This facilitates a memorization solution using norm-based partitioning of the representational space, which is symmetry-breaking and misaligned with the continuous periodic structure of Z_p. Constraining all vectors to unit norm removes this axis, forcing direction-based (e.g., Fourier) representations that generalize faster. Evidence is interventional: removing norm accelerates grokking dramatically. Break condition: Implement Π_S (L2 normalization) throughout residual stream and unembedding.

### Mechanism 2 (Attention Routing DOF)
Learned query-key interactions permit data-dependent token routing, which can build representations sensitive to operand order—a violation of commutativity for Z_p. This asymmetry requires later compression, delaying generalization. Uniform attention enforces permutation-invariant aggregation (CBOW), eliminating this unnecessary flexibility and enabling immediate generalization. Evidence: Uniform attention alone achieves 100% test accuracy instantly. Break condition: Override attention scores to uniform distribution.

### Mechanism 3 (Task-Specific Alignment)
The benefit of an architectural prior depends on its compatibility with the task's intrinsic symmetry. The spherical constraint aligns with the periodic structure of Z_p, accelerating grokking, but hinders S5, whose solutions require higher-dimensional, non-abelian representations that cannot be embedded on a bounded sphere without loss. Evidence: Negative control on S5 shows failure. Break condition: Apply same constraint to misaligned task.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Token Embeddings -> Π_S (L2-norm) -> Attention (modified or uniform) -> Π_S -> MLP -> Π_S -> ... -> Π_S -> Bounded Unembedding (τ-scaled cosine similarity).  
Uniform Attention replaces the attention module with: output = (1/seq_len) * V.

Critical path to implement spherical constraint:
1. Define Π_S(x) = x / (||x|| + ε).
2. Apply Π_S immediately after each residual addition: before attention, after attention, after MLP.
3. Normalize unembedding weights: W_unembed_norm = Π_S(W_unembed). Compute final logits as τ * (Π_S(h_final) @ W_unembed_norm).
4. Train with weight decay λ=0.0 for stability.

Design tradeoffs:
- Generality vs. Expressivity: Spherical constraint is highly task-specific; accelerates Z_p but fails on S5 by eliminating magnitude encoding.
- Optimization Stability: At higher learning rates, weight decay (λ>0) can conflict with bounded logits; λ=0 often stabilizes.
- Intervention Independence: Magnitude bounding and uniform attention work independently on Z_p; combination likely additive but untested.

Failure signatures:
- On S5 with spherical constraint: training accuracy reaches 100% but test accuracy remains at chance (~0.08% for 120-class classification), indicating memorization without generalization.
- With weight decay on spherical model at high LR: oscillatory or delayed generalization, high variance across seeds.
- If only unembedding bounded (not residual stream): prolonged grokking similar to baseline.
- Uniform attention on non-commutative tasks: catastrophic failure (test accuracy near chance).

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline grokking: Train standard 1-layer Transformer (d_model=128, 4 heads, MLP dim=512) with LayerNorm on Z_p (30% data) at LR=1e-4, WD=1.0. Verify grokking onset ~50k epochs.
2. Spherical constraint: Replace LayerNorm with Π_S and bounded unembedding; train at LR=1e-4, WD=0.0. Expect onset <3k epochs.
3. Uniform attention: Keep baseline (LayerNorm) but override attention scores to uniform; train at LR=1e-4, β2=0.98. Expect immediate 100% test accuracy (within 1k epochs).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **O1**: How can we design architectural priors that accelerate grokking for tasks with non-abelian group structures, such as S5 permutation composition? Basis: Section 5.2 notes this as an open direction; current spherical constraint fails on S5.
- **O2**: Do topological constraints like bounded spherical residual streams improve performance on real-world, heterogeneous tasks beyond synthetic symmetric tasks? Basis: Section 5.3 questions applicability beyond controlled symmetry tasks.
- **O3**: How can we mitigate representation entanglement when mixing constrained and unconstrained pathways in hybrid architectures? Basis: Section 5.3 warns of potential interference between disparate inductive biases.

## Limitations
- Positional encoding handling is unspecified; its interaction with spherical constraints could undermine the intervention.
- Data split consistency across seeds is unclear, potentially inflating variance in grokking onset measurements.
- Task coverage is narrow (only two tasks); the boundary of aligned vs. misaligned symmetries remains unexplored.

## Confidence
- Claim 1 (Unconstrained magnitude prolongs memorization): Medium (inferred from intervention outcome, not directly observed).
- Claim 2 (Attention routing DOF delays generalization): High (clean interventional evidence from uniform attention).
- Claim 3 (Task-specific alignment of priors): High (rigorous negative control on S5).

## Next Checks
1. Direct norm monitoring: Track L2 norms of residual stream vectors in baseline Z_p training; expect increasing spread during memorization, suppressed in spherical model.
2. Expand task suite: Test spherical constraint on non-cyclic abelian groups (e.g., Z_p × Z_p) and on non-commutative tasks where magnitude might help, to map alignment boundary.
3. Component-wise ablation: On Z_p and S5, independently vary residual normalization, unembedding normalization, and temperature τ to isolate which sub-interventions drive acceleration/failure.
