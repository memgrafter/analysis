# A.R.I.S.: Automated Recycling Identification System for E-Waste Classification Using Deep Learning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.17642
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17642
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** Achieved 90% overall precision, 82.2% mAP in detection, and sorting purities of 89% (metals), 85% (circuit boards), 79% (plastics) at 5 kg/s throughput and >20 FPS inference.

## Executive Summary
A.R.I.S. automates the classification and sorting of shredded electronic waste using a YOLOx deep learning model and a pneumatic paddle sorter. The system captures high-resolution images from three synchronized cameras mounted above a conveyor belt, processes them in real time, and triggers precise pneumatic actuation to divert materials into dedicated streams. This addresses the inefficiency of manual e-waste recycling by enabling continuous, high-throughput separation of metals, circuit boards, and plastics.

The integration of hardware (cameras, PLC, pneumatics) and software (optimized inference pipeline) yields high detection accuracy (90% precision, 82.2% mAP) and respectable sorting purities—89% for metals, 85% for circuit boards, and 79% for plastics—while maintaining a throughput of 5 kg/s and exceeding 20 FPS inference speed. The design prioritizes real-time performance and robustness in a challenging industrial environment.

## Method Summary
A.R.I.S. uses three Basler acA1920-155uc RGB cameras (155 FPS, 2.3 MP) mounted 550 mm above a 64-inch conveyor belt to capture a stitched 5760×1200 image of a monolayer of shredded e-waste. The image is partitioned into three 1920×1200 segments, each resized to 640×640 and batched into a single [3,3,640,640] tensor. A YOLOx model (initialized from COCO weights and fine-tuned on a proprietary ~6,000-image dataset) performs a single forward pass, followed by class-wise NMS and coordinate remapping to global pixel space. Detected centroids are translated into paddle numbers (x-axis) and flick times (y-axis) based on constant belt speed (~1.2 m/s) and mechanical geometry. OPC-UA messages from a Mac mini to a Siemens S7-1200 PLC schedule solenoid actuation via a FIFO queue, driving 64 pneumatic paddles spaced 1 inch apart to strike fragments 6 inches below the belt surface, 8 inches from the edge, diverting them into bins.

## Key Results
- Detection: 90% overall precision, 82.2% mAP@0.50 on held-out test set.
- Sorting purity: metals 89%, circuit boards 85%, plastics 79%.
- Throughput: 5 kg/s; inference speed: >20 FPS.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### 1. YOLOx anchor-free detection and SimOTA assignment
YOLOx predicts object centers directly without anchor boxes, avoiding manual design for irregular e-waste shapes. Its SimOTA assignment dynamically matches predictions to ground truth based on quality, mitigating issues from densely packed, overlapping fragments and class imbalance (e.g., metal dominance). This assumes visual features (color, texture, shape) are sufficiently distinctive. Evidence: [section 4.1] details the approach; the model achieves 90% precision and 82.2% mAP [abstract]. Break condition: if fragments exhibit novel composites or extreme occlusions beyond the training distribution, detection recall and IoU degrade.

### 2. Batched, partitioned inference pipeline
The stitched frame is split into three segments, resized, and stacked into a batch tensor, enabling a single forward pass instead of three sequential ones. This reduces computational overhead and leverages hardware acceleration (CoreML). Preprocessing (Numba kernels) and post-processing (class-wise NMS, coordinate remapping) are streamlined. Core assumption: the fixed spatial mapping between three cameras and the 64-inch belt is perfectly calibrated, so partitioning aligns with paddle positions. Evidence: [section 4.3] describes the batching; >20 FPS is reported [abstract]. Break condition: camera synchronization drift or non-uniform belt speed causes misalignment between detected centroids and physical paddle positions.

### 3. PLC-controlled pneumatic paddle sorter
The system translates 2D centroid coordinates into actuation timing: x-coordinate maps to a specific paddle (1-inch spacing); y-coordinate calculates time-to-impact (`T_flick = T_belt-edge + T_to-hit + T_offset`) based on constant belt speed and geometry. The PLC’s FIFO queue schedules solenoid activation with precise ON-times (TON). Evidence: mapping in [section 5.1], timing formula in [section 5.2], paddle geometry in [section 3.4]. Core assumption: fragments follow predictable ballistic trajectories after leaving the belt, and the 6-inch striking point is optimal for all shapes/sizes. Break condition: irregular aerodynamics (e.g., light plastics catching air) or clumping cause trajectory deviations, leading to mis-sorts despite correct detection.

## Foundational Learning

- **Anchor-Free Object Detection (YOLO Family)**  
  Why needed: Anchor-based detectors require hand-designed anchor boxes, which is infeasible for highly irregular, non-rigid e-waste fragments. YOLOx’s anchor-free design directly predicts centers and sizes, adapting to arbitrary shapes.

- **Real-Time Embedded Inference Pipelines**  
  Why needed: Processing 5760×1200 frames at >20 FPS on a Mac mini requires careful optimization of memory layout, batching, and hardware acceleration (CoreML) to meet latency constraints.  
  Quick check: Why is partitioning the stitched frame into three segments and batching them more efficient than processing each segment sequentially?

- **PLC-Based Motion Control and OPC-UA Communication**  
  Why needed: Deterministic actuation timing on a moving belt relies on a PLC’s real-time capabilities and FIFO scheduling to handle network/processing delays from the inference engine.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
3x Basler acA1920-155uc cameras (550 mm above belt) -> Mac mini (CoreML-optimized YOLOx) via Thunderbolt/USB3 -> Ethernet (OPC-UA) -> Siemens S7-1200 PLC -> 64 pneumatic paddles (1" spacing, 6" below belt, 8" from edge). Supporting systems: 1.6m LED bar illumination, vibratory feeder, 64" seamless green conveyor belt (VFD, ~1.2 m/s), air supply regulator (90 PSI).

**Critical path:**  
1. PLC issues PWM trigger → synchronized camera capture.  
2. Stitched frame (5760×1200) partitioned → resized → batched tensor [3,3,640,640].  
3. YOLOx inference → detections (class, confidence, bbox) → class-wise NMS → remap to global coordinates.  
4. Centroid (x,y) → paddle number (x) / `T_flick` (y) → OPC-UA string to PLC.  
5. PLC FIFO scheduler → solenoid activation at calculated timestamp.  
6. Paddle flick → fragment diverts into positive bin or continues to negative bin.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Cost vs. Resolution:* Used three 2.3 MP cameras instead of fewer high-resolution sensors to cover 64" belt cost-effectively while maintaining 3.5 pixels/mm.  
- *Speed vs. Accuracy:* Prioritized >20 FPS inference (critical for 1.2 m/s belt) over higher mAP; chose single-stage YOLOx over slower two-stage detectors.  
- *Hardware simplicity vs. Flexibility:* Repurposed fixed agricultural pneumatic sorter (64 paddles) instead of robotic arms—sacrificed per-fragment trajectory adjustment for throughput and reliability.  
- *Recall vs. Precision for plastics:* Model tuned for very high plastic precision (99.7%) at cost of recall (56.2%) to avoid contaminating valuable metal/board streams.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Visual:* Overexposed/reflective surfaces → misclassification as metal; plastic-like circuit boards → misclassified as plastic.  
- *Systemic:* Dropped OPC-UA packets → missed actuations; PWM desync → stitched image seams; air pressure drop → weak paddle strikes.  
- *Performance:* Plastic recall plateau ~56% despite augmentation → inherent feature indistinctness; fragments <1" often missed → dataset lacks fine-grained samples.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Camera Calibration & Synchronization Validation:** Trigger all 3 cameras with PLC PWM; verify no frame drops or temporal misalignment by imaging a high-speed strobe pattern; check stitched image continuity at boundaries.  
2. **Model Inference Benchmark:** Run batched pipeline on 1000 representative field frames; measure per-frame latency (target: <50 ms); verify remapped bboxes align with calibration grid on conveyor.  
3. **PLC Actuation Timing Test:** Send test OPC-UA strings with predetermined paddle numbers and `T_flick`; use high-speed camera to measure actual paddle strike timing relative to simulated fragment position on belt; adjust `T_offset` empirically.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **How can detection of very small e-waste fragments (e.g., crumbs, dust) be improved?**  
   Basis: The paper notes limitations in detecting tiny fragments and suggests future work with expanded datasets, fine-grained augmentations, and multi-scale inference (Page 18). Unresolved because tiny fragments occupy few pixels, making features insufficient; current model lacks multi-scale processing and adequate samples.

2. **How can plastic recall be raised while maintaining high precision?**  
   Basis: Plastics achieved 99.7% precision but only 56.2% recall; the model is highly conservative for plastics (Page 14). Unresolved because raising recall would likely lower precision; a better threshold trade-off is needed.

3. **Can the system correctly classify partially composite fragments (multiple materials) into their dominant material stream?**  
   Basis: The authors claim handling of partially liberated components based on dominant composition (Page 3) but provide no evaluation for composites; metrics are only for pure classes. Unresolved because model trained on pure classes may not generalize to mixed fragments.

4. **Does the system remain accurate when particles overlap (non-monolayer conditions)?**  
   Basis: The design enforces a monolayer because overlapped particles present identification challenges (Page 6), but no overlap tests are reported. Unresolved because overlap causes occlusions that could reduce detection rates and centroid accuracy; robustness to overlap is uncertain.

## Limitations
- **Proprietary dataset:** The custom ~6,000-image dataset is not public, preventing independent verification and possibly hiding biases (e.g., fragment size distribution, lighting).
- **Physical calibration gaps:** Critical timing constants (`T_belt-edge`, `T_to-hit`, `T_offset`) and precise mechanical alignment details are empirical and underspecified, limiting reproducibility of the pneumatic actuation logic.
- **Hardware/ecosystem lock-in:** The system is tuned for specific cameras (Basler), PLC (Siemens S7-1200), and Mac mini/CoreML; performance on alternative hardware is unknown.

## Next Checks

1. **Dataset & model replication:** Train YOLOX on a public e-waste dataset (e.g., EWasteNet) using reported augmentations and hyperparameters; validate whether mAP@0.50 approaches 82.2% and whether plastic recall remains constrained (~56%) despite balanced classes.
2. **Inference pipeline latency audit:** Implement the exact batched preprocessing/resizing/NMS/remap sequence on target hardware (Mac mini or equivalent); profile per-stage latency to confirm total ≤50 ms at 5760×1200 input and verify no frame drops at sustained 20+ FPS.
3. **Actuation timing calibration test:** In a controlled setup, mark belt positions corresponding to known centroid y-coordinates; send OPC-UA commands with calculated `T_flick`; use a high-speed camera to measure actual paddle strike timing relative to the mark; quantify required `T_offset` adjustment and its sensitivity to belt speed variations.
