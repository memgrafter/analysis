# Label Shift Estimation With Incremental Prior Update

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01651
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01651
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** LEIP achieves lower mean squared error (MSE) than EM and RLLS in label shift estimation under bias-corrected calibrations on CIFAR-10 and MNIST, with consistent improvements across shift intensities and validation sizes.

## Executive Summary
Label shift occurs when the label distribution differs between training and test data while the class-conditional densities remain unchanged. This paper introduces the Label Shift Estimation with Incremental Prior Update (LEIP) method to estimate the target prior \( p_t(y) \). LEIP selects a high-confidence test subset as an anchor to obtain an initial prior estimate, then incrementally updates posteriors for the remaining samples in ascending order of confidence, dynamically refining the prior. Unlike EM-based approaches, LEIP avoids iterative aggregation and requires only top-label calibration rather than full probability calibration. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and MNIST with synthetic Dirichlet shift show that LEIP consistently outperforms EM and RLLS in terms of MSE when using bias-corrected calibrations. For example, on CIFAR-10 with shift intensity \(\alpha=1\) and a validation size of 2500, LEIP attains an MSE of \(0.57\times10^{-3}\) compared to EM’s \(1.23\times10^{-3}\). The results hold across alternative weight calculations, demonstrating robust and accurate label shift estimation.

## Method Summary
LEIP proceeds in a single pass over the test data. First, a threshold \(\tau\) is derived from a validation set confusion matrix (via a min-recall heuristic) to partition the test samples into a high-confidence anchor set \(X_A\) and the remainder \(X_B\). The empirical distribution of top predictions on \(X_A\) yields an initial target prior estimate \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\). Next, samples in \(X_B\) are sorted by descending confidence (maximum predicted probability). For each sample, its posterior is adjusted using Bayes’ theorem with the current prior estimate (Equation 4.3), the top label of this adjusted posterior is added to the anchor set, and the prior estimate is updated incrementally. After processing all \(X_B\) samples, the final prior estimate is applied to all test samples to produce the final target label distribution \(\hat{\tilde{p}}_t(y)\). The method’s efficiency stems from its linear time complexity and its reliance on a weaker calibration notion—only the correctness of the top label after prior adjustment—making it suitable when full calibration is unavailable.

## Key Results
- LEIP achieves lower MSE than EM and RLLS on CIFAR-10 and MNIST under bias-corrected calibrations (BCTS, Vector Scaling) across varying shift intensities and validation sizes.
- On CIFAR-10 (\(\alpha=1\), validation size 2500, BCTS), LEIP attains MSE \(0.57\times10^{-3}\) vs. EM’s \(1.23\times10^{-3}\).
- Results are robust to alternative weight calculations, showing consistent improvement in label shift estimation accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: High-Confidence Anchor for Initial Prior
- **Claim:** A subset of test samples with high classifier confidence serves as a reliable anchor for initial target label distribution estimation.
- **Mechanism:** A threshold \(\tau\) applied to the maximum predicted probability selects samples \(X_A\) where predictions are highly confident. The empirical label distribution of \(X_A\)’s top predictions \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\) approximates the true target prior \(p_t(y)\).
- **Core assumption:** The classifier’s high-confidence predictions are highly accurate (Assumption 1: Accurate Classifier) and exhibit a long-tailed confidence distribution (Assumption 2), ensuring \(X_A\) is accurate and sufficiently large.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "incrementally updates sample-wise priors using a thresholded, high-confidence subset as an anchor."
  - Section 4: Describes selecting \(X_A\) via \(\tau\) and using \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\) as the starting prior.
  - Corpus: Neighboring work uses high-confidence anchoring but LEIP’s incremental update is distinctive.
- **Break condition:** If the classifier is poorly calibrated or inaccurate, the high-confidence set may contain significant error, biasing \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\).

### Mechanism 2: Incremental Update in Confidence Order
- **Claim:** Incrementally updating posteriors for lower-confidence samples in descending confidence order minimizes error propagation.
- **Mechanism:** The remaining samples \(X_B\) are sorted by descending confidence. Each sample’s posterior is updated using Equation 4.3 with the *current* estimate \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\) (derived from all previously processed samples). The top label of the updated posterior is added to the anchor set, dynamically refining \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\).
- **Core assumption:** Processing from higher to lower confidence ensures the prior estimate is refined with increasingly less reliable data, preventing early errors from dominating.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 4: "B = {ˆq(b)1 ≥ ˆq(b)2 ≥ ... ≥ ˆq(b)n}" and "perform the incremental prior update in the ascending order of confidence."
  - Abstract: "adjusts each posterior for more accurate label shift estimation."
  - Corpus: Incremental processing appears in neighboring performance monitoring work but with different objectives.
- **Break condition:** If confidence ordering does not correlate with accuracy (violating Assumptions 1 & 2), incremental refinement could amplify errors.

### Mechanism 3: Weak Calibration Requirement
- **Claim:** LEIP requires only “top-label calibration in changing prior,” a weaker requirement than EM’s need for complete calibration.
- **Mechanism:** LEIP updates posteriors (Eq 4.3) but subsequently uses only the *argmax* of the updated posterior for label counting and further updates; full probability values are not aggregated.
- **Core assumption:** Correctness of the top label after prior adjustment is sufficient; exact calibrated probabilities for non-top classes are unnecessary.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 4.2: Defines "Top label Calibration in Changing Prior" and contrasts with complete calibration required by EM.
  - Abstract: "relies on a weaker notion of calibration compared to other methods."
  - Corpus: Calibration surveys establish the hierarchy of calibration notions.
- **Break condition:** If prior updates frequently cause the top-label to flip for many samples, the method fails—plausible under extreme shift or severe miscalibration.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Label Shift**  
- **Why needed here:** The entire problem assumes \( p(x|y) \) is stable while \( p(y) \) changes; distinguishing covariate shift from label shift is fundamental.  
- **Quick check question:** If a classifier was trained on a dataset with 90% Class A but tested on one with 10% Class A, what changes and what stays the same between source and target distributions?

**Concept: Bayes' Theorem for Posterior Adjustment**  
- **Why needed here:** Equation 4.3 (derived in Appendix B) is the core operational formula for converting source posteriors \( p_s(y|x) \) to target posteriors \( p_t(y|x) \) given a prior ratio.  
- **Quick check question:** Given \( p_s(y|x) \), \( p_s(y) \), and an estimate of \( p_t(y)/p_s(y) \), how would you compute \( p_t(y|x) \)?

**Concept: Calibration Notions (Complete vs. Confidence vs. Top-label)**  
- **Why needed here:** LEIP’s advantage hinges on needing only top-label calibration post-adjustment, not full probability calibration, which defines its applicability with coarser calibration methods.  
- **Quick check question:** What is the minimal calibration property required so that, after applying a prior shift factor, the most likely class label remains correct?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
1. Input: \(\hat{p}_t(y|x)\) (test set probabilistic outputs from base classifier), \(p_s(y)\) (source prior from training/validation set).
2. Threshold Selector: Computes \(\tau\) from validation set confusion matrix (min recall) to partition test samples into \(X_A\) (high-confidence) and \(X_B\) (low-confidence).
3. Anchor Estimator: Computes initial \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\) from top-labels of \(X_A\).
4. Incremental Updater: Sorts \(X_B\) by descending \(\max(\hat{p}_t(y|x))\). Iterates through \(X_B\), applying Equation 4.3 using current \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\), updates \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\) with new top-label after each sample.
5. Final Aggregator: Uses final \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\) (from all samples) in Equation 4.3 on all test samples to get final \(\hat{\tilde{p}}_t(y)\).

**Critical path:** Thresholding → Anchor estimation → Sorting \(X_B\) → Loop through \(X_B\) updating posteriors and \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\) → Final full-set update.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- τ selection: Higher τ yields a purer but smaller anchor set; lower τ risks a noisy anchor. The paper uses min-recall from validation to automate this trade-off.
- Non-iterative vs. iterative: LEIP’s single pass (linear time) avoids EM’s convergence issues but depends critically on the initial anchor quality.
- Calibration independence: Can work with coarser calibration but performance degrades if the base classifier is very inaccurate.

**Failure signatures:**
- High MSE on \(\hat{\tilde{p}}_t(y)\) despite low training error → likely anchor set contamination (poor τ or inaccurate classifier).
- Discrepancy between \(\hat{p}^{(a)}(y)\) from anchor and true \(p_t(y)\) → Assumption 2 violated; confidence distribution not long-tailed.
- Performance drops significantly with uncalibrated classifiers → may need bias-corrected calibration (BCTS/VS as shown in experiments).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Validate τ heuristic: Run LEIP with τ set to different percentiles of the validation set’s max-probability distribution; compare MSE to using the proposed min-recall rule.
2. Calibration sensitivity test: Evaluate LEIP (and EM/RLLS) with increasing levels of artificial mis-calibration (e.g., temperature scaling >1) to confirm robustness to weaker calibration.
3. Anchor set purity measurement: For a known shifted test set, compute the actual accuracy of samples in \(X_A\) (selected by τ). Correlate this accuracy with final estimation MSE to directly test the anchor reliability assumption.

## Limitations
- Assumptions 1 & 2: If the classifier is inaccurate or confidence not long-tailed, the anchor set is biased, breaking prior estimation.
- Incremental ordering relies on confidence correlating with accuracy; overconfident errors may accumulate.
- Top-label calibration may not suffice if prior updates cause top-label changes, especially under extreme shift or severe miscalibration.
- τ selection heuristic lacks theoretical grounding; its robustness across domains is uncertain.
- No convergence guarantees beyond anchor purity; behavior under distribution violations is unclear.
