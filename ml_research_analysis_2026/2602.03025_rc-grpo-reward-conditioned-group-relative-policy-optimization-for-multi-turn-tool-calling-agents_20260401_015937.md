# RC-GRPO: Reward-Conditioned Group Relative Policy Optimization for Multi-Turn Tool Calling Agents
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.03025
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03025
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: RC-GRPO with Qwen2.5-7B achieves 85.00% accuracy on BFCLv4, surpassing all closed-source models (best 61.25%).

## Executive Summary
RC-GRPO addresses the vanishing update problem in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for multi-turn tool calling, where within-group reward variance collapses after supervised fine-tuning. The method introduces a reward-conditioned trajectory policy (RCTP) fine-tuned on mixed successes and failures with discrete reward tokens, enabling controllable generation of diverse-quality trajectories. During RL, RC-GRPO samples varied reward tokens per group to condition rollouts, guaranteeing within-group variance. On the BFCLv4 benchmark, RC-GRPO with Qwen2.5-7B obtains 85.00% accuracy, beating all closed-source models (best 61.25%). With LLaMA3.1-8B it scores 48.75% vs. 35% for SFT+GRPO. Ablations confirm the necessity of RCTP initialization and reward conditioning, and training dynamics show high advantage spread with low entropy, demonstrating stable diversity.

## Method Summary
RC-GRPO operates in two stages. First, Reward-Conditioned Trajectory Policy Fine-Tuning (RCTP-FT) fine-tunes a base model on a mixture of successful and failure trajectories. Each trajectory is prefixed with a reward token—"<|high reward|>" for successes or "<|low reward|>" for failures—during training, encouraging the model to associate the token with trajectory quality. This yields a reference policy π_ref capable of generating both high- and low-reward trajectories on demand. Second, RC-GRPO initializes the RL policy π_θ from π_ref. For each training prompt, it samples G=5 reward tokens from a distribution (probability 0.5 for high vs. low) and generates full trajectories conditioned on each token. These form a group; within-group advantages are computed relative to the group's mean reward. The policy is updated via a PPO-clipped objective plus a KL penalty term (β=0.1) that restricts deviation from π_ref. Inference always conditions on the "<|high reward|>" token to bias outputs toward success. The reward function is binary: R(τ)=1 iff the final environment state matches the gold reference and all essential tool calls appear with correct parameters.

## Key Results
- On BFCLv4, RC-GRPO with Qwen2.5-7B achieves 85.00% accuracy, exceeding all closed-source models (best 61.25%).
- With LLaMA3.1-8B, RC-GRPO scores 48.75% compared to 35% for SFT+GRPO.
- Ablations show that removing either RCTP initialization or reward conditioning causes performance to collapse, underscoring their necessity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RC-GRPO resolves the vanishing update problem in GRPO by engineering within-group reward variance. Standard GRPO normalizes advantages within a group of rollouts for the same prompt; if all rollouts have similar rewards (e.g., all succeed or all fail), the advantages become nearly zero and policy updates vanish. This commonly occurs after SFT, which produces highly proficient but deterministic policies. RC-GRPO introduces two countermeasures. First, RCTP fine-tuning on mixed-quality data with explicit reward tokens teaches the policy to generate both successful and failure trajectories depending on the token, establishing a conditional behavior manifold. Second, during RL, sampling diverse reward tokens per prompt ensures that each group deliberately contains trajectories of varying quality, thus maintaining non-zero advantage spread. The KL penalty to the reference policy stabilizes training by preventing excessive divergence, while the PPO clipping ensures reliable updates. The combination yields stable learning even when the base policy is highly competent.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:
Base Model -> RCTP-FT -> Reference Policy (π_ref)
Reference Policy -> Policy Initialization (π_θ)
Task Pool -> Sample Task IDs -> Reward Token Sampler -> (for each token) -> Policy Rollout (π_θ) -> BFCL Env -> Reward Computation -> Group Advantage Normalization -> PPO Update (π_θ) with KL(π_θ || π_ref) penalty -> Updated π_θ
π_ref remains fixed throughout RL.

**Critical Path**: The core training loop: (1) sample a batch of task IDs; (2) for each task, sample G reward tokens; (3) generate full trajectories conditioned on each token using π_θ; (4) execute trajectories in BFCL to obtain rewards; (5) compute group-normalized advantages; (6) compute PPO+KL loss; (7) update π_θ. The reference policy is only used for KL penalty and initialization.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Group size (G=5): Larger groups improve advantage estimation but increase computational cost. Smaller groups may yield noisy advantages.
- Reward token sampling probability (p=0.5): Balances high/low token usage; skewing may bias group composition and affect variance.
- KL penalty coefficient (β=0.1): Higher values anchor π_θ closer to π_ref, improving stability but possibly slowing learning; lower values allow more exploration but risk divergence.
- RL steps (400): More steps could further improve performance but risk overfitting to the training tasks; fewer steps may underfit.

**Failure Signatures**:
- **Advantage collapse**: Within-group advantage standard deviation near zero across steps. Indicates reward tokens are not producing diverse trajectories; check RCTP conditioning strength.
- **Entropy misalignment**: If trajectory entropy increases strongly with reward, exploration is not properly conditioned; the policy may be relying on random decoding rather than token guidance.
- **KL divergence explosion**: KL(π_θ || π_ref) grows without bound, suggesting the policy is diverging from the reference; remedy with lower learning rate or higher β.
- **Test performance drop**: Could indicate overfitting or data leakage; verify strict ID-based split and monitor training loss.

**Exactly 3 First Experiments**:
1. **RCTP Token Conditioning Test**: After RCTP-FT, generate 20 trajectories per reward token for a handful of prompts. Compute success rates for each token; expect a significant gap (high token > low token).
2. **Environment Wrapper Validation**: Implement the BFCL wrapper that executes tool calls and returns final state. Verify that for expert trajectories, the reward function yields 1, and for obviously flawed trajectories yields 0.
3. **Group Advantage Calculation Check**: Precompute rewards for a small set of groups (e.g., two prompts, each with 5 token-sampled trajectories). Manually compute group-normalized advantages and compare against the implementation’s output to ensure correctness.

## Limitations
- Decoding hyperparameters (temperature, top_p) during RL rollouts are unreported, potentially affecting trajectory diversity and reward distribution.

- Training duration ("Epochs 400") and batch size interpretation (prompts vs. trajectories) are ambiguous, which may impact convergence and final performance.
