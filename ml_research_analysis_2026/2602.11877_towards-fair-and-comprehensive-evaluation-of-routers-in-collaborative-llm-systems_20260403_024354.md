# Towards Fair and Comprehensive Evaluation of Routers in Collaborative LLM Systems
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11877  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11877  
- **Reference count:** 35  
- **Primary result:** ProbeDirichlet achieves 16.68% and 18.86% relative improvements over best baselines in router ability and high-accuracy scenarios (HCR), with consistent performance across model families, scales, heterogeneous tasks, and agentic workflows.
## Executive Summary
The paper identifies a critical gap in evaluating routers for collaborative LLM systems: existing evaluations are unsystematic, ignoring scenario-specific requirements and robustness to out-of-distribution queries. It introduces RouterXBench, a three-dimensional evaluation framework that measures router ability (intrinsic discrimination), scenario alignment (performance under cost/accuracy constraints), and cross-domain robustness. To demonstrate the framework, the authors propose ProbeDirichlet, a router that leverages internal hidden states of a small model aggregated via learnable Dirichlet distributions. ProbeDirichlet yields substantial relative improvements (16.68% in router ability, 18.86% in HCR) over state-of-the-art baselines while maintaining consistent performance across diverse model families, scales, tasks, and workflows.
## Method Summary
ProbeDirichlet uses mean-pooled hidden states from all layers of a small model (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct). For each query, per-layer vectors are aggregated with weights sampled from a Dirichlet distribution with learned concentration parameters β during training. This stochastic sampling acts as layer dropout, preventing over-reliance on specific layers and encouraging robust use of information across the hierarchy. At inference, the expected weights (β normalized) are applied deterministically. The aggregated representation passes through a linear classifier to produce a routing score. Training uses a multi-domain dataset (12K examples from Alpaca, BigMath, MMLU) with binary cross-entropy loss, teaching the router general difficulty signals like reasoning depth or context length. The RouterXBench then evaluates routers across three dimensions: AUROC (intrinsic ability), LPM/MPM/HCR (scenario-specific metrics), and cross-domain robustness.
## Key Results
- Relative improvement in router ability (AUROC): 16.68% over best baseline.
- Relative improvement in high-accuracy scenario (HCR): 18.86% over best baseline.
- Consistent performance across diverse conditions: model families (Llama, Qwen), scales (2B–70B), heterogeneous tasks, and agentic workflows.
## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Routing on internal hidden states yields more robust uncertainty estimates than output probabilities or external embeddings because hidden states capture the model's intermediate reasoning before overconfident softmax normalization.
- **Mechanism**: The small model's hidden states at different layers encode representations of the query and the model's internal processing. These states reflect the model's understanding and uncertainty prior to final answer generation. By using these directly, the router avoids the overconfidence issue of softmax outputs (Guo et al., 2017) and leverages model-specific semantics that external embeddings might miss.
- **Core assumption**: The small model's internal representations correlate with its likelihood of answering correctly; i.e., when the model is uncertain or lacks knowledge, its hidden states will differ systematically from those when it is confident.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Unlike prior work that relies on output probabilities or external embeddings, we utilize internal hidden states that capture model uncertainty before answer generation."
  - [section 4]: "Internal hidden states directly capture model uncertainty before answer generation, proving more reliable than output probabilities that suffer from softmax overconfidence."
  - [corpus]: Corpus shows multiple papers on LLM uncertainty estimation (e.g., SemanticEntropy, ConfidenceMargin) but fewer on hidden-state routing; ProbeDirichlet's focus on cross-layer hidden states is a distinct design point.
- **Break condition**: If the small model's hidden states are not discriminative for the task (e.g., for very simple queries or when the model is consistently wrong in similar ways), the router may not improve over baseline methods.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Dirichlet aggregation of cross-layer hidden states with probabilistic training prevents over-reliance on specific layers, leading to better generalization across tasks and domains.
- **Mechanism**: During training, layer weights are sampled from a Dirichlet distribution with learned concentration parameters. This introduces regularization similar to layer dropout: the model cannot depend on a fixed subset of layers because weights vary. At inference, the expected weights are used, providing a stable aggregation. This encourages the router to utilize information from all layers, making it robust to distribution shifts.
- **Core assumption**: Different layers contribute complementary information about query difficulty, and a global (input-independent) distribution over layer weights can effectively combine them without overfitting to any single layer's patterns.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4]: "We instead model layer importance using a Dirichlet distribution with learned concentration parameters. This enables stochastic training with deterministic inference, acting as layer dropout to prevent overfitting specific layers."
  - [section 4]: "This stochastic sampling acts as a form of layer dropout, preventing the model from over-relying on a narrow subset of layers and encouraging robust aggregation across the entire hidden hierarchy."
  - [corpus]: The corpus includes work on MoE routers (e.g., Optimizing MoE Routers) that consider layer/experts, but Dirichlet-based aggregation is novel in this context.
- **Break condition**: If the optimal layer importance is highly query-dependent, a global Dirichlet distribution may be too restrictive; also, if training data is limited, the learned concentration may not capture true layer relevance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Multi-domain training data composition teaches the router generalizable difficulty signals (e.g., reasoning depth, context length) rather than domain-specific quirks, enabling robust out-of-distribution performance.
- **Mechanism**: By mixing examples from diverse domains (general instruction, knowledge, math) during training, the router is forced to find common features that predict the small model's performance. This reduces the risk of learning spurious correlations present in any single domain. The paper's experiments show additive gains: mixing domains improves performance on new domains without hurting existing ones.
- **Core assumption**: There exist cross-domain factors that influence a small model's ability to answer correctly, and these factors are present in the hidden states across domains.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4]: "We therefore adopt a multi-domain training strategy, training across multiple domains simultaneously. This forces the router to learn cross-domain difficulty signals—such as reasoning depth or context length—rather than domain-specific artifacts, enabling robust transfer to unseen distributions."
  - [Table 6]: "Generalization Behavior under Different Dataset Compositions" shows that mixed training (Alpaca+BigMath) improves BigMath performance while preserving Alpaca performance, indicating no interference.
  - [corpus]: Multi-domain training is a standard technique in ML for robustness; corpus contains related ideas but not specifically for routers.
- **Break condition**: If domains are too heterogeneous and the small model's failure modes differ drastically, mixing may introduce noise; however, paper finds no interference, so this may be mitigated by sufficient data per domain.
## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Router Evaluation Metrics (AUROC, LPM, MPM, HCR)  
  *Why needed here*: The paper's entire framework relies on these metrics to disentangle router ability from scenario alignment and cross-domain robustness. Understanding these is key to interpreting results.  
  *Quick check question*: What does AUROC measure that scenario-specific metrics like LPM or HCR do not? (Answer: AUROC measures intrinsic discriminative ability independent of deployment thresholds, while LPM/HCR measure performance under specific cost/accuracy constraints.)

- **Concept**: Internal Hidden States of LLMs  
  *Why needed here*: ProbeDirichlet routes on hidden states rather than outputs; these states are the primary input features. Understanding what hidden states represent (intermediate representations) is essential.  
  *Quick check question*: Why are hidden states considered more reliable than output probabilities for routing? (Answer: Hidden states capture pre-generation uncertainty and avoid softmax overconfidence.)

- **Concept**: Dirichlet Distribution  
  *Why needed here*: The Dirichlet distribution is used to sample layer weights during training, providing a regularization effect. Knowing its properties (concentration parameters, support on simplex) is important.  
  *Quick check question*: How does sampling from a Dirichlet distribution during training act as regularization? (Answer: It introduces variability in layer weights, preventing the model from over-relying on a fixed set of layers, similar to dropout.)

- **Concept**: Cross-Domain Training  
  *Why needed here*: The paper emphasizes that diverse training data is crucial for robustness. A learner must understand why mixing domains helps.  
  *Quick check question*: Why might single-domain training lead to poor out-of-distribution performance? (Answer: The router may learn domain-specific shortcuts rather than general difficulty signals.)
## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  - **Small Model (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct)**: Processes the query and returns hidden states from each layer (mean-pooled over tokens).
  - **ProbeDirichlet Module**:
    - *Layer Weight Generator*: Computes Dirichlet concentration parameters (β) from training; during inference, outputs expected weights ¯α.
    - *Aggregator*: Weighted sum of layer representations using ¯α.
    - *Linear Classifier*: Maps aggregated representation to a routing score.
  - **Thresholding Module**: Applies a threshold (θ) to the score to decide routing (1=large model, 0=small model).
  - **Evaluation Module**: Computes RouterXBench metrics (AUROC, LPM, HCR, MPM) using ground truth labels (based on small model correctness).

- **Critical path** (from query to routing decision):
  1. Query is tokenized and passed through the small model.
  2. Hidden states from all layers are extracted and mean-pooled over tokens to get per-layer vectors z^{(l)}.
  3. Dirichlet expected weights ¯α are computed from learned β.
  4. Aggregated representation ˆz = Σ_l ¯α_l z^{(l)}.
  5. Linear classifier produces score s = Linear(ˆz).
  6. Decision r = 1 if s ≥ θ else 0.
  7. If r=1, query is sent to large model; else small model continues.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Signal source**: Internal hidden states vs. output probabilities or external embeddings. Hidden states are more informative but require model access.
  - **Aggregation**: Simple mean pooling vs. learned Dirichlet. Dirichlet adds robustness via stochastic training but requires learning β.
  - **Probe complexity**: Linear vs. MLP. Paper shows linear suffices; deeper probes overfit.
  - **Training data**: Single vs. multi-domain. Multi-domain improves robustness with additive gains.
  - **Deterministic vs. stochastic inference**: Dirichlet uses expected weights at inference for efficiency; no sampling overhead.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Low AUROC across domains**: Router not learning meaningful signals; check if probe is too simple or hidden states not discriminative.
  - **High variance in layer weights** (if sampled during inference? but inference uses expected; maybe monitor learned β): Very uneven β may indicate some layers dominate; consider regularization.
  - **Poor OOD performance**: Training data not diverse enough; need to include more domains.
  - **Degradation on certain tasks**: E.g., mathematical reasoning may require deeper layers; if Dirichlet concentrates only on early layers, adjust.
  - **High train-validation loss gap** when using MLP probe: overfitting; revert to linear.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Reproduce main results**: Train ProbeDirichlet on mixed data (Alpaca, BigMath, MMLU, 4K each) with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as small model, GPT-5 as large. Evaluate on in-domain (Alpaca, BigMath, MMLU) and out-of-domain (Magpie, MATH, MMLU-Pro) benchmarks. Report AUROC and scenario metrics (LPM, HCR) and compare to EmbeddingMLP and other baselines. Expect ~16.68% relative improvement in AUROC.
  2. **Ablation on aggregation method**: Compare three variants: Final layer only, Mean Pooling, Dirichlet. Use same training data and probe architecture (linear). Measure AUROC on all benchmarks to confirm that cross-layer aggregation helps and Dirichlet edge over mean pooling.
  3. **Training data diversity analysis**: Train probes on single domains (Alpaca only, BigMath only, MMLU only) vs. mixed. Evaluate on all benchmarks (including OOD). Verify that mixed training yields additive gains and no interference (performance on each in-domain task is preserved or improved). Also visualize learned Dirichlet concentrations to see layer importance patterns.

## Limitations
- Metric definitions unclear: Exact thresholds for LPM (call rate band d₁) and HCR (relative performance interval [0.85, 0.95]) are unspecified, risking metric mismatch.
- Labeling dependency: Reliance on unavailable judges (xVerify-9B-C, GPT-5) introduces unverifiable label noise; replication must substitute with less consistent alternatives.
- Data split opacity: Training/validation splits and sampling seeds are undisclosed, preventing deterministic reproduction and possibly altering distribution.
## Confidence
- Hidden-state routing superiority: High
- Dirichlet aggregation robustness: Medium
- Multi-domain training generalizability: Medium
- Overall 16–19% relative improvement: Medium
