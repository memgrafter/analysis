# Grounded Concreteness: Human-Like Concreteness Sensitivity in Vision-Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.18065
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18065
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: Vision-language models (VLMs) exhibit more human-like concreteness sensitivity than text-only LLMs across performance, representations, attention, and elicited ratings.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether visual grounding during training endows vision-language models (VLMs) with a more human-like sensitivity to linguistic concreteness compared to matched text-only large language models (LLMs). Using Llama 3.1 (8B, 70B) and Llama 3.2 (11B, 90B) families, the authors evaluate models on text-only question-answering benchmarks and analyze their internal representations and attention patterns. Results show that VLMs demonstrate steeper accuracy improvements as question concreteness increases, tighter clustering of words by concreteness in embedding space, a stronger negative correlation between token concreteness and attention entropy, and better alignment with human concreteness norms in elicited ratings. These findings hold across model scales, suggesting that multimodal training induces a graded concreteness organization similar to humans.

## Method Summary
The authors compare text-only LLMs (Llama 3.1 8B/70B) and vision-language models (Llama 3.2 11B/90B) on six QA datasets (ARC-Easy/Challenge, BoolQ, WinoGrande, CommonsenseQA, PIQA, SIQA). Models are evaluated with text-only prompts; no images are used at inference. Question concreteness is computed as the average concreteness score (1–7) of its content words, using Brysbaert et al. (2014) norms; out-of-vocabulary proper nouns are assigned 5, function words 0. Questions are binned by concreteness to assess accuracy gradients. Additional analyses include: (1) t-SNE visualization of last-layer token embeddings averaged by word type, measuring within-bin dispersion; (2) correlation between token concreteness and attention entropy across layers/heads; (3) eliciting token-level concreteness ratings from 70B/90B instruct models via JSON prompts and computing symmetric KL divergence with human distributions, then examining the trend of divergence across concreteness bins.

## Key Results
- VLMs achieve larger accuracy gains on more concrete questions than LLMs.
- VLMs exhibit tighter intra-concreteness clusters in t-SNE embedding space and a more negative correlation between token concreteness and attention entropy.
- VLM-elicited concreteness ratings align more closely with human norms, with alignment improving more steeply as concreteness increases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not propose a specific mechanistic explanation but attributes the findings to visual grounding during multimodal training. The consistent scaling effects suggest that exposure to visual data during training shapes language representations to better mirror human conceptual organization, where concrete concepts are more perceptually grounded.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concreteness**: A psycholinguistic rating (1–7) of how tangible a word is. *Why needed*: It defines the independent variable used to segment language and measure model sensitivity. *Quick check*: Is "dog" more concrete than "freedom"?
- **Visual Grounding**: The process of linking language to visual percepts during training. *Why needed*: Central hypothesis: grounding makes models more human-like in concreteness handling. *Quick check*: Does a model trained on image-text pairs internalize visual features of concrete words?
- **t-SNE**: A nonlinear dimensionality reduction technique for visualizing high-dimensional embeddings. *Why needed*: To compare how words of different concreteness are organized in representation space. *Quick check*: Do points of similar concreteness form tighter clusters in VLMs?
- **Attention Entropy**: The Shannon entropy of attention weights across heads, indicating how focused or diffuse attention is. *Why needed*: To quantify how concretely-targeted the model's attention patterns are. *Quick check*: Should attention be more concentrated for concrete tokens?
- **Symmetric KL Divergence**: A symmetric measure of difference between two probability distributions. *Why needed*: To compare the distribution of model-elicited concreteness ratings against human norms. *Quick check*: Does a lower KL indicate better alignment with humans?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
Text-only inference: Text tokens → Llama Language Model (VLM variant) → Output logits.  
Multimodal training: Image → Vision Encoder → Projected visual tokens → Concatenated with text tokens → Llama LM → Output.

**Critical path**: Multimodal training with image-text data modifies the language model weights; these altered weights drive the observed concreteness sensitivity during subsequent text-only evaluation.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Using a single language model for both modalities is parameter-efficient but risks interference.
- Vision encoder architecture (e.g., Transformer vs. CNN) affects feature extraction quality.
- Length of visual token sequence impacts computational cost and integration with text.

**Failure signatures**:
- Absence of a concreteness gradient in VLMs may indicate insufficient multimodal data or domain mismatch.
- Malformed JSON outputs in rating elicitation reflect poor instruction following.
- High variance in t-SNE dispersion across random seeds indicates unreliability.

**First experiments**:
1. Verify model functionality: Run the LLM and VLM on a small set of QA examples to obtain baseline accuracies.
2. Validate embedding methodology: Extract token embeddings for a subset of words, perform t-SNE, and check that similar-concreteness words appear proximate.
3. Test rating elicitation: Prompt the 90B instruct model for a few word ratings, parse the JSON, and ensure outputs are sensible and match expected concreteness ordering.

## Limitations
- t-SNE hyperparameters (perplexity, learning rate) are not reported, hindering exact replication of cluster dispersion metrics.
- The KL divergence computation lacks details on smoothing epsilon, which may lead to infinite values or biased trends.
- The mapping from word-level concreteness to subword tokens is ambiguously described, potentially affecting token-level analyses.

## Confidence
- QA gradient: Medium
- Embedding tightness: Low
- Attention separation: Medium
- Rating alignment: Low
