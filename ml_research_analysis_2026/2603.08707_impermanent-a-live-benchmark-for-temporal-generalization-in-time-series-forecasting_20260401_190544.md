# Impermanent: A Live Benchmark for Temporal Generalization in Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08707  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08707  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** TimesFM achieves best median scaled MASE (0.609) and CRPS (1.055); foundation models dominate top ranks but model rankings evolve over time, invalidating static splits.

## Executive Summary
Impermanent addresses a critical flaw in time series forecasting benchmarks: static train-test splits risk data leakage and fail to evaluate temporal generalization under distribution shift. It introduces a live benchmark that sequentially scores forecasts on continuously updated GitHub activity streams (400 repositories, 4 event types, multiple frequencies) using a prequential, rolling-window evaluation with daily updates. Results show TimesFM leads in accuracy, with foundation models occupying the top positions. However, model rankings drift significantly over time, demonstrating that static evaluation snapshots are unstable and sustained, live testing is necessary to measure true temporal robustness.

## Method Summary
The benchmark ingests hourly GitHub Archive data, filters events (issues, PRs, pushes, stars), and aggregates counts per repository. Series are rolled up to daily, weekly, and monthly frequencies, applying completeness thresholds (90% daily, 95% weekly, 99% monthly) to ensure quality. Evaluation uses a prequential approach: at each cutoff spaced by the horizon *h*, models forecast *h* steps from a context window, then scores are computed after outcomes arrive. Metrics are scaled MASE (point) and scaled CRPS (9 quantiles), scaled by a zero-model per series, with median aggregation across series and mean rank for ordering. Implementation uses TimeCopilot to dispatch forecasts via Modal to S3 storage; statistical models run on CPU, foundation models on A10G GPUs with batch size 64.

## Key Results
- TimesFM achieves the best median scaled MASE (0.609) and CRPS (1.055).
- Foundation models (Chronos-2, Moirai, TimesFM, TiRex) occupy the top ranks.
- Model rankings evolve over the evaluation period, showing static snapshots are unstable.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The prequential, rolling-window design ensures test data is always strictly future relative to training, eliminating contamination from present or past data. Continuous daily updates expose models to evolving distributions, mimicking real-world deployment where data generating processes shift. By scoring forecasts only after outcomes arrive, the benchmark measures performance on genuinely unseen data. This sustained evaluation captures temporal drift, revealing model robustness (or lack thereof) under distribution shift—something static splits cannot detect because they represent a single, potentially unrepresentative moment.

## Foundational Learning
**Prequential evaluation** – Sequential training and testing on temporal data; needed to prevent leakage; quick check: verify cutoffs are strictly increasing and forecasts exclude future data.  
**Temporal generalization** – Model performance under distribution shift over time; needed for real-world reliability; quick check: correlate error scores with cutoff dates to observe drift.  
**Time series foundation models** – Large models pre-trained on diverse series for zero-shot forecasting; needed to bypass task-specific training; quick check: compare against classical baselines like ETS/ARIMA.  
**Rolling-window forecasting** – Sliding context and horizon; needed for continuous assessment; quick check: ensure context length + horizon matches series availability at each cutoff.  
**Scaled error metrics** – MASE/CRPS normalized by a naive zero-model; needed for comparability across series; quick check: confirm scaling uses per-series baseline and floor τ₀.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
GH Archive -> Ingest (DuckDB aggregation) -> S3 (cleaned series) -> Forecast (TimeCopilot dispatches to Modal) -> S3 (predictions) -> Evaluate (scoring) -> Leaderboard

**Critical path:** Ingest data preparation → Forecast model inference → Evaluate scoring and ranking

**Design tradeoffs:** Live updates provide ecological validity but incur high computational cost (continuous GPU use); Modal enables scaling but adds orchestration complexity; foundation models offer strong zero-shot performance but require version control and preprocessing transparency.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Ingestion: Series count far below expected (≈400×4×freqs) indicates completeness threshold failures.  
- Forecasting: Modal logs show CUDA OOM errors; may need smaller batch or larger GPU.  
- Evaluation: Cutoff dates not spaced exactly by *h* suggests timezone or scheduling bugs; scoring errors like division by zero imply constant/short series.

**First experiments:**  
1. Run ingest on a one-week sample, verify series count matches expected (400 repos × 4 events × each frequency) and no missing dates.  
2. Execute forecast for a single cutoff across all models, confirm predictions are generated without errors and have shape (series, horizon).  
3. Evaluate that cutoff, generate leaderboard, check TimesFM ranks near top and that scaled scores are finite.

## Limitations
- The exact list of 400 repositories (and selection date) is unpublished, introducing potential selection bias.
- MASE scaling details (seasonal period *m*, floor τ₀) are undisclosed, affecting score reproducibility.
- Foundation model checkpoints and preprocessing in TimeCopilot lack transparency (versions, normalization).
- The leaderboard snapshot cutoff (2026-02-12) may not synchronize with a live run, complicating exact replication.

## Confidence
- Live design reduces static contamination: **High** (intrinsic to prequential setup).  
- TimesFM best median scaled MASE 0.609, CRPS 1.055: **Medium** (depends on unreported scaling and repo set).  
- Foundation models dominate top ranks: **Medium** (same dependencies).  
- Rankings drift over time, invalidating static splits: **High** (directly observed in results).
