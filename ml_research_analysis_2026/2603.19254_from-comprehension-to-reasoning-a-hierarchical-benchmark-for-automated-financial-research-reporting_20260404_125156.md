# From Comprehension to Reasoning: A Hierarchical Benchmark for Automated Financial Research Reporting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19254
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19254
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** FinReasoning introduces a three-stage hierarchical benchmark that reveals a universal understanding-execution gap in LLMs; top models (Doubao-Seed-1.8, GPT-5, Kimi-K2) score above 93 on normalized overall scores, while financial fine-tuned models lag by 26.4 points on Semantic Consistency.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents FinReasoning, a benchmark for end-to-end financial research report generation. It decomposes the task into three stages aligned with analyst workflows: Semantic Consistency (error diagnosis/correction), Data Alignment (structured data grounding), and Deep Insight (research-grade analysis). Evaluating 19 models uncovers that while LLMs can identify errors, they struggle to correct them, and face difficulties with format compliance and causal reasoning. Financial fine-tuned models underperform significantly, indicating that domain adaptation alone does not yield robust reasoning. The benchmark uses a 12-indicator rubric and LLM-as-judge scoring to provide granular diagnostics.

## Method Summary
FinReasoning constructs a dataset from Chinese financial texts and A-share market data. Controlled error injection (nine types) creates Semantic Consistency samples; rule-based QA generation produces Data Alignment tasks; evidence extraction enables Deep Insight questions. Evaluation combines automatic metrics (e.g., F1, BERTScore) for the first two stages and an LLM-as-jury (DeepSeek-V3) applying an expert-designed 12-indicator rubric for Deep Insight. Model outputs are normalized per track using Z-score followed by Min-Max scaling to produce an overall comparable score. The approach isolates failure modes across stages, enabling targeted analysis of model capabilities.

## Key Results
- Models exhibit a universal understanding-execution gap: they detect errors but fail to correct them (gap up to 20.2 points on terminology correction).
- Data Alignment reveals format compliance issues; reasoning-specialized models over-reason on simple lookups.
- Deep Insight shows only frontier models can construct multi-step causal chains; financial fine-tuned models lag 26.4 points on Semantic Consistency.
- Top performers: Doubao-Seed-1.8 (96.8), GPT-5 (95.4), Kimi-K2 (93.3) on normalized scores.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Hierarchical decomposition aligns with analyst workflows
- **Claim:** The three-stage hierarchical decomposition (Semantic Consistency → Data Alignment → Deep Insight) aligns evaluation with real analyst cognitive workflows, enabling diagnosis of specific failure modes.
- **Mechanism:** By isolating capabilities into sequential, dependency-aware stages—error detection/correction, structured data grounding, and causal analysis—the benchmark isolates where breakdowns occur (e.g., a model may pass SC but fail DA). This mirrors how analysts iterate: ensure text fidelity first, then anchor in data, then build insights.
- **Core assumption:** Financial report generation is a pipeline where errors in early stages propagate; isolating stages allows targeted mitigation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "decomposes Chinese research-report generation into three stages aligned with real analyst workflows"
  - [section 3.2] Details the sequential construction: SC (error-injected text), DA (database-grounded rules), DI (evidence-to-insight chain).
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., FinAuditing, FinDeepResearch) also pursue hierarchical or pipeline evaluation, suggesting community recognition of staged complexity.
- **Break condition:** If stages are not truly sequential (e.g., DI can be performed without perfect DA), the diagnostic value diminishes. Paper evidence: Finding 2 in DA shows models fail at format compliance even when retrieval is conceptually simple, implying stage interdependence.

### Mechanism 2: Error injection creates gold-standard tests for semantic consistency
- **Claim:** Injecting controlled, diagnostically meaningful errors into source texts (nine types across terminology, fact, logic) creates a gold-standard test for semantic consistency and correction ability.
- **Mechanism:** Error injection builds samples where the location, type, and correction are known. Evaluation then measures not just detection (location F1) but repair quality (explanation + corrected text via LLM-judge). This tests whether models understand financial writing conventions well enough to fix errors without introducing new ones.
- **Core assumption:** Models can detect an anomaly but lack the domain-specific procedural knowledge to generate a contextually appropriate, factually correct revision.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2.1] "design nine controllable error-injection mechanisms... introducing 1–3 diagnostically meaningful perturbations"
  - [section 4.2.1] Finding 1: "Models can explain errors but struggle to correct them... gaps of 20.2 points for Terminology... Logic errors require reconstructing an entire reasoning chain."
  - [corpus] Weak direct corpus support; neighboring benchmarks (FAITH, PHANTOM) focus on detection, not correction. FinReasoning's correction emphasis is a distinctive mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If injected errors are not realistic or if correction requires knowledge beyond the provided context, the task measures recall rather than reasoning. The paper mitigates this by using real financial literature and expert verification (80% annotation review).

### Mechanism 3: LLM-as-judge with expert rubric assesses multi-dimensional insight
- **Claim:** Using an expert-designed 12-indicator rubric within an LLM-as-judge framework for Deep Insight forces assessment of multi-dimensional analytical quality (causal depth, factuality, comparative analysis, structural rigor), not just surface coherence.
- **Mechanism:** Open-ended DI questions require models to transform evidence into research-grade analysis. The rubric breaks "insight" into graded, operational indicators (e.g., "causal chain completeness," "balancing counterarguments"). LLM-as-judge applies this rubric consistently, scaling expert evaluation.
- **Core assumption:** High-quality financial analysis is multi-faceted; a single score hides which analytical skill is weak (e.g., a model may be factual but lack causal depth).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] "incorporates an expert-designed rubric through an LLM-as-a-judge to score... four dimensions comprising twelve indicators"
  - [section 4.2.3] Finding 1: "JCD displays a strong split... only a few frontier models can reliably form multi-step causal links." The rubric exposes this bottleneck.
  - [section 2.2] Critiques prior benchmarks for "lack structured measures for deeper analytical skills."
- **Break condition:** If LLM-as-judge cannot reliably mimic expert scoring (e.g., mode collapse, bias toward length), the rubric's granularity is illusory. Paper addresses this: "Two financial experts reviewed 50%... reached an 83.7% agreement rate."

## Foundational Learning

### Hierarchical Task Decomposition
- **Why needed:** FinReasoning's core innovation is decomposing "financial report generation" into three specific, ordered subtasks. Understanding this decomposition is essential to interpret scores and diagnose model weaknesses.
- **Quick check:** If a model scores high on Semantic Consistency but low on Data Alignment, what does that imply about its capabilities? (It suggests the model can handle text-level consistency but struggles with structured data grounding.)

### The "Understanding-Execution Gap"
- **Why needed:** The paper's key finding is that models often *identify* errors or *know* what data to retrieve but *fail to execute* the correction or formatting correctly. This gap is central to interpreting results.
- **Quick check:** Give an example from the paper (Section 4.2.1 or 4.2.2) that illustrates the understanding-execution gap.

### Domain Adaptation vs. Reasoning Transfer
- **Why needed:** The stark underperformance of financial fine-tuned models (Fin-R1, DianJin-R1) suggests that simple domain-specific continued pretraining may not instill robust, structured reasoning. This challenges a common development paradigm.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Data Construction Engine → Evaluation Orchestrator → Metric Calculator → Normalization Layer → Aggregation

**Critical path:** Data Construction → Model Inference → Output Parsing → Stage-Specific Metric Calculation → Normalization → Aggregation. Breakage often occurs at Output Parsing (format violations in DA) or LLM-Judge reliability.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *LLM-as-Judge vs. Human:* Chose scalability and consistency over potential expert nuance. Mitigated by expert review of judge outputs (83.7% agreement).
- *Single numerical score (Norm):* Enables cross-track leaderboard but masks capability distributions. Paper explicitly notes "each exhibits a distinct capability distribution."
- *Controlled DB vs. Real Agent:* Uses a static, sandboxed database for reproducibility. Tradeoff: cannot test destructive operations or dynamic data updates, limiting realism for autonomous agent evaluation (Section 4.3.2).

**Failure signatures:**
- *SC:* High Error Explanation score + low Correct Content score → "can describe what's wrong but can't fix it."
- *DA:* Low Success Rate despite reasonable Answer Accuracy → format instability; or high Retrieval Accuracy failure → "over-reasoning" on lookup tasks.
- *DI:* Disparity between F&C/C&A (evidence grounding) and JCD (causal chain) → can summarize but cannot build deep causal models.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Sanity Check:** Run a small, deterministic subset (e.g., 10 SC Terminology samples with known errors) through the full pipeline to verify parsing, metric calculation, and normalization produce expected values.
2. **Judge Reliability:** Run the LLM-Judge on a held-out set of 50 DI answers scored by human experts. Compute agreement rate; if below 80%, adjust judge prompt or model (paper uses DeepSeek-V3 as judge).
3. **End-to-End Smoke Test:** Evaluate 2-3 diverse models (e.g., Doubao-Seed-1.8, a financial model, a small open model) on all tracks. Verify: (a) overall score ranges make sense, (b) failure signatures appear as described (e.g., financial model's low SC, DeepSeek-R1's DA drop-off), (c) normalization yields a leaderboard matching paper trends (top: Doubao/GPT/Kimi; bottom: financial/Llama-8B).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How can we design end-to-end evaluation settings to quantify the propagation of hallucinations from Semantic Consistency to Deep Insight tasks?
  - *Basis:* [explicit] The authors state: "our current evaluation does not measure how hallucinations propagate into downstream tasks… Designing end-to-end settings to quantify these effects remains an important direction."
  - *Why unresolved:* The benchmark isolates stages, so cross-stage impact of hallucinations is unknown, limiting understanding of real-world failure modes in complete report generation.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Build an end-to-end pipeline that injects hallucinations at the SC stage and measures their effect on DI outputs (e.g., analytical quality, causal coherence), yielding empirical data on propagation strength and mitigation.

- **Question:** How can we construct a sandboxed financial database environment that safely permits full CRUD operations to evaluate LLMs as autonomous financial agents?
  - *Basis:* [explicit] The authors note: "Data Alignment is currently evaluated… Future work should develop a sandboxed environment that safely allows the full range of database operations, enabling models to perform realistic retrievals, updates, joins, and multi-step financial computations."
  - *Why unresolved:* The current sandbox restricts operations, preventing assessment of LLMs' ability to handle complex real-world workflows (e.g., transactional updates, multi-table joins).
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Develop a secure, extensible sandbox (e.g., containerized DB with rollbacks) and a benchmark exercising diverse operations; evaluating LLMs on this would demonstrate operational robustness.

- **Question:** What modifications can financial fine‑tuning incorporate to improve LLMs' reliability in hallucination correction and structured data reasoning, closing the gap with general models?
  - *Basis:* [explicit] The authors argue: "Future development should integrate objectives that enhance reliability and structured operations, such as hallucination detection and correction and supervised practice with database‑ and API‑grounded reasoning."
  - *Why unresolved:* Current fine‑tuned financial models significantly underperform general models on FinReasoning, indicating existing fine‑tuning fails to instill robust reasoning.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Conduct ablation studies comparing fine‑tuning curricula (e.g., adding synthetic error‑correction tasks, database interaction exercises) on FinReasoning; narrowed performance gaps would validate the proposed directions.

## Limitations
- Reproduction depends on unreleased details: exact LLM‑judge prompts/rubrics, two‑stage normalization formulas, and the 61 financial rules/database schema (Sec 3).
- Benchmark generalizability is limited to Chinese A‑share data and zero‑shot settings; real‑world deployment with dynamic data remains untested.
- Long‑term impact is uncertain given low neighbor citation averages (0.04), suggesting nascent adoption.
