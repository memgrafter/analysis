# Ablate and Rescue: A Causal Analysis of Residual Stream Hyper-Connections

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.14833
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14833
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** Parallel residual streams in Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) exhibit high representational similarity but are not functionally interchangeable; rescue experiments reveal asymmetric causal utility between streams despite similar CKA scores.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the functional roles of parallel residual streams in Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connection (mHC) architectures. It introduces an ablation-and-rescue causal framework to move beyond observational similarity metrics (like CKA) and directly test stream interchangeability during inference. Using a newly released 781M-parameter mHC language model, the analysis shows that while streams often represent similar features, their contributions to the final output are asymmetric and not mutually redundant, establishing that similarity does not imply causal equivalence.

## Method Summary
The authors train an mHC language model with four residual streams per layer, where inter-stream mixing is governed by doubly stochastic routing matrices (`H_res`, `H_pre`, `H_post`). To analyze stream function, they employ two causal interventions: (1) **Activation patching**, which replaces a specific `(layer, stream)` activation from a counterfactual prompt into a cached run and measures output KL-divergence, quantifying individual stream sensitivity. (2) **Ablation-and-rescue**, where they zero two streams at a target layer, compute the KL-divergence of the ablated output, then restore one stream's cached activation and measure the percentage recovery of the original distribution. This recovery metric directly compares functional redundancy between stream pairs.

## Key Results
*   Streams exhibit a "checkerboard" pattern of high within-group CKA similarity but low between-group similarity in middle layers, suggesting two representational subgroups.
*   Rescue experiments show functional redundancy is not symmetric: stream pairs like (0, 2) show mutual high recovery (~74–84%), while pairs like (1, 3) show strong asymmetry (~15.86% difference), contradicting CKA-based expectations.
*   Activation patching reveals stream 2 maintains strong causal influence deep into the network, unlike streams 1 and 3, demonstrating stratified functional importance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Rescue experiments quantify *functional redundancy* or *asymmetry* between residual streams by measuring how much one stream's cached activation can independently compensate for the loss of another.
**Mechanism:** By jointly ablating two streams and then restoring only one, the percentage KL-divergence recovery indicates the restored stream's ability to substitute for its partner. High mutual recovery (e.g., streams 0 and 2) implies functionally overlapping information; asymmetric recovery (e.g., stream 3 rescuing stream 1 better than vice versa) indicates uneven utility.
**Core assumption:** A stream's capacity to rescue output after another's ablation is a causal indicator of their interchangeability in the forward pass.
**Evidence anchors:**
*   [abstract] "Rescue experiments...quantify this: some stream pairs...show mutual high recovery...while others show strong asymmetry"
*   [section 3.4] Formal definition of `Recovery(+i,−j)` and the pairwise rescue matrix (Table 1).
*   Break condition: If rescue were always symmetric and perfect (~100%) for all pairs, the framework would not distinguish functional roles.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Activation patching reveals *asymmetric causal sensitivity* of individual streams to input context, showing that streams differ in influence on the final token distribution even with similar representations.
**Mechanism:** Patching a specific `(layer, stream)` activation from a counterfactual prompt into a cached run induces a KL-divergence. Persistent high patching effects for stream 2 deep into the network, contrasted with lower effects for stream 1, demonstrate stratified functional importance.
**Core assumption:** The output change upon patching a specific activation causally links that activation to the model's decision-making.
**Evidence anchors:**
*   [abstract] "Activation patching reveals asymmetric causal sensitivity."
*   [section 4.2] "Notably, streams (0, 2) demonstrate higher sensitivity...than streams (1, 3). Depth-wise patching yielded low...with the exception of stream 2 which maintains strong patching sensitivity deep..."
*   [section A] Figure 3 (Layer–stream causal sensitivity via activation patching).
*   Break condition: If all streams exhibited identical, low patching effects across all layers, it would suggest no distinctive causal leverage.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** High representational similarity (CKA) between streams is *insufficient* to predict functional interchangeability; the emergent "checkerboard" pattern reflects stable grouping but not causal role equivalence.
**Mechanism:** CKA measures linear representational geometry. The paper observes a bifurcation into two subgroups in middle layers (Figure 4). However, rescue experiments show that streams within a similar CKA cluster (e.g., 0 & 2 high mutual rescue; 1 & 3 asymmetric) can have divergent functional relationships. Thus, CKA captures geometry but not dynamic usage.
**Core assumption:** CKA is a valid metric for comparing representational geometry, but it does not directly infer how representations are used causally.
**Evidence anchors:**
*   [abstract] "while streams exhibit high representational similarity (measured by CKA), they are not functionally interchangeable..."
*   [section 4.1] "The middle layers...form a visually distinctive checkerboard-like pattern...suggesting the model learns a representational divide..."
*   [section A] Figure 4 (Within-layer CKA matrices) and Figure 9 (Rescue asymmetry contrast).
*   Break condition: If rescue symmetry perfectly correlated with CKA values for all pairs across all layers, the claim that CKA is non-predictive would be falsified.

## Foundational Learning

*   **Concept:** Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)
    *   *Why needed:* Primary observational tool to quantify representational similarity between parallel residual streams. Establishes the baseline geometric similarity that later causal experiments contradict.
    *   *Quick check:* If two streams have a CKA score of 0.95, this tells you their activation patterns are linearly highly aligned geometrically. It does *not* tell you whether the model uses them interchangeably or if one is causally critical while the other is redundant.

*   **Concept:** Activation Patching (Causal Tracing)
    *   *Why needed:* Provides the first piece of causal evidence by measuring the *individual* impact of a single stream's activation at a specific layer on the final output distribution. It isolates contributions before pairwise rescue.
    *   *Quick check:* In a patching experiment, a high KL-divergence patch means that the patched `(layer, stream)` activation has a strong causal influence on the final token distribution for that input. A near-zero KL-divergence means that activation has little to no causal effect for that specific input.

*   **Concept:** Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC)
    *   *Why needed:* Defines the architecture under study. The doubly stochastic routing matrices (`H_res`, `H_pre`, `H_post`) and their constraints are the structural basis for having multiple, interacting residual streams. Understanding the routing is key to interpreting why streams might differ.
    *   *Quick check:* The Birkhoff polytope constraint on `H_res` (making it doubly stochastic) is intended to ensure balanced, stable mixing between streams during training, preventing any single stream from dominating. This constraint may influence stream specialization by enforcing a form of distributed representation.

*   **Concept:** Ablation-and-Rescue Framework
    *   *Why needed:* The core novel methodological contribution. It directly tests functional interchangeability between pairs of streams, moving beyond observing similarity to measuring causal compensation.
    *   *Quick check:* The rescue recovery percentage (`1 - KL_rescue/KL_ablation`) answers: "After losing both stream A and stream B, how much of the original output distribution is recovered by restoring *only* stream A's information?" High recovery means stream A can largely stand in for stream B.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input token embeddings -> 36 identical mHC blocks. Each block: 4 residual streams (`x_l` matrix R^{4×d}) -> stream aggregation via `H_pre` (1×4) -> shared attention/MLP layer `F_l` -> stream redistribution via `H_post` (1×4) -> residual mixing via `H_res` (4×4, doubly stochastic) -> output streams for next block.

The critical path for a forward pass is: [streams] -> [aggregate to single vector via H_pre] -> [transform via F_l] -> [redistribute back to streams via H_post] -> [mix streams via H_res]. The `H_res` mixing is the key departure from standard residual connections.

**Design tradeoffs:** The manifold constraint (Birkhoff polytope) on `H_res` trades off unrestricted expressivity for training stability and mitigation of representation collapse. The paper implies this constraint may induce the observed stream grouping (checkerboard CKA) and asymmetry.

**Failure signatures:**
1.  **Training instability:** If `H_res` matrices deviate from doubly stochastic (e.g., norm explosion), gradients may vanish/explode. Check routing matrix statistics (Figure 6: `H_post` variance increases, but `H_res` mean/variance should be stable).
2.  **Stream collapse:** If CKA shows all streams are identical (perfect diagonal of 1s with no off-diagonals) across all layers, the multi-stream mechanism may have failed, reducing to a single effective stream.
3.  **Non-recoverable ablation:** If rescue % is near 0% for all pairs, streams may be completely complementary (no single stream carries sufficient information) or the model is highly brittle to ablations, suggesting poor distributed representation.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  Replicate intra-layer CKA: Compute CKA matrices for mid-layers (e.g., L12, L20) using the released model on Pile-10k. Confirm the checkerboard pattern exists—two high-similarity subgroups vs. low similarity between them.
2.  Run a single pairwise rescue: For a mid-layer (e.g., L15), jointly ablate streams 0 and 2, then rescue with stream 0 only and stream 2 only. Compute `Recovery(+0,-2)` and `Recovery(+2,-0)`. Expect both >70% (mutual redundancy). Then repeat for pair (1,3). Expect asymmetry (e.g., `Recovery(+3,-1) > Recovery(+1,-3)` by ~15%).
3.  Inspect routing matrix statistics: For layers 0, 18, 35, compute Frobenius norm and variance of `H_pre`, `H_post`, `H_res`. Verify `H_post` mean/variance increases with depth (as in Figure 6), while `H_res` remains stable. A wildly varying `H_res` would indicate a constraint violation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **OQ1:** What specific functional roles do residual streams play during inference, and how do they vary across layers and inputs?
*   **OQ2:** Why does high representational similarity (CKA) fail to predict functional interchangeability in mHC, and what factors cause this disconnect?
*   **OQ3:** Do the observed redundancy and asymmetry generalize to other model scales, hyper-connection types, or architectures?
*   **OQ4:** How do the learned routing matrices (H_res, H_pre, H_post) influence stream functional distribution, and can they be interpreted computationally?
*   **OQ5:** Can we train mHC models to exhibit controllable redundancy or specialization across streams, and would that improve performance or robustness?

## Limitations
*   Exact CounterFact subset and symmetric token replacement (STR) procedure are unspecified, hindering exact replication of activation patching results.
*   Evidence is limited to a single 781M-parameter mHC model with 4 streams; generalizability to other scales, stream counts, or architectures is unknown.
*   Interventions target single layers only; multi-layer ablation might uncover different stream interactions.

## Confidence
*   Rescue framework quantifies functional redundancy/asymmetry: **High**
*   Activation patching reveals asymmetric causal sensitivity: **High**
*   CKA similarity does not predict causal interchangeability: **Medium**
*   Single-model analysis limits generalizability: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1.  **Full correlation analysis:** For every layer and stream pair, compute CKA and rescue recovery, then calculate Pearson correlation across the board. Expect near-zero correlation, confirming the non-predictive relationship.
2.  **Cross-architecture replication:** Train a smaller mHC variant (e.g., with 2 or 6 streams) and repeat the rescue and patching experiments. Persistence of asymmetry and CKA patterns would strengthen the generality of the findings.
3.  **Constraint ablation study:** Compare rescue patterns between the standard Birkhoff-constrained mHC and an unconstrained variant (e.g., with softmax routing). Diminished asymmetry without constraints would support the hypothesis that the manifold constraint induces the observed functional grouping.
