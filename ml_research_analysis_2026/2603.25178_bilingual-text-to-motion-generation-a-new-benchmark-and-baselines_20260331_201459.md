# Bilingual Text-to-Motion Generation: A New Benchmark and Baselines

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.25178
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25178
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: BiMD with CLA achieves FID 0.045 and R@3 82.8% on BiHumanML3D, significantly outperforming baselines and enabling robust zero-shot cross-lingual transfer.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the first bilingual text-to-motion benchmark, BiHumanML3D, containing 13,312 motions with high-quality English-Chinese annotations created via an LLM-assisted pipeline and manual verification. It proposes Bilingual Motion Diffusion (BiMD) with Cross-Lingual Alignment (CLA), which aligns text embeddings from both languages into a unified latent space using knowledge distillation. BiMD with CLA dramatically improves motion generation quality and semantic consistency across languages, while retaining state-of-the-art performance on the original monolingual HumanML3D dataset. The work addresses a critical gap in cross-lingual motion synthesis and demonstrates effective zero-shot transfer and code-switching handling.

## Method Summary
BiHumanML3D is constructed by extending HumanML3D through a three-stage LLM pipeline: initial translation with DeepSeek, refinement with Qwen to correct artifacts (e.g., gender bias, literal translations), and human verification with rule-based checks for motion-critical terms. Bilingual Motion Diffusion (BiMD) builds on MLD’s latent diffusion framework. A VAE compresses raw motions into latents; a U-Net denoiser is conditioned on text embeddings. The key innovation is CLA: a multilingual student encoder (XLM-Base) is fine-tuned to mimic a frozen teacher (OpenCLIP) via symmetric KL divergence, aligning Chinese and English embeddings into the teacher’s English space. During training, for each motion, either its English or Chinese description is randomly selected as the condition. The model is trained with a diffusion loss and classifier-free guidance at inference.

## Key Results
- CLA substantially boosts performance: on BiHumanML3D, FID improves from 0.169 (without CLA) to 0.045 and R@3 from 80.8% to 82.8%.
- BiMD with CLA exhibits strong zero-shot cross-lingual transfer and effectively handles code-switching inputs, with user studies confirming higher semantic consistency.
- Bilingual training does not sacrifice monolingual capability; the model maintains state-of-the-art results on HumanML3D.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Cross-Lingual Alignment (CLA) aligns text embeddings from English and Chinese into a unified latent space via knowledge distillation, enabling language-invariant conditioning for the diffusion model.  
**Mechanism:** A student multilingual encoder (XLM-Base) is fine-tuned to mimic the embeddings of a frozen teacher encoder (OpenCLIP) on English texts, using symmetric KL divergence between probability distributions over embedding dimensions. This forces the student to map both English and Chinese inputs into the teacher’s semantically organized English space.  
**Core assumption:** The teacher’s English embedding space clusters motions with similar descriptions; projecting Chinese into this space preserves motion semantics.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions CLA; Section 4.1 details KL loss and teacher-student choice; corpus signal shows neighboring papers are monolingual, underscoring novelty.  
**Break condition:** If the teacher’s space lacks motion-specific semantics or the student cannot match the distribution, alignment fails.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Bilingual Motion Diffusion (BiMD) jointly trains on motion latents conditioned on either English or Chinese text, encouraging the denoiser to learn language-agnostic motion patterns.  
**Mechanism:** For each motion sample, one of its bilingual annotations is randomly selected as the condition per batch. The diffusion loss minimizes denoising error given the aligned text embedding. Random selection prevents overfitting to a single language.  
**Core assumption:** The motion itself is language-independent; the same motion can be described equivalently in both languages.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states BiMD incorporates CLA; Section 4.3 describes random language pairing; corpus indicates bilingual diffusion training is novel.  
**Break condition:** Systematic semantic mismatches in translations or uneven language exposure could cause the model to conflate distinct motions.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The BiHumanML3D dataset provides high-quality bilingual motion-text pairs through an LLM-assisted annotation pipeline, essential for training and evaluation.  
**Mechanism:** Starting from HumanML3D, an LLM translates English captions to Chinese; a second LLM refines them to fix artifacts; human annotators verify and correct remaining errors. Rule-based checks ensure consistency (e.g., directional terms).  
**Core assumption:** LLMs generate plausible first drafts, but human correction is necessary for motion-critical accuracy.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions LLM-assisted annotation and manual correction; Section 3.2 provides pipeline statistics and 91.3% inter-annotator agreement; corpus shows no existing bilingual motion datasets.  
**Break condition:** Inadequate human verification could leave translation errors; the pipeline may need adaptation for other language pairs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation**  
  Why needed: CLA uses KD to transfer the semantic structure of a teacher’s English embeddings to a student, avoiding expensive training from scratch and leveraging a well-structured space.  
  Quick check: What are the roles of teacher and student, and why symmetric KL divergence instead of MSE?

- **Concept: Latent Diffusion Models**  
  Why needed: Diffusion directly on motion sequences is computationally heavy; a VAE compresses motions into a latent space where diffusion is more efficient and robust.  
  Quick check: How does VAE reconstruction loss interact with diffusion training, and why is a separate latent space preferred?

- **Concept: Cross-lingual Representation Alignment**  
  Why needed: To ensure semantically identical motion descriptions in different languages produce similar conditioning vectors, eliminating language bias and enabling code-switching.  
  Quick check: Why not simply use a pretrained multilingual encoder without further alignment? What limitations does the paper identify?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Raw motion -> VAE encoder -> latent z ; Text (EN/CN) -> student encoder (XLM-Base) -> aligned embedding -> projection -> conditioned denoiser (U-Net) -> predicted noise ; Teacher (OpenCLIP, frozen) parallel to student for CLA loss ; Loss aggregator combines CLA loss (alignment phase) and diffusion loss (training phase).

Critical path:  
1. Filter and collect motion sequences from HumanML3D.  
2. Run LLM annotation pipeline to obtain Chinese captions; perform human verification.  
3. Fine-tune XLM student to match OpenCLIP teacher on English and Chinese captions using symmetric KL loss until embeddings align.  
4. Train diffusion model: encode motions to latents via VAE; for each batch, randomly select English or Chinese caption, compute student embedding; optimize denoising loss with classifier-free guidance dropout.  
5. At inference, sample noise, denoise conditioned on bilingual text, decode to motion via VAE decoder.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Teacher choice (OpenCLIP) provides high-quality English space but may not cover Chinese-specific semantics; student learns the projection.  
- Multilingual student (XLM) increases memory vs. monolingual but is necessary for Chinese support.  
- Bilingual random selection doubles language diversity but may slow convergence if one language dominates.  
- CLA adds compute for embedding extraction and KL loss but yields large gains in cross-lingual consistency.

Failure signatures:  
- Persistent performance gap between English and Chinese in Table 2 indicates incomplete alignment.  
- High FID and low R-Precision when translating without CLA (direct translation baselines) show dataset quality issues.  
- Code-switching inputs produce nonsense motions if the model hasn’t seen mixed-language examples during training.  
- Zero-shot transfer failure (EN→CN or CN→EN) shows alignment did not generalize.

First 3 experiments:  
1. Ablation: remove CLA (BiMD w/o CLA) to quantify alignment impact on metrics (FID, R@3).  
2. Evaluate bilingual generation on BiHumanML3D for both languages, comparing with retrained monolingual baselines.  
3. Zero-shot cross-lingual test: train on one language only, evaluate on the other, with and without CLA.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can culturally specific motion terms without direct translations be accurately represented?**  
  Basis: Motions like "ZuoYi" and "TanglangQuan" have cultural connotations absent in other languages. Unresolved: CLA aligns semantic representations but may not capture full kinematic details without extra context. Evidence needed: Curate a subset of culturally specific motions with multimodal annotations (video, kinematics) and test performance.

- **Can the LLM-assisted pipeline be fully automated to scale to multiple languages while maintaining quality?**  
  Basis: Human verification is needed for 53% of entries due to LLM limitations (stage success rates, inter-annotator agreement). Unresolved: Human effort is a bottleneck for other language pairs. Evidence needed: Develop self-correcting LLM pipelines with confidence scoring and minimal human-in-the-loop, validated on new language pairs.

- **Does CLA generalize to language pairs with greater linguistic divergence than English-Chinese?**  
  Basis: CLA is tested only on EN-ZH; a multilingual teacher underperformed. Unresolved: Asymmetric teacher-student design may not hold for distant pairs. Evidence needed: Evaluate CLA on diverse pairs (e.g., EN-Finnish, ZH-Arabic) using standardized alignment and motion metrics.

## Limitations
- The BiHumanML3D pipeline’s LLM prompts and human verification criteria are not fully disclosed, hindering faithful reproduction.
- OpenCLIP as a teacher is image-pretrained and may not perfectly capture motion-specific semantics.
- Evaluation for Chinese uses a custom evaluator lacking broad validation.
- The study is restricted to English-Chinese; applicability to other language pairs is unknown.
- Code-switching tests are limited and rely partly on subjective user studies.

## Confidence
- Dataset construction: **Medium** (high inter-annotator agreement but opaque pipeline details).
- CLA effectiveness: **High** (ablation shows large metric gains; consistent across experiments).
- Zero-shot transfer & code-switching: **Medium** (improvements shown but performance below bilingual training, small-scale studies).

## Next Checks
1. Replace OpenCLIP with a motion-adapted multilingual teacher (e.g., fine-tune CLIP on HumanML3D) and re-evaluate CLA gains.
2. Conduct independent human evaluation on a stratified sample of BiHumanML3D Chinese captions to quantify translation errors and motion-semantic fidelity.
3. Extend BiMD to a new language pair (English-Spanish) using the same pipeline; assess whether FID/R-Precision improvements hold, testing generality of CLA.
