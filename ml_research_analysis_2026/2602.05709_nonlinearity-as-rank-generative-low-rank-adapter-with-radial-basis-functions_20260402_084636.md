# Nonlinearity as Rank: Generative Low-Rank Adapter with Radial Basis Functions
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05709
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05709
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GenLoRA uses generative RBFs to synthesize weight updates from small latents, drastically reducing parameters while outperforming LoRA and variants on multiple models and tasks.

## Executive Summary
GenLoRA addresses the parameter redundancy in standard LoRA by replacing explicit low-rank basis matrices with a generative process: small latent vectors are mapped to basis vectors via lightweight radial basis function (RBF) generators. This reduces storage complexity from O(r(m+n)) to O(m+n+r|θ|) with |θ| ≪ min(m,n). Evaluated on LLaMA3-8B, Gemma-7B, and Qwen2.5-14B across mathematical (Math10K), commonsense (Commonsense170K), and code (HumanEval+) tasks, GenLoRA achieves higher accuracy with far fewer parameters and comparable training time.

## Method Summary
GenLoRA reimagines the LoRA update ΔW = BA. Instead of storing explicit matrices B (m×r) and A (r×n), it learns a small latent vector per target weight matrix and a lightweight RBF generator network. The generator synthesizes the basis vectors on-the-fly during forward passes. This shifts storage burden from fixed matrices to compact latents and generator parameters, theoretically preserving the low-rank structure and ensuring bounded gradients. The synthesis process introduces nonlinearity without increasing the effective rank.

## Key Results
- On Math10K, GenLoRA (r=8, 0.98M) averages 70.17% vs LoRA 67.87% (4.72M), and at r=32 (2.16M) reaches 71.05%.
- On HumanEval+, Pass@1 rises from LoRA's 20.79% to GenLoRA's 25.42% (r=8) and 28.30% (r=32).
- Across LLaMA3-8B, Gemma-7B, Qwen2.5-14B, GenLoRA consistently surpasses LoRA, DoRA, HiRA, and Aurora with significantly fewer parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The generative approach eliminates parameter redundancy by not storing full basis vectors. RBF generators provide nonlinear, localized mappings that can approximate complex weight updates with few parameters. The latent vectors capture the essential degrees of freedom, while the RBFs efficiently produce the required basis matrices during computation. Theoretical guarantees ensure that the synthesized matrices remain low-rank and that gradient signals are well-behaved, enabling stable training despite the synthesis overhead.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Latent Vector -> RBF Generator -> Basis Vectors -> Weight Update (ΔW) -> Adapted Weight (W + ΔW)
- **Critical Path**: Forward: latents pass through RBF networks to produce basis matrices, multiplied to form ΔW, added to frozen weights. Backward: gradients flow through RBFs to update latents and generator parameters.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Parameter reduction vs added RBF synthesis computation; potential inference latency if synthesis unoptimized; nonlinear flexibility vs interpretability.
- **Failure Signatures**: Poor performance with bad RBF initialization; rank deficiency if generators produce non-full-rank bases; overfitting on small datasets due to generator capacity.
- **First 3 Experiments**: 1) Compare GenLoRA vs LoRA at fixed ranks (r=4,8,16) on validation set to confirm tradeoffs. 2) Ablate RBF generator size (|θ|) to measure synthesis quality and training stability. 3) Profile forward pass time per layer to quantify synthesis overhead vs parameter savings.
