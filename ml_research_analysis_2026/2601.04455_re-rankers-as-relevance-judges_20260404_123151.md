# Re-Rankers as Relevance Judges

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04455  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04455  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Re-ranker-based judges (especially reasoning-based Rank1) outperform the state-of-the-art LLM judge UMBRELA in 40–50% of TREC-DL evaluations, but exhibit strong systematic biases.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the overlap between re-ranking and relevance judgment prediction, proposing to repurpose existing pointwise re-rankers as automatic judges. Two adaptation strategies are introduced: (i) direct generation using special tokens (“true”/“false”) for token-generating models, and (ii) score thresholding converting continuous scores to binary labels. Evaluated across TREC-DL 2019–2023, re-ranker judges are compared against the LLM-based UMBRELA; re-ranker judges achieve superior performance (in terms of Cohen’s κ and Kendall’s τ) in a substantial fraction of cases. Notably, the reasoning-based Rank1 model demonstrates the best and most robust judging capability. However, all re-ranker judges reveal significant biases: they favor their own or same-family systems, display cross-family preferences, and overestimate BM25’s effectiveness.

## Method Summary
The authors adapt pointwise re-rankers to produce binary relevance judgments for TREC-DL query-document pairs. For models that generate special tokens (monoT5, Rank1), they greedily decode the final token and map “true” to relevant, “false” to irrelevant. For any pointwise re-ranker, they compute continuous scores and apply a threshold (selected on a source dataset to maximize Cohen’s κ) to assign binary labels. The adapted judges are then evaluated by measuring agreement with human qrels (Cohen’s κ) and correlation in system ranking (Kendall’s τ based on MAP@100). Additionally, each judge is used to evaluate nine systems (BM25 plus eight re-ranked runs), and the resulting rankings are analyzed for biases such as self-preference and BM25 overestimation.

## Key Results
- Re-ranker judges (especially Rank1) outperform UMBRELA in 40–50% of dataset/metric combinations, with Rank1 showing the most robust performance.
- Score thresholding is highly sensitive for monoT5 and RankLLaMA, but Rank1’s scores concentrate at extremes, making its performance flat across thresholds.
- All re-ranker judges exhibit systematic biases: self-preference (judges rank their own re-ranker highest), same-family preference, and a tendency to overestimate BM25’s performance relative to human rankings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Re-rankers can be adapted as relevance judges because both tasks are forms of relevance prediction, sharing underlying modeling objectives.
- **Mechanism**: A re-ranker learns to predict a relevance score for a query-document pair. This scoring function can be repurposed to produce binary labels by either interpreting generated tokens or thresholding continuous scores, avoiding the need to design new judges from scratch.
- **Core assumption**: The relevance representation learned by a re-ranker is transferable to binary judgment prediction without fundamental task divergence.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  [abstract]: "predicting relevance judgments is essentially a form of relevance prediction, a problem extensively studied in tasks such as re-ranking."  
  [section 1]: Quotes from Soboroff and Clarke & Dietz arguing no fundamental difference between ranking and relevance assessment.  
  [corpus]: Neighboring papers show research overlap but this paper is the first to systematically adapt re-rankers as judges.
- **Break condition**: If the re-ranker’s training objective optimizes for ranking order rather than absolute relevance (e.g., pairwise loss), or if the judgment task requires a different granularity (e.g., conversational relevance).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The adaptation strategies (direct generation and score thresholding) effectively bridge the format gap between re-rankers’ continuous outputs and discrete relevance labels.
- **Mechanism**: Direct generation leverages special tokens (“true”/“false”) that some re-rankers (monoT5, Rank1) already generate, mapping them to binary labels. Score thresholding converts any continuous score to binary by applying a threshold, generalizing to re-rankers without token generation.
- **Core assumption**: The re-ranker’s output distribution contains information separable by a threshold or token choice that aligns with binary relevance.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  [abstract]: "We design two adaptation strategies: (i) using binary tokens ... and (ii) converting continuous re-ranking scores into binary labels via thresholding."  
  [section 2]: Formal definition of adapting a re-ranker to output discrete labels.  
  [section 3.2.2]: Implementation details (token mapping, threshold selection).  
  [corpus]: Neighbors focus on prompt design while this paper’s strategies are model-agnostic adaptations.
- **Break condition**: Direct generation fails for models without special tokens (RankLLaMA). Score thresholding fails if scores are not discriminative or threshold generalization across datasets is poor.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Re-ranker-based judges exhibit systematic biases (self-preference, cross-family bias, BM25 overestimation) due to their training objectives and architectural similarities.
- **Mechanism**: A re-ranker’s judgment reflects its ranking preferences learned during training. Judges favor their own or same-family re-rankers because those systems produce outputs similar to the judge’s training distribution. Cross-family bias arises from shared architectural traits (e.g., non-reasoning models prefer non-reasoning systems). Overestimation of BM25 occurs because BM25’s surface-level matching aligns with patterns the re-ranker associates with relevance.
- **Core assumption**: The re-ranker’s relevance prediction is not independent of its own ranking function; it inherits biases from training data and model architecture.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  [abstract]: "all re-ranker-based judges exhibited strong biases, favoring their own and same-family re-rankers, showing cross-family preferences, and overestimating BM25’s performance."  
  [section 4.3]: Scatter plots: self-preference (RankLLaMA-7B judge ranks RankLLaMA-7B first), cross-family bias (monoT5 and RankLLaMA judges rate each other higher than Rank1), BM25 overestimation (monoT5/RankLLaMA assign higher MAP@100 to BM25 than humans).  
  [corpus]: Neighbors document LLM judge inflation, supporting bias existence.
- **Break condition**: If the re-ranker is trained on a diverse mixture of retrieval systems or if adaptation includes explicit debiasing.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Pointwise Re-ranking  
  - *Why needed*: The paper adapts pointwise re-rankers, which score each query-document pair independently. This property enables straightforward binarization via thresholding and aligns with the pointwise judgment task.  
  - *Quick check*: What is the key difference between pointwise and listwise re-ranking in terms of training objective and output?

- **Concept**: Evaluation Metrics for Judgment Quality (Cohen’s κ and Kendall’s τ)  
  - *Why needed*: These metrics quantify judgment agreement with humans (κ) and system-ranking correlation (τ), respectively. They are the primary evidence for the paper’s claims about judge effectiveness.  
  - *Quick check*: If a judge has high Cohen’s κ but low Kendall’s τ, what might that imply about its judgment style?

- **Concept**: Reasoning-based Re-ranking  
  - *Why needed*: The paper highlights Rank1 (a reasoning-based re-ranker) as the most robust judge. Understanding its “generate reasoning traces before scoring” design explains its superior score concentration and threshold robustness.  
  - *Quick check*: How does generating a reasoning trace before scoring potentially improve a re-ranker’s robustness?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
(Query, Document) -> Re-ranker Model (Token/Score) -> Adaptation Layer (Direct Generation or Thresholding) -> Binary Labels per Document -> Aggregated Judgment Set -> Evaluation (Cohen’s κ, Kendall’s τ) & Bias Analysis (Self/Family Preference, BM25 Overestimation)

**Critical path**:
1. Input a query-document pair into the re-ranker.
2. For direct generation: decode one token; map “true”→1, “false”→0. For thresholding: compute score; if ≥ threshold →1 else 0.
3. Aggregate labels across queries to form a judgment set.
4. Compute evaluation metrics or system rankings.
5. For bias analysis: repeat steps 1–3 for all runs (BM25 + re-rankers), then rank runs by evaluation metric and analyze preferences.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Direct generation is low-latency and uses native outputs but limited to token-generation models (monoT5, Rank1). Score thresholding is universal but requires threshold selection; performance varies widely across thresholds for some models (RankLLaMA) but is stable for others (Rank1).
- Using a single re-ranker as a judge is simple but introduces bias; ensemble judges would reduce bias at the cost of complexity and compute.
- Computing thresholds on a development set may overfit if distributions shift (e.g., corpus change).

**Failure signatures**:
- **Low judgment agreement**: Cohen’s κ < 0.3 (e.g., monoT5 on TREC-DL 22/23) indicates poor label quality.
- **Threshold sensitivity**: Large swings in κ or τ across small threshold changes (e.g., RankLLaMA-13B) signal unstable score distributions.
- **Bias patterns**: Judge consistently ranks its own family first (self-preference) or ranks reasoning systems (Rank1) abnormally low (cross-family bias). BM25’s judged MAP far exceeds human estimates suggests overestimation.
- **Zero-shot degradation**: Performance collapse on out-of-corpus datasets (e.g., monoT5 drop on TREC-DL 22/23) indicates poor generalization.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Direct generation replication**: Run monoT5-base on TREC-DL 19 test queries with greedy decoding, map tokens to labels, compute Cohen’s κ against TREC qrels. Expected: ~0.389.
2. **Threshold sensitivity analysis**: For monoT5-3B, compute continuous scores on TREC-DL 22, vary threshold from 0 to 1 in steps of 0.05, plot κ and Kendall’s τ (MAP@100). Observe peak around 0.4–0.5 and rapid drop elsewhere.
3. **Bias analysis sanity check**: Using monoT5-base as judge (best threshold from TREC-DL 19), evaluate BM25 and the 8 re-ranked runs on TREC-DL 19. Compute MAP@100 for each run, rank them, and calculate Kendall’s τ vs. human ranking. Expect τ ~0.444. Verify self-preference: monoT5-base run should rank highest or second-highest.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the strong biases exhibited by re-ranker-based judges—including self-preference, same-family preference, and BM25 overestimation—be effectively mitigated?
- **Basis in paper**: RQ3 and Section 4.3 document these biases. Section 6 lists “Mitigating biases in re-ranker-based judges” as future work (e.g., adapter parameters) but provides no experiments.
- **Why unresolved**: Bias is characterized but not investigated; no proposed or evaluated mitigation strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled experiments testing debiasing methods (e.g., fine-tuning on balanced data, architectural changes) with quantitative evaluation showing reduced bias without sacrificing judging accuracy across multiple families and datasets.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can the proposed adaptation strategies be extended to support graded (multi-level) relevance judgments?
- **Basis in paper**: Section 3.2.2 explicitly states extension to graded judgments is future work; the study focuses solely on binary labels.
- **Why unresolved**: Graded relevance is standard in many IR tasks. The paper does not explore conversion methods or evaluate performance on multi-level qrels.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extending the framework to use TREC-DL’s native 4-level judgments (or other graded collections), defining mappings from re-ranker outputs to multiple grades, and measuring agreement (e.g., quadratic weighted κ) and ranking correlation.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: To what extent do the findings generalize beyond pointwise re-rankers to listwise or pairwise re-rankers?
- **Basis in paper**: Section 6 lists adapting non-pointwise rankers as future work; all evaluated models are pointwise.
- **Why unresolved**: Listwise and pairwise rankers have different objectives/output structures; it is unclear if the adaptation strategies apply or produce different biases.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Apply analogous adaptation to representative listwise/pairwise rankers (e.g., RankZephyr, a BERT pairwise ranker), then compare judging performance (κ, τ) and bias profiles against pointwise results.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: What is the underlying cause of the cross-family bias where non-reasoning re-rankers deprioritize reasoning-based (Rank1) outputs?
- **Basis in paper**: Inferred from Figure 4–6: monoT5 and RankLLaMA judges rank Rank1 systems close to BM25, while UMBRELA and Rank1 judges accurately rate BM25. The bias is documented but not analyzed.
- **Why unresolved**: Potential causes (training data, reasoning traces, score distributions) are not investigated; the bias remains unexplained.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablations or controlled fine-tuning isolating factors (e.g., adding/removing reasoning traces), or analysis of internal representations when evaluating Rank1 vs. non-Rank1 outputs.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: Is Rank1’s threshold robustness (insensitive to threshold) a direct consequence of its chain-of-thought reasoning or an artifact of its data/calibration?
- **Basis in paper**: Inferred from Section 4.2/Figure 3: Rank1’s scores are highly concentrated at extremes; the paper hypothesizes a link to reasoning but does not prove causality.
- **Why unresolved**: Correlation between reasoning, score concentration, and robustness is suggestive but not causal; could be due to training data or calibration.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Compare Rank1 against a non-reasoning variant fine-tuned on same data without reasoning traces. If the non-reasoning variant’s scores are less concentrated and more threshold-sensitive, it supports the reasoning hypothesis.

## Limitations
- **UMBRELA reproduction blocked**: No public UMBRELA judgments or API access prevents validation of the claim that re-ranker judges outperform UMBRELA in 40–50% of cases.
- **Ambiguous thresholding**: The paper does not specify whether Cohen’s κ or Kendall’s τ is optimized during threshold selection, leading to potential reproducibility issues.
- **Biases and generalizability**: Systematic biases (self-preference, BM25 overestimation) are identified but not mitigated; results are limited to TREC-DL 2019–2023 and may not generalize.
- **UMBRELA comparison unverifiable** and **threshold selection metric unspecified** are the two critical blockers; the third concerns **bias mitigation**.
