# Affordances Enable Partial World Modeling with LLMs
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10390
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10390
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Partial world models induced by affordances reduce Monte-Carlo search branching and achieve higher rewards than full world models in tabletop stacking tasks.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) are prohibitively inefficient and inaccurate when used as full world models for planning due to the enormous state-action space and propensity for hallucinations. This paper introduces a novel framework that constructs partial world models from affordances—state-action pairs that reliably achieve task-agnostic, language-conditioned intents. The authors theoretically prove that any agent capable of achieving such intents must internally encode a predictive partial model, and that using this model in search provably reduces the branching factor. Practically, they implement the approach with LLM-generated distribution-robust affordances and an LLM-based dynamics model, incorporating an adaptive correction to cover missing intents. Experiments in tabletop robotics stacking tasks (3, 5, and 7 blocks) demonstrate that the partial model significantly outperforms a full LLM world model, achieving higher rewards with far fewer simulations and steps.

## Method Summary
The method begins by defining a finite set of task-agnostic intents (e.g., “pick up an unobstructed block”) that are robust across a distribution of tasks. An affordance model, prompted with the current state and intent descriptions, outputs a small set of abstract actions (o) that are afforded—they reliably satisfy the intents from that state. These affordances define a partial world model: a dynamics model (also an LLM) predicts next states only for the afforded actions, drastically limiting the branching factor in planning. Search is performed with Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), which expands only nodes corresponding to afforded actions. To guarantee completeness, an adaptive mechanism occasionally samples from a full model with a small probability ε_cor, ensuring that even tasks requiring an intent outside the initial set can eventually be solved. The authors evaluate on a tabletop stacking simulator, comparing the partial model (with LLM-generated affordances) against a full LLM world model baseline, across tasks of increasing difficulty.

## Key Results
- Theoretically, any agent that reliably achieves task-agnostic, language-conditioned intents must encode a predictive partial world model, and search over such models reduces the branching factor.
- On 3-block stacking, partial models achieve a score of 3.75 vs 0.0 for full models, with fewer simulations (2.75 vs >4) and fewer steps to completion (5 vs >10).
- On harder tasks (5 and 7 blocks), partial models improve accumulated reward by >40% (0.921 vs 0.650) and online evaluation by >97% (1.56 vs 0.790); full models fail at 7 blocks (negative reward -0.187).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** An agent that reliably achieves a set of language-conditioned, task-agnostic intents must internally encode a predictive partial world model focused on the state-action pairs associated with those intents.
- **Mechanism:** The agent’s successful policy, when constrained to perform certain abstract actions (`o`) from states where they are *afforded* (`(s,o) ∈ AF_I`), implicitly defines transition probabilities `ˆP_par(s'|s,o)`. This model is *partial* because it is only defined and accurate for the affordance set, not the entire state-action space.
- **Core assumption:** The agent is `(n, ζ, δ)`-optimal for a distribution of tasks, meaning it solves depth-`n` tasks with low regret (`ζ`) and low probability (`δ`) of violating that regret across the task distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States the theoretical result: "proving that agents achieving task-agnostic, language-conditioned intents necessarily possess predictive partial-world models."
  - [section 4.1] Provides Theorem 1 and its proof, deriving a bound on the error `|ˆP_par - P|` for `(s,o) ∈ AF_I` that decays with task depth `n` and is controlled by intent satisfiability `ζ` and confidence `δ`.
  - [corpus] Neighbor evidence is weak; related papers focus on planning with LLMs or affordance recognition, not on *inducing* a partial model from intent achievement.
- **Break condition:** If the agent cannot reliably achieve the specified intents across the task distribution (i.e., violates the `(n, ζ, δ)`-optimality condition), the guarantee that its policy encodes an accurate partial model does not hold. Hallucinations or systematic failures on afforded actions would invalidate the learned `ˆP_par`.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Using an affordance-induced partial world model for search provably improves sample efficiency (reduces branching factor) compared to using a full world model, under the condition that the partial model’s intent set is sufficiently covered.
- **Mechanism:** Search (e.g., Monte-Carlo Tree Search) only expands branches for actions `o` that are in the affordance set `AF_I`. This reduces the branching factor from `n=|O|` to `k=|I_par|` (where `k << n`). The adaptive corrected model (`P_ada`) further ensures robustness by occasionally sampling from the full model to cover missing necessary intents, with only a small `O(L)` penalty in sample complexity.
- **Core assumption:** The set of task-agnostic intents `I_par` is finite and much smaller than the full action space (`k << n`), and there exists an `ε_cor` such that the corrected model can solve all tasks with non-zero probability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Claims "provably efficient search by reducing the branching factor" and references Theorem 2.
  - [section 4.2] Formalizes the corrected model `P_cor` and presents Theorem 2, proving the adaptive model `P_ada` achieves success within a factor `e(2L+3)` of the best `ε_cor` per task.
  - [section 5.1, Table 1] Empirically shows fewer MC simulations (2.75 vs >4) and steps to completion (5 vs >10) for the 3-block task when using the partial model vs. the full LLM model.
- **Break condition:** If the predefined affordance set `AF_I` completely omits an intent necessary to solve even a single task in the distribution (i.e., `P_cor(success_i) = 0` for some `i`), then `N_ada` becomes infinite. The adaptive model’s `ε_cor` must be large enough to eventually sample the missing intent.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Distribution-robust affordances—those that achieve task-agnostic intents with high probability (`ζ`) across the task distribution—enable partial models that generalize efficiently to novel tasks within the same distribution.
- **Mechanism:** Task-agnostic intents (e.g., "pick up an unobstructed block") are grounded in agent embodiment and invariant across tasks. The affordance model `f_affordances(s, m)` identifies `m` such affordances per state, inducing a partial model `P_par` that focuses on these robust transitions. This shared structure reduces search branching and improves reward accumulation on unseen tasks.
- **Core assumption:** The task distribution `Dist(T)` has common structure such that the same set of task-agnostic intents (`I_agn`) are achievable with high probability (`δ`-robust) across most tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3] Introduces Definition 4 (δ-robust affordances) and Figure 2, contrasting task-agnostic (distribution-robust) vs. task-specific affordances.
  - [section 5.2, Table 2] Shows improved accumulated reward (+40% for 5 blocks, positive vs. negative for 7 blocks) and online evaluation (+97% for 5 blocks) using the partial model over the full model as task difficulty increases, demonstrating cross-task generalization.
  - [abstract] States that distribution-robust affordances "generalize across tasks" and yield "higher rewards compared to full world models."
- **Break condition:** If the task distribution lacks stable, shared structure—i.e., no set of intents is affordably achievable with bounded `δ` across tasks—then the distribution-robust affordance set `AF_I` will be small or empty. The partial model would then degenerate to a full model or provide no useful constraint, losing efficiency gains.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Partial Models**  
  - **Why needed here:** The core innovation is shifting from an intractable full world model (`P(s'|s,o)` for all `s,o`) to a tractable partial model defined only on affordances (`P_par(s'|s,o)` for `(s,o) ∈ AF_I`). Understanding this distinction is fundamental to grasping the efficiency claim.  
  - **Quick check question:** *What is the key difference between a full world model and a partial world model in this paper’s framework?*

- **Concept: Affordances (as ζ-affordance sets)**  
  - **Why needed here:** Affordances are the mechanism that defines the scope of the partial model. They are not just "possible actions" but state-action pairs that reliably achieve a specified intent to a degree `ζ` (Definition 3). This precise definition links intent satisfaction to model accuracy.  
  - **Quick check question:** *According to Definition 3, what two conditions must hold for an action `o` in state `s` to be considered an affordance?*

- **Concept: Task-Agnostic vs. Task-Specific Intents**  
  - **Why needed here:** The multi-task extension hinges on this delineation (Section 3). Task-agnostic intents (e.g., "pick up a free block") are robust across tasks and generate useful, generalizable affordances. Task-specific intents are handled by the reward signal. Confusing these leads to overfitting affordances to a single task.  
  - **Quick check question:** *Give one example of a task-agnostic intent and one example of a task-specific intent in the tabletop stacking domain.*

- **Concept: Distribution-Robust Affordances**  
  - **Why needed here:** To generalize across a distribution of tasks, affordances must be achievable with high probability (`δ`) across tasks. This is the notion of distribution-robustness (Definition 4). Without it, the partial model may not cover necessary transitions for new tasks.  
  - **Quick check question:** *What does Definition 4 require for an affordance set to be δ-robust?*

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** State -> Affordance Model -> Partial World Model -> Search/Planner (MCTS) -> Action -> Environment -> New State. Task Distribution provides tasks, and Evaluator computes rewards for leaf nodes.
- **Critical path:** State `s` -> `f_affordances(s, m)` -> `AF_I` -> For each `o ∈ AF_I`, query `P_par(s'|s,o)` -> Run MCTS using these constrained dynamics -> Select action `o*` -> Execute in env -> New state `s'`. The adaptive layer injects random full-model actions with low probability `ε_cor`.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Bias vs. Efficiency:** Narrow `I_agn` (few intents) yields high efficiency (`k` small) but risks missing task-solving paths (bias). Wide `I_agn` increases coverage but may approach full-model inefficiency.  
  - **LLM Prompt Engineering:** The quality of `AF_I` is highly sensitive to the prompt for `f_affordances` and the few-shot examples for `P_par`. Hand-specified intents (oracle) work best but are unscalable.  
  - **Adaptive `ε_cor`:** Setting `ε_cor` too low risks failure on tasks needing missing intents; too high erodes efficiency. The adaptive scheme (`P_ada`) mitigates this but adds `O(L)` overhead.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Affordance mis-specification:** If `f_affordances` fails to output a truly afforded action (e.g., calls "pick up" on an occluded block), the partial model never explores that branch, leading to irrevocably poor plans. This is catastrophic under tight search budgets.  
  - **Distribution shift:** If test tasks involve novel object types or physics not captured by `I_agn`, `AF_I` may be sparse or empty, causing the planner to fall back to the full model (if adaptive) or fail.  
  - **LLM Hallucination in `P_par`:** Even for afforded actions, the LLM dynamics model may predict unrealistic states `s'`. This corrupts the search tree. The paper notes this is less severe than with a full model because queries are constrained.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Oracle Affordance Baseline:** Run the pipeline with programmatically generated, perfect `AF_I` (ground truth affordances). This isolates the *efficiency benefit* of constraints from the *accuracy* of the LLM affordance model. Expect perfect success with minimal simulations.  
  2. **LLM Affordance vs. Full Model (3 blocks):** Compare `P_AFF-LLM` vs. `P_LLM` on the simplest stacking task. Verify reduced simulations and higher reward.  
  3. **Scaling to 5 & 7 blocks:** Test if the same `I_agn` (from experiment 2) generalizes to harder tasks. The partial model should maintain or improve relative performance as branching increases.

## Limitations
- **Affordance model reliability:** LLM-generated affordances may be incomplete or hallucinated, breaking the theoretical link between intent achievement and partial model accuracy.
- **Distribution robustness assumption:** The strong gains rely on a stable, shared set of task-agnostic intents across the task distribution; this may not hold for diverse or open-ended task families.
- **Theoretical assumptions:** The core proofs assume a `(n, ζ, δ)`-optimal agent, which real LLM-based agents do not satisfy, making the derived error bounds aspirational.

## Next Checks
1. **OOD Generalization Test:** Evaluate the same `I_agn` and `P_par` on a different tabletop task distribution (e.g., tool use, articulated objects) to directly test the assumption that a fixed set of task-agnostic intents remains affordably achievable and useful.
2. **Affordance Fidelity Audit:** For a subset of states, manually annotate ground-truth affordances (valid `(s,o)` for `I_agn`) and compare to the LLM-generated `AF_I` to quantify precision and recall. Low recall, especially on states where the full model succeeds, would identify a key failure mode.
3. **Adaptive Mechanism Ablation:** Fix `ε_cor = 0` (no full-model fallback) and measure the failure rate on tasks known to require an intent outside `I_agn`. Then systematically vary `ε_cor` to map the performance/safety trade-off.
