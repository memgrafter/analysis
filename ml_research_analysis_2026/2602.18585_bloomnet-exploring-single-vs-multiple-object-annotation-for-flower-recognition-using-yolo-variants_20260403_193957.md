# BloomNet: Exploring Single vs. Multiple Object Annotation for Flower Recognition Using YOLO Variants

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18585
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18585
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: YOLOv8m with SGD optimizer achieved the highest precision and recall for sparse flower detection (SISBB), while YOLOv12n with SGD was most robust for dense multi-object detection (SIMBB) on the FloralSix dataset.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the FloralSix dataset (2,816 images across six flower species) to investigate how annotation density—single-image single-bounding box (SISBB) versus single-image multiple-bounding box (SIMBB)—affects object detection performance for precision agriculture. Benchmarking various YOLO architectures under both regimes reveals that scene density dictates optimal model choice: precision-oriented models (e.g., YOLOv8m) excel in sparse settings, whereas recall-optimized models (e.g., YOLOv12n) handle dense clusters better. Across the board, the SGD optimizer outperforms AdamW, attributed to better generalization on natural variations. The study underscores the importance of aligning annotation strategy, model architecture, and optimizer with the target deployment environment.

## Method Summary
The authors curated the FloralSix dataset from garden images, providing two annotation sets: SISBB (one bounding box per image, indicating a single salient flower) and SIMBB (multiple boxes per image, labeling all visible flowers). Images were resized to 640×640 and split into training (75%), validation (15%), and test (15%) sets. A suite of YOLO models (v5s, v8n/s/m, v12n) was trained separately on each annotation regime using either SGD or AdamW optimizer (initial LR 0.01, batch 16, up to 100 epochs with early stopping). Performance was evaluated on the held-out test set using standard detection metrics: Precision, Recall, mAP@0.5, and mAP@0.5:0.95.

## Key Results
- SISBB: YOLOv8m trained with SGD achieved Precision 0.956, Recall 0.951, mAP@0.5 0.978, and mAP@0.5:0.95 0.865—the highest among all models.
- SIMBB: YOLOv12n with SGD attained mAP@0.5 0.934 and mAP@0.5:0.95 0.752, demonstrating strongest robustness in dense scenes.
- Optimizer: SGD consistently outperformed AdamW across all models and both annotation regimes, with the most pronounced gains in mAP@0.5:0.95.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The differing annotation regimes fundamentally reshape the detection task. SISBB simplifies learning to find one prominent object per image, reducing spatial ambiguity and enabling models to specialize in high-precision single-instance localization. SIMBB introduces multiple, often overlapping targets, forcing the model to develop richer feature discrimination and non-maximum suppression skills for dense scenes. This explains why precision-oriented models like YOLOv8m thrive in sparse contexts, while recall-optimized ones such as anchor-free YOLOv12n cope better with clusters. Additionally, SGD’s noisier gradient updates promote flatter minima in the loss landscape, enhancing generalization to natural variations in flower appearance, lighting, and background clutter—thus its consistent superiority over AdamW on this dataset. Finally, architectural choices matter: larger models have capacity for fine-grained features useful for isolated objects, whereas anchor-free designs mitigate anchor mismatch in dense arrangements.

## Foundational Learning
- **Object Detection Metrics (Precision, Recall, mAP@0.5, mAP@0.5:0.95)**  
  *Why needed:* These metrics quantify model accuracy and localisation quality; mAP@0.5 is lenient, mAP@0.5:0.95 is strict. In SIMBB, recall indicates success at finding multiple instances.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a model show high mAP@0.5 but low mAP@0.5:0.95 in SIMBB?  
- **Annotation Regimes (SISBB vs. SIMBB)**  
  *Why needed:* The regime defines the learning objective: SISBB trains to detect “a flower”, SIMBB trains to detect “all flowers”. This choice shapes model behavior.  
  *Quick check:* If you deploy a SISBB-trained model on a dense field image, what outcome is expected?  
- **YOLO Architectural Families (v5, v8, v12)**  
  *Why needed:* Each generation introduces design changes (e.g., anchor-free prediction in v12) that differentially suit sparse or dense scenes.  
  *Quick check:* What key architectural difference between YOLOv8 and YOLOv12 might explain v12’s better SIMBB performance?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** FloralSix Dataset -> YOLO Model Zoo (v5s, v8n/s/m, v12n) -> Training Engine (PyTorch, SGD/AdamW) -> Evaluation Module (metrics computation)
- **Critical path:** Data annotation (SISBB/SIMBB) -> Model initialization -> Training (optimizer selection) -> Validation -> Test evaluation -> Metric comparison
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  • Annotation cost: SIMBB (~2.5× more boxes) vs. SISBB simplicity  
  • Model size: YOLOv8m (49.6MB, high SISBB accuracy) vs. YOLOv12n (5.6MB, efficient SIMBB inference)  
  • Optimizer: SGD (slower convergence, better generalization) vs. AdamW (faster, may underfit)
- **Failure signatures:**  
  • SISBB model on SIMBB scenes: very low recall (<0.7), missed flowers  
  • AdamW-trained models: systematically lower mAP, especially recall in SIMBB  
  • YOLOv5s: consistently inferior mAP vs. v8/v12, indicating architectural limits
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replicate SISBB baseline:** Train YOLOv8m-SGD on SISBB; expect mAP50 >0.97, mAP50-95 ~0.86.  
  2. **Optimizer impact:** For YOLOv8n, compare SGD vs. AdamW under both regimes; expect SGD > AdamW by >0.02 mAP50-95.  
  3. **Density stress test:** Evaluate best SIMBB model (YOLOv12n-SGD) on a high-density subset (>15 flowers/image); measure recall drop versus overall SIMBB recall.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What standardized protocols and dataset characteristics are needed for large-scale multi-species flower detection benchmarks that ensure robust generalization across botanical diversity and environmental conditions?  
- Which architectural innovations in YOLO variants (anchor-free prediction, CSP module design, neck structure) are most critical for enhancing dense flower detection, and how can they be systematically optimized?  
- How do SISBB and SIMBB annotation strategies impact deployment efficiency and task accuracy in real-world precision agriculture (e.g., robotic pollination, phenotyping) beyond standard detection metrics?  
- Is the observed superiority of SGD over AdamW for YOLO training on flower detection consistent across diverse datasets, model sizes, and densities, or is it specific to FloralSix and the chosen hyperparameters?  
- To what extent can models trained on region-specific FloralSix generalize to floral species, backgrounds, and climatic conditions from other continents or seasons?

## Limitations
- Exact training augmentations, random seeds for data splits, and Ultralytics version are not reported, hindering faithful reproduction.  
- The study lacks cross-dataset validation; conclusions are based solely on FloralSix, which covers only six species from a single region.  
- No ablation experiments isolate the contributions of specific architectural components to performance differences across density regimes.

## Confidence
- Annotation regime effect on model suitability: **Medium** (supported by comparative metrics but limited analysis of mixed-density scenarios)  
- SGD superiority over AdamW: **Medium** (consistent across tables but without hyperparameter sensitivity analysis)  
- Model-architecture match to density (v8m for sparse, v12n for dense): **Medium** (plausible anchor-free rationale but no controlled component swaps)

## Next Checks
1. **Optimizer controlled test:** Fix seed, augmentations, and model (e.g., YOLOv8n); run 5 replicates with SGD and AdamW on both annotation regimes; confirm SGD’s mAP50-95 advantage >0.02 with statistical significance.  
2. **Density robustness probe:** Using YOLOv12n-SGD (best SIMBB), evaluate on a held-out high-density subset (≥15 flowers/image); quantify recall drop vs. overall SIMBB test recall to identify density ceiling.  
3. **Annotation consistency audit:** Randomly sample 50 SIMBB training images; verify all visible flowers are labeled and boxes accurate; if >5% of flowers are unannotated, SIMBB performance may be underestimated.
