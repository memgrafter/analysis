# ArtifactLens: Hundreds of Labels Are Enough for Artifact Detection with VLMs
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.09475
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09475
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Achieves SOTA F1=0.82 on human artifact benchmarks with Gemini-2.5-Pro, outperforming fine-tuned models by 8% using only ~10% of their training data.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ArtifactLens, a system for detecting subtle artifacts (e.g., distorted hands) in AI-generated images using frozen pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) with minimal labeled data. The method scaffolds VLMs with three key innovations: decomposing detection into specialist modules for different artifact subtypes, using counterfactual in-context learning demonstrations, and optimizing prompts across a spectrum of confidence thresholds to overcome VLM bias. Evaluated on five human artifact benchmarks, ArtifactLens achieves state-of-the-art F1 scores (e.g., 0.82 with Gemini-2.5-Pro) while using only about 10% of the training data required by fine-tuned models. It also generalizes to other artifact types and improves zero-shot VLM performance by 31–54%.

## Method Summary
ArtifactLens is a compound AI system built around frozen VLMs. It first uses GroundingDino to propose regions of interest (human body parts) and assigns each region to a specialist module tailored to a specific artifact subtype (e.g., deformed hand). Each specialist applies an optimized VLM prompt to the cropped region, leveraging in-context learning with counterfactual demonstration pairs (visually similar images with opposite labels) to adapt the VLM with few examples. Prompt optimization is performed via an LLM using the COPRO algorithm, augmented with full-spectrum hints to generate instructions ranging from high-recall to high-precision, addressing the VLM's inherent bias toward conservative predictions. Predictions from all specialists are aggregated via logical OR to produce the final image-level label. The system is implemented in the DSPy framework and optimized independently for each specialist using its own training data.

## Key Results
- Achieves SOTA F1=0.82 on human artifact benchmarks with Gemini-2.5-Pro.
- Outperforms fine-tuned models by 8% while using only ~10% of their training data.
- Maintains performance with only 200 training examples (9% degradation) and generalizes to other artifact types, improving zero-shot VLM F1 by 31–54%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decomposition into Specialists
- **Claim:** Artifact detection performance improves by decomposing the task into multiple specialist modules, each optimized for a specific artifact subtype (e.g., "deformed hand").
- **Mechanism:** Each specialist independently processes candidate image regions (via cropping) and applies a text-optimized VLM to its subtype. Predictions are aggregated via logical OR. This isolates learning signals and reduces interference between visually distinct artifact types.
- **Core assumption:** Human artifact subtypes (hand, face, limb) are sufficiently separable; a model tuned for one subtype does not suffer significant negative transfer to others.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "decomposes detection into specialized sub-tasks (e.g., “deformed hand”)"
  - [section] Section 3.2 describes the specialist architecture and logical aggregation.
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., *See and Fix the Flaws*) also explore decomposition, but direct evidence for *ArtifactLens*'s specialists is from internal ablations (Table 3).
- **Break condition:** If artifact subtypes are highly correlated in the data (e.g., hand and face errors always co-occur), specialization may not yield gains and could increase inference cost unnecessarily.

### Mechanism 2: Counterfactual Demonstrations for ICL
- **Claim:** Selecting in-context learning (ICL) demonstrations as pairs of visually similar images with opposite labels ("counterfactual" pairs) improves the VLM's ability to learn the artifact concept from few examples.
- **Mechanism:** By contrasting near-identical images that differ primarily in the presence/absence of the target artifact, the VLM's attention is steered toward the artifact feature rather than dataset-wide spurious correlations.
- **Core assumption:** CLIP-based similarity metrics can retrieve image pairs where the artifact is the primary distinguishing visual feature.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section] Section 3.3 defines counterfactual demonstrations; Figure 3 illustrates the pairing logic.
  - [corpus] Weak; neighboring work on ICL (e.g., *VL-ICL Bench*) studies demo selection but not counterfactual pairing.
  - [abstract] "novel improvements to each" refers to CF demos as an ICL contribution.
- **Break condition:** If CLIP similarity fails to isolate the artifact (e.g., paired images differ in lighting, pose, or context), counterfactual demos could introduce irrelevant contrastive signals and degrade performance versus random sampling.

### Mechanism 3: Full-Spectrum Prompt Optimization
- **Claim:** Automatically generating text instructions that span a spectrum of confidence thresholds (from high-recall to high-precision) overcomes the inherent VLM bias toward conservative, low-recall predictions on artifact detection.
- **Mechanism:** LLM-based prompt generation is seeded with hints that explicitly vary the desired confidence threshold (e.g., "flag even with 30% confidence" vs. "require 90% confidence"). This ensures the candidate prompt pool includes instructions that directly counteract the VLM's learned caution.
- **Core assumption:** Zero-shot VLMs are systematically biased against predicting the positive "artifact" class (low recall), and this bias is instruction-sensitive.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section] Section D.2 quantifies that 96% of baseline COPRO prompts are "high precision"; full-spectrum yields 74% "high recall" and improves F1 by 0.049 (Table 6).
  - [abstract] "overcome VLM bias against labeling artifacts."
  - [corpus] Neighbors (*AlignGemini*) also address VLM bias but not via full-spectrum prompting.
- **Break condition:** If a specific VLM backbone has no systematic recall bias (e.g., is already calibrated), full-spectrum prompting may add unnecessary variance without benefit.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**: *Why needed*: The entire scaffold relies on frozen pretrained VLMs as the perceptual engine. Understanding their zero-shot capabilities and limitations (e.g., bias, in-context learning) is essential. *Quick check*: "How does a VLM typically behave on a binary classification task for a rare visual concept without adaptation?"
- **In-Context Learning (ICL)**: *Why needed*: ICL is the primary mechanism for adapting frozen VLMs with minimal data. Grasping demo selection strategies (random, retrieval, counterfactual) is key to replication. *Quick check*: "Why might retrieval-based demo selection outperform random sampling for a fine-grained visual task like artifact detection?"
- **Compound AI Systems / Workflows**: *Why needed*: ArtifactLens is a fixed-topology system of multiple components (retriever, specialists, optimizer). Understanding this paradigm—vs. monolithic model fine-tuning—is central to the paper's contribution. *Quick check*: "What are the trade-offs between a single-VLM call and a multi-specialist architecture for a detection task with sub-types?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
1. Region Proposer: GroundingDino detects humans/body parts; crops regions per specialist type.
2. Specialist Modules: K independent modules; each takes original image + crop, runs a VLM with optimized prompt for one artifact subtype.
3. ICL Retriever: For each specialist, retrieves demo images from its training set (CLIP similarity) to form in-context pairs.
4. Prompt Optimizer: LLM generates/rewrites prompts; aggregator selects best via validation set.
5. Aggregator: Logical OR over specialist predictions yields final image-level label.

**Critical path**: Input image → parallel crop & specialist calls → collect all specialist outputs → OR aggregation → output label. ICL and prompt optimization are pre-execution (training-time) steps.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Specialist granularity: More specialists → higher potential accuracy but more VLM calls per image. Combine rare sublabels to save cost.
- ICL vs. TextOpt: ICL (demo selection) often yields larger gains, but TextOpt (full-spectrum) is cheaper computationally during inference.
- Validation set size: Smaller datasets risk overfitting during prompt optimization; the paper shows ~200 samples still works well.

**Failure signatures**:
- Low recall: Specialist prompts are too conservative; check if full-spectrum hints were applied.
- Low precision: Specialists over-trigger; inspect demo quality or crop regions (too tight/loose?).
- High inter-specialist conflict: Many images where some specialists predict artifact and others don't; may indicate sublabel definitions overlap or cropping misses relevant regions.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Zero-shot baseline: Run base VLM (no scaffolding) on 100-sample subset. Expect very low recall (<0.4 F1), confirming the need for adaptation.
2. Single vs. Multi-specialist: Implement a single-VLM version (all sublabels in one prompt). Compare F1 on 500-sample validation; expect ~0.05 drop (Table 3).
3. CF demos validation: For one specialist (e.g., "hand artifact"), compare DynamicFewShot (CF) vs. LabeledFewShot on 200 training samples. Expect CF to yield higher recall without harming precision (Table 6).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: To what extent do pretrained VLMs inherently possess artifact detection knowledge versus acquiring it through the scaffolding process?
- **Basis in paper**: The core claim states that "pretrained VLMs already encode the knowledge needed to detect artifacts — with the right scaffolding, this capability can be unlocked."
- **Why unresolved**: The scaffolding process includes in-context learning and prompt optimization, which may teach the VLM the task rather than merely unlocking pre-existing knowledge. The paper does not isolate whether the knowledge was present in pretraining or induced by the adaptation components.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled experiments probing VLM internal representations before and after applying scaffolding; evaluating the transfer of optimized prompts and ICL demonstrations to entirely novel artifact categories without any further adaptation; comparing with a VLM that has been exposed to artifact data during pre-training.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What are the limits of generalization of artifact detection scaffolding across diverse generator architectures and novel artifact types?
- **Basis in paper**: The paper demonstrates generalization to object morphology, animal anatomy, and AIGC detection on limited benchmarks, but does not test against specialized detectors or on truly novel domains (e.g., video generation, 3D render artifacts).
- **Why unresolved**: Real-world deployment faces constantly evolving generative models and artifact types. The evaluation only covers human-centered artifacts and does not systematically probe failure modes or boundary conditions for generalization.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Benchmark suite with a wide variety of artifact types (e.g., logical inconsistencies, temporal artifacts in video) from emerging generators; zero-shot transfer tests where training and test artifact distributions are disjoint; detailed error analysis identifying systematic blind spots.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the root cause of the VLM bias against labeling artifacts, and can full-spectrum prompting fundamentally address it or only temporarily circumvent it?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors observe that VLMs are "biased against the artifact class" and propose full-spectrum prompting to cover a range of confidence thresholds.
- **Why unresolved**: The source of the bias (e.g., training data imbalance, RLHF objectives, architectural priors) is not investigated. Full-spectrum prompting may be a heuristic workaround rather than a solution if the bias is deeply embedded in the VLM's decision-making.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Analysis of pretraining data distributions for artifact prevalence; probing VLM internal states when processing artifact vs. non-artifact images; comparing bias magnitude across VLM families and sizes; interventions that directly adjust decision thresholds at the representation level.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: What is the optimal trade-off between multi-component specialist architecture and single-VLM optimization regarding accuracy, data efficiency, and computational cost?
- **Basis in paper**: An ablation shows specialists improve performance (~0.05 F1) but increase inference cost. However, the design space (number of specialists, clustering strategies, adaptive routing) is not thoroughly explored.
- **Why unresolved**: Practitioners need to know if the accuracy gain justifies the cost under varying data budgets and latency constraints. Diminishing returns with more specialists or potential for cost-effective routing are open.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic study varying the number and grouping of specialists, measuring F1 versus inference FLOPs/latency; analysis of per-specialist performance to identify which error types benefit most; learning routing policies that activate only relevant specialists per image.

## Limitations
- Exact model checkpoints/APIs for VLM and LLM components are unspecified, hindering replication.
- Hyperparameter details (e.g., COPRO iterations, pool size, full-spectrum hint phrasing) lack sensitivity analysis.
- Reliance on CLIP similarity for counterfactual pairs may fail if artifacts co-vary with other visual factors; decomposition assumes sublabel independence, which may break if errors co-occur.

## Confidence
- Decomposition into Specialists: Medium
- Counterfactual ICL: Low
- Full-Spectrum Prompt Optimization: High
