# SplitLight: An Exploratory Toolkit for Recommender Systems Datasets and Splits

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19339  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19339  
- **Reference count:** 34  
- **Primary result:** Minor preprocessing decisions (e.g., shuffling timestamp collisions, retaining consecutive repeats, including cold items) can change NDCG@10 by up to 60%, revealing hidden biases that undermine reproducibility in recommender system evaluations.

## Executive Summary
SplitLight is an open-source toolkit that audits recommender system datasets and splitting strategies by quantifying hidden preprocessing choices—such as handling timestamp collisions, repeat consumption, and cold-start exposure—that materially impact offline evaluation metrics. By standardizing diagnostics (e.g., temporal leakage, distribution shifts) and enabling side-by-side comparison of multiple splits, it transforms informal decisions into measurable, reportable quantities. Applied to six datasets, SplitLight demonstrates that seemingly minor curation variations lead to NDCG@10 changes of up to 9.5% (shuffling collisions), 60% (retaining repeats), and 1.2–20% (including cold items), underscoring the necessity of transparent data auditing for reproducible research.

## Method Summary
SplitLight analyzes interaction logs (user, item, timestamp) to compute core statistics (users, items, interactions), temporal properties (timeframes, user lifetimes, timestamp collisions), and split-specific diagnostics (leakage shares, cold-start ratios, distribution shifts). Users define splitting strategies (e.g., leave-one-out, global temporal splits) and receive dashboards with histograms, timelines, and diagnostic cards. The toolkit was evaluated on six datasets, with SASRec as a baseline model. Perturbations tested: (1) shuffling timestamp collisions in LOO splits, (2) removing vs. keeping consecutive duplicates in GTS‑q0.9, and (3) excluding vs. including cold items in evaluation. NDCG@10 was measured across conditions to quantify metric sensitivity.

## Key Results
- Shuffling timestamp collisions in LOO splits changes NDCG@10 by up to **9.5%**.
- Retaining consecutive repeats in GTS‑q0.9 increases NDCG@10 by up to **60%**.
- Including cold items in evaluation for GTS‑q0.9 reduces NDCG@10 by **1.2–20%**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SplitLight mitigates irreproducibility by transforming hidden preprocessing choices into quantifiable metrics.  
- **Mechanism:** The toolkit systematically extracts and reports core dataset statistics (users, items, interactions), temporal properties (timeframes, user lifetimes, timestamp collisions), and split-specific diagnostics (leakage shares, cold-start ratios, distribution shifts). This replaces informal, undocumented decisions with standardized, comparable numbers.  
- **Core assumption:** Reproducibility requires that all data curation decisions are explicitly measured and reported; otherwise, minor variations can invalidate comparisons.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "makes these decisions measurable, comparable, and reportable"  
  - [section 2.3] Lists the six key audit questions and six analysis areas (e.g., repeat consumption patterns, temporal leakage).  
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers confirm splitting strategies materially affect outcomes but lack a unified auditing tool.  
- **Break condition:** If the input interaction log lacks timestamps or user/item identifiers, temporal or statistical properties cannot be computed.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** SplitLight enables objective selection of splitting strategies by allowing direct, visual comparison of their downstream impact on evaluation subsets.  
- **Mechanism:** It aggregates split results into side-by-side dashboards showing core/temporal statistics, interaction timelines, histograms of time-gaps and item positions, and diagnostic cards for leakage/cold-start. Users can overlay multiple splits to see trade-offs (e.g., cold-start rate vs. data sufficiency).  
- **Core assumption:** The best split is not universal; it depends on the intended deployment scenario and must be chosen by contrasting measurable outcomes.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "allows side-by-side comparison of alternative splitting strategies"  
  - [section 3.5] Describes the summary dashboard with configurable color-coded cards.  
  - [section 4.4] Shows how different splits (LOO vs. GTS) yield vastly different cold-start rates and NDCG changes.  
- **Break condition:** If only one split is provided, comparison is impossible; the tool can only audit that single split's properties.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** SplitLight reduces evaluation bias by flagging split-induced distribution shifts that misalign offline metrics with real-world performance.  
- **Mechanism:** It quantifies shifts between training and evaluation targets using Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests on time-gap and item-position distributions. Significant shifts are highlighted as warnings, prompting users to adjust splits or interpret results cautiously.  
- **Core assumption:** Offline evaluation is trustworthy only if the test distribution matches the expected deployment distribution; otherwise, metrics reflect artifacts, not model quality.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 3.4.4] compares target time-gap distributions using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic.  
  - [section 4.3] notes GTS‑q0.9 for ML‑1M yields a target subset spanning 76% of the timeframe, indicating severe temporal skew.  
  - [abstract] mentions "distribution shifts" as a key diagnostic.  
- **Break condition:** If the split lacks clearly defined input/target boundaries, the tool cannot isolate evaluation targets for distribution comparison.

## Foundational Learning

### Temporal Data Splitting
- **Why needed here:** SplitLight's core diagnostics (leakage, timeframe overlap) assume interactions are split chronologically. Understanding global temporal splits vs. leave-one-out is essential to interpret audit results.  
- **Quick check:** How does a leave-one-out split inherently cause near-100% train-test temporal overlap, and why does that matter for leakage?

### Cold-Start Problem
- **Why needed here:** SplitLight measures cold user/item exposure in evaluation subsets. A practitioner must know that cold-start evaluation targets different model capabilities (e.g., content-based vs. collaborative filtering) to act on the reported ratios.  
- **Quick check:** If a split yields 80% cold items in the test target, what does that imply about the model's training data coverage?

### Repeat Consumption Patterns
- **Why needed here:** SplitLight distinguishes repeated interactions and consecutive repeats, as they bias metrics. Understanding this distinction is key to deciding whether to filter repeats during preprocessing.  
- **Quick check:** Why might retaining consecutive duplicates in training inflate NDCG@10, and how does SplitLight detect this?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Data loader -> Analyzer modules (CoreStats, TemporalStats, RepeatConsumption, LeakageDetector, ColdStartAnalyzer, DistributionShift) -> Visualizer (Streamlit UI & Python API) -> SplitComparator

### Critical path
1. Load raw or preprocessed interaction data (user, item, timestamp).
2. Define split subsets (train/val/test, input/target) via built-in splitters or externally.
3. Run `analyze()` on each subset and reference (e.g., raw data).
4. Generate summary dashboard; drill into specific diagnostics (e.g., leakage timeline).
5. Use `compare_splits()` to overlay metrics from different splitting strategies.

### Design tradeoffs
- Standardization vs. flexibility: Predefined metrics (e.g., KS statistic) ensure comparability but may not capture domain-specific shifts; users can extend analyzers.
- UI vs. API: Streamlit lowers entry barrier but limits batch processing; Python API allows integration into pipelines.
- Granularity: Aggregated summary cards risk oversimplifying; detailed histograms/timelines provide depth but require interpretation.

### Failure signatures
- Extremely high timestamp collision rate (>40% in ML‑1M) suggests logging issues; splits based on time become unreliable.
- Train-test timeframe overlap >0% indicates temporal leakage; target leakage share near 100% (as in Diginetica LOO) invalidates results.
- Cold-start ratio >70% in test may indicate misaligned split (e.g., GTS on short-lived datasets) or deliberate cold-start evaluation; must be explicitly justified.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Dataset health check:** Load a raw dataset (e.g., ML‑1M), run all analyzers, review summary dashboard. Note timestamp collision rate (52.89%) and median user lifetime (1 hour)—flag potential session-based misuse.
2. **Split comparison:** Create two splits: GTS‑q0.9 (last-item) and LOO. Use `compare_splits()` to contrast cold-start rates and leakage. Observe LOO's near-100% overlap vs. GTS's cleaner separation.
3. **Repeat consumption impact:** Preprocess with and without removing consecutive duplicates. Compare NDCG@10 on same model (SASRec) using SplitLight's repeat stats to explain performance delta (up to 60% as in Table 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can SplitLight's diagnostic framework be extended to handle rich interaction data with additional features (e.g., ratings, contextual signals, side information) while maintaining its core utility for auditing splits?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The toolkit intentionally excludes non-essential features due to inconsistent availability across datasets, limiting its scope to implicit (user, item, timestamp) logs. This excludes many real-world scenarios where richer data is common.  
- **Why unresolved:** Current analysis focuses on basic temporal and repeat patterns, but features like rating values or session context could reveal additional quality issues or split biases not captured by the existing metrics.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An extended version of SplitLight that incorporates feature-aware diagnostics (e.g., distribution shifts in rating distributions, context-specific cold-start) and demonstrates added value on datasets with side information.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What systematic, data-driven approach can determine optimal splitting parameters (e.g., quantile thresholds for global temporal splits) to ensure evaluation subsets realistically mirror deployment conditions?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper shows that common choices like GTS at q0.9 can create evaluation periods spanning >75% of dataset timeframe (ML‑1M) or induce extreme cold-start rates (Diginetica), yet offers no method to select parameters aligned with target scenarios.  
- **Why unresolved:** Without objective criteria, practitioners default to heuristics, risking misaligned evaluations. The toolkit flags misalignment but does not prescribe corrections.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A methodology that links split parameters to measurable deployment characteristics (e.g., desired recency, cold-start tolerance), validated by correlation with online performance or expert judgments across diverse datasets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What underlying dataset properties mediate the magnitude of evaluation metric changes caused by specific curation decisions (e.g., handling consecutive repeats, cold items), and can these be used to predict sensitivity?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Table 4 reveals large variation: consecutive repeats increase NDCG@10 by up to 60% in Zvuk but have negligible effect in rating datasets; cold item inclusion reduces metrics by 1.2–20% depending on dataset. The paper documents this but does not explain it.  
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding mediators would allow researchers to anticipate which curation decisions are critical for a given dataset, prioritizing audit efforts and improving cross-dataset comparability.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A statistical analysis identifying correlates (e.g., raw repeat rate, user lifetime distribution, item turnover) of sensitivity magnitude, possibly formalized as a predictive model of curation impact.

## Limitations
- SASRec training details (hyperparameters, early stopping patience) are not fully specified; reliance on defaults from the original paper may diverge from authors' setup.
- LOO validation split construction and GTS‑q0.9 target extraction procedure are ambiguous, hindering exact replication.
- Diagnostic thresholds for significance (e.g., KS statistic) lack empirical grounding in online performance.

## Confidence
- **Auditable metrics:** High (clear, measurable diagnostics with open-source code enable verification).  
- **Side-by-side strategy comparison:** Medium (dashboard facilitates comparison but claim of objectivity lacks controlled study support).  
- **Bias reduction via distribution-shift detection:** Medium (KS tests are theoretically sound but no demonstration of correlation with online performance).  
- **Substantial impact of minor decisions:** Medium (strong empirical evidence but missing experimental details hinder causal attribution).
