# MambaNet: Mamba-assisted Channel Estimation Neural Network With Attention Mechanism

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.17108
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17108
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** MambaNet reduces MSE by 0.00072 vs. HA02 and 0.000065 vs. Channelformer on the 3GPP ETU channel while cutting tunable parameters by 73–76% (to 0.35 M).

## Executive Summary
MambaNet tackles efficient channel estimation for large-scale OFDM systems where transformer-based models suffer quadratic complexity. It combines multi-head attention with a custom Mamba block that uses a bidirectional selective scan to capture non‑causal long‑range dependencies across subcarriers with linear complexity. Evaluated on the 3GPP Extended Typical Urban (ETU) channel, MambaNet achieves lower MSE than state‑of‑the‑art HA02 and Channelformer and reduces parameter count by over 70%.

## Method Summary
The network takes least‑squares (LS) pilot estimates as input, flattens them into a sequence, and processes them through a multi‑head attention module to capture global correlations. The attention output is fed into a customized Mamba block that performs bidirectional selective scanning over the sequence, efficiently propagating information with linear complexity. The Mamba output is reshaped and passed through a residual convolutional network for fine‑grained reconstruction, followed by bilinear upsampling and a final convolutional layer to produce full channel estimates. Training uses the Huber loss with Adam optimizer across a wide SNR range.

## Key Results
- MambaNet attains MSE ≈ 0.00065 at 30 dB SNR on ETU, outperforming HA02 (≈ 0.00137) and Channelformer (≈ 0.000715).
- Tunable parameters are reduced by 73–76% compared to transformer baselines (0.35 M vs. 0.93 M–1.1 M).
- The bidirectional selective scan effectively models non‑causal subcarrier correlations, yielding significant gains over unidirectional scanning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Bidirectional selective scanning:** The Mamba block computes both forward and backward state recurrences over the attention‑processed sequence, summing them to aggregate information from both directions. This captures the non‑causal correlations present in OFDM channel gains across subcarriers, as justified for the ETU model. The recurrence is linear in sequence length thanks to parallelizable cumulative operations.
- **Attention pre‑processing + Mamba refinement:** Multi‑head attention first enriches the LS estimates with pairwise global context. This representation is then efficiently propagated and refined by the selective SSM, combining the benefits of full attention (strong initial correlation) with linear‑complexity sequential modeling.
- **Selective SSM for parameter efficiency:** The state‑space model uses data‑dependent parameters (`a`, `b`, `g`) generated from the input, allowing adaptive dynamics while keeping parameter count independent of sequence length (only dependent on hidden dimension). This yields drastic reductions versus quadratic self‑attention parameters for large numbers of subcarriers.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** LS Pilots → Tokenization → Multi‑Head Attention → Mamba Block → Residual CNN Stack → Bilinear Upsampling → Final Conv → Output

**Critical path:** The input LS estimates are flattened into a sequence, globally contextualized by attention, efficiently scanned via the bidirectional selective SSM, then reshaped and refined with convolutional layers to reconstruct the full frequency‑time channel matrix.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Attention provides strong global context but incurs `O(L²)` cost; Mamba replaces most of that with `O(L)` sequential scanning after an initial attention layer.
- The bidirectional scan captures non‑causal dependencies at the expense of roughly doubling the state computation compared to a unidirectional scan, yet remains linear.

**Failure signatures:**
- Shape mismatches in the Mamba block due to incorrect flattening/reshaping (e.g., sequence length `N_pilot * N_f / L_s`).
- Abnormally high MSE (> 0.001 at 30 dB) suggests errors in channel generation, loss function (Huber δ), pilot pattern, or missing layer norms.
- Training instability may arise from saturated `a` (sigmoid) or poor initialization of the custom linear layers; monitor gradient norms.

**First three experiments:**
1. **Baseline replication:** Implement the described architecture with `N_f=228`, `N_s=14`, `L_s=4`, `N_pilot=4`. Train on 95k/5k split, SNR 5–30 dB. Verify MSE/BER curves and parameter count (0.35 M).
2. **Bidirectional scan ablation:** Replace the bidirectional scan with a forward‑only recurrence. Retrain and compare performance; expect degradation, especially at high SNR, confirming the non‑causal correlation hypothesis.
3. **Scaling behavior:** Train models with varying `N_f` (e.g., 128, 228, 512, 1024). Plot test MSE and parameter count vs. `N_f` for MambaNet, HA02, and Channelformer. Expect near‑linear parameter growth for MambaNet and quadratic for transformers, with ≤ 76% reduction at large `N_f`.

## Limitations
- **Pilot pattern ambiguity:** Exact subcarrier indices within each `L_s=4` block are not clearly specified, which may affect reproducibility.
- **Huber loss δ:** The smoothing parameter is not provided; its choice influences training stability and final errors.
- **ETU channel parameters:** Specific path delays, Doppler shifts, and path count per 3GPP TS 36.101 are omitted, requiring lookup in the standard to faithfully replicate the data generation.

## Confidence
- **Performance metrics (MSE/BER):** High – directly reported with numerical values.
- **Parameter efficiency (73–76% reduction):** High – explicit counts given.
- **Bidirectional scan captures non‑causal dependencies:** Medium – justified but lacks direct ablation in the paper.
- **Attention pre‑processing enables efficient Mamba refinement:** Medium – plausible architecture design without isolation experiments.
- **Selective SSM provides linear complexity:** High – mathematically sound and inherent to the design.
