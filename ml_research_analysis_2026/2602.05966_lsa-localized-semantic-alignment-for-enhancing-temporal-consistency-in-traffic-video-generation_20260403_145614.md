# LSA: Localized Semantic Alignment for Enhancing Temporal Consistency in Traffic Video Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.05966
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05966
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: LSA fine-tuning of a pre-trained video diffusion model substantially improves temporal consistency in traffic video generation without any inference-time overhead.

## Executive Summary
Temporal inconsistency in generated traffic videos poses a significant barrier to autonomous driving applications, often necessitating external control signals during inference. This paper introduces Localized Semantic Alignment (LSA), a fine-tuning framework applied to the pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model. LSA incorporates a semantic feature consistency loss that aligns DINOv2 embeddings of generated frames with corresponding ground-truth frames, with a focus on dynamic object regions via provided bounding-box masks. This approach requires only one epoch of U-Net fine-tuning, preserving the original inference pipeline. Evaluated on nuScenes and KITTI datasets, LSA yields marked improvements in temporal coherence and semantic metrics, outperforming a recent controllable generation method (Ctrl-V 1-to-0) while being significantly more efficient.

## Method Summary
LSA is a training-time fine-tuning strategy for pre-trained video diffusion models. It augments the standard diffusion loss with a novel semantic feature consistency loss. This loss computes the mean squared error between DINOv2 embeddings of generated frames and their corresponding ground-truth frames. Critically, the loss is masked using bounding-box annotations that delineate dynamic objects (e.g., moving vehicles, pedestrians), thereby directing the learning signal toward regions most indicative of temporal inconsistency. Only the U-Net backbone of SVD is fine-tuned for a single epoch, leaving the text encoder and other components unchanged. This design ensures that the improved model maintains identical inference requirements and architecture as the original SVD.

## Key Results
- On nuScenes, LSA increases detection mAP from 16.75 to 24.92 (+48.8% relative) and segmentation mIoU from 79.08 to 80.65.
- Frechét Video Distance (FVD) improves from 256.12 to 229.26 and Frechét Inception Distance (FID) from 19.67 to 18.08.
- LSA outperforms Ctrl-V 1-to-0 across all reported metrics while requiring no additional inference time or parameters (Ctrl-V uses 3× more inference time and 130% more parameters).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism is the explicit regularization of semantic feature trajectories in dynamic regions. By aligning DINOv2 embeddings—which capture high-level, semantically rich visual features—between generated and real frames, LSA forces the model to maintain consistent object appearances, poses, and identities over time. The use of bounding-box masks ensures this alignment loss is concentrated on moving objects, which are the primary sources of temporal flicker and inconsistency in traffic scenes. This targeted approach prevents the loss from being diluted by static background regions, leading to more effective fine-tuning of the U-Net's temporal reasoning capabilities with minimal additional training.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Pre-trained SVD (U-Net + Text Encoder) -> LSA Loss Computation (DINOv2 encoder + GT mask application) -> Gradient Update (U-Net only)

**Critical Path**: During training, for a batch of video clips: (1) Generate video latents via SVD U-Net. (2) Decode latents to frames. (3) Extract DINOv2 features for all generated and corresponding ground-truth frames. (4) Apply spatial bounding-box masks to these features. (5) Compute mean squared error between masked features. (6) Sum with diffusion loss and backpropagate to update only U-Net parameters.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Reliance on ground-truth bounding-box masks: Improves focus on dynamics but requires annotated data, limiting datasets without such labels.
- Single-epoch fine-tuning: Extremely efficient and avoids catastrophic forgetting but may be suboptimal for larger or more diverse datasets.
- Fixed masking strategy: Simple and effective but does not adapt to uncertain or missed dynamic objects.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Poorly accurate or missing bounding-box masks lead to misaligned loss application, potentially degrading static background quality or missing key dynamic inconsistencies.
- Overfitting on a small dataset after one epoch might occur if the fine-tuning batch is not sufficiently diverse, though not observed in the paper's two datasets.
- The method may not significantly improve videos with minimal motion or where temporal inconsistency stems from global scene changes rather than object dynamics.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. Train an LSA model with the masking module disabled (i.e., loss applied uniformly) and compare metrics to the masked version to quantify the contribution of localization.
2. Apply the trained LSA model (from nuScenes) directly to videos from the Waymo Open Dataset without further fine-tuning to test cross-dataset generalization.
3. Fine-tune SVD with LSA for 1, 3, and 5 epochs on nuScenes, tracking all metrics to identify potential overfitting or performance saturation.

## Limitations
- The method depends on the availability of accurate ground-truth bounding-box masks for dynamic objects during training, which may not exist for many video datasets.
- Reported improvements in FVD and FID are relatively modest, and the absence of a human perceptual study leaves the actual visual quality gain uncertain.
- The comparison to Ctrl-V 1-to-0 lacks detailed experimental configuration and metric computation specifics, making a fair assessment of relative performance difficult.

## Confidence
- **Temporal consistency improvements on nuScenes/KITTI**: Medium (consistent gains across multiple metrics but on only two datasets without statistical tests).
- **Outperformance of Ctrl-V 1-to-0**: Low (insufficient baseline details and unclear experimental parity undermine the claim).
- **Efficiency (one-epoch fine-tuning, no inference overhead)**: High (straightforward to verify and clearly stated).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of localized masking**: Train without bounding-box masks to isolate the contribution of the localization mechanism.
2. **Cross-dataset evaluation**: Test LSA on another driving dataset (e.g., Waymo Open Dataset) to assess generalization.
3. **Statistical robustness**: Repeat experiments with ≥5 random seeds and report mean ± standard deviation for all metrics.
