# Interpreting Speaker Characteristics in the Dimensions of Self-Supervised Speech Features

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03096
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03096
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** Principal dimensions in utterance-averaged WavLM features orthogonally encode specific speaker traits (e.g., pitch, intensity), enabling linear, isolated control in synthesis via simple vector manipulation.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how individual dimensions of self-supervised speech (SSL) features, specifically from WavLM layer 6, represent speaker characteristics. By applying principal component analysis (PCA) to utterance-averaged embeddings, the first principal dimension strongly correlates with pitch and gender, while subsequent dimensions capture traits like intensity, formants, and spectral rolloff. Synthesis experiments demonstrate that modifying these dimensions linearly controls the corresponding acoustic attribute with minimal effect on unrelated traits, providing a training-free method for voice attribute manipulation.

## Method Summary
The method extracts frame-level WavLM layer 6 features for utterances, averages them per utterance, and fits PCA on LibriSpeech train-clean-100 to define 50 principal dimensions. Correlations between these dimensions and speaker characteristics (pitch, formants, intensity, jitter, shimmer, etc.) are computed on a curated development set. For synthesis, a target principal dimension is modified by adding a scaled eigenvector (α·σ_i·v_i) to every frame of the original SSL features, which are then fed to a pretrained HiFi-GAN vocoder to generate audio. The output’s acoustic parameters are measured to evaluate control linearity and isolation.

## Key Results
- The first principal dimension explains the most variance and correlates overwhelmingly with pitch and gender (Cohen’s κ = 0.96 for gender).
- Other principal dimensions show significant correlations with intensity, F2, F3, spectral rolloff, and noise levels.
- Synthesis experiments confirm linear control over pitch and intensity via their associated dimensions, with control largely isolated (e.g., varying pitch dimension minimally affects intensity). Some traits like jitter and shimmer show no systematic change.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Principal components from PCA on utterance-averaged WavLM features form an approximately orthogonal basis where individual dimensions selectively encode specific speaker characteristics.
- **Mechanism:** PCA orders dimensions by descending variance. The first captures maximum variance across speaker averages, aligning with high-variance traits like pitch/gender. Subsequent orthogonal dimensions capture residual variance, aligning with narrower acoustic traits because utterance averaging suppresses temporal dynamics, leaving stable speaker-specific spectral/prosodic patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Speaker identity manifests as statistically independent subspaces in SSL embeddings when temporally averaged.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states principal dimension encodes pitch/gender; [section III] Fig. 2 shows distinct dimensions for different characteristics; neighboring work on SSL space geometry supports orthogonal encoding.
- **Break condition:** If speaker traits are dynamic or SSL space is non-linear/isotropic, PCA may not yield separable dimensions. Poor control of jitter/shimmer suggests non-linear encoding or entanglement.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Manipulating a single principal dimension induces linear/monotonic changes in the corresponding speaker characteristic in synthesized audio.
- **Mechanism:** Adding α·σ_i·v_i to every frame shifts the representation along a semantic axis. The HiFi-GAN vocoder, trained to invert SSL features, interprets this shift as a change in that characteristic, producing audio with altered attribute.
- **Core assumption:** The vocoder acts as a linear/near-linear decoder for the manipulated attribute within the SSL feature distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes most characteristics can be controlled; [section IV.B] Fig. 3b shows linear intensity change; Fig. 4 shows pitch control with minimal intensity effect.
- **Break condition:** If vocoder misalignment or non-linear decodability occurs, manipulation fails. Pitch control plateaus at extremes indicate non-linearity outside training distribution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Control over a target characteristic via its associated principal dimension is largely isolated; uncorrelated traits remain stable.
- **Mechanism:** PCA produces orthogonal dimensions. If characteristics are encoded in separate dimensions, modifying one moves the representation only along its axis, leaving projections onto other axes unchanged.
- **Core assumption:** The mapping from SSL space to acoustic characteristics is linear and PCA aligns with true independent factors.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states control is largely isolated; [section IV.B] Fig. 4 shows intensity stable when pitch varied.
- **Break condition:** If characteristics share unmodeled factors (e.g., gender affects pitch and resonance) or vocoder introduces cross-talk, isolation breaks. Dimension 1’s correlation with jitter/HNR shows isolation is not universal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self-supervised learning (SSL) for speech (e.g., WavLM):** SSL models learn representations from raw audio without labels; different layers capture different information levels (phonetic vs. speaker). *Why needed:* Analysis operates on pretrained SSL features; understanding layer choice and averaging effects is essential. *Quick check:* Why might averaging SSL features over an utterance accentuate speaker information while suppressing phonetic content?
- **Principal Component Analysis (PCA):** PCA reduces dimensionality and finds orthogonal directions of maximum variance. *Why needed:* Core method to discover which dimensions encode which speaker traits and define manipulation vectors. *Quick check:* If two speaker characteristics are correlated (e.g., pitch and gender), will PCA necessarily assign them to the same principal dimension? Why or why not?
- **Acoustic phonetics parameters (F0, formants, intensity, jitter, shimmer, HNR, spectral rolloff, ZCR):** These quantify vocal tract shape, glottal source, and spectral properties. *Why needed:* They are the ground-truth traits correlated with PCA dimensions and used to evaluate synthesis control. *Quick check:* How might jitter (pitch instability) and shimmer (amplitude instability) relate to vocal quality, and why might they be harder to control via linear PCA manipulation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Pretrained WavLM Encoder (layer 6) -> Utterance Pooling & PCA Module (LibriSpeech-trained) -> Manipulation Engine (modifies specified dimension by α·σ_i·v_i) -> Inverse Projection to Frame-Level -> Pretrained HiFi-GAN Vocoder -> Waveform Output -> Feature Extractor & Metric Calculator (Praat/Librosa)
- **Critical path:** WavLM inference on audio -> utterance averaging -> PCA projection -> dimension manipulation -> inverse projection to frame-level -> HiFi-GAN synthesis -> acoustic analysis. PCA model must be trained once on a large, diverse speaker set.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Layer choice: Layer 6 balances speaker/content; other layers may yield different PCA interpretations.
  - Pooling: Utterance averaging assumes constant speaker traits; other statistics could capture dynamics.
  - Manipulation scope: Adding same α to every frame assumes utterance-constant trait; time-varying traits need frame-wise manipulation.
  - Vocoder dependency: Control efficacy depends on vocoder’s alignment with SSL space; a different vocoder may break linear controllability.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Plateauing/saturation: Extreme α yields diminishing returns (e.g., pitch control), indicating vocoder manifold exit or non-linearity.
  - Unintended attribute changes: If a PCA dimension correlates with multiple traits (e.g., dim1 with pitch and jitter), varying it may change more than target.
  - Audio degradation: Large α causes quality drop, suggesting out-of-distribution SSL features.
  - No systematic change: For jitter/shimmer, no trend when varying associated dimension, indicating incorrect correlation or non-linear encoding.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce correlation analysis: Fit PCA on WavLM layer 6 utterance averages from LibriSpeech train-clean-100. Compute R²/κ between principal dimensions and acoustic features on curated dev set. Expect dim1→pitch (R² ≥ 0.89), dim2→intensity (R² ≈ 0.4).
  2. Replicate pitch control: Vary dimension 1 (α ∈ [−8,8] step 0.2) on 10 test utterances, synthesize with HiFi-GAN, measure mean F0. Confirm monotonic change up to |α|≈4, then plateauing.
  3. Isolation check: Vary dimension 1 (pitch) and dimension 2 (intensity) separately on 20 utterances. Compute Pearson correlation between α and Δ in all other characteristics. Expect |r| < 0.1 for uncorrelated pairs (e.g., pitch→intensity), but non-zero for traits sharing dimension 1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do other self-supervised speech models and their different hidden layers encode speaker characteristics within individual dimensions?
- **Open Question 2:** Which SSL model and hidden layer combination yields the highest degree of isolated control over speaker characteristics when manipulating principal dimensions?
- **Open Question 3:** Why are some speaker characteristics (e.g., jitter, shimmer) not systematically controllable through principal dimension manipulation, while others (e.g., pitch, intensity) are?

## Limitations
- Exact acoustic feature extraction parameters (Praat settings, Librosa hops) are unreported, potentially affecting reliability of jitter/shimmer/HNR.
- HiFi-GAN vocoder checkpoint and training domain are unspecified; mismatches may degrade synthesis quality and control linearity.
- PCA implementation details (centering/scaling) are ambiguous; deviations could alter dimension interpretations.
- Curation criteria for “clean” speaker subsets are vague, limiting reproducibility of correlation results.

## Confidence
- **Orthogonal encoding of speaker traits:** High. Strong correlations (R² > 0.9 for pitch, κ = 0.96 for gender) support distinct principal dimensions; corroborated by neighboring work.
- **Linear controllability via PCA manipulation:** Medium. Pitch and intensity show clear linear trends, but jitter/shimmer/HNR show no systematic change, indicating incomplete/non-linear encoding for some traits.
- **Isolation of control (minimal cross-talk):** Low. While pitch manipulation spares intensity, dimension 1 correlates with multiple traits; isolation holds only for uncorrelated traits, not universally.
