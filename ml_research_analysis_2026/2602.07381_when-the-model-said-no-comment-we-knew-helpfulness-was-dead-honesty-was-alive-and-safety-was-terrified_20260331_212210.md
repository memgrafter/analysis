# When the Model Said 'No Comment', We Knew Helpfulness Was Dead, Honesty Was Alive, and Safety Was Terrified

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07381
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07381
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: AlignX resolves Axis Collapse in LLM alignment, achieving up to +171.5% relative win rate gain, +110.1% truthfulness-informativeness improvement, and 4.3% fewer safety violations versus baselines.

## Executive Summary
Multi-objective LLM alignment (helpful, harmless, honest) often suffers from Axis Collapse, where catastrophic forgetting and misrouted experts degrade one objective while improving another. AlignX introduces a two-stage framework to mitigate this problem. Stage 1 uses prompt-injected fine-tuning on each axis separately to extract axis-specific task features and fuse them into compact representations. Stage 2 employs a Mixture-of-Calibrated-Experts (MoCaE) module that dynamically routes queries to the most appropriate expert, calibrated by fractal dimension and natural clustering metrics. Experiments on LLaMA‑2‑7B and three other models show substantial gains across all alignment axes, improved efficiency, and robust generalization.

## Method Summary
Stage 1: For each alignment axis (helpful, harmless, honest), fine‑tune the base model on its respective dataset (Alpaca, BeaverTails, TruthfulQA) augmented with property‑focused system prompts. Compute the task vector Δθₐ = θₐ − θ₀ (θₐ: weights after fine‑tuning; θ₀: base weights). Run the fine‑tuned model on the injected data and extract average hidden activation Fₐ from a fixed layer. Fuse Δθₐ and Fₐ via learned projections: Tₐ = W₁Δθₐ + W₂Fₐ, yielding a 256‑dimensional axis embedding Tₐ.

Stage 2: Add a shared encoder and three expert FFNs (one per axis). For a query x_q, obtain representation h_q from the encoder. Compute initial routing weights α = softmax(Wᵣ·h_q + bᵣ). Identify the top expert a*. Use T_{a*} to compute a calibration score: fractal dimension FD_{a*} (via clustering h_q with resolution ε=0.05) and semantic cluster score from k‑means on the expert’s token activations (variance‑based). Combine FD and score with λ₁=0.6, λ₂=0.4, normalize to ŝₐ, and blend the three expert outputs zₐ = Eₐ(h_q) as h_final = Σₐ ŝₐ·zₐ. Reinject h_final into the decoder’s residual stream before generation.

## Key Results
- On LLaMA‑2‑7B, AlignX achieves +171.5% relative win rate gain on Alpaca‑Eval, +110.1% improvement in truthfulness‑informativeness on TruthfulQA, and 4.3% fewer safety violations on BeaverTails compared to baselines.
- Inference latency and memory usage are reduced by over 35% relative to H3Fusion.
- Generalizes across four LLMs; DeepSeek‑7B reaches 97.1% win rate and 93.25% truthfulness‑informativeness on combined benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
AlignX combats Axis Collapse by decoupling axis‑specific feature learning from shared inference. Prompt‑injected fine‑tuning isolates objective‑specific updates, preventing catastrophic interference; the fusion ϕ produces compact, disentangled task representations. MoCaE’s two‑pronged calibration—fractal dimension (geometric complexity of the expert’s latent space) and natural cluster score (semantic purity)—dynamically adjusts routing weights, ensuring the correct expert (helpful, harmless, or honest) dominates for a given query. This design preserves a single decoder and shared parameters, yielding efficiency gains while maintaining alignment across objectives.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Catastrophic Forgetting**  
   - *Why needed*: In multi‑objective LLM alignment, optimizing one objective can degrade others, causing Axis Collapse.  
   - *Quick check*: After joint training, evaluate each objective separately; a sharp drop in non‑target objectives signals forgetting.

2. **Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE)**  
   - *Why needed*: MoE allocates different sub‑networks (experts) to different inputs, enabling specialization without increasing per‑token computation.  
   - *Quick check*: Inspect expert activation histograms on a mixed dataset; a uniform distribution suggests poor specialization.

3. **Task Vector Arithmetic**  
   - *Why needed*: Task vectors (Δθ = θ_fine − θ_base) capture parameter shifts induced by fine‑tuning on a specific objective, enabling modular recombination.  
   - *Quick check*: Compute cosine similarity between task vectors of different axes; low similarity indicates disentangled objectives.

4. **Fractal Dimension for Calibration**  
   - *Why needed*: The fractal dimension of expert activations quantifies the complexity of the data manifold; it helps adjust routing when an expert’s space is too sparse or dense.  
   - *Quick check*: Calculate FD for an expert on a validation set; values typically range between 1.0 (linear) and 2.0 (planar) for intermediate layers.

5. **Unsupervised Clustering for Semantic Purity**  
   - *Why needed*: Clustering (e.g., k‑means) expert outputs reveals how well the expert groups semantically related queries; purity correlates with the expert’s strength on its axis.  
   - *Quick check*: Compute average intra‑cluster cosine similarity; high values indicate coherent clusters.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**  
Base model (LLaMA‑2‑7B etc.) → Stage1 (prompt‑injected fine‑tuning per axis) → Task vectors Δθₐ and activation statistics Fₐ → Fusion ϕ (linear projections) → Axis embeddings Tₐ.  
Inference: Input → Shared encoder (first N layers) → query representation h_q → Gating network (softmax) → expert routing weights α → top expert selection (a*) → for a*: compute fractal dimension (clustering h_q, ε=0.05) and cluster score (k‑means on expert activations) → calibration score ŝₐ → blend expert outputs (zₐ = Eₐ(h_q)) using ŝₐ → add blended h_final to decoder residual stream → generate response.

**Critical Path**  
Accurate extraction of Tₐ in Stage1 is critical; inaccurate task vectors lead to poor calibration. The gating network must route to the correct expert; otherwise blending may combine conflicting objectives. Fractal and cluster computations must be stable and fast to avoid latency spikes.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Two‑stage vs. end‑to‑end: Separating feature extraction and routing simplifies training but limits joint optimization.  
- Shared encoder reduces parameters but may underfit axis‑specific patterns; expert depth controls specialization.  
- Fractal/cluster calibrators improve routing accuracy but add overhead; approximations (e.g., cached statistics) trade some accuracy for speed.  
- Prompt injection design influences Stage1; overly strong injections might bias features.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Single‑axis performance collapse on held‑out data (e.g., helpfulness drops after training on harmlessness).  
- Gating distribution nearly uniform across experts on any query, indicating poor routing.  
- Fractal dimension values clustering near 2.0 for all experts, suggesting lack of discrimination.  
- Inference latency significantly exceeds baseline models.  
- High safety violation rate despite harmlessness training.

**First Experiments**
1. **Stage1 Sanity Check**: Fine‑tune the base model individually on Alpaca, BeaverTails, and TruthfulQA with prompt injections. Evaluate each model on its target benchmark (Alpaca‑Eval, safety classifier, TruthfulQA) to confirm that task vectors capture intended behavior without interference.
2. **Routing Validation**: After training MoCaE, run a mixed validation set where queries are labeled by their primary axis (from dataset source). Compute the average routing weight assigned to the correct expert; expect >70% weight on the correct expert.
3. **End‑to‑End Smoke Test**: Train AlignX on 10% of each dataset for one epoch. Compute Combined Avg score on a held‑out mix and compare to single‑axis SFT baselines. A modest improvement validates the full pipeline before scaling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**  
- **Question**: How can we overcome catastrophic forgetting and miscalibrated expert routing to resolve Axis Collapse without requiring retraining or extensive supervision?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Posed as a central question in Section 1, noting that prior works fail to jointly optimize HHH objectives due to axis interference, forgetting, and unreliable routing.  
- **Why unresolved**: AlignX still relies on prompt‑injected fine‑tuning (retraining) in Stage 1 to extract task features, so it does not eliminate the need for retraining. The “without extensive supervision” aspect also remains unverified, as prompt design itself requires careful curation.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Develop and evaluate a variant of AlignX that achieves comparable HHH alignment using only inference‑time adaptations (e.g., dynamic routing or prompt engineering) without any parameter updates, tested across multiple LLMs and datasets.

**Open Question 2**  
- **Question**: To what extent does the task‑feature fusion mechanism in AlignX remain effective under extreme distribution shifts or tasks with high semantic ambiguity across alignment axes?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Acknowledged in the Limitations (Page 8) that effectiveness may diminish under “extreme distribution shifts or for tasks that exhibit high semantic ambiguity across alignment axes.”  
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only tests on standard, in‑distribution benchmarks (Alpaca, BeaverTails, TruthfulQA) with clear axis separation. No experiments probe robustness to out‑of‑distribution queries, adversarial inputs, or tasks where HHH objectives inherently conflict (e.g., being helpful while honest about harmful content).  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematically evaluate AlignX on benchmarks designed for distribution shift (e.g., domain‑adapted HHH datasets) and on ambiguous prompts where alignment axes pull in opposing directions, measuring degradation in alignment scores and feature‑space drift.

**Open Question 3**  
- **Question**: Can the computational overhead of MoCaE’s fractal and natural calibrators be reduced to enable truly low‑latency deployment without sacrificing alignment gains?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Noted in Limitations (Page 8) that reliance on expert‑specific calibrators “can introduce computational overhead in low‑latency deployment settings.”  
- **Why unresolved**: While AlignX shows reduced inference time compared to H3Fusion (Table 5), absolute latency (3.80–6.20 ms) and memory use (~1.7 GB) are still reported only on LLaMA‑2‑7B. The overhead of fractal dimension calculations and k‑means clustering during inference is not broken down, and scalability to larger models or real‑time constraints is unexplored.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Profile per‑component latency and memory footprint of MoCaE across diverse hardware (e.g., edge devices), then design optimized approximations (e.g., cached fractal metrics, simplified clustering) and measure trade‑offs with alignment performance.

**Open Question 4**  
- **Question**: Does AlignX generalize to multilingual contexts and highly diverse instruction distributions beyond the English‑centric, single‑domain benchmarks used in the paper?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Stated in Limitations (Page 8): “we do not address instruction diversity or multilingual generalization, which are important directions for future work.”  
- **Why unresolved**: All experiments use English datasets (Alpaca, BeaverTails, TruthfulQA) and evaluate on English queries. The prompt injection templates and task‑feature matrices are likely English‑biased, and no multilingual alignment benchmarks (e.g., X‑Align, MT‑Bench) are considered.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluate AlignX on established multilingual alignment benchmarks covering multiple languages and scripts, and on instruction corpora with high topical/format diversity (e.g., cross‑domain instruction sets), reporting alignment consistency across languages and instruction types.

## Limitations
- Fusion mechanism ϕ and projections W₁, W₂ lack explicit training details and depend on unspecified layer dimension d, hindering replication.
- MoCaE components (Frare, Ffreq) and clustering parameters (number of clusters K, Sim(Ck) definition) are incompletely described, making faithful implementation nontrivial.
- Evaluation relies on external judges (GPT‑Judge, beaver‑dam‑7B) whose own biases may affect reported metrics; no human validation is provided.

## Confidence
- Axis Collapse Mitigation: Medium
- Performance Improvements (+171.5% WR, +110.1% TI, 4.3% fewer violations): Low
- Efficiency Gains (>35% latency/memory reduction): Low
- Cross‑Model Generalization: Low
