# Identifying Latent Actions and Dynamics from Offline Data via Demonstrator Diversity
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.17577
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17577
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: Identifiability of latent discrete actions and shared transition dynamics from action‑free offline trajectories with demonstrator identity tags, up to permutation, under sufficient policy diversity and rank conditions.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies how to recover a latent discrete action space and the underlying environment dynamics from offline data that lacks explicit action labels but includes which demonstrator generated each trajectory. It shows that if each demonstrator follows a distinct policy and the environment dynamics are shared across demonstrators, the mixture of demonstrator‑specific policies induces a per‑state nonnegative matrix factorization of the transition distribution. Under conditions of sufficient policy scattering, full rank of the transition matrix, and enough demonstrators (at least as many as latent actions), the factorization is unique up to a permutation of the latent actions. For continuous observation spaces, an embedding‑based volume criterion combined with continuity arguments yields global consistency of the permutation, which can be fixed with a small labeled dataset. The authors propose a practical estimator that maximizes data likelihood while regularizing the volume of the latent transition simplex and enforcing policy diversity via a barrier term, and demonstrate recovery in synthetic experiments.

## Method Summary
The core model is \(p(o'|o,e) = \sum_a p(o'|o,a) \pi_e(a|o)\), where \(p(o'|o,a)\) are latent action‑conditioned transitions shared across demonstrators, and \(\pi_e(a|o)\) is the policy of demonstrator \(e\). For each state \(o\), the mixture coefficients across demonstrators form a column‑stochastic matrix \(\Pi_o\), and the transitions form a matrix \(T_o\) with columns \(p(\cdot|o,a)\). The observable transition matrix \(M_o = T_o \Pi_o\) is a nonnegative factorization. Identifiability up to permutation is proved if (i) \(\Pi_o\)’s columns are sufficiently scattered (no conical combination of fewer than \(k\) others), (ii) \(\text{rank}(T_o)=k\), and (iii) number of demonstrators \(m \ge k\). For continuous observations, the authors embed distributions into a feature space via a mapping \(\Phi\) and minimize the determinant of the Gram matrix \(G_\theta(o) = \sum_a \mu_{\theta,a}(o) \mu_{\theta,a}(o)^\top\), where \(\mu_{\theta,a}(o)=\Phi(p_\theta(\cdot|o,a))\). Continuity of the transition maps over a connected observation space then forces all local permutations to coincide, yielding a single global permutation. The practical estimator combines: (1) standard negative log‑likelihood \(L_{\text{fit}}\), (2) volume regularization \(R_{\text{vol}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum \log \det(G_\theta(o_i)+\varepsilon I)\), (3) a policy diversity barrier \(R_{\text{pol}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum [\tau - \log \det(\Pi_\psi(o_i)\Pi_\psi(o_i)^\top+\varepsilon I)]_+\) to keep policies distinct, and (4) an optional anchor loss \(L_{\text{anchor}}\) using a small labeled set to resolve the permutation. Both \(p_\theta\) and \(\pi_\psi\) are neural networks; \(p_\theta\) outputs parameters of a distribution (e.g., Gaussian), and \(\pi_\psi\) shares an observation encoder but has a separate linear head per demonstrator followed by softmax.

## Key Results
- Statewise identifiability of latent actions and transitions up to permutation under the scattering, rank, and demonstrator count conditions for finite observation spaces.
- Global permutation consistency across all states in continuous spaces, removable with a few labeled examples.
- A regularized maximum‑likelihood estimator that successfully recovers ground‑truth factors in synthetic experiments, avoiding policy and transition collapse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mixture structure creates a linear decomposition of the observable transition matrix into the product of the unknown transition matrix and the policy matrix. Nonnegative matrix factorization is unique under the “sufficient scattering” condition, which guarantees that no column of the policy matrix lies in the conical hull of the others. The rank condition ensures that the transition matrix has full column rank, preventing degenerate factorizations. Having at least as many demonstrators as latent actions (\(m \ge k\)) gives enough columns to satisfy scattering. In continuous spaces, the volume regularization forces the latent transition vectors to span a full‑dimensional simplex, which combined with continuity of the embedding yields that the permutation ambiguity is the same everywhere; thus a global labeling suffices. The diversity barrier directly enforces scattering by keeping the determinant of the policy Gram matrix above a threshold, while the likelihood term aligns the model to data. The anchor loss then aligns the global permutation to the true one.

## Foundational Learning
- **Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF)**: Why needed: The core identifiability argument reduces to a column‑stochastic NMF of \(M_o\). Quick check: Verify factor matrices are nonnegative and column‑stochastic; check that scattering condition holds (e.g., no column in cone of others).
- **Sufficient Scattering**: Why needed: Guarantees uniqueness of NMF up to permutation. Quick check: For each policy vector, solve a linear program to see if it can be expressed as a convex combination of others; all should be extreme points.
- **Rank Condition (\(\text{rank}(T_o)=k\))**: Why needed: Ensures the latent action vectors are linearly independent; otherwise multiple factorizations exist. Quick check: Compute rank of \(T_o\) from samples; if \(<k\), identifiability fails.
- **Volume Regularization (\(\log \det(G_\theta)\))**: Why needed: In continuous spaces, prevents collapse of latent transitions to a lower‑dimensional set, maintaining full rank of the Gram matrix. Quick check: Monitor \(\det(G_\theta(o))\) across states; it should be positive and bounded away from zero.
- **Policy Diversity Barrier**: Why needed: Directly enforces scattering in learned policies, preventing demonstrators from collapsing to identical policies. Quick check: Track \(\min \det(\Pi_\psi\Pi_\psi^\top)\) over states; it should stay above threshold.
- **Anchor Data for Permutation**: Why needed: Resolves the remaining global permutation ambiguity. Quick check: With a few labeled transitions, compare recovered latent actions to ground truth after permutation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text)**:
Data \((o,o',e)\) -> Encoder\_p \((\theta)\) -> Transition distributions \(p_\theta(\cdot|o,a)\)
Data \((o,e)\) -> Encoder\_\(\pi\) \((\psi)\): shared o‑encoder + per‑e linear head + softmax -> Policy matrix \(\Pi_\psi(o)\)
Transition distributions + Policy matrix -> Mixture \(p_{\theta,\psi}(o'|o,e)\)
Likelihood + Volume term + Diversity barrier + Anchor loss -> Total loss \(L\)
Loss -> Adam optimizer -> Parameter updates \((\theta, \psi)\)

**Critical path**: A minibatch passes through both encoders to produce \(\Pi_\psi(o)\) and \(p_\theta\) distributions; the mixture computes log likelihood; the volume and diversity regularizers are computed on the batch; loss is backpropagated to update networks.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- \(\lambda_{\text{vol}}\): too high forces artificially inflated volumes, possibly distorting transitions; too low risks collapse.
- \(\lambda_{\text{pol}}\) and \(\tau\): balance between enforcing diversity and allowing semantically similar demonstrators to cluster; overly strict barrier may force unnatural separation.
- \(\varepsilon\) in \(\log \det\): stabilizes numerics but too large can mask near‑singularities.
- Anchor data size: few labels are enough, but too few may not cover all actions equally.

**Failure signatures**:
- Policy collapse: \(\det(\Pi_\psi\Pi_\psi^\top) \approx 0\) across states; pairwise policy KL \(\approx 0\).
- Transition collapse: \(\det(G_\theta(o)) \rightarrow 0\) for many states; action‑conditioned distributions become nearly identical (low MMD).
- Numerical NaNs in loss due to non‑PSD Gram; increase \(\varepsilon\) or use robust Cholesky.
- Permutation mismatch without anchor: alignment metrics drop; Hungarian post‑processing needed.

**First three experiments**:
1. Finite‑O synthetic test: generate scattered \(\Pi^*\) and full‑rank \(T^*\), train and measure recovery error (TV/KL) as \(m\) and scatter vary.
2. Continuous‑O test: use Gaussian transitions with distinct means, kernel embedding (e.g., RBF), evaluate latent action recovery via MMD on samples.
3. Regularizer ablation: remove \(R_{\text{vol}}\) or \(R_{\text{pol}}\) one at a time, and sweep \(\lambda\) values to observe collapse phenomena and optimal ranges.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can we empirically verify that demonstrator policies satisfy the sufficient scattering condition needed for identifiability? The paper does not provide a data‑driven statistic to check this from finite trajectories, leaving it unclear when the theory applies in practice.
2. Can the global permutation of latent actions be determined without labeled data, perhaps by leveraging asymmetries in transition dynamics or additional structural assumptions? The paper relies on a small labeled set to fix the permutation but does not explore whether this can be avoided.
3. What are the finite‑sample statistical guarantees (e.g., consistency, convergence rates) for the proposed estimator? The paper explicitly states that the heuristic surrogate is not an exact finite‑sample analogue, and no learning theory is provided, leaving dataset size and regularization choices without guidance.

## Limitations
- The “sufficiently scattered” condition for policy matrices lacks an operational definition, particularly in continuous observation spaces, making it difficult to assess whether a given dataset meets the identifiability assumptions.
- The embedding function \(\Phi\) and the computation of Gram determinants for general transition models are not concretely specified; only kernel mean embeddings are mentioned, leaving implementation choices (kernel type, bandwidth, sampling) ambiguous.
- Critical hyperparameters (\(\lambda_{\text{vol}}\), \(\lambda_{\text{pol}}\), \(\tau\), \(\varepsilon\)) and the architectures of the networks \(p_\theta\) and \(\pi_\psi\) are not justified, creating a risk of non‑reproducibility and high sensitivity to these choices.

## Confidence
- Finite and continuous identifiability theorems: High
- Practical estimator (regularized MLE): Medium
- Permutation resolution via anchor data: High
