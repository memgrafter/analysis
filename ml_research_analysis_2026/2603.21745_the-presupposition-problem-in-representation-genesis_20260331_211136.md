# The Presupposition Problem in Representation Genesis
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.21745
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21745
- **Reference count**: 8
- **Primary result**: The paper identifies a "Representation Presupposition" pattern in five major frameworks (LOT, teleosemantics, predictive processing, enactivism, genetic phenomenology) that leads to an infinite "Representation Regress", and derives two minimal adequacy conditions (Non‑Presupposition and Transition Intelligibility) for any future theory of representation genesis.

## Executive Summary
The paper diagnoses a structural flaw in explaining representation genesis—the first emergence of content‑manipulable representations—by analyzing five major philosophical frameworks. Each framework, when applied to genesis, deploys explanatory concepts that already presuppose a representational system, generating a "Representation Regress" of indefinite deferral. The primary result is the identification of this "Representation Presupposition" pattern and the derivation of two minimal adequacy conditions for any future theory: it must not employ cognition‑side concepts (Non‑Presupposition) and must render the transition intelligible within non‑presupposing terms (Transition Intelligibility). Large language models are presented as a diagnostic case, illustrating that high functional performance via statistical encoding does not equate to content‑manipulable representation, thus making the absence of a genesis theory practically consequential.

## Method Summary
The author employs a conceptual analysis methodology. First, core concepts are defined: content‑manipulable representation (distinguished from natural information and statistical encoding), the cognition‑side concept (CSIDE), Representation Presupposition (RPT), and the Representation Regress. An analytical template derived from these definitions is then applied to five established frameworks to identify CSIDE steps. The analysis shows each framework exhibits RPT, leading to an infinite regress. Two adequacy conditions (CC‑1: Non‑Presupposition; CC‑2: Transition Intelligibility) are derived as necessary to escape the regress. Additionally, large language models are evaluated via the Content‑Misalignment Test (CMT) principle: performance degrades when distributional statistics diverge from logical content, suggesting they lack content‑manipulable representations.

## Key Results
- **Representation Presupposition identified**: All five examined frameworks invoke CSIDE concepts when explaining the first emergence of representation, creating a systematic pattern.
- **Infinite Representation Regress formalized**: Any explanation using CSIDE concepts requires further explanation, leading to indefinite deferral without a non‑presupposing foundation.
- **Minimal adequacy conditions derived**: CC‑1 (Non‑Presupposition) and CC‑2 (Transition Intelligibility) are necessary to avoid the regress and achieve a genuine genesis account.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Major philosophical frameworks share a Representation Presupposition structure when pressed to explain representation genesis.  
- **Mechanism:** The author inspects five frameworks (LOT, teleosemantics, predictive processing, enactivism, genetic phenomenology) for explanatory steps where a concept’s role depends on the system already possessing content‑manipulable representation. Each framework introduces at least one cognition‑side concept (CSIDE), revealing the presupposition pattern.  
- **Core assumption:** The frameworks are all attempting to explain the same target (first acquisition of content‑manipulable representation) and their explanations can be decomposed into discrete steps.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: “each deploys, at some explanatory step, concepts whose explanatory purchase depends on the system already being organized as a representer.”  
  - [section]: Section 3 provides detailed case analyses, identifying specific CSIDE concepts in each framework.  
  - [corpus]: Neighboring papers (e.g., “Do Language Models Know Theo Has a Wife?” on presupposition) show thematic relevance but do not directly address the regress; corpus support is weak.  
- **Break condition:** A single framework is demonstrated to avoid CSIDE entirely (i.e., uses only non‑presupposing concepts) when explaining first acquisition.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Representation Presupposition generates an infinite explanatory deferral—the Representation Regress—within the existing categorical vocabulary.  
- **Mechanism:** Formally, any explanation E₁ for first acquisition of target R uses a CSIDE concept C₁, which presupposes some representational organization R*. Explaining R* requires a further explanation E₂, which by the same pattern uses another CSIDE concept, and so on. No finite sequence can terminate with a non‑CSIDE step.  
- **Core assumption:** The regress is evaluated within the same categorical framework; switching to a wholly new vocabulary might circumvent it.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: “generates systematic explanatory deferral… Representation Regress.”  
  - [section]: Section 4 formalizes the regress with predicates (EXP, DEP, CSIDE, REP) and argues that any terminating step either reintroduces CSIDE or stops at physical description, which is not yet a genesis explanation.  
  - [corpus]: Little direct corpus evidence; neighboring LLM‑centric papers do not treat the regress structure, so corpus support is minimal.  
- **Break condition:** An explanation within the existing vocabulary is found that accounts for the first transition without any CSIDE concepts, thereby terminating the sequence.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The regress motivates two minimal adequacy conditions for any future theory of representation genesis: Non‑Presupposition (CC‑1) and Transition Intelligibility (CC‑2).  
- **Mechanism:** Since every known framework defers, a successful theory must avoid CSIDE concepts entirely and must describe the transition from non‑representation to representation in intelligible, non‑arbitrary terms using only non‑presupposing resources. These conditions are derived as necessary to escape the regress.  
- **Core assumption:** Satisfying both conditions is sufficient to avoid the identified deferral pattern (though not necessarily to provide a full theory).  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: “derive two minimal adequacy conditions for any future theory: it must not employ cognition‑side concepts (Non‑Presupposition) and must render the transition intelligible within non‑presupposing terms (Transition Intelligibility).”  
  - [section]: Section 5 explicitly formulates CC‑1 and CC‑2 and explains their connection to the regress.  
  - [corpus]: No strong corpus parallels; related work on category adequacy is scarce, so corpus evidence is weak.  
- **Break condition:** A theory meets CC‑1 and CC‑2 yet still encounters a similar regress, indicating the conditions are insufficient.

## Foundational Learning
- **Content‑manipulable representation**  
  - *Why needed here:* Distinguishes the target phenomenon from mere statistical encoding or natural information; its three core marks (target‑directedness, content‑sensitive downstream use, misrepresentation possibility) define what must be explained.  
  - *Quick check question:* “Can the state misrepresent? If not, it is not content‑manipulable.”  

- **Representation Presupposition (RPT)**  
  - *Why needed here:* The central thesis that frameworks, when applied to genesis, invoke cognition‑side concepts (CSIDE). Recognizing this pattern is key to understanding the regress.  
  - *Quick check question:* “When applying a theory to the first emergence of representation, does it at any point assume the system already has a representational state? If yes, that step is CSIDE.”  

- **CSIDE concept**  
  - *Why needed here:* The diagnostic tool that identifies presuppositions; a concept whose explanatory purchase depends on the system already being organized as a representer.  
  - *Quick check question:* “Is the concept’s use in the explanation contingent on the system already possessing content‑manipulable representation?”  

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Target definition (Sec 2.1) -> Framework analysis (Sec 3) -> Regress formalization (Sec 4) -> Adequacy conditions (Sec 5) -> LLM diagnostic (Sec 6)

**Critical path:**  
a. Establish the target phenomenon with precision.  
b. Examine each framework for CSIDE steps; generalize to RPT.  
c. Formalize the regress using the CSIDE pattern.  
d. Derive minimal adequacy conditions from the regress.  
e. Use LLMs as a test case to illustrate the practical stakes.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
• *Breadth vs. depth* – analyzing many frameworks broadens support but risks superficial treatment; focus on five major ones.  
• *Formalism vs. accessibility* – the formal regress (Section 4) is precise but may obscure the core intuitive problem.  
• *Normative vs. descriptive* – the paper diagnoses existing theories without offering a new positive theory; this keeps the argument clean but leaves open how to build a satisfactory account.

**Failure signatures:**  
– No clear CSIDE step identified in a framework (the presupposition may be hidden).  
– The proposed starting point already exhibits core marks of content‑manipulability (e.g., positing innate mental symbols).  
– Reliance on purely physical/neural descriptions without explaining representational status, stopping at substrate rather than transition.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Apply the CSIDE diagnostic* to a novel framework (e.g., radical embodied dynamical systems) to see whether it truly escapes the presupposition pattern.  
2. *Test hybrid theories* (e.g., combining information theory with development) against CC‑1 and CC‑2: do any components still invoke CSIDE?  
3. *Empirical CMT test on LLMs*: design tasks where distributional statistics and logical content diverge (e.g., nonce‑word analogies) to measure whether performance tracks content as predicted for a system with content‑manipulable representations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What conceptual categories could underwrite a theory of representation genesis that avoids cognition‑side presuppositions?**  
   The paper derives CC‑1/CC‑2 as necessary but leaves open what non‑presupposing resources could exist.  
2. **Do large language models instantiate content‑manipulable representations or only statistical encodings?**  
   The distinction is conceptually clear but empirical verification is challenging.  
3. **Can a hybrid theory combining elements from existing frameworks satisfy CC‑1 and escape the Representation Regress?**  
   Mere aggregation of existing frameworks likely fails; a concrete hybrid that avoids all CSIDE concepts is needed.  
4. **Is the Representation Presupposition Thesis (RPT) universally true, or could there exist a counter‑example framework that naturally avoids cognition‑side presuppositions?**  
   The analysis covers five frameworks but cannot prove universality; alternative frameworks may exist that satisfy CC‑1.

## Limitations
- **Definitional boundaries**: The account of content‑manipulable representation is precise but theory‑laden; interpretive flexibility may affect CSIDE identification.
- **Regress assumption**: The inevitability of the Representation Regress hinges on the claim that any explanatory vocabulary must eventually invoke CSIDE; this remains unproven.
- **Empirical support**: Corpus evidence for RPT is weak; analysis covers only five frameworks without systematic external validation.

## Confidence
- RPT pattern across frameworks: Medium
- Inevitable Representation Regress: Medium
- Adequacy conditions CC‑1/CC‑2: Medium
- LLM diagnostic (CMT): Low–Medium

## Next Checks
1. **CSIDE diagnostic on a new framework**: Apply the template to a tradition not covered (e.g., radical enactivism). Escape from CSIDE would falsify RPT universality.
2. **Hybrid theory test**: Combine information‑theoretic and developmental approaches; check for violations of CC‑1/CC‑2, challenging their sufficiency as adequacy conditions.
3. **Empirical CMT benchmark**: Replicate Wan et al.’s tasks (e.g., logical reasoning with permuted lexical items) on current LLMs. Performance degradation when statistics diverge from content confirms CMT; resilience refutes it.
