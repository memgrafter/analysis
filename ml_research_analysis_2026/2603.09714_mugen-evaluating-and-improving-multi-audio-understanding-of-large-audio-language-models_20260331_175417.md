# MUGEN: Evaluating and Improving Multi-audio Understanding of Large Audio-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.09714
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09714
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: MUGEN exposes input scaling bottleneck and positional bias in LALMs; APSC improves accuracy by up to 6.28% (6.74% with CoT).

## Executive Summary
Multi-audio understanding—the ability to reason over multiple simultaneous audio inputs—remains a critical but underexplored capability in Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs). This paper introduces MUGEN, a comprehensive benchmark comprising 1,750 instances across 35 tasks spanning seven dimensions (speech, general audio, music) and framed as multiple-choice audio grounding. Evaluations of seven LALMs reveal systematic weaknesses, particularly on non-semantic attributes such as emotion and temporal cues. Crucially, performance degrades sharply as the number of concurrent audio candidates increases, identifying an input scaling bottleneck. To mitigate positional bias, the authors propose Audio-Permutational Self-Consistency (APSC), a training-free strategy that permutes candidate order and aggregates predictions, yielding up to a 6.28% absolute accuracy improvement. Combining APSC with Chain-of-Thought reasoning further boosts gains to 6.74%. These findings expose fundamental blind spots in current LALMs and establish MUGEN as a foundation for future research on complex auditory comprehension.

## Method Summary
MUGEN is constructed as a multiple-choice audio grounding benchmark. Each instance presents a textual constraint and a set of candidate audio clips (typically five, or six including a reference) averaging 8.6 seconds in duration. The dataset covers 35 tasks grouped into seven dimensions, ensuring balanced evaluation across speech, general audio, and music. Models are evaluated on accuracy, with predictions extracted from free-form outputs using an LLM-as-a-judge (Claude Haiku 4.5) with a fixed decision format. The paper assesses seven LALMs under baseline decoding and with three training-free strategies: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, Self-Consistency (SC) with 10 sampled responses, and Audio-Permutational Self-Consistency (APSC). APSC generates 10 responses per instance, each with a random permutation of the candidate audio order, then aggregates via majority voting. For APSC+CoT, both techniques are combined. The benchmark and evaluation code are released to enable reproducibility.

## Key Results
- LALMs show consistent weaknesses on non-semantic dimensions (affective, temporal, acoustic) compared to semantic tasks.
- Accuracy declines steeply as the number of concurrent audio candidates increases (e.g., from 2 to 5), confirming an input scaling bottleneck.
- APSC reduces positional bias and improves absolute accuracy by up to 6.28%; APSC combined with CoT yields a further gain of 6.74%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Input Scaling Bottleneck
- **Claim:** Performance degrades sharply as concurrent audio inputs increase due to limited integration capacity.
- **Mechanism:** The model's representation or attention mechanism becomes overloaded, causing interference and loss of discriminative power for cross-audio comparison.
- **Evidence:** Experiments with 2–5 candidates show significant accuracy drops; for example, Qwen2.5‑Omni retains only ~66% (no reference) and ~48% (with reference) of its two‑candidate accuracy when given five candidates.

### Mechanism 2: Positional Bias
- **Claim:** LALMs over‑rely on the absolute position of audio candidates, harming robustness.
- **Mechanism:** Predictions are anchored to positional tokens or sequential processing artifacts, making decisions sensitive to candidate order rather than intrinsic content.
- **Evidence:** APSC consistently outperforms standard SC by permuting candidate order, indicating that models lack inherent order‑agnostic set comparison.

### Mechanism 3: Non‑Semantic Attribute Weakness
- **Claim:** Current LALMs are fundamentally weaker in perceiving and reasoning over non‑semantic auditory attributes (e.g., emotion, prosody, acoustic scene).
- **Mechanism:** Training objectives and data mixtures over‑emphasize semantic speech transcription/translation, under‑developing acoustic feature extraction and paralinguistic reasoning circuits.
- **Evidence:** Across all evaluated models, scores on semantic dimensions exceed those on affective, temporal, and acoustic dimensions in MUGEN's balanced evaluation.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Multi‑Audio Set Comparison**  
- *Why needed:* MUGEN requires the model to represent each audio distinctly and perform pairwise or global comparisons across a set, a skill distinct from single‑audio QA or sequential reasoning.  
- *Quick check:* If you shuffle the audio candidates, the correct answer's textual label changes accordingly; can the model maintain a consistent decision on the underlying content?

**Concept: Acoustic Perceptual Reasoning**  
- *Why needed:* Many tasks demand discrimination of subtle acoustic properties (e.g., anger intensity, exact duration, sound event hierarchy) that are not easily transcribed to text.  
- *Quick check:* For an emotion task where all candidates have identical transcriptions, can the model still identify the angriest one? Current models often cannot.

**Concept: Training‑Free Inference Strategies**  
- *Why needed:* APSC demonstrates that inference‑time techniques can mitigate bias without additional training, highlighting the importance of robustness in model deployment.  
- *Quick check:* What is the key difference between standard Self‑Consistency (SC) and Audio‑Permutational SC (APSC) in implementation and intended effect?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Audio Encoder → Projection/Adapter → LLM Backbone → Output Head

**Critical path:**  
Input (Text + [Audio1, Audio2, ..., AudioN]) → Audio Encoder (N parallel streams) → Projection (N embeddings) → LLM (processes interleaved sequence: [Text, Audio1_emb, Audio2_emb, ...]) → Output generation (selects index)

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Fixed vs. Dynamic Audio Tokens: Fixed token count simplifies capacity management but may lose temporal precision; dynamic length requires robust pooling and affects multi‑audio scalability.  
- Interleaving Strategy: The method of injecting audio embeddings into the text stream influences positional bias and cross‑attention patterns.  
- Reasoning Load: Performing comparisons during autoregressive generation risks scaling issues; a separate reasoning phase might alleviate this.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Scaling Collapse: Accuracy drops steeply when N increases from 2 to 5, especially on non‑semantic tasks.  
- Positional Locking: Changing the order of an identical candidate set alters the prediction.  
- Semantic Bypass: High performance on semantic tasks even when non‑semantic cues are contradictory.  
- Cascade Parity: Performance similar to an ASR+LLM cascade, suggesting the audio encoder contributes little beyond semantic transcription.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Scaling Test:** Run a strong LALM (e.g., Qwen2.5‑Omni) on MUGEN with N=2,3,4,5 candidates; plot accuracy vs. N to confirm the sharp degradation claim.  
2. **Positional Bias Probe:** For a fixed 5‑candidate instance, generate responses for all 120 permutations; compute prediction consistency (e.g., entropy, mode share). Low consistency confirms strong positional bias.  
3. **APSC Replication:** Implement APSC with 10 random permutations and majority voting; evaluate on full MUGEN and the scaling subsets. Verify gains (≈2‑6%) and correlate improvement with bias scores and task dimensions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What specific architectural or training limitations cause LALMs to exhibit systematic weaknesses in non‑semantic and paralinguistic attribute understanding within multi‑audio settings?
2. What is the underlying cause of the sharp performance degradation as the number of concurrent audio inputs increases, and can this scaling limitation be fundamentally overcome?
3. Why does Chain‑of‑Thought prompting, effective for text‑based reasoning, fail to improve multi‑audio understanding in LALMs, and what alternative reasoning paradigms could be effective?
4. Is the positional bias in multi‑audio LALMs an inherent architectural flaw or a consequence of training data and objectives, and can permutation‑based mitigation like APSC fully eliminate it?
5. How generalizable are the findings from MUGEN’s multiple‑choice audio grounding formulation to other multi‑audio scenarios, such as open‑ended description, reasoning over variable numbers of inputs, or real‑time applications?

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility blockers:** Exact multimodal prompt templates and permutation details are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **Internal mechanism uncertainty:** The precise cause of the scaling bottleneck and positional bias (e.g., attention saturation, context limits) is inferred but not directly probed.
- **Judge dependency:** Final accuracy relies on an LLM‑as‑a‑judge (Claude Haiku), which may introduce bias or reflect the judge’s own multi‑audio limitations.

## Confidence
- Input scaling bottleneck: **High**
- Positional bias: **Medium**
- Non‑semantic attribute weakness: **High**

## Next Checks

2. **Positional Bias Quantification:** For a fixed 5‑candidate instance, generate responses for all 120 permutations. Compute prediction entropy and consistency rate; break if consistency >90% and entropy low, indicating negligible positional bias for that model/task.
3. **APSC Gain Correlation Analysis:** For a given model, compute per‑instance APSC improvement.
