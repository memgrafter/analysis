# IntentRL: Training Proactive User-intent Agents for Open-ended Deep Research via Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03468  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03468  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Achieves Intent Precision 36.44% and Recall 27.49% on DeepResearch Bench, with report overall score 43.65, far exceeding baselines.

## Executive Summary
IntentRL tackles the autonomy-interaction dilemma in open-ended Deep Research (DR) agents, where ambiguous user queries lead to costly, unsatisfactory long-horizon executions. It trains a proactive agent to clarify latent user intent before research begins. The approach introduces a scalable pipeline that expands a few seed samples into high-quality dialogues via a Clarification DAG (C-DAG), modeling both shallow (query constraint removal) and deep (rubric-derived preferences) intents. A two-stage reinforcement learning strategy first learns general interaction from offline expert trajectories, then refines the policy via online rollouts using an intent-aware user simulator. Extensive experiments show IntentRL significantly improves intent capture and downstream report quality across multiple DR agents and benchmarks.

## Method Summary
IntentRL constructs a C-DAG from seed tasks (50 from DeepResearch Bench) to represent the space of user intents. Shallow intents are derived by fuzzifying queries, while deep intents come from translating rubrics into first-person preferences. The C-DAG is expanded to a depth of ~20 nodes, each with 2-3 options, and traversed via depth-first search to generate intent trajectories. These trajectories seed the simulation of offline dialogues (~2,347 turns) using a strong LLM (e.g., GPT-4.1) with a system prompt that steers the agent toward the target intent stack. The proactive agent (Qwen2.5-7B) is then trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) in two stages: Stage I uses offline expert trajectories with a reward that combines embedding-based intent similarity (R_con) and a formatting bonus (R_fmt); Stage II mixes in online rollouts generated by the current policy interacting with a hybrid simulator (rule-based repetition/relevance checks with all-mpnet-base-v2 thresholds τrep=0.92, τrel=0.8, and an LLM-judge for response style). After T=9 clarification turns, a summary agent (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) synthesizes the dialogue into a refined query q_f, which is fed to downstream DR agents.

## Key Results
- Intent Precision: 36.44%, Intent Recall: 27.49% on DeepResearch Bench (vs. baseline 21.11% and 18.45%).
- Report overall score: 43.65 on DeepResearch Bench, outperforming built-in clarify modules and proactive LLM baselines.
- Gains hold across multiple downstream DR agents (Qwen, Gemini, OpenAI, Tavily) and generalize to Rigorous Bench and PDR-Bench.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The C-DAG provides a structured, scalable source of diverse intent trajectories, covering both superficial and nuanced user preferences. Offline RL on expert trajectories teaches the agent a general strategy for intent-seeking questions. Online RL with the intent-aware simulator then refines this policy by exposing it to its own outputs and applying penalties for repetitive, irrelevant, or insignificant questions. The embedding-based reward (R_con) directly optimizes for alignment with target intents, while R_fmt ensures well-formed questions. This two-stage approach separates learning broad interaction patterns from adapting to the specific dynamics of the clarification task, leading to robust performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Clarification DAG (C-DAG):** Why needed: Explicitly models the hierarchy and dependencies of user intents (shallow and deep) to generate diverse, coherent clarification trajectories. Quick check: Does the constructed C-DAG cover intents unseen in seed tasks?
- **Two-stage RL (Offline → Online):** Why needed: Offline stage bootstraps from expert data; online stage adapts to the agent’s own distribution and penalizes failures. Quick check: Does performance improve monotonically from Stage I to Stage II?
- **Intent-aware user simulator:** Why needed: Provides scalable, low-cost interaction for online RL without requiring human users. Quick check: Does the simulator’s response diversity and intent alignment match human behavior?
- **Embedding-based reward shaping (R_con):** Why needed: Automatically measures question-intent similarity without manual labeling. Quick check: Is the cosine similarity threshold (e.g., >0.8) well-calibrated for distinguishing relevant from irrelevant questions?
- **GRPO algorithm:** Why needed: Efficient policy optimization for large language models with group-relative advantages. Quick check: Does GRPO stabilize training compared to standard PPO in this setting?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Seed tasks → C-DAG construction → Offline trajectory generation (LLM simulation) → Stage I GRPO training → Online rollout with simulator (rule-based + LLM-judge) → Stage II GRPO training → Proactive agent (Qwen2.5-7B) → Summary agent (Qwen2.5-72B) → Refined query → Downstream DR agent

**Critical path:** The training pipeline from data generation (C-DAG and offline dialogues) through two-stage RL to the final proactive agent.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Offline vs. online data: Offline provides expert demonstrations; online adapts to policy but risks distribution shift.  
- Rule-based vs. LLM-judge in simulator: Rule-based is cheap and consistent; LLM-judge adds realism but cost and variability.  
- Embedding vs. LLM for intent matching: Embedding is fast and scalable; LLM is more accurate but slower.  
- Two-stage vs. single-stage RL: Two-stage decouples general learning from online refinement but requires careful mixing.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Overfitting to C-DAG structure: low performance on Rigorous Bench.  
- Simulator mismatch: unnatural user replies, low entropy in responses.  
- Reward hacking: high R_con but low R_fmt or short, uninformative questions.  
- Stage II degradation: Intent Precision drops during online training.

**First experiments:**  
1. Build a minimal C-DAG from 2 seed tasks, traverse to 10 intents, and annotate shallow/deep coverage.  
2. Train Stage I for 1 epoch on 100 offline trajectories; compute Intent Precision on a held-out set.  
3. Run the simulator with random agent for 20 rollouts; measure response entropy and embedding similarity to target intents.

## Limitations
- High uncertainty: exact LLM used for offline dialogue simulation (only gemini-3-pro-preview mentioned for graph construction).  
- Medium uncertainty: precise thresholds and calculation for the "insignificance" penalty (P_inv).  
- Low uncertainty: whether offline expert trajectories are fully synthetic or involve human annotation.

## Confidence
- Core method design (C-DAG, two-stage RL): High  
- Reported performance numbers (Intent Precision/Recall, Report scores): Medium  
- Broad generalization claims beyond evaluated benchmarks: Low  

## Next Checks
1. Replicate C-DAG data generation using an alternative strong LLM (e.g., GPT-4o) and measure divergence in intent distribution and dialogue quality.  
2. Ablate online penalty parameters (τrep, τrel, P_inv logic) in Stage II training to quantify sensitivity of Intent Precision/Recall.  
3. Test zero-shot transfer by evaluating the trained clarification agent on held-out Rigorous Bench tasks to assess overfitting.
