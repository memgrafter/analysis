# Interpretable All-Type Audio Deepfake Detection with Audio LLMs via Frequency-Time Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2601.02983  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02983  
- **Reference count**: 23  
- **Primary result**: FT-GRPO (two-stage training for Audio LLMs) achieves 99.75% accuracy on ASVspoof2019LA and 87.38% average accuracy across all audio types, while producing interpretable frequency-time rationales.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles all-type audio deepfake detection (ADD) — a task requiring models to generalize across speech, environmental sounds, singing, and music while providing interpretable decisions. The authors introduce FT-GRPO, a two-stage training paradigm for Audio LLMs (ALLMs). First, an automatic pipeline generates ~340K frequency-time (FT) structured chain-of-thought (CoT) rationales via captioning and polishing. Second, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) cold-starts the model on the consistent “think” rationales, followed by reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a composite reward that enforces accuracy, format compliance, and FT reasoning. Using Qwen2.5-Omni-3B, the method reaches near-perfect accuracy on the speech benchmark (99.75% on ASVspoof2019LA) and a strong 87.38% average across all types, outperforming prior SOTA. The model outputs interpretable explanations grounded in frequency and time domains, enhancing trust for security-critical applications.

## Method Summary
FT-GRPO comprises a data construction phase and a two-stage training regime. In data construction, raw audio is processed by a two-step pipeline: (1) a captioning model (Qwen3-Omni-Captioner) produces an initial description with FT reasoning; (2) a larger model (Qwen3-235B) polishes the text, removes irrelevance, and checks label-reasoning consistency. Consistent samples become “think” demonstrations; inconsistent ones are flagged as “non-think.” The resulting ~340K “think” data serves as high-quality demonstrations. For training, the ALLM (Qwen2.5-Omni-3B) is adapted with LoRA. Stage 1: SFT cold-start on the “think” data, teaching the model the exact output format (both Frequency Domain and Time Domain sections followed by an answer). Stage 2: GRPO fine-tuning on a mix of “think” and “non-think” samples. The reward combines three terms: binary accuracy, format compliance, and a rule-based FT reasoning completeness score (presence of both sections with at least one sentence each). Group-relative advantages within each sampled batch stabilize learning. This approach yields models that are both highly accurate and consistently generate interpretable, FT-grounded rationales.

## Key Results
- FT-GRPO (speech-trained) achieves 99.75% accuracy on ASVspoof2019LA and 87.38% average accuracy across all audio types, surpassing prior SOTA.
- Co-training on all audio types further improves the average to 90.10%.
- The method produces structured, frequency-time rationales for every prediction, enabling interpretability without sacrificing performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Automatic FT-CoT Generation Provides High-Quality Demonstrations
- **Claim**: The automatic pipeline generates reliable, FT-structured chain-of-thought rationales that teach the ALLM a principled reasoning schema for ADD across all audio types.
- **Mechanism**: Step 1 uses a strong captioner (Qwen3-Omni-Captioner) to produce raw captions with FT reasoning prompts. Step 2 employs a more capable model (Qwen3-235B) to polish, remove irrelevance, and check label-reasoning consistency. Consistent samples become “think” demonstrations; inconsistent ones (“non-think”) are reserved for later reinforcement.
- **Core assumption**: High-quality FT rationales can be constructed at scale without human annotation and will improve both generalization and interpretability when used for cold-start training.
- **Evidence**: The abstract and Section 3.1 describe the pipeline; the resulting ~340K “think” data is used for SFT. The paper’s strong results on all-type generalization indirectly support the quality of these automatic rationales. Neighboring works (e.g., ALLM4ADD) show that label-only SFT yields black-box models, motivating the need for CoT.
- **Break condition**: If the pipeline produces many low-quality or factually incorrect rationales (high “non‑think” rate after polishing), the cold-start data becomes noisy, potentially harming both performance and interpretability.

### Mechanism 2: SFT Cold‑Start Establishes Stable Reasoning Schema
- **Claim**: SFT exclusively on the polished “think” data initializes the ALLM to generate outputs in the desired FT-structure, providing a stable foundation for subsequent RL.
- **Mechanism**: The model is trained via maximum likelihood to reproduce the exact sequence: `<Frequency Domain>` section, `<Time Domain>` section, and `<answer>`. This teaches the required format and the bi-dimensional reasoning pattern before any reward signals are introduced.
- **Core assumption**: SFT on demonstrations with the target structure is necessary to bias the policy toward coherent, domain‑specific explanations; sparse RL rewards alone cannot reliably induce this format.
- **Evidence**: Section 3.2 and Section 5.2 ablation show that including “non‑think” samples in SFT degrades token-level accuracy and final performance; the best strategy (S④) uses only “think” for SFT. The ablation demonstrates that a “clean” cold start is critical.
- **Break condition**: If the “think” demonstrations contain systematic biases or invalid reasoning patterns (e.g., misidentifying artifacts), SFT would teach incorrect heuristics that RL might reinforce.

### Mechanism 3: GRPO with Composite Reward Optimizes Accuracy and Forced FT Reasoning
- **Claim**: GRPO with a composite reward (accuracy, format, FT reasoning) improves detection accuracy while actively encouraging complete, frequency-time grounded rationales and mitigating reward hacking.
- **Mechanism**: For each audio, the model samples G=8 responses. Rewards: `r_acc` (binary correct label), `r_fmt` (binary correct schema), `r_ft` (0/0.5/1 based on presence and completeness of both FD and TD sections). Group-relative advantages normalize rewards within the batch. The policy updates with a KL penalty to the reference SFT model. “Non‑think” samples are included in RL but not SFT, exposing the model to challenging cases without contaminating the cold-start.
- **Core assumption**: The rule-based `r_ft` provides a dense, targeted signal to shape the reasoning trajectory toward bi‑dimensional structure, even though `r_acc` is sparse. Including “non‑think” in RL (not SFT) helps the model correct inconsistent reasoning without learning an incorrect format.
- **Evidence**: Section 3.2 defines the reward and GRPO objective; Section 5.2 shows S④ (SFT on think only, GRPO on mixed) yields the best results (99.75% Speech ACC, 87.38% AVG). Section 5.3 compares vanilla SFT and vanilla GRPO; vanilla GRPO produced degenerate rationales, while FT‑GRPO maintained interpretability. The composite reward design is validated by these ablations.
- **Break condition**: If `r_ft` is too simplistic (e.g., satisfied by short generic sentences), the model may pad both sections with non‑informative text to maximize reward without learning genuine artifact‑spotting reasoning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT) Reasoning for Interpretability**  
  *Why needed*: ADD requires not just a binary decision but an auditable explanation. CoT forcing intermediate reasoning steps ensures transparency and trust.  
  *Quick check*: How does mandating separate `<Frequency Domain>` and `<Time Domain>` sections constrain the explanation compared to an open‑ended “think” block? It explicitly bi­ases the model toward two complementary analysis perspectives, reducing omission of one domain.

- **Reinforcement Learning from Human/Atomic Feedback (RLAIF) with Composite Rewards**  
  *Why needed*: Sparse accuracy rewards alone lead to reward hacking (e.g., ignoring the thinking process). Composite rewards provide denser, multi‑faceted feedback to shape both final answer and reasoning process. GRPO’s group‑relative normalization stabilizes training.  
  *Quick check*: Why use group‑relative advantage instead of absolute reward? It reduces variance by normalizing within a batch, preventing outlier responses from dominating updates and improving stability when rewards are sparse or noisy.

- **Frequency‑Time Domain Artifact Analysis in Audio**  
  *Why needed*: Deepfake artifacts often manifest as spectral (frequency) anomalies (e.g., missing harmonics) or temporal (time) irregularities (e.g., unnatural pauses). Structuring reasoning along these axes aligns with the underlying discriminative cues.  
  *Quick check*: What artifact would primarily appear in the time domain vs. frequency domain? Time: unnatural silences, sudden cuts; Frequency: missing high‑frequency content, phase inconsistencies.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Raw audio → Annotation pipeline (Qwen3‑Omni‑Captioner → Qwen3‑235B) → D_think / D_non‑think → Base model (Qwen2.5‑Omni‑3B) with LoRA (rank=64, α=16) → SFT cold‑start (on D_think) → GRPO fine‑tuning (on D_think ∪ D_non‑think, group size=8, reward weights [1,0.1,0.1]) → Final policy (outputs structured <think> with FD/TD sections and <answer>)

**Critical path**:
1. Prepare datasets (19LA, ESDD, CtrSVDD, FakeMusicCaps) using provided splits.
2. Run annotation pipeline to create ~340K FT‑CoT demonstrations and split into “think” (consistent) and “non‑think” (mismatch) sets.
3. Perform SFT cold‑start: Train LoRA on “think” data for N epochs (2–3 depending on type) with cross‑entropy loss.
4. Initialize GRPO from SFT checkpoint. For each batch, sample G=8 responses, compute composite reward (`r_acc + α r_fmt + β r_ft`), compute group‑relative advantages, update policy with KL penalty.
5. Evaluate: Parse output for `<answer>` to compute accuracy; qualitatively check FD/TD presence.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- LoRA rank: Higher rank (r=64) improves performance but increases compute; r=64, α=16 found optimal.
- Cold‑start data purity: SFT on only “think” yields best results; mixing “non‑think” harms format adherence. However, “non‑think” is valuable in RL to teach correction of inconsistent reasoning.
- Reward weights: α=β=0.1 for format and FT reasoning. Small weights prevent over‑optimization on these auxiliary objectives at the cost of accuracy; this setting was not extensively tuned.
- Audio encoder trainable: Fine‑tuning the audio encoder significantly outperforms freezing it, indicating that pretrained features need adaptation to deepfake artifacts.

**Failure signatures**:
- Degenerate reasoning: `<think>` contains generic, non‑artifact‑specific text or identical sentences for FD/TD. Indicates `r_ft` is being gamed or SFT failed.
- Format collapse: Model omits required tags or outputs reasoning outside `<think>`. Suggests `r_fmt` weight too low or insufficient SFT.
- Catastrophic forgetting: In‑domain accuracy drops after GRPO (e.g., speech ACC from 99.04% SFT to 96.32% GRPO). Indicates RL overly aggressive (high KL penalty needed) or conflicting rewards.
- Non‑think leakage: Including “non‑think” in SFT leads to higher non‑think rate in outputs and lower accuracy; training instability increases.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline SFT without CoT**: Train a model on each audio type with only binary labels (no rationales). Measure per‑type and average ACC. This quantifies the black‑box baseline and the generalization gap.
2. **Vanilla GRPO vs. SFT**: Take the best speech‑trained SFT model (~99% ACC). Apply GRPO with only `r_acc + r_fmt` (no `r_ft`). Observe changes in ACC and inspect rationales for coherence. Expect: slight ACC change but likely degenerate reasoning.
3. **FT‑GRPO Ablation (S④ vs. Other Strategies)**: Implement four strategies for handling “non‑think” data (S① discard, S② SFT on non‑think then think then GRPO, S③ mix in SFT, S④ SFT think‑only then GRPO on mixed). Train each fully and compare final ACC on all test sets plus token‑level format adherence. This validates the core design choice of separating data phases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generality beyond Qwen ALLMs**: The study focuses on Qwen‑Audio/Omni models; it is unknown if FT‑GRPO works for other ALLM architectures.
2. **Reliability of automatic FT‑CoT**: No human validation of rationales; potential for noise, hallucinations, or biases in the automatic pipeline.
3. **Robustness to real‑world variations**: Evaluation datasets are clean and full‑utterance; performance under noise, partial spoofing, or mixed audio types remains untested.
4. **Sufficiency of FT reasoning**: The method restricts reasoning to frequency and time domains; other signal domains (e.g., phase, harmonic‑percussive separation) might enhance detection and explanation.

## Limitations
- Automatic rationale generation lacks human verification; errors may propagate to training.
- The `r_ft` reward is a simplistic rule (tag + sentence) that can be gamed without genuine artifact reasoning.
- Evaluation is limited to standard test sets; real‑world robustness (noise, partial attacks, mixtures) is unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Speech ACC (99.75% on ASVspoof2019LA)**: High  
- **All‑type average ACC (87.38%)**: Medium  
- **Interpretability claim (structured rationales)**: Low (only format checked, not semantic correctness)  
- **Two‑stage training efficacy (S④ advantage)**: High  

## Next Checks
1. **Human rationale audit**: Recruit audio forensics experts to blind‑rate a stratified sample of generated rationales for correctness, completeness, and FT grounding.
2. **Gold‑standard pipeline comparison**: Manually annotate a subset (e.g., 1K examples) with correct FT‑CoT; train models on automatic vs. gold data to quantify pipeline noise impact.
3. **Reward shaping ablation**: Replace the rule‑based `r_ft` with a stricter or learned reward (e.g., based on artifact detector outputs), sweep weights, and measure effects on accuracy and rationale depth.
