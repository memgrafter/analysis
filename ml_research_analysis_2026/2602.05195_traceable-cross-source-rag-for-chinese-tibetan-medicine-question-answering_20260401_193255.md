# Traceable Cross-Source RAG for Chinese Tibetan Medicine Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05195
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05195
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: DAKS improves primary-source accuracy to 0.560 (from 0.500) and reduces encyclopedia dominance to 0.362 on a 500-query benchmark.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a traceable retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system for Chinese Tibetan medicine question answering that operates across three heterogeneous knowledge bases: encyclopedia, classics, and clinical papers. The core challenge is that encyclopedia entries are dense and often dominate retrieval, even when classics or clinical papers provide more authoritative evidence. To address this, the authors propose DAKS, a routing module that uses probe-based score statistics and authority priors to allocate retrieval budgets across knowledge bases, mitigating density-driven bias. Additionally, an alignment graph enables cross-KB evidence fusion by linking entities across sources, and a coverage-aware packing algorithm selects evidence under token constraints while ensuring required KB coverage. Evaluated on a 500-query benchmark without training, the system achieves improved primary-source accuracy, reduced encyclopedia dominance, and strong cross-KB evidence coverage, demonstrating effective traceability and reduced hallucinations.

## Method Summary
The system follows a zero-shot pipeline over three partitioned knowledge bases. First, DAKS performs KB routing: for each KB, a lightweight probe retrieval extracts distributional features (e.g., peak score, entropy) from top-L chunks; a weighted combination plus an authority prior yields a KB score, from which soft budgets are derived. Retrieval from each KB according to budgets forms a dense candidate set. Candidate consolidation adds structure-aware neighbors, deduplicates, and applies diversity caps. Next, an alignment graph—a bipartite graph linking chunks to typed entities—is used to discover cross-KB bridging evidence via graph traversal; graph support scores (entity overlap, proximity, cross-KB penalty) are fused with base relevance scores to rank candidates. Finally, a coverage-aware greedy packing algorithm selects evidence under a token budget, first satisfying required KB coverage then filling by score with per-document caps. The packed evidence is fed to openPangu-Embedded-7B to generate cited answers.

## Key Results
- Primary-source accuracy improved to 0.560 (baseline 0.500) with DAKS.
- Encyclopedia dominance rate reduced to 0.362.
- Cross-KB evidence coverage (CrossEv@5) reached 0.780; faithfulness 0.805; citation correctness 0.760.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: DAKS Routing
DAKS mitigates encyclopedia dominance by using probe-based KB score statistics (peak, top-M mean, margin, entropy, coverage proxy) and authority priors to allocate soft budgets. For each KB, a probe retrieval (top-L chunks) extracts features; a linear combination plus prior yields a KB score S_k. Softmax over S_k gives budgets b_k under total budget B and minimum b_min, ensuring all KBs contribute. This avoids allocating most budget to the KB with dense, high-scoring chunks (encyclopedia). Evidence: [abstract] describes bias mitigation; [section 3.2] formalizes ϕ_k and Algorithm 1; break condition: if probe size L is too small or authority priors mis-specified, routing degrades.

### Mechanism 2: Alignment Graph Fusion
The alignment graph enables cross-KB evidence discovery by modeling entity-centric connections between chunks. A bipartite graph links chunk nodes to typed entity nodes. Given query entities and seed entities from top candidates, graph traversal (h hops) retrieves bridging chunks that connect concepts across KBs. For any candidate, a graph support score combines entity overlap with query, graph proximity to seeds, and a penalty if the chunk's KB differs from the DAKS-predicted primary KB. This score is fused with semantic relevance to produce final ranking. Evidence: [abstract] mentions graph-guided fusion; [section 3.4] details graph construction and scoring; [section 4.6] shows cross-KB gains. Break condition: entity extraction errors or inconsistent typing break connectivity; wrong primary KB prediction may misguide the penalty term.

### Mechanism 3: Coverage-Aware Packing
This selects final evidence under token constraints while satisfying required KB coverage and controlling redundancy. Given a ranked list, Algorithm 2 first selects the highest-scoring chunk per required KB that fits the budget, then greedily appends remaining chunks by descending score, enforcing per-document diversity caps. This directly optimizes for cross-KB verification and avoids naive concatenation pitfalls. Evidence: [abstract] notes coverage-aware packing; [section 3.4.3] formulates the constrained optimization and algorithm; [section 4.6] shows packing improves CrossEv@5 and reduces evidence diversity rate. Break condition: if required KB set is wrong or token budget too tight, coverage fails; greedy nature may be suboptimal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Heterogeneous Knowledge Base Indexing**: Separate indexing preserves source identity and prevents flattening of provenance, which would amplify density bias. Quick check: If encyclopedia chunks are shorter but denser in relevant terms than clinical chunks, naive BM25/vector search would over-retrieve encyclopedia; separate indexing allows routing to balance sources.
- **Authority-Aware Routing vs. Pure Relevance**: Relevance scores alone cannot prioritize authoritative sources (classics/clinical) over dense encyclopedia summaries. DAKS uses authority priors and probe statistics (e.g., margin, entropy) to detect "too easy" matches. Quick check: For a query about a specific clinical trial, why might encyclopedia have higher s_ret than clinical papers? How might DAKS's margin or entropy signal this?
- **Entity-Centric Cross-Source Alignment**: Entities serve as stable anchors across heterogeneous KBs (e.g., disease names in different transliterations). The alignment graph bridges concepts via entity links. Quick check: How does graph bridge retrieval differ from simply pooling top chunks from all KBs? What failure of naive concatenation does it solve?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Indexing -> DAKS Routing -> Consolidation -> Graph Fusion -> Packing -> Generation

### Critical Path
DAKS routing (budget allocation) -> consolidation -> graph fusion (ranking) -> packing. Failure in DAKS propagates; packing must respect coverage.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Probe size L: larger L improves ϕ reliability but adds latency.
- Token budget T_max: larger T_max increases coverage potential but risks noise; greedy packing may be suboptimal.
- Graph hops h: more hops find distant bridges but increase noise and compute.
- Authority prior a_k: static weights assume fixed authority ordering; query-dependent priors would be more flexible but require training.

### Failure Signatures
- High Encyclopedia Dominance Rate (EDR): DAKS routing fails to suppress encyclopedia.
- Low CrossEv@5 on cross-KB queries: packing fails coverage or graph fusion misses bridges.
- Low faithfulness/citation correctness despite high CrossEv@5: packed evidence may not actually support generated statements.
- Runtime blowup: unconstrained graph traversal or large L.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Ablate DAKS authority prior: Set λ=0. Expect PrimaryAcc drop and EDR rise.
2. Ablate graph bridge retrieval: Compare full system (with R_graph) vs. graph-fusion only (no R_graph) on CrossEv@5. Expect decrease.
3. Stress test packing budget: Vary T_max (512, 1024, 2048) and plot CrossEv@5 and CitCorr. Identify operating range.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does reliance on a single judge model (GLM-4.7) systematically bias evaluation for Tibetan medicine QA?
- Can DAKS and alignment graph generalize to other domains with partitioned heterogeneous KBs?
- How sensitive is alignment graph fusion to errors in entity extraction and incomplete typing?
- Would training the retriever with Tibetan medicine-specific supervision improve routing and fusion?

## Limitations
- Critical implementation details unreported: specific retriever/reranker models, hyperparameter values, entity extraction tool, judge prompts.
- Heavy reliance on high-quality entity linking; poor entity coverage collapses the graph.
- Static authority priors may not reflect query-specific source appropriateness.
- Greedy packing heuristic lacks optimality guarantees.
- Evaluation restricted to a single 500-query benchmark, limiting external validity.

## Confidence
- DAKS routing effectiveness: Medium (well-motivated but hyperparameters not fully reported)
- Alignment graph for cross-KB fusion: Medium (compelling but hinges on entity extraction quality)
- Coverage-aware packing: Medium (likely helps but no optimality comparison)
