# ReLA: Representation Learning and Aggregation for Job Scheduling with Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03646
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03646
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** ReLA significantly reduces the optimality gap for flexible job shop scheduling, especially on large-scale instances (100+ jobs), by learning rich multi-faceted representations and aggregating them for robust RL decision-making.

## Executive Summary
ReLA introduces a structured multi-module representation learning framework for reinforcement learning-based flexible job shop scheduling (FJSP). It addresses the limitations of prior RL schedulers that rely on single representation types (e.g., attention-only) by combining self-attention, convolution, and cross-attention to capture complementary scheduling characteristics—global precedence, local patterns, and operation-machine compatibility. These modules operate at multiple scales, and their outputs are aggregated to form enriched state representations for the policy. Empirical results show ReLA reduces the state-of-the-art optimality gap by 13.0% to 7.3% on small/medium instances and dramatically by 78.6% to 2.1% on large-scale instances (100+ jobs), while maintaining fast inference times in sampling mode.

## Method Summary
ReLA processes an FJSP state by first embedding raw operation and machine features. Three distinct learning modules—a graph attention layer (self-attention), a 1D convolutional network, and a cross-attention mechanism—are applied in parallel across two representation scales (e.g., 32- and 8-dimensional). Self-attention models long-range dependencies among same-type entities (operations or machines), convolution captures local structural patterns, and cross-attention explicitly models pairwise operation-machine interactions. For each feasible operation-machine pair, the system aggregates the raw entity features with mean-pooled contextual summaries from each module’s outputs at both scales, forming three separate representations. These are scored in parallel by six actor networks (3 modules × 2 scales), and the scores are summed to produce final action logits. A critic network evaluates a pooled multi-scale context to estimate state value. The policy is trained with PPO-Clip using a reward based on estimated makespan reduction per assignment.

## Key Results
- Reduces optimality gap vs. OR-Tools on SD1 10x5 from SOTA 13.0% to 7.3%.
- On large-scale SD1 instances (100x10), reduces gap from SOTA 78.6% to 2.1%, demonstrating exceptional scalability.
- Outperforms attention-only baselines (e.g., DANIEL) via ablations confirming additive value of each representation module and multi-scale design.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Using multiple, structurally distinct representation learning modules (self-attention, convolution, cross-attention) captures complementary scheduling characteristics (global precedence, local patterns, inter-entity compatibility) better than a single representation method.
- **Mechanism:** Separate modules process initial embeddings of operations and machines. Self-attention (via GAL) models long-range dependencies among same-type entities (e.g., job precedence). Convolution captures local structural patterns within entity neighborhoods. Cross-attention explicitly models pairwise operation-machine interactions.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "...using two intra-entity learning modules with self-attention and convolution and one inter-entity learning module with cross-attention." [section 3.2]: Detailed equations (2)-(4) and text describing each module's purpose. [corpus]: Ablation in Table 3 shows each module contributes additively.
- **Break condition:** If the structural properties of the specific FJSP variant are such that one mechanism (e.g., local patterns) dominates all others, the overhead of multiple modules may not yield a net benefit, or could introduce noise.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Aggregating outputs from diverse representation modules and scales into pair-wise candidate representations provides a richer, multi-faceted context for the RL policy than using any single representation.
- **Mechanism:** For each feasible operation-machine pair, the system constructs three aggregated representations by concatenating raw entity features with mean-pooled contextual summaries from each module's outputs at multiple scales. These are fed in parallel to actor networks; scores are summed for final action logits.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "These modules are applied in a multi-scale architecture, and their outputs are aggregated to support RL decision-making." [section 3.3]: Describes aggregation (Eqs. 5-8) and parallel actor score summation (Eq. 11). [corpus]: Ablation in Table 3 shows full ReLA (all modules) outperforms partial variants.
- **Break condition:** If the representations from different modules become highly correlated (redundant) for a given problem distribution, the aggregation provides diminishing returns and increases computational cost without proportional accuracy gain.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Applying representation learning at multiple scales (with different output dimensionalities) and using deep supervision (utilizing all scales during training) preserves information at different abstraction levels, stabilizing learning and improving generalization, especially to unseen large problem scales.
- **Mechanism:** Each representation learning module produces outputs at two parallel scales (e.g., 32-dim and 8-dim). Representations from both scales are used during training as supervision signals and are aggregated for the final policy. The critic uses only the pooled multi-scale context.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "adopts a multi-scale learning architecture... representations from multiple scales are utilized together as representation guidance." [section 3.2]: "To facilitate effective learning... ReLA adopts a two-scale representation learning architecture... representations produced at both scales are utilized during training as deep supervision." [section 4.2 & Table 3]: L=2 (default) outperforms L=8; model trained on 10x5 generalizes well to 100x10 and 200x5.
- **Break condition:** If the optimal abstraction level for a specific problem size is singular, multiple scales could introduce conflicting gradients or representational interference. The L=8 ablation shows breakdown when scales are excessive.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Self-Attention (Graph Attention Layer)**
  - **Why needed here:** To model long-range precedence constraints and dependencies among operations within jobs or workload distribution among machines, where the influence is not limited to immediate neighbors.
  - **Quick check:** Can you explain how a self-attention head allows an operation to incorporate information from a non-adjacent operation in the same job's route, and why a standard feedforward layer might fail at this?

- **Concept: Cross-Attention for Pairwise Scoring**
  - **Why needed here:** To explicitly learn a compatibility representation between an operation (query) and a candidate machine (key/value), directly informing the score for that specific assignment. This goes beyond independent entity embeddings.
  - **Quick check:** In the cross-attention equation (4), what does the query `ˆhop i,k` represent, and what does attending over `{ ˆhma j }` allow the operation's representation to capture?

- **Concept: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO-Clip) for Combinatorial Actions**
  - **Why needed here:** The action space (selecting a feasible operation-machine pair) is dynamic and combinatorial. PPO-Clip provides a stable policy gradient method for large action spaces with masked feasible actions, handling the non-stationarity of the action set as the schedule builds.
  - **Quick check:** How does the `mask` function in the softmax (Eq. 12) ensure the RL policy only considers feasible assignments at each step, and why is this critical for FJSP?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Encoder (`ϕ op`, `ϕ ma`) -> Intra-Entity Modules (Self-Attn, Conv) -> Inter-Entity Module (Cross-Attn) -> Aggregation (per candidate pair) -> RL Agent (6 Parallel Actors + 6 Critics) -> PPO-Clip
- **Critical path:** Raw Features -> Initial Embedding -> (Intra-Entity -> Inter-Entity) -> Multi-Module Aggregation (per pair) -> Parallel Actor Scoring -> Action Selection (masked softmax). Critic path diverges after learning modules using only pooled context.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Compute vs. Scale: Adding modules/scales increases parameters and per-step latency but improves solution quality, especially on hard/large problems.
  - Generalization vs. Specialization: Training on small instances (10x5) and zero-shot transfer to large (100x10, 200x5) works, suggesting features are size-agnostic; retraining per size could help but is costly.
  - Representation Diversity vs. Redundancy: More modules/scales increase coverage of scheduling aspects but risk correlated outputs (L=8 ablation underperforms L=2).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High variance in makespan across random seeds suggests unstable representation learning; check if deep supervision (multi-scale) is active.
  - Performance collapse on large instances while doing well on small ones may indicate representations fail to capture higher-order interactions at scale; consider increasing scale count or model width.
  - Actor/critic loss divergence during PPO often points to reward design issues (Eq. 16) or overly optimistic value estimates; verify GAE parameter `λ` and reward scaling.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation on SD1 10x5 (Greedy): ReLA (all modules, L=2) vs. DANIEL (attn-only) vs. Variants (A: +c-attn, B: +conv). Confirms additive contribution of each module.
  2. Zero-shot Scale Transfer: Train on SD1 10x5, test on SD1 40x10 and 100x10. Checks if multi-scale features generalize. Expect non-trivial gap but ReLA should beat DANIEL.
  3. Public Benchmark (mk/la) Sampling Mode: Compare to DANIEL/HGNN on irregular routing patterns. Tests if learned representations are dataset-agnostic, not just fitting synthetic distributions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What is the optimal number of representation scales (L) in ReLA's multi-scale architecture for different FJSP instance complexities?
- How does the computational overhead of ReLA's diverse representation modules trade off with scheduling performance, and can this trade-off be optimized?
- Is the single-objective makespan reduction reward function sufficient for ReLA to generalize to multi-objective real-world scheduling (e.g., minimizing tardiness, energy use)?
- How robust is ReLA to dynamic, real-world FJSP characteristics like stochastic job arrivals, machine failures, or highly irregular machine-job compatibility graphs?

## Limitations
- MLP architectures of actor/critic networks are not fully described, hindering exact reproduction.
- Multi-scale implementation details (parameter sharing, inter-scale interaction, deep supervision weighting) are missing.
- Evaluation is restricted to synthetic benchmarks; real-world FJSP instances are absent, limiting practical confidence.
- Decision-making runtime for large instances is unreported, leaving scalability claims only partly supported.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (multi-module complementarity):** Medium. Ablations show each module adds value, but increased capacity may explain gains; break condition plausible when one structural bias dominates.
- **Mechanism 2 (aggregation of multiple views):** High. Full ReLA outperforms partial variants; parallel-sum design directly aggregates scores, and redundancy break condition appears not triggered.
- **Mechanism 3 (multi-scale + deep supervision):** Medium. L=2 beats L=8, but single-scale baseline is absent; zero-shot generalization could also stem from other modules.

## Next Checks
1. **Aggregation isolation:** Implement a variant that concatenates all module outputs into a single representation fed to one actor network, versus the current parallel-sum approach. Compare performance to isolate aggregation benefits from raw representation diversity.
2. **Single-scale baseline:** Train ReLA with only one representation scale (e.g., L=1) and evaluate zero-shot on larger instances. A significant drop versus L=2 would confirm the advantage of multi-scale processing.
3. **Attention interpretability:** Visualize attention weights from self-attention (GAL) and cross-attention modules on diverse schedules. Check whether they align with known heuristic patterns (precedence constraints, machine suitability), providing mechanistic evidence.
