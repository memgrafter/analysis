# A Power-Weighted Noncentral Complex Gaussian Distribution

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26344
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26344
- **Reference Count:** 23
- **Primary Result:** A new distribution for complex-valued data that preserves planar geometry and unifies classical amplitude models via a shape parameter α, showing statistically significant improvements on speech power spectra.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a flexible probabilistic model for complex-valued signals, addressing limitations of the standard complex Gaussian which cannot capture diverse amplitude statistics. The proposed power-weighted noncentral complex Gaussian is defined directly on the complex plane, introducing a shape parameter α that induces nonlinear phase diffusion—a coupling between amplitude and phase that allows continuous control from origin-concentrated to arc-shaped distributions. The model’s marginal amplitude and power distributions unify several classical families (Rayleigh, Rice, Nakagami, gamma) as special cases. Theoretical analysis yields closed-form moments and a sampling algorithm. Empirical evaluation on TIMIT speech power spectra demonstrates consistent and statistically significant log-likelihood improvements over exponential, gamma, and noncentral gamma models.

## Method Summary
The core of the method is a density on ℂ: p(z) ∝ |z|^{2α−2} exp(−|z−µ|²/σ²), where α > 0 shapes the distribution. In polar coordinates, the Jacobian yields a radial term r^{2α−1} and a von Mises phase conditional with concentration proportional to r, creating amplitude‑phase dependence. The power distribution (for x = |z|²) is p(x) ∝ x^{α−1} e^{−βx} I₀(2√(βλx)), with β = 1/σ² and λ = β|µ|². Parameter estimation uses maximum likelihood via numerical optimization (quasi‑Newton), aided by closed‑form moments. Sampling proceeds by drawing a discrete index n from a Poisson‑type mixture (Eq. 21), then a gamma variable, and finally a von Mises phase conditioned on that amplitude.

## Key Results
- The shape parameter α enables continuous tuning between sparse (α < 1) and arc‑spread (α > 1) geometries via nonlinear phase diffusion.
- The amplitude and power distributions encompass Rayleigh (α=1, ν=0), Rice (α=1, ν>0), Nakagami (α=m, ν=0), and gamma (λ=0) as special cases.
- On TIMIT speech power patches, the proposed model yields higher average log‑likelihood than exponential, gamma, and noncentral gamma baselines (p < 0.0001).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Geometric Consistency on the Complex Plane
The distribution is formulated directly on ℂ² with a power weight |z|^{2α−2}, preserving the intrinsic 2‑dimensional structure of complex observations. Unlike hyperspherical models (e.g., noncentral chi, κ‑µ) that assume a higher‑dimensional spherical manifold and project onto ℂ, this approach starts with a planar density. The resulting joint polar density (Eq. 3) exhibits explicit amplitude‑phase coupling through a cos(θ−ϕ) term, which arises naturally from the complex exponential without dimensional mismatch.

### Mechanism 2: Nonlinear Phase Diffusion via α
The power‑law factor |z|^{2α−2} distorts the radial term in polar coordinates to r^{2α−1}. Combined with the von Mises phase conditional (concentration 2νr/σ²), this creates a monotonic relationship between amplitude and phase uncertainty. For α < 1, the radial penalty pulls mass toward the origin (sparse amplitude). For α > 1, the phase concentration grows with r, producing elongated arcs (phase diffusion). This provides a continuous interpolation from the α = 1 circularly symmetric complex Gaussian.

### Mechanism 3: Unification via Fixed‑Order Bessel Function
The power distribution (Eq. 26) has the form p(x) ∝ x^{α−1} e^{−βx} I₀(2√(βλx)), where the Bessel order is fixed at 0 while the power of x varies with α. This contrasts with the noncentral gamma distribution, whose Bessel order equals α−1. Setting α = 1 yields the Rice (noncentral chi) distribution; setting λ = 0 recovers the gamma; and integer α with λ = 0 gives Nakagami‑m. The fixed order allows distinct tail behavior, especially for α < 1 (heavier tails than noncentral gamma, Fig. 7).

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Core PDF (Eq.1) → Polar transformation (Jacobian r) → Joint amplitude‑phase density (Eq.3) → Marginal amplitude (Eq.9) and conditional phase (Eq.5) → Power transformation (x = r²) → Power PDF (Eq.26) → Sampling via discrete n + gamma + von Mises (Sec.4)

### Critical Path
1. Choose modeling scale: either full complex z (Eq.1) or power x (Eq.26). For full spectra use Eq.1; for power‑only use Eq.26.
2. Estimate parameters (µ, σ², α) or (α, β, λ) via MLE using quasi‑Newton optimization, aided by closed‑form moments (Eq.47).
3. Evaluate fit by log‑likelihood (Eq.26 for power data) and compare baselines.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Flexibility vs. Computation: α adds tail control but introduces confluent hypergeometric L functions in normalizers, costlier than Gamma/exponential.
- Sampling: Discrete mixing (Eq.21) requires truncation (choose N) or Metropolis‑Hastings (may be inefficient for large α).
- Interpretation: α < 1 suits sparse amplitudes (e.g., voiced speech); α > 1 suits arc/spread. But for power‑only fitting λ often collapses to 0, reducing to Gamma—so full complex (phase‑aware) modeling may be needed to exploit noncentrality.

### Failure Signatures
- Laguerre L evaluation errors causing invalid PDFs. Diagnostic: numerically integrate over grid; normalization should be ≈1.
- MLE not converging or stuck in local optima. Diagnostic: multiple random restarts; monitor gradient norms.
- Metropolis‑Hastings acceptance rate very low for α ≫ 1. Diagnostic: track acceptance; increase proposal variance or switch to truncated approximation.
- Edge effects in patch extraction. Diagnostic: visualize power histograms; ensure uniform frequency coverage.

### First Three Experiments
1. **Recovery of Known Distributions:** Generate Rayleigh (α=1, ν=0), Rice (α=1, ν>0), Nakagami‑m (α=m, ν=0) data. Fit the proposed model and verify estimated α matches theoretical value and λ = ν²/σ² within tolerance.
2. **Synthetic Power Likelihood:** Generate power from Eq.26 with α≠1, λ>0. Compare fitted log‑likelihood of proposed vs. Gamma and noncentral Gamma. Confirm proposed yields higher likelihood when α≠1 and λ>0.
3. **Speech Spectra Patch Likelihood (Replication):** Extract non‑overlapping 3×20 patches from TIMIT power spectrograms. Fit exponential, gamma, noncentral gamma, and proposed models. Perform paired one‑sided t‑tests on patch log‑likelihoods to confirm statistically significant improvement (as in Table 2).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- The paper’s experiments use only power spectra, so the claimed geometric consistency and phase diffusion lack direct empirical validation on full complex data. No phase‑sensitive tasks (e.g., speech synthesis, source separation) are evaluated.
- Theoretical properties of MLE (identifiability, consistency, asymptotic normality) are not discussed; the likelihood may have non‑convexities or boundary issues (λ=0 reduces to gamma).
- No comparison to heavy‑tailed complex distributions (complex Student‑t, α‑stable) on impulsive non‑speech signals (e.g., radar, biomedical).
- Sampling algorithms (truncated series vs. Metropolis‑Hastings) lack efficiency analysis; runtime, acceptance rates, and scalability across (α, λ) are unknown.
- Multivariate extension to ℂ^d (e.g., multichannel audio) is not addressed; preserving planar geometry in higher dimensions remains open.

## Limitations
- Numerical instability of Laguerre L functions for extreme α, λ may hinder practical use and MLE.
- MLE optimization details (initialization, convergence criteria) are omitted, risking irreproducibility.
- Experimental validation is limited to amplitude‑only (power) data; phase‑dependent claims remain unverified.
- Sampling for α ≫ 1 may be inefficient due to poor Metropolis‑Hastings proposal match.

## Confidence
- Geometric consistency (direct planar formulation): Medium (theoretically strong, but empirical phase evidence lacking)
- Phase diffusion (α‑controlled amplitude‑phase coupling): Medium (derivation clear, no phase‑experiment)
- Classical unification (Rayleigh, Rice, Nakagami, gamma as special cases): High (straightforward math)
- Empirical superiority on speech power: High (statistically significant, domain‑specific)
