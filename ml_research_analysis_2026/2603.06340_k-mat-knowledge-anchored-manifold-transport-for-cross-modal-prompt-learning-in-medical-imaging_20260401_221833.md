# K-MaT: Knowledge-Anchored Manifold Transport for Cross-Modal Prompt Learning in Medical Imaging

## Quick Facts
*   **Paper:** K-MaT: Knowledge-Anchored Manifold Transport for Cross-Modal Prompt Learning in Medical Imaging
*   **ArXiv ID:** 2603.06340
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06340
*   **Reference Count:** 18
*   **Primary Result:** Addresses catastrophic forgetting in high-end to low-end medical VLM transfer via factorized prompts anchored to LLM clinical text and aligned with Fused Gromov-Wasserstein transport, achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot cross-modal generalization.

## Executive Summary
K-MaT tackles a critical challenge in medical vision-language models (VLMs): transferring knowledge from high-end imaging modalities (e.g., CT) to low-end ones (e.g., ultrasound) without retraining on low-end images, which are often scarce. Standard prompt learning methods like CoOp suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where prompts overfit to high-end statistics and fail on low-end data. K-MaT's solution factorizes prompts into class-specific semantics and modality-specific context, anchors these learnable prompts to clinically grounded LLM-generated descriptions to prevent semantic drift, and aligns the low-end prompt manifold to the visually-anchored high-end manifold using Fused Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport. This design enables zero-shot deployment on low-end modalities while preserving high-end performance, yielding significant gains in average harmonic mean accuracy and macro-F1, and crucially preventing performance collapse on challenging tasks like breast imaging.

## Method Summary
K-MaT is a cross-modal prompt learning framework for a frozen biomedical VLM (BiomedCLIP). It learns two separate sets of factorized prompts—each combining class-specific and modality-specific tokens—for a high-end source modality (with training images) and a low-end target modality (without training images). The high-end prompts are trained with standard cross-entropy loss on source data. Both prompt sets are regularized by an anchoring loss that pulls their textual embeddings toward fixed prototypes derived from GPT-5 clinical descriptions. A Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) alignment loss then enforces that the relational geometry (pairwise distances) of the low-end prompt manifold mirrors that of the fixed, high-end manifold. This transfers diagnostic decision structure without requiring low-end visual data. At inference, only the learned low-end prompts and the frozen visual encoder are used for zero-shot classification on low-end images.

## Key Results
*   Improves average harmonic mean accuracy to 44.1% (from 42.0% in prior work) and macro-F1 to 36.2% on cross-modal medical imaging benchmarks.
*   Prevents catastrophic collapse on the challenging breast imaging task, raising low-end accuracy from 27.0% (CoOp) to 38.4% while maintaining high-end performance.
*   Achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot generalization from high-end (CT/MRI) to low-end (Ultrasound/X-ray) modalities without using any low-end training images.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Factorizing prompts into class-specific and modality-specific contexts prevents interference between high-end and low-end modalities during training.
*   **Mechanism:** Learnable prompts `t_i,m = [v1_i,m, v2_i,m, v3_i,m, v4_i,m, c_i]` separate shared class semantics (`c_i`) from modality-adaptive context (`v_k_i,m`). This allows the model to preserve modality-invariant diagnostic concepts while learning to adjust textual representations for modality-specific appearance statistics.
*   **Core Assumption:** Diagnostic semantics for a given pathology (e.g., "malignant mass") share a core textual description across modalities, while visual features differ.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "...factorizes prompts..."; [section 2] "...we introduce Class-Specific Context (CSC) and Modality-Specific Context (MSC), learning distinct prompts for each class across different modalities."; [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., "Domain Prompt Learning with Quaternion Networks") explore prompt factorization but do not directly validate this mechanism.
*   **Break Condition:** If class-specific and modality-specific contexts become entangled during optimization (e.g., due to poor initialization or high learning rates), the separation fails, leading to modality collapse.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Anchoring learnable prompts to LLM-generated clinical text descriptions (`L_anc`) prevents semantic drift into modality-specific shortcuts.
*   **Mechanism:** Fixed textual prototypes `p̄_i,m` (from averaged LLM descriptions) act as semantic anchors. The Euclidean distance loss `L_anc = ||w_i,m - p̄_i,m||^2` constrains the learnable embeddings `w_i,m` to stay close to clinically grounded semantics, even when trained only on high-end images.
*   **Core Assumption:** LLM-generated descriptions capture sufficient, modality-agnostic clinical semantics to serve as a grounding reference.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "...anchors them to clinical text descriptions..."; [section 2] "...treating LLM-generated textual prototypes as semantic anchors to prevent deviation from clinically meaningful semantics."; [corpus] Weak; neighbor papers discuss LLM knowledge in VLMs but not as a zero-shot anchor for cross-modal transfer.
*   **Break Condition:** If LLM descriptions are clinically inaccurate or miss modality-critical nuances, the anchors mislead rather than guide, harming performance on both modalities.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Aligning the low-end prompt manifold to the high-end manifold via Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) optimal transport (`L_fgw`) transfers relational decision structure without low-end visual data.
*   **Mechanism:** FGW minimizes a combined cost: (1) feature alignment (Wasserstein term) between high-end and low-end class embeddings, and (2) structural alignment (Gromov-Wasserstein term) preserving intra-manifold relational distances (e.g., similarity between "benign" and "malignant" embeddings). High-end embeddings are fixed; gradients update only low-end prompts.
*   **Core Assumption:** The relational geometry (pairwise distances) of the high-end prompt manifold encodes transferable diagnostic semantics that the low-end manifold should mirror.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "...aligns the low-end prompt manifold to the visually-grounded high-end space using Fused Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport."; [section 2] "...enforcing the learned low-end prompt manifold to strictly mirror the relational structure of the high-end prompt manifold anchored by clinical text knowledge."; [section 3.3] t-SNE visualizations (Fig 2a) show low-end embeddings preserving high-end relational structure only when `L_fgw` is active.
*   **Break Condition:** If the high-end and low-end modalities have irreconcilable feature-space geometry (e.g., ultrasound vs. mammography with entirely different tissue representations), structural alignment forces inappropriate constraints, degrading low-end accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Frozen BiomedCLIP Visual Encoder (ϕ) and Text Encoder (θ) -> Learnable High-End Prompts (t_i,H = CSC + MSC) -> High-End Text Embeddings (w_i,H) -> L_ce Loss (with High-End Images)
Learnable Low-End Prompts (t_i,L = CSC + MSC) -> Low-End Text Embeddings (w_i,L)
LLM Knowledge Bank (Fixed p̄_i,H, p̄_i,L) -> L_anc Loss (pulls w_i,H, w_i,L toward anchors)
Fixed High-End Manifold (w_i,H) -> FGW Alignment (L_fgw) -> Updates Low-End Prompts (t_i,L)

### Critical Path
1.  **Data flow:** High-end image `x_H` → frozen `ϕ` → visual feature; class `i` → `t_i,H` → `θ` → `w_i,H`.
2.  **Supervised signal:** `L_ce` computes classification loss using `x_H` and `w_i,H`; gradients update `t_i,H` (both CSC and MSC).
3.  **Regularization flow:** `L_anc` computes Euclidean distance between `w_i,H`/`w_i,L` and their fixed `p̄_i,m`; gradients update both prompt sets to stay near anchors.
4.  **Cross-modal flow:** `L_fgw` constructs cost matrices from fixed `w_i,H` and current `w_i,L`; solves for transport plan Γ; computes FGW loss; gradients update *only* `t_i,L` to align low-end manifold geometry to the fixed high-end manifold.
5.  **Zero-shot deployment:** Discard `t_i,H`; load trained `t_i,L`; for a new low-end image `x_L`, extract feature with `ϕ`, compare to all `w_i,L` from `θ(t_i,L)` for classification.

### Design Tradeoffs
*   **`λ_anc` vs. `λ_fgw` tension:** High `λ_anc` keeps prompts clinically grounded but may resist beneficial low-end adaptation; high `λ_fgw` strongly aligns manifolds but risks over-constraining low-end prompts if high-end geometry is incompatible. Tuned on high-end validation set only.
*   **LLM dependency:** Performance hinges entirely on quality of GPT-5 descriptions. No low-end visual data means no correction if LLM descriptions miss key low-end features, potentially poisoning the anchor.
*   **Computational cost:** FGW via Sinkhorn iterations is O(n³) for n classes. Scalable to typical fine-grained medical tasks (n=3-6) but may become costly for very large label sets (n>20).

### Failure Signatures
1.  **Complete collapse (low-end Acc ~ random):** `L_fgw` disabled or fusion weight α=0. Manifolds unaligned; low-end prompts overfit high-end statistics, failing on low-end distribution.
2.  **High-end degradation:** `L_anc` weight (`λ_anc`) too high. Learnable prompts cannot deviate enough from potentially mismatched LLM anchors, harming source modality performance.
3.  **Modality confusion:** CSC/MSC factorization not used or poorly initialized. Prompts for different modalities interfere, blending representations that become suboptimal for both.
4.  **Training instability:** Sinkhorn entropy regularization parameter `ε` (temperature) too low → hard assignment, gradient norms vanish; too high → alignment effect weak, convergence slow.

### First 3 Experiments
1.  **Ablation 1 (Table 3, Row 1 vs. Row 8):** Train baseline with only `L_ce` (no factorization, no regularization). Confirm catastrophic forgetting (low H-score). Show adding all components (CSC, MSC, `L_anc`, `L_fgw`) yields highest harmonic mean, validating full framework necessity.
2.  **Ablation 2 (Table 3, Rows 5-7):** Isolate `L_fgw` impact. Compare variant with `L_anc` but no `L_fgw` (Row 5) to full model (Row 8). Expect a significant drop in low-end accuracy without `L_fgw`, confirming manifold alignment is key for zero-shot transfer.
3.  **Visualization (Fig 2a):** Plot t-SNE of final textual embeddings (`w_i,H`, `w_i,L`) for the Chest X-ray classification task. Without `L_fgw`, low-end embeddings (`w_i,L`) are scattered with no clear structure; with `L_fgw`, they form distinct, well-separated clusters that mirror the relational layout of the high-end clusters. This visually confirms FGW's role in preserving relational structure.

## Limitations
*   **LLM Quality Dependency:** The entire anchoring mechanism depends on the clinical accuracy and completeness of GPT-5 generated descriptions. There is no ablation over different LLMs or human-verified texts.
*   **Geometric Transfer Assumption:** The FGW alignment assumes the high-end prompt manifold's geometry is fully transferable to all low-end modalities. This is untested on semantically divergent pairs (e.g., ultrasound vs. fundus photography) where inherent feature relationships may differ.
*   **Scalability Profile:** The O(n³) complexity of FGW with Sinkhorn is not profiled beyond 6 classes. Practical deployment on fine-grained tasks with dozens of pathology subtypes may face computational/memory bottlenecks.

## Confidence
*   **Prompt factorization prevents interference:** Medium. Supported by ablation results (Table 3) showing benefit of CSC/MSC, but no direct measurement of entanglement or representation separation (e.g., probing).
*   **LLM anchoring prevents semantic drift:** Low. Relies on weak external evidence (neighbor papers) and lacks failure-mode testing (e.g., what if anchors are wrong?). No clinical validation of GPT-5 outputs.
*   **FGW alignment transfers structure:** Medium. Supported by t-SNE visualizations (Fig 2a) and ablation lifts, but no stress tests probing limits of geometric compatibility between modalities.

## Next Checks
1.  **Anchor Sensitivity Validation:** Replace GPT-5 with open-source LLMs (e.g., Llama-3-70B) and/or human-curated clinical text. Quantify performance changes to identify anchor quality failure thresholds.
2.  **Geometric Compatibility Stress Test:** Test K-MaT on pairs of modalities with known, significant semantic gaps in visual representation (e.g., retinal fundus vs. optical coherence tomography). Measure if forced manifold alignment degrades accuracy, probing the core transfer assumption.
3.  **Scalability Profiling:** Implement and profile FGW's computational and memory cost as the number of classes scales from 10 to 50. Measure corresponding accuracy trade-offs to validate practical deployment constraints for fine-grained diagnosis.
