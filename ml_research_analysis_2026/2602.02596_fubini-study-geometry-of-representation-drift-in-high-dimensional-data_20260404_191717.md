# Fubini Study geometry of representation drift in high dimensional data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02596  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02596  
- **Reference count:** 24  
- **Primary result:** The Fubini-Study metric isolates intrinsic representational change from gauge artifacts (e.g., sign flips), revealing that conventional cosine drift overestimates true change by 36.43 rad due solely to sign-flip events in PCA trajectories.

## Executive Summary
Conventional drift metrics like Euclidean and cosine distance conflate genuine shifts in representation with benign parametrization artifacts such as sign flips or scaling. This paper introduces the Fubini-Study metric, operating on real projective space, which identifies vectors that differ only by nonzero scalar multiples. Applied to trajectories of principal component directions from sliding windows of a high-dimensional dataset, the Fubini-Study drift (14.72 rad) was substantially lower than cumulative cosine drift (51.15 rad). The difference (36.43 rad) statistically corresponded to 17 sign-flip events, demonstrating that much of the apparent drift was gauge-induced noise. Euclidean drift grew even faster due to sensitivity to magnitude changes. The Fubini-Study metric thus provides a geometrically principled way to isolate meaningful representation evolution.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze representation drift by extracting the first principal component (PC1) from each sliding window of a high-dimensional dataset (scikit-learn digits). For each consecutive pair of unaligned PC1 vectors, they compute three stepwise distances: Euclidean (ℓ₂ norm of difference), cosine (arccos of inner product), and Fubini-Study (arccos of absolute inner product). These are summed along the trajectory to yield cumulative drift curves. Sign-flip events are detected when the raw inner product between consecutive PCs is negative. The cumulative gap between cosine and Fubini-Study drift is interpreted as direct measurement of gauge-induced churn, since the metrics diverge only at sign-flip steps.

## Key Results
- Cumulative cosine drift (51.15 rad) substantially exceeded Fubini-Study drift (14.72 rad), a difference of 36.43 rad.
- The gap exactly matched the contribution from 17 detected sign-flip events (each contributing ~π), confirmed by a sign test (p < 0.001).
- Euclidean drift increased even more rapidly than cosine drift, highlighting its sensitivity to magnitude variations beyond pure directional change.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
The Fubini-Study metric computes distance on the real projective space ℝℙ<sup>D-1</sup>, where vectors differing by any nonzero scalar (including sign) are identified. Its formula uses the absolute value of the inner product: *d<sub>FS</sub> = arccos(|⟨r̂<sub>k</sub>, r̂<sub>k+1</sub>⟩|)*. This enforces antipodal equivalence, so sign flips contribute zero distance. Thus, it isolates intrinsic orientation changes from gauge fluctuations by mathematical construction, whereas cosine distance treats sign flips as a maximal change of π.

### Mechanism 2
The cumulative difference Δ<sub>C</sub>(n) − Δ<sub>FS</sub>(n) provides a direct, computable tally of gauge-induced churn. Since the two stepwise distances are identical except when ⟨r<sub>k</sub>, r<sub>k+1</sub>⟩ < 0 (where cosine adds ~π and Fubini-Study adds 0), their accumulated divergence increases monotonically by π for each sign-flip event. This operationalizes the abstract gauge freedom into a measurable quantity.

### Mechanism 3
Euclidean drift’s orders-of-magnitude faster growth compared to Fubini-Study arises because Euclidean distance depends on both direction and vector magnitude. Any non-stationarity in the PC1 norms (e.g., from changing window variances) or other scaling variations contributes to Euclidean distance even when the projective orientation is stable. The logarithmic ratio log<sub>10</sub>[(ΔE+ε)/(ΔFS+ε)] increasing steadily demonstrates this conflation of parametrization noise with intrinsic drift.

## Foundational Learning
- **Projective Geometry (Real Projective Space ℝℙ<sup>D-1</sup>)**  
  *Why needed:* PCA directions are defined only up to scale and sign; the correct space to compare them is one that identifies all nonzero scalar multiples.  
  *Quick check:* If **v** and **−v** represent the same statistical direction, the appropriate space is ℝℙ<sup>D-1</sup>, and the distance between them is 0.

- **Gauge Freedom / Representation Non-Identifiability**  
  *Why needed:* The drift artifact stems from a symmetry: multiple numerical vectors encode the same underlying concept (e.g., PC1 sign ambiguity). Recognizing this symmetry is essential to separate real change from coordinate noise.  
  *Quick check:* In PCA of centered data, if the first PC direction is **v**, then −**v** represents the exact same principal component direction.

- **Cumulative Drift as Path Integral**  
  *Why needed:* The paper compares total change along an ordered trajectory of representations; this is the sum (path length) of stepwise distances in a chosen geometry. Different geometries yield different lengths for the same sequence.  
  *Quick check:* Given representations r₁, r₂, r₃, cumulative drift from r₁ to r₃ using metric *d* is *d(r₁,r₂) + d(r₂,r₃)*.

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Loader -> Windowing Engine -> Representation Extractor -> Distance Module -> Accumulator -> Event Logger -> Visualizer

**Critical path:** Load data -> generate sliding windows -> compute PCA per window to extract raw PC1 -> compute stepwise Euclidean, cosine, Fubini-Study distances -> accumulate -> plot. PCA per window is the heaviest computation. The logic requires preserving raw, unaligned PCA signs across windows.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Invariance vs. Information:* Fubini-Study removes all sign/scale noise but discards magnitude information retained by Euclidean. Choose based on whether gauge freedom is a known confounder.  
- *Computational cost:* Sliding-window PCA is O(N·W·min(W,D)²). For large data, consider incremental PCA or random projections.  
- *Event detection sensitivity:* The sign-flip diagnostic relies on unaligned PCA signs. Any external sign alignment (e.g., making largest component positive) invalidates the measurement.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Euclidean drift does not grow significantly faster than Fubini-Study: suggests window sizes too small or data already scale-normalized.  
2. No sign-flips detected: ΔC and ΔFS trajectories coincide; could indicate overly smooth windows or accidental sign alignment.  
3. ΔC − ΔFS decreases: indicates accumulation bug or unhandled sign-flip reversal; should be non-decreasing.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Synthetic sign-flip validation: create a 2D trajectory with gradual rotation and periodic sign flips; verify ΔC−ΔFS increases by π at each flip.  
2. Digit dataset replication: use scikit-learn digits with assumed W=200, s=50; reproduce cumulative drift curves and stepwise dot product sign.  
3. Window parameter sensitivity: vary W and s; observe how trajectory smoothness and sign-flip frequency affect ΔC−ΔFS total.

## Limitations
- Unknown exact window parameters (size, step) and shuffling used, preventing precise replication of the 17 sign-flip count and cumulative values.
- Validation is limited to a single dataset (digits) with PCA-extracted PC1; generalizability to other data, representations, or dimensionality reduction methods is unclear.
- Analysis assumes sign flips are the sole gauge artifact; other symmetries (e.g., rotations within null spaces) could contribute to the cosine-Fubini-Study gap but are not examined.

## Confidence
- *Fubini-Study isolates intrinsic drift:* **Medium** (theoretically sound but empirically limited to one dataset).
- *Cosine-FS gap measures sign-flip churn:* **Medium** (statistically supported but may miss non-sign gauge effects).
- *Euclidean drift overestimates:* **High** (mathematically evident and observed across contexts).
