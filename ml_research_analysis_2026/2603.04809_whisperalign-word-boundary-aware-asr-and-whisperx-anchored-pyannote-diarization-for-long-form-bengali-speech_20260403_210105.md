# WhisperAlign: Word-Boundary-Aware ASR and WhisperX-Anchored Pyannote Diarization for Long-Form Bengali Speech

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04809
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04809
- **Reference count:** 3
- **Primary result:** Reduced ASR WER from ~0.68 to ~0.25 (public/private) and achieved diarization DER of 24-28%, far surpassing baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles long-form Bengali speech recognition and speaker diarization in a low-resource setting. For ASR, the authors introduce a word-boundary-aware chunking strategy that leverages whisper-timestamped and Silero VAD to create context-preserving segments without truncating words, which are then used to fine‑tune a Bengali Whisper model. For diarization, they fine‑tune Pyannote’s segmentation module on Bengali data and combine its output with WhisperX’s Silero VAD through a logical AND intersection to ensure strict temporal alignment. Both pipelines are computationally efficient, processing hour‑long audio in minutes while achieving substantial improvements over strong baselines.

## Method Summary
The ASR pipeline first strips non‑speech regions via Silero VAD, splits the audio into 28‑second windows, and runs `whisper-timestamped` on a Bengali Whisper checkpoint to obtain per‑word timestamps. These timestamps are transferred to the ground‑truth paragraph text using sequence matching, and the audio is greedily partitioned into 20–28‑second chunks that respect word boundaries. The resulting chunks fine‑tune a Bengali Whisper medium model (5 epochs, AdamW, LR=1e-5). At inference, Silero VAD cleans the audio, which is chunked with 1‑second overlap; the fine‑tuned model transcribes each chunk with `condition_on_previous_text=False`, followed by repetition and blacklist post‑processing. The diarization pipeline freezes the ResNet34 speaker embedding extractor and fine‑tunes only Pyannote’s `segmentation-3.0` head (~1.4M parameters) on Bengali speaker annotations. Inference begins with a Silero VAD mask, runs the Pyannote `speaker-diarization-community-1` pipeline with the fine‑tuned segmentation, applies `exclusive_speaker_diarization`, adaptive merging, and transient filtering, and finally intersects the segments with the initial Silero VAD mask to discard out‑of‑speech hallucinations.

## Key Results
- ASR: Public WER reduced from 0.675 to 0.252; Private WER reduced from 0.702 to 0.278.
- Diarization: Estimated DER of 24–28%, improving upon the Bengali‑Loop baseline of 40.08%.
- Efficiency: Both pipelines process hour‑long audio in minutes on modest hardware.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Word‑Boundary‑Aware Chunking
**Claim:** Word‑boundary‑aware chunking reduces ASR hallucinations and context loss in long‑form Bengali speech by ensuring segments never truncate words and respect Whisper’s decoding window.  
**Mechanism:** (1) Silero VAD removes non‑speech; (2) `whisper‑timestamped` extracts per‑word timestamps from a Bengali Whisper checkpoint via cross‑attention heads; (3) ground‑truth words are aligned to these timestamps via `difflib.SequenceMatcher`, borrowing timestamps for correct/mis‑transcribed words and interpolating for missing ones; (4) audio is greedily partitioned into 20–28 second segments strictly on word boundaries.  
**Core assumption:** Whisper’s internal cross‑attention timestamps are accurate enough for boundary alignment when mapped to ground‑truth spelling via sequence matching.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “robust audio chunking strategy utilizing whisper‑timestamped”; Section II.A describes timestamp extraction and sequence matching; Table II shows the largest WER reduction from the fine‑tuned model with this chunking.  
**Break condition:** If `whisper‑timestamped` fails to produce reliable per‑word timestamps (e.g., due to poor calibration or ambiguous Bengali phonetics), the alignment propagates errors, potentially corrupting the training dataset.

### Mechanism 2: Parameter‑Efficient Pyannote Fine‑Tuning
**Claim:** Fine‑tuning only Pyannote’s segmentation head on Bengali data adapts the model to language‑specific prosody and turn‑taking, reducing diarization error without full retraining.  
**Mechanism:** The frozen `wespeaker‑voxceleb‑resnet34‑LM` embeddings provide robust acoustic features; only the lightweight `segmentation‑3.0` head (~1.4M parameters) is trained on Bengali audio with precise speaker boundaries for ~1 hour on a T4 GPU.  
**Core assumption:** Bengali conversational prosody differs sufficiently from Pyannote’s pretraining data, and a small parameter update can capture this shift without catastrophic forgetting.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “domain‑specific fine‑tuning of the Pyannote segmentation model… capture the nuances of Bengali conversational dynamics”; Section VII.A notes isolation of the segmentation head and fast convergence; Table IV shows DER drop from 0.314 to 0.200 after fine‑tuning.  
**Break condition:** If the Bengali fine‑tuning data is too small or not representative (e.g., lacks diverse speakers/overlaps), the segmentation head may overfit or fail to generalize, yielding marginal or negative gains.

### Mechanism 3: WhisperX VAD Intersection
**Claim:** Logical AND intersection between Pyannote’s diarization output and WhisperX’s Silero VAD boundaries enforces temporal consistency, eliminating boundary hallucinations and ensuring speaker tracks align with actual speech regions.  
**Mechanism:** Pyannote produces speaker‑annotated segments independently; WhisperX’s Silero VAD generates a binary speech mask. A pixel‑wise logical AND masks any Pyannote segment that extends outside the Silero speech mask, forcing diarization boundaries to coincide strictly with speech regions identified by the ASR pipeline’s VAD.  
**Core assumption:** Silero VAD is a more reliable indicator of true speech activity for Bengali audio than Pyannote’s internal VAD, and the intersection does not discard valid overlapping speech segments that Silero erroneously filters.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “integration of the WhisperX (Silero) VAD intersection was the definitive factor that produced our highest overall score”; Section VII.B details the “Dual‑VAD Intersection” and its effect; Section IX explicitly credits the intersection for final DER gains.  
**Break condition:** If Silero VAD has high false‑negative rates in Bengali (e.g., missing low‑energy phonemes or overlaps), the AND intersection will clip valid speech segments from Pyannote’s output, harming recall.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Whisper’s Encoder‑Decoder Architecture & Limitations**  
  **Why needed:** Whisper’s 30‑second maximum input and susceptibility to hallucinations on non‑speech regions directly motivate the chunking strategy and VAD pre‑filtering.  
  **Quick check:** What architectural constraint of Whisper necessitates segmenting long audio, and what failure mode does VAD aim to prevent?

- **Concept: Voice Activity Detection (VAD)**  
  **Why needed:** Silero VAD is used twice: first to clean audio for chunking, and second as the ground‑truth mask for the VAD intersection step. Understanding its role in speech/non‑speech discrimination is critical.  
  **Quick check:** Why use WhisperX’s Silero VAD specifically for the intersection rather than Pyannote’s native VAD?

- **Concept: Diarization Error Rate (DER) Components**  
  **Why needed:** The competition requires exclusive speaker tracks (no overlaps). Understanding that DER = Speaker Error + Missed Speech + False Alarm, and how `exclusive_speaker_diarization` modifies clustering outputs, is key to interpreting results.  
  **Quick check:** How does `exclusive_speaker_diarization` attempt to resolve overlapping speech, and what is a potential side effect?

- **Concept: Parameter‑Efficient Fine‑Tuning**  
  **Why needed:** The paper highlights fine‑tuning only the segmentation head (not the entire Pyannote model) as a cost‑effective adaptation strategy. This requires understanding the separation between feature extractor (frozen) and task‑specific head (trained).  
  **Quick check:** In the Pyannote pipeline, which components are typically frozen vs. fine‑tuned in this approach, and why?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
ASR: Input Audio → Silero VAD → 28s windowing → `whisper‑timestamped` (Bengali checkpoint) → word‑timestamp extraction → `difflib` alignment to ground‑truth words → greedy chunk partitioning into 20–28s segments → filtered chunks → fine‑tuned Bengali Whisper (medium).  
Diarization: Input Audio → Silero VAD (asymmetric thresholds) → `speaker‑diarization‑community‑1` with fine‑tuned `segmentation‑3.0` → `exclusive_speaker_diarization` → adaptive merging & transient filtering → logical AND with initial Silero VAD mask → final RTTM.

**Critical path:**  
- ASR: The chunking‑and‑align data preparation stage is the prerequisite for meaningful fine‑tuning. Without valid word‑boundary segments, fine‑tuning amplifies errors.  
- Diarization: Fine‑tuning the segmentation module must precede the VAD intersection step; the intersection only works if both streams are already reasonably accurate.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Chunk duration:** 20–28s balances context preservation (longer) against Whisper’s decoding limit and gradient stability (shorter). Shorter chunks (<20s) were discarded due to degraded model performance.  
- **Fine‑tuning scope:** Updating only the segmentation head (1.4M params) vs. full model. Saves compute and prevents catastrophic forgetting but limits adaptation capacity.  
- **Exclusive diarization:** Solves strict non‑overlap requirement natively but may arbitrarily assign overlapping speech to one speaker, harming speaker recall in dense overlaps.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **ASR:** Public WER plateauing >0.3 after fine‑tuning suggests chunking boundary errors or insufficient/biased fine‑tuning data.  
- **Diarization:** Public DER >0.3 after fine‑tuning but before VAD intersection indicates poor Bengali adaptation; if DER *increases* after VAD intersection, Silero is likely over‑filtering speech.  
- **System:** Temporal mismatch between ASR transcripts and diarization labels in final output indicates failed VAD intersection logic.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Validate chunking pipeline:** Run the full data preparation (Section III.B) on a single ~1‑hour training file. Visually inspect (via waveform plots) that all chunk boundaries fall in silence regions and that aligned word timestamps match the audio. Compute chunk duration distribution.  
2. **Ablate VAD intersection:** Train the Pyannote segmentation model, then run inference with and without the final Silero VAD AND mask on a small validation set. Measure boundary‑level mismatches (e.g., segments starting/ending in silence) and DER delta.  
3. **Test `exclusive_speaker_diarization`:** Compare DER with `exclusive_mode=True` vs. `False` on a short multi‑speaker sample with known overlaps. Quantify change in speaker error vs. missed speech.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does the accuracy of the word‑boundary alignment (via sequence matching and interpolation) affect downstream ASR performance under high transcription error or overlapping speech?**  
   *Basis:* The chunking pipeline relies on Whisper’s timestamps aligned to ground truth; alignment failures from Whisper errors could misalign training segments, but this is not evaluated.  
   *Why unresolved:* The paper does not quantify alignment errors or correlate them with WER improvements from fine‑tuning.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Compute alignment error rates against ground‑truth boundaries on varied audio (e.g., synthetic overlaps), then measure fine‑tuned model WER on resulting chunked datasets.

2. **What is the optimal minimum chunk duration for training Bengali Whisper models, and can adaptive chunking based on semantic or acoustic density improve over the fixed 20–28 second range?**  
   *Basis:* The 20‑second cutoff is empirically chosen without ablation; adaptive schemes might better preserve context for varying content.  
   *Why unresolved:* The 20‑second threshold is not systematically optimized.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Train models on datasets with varying minimum durations (e.g., 15s, 18s, 20s, 25s) and compare WER; also test adaptive partitioning using pause detection or spectral features.

3. **To what extent does the WhisperX (Silero) VAD intersection reduce diarization DER compared to Pyannote’s native VAD, particularly under high speaker overlap or Bengali‑specific phonetics?**  
   *Basis:* The paper credits VAD intersection for final DER gains but does not isolate its contribution or analyze performance across overlap conditions.  
   *Why unresolved:* No controlled experiments comparing intersection vs. no intersection, especially on overlap‑heavy segments.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Run diarization with Pyannote’s internal VAD and with Silero VAD intersection on test sets stratified by overlap ratio and speaker count, measuring DER differences.

4. **What specific error types (e.g., speaker confusion, missed speech) dominate the remaining 24–28% DER in the Bengali diarization system, and how do they relate to overlap handling?**  
   *Basis:* Overall DER is reported but not decomposed; `exclusive_speaker_diarization` may introduce speaker confusion during overlaps.  
   *Why unresolved:* Without error analysis, it is unclear whether further gains require better VAD, clustering, or overlap resolution.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Apply `pyannote.metrics` to decompose DER into miss, false alarm, and speaker error rates, correlated with ground‑truth overlap durations and speaker counts.

## Limitations
- **Dataset access:** The exact 113‑hour competition audio and diarization annotations are not publicly released, blocking faithful reproduction.
- **Hyperparameters:** Critical post‑processing details (n‑gram repetition detector, string blacklist rules, adaptive merging function) are unspecified.
- **Evaluation:** The reported DER range (24–28%) lacks a single definitive value and unclear scoring protocol (collar, overlap handling).

## Confidence
- **ASR chunking mechanism:** High confidence (supported by ablation in Table II and clear pipeline description).
- **Pyannote fine‑tuning:** Medium confidence (DER improvement shown but dependent on limited fine‑tuning data representativeness).
- **VAD intersection:** Medium‑Low confidence (claimed as “definitive” but no direct ablation isolating contribution; relies on Silero VAD reliability for Bengali).
