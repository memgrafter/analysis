# Reading or Reasoning? Format Decoupled Reinforcement Learning for Document OCR

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.08834
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08834
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: FD-RL achieves a state-of-the-art overall score of 90.41 on OmniDocBench, with formula recognition CDM 88.67 and table parsing TEDS 87.35.

## Executive Summary
Document OCR models face particular difficulty with format-intensive content such as mathematical formulas and tables, where they exhibit high output entropy, signifying uncertainty and diverse reasoning pathways. This work introduces Format Decoupled Reinforcement Learning (FD-RL), a two-stage training pipeline combining supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with reinforcement learning (RL). A key innovation is the use of entropy-based data filtration to prioritize challenging samples, coupled with format-specific reward functions tailored for plain text, formulas, and tables. Experiments on OmniDocBench show that FD-RL sets a new record with an overall score of 90.41, notably improving formula and table recognition. Ablations confirm the contribution of each component, demonstrating the effectiveness of the decoupled reward design and the entropy-driven selection strategy.

## Method Summary
FD-RL addresses the uncertainty in format-rich documents by first measuring output entropy on candidate samples and filtering for those with high entropy, which are presumed to be more challenging. The model is initially trained via supervised fine-tuning on this curated dataset. In the second stage, reinforcement learning fine-tunes the model using rewards computed separately for each document format: exact string match for plain text, LaTeX expression correctness for formulas, and structural coherence for tables. This decoupled reward structure allows the model to optimize for the specific criteria that matter most for each content type. The approach leverages multiple data sources and a two-stage paradigm to achieve robust performance across diverse document elements.

## Key Results
- SOTA overall score of 90.41 on OmniDocBench, surpassing prior end-to-end models.
- Formula recognition (CDM) reaches 88.67, and table parsing (TEDS) reaches 87.35.
- Ablation studies validate the necessity of multi-source data, the two-stage training, entropy-based filtration, and the decoupled rewards.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
High entropy on format-intensive content indicates that the model explores multiple plausible outputs, suggesting a lack of clear supervision. By filtering for high-entropy samples, training focuses on the most ambiguous and complex examples, forcing the model to refine its internal representations. The decoupled reward design ensures that each format is evaluated by its own appropriate metric—string match for plain text, symbolic equivalence for formulas, and structural metrics for tables—preventing one format from dominating the optimization. The two-stage setup first establishes a solid supervised foundation, which stabilizes RL training that would otherwise be noisy when optimizing for non-differentiable rewards. Together, these elements guide the model to develop specialized reasoning pathways for each format, leading to significant gains where traditional OCR models struggle.

## Foundational Learning
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Establishes baseline proficiency on the OCR task. *Why needed*: Provides a stable initialization before RL, avoiding catastrophic divergence. *Quick check*: What risks arise if RL is applied without SFT?
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Optimizes model outputs w.r.t. non-differentiable rewards derived from format-specific metrics. *Why needed*: Enables direct optimization for end goals (e.g., table structure) that cross-entropy cannot capture. *Quick check*: How does an RL policy gradient update differ from standard backpropagation?
- **Entropy-Based Data Selection**: Selects samples with high predictive uncertainty. *Why needed*: Prioritizes training on hard examples that are most likely to improve model robustness. *Quick check*: Does high entropy always correspond to human-perceived difficulty?
- **Format-Specific Reward Design**: Distinct reward functions for plain text (string match), formulas (expression correctness), and tables (structural coherence). *Why needed*: Aligns training signal with the unique correctness criteria of each format, avoiding reward misalignment. *Quick check*: Why might a single unified reward fail for mixed-format documents?
- **Two-Stage Training Paradigm**: Sequential SFT then RL. *Why needed*: Separates the learning of general token-level patterns from the refinement of format-specific optimization. *Quick check*: Could the stages be trained jointly? What are the trade-offs?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Raw Documents -> Entropy Filter (select challenging samples) -> SFT Trainer -> RL Fine-Tuner (with Decoupled Reward Modules: Text Reward, Formula Reward, Table Reward) -> Final OCR Model

**Critical path**: The training pipeline begins with collecting a diverse corpus. Entropy scores are computed on a preliminary model; top-entropy samples are retained. The SFT stage trains the model on these samples with standard cross-entropy. The RL stage then updates the policy using format-specific rewards, requiring repeated inference and reward calculation. Evaluation is performed on OmniDocBench.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Decoupled rewards increase implementation complexity but yield better per-format performance; however, balancing reward magnitudes may be tricky.  
- Entropy filtration reduces training data volume, which can accelerate training but risks losing useful examples.  
- Two-stage training demands more computational resources and careful hyperparameter tuning between stages.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Model overfits to superficial patterns in one reward (e.g., exact string match) while neglecting semantic correctness in others.  
- Poor generalization to layouts or formats not represented in the high-entropy training subset.  
- Instability during RL if the SFT foundation is weak or reward weights are mis-scaled.

**First experiments**:  
1. Ablate the entropy filter (train on random subset) and compare overall and format-specific scores.  
2. Replace decoupled rewards with a uniform weighted sum and evaluate degradation per format.  
3. Test the final model on a different benchmark (e.g., PubTabNet) to measure cross-dataset generalization.

## Limitations
- External Generalization: Results are confined to OmniDocBench; transferability to other document types (historical manuscripts, non-Latin scripts, noisy scans) remains uncertain.
- Reward Design Specificity: The format-specific reward heuristics may overfit to OmniDocBench conventions rather than capturing true semantic understanding.
- Entropy Heuristic Scope: The correlation between entropy and human-interpretable reasoning complexity is unvalidated; high entropy could also stem from noise or font variation.

## Confidence
- FD-RL achieves SOTA on OmniDocBench (90.41): Medium  
- Two-stage SFT+RL with decoupled rewards is effective: Medium-High  
- High output entropy indicates diverse reasoning pathways for format-intensive content: Low-Medium  
- Gains in formulas (CDM 88.67) and tables (TEDS 87.35): High  

## Next Checks
1. Cross-benchmark replication: Evaluate FD-RL on at least two additional document OCR/parsing benchmarks (e.g., PubTabNet, TableBank) to test generalization.
2. Reward ablation with human evaluation: Remove each format-specific reward and measure impact via human judgment on output plausibility, especially for formula and table structural correctness.
3. Deployment stress test: Apply the model to a challenging, out-of-distribution corpus (e.g., multi-column academic papers with integrated figures, low-quality historical documents) and analyze failure modes to determine if entropy-based training improves robustness or merely adapts to OmniDocBench.
