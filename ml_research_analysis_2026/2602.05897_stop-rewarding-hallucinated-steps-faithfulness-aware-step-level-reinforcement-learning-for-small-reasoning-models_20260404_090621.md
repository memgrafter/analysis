# Stop Rewarding Hallucinated Steps: Faithfulness-Aware Step-Level Reinforcement Learning for Small Reasoning Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05897
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05897
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: FaithRL reduces faithfulness hallucinations in small reasoning models by 3.86% in F1 and 3.48% in judge-rated faithfulness on average across open-book QA benchmarks, while also reducing CoT hallucination rates and showing stronger robustness under adversarial attack.

## Executive Summary
Small reasoning models (SRMs) often produce chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning with hallucinations—incorrect intermediate steps that can still lead to correct final answers. This misleads standard reinforcement learning (RL) that only rewards final answer correctness. The paper introduces FaithRL, a faithfulness-aware step-level RL framework for SRMs. FaithRL combines explicit step-level faithfulness rewards from a process reward model (PRM) with an implicit dynamic truncated resampling (DTR) strategy that generates contrastive signals from faithful prefixes. Experiments on multiple SRMs and open-book QA benchmarks show that FaithRL reduces answer hallucinations by an average of 3.86% in F1 score and 3.48% in LLM-as-a-judge faithfulness compared to the best baseline, while also significantly lowering CoT hallucination rates and demonstrating improved robustness under adversarial attack.

## Method Summary
FaithRL operates within the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework. For each training prompt, the policy model generates multiple CoT rollouts. A pre-trained PRM (HHEM-2.1) evaluates each sentence for faithfulness to the context. If an unfaithful step is detected, the trajectory is truncated and a new rollout is sampled from the faithful prefix, implicitly forming a contrastive pair. When the final answer is correct, each token receives a reward composed of: (1) the PRM's binary faithfulness score for its sentence, (2) information gain penalties to suppress verbosity (based on sentence count and tokens per sentence thresholds), and (3) an n-gram repetition penalty. Incorrect answers receive a uniform negative reward. The GRPO update uses these token-level rewards relative to the group mean to fine-tune the SRM.

## Key Results
- FaithRL reduces answer hallucinations by an average of 3.86% in F1 score and 3.48% in LLM-as-a-judge faithfulness compared to the best baseline across five open-book QA benchmarks.
- It significantly lowers CoT hallucination rates and exhibits stronger robustness under adversarial attack, outperforming outcome-only GRPO by large margins.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both explicit PRM rewards and implicit DTR signals, as well as the information/repitition penalties for training stability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Dynamic Truncated Resampling (DTR) generates implicit step-level contrastive rewards by preserving faithful prefixes and penalizing divergent, unfaithful continuations.
- **Mechanism:** When a Process Reward Model (PRM) detects an unfaithful sentence at step *t*, the trajectory is truncated. The faithful prefix `{S₁,...,Sₜ₋₁}` is used to prompt a new rollout `{S₁,...,Sₜ₋₁,Sᵣₜ,...}`. The original (unfaithful suffix) and resampled (hopefully faithful) trajectories form a pair. Their total reward difference `(Rᵣ - Rₖ)` is implicitly distributed, assigning lower reward to tokens in the unfaithful suffix and higher reward to the resampled suffix.
- **Core assumption:** The faithful prefix is itself correct and the PRM’s detection of an unfaithful step is accurate enough to trigger useful resampling.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...implicit dynamic truncated resampling strategy that generates contrastive signals from faithful prefixes."
  - [section 4.1] "If an unfaithful step is detected at position *t*, the trajectory is truncated... A revised rollout is generated from *P'*... This tree-structured sampling naturally yields step-level contrastive signals."
  - [corpus] Mixed signal—neighboring papers discuss process supervision but not this specific DTR mechanism. **Assumption:**
- **Break condition:** If the PRM consistently misidentifies faithful steps as unfaithful (high false positive rate), DTR would repeatedly truncate correct reasoning, degrading performance by discarding valid prefixes.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Explicit step-level rewards from a PRM directly penalize faithfulness hallucinations at the sentence where they occur, preventing outcome rewards from reinforcing flawed CoT.
- **Mechanism:** Each generated CoT sentence is independently evaluated by a PRM (HHEM-2.1). A reward of `+1` (faithful) or `-1` (unfaithful) is assigned and propagated to all tokens within that sentence. When the final answer is correct, this step-level reward replaces a uniform answer reward, ensuring the model is credited only for faithful reasoning steps.
- **Core assumption:** The PRM (HHEM-2.1) is a sufficiently accurate and reliable judge of sentence-level faithfulness to the context.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "explicit faithfulness rewards from a process reward model."
  - [section 4.2] "We use HHEM-2.1 as PRM to verify the faithfulness of each sentence... For the *j*-th sentence... reward function is defined as: `R_fact = 1` if faithful, `-1` otherwise."
  - [section B.1 & Table 8] HHEM-2.1 was selected over Qwen3-8B for better overall performance on faithfulness tasks.
- **Break condition:** PRM calibration drift (e.g., on out-of-distribution contexts) where it frequently labels faithful sentences as unfaithful (`R_fact = -1`) would suppress correct reasoning, or vice versa (false negatives) would fail to penalize hallucinations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Information gain (`R_inf`) and repetition (`R_rep`) rewards mitigate "reward hacking" by discouraging verbosity and redundancy, ensuring the model optimizes for concise, informative reasoning rather than gaming the step-level faithfulness signal.
- **Mechanism:** `R_inf` combines sentence-count (`R_sent`) and token-per-sentence (`R_tok`) penalties beyond thresholds `(L_s, L_t)`. `R_rep` penalizes low unique n-gram ratios. These penalties are added to the faithful step reward (`R_fact`) when the answer is correct, discouraging the model from inflating token counts or repeating statements to pad "correct" steps.
- **Core assumption:** Verbosity and repetition are strong proxies for uninformative or hallucinatory reasoning in CoT, and penalizing them aligns the model toward denser, more faithful reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Not explicitly mentioned.
  - [section 4.2] "To penalize this verbosity without value, we propose an information gain reward... By implementing a dual constraint on both sentence count and tokens per sentence, we effectively suppress redundant CoT expansions... Following Yeo et al. (2025), we introduce an n-gram repetition penalty `R_rep`."
  - [section 5.3 & Table 4] Ablation shows removing `R_sent` or `R_tok` causes severe Acc. drops (21.35% and 42.40% respectively), and removing `R_rep` decreases average Faith by 2.57%. Omitting `R_inf` caused training collapse due to excessive length.
- **Break condition:** Overly aggressive penalty thresholds (`L_s`, `L_t`) or `λ` values could force the model to generate overly terse CoTs that omit necessary reasoning steps, harming accuracy on complex multi-hop questions.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning
- **Why needed here:** FaithRL operates on and modifies the CoT generation process. Understanding that CoT provides an explicit, sequential "reasoning trace" distinct from the final answer is fundamental. The core problem is that hallucinations can occur *within* these intermediate steps while the final answer remains correct.
- **Quick check question:** What is the key danger of using only the final answer as a reward signal when a model uses CoT reasoning?

### Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Human/Verifier Feedback (RLHF/RLVR) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
- **Why needed here:** FaithRL is an RL algorithm built upon the GRPO framework. GRPO generates multiple rollouts per prompt and normalizes rewards relative to the group mean. FaithRL modifies the reward computation to be step-level and faithfulness-aware, but the underlying policy gradient update follows the GRPO paradigm.
- **Quick check question:** How does GRPO's use of a group-relative baseline differ from a standard REINFORCE-style update, and why is this useful for reducing variance in RL for LLMs?

### Concept: Process Reward Models (PRMs)
- **Why needed here:** The explicit reward component of FaithRL depends on a PRM to score the faithfulness of each CoT sentence. A PRM is a separate model trained or prompted to judge the quality/correctness of intermediate reasoning steps, distinct from an outcome reward model that judges only the final answer.
- **Quick check question:** What is the primary difference in training data and objective between a PRM and an outcome reward model (ORM)?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
1.  **Policy Model (SRM):** The Small Reasoning Model being fine-tuned (e.g., DPSK-1.5B, Qwen3-0.6B). Generates CoT `S = {S₁,...,Sₙ}` and answer `A`.
2.  **Process Reward Model (PRM):** A separate, fixed model (HHEM-2.1) used as a judge. Takes context `C` and a CoT sentence `Sⱼ`, outputs a binary faithfulness verdict.
3.  **Dynamic Truncation & Resampling Module:** Orchestrates the implicit reward strategy. For each rollout:
    a.  Run PRM on each sentence.
    b.  If an unfaithful sentence at `t` is found, truncate the trajectory at `t-1`.
    c.  Form new prompt `P' = P + {S₁,...,Sₜ₋₁}`.
    d.  Request a fresh rollout from `P'` (resampled continuation).
    e.  Pair original and resampled trajectories.
4.  **Reward Calculator:** For each token in each trajectory:
    a.  If answer `A` is incorrect (`R_answer = -1`), assign `-1` to all tokens.
    b.  If answer `A` is correct (`R_answer = +1`), calculate sentence-level reward `R_cot = R_fact + R_inf + R_rep` for the containing sentence, and assign this to all tokens in that sentence. For answer tokens, assign `R_answer`.
5.  **GRPO Optimizer:** Takes the batch of paired/grouped trajectories and their token-level rewards. Computes advantages relative to the group mean and updates the policy model via the standard GRPO loss (no KL penalty mentioned in this paper's training details).

### Critical path
1.  **Input:** `(Context C, Question Q)`
2.  **Generation:** Policy model generates initial CoT `S` and answer `A`.
3.  **Verification & Resampling:** PRM checks `S`. If unfaithful step found, trajectory is truncated and prefix used for resampling, generating a paired trajectory `S'`.
4.  **Reward Assignment:** `R_answer` checks correctness. If correct, `R_fact`, `R_inf`, `R_rep` are computed per sentence in `S` (and `S'` for the resampled suffix). Token rewards are set accordingly.
5.  **Optimization:** GRPO loss computed on the group of trajectories (original, resampled, and potentially other rollouts) using the token rewards. Policy gradients applied to update the SRM.
6.  **Iteration:** Repeat for next batch.

### Design tradeoffs
*   **PRM Choice (HHEM-2.1 vs. LLM-as-judge):** HHEM-2.1 is a dedicated, smaller model likely faster and cheaper than a large LLM judge, but its accuracy is fixed. Using a more powerful LLM (e.g., Qwen3-32B as in analysis) might be more accurate but prohibitively expensive for training-time scoring of thousands of sentences. (See Table 8 comparison).
*   **DTR vs. Full Fixed Budget (Random Truncation):** Table 5 shows `Resample+Full` (only resample when needed, then fill budget with fresh rollouts) works best. Pure `Random` truncation harms performance by introducing noise. `Resample` only (no budget filling) uses fewer samples but may be less stable. This balances efficiency and signal quality.
*   **Step-Level Granularity (Sentence vs. Token):** Sentence-level is computationally feasible and aligns with human-interpretable reasoning steps. Finer-grained token-level PRM scoring would be more precise but vastly more expensive and might not correlate well with logical unit boundaries.

### Failure signatures
*   **Training Collapse / Excessive Verbosity:** If `R_inf` (both `R_sent` and `R_tok`) is removed or thresholds `L_s, L_t` are set too high, the model generates extremely long, repetitive CoTs to pad "correct" steps, making training unstable or diverging (Table 4 ablation).
*   **Stagnant or Decreasing Faithfulness:** If the PRM is weak (e.g., using Qwen3-8B instead of HHEM-2.1 on some models, Table 8) or if `R_rep` is removed, the model may still find ways to generate unfaithful but reward-hacked CoTs (repetitive but "correct" sentences), limiting gains in Faithful Rate.
*   **Poor Performance on Complex QA:** If the DTR resampling budget (`Full` vs. `Random`) is misconfigured, or if the PRM's false positive rate is high on the target dataset, the model can be penalized for correct prefixes, harming performance on benchmarks requiring deep reasoning (TriviaQA/HotpotQA drop in Table 5 w/o `Resample`).

### First 3 experiments
1.  **Ablation of Reward Components (Table 4):** Systematically remove `Resample`, `R_rep`, `R_sent`, `R_tok` to measure their individual contribution to both Faith and Acc. This isolates which parts of the mechanism are essential for stability and performance.
2.  **Resampling Strategy Comparison (Table 5):** Test `Resample`, `Random`, and `Full` configurations. This experiments with the *implicit reward* generation mechanism, validating that contrastive signals from *faithful* prefixes (not random ones) are crucial.
3.  **Adversarial Attack Evaluation (Table 6):** Evaluate models on questions constructed from *hallucinated* CoT steps of the base model. This tests whether the training has genuinely reduced the *harmfulness* of hallucinations. A robust model should show a smaller performance drop on the attack set compared to the original test set. FaithRL aims to show larger relative improvement over GRPO here.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How well does FaithRL generalize to non-QA reasoning tasks, such as mathematical problem-solving or logical deduction, where hallucinations may manifest differently?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The evaluation is restricted to open-book QA benchmarks; no results are reported on tasks like GSM8K or ProofWriter, leaving generalizability unexplored.
- **Why unresolved:** Task-specific reasoning structures (e.g., symbolic manipulation in math) might require tailored step supervision or interact differently with DTR and PRM-based rewards.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate experiments on diverse reasoning benchmarks, measuring task accuracy and faithfulness metrics, and analyze how CoT hallucination patterns change across domains.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How sensitive is FaithRL to the accuracy and bias of the Process Reward Model (PRM), and can the method tolerate PRM errors without performance degradation?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] FaithRL assumes HHEM-2.1 is a reliable PRM but does not test robustness to PRM mistakes or evaluate alternative PRMs beyond a brief comparison in Appendix B.1.
- **Why unresolved:** PRM errors could misguide step-level rewards, either punishing valid reasoning or rewarding flawed steps, especially since DTR relies on PRM decisions for truncation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Inject synthetic noise into PRM labels or use ensembles of PRMs with known error profiles; measure changes in FaithRL's faithfulness and accuracy metrics to quantify robustness.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the optimal hyperparameter settings for the information gain reward (Ls, Lt, λs, λt) across different SRMs and datasets, and how do they affect the trade-off between conciseness and completeness?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses fixed thresholds without analysis; ablations remove components entirely but do not explore value variations, as seen in Section 4.2 and Table 4.
- **Why unresolved:** Hyperparameters directly control verbosity penalties; inappropriate values may over-truncate necessary reasoning or fail to curb redundant hallucinations, impacting training stability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform systematic hyperparameter sweeps on validation sets for each SRM, report performance landscapes, and derive heuristic rules (e.g., scaling with model size) for setting them.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the implicit step-level rewards from dynamic truncated resampling (DTR) be theoretically analyzed to understand its advantages over explicit rewards, and how does it interact with the PRM's quality?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] DTR is motivated intuitively as yielding contrastive signals, but no formal RL framework or comparison to other implicit methods (e.g., self-play) is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical insights could clarify why DTR outperforms full resampling (Table 5) and justify its design, potentially improving efficiency and robustness in broader settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Model DTR as a Markov Decision Process with biased rewards, derive expected reward bounds, and empirically compare sample complexity and convergence with explicit step-reward baselines under varying PRM accuracies.

## Limitations

- **Hyperparameter Specification:** Critical values for information gain penalties (`λ_s`, `λ_t`, `L_s`, `L_t`) and repetition penalty (`λ_rep`) are unreported, blocking exact replication and requiring manual tuning.
- **Sentence Segmentation:** The method for splitting CoT into discrete steps (sentences) is unspecified, introducing variance in step-level reward assignment and DTR trigger points.
- **PRM Generalization:** FaithRL's performance hinges on HHEM-2.1's accuracy. Its calibration on out-of-distribution contexts or different QA domains is unknown, risking false positives/negatives that degrade training.

## Confidence

- **Mechanism 1 (DTR Contrastive Signals):** Medium. The algorithmic description is clear, but its efficacy is entirely conditional on the PRM's precision in detecting unfaithful steps.
- **Mechanism 2 (Explicit PRM Rewards):** High. The use of a fixed PRM (HHEM-2.1) for step-level scoring is well-defined and empirically validated via ablation, though dependent on the PRM's inherent reliability.
- **Mechanism 3 (Information/Repetition Penalties):** Medium. Ablation proves their necessity, but the specific thresholds and coefficients are unknown, making the mechanism's stable application uncertain.
- **Overall Claim (FaithRL Reduces Hallucinations):** Medium-High. Supported by multi-benchmark results and ablations, but the unreported hyperparameters and PRM dependency leave room for performance variance in independent runs.
