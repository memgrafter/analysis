# Interpretable PM2.5 Forecasting for Urban Air Quality: A Comparative Study of Operational Time-Series Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25495
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25495
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Under weekly walk-forward refitting, Facebook Prophet offers the best accuracy–speed trade-off (MAE 37.61, 15 min runtime); with frozen models and online residual correction, SARIMAX achieves the lowest MAE (32.50) while corrected Prophet nears its walk-forward accuracy in just 46.60 seconds.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates three interpretable time-series models—SARIMAX, Facebook Prophet, and NeuralProphet—for hourly PM2.5 forecasting in Beijing under two operational regimes mimicking real-world deployment: (1) weekly walk-forward refitting and (2) frozen-model forecasting with lightweight online residual correction. In the walk-forward regime, Prophet achieved the strongest balance of accuracy and computational efficiency. In the frozen regime, applying an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) bias correction to SARIMAX and Prophet substantially improved their performance, with corrected SARIMAX attaining the lowest overall error. NeuralProphet proved unstable and unresponsive to correction in both regimes. The findings champion simple, decomposable additive models with efficient adaptation mechanisms as robust choices for operational air-quality forecasting, emphasizing computational thrift and interpretability over increased neural complexity.

## Method Summary
The study uses hourly pollutant (PM2.5, PM10, CO, NO, NO2, SO2, O3, NH3) and meteorological data for Beijing from Dec 2020–Jun 2025, sourced from OpenWeather and Open-Meteo APIs. After chronological splitting (90% train, 10% test), preprocessing applies leakage-safe winsorization (1st/99th percentiles from training) and standardization of exogenous variables using training statistics only. Feature selection via filter methods (Pearson, Mutual Information, mRMR) on training data yields a final set of precursors: `{NO, NO2, CO, SO2}`. Models are evaluated under two adaptive regimes over weekly (168-hour) test horizons: (A) weekly walk-forward refitting on expanding data, and (B) frozen base model with online EWMA residual correction (α=0.3). Performance is aggregated across all test weeks using MAE and RMSE, with total runtime recorded.

## Key Results
- **Walk-forward refitting:** Facebook Prophet achieves MAE 37.61 / RMSE 50.10 in 15m21s, outperforming SARIMAX (which fails due to memory issues) and unstable NeuralProphet.
- **Frozen regime + correction:** Online residual correction improves Prophet (MAE 37.67) and SARIMAX (MAE 32.50, lowest overall). Corrected Prophet nearly matches its walk-forward MAE while reducing runtime to 46.60 seconds.
- **NeuralProphet:** Remains less accurate and highly unstable across both regimes; residual correction harms its performance, indicating a mismatch between the model's error structure and the scalar bias adaptation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Facebook Prophet's decomposable additive design efficiently captures multi-scale seasonality and trend without heavy retraining, providing a favorable accuracy–speed trade-off for operational PM2.5 forecasting.
- **Mechanism:** Prophet separates the time series into interpretable components (trend, seasonality, exogenous effects) using Fourier series for seasonality and piecewise linear trends with changepoints. This parametric, modular structure allows rapid fitting on new data during walk-forward refitting and stable inference when frozen, because seasonal patterns are explicitly modeled rather than learned from scratch in each retrain.
- **Core assumption:** Air quality data exhibit recurring daily, weekly, and yearly patterns that are approximately stable over time, allowing pre-specified seasonal forms to remain valid across multiple weeks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Facebook Prophet achieved the strongest completed performance... while also requiring substantially less execution time than NeuralProphet."
  - [section: Facebook Prophet] "Prophet is a decomposable additive model... well-suited for pollution time series in which long-term drift and recurring temporal patterns coexist with covariate-driven variability."
- **Break condition:** Abrupt, permanent shifts in seasonal patterns (e.g., due to new emission regulations) would degrade Prophet's fixed seasonal Fourier terms, requiring changepoint re-estimation or model refit.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Online residual correction via exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) effectively compensates for systematic drift in frozen models by adapting a scalar bias term, improving accuracy at minimal computational cost.
- **Mechanism:** The method tracks the moving average of recent one-step forecast residuals (observed minus predicted) and applies this estimated bias to future forecasts. It assumes forecast errors contain a slowly varying mean component (drift) that can be separated from noise. Updating only a single bias parameter each week is computationally trivial compared to full model refitting.
- **Core assumption:** Model errors exhibit a slowly drifting mean (level shift) rather than rapidly changing dynamics or heteroscedastic variance; the residual process is approximately mean-reverting around a time-varying bias.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "In the frozen-model regime, online residual correction improved Facebook Prophet and SARIMAX... corrected Facebook Prophet reached nearly the same error as its walk-forward counterpart while reducing runtime from 15 min 21.91 sec to 46.60 sec."
  - [section: Adaptive forecasting regimes] "Adaptation is achieved through a lightweight online correction... computed as the mean residual over week w... updated recursively using an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA)."
- **Break condition:** Residuals become non-stationary with strong autocorrelation or periodic bias (e.g., systematic under-prediction at rush hour), which a single scalar bias cannot capture; correction would then require modeling residual dynamics explicitly.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** NeuralProphet's instability and poor performance stem from its hybrid architecture's sensitivity to hyperparameterization and training dynamics, which outweighs its added flexibility in this data regime.
- **Mechanism:** NeuralProphet combines additive decomposition with autoregressive (AR) neural components and lagged inputs (`n_lags=168`). This increases model capacity but also introduces optimization challenges (e.g., vanishing gradients, local minima) and risk of overfitting on limited–moderate data. The AR component may also conflict with the seasonal decomposition if not properly regularized.
- **Core assumption:** The dataset (multi-year hourly data) is sufficiently large and clean to support stable training of a neural network with 168-hour autoregressive context, and the added nonlinear capacity yields net benefit over simpler additive forms.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "NeuralProphet was less accurate and unstable... NeuralProphet remained less accurate and less stable across both regimes, and residual correction did not improve its forecasts."
  - [section: Results] "For NP, performance varied widely across the rolling windows, with the best weekly MAE observed in week 23 of 23 (28.46) and the worst in week 2 of 23 (424.28), indicating substantial instability under walk-forward retraining."
- **Break condition:** Insufficient training epochs, poor learning rate choice, or inadequate regularization would exacerbate NeuralProphet's instability; conversely, with extensive hyperparameter tuning and larger data, its performance might improve.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Perfect Prognosis assumption**
  - *Why needed here:* Isolates forecasting model behavior by providing true future exogenous driver values (e.g., temperature, other pollutants) during test evaluation, removing confounding from external variable prediction errors.
  - *Quick check question:* If future exogenous values were predicted instead of given, how would that change the relative model comparison and the effectiveness of residual correction?
- **Concept: Walk-forward validation (rolling origin)**
  - *Why needed here:* Mimics real-time deployment by sequentially refitting or updating models as new data arrives, preventing information leakage and assessing stability over time.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a fixed train/test split with random shuffling inappropriate for time-series forecasting?
- **Concept: Leakage-aware preprocessing**
  - *Why needed here:* Ensures all transformations (winsorization bounds, standardization scalers) use only training data to avoid optimistic bias in performance estimates.
  - *Quick check question:* If you compute winsorization percentiles on the full dataset before splitting, what specific type of leakage occurs and how would MAE/RMSE be artificially affected?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data ingestion & alignment -> Preprocessing (chronological split, winsorization, leakage-safe scaling) -> Feature selection (filter methods on training data only) -> Initial model training (SARIMAX/Prophet/NeuralProphet) -> Weekly loop over test period: Regime A (walk-forward refit) OR Regime B (frozen + EWMA residual correction) -> Metric aggregation (MAE/RMSE, runtime).
- **Critical path:** Data preparation -> feature selection -> initial model fit -> weekly loop (Regime A or B) -> metric aggregation. Any error in chronological splitting or leakage in preprocessing will invalidate all downstream results.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Accuracy vs. computational cost:* Walk-forward refitting maximizes adaptivity but is expensive (Prophet: 15 min; SARIMAX failed due to memory); frozen+correction reduces runtime (Prophet: 46 sec) with small accuracy loss for Prophet, large gain for SARIMAX.
  - *Complexity vs. interpretability:* NeuralProphet's nonlinear AR component increased instability; SARIMAX/Prophet offer clear component decomposition (trend/seasonality/regressors).
  - *Correction applicability:* Residual correction helps models with stable base structure (Prophet, SARIMAX) but harms NeuralProphet, suggesting correction assumes base model errors are mainly bias, not model misspecification.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - SARIMAX memory blow-up under repeated refitting – indicates need for rolling ARIMA with state reset or different implementation.
  - NeuralProphet's high week-to-week MAE variance – signals training instability or hyperparameter mismatch (e.g., `n_lags=168` may be excessive for this dataset).
  - If residual correction worsens MAE (as with NeuralProphet), base model's errors likely contain strong autocorrelation or nonlinear patterns not captured by a scalar bias.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline walk-forward test:** Run all three models for just 3 weekly windows to verify pipeline integrity and capture initial MAE patterns; check that Prophet completes faster than NeuralProphet and SARIMAX doesn't crash early.
  2. **Frozen regime sanity check:** Train each model once on initial training data, generate first test week forecast, compute residual mean, apply EWMA update (α=0.3) to second week; confirm bias update logic works and see immediate correction effect (SARIMAX should show largest drop).
  3. **Feature set ablation:** Compare performance using all pollutants vs. the selected `{NO, NO2, CO, SO2}` set; ensures feature selection didn't discard critical information and that redundancy removal helped stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Among the evaluated models and operational regimes, which specific combination provides the optimal balance of predictive accuracy, computational efficiency, and stability for hourly PM2.5 forecasting in a real-world deployment context?
  - *Basis:* The paper's core result compares models under two regimes, identifying Prophet's favorable balance in walk-forward mode and corrected SARIMAX's lowest error in frozen regime, but does not formally optimize trade-offs across deployment priorities.
  - *Why unresolved:* The "optimal" balance depends on stakeholder constraints (e.g., runtime vs. error cost). The study highlights trade-offs but does not generalize or quantify them across contexts.
  - *Resolution evidence:* A multi-criteria decision analysis (e.g., Pareto front) incorporating error cost, energy consumption, and maintenance across diverse urban settings.
- **Open Question 2:** What is the mechanistic reason for NeuralProphet's pronounced instability under walk-forward refitting and its complete lack of benefit from online residual correction, while SARIMAX and Facebook Prophet improve significantly with the same correction?
  - *Basis:* Results show NeuralProphet has large, variable errors and correction harms it, but the paper does not investigate causes beyond noting it is "less accurate and less stable."
  - *Why unresolved:* The failure could stem from neural architecture, training dynamics, scaling, or interaction between AR lags and correction. No ablation or diagnostic analysis is provided.
  - *Resolution evidence:* An ablation isolating NeuralProphet's components (trend, seasonality, AR lags, regressors) under both regimes, plus training loss/gradient diagnostics during walk-forward updates.
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent do the findings—particularly the superiority of simple additive models and the efficacy of residual correction—generalize to cities with different emission profiles, meteorological regimes, or data sources (e.g., raw ground sensor networks vs. API-aggregated estimates)?
  - *Basis:* Limitations state results reflect Beijing’s emission profile and API-sourced aggregated data, which may not transfer.
  - *Why unresolved:* The study tests only one city's API data. Generalizability to other urban contexts, sensor networks, or pollution drivers is unknown.
  - *Resolution evidence:* Replication studies on diverse datasets (e.g., EPA or CPCB station data from multiple global cities) and sensitivity analysis with synthetic regime shifts or sensor noise.
- **Open Question 4:** How does the "perfect prognosis" assumption—providing ground-truth exogenous regressors during forecasting—mask the real-world challenges of future covariate prediction, and how would the relative model rankings change under a more realistic "imperfect prognosis" setting?
  - *Basis:* Methodology uses Perfect Prognosis to isolate model behavior, but acknowledges real deployment requires forecasting future meteorology/precursors.
  - *Why unresolved:* The study does not evaluate robustness when exogenous inputs are predicted rather than known. Models reliant on exogenous drivers (SARIMAX, Prophet) may degrade more than self-contained NeuralProphet.
  - *Resolution evidence:* An experiment where exogenous regressors are replaced with their own forecasts (e.g., from a simple model) and performance drop compared across models and correction regimes.

## Limitations
- **Data provenance & preprocessing fidelity:** Exact API endpoints, Beijing coordinate selection, and variable harmonization are unspecified, creating replication risk. Leakage-safe preprocessing relies on correct chronological split interpretation.
- **Hyperparameter & training stability:** NeuralProphet's poor performance may stem from suboptimal `n_lags=168`, learning rate, or epochs rather than inherent architectural flaws; SARIMAX's memory blow-up may be implementation-specific.
- **Generalizability:** Results are confined to Beijing (2020–2025) under Perfect Prognosis. Real-world deployment with predicted exogenous inputs and different city dynamics may alter rankings.

## Confidence
- **High:** Directly reported performance metrics (Prophet walk-forward MAE 37.61, runtime 15m21s; corrected SARIMAX MAE 32.50; corrected Prophet runtime 46.60s).
- **Medium:** Mechanistic explanations (Prophet's additive design enabling speed; EWMA correction compensating drift) are plausible and supported by paper commentary but not causally isolated.
- **Low:** Extrapolating findings to other cities, pollutant mixes, or imperfect prognosis settings; attributing NeuralProphet's instability solely to architecture rather than tuning.
