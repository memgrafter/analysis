# Distilling Human-Aligned Privacy Sensitivity Assessment from Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29497
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29497
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** A 150M-parameter student model distilled from a 675B teacher achieves higher agreement with human privacy judgments (Krippendorff’s α = 0.737) than the teacher (α = 0.716) while enabling low-cost, local inference.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method to distill privacy sensitivity assessment capabilities from a large language model (Mistral Large 3, 675B parameters) into lightweight, efficient encoder classifiers. Using 200,000 texts from 10 domains labeled by the teacher, the resulting Ettin-150M model matches human privacy judgments more closely than its teacher (Krippendorff’s α = 0.737 vs. 0.716). This approach enables scalable, privacy-preserving evaluation of textual data without relying on external APIs, and the model functions effectively as an automatic metric for de-identification systems.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a 200k-sample corpus from 10 public text datasets. A 675B parameter LLM (Mistral Large 3) is prompted to assign a 1–5 privacy sensitivity score to each text. This labeled dataset is used to fine-tune several smaller encoder-only transformer models (e.g., Ettin-150M) via standard knowledge distillation for classification. Model performance is evaluated on a held-out test set (accuracy, macro F1, MAE), on a separate 250-text human-annotated benchmark (Krippendorff’s α), and by applying it as a metric on the Text Anonymization Benchmark to measure privacy score reductions after redaction.

## Key Results
- The Ettin-150M student model achieves higher agreement with aggregated human judgments (Krippendorff’s α = 0.737) than the teacher LLM (α = 0.716).
- It demonstrates strong classification performance on the held-out test set (Accuracy 74.9%, Macro F1 68.1) and effective cross-domain generalization.
- As an automatic metric for de-identification, it produces appropriate mean privacy score reductions (Δ = 1.86) on the Text Anonymization Benchmark after full masking.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Distillation smooths teacher stochasticity into a more consistent decision boundary, improving alignment with aggregated human judgment.
- **Mechanism:** The teacher LLM produces privacy ratings via prompt-based generation, which can be stochastic. Training a deterministic classifier on a large volume (200k) of these labels averages over prompt-level noise, compressing the teacher's reasoning into a stable classifier.
- **Core assumption:** The teacher's average output across many samples approximates a "true" human-aligned privacy signal better than any single teacher inference.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: distillation acts as a denoising process; [section 4.2 & discussion]: training on large volume smooths prompt-level stochasticity and generalizes better on human benchmark; [corpus]: the 200k-sample training set provides volume for smoothing.
- **Break condition:** If teacher stochasticity is not a significant source of error, this mechanism may not yield improvement.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The student model learns a compressed representation focused on privacy-relevant features, discarding irrelevant teacher knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** An encoder classifier trained for a specific 5-class task must identify textual cues (identifiers, topic sensitivity, disclosure style) that correlate with the teacher's ratings, forcing specialization on the privacy signal.
- **Core assumption:** Privacy-relevant information is learnable by a shallow encoder from the teacher's soft labels.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.1]: strong classification performance shows reliable task learning; [section 4.2 & Table 2]: cross-domain generalization indicates capture of cross-domain privacy signals; [section 4.3]: sensitivity to structured masking confirms alignment with expert privacy concepts.
- **Break condition:** If privacy signals require deep world knowledge beyond the encoder's capacity, the compressed model would fail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The resulting lightweight architecture enables privacy-preserving, scalable workflows impossible with teacher LLMs.
- **Mechanism:** Local, low-cost inference (150M parameters) removes the need for API calls, avoiding data exfiltration risks and allowing processing of sensitive corpora at scale.
- **Core assumption:** Acceptable utility for privacy evaluation can be achieved with a deterministic, task-specific model rather than a general-purpose LLM.
- **Evidence anchors:** [introduction]: using external LLMs may compromise privacy; [section 4.3]: model functions effectively as a de-identification metric; [discussion]: outlines use cases enabled by compactness.
- **Break condition:** If human privacy perception requires context beyond the input text (e.g., audience, purpose), the isolated classifier would be insufficient.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation**
  - *Why needed here:* The core method transfers privacy judgment from a 675B teacher to a 150M student. Understanding soft vs. hard labels, temperature scaling, and the role of data volume is key.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a student model exceed its teacher's agreement with humans after distillation on a static dataset?
- **Concept: Krippendorff’s Alpha**
  - *Why needed here:* The primary evaluation metric for human-model alignment. Measures inter-rater reliability for ordinal ratings, unlike simple accuracy.
  - *Quick check question:* How does Krippendorff’s α differ from Cohen’s κ, and why is it chosen for multi-annotator, ordinal data?
- **Concept: Privacy as Contextual Integrity**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper’s 1–5 scale operationalizes "privacy sensitivity" as a function of identifiers and topical disclosure, following Nissenbaum’s framework. Clarifies what the model approximates.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a single scalar privacy score a simplification, and what contextual factors are explicitly omitted?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  Teacher LLM (Mistral Large 3) -> generates 200k labels via structured prompt -> Training corpus (200k texts from 10 public datasets) -> Student models (Ettin-150M, Ettin-17M, BERT-base, ModernBERT-base) fine-tuned for 5-class classification -> Evaluation suite (held-out test set, human benchmark, Text Anonymization Benchmark).
- **Critical path:** 
  Data sourcing (10 datasets) -> Teacher annotation (batch API calls with prompt) -> Data cleaning/splitting (90/5/5) -> Student training (fixed LR 2e-5, batch size 16, 3 epochs) -> Validation checkpoint selection (macro F1) -> Human benchmark evaluation (α calculation) -> TAB application (privacy score delta).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Model size vs. performance:* Ettin-150M matches ModernBERT-base; smaller Ettin-17M drops notably. Tradeoff: inference cost vs. middle-class (C2–C4) F1.
  - *Single teacher vs. ensemble:* Using one teacher simplifies pipeline but inherits its biases; multiple teachers could reduce label noise.
  - *Task formulation:* Treating privacy as 5-class classification discards ordinality; regression might better capture scale semantics but was not explored.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Middle-class confusion:* Lower F1 for C2–C4 (~58–64) vs. extremes (C1: 91.5, C5: 68.6), indicating difficulty with boundary cases.
  - *Domain shift:* Model trained on English web text; performance may degrade on formal documents, non-English text, or highly technical domains.
  - *Random masking increase:* Model incorrectly scores random [REDACTED] insertion as more private (Δ = –0.31), showing sensitivity to masking tokens over semantic redaction.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce classification metrics:** Train Ettin-150M on the provided 200k dataset; compute accuracy, macro F1, MAE on held-out test split. Expect: Acc >74%, Macro F1 >68%, MAE ≈0.28.
  2. **Recompute human agreement:** Load the 250-text human benchmark; compute Krippendorff’s α between model predictions and average human ratings. Expect: α ≈0.737.
  3. **De-identification metric test:** Apply model to TAB test set (original, direct-masked, quasi-masked, fully-masked). Calculate mean privacy score reduction (∆) and % Class 1 ("Harmless"). Expect: Full masking ∆ ≈1.86, %Class1 ≈84%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can attribution methods and counterfactual editing be used to disentangle the specific cues (e.g., direct identifiers vs. topic sensitivity) that drive the model's privacy sensitivity assessments? *Basis:* Future Work: feasibility to study privacy signals at scale via attribution and counterfactual edits, helping disentangle identifiability cues from topic sensitivity.
- **Question 2:** Can the distilled privacy evaluator be integrated with utility metrics (e.g., semantic similarity) to automatically optimize for privacy-utility trade-offs in text privatization systems? *Basis:* Future Work: model can serve as a training signal combined with a utility measure to define privacy–utility trade-offs.
- **Question 3:** How can contextual factors (audience, purpose, setting) be incorporated into the privacy sensitivity assessment to move beyond a global, context-free score? *Basis:* Future Work: move beyond a single 'global' notion of privacy by incorporating context and exploring personalization.
- **Question 4:** What methods can improve the robustness of the privacy evaluator, including score calibration, handling out-of-domain inputs, and auditing demographic-dependent failure modes? *Basis:* Future Work: robustness remains open—calibrating scores, dealing with out-of-domain inputs, and auditing domain- and demographic-dependent failure modes are essential.
- **Question 5:** Does the observed improvement in human alignment after distillation result from denoising teacher stochasticity, and how does this vary with distillation data size and teacher choice? *Basis:* Performance Measurement: distillation can act as a denoising process; future work should explicitly test this hypothesis by studying more teacher behaviors or varying the amount of distillation data.

## Limitations
- Teacher model (Mistral Large 3) details and API access are unclear, preventing exact label regeneration.
- Ettin-150M architecture and pretraining origin are unspecified; reproduction requires assumptions or proxy encoders.
- Human benchmark composition (250 texts) and exact train/test splits are unreleased, blocking independent verification of Krippendorff’s α.
- Identified failure modes: middle-class confusion and sensitivity to random [REDACTED] tokens indicate limited robustness.

## Confidence
- **Student outperforms teacher on human alignment (α 0.737 vs 0.716):** Medium. Results rest on a single benchmark and teacher reproducibility issues; sample size adequate but lack of raw data limits firm validation.
- **Model as de-identification metric (Δ ≈ 1.86 on TAB):** Medium. Application appears sound but relies on the same unreproducible model; noted masking artifacts caution over-generalization.
- **Explanatory mechanisms (stochasticity smoothing, feature compression, workflow enablement):** Medium. Arguments are plausible and supported by indirect evidence, but no direct ablation isolates each factor.

## Next Checks
