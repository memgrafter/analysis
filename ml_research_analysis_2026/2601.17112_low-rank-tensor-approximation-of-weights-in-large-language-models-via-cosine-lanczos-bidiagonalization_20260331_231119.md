# Low-Rank Tensor Approximation of Weights in Large Language Models via Cosine Lanczos Bidiagonalization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17112
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17112
- **Reference count:** 25
- **Primary result:** TLASER (Tensor Layer-Selective Rank Reduction) improves accuracy on GPT‑J‑6B with TruthfulQA by 0.40% and lowers reconstruction error to 0.13–0.14 from 0.31–0.35 versus matrix LASER for single‑layer FFN interventions, without retraining.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) suffer from high memory and computational costs due to massive weight matrices. This paper introduces **TLASER**, a compression method that tensorizes weight matrices into third‑order tensors aligned with Transformer multi‑head/block structure, applies a low‑rank approximation via the **DCT‑based c‑product** and **tensor Lanczos bidiagonalization**, and then reconstructs the compressed weights. Evaluated on GPT‑J‑6B using TruthfulQA, TLASER achieves a modest but positive accuracy gain (56.40% vs 56.00%) and a 2.5× reduction in reconstruction error compared to the matrix‑based LASER baseline, all without any fine‑tuning. The approach demonstrates that preserving multi‑dimensional structural correlations can yield more efficient weight compression.

## Method Summary
TLASER proceeds as follows: First, a pretrained weight matrix (e.g., the `U_in` matrix of a feed‑forward network) is **tensorized** into a third‑order tensor whose third mode indexes semantically meaningful structural units (e.g., attention heads or FFN expansion blocks). Next, a **c‑product** is applied: a Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) is performed along mode‑3, converting the tensor into the transform domain. In this domain, each frontal slice is treated as a matrix and a (partial) singular value decomposition is computed either by a full slice‑wise SVD (Algorithm 1) or by an iterative **tensor Lanczos bidiagonalization** (Algorithm 2) to approximate the leading singular triplets efficiently. A rank `r` is selected per layer based on an energy threshold (e.g., cumulative singular tube energy ≥ τ). The truncated factors are then inverse‑DCT transformed and re‑assembled to obtain a compressed tensor, which is finally **inverse‑tensorized** back to a 2D weight matrix. No retraining is performed; the compressed weights are directly substituted into the model.

## Key Results
- Single‑layer FFN intervention (layer 27, rank ratio ρ=0.70) on GPT‑J‑6B: **+0.40%** accuracy on TruthfulQA (56.40% vs 56.00%).
- Same setting: reconstruction error reduced to **0.13–0.14**, compared to **0.31–0.35** for matrix LASER.
- Tensorization and DCT‑based approximation deliver better compression at similar or lower rank budgets, especially when only one FFN matrix per layer is compressed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Structural Correlation Preservation
- **Claim:** Reshaping 2D weight matrices into 3D tensors aligned with Transformer structure preserves intra‑structural correlations that are destroyed by matrix SVD.
- **Mechanism:** Tensorization operators (Φ_attn, Φ_in, Φ_out) reshape weights so that mode‑3 explicitly indexes attention heads or FFN blocks. This reorganization retains multi‑dimensional relationships across these units.
- **Core assumption:** Multi‑head/block structure contains exploitable cross‑unit correlations lost in a flat matrix.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “exploits the multi‑dimensional structure … captures higher‑order correlations beyond traditional SVD.” Section 6.1: “Mode‑3 indexes the r expansion blocks … ensures DCT … captures inter‑block correlations.”
- **Break condition:** If structural dimensions have near‑zero cross‑correlation, tensorization adds overhead with no benefit over matrix methods.

### Mechanism 2: Energy Compaction via DCT‑Based c‑Product
- **Claim:** The DCT‑based c‑product enables efficient, real‑valued low‑rank approximation by compacting energy into fewer frontal slices in the transform domain.
- **Mechanism:** DCT along mode‑3 transforms the tensor, after which slice‑wise matrix SVD is performed. The DCT’s energy compaction property concentrates dominant singular values into fewer coefficients.
- **Core assumption:** DCT is an appropriate invertible transform that promotes energy compaction for the correlation patterns across mode‑3.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.0.1: “DCT‑based c‑product is real‑valued and achieves superior energy compaction …” Algorithm 1 performs cA = DCT(A,3) then SVD on each slice.
- **Break condition:** If inter‑structural correlations are not aligned with DCT basis functions, energy compaction is poor and the approximation may be less efficient than alternative transforms.

### Mechanism 3: Scalable Partial Decomposition via Tensor Lanczos
- **Claim:** Tensor Lanczos bidiagonalization computes an approximate c‑SVD using few iterations, avoiding full decomposition cost while targeting dominant spectral components.
- **Mechanism:** The tensor generalization of Golub‑Kahan builds small Krylov subspaces (P_k, Q_k) and a compact bidiagonal tensor B_k. The singular triplets of B_k approximate those of the full tensor, enabling rank‑r truncation without full decomposition.
- **Core assumption:** Leading singular triplets capture essential redundant structure, and Lanczos converges quickly for these components.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4 derives the recurrence and approximation guarantee (Equation 4.6). Abstract reports reduced error versus LASER. Neighboring work (e.g., “A Computationally Efficient Multidimensional Vision Transformer”) uses similar tensor cosine products, supporting viability.
- **Break condition:** Slow singular value decay or large required rank `r` may make `k` iterations insufficient, leading to poor approximation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Input (weight matrix `W`) → Tensorization Module (`Φ`) → Transform & Decomposition Module (DCT → slice‑wise SVD/Lanczos) → Rank Selection Module (energy threshold) → Truncation & Reconstruction (inverse DCT) → Inverse Tensorization (`Φ^{-1}`) → Modified weight matrix `W_r`.

- **Critical path:**  
  `W` → `Φ` → tensor `W_t` → DCT(`W_t`) → (full SVD or Lanczos iterations) → truncation → inverse DCT → `W_t_r` → `Φ^{-1}` → `W_r`. The Lanczos path replaces full SVD with iterative c‑product applications to build small `P_k`, `Q_k`, `B_k` and lift approximate singular triplets.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  • **Full c‑SVD vs. Lanczos:** Full c‑SVD is exact but costly (`O(p·min(m,n)^2·max(m,n))`); Lanczos is approximate but scalable (`O(k·m·n·p)` for `k` iterations).  
  • **Rank allocation:** Global fixed rank ratio vs. per‑layer adaptive rank. Adaptive better handles heterogeneity but requires per‑layer spectral analysis.  
  • **Intervention scope:** Single‑layer vs. multi‑layer compression; single‑layer edits show benefit while compressing both FFN matrices in the same layer may degrade accuracy.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  • **High reconstruction error:** Rank `r` too low or DCT ineffective for that weight’s correlation pattern.  
  • **Significant accuracy drop:** Over‑aggressive rank selection or targeting a sensitive layer.  
  • **Lanczos non‑convergence / poor Ritz values:** Insufficient reorthogonalization or too few iterations `k`.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate the paper’s single‑layer TLASER on GPT‑J‑6B (layer 27, ρ=0.70, target `U_in`), measure TruthfulQA accuracy & loss versus matrix LASER.  
  2. Sweep single‑layer `U_in` interventions across all layers (ℓ = 1…28) at fixed ρ=0.70 to identify optimal layers and test the claim that intermediate layers are most compressible.  
  3. Ablate the c‑product: for a fixed layer and rank, compare TLASER against matrix LASER, tensor t‑product (FFT‑based), and full Tucker/CP decompositions, measuring reconstruction error and accuracy to isolate the DCT contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Optimal layer selection for TLASER in GPT‑J‑6B:** The paper tested only layer 27; a systematic sweep is needed to find the best intervention points, as the optimal layer for TLASER may differ from LASER’s early‑layer optimum.
- **Statistical significance and reproducibility of accuracy gains:** The reported +0.40% improvement is based on a 500‑sample subset without variance estimates; full test set evaluations with multiple seeds and confidence intervals are required.
- **Why TLASER’s advantage vanishes when compressing both FFN matrices (`U_in` and `U_out`) in the same layer:** Interaction effects after the nonlinearity are hypothesized but not experimentally verified; ablations on activation distributions or gradient interactions are needed.

## Limitations
- Tensor Lanczos algorithmic details (reorthogonalization, convergence thresholds) are underspecified, risking reproducibility and efficiency claims.
- Layer selection and rank allocation rely on ad‑hoc choices (e.g., layer 27, ρ=0.70); adaptive per‑layer thresholds remain only conceptually described.
- Evaluation is narrow: only GPT‑J‑6B on TruthfulQA; no cross‑architecture or multi‑task validation.

## Confidence
- **Tensorization preserves structural correlations:** Medium — theory is sound but empirical validation across diverse layers/heads is missing.
- **DCT c‑product energy compaction superiority:** Low — assertion based on DCT properties; no comparative experiments with alternative transforms on actual weight tensors.
- **Tensor Lanczos bidiagonalization efficiency:** Low — Algorithm description high‑level; runtime/comparison to full c‑SVD and approximation error vs. iterations unmeasured.
- **Empirical accuracy gain and error reduction:** Medium — results are concrete but single‑shot; reproducibility and variance unknown.
