# RePO: Bridging On-Policy Learning and Off-Policy Knowledge through Rephrasing Policy Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10819
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10819
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: RePO achieves 78.2 pass@1 on MATH-500 and 61.8 on GPQA while improving training stability.

## Executive Summary
RePO addresses the challenge of combining the stability of on-policy reinforcement learning with the data efficiency of off-policy knowledge. It does so by rephrasing expert trajectories into the policy's own distribution and dynamically replacing low-reward rollouts based on group failure rate. Evaluated on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, RePO substantially outperforms base models and prior methods (e.g., 78.2 vs 43.6 on MATH-500). It also shows strong results on general knowledge (GPQA) and financial domain adaptation, while demonstrating improved training stability through controlled entropy and gradient norms.

## Method Summary
RePO's core method consists of two steps. First, it prompts the current policy to rephrase a set of expert trajectories, producing on-policy versions of high-quality demonstrations. Second, during RL training, it generates rollouts and computes a group failure rate (proportion of low-reward trajectories in a group). If this rate exceeds a threshold, the low-reward rollouts are replaced with the rephrased expert trajectories before the policy update. This mechanism ensures that the policy learns from both its own experience and curated expert knowledge, while avoiding the instability of directly mixing off-policy data.

## Key Results
- Mathematical reasoning: 78.2 pass@1 on MATH-500 (vs 43.6 base), 13.1 on AIME24, and 48.5 on AMC.
- General knowledge: 61.8 on GPQA, surpassing GRPO (59.2) and LUFFY (49.8).
- Financial domain: 68.08 on FinLLM-Eval (vs 39.51 SFT) with maintained reasoning (69.2 on AIME24 vs 41.7 SFT).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Rephrasing expert trajectories transforms them into the policy's own distribution, reducing distribution shift and enabling effective off-policy learning without the instability typically associated with mixing data sources. The dynamic replacement based on group failure rate ensures that only unproductive rollouts are replaced, preserving the on-policy nature of the training data while injecting high-quality demonstrations when needed. This leads to a stable training curve with controlled entropy and gradient norms, as evidenced by the analysis.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Expert Trajectories -> Rephrasing Module (using current policy) -> Rephrased Trajectories  
  Current Policy -> Rollout Generation -> Rollout Buffer -> Group Failure Rate Calculation -> Dynamic Replacement (with Rephrased Trajectories) -> Updated Training Data -> Policy Update -> Updated Policy
- **Critical path**: The training loop iterates: (1) generate rollouts, (2) compute group failure rate, (3) if failure rate exceeds threshold, replace low-reward rollouts with rephrased trajectories, (4) update policy using combined data, (5) repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Rephrasing vs direct imitation: Rephrasing adapts to policy distribution but adds computational overhead; direct imitation is simpler but may cause distribution shift.  
  - Replacement threshold: Balances conservatism (fewer replacements) vs aggressiveness (more expert data injection); requires tuning.  
  - Group size: Larger groups yield more reliable failure rate estimates but delay response; smaller groups are more sensitive to noise.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Entropy drops sharply to near zero, indicating collapse.  
  - Gradient norms spike or become NaN, signaling instability.  
  - Validation performance plateaus or degrades despite training.  
  - Replacement rate remains near 100%, suggesting the policy is consistently failing to produce good rollouts.
- **First experiments**:  
  1. Compare RePO with a fixed replacement rate (no dynamic threshold) to measure the impact of adaptive replacement.  
  2. Ablate rephrasing by directly using raw expert trajectories for replacement and compare performance.  
  3. Monitor entropy and gradient norm statistics during training to verify stability claims.
