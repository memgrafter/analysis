# FOCA: Frequency-Oriented Cross-Domain Forgery Detection, Localization and Explanation via Multi-Modal Large Language Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18880
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18880
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Integrates RGB spatial and frequency-domain features via a multimodal LLM to achieve state-of-the-art forgery detection (96.2% accuracy), localization (+0.7 IoU over SIDA), and explanation quality on the FSE-Set dataset.

## Executive Summary
FOCA is a novel framework that overcomes key limitations in image forgery detection by combining RGB spatial information with frequency-domain cues using a large language model. It introduces a Frequency Attention Fusion (FAF) module that leverages discrete wavelet transform to extract high-frequency sub-bands, which are fused with RGB features via cross-attention. This fusion enables the multimodal model to detect tampering, localize forged regions, and generate human-interpretable explanations referencing both semantic and forensic traces. To support training and evaluation, the authors created the FSE-Set dataset containing 100K images with pixel masks and dual-domain annotations. Experiments show FOCA achieves 96.2% image-level accuracy and F1 score, outperforms prior MLLM-based methods in localization (e.g., +0.7 IoU over SIDA), and attains top scores in explanation quality. The approach also demonstrates robustness in detecting AI-generated edits and subtle artifacts.

## Method Summary
FOCA processes an image and a text instruction. The image is passed through a Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) to obtain the high-frequency HH sub-band. A Frequency Attention Fusion (FAF) module then performs cross-attention with the original RGB features (using HH as Query, RGB as Key/Value) and adds a residual connection to produce a fused feature representation. This fused representation, along with the text instruction, is fed into a frozen MLLM backbone (LISA-7B) that has been fine-tuned via LoRA. Special tokens `[CLS]` and `[SEG]` are added to the vocabulary; their final embeddings are extracted for downstream tasks. A detection head classifies the image as real or tampered using `[CLS]`, while a segmentation head (using `[SEG]` and features from a frozen SAM image encoder) generates a pixel-level mask. The loss function combines prediction losses (text cross-entropy, detection cross-entropy, segmentation BCE+Dice) with an auxiliary contrastive learning loss on pooled fused features to stabilize training. The FAF module, segmentation decoder, and LoRA adapters are trained while freezing the remaining MLLM and SAM encoder weights.

## Key Results
- Image-level detection accuracy and F1 score: 96.2% on FSE-Set.
- Localization improvement: +0.7 IoU over state-of-the-art SIDA.
- Explanation quality: highest ROUGE-L, CSS, and LLM-Judge scores among evaluated methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** FOCA improves forgery detection accuracy and localization precision by adaptively fusing high-frequency forensic cues with spatial-semantic features.
- **Mechanism:** The Frequency Attention Fusion (FAF) module uses a cross-attention mechanism where the wavelet-derived high-frequency sub-band (`x_HH`) acts as the Query, and the original RGB image acts as Key and Value. This allows the model to retrieve and amplify spatially coherent regions that contain subtle high-frequency artifacts (e.g., compression inconsistencies, noise residuals) relevant to tampering, while preserving broader semantic context via the residual connection.
- **Core assumption:** Tampering artifacts manifest as discernible disruptions in high-frequency components that are structurally correlated with their spatial location in the RGB image.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “integrates discriminative features from both the RGB spatial and frequency domains via a cross-attention fusion module.” [section 2.2] “DWT decomposes… the `x_HH` sub-band demonstrates strong generalization capability… integrated with spatial features through the FAF module… adaptively retrieves structurally relevant regions from spatial features under the guidance of high-frequency cues.”
- **Break condition:** If high-frequency artifacts are not statistically correlated with tampering (e.g., in perfectly simulated forgeries), or if the cross-attention fails to learn meaningful spatial-frequency correspondences during training.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The auxiliary contrastive learning loss (`L_cl`) stabilizes training and enforces the fused representation (`x_f`) to be more discriminative between authentic and tampered images.
- **Mechanism:** After FAF fusion, features are pooled and projected into a latent space. The InfoNCE loss (`L_cl`) maximizes similarity between different augmentations/views of the same image (positive pair) while minimizing similarity to features from other images in the batch (negatives). This encourages the model to learn representations where tampering status is a defining factor, complementing the primary detection/segmentation losses.
- **Core assumption:** Tampered and authentic images form separable clusters in the feature space induced by the FAF module, and contrastive learning can tighten intra-class clustering.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 2.2] “To stabilize training and enhance robustness, we introduce… contrastive learning… maximiz[es] agreement between positive pairs while minimizing similarity with negatives, thereby enforcing discriminative, tampering-aware representations.” [section 2.4] Overall loss `L = L_pred + λ_c L_cl` shows explicit integration.
- **Break condition:** If task-specific features for tampering are overwhelmed by semantic content variations (e.g., image scene, lighting), making it impossible for contrastive loss to create tampering-based clusters without additional constraints.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The FSE-Set dataset with dual-domain (RGB + frequency) textual annotations enables the MLLM to generate explanations that reference both semantic inconsistencies and low-level forensic traces, improving interpretability and potentially localization accuracy through richer training signal.
- **Mechanism:** During training, the MLLM (fine-tuned via LoRA) learns to associate visual tokens from the fused `x_f` with textual tokens describing artifacts in *both* domains (e.g., “unnatural texture edges” *and* “high-frequency noise pattern mismatch near the spliced object”). This dual-domain supervision teaches the model to ground its explanations in a broader set of visual evidence.
- **Core assumption:** Human-interpretable explanations for image tampering often require referencing both *what* is semantically inconsistent and *how* the low-level forensic evidence manifests.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “providing explicit, human-interpretable cross-domain explanations.” [section 2.3] “To provide dual-domain insights… we further employ Claude [20] to jointly analyze the RGB image and its HH frequency sub-band, producing fine-grained explanations in both domains.” [section 3.2] Explanation evaluation (Fig. 3) shows top ROUGE-L, CSS, and LLM-Judge scores, indicating the trained model successfully generates such cross-domain explanations.
- **Break condition:** If the MLLM’s inherent capacity is insufficient to learn the mapping from fused visual features to nuanced dual-domain textual descriptions, or if the frequency-domain explanations in FSE-Set are noisy or misaligned with actual artifacts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) for Feature Extraction:** DWT decomposes an image into multiple sub-bands (LL, LH, HL, HH) capturing different frequency components. The HH sub-band is particularly sensitive to edges and high-frequency noise, making it useful for detecting tampering artifacts. The HH band retains spatial resolution, aligning with localized forensic cues.
  - **Quick check:** Why might the HH sub-band be more sensitive to splicing boundaries than LH or HL, and how does spatial preservation help?
- **Cross-Attention in Multimodal Fusion:** Cross-attention allows one modality (Query) to attend to another (Key/Value). In FAF, the frequency features (HH) query the RGB spatial features, enabling the model to highlight regions where high-frequency cues co-occur with semantic content. This adaptive fusion is learned during training.
  - **Quick check:** If the HH map is noisy, how might attention weights be affected, and why does a residual connection help?
- **Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE Loss) for Representation Learning:** Contrastive learning pulls together representations of the same class (positive pairs) and pushes apart different classes (negatives). Applied on the fused feature, it encourages separation of authentic and tampered images in the latent space, acting as a regularizer.
  - **Quick check:** Since pairs are defined at the image level, what limitation arises when the forged region is small and local?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (RGB image, text instruction) → DWT → HH sub-band → FAF (cross-attention: Query=HH, Key/Value=RGB) → fused feature `x_f` → MLLM backbone (LISA-7B, LoRA fine-tuned) → extract `[CLS]` token → Detection head (`ϕ`) → tamper class `D_hat`; extract `[SEG]` token → Projection `γ` → `h_seg` + frozen SAM features `f` → Segmentation decoder `S_dec` → mask `M_hat`; text generation → explanation `T_hat`. Losses: `L_pred` (text CE + detection CE + mask BCE+Dice) + `L_cl` (InfoNCE on pooled `x_f`).

**Critical path:** The FAF module must precede MLLM input to embed frequency cues into the token stream. LoRA adapters are applied to the MLLM to keep pretrained knowledge intact. The segmentation head relies on both the `[SEG]` token and frozen SAM encoder features, requiring proper alignment.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Frozen MLLM + LoRA preserves vast pretrained knowledge and keeps training efficient, but limits adaptation depth.
- DWT is computationally simple and provides spatially aligned frequency bands, but may be less expressive than learned transforms.
- Using a single FAF layer is simpler and faster than multi-scale fusion, possibly sacrificing finer-grained interactions.

**Failure signatures:**
- High detection but low IoU suggests the model recognizes tampering globally but fails to pinpoint exact regions; FAF may not be linking frequency cues to spatial details effectively.
- High IoU/F1 but poor explanation scores (ROUGE-L, LLM-Judge) indicates correct localization but insufficiently coherent or dual-domain textual output; could be due to weak LoRA tuning or dataset noise.
- Poor performance on AI-generated edits compared to traditional manipulations suggests frequency bands (HH) might not capture diffusion-specific artifacts; FAF attention may be misaligned.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ablate FAF:** Replace FAF with simple concatenation of HH and RGB features; expect degradation in detection/localization, especially on subtle artifacts.
2. **Ablate contrastive loss (`L_cl`):** Train without `L_cl`; monitor training stability (loss spikes) and final detection F1; a drop would confirm its regularizing role.
3. **Frequency input ablation:** Swap HH sub-band with LH or HL, or use an SRM-based noise map; compare performance to validate HH’s superiority.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Primary Question 1:** *How does the selection of the wavelet sub-band (e.g., HH, LH, HL, LL) influence the effectiveness of the Frequency Attention Fusion module?* The paper adopts HH based on prior literature but does not compare alternatives. An ablation with different sub-bands across diverse forgery types would clarify optimal choices.
- **Primary Question 2:** *Do automatic evaluation metrics (ROUGE-L, CSS, LLM-Judge) reliably reflect human judgment of explanation quality?* The paper lacks human evaluation; an expert user study comparing explanations against these metrics is needed.
- **Primary Question 3:** *Does FOCA generalize to unseen forgery types (e.g., GAN-based edits, diverse diffusion models, advanced retouching)?* FSE-Set primarily includes Stable Diffusion inpainting and traditional manipulations; zero-shot testing on out-of-distribution forgeries is required.
- **Primary Question 4:** *What is the individual contribution of the contrastive learning objective?* The paper does not isolate `L_cl`’s effect; training with and without it while keeping all else constant would quantify its impact.

## Limitations
- FSE-Set dataset is not publicly released, preventing external verification of its scale, diversity, and annotation quality.
- Key implementation details are unspecified: DWT filter type, LoRA hyperparameters (rank/alpha), segmentation decoder architecture, and contrastive loss temperature.
- Generalization to unseen AI-generated edits is uncertain; frequency patterns from Stable Diffusion may not transfer to other generative models or manipulation techniques.

## Confidence
- FAF fusion mechanism: High
- Contrastive loss utility: Medium
- Explanation quality (LLM-Judge alignment): Low
