# M3D-BFS: a Multi-stage Dynamic Fusion Strategy for Sample-Adaptive Multi-Modal Brain Network Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01667  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01667  
- **Reference count:** 37  
- **Primary result:** M3D-BFS achieves 81.21% accuracy on HCP (gender) and 80.85% on ZDXX (depression), outperforming the best baseline by +2.28% and +2.73% respectively.

## Executive Summary
Static multi-modal fusion methods in brain network analysis process all samples identically, ignoring inherent sample-specific differences in connectivity patterns. M3D-BFS introduces a novel multi-stage dynamic fusion strategy based on Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) that selects modality-specific and fusion experts per sample via a gating mechanism. To ensure stable training, it employs a three-stage progressive pipeline: (1) pretrain uni-modal encoders, (2) pretrain single experts with auxiliary losses, and (3) finetune the full MoE model while freezing earlier modules. A multi-modal disentanglement loss further enhances the orthogonality and task relevance of the final representations. Evaluated on two real-world datasets—HCP for gender classification and ZDXX for depression—M3D-BFS significantly surpasses state-of-the-art static fusion methods, demonstrating the effectiveness of sample-adaptive dynamic fusion.

## Method Summary
M3D-BFS processes structural (SC) and functional (FC) brain connectivity graphs through separate GCN encoders. After projection to a common space, three MoE blocks (one per modality and one for fusion) dynamically route node features to experts based on sample-specific gating weights. The training proceeds in three stages: first, unimodal encoders are trained individually; second, a single expert for each MoE is pretrained with distillation and contrastive losses to preserve unimodal knowledge; third, the single experts are expanded into MoEs (initialized from the pretrained weights) and finetuned with a loss combining classification, expert load balancing, and a disentanglement objective that encourages orthogonality between the pooled outputs of the three branches. Only the MoE blocks are updated in the final stage, with encoders frozen.

## Key Results
- On HCP (gender classification): **81.21% accuracy**, +2.28% over the best baseline.  
- On ZDXX (depression classification): **80.85% accuracy**, +2.73% over the best baseline.  
- Significant improvements across all metrics (SEN, SPE, F1, ROC-AUC) compared to static fusion and other SOTA methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dynamic Sample-Adaptive Routing via MoE Gating
- **Claim:** The gating network in each Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) block dynamically routes brain regions to different experts based on the input sample, enabling sample-specific fusion.
- **Mechanism:** For SC, FC, and fusion, separate MoE blocks contain multiple expert networks (MLPs) and a shared gating network. The gating network processes the modality representation and produces a weight distribution over experts. During both training and inference, each brain region's feature is sent to the top-k (k=1) expert according to these weights. Because the gating output depends on the input, different samples activate different experts, allowing the model to adapt its computation to sample-specific topology.
- **Core assumption:** Brain network samples exhibit inherent differences in connectivity patterns that benefit from being processed by specialized expert pathways.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "dynamically select modality-specific and fusion experts per sample via a gating mechanism."
  - [section 3.5] "As the output of G m depends on input X, it can dynamically assign brain regions to different experts according to different data samples."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like MoGE (ref [3]) and DynMM (ref [28]) apply dynamic fusion in vision/audio, supporting the concept but not directly in brain networks.
- **Break condition:** If gating weights become uniform or all samples route to the same expert (expert collapse), the model loses its sample adaptivity and reverts to static computation. This is mitigated by the importance/load loss (L_moe) and progressive pretraining.

### Mechanism 2: Progressive Pre-training to Stabilize MoE Training
- **Claim:** The three-stage training pipeline (uni-modal encoder pretraining → single expert pretraining → MoE finetuning) prevents expert collapse by warm-starting experts and preserving unimodal knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 trains separate GCN encoders for SC and FC. Stage 2 freezes these encoders and trains a *single* MLP expert for each MoE (SC-specific, FC-specific, fusion) with auxiliary losses: unimodal distillation (L_distill) and SC-FC contrastive loss (L_contrast). Stage 3 replaces each single expert with an MoE containing multiple experts, initializes all experts with the Stage 2 weights, and finetunes only the MoE blocks while keeping earlier modules frozen. This ensures experts start from a strong, aligned initialization.
- **Core assumption:** Initializing multiple experts from a well-trained single expert provides a stable baseline and avoids the randomness that leads to collapse; freezing earlier layers prevents catastrophic forgetting of unimodal representations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "To alleviate issue of MoE where training of experts may be collapsed, we divide our method into 3 stages: (1) pretrain uni-modal encoders, (2) pretrain single experts, (3) finetune the full MoE model."
  - [section 3.2] "We first train uni-modal encoders for SC/FC modality respectively. Then we pretrain our model by training single experts for each MoE respectively."
  - [section 3.5] "We apply the MLP weights saved in stage 2 to initialize all the expert networks in corresponding MoEs."
- **Break condition:** If Stage 2 single expert performance is poor (e.g., low unimodal accuracy), initializing multiple experts from it may not help. Also, if the distillation loss weight (β) is too low, unimodal knowledge may degrade during Stage 2.

### Mechanism 3: Multi-modal Disentanglement Loss for Representation Enhancement
- **Claim:** The disentanglement loss (L_dis_en) encourages the final uni-modal (SC, FC) and fusion representations to be mutually orthogonal yet individually predictive of the task, reducing redundancy and improving downstream accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** After MoE blocks, pooled outputs z_SC, z_FC, z_fusion are obtained. For each sample, an anchor vector z_anchor is formed by concatenating these three. For each representation z_m, a contrastive loss treats z_anchor as positive and the other two representations as negatives (Eq. 7). This pushes each representation to capture unique information while aligning with the task-relevant anchor.
- **Core assumption:** Task performance improves when each modality and fusion embedding carry complementary information, and orthogonality in the embedding space reflects this complementarity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "A multi-modal disentanglement loss enhances final representations."
  - [section 3.5] "We design our multi-mdoal disentanglement loss to learn better uni-modal and multi-modal representations ... through L_dis_en, we can disentangle different representations while increasing their relevance to downstream tasks."
  - [section 3.5] Equation (7) defines L_dis_en as a contrastive loss over the triplet of representations.
- **Break condition:** If the task inherently requires correlated information across modalities (e.g., strong SC-FC coupling), excessive orthogonality may discard useful shared signals. The loss weight (α) must be tuned; too high may over-penalize correlation.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map (plain text):**  
  Input (SC graph, FC graph) -> Uni-modal GCN encoders (f_phi_SC, f_phi_FC) -> Node embeddings -> Projection layers -> MoE blocks (MoE_SC, MoE_FC, MoE_fusion) -> Readout (pooling) -> Concatenation (z_SC, z_FC, z_fusion) -> Classifier MLP (g_theta) -> Prediction.

- **Critical path (training):**  
  1. Stage 1: Train uni-modal GCNs separately with cross-entropy loss; save encoder weights.  
  2. Stage 2: Freeze encoders; replace each MoE with a single expert MLP; train with L_ce + β(L_distill + L_contrast); save expert weights.  
  3. Stage 3: Initialize each MoE with N_experts copies of the corresponding single expert weights; freeze encoders and projections; train only MoE blocks (experts + gating) with L_ce + αL_moe + (1-α)L_dis_en.  
  4. Inference: Full model with gating selecting top-1 expert per region per sample.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Number of experts (k): More experts increase capacity and specialization potential but raise computational cost and collapse risk; requires proper L_moe weighting.  
  - Top-k in gating: k=1 ensures sparsity and efficiency; higher k may underfit or reduce specialization.  
  - Loss weights (α, β): Balance classification vs regularization; β preserves unimodal knowledge in Stage 2, α balances classification and disentanglement in Stage 3. Grid search recommended.  
  - Freezing strategy: Freezing encoders in Stage 3 protects unimodal features but prevents their further adaptation; may be suboptimal if encoders are initially weak.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Expert collapse:** One expert receives >80% of routing; symptoms: stagnant/dropping accuracy. Mitigate via L_moe and three-stage pretraining.  
  - **Unimodal degradation:** Disentanglement loss may hurt modality-specific performance if α too high; monitor individual branch accuracy.  
  - **Training instability:** Sudden loss spikes or high fold variance suggest poor hyperparameters or insufficient Stage 2 pretraining.  
  - **Poor generalization:** Early divergence between train/val indicates overfitting; consider reducing experts, adding dropout, or earlier stopping.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline comparison:** Run M3D-BFS and all listed baselines (GCN, BrainNPT, SVM, RandomForest, MMGNN, AL-NEGAT, Cross-GNN, RH-BrainFS, NeuroPath) on HCP and ZDXX with 10-fold CV; expect M3D-BFS to achieve highest ACC with statistical significance.  
  2. **Ablation study:** Systematically remove components: (A) no Stage 1 (random encoders), (B) no Stage 2 (direct MoE from random), (C) no L_dis_en; compare ACC to full model (Table 2).  
  3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Sweep number of experts k∈{2,4,6} and loss weights (α, β) on validation; plot accuracy heatmap (like Figure 4) to identify robust ranges; expect optimal α≈0.6, β≈0.3 on HCP.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How does inherent data bias or distribution shift across different patient populations affect the robustness and generalizability of the M3D-BFS dynamic routing mechanism? The paper notes this as future work but does not evaluate across diverse sites or demographics.
- **Question 2:** Is the proposed three-stage progressive training pipeline strictly necessary for stable MoE training in this domain, or could alternative training curricula (e.g., Two-stage, end-to-end with modified loss) achieve comparable or better performance with reduced computational cost? No ablation compares different training orders.
- **Question 3:** What is the specific contribution of the multi-modal disentanglement loss (L_disen) to the observed performance gain? Is it primarily improving orthogonality, enhancing label correlation, or both, and how does this interact with dynamic fusion? The paper does not isolate its effect or analyze representation properties.
- **Question 4:** What sample-specific characteristics (e.g., graph topological properties, modality quality, disease-related patterns) drive the dynamic routing decisions in the MoE gating networks, and do these "routes" correlate with meaningful neurobiological or clinical interpretations? No analysis links gate outputs to input features or phenotypes.
- **Question 5:** Can the M3D-BFS framework, designed for SC/FC fusion, be effectively and efficiently transferred to scenarios with more than two modalities (e.g., adding metabolic imaging) or to other domains requiring multi-modal graph fusion? The scalability to k>2 modalities is untested.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter gaps: missing values for projection dimension, expert MLP size, gating noise σ, READOUT type, loss temperatures, and final FFN architecture.
- Graph construction thresholds for SC/FC not specified; these thresholds critically affect network topology and may influence results.
- Disentanglement loss imposes orthogonality that could discard modality-shared signals; optimal weight α likely dataset-dependent.
- Evaluation restricted to two datasets (HCP, ZDXX); no test on independent cohorts or cross-site generalization.
- Training stability: risk of expert collapse despite three-stage pipeline; gating distribution statistics absent.

## Confidence
- **Dynamic routing:** Medium – mechanism described, but actual per-sample variability and its impact on accuracy not fully quantified.
- **Progressive pre-training:** High – ablations demonstrate each stage's contribution and training stability observed.
- **Disentanglement loss:** Medium – ablation shows gain, but theoretical link between orthogonality and task performance not proven; may not generalize.
- **SOTA performance:** Medium – strong numbers on given datasets, but reproducibility concerns due to incomplete details; unknown if advantage holds with alternative preprocessing.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of progressive stages:** Train models without Stage 1 (random encoders), without Stage 2 (direct MoE from random), and without disentanglement loss; compare ACC to full M3D-BFS to isolate contributions.
2. **Gating adaptivity analysis:** Compute Shannon entropy of gating weights per sample; test correlation with phenotypic traits (e.g., gender, depression score). Expect higher entropy for more heterogeneous samples.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Grid search over projection dim ∈ {64,128,256}, expert count ∈ {2,4,6}, and adjacency threshold ∈ {0.1,0.2,0.3} on validation folds; report accuracy heatmaps to identify robust ranges.
