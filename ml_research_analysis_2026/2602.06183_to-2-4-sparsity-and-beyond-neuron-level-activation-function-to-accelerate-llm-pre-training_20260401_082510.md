# To 2:4 Sparsity and Beyond: Neuron-level Activation Function to Accelerate LLM Pre-Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06183
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06183
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** Matches dense model accuracy on standard benchmarks while achieving 1.4–1.7× end-to-end training speedup.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the high computational cost of matrix multiplications in Transformer feed-forward networks (FFNs) during large language model pre-training. It introduces a method combining two hardware-accelerated sparsity formats: 2:4 weight sparsity via soft-thresholding and Venom (v:n:m) activation sparsity via a neuron-level router applied to SquaredReLU activations. Training alternates between sparse steps (with both sparsity types) and dense steps to recover accuracy. On 1B and 7B models, the approach yields 1.4–1.7× speedup with negligible accuracy loss on Eval-Harness benchmarks. The technique is orthogonal to quantization and applicable to NVIDIA A100+ GPUs.

## Method Summary
The method targets FFN layers in Transformers. Weights (W1, W2 and their transposes) are sparsified to a 2:4 pattern using soft-thresholding—a continuous operation that subtracts the second-smallest magnitude in each group of four from all elements, zeroing the two smallest. This avoids loss discontinuities during training. Activations from the SquaredReLU function (which naturally yields >90% sparsity) are routed through a learned neuron-level router: during a warmup phase, columns of W1 are clustered into experts; then each token is assigned to its nearest expert, and tokens are reordered in the batch so those sharing an expert become contiguous. This reordering formats activations into the Venom pattern, enabling highly efficient GEMMs. A training scheduler alternates between sparse steps (applying both weight and activation sparsity) and dense steps (full precision) in a model-dependent ratio (e.g., 1:1 for 1B, 1:3.5 for 7B) to recover accuracy.

## Key Results
- End-to-end training speedup of 1.4–1.7× for 1B and 7B models (1.352× for 1B, 1.387× for 7B) with negligible accuracy loss on Eval-Harness.
- Microbenchmarks confirm up to 1.5× speedup for 2:4 GEMMs and 6–8× for Venom GEMMs at >90% sparsity.
- The method matches dense baseline training loss and evaluation accuracy across ARC, PIQA, OBQA, HellaSwag, and Winogrande.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Continuous Weight Pruning via Soft-Thresholding
- **Claim:** Soft-thresholding enables stable 2:4 weight sparsification during training without degrading the loss landscape continuity.
- **Mechanism:** For each group of 4 weight elements, the function subtracts the magnitude of the second-smallest element from all values, setting the two smallest to zero. This creates a piecewise-linear, continuous transformation (unlike hard magnitude pruning), which provably avoids discontinuous loss jumps.
- **Core assumption:** The continuity induced by soft-thresholding is sufficient to maintain gradient flow and optimization dynamics comparable to dense training, even with a dynamic 50% sparsity pattern.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.2] "Naively applying a magnitude based pruning mask results in a discontinuous loss function... To mitigate this issue, we take inspiration... and apply soft-thresholding that provably removes discontinuity and results in better evaluation performance."
  - [abstract] Mentions "soft-thresholding" as part of the 2:4 weight sparsity method.
- **Break condition:** If the scale of the threshold (implicitly tied to weight distributions) becomes misaligned across layers or stages, it could cause excessive zeroing or retention of low-magnitude weights, harming expressivity.

### Mechanism 2: Neuron-Level Routing for High-Sparsity Activations
- **Claim:** A learned, per-token router reorganizes activations from SquaredReLU into the Venom (v:n:m) format, enabling 6–8× hardware-accelerated GEMMs for >90% sparse activations.
- **Mechanism:** 1) During warmup dense steps, cluster columns of the first FFN weight matrix (W1) into non-overlapping subsets (experts). 2) For each token (input row), compute its assignment to the nearest expert (via `x@ExpertMeans`). 3) Permute token rows in the batch so tokens assigned to the same expert are contiguous. 4) The resulting grouped activation matrix has a structured sparsity pattern (column subsets per group) that conforms to Venom, where after 2:4 pruning within retained columns, the overall sparsity is N/(N-2).
- **Core assumption:** Grouping tokens by expert creates a consistent, hardware-friendly sparsity pattern across the batch dimension that can be exploited by Venom GEMMs, and the routing decisions are learnable and stable enough not to disrupt training.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1] "We use a router that selects which specific features are kept for each token. We then reorder the tokens within the batch so that consecutive tokens have the same sparsity pattern... this is the Venom format."
  - [section 2.3] States SquaredReLU yields >90% sparsity and Venom gives "6-10x speedups for 80-99 percent sparsity levels."
- **Break condition:** If the routing function becomes a bottleneck (computation or memory), if token assignments are too noisy (high variance per batch), or if the permutation overhead on non-Hopper GPUs (lacking scatter/gather) dominates the compute savings.

### Mechanism 3: Alternating Sparse and Dense Steps for Accuracy Recovery
- **Claim:** A specific schedule of sparse (sparsity applied) and dense (full-precision) training steps recovers the accuracy loss inherent to persistent sparsification.
- **Mechanism:** Train for an initial phase with both weight (2:4) and activation (Venom) sparsity active to gain speedup. Then, switch to a regime of only dense computations for a tuned ratio of steps (e.g., 1:3.5 sparse:dense for 7B). The dense steps allow the model to fine-tune and adapt away errors introduced by the structured sparsity masks.
- **Core assumption:** The optimization trajectory under sparsity diverges sufficiently from the dense optimum that dedicated dense fine-tuning steps are necessary to close the gap, and this gap is bridgeable without full retraining.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Our recipe relies on sparse training steps to accelerate a large part of the pretraining, associated with regular dense training steps towards the end."
  - [section 5.2.2] "We see that we need to train densely on half the number of steps in order to recover the accuracy." Presents ablation tables showing accuracy recovery with mixed schedules.
- **Break condition:** If the required dense step ratio is too high (e.g., >50%), net speedup vanishes. If the accuracy gap is not linear in the number of dense steps, the simple fixed ratio may be sub-optimal or model-size dependent.

## Foundational Learning
### Semi-Structured Sparsity (2:4 and Venom)
- **Why needed here:** The entire acceleration strategy depends on matching sparsity patterns to NVIDIA's sparse tensor core formats (2:4) and the more flexible Venom (v:n:m) format. Understanding the fixed 2-out-of-4 pattern vs. the column-pruned Venom pattern is key to predicting speedups.
- **Quick check:** What is the fundamental hardware difference between a 2:4 sparse GEMM and a Venom GEMM, and why does Venom achieve higher speedups at >87.5% sparsity?

### Soft-Thresholding as a Continuous Pruning Operator
- **Why needed here:** It is the proposed solution to the discontinuity problem in weight sparsification during training. One must grasp how a simple element-wise shift/subtraction creates a continuous function versus a hard threshold.
- **Quick check:** Why does subtracting the second-smallest value in a group of four, rather than just thresholding at zero, yield a continuous function with respect to the input weights?

### SquaredReLU and Natural Activation Sparsity
- **Why needed here:** The high (>90%) and dynamic sparsity in activations is the prerequisite for attempting Venom sparsification. The choice of SquaredReLU over ReLU is crucial as its squaring operation exaggerates sparsity without harming model quality (per Haziza et al. 2025).
- **Quick check:** How does the mathematical form of `(ReLU(x))^2` lead to a higher proportion of exact zeros compared to `ReLU(x)` for typical pre-activation distributions in Transformers?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
FFN Block (Transformer) -> Router Module -> Sparsification Modules (Soft-Thresholding, MoEToVenom) -> Training Scheduler

### Critical path
Forward: `X -> (sparsify24(W1)) -> y1 -> SquaredReLU -> MoEToVenom(y2) -> (Venom matmul with W2)`. Backward: Algorithm 2 ensures symmetric sparsity application, with one operand sparse (2:4 or Venom) in each GEMM.

### Design tradeoffs
- *Sparsity Level vs. Speedup:* Higher activation sparsity (via Venom's M parameter) yields higher GEMM speedup (6–8×) but may increase accuracy loss. Weight sparsity is fixed at 50% (2:4).
- *Router Complexity vs. Overhead:* More experts (smaller column clusters) may improve routing granularity but increase `x@ExpertMeans` cost and scatter/gather permutation overhead.
- *Sparse:Dense Ratio vs. Total Time:* Optimal ratio (e.g., 1:1 for 1B, 1:3.5 for 7B) is model-size dependent. More dense steps improve accuracy but reduce net speedup.
- *Hardware Dependency:* Permutation step is memory-bound and may be eliminated on newer Blackwell GPUs with scatter/gather support, changing the cost-benefit.

### Failure signatures
- **Accuracy drop >0.5% on average benchmarks:** Likely due to excessive sparsity (Venom M too large), insufficient dense steps, or router instability.
- **No speedup or slowdown (<1.0×):** Overhead from routing/permutation not compensated by GEMM speedup; or sparsity pattern not properly aligned with hardware (e.g., misaligned 2:4 groups).
- **Training instability (loss spikes):** Soft-thresholding scale inappropriate for weight distribution; or router causing chaotic token reordering.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablation: Weight Sparsity Alone.** Train with only 2:4 soft-thresholding on weights (W1, W2, and their transposes) for all steps. Measure training loss degradation vs. baseline (Table 3 baseline). Expected: ~0.03–0.04 loss increase.
2. **Ablation: Activation Sparsity Format Comparison.** Train with SquaredReLU but test two paths: (a) Force activations into 2:4 sparsity (simple thresholding at high magnitude), (b) Use proposed router+Venom. Compare loss and microbenchmark GEMM speedup. Expected: Venom gives much higher speedup (6–8×) but similar loss hit to 2:4 activation sparsity.
3. **Recipe Search: Sparse:Dense Step Ratio Sweep.** For a fixed model (e.g., 1B), train with total steps fixed (e.g., 60k), varying sparse steps from 0 to 60k and dense steps filling remainder. Plot average evaluation accuracy vs. computed end-to-end speedup (Figure 5). Expected: Pareto front showing optimal tradeoff point (e.g., 30k sparse/30k dense for 1B).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Optimal balance across scales:** What is the optimal balance between 2:4 weight sparsity and Venom activation sparsity across different model scales (e.g., 70B, 405B) to maximize end-to-end speedup without accuracy loss? The paper studies tradeoffs for 1B/7B but theoretical analysis suggests higher speedups for larger models.
2. **Routing overhead at scale:** How does the overhead of the neuron-level routing and permutation scale with model dimension and sequence length, and does it negate the theoretical Venom GEMM speedup for very large hidden states? The claim that overhead is negligible is based on small-scale timing.
3. **Mechanism of accuracy recovery:** How does the alternating sparse/dense step schedule recover accuracy? The paper observes empirical recovery but does not explain whether dense steps act as finetuning or reshape the optimization trajectory.
4. **Transferability to other activations:** How transferable is the neuron-level router (learned during pre-training with SquaredReLU) to other activation functions (e.g., GELU, SiLU) or downstream fine-tuning tasks? The paper only evaluates pre-trained models on standard benchmarks.
5. **Compounding with other optimizations:** What is the upper bound on speedup achievable by combining this sparsity method with other orthogonal techniques like FP8 quantization or Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing, and are there compounding overheads? Orthogonality is claimed but not tested.

## Limitations
- The exact implementation details of Venom-compatible GEMM kernels and data layout for A100/H100 are not fully specified, which is critical for reproducibility.
- The neuron-level router's clustering algorithm (number of experts, metric, frequency) and permutation implementation cost on non-Hopper GPUs remain unclear.
- The method's dependence on SquaredReLU is not tested for generalization; its effectiveness with standard ReLU or GELU is unknown.

2. **Schedule Pareto Frontier Replication:** For a 1B model, systematically sweep the sparse:dense step ratio (e.g., 1:0, 2:1, 1:1, 1:2, 0:1) with a fixed total step budget. Plot the trade-off curve between final evaluation accuracy and predicted end-to-end speedup (using measured sparse/dense step times) to independently locate the optimal operating point.
3. **Activation Function Generality Test:** Replace SquaredReLU with standard ReLU in the same router+Venom framework for a short pre-training run (e.g., 5k steps). Measure (a) the achieved activation sparsity percentage and its variance across batches, and (b) the stability of expert assignments. A severe drop in sparsity or massive assignment churn would confirm the method's current dependence on SquaredReLU.
