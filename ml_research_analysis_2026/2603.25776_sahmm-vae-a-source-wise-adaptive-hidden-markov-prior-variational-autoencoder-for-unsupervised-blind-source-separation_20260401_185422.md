# SAHMM-VAE: A Source-Wise Adaptive Hidden Markov Prior Variational Autoencoder for Unsupervised Blind Source Separation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25776
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25776
- **Reference count:** 3
- **Primary result:** SAHMM-VAE achieves near-perfect source recovery (correlation ~1) on synthetic BSS tasks via source-wise adaptive HMM priors and joint optimization.

## Executive Summary
SAHMM-VAE introduces a novel framework for unsupervised blind source separation by embedding source-specific adaptive Hidden Markov Model priors into a variational autoencoder. Each latent dimension is paired with its own HMM prior, and separation emerges through joint optimization of the encoder, decoder, and priors. The KL divergence term couples each posterior trajectory to its dedicated prior, causing latent dimensions to specialize according to distinct source dynamics. Three prior branches—Gaussian-emission, Markov-switching autoregressive, and state-flow HMM—all yield stable convergence and high-fidelity source recovery, though increased expressiveness may reduce interpretability of hidden-state paths. The results demonstrate that structured temporal priors can induce separation without supervision and that the encoder learns an approximate demixing inverse under this guidance.

## Method Summary
SAHMM-VAE formulates BSS as a variational inference problem where each latent dimension corresponds to one source and is regularized by an adaptive HMM prior. The encoder maps observations to posterior means µ_t for each source; a per-source variance σ_j² defines the posterior. Latent samples s_t,j = µ_t,j + σ_jϵ_t,j are fed to the decoder to reconstruct observations. Critically, each source j has its own HMM prior (initial distribution π_j, transition matrix A_j, and branch-specific state emission parameters). The prior log-likelihood log p(s_:,j) is computed via the forward algorithm, and the ELBO balances reconstruction loss (MSE) with a KL term coupling each posterior to its prior. All components—encoder, decoder, per-source variances, and HMM parameters—are trained end-to-end. Three branches implement different state emissions: (I) Gaussian per-state mean/variance; (II) Markov-switching AR coefficients; (III) state-flow normalizing transformations for complex innovations.

## Key Results
- Near-perfect source recovery: correlations with ground-truth sources approach 1 across all three prior branches.
- Stable convergence: The model trains reliably, and adaptive priors successfully capture distinct source-wise temporal organizations.
- Separation emerges from KL coupling: Bidirectional optimization aligns posterior trajectories with heterogeneous HMM priors, driving latent specialization.
- Trade-off with expressiveness: More expressive priors (state-flow) maintain reconstruction fidelity but may obscure interpretability of hidden-state sequences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: KL coupling between posterior and prior drives separation.**  
Each latent dimension is paired with its own adaptive HMM prior. The KL term repeatedly compares the posterior to its dedicated prior; during joint optimization, latent dimensions align with different prior configurations, pulling them toward explaining different source processes. This bidirectional coupling makes separation an intrinsic byproduct rather than a post-hoc step. Core assumption: sources have distinct temporal switching structures. Evidence: Paper states that "gradual alignment between posterior source trajectories and heterogeneous HMM priors becomes the mechanism." Break condition: if all posteriors converge similarly despite different priors (e.g., due to dominant reconstruction loss), separation fails.

**Mechanism 2: Encoder learns an approximate demixing inverse.**  
The decoder models the generative mixing process, while the encoder is optimized to produce latents that both reconstruct observations and conform to source-specific priors. This dual pressure implicitly trains the encoder to invert the mixing transformation. Core assumption: mixing is sufficiently structured and model capacity adequate. Evidence: Abstract claims encoder "progressively learns an inference map that behaves like an approximate inverse." Break condition: if decoder collapses or learns a degenerate mixing map, the inverse pressure dissipates.

**Mechanism 3: Expressiveness reduces state uniqueness while preserving reconstruction.**  
More expressive state-conditional densities (e.g., flows) allow a single source trajectory to fit multiple combinations of state sequence and flow parameters, weakening identifiability of the discrete hidden-state path. Reconstruction depends on correct source trajectories, not a unique state segmentation. Core assumption: source signals contain complex local variations absorbable by either switches or state-wise transformations. Evidence: Section 4.4 notes that greater representational freedom admits "multiple plausible explanations" and looser state identifiability. Break condition: if expressiveness leads to posterior collapse or over-regularization (e.g., all data explained by one state), both reconstruction and meaningful switching suffer.

## Foundational Learning
- **Variational Autoencoder (VAE) ELBO and posterior collapse**: Needed because SAHMM-VAE builds on the VAE framework; understanding the reconstruction/KL balance is crucial to see how prior matching drives separation. Quick check: If β is too low, what happens? (Answer: Latents may ignore priors and fail to specialize.)
- **Hidden Markov Model (HMM) forward algorithm**: Needed because each source prior is an HMM; log p(s_:,j) is computed via forward recursion. Quick check: Why run in log-space? (Answer: To avoid underflow over long T; log-sum-exp ensures numerical stability.)
- **Source-wise parameterization in latent variable models**: Needed because per-source priors and per-source posterior variances break symmetry across latent dimensions, enabling heterogeneity. Quick check: In Eq. (4), why is σ²_j shared across time for a source j? (Answer: Reduces parameters and assumes stationary uncertainty per source, while allowing different noise levels across sources.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Encoder fϕ (maps Y → µ_t) → Sampling (s_t,j = µ_t,j + σ_jϵ_t,j) → Decoder gθ (maps s_t → ŷ_t). Each source j also has its own HMM prior (π_j, A_j, state parameters) that scores s_:,j via forward algorithm.

**Critical path:**  
1. Initialize encoder, decoder, per-source σ_j², and HMM parameters (π_j, A_j, state parameters).  
2. Forward pass: compute µ_t = fϕ(y_t), sample s_t, reconstruct ŷ_t = gθ(s_t).  
3. Compute reconstruction loss L_rec.  
4. For each source j, compute log p(s_:,j) using its HMM and branch-specific scoring.  
5. Compute posterior log-density log q_ϕ(S|Y) from µ_t, σ_j.  
6. Form total loss L = L_rec + β (log q_ϕ - Σ_j log p_j) and backpropagate through all components.  
7. Repeat until convergence (monitor source correlations and loss).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Prior complexity vs. interpretability: Branch I (Gaussian) yields clear state segmentation; Branch III (flow) may fit waveforms better but obscures state paths. Choose based on need for discrete labels.  
- β scheduling: Fixed β may imbalance reconstruction/prior; warm-up (slowly increase β) helps avoid early collapse into poor local optima.  
- Number of states K: Too few underfits switching; too many over-segments. Start with K ≈ expected number of source regimes.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Collapsed latents: All source correlations low, latent trajectories nearly identical. Likely cause: β too low or priors failing to differentiate.  
- Unstable training: Loss spikes/diverges. Likely cause: learning rates too high for HMM parameters or flow Jacobian instability.  
- Poor state recovery (Branch II/III): Source correlations high but true state match low. Could be inherent (expressive prior absorbs variation) or a failure (check transition matrices for uniformity/diagonality).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Convergence sanity check: Train all three branches on a simple 2-source synthetic dataset with known switching (e.g., one source with two amplitude regimes). Verify total loss decreases and per-source Pearson correlation > 0.95.  
2. Prior adaptation visualization: For each source j, plot learned state means (Branch I) or state-conditional AR coefficients (Branch II) across epochs. They should diverge across sources and stabilize.  
3. State-path sensitivity: For Branch III, artificially smooth one source’s waveform. Check if inferred state path for that source becomes less switchy while reconstruction remains accurate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Under what conditions can source-wise adaptive HMM priors guarantee identifiability of latent sources in nonlinear BSS, considering permutation invariance and posterior uncertainty?
2. How can we design regularizers or architectural constraints that preserve the expressive power of state-flow HMM priors while improving the uniqueness and interpretability of inferred hidden-state sequences?
3. Does the SAHMM-VAE framework remain robust under more challenging mixing conditions (e.g., strongly nonlinear, noisy, or underdetermined mixtures) and how do the three prior branches comparatively handle such scenarios?
4. What is the precise role of the KL coupling term in driving latent dimensions to separate—does it consistently encourage decorrelation between source-specific prior trajectories, and can this be formalized as an implicit independence-inducing mechanism?

## Limitations
- Architectural details (layer sizes, activations) and hyperparameters (β schedule, learning rates) are omitted, hindering exact replication and full robustness assessment.
- Experiments are limited to synthetic linear mixtures; performance under noisy, nonlinear, or underdetermined conditions remains untested.
- The core mechanism relies on each source having a distinct temporal switching structure; applicability to sources with similar dynamics or non-switching patterns is unclear.
