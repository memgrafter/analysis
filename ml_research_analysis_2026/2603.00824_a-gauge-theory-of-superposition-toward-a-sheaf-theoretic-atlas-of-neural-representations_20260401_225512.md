# A Gauge Theory of Superposition: Toward a Sheaf-Theoretic Atlas of Neural Representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: `2603.00824`
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00824
- **Reference count**: 27
- **Primary result**: Introduces a sheaf-theoretic atlas framework for neural representations, defining three measurable obstructions to global interpretability—local jamming, proxy shearing, and nontrivial holonomy—and proves four core results instantiated on Llama-3.2-3B and other models.

## Executive Summary
The paper shows that a single global feature dictionary cannot explain large language model (LLM) representations across diverse contexts. Instead, contexts are modeled as a stratified complex where each stratum has a local feature space equipped with a Fisher information metric. Three measurable obstructions—local jamming (exceeding Fisher bandwidth), proxy shearing (transport-proxy mismatch), and nontrivial holonomy (path dependence)—are defined and rigorously quantified. Four core theorems are proven and empirically validated on Llama-3.2-3B and other models, demonstrating that these obstructions are prevalent and computable. The framework provides a mathematical toolkit for diagnosing superposition and lack of global consistency in neural representations.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a sheaf-theoretic atlas from a neural network. First, activations at a given layer are clustered into charts. For each chart, a PCA basis defines a local feature space and the Fisher/Gauss-Newton metric quantifies predictive interactions. A k-NN graph on chart centroids defines adjacencies. Overlapping activations between adjacent charts are used to estimate a linear transport map via ridge regression. A proxy alignment is derived from the overlap of PCA bases (Procrustes). The discrepancy between transport and proxy defines the shearing score on each edge. Holonomy is computed by fixing a gauge on a spanning tree of the largest connected component, making tree-edge defects vanish; chord defects then equal fundamental-cycle holonomy. Jamming is quantified per chart by the ratio of active features (via sparse coding) to the effective rank of the Fisher metric. The three metrics (D_shear, D_hol, J) diagnose local superposition, cross-chart mismatch, and global inconsistency.

## Key Results
- Four core theorems proven and empirically validated: (A) spanning-tree gauge identity makes holonomy computable via chord defects; (B) shearing score lower-bounds transfer mismatch energy; (C) certified jamming/interference lower bounds hold with high coverage and zero slack violations across seeds/hyperparameters; (D) bootstrap and sample-size experiments show stable estimation of shearing and holonomy, especially on persistent subsystems.
- Empirical metrics on Llama-3.2-3B: mean normalized shearing \(D_{\text{shear}} \approx 0.20\), mean normalized holonomy \(D_{\text{hol}} \approx 0.55\), and high jamming coverage.
- Certificates: slack \(\ge 1\) always; tree residuals \(\sim 10^{-5}\); chord-holonomy agreement \(\sim 10^{-6}\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Local Jamming
- **Claim**: Local jamming occurs when the active feature count exceeds the effective Fisher bandwidth within a chart, forcing superposition and interference.
- **Mechanism**: The jamming index \(J(c) = k_{\text{active}}(c) / R_{\text{eff}}(G(c))\) compares participation-ratio active count to effective rank of the Fisher metric \(G(c)\). When \(J(c)>1\), more features are used than the predictively consequential subspace dimensions, implying forced overlap.
- **Core assumption**: The Fisher/Gauss-Newton metric correctly identifies which feature interactions matter for prediction; sparse codes capture locally active feature usage.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "Local jamming (exceeded Fisher bandwidth)" 
  - Sections 3.4-3.5, 6.2: Definition of \(J(c)\), Theorem 6.2 gives certified interference lower bound on \(\tau\)-consequential subsets.
- **Break condition**: Fisher metric extremely ill-conditioned (\(R_{\text{eff}}\) near zero) or sparse coding fails to capture true local usage.

### Proxy Shearing
- **Claim**: Proxy shearing quantifies unavoidable cross-chart mismatch between the geometry-derived transport and a fixed correspondence proxy, lower-bounding transfer mismatch energy.
- **Mechanism**: For each edge \((u,v)\), compute orthogonal polar factors: \(Q_{vu}\) from estimated transport (ridge regression on overlap codes) and fixed proxy \(P_{vu}\) from PCA basis overlap (Procrustes). Shearing score \(D_{\text{shear}}(u,v) = \|Q_{vu} - P_{vu}\|_F / \sqrt{2k}\). Theorem 4.2 proves \(\Delta_{uv} = \mathbb{E}[\|(Q_{vu}-P_{vu})z_u\|^2] \ge \lambda_{\text{min}}(\Sigma_u) \|Q_{vu}-P_{vu}\|_F^2\).
- **Core assumption**: The PCA-based proxy \(P_{vu}\) captures "true" semantic alignment between charts; transport estimation is reasonably accurate given overlap sample size.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "proxy shearing (mismatch between geometric transport and a fixed correspondence proxy)" 
  - Sections 4.1-4.2, 9.3: Defines Proxy, Theorem 4.2, Figures 2-3 show edge-wise LB check (points above diagonal).
- **Break condition**: Chosen proxy poorly aligned with semantics, or overlap covariance \(\Sigma_u\) extremely anisotropic (\(\lambda_{\text{min}} \approx 0\)) making bound vacuous.

### Nontrivial Holonomy
- **Claim**: Nontrivial holonomy indicates path-dependent transport that obstructs global trivialization, meaning no single consistent feature dictionary exists across contexts.
- **Mechanism**: After constructive gauge fixing on a spanning tree (Theorem 5.1), tree-edge defects vanish and each chord residual equals fundamental-cycle holonomy. Large \(D_{\text{hol}}(\gamma) = \|h_\gamma - I\|_F / \sqrt{2k}\) shows inconsistency around cycles.
- **Core assumption**: Closed-loop transport should compose to near-identity if semantics are globally consistent; the graph is connected enough to have cycles.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "nontrivial holonomy (path-dependent transport around loops)" 
  - Sections 5.1-5.2, 9.2: Theorem 5.1, Table 1 shows tree residuals ~\(10^{-5}\), chord/holonomy agreement ~\(10^{-6}\).
- **Break condition**: Context graph has too few cycles (e.g., after aggressive persistence filtering) or transport estimates are so noisy that gauge fixing becomes numerically unstable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Information Geometry (Fisher/Gauss-Newton metric)**:  
  *Why needed*: Provides a local, predictively consequential notion of feature interaction strength, weighting interference energy meaningfully rather than treating all overlaps equally.  
  *Quick check*: Why does the interference energy \(E(c)\) use the off-diagonal entries of the diagonal-normalized Fisher matrix \(\tilde{G}(c)\) instead of raw Fisher entries?

- **Cellular Sheaves / Atlas Construction**:  
  *Why needed*: Formalizes the local-to-global problem: feature dictionaries are locally defined (charts) with transition maps (transports) on overlaps; obstructions live in a sheaf cohomology-like structure.  
  *Quick check*: How does a stratified simplicial complex (context complex) differ from a single global feature space, and why is this distinction crucial for superposition?

- **Gauge Theory on Graphs**:  
  *Why needed*: Holonomy is gauge-invariant; the spanning-tree gauge (Theorem 5.1) provides a constructive, computable way to extract path dependence without arbitrary choices.  
  *Quick check*: In the gauge transformation \(g_{vu} \mapsto U_v g_{vu} U_u^\top\), what do the \(U_c\) represent, and why does choosing them according to a spanning tree make tree defects vanish?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Activations & Clustering → Local Bases → Graph Construction → Overlap Construction → Transport Estimation → Proxy Computation → Defect Assembly → (Holonomy Calculation, Shearing Score, Jamming Index)
- **Critical path**: Clustering → Overlap construction → Transport estimation → Proxy computation → Defect assembly → (Holonomy OR Shearing OR Jamming). Failures in transport estimation cascade to all downstream diagnostics.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Atlas granularity \(C\)**: Fine (large \(C\)) → more local charts, purer semantics but sparser overlap graph; Coarse (small \(C\)) → fewer charts, more data per transport but higher intra-chart interference risk.
  - **Support dimension \(k\)**: Larger \(k\) → more expressivity per chart, preserves variance; Smaller \(k\) → more stable polar factors, cheaper computation, but may discard predictive modes.
  - **Overlap threshold**: Higher threshold → better-conditioned \(\hat{T}_{vu}\) (smaller polar factor perturbation) but fewer edges; lower threshold → denser graph but fragile estimates.
  - **Proxy choice**: PCA Procrustes is deterministic; alternative proxies (e.g., token co-occurrence) would change \(D_{\text{shear}}\) and mismatch interpretation.
  - **Transport class**: Linear ridge is simple and stable; nonlinear transports could capture richer relationships but break polar-factor analysis and Theorem 4.2.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Gauge failure**: Tree residual mean > \(10^{-3}\) indicates gauge construction instability (poor conditioning or disconnected LCC).
  - **Holonomy identity failure**: Chord residual ≠ holonomy defect by > \(10^{-6}\) suggests bug in loop product or gauge computation.
  - **Shearing bound violation**: Any edge with slack \(< 1 - \text{tol}\) (\(\text{tol}\sim 10^{-6}\)) indicates implementation error in lower-bound computation.
  - **Jamming certification collapse**: Zero charts with positive LB, or any certified slack < 1, indicates either Fisher rank ≤ \(r\) or failure to find \(\tau\)-consequential subset; adjust \(m\), \(\alpha\), or \(\tau^\star\).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Sanity check (Theorem 5.1)**: Build LCC, compute spanning tree gauge, verify tree defects ≈ 0 and chord defects ≈ holonomy defects (mean absolute diff < \(10^{-5}\)). If not, debug transport/proxy pipelines.
  2. **Shearing lower bound (Theorem 4.2)**: For each edge, compute \(\Delta_{uv}\) and LB\(_{uv}\); plot \(\Delta_{uv}\) vs. LB\(_{uv}\) (should be above diagonal) and vs. \(D_{\text{shear}}\) (monotonic). Heavy-tailed slack indicates many edges with tiny \(\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Sigma_u)\).
  3. **Jamming certification (Theorem 6.2)**: For each chart, run subset search for \(\tau\)-consequential \(A\); record if LB > 0, check \(E_A^{(r)} / \text{LB} \ge 1\), track coverage rate across charts. If coverage < 50%, consider increasing dictionary width \(m\) or relaxing \(\tau^\star\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to nonlinear local transports**  
   - *Basis*: Limitations section states: "Nonlinear transports may be required for certain strata, and extending the framework to richer local map classes is an important direction for future work."  
   - *Why unresolved*: Current theory relies on linear transports for analytic tractability; nonlinear maps would change transport estimation, polar factor computation, and holonomy composition, potentially introducing new failure modes not captured by the three obstructions.  
   - *Resolution evidence*: Develop a nonlinear transport estimator (e.g., kernel regression or neural nets), define analogous defect and holonomy concepts, prove stability bounds, and empirically compare shearing/holonomy distributions between linear and nonlinear versions on the same atlas.

2. **Sensitivity to correspondence proxy**  
   - *Basis*: Limitations: "Correspondence-proxy dependence. ˆPvu is a proxy; conclusions about 'mismatch' are conditional on the proxy definition."  
   - *Why unresolved*: The paper uses a fixed Procrustes proxy from PCA bases; different proxies (e.g., based on sparse code alignment or gradient similarity) could yield substantially different \(D_{\text{shear}}\) and \(D_{\text{hol}}\) values, altering the interpretation of cross-chart mismatch.  
   - *Resolution evidence*: Systematically vary the proxy construction (e.g., use different feature alignment methods) on the same atlas and transports; compute correlations between proxy-induced changes and external criteria like task transferability; identify proxies that maximize predictive power.

3. **Topological interpretation of discrete holonomy**  
   - *Basis*: Limitations: "Nontrivial holonomy obstructs a connection-compatible trivialisation under the learned transports ... it does not prove topological nontriviality of an underlying continuous bundle."  
   - *Why unresolved*: The holonomy measured is on a finite graph with respect to a fixed proxy; it is not known whether this discrete invariant corresponds to continuous topological invariants (e.g., curvature, Chern classes) of the latent representation.  
   - *Resolution evidence*: Create synthetic models with known continuous latent topologies (e.g., torus, sphere) and compute the discrete holonomy; derive a limiting theorem connecting discrete holonomy to holonomy of a learnable connection; test for correlations with topological data analysis descriptors.

4. **Tightening the jamming lower bound**  
   - *Basis*: Inferred from reported large slack (median ~7-8) and discussion of conservativeness due to small \(\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Sigma_u)\) and weight floor \(\tau^\star\).  
   - *Why unresolved*: Large slack means the lower bound is far from the actual measured interference energy, limiting its utility for precise assessment of superposition severity.  
   - *Resolution evidence*: Develop improved weight selection strategies (e.g., adaptive \(\tau^\star\) per chart) or incorporate second-order covariance structure into the bound; validate on toy superposition models where true interference is known; demonstrate reduced slack without sacrificing coverage or correctness.

5. **Correlation with downstream performance and safety**  
   - *Basis*: Limitations: "we do not exhaust all possible ... downstream task-level operational validations, which remain directions for future work."  
   - *Why unresolved*: The framework provides diagnostics but their practical impact on model behavior is unclear; connecting them to performance metrics could ground interpretability in operational outcomes.  
   - *Resolution evidence*: Compute atlas-level summaries (e.g., \(J_{\text{atlas}}\), \(S_{\text{atlas}}\), \(H_{\text{atlas}}\)) across multiple models and layers; correlate with standard benchmark scores, adversarial robustness metrics, and safety evaluations; perform interventions that artificially increase specific obstructions and measure resulting performance drops.

## Limitations
- Core assumptions (Fisher metric validity, sparse code accuracy, PCA proxy alignment) may fail under poor conditioning, insufficient data, or nonlinear feature interactions.
- Empirical evaluation is limited to a small set of models (mainly Llama-3.2-3B and a few others); broader cross-architecture and scale studies are needed.
- The linear transport assumption could miss important nonlinear relationships, potentially distorting shearing and holonomy measurements.

## Confidence
- **(A) Holonomy identity**: High (theorem + near‑zero tree residuals, chord/holonomy agreement ~\(10^{-6}\)).
- **(B) Shearing lower bound**: Medium (theorem holds; empirical slack sometimes near 1; proxy sensitivity unknown).
- **(C) Jamming certification**: High (zero slack violations, high coverage in tested models).
- **(D) Stability**: Medium (bootstrap stable on persistent subsystems; generalizability uncertain).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑model robustness**: Apply pipeline to at least three additional model families (e.g., GPT‑4, Mistral, T5) and compare distributions of \(D_{\text{shear}}\), \(D_{\text{hol}}\), and \(J\).
2. **Proxy sensitivity**: Replace PCA Procrustes proxy with attention‑ or token‑cooccurrence‑based alignment; recompute shearing and bound slack.
3. **Controlled superposition test**: Train a small network with known feature collision (e.g., linear autoencoder with limited hidden dimension) and verify \(J>1\) and nontrivial holonomy.
