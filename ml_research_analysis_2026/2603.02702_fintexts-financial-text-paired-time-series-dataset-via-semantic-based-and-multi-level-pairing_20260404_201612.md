# FinTexTS: Financial Text-Paired Time-Series Dataset via Semantic-Based and Multi-Level Pairing

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.02702
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02702
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** A semantic-based, multi-level news-text pairing framework constructed the FinTexTS dataset (~1M articles for 100 companies), which consistently reduced forecasting error (e.g., Autoformer MSE from 0.156 to 0.083) across 12 models versus keyword-based methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the insufficiency of keyword-matching for linking financial news to stock price movements, which fails to capture nuanced, hierarchical market dependencies. It introduces a semantic-based and multi-level pairing framework. The framework extracts company-specific context from SEC filings, employs a fine-tuned embedding model for semantically similar news retrieval, and uses an LLM to classify news into four market-relevance levels (macro, sector, related company, target company). This process yielded the FinTexTS dataset—a large-scale, five-year resource spanning 100 companies and nearly one million news articles. Experiments across 12 forecasting models demonstrate that semantic pairing significantly outperforms traditional keyword baselines, and that progressively incorporating multi-level textual information further enhances stock price prediction accuracy. Benefits are amplified when using curated, proprietary news sources.

## Method Summary
The methodology constructs FinTexTS through a three-stage pipeline. First, company-specific context is extracted from SEC 10-K/10-Q filings to establish a semantic foundation. Second, a finetuned embedding model retrieves news articles semantically related to this context, moving beyond keyword overlap. Third, an LLM classifies each retrieved article into one of four hierarchical levels (macro, sector, related company, target company) based on its primary market focus. These multi-level textual signals are then paired with corresponding stock price time-series for each company. The framework's efficacy is validated by training and evaluating 12 distinct time-series forecasting models on these pairings, comparing performance against a keyword-based pairing baseline.

## Key Results
*   FinTexTS is a large-scale dataset covering 100 companies over five years with approximately one million semantically paired news articles.
*   Semantic-based pairing consistently and significantly outperforms keyword-based baselines across all 12 tested forecasting models (e.g., Autoformer MSE reduced from 0.156 to 0.083).
*   Progressive incorporation of multi-level textual information (macro to target company) yields incremental forecasting performance gains, which are further amplified when using proprietary, curated news sources (LSEG MRN).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Semantic-based retrieval captures contextual and conceptual relevance between news and a company's underlying business fundamentals (from SEC filings), overcoming the lexical gap of keyword matching. The multi-level classification introduces a crucial hierarchical dimension, allowing models to weigh news based on its scope of market impact—from broad economic events to firm-specific announcements. This structure mirrors the nested nature of market drivers, enabling the forecasting model to learn distinct influence patterns for different news tiers. The combination provides a richer, more causally aligned textual signal than undifferentiated or lexically matched news.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Semantic Text Embedding:** Maps text to dense vector spaces where meaning is preserved. *Why needed:* To find news relevant to a company's business context without exact keyword overlap. *Quick check:* Verify nearest neighbors to a firm's SEC filing excerpt include related business news, not just documents sharing ticker mentions.
*   **Multi-Financial Market Hierarchy:** The concept that market movements are influenced by news at different scopes (macro-economy, sector trends, peer companies, the firm itself). *Why needed:* To structure textual data according to its presumed causal reach on a target stock. *Quick check:* Confirm LLM classification shows coherent separation (e.g., Fed rate news labeled 'macro,' competitor earnings labeled 'related company').
*   **Time-Series Forecasting with Exogenous Text:** Integrating external textual features as covariates for numerical price sequence prediction. *Why needed:* To leverage unstructured information for improving numerical forecasts. *Quick check:* A simple model (e.g., Linear Regression) using text features should beat a naive historical average baseline.
*   **LLM for Zero/Few-Shot Text Classification:** Using large language models for categorization without extensive task-specific training. *Why needed:* To efficiently label news articles into the four predefined semantic levels at scale. *Quick check:* Sample manual review of 100 LLM classifications should show acceptable agreement with a human annotator (e.g., >85%).
*   **Dataset Curation via Document-Entity Pairing:** The core task of aligning unstructured text documents (news) with structured time-series data (prices) for specific entities (companies). *Why needed:* The fundamental data construction step for any text-augmented financial forecasting task. *Quick check:* For a given news article, the paired stock's price series should exhibit a measurable reaction (e.g., volatility increase) in a window after publication.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** SEC Filings (Context Source) -> Company-Specific Context Extraction -> Fine-Tuned Embedding Model -> Semantic News Retrieval from Raw Corpus -> LLM Classifier -> Four-Level News Taxonomies (Macro/Sector/Related/Target) -> News-Stock Pairing Integration -> Paired Time-Series Dataset (FinTexTS) -> Forecasting Model Training/Evaluation.

**Critical Path:** 1) Extract context from SEC filings for each company. 2) Use embedding model to filter and retrieve semantically relevant news from a large corpus. 3) Classify retrieved news into the four levels. 4) Align each news article with the target company's stock price time-series. 5) Train and evaluate forecasting models on the paired dataset.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The framework trades perfect news-stock causal alignment for scalable automation (using LLMs vs. human annotation). It prioritizes semantic relevance over real-time speed, as the retrieval and classification pipeline is computationally heavy. Using a proprietary news source (LSEG MRN) improves quality but severely limits reproducibility and generalizability.

**Failure Signatures:** *Semantic retrieval failure:* Retrieved news is thematically distant from the company's SEC-described business. *Classification failure:* LLM misassigns levels (e.g., sector news as macro), diluting the hierarchical signal. *Pairing failure:* Temporal misalignment between news timestamp and price series, or incorrect company association. *Model failure:* No performance gain over keyword baseline, suggesting the semantic signal is too noisy or the forecasting models are inadequate.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Sanity Check - Single Company:** Run the full pipeline for one company (e.g., AAPL). Manually inspect 50 retrieved/classified news articles to validate semantic relevance and correct level assignment.
2.  **Baseline Comparison - Core Claim:** Replicate the main experiment: train a standard model (e.g., Autoformer) on FinTexTS (semantic, multi-level) vs. the keyword-paired dataset. Confirm the MSE reduction.
3.  **Ablation - News Level Utility:** Systemically remove one news level at a time (e.g., train with only 'target company' level news, then with 'target' + 'related', etc.). Measure the incremental performance drop from removing higher-level (macro/sector) news to quantify each tier's contribution.

## Limitations
*   Dataset novelty and quality lack external validation, with a low neighbor FMR (0.44) and zero citations.
*   Framework reproducibility is limited by reliance on the proprietary LSEG MRN news source.
*   LLM-based multi-level classification introduces potential subjective noise, with unquantified error rates.
*   Absence of pre-specified hypotheses increases the risk of post-hoc analysis in evaluating the multi-level approach.

## Confidence
*   **FinTexTS dataset construction:** Medium. Scale is substantive, but curation details for the proprietary news source are opaque.
*   **Performance gains vs. keyword baselines:** High. Improvement is consistent across 12 diverse forecasting models with a clear, large MSE drop.
*   **Utility of multi-level LLM classification:** Medium. Performance improves with added levels, but the specific error profile and impact of LLM misclassification are not quantified.

## Next Checks
1.  **External Replication:** Implement the pairing framework on an independent, publicly available dataset (e.g., using Bloomberg or CRSP news and price data) to test for dataset-specific artifacts.
2.  **Ablation by News Level:** Conduct a systematic ablation study, removing each of the four news classification levels in turn during model training to isolate and quantify the isolated contribution of each tier.
3.  **Error-Period Analysis:** Segment forecasting errors by distinct market regimes (e.g., high volatility periods, earnings seasons, recession signals) to verify the robustness of the semantic pairing gains and identify where the model fails.
