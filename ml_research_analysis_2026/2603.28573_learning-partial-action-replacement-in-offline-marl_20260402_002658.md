# Learning Partial Action Replacement in Offline MARL

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28573  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28573  
- **Reference count:** 29  
- **Primary result:** PLCQL adaptively selects which agents’ actions to replace via a learned PAR policy, reducing computational cost by enumerating only one subset per update while achieving state-of-the-art performance on 66% of offline MARL tasks.

## Executive Summary
Offline multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) suffers from exponential growth of the joint action space, leading to poor coverage of off-policy actions in static datasets. This paper introduces PLCQL, a method that mitigates this issue by formulating partial action replacement (PAR) as a contextual bandit. Instead of enumerating all possible subsets of agents whose actions are replaced, PLCQL learns a state-dependent policy that samples a single subset size per bootstrap state. The selection is guided by an uncertainty-weighted reward that penalizes high-variance Q-estimates, ensuring conservative updates. Theoretically, the value error is bounded linearly by the expected number of replaced agents. Empirically, PLCQL attains the highest normalized scores on 66% of tasks across MPE, MaMuJoCo, and SMAC benchmarks, outperforms SPaCQL on 84% of tasks, and reduces per-iteration Q evaluations from n to 1, yielding substantial computational savings.

## Method Summary
PLCQL builds on conservative Q-learning by integrating a learned Partial Action Replacement (PAR) policy. Given an offline dataset, the algorithm maintains an ensemble of Q-networks for value estimation and uncertainty quantification. During each update, for a transition (s, a, r, s', a'), it samples a subset size k from the PAR policy π_par(s'), then uniformly selects k agents to replace their next actions a' with actions from the current agent policies, while anchoring the remaining agents to the dataset actions. The target Q-value uses the minimum across the ensemble to avoid overestimation. A conservative penalty (from CFCQL) further regularizes the Q-loss. The PAR policy is trained via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) using a reward that combines the Q-estimate for the constructed joint action and an uncertainty weight derived from the ensemble standard deviation; this steers the bandit away from unreliable updates. Each agent's individual policy is updated by policy gradient with respect to the Q-ensemble. The entire system is trained end-to-end from the offline data without additional interaction.

## Key Results
- PLCQL achieves the highest normalized scores on 66% of tasks across MPE, MaMuJoCo, and SMAC.
- It outperforms SPaCQL on 84% of tasks while reducing per-iteration Q-function evaluations from n to 1.
- Ablations confirm that adaptive PAR selection and uncertainty weighting are critical for performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Contextual Bandit for Adaptive Subset Selection
- **Claim:** Formulating PAR subset selection as a contextual bandit enables state-adaptive selection of how many agents to replace, improving the balance between policy improvement and conservative value estimation.
- **Mechanism:** The PAR policy π_par(s') maps the bootstrap state s' to a distribution over subset sizes k ∈ {1,...,n}. At each update, a single k is sampled, and a uniform random subset of k agents has its actions replaced by the current policy while others are anchored to dataset actions. This replaces the costly enumeration of all configurations with a single, context-aware decision.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal number of agents whose actions should be replaced is state-dependent and can be learned from offline data via reward signals derived from the value update.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - The abstract and Section 4 detail the contextual bandit formulation and PPO training.
  - Ablations show that a fixed k (either 1 or n) underperforms the adaptive policy.
- **Break condition:** If the state distribution is extremely narrow or the reward signal uninformative, the bandit may not learn a meaningful state-dependent policy.

### Mechanism 2: Uncertainty-Weighted Reward for Conservative Updates
- **Claim:** An uncertainty-weighted reward for the PAR policy steers it away from subset selections that produce high-variance Q-estimates, thereby prioritizing value updates with reliable dataset support.
- **Mechanism:** For a selected (s', k, a'(k)), the reward r_par = (σ(-u_Q·T) + 0.5) * Q_θ1(s', a'(k)), where u_Q is the standard deviation across a Q-ensemble. High ensemble disagreement reduces the reward, discouraging the bandit from choosing k values that lead to out-of-distribution joint actions.
- **Core assumption:** Ensemble disagreement is a valid proxy for the reliability of a Q-estimate; actions with high variance are poorly covered and risk poor policy updates.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Section 4.1 defines the reward and motivation.
  - Section 5.4 ablation removing the uncertainty penalty (w=1) degrades performance, especially on Medium-Replay datasets.
- **Break condition:** If the Q-ensemble is poorly calibrated or lacks diversity, the uncertainty signal becomes noisy or misleading.

### Mechanism 3: Theoretical Error Bound via Expected Deviations
- **Claim:** The value estimation error under the PLCQL operator is bounded linearly by the expected number of deviating agents (E[k|s] under π_par), linking PAR behavior to learning stability.
- **Mechanism:** Each Bellman operator T^(k) replaces k agents' actions; the resulting distribution shift from the dataset scales with k. The PLCQL operator mixes over k with weights π_par(k|s), so overall shift is proportional to E[k|s].
- **Core assumption:** The ℓ∞-contraction property of each T^(k) holds and the total variation distance decomposes linearly; this extends prior PAR analysis to state-dependent k.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Section 4.3 presents Theorem 1 with the bound and proof sketch.
  - The bound is a novel contribution of this paper.
- **Break condition:** The bound may be loose under severe function approximation error or if replacements violate independence assumptions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Decentralized Markov Decision Process (Dec-MDP):** The problem setting where each agent has its own action space; the joint action space grows exponentially with the number of agents, which is the core challenge PLCQL addresses.
  - *Quick check:* In a Dec-MDP with n agents each having |A_i| actions, the total number of joint actions is ∏_{i=1}^n |A_i|. How does this scale with n?
- **Partial Action Replacement (PAR):** A technique to mitigate out-of-distribution joint-action evaluation by fixing (anchoring) a subset of agents to their dataset actions while replacing the rest with the current policy.
  - *Quick check:* If k=1, what fraction of the joint action comes from the dataset versus the current policy? How does this fraction change as k increases?
- **Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) / Offline RL constraint:** An approach to prevent overestimation on out-of-distribution actions by penalizing Q-values of actions not well-covered by the dataset. PLCQL incorporates a conservative penalty term.
  - *Quick check:* What is the purpose of the conservative penalty in offline RL? How does PAR provide an alternative or complementary form of conservatism?
- **Contextual Bandit:** A learning framework where an agent selects actions based on context (state) to maximize immediate reward, without considering long-term state transitions. PLCQL uses it to decide the subset size k.
  - *Quick check:* Why is a contextual bandit sufficient for PAR subset selection instead of a full MDP? What does the lack of state transitions imply?
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** A policy gradient method that uses a clipped surrogate objective to ensure stable updates. PLCQL employs PPO to train the PAR policy due to its reliability on low-dimensional action spaces.
  - *Quick check:* What problem in policy gradient methods does PPO's clipping mechanism address? Why might PPO be suitable for the relatively low-dimensional action space (k ∈ {1..n})?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Replay Buffer (D) -> Sample Batch -> PAR policy (π_par) selects subset size k -> Construct a'(k) (mix dataset and policy actions) -> Compute Q-targets (min over Q-ensemble) and Q-loss -> Update Q-ensemble (θ) and Target Q-ensemble (θ̄) -> Compute ensemble uncertainty (u_Q) -> Compute PAR reward (r_par) -> Update Value baseline (V_par) and PAR policy (π_par) via PPO -> Update individual agent policies (π_i) via policy gradient.
- **Critical path (per iteration):**
  1. Sample minibatch from replay buffer D.
  2. For each transition: sample k ~ π_par(s'), uniformly select k agents to replace, construct a'(k) by mixing dataset actions and policy actions.
  3. Compute TD target Y_par = r + γ·min_j Q_target_j(s', a'(k)).
  4. Compute Q-loss L_Q for each ensemble member, add conservative penalty ξ_c.
  5. Compute uncertainty u_Q = std_dev(Q_ensemble(s', a'(k))) and PAR reward r_par = (σ(-u_Q·T)+0.5)·Q_θ1(s', a'(k)).
  6. Update Q-ensemble parameters θ and target network via Polyak averaging.
  7. Update value baseline V_par by mean-squared error to r_par.
  8. Update PAR policy π_par using PPO with advantage A = r_par − V_par(s').
  9. Update each agent policy π_i by gradient ascent on E_{s,a_{-i}∼D, a_i∼π_i}[Q_θ1(s,a)].
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Ensemble size: More Q-networks improve uncertainty but increase computational cost; paper uses 10.
  - PAR policy network capacity: Larger networks may capture state-dependent patterns but risk overfitting; paper uses two 64-unit layers.
  - Temperature T in uncertainty weighting: Controls sensitivity; higher T reduces aggressiveness; tuning required.
  - PPO clipping ϵ: Standard 0.1–0.2; trade-off between stability and learning speed.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - PAR policy collapses to always selecting k=n or k=1, indicating uninformative reward or PPO failure.
  - High Q-ensemble disagreement across all states, suggesting poor value learning or dataset coverage issues.
  - Performance degrades sharply on Medium-Replay datasets compared to Expert, possibly due to over-penalizing by uncertainty.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation: Compare adaptive PAR (full PLCQL) against forced k=1 and k=n on CN Medium-Replay and 2s3z Medium to show no fixed k dominates.
  2. Ablation: Remove uncertainty weighting (set w=1) and measure performance drop, especially on Medium-Replay datasets.
  3. Computational efficiency: Count Q-function evaluations per iteration for PLCQL vs. SPaCQL on 6-agent SMAC maps; expect ~6× reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can the PAR policy be extended to select specific agents for replacement based on their individual contributions to coordination, rather than uniform sampling after choosing subset size?**  
   The paper notes that uniform selection ignores agent heterogeneity and task-specific roles, potentially missing subsets that enhance coordination.
2. **What alternatives to Q-function ensembles can provide reliable uncertainty estimates for large-scale MARL without the O(n) computational and memory overhead?**  
   The authors point out that reliance on ensembles may limit scalability to environments with very large numbers of agents, suggesting the need for more efficient uncertainty quantification methods.
3. **Does the contextual bandit context (bootstrap state s') fully capture the state-dependent optimal PAR strategy, or would incorporating historical information improve adaptation in tasks requiring memory?**  
   The current design assumes the next state alone suffices for PAR decisions; tasks with long-term coordination might require richer context such as recurrent states or recent history.

## Limitations
- Missing precise hyperparameters (e.g., conservative penalty coefficients, PPO settings) hampers exact replication.
- Ambiguity in the agent policy update (Algorithm 1, line 22) may lead to implementation variance.
- Theoretical bound assumes ℓ∞-contraction and linear decomposition; it may be loose under deep function approximation.
- Performance depends critically on the calibration of the Q-ensemble; unreliable uncertainty estimates could cause the PAR policy to degenerate.
