# Automated Motif Indexing on the Arabian Nights

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19283
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19283
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A fine-tuned Llama3 model achieved 0.85–0.87 F1, demonstrating the first high-performance automated motif indexing system.

## Executive Summary
Automated motif indexing in folkloristic texts was previously infeasible due to a lack of digitized, indexed corpora. This work overcomes that barrier by aligning a widely available translation of *The Arabian Nights* with El-Shamy's detailed motif index, creating a manually annotated corpus of 58,450 sentence–motif pairs. The study evaluates retrieval, embedding, and generative approaches, finding that parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) of a Llama3 model yields strong performance (F1 0.85–0.87). However, the task remains challenging for off-the-shelf large language models without fine-tuning or few-shot prompting, especially for complex motif expressions.

## Method Summary
The task is framed as binary classification: given a sentence and a motif definition, predict whether the sentence expresses that motif. The core innovation is a precisely aligned corpus mapping motif index page numbers to specific sentences in a modern translation. This was achieved by adapting the Needleman-Wunsch algorithm with WordNet synonym detection to align Burton's 1885 translation to Irwin's 2010 edition. An annotation pipeline using a retrieve-and-rerank system (BM25 + BERT cross-encoder) bootstrapped the creation of 2,670 positive examples across 200 motifs. Models were evaluated on motif-level splits (140 train, 30 validation, 30 test) with F1 as the primary metric, stratified by two complexity dimensions: conceptual (motif structure) and expression (textual divergence).

## Key Results
- First high-performance automated motif indexing system, achieving 0.85–0.87 F1 with a fine-tuned Llama3 model.
- Off-the-shelf LLMs perform poorly without fine-tuning or few-shot examples, highlighting the task's difficulty.
- Performance drops significantly on motifs rated as complex in both conceptual structure and expression, from >0.8 F1 for simple/simple to ~0.5–0.7 for complex/complex cases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Aligned Text Corpus Enables Accurate Motif Localization
- **Claim:** Precise alignment between Burton’s archaic translation and Irwin’s modern edition allows faithful mapping of motif index page numbers to specific sentences, forming a reliable annotated corpus.
- **Mechanism:** The Needleman-Wunsch algorithm with synonym detection aligns word sequences in progressive 100-word windows, scoring matches and partial matches (synonyms) to handle translation differences, achieving 0.99 accuracy in validation.
- **Core Assumption:** Narrative continuity is preserved across editions, so motif expressions appear in aligned segments.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract mentions pairing the Nights with El-Shamy's index; Section 3.3.2 details the alignment method and its 0.99 accuracy; corpus neighbors focus on other domains and do not address historical text alignment.

### Mechanism 2: Complexity Analysis Structures Task Understanding and Evaluation
- **Claim:** Decomposing motifs into conceptual and expression complexity dimensions creates a predictable difficulty gradient, guiding annotation and model assessment.
- **Mechanism:** Motifs are classified as simple/complex in structure and expression based on semantic graph size and textual divergence from index descriptions. This categorization correlates with performance drops in evaluation tables.
- **Core Assumption:** These dimensions capture key sources of task variability, such that models struggle more on complex/complex cases.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Section 4 defines the dimensions and provides examples; Section 5 uses it to guide motif selection and annotation; Tables 5–8 show performance stratification by complexity.

### Mechanism 3: Fine-Tuned LLMs Learn Abstract Motif Representations Beyond Surface Form
- **Claim:** LoRA fine-tuning adapts pre-trained LLMs to recognize motif semantics from limited examples, achieving high F1 by capturing implicit narrative patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Llama3 and Mistral are fine-tuned on sentence-motif pairs with a binary classification objective, updating low-rank matrices to specialize the model for motif detection without full fine-tuning.
- **Core Assumption:** LLMs contain sufficient world knowledge to infer motif meanings when exposed to annotated exemplars, and LoRA prevents overfitting on small datasets.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract reports 0.85–0.87 F1 for fine-tuned Llama3; Section 6.3.3 details LoRA fine-tuning; Table 8 shows fine-tuned LLMs outperforming other approaches, especially on complex expressions.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data layer (Burton’s text, Irwin’s text, El-Shamy’s index) -> Alignment module (Needleman-Wunsch with synonyms) -> Annotation engine (retrieve-and-rerank: BM25 + BERT cross-encoder) -> Model suite (retrieve-and-rerank, embedding models, generative LLMs with zero/few-shot and LoRA fine-tuning).
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Align texts to create a sentence-indexed corpus.  
  2. Annotate sentence-motif pairs using initial retrieve-and-rerank to bootstrap data.  
  3. Train and evaluate models on stratified splits (by complexity) to benchmark performance.  
  4. Iterate: analyze errors on complex motifs, expand annotation, and refine models (e.g., fine-tune LLMs).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Sentence-level vs. multi-sentence: Chose sentence-level because only 1.1% of expressions span multiple sentences, simplifying annotation and modeling.  
  - Retrieve-and-rerank vs. LLMs: Started with retrieve-and-rerank for rapid data collection despite lower ceiling; transitioned to fine-tuned LLMs for peak performance.  
  - Fine-tuning data size: Used 2,670 positive examples; more data may improve complex motif handling but annotation cost is high.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Alignment errors: Misaligned pages cause systematic annotation mistakes; check with manual spot-checks (e.g., 50 pages as in paper).  
  - Low annotator agreement (κ < 0.7): Indicates ambiguous guidelines or complex motifs; revisit annotation guide and complexity definitions.  
  - Performance collapse on complex/complex motifs: Models achieve >0.8 F1 on simple/simple but drop to ~0.5–0.7 on complex/complex; indicates need for more data or architectural changes.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Implement and validate the Needleman-Wunsch alignment by checking 50 random pages; ensure >0.95 accuracy before proceeding.  
  2. Run retrieve-and-rerank (BM25 + BERT cross-encoder) on a subset of motifs to estimate initial recall and guide annotation.  
  3. Test zero-shot Llama3 on 100 held-out sentence-motif pairs to establish baseline; then fine-tune with LoRA on the full training set and compare F1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can neural models be adapted to detect motif expressions that span multiple sentences? The authors note that 1.1% of motif expressions require multi-sentence context, but they limited annotation to single sentences due to rarity, leaving multi-sentence detection for future work (Section 5 and 7). Unresolved because current models process sentences in isolation, missing cross-sentence narrative dependencies. Evidence needed: Benchmark evaluation on a held-out set of multi-sentence motif expressions using models with extended context or hierarchical architectures, measuring F1 against human annotations.
### Open Question 2
Can retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with external folkloristic resources replace fine-tuning for motif indexing? The authors propose RAG as a potential alternative to fine-tuned embeddings, using resources like the *Arabian Nights Encyclopedia* (Section 7). Unresolved because fine-tuning yields high performance but requires labeled data; RAG might achieve similar performance without task-specific training. Evidence needed: Comparative experiments where RAG systems ingest index entries and encyclopedia excerpts, with and without fine-tuning, evaluated on the test set.
### Open Question 3
How do finer-grained distinctions in motif conceptual and expression complexity affect model accuracy, and can an automated complexity metric be derived? The authors created a binary complexity classification but state they "would like to explore this idea more deeply" (Section 4.3). Unresolved because binary labels may oversimplify; a continuous or multi-level scale could better predict difficulty. Evidence needed: Develop a multi-axis complexity scoring system (e.g., number of narrative events, semantic opacity) by expert folklorists, then correlate scores with model performance across motifs.
### Open Question 4
What causes lower inter-annotator agreement for complex motif expressions, and how can annotation protocols be improved? Cohen's Kappa for complex-expression motifs was 0.53 (moderate), compared to 0.87 for simple ones (Table 4), with the authors noting the task is "challenging" for such cases (Section 5.4). Unresolved because inconsistent labeling degrades training data quality, harming performance on difficult cases. Evidence needed: Qualitative error analysis of annotator disagreements on complex expressions to identify common failure modes (e.g., ambiguity in index descriptions, contextual reliance), then iteratively refine guidelines and measure Kappa improvement.

## Limitations
- Dataset is not public (release contingent on acceptance), blocking external validation and broader testing.
- Key LoRA hyperparameters (rank, alpha, dropout) are omitted, risking irreproducibility of fine-tuning results.
- Generalizability is constrained: findings hinge on a single, meticulously aligned corpus; multi-sentence motifs (1.1% of cases) are excluded; cultural specificity of *Arabian Nights* may limit transfer to other folkloristic traditions.

## Confidence
- **Alignment accuracy:** High (0.99 validation reported via Needleman-Wunsch).
- **Complexity dimensions:** Medium (performance stratification in Tables 5–8 supports correlation, but dimensions are internally defined).
- **Fine-tuned LLM efficacy:** Medium (F1 0.85–0.87 achieved, but relies on non-public dataset).
