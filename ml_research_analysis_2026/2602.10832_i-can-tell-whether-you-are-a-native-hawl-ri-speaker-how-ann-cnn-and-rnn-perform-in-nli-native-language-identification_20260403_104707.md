# I can tell whether you are a Native Hawlêri Speaker! How ANN, CNN, and RNN perform in NLI-Native Language Identification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10832
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10832
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** An RNN model achieved 95.92% accuracy in identifying native vs. non-native Hewlêri Kurdish speech using 5-second audio segments and oversampled training data.

## Executive Summary
This study introduces the first Native Language Identification (NLI) dataset for the Hewlêri subdialect of Sorani Kurdish, comprising 23 hours of speech from 40 participants. The research evaluates three neural architectures—ANN, CNN, and RNN—trained on MFCC features across various audio segment lengths (1–60 seconds) and data balancing strategies. Key findings reveal that RNNs perform best, reaching 95.92% accuracy with short (5-second) segments and oversampling, while CNN reaches 94.47% with 10-second segments. Shorter segments and oversampling (improving accuracy by 7–12%) are critical factors, suggesting that temporally-localized phonetic cues are highly discriminative for this dialectal NLI task.

## Method Summary
A speech corpus was collected, preprocessed (noise reduction, silence removal, interviewer voice stripping, normalization), and segmented into fixed-length chunks (1–60 seconds). Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) were extracted from each chunk to form time-frequency matrices. Three neural models—a feedforward ANN (flattened MFCC input), a 1D CNN (local time-frequency pattern detection), and an RNN/LSTM (sequential modeling)—were trained on 80:10:10 train/validation/test splits. The minority (native) class was oversampled in the training set to address duration imbalance. Early stopping monitored validation loss, and best models were evaluated on a held-out test set.

## Key Results
- RNN achieved **95.92%** accuracy using **5-second** segments and oversampled data.
- CNN reached **94.47%** accuracy with **10-second** segments.
- ANN peaked at **82.92%** with 5-second segments.
- Shorter segments (1–10 seconds) and random oversampling (yielding a 7–12% accuracy gain over undersampling) were consistently beneficial.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: RNN's Sequential Modeling Superiority
- **Claim:** The RNN (LSTM) architecture's ability to model temporal sequences is the primary causal factor for its superior accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** RNNs maintain a hidden state that evolves over time, enabling them to learn long-range dependencies and transitional patterns in the MFCC sequence. For Hewlêri NLI, discriminative cues—such as L1-influenced vowel transitions, consonant clusters, or prosodic contours—manifest as evolving temporal signatures within short utterances.
- **Core assumption:** The most salient native/non-native differences are encoded in the temporal progression of acoustic features over brief, coherent speech spans (3–10 seconds), not in static spectral snapshots.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper reports RNN's highest accuracy at 5 seconds; section 4.5 explicitly attributes this to sequential modeling capturing evolving dialectal features; performance declines as segment length increases, suggesting dilution of temporal cues.
- **Break condition:** If discriminative cues were primarily suprasegmental or content-dependent, longer segments would perform better, contradicting the observed trend.

### Mechanism 2: Short-Segment Concentration
- **Claim:** Fixed-length short audio segments (1–10 seconds) enhance performance by isolating temporally-localized nativity cues and reducing intra-class variance from topic or discourse variation.
- **Mechanism:** Short segments restrict analysis to a contained linguistic unit (e.g., a phrase), forcing the model to focus on fine-grained phonetic and phonological implementation details—the likely locus of L1 interference—while minimizing confounding noise from speaker idiosyncrasies or longer prosodic patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Acoustic-phonetic markers of non-native Hewlêri pronunciation are consistently detectable within brief, coherent speech snippets.
- **Evidence anchors:** Both RNN and CNN peak at 5–10 seconds; accuracy trends downward as length increases to 60 seconds; human evaluators actually benefit from longer samples, highlighting the model's unique strength in short-discourse analysis.

### Mechanism 3: Oversampling Mitigates Class Imbalance
- **Claim:** Random oversampling of the minority native class improves accuracy by preventing the model from developing a bias toward the majority non-native class.
- **Mechanism:** Duplicating native training samples balances the class distribution, ensuring the loss function is not dominated by majority-class gradients. This compels the model to learn the native class's feature distribution more effectively.
- **Core assumption:** Native speech samples possess a consistent, learnable acoustic pattern distinct from non-native speech, and their under-representation previously hindered learning.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states oversampling provided a 7–12% accuracy increase; section 4.4 compares oversampling favorably to undersampling; class distribution figures illustrate the balancing effect.
- **Break condition:** Severe overfitting to duplicated samples would cause a large gap between cross-validation and test performance. The reported small drop (0.81%) suggests overfitting is controlled.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs)**
  - **Why needed here:** MFCCs compress speech spectra into a perceptually relevant representation, capturing formant structures and noise characteristics that reflect L1 phonological influence.
  - **Quick check:** Why might MFCCs be more suitable for dialectal NLI than raw spectrograms? (Answer: MFCCs efficiently encode vowel quality and consonant resonances critical for L1 transfer, while being robust to small timing variations.)

- **Concept: Data Segmentation Strategy for Audio**
  - **Why needed here:** Fixed-length segmentation creates numerous uniform samples and aligns with the hypothesis that nativity cues are temporally bounded within short phrases.
  - **Quick check:** What is a potential downside of very short segments (e.g., 1 second) that might explain why 5s was optimal? (Answer: Extremely short segments may lack sufficient phonetic context or represent only partial phonemes, making cues inconsistent.)

- **Concept: Handling Class Imbalance (Oversampling vs. Undersampling)**
  - **Why needed here:** The dataset duration imbalance (natives 10h51m vs. non-natives 12h35m) can bias models toward the majority class.
  - **Quick check:** Why might random oversampling be preferred over undersampling here? (Answer: Undersampling discards valuable majority-class data; with limited total data (~23h), duplicating the minority class preserves all examples while balancing.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw WAV audio -> Preprocessing & Segmentation -> MFCC Feature Extraction -> Model Input (time-frequency matrix) -> ANN (flattened dense) / CNN (1D conv + pooling) / RNN (LSTM sequence) -> Output (Native/Non-Native class)
- **Critical path:**  
  1.  Load WAV files (44.1 kHz, 16-bit PCM) and preprocess (noise reduction, silence removal, interviewer voice stripping, normalization).  
  2.  Split into fixed-duration chunks (e.g., 5 seconds) using pydub.  
  3.  Extract MFCCs per chunk, producing a 2D matrix (time steps × coefficients).  
  4.  Split data: hold out 10% final test set; from remaining 90%, stratify by speaker ID into 80% train, 10% validation (CNN/RNN) or 20% test (ANN).  
  5.  Apply random oversampling **only** to the training set to balance native/non-native samples.  
  6.  Define model architecture (ANN/CNN/RNN) and train with early stopping (patience 10) monitoring validation loss.  
  7.  Evaluate best model on untouched test set and report accuracy.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *RNN vs. CNN:* RNN (LSTM) captures long-range temporal dependencies ideal for sequential cues but is slower on CPU; CNN detects local time-frequency patterns faster but may miss order-dependent features beyond kernel size. RNN edged out CNN here.  
  *Segment duration:* Short segments (1–5s) concentrate cues but risk insufficient context; long segments (30–60s) introduce noise from topic/variability. The 5s optimum suggests cues are temporally compact.  
  *Balancing:* Oversampling boosts accuracy but risks overfitting to duplicated samples; cross-validation indicated minimal overfitting, but the risk remains with limited data.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1.  Accuracy drops sharply as segment length increases beyond 10 seconds → model fails to generalize; cues likely not robust in longer contexts.  
  2.  Large performance gap (>5%) between cross-validation and test set after oversampling → severe overfitting to duplicated native samples.  
  3.  All models converge to ~50% accuracy on imbalanced data without balancing → model learned majority-class bias; balancing likely misapplied (e.g., before splitting).  
  4.  RNN training extremely slow or out of memory → unoptimized sequence handling; check input shape or GPU usage.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1.  **Segment length sweep:** Train RNN on 1, 3, 5, 10, 20, 30, 60-second segments without balancing to confirm the short-segment advantage and locate the accuracy peak.  
  2.  **Balancing A/B test:** At the optimal segment length (e.g., 5s), compare RandomOverSampler vs. RandomUnderSampler on training set only; expect oversampling to improve test accuracy by ~7–12%.  
  3.  **Model comparison:** At optimal segment length with oversampling, train ANN, CNN, and RNN with identical splits; compare test accuracies and training times; expect RNN > CNN > ANN accuracy, with RNN fastest on GPU.

## Limitations
- Exact MFCC extraction parameters (number of coefficients, window/hop lengths, sample rate conversion) are unreported, risking feature mismatch in reproduction.
- Data splitting procedure is ambiguous: the relationship between the "final test" (10%) and the 80:10:10 split is unclear, raising potential data leakage risks.
- Feature normalization strategy (global vs. per-file, statistics source) and details of stereo conversion, noise reduction, and interviewer voice removal are unspecified.

## Confidence
- **RNN superiority (95.92% accuracy):** Medium – results are clear but architecture/hyperparameters are unreproducible.
- **Optimal short segments (5s peak):** Medium – trend is reported, but exact peak may shift with different MFCC/segmentation parameters.
- **Oversampling benefit (7–12% boost):** Medium – improvement is claimed, but risk of overfitting is not fully mitigated in evaluation.
- **First Hewlêri NLI dataset:** High – explicitly stated and appears novel.
- **Mechanisms (temporal cues, segment concentration):** Medium – plausible and supported by results, but not causally isolated.

## Next Checks
1.  **MFCC parameter reconciliation:** Extract features using standard defaults (13 coefficients, 25ms window, 10ms hop, mono at 16kHz) and compare temporal dimensions/statistics against the paper's reported sample counts to infer likely parameters.
2.  **Leakage audit for oversampling:** Reimplement 80:10:10 split with strict speaker stratification, apply oversampling **only** to the training subset, and measure test accuracy; a drop >2% from 95.92% suggests prior leakage or overfitting.
3.  **Segment duration replication sweep:** Fix all other variables (approximated RNN architecture, oversampling) and evaluate RNN on 1, 3, 5, 10, 20, 30, 60-second segments; confirm the existence and location of the performance peak; if the optimum shifts beyond 10s, the short-segment mechanism weakens.
