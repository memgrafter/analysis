# Calibrated Confidence Expression for Radiology Report Generation

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.29492
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29492
*   **Reference Count:** 32
*   **Primary Result:** ConRad reduces report-level ECE from 0.642 to 0.106 and improves alignment with radiologist judgment (Spearman 0.268 vs. 0.069) by fine-tuning an LVLM to output calibrated verbalized confidence without altering report content.

## Executive Summary
Radiology report generation models often produce overconfident and uncalibrated confidence expressions, posing risks for unsafe clinical deployment. This paper introduces ConRad, a reinforcement learning framework that fine-tunes a medical LVLM (MedGemma-4B) to produce well-calibrated confidence scores at both the report and sentence levels. Using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a logarithmic scoring rule reward, ConRad optimizes confidence prediction against an automated correctness metric (GREEN) while masking the loss to preserve the original report content. Experiments on MIMIC-CXR and IU-Xray show substantial improvements in calibration metrics and better correlation with radiologist assessments, demonstrating a path toward safer, more trustworthy AI-assisted reporting.

## Method Summary
ConRad treats confidence calibration as a reinforcement learning problem. A base LVLM (MedGemma-4B with LoRA) generates a report followed by a verbalized confidence score (e.g., "Confidence: 8/10"). The reward is a logarithmic scoring rule: `R = λ * (s * log(ṗ) + (1-s) * log(1-ṗ))`, where `s` is the correctness signal from the GREEN metric (continuous [0,1] for reports, binary per-sentence) and `ṗ` is the model's normalized confidence prediction. GRPO updates the policy by comparing group-relative rewards. Crucially, the loss is masked so gradients apply only to the tokens representing the confidence score, ensuring the report generation itself remains unchanged. Training uses a small, fixed set of samples (3000 for report-level, 1500 for sentence-level) for one epoch.

## Key Results
*   **Report-level calibration** on MIMIC-CXR: ECE reduced from 0.642 (Verbalize Base) to 0.106; Pearson correlation between confidence and GREEN increased to 0.431.
*   **Sentence-level calibration** on MIMIC-CXR: ECE reduced from 0.438 to 0.239; AUROC for sentence correctness reached 0.633.
*   **Clinical alignment & generalization:** Confidence scores better aligned with radiologist judgments (Spearman 0.268 vs. 0.069) and generalized well to out-of-distribution IU-Xray data (ECE 0.034).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Proper Scoring Rule Drives Calibration
The logarithmic scoring rule reward mathematically incentivizes the model to predict the true probability of correctness. Maximizing expected reward under this strictly proper scoring rule is equivalent to minimizing the cross-entropy between the predicted confidence distribution and the true correctness distribution (represented by GREEN). This guarantees that, given sufficient model capacity and a reliable correctness signal, the optimal strategy is to output perfectly calibrated confidence. Evidence is provided in the paper's methodological sections linking the reward to proper scoring rules.

### Mechanism 2: Loss Masking Preserves Report Content
During RL training, the loss function is explicitly masked so that only the tokens corresponding to the confidence expression contribute to the gradient. All tokens comprising the radiology report have their loss contributions zeroed. This constraint isolates the optimization to the confidence prediction pathway, preventing any drift in the base model's report generation policy while it learns to align confidence with correctness. The paper's implementation details confirm this masking strategy.

### Mechanism 3: GRPO Stabilizes Noisy Reward Optimization
Confidence calibration reward signals (from GREEN) can be noisy and lack a clear absolute scale. GRPO stabilizes training by sampling multiple outputs per input and computing advantages relative to the group mean. This avoids training a separate value network and makes the update robust to reward noise, as only the relative ordering of confidence quality within a batch matters. The paper adopts GRPO from recent RL literature for this variance-reduction benefit.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept 1: Proper Scoring Rules**
    *   **Why needed:** The logarithmic reward is a proper scoring rule; its strict properness is the theoretical foundation ensuring that truthful (calibrated) probability reporting is the unique optimal strategy to maximize expected reward.
    *   **Quick check:** What key property of a scoring rule makes truthful reporting the optimal strategy? (Strict properness.)
*   **Concept 2: Reinforcement Learning for Continuous Targets**
    *   **Why needed:** Confidence is a continuous value with ordinal semantics. RL via a reward function (log-score) can optimize this continuous quality directly, avoiding the discretization and loss-function mismatches of supervised approaches.
    *   **Quick check:** Why might standard supervised fine-tuning (e.g., MSE loss) be suboptimal for training a model to output calibrated confidences? (It treats the target as a fixed label rather than a quality to be ranked; RL can optimize an ordinal reward.)
*   **Concept 3: Loss Masking in Autoregressive Decoding**
    *   **Why needed:** To update only the confidence expression while leaving the generated report text untouched, the loss computation must be selectively applied to specific token positions in the sequence.
    *   **Quick check:** How would you implement masking in the token-level loss of an autoregressive decoder to train only a specific segment of the output sequence? (Zero out the per-token loss weights for non-target tokens before reduction and backpropagation.)

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Input (Image + Indication) -> Base LVLM (MedGemma-4B, LoRA) -> Autoregressive Generation (Report + Confidence Tokens) -> Parse Confidence (ṗ) -> Compute GREEN Correctness (s) -> Calculate Reward (λ log-score) -> GRPO Update (Loss masked to Confidence Tokens) -> Updated LoRA Weights.
*   **Critical path:** The training loop requires a forward pass to generate outputs, a correctness evaluation pass via GREEN, reward computation, and a GRPO parameter update where the loss is computed only on the confidence token positions.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** GRPO removes the need for a value network but requires diverse outputs per batch. LoRA protects the base model's knowledge but limits adaptation capacity. GREEN provides automated, scalable supervision but may not perfectly reflect true clinical correctness.
*   **Failure signatures:** Persistent high ECE (>0.5), significant GREEN score degradation (>5% drop), confidence distribution collapse (e.g., all predictions near 0.9), training loss instability, or severe performance drop on the OOD dataset.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Report-level calibration on MIMIC-CXR test set, comparing ECE and Pearson correlation against baselines (Verbalize Base, etc.).
    2.  Sentence-level calibration on MIMIC-CXR, measuring ECE and AUROC for per-sentence correctness.
    3.  Zero-shot transfer to IU-Xray dataset to evaluate generalization, computing ECE without further training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** What architectural or methodological modifications could significantly improve sentence-level confidence calibration compared to the report-level setting? The paper notes sentence-level calibration is more challenging but does not investigate targeted solutions.
*   **Open Question 2:** How does ConRad's confidence-guided review actually impact radiologist workflow efficiency and diagnostic accuracy in real clinical settings? The evaluation measures alignment with radiologist judgment but not practical outcomes like time saved or error detection rates.
*   **Open Question 3:** To what extent does ConRad's calibration performance depend on the choice of automated correctness metric (GREEN), and would alternative quality metrics yield different calibration results? The method relies solely on GREEN, which has known limitations.
*   **Open Question 4:** Does the ConRad approach generalize to other medical imaging modalities (e.g., CT, MRI) beyond chest X-rays? All experiments use chest X-ray datasets; different modalities may require adaptation.

## Limitations
*   **Hyperparameter gaps:** Critical GRPO hyperparameters (KL coefficient, group size, reference update frequency) and exact training details (data splits, validation, early stopping, ε value) are not specified, hindering exact replication.
*   **Metric dependency:** Calibration is optimized against GREEN, an automated metric whose biases and errors may be inherited by the confidence scores. Its correspondence to true clinical correctness is not validated.
*   **Evaluation scope:** Clinical validation is limited to a small set (50 reports) measuring correlation with radiologist ratings, not assessing real-world impact on workflow or diagnostic errors. OOD testing is limited to one dataset (IU-Xray).

## Confidence
*   **Calibration without content change:** Medium (Empirical results strongly support the claim, but reproducibility is uncertain due to missing implementation details).
*   **Logarithmic reward as driver of calibration:** Medium (Theory is sound based on proper scoring rules, but real-world effectiveness depends on GREEN's reliability).
*   **Out-of-distribution generalization:** Low (Positive result on one OOD dataset, but generalizability to other settings or modalities is unproven).
*   **Alignment with radiologist judgment:** Low (Evaluation is small-scale and correlational; no evidence of improved clinical outcomes).
