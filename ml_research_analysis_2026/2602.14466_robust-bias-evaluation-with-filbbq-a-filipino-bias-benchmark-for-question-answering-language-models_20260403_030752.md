# Robust Bias Evaluation with FilBBQ: A Filipino Bias Benchmark for Question-Answering Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.14466  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14466  
- **Reference count**: 0  
- **Primary result**: Multi-seed aggregation significantly stabilizes bias scores, revealing strong sexist (up to 0.57) and homophobic (up to 0.31) biases in three Filipino-capable QA models.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses two key shortcomings in bias evaluation for QA language models: the lack of benchmarks for low-resource languages and the unreliability of single-seed assessments. It presents FilBBQ, a culturally adapted Filipino bias benchmark containing 10,576 prompts derived from 123 templates (52 original + 71 new). To mitigate stochastic variance, the authors implement a robust protocol that aggregates responses over 50 inference seeds before computing bias scores. Evaluation on three Filipino-capable QA models confirms large score fluctuations across individual seeds, validating the multi-run approach. The analysis uncovers pronounced stereotypes, particularly linking women to emotionality/domesticity (bias scores up to 0.57) and associating queer identities with unconventional interests and polygamy (scores up to 0.31). The study demonstrates the necessity of multi-seed evaluation and culturally sensitive benchmarks for trustworthy bias measurement in low-resource settings.

## Method Summary
FilBBQ was constructed by adapting the existing BBQ bias benchmark to Filipino cultural contexts. The authors retained 52 BBQ templates and created 71 additional templates capturing local social dynamics, yielding 123 unique templates that generate 10,576 test prompts. Three QA models with Filipino language capability were selected for evaluation. For each prompt, the model produced answers under 50 distinct random inference seeds. Per-seed bias was measured by comparing the model’s preference for stereotypical versus anti-stereotypical answer options. Final bias scores were obtained by averaging across the 50 seeds, effectively reducing noise. The aggregated results revealed consistent bias patterns across all models, proving both the benchmark’s relevance and the protocol’s robustness.

## Key Results
- Substantial variance in bias scores across single seeds; aggregation over 50 seeds yields stable estimates.
- Strong sexist biases detected, with scores up to 0.57 linking women to emotionality and domesticity.
- Notable homophobic biases, with scores up to 0.31 associating queer identities with unconventional interests and polygamy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Stochastic decoding introduces variability in model outputs, which can drastically alter bias metrics computed from a single run. Averaging responses across many seeds smooths out this randomness, producing a more accurate picture of the model’s inherent biases. Additionally, FilBBQ’s culturally tailored prompts surface biases specific to Filipino society, avoiding the Western-centric blind spots of earlier benchmarks. This combination yields a context‑aware and statistically reliable bias detection method for QA models in low‑resource languages.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
FilBBQ -> Prompt Generator -> Inference Engine (seed management) -> Model QA System -> Response Collector -> Bias Scorer -> Aggregator -> Final Bias Scores  

**Critical path**:  
1. For each template, generate all associated prompts.  
2. For each prompt, run inference across 50 seeds.  
3. Compute per‑seed bias score.  
4. Aggregate scores across seeds to produce template‑level and overall metrics.  

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- More seeds → lower variance but higher compute cost.  
- Larger template set → better coverage but potential noise increase.  
- Single model family → focused analysis but limited generality.  

**Failure signatures**:  
1. High residual variance after 50 seeds may indicate other stochastic sources (e.g., model parallelism).  
2. Very low bias on known stereotypical topics could suggest benchmark contamination or poor cultural adaptation.  
3. Model refusals or invalid outputs break the scorer; requires fallback handling.  

**First experiments**:  
1. **Baseline Variance Check**: Compare a single‑seed bias score with the 50‑seed aggregated score for one model; observe large stability gain.  
2. **Seed Convergence**: Plot aggregated bias as seeds increase (5, 10, 25, 50); expect diminishing returns.  
3. **Cross‑Benchmark Comparison**: Evaluate the three models on original English BBQ and FilBBQ; compare bias profiles to highlight cultural specificity.

## Limitations
- Scope is limited to three specific Filipino‑capable QA models; findings may not generalize to other architectures or generative LLMs.
- Despite cultural adaptation, 123 templates cannot capture the full range of Filipino biases; regional, class, and religious biases remain unexplored.
- The 50‑seed protocol is computationally expensive; optimal seed count and aggregation strategies are still uninvestigated.

## Confidence
- Empirical demonstration of variance across seeds: **High**  
- Identification of specific bias profiles (sexist, homophobic): **Medium** (depends on benchmark validity)  
- Conclusion that single‑seed evaluations are unreliable: **High**
