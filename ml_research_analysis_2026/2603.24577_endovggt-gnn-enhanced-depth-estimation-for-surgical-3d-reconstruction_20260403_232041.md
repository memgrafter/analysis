# EndoVGGT: GNN-Enhanced Depth Estimation for Surgical 3D Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv**: 2603.24577
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24577
- **References**: 40
- **Primary result**: On SCARED, improves PSNR by 24.6% to 34.348 and SSIM by 9.1% to 0.939; zero-shot on EndoNeRF achieves SSIM 0.915; feature-level DeGAT reduces LPIPS by 15.8%.

## Executive Summary
Endoscopic 3D reconstruction is hindered by low-texture surfaces, specular highlights, instrument occlusions, and tissue deformations. EndoVGGT tackles these challenges by introducing a Deformation-aware Graph Attention (DeGAT) module that dynamically constructs semantic graphs in feature space to capture long-range correlations among coherent tissue regions, even across occlusions. Integrated as a pre-transformer feature-level refinement, DeGAT significantly boosts depth estimation quality. On the SCARED dataset, EndoVGGT delivers a 24.6% PSNR increase to 34.348 and a 9.1% SSIM increase to 0.939 over prior state-of-the-art. Moreover, the model shows strong zero-shot cross-dataset generalization, attaining an SSIM of 0.915 on EndoNeRF’s deformation-heavy scenes. The best-performing variant applies DeGAT at the feature level, also reducing LPIPS by 15.8% relative, highlighting the effectiveness of dynamic graph-based feature aggregation.

## Method Summary
EndoVGGT builds upon the VGGT geometry-grounded transformer for large-scale 3D reconstruction. Input consists of N endoscopic frames. A frozen DINOv2 backbone extracts patch tokens from each image, providing rich semantic representations. The core innovation is the Deformation-aware Graph Attention (DeGAT) module, applied as a pre-transformer feature-level refinement. DeGAT dynamically constructs a K-nearest neighbor graph (K=9) in feature space using cosine similarity between patch tokens, linking semantically similar regions regardless of spatial fragmentation caused by deformation or occlusion. Each token aggregates neighbor information via an attention mechanism with a residual update (ELU activation), producing refined tokens. These tokens, together with camera tokens (encoding view parameters), are fed into a series of transformer blocks for global self-attention across all tokens. The final token representations are decoded by a DPT head to predict depth maps, while the camera token is processed by an MLP to regress camera pose (rotation, translation, focal length). Training uses a composite loss: camera regression (L1 on translation and rotation, focal length) and depth regression (including an uncertainty-weighted photometric term and a depth gradient consistency term). The model is trained end-to-end on a single surgical dataset (e.g., EndoSLAM) to learn domain-agnostic geometric priors, enabling zero-shot generalization to other surgical datasets.

## Key Results
- **SCARED benchmark**: Prior SOTA is surpassed by 24.6% in PSNR (34.348) and 9.1% in SSIM (0.939).
- **Zero-shot transfer**: On EndoNeRF (extreme deformations) without fine-tuning, EndoVGGT achieves SSIM 0.915.
- **Feature-level DeGAT advantage**: This variant yields the best reconstruction and reduces LPIPS by 15.8% relative to other integration levels.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Dynamic Graph Construction
DeGAT builds a dynamic graph in feature space by computing cosine similarity between DINOv2 patch tokens to select K=9 nearest neighbors per token. This links semantically similar tissue regions even when spatially fragmented by deformation or occlusion, enabling propagation of long-range structural cues. The underlying assumption is that semantic similarity in the learned feature space reliably corresponds to geometric coherence across the scene. Supporting evidence includes the abstract and Sec. 3.1, which detail the graph construction and its adaptation to fragmentation. Potential break conditions: feature collapse (yielding random neighborhoods) or an inappropriately large K (introducing noise and over-smoothing).

### Stable Attention Aggregation
The attention aggregation in DeGAT uses a residual update: logits from a linear projection of concatenated (center, neighbor) features via LeakyReLU, followed by a softmax over neighbors to produce row-stochastic weights. The aggregated message (weighted sum of neighbor values) passes through an ELU activation before being added to the original token. This design ensures the update is a convex combination of value vectors, and with ELU’s non-expansiveness the norm is bounded (Proposition 1), preventing gradient explosion. Evidence: Sec. 3.1 (Equations 2–4 and Proposition 1) and Appendix (Lemmas, proofs). Break conditions: using a non-monotonic/expansive nonlinearity (e.g., ReLU without normalization) or corrupting the row-stochastic property (e.g., adding bias to softmax) would undermine stability.

### Pre-Transformer Feature-Level Integration
Applying DeGAT immediately before the transformer blocks (feature-level) yields optimal performance. It refines tokens with geometry-aware context via residual graph operations, establishing stable local structure that serves as a stronger foundation for subsequent global self-attention. Token-level and attention-level placements were inferior. Core assumption: local geometric priors should be encoded early; injecting them later interferes with the transformer’s high-level semantic interactions. Evidence: ablation study (Table 1, Sec. 4.2) shows feature-level DeGAT achieves the highest PSNR/SSIM; visual results (Fig. 4) confirm sharper boundaries and smoother surfaces. Break conditions: with an extremely shallow transformer (insufficient global reasoning) or if backbone features are already highly geometry-aware, the benefit may diminish.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Input images → DINOv2 backbone → DeGAT (feature-level) → token + camera aggregation → Transformer blocks → DPT head (depth) + MLP head (camera pose).

**Critical path**  
1. DINOv2 extracts patch tokens per frame.  
2. DeGAT refines these tokens via dynamic graph attention.  
3. Refined tokens are combined with camera tokens (conditioned by a learnable token).  
4. Transformer layers perform multi-head self-attention across all tokens.  
5. DPT head predicts depth maps; MLP head predicts camera parameters.  
6. Composite loss (camera + depth) computed; backpropagation updates all trainable parameters (backbone frozen).

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **DeGAT placement**: pre-Transformer feature-level yields best results but adds computational cost; token- and attention-level alternatives underperform.  
- **Neighbor count K**: Smaller K reduces computation but may lose useful context; larger K increases cost and risks over-smoothing; optimal K=9 on SCARED.  
- **Graph metric**: Feature-space cosine similarity captures semantic coherence across occlusions; pixel-space Euclidean distance is less robust to deformation and illumination changes.  
- **Backbone tuning**: Using frozen DINOv2 ensures stable features and faster training; fine-tuning could improve adaptation but risks catastrophic forgetting and instability.

**Failure signatures**  
- **Degenerate graph**: If DeGAT yields uniform attention or disconnected neighbor sets, reconstruction quality drops. Diagnostic: monitor neighbor entropy and attention distribution; visualize nearest-neighbor indices.  
- **Training instability**: Exploding gradients or divergence after DeGAT insertion. Diagnostic: check norm bounds of updates, gradient norms of `W_val` and `W_proj`; ensure ELU is used and K is not excessive.  
- **Poor zero-shot generalization**: Model overfits to training dataset’s textures or artifacts. Diagnostic: confirm no data leakage; evaluate feature diversity (e.g., t-SNE) to ensure domain-agnostic representations.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Ablation of DeGAT integration levels**: Comparing token-level, attention-level, and feature-level placements. Feature-level DeGAT achieves significantly higher PSNR (34.348) and SSIM (0.939) than alternatives (Table 1).  
2. **Ablation on neighbor count K**: Systematically varying K shows performance peaks at K=9; too few or many neighbors degrade metrics (Fig. 5).  
3. **Zero-shot cross-dataset generalization**: Training exclusively on EndoSLAM, then testing on SCARED and EndoNeRF. EndoVGGT maintains SSIM >0.90 on EndoNeRF’s extreme deformations, while baselines (VGGT, EndoSurf) collapse, demonstrating robust domain generalization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Temporal consistency integration** – The conclusion mentions extending toward temporal consistency as future work. The current feed-forward approach processes frames independently, potentially causing flickering in video reconstructions. Integrating explicit temporal modules (e.g., ConvLSTM, optical flow guidance) and evaluating on temporal metrics would resolve this.  
2. **Optimality of pre-Transformer feature-level DeGAT** – While feature-level integration outperforms other placements, the underlying reason remains speculative. Direct analysis of feature distributions and attention patterns at different insertion depths is needed to understand why early geometry encoding works best.  
3. **Generality across surgical scenarios** – Zero-shot tests on SCARED and EndoNeRF suggest domain-agnostic priors, but evaluation is limited to two datasets with similar characteristics. Testing on diverse procedures (e.g., colorectal, cardiac), imaging devices, and patient anatomies is necessary to confirm widespread applicability.  
4. **Sensitivity to graph construction parameters** – K=9 and cosine similarity were chosen via limited ablation on one dataset. It is unclear how these choices interact with surgical conditions like specular highlights or low texture. Systematic ablations under controlled corruptions (e.g., synthetic highlights) with alternative metrics (Euclidean, learned affinities) would clarify robustness.

## Limitations

- **Reproduction unknowns**: Exact VGGT architecture variations, training hyperparameters (batch size, learning rate schedule, epochs), DINOv2 feature extraction pipeline (resolution, normalization), and the camera loss formulation (especially rotation) and weighting are not fully disclosed, hindering faithful replication.
- **Limited evaluation**: The study only reports results on two endoscopic datasets (SCARED, EndoNeRF). Generalization to other surgical scenes (different organs, lighting conditions) or to non-surgical endoscopic video remains unevaluated.
- **Stability assumptions**: The theoretical norm bound for DeGAT assumes strict adherence to the proposed attention design (row-stochastic weights, ELU nonlinearity). Practical numerical issues or deviations (e.g., added biases) could break the guarantee, but this is not thoroughly stress-tested.

## Next Checks

1. **Graph Quality Diagnostic**: Compute average nearest-neighbor entropy and overlap ratio with ground-truth tissue labels on validation sets; high entropy would indicate degenerate dynamic graphs.
2. **Ablation on Graph Construction Metric**: Replace cosine similarity with pixel-space Euclidean distance and compare reconstruction metrics; a significant drop would confirm the necessity of feature-space semantics.
3. **External Zero-Shot Test**: Train EndoVGGT on EndoSLAM only and evaluate on a third endoscopic dataset (e.g., Hamlyn Centre dataset) without fine-tuning; consistent SSIM >0.90 would strengthen the domain-agnostic prior claim.
