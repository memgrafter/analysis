# INDIBATOR: Diverse and Fact-Grounded Individuality for Multi-Agent Debate in Molecular Discovery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.01815
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01815
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Multi-agent debate with individualized scientist profiles derived from real publication and molecular histories consistently outperforms generic persona baselines in molecular discovery tasks, achieving AUCs of 0.942 (GSK3β), 0.950 (DRD2), and 0.914 (JNK3).

## Executive Summary
INDIBATOR introduces a multi-agent framework for molecular discovery where each agent is grounded in a unique scientist profile built from that scientist’s publication history (via RAG over PubMed) and molecular history (previously discovered compounds). This addresses the limitation of existing systems that use generic, keyword-based personas. Agents engage in structured, multi-turn debates across proposal, critique, and voting phases to generate and refine candidate molecules. Evaluated on protein-conditioned generation, bioactivity optimization, and lead optimization, the system demonstrates superior performance, validating that capturing fine-grained “scientific DNA” from real-world data enhances both diversity and factuality in AI-driven drug design.

## Method Summary
The system uses DeepSeek-V3.2 as its LLM backbone. A supervisor agent first selects N=50 scientists by querying PubMed via RAG for papers relevant to the task, extracting first/last authors. Each scientist agent is initialized with a profile E_i containing:
- **Publication history**: Titles/abstracts of papers authored by the scientist, retrieved and ranked by keyword relevance.
- **Molecular history**: SMILES strings of molecules previously associated with the scientist, retrieved based on structural similarity (e.g., Tanimoto) to a seed molecule if available.
Agents then participate in up to 20 debate rounds. Each round has three phases: (1) **Proposal**: each agent generates k=30 novel molecules as SMILES; (2) **Critique**: agents review their own and others’ proposals, providing rationales; (3) **Voting**: agents score candidates 0–1, and top molecules are aggregated for the next round. Outputs are structured as JSON to enforce format compliance.

## Key Results
- Protein-conditioned generation: Higher Top-1/Top-10 binding affinity (via Boltz-2) and internal diversity (IntDiv) compared to baselines (VANILLA, KEYWORDDEBATE, ROLEPERSONA).
- Bioactivity-guided optimization (PMO-1K): Top-10 AUC scores of 0.942 for GSK3β, 0.950 for DRD2, and 0.914 for JNK3.
- Lead optimization: Generates molecules with better docking scores while satisfying QED≥0.6, SA≤4, and Tanimoto≥0.6 to a seed molecule.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
INDIBATOR works because it replaces simplistic, generic agent personas with **fact-grounded, individualized profiles** ("scientific DNA") derived from two real-world data sources:
1. **Publication history** provides domain-specific knowledge, methodological preferences, and research trajectories.
2. **Molecular history** encodes structural priors and chem intuition from the scientist’s prior discoveries.
During debate, these biases manifest as diverse, style-aware proposals and critiques, leading to more creative yet chemically plausible exploration. The multi-agent structure further amplifies diversity through cross-pollination of these individualized heuristics, while voting aggregates the most promising candidates. This combination yields molecules that are both high-quality (better affinity/docking) and diverse, outperforming systems that lack such grounded individuality.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (forward flow):
PubMed Database -> RAG Module -> Supervisor Agent -> [Scientist Agent 1 ... N] -> Debate Loop (Proposal -> Critique -> Voting) -> Candidate Molecule Pool -> Evaluator (Boltz-2/PMO-1K/Docking) -> Final Selection

Critical path:
1. Data collection: RAG over PubMed + molecular history retrieval.
2. Profile construction: E_i for each scientist.
3. Debate execution: Iterative proposal/critique/voting rounds.
4. Evaluation: Scoring and baselining.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Agents (N)**: More agents increase diversity but raise compute cost and context management complexity.
- **Profile size**: Longer publication/molecular lists improve factuality but risk context window overflow.
- **Temperature**: Higher temperature boosts creativity but may increase invalid SMILES.

Failure signatures:
- Invalid SMILES outputs (detect with RDKit validation).
- Low internal diversity (IntDiv) and high #Circles (over-similarity).
- JSON format errors in debate phases.
- Context length exceeded when concatenating large E_i.

First three experiments:
1. Replicate the RAG module with different embedding models (e.g., `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` vs. `text-embedding-ada-002`) and assess profile stability and downstream diversity.
2. Ablate profile components: compare runs using (a) publication-only, (b) molecular-only, and (c) combined profiles to quantify each contribution.
3. Vary the number of agents (N=20, 50, 100) to measure the trade-off between diversity (IntDiv) and computational cost.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can additional data modalities beyond publication and molecular history (e.g., lab notebooks, conversation records, failed experiments) be systematically integrated into scientist agent profiles to enhance realism and capture tacit knowledge?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Conclusion states: "we believe that our framework establishes a foundation for incorporating broader modalities, such as conversation records, further enhancing the fidelity of agents in domain-specific environments."
- **Why unresolved:** The current framework relies solely on two structured, public data sources. Integrating unstructured, proprietary, or multi-modal data presents significant technical challenges in retrieval, representation, and ethical sourcing.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and evaluation of a pipeline that ingests alternative data sources (e.g., anonymized lab meeting transcripts) and demonstrates measurable improvement in agent performance compared to the baseline.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific safety guardrails and real-time monitoring mechanisms are necessary to prevent the autonomous generation of toxic, addictive, or environmentally harmful molecules in a system like INDIBATOR?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Impact Statement warns: "our system could be misused for unethical purposes... future work should explore safeguards and establish ethical guidelines."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper acknowledges the risk but provides no implementation details. The debate structure may resist simple blocklists, and defining "harmful" in a chemically robust way is non-trivial.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A documented safety layer that intercepts and evaluates candidates against a dynamic database of toxicophores and regulated substances, benchmarked for effectiveness and false-positive impact.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does the "Clever Hans" phenomenon—where models learn spurious correlations between molecular structures and author identities from noisy bibliographic data—actually degrade the factuality and reproducibility of the agents' reasoning?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper reinterprets the Clever Hans finding as a positive "blueprint," but does not empirically disentangle useful stylistic priors from harmful spurious correlations.
- **Why unresolved:** It assumes the correlation between a scientist name and their molecules is clean. In reality, author name disambiguation is imperfect, and publications list many molecules not primarily associated with that author, potentially introducing noise.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An analysis where scientist profiles are intentionally corrupted (e.g., swapping molecules between scientists) and measuring changes in outcome quality and agent rationales.

## Limitations
- **Molecular history sourcing**: The method to attribute specific molecules to individual scientists (e.g., via patents, ChEMBL discovery flags) is unspecified, risking arbitrary or noisy profile construction.
- **RAG implementation details**: Lacks specifics on embedding models, chunking strategy, keyword extraction method, and molecular similarity thresholds, hindering replication.
- **Supervisor agent logic**: Unclear how the supervisor deduplicates and selects N=50 scientists from RAG results (e.g., author disambiguation, relevance thresholds).

## Confidence
- **Debate framework structure** (proposal/critique/vote phases, JSON schema): **High** (explicitly described).
- **Reported metric values** (AUCs, docking scores): **Medium** (specific but depend on undisclosed evaluation nuances like Boltz-2 version).
- **Core claim that individualized "scientific DNA" provides essential diversity**: **Low** (performance gains could stem from multi-agent debate itself; ablations do not isolate profile individuality from general debate effects).
