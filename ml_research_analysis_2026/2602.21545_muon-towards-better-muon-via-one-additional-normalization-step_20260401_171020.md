# Muon+: Towards Better Muon via One Additional Normalization Step

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21545
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21545
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Muon+ adds a normalization step after gradient orthogonalization, consistently lowering validation perplexity vs. Muon across GPT and LLaMA models, with reductions up to 2.02 points.

## Executive Summary
Muon+ is an extension of the Muon optimizer that introduces an additional normalization step after the gradient orthogonalization. Evaluated on GPT models (130M–774M) and LLaMA models (60M–1B) trained on the FineWeb dataset, Muon+ consistently achieves lower validation perplexity than Muon under both compute‑optimal (token‑to‑parameter ratio ~20) and overtraining (ratio ~200) regimes. The largest improvements reach 2.02 points (e.g., GPT‑Small from 29.66 to 27.64, LLaMA‑350M from 14.02 to 13.41). The benefit is robust to the choice of normalization direction (row, column, or composed), learning rate variations, and the orthogonalization method (You, Jordan, PolarExpress). This suggests that normalizing the orthogonalized update matrix stabilizes optimization and reduces sensitivity to the global learning rate.

## Method Summary
Muon+ modifies Muon by applying a row‑wise, column‑wise, or combined ℓ₂ normalization to the semi‑orthogonal matrix obtained from the momentum’s polar decomposition. The core Muon procedure maintains an exponential moving average of gradients (momentum) and approximates its orthogonal component via a fixed number of Newton–Schulz iterations. Muon+ inserts an extra normalization operation after this orthogonalization: the resulting matrix is scaled so that each row or column has unit ℓ₂ norm (or both). The final update is then scaled by the learning rate and the standard factor √(m/n) to preserve the effective step size. This additional step acts as an implicit per‑row/column adaptive stepsize, constraining update magnitudes and improving stability. The change is computationally negligible (<0.1% overhead) and is applied to all weight matrices except embeddings, normalization layers, and positional encodings, which continue to use AdamW.

## Key Results
- Consistent validation perplexity reductions on GPT (130M–774M) and LLaMA (60M–1B) models compared to Muon.
- Maximum improvements of 2.02 points (GPT‑Small: 29.66 → 27.64; LLaMA‑350M: 14.02 → 13.41).
- Gains persist under overtraining (token‑to‑parameter ≈200), e.g., GPT‑Base: 16.97 → 15.84.
- Effectiveness independent of normalization direction, learning rate, and orthogonalization approximation method.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The added normalization step after orthogonalization stabilizes the update magnitude and direction, preventing pathological update scales that can harm convergence.  
**Mechanism:** Muon’s orthogonalization yields a semi‑orthogonal matrix (UVᵀ) with unit singular values, but its element‑wise magnitudes can vary depending on the momentum structure. The subsequent ℓ₂ normalization (row‑wise or column‑wise) rescales each row or column to unit norm, constraining the per‑neuron or per‑output‑channel update scale. This acts as an implicit adaptive stepsize at the parameter‑matrix level, analogous to per‑parameter scaling in Adam but applied uniformly across rows/columns of a layer’s weight matrix.  
**Core assumption:** Variability in the raw orthogonalized update’s row/column norms is detrimental to stable optimization.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract states Muon+ provides a consistent boost; Section 5.2 shows normalization *always* beats the “none” baseline; related work (NorMuon, Mano) also explores normalization variants.  
**Break condition:** If normalization direction had no consistent impact, but row‑wise and composed directions (col_row/row_col) consistently outperformed column‑wise, indicating the benefit is not trivial scaling but direction‑dependent.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Normalization reduces sensitivity to the global learning rate, making Muon+ more robust to suboptimal LR choices, especially at larger scales.  
**Mechanism:** Normalization makes the effective update magnitude less dependent on the singular‑value spectrum of the momentum matrix. The global learning rate η now scales a matrix with controlled per‑row/column norms, decoupling optimal LR from the raw scale distribution of orthogonalized updates, which can fluctuate across layers and training stages.  
**Core assumption:** A portion of Muon’s LR sensitivity stems from unconstrained update magnitudes post‑orthogonalization.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5.1 (Figure 3, Table 14) shows Muon+ degrades more gracefully with overly large LRs (e.g., LLaMA‑350M at LR=0.08) and has a flatter performance curve; optimal LRs are often the same as for Muon but with a broader “good” band.  
**Break condition:** If Muon+ showed equal or greater LR sensitivity than Muon, or if its optimal LR shifted drastically.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The normalization benefit is largely orthogonal to the specific orthogonalization method, making Muon+ robust to different SVD approximation schemes.  
**Mechanism:** Normalization acts on the output of Ortho(·) regardless of whether it is approximated via Jordan, You, or PolarExpress iterations. These methods all produce a matrix with orthonormal rows/columns (up to approximation error). The final scaling step provides a consistent stabilizing effect irrespective of minor spectral variations.  
**Core assumption:** The primary instability addressed is in the element‑wise distribution of the orthogonalized matrix, not the quality of its orthogonality.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5.3 (Table 6) shows Muon+ outperforms Muon consistently across the three Ortho methods on LLaMA‑350M; the paper explicitly states effectiveness is largely orthogonalization‑agnostic.  
**Break condition:** If a specific Ortho method (e.g., with poor approximation) eliminated the Muon+ benefit.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
1. **Base Optimizer:** AdamW (for embeddings, norms, etc.) and Muon (for linear layer weights).
2. **Muon Core:** Maintains momentum `M`, computes `M = μ*M_prev + (1-μ)*G`.
3. **Orthogonalization:** `O_ortho = Ortho(M)` via 5 Newton–Schulz iterations.
4. **Muon+ Add‑on (New):** `O_final = Norm(d)(O_ortho)`. This is the only new component.
5. **Update:** `W = W - lr * sqrt(m/n) * O_final`.

### Critical Path
`Gradient (G)` → `Momentum (M)` → `Orthogonalization (Ortho)` → `Normalization (Norm)` → `Scaled Update (O_final)` → `Weight Update`.  
The normalization must be inserted *after* the orthogonalization approximation and *before* the final scaling by `sqrt(m/n)`.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Computational Cost:** Adding `Norm` introduces two element‑wise sums and a sqrt per row/column, which is negligible (<0.1% of total) compared to the orthogonalization iterations.
- **Stability vs. Aggressiveness:** Row‑normalization (`norm_row`) performed best. This suggests updating with unit‑row‑norm matrices is more stable for the model’s forward/backward dynamics than unit‑column‑norm.
- **Direction Composed Norms:** `col_row` and `row_col` often performed best, indicating bilateral constraint may be beneficial, but with diminishing returns over single‑direction.

### Failure Signatures
- **No Improvement / Degradation:** Using `col` normalization instead of `row` or composed norms yields smaller gains or worse results.
- **LR Sensitivity Re‑emergence:** If the added normalization is omitted or incorrectly scaled (e.g., missing the `sqrt(m/n)` factor), the LR sensitivity returns.
- **Inconsistent Gains:** If the benefit vanishes under overtraining (T2P≈200), but the paper shows it persists.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Direction Sanity Check:** On a small model (e.g., LLaMA‑60M), run a full LR sweep for `none` (Muon), `col`, `row`, `col_row`. Confirm `row` and `col_row` beat `col` and `none` across LRs.
2. **Orthogonalization Robustness:** On a mid‑scale model (e.g., LLaMA‑350M), fix LR and norm direction (`row_col`). Compare Muon vs. Muon+ using 5 iterations of Jordan, You, and PolarExpress for `Ortho`. Expect consistent Muon+ advantage.
3. **Overtraining Validation:** Train a compute‑optimal model (e.g., GPT‑Base) to its standard token budget, then extend training to T2P≈200. Plot training/validation loss for Muon and Muon+. Expect Muon+ to maintain a stable gap.

## Limitations
- Exact Newton–Schulz iteration details (initialization, numerical tricks) are not fully documented.
- Missing warmup steps, cosine decay parameters, and potential gradient clipping across models.
- Dataset preprocessing (FineWeb shuffling, tokenization) may vary, affecting reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **Overall perplexity improvement:** Medium (consistent across scales, but hyperparameter gaps could influence absolute gains).
- **Normalization direction superiority (row/composed):** High (systematic ablations show clear, repeatable ordering).
- **Orthogonalization method agnosticism:** High (benefit stable across You, Jordan, PolarExpress).
- **Learning rate robustness:** Medium (evidence limited to a single model, LLaMA‑350M).
- **Overtraining benefit:** High (gains persist at token‑to‑parameter ratio ~200).
