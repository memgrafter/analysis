# Scaling Search Relevance: Augmenting App Store Ranking with LLM-Generated Judgments

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23234
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23234
- Reference count: 36
- Primary result: Fine-tuned 3B LLM produced high-quality relevance labels that, when used to augment training, shifted the Pareto frontier and yielded a +0.24% conversion lift, especially on tail queries.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method to improve app store search ranking by leveraging LLM-generated textual relevance labels to augment training data. The core idea is to fine-tune a small in-house LLM on a limited set of human judgments, then use it to produce millions of labels for query-app pairs. These labels are mixed with behavioral signals (clicks/downloads) to train a multi-objective ranker. Offline evaluations show improvements in both textual and behavioral relevance metrics, and an online A/B test demonstrates a significant increase in conversion rate, with the largest gains observed for tail queries where user behavior data is sparse. The approach effectively addresses the label scarcity problem while maintaining or improving multiple relevance objectives.

The study demonstrates that a cost-effective fine-tuned LLM can rival larger pretrained models in relevance judgment, and that synthetic textual labels can enhance a ranker's performance across objectives, particularly for long-tail queries. This has practical implications for scaling search relevance in domains with limited human annotation.

## Method Summary
The authors address the scarcity of textual relevance labels for app store ranking by fine-tuning a small in-house LLM (3B parameters) on a dataset of human query-app relevance judgments using a 5-level scale. The fine-tuned model (FT-3B) significantly outperformed larger pretrained models in label quality (F1 0.800 vs. 0.382). It was then used to generate millions of textual relevance labels for query-app pairs extracted from logs. These synthetic labels were combined with existing human labels and large-scale behavioral data (clicks and downloads) to train a multi-objective ranker. The training employed scalarization, mixing the different label sources in a single loss function. The resulting model was evaluated offline on held-out sets for both textual and behavioral relevance, and subsequently deployed in a worldwide A/B test.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned 3B LLM achieved F1 0.800, outperforming larger pretrained models (F1 0.382).
- Augmentation improved offline NDCG@1 for textual relevance (0.867→0.868) and behavioral relevance (0.646→0.652).
- Worldwide A/B test: +0.24% conversion rate, with strongest gains on tail queries.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Fine-tuning yields high-quality labels**  
Fine-tuning a small LLM on human judgments teaches it the specific rubric for app-query relevance, overcoming misalignment of generalist models. Evidence: FT-3B achieved F1 0.800 vs. PT-30B's 0.382 (Table 1). Break condition: If the human dataset is unrepresentative, the fine-tuned model may learn a biased rubric.

**Mechanism 2: Augmentation shifts Pareto frontier**  
Augmenting the ranker's training with millions of LLM-generated labels shifts the Pareto frontier outward, improving both textual and behavioral relevance simultaneously. Scalarization mixes data sources; the added textual labels strengthen the gradient signal and improve semantic understanding that generalizes to behavioral metrics. Evidence: Offline NDCG@1 improved for both objectives (Table 2). Break condition: Systematic biases in LLM labels could degrade behavioral relevance.

**Mechanism 3: Tail queries benefit most**  
The improvement is most substantial for tail queries because textual relevance provides a robust signal where behavioral data is sparse. For head queries, abundant clicks suffice; for tail queries, LLM labels fill the gap with consistent semantic relevance. Evidence: A/B test showed largest conversion lift in lowest query frequency buckets (Figure 2). Break condition: If tail queries have characteristics not captured during fine-tuning, labels could be misleading.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi-Objective Optimization via Scalarization (Data Mixing)**: Needed to optimize competing objectives (textual & behavioral relevance) within a single ranker without complex architecture changes. Quick check: Varying the mixing ratio moves along the Pareto frontier; to shift it outward, increase total high-quality textual data.
- **Pointwise Relevance Labeling for Learning to Rank**: Needed to provide a relevance score for each query-app pair for training. Quick check: Pointwise is cheapest to scale but may not capture relative order as well as pairwise/listwise.
- **Domain Adaptation via Fine-Tuning**: Needed to align a generalist LLM with the specific rubric and metadata format of app relevance. Quick check: Risk of overfitting to a small, noisy human dataset.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: LLM Judge Module -> Data Augmentation Pipeline -> Multi-Objective Ranker Trainer -> Evaluation & Serving

**Critical path**:
1. Benchmark & select best LLM configuration (FT-3B vs. pretrained, prompt design) using a held-out human validation set.
2. Run FT-3B on large-scale query-app log data to generate millions of new textual labels.
3. Construct mixed training datasets with varying ratios of behavioral:human textual:LLM textual data.
4. Train candidate ranker models, evaluate offline (NDCG@k on both objectives).
5. Select best model (here, one with significant LLM augmentation) for online A/B test.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- LLM Cost vs. Label Quality: Smaller fine-tuned model is cheaper but may have lower quality ceiling; evidence suggested FT-3B was sufficient.
- Data Mixing Ratio: More LLM labels strengthens textual objective but could dilute behavioral signal; optimal ratio is a hyperparameter.
- Prompt Complexity: Few-shot prompts improved quality but require maintaining examples; zero-shot simpler but lower quality.

**Failure signatures**:
- Offline: No improvement or degradation in textual NDCG@k on held-out human set; degradation in behavioral NDCG@k.
- Online: Conversion rate lift negative, especially on tail queries; increased bounce rate.
- Data-centric: High disagreement between FT-3B and human labels on validation set; synthetic label distribution vastly different from human.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. LLM Judge Validation: On held-out human judgments, compute precision, recall, and F1 for FT-3B vs. pretrained models; confirm FT-3B meets a threshold (e.g., >0.75).
2. Offline Ranker Ablation: Train rankers with (a) only human textual labels, (b) human+LLM at 1:1 mix, (c) human+LLM at 1:3 mix; evaluate both textual and behavioral NDCG on holdout; document Pareto frontier shift.
3. Tail Query Sanity Check: Run small short-duration A/B test on bottom 10% of queries; measure conversion lift relative to production; significant lift confirms tail hypothesis before worldwide rollout.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Fine-tuning larger models**: Does fine-tuning a 30B model further improve label quality and ranker performance? The authors note this as future work but did not test due to computational costs; the potential gains remain unknown.
2. **Pairwise/listwise prompts**: Would alternative prompting strategies (pairwise, listwise) yield more effective labels? The paper only used pointwise prompting; the advantages of comparative signals are unexplored.
3. **Cross-locale consistency**: Are label quality and ranker improvements consistent across all languages and storefronts? The paper reports gains in 89% of storefronts but lacks per-locale validation, risking uneven performance.
4. **Causal mechanism of Pareto shift**: Why does textual label augmentation simultaneously improve behavioral relevance? The observed Pareto improvement is correlational; the underlying mechanism (e.g., improved semantic understanding) is not isolated.

## Limitations
- The human judgment dataset's size and diversity are undisclosed, risking overfitting to a narrow rubric or annotation artifacts.
- Offline NDCG gains are minimal (e.g., +0.001 textual, +0.006 behavioral absolute), raising questions about practical significance.
- Online conversion lift (+0.24%) is modest and may be influenced by concurrent experiments or session-level confounders.

## Confidence
- Fine-tuned small LLM outperforms larger pretrained models: High
- Augmentation shifts Pareto frontier: Medium
- Tail queries benefit most: High

## Next Checks
1. Tail label quality audit: Sample 1,000 tail query-app pairs; obtain fresh expert labels; compute Cohen's κ with FT-3B outputs to quantify reliability/bias.
2. Mixing ratio sweep: Train rankers with LLM-textual proportions (0%, 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%) while holding total examples constant; evaluate multi-objective NDCG to identify optimal trade-offs.
3. Sustained tail A/B test: Run a 4-week online experiment restricted to the bottom 20% of queries (by frequency); monitor conversion rate stability and detect potential drift.
