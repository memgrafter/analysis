# WideSeek: Advancing Wide Research via Multi-Agent Scaling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02636
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02636
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** WideSeek-8B-SFT-RL achieves 12.87% Item F1 on WideSeekBench (+5.50% over base), scales sub-agent usage by 6.36×, and generalizes to deep research with 26.42% accuracy on BrowseComp-Plus (+12.20%).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces WideSeek, a system addressing the "Wide Research" paradigm—parallel information retrieval under complex constraints—contrasting with sequential "Deep Research." It contributes two main elements: (1) WideSeekBench, a 5,156-task benchmark for General Broad Information Seeking built via a knowledge graph pipeline that controls information breadth and constraint complexity; (2) a dynamic hierarchical multi-agent architecture where a main agent autonomously forks parallel sub-agents, trained with an end-to-end RL framework (GRPO) that linearizes trajectories. Results show significant improvements in recall and scalability across constraint types and domains, with generalization to deep research tasks. Even top proprietary models score only ~21% Item F1, highlighting the challenge and the effectiveness of multi-agent scaling.

## Method Summary
The approach uses a two-phase training pipeline on top of a Qwen3-8B base model. First, a cold-start phase distills high-quality teacher trajectories (Item-F1 > 0.6) from models like DeepSeek-V3.2 via supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Second, end-to-end reinforcement learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) optimizes a unified policy. The execution follows a dynamic hierarchical MDP: a main agent observes the query and prior results, then decides via its policy to either plan, fork a dynamic number of sub-agents (each executing `search` and `open_page` tools in parallel), or synthesize a final answer. The full trace—interleaving main-agent thoughts and all sub-agent trajectories—is linearized into a single sequence and rewarded using Item-F1 minus a format penalty. This unified training optimizes the entire system jointly.

## Key Results
- WideSeek-8B-SFT-RL achieves 12.87% Item F1 on WideSeekBench, a +5.50% absolute gain over the base model.
- The dynamic policy scales sub-agent usage by 6.36× compared to the base, with consistent improvements across all constraint types (AND, OR, NOT, composites) and domains.
- The approach generalizes to deep research: WideSeek-8B-RL (without SFT) reaches 26.42% accuracy on BrowseComp-Plus (+12.20% over base).
- Even top proprietary models attain only ~21% Item F1 on WideSeekBench, underscoring the benchmark's difficulty and the value of multi-agent scaling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: KG-Based Benchmark Synthesis
- **Claim:** Wide Research requires benchmarks that systematically control information breadth and constraint complexity, unlike Deep Research benchmarks.
- **Mechanism:** A knowledge graph provides atomic information clusters. Formal set operations (AND/OR/NOT) define target entity sets via SPARQL filters. Dynamic attribute selection then builds ground truth tables with specified volume and schema.
- **Core assumption:** Knowledge graphs enable scalable, compositional generation of diverse factual constraints.
- **Break condition:** Limited KG coverage in certain domains or constraint combinations could reduce benchmark diversity and generalization.

### Mechanism 2: Dynamic Hierarchical Forking
- **Claim:** A main agent that autonomously decides how many sub-agents to fork improves performance by adapting breadth to task needs.
- **Mechanism:** The main agent's policy selects actions: plan, fork *k* sub-agents (where *k* is dynamic), or terminate. Sub-agents execute independently; results are aggregated.
- **Core assumption:** The policy can learn effective decomposition and forking strategies via RL, balancing recall against computational cost.
- **Break condition:** Difficult credit assignment across many parallel trajectories may cause over- or under-forking.

### Mechanism 3: End-to-End RL with Linearized Trajectories
- **Claim:** Jointly optimizing the hierarchical system via RL over a unified linearized trajectory overcomes gaps from imitation-only or orchestration-only training.
- **Mechanism:** Main-agent thoughts and sub-agent traces are interleaved into a single sequence. GRPO computes group-relative advantage using a global reward (Item-F1 minus format penalty).
- **Core assumption:** Linearization preserves enough structural information for effective policy learning while using standard LM RL algorithms.
- **Break condition:** If linearization discards critical branching dependencies (e.g., inter-sub-agent coordination), the learned policy may not truly optimize the hierarchy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Reinforcement Learning for LLMs (policy gradients, GRPO)
  - **Why needed:** The system is optimized via end-to-end RL; understanding reward shaping and advantage estimation in long, multi-agent sequences is key to interpreting training dynamics.
  - **Quick check:** How does group-relative advantage in GRPO reduce variance compared to standard PPO when evaluating correlated, parallel sub-agent trajectories?

- **Concept:** Multi-Agent Hierarchical MDPs
  - **Why needed:** Execution is a tree of decisions (main agent forks sub-agents); reasoning about temporal abstraction and decentralized execution is necessary to debug or extend the system.
  - **Quick check:** How does the `create_sub_agent` action break the Markov property of the main agent's state, and how does unified trajectory modeling compensate for this?

  - **Why needed:** Benchmark construction relies on formal set logic over KGs; understanding AND/OR/NOT in SPARQL is essential to diagnose benchmark quality or create new tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Main Agent (Planner) -> create_sub_agent() -> Sub-Agents (Executors) -> tools (search, open_page) -> Sub-Agents return results -> Main Agent aggregates -> Final answer. Unified Policy πθ (single LLM) powers all roles. GRPO Trainer updates πθ from unified trajectories. Simulated Environment provides Wikipedia search.

**Critical path:**
1. Main agent receives query Q.
2. Policy emits `create_sub_agent(tasks=[...])` with *k* sub-tasks.
3. System spawns *k* sub-agents in parallel; each executes tool calls until returning a sub-result.
4. Main agent state updates with all sub-results; it may fork more sub-agents or synthesize final answer.
5. Entire trace (main thoughts + all sub-trajectories) is linearized and sent to reward computation (Item-F1 minus format penalty).
6. GRPO updates πθ using batches of such unified trajectories.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Dynamic vs. fixed forking: Dynamic adapts but introduces RL non-stationarity; fixed is simpler but may be inefficient.
- Unified policy vs. separate: One policy simplifies training but mixes planning/execution dialects; separate could improve specialization but requires more data.
- End-to-end RL vs. imitation: RL optimizes final reward but needs careful shaping; SFT provides a conservative cold start but may imprint harmful biases (e.g., early stopping).

**Failure signatures:**
- Never forking or forking excessively (context overflow, wasted calls).
- Reward instability (loss spikes) during RL due to poor advantage normalization.
- High format error rates from malformed tool calls.
- Low recall (precision-recall imbalance) despite adequate tool usage, indicating poor information extraction.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Forking adaptivity ablation:** Force the main agent to fork exactly *k* = 1, 2, 4 sub-agents at every decomposition step. Compare Item-F1 and average sub-agent count against the dynamic policy to assess the necessity of autonomous forking.
2. **Linearization necessity test:** Train a variant where sub-agent trajectories are kept as separate tree branches and optimized with a custom hierarchical RL loss (e.g., multi-level advantage). Compare performance to the unified linearization to detect information loss.
3. **Cold-start source analysis:** Compare models initialized from: (a) all teacher trajectories, (b) only high-reward trajectories (Item-F1 > 0.8), and (c) synthetic self-generated data. Measure final Item-F1 and scaling behavior to isolate the impact of teacher bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Set-difference bottleneck:** Why are NOT constraints a distinct reasoning bottleneck, and what architectural innovations could mitigate this?
- **Early stopping bias:** How can the premature termination bias from SFT distillation be overcome to ensure scalable search on high-volume tasks?
- **Performance ceiling:** What fundamental limitations (model size, reasoning depth, tool reliability) explain the large gap between current wide research agents (≤12.87% Item F1 for 8B models, ~21% for top proprietary models) and human-level capability?
- **Unified vs. specialized architectures:** Can a single agent and training regimen achieve state-of-the-art on both wide and deep research without trade-offs, or are specialized designs required?

## Limitations
- **Benchmark construction:** Wikidata coverage may bias domain/constraint diversity; property blacklist details are unreported.
- **Training specifics:** Key hyperparameters (reward penalty λ, GRPO group size, exact linearization protocol) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- **Scalability ceiling:** Dynamic forking's credit assignment challenges may cap gains on extremely broad tasks; sub-agent coordination remains implicit and unexamined.

## Confidence
- **WideSeekBench validity:** Medium. The KG pipeline is rigorous but lacks human validation of task naturalness; coverage gaps are plausible.
- **Dynamic hierarchical forking efficacy:** High. Strong scaling results (×6.36 sub-agents, +5.50% Item-F1) across constraints support the claim, though credit assignment limits could curb further gains.
- **End-to-end RL with linearization:** Medium. Reward improvements are clear, but the risk of information loss from linearization is not ruled out (no hierarchical RL baseline).
- **Generalization to deep research:** Medium. Positive transfer is shown, but absolute performance (26.42%) remains far below specialized deep research agents; scope of transfer is unclear.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate forking adaptivity:** Force fixed *k* = 1, 2, 4 at every decomposition. If fixed *k* matches dynamic performance, the autonomy claim weakens.
2. **Test linearization necessity:** Implement a true hierarchical RL variant (tree-structured policy gradient) and compare to the unified linearization approach.
3. **Probe teacher trajectory bias:** Compare models warm-started from (a) all teacher data, (b) only high-reward trajectories (Item-F1 > 0.8), and (c) self-generated synthetic data. Large gaps would reveal distillation harm.
