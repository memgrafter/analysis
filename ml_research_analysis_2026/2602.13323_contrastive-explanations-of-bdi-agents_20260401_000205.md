# Contrastive explanations of BDI agents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.13323
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13323
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Extends BDI explanation generation to contrastive "why X instead of F?" questions, yielding shorter explanations and scenario-dependent improvements in trust and understanding.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of generating explanations for BDI agents that answer contrastive questions, such as "why did you perform action X instead of alternative F?" The authors extend prior work on full ("why X?") explanations by introducing a filtering mechanism that retains only factors that differentiate the chosen action from the foil. A computational study on 1000 random goal-plan trees shows contrastive explanations are significantly shorter, with median size fixed while full explanations grow. A human subject experiment (n=104) indicates that contrastive explanations are sometimes preferred and can increase trust, perceived understanding, and confidence in system correctness; surprisingly, full explanations occasionally reduce trust compared to no explanation. The paper also discusses handling implicit contrastive questions by considering all valid foils.

## Method Summary
The method modifies the non-contrastive explanation generation algorithm for BDI agents operating on goal-plan trees. For an explicit contrastive query with foil F, it computes an explanation by filtering the full explanation to include only ancestors of the chosen action X that are not ancestors of F, along with node conditions and valuings that differ along the paths. For implicit "why X?" queries, the system first identifies all valid foils (actions that are possible alternatives under a shared Or ancestor) and takes the union of individual contrastive explanations to form a minimally filtered explanation. The approach is evaluated computationally by measuring explanation length across random trees and through a human study comparing full, contrastive, and no explanations across scenarios.

## Key Results
- Computational evaluation (1000 random trees): contrastive explanations are significantly shorter than full explanations; median contrastive size remains constant while full explanation size increases with tree complexity.
- Human study (n=104): In some scenarios, contrastive explanations led to higher trust, perceived understanding, and confidence in correctness compared to full explanations or no explanation.
- Surprisingly, providing full explanations sometimes reduced trust compared to providing no explanation at all.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Contrastive explanations are generated by filtering full explanations to retain only factors that differentiate the chosen action X from a specified alternative action F (the foil).
- **Mechanism**: The algorithm replaces the `ancest` predicate with `ancest_F`, which includes ancestors of X that are not ancestors of F. For node conditions and valuations, it uses `ancest_ca_F` (closest common ancestor) and restricts pre-condition filtering to X's pre-condition only. For sequential-or (`sone`) nodes, it filters sibling conditions to those after the foil's path.
- **Core assumption**: The asker implicitly indicates that X is the earliest unexpected action, so preceding actions/conditions are not relevant to the contrast.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and section 3 formal definition; corpus neighbors discuss contrastive explanations in other domains but do not directly validate this BDI-specific filtering.
- **Break condition**: If F shares no common ancestor with X, or if F is not a valid foil, the filtering yields an empty or irrelevant explanation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: For implicit contrastive questions ("why X?" interpreted as "why X instead of some F?"), the system identifies all valid foils F and takes the union of individual contrastive explanations.
- **Mechanism**: Valid foils are actions not in the trace that share a common Or ancestor with X and are possible first actions in sibling branches. The explanation `E^T_{X/?}` is the union over all f in `vf(X)` of `E^T_{X/f}`.
- **Core assumption**: The weakest filter (union) corresponds to the most conservative interpretation, ensuring no potentially relevant differentiating factor is omitted.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and section 3 definitions; limited direct corpus evidence.
- **Break condition**: If `vf(X)` is empty, the contrastive explanation defaults to full explanation or may be undefined.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Contrastive explanations are systematically shorter than full explanations because they exclude factors that do not contribute to distinguishing X from F.
- **Mechanism**: Filtering removes desires that are ancestors of both X and F, belief conditions common to both paths, and valuings for options not available or ancestors of F. Computational evaluation shows median contrastive size fixed while full size grows, implying growing absolute savings.
- **Core assumption**: Human askers of contrastive questions are interested specifically in the difference that led to choosing X over F, not the entire rationale.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract, section 4 (computational evaluation, Figure 2), section 6; corpus papers on contrastive explanations in other contexts also report brevity.
- **Break condition**: If the full explanation is already minimal, contrastive and full explanations may coincide.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Goal-Plan Tree Input -> Trace Generator -> Foil Identifier -> Explanation Engine -> Output Formatter
- **Critical path**: User query ("why X?") → Determine if contrastive (explicit foil F or implicit) → Generate trace T for X → Identify foil(s) F → For each F, compute `E^T_{X/F}` → If implicit, take union → Render output.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Contrastive length vs. foil validity: Shorter explanations depend on meaningful foil; invalid foil yields irrelevant filtering.
  - Implicit foil union: Guarantees coverage but may yield longer explanations than a user’s mental foil.
  - Pre-condition filtering: Assumes preceding actions are "correct" (Grice's maxim), simplifying but may not hold if user questions an earlier step.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Empty contrastive explanation: Occurs if `vf(X)` is empty or if `E^T_{X/F}` filters out all factors. Check foil validity logic.
  - Mismatched explanation: Explanation references conditions not in user's mental model. Verify foil aligns with user expectations.
  - Trace generation inconsistency: If `generate_trace` incorrectly sets `held`/`nheld` for conditions along X's path, belief factors will be wrong.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Unit test filtering logic: Create a tiny goal-plan tree, manually compute full and contrastive explanations, verify only differentiating factors retained.
  2. Implicit foil handling test: In a tree with 3 branches under an Or node, confirm `vf(X)` includes two sibling branches not taken, and `E^T_{X/?}` is union of two contrastive explanations.
  3. Trace-consistency check: For given tree and X, run `generate_trace` and ensure all pre-conditions and node conditions on X's path are `held=True`, and for a `one` node, sibling conditions are `nheld=True` if not selected.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Automatic foil selection**: How can BDI agents automatically select appropriate contrastive foils that align with user mental models without explicit user input? The authors note the importance of foil matching user expectations but burden users with specification.
2. **Effectiveness moderators**: What scenario and user characteristics moderate the effectiveness of contrastive explanations? The human evaluation found scenario-dependent results with only two simple domains, limiting generalizability.
3. **Trust paradox**: Why do full (non-contrastive) explanations sometimes reduce trust compared to providing no explanation? The authors speculate about length or complexity but lack direct causal evidence.
4. **Adaptation to non-BDI agents**: Can the contrastive explanation filtering approach be adapted for non-BDI autonomous systems? Such systems lack explicit BDI representations, making it unclear how to extract comparable factors.

## Limitations
- Trust paradox: Full explanations sometimes reduce trust vs. no explanation; the mechanism is unclear and may limit deployment.
- Foil validity assumptions: The definition of valid foils (`vf(X)`) requiring shared Or ancestors and sibling first actions may be too restrictive for complex BDI plans.
- Generalizability: Computational evaluation used random goal-plan trees that may not capture structure of real-world BDI implementations, risking overestimation of savings.

## Confidence
- **High**: Formal filtering mechanism is mathematically precise and supported by computational results showing consistent length reduction.
- **Medium**: Human subject findings (n=104) are suggestive but limited by scenario specificity and sample size; trust effect needs replication.
- **Low**: Claims about applicability to arbitrary BDI codebases are weakly supported—evaluation used abstract trees, not compiled agent programs.
