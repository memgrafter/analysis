# InfiCoEvalChain: A Blockchain-Based Decentralized Framework for Collaborative LLM Evaluation
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08229
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08229
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Reduces standard deviation of LLM evaluation scores dramatically (e.g., HumanEval: 1.67 → 0.28).

## Executive Summary
Accurate evaluation of large language models (LLMs) is hampered by high variance across repeated runs, making it difficult to discern meaningful performance differences. InfiCoEvalChain addresses this statistical fragility by proposing a blockchain-based decentralized framework that aggregates evaluations from diverse hardware and inference configurations. The core mechanisms include a token-staking commitment scheme to ensure Sybil resistance and a median-proximity reward system that incentivizes honest participation and consensus. Empirical results demonstrate substantial reductions in evaluation standard deviation—for instance, HumanEval scores drop from 1.67 to 0.28—leading to more stable model rankings and narrower confidence intervals. This work provides a practical pathway to robust LLM assessment without requiring changes to the models themselves.

## Method Summary
InfiCoEvalChain orchestrates a network of independent evaluators who stake tokens and run benchmark tasks on their own hardware with self-chosen inference parameters. Each evaluation round employs a commit-reveal protocol: evaluators first submit a cryptographic commitment of their score and a secret salt; later they reveal both. A blockchain smart contract validates commitments, aggregates all revealed scores, and computes the consensus median. The fixed reward pool is distributed proportionally to a Gaussian decay weight, which decays with an evaluator's deviation from the median; the decay bandwidth adapts to the batch's Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) to remain robust to outliers. This design aligns incentives: honest evaluators near consensus receive high rewards, while outliers are penalized through near-zero rewards and potential stake slashing. By aggregating across heterogeneous configurations, the framework significantly reduces evaluation variance compared to centralized single-configuration baselines.

## Key Results
- Standard deviation reduction on HumanEval: from 1.67 to 0.28.
- Significant variance reduction on other benchmarks (e.g., Qwen-3-14B on GPQA-Diamond: 4.25 → 0.92).
- Achieves higher confidence intervals and more stable model rankings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Aggregating evaluations across heterogeneous hardware and inference configurations reduces the standard deviation of LLM benchmark scores by neutralizing environment-specific stochastic noise.
- **Mechanism:** Evaluators run the same benchmark tasks under diverse, independently selected configurations (GPU type, inference parameters, data subset). The final score is derived from the consensus (median) of these diverse results. The Central Limit Theorem suggests that averaging across independent noisy sources reduces variance.
- **Core assumption:** The noise sources (hardware, sampling) are independent across configurations, and deviations are symmetrically distributed around a true mean.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Reports reduction from std 1.67 → 0.28 (HumanEval) and 4.25 → 0.92 (GPQA-Diamond).
  - [section 2.2] Presents a variance decomposition formula (Eq. 13) and links reduced standard error to increased independent runs `n`.
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers (e.g., AIArena) discuss decentralized training, not evaluation noise reduction. The mechanism's specific statistical framing is novel to this work.
- **Break condition:** If the noise sources are not independent (e.g., correlated hardware faults) or if the model's performance distribution is heavily skewed, aggregation may not converge to a stable mean, and variance reduction could be limited.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A token-staking requirement enforces Sybil resistance and aligns evaluator incentives with honest participation by ensuring "skin in the game."
- **Mechanism:** Nodes must stake tokens to qualify for evaluation tasks. This economic deposit is forfeited (slashed) if malicious behavior (e.g., inconsistent submissions, failure to reveal) is proven via the commit-reveal scheme.
- **Core assumption:** Rational actors will avoid losing their stake; the cost of dishonesty outweighs potential gains from manipulation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.3] Describes staking as a "Sybil-resistance mechanism" and links it to the "Schelling Point" coordination game where honest consensus is the focal point.
  - [corpus] Neighbors (LLM-Net, AIArena) use similar token-based staking for AI training/computation, providing indirect support for the economic design pattern in decentralized AI contexts. No direct experimental proof of staking efficacy is given in this paper.
- **Break condition:** If the token value is negligible or slashing conditions are unenforceable (e.g., blockchain finality issues), the economic deterrent fails, enabling Sybil attacks or dishonest submissions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A reward function based on proximity to the batch median (using Gaussian decay) robustly incentivizes convergence on the consensus score while minimizing the influence of outliers.
- **Mechanism:** For a batch of submitted scores, the median `M` is the Schelling Point. Each evaluator `i`'s reward weight `w_i = exp(-(s_i - M)^2 / (2σ^2))` decays non-linearly with deviation from `M`. The total fixed reward pool is split proportionally to these weights. `σ` is scaled by the Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) to adapt to the batch's spread.
- **Core assumption:** The median is the correct consensus target and is robust to outliers; evaluators can accurately estimate the likely median before reveal; Gaussian decay appropriately penalizes deviation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.4] Defines the reward formula (Eq. 8-9) and explains MAD-based `σ` tuning for robustness.
  - [section 3.2.1] Shows the reward distribution histogram, noting a Gaussian-like center and a low-reward outlier cluster (~3% near zero), which is interpreted as successful penalty of deviant nodes.
  - [corpus] The specific median-proximity reward is novel. Neighbors use various incentive models (e.g., contribution-based in FLock, training-loss-based in AIArena), but not this exact consensus-proximity scheme.
- **Break condition:** If the true consensus is not the median (e.g., in a bimodal distribution) or if evaluators cannot form a reliable prior about `M` before revealing, the mechanism may reward deviation or penalize honest but early submissions. A pathological MAD (e.g., zero) would also break `σ` calculation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Variance Decomposition & Central Limit Theorem (CLT)**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's core argument relies on aggregating independent noisy evaluations to reduce variance. Understanding `Var(S) = E_C[Var(S|C)] + Var_C(E[S|C])` (Eq. 13) and the CLT is essential to grasp why more diverse runs improve confidence intervals.
  - *Quick check question:* If `Var_C(E[S|C])` is large (high configuration bias), does increasing the number of runs `n` under a *single* configuration fully resolve the instability? Why or why not?

- **Concept: Schelling Point (Focal Point) in Mechanism Design**
  - *Why needed here:* The consensus mechanism hinges on rational, uncoordinated agents converging on the median as the natural, self-enforcing equilibrium. This game-theoretic concept justifies using the median as the truth anchor without trusted coordination.
  - *Quick check question:* In the commit-reveal scheme, why is it critical that evaluators cannot see others' scores before committing? How does this preserve the independence required for the Schelling Point to be the median?

- **Concept: Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) as a Robust Scale Estimator**
  - *Why needed here:* The reward's decay parameter `σ` is set to `k * MAD` to make the incentive mechanism robust to adversarial outliers. MAD is less sensitive to extreme values than standard deviation.
  - *Quick check question:* If an evaluation batch contains 10% extremely low scores (by malicious nodes), how does using MAD instead of standard deviation affect the `σ` parameter and, consequently, the rewards for honest evaluators near the true median?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Collaborative Layer:** Node selection module (Appendix A: quality score + MMR diversity), Task partitioning module (Appendix B: stratified redundant splitting).
  - **Blockchain Layer:** Peer-to-peer network, Smart contracts (staking, commit-reveal logic, reward calculation), Consensus blocks (Commit, Reveal, Task, Consensus).
  - **Evaluation Engine:** Heterogeneous compute nodes running benchmark tasks with configurable parameters.
- **Critical path:** 1) Task creation & dataset partitioning → 2) Qualified node selection (stake check, MMR diversity) → 3) Task assignment to selected nodes → 4) **Commit Phase:** Nodes submit hash `Hash(s_i || r_i)` → 5) **Reveal Phase:** Nodes submit `(s_i, r_i)` → 6) Smart contract validates commitments, aggregates scores → 7) Calculate median `M`, compute weights `w_i` via MAD-scaled Gaussian → 8) Distribute fixed reward `R_total` proportionally; slash stakes for invalid/missing reveals.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Redundancy (ρ) vs. Cost:** Higher `ρ` (more evaluations per datapoint) improves consensus reliability and outlier detection but linearly increases total compute cost.
  - **Median vs. Mean:** Median is robust but less statistically efficient if the distribution is truly normal. The fixed reward pool with weight normalization assumes the weight distribution sums meaningfully; a degenerate batch (all same score) gives equal rewards regardless of effort.
  - **Commit-Reveal Timing:** Long commit windows increase Sybil risk (nodes can observe early commitments); short windows may exclude validators with slower hardware. The blockchain's block time is a key constraint.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High outlier reward ratio:** If >5-10% of nodes receive near-zero rewards consistently, either the consensus mechanism is failing (no clear median) or malicious participation is high.
  - **Consensus delay:** The Reveal phase stalls with many unopened commitments, indicating node dropout or network issues, potentially freezing reward distribution.
  - **Partition imbalance:** Performance variance across data subsets (Table 4) exceeds expected sampling variance, suggesting flawed stratification algorithm (Appendix B).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Variance Reduction Sanity Check:** Run a single model (e.g., Qwen-2.5-7B) on a simple benchmark (GSM8K) with 10 centralized runs vs. 10 decentralized runs across 3 hardware types. Verify std dev drops as in Table 2 (centralized ~0.9, decentralized <0.2).
  2. **Incentive Distribution Dry Run:** Simulate 100 evaluators with scores sampled from `N(60, 5)`, plus 5 malicious nodes with scores at 0 or 100. Run the reward calculation (Eq. 8-9) and confirm <5% of honest nodes receive <10% of average reward and all malicious nodes receive ~0.
  3. **Task Partition Equivalence Test:** For a stable model (e.g., DeepSeek-V3.2 on GSM8K), compare scores from two partitions generated by the stratified algorithm. Assert difference is within statistical sampling error (e.g., <0.5%) as in Table 4, confirming difficulty balancing.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can InfiCoEvalChain be extended to evaluate multi-modal models?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states that future work will extend the paradigm to multi-modal models.
- **Why unresolved:** The current prototype is limited to text-based benchmarks; multi-modal tasks require adapted evaluation metrics, consensus mechanisms, and possibly new partitioning strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementation and empirical validation on standard multi-modal benchmarks (e.g., VQA, image captioning) with modified task partitioning and reward distribution.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What economic models and incentive designs ensure long-term sustainability and fair participation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion mentions conducting user analysis and surveys to explore economic models and maintain sustainability post-release.
- **Why unresolved:** The platform is not yet publicly deployed; token economics, reward adequacy, and user retention remain theoretical.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Longitudinal studies after release, analyzing user participation data, token circulation, and survey feedback on incentive perceptions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the decentralized evaluation process introduce systematic biases in mean model scores?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Table 2 shows slight mean differences between decentralized and centralized evaluations, but the paper does not test for bias.
- **Why unresolved:** The focus is on variance reduction; consistent mean shifts could mislead model comparisons despite improved stability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Rigorous statistical analysis (e.g., paired t-tests) of mean scores across multiple models and benchmarks, controlling for configuration diversity.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How robust is the median-based consensus against collusion or coordinated manipulation?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The consensus relies on independent evaluators converging on a Schelling point; collusion risks are not analyzed.
- **Why unresolved:** Malicious nodes could submit correlated scores to skew the median and rewards, undermining fairness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Game-theoretic modeling or simulations of collusion attacks with varying colluder proportions, measuring impact on median stability and reward distribution.

## Limitations
- Exact hardware/inference configurations and partitioning algorithm not disclosed.
- Blockchain smart contract logic and slashing conditions omitted; Sybil resistance unverified.
- Staking amounts and token economic parameters unspecified; incentive alignment untested.

## Confidence
- **Variance reduction**: High. Large empirical std reductions observed (HumanEval 1.67→0.28) across several benchmarks.
- **Staking-based Sybil resistance**: Medium. Theoretical soundness and analogies exist, but no direct evidence; token value sensitivity unknown.
- **Median-proximity reward**: Medium. Histogram shows outlier penalization, yet robustness to adversarial MAD and fairness not fully proven.
