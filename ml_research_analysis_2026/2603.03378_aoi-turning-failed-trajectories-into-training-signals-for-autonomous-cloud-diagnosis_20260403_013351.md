# AOI: Turning Failed Trajectories into Training Signals for Autonomous Cloud Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03378
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03378
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** AOI achieves 66.3% best@5 on 86 cloud diagnosis tasks (24.4-point gain over SOTA), and a distilled 14B model attains 42.9% avg@1 on held-out tasks, surpassing Claude Sonnet 4.5.

## Executive Summary
Autonomous cloud diagnosis suffers from data privacy constraints, unsafe action execution, and an inability to learn from failures. AOI addresses these with a read-write separated multi-agent runtime and a Failure Trajectory Evolver that converts failed attempts into corrective training signals via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The runtime isolates exploration (Probe) from state mutation (Executor) using a strict permission matrix, while a Compressor maintains bounded context. The Evolver repairs failed diagnostic trajectories, and GRPO distills expert knowledge into a locally deployable 14B model. Results show a 24.4-point best@5 improvement over prior art, a 14B model that exceeds Claude Sonnet 4.5 on held-out tasks, and a 4.8-point avg@5 gain with 35% variance reduction from Evolver repairs.

## Method Summary
AOI’s core consists of two parts: a safe multi-agent runtime and a data augmentation loop. The runtime comprises four agents: an Observer (central planner), a Probe (read-only executor), an Executor (write-gated with whitelist), and a Compressor (distills raw outputs into token-limited context). The Observer decides actions based on compressed context and maintains long-term summaries. The Failure Trajectory Evolver, itself GRPO-trained on successful seeds, takes failed diagnostic attempts and generates corrected command sequences. These corrections are injected as structured prompts into the Observer. Both Observer and Evolver are optimized with GRPO: sample multiple actions per context, score them with a six-dimension LLM judge, and compute group-normalized advantages for policy updates, avoiding pairwise preference data and critic overfitting.

## Key Results
- **Runtime performance:** 66.3% best@5 on 86 tasks, a 24.4-point gain over prior SOTA (STRATUS).
- **Observer GRPO:** A 14B model trained on 23 tasks achieves 42.9% avg@1 on 63 held-out tasks (15 unseen fault types), exceeding Claude Sonnet 4.5 (41.3%).
- **Evolver impact:** Repairing 37 failed trajectories improves end-to-end avg@5 by 4.8 points and reduces run-to-run variance by 35%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Read-write separation enforces safety
**Claim:** Read-write separation enforces safety and enables reliable diagnosis by isolating read-only exploration from state-mutating actions.  
**Mechanism:** The Observer coordinates but never executes commands. The Probe performs only read-only operations (e.g., `kubectl get`), while the Executor handles state changes under a strict whitelist. The Compressor distills raw outputs into bounded context, preventing information overload. This decomposes trajectories into observation, reasoning, and action phases, allowing safe learning over diagnostic traces while preventing unauthorized mutation.  
**Core assumption:** Separating diagnostic exploration from remediation prevents cascading failures from premature or incorrect actions, aligning with least privilege.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “read–write separated execution architecture decomposes operational trajectories into observation, reasoning, and action phases, allowing safe learning over diagnostic traces while preventing unauthorized state mutation.”  
- [section 3.1] Permission matrix: Observer cannot read raw outputs; Probe cannot write; Executor requires evidence accumulation.  
- [corpus] Related work (ReAct Meets ActRe, UI-Voyager) explores trajectory learning but lacks architectural enforcement.  
**Break condition:** If diagnostic tasks rarely require state changes, separation adds overhead without safety benefit. However, cloud diagnosis often involves remediation, making trade-off favorable.

### Mechanism 2: GRPO distills expert strategies locally
**Claim:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) distills expert diagnostic strategies into a locally deployable 14B model without exposing proprietary data, closing the capability gap with frontier models.  
**Mechanism:** GRPO samples multiple candidate actions per context, scores them with an LLM judge across six dimensions (reasoning quality, target accuracy, etc.), and computes group-normalized advantages for policy updates. This avoids pairwise preference data and critic overfitting.  
**Core assumption:** Within-group comparisons provide sufficient gradient signal for learning complex diagnostic patterns even with small models, provided training data contains high-quality successful trajectories.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to distill expert-level operational knowledge into locally deployed open-source models.”  
- [section 3.3] GRPO formulation and multi-dimensional reward (60% weight on reasoning and target accuracy).  
- [section 5.3] Observer-GRPO achieves 42.9% avg@1 on held-out tasks, surpassing Claude Sonnet 4.5 (41.3%) without multi-run sampling.  
**Break condition:** If successful trajectories are too homogeneous or tasks require diverse strategies, group normalization may suppress valid minority approaches. Mitigation tasks show no improvement, suggesting remediation needs broader knowledge.

### Mechanism 3: Failure Trajectory Evolver turns failures into signals
**Claim:** The Failure Trajectory Evolver converts failed diagnostic attempts into corrective supervision signals, enabling continuous data augmentation and robustness improvements.  
**Mechanism:** For each failed seed trajectory, the Evolver (GRPO-trained on successes) generates multiple corrected command sequences. The highest-scoring correction—evaluated on validity, completeness, correctness, effectiveness—is provided as a structured prompt to the Observer on retry.  
**Core assumption:** Failed trajectories encode “near-miss” patterns (correct intuitions with execution errors) that can be systematically repaired, and these repairs generalize to future attempts.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “Failure Trajectory Closed-Loop Evolver mines unsuccessful diagnostic trajectories and converts them into corrective supervision signals.”  
- [section 4] Evolver repairs failed seeds by generating corrected command sequences; integration provides guidance, not rigid constraints.  
- [section 5.4] Evolver-prompts improve avg@5 by 4.8 points and reduce variance by 35%; LLM judge scores rise (mean 7.18→8.27, std 0.97→0.49).  
- [corpus] Neighboring papers address learning from failures but lack a closed-loop evolver for trajectory repair.  
**Break condition:** If failures stem from fundamental capability gaps (e.g., missing domain knowledge) rather than correctable errors, repairs will be ineffective. The paper notes 29 tasks never succeed, indicating hard boundaries.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Observer (central planner) interacts with Probe (read-only) and Executor (write-gated); Compressor distills outputs for Observer; Evolver generates corrected prompts for Observer; Judge LLM provides scores for GRPO.

**Critical path:** Task → Observer decides → (Probe or Executor) executes → Compressor processes → Observer updates → Repeat until Submit → Validation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Safety vs exploration:* Read-write separation prevents cascading failures but adds iteration overhead. GRPO increases exploration steps (~9 more on average), helping Detection/RCA but harming Localization (over-exploration confuses root cause).  
- *Sampling depth vs cost:* best@k shows diminishing returns beyond k=3; 2-3 runs capture most stochastic gains. Mitigation tasks show zero variance, indicating capability gaps not solved by sampling.  
- *Training data source:* Seeds from Claude Sonnet (proxy for expert) enable GRPO, but system’s own failures may differ; closed-loop evolution assumption.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Localization tasks:* best@5 unchanged across conditions (0% for many tasks); average steps increase without success gains—indicates over-exploration harms precision.  
- *Mitigation tasks:* 0% with Evolver-prompts; Observer GRPO no effect—remediation requires fault-specific commands absent from training distribution.  
- *Detection tasks:* High per-run variance (22pp swing); benefits most from multi-round sampling.

**First experiments:**  
1. **Runtime baseline:** Run untrained AOI (Qwen3-14B) on all 86 AIOpsLab tasks, compare best@5 to STRATUS. Validates architectural gains from read-write separation.  
2. **Observer GRPO generalization:** Train Observer on 23 tasks (11 fault types), evaluate on 63 held-out tasks (15 unseen fault types). Check if avg@1 surpasses Sonnet 4.5. Tests transfer learning.  
3. **Evolver repair evaluation:** Use Evolver to repair 37 Sonnet-failed trajectories; measure end-to-end avg@5 improvement and variance reduction. Assess if failed trajectories become useful signals.

## Limitations
- Seven critical unknowns block faithful replication, notably the Purifier agent’s trajectory minimalization algorithm, exact LLM judge prompts/scoring rubrics, Evolver’s training data construction, and the official task fault-type splits.
- The closed-loop assumption that failed trajectories contain repairable “near-miss” patterns may not hold for 29 tasks (33.7%) that never succeed, suggesting fundamental capability or benchmark limits.
- Generalization claims from Observer GRPO remain partially unablated; gains could stem from Purifier or architectural safety rather than GRPO alone.

## Confidence
- **Read-write separation safety:** High
- **GRPO distillation surpasses Sonnet:** Medium
- **Evolver repairs failures robustly:** Low

## Next Checks
1. **Purifier Algorithm Audit:** Implement the Purifier as described (remove retries, dead-end explorations) and compare its output against a human-purified gold set of 10 successful trajectories; quantify sequence length reduction and correctness preservation.
2. **LLM Judge Calibration Test:** Using 20 known high-quality and 20 low-quality diagnostic steps, run the six-dimension judge; verify sufficient score variance (std > 1.5) and correlation with human ratings.
3. **Train/Test Split Leakage Analysis:** Reconstruct the fault-type to task mapping and ensure none of the 15 held-out fault types appear in the 23 training tasks; correct any overlap to prevent inflated generalization.
