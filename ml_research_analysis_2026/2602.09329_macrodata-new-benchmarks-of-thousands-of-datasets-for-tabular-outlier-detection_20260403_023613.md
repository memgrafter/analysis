# MacrOData: New Benchmarks of Thousands of Datasets for Tabular Outlier Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09329  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09329  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** MacrOData introduces a 2,446-dataset benchmark suite for tabular outlier detection, revealing that foundation models (e.g., OutFormer) significantly outperform classical and deep methods in zero-shot settings.

## Executive Summary
Existing tabular outlier detection benchmarks like ADBench are small and homogeneous, primarily containing global Gaussian outliers. This skews evaluations, inflating the perceived state-of-the-art status of distance-based and diffusion methods. MacrOData addresses this by providing a large-scale, diverse suite of 2,446 real and synthetic datasets with standardized splits, public/private partitions for fair leaderboard evaluation, and rich semantic metadata. Extensive evaluation of 14 methods shows that foundation models pretrained on diverse synthetic data achieve superior performance with no tuning, occupy the Pareto front of accuracy versus runtime, and exhibit robustness where deep models suffer from hyperparameter sensitivity.

## Method Summary
MacrOData aggregates tabular data from sources like Tablib, OpenML-CC18, and classification repositories. A multi-stage pipeline filters, deduplicates, and identifies semantic or statistical outlier targets. Datasets are split into train (50% inliers) and test (remaining inliers + all outliers). Three benchmarks are constructed: OddBench (790 datasets with semantic anomalies), OvRBench (856 with statistical outliers), and SynBench (800 synthetically generated from varied priors). A 50-dataset representative subset enables quick prototyping. Metadata (titles, keywords, domain) is auto-generated via LLM. Aprivate test set of 100 datasets per real benchmark supports a blind online leaderboard. Evaluation uses multiple metrics (Average Rank, ELO, Winrate, rAUC, Champion Delta) to ensure statistically robust cross-dataset comparisons.

## Key Results
- Foundation models (FoMo-0D, OutFormer, TabPFN-OD) significantly outperform classical and deep methods in AUPRC and AUROC across all benchmarks.
- OutFormer achieves the best overall performance (lowest average rank, highest ELO, highest winrate).
- Deep OD methods exhibit high hyperparameter sensitivity, leading to unstable performance, while foundation models offer low-latency, zero-shot inference with no tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### ADBench bias toward global Gaussian outliers inflates distance-based methods
ADBench’s outliers predominantly follow Gaussian noise distributions, aligning with distance-based and diffusion approximation methods. DTE-NP is mathematically equivalent to average KNN distance, and FoMo-0D’s pretraining uses Gaussian variance inflation, giving these methods an unfair advantage. When evaluated on MacrOData’s more diverse outliers, this advantage diminishes as foundation models with broader pretraining dominate.

### Foundation models generalize via diverse synthetic pretraining
Foundation models are pretrained on SynBench’s synthetic datasets, which cover multiple outlier archetypes (GMM, SCM, Copula). They learn a prior over normal data distributions and, at inference, treat training normal samples as context to compute outlier scores based on reconstruction or feature prediction error. This allows zero-shot adaptation to heterogeneous real-world OD tasks.

### Large scale and rigorous protocol ensure robust rankings
With 2,446 datasets, MacrOData averages out domain-specific effects. Standardized splits, held-out private test sets (preventing cherry-picking), and aggregated metrics (Average Rank, ELO, Winrate) provide fair comparisons. The single-split-per-dataset approach is offset by sheer volume, reducing variance from split randomness.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Raw tables -> preliminary filtering (null removal, size thresholds) -> target identification (semantic keywords or classification label) -> hash-based deduplication -> RF separability check (AUC > 0.6) -> manual quality control -> train/test split generator -> metadata annotation (LLM) -> public/private partitioner -> representative subset selector (evolutionary algorithm) -> packaging & leaderboard upload (Hugging Face)

**Critical path:**  
Curation begins with massive raw table collection, proceeds through automated and manual quality filters to ensure outlier relevance, applies standardized splits and metadata enrichment, partitions into public/private sets, selects a representative subset for prototyping, and finalizes with leaderboard deployment.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Scale vs. curation cost: Tablib provides massive scale but requires intensive filtering to ensure quality.  
- Real vs. synthetic: Real benchmarks offer authentic anomalies but risk label noise; synthetic benchmarks provide controlled outlier types but may miss real-world complexities.  
- Public vs. private: Hidden test labels ensure blind evaluation but reduce immediately available datasets.  
- Representative subset: Enables quick testing but may not fully capture the suite’s diversity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Data leakage: Duplicate datasets between public/private or train/test could inflate scores; mitigated by hash deduplication and manual review.  
- Mislabeling: Keyword-derived anomalies in OddBench may be noisy; RF separability filter reduces egregious cases but not all noise.  
- Metadata errors: LLM-generated titles/keywords can be inaccurate, affecting interpretability.  
- Leaderboard overfitting: Single private test set limits repeated submission overfitting but does not eliminate it.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Sanity check: Run KNN (k=5) on the 50-dataset representative subset; verify baseline metrics match reported values to confirm pipeline integrity.  
2. Hyperparameter sensitivity probe: Evaluate a deep method (e.g., DTE-C) on 5–10 public datasets with multiple HP configurations; compute interquartile range to confirm high sensitivity as shown in Figure 9.  
3. Full benchmark comparison: Evaluate your method on all public datasets of one benchmark (e.g., OddBench); compute Avg Rank, ELO, Winrate, rAUC, CΔ using provided code and compare against baselines in Table 14.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What outlier types beyond point anomalies are missing?**  
  MacrOData focuses on individual (point) anomalies—semantic, statistical, synthetic. Collective, sequential, or contextual outliers are absent. Incorporating them would increase benchmark coverage and realism. Resolution requires surveying real-world OD applications, designing generation protocols for new modalities, and augmenting MacrOData accordingly.
- **Does a single train/test split per dataset affect reliability?**  
  The paper acknowledges that single splits could introduce hidden variability but claims large scale mitigates this. This remains untested; split variability might still influence rankings, especially for smaller datasets. Evidence needed: measure performance variance across multiple splits on a representative subset and correlate with dataset size.
- **How to reduce hyperparameter sensitivity in deep OD models?**  
  Deep methods underperform primarily due to high sensitivity, but the paper offers no solutions. Resolution requires proposing and evaluating novel HP optimization, regularization, or architectures tailored for OD, tested on MacrOData with rigorous HP budgets to demonstrate improved stability.

## Limitations
- The 50-dataset representative subset may not fully reflect the diversity of the full 2,446-dataset suite, risking biased preliminary conclusions.
- SynBench’s synthetic priors might not capture all real-world outlier complexities, potentially narrowing the generalization of pretrained foundation models.
- Data leakage remains possible despite deduplication; overlapping sources between public/private splits could inflate leaderboard results.
- OddBench’s semantic labels derived from keywords may be noisy; the RF separability filter reduces but does not eliminate mislabeling.

## Next Checks
1. **Leakage audit:** Apply MinHash or similar similarity detection across all public/private dataset splits; quantify and remove any near-duplicates before re-ranking leaderboard.
2. **Label quality verification:** Randomly sample 100 OddBench datasets for expert review of anomaly relevance; compute inter-rater agreement and correlate with RF separability scores.
3. **Unseen outlier robustness:** Design novel outlier types (e.g., adversarial, contextual collective) absent from SynBench; evaluate foundation models zero-shot to measure performance degradation.
