# Unrestrained Simplex Denoising for Discrete Data. A Non-Markovian Approach Applied to Graph Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.28572
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28572
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: UNSIDE achieves state-of-the-art validity (98.87% on QM9H) and FCD (0.152) over strong baselines on graph generation benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Unrestrained Simplex Denoising (UNSIDE), a non-Markovian framework for discrete generative modeling. Unlike conventional diffusion or flow matching methods that rely on Markovian transitions, UNSIDE operates directly on the probability simplex using a Dirichlet-based noising path with full support and assumes conditional independence across noisy states. This eliminates dependence on previous steps during generation, reducing error accumulation and simplifying the reverse process. A GNN-based denoiser predicts posteriors, and generation resamples from these posteriors at each step. Empirical results on molecular and synthetic graph benchmarks show substantial improvements in validity, distribution similarity (FCD/NSPDK), and sampling speed, establishing UNSIDE as a simpler and more effective paradigm.

## Method Summary
UNSIDE represents discrete data (e.g., graph node/edge categories) as continuous probability vectors on the probability simplex. The forward process noises a clean sample x₁ to xₜ via qₜ(xₜ|x₁) = Dirichlet(1 + αₜ x₁), with αₜ increasing from 0 to ∞ over t∈[0,1). This ensures all dimensions remain positive, avoiding support collapse. The reverse process is non-Markovian: at each step, it samples a denoised x₁ from the learned posterior P_θ(x₁|xₜ) and then draws x_{t+dt} from q_{t+dt}(x_{t+dt}|x₁), thereby preventing error compounding by escaping poor local regions. A GPS-style Graph Neural Network processes the noisy xₜ along with graph structure and auxiliary features (e.g., eigenvectors, RRWP) to predict posterior logits. Training minimizes a weighted negative log-likelihood loss over t~U(0,1), and generation iterates this resampling step starting from a Dirichlet(1) or marginal-mixture prior.

## Key Results
- On QM9H, UNSIDE attains 98.87% validity versus 97.97% (NM-DD) and 92.20% (Dirichlet FM); FCD 0.152 vs. 0.366 and 0.356.
- On ZINC250K, validity 99.98% vs. 99.92% and 97.32%; FCD 1.79 vs. 2.65 and 2.79.
- It achieves perfect validity on Planar graphs and superior validity on SBM while being the fastest sampler.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Preventing Error Accumulation**  
The non-Markovian assumption qₜ(xₜ|xₛ, x₁) = qₜ(xₜ|x₁) (for s≠t) eliminates sequential dependencies between noisy states. During generation, the process resamples entirely from the learned posterior P_θ(x₁|xₜ) instead of making a local update from the current xₜ. If the posterior is inaccurate in some region, resampling allows escaping that region, preventing approximation errors from compounding as in Markovian diffusion where a poor xₜ leads to a similarly poor x_{t+dt}. Evidence: abstract states "reduces error accumulation"; Section 4.1 formalizes conditional independence; Section 4.4 contrasts resampling vs. local updates. Break condition: If P_θ systematically misestimates the true posterior across large simplex regions, errors may still propagate, though less severely.

**Mechanism 2: Continuous Simplex Representation**  
Operating on the probability simplex with continuous distributions enables smooth transitions between discrete states. Unlike discrete state changes (e.g., abruptly removing an edge), continuous trajectories degrade information gradually, avoiding discontinuities that plague discrete Markov chains. The Dirichlet noising path ensures full support on the simplex for all t, meaning all categories remain possible with positive probability until t=1. Evidence: abstract mentions "operates on the probability simplex"; Section 2.5 notes continuous noising avoids discontinuities; Section 3.1 shows linear interpolants cause support collapse. Break condition: If αₜ grows too aggressively, the posterior may degenerate; Dirichlet paths avoid this by design.

**Mechanism 3: Dirichlet Paths with Full Support**  
Using qₜ(xₜ|x₁) = Dirichlet(1 + αₜ x₁) with αₜ → ∞ as t→1 guarantees full support throughout the noising process. The Dirichlet family provides explicit probability paths with closed-form Voronoi probabilities (Proposition 3.1), enabling theoretical analysis. The increasing concentration parameter starts nearly uniform at t=0 and concentrates at x₁ by t=1. Evidence: abstract highlights "Dirichlet-based noising path guaranteeing full support"; Section 3.1 explains full support; Proposition 3.1 derives Voronoi probabilities. Break condition: If the data marginal differs greatly from the prior, initialization may be suboptimal; the paper mitigates with a marginal-weighted mixture prior.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**  
Input encoder (GPS GNN) → processes graph structure/features into node/edge representations → Noising module (training: sample t~U(0,1), draw xₜ ~ Dirichlet(1+αₜ x₁) per dimension) → Denoiser network f_θ(xₜ, t) outputs logits → Reverse sampler (inference: prior init x₀; for t=0→1-dt: compute P_θ(x₁|xₜ), sample x₁, draw x_{t+dt} ~ Dirichlet(1+α_{t+dt} x₁)). Optional initialization: marginal-weighted mixture Dir(1+κe_k).

**Critical Path:**  
- *Training*: Sample clean graph x₁ and t~U(0,1). Compute xₜ via Dirichlet noising. Feed (xₜ, t, graph, extra features) to GNN to get logits. Compute weighted negative log-likelihood loss between predicted π (softmax) and true x₁ categories (nodes/edges). Update with AdamW.  
- *Inference*: Initialize x₀ from prior (Dirichlet(1) or mixture). For t from 0 to 1-dt (or discretized steps): evaluate f_θ(xₜ, t) to get P_θ(x₁|xₜ); sample x₁; sample x_{t+dt} ~ q_{t+dt}(·|x₁). At t=1, output x₁ as generated sample.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Simplex vs. discrete*: Simplex enables smoothness but requires continuous sampling; discrete gives exact categories but causes jumps and error accumulation.  
- *Simplex vs. R^K*: Simplex avoids redundant dimensions but restricts space; Dirichlet paths must avoid support collapse (unlike linear interpolants).  
- *Non-Markovian vs. Markovian*: Non-Markovian removes the infinitesimal transition constraint, allowing larger jumps, but demands full-support transitions (provided by Dirichlet).

**Failure Signatures:**  
- High invalid rates on molecules: Dirichlet αₜ schedule too aggressive or poor initialization.  
- Slow mixing: Voronoi probability P_{V_k}(t) ≈ 1 too early; probability path too concentrated, limiting exploration.  
- Training instability: αₜ grows too fast making qₜ overly uniform → uninformative loss; adjust growth or use marginal-weighted prior.  
- Guidance issues: Classifier-free guidance weights too high reduce validity; ensure unconditional model is accurate first.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Optimal probability path on the simplex**: The fixed Dirichlet schedule (αₜ = -a log(1-t)) may be suboptimal for diverse graph types. Can the noise schedule be learned jointly with the denoiser? (Basis: conclusion; unresolved – no dataset-specific optimization or learnable schedules explored.)
- **Scaling to larger graphs**: Current all-pair GNN may not scale to hundreds/thousands of nodes. Can UNSIDE be combined with scalable architectures (e.g., subgraph/hierarchical GNNs)? (Basis: conclusion explicitly mentions scalable denoising as future work; evaluated graphs ≤200 nodes.)
- **Extension to other discrete modalities**: Could UNSIDE work for sequences or tabular data? The graph-specific architecture and conditional independence may need adaptation. (Basis: conclusion suggests exploring sequences and structured tabular data; no experiments beyond graphs.)
- **Theoretical quantification of error reduction**: Intuition that resampling prevents error compounding lacks formal bounds. Can we derive trajectory-level error bounds comparing Markovian and non-Markovian processes? (Basis: inferred – Proposition 4.4 only bounds one-step KL, no multi-step analysis.)
