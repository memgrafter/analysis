# Continually self-improving AI

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18073  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18073  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Three techniques enable AI self-improvement: EntiGraph (QuALITY QA 39.5% → 56.2%), SBP (closes 60% gap to unlimited unique data), and automated research (69.4% math accuracy, 45% pretraining time reduction).

## Executive Summary
This dissertation tackles three fundamental caps on AI self-improvement. First, Synthetic Continued Pretraining with EntiGraph amplifies small domain corpora into diverse, learnable training data, overcoming catastrophic forgetting and data inefficiency. Second, Synthetic Bootstrapped Pretraining allows a model to generate its own training data from existing documents, capturing inter-document correlations missed by standard pretraining and improving pretraining efficiency. Third, Automated AI research through execution-guided evolutionary search discovers novel training recipes that surpass human experts and state-of-the-art systems in math accuracy and pretraining speed. Collectively, these methods demonstrate pathways for AI systems to autonomously enhance their capabilities.

## Method Summary
The work introduces three self-improvement methods, each with distinct data synthesis and training pipelines. EntiGraph starts with a small corpus, extracts entities, builds a knowledge graph of relations, and prompts an LM to generate diverse textual expansions. The model then continues pretraining on this synthetic corpus mixed with replay data. Synthetic Bootstrapped Pretraining (SBP) pairs semantically similar documents via nearest-neighbor search, trains a conditional synthesizer to generate one document from another, and produces a large synthetic corpus that encodes latent inter-document concepts. Joint pretraining on real and synthetic data yields efficiency gains. For automated research, an evolutionary search loop generates natural-language ideas, automatically patches them into a codebase, runs GPU-accelerated experiments, and uses rewards to guide mutation and selection. Reproduction requires implementing these pipelines with specified data sources (QuALITY, DCLM), models (Llama 3, GPT-4), and evaluation benchmarks (QuALITY QA, MATH, nanoGPT loss).

## Key Results
- Synthetic Continued Pretraining with EntiGraph boosts closed-book QuALITY QA accuracy from 39.5% to 56.2%, with log-linear scaling as synthetic tokens increase.  
- Synthetic Bootstrapped Pretraining closes up to 60% of the performance gap to unlimited unique data under a fixed data budget, improving held-out perplexity and downstream accuracy.  
- Execution-guided evolutionary search discovers recipes achieving 69.4% math accuracy (vs. 48.0% baseline) and reducing nanoGPT pre-training wall-clock time by 45%, outperforming both human experts and current AI systems.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Synthetic Continued Pretraining with EntiGraph converts a small niche corpus into a diverse synthetic corpus that is *learnable* via standard continued pretraining, overcoming catastrophic forgetting and data-inefficient knowledge acquisition.
- **Mechanism:** EntiGraph extracts entities from a source document and prompts an LM to describe *relations* among those entities, creating a knowledge graph. This hierarchical prompting–from document to entities to pair/triplet relations–generates diverse textual representations of the same underlying knowledge. Continuing pretraining on this expanded corpus writes knowledge parametrically into the model’s weights while mixing in original pretraining data to prevent forgetting.
- **Core assumption:** The prompted LM (GPT-4 in the main experiments) can generate faithful, diverse expansions of the source document’s content when grounded in extracted entities and relations. The diversity arises from the combinatorial space of entity tuples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Synthetic Continued Pretraining with EntiGraph, which amplifies small domain corpora into diverse training data, improving closed-book QA accuracy on QuALITY from 39.5% to 56.2% with log-linear scaling."
  - [section 2.2] Details the entity extraction and relation analysis steps, showing how the knowledge graph externalizes diversity.
  - [corpus] Table 2.3 shows low lexical overlap between EntiGraph synthetic data and source documents, confirming diversity.
- **Break condition:** If the synthetic data generator LM is too weak, the generated expansions may be incoherent or hallucinated; if the source corpus lacks clear entities, the graph construction fails. Distillation effects are present at initialization but diminish as the student model is trained on the synthetic tokens.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Synthetic Bootstrapped Pretraining (SBP) enables a model to improve its *pretraining* capability—perplexity and broad-task accuracy—without any external teacher, by synthesizing text that captures inter-document correlations missed by standard next-token pretraining.
- **Mechanism:** SBP first finds semantically similar document pairs in the pretraining corpus using approximate nearest neighbors. It then trains a conditional model to generate document d₂ from document d₁. Finally, it applies this conditional model to the entire corpus itself, synthesizing a vast new corpus that encodes the latent "concept" shared by related pairs. The model’s subsequent training on {real, synthetic} data effectively distills improved understanding from this conditional objective.
- **Core assumption:** The pretraining corpus contains rich inter-document correlations (e.g., a paper and its code implementation) that standard pretraining ignores because it decomposes the joint distribution into a product of marginals. The conditional synthesizer learns to infer latent concepts shared across documents, and the synthetic data provides a training signal beyond mere repetition.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Synthetic Bootstrapped Pretraining, where models generate their own training data from existing documents, improving pretraining efficiency and closing up to 60% of the performance gap to unlimited unique data."
  - [section 3.6] Provides a Bayesian interpretation where documents are generated from latent concepts; SBP’s objective forces the model to learn the posterior P(concept|document), which standard pretraining does not.
  - [corpus] Table 3.3 shows synthesized documents have high relevance to seed documents (low pair-irrelevance) without simple copying, and non-factual rates drop at larger scales. Table B.1 shows SBP training pairs have high semantic similarity (0.79) versus random pairs (0.15).
- **Break condition:** Requires the existence of meaningful nearest-neighbor pairs; if the corpus is too homogeneous or the embedding model is weak, the synthesizer will not learn useful conditional distributions. Gains saturate after repeated iterations if the synthetic data distribution collapses to the marginal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Execution-guided evolutionary search over model-generated research ideas can discover *algorithms* that improve performance on challenging research tasks (like GRPO post-training or nanoGPT pre-training) beyond human baselines and best-of-N sampling.
- **Mechanism:** An automated idea executor implements proposed natural-language changes to a codebase, runs experiments, and returns validation metrics. This feedback guides an evolutionary search: ideas are selected, mutated, and recombined across epochs, with exploitation of high-performing variants and exploration of novel directions. The search space is unbounded (anything expressible in natural language), and the loop operates entirely on GPU clusters.
- **Core assumption:** Frontier LLMs can generate implementable research ideas whose performance quality is faithfully captured by automated execution metrics. The executor is reliable enough to provide a consistent reward signal, and the search scaffold (exploitation/exploration balance) prevents premature convergence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Automated AI research through execution-guided evolutionary search, discovering novel training recipes that achieve 69.4% math accuracy (vs. 48.0% baseline) and reduce pre-training wall-clock time by 45%, outperforming both human experts and current AI systems."
  - [section 4.5] Describes the evolutionary search scaffold and shows scaling only for Claude-4.5-Opus; Section 4.5.2 reports top performance on GRPO (69.4%) and nanoGPT (19.7 min).
  - [corpus] Table 4.1 benchmarks against human experts; Figure 4.6 shows scaling trends. Ideas include algorithmic changes beyond hyperparameter tuning (e.g., "Token-Level Ratio De-noising via Response Chunks").
- **Break condition:** The executor fails to implement complex ideas (e.g., training auxiliary models), leading to noisy rewards. Early saturation for some models (GPT-5, Claude-4.5-Sonnet) suggests ceiling effects; reward hacking can occur if metrics are not robust. The approach does not yet generalize beyond narrow benchmarks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Continued pretraining / domain adaptation**
  - **Why needed here:** Chapter 2 tackles the fundamental problem of teaching a pretrained LM knowledge that was not well-represented in its original pretraining. Standard continued pretraining fails on small corpora due to data inefficiency and catastrophic forgetting; understanding this baseline is essential to motivate synthetic continued pretraining.
  - **Quick check question:** What are two reasons why simply merging small-domain data into a continual pretraining mixture fails, and how does EntiGraph address each?

- **Concept: Synthetic data generations and bootstrapping**
  - **Why needed here:** Both Chapters 2 and 3 rely on LM-generated text to augment training. Chapter 2 allows distillation from a stronger teacher (GPT-4) to amplify a small corpus, while Chapter 3 explicitly forbids distillation to test true self-improvement. The distinction and the statistical rationale (inter-document correlations) are central.
  - **Quick check question:** Compare and contrast the synthetic data generation goals in EntiGraph vs. SBP. What is the key constraint that distinguishes them?

- **Concept: Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) and research automation**
  - **Why needed here:** Chapter 4 aims to remove human intervention from algorithm design. Understanding the landscape—from Neural Architecture Search to learned optimizers—clarifies the novelty of an unbounded, execution-grounded ideation space and why prior bounded searches are insufficient.
  - **Quick check question:** Why are traditional AutoML approaches (e.g., NAS) insufficient for open-ended AI research automation, and what two properties make the evolutionary search scaffold in Chapter 4 different?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** The full system comprises separate pipelines:
  - EntiGraph: Corpus → Entity Extraction → Relation Analysis → Synthetic Corpus → Continued Pretraining (with replay) → Evaluation.
  - SBP: Pretraining Corpus → Embedding & Nearest Neighbor Pairing → Conditional Synthesizer → Synthetic Corpus → Joint Pretraining (real+synthetic) → Evaluation.
  - Automated Research: Baseline Code → Idea Generator (LLM) → Executor (patch & run) → Metric Logger → Evolutionary Search Controller → New Training Recipes.
- **Critical path:** For EntiGraph/SBP: data synthesis → model training → downstream evaluation. For automated research: generate idea → automatically implement → run experiment → record reward → evolve population → repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Synthetic data quality vs. diversity:* EntiGraph and SBP introduce randomness to increase diversity, but this can reduce factuality and controllability; the synthetic-real mixing ratio is a key hyperparameter.
  - *Search exploitation vs. exploration:* Over-exploitation converges quickly but may miss novel regions; over-exploration wastes compute. The annealing schedule in Algorithm 1 (Chapter 4) is critical.
  - *Executor capability vs. safety:* More complex ideas (e.g., training auxiliary models) fail in the automated executor, limiting the idea space. Increasing executor capability (better code gen, sandbox permissions) risks reward hacking or instability.
  - *Compute budget allocation:* Synthetic data generation (especially SBP) consumes significant tokens; balancing between synthesis and training is non-trivial.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *EntiGraph:* Synthetic tokens increase but closed-book QA plateaus far below oracle RAG; high non-factuality or repetition in synthetic samples; poor lexical diversity metrics (high n-gram overlap).
  - *SBP:* Synthetic data becomes repetitive/irrelevant (high pair-irrelevance, duplicate rate); perplexity gains vanish; no downstream improvement; nearest-neighbor pairs lack semantic similarity.
  - *Evolutionary search:* Search curve flattens early; execution failure rate high; best-of-N performance already matches search; max reward does not increase over epochs.
- **First 3 experiments:** A new engineer should validate the pipeline end-to-end with minimal compute:
  1. **EntiGraph replication:** Run entity extraction + relation analysis on 5 QuALITY documents with GPT-4, train Llama 3 8B for 1 epoch on the resulting synthetic corpus (~10M tokens), and confirm closed-book QA accuracy improves over Raw CPT and Rephrase CPT on a small validation split.
  2. **SBP baseline vs. oracle:** Implement the SBP pipeline to train a 3B model for 50B tokens with a small unique data budget (e.g., 2B tokens), comparing against a repetition baseline. Measure held-out perplexity and confirm SBP reduces loss.
  3. **Executor smoke test:** Pick a simple algorithmic idea (e.g., "increase learning rate by 1.2×") and verify the executor correctly patches the GRPO or nanoGPT codebase, runs to completion, and returns a valid metric. Then run the evolutionary search scaffold for 2 epochs with N=10 to ensure the loop functions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can synthetic data augmentation for continued pretraining (like EntiGraph) maintain factual accuracy while scaling to larger, more diverse corpora, avoiding the hallucination risks inherent in using strong LMs as data generators?
- **Basis in paper:** The dissertation acknowledges that "Because EntiGraph synthesizes data using a prompted LM, it may hallucinate and fabricate non-existent entities" and performs a limited factuality analysis on 150 sentences (§2.5.2, §2.8.1). The proposed mathematical model assumes perfect grounding and does not address hallucination.
- **Why unresolved:** The analysis shows majority non-subjective sentences are factual for QuALITY, but this may not hold for more complex domains or significantly larger scales. The trade-off between diversity (driven by open-ended LM prompts) and factuality remains quantitatively uncharacterized beyond a small sample.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic, large-scale factuality measurements across diverse domains and corpus scales, together with algorithmic interventions (e.g., retrieval-grounding, verification modules) that explicitly decouple diversity from factual grounding.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does Synthetic Bootstrapped Pretraining (SBP) genuinely improve a model’s fundamental pretraining capability in a self-contained way (without external teacher distillation), or do observed gains collapse when scaling to frontier-level model sizes and especially to massive, diverse pretraining data (e.g., >10T tokens) that already saturate standard scaling laws?
- **Basis in paper:** The dissertation states that SBP is evaluated under a "data-constrained" regime with up to 1T tokens and notes that scaling laws for SBP are incomplete due to practical obstacles (§3.7.1): "SBP is inherently a large-scale algorithm that cannot be scaled down" and varying data or model size requires redoing expensive synthesizer-tuning and nearest neighbor matching.
- **Why unresolved:** The provided scaling law comparison (Table 3.2) is limited to a few choices of (N,D) pairs (3B/6B, 10B/50B tokens). It is unknown whether SBP’s favorable scaling trend persists at the run sizes of frontier models (e.g., training 100B+ models on 10T+ tokens) where repetition degradation is minimal and the signal from inter-document correlations may become negligible relative to the capacity to simply memorize.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compute-matched experiments at frontier model sizes and pretraining scales, measuring perplexity and broad downstream performance against strong repetition baselines with identical data mixtures but without the SBP procedure.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do AI-generated research ideas discovered via execution-guided search (e.g., for improving GRPO or nanoGPT) generalize beyond the specific, often narrow, evaluation benchmarks used in the automated executor, or are they benchmark-specific optimizations that do not transfer to new problems, datasets, or model architectures?
- **Basis in paper:** The dissertation admits this limitation: "Our current procedure does not test idea generalizability. The best-performing ideas at small scales may not transfer to larger scales or other datasets. Future work should explore methods that explicitly test generalizability" (§4.6.1). The experiments show improvements on MATH validation (GRPO) and FineWeb validation loss (nanoGPT), but no out-of-distribution tests.
- **Why unresolved:** The successfully executed ideas (e.g., "Hierarchical Position-Group Trust Region") are not validated on a held-out distribution or a different underlying model. In an open-ended research setting, overfitting to the automatic evaluation metric is a known risk with automated execution.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A protocol that, after discovering ideas on one environment, re-executes them on a suite of diverse downstream tasks and model sizes, with results reported separately on the original and new validation distributions.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can reinforcement learning from execution reward scale to improve the *upper bound* of idea generation quality (the max reward) for open-ended AI research, or does it inevitably converge to a high-average but mediocre performance plateau where truly novel, high-performing ideas fail to appear in the training distribution?
- **Basis in paper:** The dissertation shows RL training curves where "the average reward increases, but not the max reward" across both GRPO and nanoGPT environments (§C.2, Figure C.1). The authors note this is "a crucial limitation of standard GRPO for idea generation" because discovery relies on the max, not average.
- **Why unresolved:** The experiments use a single prompt and a standard GRPO setup. It remains unclear whether more sophisticated RL algorithms (e.g., with adaptive exploration, meta-learning reward models, or evolutionary strategies interwoven) can break this plateau. The fundamental Markovian nature of the idea generation "state" may also limit credit assignment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Abandoning simple policy gradient methods in favor of algorithms designed for stochastic, long-horizon invention (e.g., curiosity-driven RL, quality diversity methods) and showing a clear upward trend in max reward over training steps, ideally across multiple research environments.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Does automated idea generation truly replace or augment human intuition in AI research, or does the absence of human abstract reasoning and cross-domain analogy fundamentally limit the discoveries to incremental variations of existing techniques?
- **Basis in paper:** The dissertation frames the overarching goal as "AI-designed AI via test-time search" and notes that "the most consequential design decisions were not about individual ideas—they were about the research environments" (§4.6.2). It also speculates that the "harness becomes the primary locus of human contribution." While some rediscovered recent papers are cited, no truly novel algorithmic paradigm (e.g., a new optimizer class or a different training objective) is reported.
- **Why unresolved:** The search space is essentially a natural-language description of algorithmic modifications. The produced ideas, while varied and sometimes effective, remain within the conceptual space of the baseline codebase (e.g., tweaking hyperparameters, adding skip connections, adjusting reward normalization). Whether the system can escape to invent fundamentally new training algorithms (e.g., discovering a new optimizer class or a different training objective) is unaddressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A demonstration in which the automated researcher, starting from a minimal baseline (e.g., pure stochastic gradient descent), discovers an algorithmic innovation that is later recognized by experts as a non-obvious, general principle improving performance across multiple, dissimilar paradigms.

## Limitations
- *EntiGraph*: Teacher LM quality critical; hallucinations & factuality concerns; QA gains plateau below RAG; diversity may be limited.  
- *SBP*: Needs meaningful document pairs; sensitive to embeddings/corpus; synthetic distribution collapse on repeated iterations.  
- *Automated research*: Executor cannot handle complex ideas; narrow benchmark scope (nanoGPT/GRPO); risk of reward hacking; early saturation; high patch failure rate.

## Confidence
- EntiGraph QA improvement (39.5% → 56.2%): **Medium** (strong empirical results but reliance on GPT-4 and potential factuality issues).  
- SBP gap closure (up to 60%): **Medium** (supported by experiments but conditional on corpus structure and prone to data collapse).  
- Automated research discoveries (69.4% math accuracy, 45% time reduction): **Low** (results are task-specific, executor-limited, and show early saturation across models).
