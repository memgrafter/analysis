# Incremental Graph Construction Enables Robust Spectral Clustering of Texts

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03056
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03056
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** Incremental k‑NN graph construction guarantees connectivity and yields higher V‑measure than standard k‑NN at low k, while matching at higher k, with consistent improvements across six MTEB clustering datasets and low variance across random node orderings.

## Executive Summary
Standard k‑NN graphs used in spectral clustering of text embeddings often become disconnected when k is small, leading to degenerate clustering solutions. This paper introduces an incremental graph construction method: nodes are added sequentially, each connecting to its k nearest among the already‑added nodes, which provably yields a connected graph for any k. Evaluated on six MTEB clustering datasets using SentenceTransformer embeddings and Laplacian eigenmaps, the incremental approach significantly outperforms standard k‑NN in low‑k regimes (e.g., V‑measure 32.89% vs 7.19% at k=1 on TwentyNewsgroups) and matches it at higher k. The improvements are consistent, with variance below 1% across random orderings, and adding a minimum spanning tree does not further help. The method thus enables robust graph‑based clustering of texts, especially when operating on sparse neighborhood graphs.

## Method Summary
The study compares two graph‑construction strategies within a spectral clustering pipeline. Inputs are precomputed text embeddings (e.g., all‑MiniLM‑L12‑v2). Standard k‑NN creates a directed graph by finding each point’s k nearest neighbors among all points, then symmetrizes the adjacency by averaging. Incremental construction first randomizes node order; the initial k nodes remain isolated; each subsequent node links to its k nearest neighbors among already added nodes, followed by the same symmetrization. The resulting adjacency (binary or fractional) defines an unnormalized Laplacian L = D – A. For a target number of clusters C, the C+1 smallest eigenvectors are computed (the smallest, constant eigenvector is discarded) to form a C‑dimensional embedding. K‑means then clusters these embeddings. Performance is measured by V‑measure against ground truth. Experiments vary k from 1 to 20 and repeat incremental runs over 10 random orders to quantify stability.

## Key Results
- At k=1 on TwentyNewsgroups (S2S), incremental V‑measure reaches 32.89% while standard k‑NN collapses to 7.19%.
- At k=6, incremental yields 45.72% vs standard 14.67%, demonstrating robust improvement in low‑k regimes where standard graphs are disconnected.
- Across six MTEB datasets, incremental matches or exceeds standard k‑NN performance, with variance under 1% across random node orderings; adding a minimum spanning tree does not further improve results.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Mechanism 1 (Connectivity guarantee):** Incremental construction inductively builds a single connected component: the first k nodes are linked by the (k+1)-th node to all of them, and every subsequent node attaches to k nodes within the existing component. Core assumption: exact distance metric and exact k‑NN search. Evidence: abstract and Section 4 provide an inductive proof; connectivity is always achieved in experiments. Break condition: approximate nearest neighbors could violate the “nearest” premise and potentially disconnect the graph.
- **Mechanism 2 (Local affinity preservation):** For a node added at step t, its connections are among its true k‑NN within the subset of already‑added nodes; thus the incremental graph is a subgraph of the standard k‑NN graph (edges may appear earlier in the order). The local neighborhood structure is preserved, which is sufficient for spectral clustering once connectivity is ensured. Core assumption: global nearest‑neighbor relationships of early nodes are less critical than maintaining connectivity. Evidence: performance matches standard k‑NN at higher k where both graphs are connected. Break condition: if early nodes are heavily skewed toward a minor cluster, their disproportionate influence could degrade performance.
- **Mechanism 3 (Streaming efficiency):** Adding a new node requires only computing its k‑NN to the existing set and updating the corresponding row/column of the adjacency matrix; no existing edges are modified. This sparse update supports incremental processing and potential re‑ordering with minimal recomputation. Core assumption: the application appends new data points and does not require deletion or extensive re‑ordering. Evidence: Section 4 notes the restricted changes and suggests streaming applications; Section 7 mentions temporal community detection. Break condition: batch additions or frequent re‑randomization reduce the computational advantage.

## Architecture Onboarding
*Component map:*  
Embeddings → IncrementalGraphBuilder → LaplacianEigenmaps → KMeans → VMeasure

*Critical path:* The adjacency matrix produced by the graph builder determines the Laplacian spectrum; the eigenvectors extracted become the input to k‑means. Any flaw in connectivity or local structure propagates to the final V‑measure.

*Design tradeoffs:*
- Connectivity guarantee vs. potential bias from node ordering: The incremental method always produces a connected graph, but early nodes may acquire higher degree and exert disproportionate influence on the Laplacian.
- Sparsity vs. global structure: The incremental graph uses exactly k(N−k) edges, preserving local sparsity; augmenting with a minimum spanning tree (tested) did not improve performance, implying global structure is not the bottleneck for these embeddings.
- Theoretical guarantee vs. performance parity: The method’s advantage appears only in low‑k regimes where standard k‑NN fails; at higher k the two constructions perform similarly.

*Failure signatures:*
- **Unexpected disconnection:** If using approximate k‑NN or a non‑metric distance, the inductive guarantee may break; check that the graph has one connected component even for k=1.
- **High variance across random seeds:** Standard deviation of V‑measure >1% for k≤3 indicates node ordering significantly affecting results; the paper reports <1%.
- **Early node degree explosion:** If the first few nodes have degree ≫ k, they are being connected to many later points, potentially introducing noise; monitor the degree distribution.

*First 3 experiments:*
1. **Connectivity validation:** For a small dataset (e.g., 100 points), run incremental builder with k=1. Assert the output graph has exactly one connected component. Also verify edge count = k(N−k).
2. **Low‑k performance comparison:** On TwentyNewsgroups (S2S), run spectral clustering with k=1,3,6 for both standard and incremental. Replicate the reported V‑measure gap (e.g., ~25% absolute improvement at k=1).
3. **Stability test:** Run incremental clustering 10 times with different random node orders for k=3 on one dataset (e.g., BioRxiv P2P). Compute mean and standard deviation of V‑measure; confirm standard deviation < 0.5% as in Table 5.

## Limitations
- **Symmetrization semantics:** Averaging directed edges yields fractional weights (0, 0.5, 1); it is unknown whether binarizing would alter low‑k results.
- **Laplacian formulation:** The paper mentions “Laplacian eigenmaps” but does not specify normalized vs. unnormalized, leaving the exact variant ambiguous.
- **Approximate nearest neighbours:** The connectivity proof assumes exact k‑NN; using approximate methods (FAISS, Annoy) might break the guarantee, especially for k≤3.
