# ReBeCA: Unveiling Interpretable Behavior Hierarchy behind the Iterative Self-Reflection of Language Models with Causal Analysis
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06373
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06373
- **Reference count**: 34
- **Primary result**: ReBeCA identifies a sparse set of invariant semantic patterns that causally drive self-reflection outcomes, revealing a time-dependent behavior hierarchy and achieving up to 49.6% structural likelihood gains; interventions confirm invariance and show that combining multiple positive patterns can degrade performance.

## Executive Summary
ReBeCA introduces a causal framework to interpret the opaque self-reflection mechanisms of language models. By modeling self-reflection trajectories as causal graphs through an Invariant Causal Prediction (ICP) pipeline, it discovers invariant semantic patterns that serve as causal parents of successful outcomes. The method demonstrates up to 49.6% improvement in structural likelihood over dense baselines and uncovers a time-dependent behavioral hierarchy (e.g., Clarity and Organization at round 2 for math reasoning). Out-of-distribution intervention studies validate causal invariance (p=.013) and reveal non-additive effects: simultaneously applying multiple beneficial patterns does not guarantee better performance and can even reduce efficacy, challenging the assumption that more refinement is always beneficial.

## Method Summary
ReBeCA operates in several stages. First, it generates multi-round self-reflection trajectories from a language model on a dataset. To mitigate output stochasticity, it applies Consistency-Enhanced Self-Refine (CESR): for each feedback or refinement step, 20 outputs are sampled, clustered in a shared semantic space, and the centroid of the largest cluster is selected as the representative response. Next, each turn is annotated with a predefined set of high-level semantic patterns (binary presence/absence vectors), constructing a structured matrix S. An ICP pipeline then processes S: Stage 1 runs ensemble causal discovery using Greedy Equivalence Search (GES) with multiple scores under cross-validation to identify candidate parent sets that maximize predictive log-likelihood; Stage 2 checks uniqueness by comparing the top set’s performance against empty and full-set baselines; Stage 3 evaluates linear stability of coefficients and residuals across data folds to ensure invariance. Finally, targeted interventions on an out-of-distribution dataset (AIME) validate the discovered causal relationships and explore interactions between patterns.

## Key Results
- **Sparse causal drivers**: Only a few semantic patterns (e.g., Clarity and Organization at t=2 for math) are true causal parents of reflection success, contradicting the expectation that many factors contribute.
- **Significant performance gains**: The approach achieves up to 49.6% improvement in structural likelihood compared to dense baseline graphs.
- **Invariance confirmed**: Causal patterns remain stable across task subsets (p=.013, η²p=.071) and generalize to out-of-distribution data.
- **Non-additive effects**: Combining multiple positive patterns does not yield cumulative benefits; it can degrade performance, highlighting complex interference in self-reflection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Consistency-Enhanced Self-Refine (CESR)
- **Claim**: CESR reduces output stochasticity to yield statistically representative self-reflection trajectories.
- **Mechanism**: For each intermediate feedback/refinement step, multiple samples are drawn, clustered in a shared semantic space, and the centroid of the largest cluster is selected as the representative output.
- **Core assumption**: The most frequent semantic responses approximate the model's typical output distribution under given conditions; outliers and low-probability variations can be marginalized without biasing causal analysis.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "...drawing on vast collections of self-reflection trajectories... CESR improves the statistical significance of each self-reflection trajectory."
  - Section 4.1: "Rather than selecting the most frequent output, we identify the largest cluster and select its centroid as the representative outcome. This procedure approximates the most typical semantic response under the model’s output distribution, reducing sensitivity to outliers..."
- **Break condition**: If semantic clustering fails to converge (e.g., all samples equally dispersed), the centroid selection becomes arbitrary, potentially injecting noise into the causal graph.

### Mechanism 2: Semantic Pattern Quantization
- **Claim**: Semantic patterns provide a low-dimensional, interpretable quantization of text that enables scalable causal discovery over multi-round trajectories.
- **Mechanism**: Text outputs are encoded into fixed-length binary vectors where each bit indicates presence/absence of a predefined high-level behavior (e.g., "Clarity and Organization"). This transforms unstructured text into a structured matrix S^(T×N) compatible with causal discovery algorithms.
- **Core assumption**: A curated set of semantic patterns captures the key behavioral variations that influence self-reflection outcomes; lower-frequency patterns are pruned to ensure distinctiveness for causal discovery.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "...operationalized via semantic patterns... quantization of any text segment into a binary vector reflecting the presence/absence of each pattern."
  - Section 4.2.1: "The choice of semantic patterns over embedding models for text encoding is motivated by two factors: interpretability and computational efficiency... Since CD algorithms frequently scale exponentially with dimensionality, this reduction is critical for scalable and efficient computation."
- **Break condition**: If critical outcome-driving behaviors are not included in the pattern set (due to oversight or rarity), causal discovery will miss true causal parents, leading to incomplete graphs.

### Mechanism 3: Invariant Causal Prediction (ICP) Pipeline
- **Claim**: ICP via a three-stage pipeline identifies sparse, generalizable causal parents by enforcing cross-distribution stability, filtering spurious correlations.
- **Mechanism**: Stage 1 uses ensemble causal discovery (GES with multiple scores) to find candidate parent sets maximizing cross-validation log-likelihood (CVLL). Stage 2 verifies uniqueness by showing the optimal set outperforms empty/full-set baselines. Stage 3 tests linear stability of coefficients/residuals across data folds to ensure invariant effects.
- **Core assumption**: Valid causal relationships are stable across diverse but related task subsets (e.g., different math problems); spurious correlations will vary in strength or direction across subsets.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "...three-stage Invariant Causal Prediction (ICP) pipeline... identify invariant semantic patterns as causal parents... achieving up to 49.6% structural likelihood gains, stable across tasks where correlation-based patterns fail."
  - Section 4.2.2: "The ICP-based pipeline not only yields candidate causal parent sets but also performs selection and self-verification without reliance on ground truth."
  - Section 5.1.3: Table 5 shows mostly stable coefficients/residuals (p>.05, η²p<.06), supporting invariance.
- **Break condition**: If the task subsets are too heterogeneous (e.g., mixing math and translation), the invariance assumption may fail, causing true causal parents to be incorrectly rejected as unstable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Causal Invariance**: The principle that a true causal effect remains constant across different distributions of the same task type. Needed because ReBeCA relies on stability across task subsets to identify causal parents without ground-truth graphs. *Quick check*: If a semantic pattern's effect size changes significantly when moving from Level 1 to Level 5 math problems, does that violate invariance or indicate a new causal mechanism?
- **Semantic Abstraction**: Mapping raw text to high-level behavioral descriptors (e.g., "Specificity") to create a tractable, interpretable variable space for causal discovery. Needed to bridge unstructured text and causal models. *Quick check*: Could a pattern like "Clarity and Organization" mask multiple distinct sub-behaviors? How would that affect causal discovery?
- **Score-Based Causal Discovery (GES)**: Greedy Equivalence Search efficiently searches over DAG equivalence classes to maximize a Bayesian score (e.g., BDs). It is the engine for Stage 1 candidate generation, respecting temporal acyclicity. Needed to efficiently explore causal structures given the reduced binary feature space. *Quick check*: Why use an ensemble of score functions instead of a single one? This mitigates the risk of score-specific biases.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Trajectory Generator → CESR Processor → Semantic Annotator → Ensemble CD Engine → ICP Validator → Intervention Rig

**Critical path**: Raw self-reflection trajectories are first processed by CESR to obtain consistent per-round outputs, then encoded into a binary semantic pattern matrix. The ensemble CD engine runs GES with multiple scores under 5-fold CV to produce candidate parent sets; the ICP validator applies uniqueness and stability checks to select the final causal parents; finally, the intervention rig enforces/excludes patterns on OOD data to validate invariance and non-additive effects. Failure at any stage compromises the pipeline.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Manual pattern curation ensures interpretability but may miss novel or rare behaviors; automated clustering would sacrifice clarity.
- Fixed 20× resampling in CESR is simple but may be insufficient for highly stochastic models or wasteful for deterministic ones.
- GES is fast but assumes acyclicity and faithful distributions; latent confounders could introduce bias.

**Failure signatures**:
- CESR: multiple large clusters per round → centroid not representative.
- Ensemble CD: all candidate parent sets have nearly identical CVLLs → no clear optimal structure.
- Uniqueness check: optimal CVLL gain over empty/full sets is minimal (<0.05) → absence of strong causal structure.
- Stability test: significant ANOVA/Levene results (p<.05, η²p≥.06) → effects not invariant.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **CESR Sensitivity**: Vary resampling counts (5, 20, 50) and clustering thresholds; measure stability of final parent set (Jaccard >0.8) and cluster purity (>80%).
2. **Pattern Set Ablation**: Remove each semantic pattern one at a time, re-run Stage 1; identify which removals cause largest CVLL drop for the optimal parent set (target ΔCVLL < 0.01 for robust patterns).
3. **Cross-Task Invariance**: Apply the optimal parent set from MATH to a new dataset (e.g., TheoremQA) without refitting; compare predictive log-likelihood; <5% drop supports broader invariance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can we dynamically determine the optimal number of resampling iterations in the Consistency-Enhanced Self-Refine (CESR) pipeline for different language models, hyperparameters, and input distributions?
2. Can semantic patterns be automatically discovered or optimized to ensure both interpretability and comprehensive coverage of model behaviors without manual definition?
3. What constitutes a reliable ground-truth causal graph for self-reflection dynamics, and how can it be constructed to validate frameworks like ReBeCA?
4. Why does the simultaneous activation of multiple positive causal patterns in self-reflection often fail to yield cumulative gains, and what underlying mechanisms cause interference?
5. Is the time-dependent behavioral hierarchy (e.g., Clarity and Organization at t=2 for math) consistent across diverse model architectures (e.g., decoder-only vs. encoder-decoder) and task domains beyond reasoning and translation?

## Limitations
- Manual semantic pattern definition may omit critical or rare behaviors, risking incomplete causal discovery.
- CESR's fixed 20-sample resampling and unspecified clustering algorithm introduce potential instability; the optimal count may vary with model stochasticity.
- The invariance assumption is validated only on homogeneous math tasks; its generalization to heterogeneous domains remains uncertain.
