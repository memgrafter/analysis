# NeoNet: An End-to-End 3D MRI-Based Deep Learning Framework for Non-Invasive Prediction of Perineural Invasion via Generation-Driven Classification
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29449  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29449  
- **Reference count:** 35  
- **Primary result:** Achieved a maximum AUC of 0.7903 in 5-fold cross-validation for PNI prediction; synthetic data augmentation improved mean AUC across models from 0.6760 to 0.7417.

## Executive Summary
NeoNet is a framework for non-invasive prediction of perineural invasion (PNI) in cholangiocarcinoma from 3D T1-weighted hepatobiliary phase MRI. It addresses the dual challenges of subtle imaging biomarkers and a small, imbalanced dataset (44 PNI+ vs. 84 PNI-) through an integrated pipeline: (1) **NeoSeg** automates liver and tumor segmentation to localize the region of interest; (2) **NeoGen**, a 3D Latent Diffusion Model with ControlNet, generates anatomically conditioned synthetic MRI patches to balance the training data to a 1:1 ratio; and (3) **PattenNet**, a classifier with a frozen diffusion encoder and dual 3D attention blocks, detects subtle peritumoral features. In 5-fold cross-validation, NeoNet achieved a peak AUC of 0.7903, with synthetic augmentation providing a consistent performance boost.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with **NeoSeg** (SwinUNETR) segmenting the liver and tumor from input MRI. A **Tumor-Localized ROI Crop (TLCR)** algorithm then extracts fixed-size (96×96×48) dual-channel patches centered on the tumor: one channel masks the peritumoral region (liver+tumor), the other the tumor core. **NeoGen**—a 3D Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) paired with a ControlNet—is trained on real minority-class (PNI+) patches conditioned on their anatomical masks. During training, it generates synthetic patches for the minority class to balance the dataset. **PattenNet** uses the frozen encoder of the LDM as a feature extractor, followed by two Dual Attention Blocks (channel and spatial) and a classifier head. All models are trained per fold in stratified 5-fold cross-validation, ensuring no data leakage between training and validation sets.

## Key Results
- Maximum AUC: **0.7903** (5-fold CV)  
- Synthetic data impact: mean AUC increased from **0.6760 to 0.7417** across baseline models  
- Generation quality: FID **5.33**, SSIM **0.74**, PSNR **22.74**  

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Tumor-Localized ROI Crop (TLCR)
- **Claim:** Focusing on a peritumoral crop improves classification by concentrating computational resources on the tumor-nerve interface where PNI features are spatially localized.
- **Mechanism:** NeoSeg’s segmentation mask provides a geometric tumor center. TLCR extracts a dual-channel 96×96×48 patch centered there: Channel 1 (peritumoral liver+tumor) and Channel 2 (tumor core). This design forces the classifier to attend to the relevant boundary region.
- **Core assumption:** PNI imaging biomarkers are confined to the immediate tumor vicinity, not distributed throughout the abdomen.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.3 explicitly state the premise of spatially localized features. Ablation (Table 5) implies that without this focused input, performance drops.
- **Break condition:** Tumor segmentation failure (Dice <0.90) mislocalizes the crop center, likely excluding true PNI features and causing catastrophic performance loss.

### Mechanism 2: Synthetic Data Balancing via NeoGen
- **Claim:** A 3D LDM with ControlNet mitigates class imbalance by generating high-fidelity, anatomically plausible synthetic patches to create a balanced 1:1 training set.
- **Mechanism:** NeoGen is trained in two stages: (1) a 3D-LDM (VAE + diffusion U-Net) learns the distribution of real patches; (2) a ControlNet is conditioned on the dual-channel anatomical mask from TLCR. During inference, masks from PNI+ cases guide generation of synthetic patches that preserve anatomical structure. These augment the minority class.
- **Core assumption:** Conditionally generated synthetic data can augment the minority class without introducing distributional shift that harms classifier generalization.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes generation for balancing. Section 4.2 reports quantitative fidelity (FID 5.33, SSIM 0.74). Table 4 shows mean AUC gain (0.6760 → 0.7417) from augmentation.
- **Break condition:** Poor ControlNet conditioning (high FID >15, misaligned anatomy) introduces artifacts, leading to overfitting on synthetic patterns and degraded real-data performance.

### Mechanism 3: PattenNet Architecture with Frozen LDM Encoder and Dual Attention
- **Claim:** A frozen LDM encoder followed by two Dual Attention Blocks (DABs) enables detection of subtle PNI features by leveraging generic low-level features and applying focused 3D attention to refine them.
- **Mechanism:** The LDM encoder (pre-trained for reconstruction) is frozen and serves as a generic feature extractor. Its output passes through two DABs—each applies Channel Attention (`Mc`) to recalibrate feature channels, then 3D Spatial Attention (`Ms`, 7×7×7 kernel) to highlight spatial regions like the tumor-liver interface. Global pooled features are classified.
- **Core assumption:** Reconstruction features from a VAE encoder contain transferable low-to-mid level representations (edges, textures), and a lightweight dual-attention stack can adapt these to the subtle PNI signal without overfitting.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions frozen encoder and DABs for subtle intensity variations. Section 5 (Table 5) ablation shows removing DABs drops AUC from 0.7903 to 0.7001, and using only one attention type underperforms the dual mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Poorly trained LDM encoder (low reconstruction quality) yields noisy features. Incorrect DAB depth (e.g., 3 blocks hurt performance per ablation) prevents effective feature refinement.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: 3D Medical Image Segmentation (e.g., SwinUNETR)**  
  *Why needed here:* NeoSeg requires accurate 3D segmentation of liver and tumor to provide precise masks for TLCR. SwinUNETR’s hierarchical transformer models long-range dependencies, crucial for distinguishing irregular tumors in large organs.  
  *Quick check:* What key architectural difference between 3D U-Net and SwinUNETR improves segmentation of complex liver tumors?
- **Concept: Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) for 3D Data**  
  *Why needed here:* NeoGen must generate high-resolution 3D patches efficiently. Operating in a compressed latent space (via VAE) makes diffusion computationally tractable for 3D, avoiding prohibitive pixel-space costs.  
  *Quick check:* Why does compressing a 3D image into a latent space before diffusion drastically reduce memory/compute versus pixel-space diffusion?
- **Concept: Attention Mechanisms in 3D (Channel & Spatial)**  
  *Why needed here:* PattenNet must learn *where* to look (spatial attention at tumor-nerve interface) and *which* feature maps to emphasize (channel attention for PNI-specific patterns) from frozen encoder features. The 3D convolution captures inter-slice context.  
  *Quick check:* In a 3D patch (D×H×W×C), what dimensions does Channel Attention operate on versus Spatial Attention?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Raw MRI → NeoSeg (SwinUNETR) → TLCR → [Real Dual-Channel Patch] & [Mask for NeoGen] → [Synthetic Patches from NeoGen] → Balanced Dataset → PattenNet (Frozen LDM Encoder + 2 DABs + MLP) → PNI Probability  
- **Critical path:** All steps must preserve data isolation per cross-validation fold. Synthetic data generation (NeoGen) uses only the current fold’s training masks; PattenNet trains on the fold’s balanced set (real + synthetic). Inference on a new patient follows the same path without generative exposure.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Frozen LDM encoder:* Reduces trainable parameters (beneficial for small data) but prevents task-specific fine-tuning; assumes reconstruction features are sufficiently generic.  
  - *Synthetic data balancing:* Requires high-fidelity generation (validated by FID/SSIM). Risk: Classifier may overfit to subtle synthetic artifacts.  
  - *Dual-channel input:* Provides explicit spatial guidance (tumor vs. peritumoral) but relies entirely on segmentation accuracy; errors cascade.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **NeoSeg:** Tumor Dice <<0.95 on validation; manually inspect for under/over-segmentation.  
  - **NeoGen:** Generated patches exhibit blurry boundaries, unrealistic intensities, or mask misalignment; FID >15, SSIM <0.6.  
  - **PattenNet:** (a) High train AUC, low val AUC → overfitting to synthetic data or excessive DAB depth. (b) Both AUCs ~0.55 → frozen encoder features not discriminative or attention failing. (c) Low PNI+ recall → model ignoring subtle features.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Validate NeoSeg:** On a held-out fold, compute mean liver and tumor Dice. Target: >0.95 (liver), >0.85 (tumor) per Table 1. Visualize mask overlays.  
  2. **Qualitative Synthetic Data Review:** Generate 10 synthetic patches from PNI+ masks. Have a radiologist rank for anatomical plausibility and believable PNI-like subtlety. Aim for >80% “plausible”.  
  3. **Ablation on Validation Set:** For the best fold, train 4 PattenNet variants: (a) full (2 DABs), (b) frozen encoder only (0 DABs), (c) only channel attention, (d) only spatial attention. Expect full model AUC to exceed others by >0.05 (per Table 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multi-center generalization:** Does NeoNet generalize to external cohorts with different scanners/protocols? The study uses single-institution data.
2. **Multi-phase MRI integration:** Would adding arterial/portal venous phases improve performance beyond the single hepatobiliary phase?
3. **Optimal balancing ratio:** Is 1:1 synthetic-to-real optimal, or could other ratios (e.g., 2:1) yield higher AUC?
4. **Clinical realism of synthetic data:** Do radiologists perceive the synthetic patches as anatomically plausible representations of PNI?
5. **Fine-tuning the frozen encoder:** Would allowing gradient updates to the LDM encoder during PattenNet training improve PNI detection?

## Limitations
- **Synthetic data protocol unspecified:** Number of samples per mask, mask reuse strategy, and quality filtering are not detailed, risking overfitting or low diversity.
- **Frozen encoder transferability untested:** Assumes VAE reconstruction features contain PNI-relevant patterns; no evidence these generic features align with subtle PNI biomarkers without fine-tuning.
- **Segmentation dependency critical:** Entire pipeline hinges on NeoSeg accuracy; tumor Dice <0.90 likely cascades failure, but breakpoints are untested.

## Confidence
- **Synthetic balancing efficacy:** *Medium* (AUC gain shown, but long-term generalization with synthetic data is inferential; relies on unstated generation protocol).
- **Dual attention design:** *High* (Strong ablation evidence in Table 5; attention improves AUC by ~0.09 over baseline).
- **Tumor-localized cropping:** *Medium* (Mechanism is plausible and aligned with clinical intuition, but no direct validation that PNI features are confined to the 96×96×48 crop).

## Next Checks
1. **Segmentation robustness stress test:** Artificially degrade NeoSeg tumor Dice (e.g., 0.95→0.85) on a validation subset and measure PattenNet AUC drop. Expected: catastrophic failure below Dice ~0.90, confirming break condition.
2. **Synthetic patch diversity audit:** Generate 100 synthetic patches from 10 real PNI+ masks. Compute LPIPS diversity score and latent PCA. Target: LPIPS >0.15 and no latent clustering by source mask, indicating sufficient variation.
3. **Dual-channel input ablation:** Train PattenNet with (a) swapped channels (tumor core as channel 1), (b) single-channel (only peritumoral), and (c) random noise in one channel. Expect full dual-channel AUC to exceed alternatives by >0.05, validating spatial design premise.
