# An Approximate Ascent Approach To Prove Convergence of PPO
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.03386  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03386  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Proved finite‑time convergence for PPO with multiple epochs and minibatch reuse; identified tail‑mass collapse bias in truncated GAE; proposed finite‑time GAE, yielding faster learning on LunarLander‑v3 and Ant‑v4.

## Executive Summary
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a widely used reinforcement learning algorithm but lacks theoretical convergence guarantees, and its standard advantage estimator (truncated GAE) suffers from a tail‑mass collapse bias. This paper reinterprets PPO’s clipped surrogate update as an approximate policy gradient ascent step and analyzes its cyclic training with random reshuffling of minibatches. They derive bounds on the accumulated bias and prove a finite‑time convergence theorem for PPO under a step‑size condition. Additionally, they show that truncated GAE incorrectly assigns the infinite‑horizon tail weight to the longest available k‑step estimator, inflating variance. The authors propose a finite‑time GAE with renormalized weights that redistributes this mass to the observable horizon.

Theoretically, the convergence bound (Theorem 6.2) guarantees decay of the L²‑gradient norm when the learning rate satisfies η ≤ 1/(Lm), where L is the smoothness of the objective and m is the minibatch size. Empirically, on LunarLander‑v3 the corrected GAE yields substantial speedups and lower final episode lengths; similar improvements are observed on Ant‑v4 under default hyperparameters, confirming the practical impact of reducing advantage‑estimate variance.

## Method Summary
The paper’s method comprises two intertwined contributions. First, it formalizes PPO’s clipped surrogate objective as a biased approximation of the true policy gradient and bounds this bias under assumptions of bounded rewards, Lipschitz score functions, and strictly positive policies. Treating PPO’s training cycle (collect rollouts once, then perform K epochs of random‑shuffled minibatch updates) as a random reshuffling scheme, the authors prove convergence for small step sizes. Second, they identify that in finite‑horizon tasks the standard truncated GAE incorrectly collapses the remaining infinite‑horizon geometric mass onto the longest k‑step estimator, leading to high variance near episode termination. Their finite‑time GAE renormalizes the geometric weights by the actual remaining horizon, producing a lower‑variance advantage estimate.

Reproduction involves implementing the renormalization factor in the GAE computation, then training PPO (e.g., with Stable‑Baselines3) on LunarLander‑v3 using the corrected estimator and comparing learning curves to the standard truncated version under the same hyperparameters.

## Key Results
- Finite‑time convergence theorem for PPO with multiple epochs and minibatch reuse (Theorem 6.2), guaranteeing decay of the L²‑gradient norm under η ≤ 1/(Lm).
- Empirical validation: finite‑time GAE yields faster learning and shorter final episode lengths on LunarLander‑v3; analogous gains on Ant‑v4.
- Identification and correction of tail‑mass collapse in truncated GAE, reducing variance of advantage estimates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** PPO’s clipped surrogate objective corresponds to a biased policy gradient ascent step whose bias can be bounded relative to the true gradient.
- **Mechanism:** Formalize `gclip_PPO(θ, θ_old)` as an approximation of `∇θJ(θ)` that drops all but one importance ratio and clips it, replacing the on‑policy advantage with the old‑policy advantage. Theorem 4.2 derives a bias bound `|∇J(θ)−gclip_PPO(θ,θ_old)| ≤ R|θ−θ_old|` under assumptions on rewards, score function Lipschitzness and boundedness.
- **Core assumption:** Assumption 3.1 (bounded rewards, bounded/Lipschitz score function) and strictly positive policies.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “reinterpret PPO’s policy update scheme as approximate policy gradient ascent… show how to control the bias accumulated by the surrogate gradients”
  - [section] Section 4 (Rethinking PPO) and Theorem 4.2
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., “PPO in the Fisher‑Rao geometry”) lack bias control via clipping; this work provides the first finite‑time bias bound.
- **Break condition:** If the policy deviates far from the sampling policy (`|θ−θ_old|` large) or the clipping parameter ϵ is too small, the bias bound may no longer be informative.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** PPO’s cyclic use of data with random reshuffling (multiple epochs over a fixed rollout buffer) allows additional “free” surrogate gradient steps without harming convergence, and can even speed it up.
- **Mechanism:** Treat each cycle (A2C step + K epochs of reshuffled minibatch updates) as a cyclic RR scheme. Under L‑smoothness of J and bounded surrogate gradients (Prop C.10), the deterministic analysis (Thm 5.1) shows that for small step size η, extra biased steps improve convergence. The stochastic analysis (Sec 6) proves Theorem 6.2: with η ≤ 1/(Lm) the L²‑gradient norm decays with cycle count.
- **Core assumption:** J is L‑smooth (Prop B.5), learning rate η ≤ 1/(Lm), advantage estimates are bounded and uniformly biased (Assumption A.2), score function bounds.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “use techniques from random reshuffling to prove a convergence theorem”
  - [section] Sections 5 (Deterministic Convergence) and 6 (Reshuffling Analysis: Convergence of PPO), Theorem 6.2
  - [corpus] Prior convergence results (e.g., neighbor “Fast Convergence of Softmax Policy Mirror Ascent”) do not cover sample reuse; this paper fills that gap.
- **Break condition:** If η exceeds 1/(Lm), the ascent property in Lemma D.8 fails and convergence is not guaranteed.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Truncated GAE incorrectly collapses infinite tail mass onto the longest available k‑step estimator, increasing variance; a renormalized finite‑time GAE redistributes this mass and empirically speeds learning in tasks with strong terminal signals.
- **Mechanism:** Proposition 7.1 shows that standard truncated GAE gives the tail mass λ^{τ−t−1} to the last k‑step estimator, causing excess variance. The finite‑time GAE (Def 7.2) normalizes geometric weights to the observable horizon: `Â_t = (1−λ)/(1−λ^{τ−t}) Σ_{k=0}^{τ−t−1} λ^k  Â^{(k)}_t`. This reduces variance (verified by toy covariance analysis) and yields large gains on LunarLander‑v3 (Fig 3) and Ant‑v4.
- **Core assumption:** The underlying MDP is finite‑horizon or terminating, TD errors are zero after episode end, and the value function approximation is reasonably accurate.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “identify a previously overlooked issue in truncated Generalized Advantage Estimation… tail‑mass collapse… propose finite‑time GAE with renormalized weights”
  - [section] Section 7 (Finite‑Time GAE), Proposition 7.1, Definition 7.2, Proposition 7.3; Fig 3 and Fig 4
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., “Dropout Strategy in Reinforcement Learning”) address variance in importance sampling but not tail‑mass collapse.
- **Break condition:** If λ is very close to 1 the normalized weights still concentrate on longer horizons; performance may degrade if the bias from bootstrapping dominates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Policy Gradient Theorem**
  - Why needed here: Provides the gradient expression `∇θJ(θ) = E[∇θ log π θ · A]` that PPO approximates.
  - Quick check question: How does the discount factor γ appear in the finite‑horizon policy gradient, and why do implementations often drop it?
- **Trust Region / Clipping**
  - Why needed here: PPO’s clipped surrogate is a practical trust‑region method; understanding clipping is key to the bias analysis.
  - Quick check question: If the importance ratio r = π_θ/π_old deviates by more than ϵ, what does PPO’s gradient contribution become?
- **Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE)**
  - Why needed here: The advantage estimator defines the TD‑errors used in surrogate gradients; tail‑mass collapse concerns arise from GAE truncation.
  - Quick check question: In infinite‑horizon GAE, what is the relationship between the λ‑parameter and the effective k‑step horizon?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Policy network π_θ, value network V_θ, rollout buffer (states, actions, rewards, values, timestamps), advantage estimation module (GAE or finite‑time GAE), minibatch sampler with random reshuffling, optimizer (SGD/Adam), learning‑rate scheduler.
- **Critical path:** 1) Collect n rollouts of length T under current policy (θ_old). 2) Compute advantages using chosen GAE variant on collected data. 3) Flatten rollouts into transition buffer {(s, a, r, v, t)} of size N = n·T. 4) For each epoch (K total): permute buffer; for each minibatch (size B): compute clipped advantage‑weighted gradient `gclip_PPO` and update θ. 5) After K epochs set θ_old ← θ and repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Clipping parameter ϵ (trust region size) vs. bias; λ for GAE (variance‑bias); number of epochs K (sample reuse vs. bias accumulation); minibatch size B (variance reduction vs. correlation); learning rate η (step size and stability). Finite‑time GAE changes only the recursion factor.
- **Failure signatures:** Diverging returns if η > 1/(Lm); oscillations if K too large without proper Learning‑rate decay; poor performance on terminal‑heavy tasks if using truncated GAE (high variance near termination); catastrophic forgetting if n rollouts too small.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Compare truncated vs. finite‑time GAE on LunarLander‑v3 under default SB3‑Zoo hyperparameters (Fig 3)—should observe faster learning with termination‑time GAE.  
  2. Vary number of epochs K while keeping total gradient budget constant to test the theoretical advantage of surrogate steps (Thm 5.1).  
  3. Ablate clipping parameter ϵ and learning rate η to identify stable regions satisfying the assumptions of Theorem 6.2.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the convergence analysis be extended to include KL‑regularization as used in PPO?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 8 (Conclusion and Future Work) states that adding KL‑regularization to the analysis is plausible.
- **Why unresolved:** The current analysis ignores KL‑regularization; incorporating it requires deriving new bias bounds and convergence arguments that handle the additional constraint.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A rigorous convergence theorem for PPO with KL‑regularization, together with experiments verifying that the theoretical guarantees match practical performance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the proposed analysis apply to PPO variants that do not use a critic, such as GRPO?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 8 mentions the interest in PPO variants without a critic and asks how the analysis extends to GRPO.
- **Why unresolved:** The existing theory relies heavily on critic‑based advantage estimates; critic‑less variants alter the gradient structure and may require a fundamentally different approach.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An adaptation of the bias and convergence analysis to the GRPO setting, proving similar non‑asymptotic guarantees.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the formal differences between symmetric clipping (as analyzed) and the asymmetric clipping used in practical PPO be quantified?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 8 asks what kind of asymmetric clipping can be analysed formally and whether formal differences can be understood.
- **Why unresolved:** The analysis uses symmetric clipping for tractability, while the standard PPO clip is asymmetric. This may affect bias and convergence rates, but the impact is not quantified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A refined bias bound that explicitly incorporates the asymmetry, and a stochastic convergence analysis that reveals any practical difference in learning behavior.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the convergence analysis of PPO be improved by proving variance reduction effects of multi‑rollout flattening and by using a less crude comparison with the cycle start?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 8 suggests that Theorem 6.2 can be improved by proving variance reduction from multi‑rollout flattening and using a sharper RR analysis.
- **Why unresolved:** The current bounds are conservative and do not exploit potential gains from efficient data reuse across rollouts or a more refined treatment of the random reshuffling dynamics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Tighter non‑asymptotic error bounds that explicitly depend on the number of rollouts and a more precise pathwise bias control technique.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** For finite‑time GAE, what are the bias‑variance trade‑offs introduced by the renormalization, and under which conditions does termination‑time GAE outperform truncated GAE?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 8 calls for a comprehensive experimental study of when finite‑time GAE works better and for a theoretical quantification of bias‑variance trade‑offs in toy examples.
- **Why unresolved:** Only a single environment (LunarLander) and a simplified iid‑TD‑error model were examined; a systematic understanding across more tasks and realistic TD sequences is lacking.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A broad benchmark across diverse environments (including continuous control) with consistent hyperparameter tuning, and a theoretical framework that characterizes the covariance structure and its impact on learning under general TD‑error distributions.

## Limitations
- Theoretical assumptions (bounded rewards, Lipschitz score function, L‑smoothness, strictly positive policies) may not hold in practice, weakening the guarantees.
- The convergence step‑size condition η ≤ 1/(Lm) is restrictive and difficult to verify; using larger step sizes may break the guarantee.
- Empirical validation is limited to two MuJoCo tasks (LunarLander‑v3, Ant‑v4); the findings may not generalize to other domains.
- Finite‑time GAE implementation requires careful handling of numerical stability (e.g., division by near‑zero when λ≈1).
- Exact hyperparameters from SB3‑Zoo are not provided, making exact replication challenging.
