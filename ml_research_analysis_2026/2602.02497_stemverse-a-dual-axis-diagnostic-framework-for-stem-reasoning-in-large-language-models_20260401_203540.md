# STEMVerse: A Dual-Axis Diagnostic Framework for STEM Reasoning in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.02497  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02497  
- **Reference Count:** 40  
- **Primary Result:** STEMVerse unifies fragmented STEM benchmarks into a dual-axis capability matrix, diagnosing LLM failures as either knowledge deficit (disciplinary axis) or reasoning limitation (cognitive axis), revealing a "logic-symbolic collapse" from Apply to Analyze in Mathematics and an "instruction-tuning paradox" where base models outperform instruct variants in high-cognitive tasks.

## Executive Summary
STEMVerse addresses the fragmentation of STEM evaluation by re-aggregating over 20,374 problems from mainstream benchmarks into a unified "Discipline × Cognition" capability space. The framework maps performance across 27 academic sub-disciplines and six Bloom’s cognitive levels, enabling precise diagnosis of model weaknesses. Applied to Qwen and Llama models (3B–14B), it finds that models excel in lower cognitive tiers (Remember/Understand) but exhibit a sharp performance drop—termed "logic-symbolic collapse"—when transitioning from Apply to Analyze, particularly in Mathematics. Scaling gains are non-linear with critical thresholds (e.g., large improvements from 8B to 14B in Understand). Notably, an "instruction-tuning paradox" emerges: base models often outperform their instruct-tuned counterparts in high-cognitive tasks (e.g., Qwen3-14B base: 66.7% vs. instruct: 33.3% in Math Analysis), suggesting alignment may suppress reasoning depth. Smaller models show "siloed knowledge" where disciplinary mastery is uneven, and data composition appears more predictive of STEM success than parameter count alone.

## Method Summary
STEMVerse constructs a dual-axis diagnostic framework by:
1. **Data Re-aggregation:** Collecting problems from benchmarks (MATH500, GSM8K, AMC, MMLU, GPQA, ChemBench, etc.) and discarding original labels. Each problem is reassigned to one of 27 fine-grained sub-disciplines (e.g., Math: Analysis; Physics: Quantum Mechanics) and one of six Bloom’s levels (Remember to Create) based on content.
2. **Hybrid Annotation:** Using GPT-4o for scalable initial labeling of all problems, followed by expert manual review of a 10% random sample per discipline. Discrepancies are resolved by a second expert, achieving high Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA 0.87–0.92).
3. **Evaluation:** Models are prompted with few-shot examples aligned to MegaScience (Fan et al., 2025). Predictions are generated for all problems, and accuracy is computed per cell in the Discipline × Cognition matrix.
4. **Analysis:** Performance patterns are visualized (e.g., bubble charts) to identify collapses (e.g., Apply→Analyze drop), scaling non-linearities, base vs. instruct gaps, and disciplinary silos.

## Key Results
- **Logic-Symbolic Collapse:** Models show strong Remember/Understand performance but a sharp accuracy degradation from Apply to Analyze, especially in Mathematics, indicating failure in multi-step symbolic reasoning.
- **Instruction-Tuning Paradox:** Base models consistently outperform instruct-tuned variants in high-cognitive tasks (e.g., Qwen3-14B base: 66.7% vs. instruct: 33.3% in Math Analysis), suggesting alignment prioritizes formatting over deep reasoning.
- **Disciplinary Silos & Scaling:** Smaller models exhibit fragmented knowledge across sub-disciplines; data composition (benchmark sources) predicts STEM success more than parameter count, with non-linear scaling gains at specific thresholds (e.g., 8B→14B in Understand).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dual-Axis Disentanglement of Knowledge and Reasoning
- **Claim:** The matrix isolates whether failures stem from insufficient domain knowledge (academic specialization) or inadequate cognitive processing (Bloom’s level).
- **Mechanism:** Labeling each problem by sub-discipline and Bloom’s level maps performance onto a grid. A drop from Apply to Analyze in Mathematics indicates a "logic-symbolic collapse" where symbolic execution works but multi-step reasoning fails.
- **Core Assumption:** Knowledge and reasoning are separable dimensions; failures can be localized to one axis.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [Abstract]: framework maps capability across specialization and complexity; [Section 3.3]: matrix distinguishes failure sources; [corpus]: novelty in combining fine-grained disciplines with Bloom’s.

### Mechanism 2: Cross-Benchmark Re-aggregation
- **Claim:** Re-aggregating problems from disparate benchmarks into a unified taxonomy eliminates artificial silos and enables cross-disciplinary profiling.
- **Mechanism:** Original benchmark labels are discarded; problems are reassigned to the dual-axis taxonomy based on content, creating a common capability space.
- **Core Assumption:** Benchmark boundaries do not reflect true capability boundaries; reclassification is reliable.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [Abstract]: re-aggregates 20,000+ problems; [Section 3.2]: resolves fragmentation and isolation; [corpus]: neighbors highlight silos but lack re-aggregation for STEM.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid Human-AI Annotation
- **Claim:** Combining GPT-4o for scalability with expert human review ensures high-quality, reliable dual-axis labels at scale.
- **Mechanism:** GPT-4o annotates all problems using defined prompts (Appendix A); 10% random sample manually reviewed by experts; discrepancies resolved by a second expert. Yields IAA 0.87–0.92.
- **Core Assumption:** Experts can accurately judge discipline and cognitive level; GPT-4o is sufficiently consistent as initial annotator.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [Section 3.4]: hybrid approach with IAA scores; [Section 3.4.2]: mitigates black-box risks; [corpus]: prior annotation work exists but not for this scheme.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Bloom’s Taxonomy**  
  - **Why needed:** Defines the cognitive complexity axis, differentiating tasks from recall to synthesis. Without it, Apply could not be distinguished from Analyze.  
  - **Quick check:** List the six Bloom’s levels in order (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create). For "derive the formula for kinetic energy," is it Apply or Create? Explain.  
    *(It is Apply: deriving a known formula from principles requires using knowledge in a new situation but does not entail creating original synthesis.)*

- **Concept: Academic Sub-discipline Taxonomy (Table 2)**  
  - **Why needed:** Structures the disciplinary axis into fine-grained categories (e.g., Quantum Mechanics vs. Classical Mechanics). Reveals "siloed knowledge" where models excel in some subfields but not others.  
  - **Quick check:** Given a problem about "DNA sequencing techniques," which sub-discipline under Biology does it belong to? What about "Navier-Stokes equations"?  
    *(DNA sequencing -> Genetics; Navier-Stokes -> Fluid Dynamics.)*

- **Concept: Capability Matrix Interpretation**  
  - **Why needed:** The matrix visualizes model performance across both axes. Patterns like "logic-symbolic collapse" appear as a sharp drop from Apply to Analyze in symbolic disciplines.  
  - **Quick check:** If a model has 80% accuracy in Remember (Math) but 20% in Analyze (Math), what does that suggest about its reasoning? How would you check if this is a knowledge vs. reasoning issue?  
    *(Suggests it can recall facts but fails at logical synthesis. Check by verifying performance in related Apply problems: if Apply is also high, knowledge is sufficient and the issue is reasoning depth.)*

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component Map:**  
  Data Ingestion (benchmark sources) -> Re-aggregation Module (maps to dual-axis taxonomy) -> Annotation Pipeline (GPT-4o + expert review) -> Evaluation Engine (few-shot prompting, accuracy recording) -> Visualization Module (bubble charts, heatmaps).

- **Critical Path:**  
  1. Collect raw problems from benchmarks.  
  2. Define taxonomy (sub-disciplines, Bloom’s levels).  
  3. Annotate all problems via GPT-4o.  
  4. Expert validation on 10% sample.  
  5. Finalize labeled dataset.  
  6. For each model: run inference on all problems.  
  7. Aggregate results per matrix cell.  
  8. Analyze and visualize patterns.

- **Design Tradeoffs:**  
  - Annotation cost vs. coverage (AI reduces cost but needs human oversight).  
  - Granularity (27 sub-disciplines gives detail but yields sparse cells).  
  - Cognitive labeling subjectivity (IAA ~0.9 high but some ambiguity remains).

- **Failure Signatures:**  
  - Low IAA (<0.8) indicates labeling problems.  
  - Cells with <10 examples produce noisy estimates.  
  - Evaluation variance if few-shot prompts are not standardized.  
  - Coverage gaps in non-core STEM (e.g., engineering) limit generalizability.

- **First 3 Experiments:**  
  1. **Reproduction:** Evaluate Qwen3-14B-Base and Instruct on STEMVerse; verify reported patterns: high Remember/Understand, sharp Apply→Analyze drop in Math, and Instruct underperforming Base in Analysis.  
  2. **Ablation on Data Composition:** Remove one benchmark (e.g., GPQA) and recompute the matrix; check if "logic-symbolic collapse" persists to assess robustness to benchmark selection.  
  3. **Generalization Test:** Apply the framework to a new model family (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) to see if the same structural failures appear; validates that STEMVerse diagnoses inherent reasoning limitations rather than artifacts of the tested models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can STEM reasoning evaluations be extended to applied STEM fields that require multimodal understanding (e.g., diagrams, code execution) beyond the current text-only dual-axis framework?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section states: "the current version of STEMVerse does not yet encompass more applied fields. Evaluating LLMs in these areas often requires assessing multimodal understanding or code-execution capabilities, which are beyond the current scope of our text-based reasoning matrix."  
- **Why unresolved:** The framework is designed for symbolic text-based STEM reasoning. Applied domains introduce visual, spatial, and executable reasoning layers not captured by the Discipline × Cognition matrix.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A revised benchmark integrating multimodal data (e.g., lab diagrams, simulation outputs) with standardized textual prompts, defining analogous dual-axis labels for applied sub-disciplines and cognitive tasks involving tool use, validated via expert review.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific mechanisms within instruction tuning (e.g., loss functions, data curation, RLHF) cause the observed "instruction-tuning paradox" where base models outperform instruct variants in high-cognitive STEM tasks?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "An 'instruction-tuning paradox' emerges where base models outperform instruct variants in high-cognitive tasks, e.g., Qwen3-14B base achieves 66.7% vs. 33.3% in Math Analysis... suggesting alignment suppresses reasoning depth." Case 1 shows instruct models prioritizing "formatting-oriented reasoning."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the effect but does not analyze training configurations to isolate the cause; it hypothesizes suppression of "diverse internal reasoning paths" but lacks ablations.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments comparing multiple instruct checkpoints from the same base model, analyzing training logs/data for correlations with performance drops; probing internal representations during high-cognitive problem-solving; testing if loosening format constraints restores base-model performance.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does model scaling exhibit non-linear "critical thresholds" for cognitive gains (e.g., large leaps from 8B→14B in Understand tier) and what architectural or data factors determine these thresholds?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Scaling is non-linear with critical thresholds, e.g., large gains from 8B to 14B in Understand." Also: "data composition predicting STEM success over parameter count" and "siloed knowledge" in smaller models.  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper correlates thresholds with parameter jumps but does not investigate whether thresholds stem from emergent modularity, minimum capacity for relational knowledge synthesis, or data diversity shifts. Causality (composition vs. scale) is unclear.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Scaling experiments with matched data distributions across sizes; architectural analyses (neuron attribution, circuit mapping) at thresholds; data ablation studies to see if enriching STEM corpora for smaller models eliminates disparities.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** To what extent is Bloom’s Taxonomy—originally designed for human learning—a valid and granular metric for characterizing LLM cognitive breakdowns, given observed "knowledge-reasoning gaps" (e.g., high Remember/Apply but collapse at Analyze)?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The framework hinges on Bloom’s to map collapse (e.g., "logic-symbolic collapse" from Apply→Analyze). Case 3 (Remember) vs. Case 4 (Analyze) shows a model can recall complex facts yet fail logical synthesis, suggesting cognitive labels may not align with human stages.  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper uses Bloom’s as an external, assumed-valid scale but does not validate whether LLM failure modes correspond to human cognitive taxonomies. The "knowledge-reasoning gap" implies LLMs may have a different capability structure (associative memory vs. systematic reasoning).  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Human expert evaluations of LLM reasoning traces to verify Bloom’s assignments; alternative frameworks (e.g., dual-process theory) applied to the same set; correlation studies between Bloom’s performance and model inductive biases (CoT fidelity, symbolic modules).

## Limitations
- **Labeling Ambiguity:** Re-aggregation involves subjective Bloom’s assignment; high IAA (0.87–0.92) cannot eliminate misclassification noise, especially for boundary problems (Apply vs. Analyze).
- **Prompt Sensitivity:** Unspecified few-shot prompting aligned with MegaScience introduces performance variance; observed "logic-symbolic collapse" may be artifactually amplified or suppressed by prompt design.
- **Model Version Fidelity:** Claims about base vs. instruct performance rely on specific Qwen/Llama checkpoints; undocumented training details risk mis-pairing.
- **Taxonomy Granularity:** 27 sub-disciplines yield sparse cells (<10 problems in some), creating noisy per-cell estimates and potential overinterpretation of "siloed knowledge."

## Confidence
- **Logic-symbolic collapse (Apply→Analyze drop in Math):** High (pattern robust across models, visually clear in Figure 7, consistent with known LLM limitations).
- **Instruction-tuning paradox (base > instruct in high-cognition):** Medium (supported by Qwen3-14B data but dependent on specific variants and alignment recipes; requires cross-family replication).
- **Disciplinary hierarchies & data composition > scale:** Medium (correlation persuasive but confounded by benchmark source distributions; causality inferential).
- **Dual-axis disentanglement validity:** Medium-Low (core assumption of separable axes plausible but lacks direct validation; confounding if disciplines intrinsically demand higher Bloom’s levels).

## Next Checks
1. **Paradox Generalization Test:** Evaluate latest instruct-tuned models from diverse families (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Mistral Large) on Math Analysis cells. If base>instruct gap persists, the paradox reflects a fundamental alignment-reasoning trade-off.
2. **Prompt Robustness Ablation:** For a fixed Math Analysis subset, run inference with 5+ few-shot prompt variations (minimal, CoT, step-by-step). Recompute Apply→Analyze gap. A consistent >15% drop across prompts validates collapse as intrinsic, not prompt-induced.
3. **Composition Isolation via Dataset Ablation:** Recompute matrix after removing each source benchmark (e.g., exclude MATH500, then GPQA). If "siloed knowledge" or scaling thresholds remain stable, findings are robust to benchmark selection bias; if they vanish, results are dataset-specific artifacts.
