# Recovered in Translation: Efficient Pipeline for Automated Translation of Benchmarks and Datasets

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.22207
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22207
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** A fully automated translation framework using test-time compute scaling (USI, T-RANK) produces high-quality multilingual benchmark translations, leading to more reliable LLM evaluations and consistent downstream performance gains.

## Executive Summary
Multilingual evaluation of large language models (LLMs) is often unreliable due to low-quality benchmark translations that suffer from semantic drift and structural errors (e.g., answer leakage). This paper introduces a configurable, fully automated framework to translate major English benchmarks (MMLU, Hellaswag, ARC, Winogrande) into eight European languages. The core innovation applies test-time compute scaling strategies—Universal Self-Improvement (USI) and a new multi-round ranking method, T-RANK—to synthesize and select superior translations from multiple model candidates. Evaluations demonstrate that the framework's outputs significantly outperform existing translated resources on reference-based metrics (COMET) and LLM-as-a-judge comparisons. Downstream model evaluations on these improved benchmarks yield consistent accuracy improvements, confirming the translations' superior quality and the framework's utility for robust multilingual assessment.

## Method Summary
The framework translates benchmarks by processing each question-answer unit through one of four configurable methods. **Simple (SC)** performs a single zero-shot translation. **Best-of-N (BoN)** samples N diverse candidates (high temperature) and has the LLM score them, selecting the best. **Universal Self-Improvement (USI)** has the LLM fuse the strongest elements from all N candidates into a single refined output. **T-RANK** conducts N rounds of competitive ranking with rotated candidate positions to mitigate positional bias, then refines the top-ranked candidate. A critical "Benchmark Mode" ensures questions and answer choices are translated in a single, coherent context to prevent answer leakage. The user selects the method per target language, balancing computational cost (SC < BoN < USI < T-RANK) against expected quality uplift, which varies with language and benchmark structure.

## Key Results
*   **Translation Quality:** USI and T-RANK achieve state-of-the-art COMET scores on WMT24++ (e.g., EN→UK: 0.849) and win significant majorities in blind LLM-as-a-judge comparisons against existing resources like Global-MMLU (e.g., Ukrainian T-RANK wins 875 vs. 2016 losses).
*   **Downstream Impact:** Models evaluated on the framework's translations show consistent accuracy gains over prior translated benchmarks, averaging +3.42% on Winogrande and +2.35% on ARC-Challenge across languages.
*   **Method Efficacy:** T-RANK is favored for complex benchmark structures (MMLU, Winogrande), while USI is recommended for shorter dataset-style texts; both outperform simpler baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Test-Time Compute Scaling for Candidate Diversity and Synthesis
*   **Claim:** Sampling multiple translation candidates with higher temperature and then applying a selection/synthesis method (Best-of-N, USI, T-RANK) yields higher-quality translations than a single zero-shot pass.
*   **Mechanism:** Increased sampling temperature introduces lexical and syntactic diversity. A subsequent LLM-based evaluation step (scoring, fusion, or ranking) identifies and combines the most accurate elements from this pool, performing a bounded search over the output space at inference time.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states adapting test-time compute scaling (USI, T-RANK) allows "significantly higher quality outputs." [section 3.2] details implementation and notes USI/T-RANK achieve strongest performance.
*   **Break condition:** Computational cost (N model calls) becomes prohibitive, or the base model's zero-shot capability is sufficient for the target language/resource level, diminishing returns.

### Mechanism 2: Competitive Multi-Round Ranking to Mitigate Positional Bias and Sharpen Evaluation
*   **Claim:** T-RANK's multi-round, position-rotating competitive ranking reduces positional bias in LLM judges and leads to more reliable identification of subtle translation errors.
*   **Mechanism:** By systematically rotating candidate order across N rounds, each translation is evaluated in every possible input position. This exposes the judge to the full candidate spectrum, reducing its inherent preference for early positions and forcing more comparative, nuanced reasoning.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] describes the rotation strategy: "each candidate has been presented in each position exactly once." [section 4.1 & A.1] implies T-RANK finds "more inconsistencies" than USI.
*   **Break condition:** Candidate pool (N) is too small (<3) to make rotation meaningful, or the judge model cannot perform meaningful comparative reasoning.

### Mechanism 3: Integrated Framework with Language-Adaptive Configuration
*   **Claim:** A single, configurable framework that switches between methods based on language characteristics and cost constraints enables scalable, high-quality translation.
*   **Mechanism:** The framework separates concerns via `Dataset` vs. `Benchmark` modes. The user selects a method (SC, BoN, USI, T-RANK) per language pair, allowing a trade-off between cost and quality gain, which varies with language difficulty and model capability.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.1] lists key problems (data formats, QA structure, language phenomena). [section 3.2] contrasts method suitability: USI for short texts, T-RANK for benchmarks. [section 3.3 & Table 10] shows different model/method combos used for different languages.
*   **Break condition:** The user cannot afford evaluation costs to empirically select the best method for a new language pair, leading to suboptimal configuration.

## Foundational Learning

*   **Concept: Test-Time Compute Scaling**
    *   **Why needed here:** The core innovation applies inference-time compute (sampling, ranking, fusion) to translation, moving beyond a single pass. This paradigm shift from training-only scaling is crucial.
    *   **Quick check question:** How does generating N candidates at high temperature and then fusing/ranking them differ fundamentally from just using a larger model or more training data?
*   **Concept: Benchmark Integrity in Translation**
    *   **Why needed here:** The motivation is that flawed translations break benchmark validity. One must grasp that translating a question and its answers *as a coherent unit* is non-negotiable for tasks like Winogrande, where grammatical context can leak answers.
    *   **Quick check question:** Why is translating the question stem and answer options separately a critical flaw for sentence-completion benchmarks?
*   **Concept: LLM-as-a-Judge Biases**
    *   **Why needed here:** The framework relies on LLMs to score/rank translations. Knowing about positional bias, verbosity bias, and inconsistent scoring is essential to interpret T-RANK's design.
    *   **Quick check question:** What specific bias does T-RANK's multi-round sequential ranking aim to mitigate, and how does its structure address it?

## Architecture Onboarding

*   **Component map:** Data Handler (parses input) -> Method Executor (orchestrates SC/BoN/USI/T-RANK) -> Judge Prompter (formats candidates) -> Post-Processor (extracts final translation) -> Logger/Cost Tracker. Config Manager loads settings.
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Load config for target language `L` and benchmark type.
    2.  For each entry (Q, A): generate `N` candidate translations using base model with high temperature.
    3.  Pass candidates + original to Judge Prompter.
    4.  Execute selected method: BoN (score & pick), USI (fuse), T-RANK (multi-round rank & refine), or SC (single call).
    5.  Post-process output to schema and write.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Cost vs. Quality:** SC is cheapest but lowest quality; T-RANK is most expensive (~2N+1 calls). Tradeoff is empirical per language.
    *   **Model Choice:** Closed-source (GPT-4o, Gemini) vs. open-weight. Paper uses closed models; hypothesizes open-weight models benefit more but unvalidated.
    *   **Method Generality:** USI simpler/better for short texts; T-RANK better for complex benchmarks but has higher overhead. Framework doesn't auto-select.
    *   **Prompt Sensitivity:** Effectiveness depends on prompt wording for target language quirks.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Semantic Drift:** Output changes original meaning (e.g., "life cycle" vs. "lifespan").
    *   **Answer Leakage:** Grammatical agreement in translated stem gives away correct answer option.
    *   **Incoherent QA Pair:** Answer options no longer fit the translated question.
    *   **Positional Bias Residue:** In T-RANK logs, one input position (e.g., 2nd) consistently ranks 1st, indicating rotation failed.
    *   **Cost Overrun:** Unbounded `N`/`P` in T-RANK cause exponential cost; no automatic difficulty scaling.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Reproduce COMET Scores:** Run EN→UK MMLU with all 4 methods (N=5, temp=0.7) using provided prompts. Compute XCOMET-XL and QE scores; verify USI/T-RANK > SC/BoN.
    2.  **LLM-as-a-Judge Validation:** Use Gemini-2.5-Flash with prompt from Figure 5 to compare T-RANK translations vs. Global-MMLU on same EN→UK MMLU set. Expect win rate >50%.
    3.  **Downstream Model Evaluation Sanity Check:** Evaluate Gemma-3-12B on your T-RANK Winogrande-UK vs. MuBench-UK. Verify performance delta direction matches reported (+3.42% trend).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Under what specific benchmark and language conditions does T-RANK consistently outperform USI, or vice versa?** The paper notes it remains unclear; USI suits short/dataset translation, T-RANK for complex benchmarks, but lacks controlled variation to establish clear boundaries.
*   **Can a meta-translation system automatically select the optimal method per input based on predicted translation difficulty?** Current framework applies uniform methods per language/benchmark; adaptive selection is future work.
*   **Do open-weight models (e.g., Llama, Qwen) benefit more from test-time scaling than closed-source frontier models?** Hypothesis: greater benefit due to weaker zero-shot, but requires validation.
*   **How effective are USI and T-RANK for translating benchmarks into low-resource languages outside the European typological sphere?** Paper focuses on European languages; generalizability to radically different grammatical systems (e.g., polypersonal agreement) is unknown.
*   **For which combinations of language and benchmark type does human professional translation remain irreplaceably superior?** Evidence (e.g., Bulgarian Winogrande) shows human advantage in some cases, but no systematic analysis identifies the boundary conditions (e.g., cultural references, extreme ambiguity).

## Limitations
*   **Reproducibility gaps:** Exact hyperparameters (N, temperature) per language/benchmark, full LLM-as-a-judge protocols, and downstream model inference details are not fully specified.
*   **External validation:** Core mechanisms (test-time scaling for translation, multi-round ranking) lack independent corroboration from neighboring work; evidence is primarily internal.
*   **Generalization:** Efficacy across non-European language families and with open-weight models remains unvalidated, despite being a stated limitation.
