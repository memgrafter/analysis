# SaFRO: Satisfaction-Aware Fusion via Dual-Relative Policy Optimization for Short-Video Search

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.19585  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19585  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: SaFRO outperforms SOTA offline (avg NDCG 0.7102, satisfaction 0.8656, retention 0.8173) and yields significant online lifts (QRR –0.319%, CTR +0.136%, LPR +0.495%, watch time +0.611%) with sustained 60-day retention.

## Executive Summary
SaFRO tackles the misalignment between multi‑task fusion in short‑video search and long‑term user satisfaction. Direct reinforcement learning is hindered by data sparsity and query‑intent constraints. The proposed framework comprises three modules: (1) a satisfaction‑aware reward model that uses query‑level signals (reformulation and session gap) to capture holistic satisfaction; (2) Dual‑Relative Policy Optimization (DRPO) that stabilizes policy learning under sparse feedback via dual‑relative advantages; (3) Task‑Relation‑Aware Fusion (TRAF) to model inter‑objective dependencies and adaptively tune weights. Offline evaluation on Kuaishou data shows substantial gains over baselines, and large‑scale online A/B tests confirm improved engagement and retention.

## Method Summary
SaFRO formulates fusion weight selection as a reinforcement learning problem. The reward model \(R_\phi\) predicts satisfaction \(r_{\text{sat}} = (1-I_{\text{reform}})(\alpha \cdot s_{\text{gap}} + (1-\alpha)I_{\text{ret}})\), where \(s_{\text{gap}} = \exp(-\tilde{g}/T)\) is derived from a normalized session gap. The policy network employs TRAF: base logits \(z\) are refined via task embeddings \(u\), pairwise scores \(e_{ij}=u_i^\top u_j\), and a gated attention mechanism to produce \(\tilde{z}_i\). A discrete action space (bins for each objective with sum constraint) is sampled for each query. The composite reward \(r = r_{\text{eng}} + r_{\text{sat}} + r_{\text{fmt}}\) combines engagement gains (NDCG‑style), satisfaction, and format penalties. Training uses DRPO: for each query, sample \(G\) actions, compute dual‑relative advantage  
\(A_{\text{dual}} = \frac{r - \mu_{\text{group}}}{\sigma_{\text{group}}} + \frac{\mu_{\text{group}} - \mu_{\text{batch}}}{\sigma_{\text{batch}}}\),  
and optimize a clipped PPO objective with an entropy bonus.

## Key Results
- Offline: average NDCG 0.7102, satisfaction score 0.8656, user retention 0.8173.  
- Online A/B: Query Reformulation Rate ↓0.319%, Click‑Through Rate ↑0.136%, Long‑Play Ratio ↑0.495%, Watch Time ↑0.611%, sustained 60‑day retention increase.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The reward model converts proxy signals (reformulation, gap) into a continuous satisfaction metric that generalizes across users via personal baselines. DRPO’s dual‑relative advantage normalization reduces variance caused by sparse, binary rewards, enabling stable policy updates even when only a few long‑term outcomes are observed. TRAF learns task affinities, allowing the fusion weights to adapt to the interplay between objectives rather than optimizing them independently. Together these components align the multi‑task ranking function with long‑term user experience while respecting production constraints.

## Foundational Learning
- **Satisfaction‑aware reward modeling** – Captures long‑term value beyond clicks; quick check: correlate \(s_{\text{gap}}\) with next‑day retention (>0.3).  
- **Dual‑Relative Policy Optimization** – Stabilizes RL by group and batch normalization; quick check: per‑batch advantage mean ≈ 0, std ≈ 1.  
- **Task‑Relation‑Aware Fusion** – Models inter‑objective dependencies; quick check: attention weights non‑uniform and query‑dependent.  
- **Discrete action space for fusion** – Ensures production compatibility and simpler exploration; quick check: sampled weight sums within tolerance ξ.  
- **Composite reward design** – Balances engagement, satisfaction, and format; quick check: ablating any component degrades target metrics.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Data (logs) → Reward Model \(R_\phi\) → \(r_{\text{sat}}\)  
State (user/query/video) → Policy MLP → base logits \(z\) → TRAF (embeddings \(u\), pairwise \(e_{ij}\), attention, gating) → refined \(\tilde{z}\) → softmax → fusion weight distribution  
Fusion weights + multi‑task scores → combined score → ranked list → compute \(r_{\text{eng}}\), \(r_{\text{fmt}}\) → composite \(r\) → DRPO (dual‑relative advantage, PPO update) → policy update

**Critical path**  
1. Preprocess sessions: compute reformulation, session gap, personal 60% quantile baseline; get \(\tilde{g}\) and \(s_{\text{gap}}\).  
2. Train \(R_\phi\) to predict \(r_{\text{sat}}\) via weighted MSE.  
3. Build policy network with TRAF; define discrete bins and sum constraint.  
4. For each batch: sample \(G\) actions per query; compute composite reward; compute dual‑relative advantages; perform PPO update (clip ratio 0.2, annealed entropy).  
5. Deploy policy; monitor online signals; iterate.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Discrete weights limit granularity but simplify production and exploration.  
- Dual‑relative scaling reduces variance but introduces group/batch hyperparameters.  
- TRAF’s pairwise attention captures task relations at \(O(K^2)\) cost; for many tasks factorization may be needed.  
- Reward coefficients (implicit) require careful tuning to avoid over‑optimizing one facet.

**Failure signatures**  
- \(R_\phi\): low gap‑retention correlation, high validation loss.  
- DRPO: highly skewed advantages, exploding gradients, entropy collapse.  
- TRAF: \(\tilde{z} \approx z\) (minimal refinement), uniform attention, policy outputs static weights.  
- Overall: offline NDCG gains vanish with different seeds; online metrics decline.

**First experiments**  
1. Reward sanity: validate \(s_{\text{gap}}\) correlates positively with retention on held‑out users.  
2. DRPO ablation: replace dual‑relative advantage with standard PPO; compare stability (gradient norms, entropy) and final NDCG.  
3. TRAF relation test: visualize attention matrices; test (paired t‑test) that weights differ from uniform (p < 0.01).

## Limitations
- Unspecified hyperparameters for gap normalization (δ, T) and the exact definition of “semantically related” query reformulation.  
- Composite reward coefficients and TRAF’s gate parameterization are absent, requiring heuristic tuning.  
- Generalizability to other platforms is unvalidated; the satisfaction signal may be domain‑specific.

## Confidence
- **High**: Offline NDCG and online A/B lifts are backed by large‑scale experiments and reported metrics.  
- **Medium**: Theoretical justification for DRPO and TRAF is sound, but reliance on unstated hyperparameters lowers reproducibility confidence.  
- **Low**: Extrinsic validity (performance on other search systems) remains unproven.

## Next Checks
1. **Reward signal sanity** – Compute Pearson correlation between normalized \(s_{\text{gap}}\) and next‑day retention; expect positive correlation (~0.3–0.5).  
2. **DRPO ablation** – Replace dual‑relative advantages with standard PPO advantages; monitor gradient norms and NDCG change.  
3. **TRAF relation test** – Compare refined logits \(\tilde{z}\) to base \(z\); perform t‑test on attention weights to confirm non‑uniformity (p < 0.01).
