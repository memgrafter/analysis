# Induced Numerical Instability: Hidden Costs in Multimodal Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.04453
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04453
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Optimizing images to maximize float16 activation magnitudes induces compounded numerical errors, causing up to 59% performance drops in captioning and 5.2% in VQA across multiple LVLMs.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals a novel vulnerability in multimodal large language models (LVLMs) arising from numerical instability in low-precision floating-point operations. By constructing adversarial images that maximize the magnitude of intermediate activations during float16 inference, the authors induce imperceptible perturbations that lead to significant semantic degradation. Evaluations on four LVLMs across five vision-language benchmarks show dramatic performance declines, substantially exceeding random or Gaussian noise baselines. The attack proves effective across float16, bfloat16, and even float32, indicating a fundamental sensitivity in model computations rather than a mere low-precision artifact.

## Method Summary
The attack is a white-box optimization process that modifies an input image to maximize a proxy loss defined as the sum of absolute activation magnitudes across all elementary floating-point operations within the model’s forward pass (conducted in float16). This forces activations to larger values, where rounding errors in low-precision formats compound, introducing diffuse numerical inaccuracies throughout the network. The resulting perturbed images appear largely unchanged to humans but cause the model to generate nonsensical or incorrect outputs.

## Key Results
- Idefics3-8B’s CIDEr-D on MSCOCO dropped 59% (0.664 → 0.273).
- VQA accuracy decreased by up to 5.2% (LLaVa-v1.5-7B on VQAv2).
- Sentence-BERT similarity consistently fell (e.g., Idefics3-8B MSCOCO: 0.616 → 0.403).
- Performance degradation persisted across float16, bfloat16, and float32.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Maximizing activation magnitudes pushes values into regions where the relative error from floating-point rounding is largest. In low-precision formats like float16, the gap between representable numbers widens with magnitude, so large activations incur greater absolute rounding errors. These errors compound across successive layers and operations, corrupting the computational graph in a diffuse manner that degrades semantic understanding without creating the localized, high-norm perturbations typical of adversarial attacks.

Component map: Image -> Vision Encoder (e.g., CLIP-ViT) -> Visual Projection -> LLM Decoder -> Text Output.  
Critical path: Input image processed by vision encoder; visual tokens fed into LLM layers; attack targets all elementary operations (multiply-add, nonlinearities) within these components.  
Design tradeoffs: Low-precision inference for efficiency vs. numerical stability; white-box attack requirement limits realism.  
Failure signatures: Diffuse activation misalignment across layers, no perceptible image change, severe semantic drop (e.g., incoherent captions, wrong answers).

## Limitations
- Attack requires white-box access to intermediate activations; black-box transferability unknown.
- Evaluation limited to small (<8B) image-text LVLMs; video/audio and larger models untested.
- No theoretical derivation explaining why activation magnitude maximization compounds floating-point errors.
