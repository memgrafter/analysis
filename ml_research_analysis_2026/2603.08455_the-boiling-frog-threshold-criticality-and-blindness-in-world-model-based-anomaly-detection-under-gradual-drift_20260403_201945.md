# The Boiling Frog Threshold: Criticality and Blindness in World Model-Based Anomaly Detection Under Gradual Drift
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.08455
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08455
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Primary result**: A sharp, universal detection threshold ε* exists for gradual linear drift in world model prediction errors, but its position varies with detector sensitivity and environment dynamics; sinusoidal drift is completely undetectable, and fragile environments can collapse before detection.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the limits of using a reinforcement learning agent's learned world model—specifically its prediction error (PE)—as a self-monitoring signal for detecting gradual observation drift. Across four MuJoCo locomotion environments, three detector families, and three world model capacities, the authors injected synthetic linear and sinusoidal drifts into velocity observations. They found that a sharp, sigmoid-shaped detection threshold ε* universally emerges for linear drift, but its exact location is not fixed—it shifts based on detector configuration and, critically, on environment-specific dynamics. In contrast, sinusoidal drift remains statistically invisible in the PE signal, a property rooted in how world models internalize periodic variance. Furthermore, in the fragile Hopper environment, agents frequently physically collapse before any detector can accumulate sufficient evidence, revealing an unmonitorable failure mode. The work establishes that threshold existence is invariant, but its position reflects a complex interaction between the detector, the world model's noise floor, and the environment's response dynamics.

## Method Summary
The experimental protocol involves training a PPO agent in a MuJoCo environment, then training a forward MLP world model on the collected transitions. A baseline PE distribution is established from 300 unmodified steps. Gradual drift is injected at step 300 by additively perturbing velocity-related observations: linear drift scales as ε·t; sinusoidal drift as ε·sin(0.02πt). Three detector families (Doubt Index with z-score threshold and moving window, variance-based with sustained violation, percentile-based) are evaluated across hyperparameter sweeps. For each combination of environment (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d, Ant), world model capacity (small/medium/large), detector config, and drift intensity ε, 80 episodes (10 seeds × 8 runs) are conducted. The primary metric is detection rate versus ε, from which a sigmoid threshold ε* (e.g., 50% detection point) is extracted. Secondary analyses include survival time until physical collapse (Hopper), PE power spectra, and false positive rates.

## Key Results
- A sharp, sigmoid detection threshold ε* exists universally for linear drift, but its position varies with detector parameters and environment dynamics; within a single environment, ε* follows a power law in detector settings (R² = 0.89–0.97), but cross-environment prediction fails (R² = 0.45).
- Sinusoidal (zero-mean periodic) drift is completely undetectable (detection rate ~0%) across all detectors and intensities; spectral analysis shows PE power at the drift frequency is only 0.8× the baseline noise floor (vs. 201.6× for linear drift).
- In the Hopper environment, agents collapse physically before detection in over 99% of episodes at intermediate ε values—a “collapse before awareness” regime where time-to-collapse is shorter than time-to-detection, creating an unmonitorable failure mode.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Sharp Threshold Emergence
- **Claim**: A sharp, sigmoid-shaped detection threshold (ε*) exists universally across detector families and model capacities for gradual linear drift, but its position is not an intrinsic world model property—it emerges from a three-way interaction.
- **Mechanism**: The world model generates a prediction error (PE) signal with a learned noise floor. A detector compares incoming PE against this floor. The threshold ε* is the drift intensity where the shifted PE signal escapes the noise floor's variability envelope. The sigmoid shape arises from the cumulative distribution of the PE signal under drift; the horizontal position shifts with detector parameters (sensitivity) and the environment’s specific PE response dynamics (∂PE/∂ε).
- **Core assumption**: The PE distribution under no drift is stationary and representative of "normal" variation; detectors are calibrated relative to this baseline.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 4.1] Sigmoid shape invariance across 9 DI configs, 3 detector families, 3 model capacities in HalfCheetah and Ant. [section 4.5] Within-environment power-law fit (R²=0.89–0.97); cross-environment prediction fails (R²=0.45).
- **Break condition**: If the environment's dynamics cause the PE signal to become non-stationary even without drift, the baseline noise floor may misrepresent normal variation, blurring the sigmoid or shifting ε* unpredictably.

### Mechanism 2: Sinusoidal Blindness as World Model Property
- **Claim**: Sinusoidal (zero-mean periodic) drift is completely undetectable by all tested PE-based detectors—a fundamental property of the world model's PE signal, not a detector artifact.
- **Mechanism**: Sinusoidal perturbation induces positive and negative deviations that cancel over each cycle. The world model, optimized for model evidence (minimizing free energy), absorbs this periodic variance as part of its expected prediction error distribution. Consequently, the PE time series and its spectral power remain statistically indistinguishable from the no-drift baseline.
- **Core assumption**: The world model's training objective leads it to treat zero-mean oscillation as "noise" to be integrated into its internal generative model's variance.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 4.2] All detectors have detection rates ~0% at ε=0.5. Spectral analysis: sinusoidal PE power = 0.8× baseline vs. 201.6× for linear. [section 4.2 (bullet 3)] Blindness holds across a 10× threshold range.
- **Break condition**: If the sinusoidal drift interacts with environment dynamics to produce a non-zero-mean effect on state trajectories (e.g., resonance), the PE signal might develop a detectable component.

### Mechanism 3: Collapse Before Awareness (CBA)
- **Claim**: In "fragile" environments (e.g., Hopper), the agent's policy can collapse physically before any detector accumulates evidence to trigger—a fundamentally unmonitorable failure mode.
- **Mechanism**: Under moderate drift intensities, perturbed observations cause the policy to output destabilizing actions rapidly. Time-to-collapse (T_collapse) is shorter than time-to-detection (T_detection), which requires accumulating a statistical signal above the noise floor. Detector operating points cannot be adjusted to detect in this regime without incurring prohibitive false positives or further delaying detection.
- **Core assumption**: The policy is brittle to observation perturbations; the world model's PE signal is a faithful monitor of the prediction mismatch, but policy failure happens on a faster timescale than evidence accumulation.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 4.3] Quantifies survival gap Δ = T_collapse - T_detection; in Hopper at ε=0.05, collapse in ~25 steps, no detector fires (T_detection ~50). [section 4.4 (Hopper SDT plot)] Wide spread along sensitivity-specificity tradeoff curve.
- **Break condition**: If the detector is made extremely sensitive (e.g., single-step percentile at p=99.9) and the environment's noise floor is very low, T_detection might drop below T_collapse, but likely at the cost of unacceptable false positive rates. Alternatively, if the policy is robustified, T_collapse could increase beyond T_detection.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Reinforcement Learning (PPO) and Policy Collapse  
  *Why needed*: The paper’s setup starts with a trained PPO policy. CBA depends on this policy being fragile to observation drift—understanding that the agent's *actions* fail catastrophically is key.  
  *Quick check*: Why does the agent's physical collapse in Hopper depend on the *observation* drift, not just a faulty world model?
- **Concept**: World Model as Prediction Error Source  
  *Why needed*: The entire monitoring signal (PE) comes from a forward dynamics model. Understanding that this is a learned, stochastic predictor (not a ground-truth simulator) explains the existence of a "noise floor" and the possibility of model-specific blindness (sinusoidal).  
  *Quick check*: If the world model were perfect (MSE=0), would there still be a detection threshold ε*? What would change?
- **Concept**: Signal Detection Theory (SDT) Tradeoff  
  *Why needed*: The paper analyzes detector configurations as operating points on an ROC-like curve. This framework explains why no single detector achieves perfect sensitivity and specificity, especially in high-noise environments like Walker2d.  
  *Quick check*: In the SDT plots (Figure 2), what does a cluster in the upper-left corner versus a spread along the diagonal tell you about the environment's PE signal?
- **Concept**: Gradual Drift vs. Abrupt Change  
  *Why needed*: The study focuses on slow, continuous drift ("boiling frog" metaphor), which interacts differently with world model PE than sudden shifts.  
  *Quick check*: How might an abrupt, large-magnitude observation shift differ in its detection curve compared to a gradual linear drift?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Trained PPO Agent -> (s,a) transitions -> World Model (MLP f_θ) -> Prediction Error (PE) -> Detector (DI/Variance/Percentile) -> Alert.  
  Drift Injector modifies s_t (velocity dimensions) before agent observes it. Baseline PE statistics computed from pre-drift rollout.
- **Critical path**:  
  1. Train PPO agent to convergence in MuJoCo environment.  
  2. Collect transition dataset (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}) using trained policy.  
  3. Train world model f_θ to minimize MSE on dataset.  
  4. Run 300-step baseline rollout to compute PE mean/std/distribution.  
  5. Configure detector (family, hyperparameters).  
  6. For each ε: run evaluation episode with drift injected at t=300; record detection time and collapse time.  
  7. Repeat for all configs; extract ε* (e.g., detection rate = 50% point).
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - **Detector sensitivity vs. false positive rate (FPR)**: Lower z (DI) or k (variance) increases sensitivity but raises FPR; in high-noise environments (Walker2d), FPR may be intrinsically high, limiting achievable sensitivity.  
  - **Temporal integration (W)**: Larger windows reduce FPR but delay detection, worsening CBA risk in fragile environments.  
  - **Model capacity**: Larger models reduce baseline MSE but do not change ε* because z-score normalization cancels absolute scale.  
  - **Drift profile**: Linear drift reliably crosses threshold; sinusoidal drift never detected—tests signal absence vs. threshold crossing.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - **Sinusoidal blindness**: Detection rate ≈ 0% across all ε; PE spectral power at drift frequency ≈ baseline.  
  - **Collapse Before Awareness**: High collapse rate (>90%) at intermediate ε with zero detection; negative survival gap (T_collapse < T_detection).  
  - **High baseline FPR environment** (Walker2d): FPR at ε≈0 >10%; corrected ε* converges to narrow range (~0.002), indicating environment-dominated threshold.  
  - **Non-sigmoid curve**: Detection rate rises gradually without sharp transition—may indicate sweep missed transition region or non-stationary noise floor.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Threshold mapping sweep**: For HalfCheetah and DI detector, sweep z ∈ {2.0,2.5,3.0,3.5,4.0}, W ∈ {1,3,5,10,20} across ε ∈ logspace(10⁻⁴,0.01,16). Plot detection rate vs. ε; verify sigmoid shape; extract ε* at 50% detection. Confirms mechanism 1.  
  2. **Sinusoidal blindness control**: Run best DI config (lowest ε*) and percentile detector on sinusoidal drift at ε=0.1,0.5. Confirm detection rates ≈ 0%. Compute PE power spectrum; verify no peak at drift frequency. Confirms mechanism 2.  
  3. **CBA and SDT in Hopper**: Repeat Exp 1 but record time-to-collapse and time-to-detection. Compute survival gap Δ. Plot SDT (baseline FPR vs. detection rate at ε=0.01). Expect wide spread along tradeoff curve and negative Δ for most ε. Confirms mechanism 3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How does the detection threshold ε* generalize to vision-based observations and advanced world model architectures like RSSM or transformers?  
  *Basis*: Limitations: "Our study is limited to proprioceptive locomotion tasks with MLP world models; generalization to vision-based observations, transformer or RSSM world models, and non-locomotion tasks is unknown."  
  *Why unresolved*: The threshold phenomenon is derived from MLP-based prediction errors in specific locomotion environments; it may not hold for high-dimensional visual inputs or more complex dynamics models.
- **Open Question 2**: Do naturalistic, non-synthetic distribution shifts (e.g., gradual sensor degradation or environmental wear) exhibit the same sharp detection threshold and sinusoidal blindness as additive, axis-aligned drift?  
  *Basis*: Limitations: "Drift injection is synthetic (additive, axis-aligned); naturalistic distribution shift may exhibit different threshold behavior."  
  *Why unresolved*: Real-world drift often involves correlated, non-linear changes that might interact differently with world model prediction errors.
- **Open Question 3**: Can agents attribute the cause of detected drift (e.g., sensor failure vs. environmental change) using world model prediction errors alone?  
  *Basis*: Discussion: "We do not investigate causal attribution—the agent detects that something changed but not what."  
  *Why unresolved*: Self-monitoring without causality limits responsive actions; agents cannot distinguish between internal model corruption and external hazards.
- **Open Question 4**: How does gradual drift propagate and affect detection thresholds in multi-agent systems where agents interact and share perceptions?  
  *Basis*: Conclusion: "Looking ahead, extending these findings to multi-agent settings—where one agent’s corrupted perception propagates through interaction—are natural next steps."  
  *Why unresolved*: Single-agent thresholds may not account for social or cooperative dynamics; drift in one agent could mask or amplify signals in others.
- **Open Question 5**: What environmental properties determine the dynamics structure ∂PE/∂ε, which explains cross-environment failure in predicting ε*?  
  *Basis*: Section 4.5: cross-environment regression fails (R² = 0.45), pinpointing "environment-specific dynamics structure ∂PE/∂ε" as the missing variable.  
  *Why unresolved*: Without characterizing ∂PE/∂ε, ε* cannot be predicted a priori for new environments, limiting theoretical understanding and practical calibration.

## Limitations
- The study is confined to four MuJoCo locomotion environments with proprioceptive observations; generalizability to vision-based or non-locomotion tasks is unknown.
- Drift injection is synthetic and axis-aligned (affecting only velocity dimensions); naturalistic distribution shifts (e.g., correlated sensor degradation) may behave differently.
- Key implementation details (exact velocity observation indices, world model/PPO hyperparameters, detector baseline window specifics) are not fully specified, limiting exact replication and leaving ε* magnitude subject to these choices.

## Confidence
- **Sharp sigmoid detection threshold ε* exists**: **Medium**. Strong within-environment power-law fits (R²=0.89–0.97) and invariance across detectors/model capacities support existence, but poor cross-environment prediction (R²=0.45) and unspecified implementation details caution against over-generalization.
- **Sinusoidal drift is undetectable**: **Medium**. Robust evidence (spectral power ~ baseline, detector-agnostic failure) suggests a fundamental property of the PE signal under MLP training, but claim is narrow to the tested architectures/tasks; other model types might differ.
- **Collapse before awareness (CBA) in fragile environments**: **Medium**. Hopper data clearly show CBA, but only one environment exhibited extreme fragility; policy collapse timescales may vary with training regime or task.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain replication**: Repeat the full threshold sweep, sinusoidal test, and CBA measurement in additional continuous-control suites (e.g., Brax, PyBullet) to assess generalizability beyond MuJoCo.
2. **World model architecture ablation**: Train recurrent (LSTM) and transformer-based forward models alongside MLPs on the same data; re-evaluate sinusoidal drift detectability to determine whether blindness is architecture-dependent or intrinsic to the PE objective.
3. **Policy robustness intervention**: Train PPO policies with observation-domain randomization (e.g., additive Gaussian noise, random sensor dropouts) and re-measure time-to-collapse vs. time-to-detection; quantify how robustness shifts the CBA boundary.
