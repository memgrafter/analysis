# ALPS: A Diagnostic Challenge Set for Arabic Linguistic & Pragmatic Reasoning

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.17054
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17054
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Evaluation of 23 models on ALPS reveals a dissociation where models achieve near-human pragmatic inference but suffer high error rates (36.5%) on morpho-syntactic tasks reliant on diacritics.

## Executive Summary
ALPS is a new, expert-curated benchmark of 531 multiple-choice questions designed to diagnose deep linguistic and pragmatic reasoning in Arabic. It spans 15 subareas, covering Classical Arabic, Modern Standard Arabic, Quranic text, and poetry. Zero-shot evaluation of 23 models shows a striking pattern: top commercial models (e.g., Gemini-1.5-flash) excel at pragmatic inference like implicature, even matching human performance, yet they fail systematically on tasks requiring fine-grained morpho-syntactic understanding, particularly those dependent on diacritics. The best Arabic-native model (Jais-2-70B) approaches the human average but still trails the expert oracle, revealing a "fluency trap" where surface-level competence masks fragile syntactic knowledge.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 23 large language models (LLMs) on the ALPS suite using a zero-shot, closed-book multiple-choice QA format. Inputs are 531 Arabic questions across linguistic subareas (e.g., Semantic Role Labeling, Implicature), drawn from diverse Arabic genres. Models are prompted with a fixed Arabic system and user message template (details partially in Appendix D), with no in-context examples, chain-of-thought, or retrieval. Temperature is set to 0 for determinism (with exceptions noted). Predictions are extracted via regex matching digits 1-4 or text normalization for Arabic option outputs. Primary metrics are accuracy overall and per subarea, benchmarked against a single-pass human average (84.6%) and an expert oracle (99.2%).

## Key Results
*   **Dissociation:** Models show near-human performance on pragmatic tasks (e.g., top models 100% on Implicature) but struggle with morpho-syntactic dependencies, incurring a 36.5% error rate on diacritics-reliant tasks like Semantic Role Labeling.
*   **Best Model & Gap:** Gemini-1.5-flash achieves 94.2%, exceeding the average single-pass human (84.6%) but remaining below the expert oracle (99.2%).
*   **Fluency Trap:** The top Arabic-native model (Jais-2-70B, 83.6%) approaches human performance but exhibits a persistent gap, indicating that surface fluency does not guarantee robust syntactic understanding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not investigate an explicit theoretical mechanism for the observed performance patterns. It presents the empirical dissociation as a documented phenomenon, hypothesizing that pragmatic inference patterns are well-represented in training corpora, while Arabic's diacritic-based case-marking system is underrepresented due to the prevalence of undiacritized online text. The diagnostic power of ALPS itself is the primary tool for revealing this gap, not a tested causal model.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Arabic Morpho-Syntax:** Understanding case endings (iʿrāb) and grammatical roles encoded by diacritics is fundamental for accurate semantic role labeling and dependency parsing.
    *   *Why Needed:* Failure here leads to systematic errors in meaning assignment (e.g.,Subject vs. Object).
    *   *Quick Check:* Evaluate accuracy on Case Markers and Diacritics subareas; expect >26% error in top models.
*   **Pragmatic Inference:** Processing implicature, presupposition, and indirect speech acts requires real-world knowledge and contextual reasoning beyond literal meaning.
    *   *Why Needed:* Differentiates true language understanding from pattern matching on surface forms.
    *   *Quick Check:* Compare scores on Implicature vs. literal Semantic Interpretation tasks; high disparity suggests pragmatic brittleness.
*   **Diacritic Sensitivity:** Arabic script often omits case-marking vowels (ḥarakāt) in informal text, creating a distribution shift for models trained on such data.
    *   *Why Needed:* Diacritics are critical for resolving syntactic ambiguities; their absence in training leads to "diacritic blindness."
    *   *Quick Check:* Test model performance on minimal pairs with/without explicit diacritics in SRL tasks.
*   **Linguistic Diversity in Training:** Exposure to Classical Arabic, Quranic, and poetic structures provides necessary data for handling archaic or formulaic constructions.
    *   *Why Needed:* Modern web corpora may lack these registers, causing failures on formal/religious text.
    *   *Quick Check:* Analyze error rates specifically on Quranic and Poetry subareas versus Modern Standard Arabic.
*   **Evaluation Design:** A fine-grained diagnostic suite (vs. aggregate benchmarks) isolates specific competency failures.
    *   *Why Needed:* General benchmarks can mask weaknesses in narrow but critical linguistic phenomena.
    *   *Quick Check:* Correlate ALPS subarea scores with general Arabic benchmark (e.g., ORCA) scores; low correlation indicates complementary diagnostic value.
*   **Expert Curation:** Items are designed by linguists to target precise, often subtle, linguistic phenomena.
    *   *Why Needed:* Ensures questions test genuine knowledge rather than superficial correlations.
    *   *Quick Check:* Review item difficulty distribution; a healthy diagnostic should have a range, not all items trivial or impossibly hard.

## Architecture Onboarding
The evaluation architecture is a linear pipeline for zero-shot assessment:

Prompt Template (Arabic) -> Model Inference (API/Local) -> Answer Extraction (Regex/Text Match) -> Metric Calculation

**Critical Path:** The fidelity of the Arabic prompt template and answer extraction logic is paramount. A mismatch in wording or option formatting (e.g., Arabic vs. Latin numerals) directly causes answer parsing failures and invalid scores.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The zero-shot, no-reasoning design isolates intrinsic knowledge but sacrifices realism—real deployments often use chain-of-thought or retrieval. This tradeoff prioritizes diagnostic clarity over practical performance.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  **Diacritic Blindness:** Model ignores short vowels, consistently misassigning grammatical roles in Case Markers/SRL.
2.  **Pragmatic Literalism:** Model selects answers based on literal utterance meaning, failing on Implicature or Speech Act Theory items requiring inferred meaning.
3.  **Prompt Sensitivity:** Performance drops if prompt phrasing deviates from the template, indicating overfitting to surface cues rather than deep understanding.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Prompt Sanity Check:** Run the evaluation on a 10-item pilot set using the exact prompt template from Appendix D (once obtained) and the provided extraction rules. Verify that parsed answers match known gold labels for these items.
2.  **Diacritic Stress Test:** Isolate the 12 "diacritics-reliant" subareas. Run all models and confirm the reported >26% error rate pattern, especially comparing SRL accuracy on diacritic-present vs. diacritic-absent (if such items exist) contexts.
3.  **Arabic-Native Advantage Probe:** Compare the performance of Arabic-native models (Jais-2, ALLaM) against similarly-sized commercial models (e.g., find ~70B parameter equivalents) on the Implicature and Presupposition subareas. Measure the claimed ~5% edge and analyze if it holds when controlling for scale.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Correlation with General Benchmarks:** What is the correlation between performance on broad-coverage benchmarks like ORCA and deep linguistic diagnostic performance on ALPS? What explains discrepancies?
*   **Causal Mechanism for Dissociation:** Is the dissociation between pragmatic competence and morpho-syntactic competence due to statistical underrepresentation of diacritics in training data or fundamental architectural limitations in processing case-marking systems?
*   **Source of Arabic-Native Advantage:** To what extent do Arabic-native models' strength in pragmatic tasks stem from linguistic/cultural proximity in their training data versus model scale, architecture, or training objectives?
*   **Impact of Reasoning Modes:** How do model performance and error patterns change when employing extended reasoning modes (e.g., chain-of-thought), and does this mitigate systematic morpho-syntactic failures?

## Limitations
*   **Dataset Access:** The core 531-item ALPS dataset is not publicly released at the time of writing, blocking independent verification of items, splits, and gold answers.
*   **Prompt Specification:** The exact Arabic system prompt and formatting details (option shuffling, punctuation) are incompletely documented, riskingimplementation drift during replication.
*   **Human Baseline Opacity:** Details on the "single-pass human" and "expert oracle" annotators (number, expertise level, agreement) are lacking, limiting interpretation of the human-model gap.

## Confidence
*   **Medium:** Claims about the dissociation pattern (strong pragmatic vs. weak morpho-syntactic performance) are plausible but unverified without dataset access.
*   **Medium:** Specific error rates (e.g., 36.5% on diacritics-reliant tasks) depend on unreleased subarea definitions and test items.
*   **Medium:** The "fluency trap" narrative is compelling but cannot be evaluated without examining the dataset and model error distributions directly.
