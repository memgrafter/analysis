# Optimsyn: Influence-Guided Rubrics Optimization for Synthetic Data Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00536
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00536
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Influence-guided rubrics generation yields consistent SOTA improvements in knowledge-intensive SFT, e.g., +27.2% relative gain on HLE, and generalizes across target models and data generators.

## Executive Summary
Handcrafted rubrics for synthetic supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data are often brittle, expert-dependent, and lack measurable downstream impact. This paper introduces Optimsyn, a framework that optimizes rubrics directly for training utility by using an optimizer-aware influence estimator to score synthetic samples based on their contribution to a target model’s validation loss. A rubric generator is trained via reinforcement learning (GRPO) with influence as reward, producing rubrics that yield higher-quality synthetic data. Experiments across humanities/social sciences and medical/health domains show consistent outperformance of SOTA SFT baselines, strong generalization across multiple target models and generators, and a moderate correlation (R²≈0.55) between influence scores and downstream accuracy. Optimsyn demonstrates a data-centric pathway to improve model performance without additional human expertise.

## Method Summary
Optimsyn employs a two-stage process. First, an influence estimator computes the expected reduction in validation loss for each synthetic sample, leveraging gradient information from the target model and respecting the optimization dynamics of the training algorithm (e.g., Adam). This estimator is optimizer-aware, enabling accurate scoring of samples within the actual training pipeline. Second, a rubric generator (initialized from a large language model) is fine-tuned using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm that treats the influence score as a reward. The generator learns to output rubrics that produce synthetic samples with high influence, thereby directly optimizing the data generation process for downstream model performance. The framework is model-agnostic and can be applied to various target architectures and synthetic data generators.

## Key Results
- Optimsyn surpasses state-of-the-art SFT baselines in knowledge-intensive domains (humanities/social sciences, medical/health), with statistically significant improvements.
- Performance generalizes across target models (Qwen-4B/8B/14B, Llama-8B) and synthetic data generators (Qwen2-35B, GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro), matching or exceeding instruct model performance.
- Influence scores exhibit a moderate positive correlation with downstream accuracy (R²≈0.55), validating that the optimized rubrics capture model impact better than heuristic handcrafting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The influence score provides a direct, differentiable proxy for a sample’s training utility, aligning rubric generation with the target model’s learning objective. By incorporating optimizer-awareness (e.g., gradient steps, momentum), the estimator respects the actual optimization trajectory, avoiding optimistic bias that arises from purely static loss reduction metrics. GRPO effectively translates this continuous reward into sample-level policy updates, encouraging the generator to produce rubrics that yield high-influence samples. This closed-loop optimization bypasses the need for manual rubric design, enabling automated discovery of effective data characteristics for the specific target model and task.

## Foundational Learning
- **Influence Estimation**: Approximates a sample’s effect on model parameters or validation loss; needed to provide a training utility signal. *Why needed*: Replaces heuristic sample quality judgments with model-specific impact. *Quick check*: Does the influence estimator accurately approximate leave-one-out retraining for a subset of samples?
- **Optimizer-aware Metrics**: Incorporates details of the optimizer (e.g., Adam steps) into influence computation. *Why needed*: Ensures estimated impact matches actual training dynamics. *Quick check*: Do optimizer-aware estimates correlate more strongly with true validation loss changes than naive gradient norms?
- **Reinforcement Learning for Data Generation**: Uses RL to optimize a generator’s output based on a reward signal. *Why needed*: Enables training with non-differentiable or black-box reward (influence). *Quick check*: Does GRPO stabilize training compared to vanilla policy gradient methods?
- **Rubric-guided Synthetic Data Creation**: Human-defined or model-generated rubrics structure synthetic instance generation. *Why needed*: Provides fine-grained control over data attributes and complexity. *Quick check*: Do learned rubrics differ substantially from handcrafted ones in measurable ways?
- **Domain Generalization in SFT**: Adapting synthetic data to multiple target domains and models. *Why needed*: Ensures broad applicability. *Quick check*: Does performance hold when the target model architecture or size is changed significantly?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Rubric Generator -> Synthetic Data Sampler -> Influence Estimator -> Reward -> GRPO -> Updated Rubric Generator  
Critical path: The workflow starts with a rubric generator producing a rubric; the rubric guides a synthetic data sampler to create instances; each instance is scored by the influence estimator (which queries the target model’s gradients and validation set); the score becomes reward for GRPO; GRPO updates the rubric generator’s policy; repeat.  
Design tradeoffs: Influence estimation is computationally expensive (requires target model gradients per sample) but provides precise utility; GRPO introduces RL training overhead but handles non-differentiable reward; using a pretrained generator balances creativity and stability.  
Failure signatures: (1) Influence scores fail to correlate with actual downstream performance (low R²), indicating estimator bias; (2) Generator collapses to repetitive rubrics due to reward hacking; (3) High variance in influence estimates across random seeds.  
First experiments:
1. Validate influence estimator by comparing against ground-truth leave-one-out retraining on a small subset.
2. Ablate optimizer-awareness by replacing with simple gradient norm and measure impact on final accuracy.
3. Test transferability by applying a rubric trained on Qwen-4B to Llama-8B and measuring performance drop.

## Limitations
- Influence scores show only moderate correlation (R²≈0.55) with downstream accuracy, indicating imperfect proxy; error analysis of mismatches is absent.
- Generalization evaluated on a limited set of target models (4B–14B) and generators; performance on smaller or non-transformer architectures is unknown.
- Domain coverage restricted to humanities/social sciences and medical/health; applicability to coding, STEM, or multimodal tasks remains untested.

## Confidence
- **High**: Consistent SOTA beats across tested domains (multiple benchmarks, statistical reporting implied).
- **Medium**: Generalization across target models/generators (evidence from 3–4 models/generators per domain, but not exhaustive).
- **Low**: Claim that optimized rubrics replace expert heuristic design (no human expert rubric comparison or qualitative analysis provided).

## Next Checks
1. **Domain Stress Test**: Replicate on out-of-distribution domains (e.g., legal, STEM, multilingual) to probe rubric transferability boundaries.
2. **Efficiency Audit**: Measure wall-clock time and memory overhead of influence-guided generation versus handcrafted/heuristic baselines at scale.
3. **Failure Mode Analysis**: Manually inspect samples with high influence but low downstream accuracy (and vice versa) to identify systematic biases in the estimator.
