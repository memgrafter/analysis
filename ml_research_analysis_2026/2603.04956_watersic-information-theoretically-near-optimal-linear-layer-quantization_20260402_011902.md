# WaterSIC: information-theoretically (near) optimal linear layer quantization
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.04956
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04956
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: WaterSIC achieves lower perplexity and higher zero-shot accuracy than competing methods for 1–4 bit quantization on Llama and Qwen models.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses suboptimal post-training quantization (PTQ) of linear layers in large language models, proving that the widely used GPTQ algorithm can incur an unbounded gap from the information-theoretic rate-distortion limit. To close this gap, the authors propose WaterSIC, which allocates quantization bits per input feature (channel) based on the activation covariance, mirroring the classical waterfilling solution. WaterSIC integrates successive interference cancellation with per-channel spacing and several enhancements—LMMSE correction, activation drift compensation, residual stream correction, and attention-weighted calibration. For i.i.d. Gaussian weights, it provably achieves at most a 0.255-bit gap to the optimum. Empirically, WaterSIC establishes a new state-of-the-art Pareto frontier on Llama-3.2-1B, Llama-3-8B, Llama-2-7B, and Qwen3-8B, yielding lower WikiText-2 perplexity and higher zero-shot accuracy than methods like AWQ, GPTQ, NestQuant, and QTIP across 1–4 bits.

## Method Summary
WaterSIC treats each input feature (column) of a weight matrix as an independent channel with its own quantization step size. It first estimates the activation covariance from calibration data to determine a per-channel rate allocation via the waterfilling principle: channels with higher activation variance receive finer quantization (smaller step size), while those with lower variance are coarsely quantized. Quantization then proceeds via successive interference cancellation (SIC) with per-channel spacing to isolate inter-channel interference. Post-quantization, an LMMSE correction is applied to reduce residual error linearly. Additional modules—activation drift compensation, residual stream correction, and attention-weighted calibration—further mitigate distribution shifts and error accumulation in deep networks. This systematic approach targets the information-theoretic rate-distortion limit.

## Key Results
- Theoretical guarantee: For i.i.d. Gaussian weights, WaterSIC achieves at most a 0.255-bit gap to the information-theoretic optimum uniformly over all activation covariances.
- Empirical SOTA: On Llama-3.2-1B, Llama-3-8B, Llama-2-7B, and Qwen3-8B, WaterSIC yields lower WikiText-2 perplexity and higher zero-shot accuracy than GPTQ, AWQ, NestQuant, and QTIP for all quantization rates between 1 and 4 bits.
- Pareto dominance: WaterSIC establishes a new frontier, consistently improving accuracy-efficiency trade-offs across the evaluated bit-widths.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
WaterSIC’s per-channel rate allocation follows the waterfilling principle from communication theory, which is optimal for parallel Gaussian channels. By allocating more bits to features with higher activation variance (i.e., more "energy"), it minimizes overall distortion. Successive interference cancellation (SIC) with per-channel spacing then quantizes weights while explicitly accounting for cross-channel interference, which standard per-channel or per-tensor methods ignore. The correction modules—LMMSE, drift compensation, etc.—address real-world deviations from the ideal i.i.d. Gaussian setting, such as activation distribution shifts and error propagation through layers. This combination allows WaterSIC to closely approach the theoretical limit even in practical non-Gaussian scenarios.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Activation Covariance Estimation -> Per-Channel Rate Allocation (Waterfilling) -> SIC Quantization with Spacing -> LMMSE Correction -> Activation Drift Compensation -> Residual Stream Correction -> Attention-Weighted Calibration.

Critical path: Calibration data gathers activations → covariance matrix computed → waterfilling yields per-channel step sizes → SIC quantizes weights with those step sizes, cancelling interference → LMMSE and drift modules iteratively refine → residual and attention modules finalize.

Design tradeoffs: High accuracy requires all correction modules, increasing calibration time and memory. The waterfilling assumption of stationary activation statistics may break for long sequences or diverse inputs. Per-channel granularity improves flexibility but adds overhead versus per-tensor methods.

Failure signatures: Sudden perplexity increase when calibration data is insufficient or unrepresentative, leading to poor covariance estimates. Sharp accuracy drop on models with highly structured (non-Gaussian) weights if LMMSE and drift compensation are omitted. Calibration wall-time significantly higher than GPTQ/AWQ due to iterative corrections.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions. Notable uncertainties from the discussion include the impact of non-Gaussian weight structures on the theoretical bound and the generalizability beyond the evaluated transformer families.

## Limitations
- Theoretical bound strictly assumes i.i.d. Gaussian weights; real LLM weights are structured and non-Gaussian, so the actual gap may be larger.
- Empirical evaluation is restricted to Llama and Qwen transformers; performance on other architectures (e.g., Mixtral, CNNs) or extreme quantization (<1 bit) is unknown.
- Calibration overhead (attention-weighted, residual stream correction) is not quantified; end-to-end latency and memory trade-offs versus baselines are unmeasured.

## Confidence
- Theoretical optimality gap (≤0.255 bits): High under Gaussian assumption, Low for real LLMs.
- Empirical SOTA Pareto frontier: Medium—consistent gains on reported benchmarks but limited model diversity.
- Practical efficiency (latency, memory footprint): Low—no end-to-end throughput or bandwidth analysis provided.
