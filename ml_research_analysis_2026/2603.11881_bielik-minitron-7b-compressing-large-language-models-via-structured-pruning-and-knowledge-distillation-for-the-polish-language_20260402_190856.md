# Bielik-Minitron-7B: Compressing Large Language Models via Structured Pruning and Knowledge Distillation for the Polish Language

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.11881
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11881
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Structured pruning and knowledge distillation compress Bielik-11B (11.04B) to Bielik-Minitron-7B (7.35B) with 90.1% performance recovery and 49.6% throughput improvement.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the high computational cost of deploying large language models for low-resource languages like Polish. The authors propose a compression pipeline starting from Bielik-11B-v3.0: first, structured pruning removes entire layers and feed-forward neurons based on activation magnitudes; then, logit-based knowledge distillation recovers performance using a frozen teacher; finally, a multi-stage alignment process (SFT, DPO-P, GRPO) produces an instruct model. The resulting 7.35B-parameter model retains 90.1% of the original's capabilities on Polish and multilingual benchmarks while achieving a 49.6% throughput gain and fitting on consumer-grade GPUs (14GB FP16).

## Method Summary
The compression workflow uses NVIDIA NeMo for structured pruning. Importance scores are computed via ℓ²-norms of activations on a small calibration set, ranking layers (by Block Influence) and feed-forward neurons. The top-40 layers and top-11,264 neurons per FFN are retained, initializing the student with surviving teacher weights. Post-pruning, logit-based knowledge distillation applies a forward KL divergence loss between teacher and student logits (with temperature scaling) on unlabeled text, training the student to mimic the teacher's full output distribution. The distilled base model is then aligned through sequential stages: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on high-quality instruction pairs, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO-P) on human preferences, and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with verifiable rewards for reasoning. The final model is further quantized (GGUF, FP8, NVFP4) for efficient deployment.

## Key Results
- Parameter reduction: 33.4% (11.04B → 7.35B)
- Performance recovery: 90.1% of teacher on Polish/multilingual benchmarks
- Throughput improvement: 49.6% (81.41 vs 54.42 tokens/s on RTX PRO 6000)
- Memory footprint: Fits within 14GB FP16, enabling consumer GPU deployment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Activation-Based Structured Pruning**: Removes entire layers and feed-forward neurons with lowest activation magnitudes on a calibration set, creating a smaller student skeleton. Surviving teacher weights initialize the student, assuming low-activation components are redundant. The paper uses NVIDIA Model Optimizer and ℓ²-norm statistics (Section 3.1). However, aggressive width-only pruning (e.g., EXP_009) caused training instability, indicating sensitivity to pruning ratios. Exact calibration data details are undisclosed.
2. **Logit-Based Knowledge Distillation**: After pruning, a KL divergence loss between teacher and student logits (with temperature T) transfers the teacher's full output distribution ("dark knowledge"), recovering nuanced behaviors. The loss formulation is specified (Eq. 11, Section 3.2) and noted effective for moderate depth reduction. Hyperparameters like T and total steps are not provided; extreme pruning may require additional recovery techniques.
3. **Multi-Stage Alignment Pipeline**: Sequential SFT, DPO-P, and GRPO refine the distilled base model into an instruct variant. SFT establishes conversational format; DPO-P optimizes preferences; GRPO uses verifiable rewards to boost reasoning. The paper details each stage's data scale (Section 3.4) and reports SFT yields the largest gain, with DPO-P and GRPO providing incremental improvements. However, the alignment datasets are proprietary, limiting reproducibility.

## Foundational Learning
- **Structured vs. Unstructured Pruning**: Structured pruning removes entire units (layers, neurons) for real hardware speedups, unlike unstructured sparsity which needs specialized libraries. Quick check: Why does removing entire attention heads speed up inference on a standard GPU, but removing individual weights might not?
- **Knowledge Distillation (Logit-based)**: Distillation transfers the teacher's output distribution, not just top predictions, to recover performance post-pruning. Quick check: What is the purpose of using a high temperature T in the KL loss during distillation?
- **Alignment Post-Training (SFT → DPO → RL)**: Alignment stages instill instruction adherence and safety beyond the base model's knowledge. Quick check: How does DPO differ from SFT, and why might both be needed?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Bielik-11B-v3.0 teacher -> calibration data -> activation statistics -> pruning via NVIDIA NeMo -> pruned student initialization -> distillation with unlabeled data -> aligned model (SFT -> DPO-P -> GRPO) -> final instruct model -> quantization (GGUF, FP8, NVFP4)
- **Critical path**: 
  1. Pruning: Sensitivity analysis → select EXP_010 (40 layers, 11264 FFN dim) → execute structured pruning → initialize student.
  2. Distillation: Train student vs frozen teacher with KL loss until convergence (~8k steps, KL ~0.89).
  3. Alignment: SFT (3 epochs, 20M samples) → DPO-P (3 epochs, 114k) → GRPO (143k samples).
  4. Evaluation & Quantization: Benchmark → apply post-training quantization.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Depth vs. Width: Depth pruning gives linear speedup but harms reasoning more than width pruning. EXP_010 balanced 20% depth reduction with 21.4% width reduction.
  - Stability vs. Compression: Aggressive pruning (EXP_009) caused instability; conservative (EXP_006) missed targets. EXP_010 was found via empirical search.
  - Distillation Temperature: Higher T softens distribution but risks noisy gradients; exact T undisclosed.
  - Data Efficiency: Distillation used <3% of pre-training data; alignment used curated smaller sets.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Pruning: High Block Influence for prunable layers; instability (spiking loss) during distillation.
  - Distillation: KL loss fails to converge (<0.89) or plateaus high; poor complex benchmark performance.
  - Alignment: Degradation on Polish benchmarks after SFT; GRPO divergence with mis-specified rewards.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Pruning Configuration Sweep: Evaluate EXP_001–EXP_010, measure post-pruning perplexity, distillation stability, and throughput gain. Select stable candidates with >30% reduction.
  2. Distillation Recovery Validation: For top candidates, run full distillation, track KL loss and zero-shot Polish subset performance. Choose config achieving >85% teacher baseline (EXP_010).
  3. Quantization Impact Test: Apply weight-only (GGUF) and weight-activation (FP8, NVFP4) quantization to final model. Evaluate on Open PL LLM leaderboard; confirm Q4_K_M loses <1% and fits in <8GB VRAM.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How does pruning attention heads affect the performance-efficiency trade-off for Bielik-Minitron-7B? Basis: Paper explicitly states attention head pruning was not explored and is future work. Why unresolved: Impact on Polish tasks and speed is unknown. Evidence needed: Apply activation-based importance to heads, prune systematically, evaluate on Polish/multilingual benchmarks.
- **Open Question 2**: Can quantization-aware training or distillation recover the performance drop from FP8/NVFP4 quantization? Basis: Paper leaves this for future work. Why unresolved: FP8/NVFP4 degrade by 1.49% and 3.71% on Open PL benchmark; recovery would boost efficiency. Evidence needed: Fine-tune quantized models with QAT or distillation, re-evaluate benchmarks.
- **Open Question 3**: Why does the model's function-calling accuracy drop significantly on live, user-contributed prompts compared to curated benchmarks? Basis: Inferred from BFCL results gap (92% non-live vs 31.25% live). Why unresolved: Suggests brittleness to real-world noise; cause uninvestigated. Evidence needed: Error analysis on live prompts, controlled experiments on prompt length/ambiguity/domain mismatch.
- **Open Question 4**: How well does the structured pruning and distillation pipeline generalize to other low-resource languages beyond Polish? Basis: Paper positions it as a blueprint for European languages but only evaluates on Polish and limited multilingual set. Why unresolved: Different languages have varying complexity; optimal config may differ. Evidence needed: Replicate pipeline on other low-resource languages (e.g., Czech, Hungarian) with comparable data budgets.

## Limitations
- Exact calibration data size, domain, and preprocessing are unspecified.
- Distillation hyperparameters (temperature T, batch size, steps) are undisclosed.
- Alignment datasets (SFT/DPO/GRPO) are proprietary; public replication likely uses lower-quality data.
- Throughput/memory claims depend on H200 GPUs; consumer-grade validation requires independent testing with offloading.
- Performance recovery (90.1%) is anchored to a private benchmark suite; public benchmark results may differ.

## Confidence

- **Distillation Recovery**: Medium (standard logit-KL loss, but hyperparameters T, LR, steps missing)
- **Alignment Pipeline Efficacy**: Medium-High (sequential SFT→DPO→GRPO well-documented, but relies on inaccessible high-quality Polish data)
- **Throughput/Memory Claims**: Medium (quantitative gains stated but hardware/software dependencies unspecified for consumer replication)
- **Evidence for Exact Reproducibility**: Low (neighbor papers confirm methodological context but not this paper's specific implementation details)
