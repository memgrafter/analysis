# Pragmatic Curiosity: A Hybrid Learning-Optimization Paradigm via Active Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06104  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06104  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Introduces pragmatic curiosity—a unified active inference approach using expected free energy minimization—to simultaneously seek information and achieve goals, outperforming Bayesian optimization and Bayesian experimental design in three hybrid learning-optimization tasks.

## Executive Summary
Hybrid learning-optimization problems arise when the objective function is unknown and must be learned while simultaneously optimizing it. Traditional methods either separate learning from optimization (Bayesian optimization) or treat learning as the sole goal (Bayesian experimental design), limiting performance in coupled settings. This paper proposes a single principled framework: actions are chosen to minimize expected free energy (EFE), an information-theoretic objective that balances epistemic information gain (reducing uncertainty about latent states) and pragmatic utility (alignment with preferences). The EFE is derived from active inference and can be flexibly instantiated via a Boltzmann operator that converts any task-specific energy function into a preference distribution. The resulting acquisition function is applied to three real-world hybrid tasks: constrained system identification (locating a plume source while avoiding sensor saturation), targeted active search (discovering failure regions in autonomous driving), and composite Bayesian optimization with unknown preferences (optimizing multi-objective designs under implicit preferences). Across all tasks, pragmatic curiosity achieves superior sample efficiency—up to 40% fewer queries, 10% more failure coverage, and successful preference learning where alternatives fail—demonstrating the power of a unified curiosity-driven paradigm.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a probabilistic model of an unknown latent state \(s\) (e.g., system parameters, black-box functions, preference weights) and an observation model \(p(y|x,s)\) mapping design \(x\) and state to observations \(y\). A task-specific energy function \(h(y|D_t)\) expresses the hybrid objective (e.g., constraint violation, coverage deficit, preference misalignment). This energy is transformed into a preference distribution \(p(y) \propto \exp(-h(y|D_t)/\beta)\) via a Boltzmann operator with temperature \(\beta\). The acquisition function for active selection is
\[
\alpha(x) = \beta\, I(s;(x,y)|D_t) - \mathbb{E}_{p(y|x,D_t)}[h(y|D_t)],
\]
where the first term is mutual information (exploration) and the second is expected pragmatic cost (exploitation). Surrogate models approximate the necessary conditionals: discrete posteriors for simple latent spaces, Gaussian processes for continuous black-box functions. The mutual information for a GP has a closed form; otherwise it is estimated by Monte Carlo. At each iteration, \(\alpha(x)\) is maximized over the design space, the chosen \(x\) is queried, and the surrogate is updated with the new \((x,y)\). The curiosity coefficient \(\beta\) governs the exploration-exploitation trade-off; theoretical work (Li et al., 2026) shows that a "sufficient curiosity" condition on \(\beta_t\) ensures posterior consistency and no-regret learning. The three tasks instantiate the framework differently:
1. Constrained system ID: \(s\) = source parameters, \(h(y)=\mathbf{1}(y>y_{\max})\).
2. Targeted active search: \(s\) = mapping from scenario parameters to failure features, \(h(y)= \text{Vol}(S) - \text{Vol}(C_\delta(Y\cup y)\cap S)\) where \(C_\delta\) defines a failure region in outcome space.
3. Composite BO: \(s=(f,g)\) with unknown preference function \(g\); \(h(y)= \text{EFE}(g(y))\) that couples preference learning and outcome quality, while \(\alpha\) also includes a separate term \(\gamma I(f_x;y)\) for learning the objective.

## Key Results
- **Constrained system identification**: Pragmatic curiosity attains near-perfect estimation of the plume source with up to 40% fewer queries than Bayesian optimization, while maintaining almost zero constraint violations.
- **Targeted active search (CARLA)**: It discovers 10% more of the critical failure region compared to Bayesian experimental design baselines.
- **Composite Bayesian optimization**: The method successfully learns the unknown preference function and converges to high-preference outcomes; competing methods either fail to learn preferences or get stuck in suboptimal regions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Expected Free Energy Balance
- **Claim**: Expected Free Energy (EFE) minimization simultaneously addresses learning and goal-seeking by combining epistemic information gain (mutual information) and pragmatic preference alignment.
- **Mechanism**: The EFE is an upper bound on surprisal. Its two terms—mutual information \(I(s;(x,y)|D_t)\) and expected negative log-preferred outcome \(-\mathbb{E}_{p(y|x)}[\log p(y)]\)—explicitly represent the drive to reduce uncertainty about latent states and to realize desirable outcomes. Action selection minimizes EFE, thus improving the model while pursuing goals.
- **Core assumption**: The surrogate \(q(\cdot)=p(\cdot|D_t)\) accurately approximates the true data-generating process, and the preference distribution \(p(y)\) correctly encodes desired outcomes.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states the coupling; Theorem 3.1 rewrites EFE as \(G = -I(s;(x,y)|D_t) - \mathbb{E}_{p(y|x)}[\log p(y)]\).
- **Break condition**: Severe model mismatch (non-stationarity, mis-specified \(p(y)\)) makes the EFE bound uninformative, leading to suboptimal actions.

### Mechanism 2: Boltzmann Operator as Universal Converter
- **Claim**: The Boltzmann operator \(B_\beta[h]\) flexibly maps any problem-specific energy function \(h(y|D_t)\) into a preference distribution \(p(y)\), enabling the design of acquisition functions for hybrid problems.
- **Mechanism**: The transform \(p(y) \propto \exp(-h(y|D_t)/\beta)\) converts minimization of \(h\) into maximization of \(p(y)\). Substituting into the EFE yields \(\alpha(x) = \beta I(s;(x,y)|D_t) - \mathbb{E}_{p(y|x)}[h(y|D_t)]\), where \(\beta\) adjusts the exploration-exploitation balance.
- **Core assumption**: The energy function \(h(y|D_t)\) correctly captures the hybrid objective and is computable via the surrogate model.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 4 introduces the Boltzmann operator; Section 5.1 shows concrete \(h\) for each task (e.g., \(h(y)=\mathbf{1}(y>y_{\max})\) for plume).
- **Break condition**: Misspecification of \(h\) (wrong constraint or inaccurate metric) misguides acquisition, reducing performance.

### Mechanism 3: Sufficient Curiosity Ensures Theoretical Guarantees
- **Claim**: With a properly tuned curiosity coefficient \(\beta_t\) (satisfying a "sufficient curiosity" condition), pragmatic curiosity enjoys posterior consistency and bounded cumulative regret.
- **Mechanism**: Li et al. (2026) prove that if \(\beta_t\) is large enough initially, the epistemic term dominates early queries, forcing sufficient information gathering that drives the posterior toward the truth and bounds regret. The condition links \(\beta_t\) to the information-theoretic properties of the model.
- **Core assumption**: Latent state \(s\) is identifiable from the model; data are i.i.d. conditioned on \(x\); surrogate class is well-specified.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 1 references the theoretical guarantee; Section 4 cites Li et al. (2026) for the condition and practical design guidelines.
- **Break condition**: If \(\beta_t\) decays too quickly (insufficient exploration) or stays too large (wastes queries), the sufficient curiosity condition is violated and theoretical guarantees are lost.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bayesian posterior updating**: Surrogate models update beliefs \(q(s)\) via Bayes rule \(q(s|D_t) \propto q(s) \prod_i p(y_i|x_i,s)\). This posterior enters the EFE terms for both exploration and exploitation.
  - *Quick check*: Given prior \(p(s)=\mathcal{N}(0,1)\), likelihood \(p(y|x,s)=\mathcal{N}(sx,1)\), and data \((x_1,y_1)=(1,2)\), what is the posterior distribution \(p(s|D)\)?
- **Mutual information**: The epistemic term \(I(s;(x,y)|D_t) = H[q(s)] - \mathbb{E}_{p(y|x)}[H[q(s|x,y)]]\) quantifies expected reduction in entropy about \(s\). It drives exploration toward designs that most reduce uncertainty.
  - *Quick check*: If \(q(s)\) is Gaussian \(\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)\) and \(q(s|x,y)\) is \(\mathcal{N}(\mu',\sigma'^2)\) with \(\sigma'^2 < \sigma^2\), does \(I\) increase or decrease?
- **Gaussian process (GP) regression**: GPs provide a flexible non-parametric prior over functions; predictive mean \(\mu_t(x)\) and variance \(\sigma_t^2(x)\) enable computation of mutual information \(I(f_x;y)=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+\sigma^{-2}\sigma_t^2(x))\) (assuming Gaussian noise). This analytic form is crucial for efficient acquisition in continuous BO tasks.
  - *Quick check*: For a GP with kernel \(k(x,x')=\exp(-\|x-x'\|^2)\), what is the predictive variance at a point far from all training data?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
1. **Task Definer**: Specifies latent state \(s\), observation model \(p(y|x,s)\), energy function \(h(y|D_t)\), and design space bounds.
2. **Surrogate Manager**: Maintains the probabilistic model \(q(s)\) (e.g., discrete posterior, GP) and updates it via Bayesian learning after each query.
3. **EFE Calculator**: Computes the acquisition \(\alpha(x) = \beta I(s;(x,y)|D_t) - \mathbb{E}_{p(y|x)}[h(y|D_t)]\) for candidate designs using Monte Carlo sampling from the surrogate.
4. **Acquisition Optimizer**: Maximizes \(\alpha(x)\) over the design space (gradient-based for continuous domains, grid search for discrete).
5. **Curiosity Tuner**: Sets or adapts the curiosity coefficient \(\beta_t\) based on theoretical guidelines or heuristics.

Data flow: Task Definer → Surrogate Manager (initial prior) → after each iteration: Surrogate Manager updates posterior → EFE Calculator evaluates candidates → Acquisition Optimizer selects \(x^*\) → environment returns \(y^*\) → Surrogate Manager updates again; Curiosity Tuner influences the \(\beta\) used in EFE.

**Critical path:** Initialize dataset \(D_0\) → Loop: Surrogate update → EFE computation for candidate set → Optimize \(\alpha(x)\) → Query \((x^*,y^*)\) → Update \(D\) → Check budget → repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **β (curiosity coefficient)**: High β yields pure exploration (BED-like); low β yields pure exploitation (BO-like). Appropriate β is task-dependent and requires careful tuning to satisfy the sufficient curiosity condition without wasting queries.
- **Surrogate choice**: GPs enable analytic mutual information and smooth uncertainty quantification but assume stationarity; more flexible models (e.g., Bayesian neural nets) can handle complex dynamics but require costly sampling for MI estimation.
- **Energy function \(h\)**: Must be expressible as an expectation under \(p(y|x,D_t)\). Complex or nested energy definitions (e.g., EFE within EFE for preferences) increase computational overhead and may require careful variance reduction.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Constraint violations (plume task)**: A rising cumulative count of violations indicates β is too low or the energy function misrepresents the constraint.
- **Poor coverage (active search)**: Low volume of discovered failure region despite many queries suggests the epistemic term is too weak or the coverage metric \(h\) is inaccurate.
- **Preference collapse (composite BO)**: No improvement in the true preference \(g(y)\) over iterations points to failed preference learning (e.g., β too low, probit model mismatch, or insufficient pairwise comparisons).

**First experiments:**
1. **Constrained system identification (plume source)**: Validate that \(h(y)=\mathbf{1}(y>y_{\max})\) steers queries away from sensor saturation while β balances source parameter learning. Check constraint violation count and estimation error.
2. **Targeted active search (CARLA failures)**: Validate that \(h(y)=\text{Vol}(S)-\text{Vol}(C_\delta(Y\cup y)\cap S)\) increases coverage of the failure region. Compare parameter-space coverage \(\text{Vol}(C_\delta(X))\) and metric-space coverage \(\text{Vol}(C_\delta(Y))\).
3. **Composite BO with unknown preferences (vehicle safety, penicillin, energy)**: Validate that nested \(h(y)=\text{EFE}(g(y))\) learns the preference function \(g\) while optimizing. Track best true \(g(y)\) over iterations; ablate terms in \(\alpha\) (Eq. 4) to assess contributions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multi-agent, long-horizon, multi-fidelity extension**: How can pragmatic curiosity be adapted to settings with delayed rewards, multiple interacting agents, or varying evaluation costs? (Conclusion mentions this as future work.)
2. **Robustness to energy mis‑specification**: What happens if the energy function \(h(y|D_t)\) is incorrect? The paper acknowledges the limitation but provides no method to detect or correct mis‑specification.
3. **Performance under model mismatch or non‑stationarity**: Current surrogate assumptions (stationary GP, i.i.d. noise) may not hold in practice; how does the method behave when these assumptions are violated?
4. **Automatic adaptation of β**: Fixed β is used in all experiments; can β be tuned online to improve sample efficiency across different problem phases?

## Limitations
- The theoretical guarantees of posterior consistency and no‑regret learning depend on the unproven results from the preprint Li et al. (2026), which currently has zero citations.
- The correctness of the task‑specific energy function \(h(y|D_t)\) is assumed; mis‑specification can bias the acquisition and degrade performance, yet no systematic sensitivity analysis is provided.
- Critical implementation details are omitted: exact CARLA simulator integration, GP hyperparameter settings, truncation bounds for predictive distributions, and the schedule for β; these gaps hinder faithful reproduction.
