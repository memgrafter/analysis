# Physical Commonsense Reasoning for Lower-Resourced Languages and Dialects: a Study on Basque
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.14812
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14812
- **Reference Count:** 0
- **Primary Result:** Basque-pretrained Latxa models demonstrate robustness to dialectal variation but limited verifiability (max 30.54%) in physical commonsense reasoning tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a gap in evaluating large language models (LLMs) on non-question-answering physical commonsense reasoning for low-resource languages and dialects, focusing on Basque. The authors created **BasPhyCo**, a dataset manually translated from the Italian GITA corpus into Standard Basque and automatically adapted to the Western Basque dialect. Eight multilingual and language-specific LLMs were evaluated on a hierarchical suite of tasks: story classification (accuracy), conflict detection (consistency), and physical state classification (verifiability). Results show that while Basque-specific Latxa models performed best and were most robust to dialectal shifts, all models exhibited very low verifiability—the most demanding task—especially for states like *Location*, *Edible*, and *Conscious*. The study highlights that high accuracy on plausibility classification masks severe deficiencies in fine-grained causal reasoning and that dialectal variation poses a significant challenge for general-purpose multilingual models.

## Method Summary
The core method involves a new dataset, **BasPhyCo**, derived from the Italian GITA physical commonsense benchmark. The process began with a manual, culturally-aware translation of 356 story pairs into Standard Basque. To generate the Western Basque dialect variant (**BasPhyCo west**), the authors used few-shot prompting with the Latxa model for automatic adaptation, followed by manual validation and error correction by linguists. Evaluation was conducted using the EleutherAI LM Evaluation Harness v0.4.9 in a few-shot setting. Models were assessed on three cascading tasks: classifying a story as plausible or implausible (accuracy), identifying the conflicting sentence in implausible stories (consistency), and labeling the specific physical state change causing the conflict (verifiability). The model suite included multilingual LLMs (Llama, Gemma) and language-specific models (Latxa for Basque, Minerva/LlaMAntino for Italian).

## Key Results
-   **Latxa models show strongest dialect robustness:** Basque-pretrained Latxa-70B achieved the highest verifiability (30.54% Standard, 28.03% Western) and the smallest performance drop from Standard to Western Basque (2.51 points), significantly outperforming multilingual models like Llama-3.1-70B-IT (9.21-point drop).
-   **Verifiability is universally low:** All models' verifiability scores were dramatically lower than their accuracy scores, confirming that fine-grained, verifiable physical reasoning is a major weakness. Maximum verifiability on Basque was only 30.54%.
-   **Specific states and implausibility types are hardest:** Models struggled most with *Location*, *Edible*, and *Conscious* physical states. Furthermore, "order" based implausibility (sentences swapped) was more difficult than "cloze" based (single-word substitution) across all evaluation tiers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Language-Specific Pretraining Enables Dialect Generalization
- **Claim:** Pretraining on the target low-resource language (Basque) enhances model robustness to dialectal variation in physical commonsense reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Exposure to a broad spectrum of lexical and morphosyntactic variability during pretraining builds internal representations that generalize across dialects better than models trained on standardized/high-resource data.
- **Core Assumption:** Western Basque's lexical and grammatical differences constitute a significant distribution shift that general-purpose multilingual models are ill-equipped to handle.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Latxa models had the smallest Standard-to-Western performance drop; neighboring work on Basque dialectal NLI confirms dialectal variation is a known NLP challenge.
- **Break Condition:** If a target-language model's pretraining corpus completely excludes the specific dialect, the robustness advantage disappears.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical Tasks Reveal Shallow vs. Deep Reasoning
- **Claim:** The tiered evaluation design (classification → conflict → state) exposes a severe degradation in models' ability to provide fine-grained, verifiable explanations for physical implausibility.
- **Mechanism:** Each subsequent task requires deeper, more precise causal integration. Large accuracy-verifiability gaps indicate models rely on shallow plausibility judgments rather than constructing detailed physical state simulations.
- **Core Assumption:** Isolating the minimal causal conflict (verifiability) is cognitively more demanding than detecting overall implausibility.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Universal performance collapse from accuracy to verifiability across all models/languages; "order" stories (harder) saw greater drops than "cloze."
- **Break Condition:** If implausible stories contained only trivial, highly salient conflicts, the difficulty gradient between tasks would flatten.

### Mechanism 3: Knowledge Gaps in Implicit, Culturally-Situated States
- **Claim:** Categories like *Location*, *Edible*, and *Conscious* are systematically more challenging due to reliance on implicit, culturally-situated, or biological world knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** These states involve tracking object permanence, biological/agency norms, and cultural practices—knowledge less explicitly stated and less frequent in training corpora than concrete operational states (e.g., *Temperature*).
- **Core Assumption:** Training data frequency and contextual richness for these state categories are lower.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Verifiability per state in results: *Location* (6.06), *Edible* (0.00-4.54), *Conscious* (7.69-15.38) were lowest, while *Temperature* (41.67-50.00) and *Solid* (40.00-60.00) were highest.
- **Break Condition:** A model fine-tuned on a massive, targeted corpus rich in such narratives could close this gap.

## Foundational Learning
-   **Concept: Low-Resource Language NLP**
    - **Why needed:** Basque's limited digital text and pretraining data force reliance on transfer learning, making dataset creation (like BasPhyCo) critical.
    - **Quick Check:** How would you expect the performance gap between language-specific and multilingual models to change if Basque had 10x more unlabelled text for pretraining?

-   **Concept: Dialectal Variation as a Distribution Shift**
    - **Why needed:** Western Basque is a systematic linguistic variant, not just an accent; this distribution shift degrades model performance.
    - **Quick Check:** What is the key methodological risk when automatically generating dialectal data using a model (like Latxa) that itself may have a dialect bias?

-   **Concept: Hierarchical/Tiered Evaluation for Commonsense**
    - **Why needed:** The three tasks probe different reasoning depths. High accuracy alone masks failures in explainability (verifiability).
    - **Quick Check:** If a model scores 90% on story classification but 10% on verifiability, what does this most likely suggest about its reasoning process?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Source Italian Dataset (GITA) -> Manual Translation & Localization (Standard Basque) -> Automatic Dialect Adaptation Prompting (Latxa) -> Manual Validation & Error Correction -> BasPhyCo/BasPhyCo west -> Evaluation Harness (EleutherAI v0.4.9) with tiered prompts -> Model Inference (Llama, Gemma, Latxa, etc.) -> Output Parsing -> Cascading Metric Computation (Accuracy, Consistency, Verifiability) -> Analysis.

**Critical path:**
1.  **Dataset Construction & Validation:** The automatic dialect adaptation step is high-risk. Manual linguist review to correct hallucinations/non-dialectal errors is essential before evaluation.
2.  **Evaluation Setup & Harness Integration:** Correctly implementing the conditional, tiered logic within the Harness is critical; an error invalidates all comparative results.
3.  **Result Aggregation & Analysis:** Properly joining model outputs with ground-truth annotations (especially `confl_pairs` and state labels) for cascading metrics is the final gate.

**Design tradeoffs:**
-   **Automatic vs. Manual Dialect Creation:** Chose automatic adaptation with Latxa for scalability and consistency, at the cost of introducing errors requiring expensive manual post-editing.
-   **Generative vs. Discriminative Evaluation:** Used generative LLMs following prior work to allow open-ended answers but added complexity for prompt engineering/parsing.
-   **Few-shot vs. Zero-shot:** Used few-shot to provide task format examples, testing knowledge with prompting guidance but potentially advantaging strong in-context learners.

**Failure signatures:**
-   **Data-Level:** High correlation between Western Basque performance and Latxa-trained models, plus a large drop for multilingual models, signals dialectal robustness failure.
-   **Task-Level:** High `Accuracy` but very low `Consistency`/`Verifiability` indicates the model can spot implausibility but fails at deep causal reasoning.
-   **Label-Level:** Consistently low scores on `Location`, `Edible`, `Conscious` across models points to a systemic knowledge gap in those ontological categories.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Baseline Establishment:** Run all models on the Italian GITA test set to establish a performance upper bound and verify the evaluation pipeline replicates known results.
2.  **Dialect Impact Test:** Directly compare Standard Basque (`BasPhyCo`) vs. Western Basque (`BasPhyCowest`) results for every model. Test the primary hypothesis: Does Latxa show significantly less performance degradation than multilingual models?
3.  **Error Mode Analysis:** Manually inspect 50 randomly sampled failed `Verifiability` instances for the best model (Latxa-70B) on Western Basque. Categorize errors: (a) Wrong state label, (b) Correct state but wrong conflicting sentence, (c) Failed conflict detection, (d) Dialectal comprehension error.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions. However, the Limitations and Next Checks sections point to several unresolved issues:
- How does automatic dialect adaptation using a language-specific model (Latxa) affect the quality of the generated dialectal data, and can this noise be quantified?
- To what extent are the reported results sensitive to prompt engineering and few-shot configuration, given the lack of reported details?
- Do the findings on dialect robustness and verifiability generalize to other low-resource languages and dialect continua?
- What specific knowledge gaps cause the poor performance on states like *Location*, *Edible*, and *Conscious*, and how can they be addressed?

## Limitations
*   **Dataset Construction Validity:** Automatic dialect adaptation using Latxa may introduce hallucinations and non-dialectal errors, potentially contaminating the Western Basque evaluation results.
*   **Evaluation Pipeline Fidelity:** Exact prompts, few-shot configurations, and generation parameters are unreported; deviations in cascading metric computation could invalidate comparative claims.
*   **Generalizability:** Findings are based on a single language pair (Standard/Western Basque); mechanisms may not hold for other typologically distinct languages or dialect continua.

## Confidence
*   **High Confidence:** Basque-pretrained Latxa models show the smallest Standard-to-Dialect performance degradation on *this specific dataset and task suite*. (Strong evidence: direct Section 5 comparison).
*   **Medium Confidence:** The hierarchical task design successfully reveals a severe verifiability drop, indicating shallow reasoning. (Evidence: universal collapse, but dependent on dataset difficulty).
*   **Low Confidence:** The poor performance on `Location`, `Edible`, and `Conscious` states is primarily due to data scarcity of such knowledge in pretraining corpora. (Correlation observed, causal claim speculative).

## Next Checks
1.  **Reproduce Conditional Metrics:** Implement the exact cascading evaluation logic (verifiability only on stories with correct conflict detection, which are only implausible stories with correct classification). Verify `Verifiability < Consistency < Accuracy` holds for all models.
2.  **Prompt Template Audit:** Obtain or reconstruct the exact few-shot prompt templates used for all three tasks. Run a small ablation with 2-3 prompt variants on the Italian GITA set to confirm reported scores are not prompt-sensitive artifacts.
3.  **Dialect Error Analysis:** Manually annotate 50 `BasPhyCowest` stories where Latxa-70B's output differs most from its `BasPhyCo` output. Categorize differences as (a) valid dialectal variation, (b) grammatical error, or (c) semantic hallucination. This quantifies noise from the automatic adaptation pipeline.
