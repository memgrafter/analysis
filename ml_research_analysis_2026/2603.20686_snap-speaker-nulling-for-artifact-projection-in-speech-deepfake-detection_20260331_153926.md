# SNAP: Speaker Nulling for Artifact Projection in Speech Deepfake Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20686
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20686
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art EERs (0.35% on ASVspoof 2019 LA, 5.42% on ASVspoof 2021 DF, 15.39% on In-the-Wild) by mitigating speaker entanglement in SSL-based detectors.

## Executive Summary
SNAP addresses a critical failure mode in deepfake detection: SSL-based models overfit to speaker identity rather than synthesis artifacts due to the dominant speaker variance in WavLM embeddings. The method estimates a low-dimensional speaker subspace via PCA on per-speaker centroids and applies an orthogonal projection to nullify speaker-dependent components. The resulting speaker-agnostic residuals are fed to a lightweight logistic regression classifier. This simple, post-processing approach dramatically improves generalization, yielding state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks and demonstrating robust cross-domain performance to unseen TTS models.

## Method Summary
SNAP operates on embeddings from a frozen pre-trained WavLM-Large encoder. For each training speaker, it computes the centroid of their L2-normalized embeddings. These centroids are stacked, centered, and decomposed via PCA to obtain the top-K eigenvectors defining the speaker subspace. An orthogonal projection matrix `P_perp = I - U_K U_K^⊤` is constructed to remove components along these speaker directions. During training, all speaker embeddings are projected to residuals `˜z = P_perp z`, and a logistic regression classifier is trained on these features. At inference, the same fixed projection is applied to test embeddings before classification.

## Key Results
- **EER reduction:** Drops from 6.67% to 0.35% on ASVspoof 2019 LA (vs. WavLM-ECAPA), from 16.94% to 5.42% on ASVspoof 2021 DF (vs. AASIST), and from 22.22% to 15.39% on In-the-Wild (vs. WavLM).
- **Silhouette analysis:** After SNAP, mean speaker clustering score decreases from 0.026 to -0.002 (near-random), while real-synthetic separation increases from 0.118 to 0.181.
- **Cross-domain robustness:** Consistently outperforms baselines when evaluated on unseen TTS models (CosyVoice2, F5-TTS), often achieving near-zero EERs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Speaker Entanglement in SSL Representations
WavLM's self-supervised pre-training (utterance mixing) preserves speaker characteristics for verification tasks, causing inter-speaker variance to dominate its embedding space. As a result, detectors exploit speaker-specific correlations instead of subtle synthesis artifacts. t-SNE visualizations confirm that feature clustering is driven by speaker identity, not real-synthetic distinction. The artifact signal exists but is statistically overwhelmed.

### Mechanism 2: Orthogonal Projection for Speaker Nullification
By applying PCA to the matrix of speaker centroids, the top-K eigenvectors span the directions of maximum speaker variation. Projecting an embedding onto the orthogonal complement (`P_perp`) removes its components along these speaker directions. This linear decomposition isolates the residual subspace, which is assumed to contain synthesis artifacts and linguistic content. Silhouette analysis confirms successful suppression of speaker clustering while enhancing class separation.

### Mechanism 3: Linear Separability of Residuals
Once speaker variance is removed, the artifact-related patterns in the residual space become linearly separable. A simple logistic regression classifier suffices, avoiding the complexity and overfitting risks of deeper architectures. The model's cross-domain robustness suggests the projected features capture consistent artifact cues across diverse TTS systems.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Orthogonal Projection**
  - **Why needed:** Core mathematical operation to zero out speaker-dependent components while preserving other variance.
  - **Quick check:** If `U_K` is orthonormal basis for subspace `S`, what does `I - U_K U_K^⊤` do to a vector `z`? (Answer: Projects `z` onto the orthogonal complement of `S`.)
- **Concept: Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**
  - **Why needed:** Estimates the speaker subspace by finding directions of maximum variance in the speaker centroid matrix.
  - **Quick check:** Why is PCA applied to centroids rather than all individual embeddings? (Answer: Centroids represent prototypical speaker positions, reducing intra-speaker noise and focusing on inter-speaker differences.)
- **Concept: Self-Supervised Learning Representations**
  - **Why needed:** WavLM's embeddings inherit biases from its pre-training objective, which must be understood to diagnose speaker entanglement.
  - **Quick check:** Why might WavLM's utterance mixing objective lead to stronger speaker encoding than single-speaker pre-training? (Answer: Mixing forces the model to maintain speaker identity across mixed segments for the speaker verification auxiliary task.)

## Architecture Onboarding
Feature Extractor (WavLM-Large) -> Speaker Subspace Estimator (PCA on centroids) -> Projection Module (P_perp) -> Classifier (Logistic Regression)

**Critical path:**  
*Training:* Extract WavLM embeddings for all train utterances -> compute per-speaker centroids -> PCA on stacked centroids to get `U_K` -> compute `P_perp` -> project all train embeddings -> train logistic regression on `(˜z_train, labels)`.  
*Inference:* Extract embedding `z_test` -> apply fixed `P_perp` -> classify `˜z_test` with trained weights.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Simplicity vs. flexibility: Linear projection is interpretable and cheap but assumes speaker-artifact orthogonality; non-linear disentanglement might capture more complex factors but risks overfitting.
- Fixed K: Choosing `K=5` is heuristic; too small leaves speaker info, too large may remove artifact variance.
- Speaker-centric training: `U_K` is estimated from training speakers; performance may degrade on speakers with out-of-distribution acoustic properties.

**Failure signatures:**
1. High speaker silhouette score (>0) after projection indicates incomplete speaker removal.
2. EER increases with larger `K` suggest over-nulling of artifact information.
3. Performance collapse on cross-domain datasets (e.g., non-LibriSpeech speakers) indicates poor generalization of `U_K`.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Validate that the top-K PCA components capture substantial variance (e.g., >50%) of the centroid matrix, confirming a meaningful speaker subspace.
2. Ablate over `K ∈ {1,3,5,10,20}` and plot EER vs. `K` to identify optimal removal point.
3. Compare (a) no projection + logistic regression, (b) SNAP projection + logistic regression, (c) SNAP features + complex head (e.g., AASIST) to isolate the contribution of projection vs. classifier complexity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Transferability to other paralinguistic tasks:** Can SNAP's speaker-disentanglement framework improve emotion or pathology recognition by removing speaker as a confounder? (Basis: Conclusion mentions this as future work.)
- **Sensitivity to subspace dimension K:** No analysis is provided on how varying `K` affects the trade-off between speaker removal and artifact preservation.
- **Artifact variance quantification:** The paper does not measure what proportion of synthesis artifact variance remains in the residuals `P_perp z` versus being discarded.
- **Classifier complexity ceiling:** It is unknown whether a more complex head (e.g., shallow MLP) could further improve performance on the projected features, or if linear separation is already optimal.

## Limitations
- Speaker centroid computation details (e.g., number of utterances per speaker) are unspecified, affecting subspace estimation.
- Classifier training hyperparameters (regularization, optimizer, epochs) are omitted, hindering exact replication.
- The orthogonality assumption between speaker and artifact subspaces may fail for TTS that closely clones speaker traits.
- The speaker subspace is derived only from training speakers; performance may degrade on speakers with markedly different demographics or recording conditions.

## Confidence
- **WavLM speaker entanglement:** High (supported by t-SNE, quantitative analysis, and related work).
- **Projection-based nullification:** Medium (silhouette improvements and SOTA EERs provide evidence, but depend on linear decomposition and chosen K).
- **Linear classifier sufficiency:** Medium (strong empirical results, yet claim rests on projection effectiveness).
- **Cross-domain robustness:** Medium (positive cross-TTS results, but evaluation limited to two TTS models on LibriSpeech).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of speaker subspace dimension K:** Systematically vary `K` (e.g., 1, 3, 5, 10, 20) and measure EER and silhouette coefficients on a validation set to identify the optimal trade-off.
2. **Out-of-distribution speaker evaluation:** Test SNAP on speakers from a disjoint demographic (e.g., non-English languages, telephone speech) to assess generalization of the learned `U_K`.
3. **Artifact preservation probe:** Inject known synthesis artifacts (e.g., spectral holes, periodic patterns) into real utterances and verify that these artifacts remain detectable in the projected residuals, confirming the orthogonality assumption.
