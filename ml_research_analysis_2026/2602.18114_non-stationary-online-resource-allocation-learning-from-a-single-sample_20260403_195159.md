# Non-Stationary Online Resource Allocation: Learning from a Single Sample
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18114
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18114
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proposes a quantile-based meta-policy achieving \tilde{O}(\sqrt{T}) regret with reward-observed samples and O((\log T)^3) regret with type-only samples under a minimum-arrival-probability assumption, without variation-budget assumptions.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles online multi-resource allocation in a non-stationary environment where only a single historical sample per period is available. It introduces a novel type-dependent quantile-based meta-policy that decouples the problem into modular components: reward distribution estimation, fluid optimization for target service probabilities, and threshold-based acceptance decisions. For reward-observed samples, static thresholds yield \tilde{O}(\sqrt{T}) regret. For type-only samples, the authors prove that sublinear regret is impossible without additional structure; under a mild minimum-arrival-probability assumption, a partially adaptive policy attains \tilde{O}(\sqrt{T}) regret, while a fully adaptive resolving policy with careful rounding achieves the first poly-logarithmic O((\log T)^3) regret, significantly improving over prior dual-based methods. The framework supports multiple resource constraints and does not rely on variation-budget assumptions.

## Method Summary
The method pipeline comprises three modules. First, the **estimation module** processes historical samples (either (type, reward) or type only) to estimate arrival rates and reward cumulative distribution functions. For reward-observed data, a kernel density estimator is used; for type-only, the estimator is updated online. Second, the **optimization module** solves a fluid relaxation of the offline problem—either over the full horizon (static/partial adaptive) or with remaining capacity and future arrivals (fully adaptive)—to obtain target service probabilities \(q_j\) for each type. The fluid problem is solved via the Lagrangian dual, leveraging its threshold property. Third, the **decision meta-policy** accepts an arriving query of type \(j\) only if its reward \(r\) exceeds a threshold \(M(\hat{F}_j, q_{\pi_j})\), where the threshold is the \((1-q_j)\)-quantile of the estimated reward distribution. Three algorithmic variants are proposed: (i) **Static Thresholds** (Algorithm 3) precomputes thresholds once; (ii) **Partial Adaptive** (Algorithm 4) updates the reward distribution but keeps \(q_j\) fixed; (iii) **Fully Adaptive** (Algorithm 5) re-solves a semi-fluid problem each period and applies a rounding rule to handle extreme \(q_j\) values, ensuring feasibility and enabling poly-logarithmic regret.

## Key Results
* decoupled quantile-based meta-policy achieves \tilde{O}(\sqrt{T}) regret with reward-observed samples.
* Under a minimum-arrival-probability assumption, a partially adaptive variant attains \tilde{O}(\sqrt{T}) regret, while a fully adaptive resolving policy with rounding achieves the first O((\log T)^3) poly‑logarithmic regret for type‑only samples.
* The approach handles multiple resource constraints and does not rely on variation‑budget assumptions, improving over prior dual‑based methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decoupling the problem into separate estimation, optimization, and decision modules via type-dependent quantile thresholds enables near-optimal performance despite sparse, non-stationary data.
- **Mechanism:** The meta-policy (Subroutine 1) accepts a query of type `j` only if its reward exceeds a threshold `M(ˆFj, qπj)`, where `ˆFj` is a learned reward distribution estimate and `qπj` is a target service probability from solving an estimated fluid relaxation. This modular separation allows any distribution estimator (e.g., kernel density) to plug into the threshold calculator without altering decision logic.
- **Core assumption:** Historical samples are independent across periods and types; the reward distribution for each type `j` has a bounded density (Assumption 1).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "proposes a novel type-dependent quantile-based meta-policy that decouples the problem into modular components: reward distribution estimation, optimization of target service probabilities via fluid relaxation, and real-time decisions through dynamic acceptance thresholds."
  - [section 3] "The elegance of this meta quantile-based policy lies in its modular design. The complexity of learning reward distributions and determining appropriate service probabilities is abstracted into the inputs ˆFj and qπj."
  - [corpus] Weak direct support; neighboring papers (e.g., "Single-Sample and Robust Online Resource Allocation") also address single-sample settings but use dual-based methods, contrasting this decoupling approach.
- **Break condition:** If the reward distribution estimator ˆFj is highly biased or has large uniform error (e.g., insufficient historical samples for a type), the computed threshold `M(ˆFj, qπj)` becomes unreliable, causing systematic over- or under-acceptance and linear regret.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Under a minimum-arrival-probability assumption (Assumption 3/4), online updating of reward distributions combined with adaptive threshold recomputation achieves sublinear (˜O(√T)) or poly-logarithmic (O((log T)³)) regret.
- **Mechanism:** For type-only samples, the policy continuously updates `ˆFj,t` with each observed reward. Then, at each period `t`, it solves an estimated fluid problem using the *remaining* capacity and *future* estimated arrivals `ˆbj,t` to get current service probabilities `qj,t`, which determine the threshold. The fully adaptive variant (Algorithm 5) further applies a rounding rule to thresholds based on `qj,t` magnitude to control approximation error in the semi-fluid relaxation benchmark.
- **Core assumption:** Each query type appears with probability at least `γ > 0` in every period (Assumption 3/4), ensuring sufficient online learning samples per type. The impossibility result (Theorem 2) shows without such structure, sublinear regret is unattainable.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5] "Under a mild minimum-arrival-probability assumption, we design a partial adaptive threshold scheme... the same O(mlogT/√nT) regret bound holds."
  - [section 6] "Our fully adaptive resolving policy with careful rounding achieves the first poly-logarithmic regret guarantee of O((log T)³)... The rounding mechanism is essential because it ensures the validity of these constructed solutions even when the estimated quantile ˆq is extremely close to 0 or 1."
  - [abstract] "sublinear regret is impossible without additional structure; under a mild minimum-arrival-probability assumption... fully adaptive resolving policy with careful rounding achieves the first poly-logarithmic regret."
- **Break condition:** If the minimum arrival probability `γ` is violated (e.g., a type vanishes for long stretches), online learning stalls for that type, the estimate `ˆFj,t` remains prior-dominated, and the algorithm cannot distinguish its true reward distribution from others, leading to Ω(T) regret as in Theorem 2's counterexample.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The fully adaptive threshold with rounding attains poly-logarithmic regret by constructing feasible solutions to a semi-fluid relaxation benchmark at each step, leveraging the decoupled quantile framework to separate the analysis of distribution learning error (concentrated via Chernoff/DKW) from resource feasibility (controlled by rounding).
- **Mechanism:** At each time `t`, solve an estimated problem `ˆVt,c(It)` to get `ˆqj,t`. Instead of directly inverting `ˆFj,t`, apply rounding: if `ˆqj,t` is near 1, set threshold to `rj` (accept all); if near 0, set to `¯rj+1` (reject all); else, use `ˆF−1j,t(1−ˆqj,t)`. This creates conservative feasible solutions `{˜qj,t}` for the semi-fluid benchmark `¯V semi c(It)`, bounding instantaneous regret by O((log T)²/s) summed over `s=T−t+1`, which telescopes to O((log T)³).
- **Core assumption:** Minimum arrival probability `γ` is known; bounded reward distributions and densities (Assumption 1). The rounding constants (e.g., `2κ(·)`) are derived from concentration bounds on `|˜q∗j,t−ˆqj,t|`.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 6] "The rounding mechanism is essential because it ensures the validity of these constructed solutions even when the estimated quantile ˆq is extremely close to 0 or 1, which prevents the regret bound from deteriorating... By analyzing the instantaneous regret at each step and aggregating over the horizon, we prove Theorem 4... O((log T)³)."
  - [appendix E] Lemma 5 provides the high-probability bound `|˜q∗j,t−ˆqj,t| ≤ κ(logT/√s + logT/√(T−s+1))` that directly enables the rounding thresholds.
  - [abstract] "fully adaptive resolving policy with careful rounding that achieves the first poly-logarithmic regret guarantee of O((log T)³)."
- **Break condition:** If the rounding thresholds are not tightly coupled to the deviation bound `κ` (e.g., constants too small), the constructed `{˜qj,t}` may violate the semi-fluid constraints, invalidating the benchmark comparison and causing regret blow-up. The bound also requires `s` (remaining time) and `T−s+1` to be large enough (e.g., `s ≥ 144κ²(log T)²`) for the case analysis to hold.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Fluid relaxation and its threshold property.
  - **Why needed here:** The offline optimal solution is intractable due to stochastic rewards and non-stationarity; the fluid relaxation ( ¯V fld C ) serves as a computable benchmark with an optimal threshold structure (Lemma 2), converting the problem into choosing service probabilities `qj` per type.
  - **Quick check question:** Given optimal service probabilities `qj*`, how do you convert them into an acceptance threshold for a query of type `j` with reward `r`?
- **Concept:** Uniform convergence and concentration inequalities.
  - **Why needed here:** To bound estimation errors in `ˆdj` (arrival counts) and `ˆFj` (reward distributions) from one sample per period, ensuring the estimated fluid solution `ˆqj` is close to `qj*` with high probability over the horizon.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does the minimum arrival probability assumption `γ` help transform a uniform bound on `|ˆdj−µj|` (from Hoeffding) into a bound on `|qj*−ˆqj|` via Cauchy-Schwarz and strong concavity?
- **Concept:** Semi-fluid relaxation and adaptive resolving.
  - **Why needed here:** Static or partially adaptive policies based on full-horizon estimates incur Ω(√T) regret (lower bound). The semi-fluid relaxation ( ¯V semi c(It) ) uses remaining capacity and future arrivals, and resolving it at each `t` is necessary to achieve poly-logarithmic rates, as argued in online convex optimization literature.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the key difference between the fluid problem ` ¯V fld C ` and the semi-fluid problem ` ¯V semi c(It)` at time `t`? Why does resolving the latter adaptively improve regret?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Estimation Module:** Input: historical samples `{(ˆjt,ˆrt)}` (if reward-observed) or `{ˆjt}`. Output: `ˆdj` (type counts) and `ˆFj`/`ˆFj,t` (reward CDF estimates). Uses kernel density estimation (Section 3.3) or online updates.
  2. **Optimization Module:** Input: `ˆdj`/`ˆbj,t`, `ˆFj`/`ˆFj,t`, current capacities `C`/`c`. Output: target service probabilities `qj`/`qj,t` by solving estimated fluid problem ` ˆVC( ˆd)`/` ˆVt,c(It)` via Lagrangian (Subroutine 2).
  3. **Decision Module (Meta-Policy):** Input: current query `(jt, rt)`, threshold `M(ˆFjt, qπjt)`, remaining capacities. Output: ACCEPT/REJECT and updated capacities (Subroutine 1).
- **Critical path:** Offline preprocessing (estimate `ˆdj`, `ˆFj`) → solve initial fluid → compute static thresholds → online loop: observe `(jt, rt)` → if adaptive, update `ˆFjt` → recompute `qj,t` → apply rounding → compute `M(ˆFjt,t, qπjt,t)` → apply Subroutine 1.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Static vs. Adaptive thresholds:** Static (Section 4) is simpler but requires reward-observed samples; adaptive (Sections 5–6) works with type-only samples but needs online learning and more complex analysis.
  - **Rounding complexity:** Omitted in static/partial adaptive policies (direct quantile inversion), but essential for poly-logarithmic regret in fully adaptive, adding conditional threshold logic (Algorithm 5 lines 9–17) and tighter analysis.
  - **Sample efficiency vs. computational cost:** Kernel estimation is statistically efficient (Lemma 4) but requires bandwidth tuning; fully adaptive re-solving every period is computationally heavier but improves regret from ˜O(√T) to O((log T)³).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Constraint violation warnings:** If `cπt,i` becomes negative after an accept, the virtual buffer analysis (Section E) indicates a potential feasibility gap; actual deployment would reject queries even if threshold is met.
  - **Threshold volatility:** Large swings in `qj,t` between periods (especially early on with few samples) cause erratic thresholds, leading to high regret; monitor `|qj,t − qj,t−1|`.
  - **Impossible regime detection:** If empirical minimum type arrival rate over initial window is << `γ` (from Assumption 3/4), sublinear regret may be unattainable; revert to conservative pacing or request more data.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Estimation accuracy validation:** With synthetic stationary data, plot uniform error `supx | ˆFj(x)−Fj(x)|` vs. sample size ` ˆdj` for kernel estimator; verify O(√(log T/ ˆdj)) rate from Lemma 4.
  2. **Threshold stability test:** Under non-stationary arrival rates (switch mid-horizon), track computed thresholds `M(ˆFjt,t, qπjt,t)` for a high-value type. Check that adaptive thresholds react within O(1) periods after distribution shift, while static thresholds remain stale.
  3. **Regret curve comparison:** Implement all three policies (static, partial adaptive, fully adaptive) on a non-stationary instance with type-only samples and `γ` satisfied. Plot cumulative regret vs. `T`; verify ˜O(√T) for first two, and confirm O((log T)³) slope for fully adaptive on log-log scale. Also run the impossibility counterexample (Example 1) to confirm Ω(T) regret for any policy without `γ`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Reusable Resources**
  - *Question:* How can the quantile-based meta-policy be extended to handle reusable resources where each resource unit can be used multiple times over the horizon?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] Section 7 (Conclusion and Future Directions) lists "extending our framework to settings with reusable resources" as a future direction.
  - *Why unresolved:* The current model assumes resources are consumable (each use permanently reduces capacity). Reusable resources require modeling replenishment and potentially state-dependent availability, which breaks the static capacity assumption and complicates the fluid relaxation and threshold update logic.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Development of a new algorithm for reusable resources with theoretical regret analysis (e.g., sublinear or poly-logarithmic) and empirical validation on simulated reusable resource scenarios.
- **Contextual Information**
  - *Question:* How can contextual information about arriving queries (e.g., user demographics, time of day) be integrated into the quantile-based policy to improve allocation decisions?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] Section 7 lists "contextual information" as a future direction.
  - *Why unresolved:* The current framework treats each query type independently without side information. Incorporating context would require estimating conditional reward distributions given context, handling potentially infinite type space, and adjusting thresholds dynamically, which is not addressed in the current type-dependent design.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* A contextual extension of the quantile-based framework with algorithms that leverage context (e.g., via contextual bandits or function approximation) and corresponding regret bounds that depend on context complexity.
- **Bandit Feedback**
  - *Question:* Can the proposed framework be adapted to the bandit feedback setting where the reward of a query is only observed if it is accepted, and how does this affect the regret guarantees?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] Section 7 lists "bandit feedback where rewards are only observed upon acceptance" as a future direction.
  - *Why unresolved:* The current model observes the reward before the decision (full-information feedback). Bandit feedback introduces exploration-exploitation trade-offs because rejecting a query yields no reward information, making distribution learning more challenging and potentially requiring different thresholding or sampling strategies.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* New algorithms that handle bandit feedback, perhaps using techniques from bandit literature (e.g., optimism or Thompson sampling), with regret analysis showing the impact of the bandit feedback delay compared to the full feedback setting.
- **Empirical Validation on Real Workloads**
  - *Question:* How does the quantile-based policy perform in practice on real-world non-stationary workloads, and does it outperform existing methods in such settings?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] Section 7 lists "empirical validation on real-world non-stationary workloads" as a future direction.
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper provides theoretical guarantees but no empirical evaluation. Real-world data may violate assumptions (e.g., smoothness, bounded densities, minimum arrival probability) and exhibit complex non-stationarity patterns, which could affect practical performance and robustness.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Comprehensive experimental studies on real datasets from applications like cloud resource allocation, digital advertising, or ride-hailing, comparing the proposed policies against state-of-the-art baselines (e.g., dual-based methods) under various non-stationary scenarios.

## Limitations
- Constants (α, β, γ, kernel params) are unspecified and affect theoretical bounds and practical thresholds.
- Key assumptions (bounded reward density, minimum arrival probability) may be violated in real-world deployments, compromising regret guarantees.
- The poly‑logarithmic rate hinges on precise rounding constants; numerical fragility could lead to constraint violations or regret blow‑up.
- The impossibility result makes guarantees conditional; without the minimum‑arrival structure the algorithm may incur linear regret.
- Implementation hurdles include kernel bandwidth selection, solving the Lagrangian dual accurately, and handling rounding edge cases.

## Confidence
- **Decoupling (reward‑observed):** High – modular design, proven \tilde{O}(\sqrt{T}) regret (Thm 1).
- **Min‑arrival enabling sublinear (type‑only):** Medium – necessary per Thm 2, needs empirical validation.
- **Fully adaptive rounding O((log T)³):** Medium – complex analysis, rounding constants not empirically verified.
