# EXCODER: EXplainable Classification Of DiscretE time series Representations
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.13087
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13087
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Transforming time series into discrete latent representations (via VQ-VAE/DVAE) enables more stable and structurally meaningful explanations from standard XAI methods, measured by a novel Similar Subsequence Accuracy (SSA) metric.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of explaining deep learning models for time series classification, where raw data is high-dimensional and noisy, making post-hoc explainable AI (XAI) methods unstable and hard to interpret. The core proposal is to first compress time series into sequences of discrete tokens using models like VQ-VAE or DVAE. This creates a structured, lower-dimensional latent space where each token corresponds to a short, interpretable subsequence of the original signal. XAI methods are then adapted to operate on these token sequences, using a learned "unknown" token for masking. To evaluate whether these explanations are meaningful, the authors introduce the Similar Subsequence Accuracy (SSA) metric, which checks if the most important token's underlying subsequence is a recurring, class-discriminative motif in the training data. Experiments on three datasets suggest that explanations in this discrete space are more compact, show higher agreement between different XAI methods, and are more consistent across model random seeds compared to explanations derived from raw data.

## Method Summary
The method involves a two-stage pipeline. First, a discrete representation encoder (VQ-VAE or DVAE) is trained on all training time series to learn a codebook of tokens. A fixed-length patching mechanism maps each generated token back to a specific subsequence (e.g., 25 timesteps) in the original series. Second, a classifier (Transformer or MLP) is trained on the sequences of token indices. For explainability, standard XAI methods (Saliency Map, Integrated Gradients, RISE, LIME, Attention) are adapted to this discrete token space by replacing selected tokens with a special `[UNKNOWN]` token—a learned, neutral embedding introduced during encoder training—instead of using continuous-valued baselines or masks. The quality of the resulting explanations is evaluated not just by traditional perturbation-based sanity checks, but primarily through the new SSA metric, which directly searches the training set for instances sharing the exact same salient subsequence at the same position and measures class agreement.

## Key Results
*   LIME consistently achieves the highest SSA improvement over random baselines across models and datasets, indicating it identifies the most class-relevant subsequences in the discrete space.
*   Explanations generated in the latent token space exhibit higher agreement (cosine similarity) between different XAI methods than explanations in the raw-data space, suggesting greater stability.
*   Post-hoc perturbation-based methods (LIME, RISE) demonstrate better implementation invariance (consistency across random model seeds) than gradient-based methods (Saliency Map, Integrated Gradients) when applied to the discrete representations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Discrete latent representations (via VQ-VAE/DVAE) enhance XAI effectiveness by compressing time series into structured, noise-reduced token sequences that preserve class-discriminative temporal patterns.
*   **Mechanism:** The encoder maps a raw time series segment into a sequence of discrete tokens from a learned codebook. Each token corresponds to a fixed-length subsequence (via a patching mechanism). This quantization filters high-frequency noise and aggregates salient temporal features into compact, semantically meaningful units. XAI methods then operate on this lower-dimensional, structured space, attributing importance to entire subsequences rather than individual noisy time points.
*   **Core assumption:** The discrete codebook learns a meaningful factorization of the data manifold such that class-relevant patterns correspond to specific tokens or token sequences, and quantization does not discard critical discriminative information.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "transforming time series into discrete latent representations... enhances explainability by reducing redundancy and focusing on the most informative patterns."
    *   [section 3.1]: "We incorporate a patching mechanism... each discrete code captures a specific, localized temporal pattern... classifier operates on the sequence of discrete latent codes, providing a structured representation."
    *   [corpus]: Neighbor papers highlight challenges of high dimensionality/noise in raw time series XAI, supporting the need for compressed representations. Weak direct evidence for discrete token efficacy.
*   **Break condition:** If the codebook fails to capture class-discriminative motifs (e.g., poor reconstruction or classification accuracy), the compressed space loses essential information, and XAI attributions become misaligned.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Adapting XAI methods (SM, IG, RISE) to discrete token spaces via an "unknown" token masking strategy enables valid feature attribution while respecting the quantized representation's structure.
*   **Mechanism:** Standard continuous-space masking (e.g., zeroing pixels) is invalid for discrete embeddings. The authors introduce a learned `unknown` token embedding, akin to BERT's [MASK], inserted during training and used for masking during XAI computation. This provides a neutral, in-vocabulary baseline that minimally distorts the model's learned representation space. Gradients (SM, IG) or perturbation responses (RISE) are then computed with respect to token embeddings or token presence.
*   **Core assumption:** The `unknown` token embedding occupies a central, semantically neutral position in the continuous embedding space, so replacing a token with it approximates a "missing" or "baseline" feature without introducing out-of-distribution artifacts.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 3.2]: "we introduce an `unknown` token... to serve as a neutral placeholder during masking... The `unknown` token is incorporated as an additional embedding."
    *   [section 3.2]: Equations (1)-(4) show how SM, IG, and RISE are reformulated using token embeddings and the `unknown` token.
    *   [corpus]: Neighbors discuss masking challenges in time series XAI but do not address discrete token masking. Evidence is primarily methodological description.
*   **Break condition:** If the `unknown` token embedding is not truly neutral (e.g., it learns a specific pattern during training), masking could introduce systematic bias, leading to spurious attributions.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The Similar Subsequence Accuracy (SSA) metric provides a grounded, data-centric evaluation of XAI quality by measuring whether salient tokens map to class-discriminative subsequences present in the training set.
*   **Mechanism:** For a test instance, the XAI method identifies the most important token position. SSA then checks how frequently the exact subsequence (of length *l*) at that position appears in correctly classified training samples of the same class versus other classes. High SSA means the highlighted pattern is a recurring, class-specific motif. This moves evaluation beyond perturbation-based sanity checks to direct pattern matching in the learned discrete space.
*   **Core assumption:** Recurrence of a specific subsequence at a specific relative position in training samples of a class is a valid proxy for that subsequence being a true class-discriminative feature learned by the model.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "SSA... quantitatively assesses the alignment between XAI-identified salient subsequences and the label distribution in the training data."
    *   [section 3.3.4]: Full algorithm description and rationale: "SSA quantifies how often the most influential subsequences... reappear in correctly classified training instances of the same class."
    *   [corpus]: Neighbors focus on other evaluation metrics (perturbation, agreement). No direct evidence for SSA; it is a novel contribution of this paper.
*   **Break condition:** If the model has overfitted to spurious correlations not present as recurring subsequences (e.g., recording artifacts at fixed offsets), SSA could be low even for correct explanations, or high for misleading patterns that coincidentally repeat in training data.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Discrete Representation Learning (VQ-VAE, DVAE)
    *   **Why needed here:** The entire premise rests on converting continuous time series into a sequence of discrete tokens. Understanding vector quantization, codebook learning, and the patching mechanism is essential to grasp what information is preserved/lost.
    *   **Quick check question:** How does the VQ-VAE's codebook vector quantization differ from DVAE's categorical distribution over tokens, and what might be the implications for the continuity of the latent space used by the classifier?
*   **Concept:** Post-hoc XAI Methods (LIME, RISE, Integrated Gradients)
    *   **Why needed here:** The paper adapts these specific methods. To understand the adaptations, one must understand their original mechanisms: perturbation (LIME, RISE) and gradient integration (IG).
    *   **Quick check question:** Why is a "baseline" needed for Integrated Gradients, and why is choosing an appropriate baseline particularly challenging for discrete token embeddings compared to image data?
*   **Concept:** Time Series Patching & Fixed-Length Segmentation
    *   **Why needed here:** The link between a single token and an interpretable subsequence in the original time series is fundamental to the "explainable" claim. The patching strategy (length=25) directly determines the resolution and meaning of explanations.
    *   **Quick check question:** What are the trade-offs involved in choosing the patch length (e.g., 25 timesteps)? How might a too short or too long patch length harm interpretability or the validity of the SSA metric?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Raw Time Series Encoder (VQ-VAE/DVAE) -> Token Sequence -> Classifier (Transformer/MLP) -> XAI Adapter Layer (with [UNKNOWN] token) -> Importance Scores -> SSA Evaluator (queries training token sequences)

**Critical path:**
1. Pre-train VQ-VAE/DVAE on all training time series (reconstruction task).
2. Generate full tokenized training and test datasets (D_latent).
3. Train the classifier (Transformer/MLP) on D_train_latent.
4. For a test instance, run the chosen XAI method via the Adapter to get importance scores per token position.
5. Feed (token positions, scores) to SSA Evaluator with D_train_latent to compute SSA.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Codebook size (K) vs. Information Loss:** Larger K preserves more detail but reduces compression/denoising benefit; smaller K increases noise robustness but may merge distinct patterns.
*   **Patch length:** Shorter patches offer finer granularity but may capture less semantically meaningful patterns; longer patches are more interpretable as "motifs" but reduce the number of distinct subsequences for SSA matching.
*   **`unknown` token integration rate (p_unk):** Higher rates during training make the model more robust to masking but may degrade normal performance. Must balance adaptation needs vs. primary task accuracy.

**Failure signatures:**
*   **Low classification accuracy on D_latent:** Indicates the discrete representation is insufficient. XAI on this space is moot.
*   **Very low SSA (near random) for all XAI methods:** Suggests either the XAI methods are failing in the latent space, or the discrete tokens do not correspond to recurring, class-specific subsequences (poor codebook learning).
*   **High variance in explanation (Cosine Similarity) across random seeds:** Especially for gradient-based methods (SM, IG), indicates explanations are unstable and not implementation-invariant.
*   **SSA high but perturbation AUC low:** Potential mismatch; the highlighted subsequence appears in training data but is not actually critical for the model's decision (the model may use other features).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reconstruction & Classification Sanity Check:** Train VQ-VAE/DVAE and classifiers. Report reconstruction MSE and classification F1 on test sets. Must establish baseline performance before XAI.
2. **XAI Adaptation Sanity Check:** On a small, synthetic dataset with known ground-truth discriminative subsequences, apply adapted XAI (e.g., LIME) to the latent model. Verify that importance is concentrated on the known discriminative token positions. This tests the masking/attribution pipeline.
3. **SSA Baseline & Random Comparison:** For the best-performing model-dataset pair, compute SSA for all XAI methods and for a random baseline (randomly selecting a token position). Confirm that at least some XAI methods show significant (p<0.05) improvement over random. This validates the SSA metric itself.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question 1:** What specific data or model characteristics determine which XAI method (e.g., LIME, RISE, SM) yields the most class-discriminative explanations when applied to discrete latent representations?
*   **Question 2:** Can a model jointly learned for classification and discrete representation extraction (i.e., an end-to-end trained model without a separate pre-trained VQ-VAE/DVAE) produce explanations that are both more faithful and more compact than the current two-stage approach?
*   **Question 3:** Do the subsequences identified as salient by XAI methods in discrete latent spaces (as measured by high SSA) align with subsequences that domain experts (e.g., clinicians, welding engineers) subjectively identify as meaningful and diagnostic?
*   **Question 4:** What is the optimal patching window length (the number of raw timesteps mapped to a single discrete token) to maximize the trade-off between explanation compactness, SSA, and classification performance?
*   **Question 5:** To what extent do the observed benefits of discrete representations for XAI (e.g., higher method agreement, better implementation invariance) generalize to other time series domains (e.g., finance, audio, video) and to other discrete representation learning techniques beyond VQ-VAE and DVAE?

## Limitations
*   Exact hyperparameters for VQ-VAE/DVAE (codebook size, commitment loss, LR) and classifiers are unreported, blocking replication.
*   Patch length set to 25 timesteps is not justified; SSA’s sensitivity to patch/subsequence length *l* and exact-match neighbor search may cause sparsity.
*   XAI method hyperparameters (LIME kernel/width, IG interpolation steps, RISE masks) are unspecified.
*   The `unknown` token’s neutrality as a masking baseline is an untested assumption.

## Confidence
*   **Discrete latent representations boost XAI stability/compactness:** Medium – SSA improvements are shown, but the noise-filtering mechanism is inferred, not measured.
*   **`unknown` token masking yields valid attributions:** Medium – conceptually sound but lacks an ablation confirming neutrality.
*   **SSA metric reliably aligns explanations with training patterns:** Low – no external validation; exact-match condition may be brittle and dataset-dependent.
*   **LIME is most consistent performer:** High – based on repeated empirical results across datasets and models.
*   **Post-hoc methods show better implementation invariance:** Medium – based on reported cosine similarity scores, but dependent on specific random seed procedure.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter reporting & sensitivity:** Publish full hyperparameters and run a sensitivity analysis (codebook size, patch length, unknown rate, classifier depth) to confirm SSA gains are robust.
2. **`unknown` token baseline ablation:** Compare against alternatives (zero, mean, random token) for SM/IG/RISE/LIME; evaluate attribution stability (cosine similarity across seeds) and, on synthetic data, correlation with ground-truth important features.
3. **SSA robustness & synthetic test:** (a) Test SSA across a range of subsequence lengths and with approximate matching (e.g., DTW). (b) Generate synthetic time series with embedded discriminative subsequences; verify that SSA and other XAI metrics recover them in both latent and raw spaces.
