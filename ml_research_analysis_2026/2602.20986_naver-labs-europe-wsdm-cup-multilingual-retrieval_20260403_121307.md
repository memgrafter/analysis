# Naver Labs Europe @ WSDM CUP | Multilingual Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.20986
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20986
- **Reference Count**: 11
- **Primary Result**: SPLARE-based multilingual retrieval achieves nDCG@20 = 0.559, surpassing a strong dense baseline by +7.5.

## Executive Summary
The WSDM Cup 2026 posed a challenging multilingual retrieval problem: given English queries, retrieve relevant documents from a collection of approximately 10 million text documents in Chinese, Persian, and Russian. Naver Labs Europe addressed this with SPLARE, a learned sparse retrieval model that maps queries and documents into a language-agnostic sparse latent space using sparse autoencoders, thereby circumventing the fixed-vocabulary constraints of traditional sparse methods.

The team built a multi-stage pipeline: initial retrieval with SPLARE-7B, fusion with SPLADE-8B via Reciprocal Rank Fusion, application of the Qwen3-Reranker-4B cross-encoder to both retrieval outputs, and a final fusion of the reranked lists. This progression lifted nDCG@20 from 0.498 to 0.559, outperforming a strong dense embedding baseline (Qwen3-8B-Embed, nDCG@20=0.423) by a notable +7.5 points. The results underscore the effectiveness of learned sparse retrieval for multilingual search and provide a blueprint for combining sparse retrieval with neural reranking.

## Method Summary
The core method is SPLARE, which employs sparse autoencoders to produce high-dimensional, sparse representations of text. These representations are language-agnostic, enabling cross-lingual matching without explicit translation. SPLARE is used in conjunction with SPLADE-8B, another sparse retrieval model, and their outputs are combined using Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF). To refine precision, a two-step reranking is performed: the top candidates from both SPLARE and SPLADE are individually reranked by the Qwen3-Reranker-4B cross-encoder, after which the two reranked lists are fused again via RRF. This cascade leverages the recall strength of sparse retrievers and the fine-grained relevance judgment of the reranker.

## Key Results
- SPLARE-7B alone: nDCG@20 = 0.498
- SPLARE-7B + SPLADE-8B fusion via RRF: nDCG@20 = 0.517
- After applying Qwen3-Reranker-4B to both systems and fusing reranked lists: nDCG@20 = 0.559, a +7.5 improvement over the dense Qwen3-8B-Embed baseline (0.423)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SPLARE's sparse autoencoder is trained to reconstruct multilingual text while enforcing a sparsity constraint, forcing the model to compress semantic content into a language-neutral set of active dimensions. This latent space alignment means that queries and documents in different languages that convey similar meaning are pushed close together, enabling effective retrieval without language-specific processing. The use of Reciprocal Rank Fusion to combine SPLARE and SPLADE outputs harnesses complementary strengths: SPLARE provides a targeted retrieval based on its learned space, while SPLADE-8B may capture different lexical signals. Finally, the Qwen3-Reranker-4B cross-encoder performs token-level attention between query and document, allowing precise re-evaluation of candidates and correcting any false positives from the first stage. The successive fusion and reranking steps systematically improve ranking quality.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Sparse Retrieval**  
   - *Why needed*: Enables efficient high-dimensional vector search with low memory overhead and interpretable term-level features.  
   - *Quick check*: Confirm that the model’s embedding dimension is large (e.g., >30,000) and the average number of non-zero entries per query is small (<100).

2. **Sparse Autoencoders**  
   - *Why needed*: Learn language-agnostic representations by reconstructing input text under a sparsity penalty, aligning different languages in a shared latent space.  
   - *Quick check*: Monitor reconstruction loss and sparsity regularization during training; validate that sentences with similar meaning in different languages yield correlated sparse codes.

3. **Language-Agnostic Representations**  
   - *Why needed*: Allows a single retrieval system to serve multiple languages without building separate indexes or performing machine translation.  
   - *Quick check*: Perform zero-shot retrieval tests with queries in a held-out language and measure effectiveness; compare against a monolingual baseline.

4. **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)**  
   - *Why needed*: Merges multiple ranked lists without requiring score normalization, offering robustness to different scoring distributions.  
   - *Quick check*: Compare RRF against score interpolation (e.g., weighted sum of normalized scores) on a validation set; RRF should consistently yield higher nDCG or MAP.

5. **Neural Reranking (e.g., Qwen3-Reranker-4B)**  
   - *Why needed*: A cross-encoder reranker performs deep interaction between query and document, improving precision at the cost of added latency, and can compensate for the noisiness of first-stage retrieval.  
   - *Quick check*: Evaluate nDCG@20 before and after reranking on a fixed set of top‑k candidates; ensure the latency increase remains within system constraints.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
English Query -> SPLARE Encoder -> SPLARE Index -> Candidate Set A; English Query -> SPLADE Encoder -> SPLADE Index -> Candidate Set B; Candidate Set A -> Qwen3-Reranker-4B -> Reranked A; Candidate Set B -> Qwen3-Reranker-4B -> Reranked B; [Reranked A, Reranked B] -> Reciprocal Rank Fusion -> Final Ranking.

**Critical Path**  
An English query is simultaneously encoded by SPLARE and SPLADE encoders; each encoder retrieves a set of top‑k documents from its respective sparse index. These two candidate sets are independently reranked by the Qwen3-Reranker-4B cross‑encoder. The two reranked lists are then combined via RRF to produce the final ranking delivered to the user.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Sparse vs. dense*: Sparse representations (SPLARE, SPLADE) offer lower storage and faster retrieval but may sacrifice some semantic nuance; dense embeddings provide rich similarity but require larger indexes and more compute.
- *Single vs. multiple retrievers*: Using two complementary retrievers improves coverage and robustness but increases indexing and query cost. Fusion via RRF mitigates the need for score calibration but may dilute the signal if one retriever is consistently weaker.
- *Reranking depth*: Applying a powerful cross‑encoder to all candidates would be prohibitively slow; limiting reranking to the union of top‑k from both retrievers balances precision and latency.
- *Model choice*: SPLARE’s language‑agnostic latent space is compact and general, but training such a model requires large multilingual data; if data is scarce, the space may not align well, leading to poor cross‑lingual performance.

**Failure Signatures**
- *Poor cross‑lingual alignment*: Languages that are distant from the training distribution show lower recall (e.g., Persian performs worse than Chinese). Check by per‑language nDCG breakdown.
- *Reranker bias*: If Qwen3-Reranker-4B was trained on data overlapping with the test collection, it may artificially inflate scores. Verify by analyzing training data provenance or running a contrastive test with out‑of‑distribution queries.
- *Fusion degradation*: When one retriever consistently underperforms, RRF may still promote its irrelevant documents, harming overall quality. Monitor the contribution of each retriever’s top‑k documents in the final list.
- *Latency spikes*: The reranking step can dominate query latency, especially when candidate sets are large. Profile per‑query times and consider parallelizing reranker calls.

**First Experiments**
1. **SPLARE‑7B alone**: Baseline sparse retrieval using the SPLARE encoder and index, yielding nDCG@20 = 0.498.
2. **SPLARE + SPLADE fusion (RRF)**: Combining the two retrievers’ ranked lists with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, improving to nDCG@20 = 0.517.
3. **Reranking and fusion of reranked lists**: Applying Qwen3-Reranker‑4B to both retrieval outputs and fusing the reranked results via RRF, achieving the final nDCG@20 = 0.559.

## Limitations
- Statistical significance of the incremental gains (e.g., between fusion and reranked fusion) is not reported.
- Critical implementation details for SPLARE training, SPLADE‑8B fusion mechanics, and the exact Qwen3‑Reranker prompt are missing, hindering reproducibility.
- The evaluation is confined to a single multilingual dataset (Chinese, Persian, Russian); performance on other language families or larger corpora remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Substantial improvement over dense baseline**: High  
- **Reproducibility given omitted details**: Medium  
- **Generalizability beyond tested languages**: Low
