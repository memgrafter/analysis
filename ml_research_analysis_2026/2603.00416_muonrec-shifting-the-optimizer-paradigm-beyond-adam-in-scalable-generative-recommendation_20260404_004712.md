# MuonRec: Shifting the Optimizer Paradigm Beyond Adam in Scalable Generative Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00416  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00416  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** MuonRec reduces converged training steps by 32.4% and improves NDCG@10 by 12.6% on average, with up to 56.0% gain for TIGER on the Office dataset.

## Executive Summary
MuonRec challenges the near-universal adoption of Adam/AdamW in large-scale recommender system training by introducing the Muon optimizer for weight matrices. Muon applies orthogonalized momentum updates via Newton-Schulz iteration to 2D parameters (e.g., Transformer layer weights) while retaining Adam for 1D parameters (biases, embeddings). This hybrid strategy addresses optimization collapse and misaligned geometry, yielding faster convergence and significant improvements in ranking metrics across both sequential (GRU4Rec, SASRec) and generative (TIGER, MiniOneRec) models. The work establishes that optimizer design, tailored to parameter structure, is a critical factor for scaling generative recommendation.

## Method Summary
MuonRec partitions model parameters into two groups: **Muon** for all 2D weight matrices (e.g., `nn.Linear.weight`, attention projection matrices) and **Adam** for 1D parameters (biases, LayerNorm gains/scales), embedding tables, and output heads. For Muon parameters, momentum is accumulated and then orthogonalized via Newton-Schulz iteration to produce an update direction that promotes uniform singular value distribution. Adam is applied as usual to the remaining parameters. Hyperparameters are tuned in two stages: first sweep Adam group learning rate and weight decay; then fix those and sweep Muon group learning rate (aggressive range, e.g., 5e-3–5e-2) and weight decay. Early stopping on validation performance determines convergence steps.

## Key Results
- Average **32.4% reduction** in converged training steps compared to Adam-only training.
- Average **12.6% improvement** in NDCG@10.
- For the generative TIGER model on the Office dataset, **NDCG@10 improved by up to 56.0%**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** Muon’s Newton-Schulz orthogonalization of momentum prevents optimization collapse into low-rank subspaces. For a matrix parameter \(W\), Muon accumulates momentum \(M_t = \mu M_{t-1} + G_t\) then applies Newton-Schulz to approximate \(\text{Ortho}(M_t) \approx UV^\top\), yielding \(\Delta W \propto \text{Ortho}(M_t)\) [section 2.3]. This rotates the momentum to be uniformly distributed across singular vectors, avoiding domination by a few large eigenvalues. Break condition: numerical instability if \(M_t\) is near-singular or Newton-Schulz iterations are truncated too aggressively.

**Mechanism 2:** Muon’s matrix-structured update aligns with the linear operator geometry of weight matrices. Adam adapts per-coordinate via \(\hat{m}_t/\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}\), treating each weight independently. Muon treats the 2D weight as a single operator and takes a step in a matrix-valued metric (e.g., Frobenius norm) [section 2.4]. This respects the natural conditioning captured by singular values rather than per-coordinate variances. Break condition: harmful if applied to non-linear layers (e.g., LayerNorm) or 1D parameters.

**Mechanism 3:** The hybrid Muon+Adam strategy matches optimizer to parameter structure. Muon’s Newton-Schulz requires 2D input; Adam remains suitable for vectors and embedding lookups. The split ensures each parameter group receives geometrically appropriate updates [section 2.4, Table 2]. Break condition: misclassifying tensor shapes (e.g., treating a 1D tensor as 2D) degrades performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Model parameters --split--> Muon group (2D weight tensors: `nn.Linear.weight`, attention projection weights) and Adam group (1D tensors: biases, LayerNorm weight/bias; embedding tables; output layers).

**Critical path:**
1. Implement a hybrid optimizer wrapper that maintains separate state dictionaries for Muon and Adam groups.
2. Fuse Newton-Schulz iteration (typically 5–10 iterations) into the Muon step; ensure correct handling of momentum accumulation \(M_t\).
3. Tune learning rates separately: Muon LR in aggressive range \([5\times10^{-3}, 5\times10^{-2}]\), Adam LR in standard \([10^{-5}, 10^{-3}]\). Use two-stage protocol: first tune Adam group (LR, weight decay), then fix and sweep Muon group.
4. Validate on a small proxy model (e.g., SASRec) that Muon reduces steps without divergence before scaling to large models.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Computational overhead:* Newton-Schulz adds \(O(n^3)\) FLOPs but is <10% of total training for typical hidden sizes (128–1024). Fewer iterations reduce overhead but weaken orthogonalization.
- *LR sensitivity:* Muon tolerates aggressive LRs due to implicit spectral regularization; LRs >0.1 often cause divergence.
- *Stability vs. speed:* More iterations improve conditioning but increase compute; 5 iterations is often sufficient.

**Failure signatures:**
- *Divergence with high Muon LR:* Training loss spikes immediately; reduce Muon LR (start from 1e-3) or increase NS iterations.
- *No speedup but similar final performance:* Muon LR likely too low; try increasing by 2–3×.
- *Performance drop when Muon applied to 1D params:* Verify parameter grouping logic; ensure biases/LayerNorm are in Adam group.
- *Numerical NaNs in Muon step:* Likely due to ill-conditioned \(M_t\); add a small epsilon to diagonal or reduce iteration count.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Train GRU4Rec or Caser with hybrid Muon/Adam. Verify Muon on the output projection layer (2D) converges faster than Adam on that layer, while embedding updates remain in Adam.
2. In TIGER, replace only the encoder’s linear layer optimizers with Muon, leave others on Adam. Measure per-block gradient eigenvalue spectra (via Hessian approximations) to confirm more uniform spectra under Muon.
3. Run MiniOneRec SFT with Muon vs. Adam, then switch optimizer in RL. Observe that starting with Muon yields better final performance, confirming trajectory consistency matters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization across architectures and scales:** Does Muon provide consistent gains for non-Transformer models (e.g., Mamba, SSM) and web-scale datasets? Current evidence is limited to a few model families on small Amazon datasets.
2. **Mechanism behind representation collapse reduction:** Why does Muon particularly help generative models with quantized outputs? The paper hypothesizes reduced index collapse but lacks direct spectral analysis or ablations isolating Newton-Schulz effects.
3. **Optimizer switching disruption:** Why does switching from Adam (used in pre-trained backbones) to Muon mid-training (e.g., only in RL) cause performance drops? The explanation (“trajectory consistency”) is speculative; root cause may be incompatible update geometries.
4. **Learning rate regime for billion-parameter models:** Muon’s optimal aggressive LR (~1e-2) was found on small datasets; it may shift or cause overfitting when data volume increases.

## Limitations
- Muon implementation details are unspecified: number of Newton-Schulz iterations, precision (float32/bfloat16), and stabilization tricks (e.g., MuonClip) are not documented.
- Generative model preprocessing lacks detail: SID tokenization for TIGER/MiniOneRec, negative sampling for evaluation, and sequence truncation/padding are not described.
- Early-stopping criterion (validation metric, patience) is unclear, affecting reported “converged training steps.”
- MiniOneRec SFT+RL stage transition protocol (RL algorithm hyperparameters, KL penalty, SFT data construction) is undocumented.

## Confidence
- **Empirical gains (steps↓32.4%, NDCG↑12.6%):** High – direct results from paper experiments.
- **Mechanism 1 (orthogonalization prevents low-rank collapse):** Medium – theoretical basis exists but no RecSys-specific spectral analysis provided.
- **Mechanism 2 (matrix-aware geometry advantage):** Low – largely conceptual; limited empirical validation beyond scaling laws in other corpora.
- **Mechanism 3 (hybrid strategy necessity):** Medium – supported by Table 2 ablation, but only within this paper’s model suite.
