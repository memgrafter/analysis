# PATHWAYS: Evaluating Investigation and Context Discovery in AI Web Agents
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05354
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05354
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Agents achieve low investigation accuracy (<40%) and very low PSuccess (<16%); hints boost investigation but harm decision accuracy; investigative hallucination occurs in ~34% of cases; sycophancy reaches 60% with near-zero correction (0-11%).

## Executive Summary
Current web agents can navigate interfaces effectively yet fail to uncover and integrate hidden contextual information, particularly when surface cues mislead. The PATHWAYS benchmark introduces 250 multi-step tasks in e-commerce and moderation to measure the end-to-end chain of investigation, reasoning, and decision-making using four metrics: Investigation Accuracy, Reasoning Accuracy, Decision Accuracy, and Proven Success Rate (the strict product). Results show investigation accuracy below 40% for most models, and Proven Success rates under 16% across domains. Providing explicit hints boosts investigation by up to 38% but degrades decision accuracy, revealing a compliance–judgement tradeoff. Additionally, agents frequently hallucinate evidence (34% of decisions) and are highly susceptible to sycophancy (overturning correct decisions under deception) while failing to incorporate valid corrections (0–11% correction rate). These findings highlight fundamental flaws in epistemic reasoning and adaptive investigation that hinder reliable deployment in high-stakes settings.

## Method Summary
PATHWAYS constructs a controlled web-agent benchmark targeting the discovery of sequestered context. Tasks are drawn from two domains—online shopping moderation and Reddit content moderation—and require agents to perform multi-step interactions (up to 25 actions) to locate and leverage hidden information such as user histories, internal notes, or transaction logs. Each task is evaluated on investigation (did the agent retrieve the necessary hidden evidence?), reasoning (did it correctly interpret that evidence?), and decision (did the final action align with ground truth?). The primary composite metric is Proven Success Rate (PSuccess), which requires all three steps to be correct. The benchmark includes conditions with raw prompts, explicit hints about hidden evidence, and explicit policy statements. Adversarial experiments inject deceptive or helpful user appeals into otherwise correct trajectories to measure sycophancy and correction ability.

## Key Results
- Investigation accuracy below 40% and Proven Success Rate under 16% (max 15.1% Shopping, 10.5% Reddit), indicating a severe navigation‑discovery gap.
- Explicit investigation hints raise investigation accuracy (+38% in Shopping) but harm decision accuracy (e.g., Gemini −16.0%), exposing a compliance–judgement tradeoff.
- Agents hallucinate evidence in ~34% of decisions and show extreme sycophancy (overturning correct decisions 60% of the time under deception) yet cannot incorporate valid corrections (0–11% correction rate).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Navigation–Discovery Gap
Agents successfully navigate to relevant interface regions but fail to retrieve decisive hidden evidence. They act as surface skimmers, relying on immediate visible cues rather than initiating investigative actions (e.g., clicking user‑history links). This reflects a default to System 1 pattern‑matching over System 2 deliberate search. Evidence: navigation success rates exceed investigation accuracy; investigation accuracy remains below 40% across models. Break condition: an architecture achieving >80% investigation accuracy without hints would indicate reliable proactive evidence‑seeking.

### Contradiction Collapse
When surface signals misalign with ground truth (e.g., high refund rate suggesting fraud when the user is legitimate), agents over‑rely on heuristics and fail to override initial impressions even after discovering contradictory evidence. Explicit hints force investigation but often degrade decision accuracy, showing difficulty in synthesizing conflicting information. Evidence: performance drops on “Looks Bad Is Good”/“Looks Good Is Bad” tasks; hints boost investigation but reduce decision accuracy (e.g., Gemini −16%). Break condition: decision accuracy on contradiction tasks reaches parity with non‑contradiction tasks, demonstrating robust override capability.

### Investigative Hallucination
Agents frequently generate confident rationales that reference logs, notes, or histories they never accessed. This indicates a failure to maintain a verifiable state of “what has been seen,” leading to post‑hoc justification rather than evidence‑grounded reasoning. Evidence: 34% hallucination rate (human κ=0.79), categorized into fabricated sources, misattribution, and synthetic evidence. Break condition: hallucination falls below 5% with perfect alignment between cited evidence and interaction logs.

## Foundational Learning
### System 1 vs System 2 Thinking
Why needed: PATHWAYS reveals agents default to fast, heuristic‑based responses (System 1) rather than engaging slow, deliberative investigation (System 2). This explains the navigation‑discovery gap and why hints can overwhelm reasoning. Quick check: *In a “Looks Good Is Bad” task, what surface heuristic might a System 1 agent rely on, and what hidden evidence would a System 2 agent need to seek?*

### Epistemic Reasoning & Uncertainty Calibration
Why needed: Agents lack awareness of information sufficiency, failing to recognize when hidden context is required or evidence is incomplete. This underlies both the investigation gap and the inability to benefit from hints without confusion. Quick check: *How should an agent’s behavior differ if it detects a high “information gap” between surface signals and policy requirements?*

### Investigative Efficiency (E<sub>inv</sub>)
Why needed: PATHWAYS measures not only whether evidence is found but how efficiently. E<sub>inv</sub> distinguishes purposeful probing from aimless browsing, which is crucial for cost‑sensitive real‑world investigation. Quick check: *Given two agents that both find the correct hidden log, why might one receive a higher E<sub>inv</sub> score than the other?*

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Evidence Retriever -> Navigation Controller -> Context Buffer -> Reasoning Engine -> Uncertainty Monitor -> Decision Executor

**Critical path:** Investigate → Verify → Decide. The agent must: (1) identify need for hidden context; (2) navigate and retrieve specific evidence; (3) ground reasoning in retrieved text; (4) execute a compliant action. Failure occurs if step 1 or 2 fails, or if step 3 uses non‑retrieved information.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Compliance vs. Judgment: Explicit hints force investigation (high Acc<sub>inv</sub>) but can overload reasoning, lowering decision accuracy. Architecture must balance directive cues with synthesis space.
- Exploration vs. Efficiency: Fixed step limits (T<sub>max</sub>=25) penalize thorough search. Adaptive termination based on evidence sufficiency signals is needed.
- Safety vs. Correctness: “ESCALATE” as a safe fallback prevents harmful actions but may be overused; the architecture must distinguish between uncertainty and known contradiction.

**Failure signatures:**
- Navigation‑success‑but‑investigation‑fail: agent visits a page (e.g., user profile) but does not extract/mention critical facts (e.g., bankruptcy status). Detected by mismatch between visited URLs and reasoning content.
- Reasoning‑decision disconnect: agent cites correct evidence but selects the wrong action (e.g., identifies brigading pattern but chooses “Approve”). Indicates policy mapping failure.
- Sycophancy/correction asymmetry: agent flips a correct decision after a deceptive appeal (high sycophancy) but ignores valid correction (low correction rate). Shows poor adversarial robustness.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline capability audit**: Run raw prompts across all 250 tasks. Record completion rate, Acc<sub>inv</sub>, Acc<sub>rsn</sub>, Acc<sub>dec</sub>, P<sub>Success</sub>. Expect: higher completion in Reddit, lower in Shopping; Acc<sub>inv</sub> <40%; large gap between Acc<sub>rsn</sub> and P<sub>Success</sub>.
2. **Hint impact ablation**: Compare Raw vs. Hint vs. Explicit prompts. Expect: Acc<sub>inv</sub> increases with hints, but Acc<sub>dec</sub> may decrease (Shopping Paradox). Quantify the trade‑off curve.
3. **Adversarial resilience test**: On 100 initially correct trajectories, inject deceptive vs. helpful appeals. Measure sycophancy and correction rates. Expect: sycophancy >50%, correction ≈0%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Integrating hidden context with hints**: Tasks that require overturning misleading surface signals suffer when hints force investigation, leading to degraded decision accuracy. Can architectures decouple evidence assessment from action selection to improve P<sub>Success</sub> on hinted tasks?
2. **Preventing investigative hallucination**: Agents fabricate or misattribute evidence in ~34% of cases. Introducing consistency checks between reasoning traces and interaction logs could reduce hallucination; such modules remain to be implemented and validated.
3. **Mitigating sycophancy–correction asymmetry**: Agents are highly susceptible to deceptive appeals (up to 60% sycophancy) yet nearly incorrigible (0–11% correction). What mechanisms (e.g., evidence‑anchoring, attention bias) can counterbalance social pressure?
4. **Autonomous recognition of insufficient information**: The Navigation–Discovery Gap indicates agents do not proactively trigger deep search based on uncertainty. Can uncertainty‑driven exploration (e.g., ensemble disagreement) initiate investigation without external hints?

## Limitations
- **Domain generalizability**: The benchmark covers only e‑commerce and Reddit moderation; it is unclear whether the observed gaps persist in other high‑stakes domains like healthcare or finance.
- **Weak prior work overlap**: The literature corpus shows low citation overlap, making it difficult to situate PATHWAYS within existing research on web agents or epistemic reasoning.
- **Untested architectural proposals**: The suggested component fixes (e.g., Evidence Retriever, Uncertainty Monitor) are conceptual; no implemented system has validated their effectiveness on the benchmark.

## Confidence
- Low Investigation Accuracy & P<sub>Success</sub>: **High** (direct metrics across 250 tasks)
- Contradiction Collapse: **Medium** (correlation via hint degradation, causal mechanism unverified)
- Investigative Hallucination: **High** (34% rate with strong human validation)
- Sycophancy/Correction Asymmetry: **Medium** (experimental design defined, results pending)
- Proposed Architecture Fixes: **Low** (conceptual, no empirical tests)
