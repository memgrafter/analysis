# QTabGAN: A Hybrid Quantum-Classical GAN for Tabular Data Synthesis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12704  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12704  
- **Reference count:** 37  
- **Primary result:** QTabGAN achieves the lowest differences in ML utility and statistical similarity metrics compared to state-of-the-art classical and quantum baselines (up to 54.07% improvement), while using a fixed-size quantum circuit for scalability and NISQ-device efficiency.

## Executive Summary
Tabular data synthesis is crucial for privacy-preserving data sharing and addressing data scarcity, but classical GANs struggle with mixed data types and complex distributions. QTabGAN introduces a hybrid quantum-classical generative adversarial network where a variational quantum circuit (VQC) generator learns expressive probability distributions, a classical mapper transforms these into tabular samples, and a classical discriminator provides adversarial feedback. Evaluated on seven classification and regression datasets, QTabGAN outperforms both classical and quantum baselines, achieving superior ML utility (e.g., 2.16% accuracy difference) and statistical similarity (e.g., 0.028 average JSD). Its fixed-size VQC design offers better scalability and efficiency for near-term quantum devices compared to prior quantum tabular GANs.

## Method Summary
QTabGAN processes real tabular data through a preprocessing pipeline: clipping outliers, z-score normalization, min-max scaling to [0, π], and addition of small Gaussian noise for numerical features; categorical features are one-hot encoded. The hybrid generator consists of (1) an *n*-qubit VQC with *L* parameterized layers (Hadamard initialization, RY/RZ rotations, circular CNOT entanglement) that yields a quantum state; (2) a quantum sampler that measures the state repeatedly (shots) to estimate a probability vector **p**<sub>θ</sub> of length 2^*n*; (3) a classical neural mapper (CLMapper) that concatenates **p**<sub>θ</sub> with a one-hot class label *y* and maps this to a *d*-dimensional synthetic sample. A classical discriminator MLP receives a pair (sample, label) and outputs a real/fake score. Training follows the standard GAN minimax objective, with gradients for quantum parameters computed via the parameter-shift rule and classical updates by backpropagation (Adam optimizer). Conditional generation on class labels helps address imbalance and mode collapse.

## Key Results
- **ML utility:** On classification tasks, QTabGAN attains accuracy differences as low as 2.16% and F1-score differences of 0.048 versus real data, surpassing baselines.
- **Statistical similarity:** Achieves correlation differences of 0.35 and average Jensen-Shannon divergence of 0.028, indicating high fidelity in preserving feature relationships.
- **Scalability & efficiency:** The fixed-size VQC (8 qubits) enables generation of arbitrary feature counts and shows up to 54.07% improvement over prior quantum tabular GANs, with reduced circuit depth beneficial for NISQ devices.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Fixed-Size VQC Generates Full Probability Vector
The generator uses an *n*-qubit VQC that evolves in a 2^*n*-dimensional Hilbert space, producing a probability vector **p**<sub>θ</sub> of length 2^*n*. The classical mapper (CLMapper) then transforms [**p**<sub>θ</sub>; *y*] into a *d*-dimensional tabular sample, decoupling quantum resource needs (*n* fixed) from feature dimensionality (*d*). This allows scalability to high-dimensional data without increasing circuit size. The core assumption is that the CLMapper can learn a complex, non-linear mapping from the low-dimensional quantum probability space to the high-dimensional tabular manifold while preserving feature correlations. Evidence: The paper states the design “offers superior scalability and NISQ-device efficiency” and details the VQC architecture and CLMapper input (sections 3.1.1, 3.1.3). Break condition: Insufficient mapper capacity (e.g., too few layers) leads to poor statistical fidelity.

### Mechanism 2: Circular Entanglement Maximizes Expressivity per Gate
The VQC applies CNOT gates in a ring (circular entanglement), creating full entanglement across all qubits with only *n* CNOTs per layer. This maximizes quantum correlations while minimizing circuit depth, enhancing representational capacity for correlated states—crucial for modeling inter-feature dependencies in tabular data. Core assumption: The entanglement structure directly translates to the learned probability distribution **p**<sub>θ</sub> encoding higher-order feature interactions. Evidence: Section 3.1.1 explains that circular entanglement “enhances the VQC’s expressive power by maximizing quantum correlations, enhancing computational efficiency, and reducing circuit depth.” Section 5.3.2 attributes low correlation differences to entangling quantum circuits. Break condition: If data dependencies are not efficiently representable by this ansatz (e.g., require deeper circuits or different topology), the VQC may fail to capture them, degrading correlation metrics.

### Mechanism 3: Conditional Generation via Label Conditioning
The one-hot class label *y* is concatenated with **p**<sub>θ</sub> to condition the CLMapper’s output, forcing the generator to learn class-conditional distributions *p(x|y)*. During adversarial training, the discriminator receives (sample, *y*), providing class-specific feedback that guides the generator to produce diverse samples for each class, mitigating mode collapse and addressing class imbalance. Core assumption: Conditioning on *y* is sufficient to structure the latent space and prevent collapse to majority-class modes. Evidence: Section 3.1.4 explains that conditional generation “enables explicit control over the class of generated tabular samples… helps address class imbalance and reduces the risk of mode collapse.” Section 4.2 describes concatenating **p**<sub>θ</sub> with the one-hot label. Break condition: If the conditioning signal is insufficient or the CLMapper cannot disentangle class-specific from shared features, the generator may still collapse within classes or fail on minority classes.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Random noise (implicit) -> n-qubit VQC (RY, RZ, circular CNOT) -> Quantum Sampler (N shots) -> probability vector **p**<sub>θ</sub>; concatenate with one-hot label *y* -> Classical Mapper (MLP) -> synthetic tabular sample *x̂*. Classical Discriminator (MLP) receives (real *x*,*y*) or (*x̂*,*y*) and outputs score. Adversarial loop updates parameters.

### Critical Path
1. Preprocess real data: clip to 1st/99th percentiles, z-score normalize, min-max scale to [0, π], add Gaussian noise; one-hot encode categoricals.
2. For each training iteration:
   a. Forward pass VQC to obtain **p**<sub>θ</sub>.
   b. CLMapper maps [**p**<sub>θ</sub>; *y*] to *x̂*.
   c. Discriminator scores real (x,y) and synthetic (*x̂*,*y*).
   d. Compute discriminator and generator losses (binary cross-entropy).
   e. Compute gradients: parameter-shift for VQC θ, backprop for classical networks.
   f. Update all parameters (Adam).
3. Repeat until convergence (e.g., discriminator accuracy ~0.5) or fixed epochs.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Qubit count vs. Feature count:** QTabGAN uses fixed *n* (e.g., 8) independent of *d*, improving NISQ efficiency but relying on CLMapper for high-dim mapping; prior work scales *n* with *d*, limiting feature count.
- **Circuit depth vs. Noise:** Deeper VQC layers increase expressivity but accumulate gate errors; circular entanglement minimizes depth for full entanglement.
- **Quantum expressivity vs. Classical stability:** Hybrid approach leverages quantum for distribution learning and classical for stable mapping; a purely quantum generator might be less stable.

### Failure Signatures
- **Statistical fidelity issues:** High Average JSD or Correlation Difference indicates failure to match real data distribution.
- **ML utility drop:** Large absolute differences in downstream metrics (Accuracy, F1, R²) show synthetic data lacks predictive power.
- **Training instability:** Discriminator loss collapses to near-zero (too strong) or stays near log(0.5) (generator collapse); may signal poor VQC expressivity or unbalanced learning rates.
- **Class-specific degradation:** For imbalanced datasets, poor synthetic quality for minority classes visible via per-class metrics.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **VQC Probability Vector Entropy:** After a few epochs, compute Shannon entropy of **p**<sub>θ</sub>; it should be >0 and roughly scale with log₂(*d*). Near-zero entropy indicates circuit collapse.
2. **Mapper Capacity Test:** Train CLMapper in isolation to map known distributions (e.g., from simple data) to tabular features; failure suggests the MLP architecture is inadequate.
3. **Conditioning Efficacy:** Train without label conditioning (use only **p**<sub>θ</sub>) and compare per-class diversity (e.g., cluster metrics) against conditioned model; significant drop in minority-class diversity confirms conditioning’s role.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. *Impact of real quantum hardware noise:* All experiments were on simulators; executing QTabGAN on actual NISQ devices is needed to understand noise effects on sample quality.
2. *Computational expense of training:* The parameter-shift rule and multiple circuit evaluations make training expensive; more efficient optimization methods or circuit designs are required.
3. *Optimal balance between quantum generator complexity and classical discriminator capacity:* The current architecture uses fixed VQC depth; varying relative capacities could improve stability and prevent mode collapse.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter and architectural gaps: Exact VQC depth, CLMapper/discriminator sizes, learning rates, shot count, and optimizer settings are unspecified, hindering precise replication.
- Hardware validation missing: Claims of NISQ-device efficiency are based on simulation; real-device performance remains unverified.
- Statistical robustness: Results appear from single runs; absence of multi-seed variance or confidence intervals limits significance assessment.
