# Give Users the Wheel: Towards Promptable Recommendation Paradigm

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.18929
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18929
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: DPR (with SASRec) achieves up to 71.84% relative NDCG@10 improvement on ML1M for positive steering and 15.37% for negative suppression, while maintaining competitive standard sequential recommendation performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the Decoupled Promptable Sequential Recommendation (DPR) framework, which equips any sequential recommender backbone (e.g., SASRec, GRU4Rec) with the ability to follow explicit natural language user prompts. DPR modulates the user representation in retrieval space using a Fusion module with multi-head cross-attention to align semantic and collaborative signals, a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) to separately handle positive steering and negative suppression, and a three-stage training strategy for progressive semantic alignment. Extensive experiments on ML1M show that DPR substantially outperforms heuristic filtering and state-of-the-art LLM-based reranking/generative models on promptable tasks, without sacrificing performance on standard sequential recommendation. Ablation studies confirm the importance of the decoupled MoE and the curriculum training.

## Method Summary
DPR takes a sequential backbone (e.g., SASRec) and augments it with a prompt encoder (typically a pretrained language model) to process natural language prompts. The backbone produces a user embedding from the interaction sequence. The prompt embedding and user embedding are fed into a Fusion module that uses multi-head cross-attention to combine the semantic and collaborative signals into a fused representation. This fused representation is then processed by a Mixture-of-Experts comprising separate positive and negative experts and a gating network, which produces a modulation vector. This modulation is added to the original user embedding to obtain a prompt-conditioned user representation, which is used for retrieval (e.g., dot product with item embeddings). Training proceeds in three stages: first align the prompt encoder, then train the fusion module, and finally fine-tune the entire system end-to-end. This decoupled design resolves optimization conflicts between positive and negative intents and ensures robust controllability.

## Key Results
- On ML1M, DPR (SASRec) achieves a 71.84% relative NDCG@10 improvement over the strongest filter baseline for positive steering tasks and a 15.37% improvement for negative suppression.
- DPR significantly outperforms state-of-the-art LLM-based reranking and generative models across all promptable tasks.
- Standard sequential recommendation performance remains competitive with the vanilla backbone (SASRec).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DPR effectively enables promptable recommendation by decoupling positive and negative user intents through a Mixture-of-Experts, preventing gradient interference between opposing objectives. The Fusion module's multi-head cross-attention aligns the semantic meaning of the prompt with the collaborative user representation, creating a unified signal. The three-stage curriculum gradually introduces semantic alignment, stabilizing training and avoiding early collapse. By operating in the retrieval space (modulating user embeddings), DPR retains the efficiency of collaborative filtering while adding dynamic, language-driven control.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Backbone (SASRec/GRU4Rec) → User Embedding (u)  
Text Encoder (e.g., BERT) → Prompt Embedding (p)  
Fusion (Multi-head Cross-Attention) → Fused Representation (f)  
MoE (Gating + Positive Expert + Negative Expert) → Modulation (m)  
Final User Representation: u' = u + m  
Retrieval: Score(i) = ⟨u', item_i⟩ → Ranking

**Critical path**: Sequence and natural language prompt → backbone and text encoder produce u and p → Fusion combines into f → MoE generates m → u' = u + m → similarity scores with item embeddings → ranked list.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Decoupled MoE isolates positive/negative intents but adds parameters and gating complexity.
- Three-stage training stabilizes semantic alignment but requires careful scheduling and more training time.
- Cross-attention fusion enables flexible alignment but introduces computational overhead.

**Failure signatures**:
- Ambiguous or contradictory prompts cause MoE gating to generate ineffective modulation.
- Poorly pretrained text encoder leads to misalignment in Fusion.
- Skipping curriculum steps results in training instability or poor convergence.

**Exactly 3 first experiments**:
1. Ablate the Fusion module (e.g., replace with concatenation) to measure its contribution.
2. Replace decoupled MoE with a shared expert to assess the benefit of separation.
3. Measure inference latency and memory consumption of DPR vs. vanilla backbone across batch sizes to quantify overhead.

## Limitations
- Evaluation solely on ML1M; unknown performance on other datasets or sparsity regimes.
- Only SASRec backbone tested; backbone-agnostic claim remains unsupported.
- Computational overhead of Fusion module and MoE not analyzed; deployment impact unclear.

## Confidence
- Significant improvement on promptable tasks: Medium
- Competitive standard sequential performance: High
- Necessity of decoupled MoE and three-stage curriculum: Medium
- Backbone-agnostic applicability: Low
