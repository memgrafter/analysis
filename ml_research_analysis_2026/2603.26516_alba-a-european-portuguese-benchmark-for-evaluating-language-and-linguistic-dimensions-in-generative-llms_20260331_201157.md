# ALBA: A European Portuguese Benchmark for Evaluating Language and Linguistic Dimensions in Generative LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26516  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26516  
- **Reference count:** 18  
- **Primary result:** ALBA introduces a linguist-crafted pt-PT benchmark and LLM-as-a-judge framework; closed-source models lead (GPT-5: 91.0), open models lag (Gemma 3-12B: 51.1).

## Executive Summary
ALBA addresses the severe under-representation of European Portuguese (pt-PT) in LLM evaluation. It provides a benchmark of 800 manually constructed tasks spanning eight linguistic dimensions—Language Variety, Culture-bound Semantics, Discourse Analysis, Word Plays, Syntax, Morphology, Lexicology, and Phonetics & Phonology. Paired with a rigorously validated LLM-as-a-judge framework (Gemini-2.5-Pro with pt-PT prompts and length-diverse 3-shot examples, achieving MAE 0.475 against human experts), the benchmark measures the generative quality of LLMs on pt-PT-specific nuances. Results show a stark performance gap: top closed-source models (GPT-5: 91.0, Gemini-2.5-Pro: 90.1) far outpace the best open model (Gemma 3-12B: 51.1). All models exhibit notable weaknesses in Phonetics & Phonology and Word Plays, underscoring the nuanced challenges of European Portuguese.

## Method Summary
The ALBA benchmark consists of 800 tasks, each authored by language experts (with Master’s degrees in linguistics) and reviewed by a second expert. Tasks require open-ended generation (e.g., creating a tongue twister with alliteration) rather than multiple choice, to probe multi-level linguistic competence. Evaluation employs an LLM-as-a-judge pipeline. The judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro) is prompted in pt-PT with a detailed rubric (1–5 scale) and three few-shot examples of varying quality. Calibration against 720 expert-rated responses yields a best-case MAE of 0.475. The evaluation workflow: (1) prepare judge prompt and few-shot examples from the provided validation set; (2) generate responses from target models for all 800 tasks; (3) score each response with the judge; (4) aggregate scores into 0–100 per dimension and overall. Key design decisions include pt-PT prompt language, length-diverse 3-shot selection, and greedy decoding.

## Key Results
- Closed-source models dominate: GPT-5 (91.0) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (90.1) obtain near-human-level scores.
- Open models significantly lag: the top open-weight model, Gemma 3-12B, scores 51.1.
- All tested models struggle particularly with Phonetics & Phonology and Word Plays, revealing gaps in pt-PT phonological and playful language handling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Manual creation of linguist-crafted tasks captures nuanced pt-PT-specific linguistic and cultural phenomena more effectively than machine-translated or generic benchmarks.
- **Mechanism:** Language experts with Master’s degrees in linguistics design 800 tasks across eight dimensions (e.g., Culture-bound Semantics, Phonetics & Phonology), ensuring each task targets fine-grained, variety-aware competencies. Tasks require open-ended generation (e.g., creating a tongue twister with alliteration) rather than binary choices, engaging multi-level language skills.
- **Core assumption:** Native-speaker linguistic expertise is necessary to identify and operationalize subtle pt-PT features (e.g., regionalisms, idiomatic expressions) that are lost in translation or overlooked by automated dataset construction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "ALBA is manually constructed by language experts... to assess LLM proficiency in linguistic-related tasks in pt-PT across eight linguistic dimensions."
  - [section 3.1] "All questions were created by two language experts... Each question was authored by one expert and reviewed by the other."
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., AMALIA, CLARIN-PT-LDB) similarly emphasize native-variety development, indicating a recognized gap for pt-PT-specific resources.
- **Break condition:** If expert-authored tasks fail to cover the full spectrum of pt-PT variation (e.g., neglecting insular dialects like Azorean Portuguese) or if inter-expert reliability is low, benchmark validity diminishes.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A rigorously validated LLM-as-a-judge framework enables scalable, consistent evaluation of open-ended pt-PT responses while aligning with human expert judgment.
- **Mechanism:** The judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro) is prompted in pt-PT with a detailed rubric (1–5 scale) and three few-shot examples of varying quality (correct, moderate, incorrect). Calibration against 720 expert-rated responses minimizes MAE (best: 0.475), ensuring scores reflect linguistic quality, precision, and completeness.
- **Core assumption:** A sufficiently capable LLM, guided by native-language prompts and expert-curated examples, can approximate expert evaluation of nuanced linguistic output, balancing scalability with reliability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Paired with a validated LLM-as-a-judge framework... it measures generative quality."
  - [section 4.1–4.3] Systematic testing of prompt language (pt-PT favored), few-shot strategies (Length-Diverse 3-shot optimal), and judge models (Gemini-2.5-Pro best MAE).
  - [corpus] LLM-as-a-judge is a known paradigm (Gu et al., 2024), but ALBA’s validation is pt-PT-specific; corpus shows other Portuguese benchmarks (e.g., GlórIA) do not detail such calibration.
- **Break condition:** If the judge exhibits systematic bias (e.g., penalizing regional variants) or fails to generalize beyond the validation set, evaluation reliability erodes. The paper notes potential bias from using the same model family as judge and evaluated model.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Explicit focus on European Portuguese (pt-PT) as a distinct variety, separate from Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR), mitigates model conflation and exposes variety-specific weaknesses.
- **Mechanism:** Dimensions like Language Variety and Culture-bound Semantics directly target pt-PT-exclusive lexical items, regional expressions, and cultural references (e.g., "Valeu, cara" → "Tchau, homem"). Evaluation reveals models default to pt-BR patterns, scoring lower on these dimensions (e.g., Gemma 3-12B: Language Variety 52.8 vs. Discourse Analysis 85.5).
- **Core assumption:** LLM training data over-represents pt-BR, causing models to produce pt-BR outputs even when prompted in pt-PT; benchmarking must therefore isolate and test pt-PT-specific knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "European Portuguese (pt-PT) is particularly affected... as existing training data and benchmarks are mainly in Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR)."
  - [section 3.2.1] "The existence of multiple varieties of Portuguese... makes it challenging to obtain an output from an LLM without the influence of pt-BR."
  - [corpus] Neighbors (e.g., "Enhancing Portuguese Variety Identification") confirm the pt-PT/pt-BR conflation problem, highlighting the need for variety-sensitive benchmarks.
- **Break condition:** If models are retrained on balanced pt-PT/pt-BR data and the performance gap closes, the mechanism’s necessity weakens; but current results show persistent lag in variety dimensions for open models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Linguistic dimensions as competency axes**  
  - *Why needed here:* ALBA partitions language into eight expert-defined branches (e.g., Morphology, Phonetics & Phonology). Understanding each dimension’s scope (e.g., Morphology: affixes, verb tenses; Word Plays: pangrams, anagrams) is essential to interpret why models fail in specific areas.  
  - *Quick check question:* What pt-PT-specific challenge might a model face in the "Phonetics & Phonology" dimension versus "Syntax"?
- **Concept: Varietal differences in Portuguese**  
  - *Why needed here:* Core to ALBA’s "Language Variety" and "Culture-bound Semantics" dimensions. Key differences include lexical items ("pequeno-almoço" vs. "café da manhã"), syntactic constructions (clitic placement), and culturally bound idioms.  
  - *Quick check question:* Give one example of a pt-PT regionalism (e.g., from Alentejo) that a model might confuse with a pt-BR equivalent.
- **Concept: LLM-as-a-judge calibration**  
  - *Why needed here:* The benchmark’s scalability depends on automated scoring. Understanding how few-shot examples, prompt language, and judge model selection affect alignment with human judgment (MAE) explains the trustworthiness of reported scores.  
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a pt-PT prompt outperform an English prompt for judging pt-PT responses?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Task Bank → Validation Suite → Judge Module → Evaluation Pipeline  

  - Task Bank: 800 expert-authored questions (100 per dimension) stored with pt-PT prompts and reference answers.  
  - Validation Suite: Subset of 240 expert-rated responses (30 questions × 3 quality tiers × 8 dimensions) used to tune judge configuration.  
  - Judge Module: Configuration file specifying judge model (Gemini-2.5-Pro), prompt template (pt-PT, rubric, 3-shot length-diverse examples), and decoding settings (greedy).  
  - Evaluation Pipeline: Script that sends model responses to the judge, aggregates scores (1–5 → 0–100), and computes per-dimension and overall metrics.

- **Critical path:**  
  Task design → Expert answer creation → Judge calibration (validation set) → Model inference → Judge scoring → Results aggregation. Judge calibration must precede large-scale evaluation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Manual task creation vs. scalability: Expert crafting ensures quality but limits benchmark size (800 tasks). Automated expansion risks losing pt-PT nuance.  
  - Judge model selection: Using a high-performing closed model (Gemini) improves reliability but reduces transparency and introduces potential API costs/bias. Open-weight judges were less reliable (Figure 3).  
  - Dimension granularity: Eight dimensions cover broad linguistics but may overlap (e.g., Lexicology vs. Morphology); tradeoff between comprehensiveness and parsimony.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Judge misalignment: MAE > 0.6 on validation set indicates poor calibration; check prompt language, few-shot diversity, or judge model capability.  
  - Model collapse in specific dimensions: Consistently low scores (< 20) in Phonetics & Phonology or Word Plays suggest tokenization issues (subword vs. character-level) or insufficient pt-PT phonological training.  
  - Variety conflation: High Language Variety error rate (e.g., mistaking pt-BR terms for pt-PT) indicates model lacks variety discrimination; inspect training data or fine-tuning recipes.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Judge Calibration Replication: Run the judge on the 240 validation items with all candidate configurations (prompt language, few-shot strategies) and reproduce MAE rankings (Figure 2) to validate setup.  
  2. Dimension Ablation: Evaluate a baseline model (e.g., Gemma 3-12B) on each dimension separately to confirm expected weakness patterns (low Phonetics & Phonology, high Discourse Analysis) as in Table 3.  
  3. Prompt Sensitivity Test: For a fixed model, vary judge prompt phrasing (e.g., remove rubric, change few-shot examples) to measure score stability; acceptable variance should be < 0.3 MAE.

## Limitations
- Exact judge prompt text and few-shot examples are not publicly released, hindering exact replication.  
- Access details for Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-5 (versions, endpoints) are unspecified and may require restricted APIs.  
- Potential judge bias from using the same model family (Gemini) as both evaluator and evaluated is noted but not quantified.

## Confidence
- Benchmark construction: High  
- LLM-as-a-judge validity: Medium  
- Model performance rankings: Medium  

## Next Checks
1. Judge calibration replication: Run the published judge configuration on the 720 expert-rated validation responses. Target MAE ≤0.475; higher error indicates prompt/few-shot mismatch.
2. Bias audit: Collect human expert scores for 100 random ALBA responses and compare with Gemini-2.5-Pro judge. Also evaluate with a different judge (e.g., GPT-4) to test consistency.
3. Variety conflation test: Administer parallel pt-BR prompts to top models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro). Measure output divergence from pt-PT norms in Language Variety and Culture-bound Semantics dimensions to quantify variety leakage.
