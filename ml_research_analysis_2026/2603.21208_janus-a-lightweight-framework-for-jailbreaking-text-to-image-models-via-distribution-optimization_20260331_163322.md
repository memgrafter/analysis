# JANUS: A Lightweight Framework for Jailbreaking Text-to-Image Models via Distribution Optimization
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21208
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21208
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art attack success rate (ASR-8) on Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large Turbo, improving from 25.30% to 43.15%, while maintaining higher semantic (CLIP) and harmfulness (NSFW) scores than baselines.

## Executive Summary
JANUS is a novel, lightweight framework for jailbreaking text-to-image (T2I) models without relying on large language models (LLMs). It formulates the attack as optimizing a parametric prompt distribution that is a convex mixture of two anchored distributions: one centered on a target unsafe prompt and one on a manually crafted clean prompt sharing its core semantics. Using a policy gradient algorithm, JANUS directly maximizes a black-box reward derived from the T2I system’s safety filters and an NSFW image scorer by adjusting only a single mixing coefficient. This approach yields superior attack success across open-source and commercial T2I models, exposing fundamental vulnerabilities in filter-based safety pipelines.

## Method Summary
JANUS operates in two stages. **Stage 1** constructs two token-level Gaussian distributions. For a given target NSFW prompt \(p_t\) and a clean prompt \(p_c\) (created by removing NSFW words), it learns parameters \((\mu_t, \sigma_t)\) and \((\mu_c, \sigma_c)\) such that samples from each distribution have expected embeddings close to \(p_t\) and \(p_c\), respectively. **Stage 2** defines the attack distribution \(p_\alpha = \alpha N_t + (1-\alpha)N_c\) and optimizes the scalar \(\alpha\) via REINFORCE. For each sampled prompt \(p \sim p_\alpha\), the T2I model \(M\) and safety classifier \(C\) provide a reward \(R(p) = C(p, M(p)) \cdot S(M(p)) - \log p_\alpha(p)\), where \(S\) is an NSFW score. The gradient \(\nabla_\alpha \log p_\alpha(p)\) is computed analytically from the fixed base distributions, avoiding backpropagation through the black-box system.

## Key Results
- On Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large Turbo, JANUS improves ASR-8 from 25.30% (prior SOTA) to 43.15%.
- It achieves higher CLIP similarity (0.37 vs. ~0.30) and NSFW scores (0.33 vs. ~0.25) compared to baselines.
- The method generalizes effectively to both open-source (SDXL) and commercial (DALL·E 3, Midjourney) models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual-Anchor Semantic Stability
- **Claim:** The convex mixture of two distributions, anchored to semantically similar target and clean prompts, structurally preserves the core intent of the target during optimization.
- **Mechanism:** The expected semantic similarity (e.g., CLIP) of any sample from \(p_\alpha\) to \(p_t\) is mathematically bounded below by the weaker of the two anchored distributions. The shared core semantics of \(p_t\) and \(p_c\) ensure constructive interference, preventing drift.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 3.3] Formal proof of the lower bound (Equation 8).
  - [section 3.3] Statement that "shared core semantics... constructively interfere."
- **Break condition:** If \(p_c\) shares minimal core semantics with \(p_t\) (e.g., different subjects), the interference fails and semantic fidelity degrades.

### Mechanism 2: Lightweight Policy Gradient for Black-Box Optimization
- **Claim:** Optimizing only the mixing coefficient \(\alpha\) via policy gradient efficiently navigates the trade-off between filter evasion and NSFW generation without requiring T2I model differentiability.
- **Mechanism:** The REINFORCE-style gradient (Equation 11) depends solely on the ratio \(N_t(p)/N_c(p)\), which is cheap to compute from the fixed Gaussians. Rewards come from querying the black-box T2I system and its filters.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.4] Derivation of the gradient estimator.
  - [section 4.3] Ablation shows a fixed \(\alpha\) or static NSFW reward underperforms.
- **Break condition:** High stochasticism or ambiguity in the reward signal (from \(C\) or \(S\)) leads to noisy gradients and poor convergence of \(\alpha\).

### Mechanism 3: Gaussian Relaxation with Semantic Anchoring
- **Claim:** Relaxing discrete token selection into per-token Gaussian distributions (with means anchored to valid token embeddings) creates a differentiable, low-dimensional search space that controls semantic drift during discretization.
- **Mechanism:** Each one-hot token row is replaced by \(\delta_{\theta,i} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\theta,i}, \text{diag}(\sigma^2_{\theta,i}))\), where \(\mu\) is initialized at the target token’s embedding. Stage 1 training minimizes the cosine loss between expected sample embeddings and anchor embeddings.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] Description of the Dirac-style relaxation (Equation 5).
  - [appendix B.8] Error bound analysis showing anchoring minimizes discretization error.
- **Break condition:** Excessive variance \(\sigma\) during sampling produces continuous embeddings far from the valid token manifold, causing semantically unrelated discrete prompts after `argmax`.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Distribution Optimization (vs. Instance Search)**
  - **Why needed:** Framing jailbreak as optimizing a distribution (rather than a single prompt) allows balancing multiple objectives (semantics, evasion, harmfulness) by shaping probability mass, improving robustness.
  - **Quick check:** How does a mixture model’s explicit probability structure help manage trade-offs compared to hill-climbing on a single adversarial prompt?
- **Concept: Policy Gradient for Black-Box Optimization**
  - **Why needed:** The T2I model and safety filters are non-differentiable black boxes. Policy gradient (REINFORCE) uses reward queries and an analytically computable score function gradient to update the policy parameter \(\alpha\).
  - **Quick check:** Why is backpropagation impossible here, and how does the ratio \(N_t(p)/N_c(p)\) in the gradient estimator circumvent the black-box barrier?
- **Concept: Semantic Anchoring via Clean/NSFW Prompt Pair**
  - **Why needed:** The clean prompt \(p_c\) provides an anchor that shares core semantics with \(p_t\) but lacks explicit NSFW tokens. This forces the mixture distribution to stay on-topic while interpolating toward unsafe content.
  - **Quick check:** What happens if \(p_c\) is semantically unrelated (e.g., "a happy dog") to \(p_t\)? How does that affect the expected semantic similarity bound?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Anchor Construction (Stage 1) -> Policy Optimizer (Stage 2) -> Adversarial Prompt Generation
- **Critical path:**
  1. Input target NSFW prompt \(p_t\).
  2. Manually construct clean prompt \(p_c\) by removing NSFW words.
  3. **Stage 1:** Train anchored Gaussians \(N_t\) and \(N_c\) to stay semantically close to \(p_t\) and \(p_c\) via cosine loss minimization.
  4. Initialize mixing coefficient \(\alpha\).
  5. **Stage 2:** Iteratively sample \(p \sim p_\alpha\), query T2I model \(M\) and safety classifier \(C\), compute reward \(R(p)\), estimate \(\nabla_\alpha\) via REINFORCE, and update \(\alpha\).
  6. After convergence, sample final adversarial prompts using learned \(\alpha\).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **LLM-Free vs. Generator Capacity:** Uses a single scalar \(\alpha\) instead of a full LLM generator, reducing cost but potentially limiting expressiveness.
  - **Semantic Guarantee vs. Exploration:** Dual anchors provide a fidelity guarantee but may restrict exploration compared to a unimodal, high-variance distribution.
  - **Query Efficiency vs. Simplicity:** More query-efficient than random search or token-level GCG, but less sample-efficient than a pre-trained generative model.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High TASR, Low IASR/ASR:** Bypasses text filter but images are benign or fail image filter → \(\alpha\) or reward \(S\) mis-specified.
  - **Low TASR:** Fails text filter → \(\alpha\) too high or \(N_t\) not semantically exploitable.
  - **High CLIP, Low NSFW:** On-topic but harmless → \(\alpha\) too low or weak \(S\) signal.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Comparison:** Run JANUS and all listed baselines (MMA, MMP, QFA, PGJ, SneakyPrompt) on 200 target prompts for SD3.5LT, DALL·E3, SDXL, Midjourney. **Success metric:** Statistically higher ASR-8 and/or NSFW score.
  2. **Ablation Study:** Compare full JANUS against (a) unimodal \(N_t\) only, (b) fixed NSFW reward. **Success metric:** Full process outperforms, confirming necessity of dual anchors, dynamic \(\alpha\), and dynamic reward.
  3. **α Sensitivity Analysis:** Evaluate performance for fixed \(\alpha \in \{0.0, 0.5, 1.0\}\) vs. learned \(\alpha\). **Success metric:** Learned \(\alpha\) achieves superior Pareto balance (high ASR *and* high NSFW).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What concrete distribution-aware defenses can mitigate the vulnerabilities exposed by JANUS?** The paper concludes that findings "expose structural weaknesses... and motivate stronger, distribution-aware defenses" but proposes no specific countermeasures.
2. **How sensitive is JANUS to the semantic similarity between the target prompt and the clean anchor?** Performance may degrade if the clean prompt diverges in meaning, but this robustness is unexplored.
3. **Would more expressive base distributions than diagonal Gaussians improve JANUS's efficiency or effectiveness?** The Gaussian assumption may limit coverage of the true jailbreak manifold.
4. **How robust is JANUS to variations in the reward function, particularly the safety classifier and NSFW scorer?** No sensitivity analysis to reward noise or classifier errors is provided.
5. **Can JANUS jailbreak T2I models with advanced safety mechanisms beyond prompt/image filters?** Models with end-to-end safety training or guidance-time interventions were not tested.

## Limitations
- **Unknown clean prompt generation:** The exact NSFW lexicon and removal algorithm for constructing \(p_c\) are unspecified, directly impacting the core semantic anchoring assumption.
- **Opaque reward computation for APIs:** For commercial models (DALL·E3, Midjourney), the precise definitions of bypass signal \(C(p)\) and NSFW score \(S(M(p))\) are undocumented, hindering replication.
- **Unreported hyperparameters:** Stage 1 training iterations \(T\), Gumbel-Softmax schedule, and Stage 2 learning rate/baseline details are missing, making efficiency claims and exact performance difficult to verify.
