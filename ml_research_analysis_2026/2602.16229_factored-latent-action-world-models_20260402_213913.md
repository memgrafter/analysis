# Factored Latent Action World Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.16229
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16229
- **Reference count**: 27
- **Primary result**: FLAM factorizes scenes into K slots with shared dynamics, achieving superior video prediction (PSNR 34.9, SSIM 0.890, LPIPS 0.051, FVD 419.6) and higher DCI scores, enabling sample-efficient behavior cloning from 10k frames (Bigfish return 8.8 vs BC 3.6).

## Executive Summary
FLAM (Factored Latent Action World Model) tackles multi-entity video world modeling by decomposing a scene into K independent slots. Each slot infers its own latent action via a shared inverse dynamics model and predicts its next state via a shared forward dynamics model. This factorization reduces the joint action complexity relative to a single global latent action, improving scalability and sample efficiency. Evaluated on MultiGrid, Procgen (Bigfish, Leaper, Starpilot), and nuPlan, FLAM significantly outperforms prior latent action world models such as Genie and AdaWorld across standard metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD). Moreover, FLAM achieves higher disentanglement scores (DCI) than object‑centric methods like PlaySlot, confirming that the factorization yields action‑driven, interpretable slots. With only 10k labeled frames, pseudo‑labeled data from FLAM improves behavior cloning policies, achieving a return of 8.8 in Bigfish versus 3.6 for vanilla BC. These results demonstrate FLAM’s effectiveness for video prediction and downstream policy learning.

## Method Summary
FLAM introduces a factored latent action approach for video world modeling. The model processes a video frame through an encoder to produce K slot representations, each capturing a distinct scene entity. Given two consecutive frames, the encoder yields slot vectors for time t and t+1. A shared inverse dynamics module takes the concatenated current and next slot vectors (per slot) and infers a latent action for each slot. A shared forward dynamics module then consumes the current slot and its inferred latent action to predict the next slot representation. The predicted next slots are decoded back to an image frame. The entire system is trained end‑to‑end with a combination of reconstruction loss and potentially auxiliary losses to encourage slot independence. By sharing parameters across slots, FLAM reduces the effective size of the action space and improves sample efficiency while maintaining the ability to model complex multi‑entity dynamics.

## Key Results
- FLAM outperforms Genie and AdaWorld on video prediction: PSNR 34.9 vs 28.1 (Genie), SSIM 0.890 vs 0.862, LPIPS 0.051 vs 0.096, FVD 419.6 vs 749.5.
- Disentanglement scores (DCI: disentanglement 0.91, completeness 0.91, informativeness 0.93) surpass PlaySlot (0.81/0.81/0.86).
- With merely 10k labeled frames, pseudo‑labeled data from FLAM yields behavior cloning policies achieving Bigfish return 8.8 versus vanilla BC’s 3.6.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FLAM’s factorization into independent slots reduces the dimensionality of the latent action space from a single large vector to K smaller vectors, each acting on a single slot. This alleviates the curse of dimensionality and simplifies learning. The shared inverse and forward dynamics enforce that each slot follows a similar transition function, which regularizes the model and allows it to learn from fewer examples. Moreover, because each slot’s latent action is inferred directly from the transition of that slot, the model is driven to align slots with entities that have coherent dynamics, naturally yielding disentangled representations. The action‑driven factorization thus combines the benefits of object‑centric representations with the expressivity of latent actions, leading to improved prediction and downstream policy learning.

## Foundational Learning
- **World Models**: Learn predictive models of environment dynamics from raw observations, enabling planning and control without real interaction. Why needed: Provide a simulator for policy learning and evaluation. Quick check: Given a sequence of actions, the model can generate plausible future frames.
- **Latent Actions**: Compress high‑dimensional, potentially structured actions into a continuous low‑dimensional representation. Why needed: Simplify the action space for model‑based RL and enable learning from limited data. Quick check: Latent actions can be used to reproduce the effects of original actions in the environment.
- **Slot‑based Representations**: Decompose a scene into a set of latent vectors (slots), each representing an entity or object. Why needed: Handle multi‑entity interactions by isolating entities, reducing entanglement. Quick check: Each slot can be decoded into a coherent object mask or image region, and slots do not overlap.
- **Inverse/Forward Dynamics**: Inverse dynamics predicts the action given a state transition; forward dynamics predicts the next state given an action. Why needed: Essential for model‑based planning, imitation learning, and world model construction. Quick check: Trained dynamics models achieve high accuracy in predicting next states or actions on held‑out transitions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Input (Frame_t, Frame_{t+1}) -> Encoder -> Slots_t, Slots_{t+1} -> Shared Inverse Dynamics (per slot) -> Actions_t -> Shared Forward Dynamics (per slot) -> Predicted_Slots_{t+1} -> Decoder -> Predicted_Frame_{t+1}

**Critical Path**: The training loop samples consecutive frames, encodes them into slot vectors, applies the shared inverse dynamics to infer per‑slot latent actions, uses the forward dynamics to predict the next frame’s slots, decodes these to an image, and computes reconstruction loss. Backpropagation updates all modules jointly. For inference, the model can generate future frames by repeatedly applying forward dynamics to current slots.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Shared dynamics reduce parameter count and improve sample efficiency but may limit slot‑specific transition behaviors.  
- Factorization ignores inter‑slot interactions, which could be crucial for certain environments.  
- The number of slots K controls representation granularity versus computational cost; larger K increases capacity but also overhead.  
- Latent action dimensionality is another knob affecting expressivity and training stability.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- High reconstruction error (e.g., PSNR, LPIPS) indicating poor predictive performance.  
- Low DCI scores, suggesting lack of disentanglement.  
- Slot collapse where many slots become identical or vacant.  
- Inconsistent latent actions from inverse dynamics over similar transitions.  
- Downstream policies (e.g., behavior cloning) fail to improve over baselines.

## Limitations
- Domain limitation: Experiments only in simulated environments (MultiGrid, Procgen, nuPlan); real‑world transfer not demonstrated.
- Statistical rigor: No multi‑seed runs or significance tests; robustness of reported metrics uncertain.
- Baseline coverage: Excludes recent latent action methods (e.g., AMPLIFY), so relative advantage may shrink.
