# NERO-Net: A Neuroevolutionary Approach for the Design of Adversarially Robust CNNs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25517
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25517
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Evolved a CNN with 87% clean accuracy and 33% FGSM robustness without any adversarial training, extending to 93% clean and 47% FGSM after standard training, and 40% against AutoAttack after adversarial training, while also exhibiting unexpected L2 robustness.

## Executive Summary
NERO-Net introduces a neuroevolutionary framework to automatically design CNN architectures with intrinsic adversarial robustness. It uniquely isolates the contribution of architecture by excluding adversarial training during the evolutionary search, instead optimizing a fitness function that combines clean accuracy and FGSM-based adversarial accuracy. This approach discovered architectures that, even with only standard training, exhibit meaningful robustness. Notably, models optimized solely for an L∞ threat model demonstrated significant robustness against L2-bounded attacks, suggesting emergent cross-threat properties from the flexible search space.

## Method Summary
NERO-Net extends the Fast-DENSER neuroevolutionary algorithm. It uses a two-level genotype: an outer sequence of modules (stem, features, transition, classification) and an inner grammar defining evolvable layer blocks (`conv-block`, `pool-block`) with parameters like filter counts and activation/batch-norm positions. A key modification is a flexible connectivity graph allowing layers to skip to any predecessor within a look-back window (depth 5 for features), enabling complex topologies. The fitness function `Fβ` (β=4) is a weighted harmonic mean of clean accuracy (C) and FGSM adversarial accuracy (A) on correctly classified samples. An adaptive warm-up phase uses only C until the population mean exceeds τ=0.80, after which `Fβ` is applied. Evolution uses a (1+λ) strategy with structural and parameter mutations, training each candidate with standard SGD for a capped time (10-30 min). The fittest architecture is later retrained with standard and optionally adversarial training for final evaluation.

## Key Results
- Without adversarial training during search, the fittest individual achieved 87% clean accuracy and 33% FGSM (L∞, ε=8/255) robustness.
- After extended standard training, the same architecture improved to 93% clean and 47% FGSM robustness.
- Following adversarial training, it reached 40% robustness against the strong AutoAttack (L∞).
- It exhibited unexpected L2 robustness, achieving 58% accuracy under FGM (L∞) and 45.8% under PGD20 (L2, ε=0.5), despite being optimized only for L∞.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Fitness-Driven Intrinsic Robustness Discovery
- **Claim:** NERO-Net discovers architectures with intrinsic robustness by co-optimizing clean and FGSM adversarial accuracy during evolution, without adversarial training.
- **Mechanism:** The `Fβ` fitness function explicitly balances clean accuracy (C) and FGSM accuracy (A) with a high weight (β=4). Standard training is used exclusively, ensuring any measured robustness is an architectural property. The evolutionary search is thus directed toward topologies that maintain performance while resisting simple L∞-bounded FGSM attacks.
- **Core assumption:** Architectures possess an inherent capacity to resist perturbations that is independent of the specific training procedure.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...isolating architectural influence on robustness by avoiding adversarial training during the evolutionary loop..." [section 4.2] "individuals are optimized via standard training procedures... our quality assessment uses Fβ..."
- **Break condition:** If the fitness landscape flattens (all candidates ~0% FGSM accuracy) after warm-up, selective pressure is lost and evolution cannot differentiate intrinsic robustness.

### Mechanism 2: Adaptive Warm-up Prevents Degenerate Solutions
- **Claim:** A warm-up period initially optimizing only for clean accuracy prevents premature convergence to "robustness trap" solutions (e.g., single-class predictors with artificially high adversarial accuracy).
- **Mechanism:** Evolution starts with a fitness based solely on clean accuracy until the population mean exceeds τ=0.80. Only then is the composite `Fβ` metric introduced, allowing viable, high-performing architectures to emerge before the adversarial constraint is applied.
- **Core assumption:** The initial seeded population can evolve toward high clean accuracy before the adversarial objective introduces a deceptive gradient toward trivial solutions.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.2] "To facilitate the emergence of viable architectures, our approach incorporates a warm-up phase..." [section 5.2] "The fitness of the best individual usually drops significantly right after the transition... mainly due to the low adversarial accuracy..."
- **Break condition:** If τ is too low, population may contain many low-accuracy architectures when robustness is introduced, causing collapse. If too high, search may overfit clean accuracy patterns not conducive to robustness.

### Mechanism 3: Flexible Topology Enables Emergent Multi-Threat Robustness
- **Claim:** A flexible genotypic representation with evolvable layer blocks and unrestricted skip connections expands the search space to discover non-standard topologies that exhibit unexpected multi-threat robustness.
- **Mechanism:** The grammar defines `conv-block` and `pool-block` units grouping common layer sequences. Connectivity allows layers to reference any predecessor within a "look-back" depth (5 for features), enabling complex skip connections across macro-blocks (e.g., DenseNet-like patterns). This expressivity allows the evolution of topologies where robustness may emerge as a side-effect of other representational properties.
- **Core assumption:** Greater topological flexibility can reveal architectural motifs that confer robustness indirectly.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.3] "We introduce new non-terminal symbols (convblock and poolblock) ... allowing for skip connections to occur..." [section 6.2] "Remarkably, the discovered architecture exhibits multi-threat robustness, resisting L2-bounded perturbations despite being optimized solely for an L∞ threat model."
- **Break condition:** If grammar/connectivity rules are too permissive, phenotype generation may fail frequently or search becomes intractable within budget.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Neuroevolution for Neural Architecture Search (NAS)**
  - *Why needed here:* NERO-Net is built on Fast-DENSER, an evolutionary algorithm. Understanding genotype-phenotype mapping, variation operators, and selection is key to grasping how architectures are iteratively modified and evaluated.
  - *Quick check:* How does a mutation in the genotype (e.g., adding a connection) translate to a change in the CNN's computational graph and its training dynamics?
- **Concept: Adversarial Robustness Fundamentals**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire fitness function and evaluation hinge on threat models (L∞, ε=8/255), attack methods (FGSM, PGD, AutoAttack), and metrics (adversarial accuracy). Misunderstanding these leads to misinterpreting results.
  - *Quick check:* Why is FGSM used as a robustness *estimator* during search instead of a stronger attack like AutoAttack?
- **Concept: Multi-Objective Optimization & Fitness Design**
  - *Why needed here:* The core innovation is the `Fβ` fitness function trading off clean vs. adversarial accuracy. Understanding weighted harmonic means, Pareto fronts, and the warm-up strategy for navigating deceptive objectives is key.
  - *Quick check:* What is the practical effect of changing β from 1 to 4 in `Fβ`, and why was 4 chosen?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Genotype (two-level hierarchy) → Phenotype (CNN with blocks & skips) → Standard Training (SGD, fixed cap) → Evaluation (Clean & FGSM on holdout) → Fitness (`Fβ` post warm-up) → Selection/Mutation (structural, parameter) → Next Generation
- **Critical path:** The evolutionary loop runs for 100 generations with (1+λ) selection. A stateful branch checks if population mean fitness ≥ τ=0.80 to switch from clean-only to `Fβ` fitness. Invalid individuals (NaN/inf loss, single-class domination) are penalized.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Search Space Size vs. Discoverability:* Block-based grammar and skip connections increase expressivity but enlarge the space, requiring more evaluations. Mitigated by seeding with NSGA-Net and capping training time.
  - *Fidelity vs. Speed:* FGSM is a fast, weak proxy. Using it allows the loop to run but may bias search toward architectures robust only to this specific one-step pattern.
  - *Intrinsic Robustness vs. Final Performance:* The evolved architecture is intrinsically robust but not adversarially trained. The final deployed model requires a separate adversarial training phase, where hyperparameters may need re-tuning.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Evolutionary stagnation: Best fitness plateaus near warm-up threshold (τ=0.80) with no adversarial accuracy gain.
  - Ill-fitted individuals surge: Many flagged due to single-class prediction or unstable training, suggesting a "robustness trap" if warm-up is misconfigured.
  - Post-search degradation: Extended standard training degrades metrics (unlike paper), indicating overfitting to short training regime or fitness subset.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce fitness trajectory:** Log best individual's clean accuracy, FGSM accuracy, and `Fβ` per generation. Verify warm-up transition causes a fitness drop and subsequent recovery in adversarial accuracy.
  2. **Ablate warm-up period:** Set τ=1.0 (always `Fβ`) and τ=0.0 (always C). Compare final `Fβ`, clean accuracy, and rate of ill-fitted flags to validate warm-up's role.
  3. **Analyze evolved topology:** Visualize connectivity graph of best individual. Count skip connections and look-back depth. Correlate with final FGSM accuracy to test flexible topology hypothesis.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Do architectures evolved under standard training for intrinsic robustness remain optimal when further trained with adversarial training?
  - *Basis in paper:* The authors state it remains unclear whether the fittest model in a standard setting corresponds to the fittest under adversarial training.
  - *Why unresolved:* The fitness function optimizes for standard training; without evaluating population rankings under adversarial training, the generalizability of the "intrinsic robustness" claim is uncertain.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Re-evaluate the evolutionary population or Pareto front under both standard and adversarial training. Compare individual rankings using correlation metrics (e.g., Spearman's rank). High correlation suggests consistent architectural preferences.
- **Open Question 2:** Why does adversarial accuracy show markedly higher volatility to small genotypic changes compared to clean accuracy, and can this be mitigated?
  - *Basis in paper:* Authors note "small genotypic changes lead to more unpredictable outcomes in terms of adversarial accuracy" and call for further analysis.
  - *Why unresolved:* This volatility creates a noisy fitness landscape, hindering search efficiency. Understanding its root cause (gradient sensitivity, feature invariance, etc.) is key to better search spaces and metrics.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Perform controlled sensitivity analysis on the fittest individual. Systematically apply individual mutations and measure changes in adversarial vs. clean accuracy. Inspect internal representations (Jacobian norms, feature consistency) to identify amplifying factors.
- **Open Question 3:** What specific architectural motifs or connectivity patterns in NERO-Net's evolved models confer intrinsic robustness, and how do they enable unexpected cross-norm robustness (L∞ optimization yielding L2 resistance)?
  - *Basis in paper:* Authors call for "a more in-depth analysis... of emergent architectural patterns" and highlight the unexplained "multi-threat robustness."
  - *Why unresolved:* Without identifying these patterns, the approach remains a black box. Understanding them could yield design principles for manual robust architectures and explain transferability across threat models.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Conduct ablation studies on the fittest individual, selectively removing hypothesized robust components (skip connections, block types). Evaluate under L∞ and L2 attacks. Perform population-level analysis: extract architectural features (depth, width, skip density) and correlate with robustness metrics across norms.

## Limitations
- Exact genotype-to-phenotype mapping for the new block-based grammar and skip-connection repair logic is unspecified, blocking faithful implementation.
- Precise definition and mapping of the NSGA-Net seed architecture to NERO-Net's genotype is unclear.
- Fitness function's reliance on fast-but-weak FGSM as a robustness proxy during search may bias results toward architectures robust only to that specific attack pattern.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Fβ search isolating architecture):** High – Well-defined in text; core premise is explicit and testable.
- **Mechanism 2 (Warm-up preventing traps):** Medium – Supported by observed fitness drops post-transition, but lacks systematic ablation.
- **Mechanism 3 (Flexible topology enabling cross-norm robustness):** Low – Emergent L2 robustness is noted but not causally linked to specific topological features in the analysis.
