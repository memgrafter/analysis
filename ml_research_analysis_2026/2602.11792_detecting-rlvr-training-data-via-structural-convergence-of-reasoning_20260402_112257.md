# Detecting RLVR Training Data via Structural Convergence of Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11792
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11792
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: Proposes Min-kNN Distance, a black-box detector for RLVR training data contamination, achieving average AUC of 0.70 (17% relative improvement over baselines).

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of detecting training data contamination in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). Conventional likelihood-based membership inference methods fail because RLVR optimizes model outputs via reward feedback rather than token likelihood. The core contribution is Min-kNN Distance, a black-box detector that samples multiple completions for a prompt and measures their structural convergence via the average of the k smallest pairwise edit distances. RLVR exposure causes seen prompts to yield more rigid and similar generations, producing lower Min-kNN scores. Extensive evaluation across multiple RLVR-trained reasoning models shows an average AUC of 0.70, a 17% relative improvement over the strongest baseline, and demonstrates robustness to paraphrasing, model distillation, and different RL algorithms while requiring only sampling access.

## Method Summary
Min-kNN Distance operates with black-box sampling access to the target RLVR-tuned model. For a given input prompt, the procedure samples *m* completions using a moderate temperature (default 0.7). It then computes all pairwise normalized Levenshtein edit distances between these completions, where distance is divided by the length of the longer sequence. For each completion, the nearest-neighbor distance is defined as the minimum distance to any other completion. These *m* nearest-neighbor distances are sorted, and the average of the *k* smallest values becomes the detection score. Lower scores indicate higher likelihood of RLVR exposure because reward optimization collapses the diversity of reasoning structures for seen prompts. The default hyperparameters are *m*=32 samples and *k*=10. Thresholding on a validation set yields binary membership predictions.

## Key Results
- Average AUC of 0.70 across multiple RLVR-trained reasoning models, with 17% relative gain over the strongest baseline.
- Robustness to paraphrasing (performance drop <0.05 AUC), model distillation, and different RL algorithms (GRPO vs DAPO).
- Requires only sampling access, making it a practical black-box detector.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Diversity collapse:** RLVR training induces a measurable collapse in reasoning trajectory diversity for seen prompts; reward maximization reinforces high-reward patterns, compressing the space of plausible completions into fewer structural modes, especially in symbolic logic. Evidence: diversity metrics (EAD, NLI, embedding) decline steadily over training.
- **Stable structural clusters:** For seen prompts, multiple sampled completions cluster into a small number of recurring structural patterns (2-4 modes). Unseen prompts yield more heterogeneous structures. Evidence: hierarchical clustering reveals tight co-occurrence groups; seen prompts have a higher proportion with ≤2 clusters (44.90% vs. 22.45%).
- **Min-kNN quantification:** The detector computes the average of the *k* smallest nearest-neighbor normalized edit distances. For seen prompts, many completion pairs are very similar, producing small distances; the *k*-smallest average focuses on this tight cluster core. Unseen prompts exhibit larger nearest-neighbor distances. The metric is robust across a wide range of *k* and benefits from higher sampling temperatures.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**  
  *Why needed:* The detection problem is specific to RLVR's reward-based, self-generative fine-tuning; RLVR optimizes full trajectories rather than token likelihoods, which renders traditional perplexity-based membership inference ineffective.  
  *Quick check:* How does RLVR's optimization objective (reward on final answer) differ fundamentally from pre-training (token likelihood), and why does this difference break conventional MIA methods?

- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning Diversity Collapse**  
  *Why needed:* Min-kNN is a direct operationalization of the observed collapse in reasoning diversity. Understanding diversity (lexical, logical, semantic) and its measurement (EAD, NLI, embedding similarity) is essential to grasp the core signal.  
  *Quick check:* What do the three diversity metrics (EAD, NLI-based, embedding-based) each capture about reasoning outputs, and why is a multi-metric analysis necessary?

- **Normalized Edit Distance as a Structural Similarity Metric**  
  *Why needed:* This is the core computation of Min-kNN; understanding edit distance, normalization, and its suitability under black-box constraints explains the design choice over semantic embeddings.  
  *Quick check:* Why use normalized Levenshtein distance between token sequences instead of cosine similarity between sentence embeddings to measure structural convergence, given the black-box constraint?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Prompt → Sampling Engine → *m* completions → Pairwise Distance Calculator → *m* × *m* normalized edit distance matrix → k‑NN Aggregator → *k* smallest nearest‑neighbor distances → Scoring Module → Min‑kNN score → Thresholding/Decision → Membership prediction.

**Critical path:** The computational flow proceeds through sampling, pairwise distance computation (O(*m*²)), extraction of per‑completion nearest‑neighbor distances, selection and averaging of the *k* smallest, and score comparison. The main bottlenecks are the sampling step and the quadratic distance calculation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *k* (number of nearest neighbors): Smaller *k* (e.g., 1) increases sensitivity to outliers; larger *k* averages over more pairs but may dilute the tightest cluster signal. Performance is robust across a wide range (default *k*=10).  
- *m* (samples per prompt): More samples better capture the true cluster structure but increase computational cost quadratically. Performance plateaus after a certain point (*m*=32 default).  
- Temperature: Higher sampling temperature enhances output variability, amplifying the collapse signal; too high may introduce unrelated noise.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High variance in Min‑kNN scores for the same prompt across random seeds, indicating unstable clustering.
- AUC near 0.5 on validation set, suggesting no separation between seen/unseen distributions.
- Severe performance drop on paraphrased prompts, indicating overfitting to surface form rather than structure.
- Extreme sensitivity to decoding parameters (top_p, max_tokens) that alter output length/distribution without affecting the RLVR collapse.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Hyperparameter Sweep:** On a held‑out validation set with known labels, vary *k* (1,5,10,20,50) and *m* (8,16,32,64) to identify a stable operating point where AUC plateaus. Log runtime vs AUC.
2. **Paraphrasing Robustness Check:** Apply a simple paraphraser (e.g., back‑translation) to a validation subset. Re‑compute AUC; a small drop (<0.05) is expected, while a large drop signals overfitting.
3. **Cross‑Algorithm Generalization Test:** Evaluate detection on a model trained with a different RL algorithm (e.g., GRPO vs DAPO) on non‑overlapping RL data. This tests the claimed robustness across algorithms.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Does RLVR always cause structural collapse across models and tasks?** The assumption that collapse is observable across models and tasks is not fully validated; only a few reasoning models and algorithms were tested. Resolving this would require testing Min‑kNN on diverse model families (e.g., Llama, T5) and domains (e.g., dialogue).
- **Can Min‑kNN's computational cost be lowered without losing accuracy?** The default uses 32 samples and full pairwise distances, with no exploration of trade‑offs. A systematic study of fewer samples or approximate distance metrics, measuring AUC vs. runtime, would address this.
- **Does structural collapse indicate harmful over‑specialization and poor generalization?** The paper shows collapse but not its impact on generalization. Correlating Min‑kNN scores on training prompts with accuracy on out‑of‑distribution benchmarks would test if lower scores predict worse transfer.
- **How can RLVR contamination be detected amidst strong pretraining memorization?** Dual‑stage experiments show performance drops when pretraining contamination is high, indicating confounding. Combining Min‑kNN with pretraining detectors (e.g., perplexity) on data with known levels of both contaminations would help disentangle signals.

## Limitations
- Tokenization/normalization details for edit distance (whitespace, model‑specific tokens) are unspecified, potentially affecting reproducibility and cross‑model comparability.
- Optimal hyperparameters (*m*, temperature) may be dataset‑ and model‑specific; defaults are not theoretically guaranteed for all RLVR setups.
- The method relies on symbolic reasoning structure collapse, so applicability to non‑reasoning tasks or RLVR on non‑symbolic objectives is unknown and likely limited.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Diversity Collapse Detection):** Medium (strong empirical metrics but weak theoretical foundation and corpus support).
- **Mechanism 2 (Structural Clustering Identification):** High (directly supported by clustering analyses and cluster count statistics).
- **Mechanism 3 (Min‑kNN as Effective Detector):** Low‑to‑Medium (solid AUC but unvalidated choice of edit distance vs. semantic embeddings under black‑box constraint).

## Next Checks
1. **Tokenization Sensitivity Test:** Re‑run detection using three variants: model‑specific tokens, whitespace‑normalized text, and raw text with punctuation stripped. Measure AUC variance; high variance (>0.05) would indicate a critical undocumented dependency.
2. **Robustness to Sampling Parameters:** For multiple RLVR models, perform a grid search over *m* ∈ {8,16,32,64} and temperature ∈ {0.3,0.7,1.0}. Plot AUC heatmaps to check if a consistent “sweet spot” exists; lack of stability would undermine practical utility.
3. **Domain Transfer Failure Test:** Apply the detector to an RLVR model tuned on a non‑reasoning task (e.g., safety RL or stylistic control). Evaluate on RL training prompts vs. unseen prompts from the same domain. A drop to AUC ≈0.5 would confirm suspected domain confinement.
