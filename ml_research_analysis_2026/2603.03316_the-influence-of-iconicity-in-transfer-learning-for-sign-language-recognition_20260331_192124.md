# The Influence of Iconicity in Transfer Learning for Sign Language Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03316
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03316
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Iconic transfer learning between sign languages yields significant accuracy gains (up to +7.02%) when shared iconic concepts exist, but fails with very few shared concepts.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether iconicity—cross-linguistic similarity in sign gestures—improves transfer learning for low-resource sign language recognition. Using MediaPipe for keypoint extraction and an MLP-GRU model, the authors transfer knowledge between iconic sign subsets of Chinese→Arabic and Greek→Flemish. Results show that iconic transfer improves accuracy by +7.02% and +1.07% respectively, while non-iconic transfer mostly speeds convergence. An ablation confirms that positive transfer requires a sufficient number of shared iconic concepts. The study also finds that keypoint-based iconic transfer outperforms ImageNet RGB pre-training for one language pair but not the other, highlighting modality and language-pair dependencies.

## Method Summary
The authors construct iconic and non-iconic subsets of sign language datasets by mapping sign classes to shared iconic concepts (e.g., anatomy, sound). For each subset, they extract 3D hand and arm keypoints using MediaPipe Holistic, normalizing coordinates and filtering frames based on wrist position. A simple model comprising a single-layer MLP (spatial encoder) and GRU (temporal encoder) is trained. Transfer learning involves pre-training the source model on its subset, then initializing the target model's MLP with source weights while randomly initializing the GRU and classifier. The target model is fine-tuned on its full dataset. Baselines are trained from scratch. Hyperparameters are selected via grid search on baseline performance.

## Key Results
- Iconic transfer between Chinese Sign Language (CSL) and Kuwaiti Arabic Sign Language (KArSL) yields a +7.02% accuracy gain.
- Iconic transfer between Greek Sign Language (GSL) and Flemish Sign Language (VGT) yields a +1.07% accuracy gain.
- With only two shared iconic concepts (Iranian→French-Belgian), transfer becomes negative, underscoring the need for sufficient shared concepts.
- Compared to ImageNet RGB pre-training, iconic keypoint transfer is superior for CSL→KArSL (85.78% vs. 77.34%) but inferior for GSL→VGT (91.25% vs. 98.95%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Shared iconic concepts between source and target sign languages enable positive transfer learning by providing similar spatial keypoint patterns for the MLP layer to learn.
**Mechanism:** The MLP processes spatial keypoint coordinates. When signs share iconic meanings, their hand/arm movements occupy similar spatial regions and trajectories. Pre-training on such signs initializes the MLP weights to recognize these shared patterns, which are then fine-tuned for target-specific variations.
**Core assumption:** Iconicity directly correlates with low-level visual-spatial similarity in keypoint space across languages.
**Evidence anchors:** Accuracy gains (+7.02%, +1.07%) and Figure 3 showing spatial similarities; ablation with few concepts fails.
**Break condition:** Very low number of shared iconic concepts (e.g., 2) leads to negative transfer.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Non-iconic transfer provides a general optimization benefit via weight initialization, leading to faster convergence even without accuracy gains.
**Mechanism:** Pre-training on any set of signs initializes weights into a region closer to a good solution, improving optimization dynamics and reducing epochs to reach baseline performance.
**Core assumption:** Source task's data distribution produces useful feature extractors despite semantic misalignment.
**Evidence anchors:** Non-iconic transfer for GSL→VGT reached baseline at epoch 1815 vs. 2000; CSL non-iconic gave a +6.84% gain (slightly less than iconic).
**Break condition:** High dissimilarity in signing space or style may negate benefits.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The superiority of iconic keypoint-based transfer over ImageNet pre-training is modality-dependent and language-pair specific.
**Mechanism:** Keypoints abstract away irrelevant visual variation, focusing on movement structure. For pairs with shared kinematic structures (CSL→Arabic), this abstraction transfers well; for others (Greek→Flemish), ImageNet's RGB features may be more compatible.
**Core assumption:** Representational mismatch between ImageNet's RGB domain and keypoint domain is larger than mismatch between keypoint domains of similar iconic signs.
**Evidence anchors:** CSL→KArSL: keypoint transfer > ImageNet; GSL→VGT: ImageNet > keypoint transfer.
**Break condition:** Target language's signing style aligns better with natural image statistics than with abstracted keypoints.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Transfer Learning (Weight Initialization)**
  - *Why needed here:* The study relies on initializing target model weights from a source model's training. Understanding that transfer means saving learned weights and fine-tuning, not using a frozen model, is critical.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the difference between pre-training on ImageNet and pre-training on another sign language dataset in terms of weight initialization's semantic alignment?

- **Concept: Iconicity in Sign Linguistics**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's central thesis is that iconicity drives transfer success. Iconic signs visually resemble their meaning, creating cross-linguistic form similarities.
  - *Quick check question:* Why are facial keypoints excluded, and how does that relate to the study's focus on iconic hand/arm movements?

- **Concept: MediaPipe Holistic Landmarker & Keypoint Representation**
  - *Why needed here:* All models take normalized (x,y,z) coordinates from hands/arms. This defines the input space and its invariances (e.g., to signer body size).
  - *Quick check question:* How does using normalized keypoint coordinates instead of raw pixel frames affect what the model can learn and transfer?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** MediaPipe Holistic Keypoint Extraction -> MLP Spatial Encoder -> GRU Temporal Encoder -> Softmax Classifier
- **Critical path:** Keypoint extraction produces a sequence of spatial feature vectors via MLP, which are processed by GRU for temporal context, then classified. Transfer learning initializes the MLP from a source model.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Simplicity vs. capacity: MLP-GRU is simple, reducing overfitting for small datasets but lacks inductive biases like graph convolutions.
  - Keypoint vs. RGB: Keypoints reduce variance but lose texture/color cues; tradeoff evident in Section 4.4.
  - Shared iconic concepts: More shared concepts increase transfer gain but require manual identification.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Negative transfer: Accuracy drops below baseline (e.g., MedSLset→LSFB). Indicates too few shared iconic concepts.
  - No accuracy gain but faster convergence: Model reaches baseline sooner but not higher (e.g., GSL non-iconic). Indicates optimization benefit without semantic alignment.
  - Poor keypoint extraction: MediaPipe failures cause noisy inputs, harming all models.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline establishment:** Train target model from scratch with best hyperparameters; record baseline accuracy and convergence epoch.
  2. **Iconic Transfer:** Pre-train source on iconic subset, initialize target MLP with source weights, fine-tune on target; compare to baseline.
  3. **Ablation - Non-Iconic & Combined Transfer:** Repeat Step 2 using non-iconic source subset and combined source data; observe changes in gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What is the minimum number of shared iconic concepts required for positive transfer?**  
   *Basis:* Ablation with only 2 shared concepts resulted in negative transfer, while pairs with 3-5 showed positive or neutral.  
   *Why unresolved:* Only one pair with ≤2 concepts tested; threshold and intermediate outcomes unknown.  
   *What evidence:* Systematic experiments across multiple language pairs with varying numbers of shared iconic concepts.

2. **To what extent do input modality differences explain performance disparities?**  
   *Basis:* Keypoint transfer outperformed ImageNet for CSL→Arabic but underperformed for GSL→Flemish.  
   *Why unresolved:* Modality confounding prevents isolating iconicity vs. representation effects.  
   *What evidence:* Controlled study with same modality for both transfer approaches.

3. **Does linguistic relationship influence transfer beyond iconicity?**  
   *Basis:* Pairs from different families were used; outcomes attributed to iconicity. Linguistic distance could confound.  
   *Why unresolved:* No within-family comparisons.  
   *What evidence:* Include control pairs with similar families but low iconicity, and divergent families with high iconicity.

4. **How does class sample imbalance affect transfer gains?**  
   *Basis:* Source datasets had more samples per class than targets; imbalance not controlled for.  
   *Why unresolved:* Experimental design didn't vary sample size ratios.  
   *What evidence:* Experiments with balanced sample counts or statistical analysis across pairs.

## Limitations
- **Iconicity Definition & Mapping Uncertainty:** The exact mapping of sign classes to iconic concepts is not provided, hindering replication of subsets.
- **Dataset & Evaluation Specificity:** Unspecified train/test splits, missing detection handling, and subset sizes make results context-bound.
- **Architectural & Training Ambiguities:** Transfer protocol (which weights loaded?) is unclear; ImageNet comparison uses mismatched modalities, limiting generalizability.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1 (Shared iconic concepts enable positive transfer):** High Confidence. Directly supported by +7.02% and +1.07% gains and negative transfer ablation.
- **Claim 2 (Non-iconic transfer aids optimization/convergence):** Medium Confidence. Supported by convergence speed observations and minor gain in one case, but mechanism less directly evidenced.
- **Claim 3 (Keypoint-based iconic transfer outperforms ImageNet pre-training):** Medium-Low Confidence. Results are pair-dependent and modality-mismatched; superiority is not general.
