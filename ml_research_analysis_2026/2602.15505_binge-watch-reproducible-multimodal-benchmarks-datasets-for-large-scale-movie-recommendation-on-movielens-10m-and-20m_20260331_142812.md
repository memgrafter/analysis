# Binge Watch: Reproducible Multimodal Benchmarks Datasets for Large-Scale Movie Recommendation on MovieLens-10M and 20M

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.15505
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15505
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Released M3L-10M and M3L-20M datasets; multimodal models with audio features (e.g., VBPR+Text+Audio) significantly outperform CF, achieving Recall@10 up to 0.1938 on M3L-10M.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the scarcity of large-scale, reproducible multimodal datasets for movie recommendation by introducing M3L-10M and M3L-20M. These datasets augment the classic MovieLens-10M and -20M with textual (plot summaries), visual (posters), and acoustic/video (trailers) features extracted using state-of-the-art encoders. The full pipeline and extracted embeddings are publicly released, achieving high coverage (84.58% for ML-10M, 71.08% for ML-20M). Benchmark evaluations demonstrate that multimodal fusion consistently outperforms collaborative filtering baselines, with acoustic features from trailers emerging as a particularly strong signal across multiple models.

## Method Summary
The authors construct M3L by first aligning MovieLens item IDs with TMDB IDs. They then query TMDB for plot summaries, poster URLs, and trailer URLs. Posters and trailers are downloaded (from TMDB and YouTube via `yt_dlp`), and features are extracted using pretrained encoders: text (e.g., MPNet, CLIP-Text), image (e.g., ViT, CLIP-Image), video (e.g., MViT, SlowFast), and audio (e.g., Whisper, VGGish). Only items successfully yielding embeddings for all selected modalities are retained. The resulting dataset packages the original user-item interactions alongside modality-specific embeddings and source URLs. Models (BPR, VBPR, LATTICE, FREEDOM) are trained via the MMRec framework using an 8:1:1 temporal split, with early stopping on validation Recall@20.

## Key Results
- Multimodal models (VBPR, LATTICE, FREEDOM) consistently surpass the CF baseline (BPR) across all metrics.
- Acoustic features are present in every top-performing double-modality configuration.
- The best configuration (VBPR with MPNet text + Whisper audio) achieves Recall@10 = 0.1938 and NDCG@10 = 0.2165 on M3L-10M.
- Single-modality audio models (e.g., Whisper) often outperform single-modality text or visual models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Acoustic Features as Primary Driver
- **Claim:** Trailer audio encodes atmospheric and affective properties (music, sound design, pacing) that complement narrative and visual cues, distinguishing movies with similar plots but different emotional tones.
- **Evidence:** All best double-modality results include audio; audio shows high "Stylistic Variance" (0.85) in the paper's intrinsic evaluation; Whisper/VGGish top single-modality scores.
- **Break condition:** Replacing audio with non-audio features or unrelated sound would diminish the multimodal boost.

### Mechanism 2: Textual Features as Thematic Anchor
- **Claim:** Plot summaries encoded via transformers (MPNet) create a dense latent space strongly aligned with genre taxonomies and narrative content.
- **Evidence:** Text exhibits highest "Genre Alignment" (0.90) and "Narrative Precision" (0.95); text-only models perform strongly; text appears in all best overall pairs.
- **Break condition:** Using truncated, spoiler-filled, or non-narrative text (e.g., reviews) would reduce genre alignment and utility.

### Mechanism 3: Visual/Video Features as Stylistic Signals
- **Claim:** Posters and trailers capture stylistic dimensions (color, cinematography, pacing) non-redundant with narrative text, grouping movies by "feel" or visual language.
- **Evidence:** Video/audio show high "Spatial Distinguishability" and "Stylistic Variance"; video features appear in top pairs; examples show visually similar but narratively distinct films co-locating.
- **Break condition:** Replacing posters with random images or trailers with static slideshows would collapse stylistic metrics and remove video/image advantage.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal Fusion in Recommender Systems**
  - **Why needed:** Core to using M3L; requires understanding early vs. late fusion trade-offs and interaction learning.
  - **Quick check:** What are the trade-offs between concatenating raw embeddings (early fusion) versus using architectures (like VBPR) that learn modality interaction weights?
  
- **Concept: Feature Extraction with Pretrained Encoders**
  - **Why needed:** Datasets release fixed embeddings from specific encoders; users must know what semantic properties each captures (e.g., MPNet for semantics, Whisper for speech/audio patterns).
  - **Quick check:** Why might Whisper (a speech-to-text encoder) be effective for trailer audio, which includes music and sound effects beyond dialogue?
  
- **Concept: Dataset Coverage and Core Filtering**
  - **Why needed:** The paper avoids aggressive filtering to preserve coverage; users must understand how filtering thresholds affect sparsity, cold-start, and generalizability.
  - **Quick check:** How does retaining items with partial modality data (vs. strict "all modalities" filtering) impact evaluation of multimodal models for cold-start items?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - TMDB ID Mapping -> Raw Data Acquisition (TMDB API, yt_dlp) -> Modality-Specific Feature Extraction (sentence-transformers, torchvision, transformers) -> Coverage Filtering -> Dataset Packaging (interactions + embeddings + URLs)
  
- **Critical path:** Successful TMDB ID mapping -> successful trailer/poster download -> successful feature extraction for all chosen encoders -> filtering by coverage -> clean integration with MovieLens interactions.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Coverage vs. Modality Completeness: More items with partial data increases size but complicates model input handling.
  - Encoder Variety vs. Standardization: Multiple encoders enable ablation but increase storage and selection complexity.
  - Raw Data URLs vs. Raw Files: URLs balance reproducibility/copyright but risk link rot.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low final coverage (<70% for ML-20M) indicating widespread download/extraction failures.
  - Inconsistent embedding dimensions across encoders for the same modality.
  - Data leakage if filtering is applied after temporal splitting.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce feature extraction for 100 movies using one encoder per modality (MPNet, ViT, Whisper, MViT); validate output shapes and success rate.
  2. Validate single-modality performance: Train VBPR on M3L-10M with only MPNet text; confirm Recall@10 ≈ 0.1799.
  3. Test double-modality superiority: Train VBPR with best single (Whisper audio) and second-best (MPNet text); confirm combined model outperforms both singles and BPR baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do acoustic features consistently emerge as the strongest single-modal signal and a key component in top multimodal configurations?
- **Basis in paper:** Audio encoders achieve highest single-modality scores for their models; audio present in all best double-modality pairs; authors state "Audio modality is a key component."
- **Why unresolved:** Presented as empirical finding without investigating underlying reasons (e.g., semantic richness, mood capture).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation correlating audio feature dimensions with metadata/genre/mood labels; user study on audio cue alignment; distribution analysis vs. other domains.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How generalizable and scalable is the pipeline to other domains (e-commerce, music, news)?
- **Basis in paper:** Pipeline designed for MovieLens/TMDB/YouTube; challenges like geographical restrictions noted but not generalized.
- **Why unresolved:** Domain-specific; success relies on TMDB's comprehensive metadata and trailer availability, which may not have equivalents.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replication in another domain (e.g., Amazon with images/reviews/videos); documentation of failure rates and required adaptations.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the impact of avoiding "aggressive core filtering" on multimodal model performance, especially for cold-start?
- **Basis in paper:** Authors avoid core-10 filtering, criticizing prior works for excessive reduction, but do not compare filtered vs. unfiltered versions of M3L.
- **Why unresolved:** High coverage is positioned as an advantage but not experimentally isolated as a variable affecting metrics or cold-start.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run unimodal/multimodal models on M3L-10M with and without a 10-core filter; compare overall and cold-start (e.g., items with <5 interactions) performance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the "non-consumptive research framework" and Fair Use claim sustain long-term public release of datasets with embeddings from copyrighted audiovisual content?
- **Basis in paper:** Authors state they do not redistribute raw streams, only source IDs and transformative embeddings, citing Fair Use for non-commercial research.
- **Why unresolved:** Legal interpretations of "transformative use" for latent embeddings are unsettled; reliance on third-party platforms (YouTube, TMDB) introduces compliance risks if terms change.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Formal legal analysis or case study on embedding-based research use; monitoring for DMCA takedown notices; comparison with licensing of other multimodal datasets.

## Limitations
- **External link rot risk:** Reliance on TMDB/YouTube URLs for raw media may decay over time; Zenodo-hosted embeddings mitigate but not eliminate full re-collection issues.
- **Encoder-specificity:** Performance gains (especially audio's dominance) may be tied to chosen pretrained encoders (Whisper, VGGish) and not generalize without re-tuning.
- **Coverage-completeness trade-off:** The 71-85% item overlap with all modalities, while high, still excludes a significant fraction of items, potentially affecting cold-start representation.

## Confidence
- **Multimodal fusion improves over CF:** High (consistent across models, clear ablation).
- **Audio features are primary driver:** Medium (supported by all top configurations but lacks direct audio ablation study).
- **Dataset coverage/reproducibility:** High (public embeddings + pipeline documentation).
- **External generalizability of findings:** Low (no 3rd-party validation; 0 citations at time of writing).
- **Encoder choice neutrality:** Medium (results may not transfer to alternative models without re-tuning).
