# RECUR: Resource Exhaustion Attack via Recursive-Entropy Guided Counterfactual Utilization and Reflection

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.08214  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08214  
- **Reference Count:** 30  
- **Primary Result:** RECUR increases output length by up to 11× and reduces token throughput by ≈90% across multiple LRMs, with some models experiencing reasoning collapse (>64k tokens).

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies and exploits a critical vulnerability in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs): uncontrolled reflective reasoning can lead to self-reinforcing thinking loops that exhaust computational resources. The authors introduce RECUR, an attack that first constructs counterfactual questions to induce over-reflection, then uses a novel metric—Recursive Entropy—to guide token sampling toward stable low-entropy loops. A coherence-based trimming step compresses full loop trajectories into short, transferable prompts. Experiments on six LRMs (open- and closed-source) demonstrate severe resource exhaustion, with output length increasing by up to 11× and token throughput dropping by ~90%. The attack transfers effectively, sometimes causing models to generate tens of thousands of tokens before collapsing.

## Method Summary
RECUR operates in three stages. First, it generates counterfactual questions by replacing the true answer with a false premise (e.g., “Why is the answer 63?” for a problem whose answer is 10). This forces the LRM to oscillate between correct reasoning and the false premise, creating multiple “factual hits” that indicate over-reflection. The initial over-reflective prefix is extracted. Second, Recursive Entropy–guided sampling selects next tokens from the top‑k distribution that maximize the ratio of token probability to the clamped entropy of the subsequent distribution, steering generation into self‑reinforcing low‑entropy loops. Third, coherence‑based trimming compresses the full loop trajectory by retaining the over‑thinking prefix and a “shortcut” segment that links a direction token to a later similar step; the resulting prompt is short yet triggers the same loop when replayed (the “replay rule”). The trimmed prompt can then be deployed against the same or a different LRM to induce resource exhaustion.

## Key Results
- RECUR increases reasoning length by up to 11× (e.g., from ~1.5k to ~16k tokens) and in some cases causes models to exceed 64k tokens and collapse.
- Token throughput drops by approximately 90% under attack, severely degrading service.
- The attack transfers across model families; ablation confirms both counterfactual construction and Recursive Entropy guidance are essential, while trimming maintains success while drastically reducing input length.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Counterfactual questions induce over‑reflection
- **Claim:** Replacing a correct answer with an explicitly false one forces the model to repeatedly re‑derive the correct result while reconciling it with the false premise, generating multiple “factual hits” in the reasoning trace.
- **Core assumption:** LRMs engage in reflective verification when confronted with contradictory premises, extending reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “It constructs counterfactual questions to verify the inherent flaws and risks of LRMs.” [section 4.1] Defines three counterfactual types and uses `∑I[P(li, Q)]≥3` to detect over‑reflection.
- **Break condition:** Model ignores the counterfactual and terminates without oscillation (fails to achieve ≥3 factual hits).

### Mechanism 2: Recursive Entropy–guided sampling steers into loops
- **Claim:** Maximizing `Hr(xt+1|X) = p(xt+1|X) / Hc(p(u|X⊕xt+1))` selects tokens that are currently probable but lead to sharply peaked (low‑entropy) next distributions, creating positive feedback that traps the model.
- **Core assumption:** An upward trend in `Hr` correlates with increasing risk of entering a stable thinking loop.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “RECUR employs Recursive Entropy‑based sampling… to guide the model toward generating thinking loops.” [section 3.2] Links increasing `Hr` to low‑entropy positive feedback loops. [section 5.2] Empirically shows `Hr` decreasing under benign generation but increasing under RECUR guidance.
- **Break condition:** `Hr` decreases over successive tokens or an end‑of‑thinking token is generated prematurely.

### Mechanism 3: Coherence‑based trimming creates transferable prompts
- **Claim:** Compressing a full loop trajectory into a short prompt preserves the “replay rule,” causing the model to continue the loop when the trimmed prompt is fed as context.
- **Core assumption:** LRMs tend to “replay” or continue their own recent reasoning patterns when those patterns appear in the context.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “A coherence‑based trimming step compresses loop trajectories into short, transferable attack prompts.” [section 4.3] Details the trimming process and its reliance on the replay rule. [section 5.5] Ablation shows trimming reduces input length to ~1/3 while maintaining success.
- **Break condition:** Trimmed prompt omits the critical loop‑initiating segment or the model fails to recognize the pattern upon replay.

## Foundational Learning

  *Why needed:* RECUR exploits the explicit, multi‑step reasoning traces of LRMs; understanding normal CoT length and structure is necessary to see how over‑reflection and loops deviate.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key difference between prompting a standard LLM for CoT and an LRM’s inherent reasoning process?

- **Entropy as a measure of prediction uncertainty**  
  *Why needed:* Recursive Entropy builds on clamped entropy of the next‑token distribution; grasping high vs. low entropy is essential for the feedback loop mechanism.  
  *Quick check:* If a token has high probability *and* leads to a low‑entropy next distribution, what does that suggest about the model’s subsequent generation stability?

- **Adversarial prompt engineering for resource exhaustion**  
  *Why needed:* RECUR is situated among DoS attacks like Overthink and GCG; knowing the goal (induce long generation) and common strategies contextualizes RECUR’s approach.  
  *Quick check:* How does RECUR differ from a simple “repeat your last sentence” attack, and why is it more effective?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Basic Question → Counterfactual Generator → Over‑reflection Detector → Recursive Entropy Sampler → Coherence Trimmer → Attack Prompt Deployer → Target LRM (loop replay)

- **Critical path:**  
  Basic question → Counterfactual construction → Over‑reflection generation → Entropy‑guided loop induction → Trajectory trimming → Transferable prompt → Loop replay in target model.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Sampling access vs. realism:* Entropy‑guided sampling requires logit access (white‑box), but trimming creates input‑only attacks for black‑box transfer.  
  *Loop stability vs. input length:* Trimming drastically shortens prompts but risks breaking the loop if the shortcut is too aggressive (ablation shows success rate drop without trimming, but length reduction is key for practicality).  
  *Model specificity:* Counterfactual types may work better for certain models/datasets (e.g., reversed questions for no‑option problems); requires tuning.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. End‑of‑thinking token generated during entropy‑guided sampling.  
  2. Recursive Entropy trend decreases instead of increasing during guided phase.  
  3. Trimmed prompt fails to induce loop in transfer test.  
  4. Model ignores counterfactual premise and gives a short, correct answer.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Entropy trend validation:** For the same counterfactual, compare `Hr` over tokens for benign vs. RECUR‑guided generation; expect benign: decreasing `Hr`; RECUR: increasing `Hr`.  
  2. **Length impact:** Run 20 attack prompts per model, measure average/max reasoning length vs. baseline; expect >10× increase and loop reaching max context.  
  3. **Resource consumption:** Serve model with fixed batch of baseline vs. attack prompts, measure tokens/sec and latency; expect ~90% throughput drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What theoretical explanation accounts for the observed increase in Recursive Entropy leading to thinking loops in LRMs?
- **Basis in paper:** Recursive Entropy is defined and empirically linked to loops, but no causal or formal reasoning is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** Without a theoretical foundation, it is unclear whether Recursive Entropy is merely a proxy or a causal driver, and whether the metric generalizes to unforeseen architectures or tasks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A formal analysis linking the entropy of the next‑token distribution to the stability of the reasoning chain (e.g., proving that high Recursive Entropy corresponds to low diversity in the latent reasoning state) or controlled experiments showing that artificially increasing/decreasing Recursive Entropy causes loop termination/induction across diverse models.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can we approximate Recursive Entropy in a black‑box setting without access to log probabilities, enabling similar resource exhaustion attacks or detection mechanisms in production APIs?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 4.3 acknowledges that Recursive Entropy guided sampling requires access to system prompt template and logprobs, limiting real‑world applicability; the authors circumvent this by creating transferable prompts, but the guidance itself remains white‑box.
- **Why unresolved:** Many LRM services are black‑box; without logits, attackers or defenders cannot compute Recursive Entropy, leaving a gap in understanding loop susceptibility from only observable outputs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of a proxy metric (e.g., based on output token entropy, repetition patterns, or embedding distances) that correlates strongly with Recursive Entropy; empirical validation showing that black‑box attacks using such proxies achieve comparable loop induction to the white‑box method.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent do counterfactual question construction strategies generalize to domains lacking a unique ground‑truth answer, such as creative writing or open‑ended question answering?
- **Basis in paper:** The method relies on basic questions with a single correct answer (GSM8k) to create false premises; the authors do not evaluate on tasks where answers are not binary or verifiable.
- **Why unresolved:** In open‑ended settings, it is unclear how to define “over‑reflection” or detect when the model is stuck in a loop, making the attack design ambiguous.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments applying counterfactual generation (e.g., injecting contradictory premises) to diverse datasets (e.g., summarization, dialogue) and measuring whether Recursive Entropy‑guided loops still emerge; also investigating new criteria for identifying loops beyond factual hits.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Which model‑specific factors (e.g., size, decoding temperature, RL fine‑tuning) determine susceptibility to RECUR‑induced thinking loops, and can we design more robust decoding algorithms?
- **Basis in paper:** Table 1 and Table 2 show varying success rates across models (e.g., 57.89% on DeepSeek‑Llama‑8B vs. 25% on QwQ‑32B). The paper does not analyze the causes of these differences.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding these factors is key to building LRMs that are both capable and resistant to resource exhaustion.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation studies correlating loop induction rates with model architecture, training data, and decoding parameters, followed by the development of modified decoding strategies (e.g., entropy‑aware sampling constraints) that mitigate loops without sacrificing reasoning accuracy.

## Limitations
- The precise definition of “clamped entropy,” the loop detection heuristic inside Algorithm 1, and the “direction token” selection in trimming are not fully specified.
- The attack relies on white‑box access for entropy‑guided sampling, limiting real‑world applicability; transferability via trimmed prompts is shown but may not generalize broadly.
- Experiments use only GSM8k and a small set of models; generalizability to other tasks and model families remains unproven.

## Confidence
- **Counterfactual over‑reflection:** Medium. Ablation shows necessity, but the ≥3 factual‑hit threshold might be brittle across question types.
- **Recursive Entropy guidance:** Medium. Empirical trend matches theory, but the core metric’s definition lacks clarity and the sampling loop heuristic is missing.
- **Coherence‑based trimming:** Low–Medium. Transferability is demonstrated, but the “replay rule” is not rigorously defined and compression may fail for some models.
- **Overall impact (length ×11, throughput −90%):** Medium–High. Results are consistent across six LRMs, but depend on white‑box setup and GSM8k; broader validation is needed.
