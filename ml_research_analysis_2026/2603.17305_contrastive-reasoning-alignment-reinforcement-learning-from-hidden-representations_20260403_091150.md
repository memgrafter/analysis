# Contrastive Reasoning Alignment: Reinforcement Learning from Hidden Representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17305
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.17305
- **Reference count**: 24
- **Primary result**: CRAFT improves reasoning safety by 79.0% and final-response safety by 87.7% over base models while preserving reasoning performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles superficial safety alignment (SSA) in large reasoning models (LRMs), where models produce safe refusals but generate harmful internal reasoning traces. The proposed CRAFT framework combines contrastive latent representation learning with reinforcement learning (GRPO) to align reasoning at the hidden state level. It first structures the latent space via Latent Contrastive Learning for Reasoning (LCLR) to separate safe, unsafe, and rethink reasoning traces. Then, Reinforcement over Reasoning Latents (R2L) steers the policy using three rewards: latent semantic safety, textual output safety, and latent-textual consistency. Evaluated on Qwen3-4B-Thinking and R1-Distill-Llama-8B across JailbreakBench and StrongReject, CRAFT substantially improves safety metrics and maintains or enhances reasoning performance on math and code benchmarks, outperforming state-of-the-art alignment methods.

## Method Summary
CRAFT operates in two sequential phases. **Phase 1 (LCLR)**: Given a dataset of reasoning traces labeled safe/unsafe/rethink, a projection head maps the final hidden state of each trace to a normalized latent vector on the hypersphere. Three losses are optimized: (1) a prototype-based triplet loss pushes unsafe traces away from a safe prototype and anchors rethink traces, (2) an instance invariance loss (SimCLR-style) uses augmentations for robustness, and (3) a calibration loss aligns the latent safety score with soft labels and distills from a frozen textual safety verifier. This yields structured prototypes (μ_c) and a safety scorer (g_ψ). **Phase 2 (R2L)**: The policy model (LRM) is fine-tuned with GRPO. For each generated trajectory, rewards are computed from the final latent state: R_ls (cosine similarity to safe/unsafe prototypes), R_txt (safety of final response from an evaluator), and R_cons (1 - |p_z - p_y|, penalizing discrepancy between latent-derived and textual safety). The combined reward guides policy updates while freezing the LCLR components. This directly aligns reasoning trajectories to produce safety-consistent internal thoughts and safe outputs.

## Key Results
- Improves reasoning safety by 79.0% and final-response safety by 87.7% over base models on JailbreakBench and StrongReject, surpassing SOTA baselines (IPO, SafeKey).
- Preserves and even enhances reasoning capability, achieving an average 4.7% gain on math/code benchmarks (AIME24, MATH-500, LiveCodeBench).
- Shows minimal performance drop compared to other alignment methods, demonstrating that latent-level alignment does not sacrifice task performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: LCLR creates a geometrically structured latent space where safe, unsafe, and rethink reasoning traces occupy distinct regions, forming a "safety manifold" that enables controllable alignment.  
**Mechanism**: Three contrastive objectives—prototype-based triplet loss (separates classes), instance invariance via augmentations, and latent calibration—organize hidden representations by safety level.  
**Core assumption**: Reasoning traces with different safety labels have discriminative latent features separable in embedding space, and this separation generalizes to new patterns.  
**Evidence anchors**: PCA visualizations (Figure 2) show clear separation between safe and unsafe traces across models, with rethink traces in transitional zones.  
**Break condition**: If latent representations become entangled across safety categories after LCLR, the geometric separation collapses and downstream steering fails.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: R2L uses three synchronized rewards in GRPO to steer reasoning trajectories toward safety while maintaining output consistency, directly optimizing the latent dynamics that generate traces.  
**Mechanism**: The reward combines (a) R_ls guiding hidden states toward safety prototypes, (b) R_txt ensuring final response safety, and (c) R_cons enforcing synchronization between internal and external safety. GRPO updates policy parameters to maximize this composite reward over groups of rollouts.  
**Core assumption**: The latent space structured by LCLR contains meaningful directions toward safety that can be navigated via gradient-based policy optimization, and aligning intermediate states leads to robust outputs.  
**Evidence anchors**: Section 4.2 states R2L directly aligns latent trajectories; abstract claims theoretical elimination of SSA.  
**Break condition**: If reward components conflict (e.g., R_ls and R_txt signal divergence) or prototypes are poorly calibrated, optimization may converge to suboptimal policies that poorly balance objectives.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: The latent-textual consistency reward (R_cons) theoretically eliminates superficial safety alignment by making policies with high |p_z - p_y| non-optimal under GRPO.  
**Mechanism**: R_cons = 1 - |p_z - p_y| directly penalizes discrepancies between latent safety (p_z = σ(ψ(z))) and textual evaluator safety (p_y). Under assumptions (Lipschitz continuity, local controllability, fixed evaluator), any policy with persistent |p_z - p_y| ≥ δ can be locally improved, so it cannot be a local optimum.  
**Core assumption**: Superficial alignment corresponds to local optima where p_y ≈ 1 but p_z is low; smoothing the reward landscape via R_cons removes these optima.  
**Evidence anchors**: Theorem 5.1 proves that locally optimal policies under GRPO with R_cons satisfy E[|p_z - p_y|] ≤ ε, ruling out SSA.  
**Break condition**: If local controllability fails (i.e., modifying latents without changing outputs is impossible due to model capacity), SSA might persist as a local optimum.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Input (harmful/benign prompts with reasoning traces) -> LCLR module (hidden state extractor, projection head f_ω, normalized latent z, prototype bank μ_c, safety scorer g_ψ, instance augmentations) -> R2L module (policy network LRM generates (y₁...y_T) with hidden states (h₁...h_T), computes rewards R_ls, R_txt, R_cons) -> Aligned model.

### Critical Path
1. Collect reasoning traces (from model or dataset), label safety levels (safe/unsafe/rethink).
2. Train LCLR: optimize L_proto, L_inst, L_cal while updating prototypes via EMA. Yields f_ω, g_ψ, μ_c.
3. Freeze LCLR components, initialize R2L with same policy. For each GRPO iteration: generate groups of rollouts, compute rewards using frozen LCLR outputs (z, p_z), apply updates.
4. Evaluate on safety benchmarks (JailbreakBench, StrongReject) and reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, MATH-500, LiveCodeBench).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Sequential vs. joint training**: Paper uses two-phase; joint might be more consistent but less stable.
- **Prototype initialization**: Random vs. from data statistics; affects convergence speed.
- **Reward weights**: (w_lat, w_txt, w_cons). Overweighting R_cons might overfit to evaluator bias; R_ls alone might not guarantee output safety.
- **Evaluator choice**: Using GPT-4o is expensive; a smaller distilled model could reduce cost but might introduce bias.
- **Rollout length**: Long reasoning traces increase computational cost; truncation might affect latent quality.

### Failure Signatures
- **Latent collapse**: All z cluster together, prototypes become indistinguishable → LCLR failed.
- **Reward hacking**: Model maximizes R_cons by outputting text that matches p_z but is actually unsafe → evaluator mismatch.
- **Catastrophic refusal**: Post-CRAFT model refuses >90% of benign queries → over-alignment.
- **Reasoning degradation**: >10% drop on math/code benchmarks → safety alignment damaged core capabilities.
- **GRPO instability**: Loss oscillations, reward spikes → improper advantage normalization or learning rates.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter values for LCLR losses and R2L reward coefficients are unreported, hindering faithful replication and sensitivity analysis.
- Reliance on black-box evaluators (GPT-4o for reasoning traces, StrongReject for outputs) may introduce evaluator bias and reduce result portability.
- Theoretical guarantee (Theorem 5.1) depends on unverified assumptions (e.g., local controllability); empirical validation is lacking.

## Confidence
- LCLR latent space separation: Medium (PCA visual evidence only, no quantitative clustering metrics).
- R2L steering effectiveness: Medium (strong gains but reward weight interactions unstudied).
- Consistency-reward elimination of SSA: Medium (proof exists but assumptions untested).
