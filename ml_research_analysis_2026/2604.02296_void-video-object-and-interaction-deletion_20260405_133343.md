# VOID: Video Object and Interaction Deletion

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2604.02296
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02296
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** VOID achieves 64.8% human preference on real-world video object removal, outperforming Runway (18.4%), and leads on synthetic benchmarks (PSNR 31.49, FVD 260.31) with the highest VLM-judge scores.

## Executive Summary
Removing objects from videos that physically interact with their surroundings is a challenging problem because it requires generating content that respects object relationships, physics, and maintains temporal consistency. VOID introduces a novel pipeline that trains on counterfactual data generated from Kubric and HUMOTO simulations, uses a vision-language model to infer regions affected by the removal, employs quadmask conditioning to guide diffusion, and applies a two-pass video diffusion process to ensure stable outputs.  

Experiments on 75 real-world videos show that VOID is strongly preferred by humans (64.8% vs. Runway’s 18.4%). VLM-as-a-judge evaluations confirm its superiority, with the highest total scores and particular strength in “Interaction & Physics.” On synthetic benchmarks, VOID achieves state-of-the-art PSNR (31.49) and FVD (260.31) and attains the top VLM-judge score, demonstrating superior physical plausibility and generalization to unseen effects.

## Method Summary
VOID addresses video object removal for interacting objects by synthesizing large-scale counterfactual training data using the Kubric and HUMOTO simulators. These simulations produce diverse scenes with physically plausible interactions and provide perfect ground truth for training. At inference time, a vision-language model processes the input video and a textual description of the object to be removed, inferring the spatial regions that are affected by the object’s presence (including occluded or physically influenced areas). The inferred region is represented as a quadmask that conditions a video diffusion model. The diffusion process runs in two passes: the first pass generates a coarse inpainted video, and the second pass refines it by considering temporal context, thereby reducing flickering and improving consistency. This combination of synthetic data, high-level reasoning, and two-pass generation yields physically plausible and temporally stable results.

## Key Results
- Human preference on 75 real-world videos selects VOID 64.8% of the time, significantly ahead of Runway (18.4%) and other baselines.
- VLM-as-a-judge (Gemini-3 Pro) gives VOID a total score of 26.13/30, with strong gains in the “Interaction & Physics” dimension.
- On synthetic benchmarks, VOID leads in PSNR (31.49), FVD (260.31), and VLM-judge score (25.10/30), confirming superior physical plausibility and generalization to unseen effects.

## Why This Works
VOID works by leveraging three key ideas. First, synthetic training data from Kubric and HUMOTO simulations provides a diverse and physically grounded distribution of object interactions, enabling the model to learn realistic inpainting. Second, the vision-language model can interpret the scene and the object to be removed, producing accurate region masks that capture not only the object but also its influence on surrounding elements. Third, the two-pass video diffusion process first generates a plausible initial video and then refines it using temporal context, ensuring consistency across frames. Together, these components address the core challenges of physical plausibility and temporal stability in video object removal.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Simulated Data → VLM Region Inference → Quadmask Generation → Two-Pass Diffusion → Output Video

**Critical Path:**  
1. Receive input video and textual object description.  
2. VLM processes visual frames and text to output affected region masks.  
3. Quadmask (derived from masks) conditions the diffusion model.  
4. First diffusion pass generates a coarse inpainted video.  
5. Second diffusion pass refines the video for temporal coherence.  
6. Return final video.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Synthetic vs. real data:* Synthetic data offers perfect supervision and diversity but may not capture real-world textures and lighting, risking a domain gap.  
- *VLM accuracy vs. speed:* More powerful VLMs yield better region inference but increase computational cost and latency.  
- *Two-pass vs. single-pass:* Two-pass improves quality at the expense of doubled inference time; a single-pass is faster but may suffer from flickering.  
- *Quadmask simplicity:* Using a single mask is straightforward but might be insufficient for complex interactions involving multiple independent effects.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- *Incorrect region mask:* Leads to leftover object parts or over-inpainting into irrelevant areas, breaking physical plausibility.  
- *Temporal flickering or inconsistency:* Evident when objects or backgrounds change abruptly between frames despite a static camera.  
- *Unphysical artifacts:* Such as floating objects, distorted shapes, or implausible shadows due to the simulation–real gap.  
- *Loss of detail:* Blurry or low-frequency regions when the diffusion model fails to recover fine textures, especially after the second pass.

**First Experiments:**  
1. Validate VLM region inference by comparing predicted masks against ground‑truth masks on a held‑out set of simulated scenes.  
2. Measure temporal consistency (e.g., using FVD) between single‑pass and two‑pass diffusion on a test set to quantify improvement.  
3. Ablate quadmask conditioning by evaluating performance with and without the mask to assess its contribution to inpainting quality.
