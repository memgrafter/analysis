# Governance-Aware Vector Subscriptions for Multi-Agent Knowledge Ecosystems
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2603.20833
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20833
- **References:** 12
- **Result:** A vector subscription system that composes semantic similarity with multi-dimensional policy enforcement achieves 100% compliance on a benchmark, eliminating 21.2% of unvalidated notifications.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a mechanism to enforce complex governance policies within semantic vector-based publish-subscribe systems for multi-agent AI ecosystems. It addresses the problem of unauthorized content delivery by composing continuous vector similarity search with a conjunctive, multi-dimensional policy predicate covering processing levels, marketing restrictions, training opt-out, jurisdiction, and scientific use. Implemented in the AIngram system, the approach guarantees that notifications are only dispatched for content that passes both a semantic relevance threshold and all applicable policy constraints, while also ensuring only curated (`active`) chunks trigger content subscriptions.

## Method Summary
The method implements a `NOTIFY` predicate that requires three conditions: `SEMANTIC` (vector similarity via HNSW meets a subscription threshold), `POLICY` (a conjunctive check of the agent's declared profile against the chunk's policy flags), and `CURATION` (the chunk's status is `active`). Subscriptions declare a `trigger_status` determining whether matching occurs on chunk creation (`proposed`) or status transition to `active`. The system uses PostgreSQL with pgvector for storage and HNSW indexing, evaluating the full policy predicate in a single SQL query during the matching pipeline. An ablation study and synthetic benchmark (PASA) with 1,000 chunks and 93 subscriptions quantify compliance and the necessity of all five policy dimensions.

## Key Results
- Achieved **100% policy compliance** with zero false negatives for authorized content on the PASA benchmark, versus 49.4% violation rate in an ungoverned baseline.
- **Ablation study** confirms no single policy dimension (sensitivity level, marketing/training/scientific opt-out, jurisdiction) suffices for full compliance; all five are required.
- The **curation guarantee** eliminated 21.2% of notifications (40 of 189) that would have been dispatched for unvalidated (`proposed`) chunks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Semantic similarity matching is safely composed with multi-dimensional policy compliance by evaluating a conjunctive predicate *before* dispatch.
- **Mechanism:** For each active chunk status transition, the system performs an HNSW vector search to find candidate subscriptions meeting a similarity threshold. Each candidate is then evaluated against a policy predicate that checks the agent's declared handling level, purpose, training use, and jurisdiction *against* the chunk's policy flags (`λ`, `δ`, `τ_train`, `ρ`, `J`). Notification occurs only if *all* conditions hold.
- **Core assumption:** Agent policy declarations (handling level, purpose, jurisdiction) are truthful. Enforcement soundness is conditional on correct inputs.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] composition of matching with predicate; [section 3.2] formal `NOTIFY(σ, c) = SEMANTIC(σ, c) ∧ POLICY(a, c) ∧ CURATION(c)`.
- **Break condition:** A false in *any* policy dimension (`POLICY(a, c)`) breaks the conjunction, suppressing notification.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Full regulatory compliance requires multiple independent policy dimensions; no single dimension suffices.
- **Mechanism:** Each policy dimension is encoded as an independent boolean condition in the conjunction. A violation in any dimension blocks delivery; the ablation study shows partial sets leave residual violations.
- **Core assumption:** The selected dimensions capture the necessary regulatory scope for target frameworks (EU DSM Directive, EU AI Act).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] ablation result; [section 5.2, Table 4] incremental blocking rates with dimension combinations.
- **Break condition:** Removing any dimension from the predicate reintroduces its class of violations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The curation guarantee eliminates notifications for unvalidated content by tying subscription triggers to chunk status transitions, not runtime checks.
- **Mechanism:** Subscriptions declare a `trigger_status` (`active`, `proposed`, `both`). The matching pipeline is only invoked on the corresponding lifecycle events. Content subscriptions default to `trigger_status='active'`, meaning matching occurs *only* when a chunk transitions from `proposed` to `active` after review.
- **Core assumption:** The curation process (proposal→review→validation) is correctly enforced by the broader system.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] 21.2% elimination figure; [section 4.3] trigger descriptions; [section 5.2, Table 5] notification counts by status.
- **Break condition:** If the status transition logic is bypassed (e.g., direct insertion with `s(c)='active'` without review), the guarantee fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Vector Similarity Search (HNSW)**
  - *Why needed:* Enables semantic matching by meaning, not keywords. Approximate nearest neighbor search (HNSW) efficiently finds subscriptions within a cosine similarity threshold.
  - *Quick check:* What is the computational complexity of an HNSW query relative to the number of indexed vectors, and why is this important for real-time dispatch?
- **Concept: Declarative Policy Predicate as Conjunction**
  - *Why needed:* Policy compliance is a multi-dimensional, boolean filter applied post-similarity. It is not a single ACL but a conjunction of conditions (level, purpose, opt-outs, jurisdiction).
  - *Quick check:* If an agent is authorized on 4 out of 5 policy dimensions but fails the 5th, what does the predicate evaluate to and why?
- **Concept: Content Lifecycle & Status-Triggered Matching**
  - *Why needed:* Separates governance participation (monitoring `proposed` chunks) from knowledge consumption (receiving `active` chunks). The `trigger_status` field implements the curation guarantee structurally.
  - *Quick check:* What would happen if `CURATION(c)` were a runtime check (`if s(c)=='active'`) inside the predicate rather than a trigger condition?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  PostgreSQL (pgvector, HNSW index) <- AIngram Service -> Embedding Model (bge-m3)
  AIngram Service -> AgentLedger (profile resolution)
  AIngram Service -> Notification Dispatcher
- **Critical path:** New validated chunk → `status='active'` → AIngram detects transition → SQL query: join HNSW results with policy filter (level, purpose, opt-outs, jurisdiction) → dispatch.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Declarative trust vs. enforcement speed: assumes truthful agent declarations for low latency (~5ms); cryptographic enforcement would increase latency.
  - Curation vs. freshness: only `active` chunks trigger content subscriptions, delaying notifications for new unvalidated information.
  - Multi-dimension vs. complexity: each new dimension adds a boolean check and declaration requirement; simpler RBAC is insufficient.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Policy violation in logs: dispatched notification where `POLICY(a,c)` would be false → bug in query logic or stale agent profile.
  - High latency (>10ms at 500 subs): HNSW recall degraded or database contention on GIN/policy index.
  - Missing notifications for known authorized content: check `CURATION(c)` or `SEMANTIC` threshold.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run PASA benchmark with each policy dimension singly and in combination; confirm Table 4 pattern (no single dimension reaches 100% block rate).
  2. Use scaling script to measure p50/p95 latency at 10, 100, 1000 subscriptions; verify sub-linear growth.
  3. Create an agent misdeclaring `purpose='scientific'` but using marketing; feed a chunk with `ρ=1`; observe incorrect notification delivery.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can governance-aware vector subscriptions be federated across multiple knowledge bases with heterogeneous embedding spaces and policy schemas?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 6.3 lists "federated subscriptions" as future work, highlighting challenges in "cross-instance embedding alignment and policy reconciliation."
- **Why unresolved:** Current design assumes a single curated knowledge base (AIngram) with unified ADHP declarations; federated settings require resolving semantic and policy mismatches without central coordination.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A prototype implementing cross-base similarity mapping (e.g., via shared embeddings or projection layers) and a policy composition protocol, evaluated on a multi-instance benchmark with conflicting schemas.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What system mechanisms ensure that active vector subscriptions are automatically re-evaluated when an agent’s declared policy profile (e.g., processing level, purpose) changes?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 6.3 proposes "dynamic policy profiles" where "existing subscriptions are adjusted automatically" upon agent declaration changes.
- **Why unresolved:** Subscriptions are bound to agent profiles at creation; no provision exists to retroactively adjust pending notifications or re-filter historical content when profiles evolve.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementation of a subscription manager that tracks agent profile versions and triggers re-evaluation of unmatched chunks post-change, with correctness proofs and overhead measurements.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can contributor reputation scores be integrated into the notification predicate to allow consumers to filter by source trustworthiness while preserving policy compliance?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 6.3 suggests "reputation-weighted notifications" as future work, where contributor reputation enters the predicate.
- **Why unresolved:** The current `POLICY` predicate only considers chunk-declared metadata and agent profile; reputation is an orthogonal dimension not modeled, limiting trust-based filtering.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extension of the `POLICY` function with a reputation threshold condition, validated via simulation showing selective notification suppression based on contributor scores without violating existing policy constraints.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How effective are automated policy validation systems (e.g., rule engines or AI monitors) at ensuring accuracy of chunk policy metadata before validation, reducing reliance on contributor self-declaration?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 6.3 proposes "automated policy validation" to verify chunk metadata, addressing the threat of "sensitivity mislabeling" in Section 6.1.
- **Why unresolved:** The system assumes truthful contributor declarations; mislabeling can cause unauthorized delivery, and no automated checks are implemented in the current review process.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Deployment of an automated validator (e.g., a classifier for opt-out flags) in AIngram’s pipeline, measuring precision/recall against human-reviewed ground truth and impact on policy violation rates.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** At what scale (number of subscriptions) does approximate nearest neighbor search (HNSW) in governance-aware subscriptions introduce non-negligible false negatives for policy-compliant content, and how does this interact with multi-dimensional policy filtering?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 5.4 notes evaluation was limited to 93 subscriptions and warns that "at larger scales (10K+), approximate nearest neighbor may introduce false negatives," but this is untested.
- **Why unresolved:** Latency scaling is measured only to 500 subscriptions; the conjunctive policy predicate may exacerbate ANN recall errors by filtering candidates post-search, but this interaction is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Stress tests with synthetic and real subscription sets (10K–100K) varying HNSW parameters (`efSearch`), measuring recall of authorized chunks and analyzing per-policy-dimension failure modes.

## Limitations
- Evaluation on a synthetic corpus (1k chunks, 93 subscriptions) may not generalize to real-world scale and complexity.
- Assumption of truthful agent policy declarations; misdeclarations lead to unauthorized deliveries.
- Curation guarantee depends on correct `status` transitions; bypass risks unvalidated content.
- HNSW approximate search may cause false negatives if similarity threshold not met; interaction with policy filtering is untested at scale.
- Policy dimensions fixed to five; other regulatory aspects (e.g., temporal, provenance) not covered.
- Latency only tested up to 500 subscriptions; scalability beyond is unknown.

## Confidence
- **100% compliance claim:** Medium — sound mechanism but limited by synthetic data and trust assumption.
- **Ablation (no single dimension suffices):** High — direct experimental evidence.
- **Curation guarantee:** High — structural design eliminates proposed-chunk notifications.
- **Latency <5ms at 500 subs:** Medium — specific scale only.

## Next Checks
