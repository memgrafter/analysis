# PromptForge-350k: A Large-Scale Dataset and Contrastive Framework for Prompt-Based AI Image Forgery Localization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29386  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386  
- **Reference count:** 37  
- **Primary result:** ICL-Net achieves 62.5% IoU on PromptForge-350k, outperforming SOTA by 5.1%, with less than 1% IoU drop under JPEG/cropping and 41.5% IoU on unseen editing models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PromptForge-350k, the first large-scale dataset (354,258 pairs) for localizing edits made by prompt-based AI image generators, and ICL-Net, a novel contrastive learning framework. The dataset is built via an automated pipeline that aligns original and edited images using keypoint matching and annotates forged regions by measuring semantic divergence with a pre-trained DINO v3 model. ICL-Net employs a triple-stream backbone (Noise, Main, Frozen) and an intra-image contrastive loss to discriminatively learn features of edited versus non-edited regions. The method sets a new state-of-the-art on the dataset, demonstrates strong robustness to common image distortions, and shows promising generalization to editing models not seen during training.

## Method Summary
PromptForge-350k is constructed from existing prompt-based editing datasets (X2Edit, PicoBanana). The annotation pipeline first aligns the original and edited images using ORB keypoints, RANSAC, and affine warping to handle geometric shifts. It then extracts DINO v3 features from both images, computes cosine similarity maps, and applies Otsu thresholding to segment edited regions. ICL-Net processes an edited image through three SegFormer-B4 backbones: a high-pass filtered "Noise" branch, a semantic "Main" branch, and a fixed "Frozen" branch. The Noise and Main features are fused and partitioned using ground-truth masks to compute an intra-image contrastive loss, which pulls together features from non-edited regions and pushes apart features from edited regions. All three branches' features are concatenated and passed through a segmentation head to produce the final mask. The total loss combines this contrastive loss with Dice and Focal losses to address severe class imbalance (forged regions are very small).

## Key Results
- ICL-Net achieves **62.5% IoU** and **75.2% F1** on the PromptForge-350k test set, surpassing prior SOTA by **5.1% IoU**.
- The model exhibits **high robustness**, with less than a **1% IoU drop** under JPEG compression and cropping augmentations.
- It generalizes to **unseen editing models** (not in the training distribution) with an average **IoU of 41.5%**, significantly above chance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The fully automated mask annotation framework generates precise ground-truth masks for edited regions in prompt-based AI images.
- **Mechanism:** A two-stage pipeline: (1) Pixel-level alignment via ORB keypoint extraction, RANSAC robust matching, and affine warping to compensate for model-introduced geometric distortions. (2) Semantic-based mask annotation using DINO v3 feature extraction, cosine similarity computation, and Otsu thresholding on the similarity map to segment edited regions.
- **Core assumption:** Non-edited regions semantically preserve the original image content, while edited regions are regenerated, causing measurable feature space divergence detectable by a pre-trained vision model (DINO v3).
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states the framework leverages "keypoint alignment and semantic feature similarity." Section 3 details the alignment and semantic mask annotation pipelines, including ORB, RANSAC, DINO v3, and Otsu's method. Section 4.3 reports a user study where 86% of annotations (172/200) were rated "Perfect" or "Minor Error."
- **Break condition:** The method relies on sufficient structural similarity between original and edited images in non-edited regions. It may fail if the editing operation is purely low-level (e.g., uniform color change) where DINO v3 features are insensitive, as noted in the paper's limitation [section 7].

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** PromptForge-350k mitigates data scarcity for prompt-based AI forgery localization by providing a large-scale dataset (354,258 pairs) with accurate pixel-level masks.
- **Mechanism:** Applying the automated annotation framework to image pairs from existing prompt-based editing datasets (X2Edit, PicoBanana), filtering for local editing tasks and quality criteria (keypoint counts, inlier ratios).
- **Core assumption:** The automated annotation quality is sufficient for training effective localization models, and the selected editing models/tasks represent a meaningful distribution of real-world prompt-based manipulations.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract announces the dataset with "354,258 edited image pairs annotated with precise pixel-level edited region masks." Section 4.1 describes construction from X2Edit and PicoBanana, covering four editing models and eight tasks, with quality filtering steps. Section 4.3 provides quantitative quality assessment (86% acceptable masks).
- **Break condition:** Annotation errors could propagate to trained models. The dataset is limited to eight specific editing tasks and four models; performance may degrade on unseen editing paradigms not represented in the distribution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** ICL-Net's intra-image contrastive learning enables robust and generalizable forgery localization by learning discriminative features between edited and non-edited regions within a single image.
- **Mechanism:** A triple-stream backbone (Noise, Main, Frozen) extracts complementary features. Features from Noise and Main backbones are fused and spatially partitioned using GT masks. A contrastive loss maximizes similarity among "real" region features while minimizing similarity between "real" and "forged" region features, forcing the network to identify subtle inconsistencies.
- **Core assumption:** Edited and non-edited regions within the same image share contextual and semantic relationships, making intra-image contrast a stronger signal than inter-image contrast for isolating editing artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions "intra-image contrastive learning" for "robust and generalizable forensic features." Section 5 details the triple-stream architecture and the contrastive loss formulation (Equation 2). Section 6.5 ablation study shows removing contrastive loss drops F1 by 15.6% (from 75.20% to 59.63%), directly evidence its critical role.
- **Break condition:** If the editing is so semantically coherent that feature distributions between edited and non-edited regions become indistinguishable, the contrastive signal weakens. The paper notes lower recall on unseen models [section 6.4], suggesting the learned contrastive rules overfit to in-distribution editing patterns.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE Loss)**  
  - **Why needed here:** The core innovation of ICL-Net is the intra-image contrastive loss. Understanding how it pulls same-class (real region) features together and pushes different-class (forged) region features apart is essential to grasp why the network learns separation without explicit negative samples from other images.  
  - **Quick check question:** In the loss function (Equation 2), what do `Ff` and `Fr` represent, and how does the denominator structure enforce discrimination?

- **Concept: Semantic Feature Extractors (DINO v3)**  
  - **Why needed here:** The entire dataset pipeline hinges on using a powerful pre-trained model (DINO v3) to compute robust semantic similarities. Knowing its properties (self-supervised, semantics-focused) explains why it's chosen over pixel-differencing or handcrafted features and its limitation with color-only edits.  
  - **Quick check question:** Why is a model like DINO v3 (trained for representation learning) more suitable for the mask annotation than a model trained on ImageNet classification?

- **Concept: Class Imbalance in Segmentation**  
  - **Why needed here:** Forgery regions are small (<20%). The loss function combines Focal and Dice loss specifically to combat this. Understanding this is key to interpreting the training dynamics and why simple BCE would fail.  
  - **Quick check question:** How does Focal Loss address class imbalance differently from weighting in BCE, and why might Dice Loss complement it here?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Edited Image (I_e) -> Noise Branch (High-pass filter -> SegFormer-B4) -> Noise features  
Edited Image (I_e) -> Main Branch (SegFormer-B4) -> Main features  
Edited Image (I_e) -> Frozen Branch (SegFormer-B4, frozen) -> Frozen features  
Fusion: Channel concatenation of Noise and Main features -> Contrastive Head (spatial partition by GT mask) -> Contrastive Loss  
Fusion: Channel concatenation of Noise, Main, and Frozen features -> Classification Head (conv/upsample layers) -> Predicted mask  
Loss: L_total = λ1 * L_contrastive + λ2 * L_Dice + λ3 * L_Focal

**Critical path:** Forward pass through three backbones → fuse Noise+Main features → compute intra-image contrastive loss using GT masks → fuse all three features → pass through classification head → compute Dice and Focal losses → aggregate total loss → backpropagation. The contrastive loss computation depends on having GT masks during training to partition features.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Frozen Backbone:* Preserves pre-trained general features and mitigates catastrophic forgetting, but adds no adaptive capacity and increases memory cost.  
- *Intra-Image Contrastive:* Leverages inherent (edited vs. real) pairs within one image, avoiding large batch negatives, but relies on perfect GT masks for partitioning; annotation noise could poison the signal.  
- *Triple-Stream:* Combines high-frequency (Noise), semantic (Main), and stable (Frozen) cues, improving robustness, but triples the backbone compute/memory relative to a single stream.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **High false negatives (low recall):** Observed on closed-source Nano-Banana model, indicating over-conservative predictions on unseen forensic patterns.  
- **Poor localization on color-only edits:** Directly caused by annotation pipeline failure for low-level edits, leading to training on bad masks.  
- **Alignment failures:** Entire pipeline depends on successful keypoint matching; filtered samples (by quality criteria) represent cases where correspondence cannot be established.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Reproduce Main Metric:** Train ICL-Net on the public PromptForge-350k train split and evaluate on the test split. Confirm the reported ~62.5% IoU and 75.2% F1 to validate baseline implementation and dataset integrity.

3. **Ablation of Contrastive Loss:** Train a variant with the intra-image contrastive loss disabled (λ1=0). Should observe a significant performance drop (~15-17% absolute in F1/IoU) as reported in Table 4, confirming the mechanism's necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1:** How can the automated annotation framework be enhanced to reliably detect forgery resulting from pure color-modification edits?  
- **Basis in paper:** In Section 7 (Limitations), the authors state that annotation failures are concentrated in "scenarios involving solely object color modifications," attributing this to DINO v3's low sensitivity to color.  
- **Why unresolved:** The pipeline relies solely on semantic feature similarity (DINO v3), which prioritizes structural over chromatic information. No alternative mechanism is proposed to capture color-based inconsistencies.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A targeted user study or quantitative benchmark evaluating color-specific edit masks. Developing a multi-modal annotation strategy (e.g., integrating color-sensitive features or fine-tuning DINO on color-variant datasets) and measuring its reduction in annotation error for this failure case.

**Open Question 2:** What fundamental differences in forgery artifacts between open-source and closed-source prompt-based editing models cause the significant generalization gap observed for ICL-Net?  
- **Basis in paper:** Section 6.4 reports an IoU drop from 36.39%-57.12% (open-source) to 18.95% (closed-source Nano-Banana). The authors speculate that "closed-source models' architectures and training data likely diverge significantly," but do not analyze the specific artifacts.  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates the performance gap but provides no forensic analysis (e.g., feature distribution shifts, artifact type prevalence) to explain why artifacts from closed-source models are "more elusive."  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study comparing feature space distributions (from ICL-Net's backbones) of edited regions across open vs. closed-source models. Identifying consistent, model-agnostic forensic cues versus model-specific artifacts would clarify the generalization barrier.

**Open Question 3:** Does the intra-image contrastive learning objective in ICL-Net sufficiently capture all types of forensic inconsistencies (e.g., high-frequency noise traces, semantic context breaks, or geometric distortions) inherent to diverse prompt-based edits?  
- **Basis in paper:** The method's core premise is that "non-edited regions structurally and semantically preserve the originals" while edited regions show "subtle inconsistencies." The ablation (Section 6.5) shows contrastive loss is critical, but its design (Eq. 2) operates on fused high-level features from the noise and main backbones. It is unclear if this formulation is adequate for edits where inconsistencies are primarily low-frequency (e.g., global lighting changes) or where semantics are preserved.  
- **Why unresolved:** The evaluation covers eight editing tasks, but the paper does not analyze performance per task type or visualize what the contrastive mechanism is actually learning. It assumes semantic inconsistency is the primary cue.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A failure case analysis on editing types where ICL-Net underperforms (e.g., object state conversion). Visualizing activation maps or directly probing the learned feature space for task-specific artifact representations would test the scope of the contrastive signal.

**Open Question 4:** Is the triple-stream backbone's fixed allocation of pathways (high-pass noise, semantic, frozen) optimal, or could dynamic pathway weighting or adaptive feature fusion improve performance and robustness across the diverse editing spectrum in PromptForge-350k?  
- **Basis in paper:** The architecture design (Section 5.1) uses three parallel streams with distinct roles, but the weighting is static (simple channel concatenation). Table 4's ablation shows each component contributes, but their relative importance might vary per edit type (e.g., noise backbone may be less critical for edits altering semantics rather than texture). The paper does not explore adaptive routing or task-aware fusion.  
- **Why unresolved:** The fixed design is chosen for simplicity and robustness but may underutilize feature interactions. The strong performance on some subsets (e.g., Kontext) vs. weaker on others (Nano-Banana) suggests varying artifact patterns, hinting that a one-size-fits-all stream fusion might not be ideal.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An experiment replacing the static concatenation with a learned fusion module (e.g., attention-based) and measuring per-subset performance changes. Analyzing the correlation between each stream's output and ground-truth mask across editing tasks would reveal if adaptive weighting is beneficial.

## Limitations
- The automated annotation pipeline fails for low-level edits like uniform color changes, as DINO v3 is insensitive to chromatic changes, potentially contaminating training data for such cases.
- PromptForge-350k covers only eight editing tasks from four models; generalization to entirely new editing paradigms is not guaranteed, as evidenced by the 41.5% IoU on unseen models.
- Critical training hyperparameters (e.g., exact feature stage, contrastive temperature, augmentation ranges) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
