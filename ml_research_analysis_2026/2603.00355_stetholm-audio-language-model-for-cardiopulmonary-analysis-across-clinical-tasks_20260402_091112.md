# StethoLM: Audio Language Model for Cardiopulmonary Analysis Across Clinical Tasks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00355
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00355
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** First instruction-following audio-language model for comprehensive cardiopulmonary clinical reasoning, achieving state-of-the-art in-domain performance (71.8% BERTScore, 47.8% clinical accuracy).

## Executive Summary
StethoLM introduces a novel paradigm for AI-assisted auscultation by moving beyond narrow classification to instruction-driven clinical reasoning. It combines a medical audio encoder (EfficientNet) with a medical LLM (MedGemma) via learned audio prefix tokens, all trained on a new benchmark, StethoBench, comprising 77,027 instruction-response pairs across seven clinical tasks. This architecture enables the generation of free-text responses to diverse queries, matching or surpassing general multimodal models despite using a smaller backbone. The work demonstrates that instruction-tuning can effectively align acoustic patterns with clinical language, though challenges remain in out-of-domain generalization, safety, and differential diagnosis.

## Method Summary
StethoLM employs a two-module architecture: a medical audio encoder (EfficientNet pre-trained on cardiopulmonary sounds) processes log-Mel spectrograms into feature sequences. A trainable projection MLP compresses these into a fixed number (k=4) of prefix tokens that are injected into the input sequence of a frozen medical LLM (MedGemma-4B-IT). The LLM, fine-tuned with LoRA adapters on its attention layers, is trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to generate target responses conditioned on both audio and textual instructions. The training corpus, StethoBench, was synthetically created from 16,125 recordings across seven clinical tasks using GPT-4o. Evaluation uses BERTScore for textual similarity and a GPT-5-based judge for binary clinical accuracy.

## Key Results
- Outperforms general-purpose audio-language and large multimodal models on in-domain data (71.8% BERTScore, 47.8% accuracy) and out-of-domain data (64.8%, 25.2%).
- Achieves competitive zero-shot classification capabilities, suggesting instruction-tuning captures transferable features.
- Demonstrates strong audio grounding via ablations: removing audio reduces accuracy by ~19 points, and shuffling audio-instruction pairs reduces it by ~16 points.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Instruction-tuning transforms auscultation AI from narrow classification to diverse clinical reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Training on 77,027 instruction-response pairs across seven task types forces the model to learn a shared representation that maps acoustic features to varied text outputs based on the query, rather than a fixed label set.
- **Core assumption:** Clinically meaningful acoustic patterns are sufficiently preserved and made actionable through generative language modeling objectives.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “capable of performing instruction-driven clinical tasks across the full spectrum of auscultation analysis”
  - [section 1] “most are restricted to simple classification and offer little clinical interpretability or decision support”
  - [corpus] Neighbors like AcuLa support the paradigm shift from classification to generative medical audio understanding.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Domain-specialized pretraining on cardiopulmonary audio is essential; general audio encoders miss diagnostically relevant fine-grained patterns.
- **Mechanism:** The audio encoder is pretrained on medical sounds, exposing it to spectro-temporal features specific to heart/lung sounds (e.g., crackle duration, murmur timing). This specialized representation is then aligned to the medical LLM.
- **Core assumption:** General-purpose audio features (from music, speech, environmental sounds) lack the subtle temporal and spectral characteristics critical for auscultation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2] “diagnostically relevant features reside in millisecond-scale temporal patterns… require specialized training on medical data.”
  - [section 6.3] General audio-language models achieve <5% accuracy, while StethoLM reaches 47.8%.
  - [corpus] Neighboring datasets (e.g., Manikin-Recorded Cardiopulmonary Sounds) highlight unique acoustic properties.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Mapping audio to fixed-length prefix tokens enables effective fusion with the LLM without altering its architecture.
- **Mechanism:** The projection network (MLP) converts variable-length audio features into `k=4` prefix tokens of dimension matching the LLM (4,096). These tokens are prepended to the tokenized instruction and response, allowing the frozen LLM to attend to audio context.
- **Core assumption:** A small set of learned audio tokens can compress the essential acoustic information needed for the LLM to generate clinically relevant text.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.1] “zaudio = MP(haudio) ∈ R^{k×d}”
  - [section 7.1] Prefix length ablation shows minimal impact beyond `k=2`.
  - [section 6.6] Attention visualizations show generated tokens attend to the audio prefix region.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Medical Audio Signal Processing**
  - **Why needed:** Interpreting cardiopulmonary sounds requires understanding how pathologies manifest in the time-frequency domain (e.g., wheeze pitch, crackle duration). The model uses spectrograms; without this, one cannot diagnose model errors.
  - **Quick check:** What acoustic characteristics distinguish fine crackles from coarse crackles, and how might a spectrogram represent this difference?

- **Concept: Instruction-Tuning for Multimodal LLMs**
  - **Why needed:** StethoLM’s core innovation is moving from classification to instruction-following. Understanding how SFT and DPO shape model behavior—especially generating free-text versus predicting a label—is key to interpreting its capabilities.
  - **Quick check:** How does the training objective (Equation 6) differ from a standard cross-entropy classification loss, and why does that matter for clinical reasoning tasks?

- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)**
  - **Why needed:** The LLM backbone is mostly frozen, with LoRA adapters on attention projections. This design affects what knowledge is updated—primarily audio-text alignment, not base medical knowledge.
  - **Quick check:** If LoRA is applied only to Q, K, V, O projections, what aspects of the model’s computation are *not* being fine-tuned, and how might that limit learning?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Raw waveform (16 kHz) -> 10-second log-Mel spectrogram (64 bands) -> EfficientNet encoder (pretrained on medical sounds) -> feature sequence (N_f × 1280) -> 3-layer MLP projection -> 4 prefix tokens (4 × 4096) -> MedGemma-4B-IT (frozen, with LoRA on attention Q/K/V/O) -> autoregressive text generation.

- **Critical path:** Audio is converted to spectrogram, encoded to a feature sequence, projected to a fixed set of prefix tokens, which are concatenated with tokenized instruction and response. The LLM processes this sequence causally to generate the response token-by-token. Trainable parameters: full encoder, full projection MLP, and LoRA adapters. LLM body and embeddings are frozen.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Prefix length (`k`)**: Longer prefixes increase expressivity but add computation; ablation shows `k=4` is sufficient, with diminishing returns.
  - **Encoder choice**: CLAP vs AST vs EfficientNet—all perform similarly, indicating pipeline robustness as long as temporal-spectral patterns are captured.
  - **LLM backbone**: Medical-specific (MedGemma) vs general (Llama-3.2) shows medical pretraining helps but capacity matters more.
  - **LoRA rank**: Higher rank increases adaptation capacity but also memory; `r=16` was chosen as a balance.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Audio grounding lost**: Removing audio drops accuracy from 47.8% → 28.5%. Shuffling audio-instruction pairs drops to 31.9%.
  - **Safety degradation**: Model generates diagnostic responses even with no/mismatched audio, indicating compromised refusal behavior.
  - **Task-specific weakness**: Differential diagnosis accuracy only 30.6% (relaxed 35.5%), revealing limits in ranking subjective clinical possibilities.
  - **OOD sensitivity**: Accuracy drops from 47.8% (ID) to 25.2% (OOD), especially on FluSense (23.2% vs Qwen’s 37.1%).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Audio ablation**: Evaluate with audio removed and with shuffled audio-instruction pairs to test grounding (Section 7.1).
  2. **Encoder swap**: Replace EfficientNet with CLAP or AST and measure BERTScore/accuracy to confirm encoder robustness.
  3. **Backbone comparison**: Test with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, MediPhi-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B to isolate effect of medical pretraining and model scale.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can multimodal medical audio-language models be made to refuse output when audio input is absent or corrupted? Ablation shows the model continues generating responses without audio, indicating lack of refusal behavior.
- **Open Question 2:** Does noise in synthetic instructions (acoustic hallucinations, clinical over‑elaboration) degrade model accuracy on real clinical tasks? Appendix B reports error rates up to 20% in generated instruction‑response pairs.
- **Open Question 3:** Can instruction‑tuned auscultation models generalize to real‑world acoustic environments and uncommon events (e.g., sneezes) not seen during training? Out‑of‑domain tests reveal a large performance drop on FluSense (spontaneous events) while generalist models perform relatively better.

## Limitations
- Unknowns: Exact EfficientNet pretraining checkpoint and GPT-5 judge version block faithful replication.
- Out-of-domain performance gap is large (47.8%→25.2% accuracy), with poor results on FluSense.
- Safety: Model generates diagnoses even with no audio; refusal behavior is compromised post-multimodal training.

## Next Checks
1. **Exact replication:** Obtain authors' exact EfficientNet checkpoint and GPT-5 judge version; run full SFT pipeline; compare results; test sensitivity to alternative checkpoints.
2. **Linear separability probe:** Train linear classifiers on frozen encoder features; if accuracy comparable to StethoLM, supports the prefix token compression assumption.
3. **Instruction diversity ablation:** Train on single- or two-task subsets; evaluate on full suite; collapse on omitted tasks would validate need for broad task coverage in training.
