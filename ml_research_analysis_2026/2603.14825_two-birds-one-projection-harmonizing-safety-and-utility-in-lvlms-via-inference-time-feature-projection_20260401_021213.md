# Two Birds, One Projection: Harmonizing Safety and Utility in LVLMs via Inference-time Feature Projection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14825  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14825  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** On LLaVA-7B, TBOP reduces attack success rate on MM-SafetyBench from 38.86% to 5.09% and increases MME utility from 1715.13 to 1870.17, achieving simultaneous safety and visual reasoning improvements.

## Executive Summary
Large vision-language models (LVLMs) often suffer from a safety-utility trade‑off: improvements in visual reasoning can increase vulnerability to jailbreak attacks, while safety‑aligned training may degrade task performance. This paper identifies *modality‑induced bias*—a low‑dimensional directional shift in cross‑modal hidden states caused by imperfect vision‑LLM coupling—as a shared root cause. The proposed method, TBOP, removes this bias by projecting cross‑modal features onto the null space of the identified nuisance subspace during inference. Evaluated on LLaVA‑7B, TBOP reduces attack success rates by 88% and raises visual reasoning scores by 9% across multiple benchmarks, breaking the traditional trade‑off with only a single forward pass and a 60× speedup over prior test‑time adaptation approaches.

## Method Summary
TBOP operates in two phases. First, an *offline* stage builds a small anchor dataset of text prompts (both safety‑related and utility‑focused). Each prompt is processed with a blank image to compute a *shift vector* \(d = h(\text{text}, \text{blank}) - h(\text{text})\), capturing the modality‑induced perturbation at a chosen layer. A singular value decomposition (SVD) on the matrix of shift vectors yields the top‑\(k\) right singular vectors \(\hat{V}_k\), spanning the nuisance subspace \(W\). Second, during *online* inference, the hidden state \(h(\text{text}, \text{img})\) is extracted at the same layer, then orthogonally projected: \(\tilde{h} = (I - \hat{V}_k\hat{V}_k^\top)h\). This removes the bias component while preserving the ideal multimodal representation \(h^*\) (assumed orthogonal to \(W\)), after which the LM head continues autoregressive generation. The projection is applied in a single pass, requiring no model retraining or gradient updates.

## Key Results
- **Simultaneous safety and utility gains:** On LLaVA‑7B, attack success rate on MM‑SafetyBench drops from 38.86% to 5.09% while MME utility increases from 1715.13 to 1870.17.  
- **Trade‑off broken:** Similar improvements observed across all evaluated benchmarks, contradicting the conventional safety‑utility compromise.  
- **Efficiency:** Single‑pass inference with ~60× speedup compared to prior test‑time adaptation (ETA) methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Modality‑induced bias—a consistent directional shift in cross‑modal features caused by imperfect vision‑LLM coupling—is a shared root cause degrading both safety (increasing jailbreak susceptibility) and utility (reducing visual reasoning performance).  
- **Mechanism:** When visual inputs are integrated, the LLM backbone's safety‑aligned representation space is perturbed along a principal direction \(b\). This shift weakens refusal mechanisms for harmful queries and corrupts visual‑grounded reasoning. The authors formalize this via representation decomposition:  
  \(h(\text{text}, \text{img}) = h^*(\text{text}, \text{img}) + \alpha \cdot [h(\text{text}, \text{blank}) - h(\text{text})]\), where the difference term captures the modality‑induced shift.  
- **Core assumption:** The visual modality activation (even from a blank image) induces a structured, low‑dimensional perturbation in the LLM's hidden states, and this same subspace undermines both safety and utility.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Cosine similarity ≥0.6 between bias directions extracted from safety (MM‑SafetyBench) and utility (MME) datasets; steering features along either direction degrades performance in both tasks.  

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Orthogonal projection of cross‑modal features onto the null space of the identified nuisance subspace \(W\) removes the bias component while preserving the ideal multimodal semantic content \(h^*\), thereby mitigating vulnerability and enhancing feature quality.  
- **Mechanism:** Let \(V\) be an orthonormal basis for \(W\). The corrected feature is \(\tilde{h} = (I - VV^\top)h\). By construction (Lemmas 1‑2), the bias vector lies in \(W\) and \(h^*\) is orthogonal to \(W\). Thus, the projection annihilates the bias (\(VV^\top\Delta h = 0\)) and retains the ideal component (\((I-VV^\top)h^* = h^*\)).  
- **Core assumption:** The ideal cross‑modal representation \(h^*\) (bias‑free) is orthogonal to the nuisance subspace \(W\); the bias is an additive perturbation not entangled with semantic content.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Formal preservation and elimination properties (Eqs. 6‑7); visualization of features moving toward text‑only (safer) regions after projection; simultaneous ASR reduction and utility gain across benchmarks.  

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The nuisance subspace \(W\) can be practically approximated from a small, mixed anchor dataset (safety + utility samples) via SVD on shift vectors, yielding a robust basis \(\hat{V}_k\) for inference‑time projection that generalizes across tasks.  
- **Mechanism:** Compute shift vectors \(d^{(i)} = h(\text{text}_i, \text{blank}) - h(\text{text}_i)\) for \(N\) text prompts. Perform SVD: \(D = U\Sigma\hat{V}^\top\). Use top‑\(k\) right singular vectors \(\hat{V}_k\) as basis. Projection \(\tilde{h} = (I - \hat{V}_k\hat{V}_k^\top)h\) removes components along the dominant bias directions.  
- **Core assumption:** A finite, mixed sample of text prompts suffices to capture the principal axes of modality‑induced shift, and these axes are stable across safety/utility inputs; the rank \(k\) captures most variance (EVR >85% with \(k=32\)).  
- **Evidence anchors:** Consistent gains for \(k=16,32,64\); mixed anchor (MM‑SafetyBench + MME) yields best results; safety‑only or utility‑only anchors still help both tasks, confirming shared direction.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Modality‑induced representation shift**  
*Why needed here:* TBOP's entire premise rests on quantifying and removing the systematic feature perturbation caused by adding visual input. Understanding the decomposition \(h = h^* + \alpha\Delta h\) is essential to grasp why projection works.  
*Quick check question:* If \(h(\text{text}, \text{blank}) - h(\text{text})\) consistently points in a similar direction for different texts, what does that imply about the visual modality's effect on the LLM's representation space?  

**Concept: Orthogonal projection onto a subspace**  
*Why needed here:* The core operation—\((I - VV^\top)h\)—is a geometric intervention. Engineers must understand that this zeroes components in \(\text{span}(V)\) while preserving orthogonal components, assuming \(h^* \perp W\).  
*Quick check question:* Given a matrix \(V\) with orthonormal columns, what is the geometric interpretation of \(VV^\top h\)? What does \((I - VV^\top)h\) represent?  

**Concept: Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) for subspace estimation**  
*Why needed here:* TBOP approximates the nuisance subspace via SVD on shift vectors. Engineers need to know how SVD identifies dominant directions (right singular vectors) from a data matrix.  
*Quick check question:* In \(D = U\Sigma V^\top\), what do the columns of \(V\) represent? How does setting \(k\) (number of top singular vectors) control the approximation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Anchor Prompts (with blank image) -> Feature Extractor -> Shift Vectors -> SVD -> Basis Vectors (stored)  
Input (text, image) -> Feature Extractor -> Projection Layer (using Basis Vectors) -> LM Head -> Output  

**Critical path:**  
1. **Offline:** Build anchor dataset of text prompts. Process with blank image to extract features; compute shift vectors \(d = h(\text{text},\text{blank}) - h(\text{text})\). Perform SVD on matrix \(D\) to obtain top‑\(k\) right singular vectors \(\hat{V}_k\). Store \(\hat{V}_k\).  
2. **Online:** For each input (text, image), run forward pass to extract hidden state \(h\) at the last input token's final layer. Compute \(\tilde{h} = (I - \hat{V}_k\hat{V}_k^\top) h\). Pass \(\tilde{h}\) through LM head and continue autoregressive generation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Anchor dataset composition:* Mixed safety+utility anchors yield best generalization; safety‑only may under‑approximate \(W\).  
- *Rank \(k\):* Higher \(k\) captures more bias variance but risks discarding useful signal if \(h^*\) not perfectly orthogonal. EVR >85% (\(k=32\)) is effective.  
- *Blank image choice:* A true blank image maximizes modality activation without content; random noise works but is suboptimal.  
- *Insertion layer:* Using the last input token's final layer balances multimodal signal concentration and output proximity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Utility drop:* If projection harms reasoning (MME/MM‑ Vet scores decline), likely due to non‑orthogonal \(h^*\) or excessive \(k\).  
- *No safety gain:* If attack success rate remains high, the estimated nuisance subspace is inadequate (poor anchor coverage, too small \(k\)).  
- *Inconsistent gains across models:* May indicate that the modality‑induced shift magnitude varies; TBOP effectiveness scales with bias strength.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Bias consistency check:** Compute top‑3 principal components of shift vectors from safety (MM‑SafetyBench) and utility (MME) anchor sets; expect cosine similarity ≥0.6.  
2. **Projection sanity check:** Apply projection to a harmful query with blank image; verify output becomes a refusal and that L2 distance between original and projected features is modest.  
3. **End‑to‑end validation:** Deploy TBOP on vanilla LLaVA‑7B with \(k=32\) and mixed anchors. Measure ASR on MM‑SafetyBench and MME score; expect ASR ~5% and MME ~1870.

## Limitations
- **Core assumption fragility:** Projection may damage utility if the ideal representation \(h^*\) is not orthogonal to the nuisance subspace \(W\); bias could be entangled with semantics.  
- **Generalizability constraints:** Effectiveness relies on the modality‑induced shift being low‑rank and stable; anchor dataset choice and text distribution can impact performance.  
- **External validation gap:** The shared bias direction is supported by internal SVD/cosine analysis but lacks independent replication; neighboring works identify modality gaps as safety risks but not as joint utility degraders.

## Confidence
- **Empirical results (ASR reduction, utility gains) on LLaVA‑7B:** High  
- **Existence of shared modality‑induced bias direction:** Medium  
- **Orthogonality of ideal representation to nuisance subspace:** Low
