# 3D CBCT Artefact Removal Using Perpendicular Score-Based Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06300
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06300
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** TPDM improves projection inpainting (SSIM +1.8%, PSNR +2.87 dB over 2D DPS) and matches reconstruction quality with 40% faster sampling.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Tangent Perpendicular Diffusion Models (TPDM) for 3D cone-beam CT (CBCT) metal artifact reduction. Existing 2D diffusion inpainting methods ignore inter-slice correlations, causing inconsistencies. TPDM uses two perpendicular score-based diffusion models—a primary model on original projection slices and a secondary on orthogonal slices—combined via an alternating sampling scheme that conditions only the primary on measurements. Evaluated on synthetic pig mandible data across four CBCT scanners, TPDM outperforms 2D DPS in projection inpainting metrics and achieves comparable 3D reconstruction quality with significantly reduced sampling time.

## Method Summary
TPDM trains two unconditional score-based diffusion models (variance-exploding SDE, T=1000) on artifact-free 2D projection slices: the primary model on `i-j` planes (original direction) and the secondary on `j-k` planes (perpendicular). During sampling, the algorithm alternates between the models every `K=2` steps. The primary model steps incorporate Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) conditioning on the corrupted measurement volume `Y` to inpaint metal-affected regions. The secondary model steps are unconditional, enforcing 3D coherence by modeling natural correlations in the perpendicular orientation. The combined 3D score function is the sum of the two 2D scores, approximating the full 3D distribution without full 3D training.

## Key Results
- **Projection inpainting:** TPDM SSIM 0.9290±0.0085 vs. 2D DPS 0.9110±0.0152; PSNR 35.87±1.18 dB vs. 33.00±1.71 dB; RMSE 0.0037±0.0010 vs. 0.0044±0.0010.
- **Reconstructed volumes:** SSIM 0.9850±0.0078, PSNR 50.39±3.88 dB, RMSE 0.0008±0.0003 (similar to DPS).
- **Sampling speed:** 40% faster than DPS (8.5 hours vs. 14 hours).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Combining two perpendicular 2D diffusion models approximates a 3D distribution without full 3D training.  
**Mechanism:** The primary model learns score functions for slices along the original projection direction (`i-j` planes), while the secondary model learns scores for perpendicular slices (`j-k`). The combined score (sum of the two 2D scores) assumes independence across dimensions, enabling sampling of a coherent 3D volume by alternately applying each model.  
**Core assumption:** The 3D data distribution factorizes along two orthogonal axes, and each 2D model captures sufficient marginal statistics.  
**Evidence anchors:** Equation 6 decomposes the 3D score; Algorithm 1 alternates models; Lee et al. [9] uses similar perpendicular factorization.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Alternating sampling between models enforces cross-plane consistency during generation.  
**Mechanism:** The reverse diffusion cycles `K` steps with the primary model (conditioned on measurements) followed by `K` steps with the secondary model (unconditional). This alternation propagates information across dimensions—the primary ensures fidelity to measured projections, the secondary smooths across perpendicular slices.  
**Core assumption:** Sequential application of perpendicular score functions converges to a joint 3D distribution matching the data.  
**Evidence anchors:** Algorithm 1’s conditional step structure; TPDM’s 40% speedup over DPS suggests secondary model accelerates convergence.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Conditioning only the primary model on measurements prevents overfitting to artifacts while leveraging 2D diffusion efficiency.  
**Mechanism:** The DPS gradient (`−λ∇‖A( x̂₀)−y‖²`) is applied only in primary model steps, guiding inpainting in the original projection slices. The secondary model operates unconditionally, learning natural anatomical correlations from cleaner perpendicular contexts.  
**Core assumption:** Implant artifacts are confined to specific projection angles; perpendicular slices contain unbiased structural information.  
**Evidence anchors:** TPDM outperforms DPS (primary-only) in projection metrics; conditioning is explicitly applied only to primary model in Algorithm 1.

## Foundational Learning
- **Score-based diffusion models (SBDMs):** Learn data distributions via score function estimation; reverse SDE generates samples. *Why needed:* Provides generative prior for inverse problems like inpainting.  
  *Quick check:* How does the noise schedule `σₜ` affect sample quality in a variance-exploding SDE?
- **Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS):** Applies gradient-based measurement conditioning to diffusion models. *Why needed:* Steers sampling toward consistency with corrupted CBCT projections.  
  *Quick check:* What is the role of hyperparameter `λ` in balancing data fidelity and generative prior?
- **CBCT projection geometry and FDK reconstruction:** Understanding slice orientations (`i-j`, `j-k`) and filtered back-projection is essential for projection-domain processing. *Why needed:* TPDM operates on projection stacks; errors may average out during FDK but affect inpainting metrics.  
  *Quick check:* Why might errors in projections degrade inpainting quality even if they average out in reconstruction?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Clean `i-j` projections → Primary score model (U-Net) → Primary score function; Clean `j-k` projections → Secondary score model (U-Net) → Secondary score function; Measurement `Y` and operator `A(·)` → Combined score (Eq.6) → Alternating sampling loop (Algorithm 1: primary steps with DPS conditioning, secondary unconditional) → Inpainted projection stack `X₀` → FDK reconstruction → Volume `V`.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Train primary model on `i-j` slices of artifact-free data.  
  2. Train secondary model on `j-k` slices.  
  3. For diffusion timestep `i = T-1` to `0`: if `mod(i,K) ≠ 0` use primary model with DPS gradient; else use secondary model unconditionally.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - `K=2` balances 3D coherence and speed; larger `K` risks divergence from measurements, smaller slows sampling.  
  - Unconditioned secondary model assumes artifacts don’t corrupt perpendicular slices; may fail otherwise.  
  - 2D training reduces data/VRAM needs but relies on factorization assumption.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Inconsistent structures across adjacent slices (low inter-slice SSIM).  
  - Persistent metal streaks in reconstructions despite projection inpainting.  
  - Secondary model outputs anatomically implausible patterns (underfitting).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Train primary model only (2D DPS baseline); compare projection SSIM/PSNR to TPDM—expect TPDM higher.  
  2. Ablate `K` over `{1,2,4,8}`; measure reconstruction SSIM and sampling time—expect optimal `K=2`.  
  3. Visualize `j-k` slices after secondary-only sampling; expect coherent anatomy without implants; if noisy/structured artifacts appear, secondary model underfit.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Q1:** How does TPDM perform on real clinical CBCT data? *Basis:* Lacks publicly available paired datasets with real artifacts. *Why unresolved:* No real-world validation; synthetic simulation may not capture authentic artifact patterns. *Resolving evidence:* Release of clinical paired projection datasets with known geometry, followed by evaluation.
- **Q2:** Could a full 3D diffusion model outperform TPDM with sufficient data? *Basis:* Paper suggests 3D models need more data; no direct comparison. *Why unresolved:* Trade-off between data efficiency and 3D contextual learning remains unknown. *Resolving evidence:* Train a 3D diffusion model on large-scale data and benchmark against TPDM.
- **Q3:** Is `K=2` optimal for alternating sampling? *Basis:* Fixed arbitrarily without ablation. *Why unresolved:* No analysis of frequency trade-off between conditioning and coherence. *Resolving evidence:* Ablation over `K ∈ {1,2,4,8}` measuring projection/reconstruction metrics.
- **Q4:** Would conditioning the secondary model improve 3D consistency? *Basis:* Only primary model conditioned; secondary unconditional despite contributing to volume. *Why unresolved:* Secondary-plane pixels influence reconstruction via back-projection. *Resolving evidence:* Apply DPS gradient in secondary steps and compare.
- **Q5:** Can scatter correction be integrated into TPDM’s forward model? *Basis:* Scatter not modeled; paper suggests potential integration. *Why unresolved:* Synergy between diffusion inpainting and scatter correction unexplored. *Resolving evidence:* Extend `A(·)` to include scatter forward model and re-evaluate.

## Limitations
- **Missing implementation details:** Exact U-Net architecture, implant mask specifications, and training hyperparameters are unreferenced, hindering reproduction.
- **Factorization assumption:** Perpendicular decomposition may fail for non-local metal streaks; unconditioned secondary model could propagate inconsistencies.
- **Synthetic-only validation:** Evaluated on pig mandible simulations; real clinical robustness unproven due to lack of real datasets.

## Confidence
- **High:** Projection-domain superiority over 2D DPS (SSIM +0.018, PSNR +2.87 dB) is statistically validated.
- **Medium:** Reconstruction parity with DPS and 40% speedup; depends on correct `K=2` tuning and implementation fidelity.
- **Low:** General validity of perpendicular factorization without direct comparison to full 3D diffusion benchmarks.
