# Modeling Epistemic Uncertainty in Social Perception via Rashomon Set Agents

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.20750
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20750
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** In simulated classroom networks, collective misperception of peer rankings systematically accumulates over six exam periods (DPAE ↑ 0.066→0.124, Spearman ρ ↓ 0.934→0.876) despite periodic anchoring to true exam scores, an effect mitigated by the model's subjective graph heterogeneity and trust gating.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a multi-agent framework to model how epistemic uncertainty and divergent social perceptions emerge in bounded, information-limited environments like classrooms. Each LLM-driven "student" agent maintains a subjective social graph that dictates which peers' beliefs it can access. Agents exchange probabilistic judgments annotated with uncertainty, update their own beliefs using retrieved information weighted by dynamic, LLM-derived trust scores, and periodicallyanchor to an exogenous signal (exam scores). Over six real-world exam timepoints in a middle school, the collective ranking error increases, demonstrating a systematic accumulation of misperception. Ablations confirm that both subjective graph heterogeneity and trust gating are necessary to achieve the observed (though still imperfect) performance, which outperforms classic DeGroot consensus models by avoiding premature convergence to a wrong consensus.

## Method Summary
The framework instantiates one agent per student in a real classroom network. Each agent's "subjective social graph"—a subset of the actual peer network—constrains its information access via retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). At each timestep (corresponding to an exam), agents query their accessible peers' stored beliefs about who is performing well, incorporate these into their own probabilistic assessment of the class ranking (expressed with confidence intervals), and update internal trust scores for peers based on perceived accuracy. Crucially, agents also observe the true exam score of one randomly selected peer each period, using this as an anchoring signal to partially correct their beliefs. The core simulation loops this perception-update cycle across six actual exam dates, measuring the aggregate deviation of the agents' consensus ranking from the true ranking (DPAE) and the correlation (Spearman's ρ).

## Key Results
*   Collective misperception accumulates over time: DPAE increases from 0.066 to 0.124, and Spearman correlation decreases from 0.934 to 0.876 across six exam periods.
*   Both the subjective graph heterogeneity and the trust-gated belief update mechanism are necessary; ablating either leads to worse final ranking accuracy.
*   Compared to a DeGroot model, the proposed agent framework avoids consensus collapse to a single wrong ranking and achieves a lower final DPAE.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The model attributes persistent misperception to the interaction of three constrained conditions mirroring real classrooms: (1) **local visibility** (subjective graphs limit information sources), (2) **noisy, uncertain communication** (agents provide probabilistic assessments with confidence), and (3) **selective credibility weighting** (trust scores gate whose beliefs are incorporated). Even with periodic exposure to ground-truth signals (exam scores), the combination of incomplete information and error propagation through a trust network prevents full convergence to reality. The Rashomon Set concept manifests as multiple stable, divergent collective perceptions that can emerge from the same underlying data due to these bounded cognitive constraints.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Epistemic Uncertainty:** The irreducible uncertainty in knowledge due to limited information and evidence, not just stochastic noise. *Why needed:* Distinguishes the model's "bounded rational" agents from purely noisy or Bayesian ideal agents. *Quick check:* Do agents' stated confidences correlate with their actual error rates and network position?
*   **Subjective Social Graph:** An agent's personalized, potentially inaccurate, mental model of its peer relationships and influence pathways. *Why needed:* Formalizes the core constraint of local, imperfect information access. *Quick check:* Does initial graph accuracy predict an agent's final misperception level?
*   **Trust-Gated Belief Updates:** A mechanism where an agent weights incoming social information by dynamically computed trust scores for the source. *Why needed:* Prevents indiscriminate adoption of all available beliefs, introducing a realistic filter. *Quick check:* Is there a correlation between a peer's perceived trustworthiness and their actual ranking accuracy?
*   **Rashomon Set:** The set of all models/hypotheses that fit observed data equally well. *Why needed:* Explains how multiple divergent collective perceptions can be stable given limited data. *Quick check:* Do different random seeds produce meaningfully different final consensus rankings?
*   **Anchoring to Exogenous Signal:** Using periodic observation of a ground-truth fact (an exam score) to recalibrate beliefs. *Why needed:* Provides a minimal corrective force against complete divergence. *Quick check:* How does the frequency or randomness of anchoring affect final DPAE?
*   **DeGroot Dynamics:** A classical model of consensus where agents repeatedly average neighbors' beliefs. *Why needed:* Serves as a key baseline that fails under the same constraints (collapses to wrong consensus). *Quick check:* Does the DeGroot baseline reach a stable but incorrect ranking faster than the proposed model?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    LLM Agent (with Subjective Graph & Belief State) -> RAG Query ( Against Peers' Stated Beliefs) -> Probabilistic Assessment (With Uncertainty) -> Trust Score Update (Based on Perceived Peer Accuracy) -> Belief State Update (Trust-Weighted Blend) -> [Periodic] Exogenous Anchor (True Exam Score Observation)
*   **Critical Path:** The core loop runs on each agent: 1) retrieve accessible peers' latest belief vectors, 2) generate a new self-assessment (a distribution over rankings), 3) update trust for peers based on how their past predictions matched the latest anchor, 4) blend retrieved beliefs into own state using trust weights, 5) optionally, incorporate the new anchor directly.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   *LLM Expressiveness vs. Cost/Stability:* Using an LLM for all cognitive steps enables rich, contextual reasoning but is expensive, slow, and can produce inconsistent outputs across calls.
    *   *Subjective Graph Fidelity vs. Scalability:* Detailed, psychologically plausible graph construction is computationally heavy; simpler random or density-based graphs scale better but lose Individuality.
    *   *Dynamic Trust Complexity vs. Tractability:* Computing nuanced trust from LLM evaluations is powerful but introduces another layer of LLM calls and potential for trust pathologies (e.g., collusion, distrust loops).
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   *Graph Collapse:* All agents' subjective graphs quickly converge to identical (and likely wrong) structures, eliminating heterogeneity.
    *   *Trust Monoculture:* All agents assign equally high or low trust to everyone, nullifying the gating mechanism.
    *   *Oscillating Beliefs:* Agents' rankings flip-flop between states without anchoring stability, indicating a chaotic update rule or pathological trust cycling.
    *   *Hallucinated Graph Edges:* Agents consistently reference peers not in their supposed subjective graph, breaking the core constraint.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Subjective Graph Grounding Test:** Replace LLM-generated subjective graphs with empirically derived ones from a small student survey (e.g., "who do you talk to about grades?") and compare misperception trajectories to the default simulated graphs.
    2.  **Ablation: Trust vs. Uniform Weighting:** Run the full model but replace the trust-weighted belief update with a simple uniform average over retrieved peers' beliefs. Measure the change in DPAE accumulation rate.
    3.  **Stress Test: Anchor Removal:** Disable the exogenous exam score anchoring entirely. Quantify how rapidly and severely DPAE diverges compared to the anchored condition, testing the mechanism's reliance on corrective signals.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** Can the Rashomon Set Agents framework generalize to non-classroom social networks (e.g., online communities, workplaces)?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "Future directions include extending the framework to other settings of social learning and opinion formation."
*   **Why unresolved:** The current validation is limited to middle-school classrooms; generalizability to other network topologies, cultural contexts, or age groups is unknown.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Testing the framework on diverse real-world social network datasets (e.g., Twitter interactions, corporate collaboration networks) with appropriate exogenous signals to see if similar misperception accumulation patterns emerge.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** How would incorporating fine-grained behavioral traces (e.g., real-time interaction logs, multimodal signals) beyond questionnaire-based mappings affect the model's predictive accuracy and realism?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion notes: "incorporating richer psychological and contextual variables... future extensions could incorporate more fine-grained behavioral traces."
*   **Why unresolved:** The current model uses static questionnaire-derived parameters (e.g., αi for social anxiety) and lacks dynamic behavioral data that might capture more nuanced individual differences.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Integrating high-frequency interaction data (e.g., smartphone sensing, chat logs) into the subjective graph construction and trust gating mechanisms, then comparing model outputs against ground-truth perception measurements (e.g., longitudinal surveys) to assess fidelity improvements.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** What specific intervention strategies (e.g., targeted information exposure, trust calibration) can mitigate the accumulation of collective misperception in this model?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion mentions: "exploring optimal information interventions and guidance strategies under constrained cognition."
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates misperception accumulation but does not explore how to counteract it within the model's constraints (local visibility, noisy interactions).
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Designing simulation experiments where external interventions (e.g., providing certain agents with broader visibility, adjusting trust weighting schemes) are introduced at different time points, then measuring reductions in DPAE and uncertainty compared to baseline.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** How does the computational cost and fidelity of Rashomon Set Agents scale with network size and density, and what approximations might be necessary for large-scale deployment?
*   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The conclusion states: "the scalability of the framework to larger or more complex network structures has not yet been validated." This implies an open question about scalability.
*   **Why unresolved:** The current experiments use classrooms of ~40 students; scaling to thousands of agents (e.g., online platforms) may face tractability issues due to LLM call overhead and RAG operations.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Running stress tests with synthetic networks of varying sizes (e.g., 100, 1000, 10000 nodes) while measuring runtime, memory usage, and output stability; identifying threshold effects or necessary algorithmic simplifications (e.g., approximate RAG, trust score caching).

## Limitations
*   **No Human Validation:** The entire behavioral pipeline—subjective graph formation, trust computation, belief articulation—is generated by LLM prompts, not validated against real student data. It is unclear if LLM-simulated agents authentically replicate bounded human social perception.
*   **Over-Simplified Ground Truth:** The model anchors solely to a single, objective exam score per period, ignoring the multitude of conflicting social cues and information sources present in real classrooms.
*   **Black-Box Graph Mechanism:** The process by which an agent's subjective social graph is initialized and potentially evolves is not empirically justified or connected to psychological theory of network perception.

## Confidence
*   **Core Simulation Mechanism & Internal Dynamics:** Medium Confidence. Logic is consistent and ablations support designed roles, but foundational premise (LLMs as valid proxies for bounded agents) is not empirically established.
*   **Comparative Advantage vs. DeGroot:** High Confidence (within simulation context). The outcome (avoiding consensus collapse, lower DPAE) is a clear, reproducible result within the defined experimental framework.
*   **Generalizability to Real Social Systems:** Low Confidence. Claims about epistemic uncertainty in actual classrooms are highly speculative without validation against human behavioral data; parameterizations are likely non-transferable.

## Next Checks
1.  **Human-Agent Correspondence Study:** Conduct a controlled experiment with real students. Collect their subjective graphs, annotated peer performance beliefs, and trust assessments at multiple timepoints. Compare the structure and temporal dynamics (e.g., misperception accumulation rate) against a matched simulation run.
2.  **Sensitivity Analysis on Graph Heterogeneity:** Systematically vary initial graph heterogeneity (e.g., perceived vs. actual tie accuracy, density variance across agents). Measure impact on final DPAE and trajectory variance to identify thresholds where key findings hold or break.
3.  **Longitudinal Real-Network Pilot:** Apply the *exact* agent framework (prompts, update rules) to a publicly available longitudinal social network dataset with academic performance (e.g., school surveys & grades). Replace LLM responses with a rule-based "oracle" using *actual* survey data to simulate updates, testing if the *model structure* alone can reproduce known perception error patterns.
