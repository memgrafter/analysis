# Bayesian Optimization of Partially Known Systems using Hybrid Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.11199
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11199
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Hybrid Bayesian optimization, embedding Gaussian process surrogates as equality constraints within mechanistic models, achieved orders-of-magnitude lower regret and convergence in as few as one iteration on a flash unit design problem, where standard BO failed to converge within 25 iterations.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of slow convergence in Bayesian optimization (BO) when optimizing expensive, partially known engineering systems. It proposes a hybrid BO framework that integrates Gaussian process (GP) surrogates for unknown physical relationships (e.g., activity coefficients) as stochastic equality constraints within a mechanistic nonlinear program. This hybrid model is solved via sample-average approximation. Tested on an in-silico flash distillation unit optimization, the approach dramatically outperforms standard BO, achieving vastly superior design objectives with far fewer iterations by hard-wiring known conservation laws into the search process.

## Method Summary
The method formulates the optimization as a stochastic nonlinear program (NLP). Known mechanistic equations (mass balances, Antoine’s law) are enforced as deterministic constraints. Unknown quantities (ln γ₁) are modeled by a GP, whose mean and Cholesky functions are embedded as additional deterministic constraints, with GP randomness introduced via scenario-based sampled variables ξ. The acquisition function, expected improvement (EI), is approximated by the sample-average of scenario-specific improvements (SAA-EI) over S=25 scenarios. Each BO iteration involves: fitting the GP to current data (T, x₁, ln γ₁), formulating and solving the SAA-EI acquisition NLP to propose the next (T, p) evaluation point, and updating the dataset with the true system response.

## Key Results
- Hybrid BO converged to a near-optimal design objective in a single iteration on the flash unit case study, while standard BO showed no convergence after 25 iterations.
- The hybrid approach reduced regret by orders of magnitude compared to standard BO within a few iterations.
- By embedding mass conservation constraints, the hybrid model eliminated infeasible proposals and focused the search on physically plausible regions, achieving vastly superior sample efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hybrid BO mechanism leverages the known mechanistic model to structure the search space with physically guaranteed feasibility (e.g., mass balance). The GP surrogate is only tasked with learning the unknown residual (activity coefficient), which drastically reduces the dimensionality and uncertainty the GP must handle. By embedding the GP directly as constraints within the acquisition optimization, the expected improvement is evaluated only over designs that are *mechanistically consistent* under the current GP belief. This prevents the acquisition function from suggesting evaluations in regions that violate basic physics, focusing experimental budget on truly informative and feasible areas.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bayesian Optimization**: Sequential model-based optimization for expensive black-box functions. *Why needed*: Framework for efficient global optimization with few evaluations. *Quick check*: Understand acquisition functions (EI, LCB) and GP surrogate basics.
- **Gaussian Processes (GP)**: Non-parametric probabilistic models for function regression. *Why needed*: To model the unknown activity coefficient with uncertainty. *Quick check*: Know kernel choices (Matern52), hyperparameter estimation, and posterior mean/variance formulas.
- **Stochastic Programming & Sample-Average Approximation (SAA)**: Framework for optimization under uncertainty; SAA approximates expectations by averaging over scenarios. *Why needed*: To handle the randomness in GP predictions within the acquisition NLP. *Quick check*: Verify that increasing scenario count S improves approximation of the expected EI.
- **Mechanistic Modeling**: First-principles equations describing system physics (e.g., mass balances). *Why needed*: Provides hard constraints that reduce the effective search space. *Quick check*: Confirm model equations (e.g., Raoult’s law, Antoine) are algebraically consistent.
- **Nonlinear Programming (NLP)**: Optimization with nonlinear objective/constraints. *Why needed*: The hybrid model and acquisition are NLPs. *Quick check*: Ensure CasADi/IPOPT implementation is correctly scaled and bounded.
- **Automatic Differentiation (AD)**: Computation of gradients via algorithmic differentiation. *Why needed*: Critical for efficient gradient-based NLP solvers with embedded GP functions. *Quick check*: Validate gradients of GP mean/Cholesky functions against finite differences.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:
Data (D_T,x,γ) → GP Fitting (hyperparameters, mean/Cholesky functions) → Hybrid NLP Formulation (mechanistic constraints + GP constraints) → SAA Discretization (S scenarios) → Acquisition NLP (minimize SAA-EI) → Proposed (T,p) → True System Evaluation (NRTL) → Update Data → Repeat.

**Critical Path**: GP fitting → Acquisition NLP solve. The quality of the GP surrogate and the ability to solve the non-convex acquisition NLP with many scenarios are the two main bottlenecks.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- **SAA vs. Continuous EI**: SAA is simple but yields piecewise-constant EI that can become zero, stalling progress; continuous approximations (e.g., Gaussian moment matching) are smoother but more complex to derive.
- **Scenario Count S**: Larger S gives a better EI approximation but increases NLP size and solve time; S=25 was used as a compromise.
- **Multi-start Strategy**: Essential for non-convex acquisition NLP and GP hyperparameter fitting; increases computational cost but mitigates local optima.

**Failure Signatures**:
- SAA-EI surface becomes zero over most of the domain after a few iterations, halting exploration.
- Acquisition NLP fails to converge or consistently returns the same point, indicating poor multi-start coverage or ill-conditioned constraints.
- GP predictions become erratic (huge variance) with few data points, causing acquisition optimization to suggest out-of-bounds or infeasible points.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. **GP Embedding Verification**: On a fixed dataset, implement GP mean/Cholesky in CasADi and solve a trivial acquisition NLP (e.g., maximize posterior variance). Compare solution against analytical GP acquisition (e.g., “pure exploration”) to validate constraint embedding and gradients.
2. **SAA-EI Sensitivity Test**: With a trained GP, compute SAA-EI over a grid for increasing S (e.g., 5, 25, 100). Plot the resulting acquisition landscape to see if non-zero region shrinks artificially and if the optimum stabilizes.
3. **Single-Iteration Hybrid vs. Standard BO**: Run one full hybrid BO iteration and one standard BO iteration (using a GP on the direct objective) on the flash unit from the same 3 initial points. Compare the proposed (T,p) and underlying GP predictions to see how the hybrid model's constraint enforcement changes the search direction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can the hybrid Bayesian optimization framework be extended to incorporate known inequality constraints and to automatically learn and adapt to unknown infeasible regions during the optimization?
- **Open Question 2**: How robust is the hybrid BO approach to modeling errors in the mechanistic component (e.g., missing terms, incorrect parameters), and how can such errors be mitigated?
- **Open Question 3**: The sample‑average approximation (SAA) used to discretize the stochastic program yields an expected improvement acquisition function that often becomes zero over large regions, stalling further progress. Can continuous approximations (e.g., analytical integration or smooth surrogates) provide a more informative acquisition?
- **Open Question 4**: Will the hybrid BO methodology retain its in‑silico advantages when applied to real physical experiments, given practical challenges such as measurement noise, time delays, and safety constraints?

## Limitations
- The core GP embedding library is not publicly available; key implementation details for efficient AD and solver settings are missing, hindering exact replication.
- Evaluation is limited to a single flash unit case study; no ablation studies or tests on other partially known systems are provided.
- Small initial dataset (3 points) risks GP overfitting; no held-out validation or robustness analysis across random seeds is reported.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid BO achieves faster convergence and lower regret than standard BO**: *Medium* – strong in-silico result but limited to one problem and missing code.
- **SAA-EI acquisition with GP constraints is a valid and effective approach**: *Medium‑Low* – formulation is sound, but practical success hinges on unimplemented library details and solver tuning.
- **Enforcing mechanistic constraints improves sample efficiency**: *High* – the core idea is theoretically sound and the empirical result is dramatic.
- **Method extends to other partially known systems**: *Low* – no evidence beyond the flash unit.
