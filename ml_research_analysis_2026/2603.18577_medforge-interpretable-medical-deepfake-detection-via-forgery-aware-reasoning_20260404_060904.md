# MedForge: Interpretable Medical Deepfake Detection via Forgery-aware Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18577
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18577
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: State-of-the-art interpretable medical deepfake detection with 99.23% in-domain accuracy, 90.2% reasoning quality, and robust cross-forgery/model performance (95.93%/93.94%).

## Executive Summary
Medical deepfake detection requires trustworthy, interpretable solutions. Existing detectors are black boxes or post-hoc MLLMs that hallucinate. We introduce MedForge-90K, a benchmark of 90K realistic lesion edits across 19 pathologies with expert-guided reasoning annotations (doctor guidelines + gold edit locations). MedForge-Reasoner employs pre-hoc “localize-then-analyze” reasoning: it predicts suspicious bounding boxes before producing a verdict, trained via Forgery-aware Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) to enforce visual grounding. Experiments show state-of-the-art detection: 99.23% accuracy (In-Domain), +7.65% over SIDA-13B; robust to unseen forgeries/models (Cross-Forgery 95.93%, Cross-Model 93.94%). Reasoning quality, judged by MLLMs, reaches 90.2% average, reducing hallucinations by 16.2%. Ablations confirm GSPO improves grounding (+2.5% judge score) and localization (IoU 0.31).

## Method Summary
The method comprises two main contributions: the MedForge-90K dataset and the MedForge-Reasoner model. The dataset construction involves a writer-editor-diagnoser loop to generate realistic lesion edits. Change Vector Analysis (CVA) computes per-pixel change between real and forged images to obtain manipulation masks, which are converted to bounding boxes. An MLLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) is prompted with these boxes and hierarchical expert guidelines to produce structured chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, ensuring textual rationale is anchored to visual evidence. MedForge-Reasoner uses a pre-trained MLLM backbone (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B) trained in two stages: first, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with LoRA on (image, prompt, bbox+reasoning+label) tuples; second, Forgery-aware Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) where multiple outputs per input are sampled and a reward R = R_bbox + R_form + R_clas is computed. R_bbox measures coverage of the CVA mask, R_form enforces required XML tags, and R_clas penalizes incorrect labels. The policy is updated via importance sampling with a KL penalty, aligning the model to produce grounded, well-structured responses.

## Key Results
- In-domain detection accuracy reaches 99.23%, outperforming SIDA-13B by +7.65%.
- Reasoning quality (judge score) averages 90.2% with a 16.2% reduction in hallucinations.
- The model generalizes robustly: cross-forgery accuracy 95.93% and cross-model accuracy 93.94%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: High-Fidelity, Expert-Annotated Dataset
The MedForge-90K dataset provides high-fidelity, expert-validated reasoning annotations that tightly couple forgery localization with medically coherent analysis. A writer-editor-diagnoser loop iteratively refines prompts to generate realistic lesion edits. CVA computes per-pixel change magnitude between real and forged images; thresholding yields a manipulation mask, which is converted to a bounding box. An MLLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) is then prompted with these boxes and hierarchical expert guidelines (general and modality-specific) to produce structured CoT reasoning, ensuring the textual rationale is anchored to the visual evidence of tampering. Core assumption: CVA accurately identifies the edited region and the expert guidelines are sufficient to steer the MLLM toward medically plausible, visually grounded explanations. Evidence anchors: [abstract] "expert-guided reasoning supervision via doctor inspection guidelines and gold edit locations."; [section 3.2] "We apply Change Vector Analysis (CVA) to compute a per-pixel change magnitude... These modified regions serve as the key visual components... We integrate these CVA-derived coordinates with the hierarchical expert guidelines to construct a visually-grounded reasoning prompt." Break condition: If CVA fails to capture subtle edits (e.g., low-contrast lesions) or the prompting fails to enforce guideline adherence, the annotation quality degrades, leading to noisy supervision.

### Mechanism 2: Pre-hoc Localization-then-Analysis Reasoning
Pre-hoc "localize-then-analyze" reasoning reduces hallucination by forcing the model to commit to a suspicious region before textual justification, making explanations verifiable. The model is trained to generate a sequence `[bbox, reasoning, label]`. The bbox prediction is placed first, anchoring subsequent textual analysis to specific image pixels. This constraint ensures the explanation references inspectable evidence rather than fabricating details. Core assumption: Explicit spatial grounding at the start of generation prevents the model from describing non-existent features or generic patterns. Evidence anchors: [abstract] "pre-hoc 'localize-then-analyze' reasoning: it predicts suspicious bounding boxes before producing a verdict."; [section 4.1] "Given a medical image x, the model is trained to generate a sequence S = [ ˆM_bbox, <reasoning>, ˆy]."; [section 5.4] "w/o Bbox Grounding... its Judge Score collapses to 53.9%... without explicit spatial supervision, the model tends to 'hallucinate' justifications." Break condition: If the model learns to ignore the bbox (e.g., always output a default or irrelevant box) or if the bbox is too coarse to capture subtle artifacts, the grounding fails and hallucinations persist.

### Mechanism 3: Forgery-aware Group Sequence Policy Optimization
Forgery-aware GSPO aligns the model with factual visual evidence by rewarding localization coverage, format compliance, and classification accuracy, thereby reducing hallucinations and improving reasoning quality. GSPO samples multiple outputs per input, computes a reward R = R_bbox + R_form + R_clas, and updates the policy via importance sampling. R_bbox uses a sigmoid-shaped coverage of the CVA mask; R_form incentivizes mandatory CoT tags; R_clas penalizes wrong labels. This multi-faceted reward steers the model toward grounded, well-structured responses. Core assumption: Sequence-level rewards with grouped sampling can effectively reduce hallucination without sacrificing detection accuracy. Evidence anchors: [abstract] "trained via Forgery-aware Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) to enforce visual grounding."; [section 4.3] "Our reward function R(x, yi) is composed of two parts... R_bbox + R_form + R_clas."; [section 5.3] "By incorporating the proposed GSPO... achieves a leading average Judge Score of 90.2%... reducing hallucinations by 16.2%."; [section 5.4] "GSPO w/o R_bbox results in a 0.02 decrease in IoU... full GSPO achieves the best balance." Break condition: Poorly tuned reward weights may cause the model to over-optimize coverage (e.g., large boxes) at the cost of classification, or to exploit format tokens without genuine grounding. Small group size G can make updates noisy.

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component Map
Input: Medical image (resized to 1024×1024) → Visual Encoder (MLLM's ViT) extracts features → Language Decoder (Transformer) generates text auto-regressively → Output Head predicts bbox coordinates (linear layer) and generates CoT tokens. Training stages: Stage 1 (SFT): LoRA fine-tuning on (image, prompt, bbox+reasoning+label) pairs. Stage 2 (GSPO): Reinforcement alignment with reward computation (coverage, format, classification) and policy updates. Reward calculator: computes IoU coverage via CVA mask, checks required XML tags and bbox-label consistency, verifies label correctness.

#### Critical Path
1. Build MedForge-90K: collect real images → writer-editor-diagnoser loop produces forgeries → CVA yields masks/bboxes → MLLM generates guideline-grounded CoT.
2. Initialize model with pre-trained MLLM weights.
3. SFT: train on MedForge-90K to learn output structure and basic reasoning patterns.
4. GSPO: for each batch, sample G outputs, compute reward R for each, calculate advantages, update policy with clipped objective and KL penalty.
5. Inference: input image → visual encoder → generate bbox first → generate reasoning → generate label.

#### Design Tradeoffs
- Accuracy vs Interpretability: Enforcing bbox and reasoning may slightly lower pure detection accuracy (binary 99.42% vs proposed 99.23%) but yields inspectable explanations required for clinical trust.
- Backbone selection: Larger models (Qwen3-VL-Plus) give better reasoning but are costlier; smaller ones (InternVL3.5-8B) are efficient but may sacrifice some accuracy and judge score.
- Reward shaping: R_bbox uses a sigmoid with threshold τ; mis-setting τ could either ignore partial matches or fail to reward high IoU. Balancing weights among R_bbox, R_form, R_clas is critical.

#### Failure Signatures
- High detection accuracy but low judge score: model predicts correct label but with hallucinated or ungrounded reasoning.
- Low IoU (< 0.2) between predicted and gold bbox: localization is poor; mechanism 2 fails.
- Format errors: missing CoT tags, bbox output for real images, or contradictory bbox/label.
- Large performance drop in cross-model or cross-forgery settings: indicates overfitting to training generator artifacts or forgery types.

#### First 3 Experiments
1. In-domain evaluation: Train on full MedForge-90K, test on held-out test set; report accuracy, F1, IoU, judge score. Confirms basic learning.
2. Ablation on grounding: Train a variant that predicts only label + reasoning (no bbox supervision) and compare judge score; expected collapse.
3. Cross-model robustness: Train while excluding 4 editors (e.g., Stable Diffusion, GPT-Image), test on those excluded editors; measure performance drop to assess generalization.

## Limitations
- Forgery Localization Fidelity: CVA masks may miss subtle, low-contrast edits or introduce false positives in noisy medical images, potentially poisoning reasoning supervision.
- Clinical Reasoning Validity: MLLM-generated "expert-grounded" reasoning is evaluated by other MLLMs, not clinicians. Unknown whether the rationales are clinically accurate, useful, and free of domain hallucinations in practice.
- Generalization Scope: Robustness is demonstrated on a curated set of 19 pathologies and 4 editing models. Unknown performance on completely unseen modalities, acquisition protocols, or real-world unauthorized edits.

## Next Checks
1. External Clinical Validation: Deploy MedForge-Reasoner on a completely independent, hospital-scale dataset containing both authentic and real (not synthesized) altered medical images. Measure detection accuracy, localization IoU, and have clinicians blind-rate explanation plausibility.
2. Failure Mode Dissection: Systematically analyze all test cases where predicted IoU < 0.2 or judge score < 70%. Categorize failure causes: CVA mask error, guideline mismatch, MLLM factual error, or unseen artifact. Quantify prevalence of each.
3. Reward Hacking Probe: Stress-test GSPO by intentionally corrupting 10% of CVA masks (e.g., random shifts, empty masks). Monitor if reward-driven learning descends into format compliance (correct tags) while ignoring visual grounding (low IoU), exposing reward specification fragility.
