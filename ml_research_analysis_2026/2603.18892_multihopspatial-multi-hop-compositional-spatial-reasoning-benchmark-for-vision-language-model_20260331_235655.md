# MultihopSpatial: Multi-hop Compositional Spatial Reasoning Benchmark for Vision-Language Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18892
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18892
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduced Acc@50IoU metric that exposes a drastic gap between MCQ accuracy and grounded performance; post‑training with MultihopSpatial‑Train via GRPO improves Qwen3‑VL‑4B’s Acc@50IoU from 31.0% to 53.8% and boosts downstream VLA task success.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the lack of benchmarks for multi‑hop compositional spatial reasoning and visual grounding in vision‑language models (VLMs). It introduces MultihopSpatial, a dataset with 1‑3 hop queries across ego and exo views, and the Acc@50IoU metric that requires both a correct answer choice and a bounding box with IoU≥0.5. Evaluating 37 VLMs reveals a large gap between standard MCQ accuracy and grounded performance, indicating widespread ungrounded reasoning. Post‑training on the MultihopSpatial‑Train corpus using GRPO with a composite reward (answer correctness + normalized GIoU) substantially improves both reasoning and grounding on the benchmark and transfers to better performance on embodied VLA tasks (CALVIN, LIBERO).

## Method Summary
The authors construct MultihopSpatial by manually annotating 4.5K QA pairs from 3,563 COCO/PACO-Ego4D images, covering 1‑3 compositional hops (attribute, position, relation) and both ego and exo perspectives. Each item includes a four‑option multiple‑choice question and a ground‑truth bounding box. For training, they create a 6,791‑sample corpus. They fine‑tune a base VLM (Qwen3‑VL‑4B‑Instruct) via GRPO, sampling G=4 responses per step. The reward combines format compliance, MCQ correctness, and a normalized GIoU score for the predicted bbox: R = R_format + α·R_mcq + β·R_bbox with α=β=1. The model’s outputs must follow a strict template specifying “Answer: (X)” and “Bounding Box: {{"bbox_2d": [x1,y1,x2,x2]}}” with normalized coordinates. The trained VLM is integrated into a Vision‑Language‑Action (VLA) framework (VLM4VLA) by inserting action tokens and an MLP head, then fine‑tuned on CALVIN and LIBERO without LoRA to evaluate transfer.

## Key Results
- The Acc@50IoU metric uncovers a drastic performance drop compared to MCQ accuracy; e.g., GPT‑5.2‑Thinking falls from 45.8% to 9.4% on 3‑hop queries.
- GRPO post‑training with the composite reward raises Qwen3‑VL‑4B’s MCQ accuracy from 37.8% to 62.9% and Acc@50IoU from 31.0% to 53.8%.
- The same training improves downstream VLA success: CALVIN average tasks solved increases from 3.75 to 3.98, and LIBERO success rate from 35.8% to 40.0%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Acc@50IoU exposes ungrounded reasoning
The metric couples textual correctness with verifiable spatial localization (IoU≥0.5). This filters out models that answer correctly via linguistic shortcuts but produce irrelevant bounding boxes, creating a discriminative signal for genuine visual grounding. Evidence: abstract and section 5.3 show a severe MCQ vs. grounded gap.

### GRPO with composite reward improves reasoning and grounding
The reward includes a dense, differentiable bbox term (normalized GIoU) alongside MCQ correctness. This directly optimizes geometric accuracy and forces the policy to align its reasoning chain with spatial outputs, overcoming the mismatch between token‑level loss and IoU in supervised fine‑tuning. Evidence: section 5.5, Table 4 shows GRPO (R_mcq+R_bbox) outperforms SFT and GRPO with only R_mcq.

### MultihopSpatial‑Train transfers to embodied VLA tasks
The dataset’s compositional multi‑hop structure trains the model to maintain and reason over intermediate spatial constraints, which generalizes to VLA settings where language must be translated into spatially grounded actions over long horizons. Evidence: abstract and section 5.5 report improvements on CALVIN and LIBERO after post‑training.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multi‑hop compositional spatial reasoning**  
  *Why needed:* The benchmark requires chaining multiple spatial constraints (attribute, position, relation) across 1‑3 hops, mirroring real‑world embodied instructions like “place the red cup to the left of the blue box.”  
  *Quick check:* For the query “From the driver’s perspective, which vehicle to the right of the bus is farthest from the intersection?”, identify the hops and categories (attribute/position/relation).

- **Concept: Visual grounding with IoU metric**  
  *Why needed:* Acc@50IoU ensures that a model’s textual answer corresponds to a correctly localized bounding box, preventing “shortcut” learning that ignores the image.  
  *Quick check:* Why is a model with 90% MCQ accuracy but 10% Acc@50IoU considered ungrounded?

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**  
  *Why needed:* GRPO uses rule‑based rewards (format, MCQ, bbox) to optimize the model without human‑labeled reasoning traces, suitable for tasks with binary/continuous correctness signals.  
  *Quick check:* How does the bounding box reward R_bbox = (GIoU + 1)/2 provide a dense training signal compared to a binary IoU≥0.5?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** MultihopSpatial‑Train data → GRPO training (Qwen3‑VL‑4B + LoRA) → trained VLM → VLM4VLA integration (action tokens + MLP head) → CALVIN/LIBERO evaluation.
- **Critical path:** 1) Prepare data with normalized bbox coordinates [0,1]; 2) Configure GRPO with G=4, α=β=1, LoRA on LLM backbone; 3) Train for 10 epochs, monitor MCQ and Acc@50IoU; 4) Insert action tokens into the trained VLM and fine‑tune end‑to‑end on VLA benchmarks.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Reward weighting:* Balancing α and β is crucial; over‑emphasizing bbox (β) can drop MCQ accuracy (Table 5).  
  *Model scale vs. grounding:* Larger LLMs alone do not guarantee better grounding; vision encoder capacity matters (Fig. 13).  
  *Ego vs. Exo evaluation:* Ego‑centric tasks are harder and compress performance ranges (Fig. 5), necessitating both for full diagnosis.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  *High ungrounded ratio (>80%):* Correct MCQ answers with irrelevant bboxes.  
  *3‑hop ego‑centric collapse:* Both MCQ and Acc@50IoU drop sharply (Fig. 6).  
  *Perspective‑taking errors:* Rationale acknowledges perspective but final bbox ignores it (Fig. 15‑17).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Run all 37 VLMs on MultihopSpatial; measure MCQ accuracy and Acc@50IoU to confirm the grounding gap.  
  2. Ablate training methods: train Qwen3‑VL‑4B with SFT (next‑token bbox) vs. GRPO (R_mcq+R_bbox); compare Acc@50IoU improvements.  
  3. Transfer test: integrate GRPO‑trained VLM into VLM4VLA, fine‑tune on CALVIN ABC→D and LIBERO‑10, and report task success rates.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling with VLM size:** How do improvements from MultihopSpatial‑Train RL post‑training scale with larger VLMs? The paper only tested a 4B model; scaling curves for multi‑hop spatial reasoning and VLA transfer remain unknown.
2. **Extension to video:** Can the compositional spatial reasoning framework be extended from static images to video, and what architectural changes are needed for spatial‑temporal dependencies? The current benchmark and training corpus are image‑only.
3. **Specialized spatial reasoning models (SRMs):** Why do SRMs consistently underperform general‑purpose VLMs of comparable size on multi‑hop tasks? The root cause (dataset bias, architecture, visual capacity) is unclear, hindering targeted SRM redesign.
4. **Reducing ungrounded answers:** What architectural or training objective changes can reduce the high ungrounded answer ratio (correct MCQ but incorrect bbox) observed in most VLMs? The composite reward helps but does not eliminate the decoupling.

## Limitations
- **Data availability:** The MultihopSpatial‑Train and benchmark datasets are not publicly released as of the paper, blocking direct replication.
- **GRPO details unspecified:** Exact advantage normalization (e.g., within‑group standardization) and precise response parsing rules are not described, affecting reward computation.
- **VLA integration ambiguity:** It is unclear whether the VLM weights are frozen or updated during VLA fine‑tuning, and the exact action token insertion scheme is not detailed.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Acc@50IoU metric effectively reveals ungrounded reasoning; GRPO with composite reward improves grounded performance over SFT.
- **Medium confidence:** Observed VLA transfer benefits are real but the causal link to multi‑hop spatial priors is inferential; the effectiveness of the method may be sensitive to reward weighting and exact hyperparameters.
- **Low confidence:** Claims about scaling to larger VLMs and extension to video remain speculative; without experiments, their feasibility is uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. **Metric stress test:** Run models known to rely on shortcuts; verify that Acc@50IoU penalizes them heavily despite high MCQ accuracy.
2. **GRPO ablation:** Re‑implement GRPO with alternative advantage normalizations (mean‑only, no normalization) and varied α/β ratios; measure the impact on MCQ–Acc@50IoU balance.
3. **Transfer specificity:** Evaluate the GRPO‑trained VLM on a VLA task that emphasizes dynamic force/velocity reasoning (outside the static spatial QA distribution); a significant drop would indicate narrow transfer.
