# Automating Clinical Information Retrieval from Finnish Electronic Health Records Using Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26434
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26434
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Llama-3.1-70B achieved 95.3% accuracy and 97.3% consistency across semantically equivalent question variants.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the time-consuming and error-prone task of clinicians manually searching multilingual Finnish electronic health records (EHRs). It introduces a locally deployable Clinical Contextual Question Answering (CCQA) framework that leverages open-source large language models (LLMs) to answer patient-specific clinical questions directly from complete longitudinal records without external data transfer. Evaluated on 1,664 expert-annotated question-answer pairs from 183 breast cancer patients, the framework achieved high accuracy (95.3%) and consistency (97.3%) using Llama-3.1-70B, with a smaller 30B-parameter model performing comparably. Four-bit quantization reduced GPU memory requirements while preserving performance, and notably, domain- or language-specialized models did not show systematic advantages over generalist LLMs.

## Method Summary
The CCQA framework processes entire structured EHRs as a single long-context input, avoiding retrieval-based pre-selection. Each patient's EHR, comprising 26 fields (including free-text notes, lab results, and diagnoses), is concatenated into a chronological text string and combined with a clinical question and predefined answer options. This prompt is fed to an instruction-tuned LLM, which generates a constrained categorical or numeric answer. The system is designed for on-premise deployment, enabling data to remain within institutional boundaries. Performance is evaluated by comparing model outputs against expert annotations across multiple question variants to assess both accuracy and consistency.

## Key Results
- Llama-3.1-70B achieved 95.3% accuracy and 97.3% consistency across semantically equivalent question paraphrases.
- Four-bit quantization reduced GPU memory from 148.2 GB to 53.6 GB with a modest accuracy drop to 92.1%, demonstrating feasibility for resource-constrained environments.
- Clinical review identified clinically significant errors in 2.9% of outputs, and 0.96% of cases showed inconsistency (one correct and one erroneous response) across paraphrases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Retrieval-free long-context integration
- **Claim:** Processing complete longitudinal EHRs without retrieval-based pre-selection enables accurate patient-specific QA by preserving temporal context and cross-document relationships.
- **Mechanism:** The CCQA framework feeds the entire structured EHR (all 26 fields) as a single long-context input to an instruction-tuned LLM, allowing the model to internally synthesize information across years of heterogeneous Finnish/English clinical text without losing contextual links between temporally separated events.
- **Core assumption:** Relevant clinical signals are distributed non-locally across the record, and modern long-context transformers can integrate these signals when provided the full history.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "...answers clinical questions directly from EHRs without transmitting data outside institutional boundaries... Llama-3.1-70B achieved 95.3% accuracy..."
  - [section 3] "Rather than segmenting records using retrieval pipelines... we examined retrieval-free ingestion of complete patient histories..."
- **Break condition:** If EHR length exceeds the model's effective context window, causing information loss or severe latency.

### Mechanism 2: Low-precision quantization for deployability
- **Claim:** Low-precision weight quantization (4-bit) preserves near-full predictive performance while drastically reducing GPU memory, enabling feasible on-site deployment.
- **Mechanism:** Quantization compresses model weights to lower precision via libraries like bitsandbytes, reducing memory bandwidth and storage needs during inference while maintaining sufficiently precise representations.
- **Core assumption:** The representational capacity required for extracting structured clinical variables from Finnish EHRs is robust to moderate precision loss.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Low-precision quantization (4-bit and 8-bit) preserved predictive performance while reducing GPU memory requirements..."
  - [section 2/Table 2] Llama-3.1-70B accuracy: 95.3% (bf16) → 92.1% (4-bit) with memory drop from 148.2 GB → 53.6 GB.
- **Break condition:** Architecture-specific degradation (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B in 8-bit dropped to 65.1%; R1-Distill-Llama-70B to 11.2%), indicating model-dependent sensitivity.

### Mechanism 3: Generalist LLMs suffice without specialization
- **Claim:** Neither medical domain adaptation nor Finnish language specialization confers systematic accuracy advantages over generalist LLMs for this task.
- **Mechanism:** Generalist models trained on diverse multilingual corpora possess sufficient latent capability to interpret Finnish clinical narratives with embedded English/Latin terms when presented with clear instructions and complete context.
- **Core assumption:** The task's requirements (extracting predefined categorical/numeric values from structured EHR fields) are adequately covered by general pretraining data diversity and instruction tuning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Models adapted to medical tasks or to the Finnish language did not demonstrate systematic advantages over general-purpose LLMs."
  - [section 3] "Neither language specialization nor medical domain specialization conferred an advantage..."
  - [Table 1] MedGemma-27B (92.1%) vs. Gemma-3-27B (92.7%); Llama-Poro-2-70B (90.6%) vs. Llama-3.1-70B (95.3%).
- **Break condition:** If the target clinical language or documentation style substantially diverges from the model's pretraining distribution.

## Foundational Learning
### Long-context transformer inference
- **Why needed:** EHRs span thousands of tokens (median 1,792 words); models must attend across the entire sequence to resolve temporal and cross-reference questions (e.g., "neoadjuvant therapy before surgery").
- **Quick check:** How does positional embedding (e.g., RoPE) affect attention to early vs. late tokens in a 20k-token EHR?

### Instruction tuning for constrained output
- **Why needed:** Models must output only predefined categorical/numeric options (e.g., "Left/Right/Bilateral") without extraneous text to allow reliable automated grading.
- **Quick check:** Why might deterministic decoding (temperature=0) be clinically necessary despite reduced creativity?

### Multilingual code-switching in clinical text
- **Why needed:** Finnish EHRs contain embedded English/Latin terminology (drug names, codes like "pT1b"); models must handle mixed-language input without explicit translation.
- **Quick check:** What risks arise if a model over-relies on English medical terms and misinterprets Finnish clinical phrasing?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** EHR encoder/stringifier → Prompt constructor → LLM inference engine → Answer parser & grader
- **Critical path:** Raw CSV → Stringification → Prompt assembly → Model forward pass → Token sampling (deterministic) → String matching → Accuracy/consistency metrics.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Context length vs. latency:* Longer contexts improve completeness but increase memory and latency.
  - *Quantization vs. accuracy:* 4-bit reduces memory ~3× but may cause model-specific crashes or degradation.
  - *Generalist vs. specialized models:* Specialized models add maintenance overhead without proven gains.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Memory overflow (OOM) on long EHRs.
  - Accuracy collapse on specific questions (e.g., CQ6 neoadjuvant therapy: 4.7%–98.7% range).
  - Inconsistency across paraphrases (0.96% of cases).
  - Poor calibration (high ECE) even with high accuracy.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity check:** Run 10 shortest EHRs through full pipeline; confirm >90% accuracy on trivial questions.
  2. **Memory profiling:** Incrementally increase synthetic token length to locate max context within 160 GB budget; verify 4-bit/8-bit savings.
  3. **Consistency probe:** For 50 patient records, generate responses for V1/V2 question variants; compute inconsistency rate; flag discordant pairs for review.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to breast cancer EHRs (183 patients); generalizability to other specialties is uncertain.
- Real-world deployment factors (latency, integration costs for full-record processing) remain unmeasured.
- Clinically significant errors (2.9%) lack detailed categorization and mitigation strategies.

## Confidence
- Main outcome (95.3% accuracy): High
- Mechanism 1 (retrieval-free context integration): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (4-bit quantization viability): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (no specialization advantage): Medium
- Generalizability beyond Finnish breast cancer: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-specialty replication:** Re-run evaluation on at least two additional medical domains using locally available Finnish EHRs to test generalizability.
2. **End-to-end latency benchmark:** Measure wall-clock inference time for full-record processing (varying token lengths) on target clinical hardware (e.g., single 80GB GPU).
3. **Error-type analysis:** Re-analyze the 2.9% clinically significant errors to categorize failure modes (temporal reasoning, terminology, medication dosing) and correlate with EHR length or question type.
