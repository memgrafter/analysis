# How Auditory Knowledge in LLM Backbones Shapes Audio Language Models: A Holistic Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.19195  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19195  
- **Reference count**: 0  
- **Primary result**: Text-only LLMs exhibit measurable auditory knowledge that strongly predicts their performance when fine-tuned into audio-grounded models; phonological reasoning remains a systematic weakness; cascade pipelines (audio→text→LLM) can match state-of-the-art end-to-end audio-language models.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how much auditory knowledge is already present in large language models (LLMs) after text-only pretraining, and how that knowledge affects their performance when adapted to audio-language tasks. The authors introduce the Auditory Knowledge Benchmark (AKB-2000) to measure textual auditory knowledge, evaluate LLMs in a cascade setup (using an external audio captioner), and fine-tune LLMs into audio-grounded Large Audio Language Models (LALMs) via a controlled self-distillation framework (DeSTA). Across 12 open-weight LLMs, they find significant variation in auditory knowledge, strong correlations (r=0.71–0.82) between text-only and audio-grounded performance, and a consistent deficit in phonological reasoning. Notably, cascade pipelines using a strong captioner perform on par with or better than some end-to-end LALMs, indicating a bottleneck in the audio encoder and modality projection. The study concludes that the choice of LLM backbone is a critical, yet understudied, factor in LALM performance.

## Method Summary
The study employs a three-pronged evaluation strategy. First, LLMs are directly tested on AKB-2000, a multiple-choice QA dataset covering six auditory categories. Second, a cascade evaluation uses Gemini-2.5-Pro to generate text captions from audio, which are then fed to LLMs to answer the same QA tasks. Third, a controlled fine-tuning process transforms LLMs into LALMs using the DeSTA framework: Stage 1 uses the frozen LLM to generate target responses from textual metadata; Stage 2 freezes the LLM and a Whisper audio encoder while training only a modality connector (a 6‑layer Q‑Former) to map audio features to those targets. All resulting LALMs are evaluated on the audio-based MMAU and MMAR benchmarks. This design isolates the effect of the LLM backbone’s inherent auditory knowledge by keeping the audio encoder, connector structure, and training data constant across models. Performance is measured by accuracy, and correlations between text-only and audio-grounded results are computed.

## Key Results
- LLMs vary widely in auditory knowledge (e.g., Qwen models consistently outperform Llama and Phi models), with a notable gap in the Phonetic category (~10–15% lower than other categories).
- Strong positive correlations (Pearson r = 0.71–0.82) between AKB-2000 scores and audio-grounded LALM performance, confirming that text-only auditory knowledge is a strong predictor of multimodal success.
- Cascade pipelines (Gemini-2.5-Pro captioning + LLM reasoning) match or exceed several state-of-the-art end-to-end LALMs, highlighting a bottleneck in the audio encoder and modality connector that limits the LLM’s reasoning capacity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The inherent auditory knowledge encoded in a text-only LLM backbone is a primary determinant of its eventual performance as an audio-grounded Large Audio Language Model (LALM).
- **Mechanism:** An LLM's textual training data implicitly teaches it about auditory concepts (e.g., sound sources, acoustic properties, music theory) through descriptions. This pre-acquired knowledge forms a "reasoning foundation" that is leveraged during multimodal fine-tuning. When a frozen LLM backbone is paired with an audio encoder via a modality connector, the model's ability to interpret and reason over the projected audio representations depends significantly on the auditory concepts already present in its latent space.
- **Core assumption:** The modality connector's role is primarily to align acoustic features with the LLM's pre-existing textual/auditory semantic space, not to impart core auditory reasoning ability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Strong correlations (r=0.71-0.82) exist between text-only and audio-grounded performance, indicating AKB-2000 can proxy for LALM backbone selection."
  - [section 5.1] "text-only results are strongly correlated with audio-grounded performance... the choice of the base LLM alone can result in over a 10% absolute performance difference in the resulting LALM."
  - [corpus] Neighbors (e.g., *AudioBERT*, *AuditoryBench++*) probe similar gaps in auditory commonsense knowledge within text-only LLMs, supporting the premise that such knowledge variation exists and is measurable.
- **Break condition:** If the correlation between text-only auditory knowledge benchmarks and audio-grounded performance were low (e.g., r < 0.3), it would suggest the auditory knowledge is not transferable or that the audio encoder/connector training overwrites the backbone's capabilities instead of building upon them.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Text-only pre-training creates a systematic deficiency in phonological reasoning (e.g., rhyme, stress, homophones) because written text corpora lack the acoustic realization of speech sounds.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs learn statistical relationships between word tokens (spelling, semantics) but are never exposed to the phonetic acoustic signatures that cause words like "flour" and "flower" to sound identical. Without direct acoustic supervision or a phoneme-grounded training objective, models cannot learn the sound-based associations crucial for phonological tasks.
- **Core assumption:** Auditory knowledge acquired via text-only data is semantically mediated (knowing *what* things are) rather than acoustically grounded (knowing *how* they sound).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Systematic weaknesses are found in phonological reasoning."
  - [section 5.2] "Four of the five most challenging subcategories require reasoning about pronunciation, prosody, or phonological structure... This failure pattern reveals a fundamental limitation of text-only pre-training."
  - [corpus] Evidence is indirect; neighbor papers (e.g., *AuditoryBench++*) also find LLMs struggle with low-level acoustic attributes, but this paper uniquely isolates phonology as the starkest deficit.
- **Break condition:** Introduction of phonology-aware training data (e.g., text paired with IPA transcriptions, forced-aligned phonetic annotations) or fine-tuning that explicitly includes phonological tasks would reduce the performance gap in the Phonetic AKB-2000 subcategories.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** In current LALM architectures, the audio encoder and modality connector act as a primary bottleneck, limiting the utilization of the LLM's inherent auditory reasoning capabilities, as evidenced by strong cascade pipeline performance.
- **Mechanism:** A cascade system uses a specialized, high-quality audio captioner (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro) to convert audio into rich, detailed text descriptions. The LLM then reasons purely on this text. If this pipeline matches or exceeds an end-to-end LALM's performance, it implies the LALM's audio encoder fails to preserve the same level of discriminative acoustic detail that the captioner explicitly describes. The end-to-end model's bottleneck is in the cross-modal projection stage, not the LLM's reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** The captioner extracts and articulates auditory information more completely than the learned audio encoder-to-text projection in the LALM.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "cascade pipelines match or exceed some state-of-the-art end-to-end LALMs, highlighting audio encoder bottlenecks."
  - [section 5.4] "the cascade results reveal that captioner quality remains a critical bottleneck... Cascade pipelines leveraging a strong captioner already match or surpass... state-of-the-art LALMs."
  - [section 5.4] "point to a potential audio-text alignment bottleneck in end-to-end LALMs, where the audio encoder may fall short of preserving fine-grained details."
- **Break condition:** If future LALMs significantly surpass the best cascade pipelines (e.g., >5% absolute gain on MMAU/MMAR), it would indicate the audio encoder bottleneck has been overcome, allowing the LLM's full auditory reasoning capacity to be applied directly to acoustic features.

## Foundational Learning
- **Text-Only Auditory Knowledge**  
  Why needed: This is the core latent variable being measured (via AKB-2000). It's the "fuel" the LLM brings to the LALM. Understanding that this knowledge is inferred from text descriptions, not acoustic experience, is key to interpreting results.  
  Quick check: *Why might an LLM know that a siren wails but struggle to identify the rhyme scheme of a poem?*

- **Self-Distillation Framework (DeSTA)**  
  Why needed: This is the controlled method for creating LALMs. It ensures the only variable is the LLM backbone by using the same audio encoder, connector, and source data, but with model-generated text targets. This isolates the effect of the backbone's knowledge.  
  Quick check: *In the DeSTA framework, what is the purpose of the first stage (LLM generating responses from textual metadata)?*

- **Cascade vs. End-to-End Evaluation**  
  Why needed: These are the two paradigms for testing if auditory knowledge is *usable*. Cascade tests pure reasoning on human/expert-curated text descriptions. End-to-End tests the full system's ability to extract and reason from audio. Comparing them diagnoses where failures occur (encoder vs. reasoning).  
  Quick check: *If a cascade system (Captioner + LLM) performs similarly to an end-to-end LALM on a benchmark, what does that suggest about the LALM's audio encoder?*

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
LLM Backbone (frozen pre-trained model, e.g., Qwen3-14B) → Audio Encoder (frozen Whisper-large-v3) → Modality Connector (trainable 6-layer Q-Former) → LLM input. Additionally: Training Data = DeSTA-AQA500K (audio + textual seed descriptions + prompts); Evaluation Benchmarks = AKB-2000 (text-only QA), MMAU/MMAR (audio and cascade QA).

**Critical Path**:  
1. Select LLM backbone based on text-only AKB-2000/cascade results.  
2. For 8 selected models, run Stage 1: use frozen LLM + seed text to generate target responses.  
3. Run Stage 2: freeze LLM & audio encoder, train only modality connector to map audio input to the Stage 1 targets.  
4. Evaluate resulting LALMs on MMAU/MMAR with audio input.  
5. Correlate all results (AKB-2000, cascade, LALM) to validate knowledge transfer.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- *Frozen LLM vs. Full Fine-Tuning*: Freezing tests knowledge transfer; full fine-tuning could let the LLM compensate for a weak backbone or poor encoder.  
- *Captioner Choice in Cascade*: Stronger captioner gives better text, isolating LLM capability; weaker captioner introduces noise.  
- *Parameter Scale vs. Family*: Comparing across families at similar scales reveals architectural/training data influences; scaling within a family tests if knowledge scales.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- **Phonetic Gap**: Model scores ~10-15% lower in AKB-2000 Phonetic category vs. others.  
- **Low Speech Correlation**: Cascade vs. audio-grounded speech scores have low correlation (r~0.25) suggests domain-specific training data insufficiency.  
- **Cascade > End-to-End**: Consistently higher cascade scores indicate audio encoder/connector information loss.

**First 3 Experiments**:  
1. **Baseline Auditory Knowledge Profiling**: Evaluate all 12 open-weight LLMs on AKB-2000 zero-shot multiple-choice to establish ranking and identify category weaknesses.  
2. **Controlled Cascade Evaluation**: Using Gemini-2.5-Pro captions, run all LLMs on MMAU/MMAR text-only version. Compare ranking to AKB-2000.  
3. **Controlled LALM Fine-Tuning & Evaluation**: Select 8 LLMs spanning the spectrum. Fine-tune each into a LALM via DeSTA. Evaluate on MMAU/MMAR with audio. Correlate text-only and audio-grounded scores.

## Limitations
- Controlled DeSTA setup (frozen LLM/encoder, single Q-Connector) may not reflect real-world LALM training dynamics where all components are jointly fine-tuned.  
- Reliance on a proprietary captioner (Gemini-2.5-Pro) for cascade evaluation introduces an oracle bias and may not generalize to other captioners.  
- Evaluation benchmarks (AKB-2000, MMAU, MMAR) cover only a subset of auditory skills and may contain dataset biases.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (backbone knowledge → LALM performance): **High** (consistent r=0.71–0.82 across families).  
- **Mechanism 2** (phonological deficit): **Medium** (strong subcategory gaps but mitigation not tested).  
- **Mechanism 3** (audio encoder bottleneck): **Medium** (cascade advantage shown, but depends on captioner quality).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate with varied audio encoders (e.g., BEATs, CLAP) and allow LLM fine-tuning to test robustness of the correlation between text-only knowledge and audio-grounded performance.  
2. Introduce phonetics-targeted fine-tuning (IPA transcriptions, forced alignment) into LALM training and reassess Phonetic AKB-2000 performance.  
3. Compare end-to-end LALMs with enhanced modality connectors (e.g., cross-attention fusion) against cascade to determine if the audio encoder bottleneck can be overcome.
