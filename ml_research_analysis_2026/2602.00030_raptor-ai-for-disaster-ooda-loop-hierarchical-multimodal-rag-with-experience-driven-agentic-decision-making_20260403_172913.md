# RAPTOR-AI for Disaster OODA Loop: Hierarchical Multimodal RAG with Experience-Driven Agentic Decision-Making

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.00030
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00030
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** Achieves 23% higher retrieval precision, 31% better situational grounding, and 27% improved task decomposition accuracy versus baselines, with efficient scaling to 3,000 document chunks.

## Executive Summary
Disaster response demands rapid, accurate synthesis of heterogeneous information (text, imagery) to support the OODA loop. RAPTOR-AI introduces an agentic multimodal RAG framework that integrates hierarchical knowledge organization, entropy-aware dynamic retrieval, and LoRA-based experiential learning. Evaluated on 46 tsunami documents (2,378 pages), it significantly outperforms baselines in retrieval precision, situational grounding, and task decomposition, while scaling efficiently to large corpora. The system dynamically adapts to query complexity and leverages historical disaster patterns to enhance decision-making without compromising base model generality.

## Method Summary
RAPTOR-AI comprises three core innovations. First, it constructs a hierarchical multimodal retrieval tree: text chunks and BLIP-generated image captions/features are fused (weighted α=0.7) into ColVBERT embeddings, recursively clustered using silhouette+DBI optimization, and summarized at each level via GPT-OSS-20b. Second, an entropy-aware agentic controller computes query entropy over semantic types (factual, procedural, etc.) to dynamically select among DirectSearch, HierarchicalTraversal, or MultimodalFusion strategies, updating performance via exponential moving average. Third, LoRA adapters (rank r=16) inject distilled experiential knowledge from historical disasters (e.g., 2011 Tohoku) into the base LLM, preserving general reasoning while encoding domain-specific patterns.

## Key Results
- +23% improvement in retrieval precision (NDCG)
- +31% improvement in situational grounding accuracy (SGA)
- +27% improvement in task decomposition accuracy (TDA)
- Scales efficiently to 3,000 document chunks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Hierarchical Multimodal Knowledge Integration
**Claim:** Hierarchical multimodal knowledge organization improves retrieval by preserving cross-modal semantic relationships across varying abstraction levels.
**Mechanism:** Text chunks and BLIP-generated image captions/embeddings are fused (α=0.7) into ColVBERT vectors. These are recursively clustered using silhouette+DBI optimization, with GPT-OSS-20b generating summaries at each level to form a tree enabling coarse-to-fine retrieval.
**Break condition:** If clustering fails to group semantically related multimodal elements (e.g., text description and corresponding image), hierarchical retrieval may miss critical cross-modal evidence.

### Mechanism 2: Entropy-Aware Agentic Control
**Claim:** Entropy-aware agentic control dynamically selects retrieval strategies to match query complexity and situational uncertainty.
**Mechanism:** Query entropy H(q,I) is computed over semantic types (factual, procedural, etc.). Low entropy (<0.3) triggers DirectSearch; medium (0.3–0.7) triggers HierarchicalTraversal; high (≥0.7) triggers MultimodalFusion. Strategy performance is updated via exponential moving average (β=0.9).
**Break condition:** Incorrect entropy estimation due to out-of-distribution query patterns (e.g., novel disaster type) leads to suboptimal strategy selection, degrading response quality.

### Mechanism 3: LoRA-Based Experiential Learning
**Claim:** LoRA-based experiential knowledge integration enhances model generalization to unseen disasters by encoding historical patterns without compromising base model capabilities.
**Mechanism:** Low-rank adapters (rank r=16) are trained on distilled lessons from historical events (e.g., 2011 Tohoku). These are injected into the base LLM, adjusting weights via ΔW=BA while freezing original parameters.
**Break condition:** Overfitting to historical disaster specifics (e.g., tsunami-only patterns) reduces adaptability to novel disaster types (e.g., volcanic eruption).

  Why needed: Disaster data is inherently text+imagery (e.g., manuals + damage photos); unified retrieval is essential for situational grounding.  
  Quick check: How does ColVBERT’s late-interaction differ from CLIP-style embedding fusion for disaster imagery?

- **Hierarchical clustering for retrieval:**  
  Why needed: 2,378 pages require scalable, multi-granularity access; flat search cannot efficiently support both factual and analytical queries.  
  Quick check: Why use silhouette+DBI instead of k-means alone for disaster document clustering?

- **Entropy-based query analysis:**  
  Why needed: Disaster queries vary from simple ("how to shut gas?") to complex ("coordinate shelter logistics"); strategy must adapt in real-time.  
  Quick check: What disaster-domain factors might cause entropy thresholds (0.3, 0.7) to be misaligned?

- **LoRA for domain adaptation:**  
  Why needed: Full fine-tuning is costly and risks catastrophic forgetting; LoRA efficiently injects historical lessons while preserving general reasoning.  
  Quick check: Why choose rank r=16? What happens with r=4 or r=64 for disaster knowledge?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
PDF extractor (OCR + layout preservation) → BLIP image captioning/feature extraction → Text chunking (800-token, 150 overlap) → ColVBERT embedding fusion (α=0.7) → Hierarchical clustering (silhouette+DBI) → GPT-OSS-20b summarization → Entropy-aware agentic controller → LoRA adapters (r=16) → Phase-specific response generator (rescue/recovery/reconstruction).

**Critical path:**  
Data ingestion → Tree construction → Query → Entropy calc → Strategy selection → Retrieval (leaf/cluster/root) → LoRA-enhanced generation → Output. *Break any step disrupts end-to-end response.*

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Speed vs. accuracy: DirectSearch is fast but only for low-entropy queries; HierarchicalTraversal is accurate but slower.
- Modality weight α=0.7 assumes text is denser than images for disaster info; higher α may underutilize visual damage cues.
- Model size: GPT-OSS-20b gives high summary fidelity but slows indexing; smaller models risk abstraction quality.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High entropy queries misrouted to DirectSearch → incomplete multimodal synthesis.
- Clustering artifacts (e.g., splitting procedural steps) → fragmented retrieval.
- LoRA overfitting → responses overly reliant on past disasters, poor generalization.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Indexing validation: Process 46 PDFs; verify tree structure (leaf/intermediate/root counts) and embedding coherence via t-SNE visualization of fused vectors.
2. Entropy calibration: Run 500 eval queries; confirm strategy selection distribution matches expected phase needs (e.g., rescue→mostly DirectSearch; recovery→HierarchicalTraversal).
3. LoRA impact test: Compare full system vs. w/o LoRA on expert vs. non-expert query sets; measure TDA improvement gap to validate experiential knowledge utility.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Cross-disaster generalization of LoRA-based experiential knowledge:** How well does the system transfer patterns learned from tsunami data to other disaster types (e.g., earthquakes, floods)? The paper acknowledges this as future work; evaluation was limited to tsunami documentation.
2. **Adaptive entropy thresholds:** Can the agentic controller dynamically adjust strategy thresholds (T₁=0.3, T₂=0.7) during deployment, or are they fixed after empirical tuning? Fixed thresholds may misalign with evolving disaster contexts.
3. **Modality fusion weight sensitivity:** How does the multimodal embedding fusion weight (α=0.7) affect performance across varying visual-textual content ratios (e.g., imagery-heavy damage reports vs. text-heavy protocols)? The optimal balance may shift for different document types.
4. **Streaming data scalability:** Does the hierarchical structure scale efficiently for real-time ingestion of streaming multimodal data (e.g., live drone footage, social media feeds) during active disasters? The current implementation processes static PDFs only.

## Limitations

- **BLIP model and prompts unspecified:** The exact BLIP variant and captioning prompts used for visual feature extraction are not detailed, risking inconsistent reproduction.
- **Query entropy calculation ambiguous:** The mechanism to categorize queries into semantic types (factual/procedural/etc.) for entropy estimation is unclear; likely an LLM or classifier but implementation is absent.
- **GPT-OSS-20b accessibility:** The 20.9B parameter model used for tree summarization is not publicly released, hindering exact replication; substitute models may affect fidelity.
- **LoRA training details undefined:** Construction of historical lesson training data and LoRA hyperparameters (learning rate, epochs) beyond rank=16 are unspecified, raising overfitting/generalization concerns.

## Confidence

- **Hierarchical multimodal fusion:** Medium (tree metrics documented, but clustering robustness and fusion parameters unvalidated).
- **Entropy-aware agentic control:** Medium (thresholds and strategy mapping specified, but domain-specific calibration and OOD handling unproven).
- **LoRA experiential learning:** Low (rank and integration described, but training data, generalization bounds, and catastrophic forgetting risks lack empirical detail).
