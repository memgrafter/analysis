# Transfer Learning for an Endangered Slavic Variety: Dependency Parsing in Pomak Across Contact-Shaped Dialects

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28033
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28033
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuning a Greek-variety parser on a small Turkish-variety corpus improves UAS/LAS by 9/11 points, achieving 68% UAS and 62% LAS on Turkish test data.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles cross-dialect dependency parsing for endangered Pomak, where existing Universal Dependencies resources cover the Greek variety but the target is the Turkish variety, which exhibits contact-induced grammatical differences from Turkish. Training a biaffine parser solely on the Greek treebank yields moderate accuracy on Turkish test data (59% UAS, 51% LAS). A newly compiled Turkish mini-corpus (650 sentences) alone performs worse (50% UAS, 44% LAS) due to data scarcity. However, fine-tuning the Greek-trained parser on this small Turkish corpus substantially boosts performance on the held-out Turkish test set (68% UAS, 62% LAS). The results demonstrate that targeted transfer learning—combining broad out-of-variety supervision with limited dialect-specific data—effectively mitigates parsing degradation caused by contact-shaped variation.

## Method Summary
The authors compare three training regimes using a biaffine graph-based neural dependency parser. **Dataset A** is the larger existing Pomak UD treebank (Greek variety). **Dataset B** is a newly compiled Turkish-variety mini-corpus (520 training, 130 development sentences). **Dataset C** is a held-out Turkish test set (90 sentences, speaker-disjoint from B). Regime 1 (baseline) trains only on A and tests on C. Regime 2 (in-variety) trains only on B-train and tests on C. Regime 3 (transfer) first pre-trains on A to convergence, then fine-tunes on B-train, and finally evaluates on C. All data are normalized and tokenized to conform to UD guidelines. The parser uses token representations combining word and character-level embeddings, contextualized by a BiLSTM, with separate biaffine scoring layers for arcs and relations, decoded via the Chu-Liu/Edmonds algorithm. Loss combines arc and relation negative log-likelihoods with L2 regularization.

## Key Results
- Greek-only baseline on Turkish test: 59% UAS, 51% LAS.
- Turkish mini-corpus only on Turkish test: 50% UAS, 44% LAS.
- Transfer (Greek pre-train + Turkish fine-tune) on Turkish test: 68% UAS, 62% LAS.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Pre-training on the larger Greek-variety UD treebank provides a robust initialization that captures general syntactic patterns, which is then refined by fine-tuning on the small Turkish-variety corpus, leading to higher accuracy on Turkish evaluation.
- **Mechanism:** The Greek dataset offers diverse examples of dependency structures, head-relation configurations, and morphological patterns. The biaffine parser learns initial parameters that encode these generalizations. Fine-tuning on the Turkish corpus adjusts the model’s internal representations to the target distribution’s specific features (e.g., different case-marking strategies) without having to learn syntactic structure from scratch.
- **Core assumption:** The Greek and Turkish varieties share enough underlying syntactic structure for transfer to be beneficial, despite contact-induced surface differences.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "fine-tuning the Greek-trained parser on this small Turkish corpus substantially improves accuracy... demonstrates that targeted transfer learning combining broad out-of-variety supervision with limited dialect-specific data effectively mitigates performance degradation from contact-shaped variation."
  - Section 4.1 (Table 3): Transfer regime (A→B→C) achieves 68% UAS and 62% LAS, compared to 59% UAS and 51% LAS for the A-only baseline.
  - Neighboring work on cross-lingual transfer for low-resource languages aligns with the observed benefit of combining large source and small target data.
- **Break condition:** If the source and target varieties diverge too much in core typology (e.g., basic word order or dependency direction), pre-training might lock in parameters that are maladaptive, and fine-tuning may not fully compensate, especially with limited target data.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Turkish-variety mini corpus specifically targets contact-induced grammatical differences (e.g., the "na" dative marker vs. inflectional dative) and thus fine-tunes the parser to correctly map these surface forms to the appropriate Universal Dependencies relations.
- **Mechanism:** Contact with Turkish has altered the realization of oblique arguments in the Turkish variety. The model trained only on Greek mis-interprets these tokens; fine-tuning on authentic Turkish sentences updates the weights so that the model learns the correct association between markers like "na" and typical head-dependent relations (e.g., `obj` or `iobj`), improving relation labeling as well as attachment.
- **Core assumption:** These contact-induced changes are systematic and can be learned from a few hundred sentences, affecting the distribution of UD labels.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract highlights "contact-shaped differences" and that fine-tuning "substantially improves accuracy", implying the mini corpus addresses those specific differences.
  - Section 2 gives an example contrasting Greek (inflectional dative) and Turkish ("na" marker) realizations. Section 5 notes that both UAS and LAS improve, with the latter suggesting better handling of relation labels tied to functional marking.
  - Neighboring work on dialect adaptation shows that dialect-specific data helps performance on non-standard varieties.
- **Break condition:** If the mini corpus fails to include enough instances of the contact-sensitive constructions (e.g., ditransitives, oblique marking), fine-tuning may not fully correct those errors. Also, if the contact-induced changes affect deep syntactic structure beyond surface markers, a small corpus may be insufficient to adjust the parser's higher-level decisions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The chosen biaffine graph-based parser architecture, with character-level token representations, is inherently robust to the morphological complexity and orthographic variation typical of endangered, contact-influenced languages like Pomak.
- **Mechanism:** Character-level embeddings allow the model to generalize to unseen words and cope with non-standard orthography by composing subword features. The BiLSTM captures contextual dependencies. The biaffine scoring mechanism separately scores head and dependent, which is robust to long-distance dependencies common in Slavic languages with flexible word order. This architecture allows the model to generalize despite sparse morphological features.
- **Core assumption:** Contact-induced variation often introduces novel morphemes or altered morphological patterns that benefit from character-level generalization; graph-based global decoding handles long-distance dependencies better than greedy transition systems.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract explicitly states use of a "graph-based parser" and reports improved results after fine-tuning.
  - Section 3.1 details the architecture: token representation combines word and character embeddings, followed by BiLSTM, and biaffine arc/label scoring. It also states that graph-based parsers "have been shown to be robust for morphologically rich languages".
  - Evidence is indirect based on general findings in morphologically rich languages; no direct evaluation on Pomak in neighboring work.
- **Break condition:** If contact-induced changes produce extremely long-distance dependencies or non-projectivity that exceed the model's capacity, or if orthographic variation is too extreme (e.g., mixed scripts), character-level features alone may not suffice. Additionally, the O(n³) decoding complexity may become prohibitive for long sentences, though not mentioned as an issue here.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:  
Normalization/Tokenization -> TokenEncoder (word + CharEnc) -> BiLSTM -> ArcScorer (MLP + biaffine) -> LabelScorer (MLP + biaffine) -> Decoder (Chu-Liu/Edmonds) -> Loss (arc + rel NLL + L2)

Critical path:  
1. Prepare data (normalize, tokenize, annotate).  
2. Build vocabulary and (optionally) load pretrained embeddings.  
3. Initialize model parameters.  
4. Pre-train on Dataset A.  
5. Fine-tune on Dataset B (using A checkpoint).  
6. Evaluate on held-out test set C.  
7. Compute UAS and LAS (exclude punctuation).

Design tradeoffs:  
- Graph vs transition: Graph-based yields global optimum and better for long dependencies but O(n³) decoding; transition-based is linear but greedy.  
- Character-level vs subword: Character-level handles orthographic variation and unknown words well but is heavier; subword (BPE) is faster but depends on training corpus.  
- Fine-tuning strategy: Sequential fine-tuning is simple but risks catastrophic forgetting; multi-task or elastic weight consolidation could preserve source knowledge.  
- Dataset size: Combining a large source with a small target gives the best of both; if source is too large, learning rate scheduling becomes critical to avoid overwriting learned features.

Failure signatures:  
- High relation‑labeling error relative to attachment: indicates difficulty with functional morphology or dialect‑specific markers (e.g., "na").  
- Overfitting to B: B‑training loss decreases while B‑dev loss rises; limited data size.  
- Catastrophic forgetting: performance on source variety (A) degrades after fine‑tuning; overly large learning rate or excessive fine‑tuning epochs.  
- Decoding failures: non‑tree outputs (unlikely with Chu-Liu/Edmonds) or many punctuation errors; often point to bad arc scores.

First 3 experiments:  
1. Baseline: Train on Dataset A (Greece) alone → evaluate on C (Turkish test). Quantifies cross‑variety degradation.  
2. In‑variety lower bound: Train on Dataset B (Turkish) alone → evaluate on C. Shows the data‑scarcity ceiling.  
3. Transfer: Pre‑train on A then fine‑tune on B → evaluate on C. Demonstrates the benefit of combining resources.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Which contact-induced grammatical differences between Greek and Turkish Pomak cause the most parsing errors and are fixed by fine-tuning?**  
  *Basis:* Authors admit they "do not yet isolate parser behavior on dialect-sensitive constructions" like oblique-marking, ditransitives, case-marking asymmetries, or function-word realizations.  
  *Why unresolved:* Identifying these is essential to understand contact-induced parsing challenges and guide annotation.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Error analysis categorizing mistakes by construction; targeted annotation of diagnostic examples; measuring accuracy change.
- **How does increasing Turkish-variety corpus size and speaker diversity affect cross-variety transfer gains?**  
  *Basis:* The 650-sentence corpus is "small"; authors suggest expanding by adding more sentences and diverse speakers.  
  *Why unresolved:* Scaling effects and speaker diversity impact are unknown, crucial for resource planning.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Train with varying target data sizes (e.g., 650, 1300, 2600) and diverse speakers; measure UAS/LAS improvements.
- **Do orthographic normalization and subword segmentation choices affect cross-dialect parsing accuracy?**  
  *Basis:* Authors state "orthographic and tokenization choices likely interact with character-level modeling" and recommend systematic comparison.  
  *Why unresolved:* Pomak's non-standard orthography could make representation choices critical, but no empirical comparison exists.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Compare parser performance across different normalizations (e.g., phonemic, etymological) and segmentations (BPE, character) on the Turkish test set.

## Limitations
- Dataset A (Greek UD treebank) exact size and split are unreported, hindering assessment of pre-training robustness.
- Specific normalization and tokenization rules for the Turkish mini-corpus (Dataset B) are not detailed, risking misalignment with Greek data.
- Key hyperparameters (embedding sizes, BiLSTM layers, dropout rates, learning schedule) are absent, making exact replication of scores speculative.
- The 650-sentence Turkish corpus may not adequately cover all contact-induced constructions (e.g., ditransitives) critical for fine-tuning.

## Confidence
- **Transfer benefit observation:** High (clear experimental improvement, supported by analogous work).
- **Reproducibility of exact scores:** Medium/Low (missing dataset specifications and hyperparameters prevent precise replication, though the general trend is robust).
