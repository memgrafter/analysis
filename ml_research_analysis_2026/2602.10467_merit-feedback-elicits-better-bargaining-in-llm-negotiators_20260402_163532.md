# MERIT Feedback Elicits Better Bargaining in LLM Negotiators

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10467
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10467
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** MERIT-guided in-context learning (ICL-MF) and fine-tuning (SFT) substantially outperform baselines in human-aligned bargaining, with ICL-MF increasing average buyer MERIT by ~0.3-0.5 points and deal rates by 5-30% across GPT and Gemini models.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses poor strategic depth and misalignment with human bargaining preferences in LLM negotiators. It introduces MERIT, a multi-faceted evaluation metric combining consumer surplus, negotiation power, and acquisition ratio, validated via human studies to achieve 0.80 ROC AUC preference alignment versus 0.68 for profit-only metrics. Using MERIT as feedback, the authors develop an in-context learning method (ICL-MF) and fine-tune a model on human-preferred dialogues. Across nine economic simulations in AGORABENCH, both approaches significantly outperform ReAct and OG-Narrator baselines, demonstrating that human-aligned utility feedback yields more effective negotiation strategies.

## Method Summary
The core framework revolves around the AGORABENCH simulator, which generates `Thoughts–Talk–Action` dialogues for bilateral bargaining across nine market conditions (e.g., deceptive, multi-product). The MERIT metric scores final outcomes using a weighted sum: `α·CS + β·NP + γ·AR`, where CS is normalized consumer surplus, NP measures anchoring shift, and AR is product similarity. Coefficients (α'=1.0139, β'=0.8812, γ'=1.1049) are fit globally via Bradley-Terry on human pairwise preferences. Two MERIT-guided agents are proposed: **ICL-MF**, which embeds the private MERIT formula and opponent-aware reasoning (OAR) prompts into the system message for in-context learning; and **SFT**, which fine-tunes an open-source model (gpt-oss-20b) on a dataset of human-preferred dialogues collected from deceptive multi-product negotiations.

## Key Results
- MERIT achieves 0.80 ROC AUC for predicting human preferences, outperforming profit-only metrics (0.68).
- ICL-MF increases average buyer MERIT by ~0.3-0.5 points and deal rates by 5-30% over ReAct and OG-Narrator across multiple LLMs (GPT, Gemini).
- SFT excels in complex multi-product negotiations due to stable strategy imitation, while ICL-MF is more effective in single-product settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Using MERIT as an in-context learning (ICL) feedback signal improves buyer negotiation performance by aligning the model's strategy with human preferences over both outcomes and process.
- **Mechanism:** The agent is provided with a system prompt containing the private MERIT reward function. During multi-turn bargaining, it internally estimates hidden variables (e.g., seller cost) to compute approximate CS and NP values for potential deals, using this feedback to shape offers and concessions toward maximizing the composite score.
- **Evidence anchors:** ICL-MF increases average buyer MERIT by ~0.3-0.5 points and deal rates by 5-30% [abstract]; agents hypothesize seller cost (e.g., "closer to $400") and calculate CS/NP to evaluate deals [section 6.1].

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on a dataset of human-preferred negotiation dialogues instills robust, human-like negotiation strategies that generalize across diverse market conditions, particularly in complex multi-product settings.
- **Mechanism:** A dataset of LLM-generated dialogues, filtered by human preferences (MTurk surveys), is used to fine-tune an open-source model via LoRA. The model learns turn-level patterns (thought, talk, action) of human-preferred negotiators, encoding stable trade-off management and opponent-aware reasoning without explicit MERIT computation at inference.
- **Evidence anchors:** SFT proves superior in multi-product negotiations requiring stable management of diverse products and complex trade-offs [section 6.1]; dataset collected from deceptive multi-product settings with human preferences via MTurk [section C].

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The MERIT metric's multi-dimensional structure (CS, NP, AR) better captures human bargaining preferences than profit-only metrics because it accounts for both cardinal (economic surplus) and ordinal (product preference alignment) utility.
- **Evidence anchors:** MERIT aligns more closely with human preferences than traditional profit-based ones, achieving ROC AUC 0.80 vs. 0.68 [section 4.2, abstract]; coefficients optimized globally using Bradley-Terry model on MTurk data [section 4.2].

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Utility Theory & Consumer Surplus**  
  *Why needed:* MERIT's CS component uses `(WTP - Deal Price) / (WTP - Cost)`. A CS of 0.5 means the buyer captured half the maximum possible surplus between their willingness to pay and the seller's cost.  
  *Quick check:* If WTP=$500, cost=$300, deal=$400 → CS = (500-400)/(500-300) = 0.5.
- **Concept: Bargaining Power & Anchoring**  
  *Why needed:* MERIT's NP = `(Initial Ask - Deal Price)/(Initial Ask - Cost)` quantifies how much the buyer shifted the anchor. NP near 1 implies the buyer drove the price close to the seller's cost, indicating strong negotiation leverage.  
  *Quick check:* Initial ask=$600, cost=$350, deal=$450 → NP = (600-450)/(600-350) = 0.6.
- **Concept: Human Preference Alignment via Bradley-Terry**  
  *Why needed:* The model `log(P(i preferred)/P(j preferred)) = θ_i - θ_j` estimates latent preference strengths from binary comparisons, used to optimize MERIT's linear coefficients (α, β, γ) to best predict human choices.  
  *Quick check:* The equation models the log odds of preferring option i over j as the difference in their latent quality parameters.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  AGORABENCH Simulator -> Negotiation Dialogue -> MERIT Calculator Module -> (For ICL-MF) ICL-MF Prompt Generator -> LLM Agent; (For SFT) Human Preference Dataset -> SFT Pipeline (LoRA) -> Fine-Tuned Model; Evaluation Harness runs all configurations and aggregates results.
- **Critical path:** Simulator defines scenario → dialogue generated → MERIT scores outcome → ICL-MF uses score in next prompt; SFT uses dialogue pairs to update model weights → new negotiation → evaluation compares baselines.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - MERIT complexity (3 components, hidden cost estimation) vs. interpretability; simpler profit-only is stable but misaligned.  
  - ICL-MF (flexible, per-trial adaptation) vs. SFT (stable strategy but requires training data, risks overfitting).  
  - Explicit Opponent-Aware Reasoning (OAR) prompts boost performance but increase prompt length.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Unstable anchoring (consecutive offers backtracking).  
  - Rigid linear price schedules (OG-Narrator).  
  - Market condition blindness (deal rate drops unexpectedly in certain regimes).  
  - Low OAR mention in thoughts (<2.1% in ReAct vs >25% in ICL-MF).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate ReAct baseline on deceptive single-product camera task: expect ~84% deal rate, MERIT ~1.35.  
  2. Implement ICL-MF prompt for single-item: expect MERIT increase of ~0.3-0.5 points vs. ReAct.  
  3. Ablate OAR: compare ReAct, ReAct+explicit OAR prompt, ICL-MF; measure OAR sentence % and MERIT.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can MERIT be extended to incorporate seller-specific objectives such as inventory risk or tiered profit targets?
- To what extent do cultural and regulatory contexts impact LLM negotiation strategies, and how can AGORABENCH be expanded to include these factors?
- Why do some LLMs exhibit irrational concession patterns like unstable anchoring, and can fine-tuning with MERIT feedback eliminate these behaviors?
- Are globally optimized MERIT coefficients (α, β, γ) universally optimal across all market regimes, or should they be dynamically adjusted per scenario?
- Does the human preference dataset, collected solely in deceptive multi-product settings, generalize to other market types (e.g., vanilla, monopoly) for training robust negotiators?

## Limitations
- Code and data for AGORABENCH and the human preference dataset are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- MERIT’s coefficients are fit globally; transferability across cultural or product contexts is unknown.
- SFT’s claimed generalization may be limited by overfitting to the specific deceptive multi-product training dialogues.

## Confidence
- **ICL-MF substantially improves buyer MERIT and deal rates** – High (empirically validated across nine market simulations, multiple LLMs).
- **SFT yields robust multi-product negotiation strategies** – Medium (evidence from one fine-tuned model on one dataset).
- **MERIT aligns better with human preferences than profit-only metrics** – High (direct MTurk evaluation, ROC AUC 0.80 vs 0.68).

## Next Checks
- Run an independent replication of the human preference study with a demographically diverse sample to validate MERIT coefficients and the 0.80 ROC AUC.
- Re-optimize MERIT weights on region-specific preference data and measure performance shifts in corresponding simulated markets.
- Evaluate MERIT-guided agents against sellers that learn and adapt over time (e.g., RL-based negotiators) to probe robustness beyond static opponent policies.
