# Can we Trust Unreliable Voxels? Exploring 3D Semantic Occupancy Prediction under Label Noise

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06279
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06279
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** DPR-Occ achieves up to 2.57% higher mIoU and 13.91% higher IoU at 90% noise over adapted baselines, preventing geometric and semantic collapse under extreme corruption.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces OccNL, the first benchmark for 3D semantic occupancy prediction under realistic label noise, including asymmetric category flips and spatiotemporal trailing artifacts from dynamic objects. The authors demonstrate that state-of-the-art 2D noisy-label learning methods fail in sparse 3D spaces. To address this, they propose DPR-Occ, which leverages dual-source evidence—temporal consensus from an EMA teacher and structural affinity from class prototypes—to dynamically construct candidate label sets. It combines partial label learning, negative learning, and self-distillation to train without direct supervision. On SemanticKITTI, DPR-Occ significantly outperforms baselines (e.g., up to 2.57% higher mIoU at 90% noise) and resists both geometric and semantic collapse.

## Method Summary
DPR-Occ extends the ProtoOcc backbone with a robust training pipeline for noisy labels. It first warms up the student network for 12 epochs on noisy data with a standard base loss. Then, it activates a robust phase where an EMA teacher (momentum-updated copy of the student) provides stable predictions and features, and a prototype bank supplies class-wise feature centers updated from student features using both scene-adaptive and scene-agnostic components. For each voxel, a partial label set is formed by taking the union of top-K classes from the EMA teacher's predictions and top-K classes most similar to the prototypes. K is dynamically scheduled to decay from 9 to 2 to balance coverage and purity. The total loss combines the base loss with three regularization terms: Partial Label Learning (PLL) restricts predictions to the candidate set; Negative Learning (NL) penalizes the complementary set; Self-Not-True Distillation (SNTD) aligns the student with the EMA teacher in the not-true space. This design prevents overfitting to corrupted labels while preserving true semantics.

## Key Results
- DPR-Occ significantly outperforms adapted 2D noisy-label baselines on OccNL, achieving up to 2.57% higher mIoU and 13.91% higher IoU at 90% asymmetric noise on SemanticKITTI.
- It prevents both geometric collapse (occupancy boundary degradation) and semantic collapse (loss of rare classes) that affect other methods under extreme corruption.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of all three core components: dual-source candidate construction, dynamic‑K scheduling, and the triple‑loss combination.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Dual-source candidate construction
DPR-Occ builds the partial label set `PL_v` for each voxel by taking the union of top‑K classes from the EMA teacher's predictions and top‑K classes with highest feature‑prototype similarity. This dual-source strategy expands the candidate space to include the true label even if it ranks low in one source, while the union reduces the likelihood of including noise compared to a single source. The approach assumes the true label appears in at least one source; otherwise it is omitted, blocking learning for that voxel.

### Dynamic‑K scheduling
The hyperparameter K starts large (e.g., 9) during early training to maximize ground‑truth coverage, then linearly decays to a smaller value (e.g., 2) to simplify disambiguation and improve candidate purity later. The schedule relies on the assumption that model confidence improves monotonically with epochs. An improperly tuned schedule (too fast decay risks excluding true labels; too slow retains noise) degrades robustness.

### Triple‑loss regularization
Three loss components jointly regularize training: (1) PLL confines predictions to `PL_v`; (2) NL penalizes probabilities on the complementary set `NL_v`; (3) SNTD aligns the student distribution with the EMA teacher's on the not‑true space (masking noisy labels). These constraints prevent overfitting to corrupted labels and stabilize gradients. If candidate/complement sets are poorly defined (due to flawed evidence sources), these losses may misguide.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Data → Student (F_θ) → predictions & features → Prototype Bank update; Student ↔ EMA Teacher (momentum); Candidate Set Constructor uses EMA predictions & prototype affinities (top‑K each) → PL_v & NL_v; Loss Calculator: PL_v → L_PLL; NL_v → L_NL; EMA not‑true space → L_SNTD; Dynamic‑K Scheduler → K; Loss Aggregator: L_base + L_robust.

### Critical path
1. Input noisy label ˜y_v and student prediction.
2. Warm‑up (epochs ≤ E_w, e.g., 12): optimize L_base only; update student, EMA teacher, prototype bank.
3. Robust phase (epochs > E_w): compute EMA predictions/features, prototype similarities; form PL_v (union of top‑K); define NL_v (complement); compute L_PLL, L_NL, L_SNTD.
4. Total loss = L_base + L_robust; update student.
5. Update EMA teacher weights (momentum).
6. Update prototype bank from current student features.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Candidate set size (K):** Larger K increases true‑label hit rate but adds noise; smaller K purifies but risks omission. Dynamic schedule attempts to balance.
- **Dual‑source vs single‑source:** Dual‑source adds overhead but yields complementary evidence; single‑source is cheaper but more vulnerable to source‑specific failures.
- **Warm‑up duration (E_w):** Too short yields unstable evidence; too long risks overfitting to noise before robust methods activate. Optimal values are dataset‑specific.

### Failure signatures
- **Geometric collapse:** Sharp drop in occupancy IoU, indicating failure to learn boundaries.
- **Semantic extinction of rare classes:** Per‑class IoU for minority classes approaches zero while common classes remain stable.
- **Stagnant mIoU:** No improvement despite training, often due to poorly defined PL_v/NL_v.
- **Over‑smoothed predictions:** Excessively blurry class distributions, typically from excessive SNTD regularization.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Warm‑up Duration Ablation:** Vary E_w (e.g., 6–18) at high noise (70%) and assess mIoU/IoU to determine optimal warm‑up length.
2. **Dynamic‑K vs Fixed‑K:** Compare the linear schedule against fixed K values (2, 5, 9) and random K across multiple noise levels; expect the linear schedule to balance performance best.
3. **Dual‑Source Evidence Ablation:** Train variants using only EMA predictions or only prototype similarities for PL_v, then the full union; at high noise (70%) the union should significantly outperform each single source in mIoU.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Safety impact:** Does improving occupancy mIoU under noise translate to safer autonomous driving? Current work only evaluates perception metrics; closed‑loop experiments are needed.
- **Joint modeling:** How to simultaneously address long‑tailed class distributions and spatiotemporal trailing artifacts? Their interaction remains unexplored.
- **Generalization:** Is DPR‑Occ effective across different sensor modalities (e.g., LiDAR) and datasets (e.g., nuScenes, ScanNet)? Currently only SemanticKITTI is tested.
- **Component analysis:** How do EMA teacher consensus and prototype affinity each contribute across varying noise scenarios? A fine‑grained breakdown could refine the framework.

## Limitations
- Replication difficulty due to insufficient details on ProtoOcc architecture and trailing noise generation (which depends on SCPNet).
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (warm‑up length, dynamic‑K schedule, loss weights) may require per‑dataset tuning.
- Evaluation limited to SemanticKITTI; transferability to other sensors or scenes remains unproven.

## Confidence
- OccNL benchmark validity and failure of 2D noisy‑label methods: **High**
- DPR‑Occ performance gains (e.g., +2.57% mIoU at 90% noise): **Medium**
- Synergy of dual‑source, dynamic‑K, triple‑loss: **Medium**
- Generalization beyond SemanticKITTI: **Low**
