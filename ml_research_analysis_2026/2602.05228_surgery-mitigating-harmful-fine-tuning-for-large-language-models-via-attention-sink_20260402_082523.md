# Surgery: Mitigating Harmful Fine-Tuning for Large Language Models via Attention Sink

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.05228
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05228
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** Surgery reduces harmful outputs by 5.90% (BeaverTails), 11.25% (HarmBench), and 9.55% (SorryBench) compared to prior defenses, with minimal computational overhead and comparable task performance.

## Executive Summary
Harmful fine-tuning attacks can break the safety alignment of large language models by training on a small fraction of malicious data. This paper identifies that attention heads exhibit a bimodal distribution in their "sink divergence"—the difference in how much they attend to a sink token (e.g., the first token) on harmful versus refusal data. Heads with positive sink divergence correlate with learning harmful patterns. The proposed **Surgery** defense adds a regularization term during fine-tuning that penalizes positive sink divergence, steering most attention heads toward negative values. This suppresses the model's capacity to learn harmful representations while preserving performance on user tasks, offering an effective and computationally lightweight mitigation.

## Method Summary
Surgery operates during standard fine-tuning. For each attention head, it computes a **sink divergence** score (`d_h`), which measures the difference in the head's average attention to its sink token when processing a batch of harmful data versus a batch of refusal (safe) data. The training loss is augmented with a regularization term: `λ * (1/|H|) * Σ ReLU(d_h)`. This term applies a penalty only to heads with positive `d_h`. Gradient updates therefore push `d_h` toward zero or negative values, discouraging heads from adopting attention patterns associated with harmful data. The method uses full-parameter fine-tuning with a fixed regularization strength `λ` (e.g., 300), requiring only small reference batches of harmful and refusal data at each step to compute the current `d_h`.

## Key Results
*   Surgery achieves state-of-the-art harm reduction across BeaverTails, HarmBench, and SorryBench, improving harmful score (HS) reduction by 5.90%–11.25% over prior methods.
*   The defense maintains comparable fine-tune accuracy (FA) on user tasks like GSM8K and SST2, showing minimal catastrophic forgetting.
*   Over 96% of attention heads shift to the negative sink divergence group after Surgery training, validating the steering mechanism.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Separable Sink Divergence Hypothesis
The core claim is that attention heads can be partitioned by the sign of their sink divergence (`d_h`). Positive `d_h` heads correlate with harmful pattern learning, while negative `d_h` heads correlate with refusal behavior. This creates a bimodal distribution. Evidence shows that disabling positive-`d_h` heads suppresses harmfulness, while disabling negative heads increases it. The sign is a stable indicator preserved across fine-tuning. If distributions overlap significantly, the targeting basis for Surgery fails.

### Mechanism 2: Sink Divergence Suppression via Regularization
A ReLU-based penalty `ReLU(d_h)` added to the loss explicitly penalizes heads where sink values on harmful data exceed those on refusal data. Gradients then push `d_h` ≤ 0. Over 96% of heads shift negative after Surgery. This directly influences behavioral safety without catastrophic forgetting, as shown by maintained task performance. If the regularizer is too weak or too strong, it fails to balance safety and utility.

### Mechanism 3: Layer-Wise Criticality of Early Layers
Early layers show higher (less negative) sink divergence post-Surgery, indicating they are more resistant to suppression and likely encode core safety mechanisms. This aligns with other works identifying early/middle layers as safety-critical. If suppressing early layers does not correlate with harm reduction, the criticality assumption is mislocalized.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Attention Heads and Sink Tokens**
    *   **Why needed:** Surgery manipulates per-head attention to a "sink" token (often the first/bos token). Understanding that heads specialize and some consistently attend heavily to a specific token is foundational.
    *   **Quick check:** What is a sink token, and how is a head's sink value (`α_h`) computed?
*   **Concept: Fine-Tuning and Safety Alignment**
    *   **Why needed:** The paper addresses harmful fine-tuning that breaks an already aligned model. Distinguishing between alignment (safety) and downstream task fine-tuning is essential.
    *   **Quick check:** Why does mixing a small proportion of harmful samples into a fine-tuning dataset compromise a model's refusal behavior?
*   **Concept: Regularization in Optimization**
    *   **Why needed:** Surgery adds a differentiable penalty term. Understanding how regularization modifies gradient updates and constrains parameter trajectories is necessary.
    *   **Quick check:** How does a `ReLU(d_h)` term in the loss function encourage `d_h ≤ 0`?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Aligned Base Model -> Dual Reference Datasets (Harmful & Refusal) -> Sink Divergence Calculator (per-head `d_h`) -> Regularizer Module (`ReLU(d_h)` sum) -> Combined Loss -> Optimizer (AdamW) -> Fine-Tuned Safe Model
*   **Critical path:** 1) Precompute or batch-compute mean sink values `α_h` for harmful and refusal datasets. 2) During each fine-tuning step, compute `d_h` for current batch. 3) Add `λ/|H| * ΣReLU(d_h)` to standard task loss. 4) Backpropagate and update weights. 5) Monitor head distribution shift.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** `λ` intensity vs. utility (higher λ increases safety but risks task performance degradation). Batch source for reference data (fixed vs. dynamic) affects stability and compute. Fixed head group boundary at `d_h=0`; a dynamic threshold might adapt better.
*   **Failure signatures:** Harmful Score (HS) remains high → regularizer too weak or gradient signal overwhelmed. Fine-tune Accuracy (FA) drops → `λ` too high, over-constraining. No significant head distribution shift → regularizer gradient too small or `d_h` unstable.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Separation Test:** On a clean aligned model, compute `d_h` on refusal vs. harmful datasets. Confirm a bimodal distribution with ~equal head counts in positive/negative groups.
    2.  **Harmful Fine-Tuning Simulation:** Fine-tune on a dataset with `p=0.1` harmful samples *without* Surgery. Measure: (a) increase in HS, (b) shift toward more positive-`d_h` heads.
    3.  **Surgery Efficacy Check:** Apply Surgery with `λ=300`. Verify: (a) >90% of heads shift to negative `d_h`, (b) HS reduction vs. standard SFT, (c) FA comparable to other defenses. If head shift <50%, increase `λ` or adjust learning rate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Why are early layers more resistant to sink divergence suppression?** The paper observes this phenomenon but does not provide a mechanistic explanation for why early layers retain higher sink divergence or why they are harder to steer.
*   **Does Surgery scale to larger and proprietary models?** The study was limited to open-source models under 10B parameters due to computational constraints. Effectiveness on larger or closed models (with inaccessible internals) is unknown.
*   **What is the theoretical basis for the separable sink divergence hypothesis?** The hypothesis is empirically supported but lacks formal justification. Why sink divergence outperforms alternative metrics (like harmful divergence) for identifying harmful heads is not theoretically proven.

## Limitations
*   **Separability assumption:** The core mechanism relies on a bimodal `d_h` distribution. Significant overlap between safety states would break the targeting logic.
*   **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** The regularization strength `λ` requires manual tuning; no automatic guidance exists for new models or varying attack ratios `p`.
*   **Sink token ambiguity:** The paper does not clarify whether the sink token is identified per-head or globally, leaving a critical implementation detail open that may affect stability.

## Confidence
*   **Separable sink divergence hypothesis:** **Medium** – Supported by distribution plots and head-disable ablations on Llama3, but generalizability to other architectures is untested.
*   **Regularization steering efficacy:** **Medium-High** – Consistent >96% head shift to negative values across experiments, contingent on correct `d_h` computation.
*   **Early-layer criticality:** **Low-Medium** – Observational finding; other works identify different critical layers, so this may not be a universal property.
*   **Overall harm reduction:** **Medium** – Gains demonstrated on three benchmarks, but evaluation scope is limited to specific harm categories and English language.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-model bimodality validation:** Compute sink divergence distributions on at least two other aligned models (e.g., Mistral, Gemma) to verify the separable bimodal pattern is not model-specific.
2.  **Sink token definition ablation:** Implement both per-head and global sink token identification. Compare resulting head partitions, training stability, and final Harmful Score to determine the correct interpretation.
3.  **Robust hyperparameter sweep:** For harmful ratios `p ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}`, systematically sweep `λ ∈ {100, 300, 500}`. Measure the HS-FA trade-off to identify a stable, high-performance regime that does not require per-dataset tuning.
