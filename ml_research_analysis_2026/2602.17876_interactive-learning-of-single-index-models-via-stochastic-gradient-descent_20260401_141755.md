# Interactive Learning of Single-Index Models via Stochastic Gradient Descent

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.17876  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17876  
- **Reference count**: 11  
- **Primary result**: SGD with adaptive exploration achieves near‑optimal pure exploration sample complexity $\widetilde{O}(d^2/\varepsilon)$ and cumulative regret $\widetilde{O}(d\sqrt{T})$ for interactive learning of single‑index models under monotonicity and local linearity.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for interactive learning of single‑index models, where the reward is a nonlinear function $f(\langle\theta^\star,a\rangle)$ of the action $a$ on the unit sphere. The algorithm combines SGD with a specific exploration strategy: actions are mixtures of the current parameter estimate $\theta_t$ and a uniform random orthogonal direction. With a carefully chosen learning‑rate schedule, SGD naturally exhibits a two‑phase dynamics—a slow burn‑in followed by rapid learning—under monotonicity and local linearity of the link function, and either a derivative lower bound or convexity. The resulting sample complexity for pure exploration and cumulative regret match known lower bounds and prior best upper bounds, demonstrating that a single SGD procedure attains near‑optimal statistical guarantees even when the derivative is small near zero.

## Method Summary
The learner maintains a normalized parameter $\theta_t\in S^{d-1}$. At each round $t$, it selects
\[
a_t = \sqrt{1-\sigma_t^2}\,\theta_t + \sigma_t Z_t,
\]
where $Z_t$ is uniformly distributed on the sphere orthogonal to $\theta_t$. After observing the noisy reward $r_t = f(\langle\theta^\star,a_t\rangle)+\varepsilon_t$, the algorithm computes the gradient
\[
g_t = \bigl(f(\langle\theta_t,a_t\rangle)-r_t\bigr)\,f'(\langle\theta_t,a_t\rangle)\,(I-\theta_t\theta_t^\top)a_t,
\]
updates $\theta_{t+1/2} = \theta_t - \eta_t g_t$, and renormalizes $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_{t+1/2}/\|\theta_{t+1/2}\|$. The exploration variance $\sigma_t$ and learning rate $\eta_t$ follow phase‑dependent schedules: during burn‑in, $\sigma_t^2 = \Theta(1)$ and $\eta_t = \Theta(1/d^2)$ (or a tailored schedule under convexity); during learning for pure exploration, $\eta_t = \Theta(d/t \wedge 1/d)$ and $\sigma_t^2 = \Theta(1)$; for regret minimisation, $\eta_t = \Theta(1/\sqrt{t} \wedge 1/d)$ and $\sigma_t^2 = \Theta(d/\sqrt{t} \wedge \gamma_0)$. This design ensures sufficient drift while controlling variance.

## Key Results
- Pure exploration sample complexity: $\widetilde{O}(d^2/\varepsilon)$ to achieve $\langle\theta^\star,\theta_T\rangle \ge 1-\varepsilon$.
- Cumulative regret: $\widetilde{O}(d\sqrt{T})$ for interactive learning.
- SGD self‑separates into a burn‑in phase and a learning phase without algorithmic switching.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Under monotonicity and local linearity, SGD naturally separates into a burn‑in phase (slow correlation improvement) followed by a learning phase (rapid convergence).
- **Mechanism**: The drift term in the SGD update depends on the correlation $m_t=\langle\theta^\star,\theta_t\rangle$ and the derivative $f'$. When $m_t$ is small, the drift is weak (especially if $f'$ is small near zero), causing slow progress (burn‑in). Once $m_t$ exceeds a threshold (e.g., $1-\gamma_0/4$), local linearity ensures $f'$ is bounded away from zero, yielding a drift of $\Omega(\eta\,\sigma_t^2(1-m_t^2)/d)$ and linear improvement.
- **Core assumption**: Assumption 1.2 (monotonicity and local linearity of $f$).
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section 1 describe the two‑phase dynamics; Lemma 2.1 computes the drift; related works (e.g., RHJR24) also identify burn‑in.
- **Break condition**: If monotonicity fails, drift can become negative at moderate $m_t$, causing regression (Proposition 5.1). If local linearity fails (e.g., $f'$ vanishes near 1), learning may be arbitrarily slow.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The exploration strategy $a_t = \sqrt{1-\sigma_t^2}\,\theta_t + \sigma_t Z_t$ (with $Z_t$ uniform on the orthogonal sphere) ensures unbiased gradient estimates and controllable variance during both phases.
- **Mechanism**: The mixture preserves the current estimate while injecting isotropic noise orthogonal to it. The inner product $\langle\theta^\star,a_t\rangle$ has variance scaling as $\sigma_t^2(1-m_t^2)$. Scheduling $\sigma_t$ (constant during burn‑in, decaying to $\Theta(\varepsilon)$ later) balances exploration and exploitation.
- **Core assumption**: Action space is the unit sphere; $Z_t$ is uniformly distributed on the orthogonal sphere.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract defines the mixing; Section 1 gives Equation (2); Section 2 and Lemma 2.1 use spherical Stein’s lemma to compute moments.
- **Break condition**: $\sigma_t$ too small relative to $m_t$ yields biased gradients; $\sigma_t$ too large leads to excessive exploration and regret.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: A carefully designed learning‑rate schedule $\eta_t$ compensates for the varying signal‑to‑noise ratio across phases, achieving the stated near‑optimal bounds.
- **Mechanism**: $\eta_t$ is chosen to dominate the martingale variance and normalization error while ensuring sufficient drift. In the learning phase ($m_t\ge1-\varepsilon$), $\eta_t = \Theta(d/t \wedge 1/d)$ for pure exploration and $\eta_t = \Theta(1/\sqrt{t} \wedge 1/d)$ for regret. In burn‑in, a constant $\eta = \Theta(1/d^2)$ (under a derivative lower bound) or a more tailored schedule (under convexity) prevents destabilisation.
- **Core assumption**: Either a global derivative lower bound (Assumption 1.4) or convexity (Assumption 1.5) to bound the drift via $f'(m_t)$.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section 1 state the theorems; Sections 3 and 4 detail how the learning rate balances drift, martingale, and normalization.
- **Break condition**: A constant $\eta$ throughout may cause excess variance in learning or insufficient progress in burn‑in. The schedule must adapt to the phase; if $f'$ is not lower‑bounded and not convex, the burn‑in analysis fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Single‑index model**: The reward follows $y = f(\langle\theta^\star,a\rangle)+\varepsilon$, reducing the problem to learning a single direction $\theta^\star$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$. The nonlinear link $f$ introduces challenges for gradient‑based methods. *Quick check*: How does a single‑index model differ from a generalized linear model, and why is the non‑linearity a source of difficulty?
- **Stochastic gradient descent on manifolds**: Updates are performed in Euclidean space and then projected (renormalized) onto the sphere. The resulting spherical gradient differs from the Euclidean one, and the normalisation step introduces bias that must be controlled. *Quick check*: Why use the spherical gradient, and what role does the normalisation play in the analysis?
- **Burn‑in phase in nonlinear bandits**: When $f'$ is small near zero (e.g., $f(x)=x^3$), the signal is weak initially, causing a long period of slow correlation improvement. This phenomenon is distinct from the later rapid convergence once alignment is moderate. *Quick check*: What causes the burn‑in phase, and how does it differ from the learning phase?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
- **Action generator**: Takes $\theta_t$ and $\sigma_t$, outputs $a_t = \sqrt{1-\sigma_t^2}\,\theta_t + \sigma_t Z_t$ with $Z_t$ uniform on the orthogonal sphere.
- **Reward observer**: Returns $r_t = f(\langle\theta^\star,a_t\rangle) + \varepsilon_t$.
- **SGD updater**: Computes gradient $g_t$, applies learning rate $\eta_t$, then normalises $\theta_{t+1} = (\theta_t - \eta_t g_t)/\|\theta_t - \eta_t g_t\|$.
- **Hyperparameter scheduler**: Adjusts $\eta_t$ and $\sigma_t$ according to the current phase (time‑based or heuristic).
- **Initialization logic**: Sets initial $\theta_1$ (random on sphere or via hypothesis test to ensure $\langle\theta^\star,\theta_1\rangle\gtrsim 1/\sqrt{d}$).  
Overall data flow: Initialization → $\theta_1$ → Action generator → Reward observer → SGD updater → $\theta_2$ → loop; scheduler influences Action generator (via $\sigma_t$) and SGD updater (via $\eta_t$).

### Critical path
1. Initialise $\theta_1$.
2. For each round $t$:
   a. Generate $a_t$ using current $\theta_t$ and $\sigma_t$.
   b. Query reward $r_t$.
   c. Compute gradient $g_t$ using observed $r_t$ and $f'$.
   d. Update and normalise to obtain $\theta_{t+1}$.
   e. Update $\eta_t$ and $\sigma_t$ according to the schedule.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Exploration ($\sigma_t$) vs exploitation**: Larger $\sigma_t$ improves gradient information but reduces immediate reward. The schedule decays $\sigma_t$ after burn‑in to focus on regret minimisation.
- **Learning rate ($\eta_t$)**: Must be small enough to bound martingale variance and normalisation error, yet large enough to make drift progress. The analysis provides concrete time‑decay forms.
- **Normalisation**: Essential to keep $\theta_t$ on the sphere, but introduces a bias that scales with $\eta_t^2$ and must be controlled.

### Failure signatures
- Correlation $m_t$ plateaus well below the target (e.g., $<0.5$) after many $d^2$ steps: likely $\sigma_t$ too small or $\eta_t$ too small in burn‑in.
- Normalisation causes large swings in $\theta_t$: $\eta_t$ too large, making $\|\theta_{t+1/2}\|$ deviate significantly from 1.
- Algorithm fails for non‑monotonic $f$: correlation rarely exceeds $\approx 0.2$ (Proposition 5.1).

### First experiments
1. **Reproduce Figure 1**: Run with cubic link $f(x)=x^3$, $d=20$, constant $\eta=0.002$, $\sigma_t=0.5$ until $m_t>0.7$ then $\sigma_t=0.2$. Plot $m_t$ averaged over 100 runs to observe two‑phase growth.
2. **Vary learning‑rate schedules**: Compare constant $\eta$, $\eta_t\propto 1/t$, and the theoretical epoch‑based schedule for both phases. Measure sample complexity to reach $m_T\ge 0.99$.
3. **Test monotonicity necessity**: Use non‑monotonic link $f(x)=x^3-x$ with random initialisation. Verify $m_t$ stays near $1/\sqrt{d}$, confirming Proposition 5.1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Derivative‑aware sample complexity without convexity**: Can SGD achieve the sharper bound $\widetilde{O}\bigl(d^2/\max_{1/\sqrt{d}\le y\le x} f'(y)^2\bigr)$ under only monotonicity, matching RHJR24? Current analysis uses convexity to lower bound $f'(m_t)$ during burn‑in; overcoming this without knowing $m_t$ is open.
2. **Necessity of monotonicity**: Is monotonicity fundamentally required for any algorithm in this setting? The paper shows a counterexample for their exploration strategy, but impossibility for all strategies remains open.
3. **Appropriate measure for adaptive data**: The classical information exponent loses predictive power when data are adaptively collected. What is the correct statistical complexity measure (e.g., an interactive information exponent derived from the SGD differential equation)?
4. **Fully adaptive SGD**: Can we design an algorithm that automatically tunes $\eta_t$ and $\sigma_t$ without prior knowledge of $f$'s derivative properties, while retaining near‑optimal guarantees across all monotonic $f$?

## Limitations
- **Function class restrictions**: Requires monotonicity and local linearity, excluding common activations like ReLU or sigmoid.
- **Hidden constants**: The $\widetilde{O}$ bounds hide potentially large factors; empirical scaling may differ for moderate $d$.
- **Computational cost**: Generating orthogonal noise per round costs $O(d^2)$, which becomes prohibitive in very high dimensions.

## Confidence
- Two‑phase dynamics: **Medium** (sound under assumptions but sensitive to violations).
- Exploration strategy (unbiasedness & variance control): **High** (straightforward from the mixture design).
- Near‑optimal rates: **Medium** (match known lower bounds for the same model class, but constants are implicit and analysis may be loose in practice).
