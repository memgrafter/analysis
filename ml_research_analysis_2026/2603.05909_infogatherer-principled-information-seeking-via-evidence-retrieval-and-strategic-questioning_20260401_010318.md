# InfoGatherer: Principled Information Seeking via Evidence Retrieval and Strategic Questioning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.05909  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05909  
- Reference count: 29  
- Primary result: InfoGatherer achieves higher success rates (e.g., 66.5% vs. 32.8% for UoT in legal with gpt-5-nano) while using fewer dialogue turns, demonstrating effective uncertainty handling via Dempster-Shafer belief assignments.

## Executive Summary
High‑stakes domains such as medicine and law often present LLMs with underspecified queries that lead to overconfident, incorrect answers. InfoGatherer addresses this limitation by integrating retrieved domain documents with targeted user follow‑up questions, and by modeling uncertainty through Dempster‑Shafer belief assignments over a structured evidential network. This explicit representation of ambiguity and ignorance allows the system to strategically seek additional information only when needed. Evaluated on medical and legal benchmarks, InfoGatherer outperforms the prior state‑of‑the‑art UoT method (e.g., 66.5% vs. 32.8% success in legal) while engaging users in fewer dialogue turns.

## Method Summary
The system begins by retrieving relevant domain documents for a given query. It then generates targeted follow‑up questions to resolve identified uncertainties. Answers to these questions, together with the retrieved documents, are combined using Dempster‑Shafer belief assignments, which produce a structured evidential network that captures not only confidence but also ambiguity and ignorance. This network informs a final decision, optionally prompting further clarification cycles. Unlike point‑probability approaches, the belief‑based representation enables the model to recognize when information is insufficient and to request focused evidence, reducing overconfidence.

## Key Results
- **Higher success rates**: InfoGatherer surpasses UoT by a large margin (66.5% vs. 32.8% in legal tasks with gpt‑5‑nano) and shows similar gains in medical benchmarks.  
- **Efficiency**: It achieves these gains with fewer dialogue turns, indicating more strategic information requests.  
- **Ablation evidence**: Removing the BBA‑based evidence elicitation or document grounding significantly degrades performance, confirming each component’s contribution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
InfoGatherer’s effectiveness stems from its explicit handling of uncertainty via Dempster‑Shafer theory. Unlike standard probability, belief assignments can represent partial knowledge and ignorance separately, preventing the model from conflating lack of evidence with low confidence. By grounding decisions in retrieved documents and strategically soliciting missing information, the system builds a more complete evidential base before committing to an answer. This reduces the hallucination and overconfidence typical of LLMs on ambiguous queries.

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component Map
Retriever -> Evidence Collector -> Question Generator -> User -> Answer Integrator -> Dempster‑Shafer Engine -> Decision Maker -> Output

#### Critical Path
1. Given a query, the Retriever fetches relevant documents.  
2. The Evidence Collector extracts snippets and initializes belief masses.  
3. The Question Generator proposes follow‑up questions when ignorance is high.  
4. User answers are incorporated by the Answer Integrator.  
5. The Dempster‑Shafer Engine updates the evidential network.  
6. When belief converges or ignorance falls below a threshold, the Decision Maker selects the final answer.

#### Design Tradeoffs
- **Complexity vs. interpretability**: Dempster‑Shafer adds computational overhead but yields transparent belief representations; simpler Bayesian models lack explicit ignorance modeling.  
- **Interaction cost vs. accuracy**: More dialogue turns could improve accuracy but increase user friction; InfoGatherer aims to minimize turns while maximizing gain.  
- **Reliance on retrieval**: Performance degrades if the initial document set is low‑quality; however, document grounding is essential for factual accuracy.

#### Failure Signatures
- **High ignorance without question generation**: The system fails to ask clarifying questions when it should, often due to overly aggressive thresholds.  
- **Dempster‑Shafer divergence**: Belief masses do not combine well if evidence sources are highly conflicting, leading to counter‑intuitive results.  
- **Question irrelevance**: Generated questions do not target the missing information, wasting user effort.

#### First Experiments
1. **Trace belief evolution**: Run InfoGatherer on a test case and visualize how belief masses change after each user response.  
2. **Ablate document grounding**: Disable retrieval and rely solely on parametric knowledge to assess the impact on accuracy.  
3. **Compare with point‑probability baseline**: Replace the Dempster‑Shafer module with a softmax‑based confidence aggregator and measure performance drop.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to medical and legal domains with a single LLM (gpt‑5‑nano); generalizability to other fields and models is unknown.  
- Baseline comparison limited to UoT; lack of results against other interactive retrieval or clarification systems.  
- Reproducibility is hindered by missing code, detailed hyperparameters, and corpus specifications.

## Confidence
- **High**: The pipeline architecture (retrieval, targeted questioning, Dempster‑Shafer combination) is logically sound and well‑described.  
- **Medium**: Reported performance gains are substantial but rely on a single model and narrow benchmarks without statistical testing.  
- **Low**: The claim that InfoGatherer broadly resolves LLM overconfidence on underspecified queries is not yet broadly validated.

## Next Checks
