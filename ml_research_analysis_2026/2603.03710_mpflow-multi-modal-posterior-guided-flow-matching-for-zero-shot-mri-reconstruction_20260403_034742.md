# MPFlow: Multi-modal Posterior-Guided Flow Matching for Zero-Shot MRI Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03710
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03710
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** MPFlow reduces hallucinations in zero-shot MRI reconstruction by leveraging auxiliary modalities at inference without retraining, matching diffusion baselines in quality with only 20% sampling steps and improving tumor segmentation Dice by over 15%.

## Executive Summary
Zero-shot MRI reconstruction aims to recover high-quality images from undersampled k-space data without task-specific training, but suffers from hallucinations due to the ill-posed nature of the problem. MPFlow introduces a framework that uses a pre-trained rectified flow model on the target modality and a self-supervised cross-modal alignment module (PAMRI) trained on auxiliary MRI data. During inference, it guides the flow sampling process with two complementary terms: data consistency to enforce measurement fidelity, and a feature alignment loss that pulls latent patch representations toward those of the auxiliary image. This joint posterior guidance suppresses both intrinsic hallucinations (measurement inconsistencies) and extrinsic hallucinations (anatomically implausible structures) without any retraining. Evaluated on T2 super-resolution (HCP) and FLAIR k-space reconstruction (BraTS), MPFlow achieves equivalent PSNR/SSIM to diffusion models using a fraction of the sampling steps, and significantly reduces tumor segmentation hallucinations (e.g., Dice 0.740 vs. 0.639), with all improvements statistically significant.

## Method Summary
MPFlow is a zero-shot reconstruction pipeline that combines two pre-trained components: (1) a rectified flow model trained solely on high-quality target-modality images, providing a strong generative prior with efficient deterministic sampling paths; and (2) PAMRI, a self-supervised patch-level contrastive learning module trained on unpaired multi-modal data to align latent representations across target and auxiliary modalities. At test time, for a given undersampled target measurement and a fully-sampled auxiliary image, MPFlow performs a noise optimization to select a favorable initial noise seed. It then runs guided rectified flow sampling for a reduced number of steps (e.g., T=100). At each step, the current estimate is patched, features are extracted by PAMRI's fixed encoders, and the velocity field is modified by gradients from a data consistency loss and a latent patch alignment loss. This steers the generative trajectory toward solutions consistent with both the measurements and the auxiliary anatomy, all without updating the flow or PAMRI models.

## Key Results
- Matches diffusion baseline PSNR/SSIM on HCP T2 super-resolution and BraTS FLAIR reconstruction while using only 20% of the sampling steps.
- Reduces tumor segmentation Dice hallucinations by over 15% (e.g., 0.740 vs. 0.639 on BraTS) compared to a diffusion prior with data consistency only.
- Improves SSIM by 2–4% at T=500 steps, with all reported gains statistically significant.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Posterior Conditioning on Auxiliary Modality
The key insight is to condition the reconstruction on an auxiliary MRI modality during inference, shifting the posterior from `p(x|y)` to `p(x|y, x_aux)`. This joint conditioning uses the auxiliary's information to constrain solutions within the measurement null space, theoretically reducing conditional entropy by `I(x; x_aux |y)`. The core assumption is that target and auxiliary modalities are spatially registered and encode overlapping but non-identical anatomical information. Evidence includes the paper's information-theoretic formulation (section 2.2) and the demonstrated reduction in both intrinsic (measurement error) and extrinsic (anatomical implausibility) hallucinations. The mechanism fails if the auxiliary is misregistered, contains different anatomy, or provides orthogonal information (near-zero conditional mutual information).

### Mechanism 2: PAMRI's Adaptive Contrastive Alignment
PAMRI's patch-level contrastive learning with adaptive temperature creates a shared latent space that aligns anatomical structures across modalities while preserving modality-specific details. Independent encoders process patches from target and auxiliary images. The contrastive loss (adapted InfoNCE) uses a temperature `τ_i,j` that scales inversely with Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) between raw patches: high NMI (similar anatomy) uses low temperature for strict alignment; low NMI (e.g., tumor with different contrast) uses high temperature to relax constraints, preventing loss of pathology-specific features. This assumes that semantically corresponding patches share latent anatomical semantics disentangled from intensity differences. Evidence includes the abstract's claim of "shared representations" and section 2.4's loss design. If NMI estimation is unreliable (e.g., due to extreme noise), the temperature adaptation may mis-weight the loss, either over-penalizing valid differences or under-constraining misalignments.

### Mechanism 3: Joint Posterior Guidance with DC and LP
During flow sampling, the velocity field is jointly guided by a data consistency (DC) term and a latent patch (LP) alignment term from PAMRI: `v(xt|y) = v_θ(xt,t) - α_t ∇xt [ ||F(ˆx1|t)-y||² + λ_P LP(ϕ(P(ˆx1|t)), ψ(P(x_aux))) ]`. The DC term minimizes inconsistency in measurement space (targeting intrinsic hallucinations). The LP term minimizes MSE between normalized features of patches from the current estimate and the auxiliary image, pulling the solution toward anatomically plausible structures (targeting extrinsic hallucinations). The core assumption is that PAMRI's latent space is modality-invariant for anatomy and that deviations correlate with hallucinations. Evidence includes the abstract's claim of "systematically suppressing intrinsic and extrinsic hallucinations" and ablation results (Table 3a, Table 2) showing both components are necessary. If `λ_P` is too high, auxiliary over-constraining causes blur; if too low, extrinsic hallucinations persist. Poor PAMRI training can also misguide the LP term.

## Foundational Learning
- **Rectified Flow**: Provides a generative prior with near-linear trajectories between noise and data, enabling high-quality generation with far fewer sampling steps than diffusion models. This efficiency is critical for zero-shot reconstruction where each step involves expensive DC/LP computations. *Quick check:* Why do straight-line paths allow reduced sampling compared to stochastic diffusion paths?
- **Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE)**: The core of PAMRI. It learns to map semantically corresponding patches from different modalities to similar embeddings while pushing apart non-corresponding ones, creating a shared representation space independent of appearance. *Quick check:* How does the contrastive objective differ from pixel-wise reconstruction, and why is it better for cross-modal semantics?
- **Patch-based Processing**: MRI reconstruction requires fine-grained, spatially varying details. Operating on patches preserves localization and enables learning local cross-modal correspondences (e.g., tumor appearance in T2 vs. FLAIR), which global image-level losses would blur. *Quick check:* What is the trade-off between larger and smaller patch sizes for a contrastive pretraining task like PAMRI?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
PAMRI Encoders (`ϕ`, `ψ`) (fixed after pretraining) -> Rectified Flow Model (`v_θ`, unconditional) -> Posterior Guidance Module (computes DC loss and LP loss per step using `ϕ`, `ψ`) + Noise Optimization Module (selects seed `s⋆` from `S` candidates)

**Critical path:**  
1. **Offline Pretraining:** Train PAMRI on unpaired multi-modal patches (target and auxiliary) using adaptive contrastive loss and reconstruction regularization. Save `ϕ`, `ψ`.  
2. **Offline Flow Training:** Train `v_θ` on the target modality dataset only (e.g., high-quality T2 scans). Save `v_θ`.  
3. **Online Inference (per patient):** Given undersampled target `y` and full auxiliary `x_aux`: run noise optimization to select initial seed `s⋆`; run guided rectified flow sampling for `T` steps using fixed `v_θ`, `ϕ`, `ψ`.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Patch size:** Smaller captures finer detail but increases computation and may lack context; larger provides context but risks losing precise alignment. Paper uses `32x32`.  
- **`λ_P` weight:** Balances auxiliary anatomy vs. target fidelity. Too high overfits to auxiliary (blurry); too low doesn't suppress hallucinations. Paper finds `0.1` optimal.  
- **`S` (noise candidates) & `t_noise`:** More candidates and longer warm-start improve initialization but add linear overhead. Paper uses `S=8`, `t_noise=0.2T`.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Divergence/NaNs:** Likely `λ_P` too high or PAMRI features not normalized.  
- **Blurry images:** `λ_P` too high or PAMRI under-trained (`λ_rec` too low).  
- **Persistent hallucinations (extrinsic):** PAMRI encoders failed to learn cross-modal alignment (e.g., training data not representative, contrastive loss collapsed).  
- **Lost modality-specific details (e.g., tumor brightness):** PAMRI adaptive temperature failed (NMI estimator broken) or patch size too large, forcing inappropriate alignment of distinct pathologies.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **PAMRI Sanity Check:** After pretraining, visualize t-SNE of `ϕ(p_tar)` and `ψ(p_aux)` for corresponding/non-corresponding patches. Corresponding patches should cluster together. Also plot adaptive temperature `τ` vs. NMI to verify design.  
2. **Ablation of Guidance Terms:** Run inference with (a) DC only, (b) LP only, (c) DC+LP. LP only should fail due to measurement inconsistency; DC only should show extrinsic hallucinations; full should be best.  
3. **Auxiliary Modality Ablation:** Run MPFlow with the correct auxiliary vs. a *wrong* auxiliary (e.g., T1 for T2 reconstruction, or misregistered scan). The correct auxiliary should improve SSIM/Dice; wrong should harm or not help, confirming dependence on valid cross-modal information.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability:** Validated only on T2 super-resolution (HCP) and FLAIR k-space recon (BraTS); performance on other modalities (e.g., DWI), pathologies, or protocols is unknown.
- **Auxiliary dependency:** Critically depends on a spatially registered, anatomically overlapping auxiliary scan. Feasibility and failure modes when this is not met (e.g., different timepoints) are not stress-tested.
- **Theoretical guarantees:** Information-theoretic justification assumes ideal conditioning and perfect shared representation; actual realized `I(x; x_aux |y)` is not measured.

## Confidence
- **Efficiency (20% sampling steps vs. diffusion):** High (rectified flow properties are well-established).
- **Hallucination reduction (>15% Dice improvement):** High (statistically significant on clinically relevant metric across ablations).
- **PAMRI's adaptive contrastive mechanism:** Medium (ablations show benefit, but contribution of adaptive temperature vs. standard contrastive loss not isolated).
- **Break conditions (misregistration, orthogonal info):** Medium-Low (theoretically sound but not systematically failure-mode tested).

## Next Checks
1. **Auxiliary Modality Integrity Test:** Systematically degrade auxiliary input: apply controlled non-rigid misregistrations (1–5 mm), substitute with wrong modality, or use a scan from a different timepoint. Measure decay in SSIM/Dice gain to quantify tolerance.
2. **PAMRI Ablation – Fixed vs. Adaptive Temperature:** Retrain PAMRI with fixed low temperature (strict alignment) and fixed high temperature (loose alignment). Compare all three (fixed-low, fixed-high, adaptive) in full MPFlow to isolate adaptive temperature's impact.
3. **Latent Space Validation & Failure Mode Probe:** After PAMRI pretraining, compute distribution of patch-pair NMI values used for `τ` and linear evaluation accuracy of `ϕ`'s features on a downstream segmentation task. During MPFlow inference, track LP gradient magnitude in hallucination-prone regions (e.g., tumor borders). Unstable gradients would indicate latent misalignment.
