# TiMi: Empower Time Series Transformers with Multimodal Mixture of Experts

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21693
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21693
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** TiMi achieves state-of-the-art multimodal forecasting by leveraging a frozen LLM to extract causal trend knowledge from text and a dual-router MMoE for adaptive fusion, significantly lowering MSE/MAE on 16 benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
TiMi addresses multimodal time series forecasting by incorporating causally relevant textual information (e.g., news, reports) without requiring explicit semantic alignment between text and numerical series. It uses a frozen large language model (LLM) to extract structured future trend knowledge—future direction, frequency, and noise—via dataset-specific prompts. This textual guidance is integrated into a Transformer-based time series model through a lightweight Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) plug-in. MMoE dynamically routes information via two routers: a text-informed expert (TMoE) conditioned on a global text embedding, and a series-aware expert (SMoE) conditioned on a flattened representation of patch tokens. Evaluated on 16 real-world datasets, TiMi consistently outperforms unimodal (e.g., PatchTST) and multimodal (e.g., Time-MMD, AutoTimes) baselines, with particularly large gains on irregular data (Time-IMM). Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both MMoE components and the pre-trained knowledge of the frozen LLM.

## Method Summary
TiMi comprises two parallel encoders—one for text and one for time series—that interact only within the Transformer backbone via the MMoE module. Text is processed by a frozen LLM using prompts that constrain outputs to trend, frequency, and noise dimensions; per-dimension embeddings are averaged to produce a static global representation `¯H`. The series is split into overlapping patches, embedded, and fed as tokens `{h_i}` into a Transformer. Each Transformer layer first applies self-attention to `{h_i}`, then an MMoE update: a TMoE router uses `¯H` to select text-informed experts, while an SMoE router uses the concatenated patch tokens to select series-aware experts; the chosen expert outputs are summed and added to the tokens. This design allows adaptive, context-aware fusion of exogenous textual trends and endogenous series patterns without explicit alignment. A final prediction head aggregates the last layer’s patch tokens and produces the forecast.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art on 16 benchmarks, consistently lowering MSE and MAE versus unimodal (PatchTST) and multimodal (Time-MMD, AutoTimes) baselines.
- On the Agriculture dataset, reduces MSE by 20.2% over PatchTST (0.193 vs. 0.242) and by 85.3% over Time-LLM (0.193 vs. 1.032).
- On irregular Time-IMM data, cuts average MSE by 29.57% relative to PatchTST, outperforming all multimodal rivals.
- Ablation studies show removing either TMoE or SMoE degrades performance, confirming both are essential; also, using a trainable LLM instead of frozen yields minor gains but increases risk.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Frozen LLM extracts causal trend knowledge**  
- **Claim:** A frozen LLM, via structured prompting, extracts causal trend knowledge (future direction, frequency, noise) from unstructured text, which guides prediction without semantic alignment.  
- **Mechanism:** Text is processed by a frozen LLM using dataset-specific prompts that force outputs into trend/frequency/noise categories; a pooled embedding aggregates this structured knowledge.  
- **Core assumption:** LLMs pre-trained on vast corpora possess latent causal reasoning that can be distilled via prompting into dimensions useful for time series forecasting.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states LLM inferences serve as guidance; [section 3.2] details prompt design and average pooling.  
- **Break condition:** If prompts fail to constrain LLM output to causally relevant, structured information (e.g., outputs become verbose or off-topic), the extracted representation may misguide the model.

**Mechanism 2: Dual-router MMoE fusion**  
- **Claim:** The Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) dynamically gates integration of textual guidance (from TMoE) and series context (from SMoE) into each Transformer layer, enabling adaptive, context-aware fusion without explicit feature alignment.  
- **Mechanism:** For each token, two separate routers compute expert selection weights: one based on the global text embedding `¯H` (TMoE), one based on a flattened series representation `concat({h_i})` (SMoE). The outputs from the selected experts are summed and added to the token.  
- **Core assumption:** Sparse expert selection, conditioned on modality-specific global representations, allows disentangled yet complementary incorporation of exogenous (text) and endogenous (series) information.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] Equations 4–6 define TMoE/SMoE routers and their summation; [section 4.3] Table 3 shows replacing MMoE with standard MoE or removing one component degrades performance.  
- **Break condition:** If one router’s weights become uniform or dominant (e.g., TMoE router ignores text), the multimodal integration collapses to unimodal or ineffective fusion.

**Mechanism 3: SMoE global trend specialization**  
- **Claim:** The Series-aware MoE (SMoE) specializes experts to capture global temporal trends (e.g., monotonic increase/decrease) in the historical series, providing complementary long-term context to patch-wise Transformer modeling.  
- **Mechanism:** All patch tokens are concatenated into a series-level representation, which routes a shared set of experts; thus, the expert selected for a patch token depends on the overall trend of the entire input window.  
- **Core assumption:** A router conditioned on the entire series representation can identify macro-level patterns crucial for forecasting but potentially lost in local patch attention.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] describes SMoE’s use of concatenated patches; [section 4.3] Figure 6 shows experts specialize for different Mann-Kendall trend regimes, confirmed by MK test.  
- **Break condition:** If the series-level representation fails to encode meaningful global trends (e.g., due to noise or non-stationarity), router selection becomes arbitrary and SMoE provides no useful specialization.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Mixture of Experts (MoE)**  
- **Why needed:** TiMi’s core innovation is the MMoE, a conditional computation framework. Understanding sparse gating and expert specialization is prerequisite to grasping how it selectively incorporates different information sources.  
- **Quick check:** How does the router’s input representation (text embedding vs. concatenated patches) determine *which* knowledge source (textual trend vs. series pattern) influences a given token’s transformation?

**Concept: Patch-wise Time Series Representation**  
- **Why needed:** TiMi builds on Transformer-based forecasters like PatchTST, which split the series into overlapping patches. This introduces a local-global hierarchy (patch tokens vs. series-level) that SMoE exploits.  
- **Quick check:** If you increase the patch length `P`, how might that affect the information captured by the patch tokens versus the global series representation used by SMoE?

**Concept: Frozen LLM Prompting for Structured Output**  
- **Why needed:** The entire textual guidance pipeline depends on the LLM acting as a fixed feature extractor. Understanding prompt engineering for constrained outputs (trend/frequency/noise) is key to the “causal knowledge” extraction claim.  
- **Quick check:** Why use a multi-prompt strategy (trend, frequency, noise) instead of a single open-ended prompt? What risks does this mitigate?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (plain text):**  
Text ──► Frozen LLM + Prompts ──► Per-dim embeddings ──► Avg pool ──► ¯H  
Series ──► Patchify ──► PatchEmbed ──► {h_i}  
{h_i} (initial) ──► Transformer Layer 1: Self-Attention → LN → MMoE(TMoE(¯H), SMoE(concat({h_i}))) → sum → LN ──► {h_i} (next)  
... (repeat for L layers) ...  
{h_i} (final) ──► Head (flatten/agg → linear) ──► Forecast

**Critical path:**  
1. Input: raw series `x1:L` and text corpus `T`.  
2. Encode `T` offlines: `T` → frozen LLM with prompts → per-dim embeddings → average pooling → static `¯H`.  
3. Patchify series → embed → initial patch tokens `{h_i}`.  
4. For each Transformer layer:  
   a. Self-attention updates on `{h_i}`.  
   b. MMoE update: TMoE router uses `¯H` to select text experts; SMoE router uses `concat({h_i})` to select series experts; sum selected outputs → add to `{h_i}`.  
5. After final layer, aggregate patches and apply linear head to produce forecast `ˆx_{L+1:L+S}`.  
*Note:* `¯H` is fixed across all layers; no temporal alignment between text and series timestamps.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Frozen LLM vs. trainable:** Frozen LLM reduces training cost and prevents forgetting but cannot adapt to domain-specific jargon; a trainable adapter could improve performance at risk of overfitting and misalignment.  
- **Dual-router MMoE vs. single-router:** Dual routers provide interpretable modality-specific control but add complexity; a single router on concatenated `[¯H, global_series]` might simplify but loses disentanglement for ablation and interpretability.  
- **Series-level router input:** Using `concat({h_i})` is computationally cheap (O(N)) but may dilute global signal if N is large; a separate global summary token could help but adds parameters.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **TMoE failure:** Router weights based on `¯H` are uniform or random across samples. *Diagnosis:* Check TMoE router weight distribution per batch; if flat, text embedding may be uninformative or router not learning.  
- **SMoE failure:** Expert selection does not correlate with series trends per Mann-Kendall test. *Diagnosis:* Compute MK Z-scores for test set; chi-square test on expert selection vs. trend bins; non-significant association indicates arbitrary routing.  
- **Textual misguidance:** Performance degrades below unimodal PatchTST when text is noisy/irrelevant. *Diagnosis:* Ablate TMoE; if performance recovers, text is actively harmful.  
- **Training instability:** Expert starvation due to poor load balancing. *Diagnosis:* Monitor expert activation frequencies; >90% on one expert indicates routing collapse.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablation of MMoE components:** Replicate Table 3: run (a) full TiMi, (b) remove TMoE, (c) remove SMoE, (d) remove both (PatchTST backbone). Expect: (b) drops on text-rich tasks, (c) drops on series-driven tasks, (d) drops overall—confirms necessity of both.  
2. **Router interpretability validation:** For SMoE, compute Mann-Kendall trend Z-scores for all test samples; group by expert selected (top-1). Expect clear separation in Z-score distributions across experts (as in Figure 6). For TMoE, qualitatively inspect which text samples activate which expert (may need SHAP on router inputs).  
3. **Irregular data robustness:** Run TiMi on Time-IMM dataset (baseline: PatchTST, Time-MMD). Expect TiMi to significantly outperform due to no requirement for temporal alignment between text and series—tests the “non-fusion guidance” premise.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions or future work. However, the Limitations section and suggested Next Checks imply several important directions:
- How robust is the structured prompting approach to variations in text quality, domain, and language?
- Does a trainable (instead of frozen) LLM adapter improve performance without sacrificing stability, and how does it affect causal knowledge extraction?
- Can the dual-router MMoE design be extended to more than two modalities or to handle asynchronous multimodal inputs?
- What is the theoretical guarantee for the interpretability of expert specialization in SMoE across diverse temporal patterns?

## Limitations
- **Prompt robustness:** LLM output quality depends entirely on prompt design; unconstrained prompts may yield irrelevant or verbose outputs that misguide.  
- **Static text embedding:** The global representation `¯H` is fixed for the entire series window, ignoring potential time-varying relevance of textual information.  
- **Generalizability:** Limited external validation; neighboring work focuses on MoE for time series but not on LLM-guided causal extraction, raising questions about robustness across diverse domains.

## Confidence
- **MMoE dual-gating fusion:** High (Table 3 ablations confirm necessity of both routers)  
- **LLM causal extraction via prompting:** Medium (no cross-dataset prompt stress tests reported)  
- **SMoE global trend specialization:** Medium (Figure 6 shows Mann-Kendall correlation but needs broader validation)

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt ablation stress test:** Systematically degrade prompt specificity (e.g., remove trend/frequency constraints) on held-out text-rich datasets; expect significant MSE/MAE increase if prompts are critical.  
2. **Router interpretability audit:** For SMoE, compute Mann-Kendall trend Z-scores for all test samples, group by expert (top-1), and run chi-square test for expert-trend association. For TMoE, analyze router weight distributions across manually tagged text sentiment/trend categories.  
3. **Irregular data misalignment test:** On Time-IMM, introduce artificial temporal shifts between text and series (e.g., ±5 steps). TiMi should show graceful degradation versus alignment-dependent baselines (e.g., Time-MMD), confirming non-fusion design robustness.
