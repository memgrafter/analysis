# How Do Medical MLLMs Fail? A Study on Visual Grounding in Medical Images
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.14323
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14323
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Medical MLLMs significantly underperform in zero‑shot medical image interpretation due to poor visual grounding; the proposed VGRefine method improves accuracy by up to +11.3% across six Med‑VQA benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates why medical multimodal large language models (MLLMs) struggle with interpreting medical images. The authors introduce VGMED, a clinician‑co‑created dataset designed to isolate visual grounding from semantic factors, and evaluate eight state‑of‑the‑art medical MLLMs. Using novel metrics based on KL and JS divergence, they find that models exhibit much weaker attention alignment on medical images than on natural images. To address this, they propose VGRefine, an inference‑time method that refines attention distributions without additional training. Validated on over 110K samples across eight modalities, VGRefine achieves state‑of‑the‑art accuracy on six Med‑VQA benchmarks, with gains averaging +3.1% and peaking at +11.3% on PathVQA. The results point to deficient visual grounding as a pervasive bottleneck in medical MLLMs.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed VGMED, a dataset annotated by clinicians to provide ground‑truth regions of interest, enabling separation of visual (localization) and semantic (label) challenges. They then measured attention alignment of eight medical MLLMs on this dataset using KL and JS divergence between model attention maps and clinician‑defined regions. The VGRefine method operates at inference time: it extracts attention weights from selected layers, compares them to a reference distribution derived from VGMED (or similar), and applies a refinement step to reduce divergence before final output generation. No model retraining is required. Finally, VGRefine was applied to the same models on six standard Med‑VQA benchmarks (total 110K+ samples, covering X‑ray, histopathology, dermatology, etc.) to assess generalizability and accuracy gains.

## Key Results
- Attention alignment (measured by KL/JS divergence) is significantly weaker on medical images than on natural images for all evaluated medical MLLMs.
- VGRefine, a training‑free attention refinement technique, yields state‑of‑the‑art performance on six Med‑VQA benchmarks, improving accuracy by up to +11.3% (PathVQA) and an average +3.1%.
- The findings confirm that deficient visual grounding is a pervasive bottleneck limiting the generalization of medical MLLMs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
VGRefine works by directly correcting the model’s attention distribution to better match clinically relevant regions, as indicated by reference annotations. This alignment ensures that the language generation process is grounded in the correct image areas, reducing hallucinations and improving task accuracy. The method is effective because it operates at the attention level—a key mechanism linking vision and language—without requiring model retraining, thus preserving pretrained knowledge while fixing misalignment.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Image → Vision Encoder → Visual Tokens → LLM (with attention layers) → Output logits; VGRefine: Attention Extractor → Refinement Module → Attention Injector.

**Critical path:**  
During inference, the attention weights from specific LLM layers are extracted, compared to a reference distribution (e.g., from VGMED), refined via a divergence‑minimizing operation, and then re‑injected into the model before final token generation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
VGRefine is training‑free, so it can be applied to any existing MLLM without altering weights, but it adds computational overhead at inference and requires careful tuning of the alignment strength. It specifically targets attention misalignment, leaving other potential deficiencies (e.g., medical knowledge) unaddressed.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Over‑smoothing of attention, causing loss of salient features and degraded performance.  
- Reliance on accurate reference distributions; noisy or out‑of‑domain references may harm alignment.  
- Potential instability if refinement magnitude is too high, leading to oscillatory or nonsense outputs.

3. Test generalization by applying VGRefine to a different modality (e.g., dermatology images) without any modality‑specific parameter adjustments.

## Limitations
- VGMED’s ability to isolate “pure” visual grounding across diverse medical contexts is unverified; external validation on independent clinician‑sourced datasets is lacking.
- The clinical relevance of KL/JS divergence metrics for attention alignment has not been validated against real‑world diagnostic accuracy or clinician assessments.
- The evaluation covers only eight medical MLLMs; conclusions about a “pervasive bottleneck” may not hold for future architectures or models with dedicated medical pretraining.

## Confidence
- Underperformance due to deficient visual grounding: Medium  
- VGMED’s isolation of visual vs. semantic grounding: Low  
- VGRefine’s SOTA gains (+3.1% avg): Medium  
- Visual grounding as a pervasive bottleneck: Low–Medium
