# Fish Audio S2 Technical Report

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08823  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08823  
- **Reference count:** 15  
- **Primary result:** Real-time factor (RTF) of 0.195 and time-to-first-audio (TTFA) < 100 ms on a single H200 GPU, with SOTA word error rates (0.54% Chinese, 0.99% English) on Seed-TTS and 93.3% tag activation on Fish Instruction Benchmark.

## Executive Summary
Fish Audio S2 is a production-ready text-to-speech system that delivers high-quality, fine-grained instruction following with sub-second latency. Its core innovation is a Dual-Autoregressive (Dual-AR) architecture that decouples long‑range temporal semantic modeling (performed by a large language model) from depth‑wise acoustic generation (handled by a lightweight transformer). This separation enables efficient streaming inference with a real‑time factor under 0.2 and TTFA below 100 ms. A multi‑stage data pipeline reuses a speech quality model and a rich‑transcription ASR model as both pre‑training filters and reinforcement‑learning reward signals, eliminating distribution shift and ensuring robust multilingual performance. The system achieves state‑of‑the‑art word error rates on Seed‑TTS and high tag activation on instruction benchmarks, demonstrating scalable, controllable TTS for real‑world applications.

## Method Summary
The method comprises a three‑stage data pipeline and a four‑stage training procedure. Raw audio is processed through voice activity detection, quality filtering, and rich transcription (using an ASR model that outputs inline vocal feature tags). Audio is tokenized with a modified DAC codec: a causal ConvNeXt V2 encoder, an EVA‑GAN decoder, and semantic distillation from w2v‑BERT 2.0. The Dual‑AR architecture uses a Slow AR (Qwen3‑4B) to predict semantic tokens conditioned on text, and a Fast AR (a 4‑layer transformer) to generate acoustic tokens depth‑wise from the Slow AR’s hidden states. Multi‑Codebook Fusion aggregates the tokens before a vocoder produces the waveform. Training stages: (i) tokenizer training with GAN losses; (ii) pre‑training to align the LLM with audio tokens and extend context to 16k tokens; (iii) supervised fine‑tuning on high‑quality instruction‑labeled data; (iv) RL post‑training (GRPO/Dr.GRPO) with multi‑reward—ASR accuracy (R_STT), quality score (R_Pref), and voiceprint similarity (R_SIM)—plus KL penalty via LoRA weight‑swap. Inference uses SGLang with I/O bypass, extended RadixCache for multi‑token keys, and co‑scheduled vocoder decoding to achieve low latency.

## Key Results
- SOTA word error rates on Seed‑TTS: 0.54 % (Chinese), 0.99 % (English).  
- Real‑time factor 0.195 and TTFA < 100 ms on a single H200 GPU.  
- 93.3 % tag activation on Fish Audio Instruction Benchmark; 81.88 % win rate on Emergent TTS Eval.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Dual‑AR decoupling shifts the burden of long‑range semantic planning to a powerful but expensive LLM, which runs only once to produce a sequence of semantic tokens. The lightweight Fast AR then generates the full acoustic representation (all codebooks) for each timestep in parallel or with minimal sequential steps, exploiting the semantic context provided by the Slow AR’s hidden states. This reduces the computational bottleneck of traditional fully autoregressive TTS, where a single large model must generate all tokens sequentially. The staged data pipeline ensures that only high‑quality, well‑transcribed utterances enter training, while the reuse of the quality and ASR models as RL reward functions eliminates any distribution mismatch between pre‑training and fine‑tuning objectives. Multi‑reward RL further balances intelligibility (via ASR), naturalness (via quality model), speaker similarity (via voiceprint), and instruction fidelity (via tag activation), yielding robust performance across languages and speaking styles. Finally, inference‑engine optimizations—RadixCache to reuse KV states across similar prompts, I/O bypass to skip the tokenizer during generation, and co‑scheduling of the vocoder—minimize TTFA and sustain real‑time throughput.

## Foundational Learning
- **RVQ Audio Tokenization** (e.g., DAC)  
  *Why needed:* Converts continuous audio into discrete tokens suitable for autoregressive modeling.  
  *Quick check:* Reconstructed audio achieves SI‑SDR > 20 dB and natural sounding quality.  

- **Large Language Models for Semantic Planning**  
  *Why needed:* Provides strong text understanding and the capacity to generate long, coherent semantic token sequences.  
  *Quick check:* The LLM, when fine‑tuned on tokenized speech, generates token sequences that align with phoneme durations and preserve linguistic content.  

- **Dual‑Autoregressive Decoupling**  
  *Why needed:* Separates high‑level temporal dependencies (Slow AR) from low‑level acoustic detail (Fast AR), drastically reducing compute per acoustic step.  
  *Quick check:* Fast AR inference time per audio frame remains constant regardless of sequence length; no quadratic growth with context.  

- **Reinforcement Learning for TTS (GRPO)**  
  *Why needed:* Optimizes non‑differentiable objectives like tag activation and speaker similarity beyond what supervised fine‑tuning can achieve.  
  *Quick check:* RL post‑training improves tag activation rate on held‑out instructions without degrading WER.  

- **Multi‑Codebook Token Fusion**  
  *Why needed:* Combines indices from multiple RVQ codebooks into a single representation for the vocoder, preserving both content and timbre.  
  *Quick check:* Vocoded speech from fused tokens matches the original speaker’s voice and intelligibility metrics.  

- **Streaming Optimizations (RadixCache, MPS)**  
  *Why needed:* Minimizes time to first audio and achieves real‑time factor by reusing KV caches and overlapping vocoder computation.  
  *Quick check:* Enabling RadixCache and MPS scheduling reduces TTFA by > 50 % compared to naive generation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Text (with tags) → LLM Tokenizer → Slow AR (Qwen3‑4B) → Semantic tokens → Fast AR (4‑layer Transformer) → Acoustic tokens (depth‑wise) → Multi‑Codebook Fusion → Vocoder (EVA‑GAN) → Audio  

**Critical path:**  
1. Text tokenization via LLM tokenizer.  
2. Slow AR performs autoregressive forward pass to produce a sequence of hidden states and semantic tokens.  
3. Fast AR, conditioned on each Slow AR hidden state, predicts the full set of acoustic codebook indices for the corresponding timestep (depth‑wise).  
4. Acoustic tokens from all timesteps are fused across codebooks.  
5. Vocoder synthesizes the waveform in a streaming fashion, overlapping with Fast AR generation via MPS scheduling.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Decoupling*: Choosing a heavy LLM (4B parameters) for semantic modeling ensures high‑level instruction adherence but introduces latency; mitigated by limiting Slow AR to a single forward pass per generated segment. The Fast AR is intentionally shallow (4 layers) to keep per‑timestep cost low, accepting that it cannot model long‑range acoustic dependencies—this is delegated to the Slow AR.  
- *RL reward design*: Using three reward signals (ASR, quality, voiceprint) avoids over‑optimizing any single metric but requires careful balancing via loss weights; improper weighting can cause speaker drift or tag neglect.  
- *Inference optimizations*: RadixCache reduces repeated attention computation but increases memory usage; co‑scheduling with the vocoder hides latency at the cost of scheduling complexity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low tag activation rate (~< 80 %) indicates Fast AR not following instructions, likely due to insufficient conditioning or mis‑scaled R_SIM/R_STT rewards.  
- RTF > 1.0 or TTFA > 500 ms suggests RadixCache misses (low hit rate) or disabled MPS, possibly because the Fast AR grew too large or token bypass is not used.  
- Degraded speaker similarity (SIM) after RL points to an overly strong R_SIM weight or KL penalty too low, causing overfitting to reference speaker timbre.  
- Hallucinations or token skipping in long‑form generations often stem from context window overflow (exceeding 16k tokens) or incorrect RoPE handling for codebook layer IDs.

**First experiments:**  
1. **Tokenizer reconstruction** – Train the DAC variant on a small audio subset, measure SI‑SDR and mel‑spectrogram error to confirm fidelity.  
2. **Semantic token generation** – Feed text prompts with inline tags into the Slow AR; verify that the number of semantic tokens matches expected audio duration and that tag tokens appear in the output.  
3. **End‑to‑end latency measurement** – Generate a 30‑second audio with the full pipeline, measuring TTFA and RTF with and without RadixCache/MPS to validate streaming optimizations.

## Limitations
- Proprietary components (in‑house speech quality model and rich‑transcription ASR) are central to the data pipeline and RL rewards; their exact architectures and weights are not released, preventing faithful reproduction and causing distribution shift when using open‑source substitutes.  
- Precise training hyperparameters (e.g., loss weights λ, GRPO group size, KL penalty β, learning rates) are omitted; these critically affect performance but must be reverse‑engineered from code.  
- The composition and filtering thresholds of the > 10 million‑hour pre‑training dataset are undisclosed, limiting understanding of data coverage and potential biases.

## Confidence
- Dual‑AR architectural decoupling: **High** (explicitly described and conceptually sound).  
- Staged data pipeline with reused models: **Medium** (procedure clear but exact thresholds and proprietary models unknown).  
- Reported performance metrics (WER, TAR, RTF): **Low** (claims rely on unreleased proprietary models and exact training setup).  
- Streaming inference engine design: **Medium** (modifications are detailed but unverified on target hardware).
