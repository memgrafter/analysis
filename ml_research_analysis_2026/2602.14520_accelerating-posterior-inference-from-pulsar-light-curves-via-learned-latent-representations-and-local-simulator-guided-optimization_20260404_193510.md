# Accelerating Posterior Inference from Pulsar Light Curves via Learned Latent Representations and Local Simulator-Guided Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14520
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14520
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** The method achieves a 120× speedup (24 hours → 12 minutes) while matching MCMC posterior estimates and preserving multimodal and degenerate structures for PSR J0030+0451.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of computationally expensive posterior inference for pulsar light curves, which is typically performed via MCMC. They propose a two-stage framework: first, a pretrained masked U-Net encoder creates multi-scale latent embeddings from which similar simulated light curves are retrieved to form an initial empirical posterior approximation; second, this approximation is locally refined using a simulator-guided hill-climbing optimization that minimizes Poisson negative log-likelihood. Applied to real NICER data for PSR J0030+0451, the method yields a 120× speedup—reducing inference time from 24 hours to 12 minutes—while reproducing MCMC posterior estimates across all parameters and preserving multimodal and degenerate features. The approach also generalizes to a gamma-ray spectroscopy task, demonstrating broader applicability.

## Method Summary
The method consists of an offline pretraining and database construction phase followed by online inference. The pretraining stage learns a masked U-Net encoder: given a 64-bin light curve, 75% of the bins are randomly masked, and the model is trained to reconstruct only the masked values using MSE loss. This forces the encoder to develop multi-scale features that capture the underlying physics. For the simulation bank of 10 million light curves with their 11-dimensional parameter vectors, the frozen encoder extracts two sets of features: after the third encoder block (level-3, 256 channels) and after the bottleneck (512 channels). Both are globally average pooled, concatenated (768 dimensions), and ℓ2-normalized to produce an embedding. All embeddings are stored in a FAISS index for cosine similarity search.

At query time, the observed light curve is embedded, and the top 20,000 nearest neighbors (by inner product) are retrieved. These are re-ranked using the median normalized squared error (MdNSE) in flux space, and the best K=4000 are kept, yielding an empirical posterior estimate. To refine this estimate, each of the K seed parameter vectors is locally optimized using an adaptive P-dimensional (default P=5) sign-search hill climber. Each iteration randomly selects P parameters, enumerates all 2^P sign combinations, and accepts a step that strictly decreases the Poisson negative log-likelihood computed by the fast forward simulator. The step size adapts (grow/shrink) based on improvement, and the process stops after a plateau. The refined samples approximate the true posterior at a fraction of the computational cost of MCMC.

## Key Results
- 120× speedup: inference time reduced from 24 hours (MCMC) to 12 minutes.
- Posterior accuracy: parameter estimates match MCMC across all dimensions, preserving multimodal and degenerate structures.
- Retrieval converges with K=4000; hill climbing yields mean Poisson NLL within 0.84 units of the MCMC reference after jointly perturbing up to 5 parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Similar pulsar light curves (in terms of their underlying physical parameters) map to nearby points in a learned latent embedding space, enabling global retrieval of approximate posterior support.
- **Mechanism:** A masked U-Net is pretrained to reconstruct partially masked light curves. This forces its encoder to develop representations that capture the multi-scale structure necessary to infer missing values. The resulting encoder embeddings for full light curves organize the simulation bank such that cosine similarity in this space correlates with similarity in the original parameter space.
- **Core assumption:** The pretraining task (masked reconstruction) induces an encoder whose latent topology respects the true (unknown) manifold linking light curve morphology to physical parameters, even in degenerate regions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "a pretrained masked U-Net encoder creates multi-scale latent embeddings from which similar simulated light curves are retrieved"
  - [section 2] "masked U-Net... to reconstruct masked light curves, yielding an encoder that provides meaningful multi-scale features for retrieval."
  - [corpus] *Weak evidence.* Corpus neighbors focus on other posterior acceleration methods (normalizing flows, SBI) but do not directly address masked autoencoding for retrieval-based inference. The mechanism is novel to this paper.
- **Break condition:** Degeneracies not captured during training could cause non-similar embeddings for physically similar light curves, leading to retrieval failure in certain parameter regions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Local, simulator-guided hill-climbing from retrieved points sharpens the posterior density within each mode without collapsing distinct modes or erasing degeneracies, converging toward high-likelihood regions.
- **Mechanism:** Starting from each retrieved parameter vector, an adaptive P-dimensional sign-search hill-climber iteratively perturbs a subset of parameters, accepting moves that strictly decrease the Poisson negative log-likelihood (NLL) computed by the forward simulator. This moves each point uphill in likelihood within its local basin.
- **Core assumption:** The retrieved points lie within the basin of attraction of a high-likelihood region corresponding to a true posterior mode. The local, greedy optimization will not jump between disconnected modes but will refine points already near the optimum.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "this approximation is locally refined using a simulator-guided hill-climbing optimization... progressively shifting the empirical posterior toward higher-likelihood parameter regions."
  - [section 3] "local optimization sharpens posterior mass within each region of support without collapsing distinct modes or erasing parameter degeneracies captured by the retrieval stage."
  - [section 6] detailed description of the adaptive, block-based hill-climbing procedure minimizing Poisson NLL.
- **Break condition:** If the retrieved point is not in the basin of a high-likelihood mode (e.g., in a low-likelihood valley between modes), hill-climbing may converge to a nearby local optimum that does not represent the true posterior mode. The method relies on retrieval to place seeds correctly.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Multi-scale embeddings (fusing intermediate and bottleneck U-Net features) better preserve both global pulse-profile structure and localized morphological details (e.g., peak width, asymmetry) than single-scale embeddings or raw-space similarity, leading to higher-fidelity retrieval.
- **Mechanism:** The U-Net encoder produces feature maps at multiple resolutions. Features from an intermediate encoder level (with higher temporal resolution) are pooled and concatenated with pooled bottleneck features (with larger receptive fields). This combined embedding captures complementary information: local phase-resolved details and global shape.
- **Core assumption:** Pulsar light curve similarity is determined by a combination of fine-scale morphological features (best captured at intermediate layers) and coarse-scale periodic structure (best captured at the bottleneck). A single scale is insufficient.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.2] "To additionally capture local pulse morphology, we fuse intermediate features with the bottleneck... Multi-level embeddings produced retrieved curves that most faithfully match phase-dependent structure."
  - [section 7.1] "Multi-level embeddings produced retrieved curves that most faithfully match phase-dependent structure, including peak width, asymmetry, and baseline morphology."
  - [appendix C] Ablation study showing multi-level embeddings yield tighter retrieval envelopes around query curves compared to single-level or raw-space cosine similarity.
- **Break condition:** If the intermediate features are too noisy or high-frequency, concatenation could introduce irrelevant variance. The benefit assumes the intermediate layer (level-3) is the optimal "sweet spot" for capturing meaningful morphology without excessive noise.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Masked Autoencoding (Self-Supervised Pretraining)**
  - *Why needed here:* The U-Net must learn a representation where the embedding distance reflects similarity in the *underlying physical parameters*, not just pixel-space similarity. Randomly masking 75% of phase bins forces the model to build contextual representations from visible parts to reconstruct missing ones, encouraging it to learn features tied to the generative physics.
  - *Quick check question:* If a model is trained to reconstruct 75%-masked light curves, what inductive bias does this create about the informativeness of different parts of the curve for predicting the whole?

- **Concept: Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC) via Retrieval**
  - *Why needed here:* The retrieval stage (`p0(θ|x*) ∝ Σ δ(θ_i) * I[similar(x_i, x*)]`) is an instantiation of the ABC rejection algorithm, where the "distance" is computed in a learned, informative low-dimensional embedding space instead of hand-crafted summary statistics. This approximates the global posterior support non-parametrically.
  - *Quick check question:* How does using a learned embedding space for similarity search differ conceptually from using a hand-chosen set of summary statistics in ABC?

- **Concept: Local vs. Global Posterior Structure**
  - *Why needed here:* Pulsar posteriors are often multimodal and degenerate due to symmetries in the magnetic field model. The method’s two-stage design explicitly separates tasks: retrieval (global, mode discovery) vs. refinement (local, mode sharpening). Understanding this decomposition is key to why the method preserves multimodality.
  - *Quick check question:* Why would a pure local optimization from a single starting point fail for this problem, and how does the retrieval stage address that failure mode?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
- **Masked U-Net Encoder (`f_ϕ`):** Pretrained model. Input: 64-bin light curve. Output: Multi-scale features (level-3: 256x16, bottleneck: 512x8). Frozen during online inference.
- **Embedding Database:** Offline product. For 10M simulations: store (z_i, θ_i) where z_i = GAP(bottleneck) ⊕ GAP(level-3), L2-normalized.
- **k-NN Retriever:** Online. Given query x*, computes z* = f_ϕ(x*), performs top-20k cosine similarity search, then reranks top-4k using MdNSE (Eq.18).
- **Hill-Climber:** Online. Takes K=4000 seed parameters {θ_k}. For each, runs adaptive P-dimensional sign-search minimizing Poisson NLL L(θ) using simulator F(θ). Outputs refined seeds.

**Critical path:** Pretraining (offline, GPU) → Embedding DB build (offline, GPU) → Query: Encode → Retrieve → Parallel Refinement (CPU cluster). The online path is retrieval (seconds) + refinement (minutes).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **K (retrieval size):** Larger K captures more posterior support (reduces KL divergence) but increases refinement cost linearly. Paper finds convergence at K=4000.
- **P (jointly perturbed parameters):** Larger P navigates correlated landscapes better but exponentially increases candidates per step (2^P). Paper finds P=5 sufficient, with diminishing returns.
- **Embedding type:** Multi-level > Single-level > Raw-space. Multi-level adds concat cost but improves fidelity significantly (Appendix C).

**Failure signatures:**
- **Retrieval failure:** Top-k neighbors have high MdNSE to query, or retrieved parameter set shows no multimodal structure in known degenerate dimensions.
- **Refinement failure:** Hill-climbing fails to reduce NLL significantly from initial seeds, or converges to widely different NLL values for seeds from the same retrieved mode (indicating instability).
- **Multimodality collapse:** All refined seeds converge to a single parameter region, while MCMC shows distinct modes. Suggests retrieval missed modes or hill-climbing jumped between them.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Pretraining sanity check:** Train masked U-Net on synthetic bank. Verify reconstruction loss decreases and that masking ratio 0.75 is effective (visualize masked/reconstructed curves).
2. **Embedding validation:** For a synthetic bank with known parameter clusters (e.g., two distinct hot spot geometries that produce similar light curves), compute embedding t-SNE/UMAP. Verify clusters correspond to parameter clusters, not just light curve similarity.
3. **Retrieval + Refinement pipeline test:** On a held-out simulation, run full pipeline. Compare 1D marginal of refined seeds to known true parameters. Check that refinement improves NLL without collapsing distinct true modes present in the prior.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Under what conditions on the simulation bank density, embedding smoothness, and posterior geometry does the retrieval-plus-refinement method converge to the true posterior distribution?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors state that their method "does not aim to generate exact posterior samples or provide asymptotic guarantees" (Section 3) and rely on empirical alignment with MCMC for a single observation. No theoretical analysis connects nearest-neighbor retrieval in learned embedding space to Bayesian consistency, nor addresses how local hill-climbing affects global posterior structure.
- **Why unresolved:** The method is heuristic; without convergence guarantees, it is unclear when results can be trusted, especially for new domains or observation types. The retrieval approximates global support via empirical samples, but statistical properties are unknown. Refinement sharpens locally but may not preserve correct relative densities across modes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretical analysis showing that, as simulation bank size N→∞ and under smoothness assumptions on the encoder and simulator, the empirical posterior from top-K retrieval converges in some metric (e.g., total variation) to the true posterior. Additionally, analysis of hill-climbing as a manifold-constrained optimization and conditions under which it preserves separate basins corresponding to distinct modes.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the method's accuracy degrade when the observed light curve corresponds to parameters outside the support of the simulation bank (i.e., model misspecification), and can this be detected from retrieval distances?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The framework assumes the simulation bank contains enough diversity that retrieval yields "similar simulated light curves" (Introduction) and that the empirical posterior ˆp0(θ|x∗) approximates regions of parameter space supported by the observation (Section 3). The paper tests only on a single pulsar with a presumably well-covered parameter space and does not consider queries that are out-of-distribution relative to the simulation bank.
- **Why unresolved:** In practice, simulation banks are finite and may not span all physically plausible configurations. If the query is in a region with no nearby simulations, retrieval may return distant, misleading samples, leading to poor refinement. The method has no built-in mechanism to detect or handle such cases.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments where synthetic queries are generated from parameters deliberately placed outside the simulation bank's convex hull or in low-density regions. Metrics: retrieval distance distribution, NLL after refinement, coverage of true parameters if known, and comparison to MCMC extended via importance sampling from the same bank. Development of a diagnostic, such as the minimum cosine similarity or MdNSE among retrieved samples, to flag potential out-of-distribution queries.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the computational efficiency and accuracy of the method scale with the number of physical parameters P, and what is the practical limit of P for which retrieval-plus-refinement outperforms MCMC?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper demonstrates success with P=11 (pulsar) and P=4 (spectroscopy). The hill-climbing refinement uses an adaptive P-dimensional block update, but computational cost per iteration grows with the number of candidate sign patterns considered. Table 1 shows that jointly perturbing P=5 parameters captures most improvement, but P=11 is still handled via adaptive block selection rather than full 2^11 search. The paper does not explore scaling beyond P=11.
- **Why unresolved:** The curse of dimensionality affects both retrieval (embedding space becomes sparse) and refinement (local search less effective). It is unclear at what P the retrieval fails to find any good neighbors or the hill-climbing becomes too costly. The 120× speedup over MCMC is reported for one specific P=11 problem; MCMC's cost also scales poorly with P, so the speedup factor may change.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic benchmarks using synthetic simulation banks with varying P (e.g., P=5,15,25,50), keeping other factors constant. Measure: (a) retrieval precision (e.g., median MdNSE of top-K), (b) number of refinement iterations to reach target NLL, (c) total time vs. MCMC (using same simulator), and (d) posterior agreement metrics (e.g., KL to MCMC reference if feasible for moderate P). This would identify the crossover point where the method loses advantage.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can hill-climbing refinement merge distinct posterior modes that were separated by retrieval, and under what conditions does multimodality fail to be preserved?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors claim refinement "preserves multimodality while increasing posterior density" (Section 1) and show in Figure 3 that multimodal structures (e.g., for αD, ϕD) are preserved. However, this is shown for a single observation; the mechanism is described as "local" and "sharpens posterior mass within each region of support without collapsing distinct modes" (Section 3), but no proof or analysis of mode separation is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** Hill-climbing is a local optimizer; if two retrieved samples from different modes lie in the same attraction basin of the NLL surface, they could converge to the same refined point, effectively merging modes. The retrieval stage may also place samples near basin boundaries. Without understanding the NLL landscape's relation to parameter degeneracies, it is uncertain whether multimodality is always preserved, especially for higher P where basins may be complex.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** For the pulsar task, perform clustering on retrieved samples (before refinement) and track how many distinct clusters survive after refinement, measuring cluster purity relative to known symmetries. More generally, construct synthetic test problems with known multiple isolated modes (e.g., multimodal Gaussian mixtures in parameter space with simulator mapping) and check whether refinement from separate retrieval clusters converges to distinct optima. Vary step size adaptation parameters to see their effect on mode merging.

## Limitations
- Requires a large simulation bank (∼10⁶–10⁷ curves) and a fast simulator; neither is released.
- Hill-climbing hyperparameters (initial step size, grow/shrink factors, plateau patience) are underspecified.
- No theoretical guarantees on convergence or preservation of multimodal posteriors; validation limited to a single observation.
