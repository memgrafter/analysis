# Do Generative Metrics Predict YOLO Performance? An Evaluation Across Models, Augmentation Ratios, and Dataset Complexity

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18525
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18525
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Synthetic augmentation improves YOLO mAP substantially only in challenging regimes (e.g., Pedestrian +7.6%, PottedPlant +30.6%) but not in near-saturated ones; global generative metrics rarely reliably predict downstream detection performance after controlling for augmentation ratio.

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically evaluates whether standard generative metrics (e.g., FID) computed on synthetic image datasets can predict their utility for augmenting YOLOv11 object detection training. Across three single-class detection regimes with varying complexity (sparse Traffic Signs, dense Pedestrian, multi-instance PottedPlant) and six image generators, synthetic data yields significant mAP gains only in the more challenging regimes when training from scratch. After statistically residualizing for augmentation ratio, only a small subset of metrics shows consistent, significant correlations with mAP, and no single global metric universally predicts performance. Metric-effectiveness is strongly mediated by dataset characteristics and training initialization (from-scratch vs. COCO-pretrained). The results suggest that generative metrics alone are insufficient for fixed-budget generator screening in detection tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors construct three single-class detection datasets (Cityscapes Pedestrian, Traffic Signs, COCO PottedPlant). For each, they generate 3,000 synthetic images using six generative models (GANs, diffusion, hybrid). Synthetic images are labeled via a teacher YOLOv11 proposal system followed by human audit. Pre-training dataset metrics are computed under a matched-size bootstrap protocol: global feature-space distances (Inception-v3, DINOv2: FID/FD/KD, precision/recall, density/coverage) and object-centric distribution metrics (Wasserstein/JSD over bounding-box statistics, difficult-object ratio). YOLOv11 is trained from scratch and with COCO pretraining on mixtures of real and synthetic data at augmentation ratios from 10% to 150% of the real training size. Held-out real test mAP@0.50:0.95 is recorded. To isolate generator-specific signal from augmentation quantity, both mAP and metrics are residualized by regressing out augmentation ratio. Residual Spearman correlations are computed and corrected for multiple testing (Benjamini–Hochberg). Generator screening performance at fixed budgets is evaluated via Kendall τ and regret.

## Key Results
- Synthetic augmentation yields large relative mAP gains in challenging regimes (Pedestrian +7.6%, PottedPlant +30.6%) but marginal gains in near-saturated Traffic Signs (≤1.5%) and under pretrained fine-tuning (≤1.3%).
- After residual analysis and multiple-testing correction, only a few metrics show statistically significant associations (e.g., FID/FD/KD variants negatively correlated with mAP in Pedestrian/PottedPlant; object-centric difficult-object ratio differences positively associated in PottedPlant).
- Generator screening (Kendall τ) is effective only in specific settings, notably from-scratch PottedPlant (e.g., `kd_value_inception` τ=0.422, perfect Top-1 selection), but weak or inconsistent elsewhere, especially with pretrained initialization.
- No single global generative metric universally predicts downstream detection mAP; predictive power is highly regime-dependent.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Synthetic augmentation improves detection performance primarily in detection regimes with clear headroom (e.g., dense/occlusion-heavy or high-variability multi-instance scenes), but yields marginal gains in near-saturated or simpler regimes.
- **Mechanism:** When the real-only detector leaves performance headroom, synthetic data introduces new object instances, layouts, or contexts that address dataset modes missing from the real training distribution. In regimes where the real data already covers the test distribution well (e.g., sparse Traffic Signs), adding synthetic data risks distribution shift or saturation with negligible benefit.
- **Core assumption:** Real-only model performance indicates the degree of "coverage" of the test distribution by the real training set; synthetic data can fill gaps but may hurt if it introduces significant mismatch.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Synthetic augmentation yields substantial gains in Pedestrian (+7.6%) and PottedPlant (+30.6%) but marginal gains in Traffic Signs (≤1.5%) and under pretrained fine-tuning (≤1.3%).
  - Weak or inconsistent metric–performance alignment in Traffic Signs and pretrained settings.
- **Break condition:** Fails when the real training set is already near-saturated for the test distribution or when using a strong pretrained initialization that already encodes robust features.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The predictive alignment between pre-training synthetic-data metrics and downstream YOLO performance is mediated by the training initialization regime (from-scratch vs. COCO-pretrained).
- **Mechanism:** Training from scratch makes the detector highly sensitive to the distribution of its training data, so synthetic dataset properties (captured by metrics) have a stronger association with mAP. With COCO pretraining, the model starts with a rich feature prior; the relative influence of synthetic augmentation is smaller and more susceptible to distribution shift, leading to weaker metric-performance alignment.
- **Core assumption:** Pretrained representations reduce the model's dependence on the exact training data distribution for learning basic features, dampening the effect of synthetic data quality/quantity on final performance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Pretrained gains are small (≤1.3%) and concentrated at low augmentation ratios; from-scratch gains are larger and persist across wider ranges.
  - Residual correlations are generally weaker and rarely significant under pretrained fine-tuning; strongest screening signal appears in from-scratch PottedPlant.
- **Break condition:** Does not apply when a suitable pretrained model is unavailable or when domain shift is so extreme that pretraining provides little transferable prior.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Global image-level generative metrics (FID/FD, etc.) computed in standard (Inception-v3) or self-supervised (DINOv2) embeddings show inconsistent, regime-specific alignment with detection mAP after controlling for augmentation ratio; object-centric distribution metrics provide complementary but still non-universal signal.
- **Mechanism:** Global metrics summarize overall visual realism/diversity but do not explicitly measure object-level properties (instance density, scale distribution, difficulty/occlusion patterns) that are crucial for detection. Object-centric metrics (Wasserstein/JSD over label statistics) directly target these properties, but their predictive power still depends on whether the specific difficulty (e.g., small objects, occlusion) is the key challenge in the target regime.
- **Core assumption:** Detection performance depends on label-space distribution match (e.g., object count, size prevalence) in addition to image-space realism; no single metric captures all relevant aspects across diverse detection regimes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - After multiple-testing correction, only some metric–dataset pairs survive (e.g., `kd_value_inception` negatively correlated with mAP in PottedPlant but weak/positive in Traffic Signs).
  - Residual heatmaps (Figure 4) show dataset-dependent sign/magnitude of correlations.
- **Break condition:** Fails in regimes where the dominant performance drivers are not captured by the measured metrics (e.g., texture, background complexity, or annotation noise not reflected in bounding-box stats).

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Distribution Shift & Synthetic Data Gap**  
  **Why needed here:** The paper's core question hinges on whether synthetic data closes a real distribution gap. Understanding covariate shift (input distribution) vs. label shift (object statistics) is key to interpreting metric-performance relationships.  
  **Quick check:** What is the difference between a global feature-space distance (e.g., FID) and an object-centric distribution distance (e.g., Wasserstein on instance counts), and why might the latter be more informative for detection?

- **Concept: Spearman/Residual Correlation & Multiple Testing**  
  **Why needed here:** The analysis uses rank correlations and augmentation residualization to isolate generator-specific signal from the confounding effect of augmentation amount. BH–FDR correction is applied across many tests.  
  **Quick check:** Why does regressing out augmentation ratio before computing correlation better target the "fixed-budget generator screening" use-case?

- **Concept: YOLO Training Dynamics & Headroom**  
  **Why needed here:** The paper interprets results based on "headroom" (gap between real-only baseline and an upper bound). Understanding saturation effects and how YOLO responds to increased data diversity is essential.  
  **Quick check:** Why might a near-saturated dataset (Traffic Signs) show little benefit from synthetic augmentation even if the synthetic data is high-fidelity?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Curated single-class datasets (Pedestrian, Traffic Signs, PottedPlant) -> Six generative models (DiT, ADM, DiffusionGAN, StyleGAN2-ADA, ProjectedGAN, LayoutDiffusion) -> 3k synthetic images per generator at 256x256 -> Teacher YOLOv11 proposal (conf=0.25, iou=0.45) -> Human audit/correction -> Remove empty images -> Construct augmented training sets at ratios 10%–150% -> Matched-size bootstrap metric computation (global + object-centric) -> YOLOv11 training (from-scratch or COCO-pretrained, fixed hyperparams, early stopping) -> Held-out real test mAP@0.50:0.95 -> Residual analysis (regress out augmentation ratio) -> Benjamini–Hochberg correction -> Screening evaluation (Kendall τ, regret).

**Critical path:** Dataset curation -> Synthetic generation & labeling -> Metric computation (matched-size) -> YOLO training (grid of gen×aug×init) -> Held-out test mAP -> Residual correlation (regress out augmentation ratio) -> Multiple-testing correction -> Screening evaluation. The residual analysis step is critical to answer the screening use-case; skipping it conflates augmentation amount with generator quality.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Matched-size bootstrap:** Controls for sample-size bias in FID/FD but introduces noise; tradeoff between comparability across augmentation ratios and estimator variance.
- **Single-class setting:** Simplifies labeling and analysis but limits generalizability to multi-class detection.
- **Fixed YOLO recipe:** Ensures comparability but may not be optimal for each synthetic dataset; results reflect "default" YOLO response.

**Failure signatures:**
- Regime saturation: Traffic Signs shows flat augmentation curves; mAP gains <1.5% from any generator.
- Metric collapse: Many global metrics show weak/insignificant residual correlations after BH–FDR, especially under pretrained initialization.
- Non-reproducible small deltas: Multi-seed check shows pretrained gains (e.g., PottedPlant +1.2%) can fall within run-to-run variability.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline augmentation curve: Train YOLOv11 from scratch on each dataset with increasing synthetic fractions (e.g., DiffusionGAN). Verify PottedPlant shows large gains (~+30%) while Traffic Signs is flat.
2. Metric residual correlation: For PottedPlant from-scratch grid, compute residual Spearman between `kd_value_inception` and held-out mAP. Expect significant negative correlation (ρ≈–0.42) after BH–FDR.
3. Screening at fixed budget: At 100% augmentation for PottedPlant (from-scratch), rank generators by `kd_value_inception`. Check if top-ranked generator matches the best-mAP generator (should achieve perfect Top-1 if screening works).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Do global generative metrics (Inception-v3 vs DINOv2) predict YOLOv11 mAP? Alignment is regime-specific; no metric consistently predicts after augmentation control.
2. Do object‑centric distribution metrics add predictive value for YOLOv11 beyond global metrics? Show some association in specific regimes but not consistently; added value unclear.
3. How do metric‑performance relationships vary across regimes and initialization, and can metrics guide generator selection at fixed budget? Relationships highly dataset/initialization specific; screening effective only in limited cases.
4. Do the results generalize to multi‑class detection and detector architectures beyond YOLOv11? Experiments only on single‑class YOLOv11; generalizability unknown.
5. Can a learned composite predictor from the full metric suite improve fixed‑budget generator screening? Individual metrics have limited screening power; composite could generalize but is untested.

## Limitations
- The labeling pipeline for synthetic data (teacher YOLO proposals + human audit) is underspecified, raising risks of uncontrolled labeling bias.
- Results are restricted to single-class detection and YOLOv11; generalizability to multi-class and other detectors is unknown.
- The matched-size bootstrap introduces noise; while necessary for comparability, it may obscure subtle signals.

## Confidence
- **Uncertainty 1: Metric Generalizability** – The finding that only a subset of metrics show significant residual correlations is regime-specific. Confidence: **Medium** (supported by consistent patterns in Pedestrian/PottedPlant but weak results elsewhere).
- **Uncertainty 2: Residual Analysis Validity** – Regressing out augmentation ratio is critical but linearity and interactions are not exhaustively validated. Confidence: **Medium** (method is sound but alternative approaches untested).
- **Uncertainty 3: Labeling Pipeline Reproducibility** – Manual annotation protocol details (annotator count, guidelines, agreement) are missing. Confidence: **Low** (known failure mode not quantified).

## Next Checks
1. Re-run residual correlations including generator × augmentation interaction terms to test whether augmentation effects differ by generator, potentially invalidating the linear residual approach.
2. Audit synthetic annotation consistency: sample 100 synthetic images per generator/dataset; have two annotators re-annotate independently; compute IoU and count agreement with the paper’s audited boxes.
3. Test metric generalizability on an external regime: apply top-performing metrics (e.g., `kd_value_inception`) to predict YOLO performance on a new dataset (e.g., COCOAnimals subset) not used in the paper’s three regimes to probe claimed regime-dependence.
