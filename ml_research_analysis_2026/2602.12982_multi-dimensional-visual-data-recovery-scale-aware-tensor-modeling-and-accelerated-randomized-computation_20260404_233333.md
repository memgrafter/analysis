# Multi-Dimensional Visual Data Recovery: Scale-Aware Tensor Modeling and Accelerated Randomized Computation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12982  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12982  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Proposes a nonconvex gradient-domain regularizer within FCTN decomposition combined with randomized sketching, achieving up to 88% CPU time reduction while significantly improving reconstruction accuracy (PSNR/SSIM) and robustness for large-scale multi-dimensional data.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a scalable framework for recovering high-order tensors from incomplete, noisy, or quantized observations. It integrates a novel **Fully-Connected Tensor Network (FCTN) with a nonconvex gradient-domain regularizer (FCTN-GNTCTV)** that simultaneously exploits global low-rankness and local smoothness. Computational bottlenecks from repeated SVDs are mitigated via **randomized sketching** techniques with theoretical error bounds. The optimization is performed using an **ADMM-based algorithm** with convergence guarantees. Extensive experiments on color images, videos, hyperspectral data, MRI, and face datasets demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in accuracy and speed, particularly under quantized settings.

## Method Summary
The proposed method addresses multi-dimensional tensor recovery by modeling the underlying signal as an FCTN decomposition. A regularizer, FCTN-GNTCTV, computes gradient tensors along selected modes, unfolds them via permutations, and applies a nonconvex penalty Φ to their singular values to promote smoothness and low-rankness. For quantized observations, a uniformly dithered quantization model is incorporated. Optimization uses the ADMM framework: the tensor variable is updated via a proximal operator involving the FCTN-GNTCTV penalty, the sparse error term via a nonconvex ψ penalty, and gradient variables are enforced analytically. To accelerate costly SVD computations in the FCTN updates, **randomized sketching** (either fixed-rank or fixed-accuracy) approximates the necessary subspaces using random projections and QRCP. This combination yields both high accuracy and substantial speedups.

## Key Results
- Consistently outperforms state-of-the-art tensor completion/robust completion methods in PSNR, SSIM, and RSE across diverse datasets (color images, videos, hyperspectral, MRI, faces).
- Randomized variants achieve approximately **88% CPU time reduction** compared to deterministic versions with nearly equivalent accuracy.
- Demonstrates strong performance in **quantized recovery** scenarios, handling varying bit-depths and noise levels effectively.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The FCTN‑GNTCTV regularizer simultaneously encodes global low‑rankness and local smoothness, leading to superior reconstruction accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** For a tensor \(\mathbf{X}\), gradient tensors \(\mathbf{G}_t = \nabla_t(\mathbf{X})\) are computed along selected modes. Each \(\mathbf{G}_t\) is unfolded according to a set of permutations, and a nonconvex penalty \(\Phi\) is applied to the singular values of these unfolded matrices. The sum over all modes and permutations yields \(\|\mathbf{X}\|_{\mathcal{FN}} = \frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{t\in\Gamma}\sum_{k=1}^{\bar N}\alpha_k\|\mathbf{G}_t[n_1^k; n_2^k]\|_{\Phi}\), promoting sparsity in the singular values of gradient representations, which indirectly induces smoothness and low‑rankness in \(\mathbf{X}\).
- **Core assumption:** The tensor exhibits both strong global redundancy (low‑rank) and fine‑grained continuity (smooth). The FCTN rank of the gradient tensors bounds that of the original tensor (Lemma IV.1).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States the method proposes “a generalized nonconvex gradient‑domain regularization within the FCTN framework that simultaneously captures global low‑rankness and local smoothness.”
  - [section] Section IV‑A1 formally defines the regularizer (Eq. 11) and Remark IV.2 connects it to L and S priors.
  - [corpus] Prior works combine low‑rank and smoothness (e.g., TCTV‑TC), but the FCTN‑based nonconvex gradient formulation is novel.
- **Break condition:** If the data lacks both low‑rank and smooth structures, or gradient computations become too expensive for very high orders, the regularizer may not improve results.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Randomized sketching replaces costly full SVD computations in FCTN‑based recovery with low‑rank approximations, achieving up to 88% CPU reduction with bounded error.
- **Mechanism:** For each mode \(v\), a random sketching matrix \(\mathbf{G}^{(v)}\) is applied to the unfolding of the current core tensor. Subspace iteration (power steps \(q\)) and QRCP produce a low‑rank basis \(\mathbf{F}_{\rho_v}\). The core tensor is projected onto this subspace, avoiding explicit SVDs and operating on small compressed matrices. Theoretical error bounds (Theorems III.1‑III.6) guarantee that with appropriate sketch size \(l_v\) and oversampling \(p\), the approximation is close to the exact truncated HOSVD.
- **Core assumption:** The tensor admits a low multilinear rank approximation (rapid singular value decay). Random matrices satisfy restricted isometry properties (e.g., Gaussian, SRFT).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Mentions “randomized sketching techniques for computational acceleration” and reports “approximately 88% CPU time reduction.”
  - [section] Section III details Algorithms 1 and 2, sketching strategy, and error bounds.
  - [corpus] Neighboring works on randomized tensor decompositions confirm viability (e.g., “Accelerating Large‑Scale Regularized High‑Order Tensor Recovery”).
- **Break condition:** If the true rank is high (slow decay) or sketch size too small, approximation error may become unacceptable. For very small tensors, overhead may outweigh benefits.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The ADMM‑based optimization efficiently solves the resulting nonconvex models and enjoys convergence guarantees under mild conditions.
- **Mechanism:** The problem is split into sub‑problems for the tensor \(\mathbf{L}\), error term \(\mathbf{E}\), and gradient variables \(\mathbf{G}_t\). Each sub‑problem is solved via proximal operators handling penalties \(\Phi\) and \(\psi\). Lagrange multipliers and a quadratic penalty are updated standardly. Convergence follows from satisfying assumptions of nonconvex ADMM theory (proper, lower‑semicontinuous objective, appropriate penalty parameter).
- **Core assumption:** Penalty functions \(\Phi\) and \(\psi\) have tractable proximal operators (e.g., firm‑thresholding, MCP). Penalty parameter \(\rho\) and hyper‑parameters are selected to ensure descent.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Asserts “Based on the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) framework, we derive efficient optimization algorithms with convergence guarantees.”
  - [section] Section V‑A2 describes ADMM updates for non‑quantized and quantized models; convergence analysis in supplementary material.
  - [corpus] ADMM is standard for tensor completion; neighboring works employ ADMM with convergence guarantees.
- **Break condition:** Improper \(\rho\) tuning or ill‑conditioned sub‑problems can cause stagnation or divergence; nonconvexity may lead to local optima.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fully‑Connected Tensor Network (FCTN) Decomposition**  
  *Why needed:* FCTN avoids mode‑ordering sensitivity of TT/TR and captures correlations across all mode pairs, providing expressive representation for high‑order data.  
  *Quick check:* What is transpositional invariance in FCTN and why does it matter for multi‑dimensional data?

- **Nonconvex Gradient‑Domain Regularization (FCTN‑GNTCTV)**  
  *Why needed:* It jointly promotes global low‑rankness (via FCTN rank on unfolded gradients) and local smoothness (via sparsity of gradient magnitudes), which shallow convex regularizers cannot achieve as effectively.  
  *Quick check:* How does applying a nonconvex penalty \(\Phi\) to the singular values of unfolded gradient tensors reflect the smoothness of the original tensor?

- **Randomized Sketching for Low‑Rank Approximation**  
  *Why needed:* Directly computing SVDs of large unfolding matrices is a computational bottleneck; sketching reduces dimensionality while preserving spectral structure, enabling scalable algorithms.  
  *Quick check:* In the fixed‑rank randomized algorithm, what is the purpose of the power iteration step \(q\) and how does it improve accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input (observed tensor \(\mathbf{M}\)) -> FCTN_GNTCTV (computes gradient tensors, unfolds via permutations, applies Φ to singular values), Noise_Penalty (ψ on entry/tube norms), Quantization_Model (dithered quantization for quantized recovery), Randomized_Compression (approx. SVD via sketching), ADMM_Optimizer (orchestrates updates for \(\mathbf{L}, \mathbf{E}, \mathbf{G}_t\), multipliers).  
  Dependencies: Randomized_Compression feeds into ADMM_Optimizer for FCTN proximal steps; Quantization_Model modifies data fidelity in ADMM sub‑problems.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Input observed tensor (and quantized measurements if applicable).  
  2. Initialize \(\mathbf{L}^{(0)}, \mathbf{E}^{(0)}, \Lambda^{(0)}\).  
  3. Repeat until convergence:  
     a. Update \(\mathbf{L}\): solve \(\min_{\mathbf{L}} \|\mathbf{L}\|_{\mathcal{FN}} + \frac{\beta}{2}\|\mathbf{L} + \mathbf{E} - \mathbf{M} + \Lambda\|_F^2\) (or quantized variant) using Randomized_Compression.  
     b. Update \(\mathbf{E}\): apply \(\text{Prox}_{\psi,\lambda}\) (entry‑wise/tube‑wise).  
     c. Update \(\mathbf{G}_t\): enforce \(\mathbf{G}_t = \nabla_t(\mathbf{L})\) analytically.  
     d. Update multipliers and penalty \(\rho\).  
  4. Return recovered \(\mathbf{L}\) and \(\mathbf{E}\).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Fixed‑rank vs fixed‑accuracy:* Fixed‑rank needs preset target ranks but is simpler; fixed‑accuracy auto‑determines ranks at cost of extra iterations.  
  - *Sketch size \(l_v\):* Larger \(l_v\) improves fidelity but increases per‑iteration cost; typical \(l_v = r_v + p\) with small \(p\); guided by Theorems III.1‑III.6.  
  - *Power iteration \(q\):* Higher \(q\) helps slowly decaying spectra but adds \(O(q)\) mults; recommended \(q \in \{0,1,2\}\).  
  - *Nonconvex penalty choice:* Stronger penalties (e.g., MCP) yield sparser solutions but require careful \(\lambda\) tuning; firm function is robust default.  
  - *Quantization resolution \(\delta\):* Smaller \(\delta\) approximates real‑valued case better but increases effective noise variance; must adjust dithering variance.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *ADMM divergence/stagnation:* residuals increase → reduce \(\rho\) or increase max iterations.  
  - *Degraded reconstruction:* high RSE, low PSNR/SSIM → likely due to wrong rank estimates, mismatched \(\lambda\), or data not smooth; check singular value spectra.  
  - *Randomized slower than deterministic:* sketch size too small causing many inner iterations; increase \(l_v\) or disable power iteration when \(q=0\) suffices.  
  - *Memory exhaustion:* target ranks or sketch sizes too large; use fixed‑accuracy or reduce \(r_v\).  
  - *Quantized model collapse:* \(\delta\) too large relative to signal dynamic range; normalize data to \([0,1]\) and adjust \(\delta\).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Tensor Completion** (non‑quantized) on large‑scale color images/faces: compare deterministic FCTN‑GNTC and randomized R1‑/R2‑FCTN‑GNTC against SOTAs (FCTNFR, TRNNM, HTNN, etc.) under varying sampling ratios (0.1%–3%).  
  2. **Robust Tensor Completion** with sparse noise on remote sensing (MRSIs) and videos: evaluate FCTN‑GNRTC (deterministic/randomized) versus TRNN, HWTSN, TCTV‑RTC under different noise ratios and sampling rates.  
  3. **Quantized Tensor Recovery** on face, MRI, and hyperspectral video datasets: test FCTN‑GNQRTC across quantization resolutions \(\delta\), sampling ratios, and noise levels, measuring PSNR/SSIM vs. CPU time.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What are tighter upper bounds on the approximation error of the fixed‑rank randomized compression Algorithm 1 for tensors with specific structural properties?**  
  The paper’s Theorem III.1 has general bounds but notes accuracy may degrade for slowly decaying spectra unless power iteration is used, indicating potential looseness.
- **How does the choice of nonconvex penalty function \(\Phi(\cdot)\) in the FCTN‑GNTCTV regularizer affect recovery accuracy and algorithm convergence?**  
  Many penalties (firm, Log, ℓq, SCAD, MCP) satisfy assumptions, but experiments use only ℓ1 for fairness; the impact remains unexplored.
- **Can the proposed randomized FCTN framework be extended to streaming or online tensor recovery scenarios where data arrives sequentially?**  
  The paper focuses on batch processing; streaming tensors require incremental updates without full recomputation, which is not addressed.
- **What is the theoretical sample complexity relationship between quantization resolution \(\delta\), sampling ratio SR, and tensor rank for exact recovery in the quantized FCTN‑GNQTC model?**  
  The quantized model is introduced and tested empirically, but no theoretical bounds link \(\delta\), SR, and tensor properties.

## Limitations
- **Ablation Gap:** The paper does not isolate whether gains stem from FCTN structure itself, the nonconvex gradient penalty, or their combination.
- **Hyperparameter Opacity:** Specific optimal values for key parameters (e.g., \(\lambda_{1,2}\), \(\rho\), sketch sizes \(l_v\), power iteration \(q\)) per dataset are undisclosed, hindering reproducibility.
- **Quantization Model Detail:** The exact integration of dithered quantization into ADMM sub‑problems for FCTN‑GNQTC/FCTN‑GNQRTC is not fully elaborated in the main text.
- **Convergence Proof Accessibility:** The convergence guarantee for the nonconvex ADMM is claimed but the full analysis is relegated to supplementary material, leaving assumptions unverified.
