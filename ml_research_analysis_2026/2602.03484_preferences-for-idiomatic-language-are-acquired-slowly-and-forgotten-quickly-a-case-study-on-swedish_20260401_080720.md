# Preferences for Idiomatic Language are Acquired Slowly -- and Forgotten Quickly: A Case Study on Swedish

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2602.03484  
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03484  
- **References:** 32  
- **Primary result:** Idiomatic competence emerges slowly, benefits from English pretraining, and is severely degraded by machine-translated instruction tuning, while general linguistic acceptability remains stable.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how Swedish language models acquire and retain idiomatic language preferences, contrasting them with general syntactic and lexical correctness. Using minimal-pair benchmarks for conventionalized idioms and Translationese, the authors train 135M and 8B parameter models from scratch and via continued pretraining from English. Key findings reveal that idiomatic competence develops more slowly than general linguistic abilities, is significantly bolstered by English pretraining, and suffers a steep decline after instruction tuning on machine-translated data—unlike general acceptability, which proves more robust. The work highlights the fragility of idiomatic knowledge under distribution shifts and the importance of pretraining data characteristics.

## Method Summary
The core method involves training Swedish monolingual models from two initialization strategies: random scratch and continued pretraining (CPT) from English-pretrained checkpoints. Models are trained on the Swedish FineWeb2 corpus, with regular checkpointing. At each checkpoint, models are probed using three curated minimal-pair datasets: (1) linguistic acceptability (DaLAJ, ScaLA) to measure grammatical/lexical correctness; (2) conventionalized idioms (Idiomsall/challenge) to test preference for fixed idiomatic expressions over literal variants; (3) Translationese (Translationeseall/filtered) to contrast literal translations with idiomatic Swedish. Additionally, selected models are instruction-tuned on machine-translated Smol-Smoltalk data, with post-tuning probing to quantify knowledge shift. Performance is measured via full-sentence perplexity comparisons within pairs.

## Key Results
- Idiomatic competence emerges more slowly than general linguistic correctness and requires prolonged exposure; scratch-trained models plateau early at lower performance, while CPT models show steady gains.
- English pretraining provides a stronger foundation for acquiring Swedish idioms, likely by transferring general memorization capabilities for low-frequency forms.
- Instruction tuning on machine-translated data causes a sharp drop in idiomatic preference (e.g., AI Sweden Llama 8B accuracy on Idiomsall fell from 94.14% to 88.91%) and a massive decline on Translationese (from 82.08% to 59.70%), whereas general linguistic acceptability remains largely stable.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Data Scarcity of Idioms
Idioms are acquired slowly because they are relatively rare and variably distributed in typical pretraining corpora (e.g., FineWeb2), which overrepresent formal written text over spontaneous speech. Models first learn abundant surface-level syntactic and lexical patterns; idiomatic forms, being less frequent and often non-compositional, require sustained exposure to be memorized as fixed lexical units. Evidence: Section 5.1.1 notes many idioms are rare in pretraining data and that idiom performance improves with longer training, especially in larger models. If corpora were enriched with transcribed speech, the acquisition gap might narrow.

### Mechanism 2: Transfer of Memorization Capacity from English Pretraining
English-pretrained models acquire Swedish idioms more effectively because high-resource pretraining establishes robust pattern-matching and long-context memory mechanisms that facilitate learning low-frequency expressions. Continued pretraining leverages these transferable capabilities, allowing steady idiom improvement, whereas scratch-trained models plateau earlier—possibly due to less optimized learning dynamics or limited capacity. Evidence: The abstract and Section 5.1.1 show CPT models improving steadily on idioms while scratch models stall; the largest CPT model (8B) shows continued gains, suggesting scale and prior knowledge aid memorization.

### Mechanism 3: Distribution Overwrite by Machine-Translated Tuning Data
Instruction tuning on machine-translated data degrades idiomatic preferences because the tuning set exhibits systematic Translationese artifacts (lexical/syntactic transfer from English) and a formal, flattened stylistic distribution. Fine-tuning reinforces literal, anglicized constructions and suppresses the varied, speech-like idiomatic forms learned during pretraining. General syntactic correctness is more robust due to its high frequency and reinforcement. Evidence: The abstract and Section 5.2 document steep accuracy drops on idiom and Translationese probes post-tuning, with early divergence; Section 5.3 notes tuning amplifies Translationese tendencies and formal phrasing.

- **Transfer Learning in Multilingual Models**: Why needed: To understand the contrast between training from scratch versus continuing from an English model, which is central to explaining why English-initialized models acquire Swedish idioms more effectively.  
  Quick check: What specific capabilities (beyond basic syntax) might an English-pretrained model transfer to a low-resource language like Swedish?

  Quick check: How might the stylistic norms of a machine-translated instruction dataset differ from those of naturally occurring Swedish dialogue or text?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Datasets (Linguistic Acceptability, Idioms, Translationese) -> Models (Scratch/CPT 135M & 8B, plus instruction-tuned variants) -> Probing (minimal-pair perplexity at checkpoints) -> Analysis (learning curves, frequency bins, error patterns)

- **Critical path**:  
  1. **Dataset Construction**: Manual curation of idiom keywords and translationese pairs → LLM-generated fluent minimal-pair sentences → human validation → length/clarity filtering.  
  2. **Pretraining**: Train/continue-pretrain models on Swedish FineWeb2 → save regular checkpoints (e.g., every 200M tokens).  
  3. **Baseline Probing**: Evaluate all checkpoints on all minimal-pair datasets → plot learning curves.  
  4. **Instruction Tuning**: Fine-tune pretrained models on machine-translated Smol-Smoltalk → save checkpoints.  
  5. **Post-tuning Probing**: Evaluate tuned checkpoints → compare pre/post-tuning accuracy deltas.  
  6. **Analysis**: Identify inflection points, plateau behaviors, frequency correlations, and error patterns.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Dataset Scale vs. Quality*: Manual curation ensures cultural accuracy but limits size (n~500–1000); automated generation risks fluency/validity errors.  
  - *Tokenizer Dependence*: Translationese length-filtering is model-specific, reducing cross-architecture comparability.  
  - *Evaluation Sensitivity*: Small datasets increase volatility in accuracy plots, though trends remain consistent.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Scratch models on idioms: Early plateau at ~60–65% accuracy (near random for some subsets), indicating failure to acquire rare forms.  
  - After instruction tuning: Steep accuracy drop (>15 points) on Idiomsall and Translationesefiltered, converging toward 50% (random guessing), while DaLAJ/ScaLA scores remain stable or drop slightly.  
  - Translationese all: All models show very low accuracy (<50%), suggesting an inherent bias toward literal, translation-like syntax even before tuning.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Replicate acquisition curves**: Train a new 135M model from scratch and via CPT on a subset of FineWeb2. Probe every 100M tokens on Idiomsall and DaLAJlexical. Expect CPT to show steady idiom gains; scratch to plateau early.  
  2. **Frequency-memory correlation**: For a CPT model, bin idioms by their frequency in the pretraining corpus. At each checkpoint, compute accuracy per bin. Hypothesis: high-frequency idioms learned first; low-frequency ones improve steadily later.  
  3. **Ablate Translationese filtering**: Train a single model and evaluate on three versions of Translationese: (a) all pairs, (b) length-matched only, (c) length+human-filtered. Measure accuracy difference to quantify bias from length mismatch and ambiguous examples.

## Limitations
- Minimal-pair perplexity comparisons may not fully capture contextual idiomatic competence or production ability.
- Small dataset sizes (n~500–1000) increase result volatility and limit fine-grained error analysis.
- Tokenizer-dependent length-filtering in Translationese set reduces cross-architecture comparability; causal claims (e.g., data scarcity) are correlational without controlled corpus manipulations.

## Confidence
- Slow idiom acquisition vs. general acceptability: Medium (clear learning curves but limited external validation; alternative factors not fully disentangled)
- English pretraining advantage for idioms: High (direct experimental contrast shows consistent large gaps; transfer of memorization capacity is plausible)
- Instruction tuning degrades idiom preference: High (robust drop across models/datasets; link to Translationese bias supported by separate dataset and MT artifacts)

## Next Checks
1. Replicate acquisition curves with a different model family (e.g., GPT-2 small) and a larger, frequency-stratified idiom probe to confirm the scratch plateau and CPT steady gain.
2. Direct frequency-learnability test: For a CPT model, bin idioms by exact match counts in the pretraining corpus; test whether high-frequency items plateau earlier while low-frequency items improve longer.
3. Ablate Translationese filtering: Evaluate the same instruction-tuned model on (a) all Translationese pairs, (b) length-matched only, (c) human-filtered pairs. Quantify how much the accuracy drop depends on filtering criteria versus inherent Translationese bias.
