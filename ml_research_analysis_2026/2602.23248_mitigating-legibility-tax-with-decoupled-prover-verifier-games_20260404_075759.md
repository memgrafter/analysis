# Mitigating Legibility Tax with Decoupled Prover-Verifier Games
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23248
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23248
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: The decoupled prover-verifier game (DPVG) achieves 56.9% test accuracy on GSM8K-Aug with 99.8% translator faithfulness, nearly matching the solver's 57.0% and far exceeding the baseline PVG's 22.3%.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the legibility tax in prover-verifier games, where optimization for verifier-checkable outputs degrades solution accuracy. It introduces a decoupled framework: first train a solver to maximize correctness independently; then train a translator to convert the solver's solutions into a verifiable proof while preserving the answer. A sneaky translator and verifier feedback are incorporated to ensure the verifier learns to distinguish legible proofs. On GSM8K-Aug, DPVG attains near-solver accuracy (56.9% vs. 57.0%) and high translator faithfulness (99.8%), demonstrating that legibility can be improved without sacrificing correctness.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two phases. Phase 1: Train a solver (Llama-2-7B-chat) using RLOO reinforcement learning with a reward of 1.0 for correct final answers (wrapped in `\boxed{}`) on GSM8K-Aug. Phase 2: Iterative rounds (up to 8) where a verifier (Qwen2-1.5B) is trained on a mixture of outputs from both faithful and sneaky translators. Both translators are then trained with RLOO using a reward that combines the verifier's score and a role-alignment penalty (to encourage the faithful translator to mimic the solver and the sneaky to craft adversarial proofs). Early stopping occurs when the sneaky translator's average verifier score exceeds the faithful translator's on correct solver outputs (using an exponential moving average). The process yields an equilibrium where the faithful translator produces legible outputs that the verifier accepts, while the solver's accuracy is preserved.

## Key Results
- Test accuracy: 56.9% (decoupled system) vs. solver's 57.0% and baseline PVG's 22.3%.
- Translator faithfulness: 99.8% (answers match solver's).
- The decoupled game produces systems that are both faithful and highly checkable, mitigating the legibility tax observed in conventional prover-verifier games.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Decoupling separates the objectives: the solver focuses solely on solving correctly, avoiding any compromise to satisfy the verifier. The translator's role is to render the solver's reasoning legible without altering the answer. The inclusion of a sneaky translator, which deliberately tries to fool the verifier using the ground-truth answer but an adversarial proof, provides a training signal that pushes the verifier to become more rigorous. The verifier's feedback to the faithful translator then guides it to produce proofs that are both legible and convincing, while the sneaky's attempts expose weaknesses. This adversarial dynamic ensures that the verifier does not become complacent and that the faithful translator's outputs meet high legibility standards, all while preserving the solver's correctness due to the initial decoupling.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (plain text):
Solver (Llama-2-7B-chat) -> Translator(s) (faithful & sneaky) -> Verifier (Qwen2-1.5B)
Datasets: D_T (for translator) and D_V (for verifier)
Training loop: (1) Solver trained once on GSM8K-Aug; (2) For each round: verifier trained on current mixture of translator outputs; then both translators trained via RLOO with verifier feedback; (3) Check EMA gap for early stop.

Critical path: The iterative rounds are the core dynamic. Without sufficient verifier capability, the translators may not improve; if the faithful translator diverges from the solver's answer, correctness degrades. The sneaky translator's adversarial role is essential to push the verifier.

Design tradeoffs:
- Model size: Larger solver (7B) vs. smaller verifier (1.5B) to keep verification efficient but maintain solver capability.
- KL penalty (β=0.001) to prevent excessive deviation from the reference model; too high limits adaptation, too high risks instability.
- Temperatures: solver temp=0.7 for diversity, translator temp=1.0 for more exploratory proofs.
- Number of rounds: More rounds allow the verifier to improve but increase compute; early stopping based on EMA gap avoids overfitting.

Failure signatures:
- Faithfulness fails to converge (>99%): inspect reward shaping (Eq. 2) and verifier discriminativity.
- Solver accuracy drops after DPVG: translator likely not preserving answer; check answer extraction and prompt fidelity.
- Verifier score gap between sneaky and helpful never emerges: increase verifier capacity, training epochs, or λ regularization.

First experiments (critical initial validation):
1. Replicate solver training: Train solver from scratch on GSM8K-Aug with RLOO; test accuracy should be ~57% and `\boxed{}` extraction must be robust.
2. Run DPVG for at least 8 rounds: Monitor verifier scores and EMA gap; ensure sneaky overtakes helpful on correct outputs, and translator faithfulness reaches >99%.
3. Ablate the sneaky component: Train only the faithful translator with verifier feedback; verify that faithfulness remains high but verifier score gap does not emerge, proving the adversarial dynamic is necessary.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How does the decoupled prover-verifier game's legibility improvement manifest in human evaluations of solution understandability, beyond automatic metrics like faithfulness? *Basis*: The authors state conducting human evaluation studies as a future direction; currently only qualitative samples and automatic metrics exist.
- **Open Question 2**: To what extent do alternative reward formulations and RL training hyperparameters affect stability, accuracy, and legibility trade-offs? *Basis*: The paper uses a specific reward design and fixed hyperparameters; sensitivity analysis is missing.
- **Open Question 3**: Can incorporating explicit chain-of-thought reasoning before proof construction in the standard (non-decoupled) prover-verifier game mitigate the legibility tax? *Basis*: The authors hypothesize that the absence of a separate CoT stage might contribute to the tax; they did not test this in the coupled setting.
- **Open Question 4**: Does the decoupled framework maintain its advantage across diverse problem domains beyond grade-school math (e.g., formal logic, code generation, open-domain QA)? *Basis*: Experiments are limited to GSM8K-Aug; generalizability to other domains with different proof structures is unknown.
- **Open Question 5**: How sensitive are the results to the specific model architectures and sizes for solver and verifier? *Basis*: The setup fixes Llama-2-7B for solver/translator and Qwen2-1.5B for verifier; scaling and architecture effects are unexplored.

## Limitations
- Prompt formatting and sneaky translator construction details are underspecified, risking implementation drift.
- Evaluation is restricted to a single dataset (GSM8K-Aug); generalizability to other domains is unproven.
- Potential failure modes include non-convergent faithfulness, solver accuracy degradation, or missing verifier score gap due to hyperparameter choices.
