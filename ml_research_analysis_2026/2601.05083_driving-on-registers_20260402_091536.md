# Driving on Registers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.05083
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05083
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Result**: State-of-the-art end-to‑end driving with camera‑aware registers, achieving near‑human performance (NAVSIM PDMS 94.6) and a 3× throughput gain with ~40 M parameters.

## Executive Summary
DrivoR addresses the computational inefficiency of end‑to‑end autonomous driving planners that rely on Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones, which produce excessive token counts. The method introduces camera‑aware register tokens that compress each camera’s visual features into a small, fixed‑size set (e.g., 16 tokens), preserving planning‑relevant context while drastically reducing the sequence length fed to downstream modules. Two lightweight transformer decoders then generate and score trajectory proposals; the scoring branch is disentangled into interpretable sub‑scores (safety, comfort, efficiency) enabling behavior‑conditioned driving at inference. Without additional supervision or BEV layers, DrivoR achieves state‑of‑the‑art results on NAVSIM and HUGSIM benchmarks, nearing human performance, while tripling throughput and using only about 40 M parameters.

## Method Summary
DrivoR processes multi‑camera images through a ViT backbone. Instead of using all spatial tokens, it employs a set of learnable register tokens that are modulated by each camera’s features, effectively compressing the per‑camera representation to a small number of tokens. These compressed tokens from all cameras are concatenated into a short sequence and fed to a trajectory proposal decoder, which outputs a set of candidate future trajectories. A separate scoring decoder attends to the context and each proposal to predict three sub‑scores (safety, comfort, efficiency). At inference, these sub‑scores can be weighted to condition the driving behavior (e.g., more cautious or more efficient). The entire pipeline is trained with standard end‑to‑end objectives without extra supervision or bird’s‑eye‑view (BEV) conversion.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy**: NAVSIM‑v1 PDMS 94.6 (human 94.8); NAVSIM‑v2 EPDMS 54.6 (best); HUGSIM RC 49.8 and HD‑Score 35.7 (best reported).
- **Efficiency**: 3× throughput improvement (110 ms vs 400 ms) with ~40 M parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The camera‑aware register tokens act as a compressed summary of each camera’s view, retaining only the information most relevant for planning while eliminating redundant spatial tokens. This dramatically shrinks the sequence length that the heavy transformer decoders must process, reducing both memory and computation without sacrificing accuracy. The disentangled scoring branch separately evaluates safety, comfort, and efficiency, which not only makes the decision process interpretable but also allows dynamic weighting at inference to adapt the driving style to the situation or user preference. By avoiding explicit BEV transformation and additional supervision, the model remains simple yet effective, achieving high performance with a small parameter budget.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Multi-camera images -> ViT backbone -> Camera-aware register compression -> Concatenated register sequence -> Trajectory Proposal Decoder -> Candidate trajectories -> Scoring Decoder -> Behavior-weighted sub-scores -> Final trajectory  

**Critical path**: The data flows from input images through the ViT and register modules to produce a compact context representation. The proposal decoder generates a set of trajectories, and the scoring decoder evaluates each trajectory using the context and its internal representation, outputting sub-scores that are combined to select the final trajectory.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Register token count: more tokens preserve detail but increase sequence length; fewer tokens improve speed but risk losing relevant cues.  
- Number of trajectory proposals: more candidates improve coverage but raise decoder cost.  
- Disentangled vs. joint scoring: disentanglement offers interpretability and conditioning at possible cost of accuracy; joint scoring may be more accurate but less transparent.  
- Decoder capacity: deeper/wider decoders can model complex distributions but increase parameters and latency.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Collisions indicate failures in safety modeling or proposal quality.  
- Jerky or uncomfortable motion suggests poor comfort sub‑score prediction or insufficient smoothness constraints.  
- Overly cautious or inefficient trajectories point to mis‑weighted sub‑scores or dataset bias.  
- Performance drops under novel conditions (e.g., night, rain) may reveal overfitting to the training simulation domain.

**First experiments**:  
1. Ablate the number of register tokens per camera (e.g., 4, 8, 16, 32) and measure PDMS/EPDMS and latency to identify the optimal trade‑off.  
2. Compare DrivoR against a full‑token ViT baseline with matched token count (by truncation) to isolate the impact of the camera‑aware register mechanism.  
3. Evaluate behavior conditioning by varying sub‑score weights (e.g., safety weight 0.5, 0.9, 1.0) and analyzing resulting driving styles in simulation (e.g., gap keeping, acceleration smoothness).

## Limitations
- Training data composition and domain coverage are unspecified; results may not generalize beyond simulation settings.
- No ablations isolating the effect of camera‑aware register tokens; reported gains could be confounded with other choices.
- The interpretability of the disentangled sub‑scores is not validated against human experts or ground‑truth metrics.

## Confidence
- Token compression preserves planning‑relevant context: Medium  
- Disentangled scoring enables behavior‑conditioned driving: Medium  
- State‑of‑the‑art results on NAVSIM/HUGSIM: Low  
- 3× throughput gain and ~40M parameters: Medium  

## Next Checks
1. Perform systematic ablations: vary the number of register tokens per camera, compare against a full‑token Vision Transformer backbone, and measure trade‑offs in accuracy (PDMS/EPDMS) and latency.
2. Test on external, real‑world driving datasets (e.g., nuScenes, Argoverse) to assess generalization beyond the reported simulation environments.
3. Validate sub‑score interpretability: correlate predicted safety, comfort, and efficiency scores with established proxies (e.g., collision rates, jerk, traffic rule violations) and with human expert ratings on a held‑out set.
