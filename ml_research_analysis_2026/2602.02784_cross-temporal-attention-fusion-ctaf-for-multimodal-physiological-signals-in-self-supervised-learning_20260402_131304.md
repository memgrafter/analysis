# Cross-Temporal Attention Fusion (CTAF) for Multimodal Physiological Signals in Self-Supervised Learning

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.02784
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02784
*   **Reference Count:** 36
*   **Primary Result:** CTAF achieves supervised-level three-bin arousal/valence accuracy (0.62 vs. 0.58) and macro-F1 (0.61 vs. 0.57) on K-EmoCon while using fewer labels, by learning soft cross-modal token alignments without explicit warping.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal affect recognition using EEG and peripheral physiology (EDA/BVP/ECG) is challenged by temporal asynchrony between central and autonomic signals. Standard fusion methods often ignore this or rely on costly, brittle warping. This paper introduces Cross-Temporal Attention Fusion (CTAF), a self-supervised module that learns soft, bidirectional token-level alignments between modalities via time-aware cross-attention. CTAF combines these alignments with contrastive objectives, a lightweight fusion gate, and VICReg-style regularization, while being mask-aware for missing data. Evaluated on the K-EmoCon dataset using leave-one-out cross-validation, CTAF demonstrates strong cross-modal alignment (cosine margin +0.189 over time-agnostic variants) and competitive recognition performance against a supervised state-of-the-art, all with reduced label dependency.

## Method Summary
CTAF is a self-supervised fusion framework for asynchronous multimodal time series. It employs two lightweight Conv-Transformer encoders that ingest tokenized features augmented with sinusoidal time embeddings ϕ(t). The core mechanism is bidirectional cross-attention: each modality's tokens attend to the other's, producing attention matrices that serve as soft, time-aware alignment maps (Ae→p, Ap→e). These alignments guide a multi-component loss: a cross-temporal alignment loss (Lalign) with a Gaussian prior over time lags, standard clip-level contrastive loss (Lcon), within-modality view/loss consistency (Lview/Lcons), and a VICReg-inspired regularization suite (Linv, Lvar, Lcov) on projection heads to prevent collapse. Final clip embeddings are formed by fusing two summaries—a gated combination of modality-specific pooled representations and a pooled representation over the cross-attended token union—via a learned fusion gate. All operations are mask-aware, handling missing data naturally and avoiding explicit time warping.

## Key Results
*   **Cross-modal alignment:** Matched clip cosine similarity (0.240) far exceeds mismatched (0.004), yielding a cosine margin improvement of +0.189 over time-agnostic baselines.
*   **Token retrieval:** Within τ=1s, retrieval rates are substantial (EEG→physiology: 0.350; physiology→EEG: 0.265), significantly (+0.138 and +0.057 absolute) outperforming no-time baselines.
*   **Recognition:** On three-bin arousal/valence classification under LOOCV, CTAF achieves accuracy (0.62) and macro-F1 (0.61) competitive with supervised HyperFuseNet (0.58, 0.57) while using fewer labels.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** CTAF learns soft, bidirectional token-level correspondences between EEG and physiology streams despite unknown lags via time-aware cross-attention.
*   **Mechanism:** Encoders produce time-augmented token embeddings H(e) and H(p). Bidirectional cross-attention (MHA) is applied where queries from one modality attend to keys/values of the other. The resulting attention weights form matrices (Ae→p, Ap→e) representing soft alignment probabilities across time. Keys are masked based on validity masks to ignore missing data. The time features ϕ(t) injected into token embeddings allow the attention mechanism to be explicitly sensitive to temporal offsets.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states "learns soft bidirectional token-level alignments... via time-aware cross-attention." Section 3.2 defines the attention operation and masking. Section 3.3's Lalign loss directly supervises these attention-derived similarity scores with a Gaussian time-delay prior. Results in Table 2 show that removing time features (w/o Time) drastically harms token retrieval, confirming the mechanism's reliance on temporal context.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** The learned soft alignments define positive pairs for contrastive objectives, guiding representation learning to be invariant to realistic temporal misalignment.
*   **Mechanism:** Instead of assuming synchronous clip-level correspondence, CTAF uses the alignment matrices to weight or aggregate cross-modal positives. Specifically, Lalign minimizes cross-entropy between the attention-derived token similarity scores and a Gaussian distribution centered at zero lag. This trains the model to concentrate attention on tokens that are likely temporally corresponding. The resulting, alignment-informed relationship then informs the clip-level contrastive loss (Lcon) and view consistency losses, effectively telling the model which parts of the sequences should "match" in the joint embedding space.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract mentions "combines these alignments with contrastive objectives." Section 3.3 details Lalign as a cross-entropy on the soft alignment. Table 2 shows that including Lalign (via the full CTAF model) leads to a large absolute gain (+0.189) in cosine margin over time-agnostic variants, indicating better separation of matched/mismatched clips. Token retrieval rates also significantly improve when alignment is active.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** A fusion gate, masked pooling, and VICReg-style regularization create a final clip embedding robust to missing data, noise, and representation collapse.
*   **Mechanism:** After cross-attention, two modality-specific summary embeddings (z(e), z(p)) are obtained via masked mean pooling. A lightweight fusion gate (g, a sigmoid-outputting MLP on the concatenated summaries) dynamically weights these summaries into z_gate. Separately, an attention pooling over the union of cross-attended tokens produces z_tok. The final embedding zf averages z_gate and z_tok. VICReg penalties (invariance, variance, covariance) are applied to the *pre-normalized* projection heads (p(e), p(p)) to prevent the common collapse failure mode in contrastive learning. All pooling and attention steps incorporate validity masks, making the process inherently robust to missing tokens.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract cites "lightweight fusion gate... and regularization (VICReg-style)... mask-aware." Section 3.2 details the masked pooling, fusion gate equation, and final averaging. Section 3.3 defines the VICReg losses. The model's competitive performance against HyperFuseNet (which lacks this specific combination) while using fewer labels suggests the self-supervised, regularized design is effective, though the isolated contribution of the gate versus the alignment is not disentangled in the results.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Self-Supervised Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE)**
    *   **Why needed:** To learn a shared representation space for two different modalities from unpaired or weakly paired temporal streams without explicit labels. It pulls embeddings of "positive" pairs together and pushes "negative" pairs apart.
    *   **Quick Check:** In a batch of EEG and physiology clip embeddings, how does the InfoNCE loss enforce that an EEG clip's embedding is close to its *corresponding* physiology clip's embedding while being distinct from all *other* physiology clips in the batch?
*   **Concept: Cross-Attention in Transformers**
    *   **Why needed:** To compute the soft alignment matrices (Ae→p, Ap→e). Queries from one modality attend to keys from the other, with attention weights indicating the likelihood of correspondence across time. Injecting sinusoidal time features ϕ(t) makes this attention explicitly time-aware.
    *   **Quick Check:** In the equation ˜H(e) = MHA(Q=H(e), K=H(p), V=H(p)), what role does the key H(p) play, and why is the key-padding mask based on m(p)?
*   **Concept: Regularization for Representation Collapse Prevention (VICReg)**
    *   **Why needed:** Contrastive objectives alone can lead to a "collapse" where all outputs become a constant vector. VICReg's invariance (Linv), variance (Lvar), and covariance (Lcov) penalties on the projection space ensure embeddings have non-trivial variance and are not over-correlated across dimensions.
    *   **Quick Check:** What does the off-diagonal covariance penalty (Lcov) specifically discourage, and why is that important for maintaining a rich representation space?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Input (X(e), t(e), m(e); X(p), t(p), m(p)) -> Modality Encoders (with ϕ(t) injection) -> H(e), H(p) -> Bidirectional Cross-Attention (MHA) -> ˜H(e), ˜H(p) and Alignments A(e→p), A(p→e) -> Masked Mean Pooling -> z(e), z(p) -> Fusion Gate (g) -> z_gate; Union Token Set T -> Attention Pooling -> z_tok -> Final Embedding zf = ½(z_gate + z_tok) -> Projection Heads (Proj_e, Proj_p, Proj_f) -> p(e), p(p), p(f) -> Losses (Lcon, Lalign, Lview, Lcons, VICReg, Lsup).

**Critical path:** The core data flow is Input -> Encoders -> Cross-Attention -> Dual pooling (summary & token) -> Gated fusion -> zf. The alignment matrices A are intermediate byproducts used solely in the Lalign loss and are not passed forward in the embedding pipeline.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Time-aware vs. Efficiency:** Adding ϕ(t) and bidirectional cross-attention (O(Se*Sp)) increases memory/compute vs. synchronous fusion. Tradeoff: better alignment for asynchronous data vs. higher cost.
*   **Fusion simplicity vs. expressivity:** A single learned gate (g) is parameter-efficient but may be less expressive than a complex fusion network (e.g., HyperFuseNet). Tradeoff: efficiency/less overfitting vs. maximum capacity.
*   **Alignment guidance vs. collapse risk:** Using Lalign strengthens the SSL signal but requires careful weighting (βt schedule) relative to Lcon and VICReg terms to avoid optimization conflicts.

**Failure signatures:**
*   **Poor token retrieval (low retr@τ):** Alignment matrices A are diffuse (near-uniform). Indicates encoders failed to create discriminative time-aware representations or Lalign weight was too low.
*   **Clip-level cosine margin ~0:** Matched/mismatched cosine distributions overlap. Suggests Lcon/Lalign ineffective; could be due to aggressive modality dropout or poor temperature setting.
*   **Fusion gate output dominated by one modality (|g|≈1):** Could indicate one modality is noisier or SSL objectives predominantly learn from one stream, making the other redundant.
*   **High variance in LOOCV performance across subjects:** Model lacks subject generalization. Could be due to insufficient within-modality view/consistency regularization (Lview/Lcons).

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Mask Reconstruction / View Consistency Pre-test:** Train only the modality encoders with a masked token prediction or temporal jitter consistency loss on each modality *separately*. Verify each encoder learns meaningful temporal features by evaluating reconstruction quality or embedding stability under jitter.
2.  **Ablation on Time Encoding (ϕ(t)):** Train full CTAF with and without adding sinusoidal time features to encoder inputs. Evaluate token retrieval (retr@τ=1s) and clip cosine margin. Hypothesis: WITH time should show significant gains; if not, time encoding is broken or under-weighted.
3.  **Alignment Matrix Inspection:** For held-out validation windows, visualize A(e→p) and A(p→e) (post-softmax). Check for diagonal dominance or expected lag patterns (e.g., physiology lagging EEG). Compute attention-weighted time delta distributions; a very broad spread may indicate the Gaussian prior σ in Lalign is too large or lags are genuinely heterogeneous.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Generalizability:** Does CTAF generalize to other multimodal affect datasets beyond K-EmoCon? (Basis: Conclusion states future validation on broader benchmarks. Why: Only evaluated on K-EmoCon.)
*   **Computational Cost:** How can the computational cost of cross-temporal attention be reduced without degrading performance? (Basis: Identifies addressing attention cost as future work. Why: Quadratic scaling limits deployment.)
*   **Temporal Tolerance τ:** Can the token retrieval tolerance τ be selected in a data-driven way rather than fixed at 1s? (Basis: Suggests "principled τ selection" as an extension. Why: τ likely varies with dataset/subject.)
*   **Alternative Time Encodings:** Do alternative time encoding methods (e.g., learned, rotary) improve alignment over sinusoidal features? (Basis: Proposes exploring alternative encodings. Why: Sinusoidal are fixed; other methods may capture dynamics better.)
*   **Adaptive Gaussian Prior:** Can the Gaussian prior bandwidth σ in Lalign be adapted (e.g., per-subject) instead of fixed? (Basis: Suggests "sharper priors" to improve identifiability. Why: Lags may vary across subjects/states.)

## Limitations
*   **Hyperparameter ambiguity:** Exact training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, temperature, loss weights, Gaussian σ) and encoder details (layers, dimensions) are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction and sensitivity analysis.
*   **Ablation gaps:** No experimental ablations isolate the fusion gate's contribution from the cross-temporal alignment mechanism, leaving their relative importance unclear.
*   **Alignment verification:** Alignment matrices are not visualized; only indirect metrics (cosine margin, retrieval) are reported, leaving the nature of learned correspondences (e.g., lag distributions) unverified.

## Confidence
*   **Cross-temporal alignment via time-aware attention:** Medium. Supported by improved token retrieval/cosine margin vs. no-time baselines, but lacks direct alignment matrix inspection and full hyperparameter robustness.
*   **Alignment-guided contrastive learning:** Medium. Lalign loss is defined and results show gains, but without ablating Lalign its necessity/sufficiency for performance is not confirmed.
*   **Fusion gate & VICReg regularization:** Low. No comparison to simpler fusion (e.g., averaging) or controlled VICReg ablations; potential pathological gating is unexplored.

## Next Checks

2.  **Component ablation study:** Train CTAF variants with (a) time features ϕ(t) removed, (b) Lalign omitted, (c) fusion gate replaced by fixed average, and (d) VICReg terms removed. Evaluate token retrieval@τ=1s and three-bin classification accuracy to quantify each component's impact.
