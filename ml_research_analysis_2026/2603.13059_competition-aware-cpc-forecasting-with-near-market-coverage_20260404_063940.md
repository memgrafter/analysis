# Competition-Aware CPC Forecasting with Near-Market Coverage

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13059
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13059
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Competition-aware proxies (semantic, behavioral, geographic) measurably improve multi-horizon CPC forecasts, with gains concentrated on high-volatility "competitive frontier" keywords.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles volatile cost-per-click (CPC) forecasting in paid search auctions where advertisers cannot observe rival bids or auction states. It proposes approximating latent competition through three scalable proxies: semantic neighborhoods from keyword text embeddings, behavioral alignment via dynamic time warping (DTW) on historical CPC trajectories, and geographic-intent covariates. Evaluating across classical baselines, time-series foundation models (TSFMs), and spatiotemporal graph neural networks (STGNNs), competition-aware augmentation consistently improves medium- and long-horizon forecasts (6–12 weeks). The strongest gains appear on high-CPC, high-volatility keywords where budget risk is greatest, demonstrating that indirect competition signals can enhance forecast robustness under partial observability.

## Method Summary
The study builds a weekly panel of 1,811 car-rental keywords over 127 weeks from Google Ads logs. CPC is defined as cost per click. Three competition proxies are extracted: (1) a fixed semantic graph from `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` embeddings (top-10 cosine neighbors), (2) behavioral neighborhoods identified by DTW alignment of CPC histories, and (3) hierarchical geographic indicators parsed from keyword text. These proxies are routed in two ways: as exogenous covariates for TSFMs (Chronos-2, TimeGPT, Moirai) and as a fixed adjacency matrix for STGNNs (DCRNN, GConvLSTM, GraphWaveNet). Models are evaluated chronologically (last 20% hold-out) at 1, 6, and 12-week horizons using sMAPE and RMSE. The best 6-week result (27.14% sMAPE) comes from Chronos-2 with geographic covariates; DCRNN with a semantic graph excels at 1 week (25.82% sMAPE).

## Key Results
- Competition-aware augmentation reduces sMAPE vs. univariate baselines, with improvements most pronounced at 6 and 12 weeks.
- Chronos-2 with continent-level geographic covariates achieves the lowest 6-week error (27.14% sMAPE).
- DCRNN using a semantic graph yields the best 1-week forecast (25.82% sMAPE), highlighting the strength of relational modeling for short-horizon spillovers.
- Gains concentrate on the "competitive frontier" (high mean CPC, high volatility) where latent competition matters most.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Semantic neighborhoods
- **Claim:** Keywords with similar text embeddings likely compete for overlapping user intent and auction space.
- **Mechanism:** Pretrained transformer embeddings create a fixed similarity graph (cosine-based k-NN). STGNNs propagate information along these edges, allowing a keyword’s forecast to borrow strength from semantically related series.
- **Core assumption:** Lexical-semantic proximity correlates with auction overlap (e.g., “car rental” vs. “automobile hire”).
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 4.1] “encode each keyword... using pretrained transformer-based text representations... Pairwise cosine similarity is then used to identify semantically related keywords.”
- **Break condition:** If the language model’s pretraining does not capture commercial auction semantics (e.g., conflates informational and transactional intent), the graph may connect non-competing keywords, adding noise.

### Mechanism 2: Behavioral alignment via DTW
- **Claim:** Synchronized CPC trajectories indicate shared exposure to unobserved competitive or market-wide shocks, even without textual similarity.
- **Mechanism:** DTW with a Sakoe–Chiba band measures CPC trajectory similarity; neighbors’ aggregated historical CPC serve as leakage-free covariates.
- **Core assumption:** Aligned CPC movements reflect common latent demand or competitive pressure (e.g., holiday spikes affecting multiple rental keywords).
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 4.1] “Keywords with weak lexical overlap may still experience similar CPC dynamics because they are exposed to common demand shocks... construct behavioral neighborhoods using DTW.”
- **Break condition:** Spurious alignment due to coincidental noise or broad economic trends could incorporate irrelevant signals, especially in volatile series.

### Mechanism 3: Geographic-intent covariates
- **Claim:** Geographic tokens in keyword text proxy localized auction pressure and competitor sets.
- **Mechanism:** Hierarchical location indicators (continent, country, city) are added as covariates or node features, encoding market heterogeneity.
- **Core assumption:** Auction dynamics are spatially segmented (e.g., “car rental London” vs. “car rental Paris” face distinct local competition).
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 5.4 & Table 4] “Coarse geography is the most robust competition prior: continent-level encoding improves stability... outperforms country/city dummies.”
- **Break condition:** Overly granular encoding (city-level) fragments training data; overly coarse (continent-level) may wash out meaningful variation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Partial observability in auction markets**  
  Why needed: CPC alone is a noisy, indirect signal of latent competition; understanding this constraint explains why proxy modeling is necessary.  
  Quick check: Why does a univariate CPC series provide insufficient information for multi-horizon forecasting in a GSP auction?
  
- **Concept: Time-series foundation models (TSFMs)**  
  Why needed: TSFMs (Chronos-2, TimeGPT, Moirai) are key baselines that benefit from covariate augmentation; their pretraining affects how they ingest competition proxies.  
  Quick check: How might a pretrained universal forecaster like Chronos-2 differ in handling exogenous covariates compared to a model trained from scratch on CPC data?
  
- **Concept: Spatiotemporal graph neural networks (STGNNs)**  
  Why needed: STGNNs (DCRNN, GConvLSTM, GraphWaveNet) embody the relational route, propagating information over the fixed semantic graph to model competition spillovers.  
  Quick check: What is the purpose of the fixed semantic adjacency matrix, and how does it differ from learning the graph structure adaptively?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Data Pipeline -> Proxy Extractors -> Representation Routes -> Model Zoo -> Evaluation Harness  
  (1) Batch preprocessing of 1.66B logs → keyword-week panel; (2) Semantic embedder, DTW calculator, geographic parser; (3) Covariate engine for TSFMs, fixed graph constructor for STGNNs; (4) Baselines, TSFMs, STGNNs; (5) Chronological split, multi-horizon metrics, competitive frontier segmentation.
- **Critical path:** Raw logs → keyword-week aggregation → proxy extraction → route selection (covariate vs. graph) → model training → horizon-specific evaluation. Semantic graph construction is an upstream dependency for all STGNNs.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Covariate vs. relational: Covariates are simpler and pair well with TSFMs; relational graphs add inductive bias for spillovers but risk over-smoothing if noisy.  
  - Geographic resolution: Coarse (continent) > fine (city) for robustness; fine splits increase sparsity.  
  - Proxy selection: Indiscriminate stacking harms performance (e.g., all proxies at 12 weeks worse than selective); selection must be horizon- and model-aware.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Short-term (1w): STGNNs with noisy or absent graph underperform TSFMs with geo covariates.  
  - Medium-term (6w): Overly complex proxy sets degrade accuracy; model fails to stabilize on frontier keywords.  
  - Long-term (12w): Fine-grained geographic features cause overfitting; static semantic graph cannot capture evolving competition.  
  - General: High sMAPE variance across keywords indicates poor handling of heavy-tailed CPC; failures concentrated in competitive frontier quadrant.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Establish baseline: Train SARIMAX and XGBoost; report ~36–40% sMAPE at 6w.  
  2. Covariate route: Augment Chronos-2 with geographic intent (continent dummies); measure sMAPE drop vs. baseline at 6w.  
  3. Graph route: Train DCRNN with semantic graph + semantic-neighbor CPC covariates; compare against DCRNN without graph and Chronos-2 with best covariates at 1w and 6w.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does the competition proxy framework maintain forecasting accuracy in less concentrated or broader paid-search verticals?
- Can dynamic graph construction (time-varying semantic or behavioral adjacency) improve long-horizon forecasts by capturing evolving competitive relationships?
- How much incremental value do direct competitor-side signals (e.g., partial bid observations, budget constraints) add beyond observable proxies?
- Does the competition-aware design generalize to non–car-rental auction markets (e.g., retail media, real-time bidding)?

## Limitations
- Single-industry evaluation (car rental) limits generalizability.
- Static semantic graph cannot capture evolving competitive relationships.
- DTW parameter sensitivity and exact hyperparameters for STGNNs/TSFMs are not fully specified.
- Risk of data leakage in proxy construction if neighbor aggregates use future information.

## Confidence
- **Semantic neighborhoods:** Medium (plausible but relies on LM semantics matching auction overlap).
- **Behavioral alignment (DTW):** Medium (innovative but susceptible to spurious alignment).
- **Geographic-intent covariates:** High (robust gains across models and horizons, Table 4).
- **Overall competition-aware augmentation:** Medium (proxy selection horizon-dependent; gains concentrated on frontier keywords).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation:** Remove each proxy (semantic, DTW, geo) from TSFM and STGNN pipelines; measure sMAPE changes at 1, 6, 12 weeks.
2. **DTW sensitivity:** Vary Sakoe-Chiba band width and neighbor count k; also test correlation-based neighbor graphs for robustness.
3. **Cross-vertical test:** Replicate on a second ad vertical (e.g., hotel bookings); verify transferability and whether geographic-intent remains strongest prior.
