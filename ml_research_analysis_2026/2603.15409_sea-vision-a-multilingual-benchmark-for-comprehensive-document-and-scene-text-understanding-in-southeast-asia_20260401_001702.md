# SEA-Vision: A Multilingual Benchmark for Comprehensive Document and Scene Text Understanding in Southeast Asia

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15409  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15409  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: SEA-Vision exposes severe performance gaps in MLLMs for low-resource Southeast Asian languages, with parsing errors 3–5× higher and QA accuracy 5–7× lower than for English.

## Executive Summary
SEA-Vision introduces the first unified benchmark for document and scene text understanding across 11 Southeast Asian languages, addressing a critical gap in evaluation resources for linguistically diverse, low-resource regions. The benchmark combines 15,234 annotated pages for Document Parsing and 7,496 QA pairs for Text-Centric Visual Question Answering, constructed via a hybrid pipeline that blends automation with native-speaker verification. Evaluations of state-of-the-art MLLMs reveal dramatic performance disparities: low-resource scripts like Lao and Khmer suffer near-chance accuracy on scene-text QA (8–13%) versus >60% for English, while parsing error rates are 3–5× higher. The results underscore the significant challenge of extending multimodal understanding beyond high-resource languages and homogeneous document types.

## Method Summary
SEA-VISION employs a two-pronged data construction strategy. For Document Parsing, a four-stage pipeline ingests web documents, applies rule-based filtering and metadata annotation, scores pages by representativeness, and refines regions (OCR, tables, formulas) before native verification. For Text-Centric VQA, images are collected and filtered, layout and text are extracted, text is re-rendered into target-language images to preserve visual context, MLLMs generate and judge QA pairs, and native speakers verify both questions and answers. The re-rendering step avoids translation-induced misalignment by synthetically placing target-language text onto original image layouts.

## Key Results
- Document Parsing: Low-resource scripts exhibit 3–5× higher Normalized Edit Distance (e.g., NED ~0.98 on Lao vs. ~0.11 on English for some models).
- TEC-VQA: Accuracy plummets to 8.17% (Burmese) and 13.34% (Lao) compared to 62.85% (English); overall average only 32.36%.
- Models show complementary weaknesses: expert parsers struggle with complex layouts; generalist MLLMs fail at fine-grained extraction—no single approach dominates across document types and languages.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Scalable, high-quality annotation via hybrid pipeline
The hybrid pipeline (automated filtering → MLLM-assisted labeling → native verification) balances cost and fidelity. Automated rules select representative pages; for VQA, text re-rendering into target-language images prevents translation drift. Native verification corrects residual errors, especially critical for low-resource scripts where MLLM hallucinations are frequent. If verification is skipped, visual-semantic alignment degrades noticeably.

### Mechanism 2: Unified evaluation exposes complementary weaknesses
By jointly evaluating Document Parsing (extraction) and TEC-VQA (reasoning) under identical language conditions, the benchmark reveals whether strength in one task generalizes to the other. The dichotomy—expert parsers excel on structured documents but fail on complex scenes, while generalist MLLMs show the opposite pattern—would be obscured in single-task evaluations.

### Mechanism 3: Southeast Asia as a stress test for script diversity
The 11 languages cover Latin, Brahmic, Arabic, and logographic scripts with varied orthography and layout conventions. Performance gaps are not solely due to data volume but reflect script-specific inductive biases missing from pre-training (e.g., consonant clusters, lack of word spaces). If gaps persist even after synthetic data augmentation, architectural limitations (e.g., tokenizer inefficiency) may be the root cause.

## Foundational Learning

- **Document Parsing vs. Text-Centric VQA**: Why needed—different metrics (NED/TEDS vs. accuracy); unified benchmark tests whether extraction ability correlates with reasoning across languages. Quick check: Why might a model achieve moderate TEC-VQA accuracy despite very high parsing NED? What compensatory strategies does this imply?
- **Low-resource language challenges in OCR/layout analysis**: Why needed—central to interpreting dramatic performance drops on Lao, Khmer, Burmese. Quick check: Beyond data volume, which Brahmic script properties (e.g., consonant clusters, no word spaces) cause higher Normalized Edit Distance?

## Architecture Onboarding
Data ingestion -> Filtering -> Metadata annotation -> Rule-based scoring/selection -> Region correction -> Human verification. Parallel TEC-VQA pipeline: Image collection -> filtering -> layout/OCR -> text re-rendering -> MLLM QA generation -> back-translation validation -> Human QA verification. The critical path is human verification throughput; the hybrid design minimizes human time by front-loading automation. Design tradeoffs: automation vs. quality (rule-based scoring may exclude atypical layouts), script coverage vs. annotation cost (some page types are sparse), and re-rendering fidelity (font-matched inpainting may fail on complex backgrounds). Failure signatures: Parsing NED >0.8 indicates complete breakdown; TEDS near 0 on tables suggests failed cell relationship capture. TEC-VQA accuracy <10% on high-resource languages implies prompt issues; very low accuracy on low-resource languages may indicate rendering artifacts or unchecked hallucinations. First 3 experiments: 1. Baseline replication with MinerU2.5 and Qwen3-VL-32B to verify reported metrics. 2. Per-language error analysis on Khmer: categorize 10 parsing and 10 QA failures (OCR, layout, table, reasoning). 3. Re-rendering ablation: compare model accuracy on original vs. re-rendered images for 20 TEC-VQA instances; >15% drop signals artifact introduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question**: How can we unify expert models (accurate on structured documents) and general models (robust on complex layouts) to achieve consistently high performance across all SEA-Vision document types?  
  *Basis*: Explicitly stated in Section 6.2.1; current dichotomy leaves a performance gap on mixed-layout documents.  
  *Why unresolved*: No effective hybrid architectures exist; evidence needed: modular expert systems guided by generalist reasoning evaluated on SEA-Vision's diversity.
- **Question**: What systematic data acquisition strategies improve coverage of underrepresented SEA languages (e.g., Lao, Khmer) and specialized formats (e.g., newspapers) to reduce benchmark bias?  
  *Basis*: Inferred from uneven coverage noted in Section E.1 and sparse counts in tables.  
  *Why unresolved*: Current web-scraping fails to capture niche regional sources; evidence needed: collaborations with archives, script-specific crawlers, and quantified coverage improvements.
- **Question**: What are the primary factors driving severe performance degradation on low-resource SEA languages?  
  *Basis*: Inferred from 3–5× higher NED and 5–7× lower accuracy in Section 6.2.  
  *Why unresolved*: No root-cause analysis (script complexity vs. data scarcity vs. architecture); evidence needed: controlled experiments varying training data, script normalization, and model components.

## Limitations
- Native-speaker verification coverage is unspecified; could allow undetected systematic errors, especially in low-resource scripts.
- Re-rendered TEC-VQA images may introduce visual artifacts for complex backgrounds or handwritten text, confounding model performance with dataset noise.
- Generalizability limited: evaluations cover only a narrow set of MLLMs; architectural differences (e.g., tokenizers) may alter findings.

## Confidence
- **Performance gap claims**: High – Direct metrics clearly reported and align with known low-resource challenges.
- **Pipeline quality claims**: Medium – Hybrid design plausible, but no quantified verification agreement or per-stage error rates.
- **Generalizability to all MLLMs**: Low – Evaluated models are a narrow slice; fine-tuned variants may behave differently.

## Next Checks
1. **Audit verification sampling**: Request verification logs; if per-language review coverage is <20%, resample 100 instances for full manual re-evaluation.
2. **Re-rendering fidelity test**: For 50 TEC-VQA images per low-resource language, measure model accuracy on original vs. re-rendered versions; >15% drop indicates artifact issues.
3. **Tokenizer sensitivity analysis**: Test a single model (e.g., Qwen-VL) with script-specific tokenizers on Burmese/Khmer parsing; significant improvement points to tokenization as a key bottleneck.
