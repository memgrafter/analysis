# Can RL Improve Generalization of LLM Agents? An Empirical Study

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12011  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12011  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) improves intra-environment generalization significantly but yields limited and variable cross-environment transfer, highly sensitive to observation/action interfaces and semantic priors.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines whether reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) enhances the generalization of large language model (LLM) agents to unseen environments and tasks. Using Qwen2.5 models (3B and 7B), the authors conduct experiments across five diverse agent environments—WebShop, SearchQA, TextCraft, AlfWorld, and BabyAI—evaluating generalization along three axes: intra‑environment (across task difficulty), cross‑environment transfer, and sequential multi‑environment training. Results show that RFT induces strong intra‑environment generalization: agents trained on hard tasks achieve substantial gains on easy test tasks (e.g., +60.1 points on WebShop for the 7B model), and curriculum learning (easy-to-hard) further boosts performance. In contrast, cross‑environment generalization is weaker and inconsistent, with held‑out improvements averaging only +3.32 points; positive transfer occurs in some pairs (e.g., SearchQA → WebShop) while negative transfer appears in others (e.g., BabyAI → WebShop). Sequential training across environments yields downstream gains with minimal upstream forgetting, and mixture training balances performance across environments. Failure mode analysis highlights common issues like confirmation bias and state inconsistency, which worsen under distribution shift.

## Method Summary
The study employs reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) via Group‑Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on Qwen2.5‑Instruct models (3B/7B). Each environment provides textual observations and accepts textual actions; reward structures are sparse terminal rewards except for WebShop and BabyAI (dense). For intra‑environment generalization, tasks within each environment are split into easy and hard subsets. Cross‑environment transfer is assessed by training on a source environment and evaluating on a target environment, both held‑in (same environment) and held‑out (different environment). Sequential training involves fine‑tuning on a second environment after initial training on a first, measuring retention and transfer. Performance is primarily measured by average success rate over 8 rollouts (`avg@8`) with a maximum of 20 interaction turns. All experiments use default hyperparameters without exhaustive tuning.

## Key Results
- **Strong intra‑environment generalization:** RFT yields large improvements when training on hard tasks and testing on easy tasks (e.g., +60.1 points on WebShop for 7B). Curriculum learning (easy→hard) further enhances performance.
- **Weak and variable cross‑environment transfer:** Held‑in improvements are substantial (e.g., +78.62 points in AlfWorld for 3B), but held‑out gains average only +3.32 points. Transfer direction varies: positive (e.g., SearchQA → WebShop) and negative (e.g., BabyAI → WebShop).
- **Stable sequential training:** Downstream environment performance increases with minimal upstream forgetting; mixture training improves overall balance across environments.
- **Failure modes:** Confirmation bias and state inconsistency are prevalent, especially in held‑out settings (e.g., state inconsistency in WebShop held‑out rises from 4.3% to 21.9%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Efficient Exploration Within a Fixed Environment
**Claim:** RFT induces more efficient exploration within a fixed environment, enabling generalization across task difficulty levels.  
**Mechanism:** The policy gradient objective (GRPO) maximizes cumulative reward over trajectories, implicitly teaching the agent to reduce unnecessary steps and focus on goal‑relevant actions, thereby shortening trajectory lengths.  
**Core assumption:** Task difficulty differences primarily affect exploration depth (number of steps), while underlying environment dynamics (state transitions, observation patterns) remain constant.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: “RFT demonstrates strong intra‑environment generalization … Agents trained on hard tasks achieve significant gains on easy test tasks.”  
- Section 4: “RFT not only improves success rates but also encourages more concise and goal‑directed exploration … average interaction turns decrease from 10.76 to 4.19.”  
- Corpus: Neighboring work *Meta‑RL Induces Exploration in Language Agents* supports RL‑driven exploration, though direct linkage to difficulty generalization is absent.  
**Break condition:** If environment dynamics change (e.g., transition probabilities), the learned exploration policy fails to transfer.

### Mechanism 2: Cross‑Environment Limitations Due to Interface and Semantic Mismatch
**Claim:** Cross‑environment generalization is limited by mismatches in observation/action interfaces and semantic priors, often resulting in weak or negative transfer.  
**Mechanism:** RFT adapts the policy to specific observation formats (e.g., HTML, text descriptions) and action sets (discrete vs. free‑form). Interface changes cause misinterpretation of observations or invalid action generation. Semantic priors (required world knowledge) also create barriers: policies may not acquire knowledge needed for a new domain.  
**Core assumption:** Effective transfer requires sufficient overlap in the representation of states and actions between source and target environments.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: “cross‑environment generalization is weaker and sensitive to shifts in both observation/action interfaces and semantic priors.”  
- Section 5: BabyAI provides per‑step valid actions; evaluating on WebShop (no such list) yields a sharp drop (−18.34 points for 7B).  
- Corpus: Neighboring *Paying Less Generalization Tax: A Cross‑Domain Generalization Study of RL Training for LLM Agents* explicitly studies cross‑domain generalization, highlighting interface shifts.  
**Break condition:** Drastic changes in observation modality or action space cardinality lead to negative transfer.

### Mechanism 3: Sequential Training Stability via Policy Gradient
**Claim:** Sequential RFT across environments yields downstream gains with minimal upstream forgetting due to the stability of policy gradient updates.  
**Mechanism:** Policy gradient methods (GRPO) optimize the probability of high‑reward trajectories without directly fitting to specific output tokens, avoiding overwriting weights critical for prior tasks. The KL penalty constrains updates, preventing large deviations from the previous policy.  
**Core assumption:** Catastrophic forgetting arises from supervised fine‑tuning that overwrites representations; on‑policy RL maintains a balance between new learning and retention.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: “sequential training yields promising downstream gains with minimal upstream forgetting.”  
- Section 6: WebShop‑pre‑trained agent further trained on TextCraft: TextCraft improves (80.88→82.50) while WebShop drops minimally (86.50→86.32).  
- Corpus: Weak support; no strong direct neighbor cited.  
**Break condition:** After many sequential stages or with highly dissimilar environments, forgetting may accumulate.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Qwen2.5‑Instruct (3B/7B) → Environments (WebShop, SearchQA, TextCraft, AlfWorld, BabyAI) → AgentGym‑RL (GRPO) → Metrics (avg@8, interaction turns, tokens)
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Prepare environment wrappers (text actions → text observations).  
  2. Construct training datasets: split each environment’s tasks into easy/hard subsets.  
  3. Initialize model/tokenizer; set up GRPO (group size = 8, KL penalty coefficient).  
  4. Train either single‑environment (varying difficulty) or sequentially across environments.  
  5. Evaluate on all environments (held‑in/held‑out) with max turns = 20.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Fixed max turns vs. early stopping: increasing max turns may help harder tasks but masks efficiency.  
  - Reward shaping: sparse vs. dense; mixing environments may require normalization.  
  - Model scale: 7B shows stronger held‑out transfer but higher compute cost.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Negative ΔHeld‑Out (e.g., BabyAI → WebShop: −18.34 points for 7B).  
  - Upstream performance drop > 5 points in sequential training.  
  - High variance in avg@8 across seeds indicates unstable training.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Train on WebShop easy tasks only; evaluate on WebShop hard test. Expect strong gain (Table 2: +60.1 points).  
  2. Train on AlfWorld; zero‑shot evaluate on SearchQA. Expect modest positive transfer (+4.69 points for 3B) or possibly negative.  
  3. Sequential: Pre‑train on WebShop (full), then fine‑tune on BabyAI. Expect BabyAI performance ≈ single‑task (86.6→≈87.7) and WebShop retention (≈86.5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do hyperparameter choices (e.g., learning rate, KL weight) in GRPO affect RFT generalization?  
  *Basis:* The paper relies on default training/evaluation protocols and hyperparameter choices, without exhaustive tuning; more careful optimization may bring new findings.  
  *Why:* Default settings may not be optimal; hyperparameters could influence transfer and forgetting.  
  *Evidence:* Conduct a systematic sweep over key hyperparameters across environments, measuring held‑in, held‑out, and sequential performance.

- **Open Question 2:** Do alternative RL algorithms (e.g., REINFORCE++, DAPO) yield different generalization compared to GRPO?  
  *Basis:* The paper adopts GRPO rather than other approaches such as REINFORCE++ or DAPO; these are left for future work.  
  *Why:* Different algorithms have distinct optimization dynamics that may affect robustness to distribution shift.  
  *Evidence:* Train agents with GRPO, REINFORCE++, DAPO on identical environments and compare transfer metrics.

- **Open Question 3:** What is the full impact of training order in sequential multi‑environment RFT, and can optimal sequences be predicted?  
  *Basis:* The sequential multi‑environment training study does not enumerate all possible environment orderings; a broader investigation is left for future work.  
  *Why:* Only a few orders tested; many permutations unknown; order may influence final performance.  
  *Evidence:* Evaluate all possible orders (or a large sample) across the five environments, analyze patterns.

- **Open Question 4:** Which environmental characteristics (e.g., feedback sparsity, action space size) most strongly determine cross‑environment transfer success?  
  *Basis:* The paper finds cross‑environment generalization varies and correlates with shifts in observation/action interfaces and semantic priors, but does not isolate factors.  
  *Why:* Without isolating variables, it’s unclear what drives transfer difficulty; this knowledge could guide curriculum or adaptation.  
  *Evidence:* Create controlled variants of environments where only one attribute is altered, then measure transfer performance.

- **Open Question 5:** How can we mitigate the prevalent failure modes (e.g., confirmation bias, state inconsistency) in RFT agents, especially under distribution shift?  
  *Basis:* Failure analysis shows confirmation bias (>10%) and increased state inconsistency in held‑out settings (e.g., WebShop held‑out rose from 4.3% to 21.9%).  
  *Why:* These failures limit deployment; no remedies were proposed in the paper.  
  *Evidence:* Introduce techniques such as self‑reflection rewards, external memory, or diversity regularization; evaluate reduction in error rates on held‑out environments.

## Limitations
- Findings are limited to Qwen2.5 (3B/7B) and five environments; may not generalize to other architectures or domains.  
- Cross‑environment transfer is noisy (both positive and negative) and factors like interface/semantic mismatch are partially entangled, hindering causal attribution.  
- Exploration efficiency is inferred from reduced interaction turns without direct measurement of exploration behavior (e.g., state coverage).

## Confidence
- *Intra‑environment generalization via RFT*: Medium  
- *Cross‑environment limitations due to interface/semantic mismatch*: High  
- *Sequential training stability*: Medium
