# Evaluating Stochasticity in Deep Research Agents
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23271
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23271
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposed variance-based evaluation framework and mitigation methods reduce stochasticity by 22% while improving answer accuracy up to 12% on DeepSearchQA.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of high run-to-run output variance (stochasticity) in Deep Research Agents (DRAs), which hinders reliable deployment. It frames DRAs as information acquisition Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and introduces a variance-based evaluation framework that quantifies stochasticity across three aspects: final answers, intermediate findings, and citations. Through controlled temperature ablation on a ReAct-based DRA, the authors discover that stochasticity introduced early in the trajectory (during query generation and information retrieval) and within the inference/compression module contributes most to overall output variance. To address this, they propose two mitigation strategies: structured output constraints that limit the output space of the compression/inference module, and ensemble-based query generation that averages over multiple early-stage query expansions. On the DeepSearchQA benchmark, these methods achieve an average 22% reduction in stochasticity across the three metrics and yield accuracy gains of up to 12% when combined, while maintaining or improving answer quality.

## Method Summary
The study employs a ReAct-based Deep Research Agent as the testbed, operating in an MDP framework where each step involves reasoning, tool use (e.g., search), and state updates. To quantify stochasticity, the authors define variance metrics: for a set of sampled outputs, they compute variance of answer strings, of collected findings, and of citation presence. They conduct temperature ablations, varying the temperature parameter at different stages (early acquisition vs. later inference) to isolate contributions to final variance. Based on the findings, they design two mitigation techniques: structured output constraints that restrict the decoder's output format or content during compression/inference (e.g., via constrained decoding or post-hoc filtering), and ensemble query generation that creates multiple query variants at the start and aggregates retrieved information. Evaluation on DeepSearchQA compares baseline, individual, and combined mitigations using the variance metrics and answer accuracy.

## Key Results
- Ablation results pinpoint early-stage acquisition and inference module as dominant sources of stochasticity.
- Structured output constraints reduce variance in the compression/inference stage, leading to an overall 22% average stochasticity decrease.
- Ensemble query generation cuts early-stage variance; combined, the two methods not only lower stochasticity but also improve answer accuracy by up to 12%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The structured output constraints work by limiting the space of possible outputs during the compression and inference phases, thereby reducing the spread caused by stochastic decoding. The ensemble query generation mitigates early stochasticity by sampling multiple query expansions and aggregating their retrieved documents, which averages out the randomness in any single query. Together, these interventions address the two most variance-prone stages identified by the ablation, explaining the observed reduction in overall stochasticity and the concomitant accuracy gains (due to more consistent and focused information processing).

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (plain text):
User Query → Query Expander → Search Tool → Document Retriever → Content Extractor → Compression Module → Inference Module → Answer Generator → Final Output

Critical path: The critical path begins when the user query enters the system and proceeds through: (1) query generation (possibly with ensemble expansions), (2) information retrieval via search tools, (3) compression of retrieved documents into a concise representation, (4) inference to produce an answer, and (5) final answer synthesis. Variance at the early retrieval stage or during compression/inference propagates to the final output, as identified by the ablation study.

Design tradeoffs:
- Structured output constraints: Reduces stochasticity but may limit expressiveness or lead to overly terse outputs; requires careful balance.
- Ensemble query generation: Improves robustness but increases computational cost and latency due to multiple queries; ensemble size is a key hyperparameter.
- Temperature tuning: Lower temperature reduces stochasticity but may cause deterministic, repetitive outputs and reduce creativity; module-specific tuning involves tradeoffs.

Failure signatures:
- High run-to-run variance in answers, findings, or citations despite identical inputs.
- Inconsistent or missing citations; answers that contradict retrieved evidence.
- Over-constrained outputs that are empty, generic, or fail to address the query.
- Latency spikes when using ensemble methods due to multiple search calls.

First experiments:
1. **Baseline stochasticity measurement**: Run the unmodified ReAct DRA with a fixed temperature (e.g., 0.7) multiple times on a sample of DeepSearchQA queries; compute the proposed variance metrics to establish reference levels.
2. **Structured constraints test**: Apply output constraints only to the compression module (e.g., require a fixed template or limit length) and measure change in compression variance and overall stochasticity.
3. **Ensemble query generation test**: Enable ensemble-based query expansion with a small ensemble size (e.g., 3) at the early search stage; compare variance of retrieved documents and final answer stochasticity against baseline.

## Limitations
- Generalizability: Evaluation only on DeepSearchQA; performance on other research tasks or benchmarks is unknown.
- Architectural scope: Findings are based on a ReAct-based DRA; other architectures (tree search, retrieval-augmented) may exhibit different stochasticity patterns.
- Metric validity: The variance metrics for answers, findings, and citations lack human validation or correlation with real-world reliability assessments.
