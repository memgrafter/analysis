# Logit Distance Bounds Representational Similarity

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15438
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15438
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Logit distance (d_logit) provably bounds linear representational similarity (d_rep, mCCA), whereas KL divergence does not; logit-distance distillation preserves representation geometry while matching label accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines the relationship between conditional distribution similarity and internal representation similarity in neural networks, motivated by knowledge distillation. The authors introduce a distributional distance based on logit differences (d_logit) and a representational dissimilarity measure (d_rep) tied to linear identifiability classes. They prove that small d_logit guarantees high linear representational similarity, providing explicit bounds on mean canonical correlation (mCCA) and d_rep in terms of d_logit and unembedding matrix singular values. In contrast, KL divergence can be arbitrarily small while d_logit is large unless strong probability lower bounds hold. Empirical experiments on synthetic and image datasets confirm that logit-distance distillation (using L1_logit) yields students with drastically lower d_rep, near-perfect mCCA, and superior recovery of linearly encoded concepts compared to KL-based distillation, while matching label accuracy. The results establish logit distance as a stronger objective for preserving representational structure.

## Method Summary
The study focuses on models of the form \(p(y|x) = \text{softmax}(f(x)^\top g(y))\) with embedding \(f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}^m\) and unembedding \(g: \mathcal{Y} \to \mathbb{R}^m\). Two distillation objectives are compared: KL divergence between teacher and student probability distributions, and logit-distance (L1 or L2) between their raw logits. The core theoretical contribution defines a linear representational dissimilarity \(d_{\text{rep}}\) that vanishes iff two models are linearly equivalent under transformations \((f,g) \sim (Af, A^{-\top}g)\). Using singular value decompositions of shifted unembedding matrices, the authors derive bounds relating \(d_{\text{rep}}\) and mean canonical correlation (mCCA) to the logit distance \(d_{\text{logit}}\). Empirically, the paper trains teachers on synthetic spirals, CIFAR-100 (ResNet50 teacher, ResNet18 student), and SUB (bird classification with concept attributes). Students are distilled using only teacher outputs (no labels), and evaluated on label accuracy, \(d_{\text{logit}}\), \(d_{\text{rep}}\), mCCA, and linear concept probing. Results consistently show that logit-distance distillation preserves linear geometry and concept interpretability far better than KL-based distillation.

## Key Results
- Small d_logit provably implies high linear representational similarity (bounded mCCA and d_rep), while KL divergence cannot guarantee such similarity even when minimized.
- Logit-distance distilled students achieve orders-of-magnitude lower d_rep and near-perfect mCCA compared to KL-distilled students, despite matching label accuracy.
- On the SUB dataset, logit-distance students recover linearly encoded human-interpretable concepts with accuracy close to the teacher, whereas KL students perform near random.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Logit Distance Quantifies and Bounds Representational Geometry
When two models are close in logit distance \(\mathbb{E}\|u - u'\|^2\), their internal representations are guaranteed to be linearly similar up to an invertible transformation. This follows from decomposing the squared logit difference via singular values of shifted unembedding matrices, yielding explicit bounds: \(\text{mCCA} \geq 1 - d_{\text{logit}}^2/(m\mu_m)\) and \(d_{\text{rep}} \leq C \cdot d_{\text{logit}}/\sigma_{\min}\). The bounds are invariant to the linear equivalence class \(\sim_L\). The key assumption is the diversity condition: the teacher's logit distribution spans \(\mathbb{R}^m\) (i.e., \(\mu_m > 0\)). If the shifted unembedding matrices become near-singular (\(\sigma_{\min} \to 0\)) or \(\mu_m \to 0\), the bounds degenerate.

### Mechanism 2: KL Divergence Cannot Control Linear Geometry
KL divergence measures distributional overlap in probability space but does not directly constrain logit differences. KL can be arbitrarily small while \(d_{\text{logit}}\) remains large if the probability lower bound \(\tau = \min_{x,y} p(y|x)\) is tiny. The derived bound \(d_{\text{logit}}^2 \leq (4 \log(1/\tau)^2/\tau) \cdot \text{KL}\) blows up as \(\tau \to 0\), which is typical when the number of classes \(k\) is large. Thus KL-based distillation may preserve top-1 accuracy but fail to preserve representation geometry, as confirmed by a counterexample with equal embeddings but swapped unembeddings yielding \(\text{KL} \to 0\) while mCCA is bounded away from 1.

### Mechanism 3: d_rep Captures Equivalence-Class Dissimilarity
The dissimilarity measure \(d_{\text{rep}}\) averages the embedding discrepancy \(\|f(x) - A_J f'(x)\|^2\) over all pivot labels \(\tilde{y}\) and label subsets \(J\). By construction, \(d_{\text{rep}} = 0\) iff the models belong to the same \(\sim_L\) equivalence class (i.e., \(f' = Af\) and \(g' = A^{-\top}g\) for some invertible \(A\)). This makes \(d_{\text{rep}}\) the appropriate metric for measuring how far representations deviate from linear equivalence. The bound \(d_{\text{rep}} \leq C d_{\text{logit}}/\sigma_{\min}\) connects distributional closeness to this equivalence-aware measure, explaining why logit-distance distillation yields low \(d_{\text{rep}}\).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation**  
  Why needed: It's the application driver; the paper questions whether matching output probabilities (via KL) preserves internal representations and proposes logit-matching as superior.  
  Quick check: What is the theoretical relationship between minimizing \(\text{KL}(p_t \| p_s)\) and minimizing \(d_{\text{logit}}\)? Under what conditions does the former guarantee the latter?  
  Quick check: Why might we care about representational similarity in the student? Give one potential downstream benefit.

- **Concept: Linear Identifiability (\(\sim_L\))**  
  Why needed: Defines when two models are "the same" for this class. The identifiability theorem states equal distributions imply linear equivalence. The paper asks: does approximate equality imply approximate linear equivalence? The answer depends on the distance metric (logit vs KL).  
  Quick check: Write down the transformation \((A, A^{-\top})\) that relates \((f,g)\) to equivalent \((f',g')\). Why is the inverse transpose needed for \(g\)?  
  Quick check: What does the diversity condition (full-rank logits) intuitively guarantee about the teacher's prediction manifold?

- **Concept: Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) and Condition Numbers**  
  Why needed: The core bounds involve \(\sigma_{\min}\) of shifted unembedding matrices, capturing geometry: small \(\sigma_{\min}\) means some unembedding directions are nearly collinear, weakening the \(d_{\text{logit}} \to d_{\text{rep}}\) bound. Condition number \(\kappa = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}\) likely controls sensitivity.  
  Quick check: Interpret \(\sigma_{\min}(\tilde{L}_J)\): what would it mean for the representation geometry if this is very small? What about a very large \(\sigma_{\max}\)?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Input \(x\) \(\to\) embedding \(f(x) \in \mathbb{R}^m\) \(\to\) logits \(u(x) = [f(x)^\top g(y_1), \dots, f(x)^\top g(y_k)]^\top\) \(\to\) probabilities via softmax \(p(y|x)\). Unembedding vectors \(g(y)\) are centered: \(\sum_y g(y) = 0\). For a pivot \(\tilde{y}\), shifted unembeddings \(\tilde{g}(y) = g(y) - g(\tilde{y})\). For any \(m\)-label subset \(J \subseteq \mathcal{Y}\setminus\{\tilde{y}\}\), form matrix \(\tilde{L}_J = [\tilde{g}(y_1) \dots \tilde{g}(y_m)]\).

Critical path:  
1. Pre-train teacher to convergence on task (cross-entropy on labels), ensuring diversity and general position assumptions.  
2. Load teacher weights; disable teacher gradients.  
3. For each batch, compute teacher logits (raw, no softmax for logit losses).  
4. Student forward pass to get student logits \(u'(x)\).  
5. Compute distillation loss:  
   - KL: apply softmax to both sets of logits, then \(\text{KL}(p_t \| p_s)\).  
   - L1_logit: \(\|u_t - u'\|_1\).  
6. Backpropagate through student; store student embeddings \(f'(x)\) for evaluation.  
7. Monitor \(d_{\text{rep}}\) and mCCA on validation set (requires storing teacher/student embeddings/unembeddings and computing all \(A_J\) over sampled pivots/subsets).  
8. Student should match teacher accuracy while having lower \(d_{\text{rep}}\)/mCCA than KL-distilled counterpart.

Design tradeoffs:  
- L1 vs L2_logit: L1 is numerically more stable; Proposition 4.1 shows \(d_{\text{logit}} \leq 2|\log(\tau)| \cdot \text{L1}_{\text{logit}}\).  
- Dimensionality: Theory assumes teacher and student have same \(m\); mismatched dimensions force reconstruction error (Theorem F.13).  
- Unembedding centering: Necessary for clean theory; otherwise logit covariance includes spurious shifts.  
- Evaluation sampling: For large \(k\), randomly sample moderate number (200–500) of \(m\)-subsets per pivot to estimate \(d_{\text{rep}}\).

Failure signatures:  
- KL student: label accuracy matches teacher, but \(d_{\text{rep}}\) is high (orders > teacher), mCCA far below 1, and linear probes for concepts fail. Unembeddings may be rotated/scaled relative to teacher.  
- Logit-distance student: If \(d_{\text{rep}}\) remains high, check: \(\tau\) too small (many tiny probabilities), \(\sigma_{\min}(\tilde{L}_J)\) small, or diversity condition violated (\(\mu_m \approx 0\)).  
- L1 artifacts: Very large logit magnitudes can cause softmax overflow during KL computation; consider clipping logits.

First 3 experiments:  
1. **Verify theory on synthetic data**: Generate teacher \((f,g)\) with general position (e.g., random Gaussian, \(k > m\)). Compute all \(\tilde{L}_J\) and confirm \(\sigma_{\min} > 0\). Add small noise to student parameters; check \(d_{\text{logit}} \propto d_{\text{rep}}\) and mCCA \(\approx 1 - O(d_{\text{logit}})\).  
2. **Compare KL vs L1 distillation on Synth**: Train teacher on 7‑class spiral (\(m=2\)). Visualize student embeddings. KL student shows distorted geometry; L1 preserves alignment. Quantify \(d_{\text{rep}}\), mCCA; expect L1 reduces \(d_{\text{rep}}\) by an order of magnitude.  
3. **Test concept recovery on SUB**: Teacher trained with both class and concept supervision to force linear concept encoding. Distill with KL vs L1. Train linear probes on student embeddings to predict 33 concept attributes. L1 student probe accuracy near teacher (\(\sim0.9\)); KL near random (\(\sim0.03\)). Correlate with mCCA and \(d_{\text{rep}}\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: Can the framework be extended to \(k \leq m\)?  
  Basis: Limitations state extending to regimes where number of labels is equal to or smaller than representation dimension is a key next step.  
  Why unresolved: Current identifiability results and \(d_{\text{rep}}\) definition rely on \(k > m\); for \(k \leq m\), equivalence class may not be fully identifiable or bounds may differ.

- **Question 2**: Does the general position assumption hold empirically, and what if violated?  
  Basis: Authors note the assumption is satisfied with high probability for random vectors in high dimension but is stronger than diversity.  
  Why unresolved: If violated, bounds linking \(d_{\text{logit}}\) to \(d_{\text{rep}}\) may become loose or invalid; empirical necessity is unclear.

- **Question 3**: What factors beyond distributional closeness explain empirical linear similarity under cross-entropy?  
  Basis: Some works reportSimilarity under CE despite KL's theoretical weaknesses; authors suggest learning dynamics or implicit regularization.  
  Why unresolved: Theory shows KL can be small with highly non-linear representations; yet CE training often yields similar embeddings, indicating missing explanatory factors.

- **Question 4**: Do benefits extend to large autoregressive language models?  
  Basis: Authors state assumptions likely hold for language models and want experiments there.  
  Why unresolved: Experiments were on synthetic and image datasets; language models differ in scale, architecture, and distributional properties, so transfer is not guaranteed.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on the diversity condition (full‑rank logits) and a uniform probability lower bound \(\tau\), which may fail for large‑\(k\) or high‑entropy tasks, weakening bounds.
- Bound tightness depends on \(\sigma_{\min}(\tilde{L}_J)\) and the smallest logit eigenvalue \(\mu_m\); near‑singularities or vanishing eigenvalues make bounds vacuous.
- Empirical validation is limited to three datasets (Synth, CIFAR‑100, SUB) with specific architectures; scaling to large‑scale vision or language models remains unverified.
- Distillation experiments used pure distillation losses (no ground‑truth cross‑entropy), which may limit practical applicability in standard settings.
