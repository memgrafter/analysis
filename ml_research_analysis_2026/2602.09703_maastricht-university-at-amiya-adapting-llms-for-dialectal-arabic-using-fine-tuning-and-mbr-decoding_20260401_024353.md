# Maastricht University at AMIYA: Adapting LLMs for Dialectal Arabic using Fine-tuning and MBR Decoding
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.09703
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09703
*   **Reference count:** 8
*   **Primary result:** A system combining LoRA fine-tuning, TIES-Merging, and ADI2-optimized MBR decoding achieves the highest official ADI2 scores for Syrian and Saudi dialects and the best chrF++ for English/MSA→Syrian translation.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the poor performance of large language models on dialectal Arabic, primarily due to scarce and linguistically varied training data. The proposed system adapts a base Arabic LLM (JAIS-2) by training two separate LoRA adapters per dialect: one on raw monolingual dialect text to capture authentic surface forms, and another on parallel translation data to ensure semantic grounding. These adapters are merged using TIES-Merging to combine their complementary knowledge. At inference, Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding reranks multiple sampled candidates using the ADI2 metric, which explicitly optimizes for dialectal fidelity. This pipeline demonstrably improves the balance between dialect authenticity and translation accuracy, securing top positions in the AMIYA 2024 shared task for specific dialects.

## Method Summary
The method is a three-stage adaptation and decoding pipeline. First, a base multilingual Arabic model (JAIS-2) is fine-tuned with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on two distinct datasets per target dialect: (1) unstructured monolingual dialect text via a causal language modeling objective, and (2) English- or MSA-dialect parallel data framed as an instruction-following translation task, with loss applied only to the dialectal output. Second, the two resulting LoRA adapters are merged into a single adapter using the TIES-Merging algorithm, which resolves parameter conflicts by retaining only sign-agreeing updates. Third, during generation, the merged model produces multiple candidate outputs for a given input. These candidates are scored using the ADI2 metric (a product of a dialectness score and a dialect identification confidence), and the highest-scoring candidate is selected via MBR decoding.

## Key Results
*   The full system (JAIS-2 + TIES-Merged Adapters + MBR(ADI2)) achieved the highest official ADI2 scores for the Syrian and Saudi dialects in the AMIYA 2024 shared task.
*   It also achieved the best chrF++ scores for translations from English and MSA into Syrian Arabic.
*   Human evaluations noted strong fluency ratings for the Moroccan dialect system, indicating successful generation of natural, dialect-specific text.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Monolingual Fine-tuning Captures Dialect Form
*   **Claim:** Training a LoRA adapter on raw, unstructured sentences in the target dialect teaches the model the surface-level linguistic patterns (vocabulary, morphology, syntax) specific to that dialect.
*   **Mechanism:** The causal language modeling objective forces the model to internalize the statistical distribution and unique forms present in the monolingual data. This exposure encourages the learning of dialect-specific constructions without the noise or constraints of a translation alignment task.
*   **Core assumption:** A model can learn the authentic "feel" and structural conventions of a dialect from self-supervised exposure to its raw text.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states this captures "dialectal surface forms." Section 3.1.1 says it encourages the model to "naturally learn dialect-specific vocabulary, morphology, and sentence structure." Ablation results (e.g., the `mono-ft` model in Table 4) show this adapter alone achieves the highest ADI2 scores, confirming its strength in dialect authenticity.
*   **Break condition:** If the monolingual corpus is sparse, noisy, or unrepresentative (e.g., heavy code-switching, missing common colloquialisms), the adapter may overfit to corpus-specific artifacts and fail to generalize to authentic dialectal generation.

### Mechanism 2: Translation Fine-tuning Provides Semantic Grounding
*   **Claim:** Fine-tuning on parallel data provides cross-lingual semantic grounding, ensuring generated dialectal text remains faithful to an intended meaning.
*   **Mechanism:** The instruction-following setup exposes the model to aligned semantic content across languages. The loss computed only on the target dialect output teaches the model to map semantic representations from the source language (English/MSA) to the appropriate dialectal realization.
*   **Core assumption:** Exposure to aligned semantic content anchors the model's internal representations to meaning, preventing the generation of fluent but semantically deviant dialectal text.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions "semantic grounding." Section 3.1.2 explains it "helps it associate dialectal expressions with their meanings." The `mt-ft` model in Table 4 consistently achieves higher chrF++ scores than the `mono-ft` model, demonstrating its strength in semantic accuracy for translation tasks.
*   **Break condition:** If the parallel data contains poor translations, non-literal glosses, or cultural mismatches, the adapter may learn incorrect or unnatural semantic mappings, degrading translation quality even if the output is structurally dialectal.

### Mechanism 3: Merging and MBR Balance Form and Meaning
*   **Claim:** Combining the separately trained adapters via TIES-Merging and applying dialect-aware MBR decoding optimally balances dialect authenticity (ADI2) and semantic accuracy (chrF++).
*   **Mechanism:**
    1.  **TIES-Merging:** The parameters of the `mono_adapter` (strong on dialect form) and `mt_adapter` (strong on semantics) are merged. The algorithm identifies and resolves sign disagreements in parameter updates, then takes a weighted consensus. This creates a single model with a more balanced internal representation.
    2.  **MBR Decoding:** At inference, multiple candidate outputs are sampled. Instead of optimizing for likelihood, each candidate is evaluated by the ADI2 metric. The candidate maximizing this dialectal fidelity score is selected, explicitly steering generation toward the dialect-authentic region of the form-meaning trade-off space.
*   **Core assumption:** The optimal output for a dialectal task lies on the Pareto frontier between form and meaning. TIES creates a model near this frontier, and MBR with ADI2 explicitly navigates toward the dialect-authentic point on it.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 states TIES integrates "complementary dataset supervision" and MBR "enforces dialectal fidelity." Table 4 shows the TIES-merged model often achieves the best or near-best scores on both ADI2 and chrF++ compared to single-task models. Table 5 demonstrates MBR(ADI2) significantly improves ADI2 scores over standard decoding, though with some trade-off in chrF++.
*   **Break condition:**
    *   *TIES-Merging:* If the two adapters have conflicting updates beyond simple sign disagreements, or if the sign-agreement threshold is mis-set, TIES may discard too many useful parameters, resulting in a model weaker than both parents.
    *   *MBR(ADI2):* If the ADI2 metric itself has biases (e.g., over-rewarding specific lexical items), MBR will optimize for those biases rather than true authenticity, potentially harming semantic adequacy, as suggested by the chrF++ drop in some MBR configurations.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) / LoRA**
    *   *Why needed here:* Full fine-tuning of a large LLM on multiple small dialectal datasets risks catastrophic forgetting of general knowledge and is computationally expensive. LoRA allows efficient specialization by learning low-rank updates to weight matrices, preserving base knowledge while adapting to dialectal specifics.
    *   *Quick check:* Why is updating only a low-rank subset of parameters (via LoRA) a safer strategy than full fine-tuning when adapting a large model to low-resource dialects?
*   **Concept: Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) Decoding**
    *   *Why needed here:* Standard decoding (beam search, sampling) optimizes for model likelihood, not the task-specific metric of dialectal authenticity (ADI2). MBR reranks sampled candidates based on an external utility function, directly optimizing for the shared task's primary objective.
    *   *Quick check:* How does MBR decoding differ from standard beam search in terms of its optimization objective, and why is this difference crucial for a metric like ADI2?
*   **Concept: Composite Evaluation Metrics (ADI2)**
    *   *Why needed here:* Dialectal authenticity is multi-faceted (lexicon, grammar, "sound"). ADI2 combines a continuous "level of dialectness" score (ALDi) with a categorical dialect identification confidence (NADI). A model must pass both thresholds to score well, making it a stricter proxy for human judgment.
    *   *Quick check:* What are the two sub-components of the ADI2 metric, and why is their multiplication (as in Eq. 1) a stricter requirement than using either one alone?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Base LLM (JAIS-2) -> LoRA Adapters (mono_adapter, mt_adapter) -> TIES-Merging Module -> Merged Adapter (ties_adapter) -> MBR Decoder -> ADI2 Scorer (ALDi model + NADI model)
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Train `mono_adapter` on dialect monologue data (causal LM loss).
    2.  Train `mt_adapter` on translation instruction data (output-only loss).
    3.  Apply TIES-Merging to `mono_adapter` and `mt_adapter` to produce `ties_adapter`.
    4.  Apply `ties_adapter` to base JAIS-2 weights (PEFT merge).
    5.  For each input prompt, generate N=20 candidate outputs with the adapted model.
    6.  Score all candidates using the ADI2 metric (requires separate ALDi and NADI models).
    7.  Output the candidate with the maximum ADI2 score.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *MBR vs. Speed:* MBR with N=20 candidates increases inference latency by ~20x compared to greedy decoding.
    *   *TIES vs. Signal Preservation:* TIES aggressively removes "disagreed" parameters to reduce interference but may discard nuanced, complementary signals if adapter conflicts are not primarily sign-based.
    *   *ADI2-Optimized MBR vs. Semantic Accuracy:* Optimizing for ADI2 can trade off chrF++ (translation accuracy), as the system prioritizes the shared task's primary metric.
    *   *JAIS-2 vs. LLaMA 3.2:* JAIS-2, a model pre-trained specifically on Arabic, was chosen over the stronger generalist LLaMA 3.2 for its better balance on translation tasks (chrF++) despite lower monolingual ADI2, highlighting a data/architecture fit tradeoff for resource-constrained adaptation.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   *High ADI2, Low chrF++:* Model generates dialectal nonsense—superficially authentic forms but semantically unrelated to the input (a risk with MBR(ADI2) if the metric has loopholes).
    *   *Low ADI2, High chrF++:* Model generates Modern Standard Arabic or a non-target dialect, indicating insufficient monolingual fine-tuning or an MBR objective that did not prioritize dialect.
    *   *TIES-Merging failure:* The merged model's performance is worse than *both* single adapters on both ADI2 and chrF++. This indicates destructive interference, possibly from an overly aggressive merging threshold or conflicts not resolvable by sign-agreement alone.
    *   *Adapter interference during joint training (not used):* If monolingual and MT data were mixed in one training run, one objective could dominate, degrading performance on the other.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Replicate Table 4 (Ablation - Moroccan):** Train separate `mono_adapter` and `mt_adapter`. Evaluate base, mono-ft, mt-ft, and TIES-merged models. Confirm the merged adapter achieves the best or most balanced chrF++ and ADI2.
    2.  **MBR Objective Sweep (as in Table 5):** Using the TIES-merged model, run MBR with different scoring functions (ADI2, chrF++, sum). Validate the chosen configuration (MBR(ADI2)) maximizes ADI2 while managing the chrF++ trade-off.
    3.  **Backbone Model Sanity Check (as in Table 3):** Repeat the full pipeline (TIES + MBR(ADI2)) on a small sample with both JAIS-2 and LLaMA 3.2. Confirm JAIS-2's superior balance on translation tasks justifies its selection.

## Limitations
*   The monolingual fine-tuning mechanism risks overfitting to sparse or noisy dialectal corpora, limiting generalization.
*   The semantic grounding from translation fine-tuning is entirely contingent on the quality of the parallel data; poor translations introduce incorrect mappings.
*   The TIES-Merging and ADI2-optimized MBR pipeline may have undisclosed implementation details and metric biases that could lead to suboptimal performance in other settings or on unseen dialects.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (Monolingual FT):** **High**. Strongly supported by the causal LM objective and ablation results showing the `mono-ft` model's exceptional ADI2 performance.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Translation FT):** **Medium**. Logical and supported by the `mt-ft` model's high chrF++, but heavily dependent on unvalidated parallel data quality.
*   **Mechanism 3 (TIES + MBR):** **Medium/Low**. The complementary effect of TIES is shown empirically but the implementation is opaque. MBR's benefit is clear but critically depends on the unreproducible ADI2 metric and its potential biases.
