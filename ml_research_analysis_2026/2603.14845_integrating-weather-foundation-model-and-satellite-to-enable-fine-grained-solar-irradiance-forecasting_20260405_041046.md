# Integrating Weather Foundation Model and Satellite to Enable Fine-Grained Solar Irradiance Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.14845
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14845
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Baguan-solar reduces GHI RMSE by 16.08% compared to strong baselines and has been operational since July 2025.

## Executive Summary
Accurate day-ahead solar irradiance forecasting is crucial for solar power integration but is hindered by diurnal cycles and complex cloud dynamics. Existing approaches either lack fine-scale resolution (NWP, weather foundation models) or degrade at longer lead times (satellite extrapolation). This paper introduces Baguan-solar, a two-stage multimodal framework that fuses forecasts from the Baguan weather foundation model with high-resolution geostationary satellite imagery. The method decouples forecasting into predicting day-night continuous intermediates (cloud cover and satellite imagery) and then inferring irradiance, thereby preserving fine-scale cloud structures from satellite and large-scale dynamical constraints from Baguan. Evaluated over East Asia using CLDAS as ground truth, Baguan-solar reduces global horizontal irradiance (GHI) RMSE by 16.08% relative to strong baselines (ECMWF IFS, vanilla Baguan, SolarSeer) and better resolves cloud-induced transients. An operational deployment has supported solar power forecasting in an eastern Chinese province since July 2025.

## Method Summary
Baguan-solar is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 uses two independent Swin Transformer encoders to process high-resolution satellite imagery (0.05°) and coarse Baguan environmental fields (0.25°), fuses them via cross-attention (injecting large-scale context into the satellite branch), and predicts both future satellite images and total cloud cover (TCDC). Stage 2 then combines these predictions with a physics-based clear-sky GHI model and additional Baguan variables, feeding them into a single Swin Transformer to produce the final GHI forecast. The decoupled design allows modeling cloud dynamics without the diurnal discontinuity of GHI, while the fusion preserves mesoscale cloud structures and maintains dynamical consistency for longer horizons. The system runs hourly, leveraging up-to-date Baguan forecasts and satellite data to provide 24-hour forecasts at high spatial resolution.

## Key Results
- GHI RMSE reduced by 16.08% compared to baselines (ECMWF IFS, vanilla Baguan, SolarSeer).
- Better resolution of cloud-induced transients in irradiance forecasts.
- Operational deployment in an eastern Chinese province since July 2025.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decoupling the forecast into a "cloud evolution" stage followed by an "irradiance inference" stage mitigates errors from the pronounced diurnal discontinuity in GHI.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 predicts day-night continuous intermediates (total cloud cover, future satellite imagery). Stage 2 then maps these intermediates to GHI using clear-sky GHI as a physical prior. This separation ensures the first stage models cloud dynamics without being forced to directly model the sharp sunrise/sunset GHI jump, while the second stage applies a consistent, physics-based transformation (clear-sky mask) to handle the diurnal cycle.
- **Core assumption:** Cloud-related variables (TCDC, satellite imagery) vary smoothly across day-night transitions, unlike GHI which drops to zero at night. Modeling these intermediates is a more stable, continuous-time task.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "Its decoupled two-stage design first forecasts day-night continuous intermediates (e.g., cloud cover) and then infers irradiance."
  - Section 4.1-4.2: Details the two-stage architecture where Stage 1 outputs TCDC and satellite predictions, and Stage 2 uses clear-sky GHI.
  - Corpus: Weak direct support; corpus neighbors focus on multimodal/satellite methods, not the specific decoupling rationale.
- **Break condition:** If cloud cover or satellite imagery also exhibits strong abrupt changes at twilight (e.g., rapid fog formation/evaporation at dawn/dusk), the intermediate continuity assumption fails, and the decoupling may not confer an advantage.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Cross-modal fusion via cross-attention between satellite tokens (fine-scale) and Baguan tokens (large-scale forcing) preserves mesoscale cloud structures while maintaining dynamical consistency over 24-hour horizons.
- **Mechanism:** A Cloud-Morphology Encoder processes high-res satellite patches. A Cloud-Environment Encoder processes coarse Baguan environmental fields. Cross-attention injects large-scale dynamical/thermodynamical context from the Baguan branch into the satellite branch's representation before decoding. This allows the satellite branch's high-resolution structure to be guided by physically consistent large-scale weather evolution.
- **Core assumption:** Cloud evolution at the km-scale is constrained by and can be meaningfully conditioned on the larger-scale atmospheric flow and moisture fields (from Baguan), especially at longer lead times (12-24h) where pure satellite extrapolation fails.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "modality fusion jointly preserves fine-scale cloud structures from satellite and large-scale constraints from Baguan forecasts."
  - Section 4.1, Eq. 2: "Zfused = cat(Zsat + Attn(Zsat WQ, Zbg WK, Zbg WV), Zbg)." Describes the cross-attention mechanism fusing Zsat and Zbg.
  - Section 5.2: Ablation shows removing Baguan increases RMSE significantly more at 12h and 24h leads than at 1h, confirming its role in long-horizon skill.
- **Break condition:** If the coarse Baguan forecasts contain significant systematic errors in key dynamical fields (e.g., jet stream position, moisture advection) for the region, the injected "constraints" could actively misguide the high-res satellite branch, degrading performance compared to satellite-only.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Operational deployment leverages Baguan's fast inference (0.5h on 2 GPUs) and satellite latency (<30min) to enable hourly updated 24h forecasts, trading some dynamical freshness for high spatiotemporal resolution.
- **Mechanism:** The pipeline runs Baguan 4x/day (00,06,12,18 UTC). Baguan-solar runs **hourly**, each time ingesting the latest available Baguan forecast (spanning past+future) and the latest 6h of satellite imagery. This creates a frequently refreshed, high-resolution forecast that asymptotically approaches the skill of a full Baguan run but with finer detail.
- **Core assumption:** The atmospheric state changes slowly enough at the synoptic scale that a Baguan forecast initialized up to 1 hour prior remains a sufficiently accurate large-scale constraint for the 24h irradiance forecast. The high-res satellite data compensates for any small-scale degradation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "An operational deployment of Baguan-solar has supported solar power forecasting... since July 2025."
  - Section 6.1: "Baguan-solar runs at a higher frequency (hourly) to provide 24h high-resolution GHI forecasts, offering a faster but less dynamically constrained view... Baguan is executed four times per day... takes 0.5h for Baguan to perform inference."
  - Section 5.1.2: Shows the operational Baguan-solar variant (using real Baguan forecasts) performs nearly identically to the idealized variant (using ERA5 reanalysis), suggesting Baguan's forecasts are accurate enough for this purpose.
- **Break condition:** During rapidly evolving synoptic events (e.g., a fast-moving frontal system), a 1-hour-old Baguan initial state could become significantly stale, causing the large-scale constraints to misalign with the current satellite-observed cloud field, leading to forecast degradation that hourly updates may not fully correct.

## Foundational Learning
- **Swin Transformer for Vision Tasks**
  - **Why needed:** The model processes both satellite imagery (4-channel image patches) and gridded meteorological fields (multi-variable "images") as 2D token sequences. Swin Transformer's hierarchical structure and shifted windows efficiently capture multi-scale spatial patterns in cloud morphology and atmospheric fields while being computationally feasible for 512x512 inputs.
  - **Quick check:** How does the patch merging in Swin Transformer's later stages help in modeling cloud systems that span many kilometers, and why might this be preferred over a standard global self-attention?

- **Multimodal Fusion via Cross-Attention**
  - **Why needed:** Satellite (fine-scale) and Baguan (coarse-scale) data have different spatial resolutions and semantic contents. Simple concatenation would force one modality to resample to the other's scale, losing information. Cross-attention allows the high-resolution satellite representation to selectively attend to relevant large-scale patterns in the Baguan representation without altering the fine spatial detail of the satellite tokens.
  - **Quick check:** In the fusion equation `Zfused = cat(Zsat + Attn(...), Zbg)`, why is the attended output *added* to `Zsat` rather than replacing it, and why are both `Zsat` and `Zbg` eventually concatenated?

- **Clear-Sky GHI as a Physical Prior**
  - **Why needed:** GHI is bounded by the clear-sky value. Using a deterministic, physics-based model (Ineichen-Perez) as a known input provides a stable upper envelope and helps the model learn the *attenuation* due to clouds rather than having to learn the absolute diurnal solar geometry from scratch. It anchors the prediction in a physical law.
  - **Quick check:** What specific atmospheric parameters does the clear-sky model depend on (latitude, longitude, time, elevation, Linke turbidity), and why is it computationally challenging to compute on a dense 512x512 grid for every forecast hour?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Inputs (Xsat, Xbg) -> Stage 1 Encoders (Enc_sat, Enc_bg) -> Zsat, Zbg -> Fusion (cross-attention: Zsat queries, Zbg keys/values, add to Zsat, concatenate with Zbg -> Zfused) -> Stage 1 Decoder (shared Swin decoder + two heads: Sat-Head predicts Ysat, Cloud-Head predicts YTCDC) -> Stage 2 Input: concat(Ysat, YTCDC, X_bg_stage2, clear-sky GHI) -> Stage 2 Backbone (Swin Irr) -> GHI output.
- **Critical path:** Xsat (6 timesteps x 4 bands x H_sat x W_sat) + Xbg (30 timesteps x 39 vars x H_bg x W_bg, interpolated to sat grid) -> [Enc_sat(Xsat)=Zsat, Enc_bg(Xbg)=Zbg] -> cross-attention fusion -> Zfused -> Decoder -> Ysat, YTCDC -> Stage2: concat(Ysat, YTCDC, Xbg_stage2, clear-sky) -> Irr() -> Y_ghi. Spatial alignment on 0.05° grid, temporal alignment (6h history, 24h prediction).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Resolution vs. Cost: Satellite at 0.05° (~5km) provides detail but Baguan at 0.25° (~25km) is coarse. The design interpolates Baguan to sat grid, potentially losing large-scale gradient fidelity. A pure high-res Baguan would be prohibitively expensive.
  - Decoupling vs. End-to-End: The two-stage design simplifies learning (Stage 1 learns cloud physics, Stage 2 learns radiative transfer) but introduces error propagation. A single-stage model might be more optimal but harder to train and may struggle with diurnal cycles.
  - Modality Dominance: Integrated Gradients analysis shows satellite dominates short-term, Baguan long-term. The architecture explicitly handles this via separate encoders and fusion, rather than a naive mixture.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Stage 1 failure: Ysat outputs are over-smoothed, losing fine cloud textures, or YTCDC is systematically biased. Check loss weights, Swin depth, and cross-attention strength.
  - Stage 2 failure: GHI outputs have "blocky" artifacts (patch recovery issue), unphysical negative values, or fail to mask night values properly. Check clear-sky input correctness and final layer activation.
  - Fusion failure: Performance gap between "w/o Baguan" (sat-only) and full model is small, or IG analysis shows Baguan contribution is negligible at all leads. Indicates fusion mechanism is not effectively injecting large-scale context.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation - Modality Removal: Train Baguan-solar w/o Baguan (sat-only two-stage) and w/o satellite (Baguan-only). Expect: w/o Baguan error rises sharply at 12-24h leads; w/o satellite error rises sharply at 1-3h leads. Confirms modality-specific temporal roles.
  2. Ablation - Stage Decoupling: Train a "Only S1" model that predicts GHI directly from Stage 1 outputs in a single stage (no separate Stage 2). Expect higher RMSE, especially in representing day-night transition smoothness and overall physical consistency.
  3. Operational vs. Idealized Inference: Compare the "operational" variant (uses Baguan forecasts as Xbg) against the "idealized" variant (uses ERA5 reanalysis as Xbg). Expect a small but consistent performance gap, with operational being slightly worse, validating that Baguan's forecast error is the main operational bottleneck.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the decoupled design perform during twilight periods with abrupt, non-diurnal cloud changes (e.g., radiation fog formation/dissipation)? The current evidence assumes smooth intermediate continuity, but this may not hold universally.
- To what extent does interpolation of coarse Baguan fields to the satellite grid degrade large-scale gradient fidelity, and could this be mitigated by alternative fusion strategies that preserve multi-scale information without upsampling?
- Does the framework generalize to other geographic regions with different cloud climatologies (e.g., tropical convective systems versus mid-latitude stratiform clouds), or is its skill region-specific due to training on East Asian data?
- What is the sensitivity of operational performance to satellite data latency or missing observations, given the pipeline's reliance on recent imagery for hourly updates?
- Could the two-stage architecture be improved through joint end-to-end training or more sophisticated fusion mechanisms to reduce error propagation between stages?

## Limitations
- Decoupling advantage is only medium-confidence; not stress-tested for abrupt twilight cloud changes (e.g., fog).
- Error propagation from Stage 1 to Stage 2 is not decomposed across cloud regimes.
- Interpolation of coarse Baguan fields to satellite grid may degrade large-scale gradients, impact unquantified.

## Confidence
- Decoupling Advantage: Medium
- Cross-Modal Fusion Benefit: High
- Operational Viability: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Twilight Cloud Transition Stress Test: Isolate forecast performance during periods with rapid, non-diurnal cloud changes at dawn/dusk (e.g., radiation fog formation/dissipation). Compare decoupled (Baguan-solar) vs. end-to-end GHI models.
2. Synoptic Event Failure Mode Analysis: Identify fast-moving frontal systems or cyclones in the evaluation period. Quantify: age of Baguan initial state, Baguan's large-scale field error vs. reanalysis, and correlation between this error and degradation in Baguan-solar's performance relative to a satellite-only baseline.
3. Stage-Wise Error Attribution: For a stratified sample of days (clear, scattered, overcast, broken cloud), use a "perfect Stage 1" oracle (feeding ground-truth future cloud cover/satellite) into Stage 2. The performance delta between this oracle and the actual full model isolates the upper bound of Stage 1 error impact on final GHI skill.
