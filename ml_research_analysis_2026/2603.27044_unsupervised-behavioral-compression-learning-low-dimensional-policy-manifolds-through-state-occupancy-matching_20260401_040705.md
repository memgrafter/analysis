# Unsupervised Behavioral Compression: Learning Low-Dimensional Policy Manifolds through State-Occupancy Matching
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.27044
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27044
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** O-PGPE, using a latent space learned via occupancy-matching, surpasses the best policies in the dataset and outperforms DDPG, TD3, and SAC on sample efficiency and final returns in the Hopper backward task.

## Executive Summary
Deep reinforcement learning suffers from sample inefficiency due to high-dimensional, redundant policy parameter spaces. This paper proposes Occupancy-based Policy Compression (OPC) to learn a low-dimensional latent manifold that preserves behavioral diversity. OPC curates a dataset of policies using an information-theoretic uniqueness score based on state occupancy, then trains an autoencoder with a fully differentiable objective that minimizes the KL divergence between true and reconstructed mixture occupancy distributions. This learned latent space enables efficient downstream policy optimization via PGPE, significantly improving sample efficiency and performance across several control tasks.

## Method Summary
OPC operates in three stages. First, it generates a large pool of random policies and collects trajectories. Policies are scored via an information-theoretic uniqueness metric, ρ(θ) = H(d_θ) + D_KL(d_θ || d_Ψ), which combines individual occupancy entropy with diversity from the mixture, and the top percentile is curated. Second, an autoencoder (encoder: policy params → latent; decoder: latent → policy params) is trained on this curated set. Its loss directly minimizes D_KL(d_Ψ || d̂_Ψ), the divergence between the true mixture occupancy and that of the reconstructed policy population, estimated via a differentiable k-NN importance sampler over trajectories. Third, for a new task, the frozen decoder is used with a PGPE hyper-policy that searches the latent space; an optional warm start initializes the search by sampling and evaluating a few latent codes.

## Key Results
*   OPC's curation retains rare, high-reward outlier policies more effectively than action-space novelty (APC).
*   The learned latent spaces exhibit behaviorally meaningful structure and continuity.
*   On the challenging Hopper backward task, O-PGPE (with warm start) surpasses the best policies in the dataset and outperforms A-PGPE, DDPG, TD3, and SAC in sample efficiency and final returns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Information-theoretic Uniqueness Curation
*   **Claim:** Preserves rare, high-performing behaviors by scoring policies with ρ(θ) = H(d_θ) + D_KL(d_θ || d_Ψ).
*   **Mechanism:** The score encourages policies with broad state exploration (high entropy) and those that are behaviorally distinct from the average (high KL to mixture).
*   **Core assumption:** Finite-sample GMM estimates of occupancy distributions accurately reflect long-term behavior and the score correlates with novelty.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "curate the dataset generation with an information-theoretic uniqueness metric"; [section 4] derivation of ρ as marginal entropy contribution.
*   **Break condition:** Noisy occupancy estimates from insufficient trajectories lead to mis-scoring, missing true diversity or including duplicates.

### Mechanism 2: Mixture Occupancy Matching Compression
*   **Claim:** Learns a latent space that aligns global state visitation, avoiding compounding errors from myopic action matching.
*   **Mechanism:** Autoencoder loss minimizes D_KL(d_Ψ || d_g(Z)), estimated via a differentiable k-NN importance sampler over trajectories, making the objective end-to-end trainable.
*   **Core assumption:** Matching mixture occupancies preserves functional diversity; the importance sampler provides an unbiased gradient estimate.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "fully differentiable compression objective that directly minimizes the divergence between the true and reconstructed mixture occupancy distributions"; [section 4] Eq. (6) and importance weight formulation.
*   **Break condition:** High variance in the k-NN estimator or insufficient latent capacity causes instability or loss of fine-grained behavioral distinctions.

### Mechanism 3: Latent Warm Start for Adaptation
*   **Claim:** Accelerates downstream optimization by initializing the PGPE search in a high-reward latent region.
*   **Mechanism:** Before PGPE, sample a small set of latent codes, decode and evaluate policies on the target reward, and use the best code to initialize the hyper-policy mean.
*   **Core assumption:** The latent space is smooth (nearby codes yield similar behaviors) and random sampling can find a promising basin.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 4] description of warm start procedure; [section 5.3] WS-O-PGPE results showing faster convergence.
*   **Break condition:** In environments with deceptive rewards, warm start may land in an inescapable local optimum due to low exploration variance.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: State Occupancy Measure**
    *   **Why needed here:** Core to OPC; d_π(s) captures long-horizon state visitation, unlike transient actions.
    *   **Quick check:** How does occupancy differ from immediate action distribution in capturing long-horizon behavior?

    *   **Why needed here:** Used in the uniqueness score and as the primary compression loss to measure distributional discrepancy.
    *   **Quick check:** Why is KL not symmetric, and why might that matter in the mixture occupancy matching objective?

    *   **Why needed here:** The downstream optimizer that directly searches the latent space, treating the decoder as a black box.
    *   **Quick check:** How does PGPE avoid computing Jacobians of the decoder, and why is that beneficial in a compressed latent space?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Dataset Curation -> Autoencoder -> Downstream Optimization (PGPE with optional Warm Start)
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Generate 50k random policies; collect 20 deterministic trajectories each.
    2.  Score policies via ρ(θ); select top 5% for curated dataset DΘ.
    3.  Train autoencoder on DΘ using importance-sampling KL loss on mixture occupancy.
    4.  (Optional) Warm start: sample latent codes, evaluate, pick best to initialize PGPE mean.
    5.  Run PGPE to optimize latent code for target reward.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Curation threshold (e.g., top 5%): higher % increases diversity but includes more low-performers; lower % risks losing key behaviors.
    *   Latent dimensionality: lower increases compression but may lose expressivity; paper tests up to 8D.
    *   Autoencoder batch size: small (e.g., 5) prevents collapse to mean but increases gradient variance.
    *   k-NN parameter: larger k reduces variance but introduces bias in the occupancy estimator.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Autoencoder loss unstable/NaN: likely high variance in k-NN estimator; reduce batch size or adjust k.
    *   Latent space collapse: all decoded policies similar; check by sampling a latent grid and evaluating behavioral diversity.
    *   Poor GMM fit (especially in Hopper): check held-out state log-likelihood; increase components or use diagonal covariance.
    *   PGPE stagnation: plot returns vs. samples; adjust learning rates, population size, or warm start parameters.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Compare reward distributions of datasets curated by APC vs. OPC scores to show OPC retains high-reward outliers.
    2.  Ablate autoencoder training (APC/OPC scores x losses); visualize 2D/3D latent embeddings colored by performance.
    3.  Evaluate O-PGPE, A-PGPE, WS-O-PGPE against DDPG, TD3, SAC on sample efficiency and final returns across tasks.

## Limitations
*   Uniqueness curation relies on finite-sample GMM fits; poor density estimates can mis-score policies.
*   The differentiable k-NN occupancy estimator has high variance, potentially destabilizing autoencoder training in high-dimensional state spaces.
*   The warm start assumption of a smooth, reward-correlated latent manifold may not hold in deceptive or multi-modal reward landscapes.

## Confidence
*   Uniqueness Curation: Medium
*   Occupancy Matching: Medium
*   Latent Warm Start: Low
