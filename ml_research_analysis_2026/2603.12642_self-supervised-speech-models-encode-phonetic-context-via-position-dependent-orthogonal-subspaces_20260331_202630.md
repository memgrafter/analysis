# Self-Supervised Speech Models Encode Phonetic Context via Position-dependent Orthogonal Subspaces

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12642
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12642
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Single frames in self-supervised speech models compositionally encode phonological vectors for the current phone and immediate neighbors (±1), with position-dependent subspaces that are approximately orthogonal and show discrete shifts at phonetic boundaries.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how frame-level representations in transformer-based self-supervised speech models (S3Ms) capture phonetic context from neighboring phones. Using phonological vector analysis on wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, and WavLM with TIMIT and VoxAngeles datasets, the authors demonstrate that a single frame encodes phonological information both for the current phone and its immediate neighbors (±1) in a linearly compositional manner. Moreover, phonological vectors associated with different relative positions (-2 to +2) occupy approximately orthogonal subspaces, and these subspaces are aligned to phonetic boundaries, as evidenced by discrete shifts in cosine similarity at segment transitions. These findings suggest that S3Ms implicitly learn to disentangle positional contributions within their contextual representations, providing insight into how contextual information is structured in self-supervised speech models.

## Method Summary
The study analyzes representations from three S3Ms: wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, and WavLM. Models are evaluated on TIMIT and VoxAngeles datasets with phone-level alignments. The core method involves extracting phonological feature vectors (e.g., voicing, nasality) by contrasting mean representations of phones that possess versus lack a given feature, separately for each relative position within a 5-phone window centered on a target phone. These vectors are used to test frame-level compositionality via phonological analogies (e.g., [b] - [d] + [t] ≈ [p]) on single frames. Orthogonality of position-dependent subspaces is measured by computing cosine similarity between all pairs of extracted feature vectors across positions. Implicit segmentation is probed by tracking cosine similarity of a position-specific vector across frames relative to phone boundaries. Representations are taken from specific transformer layers (e.g., final layer for orthogonality, intermediate for window size) and from the center frame of phone segments to isolate frame-level encoding.

## Key Results
- Frame-level representations in S3Ms compositionally encode phonological information for the current phone and its immediate neighbors (±1); analogy success rates comparable to mean-pooling confirm compositionality at the single-frame level.
- Position-dependent phonological subspaces for relative positions (-2 to +2) are approximately orthogonal: within-position cosine similarity is significantly higher than across-position similarity.
- Cosine similarity with position-specific vectors shifts discretely at phonetic boundaries, indicating that the model’s representation implicitly tracks segmentation and aligns contextual information to relative phone positions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A single frame-level S3M representation encodes phonological information for the current phone and its immediate neighbors (±1) through linear composition of phonological vectors.
- **Mechanism:** Frame representations are constructed as a sum of position-sensitive phonological vectors (e.g., voicing, nasality) corresponding to the phone aligned with the frame and its immediate neighbors. This allows vector arithmetic (e.g., [b] = [d] - [t] + [p]) to hold at the individual frame level.
- **Core assumption:** Phonological features are linearly additive and can be superposed within the representation space without interference when associated with distinct positions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "phonological information from a sequence of neighboring phones is also compositionally encoded in a single frame... vectors corresponding to previous, current, and next phones are superposed."
  - [section 3.1] Center-pooled representations achieve phonological analogy success rates comparable to mean-pooling, indicating compositionality exists at the frame level.
  - [corpus] Evidence is indirect; neighboring corpus papers investigate orthogonal subspaces for other factors (speaker, phonetic) but not specifically for positional phonological encoding.
- **Break condition:** Success rates for phonological analogies on neighboring positions (±1) fall to baseline levels (random similarity), indicating failure to encode contextual phonological vectors.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Phonological vectors associated with different relative phone positions (-2 to +2) occupy approximately orthogonal subspaces within the single-frame representation space.
- **Mechanism:** The model learns to separate positional contributions by aligning phonological vectors for the same relative position into coherent subspaces while minimizing overlap (cosine similarity) between subspaces of different positions. This disentanglement prevents ambiguity when the same phonological feature appears in multiple positions.
- **Core assumption:** Orthogonal subspaces allow the model to maintain a consistent phonological "template" (e.g., voicing vector) across positions while preserving positional identity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Position-dependent subspaces for relative positions (-2 to +2) exhibit approximate orthogonality, with low cosine similarity across positions versus higher within-position similarity."
  - [section 3.3] Cosine similarity matrices (Figures 5-6) show within-position similarities are significantly higher than across-position similarities for all models and layers. L2 norms decay with positional distance (Figure 7), supporting weighted contribution per position.
  - [corpus] Direct support: "Self-supervised Predictive Coding Models Encode Speaker and Phonetic Information in Orthogonal Subspaces" (paper_id: 214959) demonstrates orthogonal encoding for speaker/phonetic factors, providing conceptual precedent.
- **Break condition:** Mean absolute cosine similarity across different positions approaches that within the same position, indicating subspaces are not disentangled.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The position-dependent subspaces are implicitly aligned to phonetic boundaries, not fixed temporal windows, leading to discrete shifts in cosine similarity at segment transitions.
- **Mechanism:** The model uses the boundaries between phones to reconfigure which positional subspaces are active. Cosine similarity with a position-specific phonological vector (e.g., v⁰_voicing) will be high for frames within that position's segment and low outside it, creating staircase-like transitions aligned with phonetic boundaries.
- **Core assumption:** The transformer's context aggregation implicitly tracks phonetic segment boundaries to correctly assign phonological information to its relative position.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Phonetic segmentation is implicitly encoded: cosine similarity with position-specific vectors shifts discretely at phonetic boundaries."
  - [section 3.4] Figure 9 shows cosine similarity curves for v⁻¹, v⁰, v⁺1 intersect precisely at annotated TIMIT boundaries for both onsets and offsets. Figure 10 extends this to full utterances with clear staircase patterns.
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers focus on segmentation algorithms rather than intrinsic boundary encoding in representations.
- **Break condition:** Similarity transitions are smooth and symmetric around a fixed window (e.g., gaussian-shaped) rather than step-like at phonetic boundaries, indicating fixed-window contextualization instead of boundary-aware positional encoding.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Phonological Feature Vectors**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire analysis relies on representing phones as linear combinations of binary phonological features (e.g., [+voi], [-high]). Understanding this symbolic, compositional representation is essential to grasp how the paper tests for "phonological compositionality" via vector arithmetic.
  - **Quick check question:** If the phonological vector for "voicing" is extracted as the difference between mean representations of voiced vs. voiceless phones, what would the vector operation `[b] - [d] + [t]` be expected to approximate, and why?

- **Concept: Transformer Context Network in S3Ms**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's hypotheses concern how the transformer layers (the "context network") integrate information across time. A prerequisite is knowing that the convolutional encoder provides frame-level, locally-sensitive representations, and the transformer's self-attention can mix these across a wide temporal receptive field.
  - **Quick check question:** Why can't the convolutional feature encoder alone account for encoding phonological information from neighboring phones (position ±1), as shown in the paper's results?

- **Concept: Subspace Orthogonality & Disentanglement**
  - **Why needed here:** The core finding of "position-dependent orthogonal subspaces" depends on understanding that information can be encoded in linearly independent directions within a high-dimensional space. This connects to broader representation learning principles.
  - **Quick check question:** What would a failure of positional disentanglement look like in the cosine similarity matrix between phonological vectors from positions 0 and +1? What linguistic ambiguity might it cause?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
1. **Input:** Raw waveform → **Convolutional Feature Encoder** → Sequence of local frame representations (e.g., 20ms frames, 10ms stride). This output is the input to the context network.
2. **Context Network:** Transformer encoder (12-24 layers, Large models). Takes the convolutional feature sequence. Each layer's output is a contextualized representation for every frame.
3. **Extraction Points:** Representations are extracted from specific layers (e.g., final layer for orthogonality tests; layer 9/24 for context window analysis) and from specific temporal positions within phone segments (center frame for "frame-level" tests).
4. **Analysis Pipeline:**
   a. **Phonological Vector Extraction:** `v_pos_feat = mean(r[i] | phone_at_relative_pos_has_feat) - mean(r[i] | phone_at_relative_pos_lacks_feat)`.
   b. **Analogy Test:** Compute `approx = r_target_phone - r_source1_phone + r_source2_phone` using *single frames* and check cosine similarity.
   c. **Orthogonality Check:** Compute cosine similarity matrix across all extracted `v_pos_feat` vectors.
   d. **Segmentation Check:** For each phonetic boundary, compute `cos(v_pos_feat, r[frame])` for frames pre/post boundary.

**Critical path:** The hypothesis chain is: (Convolutional features) → (Transformer contextualization) → (Frame representations compose vectors from multiple phones) → (Vectors for different positions are orthogonal) → (Boundary-aligned shifts occur). A failure at any link breaks the chain. Most critical is the transformer's ability to learn position-sensitive aggregation without explicit positional encoding for *relative* phone positions.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Model scale:** Larger models (WavLM > HuBERT > wav2vec2) show clearer positional patterns (Figure 4, 8), suggesting capacity matters for learning structured subspaces.
- **Pooling strategy:** Mean-pooling over phone segments obscures frame-level structure. **Action:** Always use center-pooling or single-frame analysis to test compositionality hypotheses.
- **Feature extraction:** Using *mean* across phones to get a phonological vector (Eq 2) assumes class-conditional averaging is sufficient. **Alternative:** Could use minimal pairs only (as in some prior work) but reduces data.
- **Positional reference:** Analysis defines positions relative to the *center phone* of a 5-phone window. This requires accurate phone alignments and filters short utterances.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **Orthogonality collapse:** Within-position cosine similarity is not higher than across-position similarity (Figure 8). Suggests model did not learn disentangled positional encoding.
2. **Context window blur:** Success rate for `p0` analogies is high for positions `±2` (Figure 4). Suggests positional encoding is not local or is smeared.
3. **Segmentation shift missing:** Cosine similarity curves in Figure 9 do not cross at the boundary or are inverted. Suggests representations are not boundary-sensitive.
4. **Layer anomaly:** wav2vec2 shows peak mask-filling at intermediate layers then degradation (Figure 11), while others improve monotonically. May indicate different training dynamics affecting contextualization.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Frame-level compositionality replication:** Use the provided code. On TIMIT, extract center-frame representations from last layer of WavLM-Large. Compute success rate for phonological analogies (e.g., `b - d + t ≈ p`) using *single frames*, not mean-pooled. Expect >0.7 success rate.
2. **Contextual vector test:** For a fixed center phone `p0`, extract its center frame. Partition test set by feature of `p-1` (e.g., voiced vs. voiceless). Test if `v_{-1,voicing}` (computed from training set) has higher cosine similarity with `r(p0)` when `p-1` is voiced. Repeat for `p+1`. Expect significant similarity for ±1 but not ±2.
3. **Orthogonality measurement:** Compute all eight phonological vectors (`v_{pos, feat}`) for positions -2,-1,0,+1,+2 on TIMIT train set using WavLM final layer. Create two sets: `within` = pairs from same position & natural class; `across` = all other pairs. Compute mean absolute cosine. Expect `within` >> `across` (Figure 8). If not, check for data leakage (e.g., same phone appearing in both positions in training pairs).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly identify open questions for future work. However, the limitations suggest several directions, such as improving alignment robustness, validating cross-linguistically, and exploring nonlinear feature interactions.

## Limitations
- Alignment dependence: results rely on accurate phone boundaries; errors could distort position-specific vectors and boundary detection.
- Linear additivity assumption: extracting phonological vectors assumes features superpose linearly; nonlinear interactions might invalidate analogy tests.
- Cross-linguistic scope: evidence limited to TIMIT (English) and VoxAngeles; unknown if findings hold for languages with diverse phonotactics or feature systems.

## Confidence
- Frame-level compositionality: Medium
- Position-dependent orthogonal subspaces: High
- Implicit phonetic segmentation: High

## Next Checks
1. **Alignment perturbation:** Add Gaussian noise (±10ms) to TIMIT boundaries, recompute cosine similarity curves for position-specific vectors. Expect step transitions to shift with noise but remain sharp; smoothing would challenge Claim 3.
2. **Multilingual orthogonality test:** Apply WavLM to five diverse languages (e.g., Spanish, Mandarin, Arabic, Finnish, Zulu). Compute within- vs. across-position cosine similarity gap; a persistent gap strengthens Claim 2's generality.
3. **Capacity ablation:** Train wav2vec 2.0 variants with reduced depth (4/8/12 layers) and constrained attention (200/500ms). Measure mean absolute cross-position cosine. Degradation with reduced capacity/longer context would link Claim 2 to model resources.
