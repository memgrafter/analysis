# Solving Parameter-Robust Avoid Problems with Unknown Feasibility using Reinforcement Learning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15817  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15817  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Feasibility-Guided Exploration (FGE) achieves over 50% more coverage than the best existing method across MuJoCo and pixel-based Kinetix tasks, while consistently attaining higher safety rates and larger coverage gains without losing coverage on easy parameters.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the mismatch between reinforcement learning (RL) and reachability/avoid problems: RL optimizes expected returns over a distribution of parameters, while reachability aims to maximize the set of safe initial conditions (parameters) for which a policy exists. To address this, the authors propose Feasibility-Guided Exploration (FGE), which simultaneously identifies a feasible subset of parameters and learns a safe policy for that subset. FGE employs a feasibility classifier to guide exploration toward promising parameters and uses a saddle-point robust optimization to improve the policy on the worst-case feasible parameters. Experiments on continuous control (MuJoCo) and pixel-based (Kinetix) benchmarks show that FGE significantly outperforms prior methods, achieving >50% higher coverage and better safety rates. Ablations confirm that both the exploration and rehearsal components are essential to its success.

## Method Summary
FGE operates by maintaining three sources of parameters for environment resets: a small uniform base distribution, an exploration distribution that rejection-samples parameters classified as feasible by a learned classifier, and a rehearsal distribution that uniformly samples from a buffer of worst-case feasible parameters (Dθ,t). The feasibility classifier qψ(f|θ) is trained via forward KL divergence on a mixture of a static set of known safe parameters (D_f) and recent on-policy rollouts, with labels indicating actual safety outcomes. The policy π is updated using PPO to minimize a saddle-point loss: it is trained on data collected from the rehearsal buffer to maximize the negative of a cost that indicates failure, while the worst-case parameters in the buffer are selected by minimizing a policy‑conditioned feasibility probability pπ(f|θ). This creates a loop where the policy improves on challenging feasible parameters, and the feasible set expands via classifier‑guided exploration. All components (classifiers, policy) are neural networks with two hidden layers of 256 units, and hyperparameters are provided per task (learning rates, entropy coefficients, etc.).

## Key Results
- FGE achieves over 50% more coverage (i.e., safe parameter set size) than the strongest baseline across MuJoCo and Kinetix tasks.  
- FGE consistently yields higher safety rates (fraction of feasible parameters where the policy is safe) and larger coverage gains relative to domain randomization.  
- Unlike some baselines, FGE does not sacrifice coverage on easy parameters, expanding the feasible set without losing previously safe regions.  
- Ablation studies indicate that removing either the exploration component (classifier‑guided sampling) or the rehearsal buffer (worst‑case training) degrades performance substantially.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism of FGE resolves the RL-reachability mismatch by separating feasibility discovery from policy optimization. The feasibility classifier actively seeks parameters that are potentially solvable, counteracting the tendency of naive RL to ignore rare but feasible regions. Meanwhile, the saddle‑point formulation forces the policy to become robust on the worst-case feasible parameters, ensuring that improvements are not wasted on easy cases alone. The mixture of reset distributions maintains a baseline coverage while focusing computation on frontier exploration and hard‑case refinement. This two‑pronged approach enables steady expansion of the safe set while simultaneously hardening the policy against failures, leading to the observed coverage and safety gains.

## Foundational Learning
**Feasibility Classifier** – Predicts whether a parameter θ admits a safe policy. Needed to steer exploration toward promising regions; quick check: evaluate accuracy on a held‑out set of known safe/unsafe parameters.  
**Saddle‑Point Optimization** – Formulates the problem as maximizing the minimum feasibility probability over θ. Provides a principled way to focus policy improvement on the hardest feasible cases; quick check: verify that the worst‑case objective increases during training.  
**Mixture of Reset Distributions** – Combines uniform base, classifier‑guided exploration, and worst‑case rehearsal. Balances coverage, exploration, and robustness; quick check: monitor the proportion of samples from each component and ensure it matches the intended weights.  
**Rehearsal Buffer** – Stores the set of worst‑case feasible parameters encountered. Enables repeated training on challenging cases; quick check: compute diversity (e.g., pairwise distances) of θ in the buffer to avoid collapse.  
**Conservative Policy Updates (PPO)** – Standard RL algorithm with clipped objectives. Ensures stable policy improvement; quick check: track KL divergence and value loss; if KL diverges, learning rate may be too high.  
**Parameter‑Robust Avoidance** – The problem setting where dynamics, initial conditions, and unsafe sets vary with θ. Defines the objective of maximizing the feasible set; quick check: evaluate coverage on a large validation set of θ.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Base Distribution → Exploration Distribution (via Feasibility Classifier qψ) → Policy Rollouts → Classifier Update (qψ, pπ) & Policy Update (PPO) → Rehearsal Buffer (Dθ,t) ← Worst‑Case Selection (minimize pπ(f|θ)) → Saddle‑Point Loss → Policy Improvement → (loop)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Sample θ from mixture of base, exploration, and rehearsal.  
2. Execute current policy π in environment with parameter θ.  
3. Record episode outcome (safe/unsafe) and update feasibility classifier qψ using new (θ, outcome) pairs and static safe set D_f.  
4. Periodically recompute worst‑case feasible parameters from Dθ,t using pπ(f|θ) and refill rehearsal buffer.  
5. Update policy π via PPO on transitions sampled from Dθ,t, maximizing negative cost.  
6. Add newly discovered best‑response parameters to Dθ,t.  
7. Repeat.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Mixture weights: high exploration (p_explore) accelerates feasible set growth but may destabilize policy; high rehearsal (p_rehearse) improves robustness but risks narrow focus.  
- Classifier threshold β: stricter threshold yields higher precision but lower recall, potentially missing feasible parameters; lower β increases recall but may include many infeasible ones, wasting samples.  
- Replay buffer size: larger buffers preserve diversity but increase memory and compute; smaller buffers may lose hard cases.  
- PPO epochs: more updates per iteration improve sample efficiency but risk overfitting; fewer epochs reduce stability.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Feasible set growth stalls → classifier too conservative (increase exploration weight, lower β, or improve classifier capacity).  
- Coverage loss on easy parameters → mixture overly dominated by hard parameters (reduce p_explore/p_rehearse).  
- Policy divergence (NaN losses) → learning rate too high or PPO clipping ε too small; reduce lr or increase ε.  
- Classifier outputs extreme probabilities → overfitting; add regularization (dropout, weight decay) or reduce classifier training steps.  
- Rehearsal buffer degeneracy (all θ near identical) → buffer lacks diversity; implement deduplication or add novelty bonus.

**First Experiments:**  
1. Train FGE on the Dubins environment with default hyperparameters and plot coverage over training steps; expect monotonic increase.  
2. Ablate the exploration component by setting p_explore=0; observe significantly reduced final coverage and slower growth.  
3. After training, evaluate the feasibility classifier on a small validation set of parameters (including known safe and unsafe); accuracy should be well above random chance, confirming it learns a meaningful boundary.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1: Convergence under approximate best‑response**  
- *Question:* Under what conditions does FGE converge to a saddle point when using approximate best‑response oracles and dealing with nonconvex‑nonconcave objectives?  
- *Basis:* The authors acknowledge that their method approximates best‑response oracles, so theoretical guarantees may not hold.  
- *Why unresolved:* Existing convergence proofs require exact oracles and often convexity/concavity; FGE uses deep RL approximations, violating these assumptions.  
- *Evidence to resolve:* Derive convergence guarantees that accommodate function‑approximation errors or identify a broader problem class where empirical success can be certified.

**Open Question 2: Stochastic dynamics and chance constraints**  
- *Question:* How can FGE be extended to handle stochastic dynamics and provide probabilistic safety guarantees (e.g., chance constraints)?  
- *Basis:* The framework assumes deterministic dynamics, so one safe rollout certifies feasibility; stochastic systems need many rollouts to estimate failure probabilities.  
- *Why unresolved:* The current feasibility classifier and exploration strategy are not designed for statistical feasibility assessment; chance constraints are more complex.  
- *Evidence to resolve:* Develop a stochastic variant that maintains failure probability estimates (e.g., via Bayesian methods), integrates them into the classifier and robust objective, and demonstrates improved coverage on stochastic benchmarks.

**Open Question 3: Classifier drift under non‑stationary policy**  
- *Question:* Does the feasibility classifier maintain accurate predictions as the policy distribution shifts during training, or do errors accumulate?  
- *Basis:* The classifier is trained on a mixture of static safe parameters and on‑policy rollouts; the policy changes continually, making the training distribution non‑stationary.  
- *Why unresolved:* No analysis of the dynamic interplay; mislabelled parameters could misguide exploration, creating negative feedback loops.  
- *Evidence to resolve:* Track classifier precision/recall on a held‑out set of feasible parameters throughout training; or develop a dynamic regret analysis linking policy update magnitude to classifier error.

**Open Question 4: Scalability to high‑dimensional parameter spaces**  
- *Question:* How does FGE scale to very high‑dimensional parameter spaces (e.g., >10 dimensions) and complex state spaces; can the feasibility classifier remain effective?  
- *Basis:* Experiments include Lander9D (9‑D parameters) and claim scalability, but no systematic scaling study.  
- *Why unresolved:* The classifier must learn a boundary in a high‑dimensional space; sample complexity grows exponentially; if the feasible set lies on a lower‑dimensional manifold, current rejection‑sampling may be inefficient.  
- *Evidence to resolve:* Systematically increase parameter dimension while controlling intrinsic dimension of feasible set; measure coverage gain and sample complexity; explore representation‑learning techniques to mitigate curse of dimensionality.

## Limitations
- Exact environment dynamics and safety functions are not described in the paper and must be obtained from the official code, hindering independent reimplementation.  
- Hyperparameters in the paper are ambiguous (e.g., “# PPO epochs” vs. rollout length) and lack full specification (e.g., classifier training schedule, minibatch size).  
- The evaluation protocol for coverage metrics—test parameter set size, sampling distribution, and feasible‑set threshold—is not fully defined, making exact replication of results difficult.  
- Random seeds used for the reported experiments are not listed, so numerical replication relies on variance tolerance.
