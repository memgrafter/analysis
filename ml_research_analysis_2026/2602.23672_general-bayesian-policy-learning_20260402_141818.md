# General Bayesian Policy Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.23672
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23672
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Introduces GBPL to maximize expected welfare via a scaled squared-loss surrogate with tunable regularization ζ, yielding a coherent posterior and competitive empirical performance.

## Executive Summary
The paper proposes General Bayesian Policy Learning (GBPL), a framework for learning decision policies that maximize expected welfare rather than predicting individual outcomes. By re-expressifying empirical welfare maximization as minimizing a squared-loss surrogate with quadratic regularization parameterized by ζ, GBPL enables loss-based Bayesian updating, resulting in a Gaussian pseudo-likelihood interpretation. Theoretical results show exact equivalences for binary and multi-action settings, extensions to bandit feedback via IPW/DR pseudo-outcomes, and PAC-Bayes generalization bounds. Empirical demonstrations on synthetic and UCI datasets show competitive test welfare and regret against baselines, with performance sensitive to ζ but validated selections robust.

## Method Summary
GBPL transforms the welfare objective into a tractable surrogate: for binary actions, the policy δ(x) is reparameterized as a bounded score f(x)∈[−1,1] via δ(x) = (1+f(x))/2. Minimizing empirical welfare over δ is equivalent to minimizing a scaled squared error between the outcome difference Y(1)−Y(0) and ζ f(x), up to a quadratic penalty (ζ/4)E[(2δ(X)−1)²]. This surrogate induces a General Bayes posterior proportional to the prior times exp(−η Σ_i ℓ(θ;z_i)), where ℓ is the squared loss. For multi-action settings, a full-vector surrogate shrinks probabilities toward uniform. Missing outcomes are handled by substituting IPW or DR pseudo-outcomes while preserving the equivalence. Implementation uses neural networks with tanh or softmax outputs, and posterior approximations include MAP, Laplace, or SGLD. Hyperparameters ζ (regularization) and η (temperature) are tuned via held-out welfare.

## Key Results
- Theoretical equivalences: Empirical welfare maximization ⇔ minimized squared surrogate with quadratic penalty (binary: Theorem 4.1; multi-action: Theorem 5.2).
- Bandit extension: IPW/DR pseudo-outcomes yield valid surrogate losses and equivalent penalized welfare objectives (Theorem 7.1).
- Generalization: PAC-Bayes bounds provided for the General Bayes procedure under sub-exponential moment assumptions.
- Empirical: GBPLNet achieves competitive test welfare and regret on synthetic and UCI datasets, with performance sensitive to ζ but validated selections robust.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Mechanism 1: Claim: Maximizing empirical welfare can be rewritten as minimizing a scaled squared-loss surrogate with quadratic regularization. Mechanism: For binary actions, let f(x)=2δ(x)−1∈[−1,1]. Then max_δ empirical welfare equals min_f (1/n)Σ_i [1/√ζ (Y_i(1)−Y_i(0)) − √ζ f(X_i)]² up to constants, with penalty (ζ/4)·(1/n)Σ_i (2δ(X_i)−1)². This provides stable gradients and tractability. Core assumption: Policy class can be reparameterized via bounded score f. Evidence: Section 4.2 states the equivalence; Section 5 extends to K actions. Break condition: If policy class cannot represent optimal rule or f constraints too loose, surrogate may misalign, especially with large ζ.

Mechanism 2: Claim: The squared-loss surrogate induces a Gaussian pseudo-likelihood, enabling computationally convenient General Bayes posteriors. Mechanism: Loss ℓ(θ;z)=½[1/√ζ (y(1)−y(0)) − √ζ f_θ(x)]² yields posterior dΠ_η(θ|D) ∝ dΠ(θ) exp(−(η/n)Σ_i ℓ(θ;z_i)), which coincides with ordinary Bayes under the working model U|X,θ ~ N(ζ f_θ(x), ζ/η). This pseudo-likelihood is a computational device, not a claim about true data generation. Core assumption: Surrogate loss tractable and informative. Evidence: Section 4.4 shows equivalence to Gaussian updating; Section 5.5 for full-vector. Break condition: Gaussian assumption poor for heavy-tailed conditional distributions; robust losses can substitute.

Mechanism 3: Claim: Missing outcomes can be handled by replacing unobserved outcome differences with IPW or DR pseudo-outcomes while preserving the welfare optimization identity. Mechanism: In bandit feedback, construct pseudo-outcomes eY_ipw(a) or eY_dr(a) that satisfy E[eY(a)|X]=γ_a(X) under unconfoundedness and overlap. Substitute into surrogate loss; Theorem 7.1 shows minimizing IPW empirical surrogate is equivalent to maximizing IPW welfare estimator with quadratic penalty. Core assumption: Consistency of pseudo-outcomes and well-behaved nuisance estimators. Evidence: Section 7.2 and 7.3 define IPW/DR; Proposition 7.2 shows conditional mean correctness. Break condition: Weak overlap causes high variance in IPW/DR; sample-splitting cross-fitting recommended.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Policy learning vs. outcome prediction**  
Why needed: GBPL targets a decision rule maximizing expected welfare, not pointwise prediction error; regression models may misalign if model class represents decision boundary well but not conditional outcomes.  
Quick check: Does the surrogate loss directly align with welfare objective via Theorems 4.1/5.2/7.1? Is ζ controlling regularization appropriately?

**Concept: Loss-based Bayesian updating (Generalized Bayes)**  
Why needed: Ordinary Bayes requires a likelihood; GBPL uses task loss directly, yielding posterior without a global probability model, coherent decision-theoretically.  
Quick check: Is loss ℓ(θ;z) convex in θ for stable gradient-based optimization? Does η scale loss appropriately relative to prior?

**Concept: Surrogate loss design for constrained optimization**  
Why needed: Welfare objective linear in δ(x) but δ(x) in constrained set (e.g., [0,1]); squared surrogate introduces penalty nudging toward interior, improving numerical stability.  
Quick check: Does policy class map naturally to bounded score class via transformation? Is penalty minimized at sensible baseline (δ=1/2 binary; δ_a=1/K full-vector)?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data (full/bandit) → Policy model (f_θ or δ_θ) → Loss module (surrogate with pseudo-outcomes) → General posterior (dΠ_η) → Validation (ζ, η) → Decision rule (deterministic/stochastic)

Critical path: 1. Choose surrogate type (binary gap vs full-vector). 2. Build policy model with output constraints (tanh/softmax). 3. Implement loss with pseudo-outcome substitution. 4. Optimize MAP objective. 5. Validate ζ (and η) on held-out welfare. 6. Deploy deterministic policy or posterior via SGLD.

Design tradeoffs: Binary gap simpler but baseline-dependent for K>2; full-vector symmetric but shrinks toward uniform. ζ: small ζ less regularization, large ζ more shrinkage; validate ζ. η scaling equivalent to prior variance; treat as calibration or fix η=1. Nuisance estimation: IPW high variance with small propensities; DR reduces variance but needs good outcome regression; cross-fitting recommended.

Failure signatures: Training loss not decreasing -> learning rate low or model capacity insufficient. High validation welfare variance across ζ -> weak action effects or insensitive surrogate. Extreme saturation of f_θ near ±1 -> ζ too large or model limited. Poor out-of-sample welfare despite low surrogate loss -> misspecification or nuisance errors. Unstable IPW/DR -> high variance from small propensities; clip or cross-fit.

First 3 experiments: 1. Synthetic binary full feedback: Verify Theorem 4.1 by comparing surrogate minimizer to penalized welfare maximizer; test ζ sensitivity; compare test welfare/regret vs DiffReg/PluginReg. 2. Multi-action synthetic full feedback: Test full-vector surrogate (Theorem 5.2); compare DirectWelfare vs GBPLNet(softmax); vary ζ and baseline dependence. 3. Counterfactual binary bandit: Logged data with known propensities; train with DR pseudo-outcomes; validate ζ and prior variance; compare vs PluginReg; repeat with IPW to show variance differences.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How should ζ be chosen in finite samples to balance bias-variance, especially with limited validation data? The paper shows ζ sensitivity and selects via validation without theoretical guidance. Unresolved because no risk characterization or data-driven selector provided.
- **Open Question 2**: What are valid generalization bounds for GBPL in missing outcome settings when propensities and outcome regressions are estimated from same data, especially under weak overlap? The paper's PAC-Bayes assumes known propensities, doesn't account for nuisance error or overlap constant; sample splitting mentioned but not formalized.
- **Open Question 3**: Can GBPL be extended to robust surrogates (e.g., Huber, Catoni) for heavy-tailed outcomes, and what are the resulting targets and guarantees? The paper assumes sub-exponential moments, notes heavy-tailed regimes may need robust losses but no development.

## Limitations
- Tuning parameter η is unexplored; fixed at 1.0 may be suboptimal; UCI semi-synthetic data lacks reproducibility details (seeds, exact shifts).
- Theoretical assumptions (policy class representability, boundedness) may be violated in real applications, especially with large ζ.
- IPW/DR rely on strong causal conditions and well-estimated nuisance models; overlap issues lead to high variance/bias.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (equivalence): High (Theorems 4.1, 5.2).
- Mechanism 2 (Gaussian pseudo-likelihood): Medium – computational device, not true likelihood.
- Mechanism 3 (bandit extension): Medium – depends on nuisance consistency and overlap.
- Empirical performance: Medium – synthetic results strong, but UCI limited and ζ-sensitive.
