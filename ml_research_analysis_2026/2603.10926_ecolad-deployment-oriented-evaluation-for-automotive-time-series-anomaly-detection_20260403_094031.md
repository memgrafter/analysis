# ECoLAD: Deployment-Oriented Evaluation for Automotive Time-Series Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10926
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10926
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** Under strict scoring-rate constraints, lightweight classical detectors (e.g., HBOS, COPOD) maintain coverage and detection lift, while several deep methods become infeasible before their accuracy degrades.

## Executive Summary
Current anomaly detection evaluations prioritize maximum accuracy under unconstrained compute, which misrepresents feasibility for in-vehicle deployment where latency and CPU limits are strict. ECoLAD introduces a deployment-oriented evaluation protocol that systematically reduces model capacity via deterministic scaling rules and enforces thread caps. It measures the best achievable AUC-PR for a given throughput target, revealing that classical methods often sustain viable performance where deep learning models fail due to inference cost. This shifts method rankings and exposes backend-sensitive scaling and large initialization overheads as critical deployment factors.

## Method Summary
ECoLAD evaluates time-series anomaly detection methods under deployment constraints by defining compute-reduction tiers. Each tier combines a fixed backend (GPU or CPU), a thread cap, and a compute-reduction factor *s*. For a method's baseline integer hyperparameters (e.g., network width, tree count), monotonic scaling functions of *s* generate tier-specific configurations, with minimal repairs to ensure validity. For each configuration, inference throughput (*wps*) and AUC-PR are measured on a reference platform (Apple M3 Max) while enforcing the thread cap. To characterize deployment feasibility, the protocol sweeps a target scoring rate τ and reports (i) coverage (fraction of entities with *wps* ≥ τ) and (ii) the best AUC-PR among feasible configurations. Crucially, it distinguishes inference-only throughput from full-run throughput to isolate online scoring costs from one-time fitting overhead.

## Key Results
- Lightweight classical detectors (HBOS, COPOD) sustain coverage and significant detection lift (up to ~2.9× random) across the full throughput sweep on automotive telemetry (anomaly rate ≈0.022).
- Several deep methods become infeasible at high τ before their accuracy degrades, fundamentally altering feasible operating points and method rankings.
- Throughput analysis reveals backend-sensitive scaling (e.g., TimesNet's *wps* drops sharply from GPU to CPU) and large inference/full-run gaps (e.g., OmniAnomaly's per-entity fitting creates ~55× overhead).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Claim:** Mechanical hyperparameter scaling (via integer-only rules tied to a compute-reduction factor *s*) systematically induces capacity reduction across detector architectures, enabling controlled comparison under constrained compute.  
- **Mechanism:** Baseline integer hyperparameters are transformed per tier using monotone functions of *s* (e.g., width = max(1, round(√*s* · *v*))). Minimal constraint repairs maintain validity.  
- **Core assumption:** Compute cost correlates monotonically with scaled hyperparameters; thus *s* induces near-proportional work reduction.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "mechanically determined, integer-only scaling rules"; [section III.F] scaling equations and repair logic.  
- **Break condition:** Non-linear hardware overheads (e.g., fixed memory copies) may decouple *s* from actual runtime; repairs may alter the intended reduction path.

**Claim:** Sweeping throughput targets τ and reporting coverage and best achievable AUC-PR among feasible configurations reveals deployment-relevant operating points that differ from accuracy-only rankings.  
- **Mechanism:** For each τ, configurations with measured *wps* ≥ τ are retained; coverage is the fraction of entities with at least one such config. The best AUC-PR among those configs is the achievable quality.  
- **Core assumption:** Throughput measured on a reference platform translates meaningfully to deployment constraints; the measured configuration lattice sufficiently samples the feasible space.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Throughput-constrained behavior is characterized by sweeping target scoring rates"; [section III.H] formal definitions of coverage and achievable performance.  
- **Break condition:** If the configuration lattice poorly samples the true performance space, the reported "best achievable" may be suboptimal; platform heterogeneity may invalidate direct *wps* translation.

**Claim:** Distinguishing inference-only throughput (*tinf*) from full-run throughput (*te2e*) is critical for streaming deployment, as offline training overhead (especially per-entity fitting) can mask online scoring capacity.  
- **Mechanism:** Timing is instrumented to capture scoring-only time where available; otherwise full time is used as proxy. The ratio of *N/te2e* to *N/tinf* exposes fit-overhead gaps.  
- **Core assumption:** Online scoring must meet τ independently of offline training; thus inference throughput is the correct metric for deployment latency.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [section III.G] "inference-time ... is treated as an offline diagnostic"; [Table V] inference/full-run ratios (e.g., ~55× for OmniAnomaly).  
- **Break condition:** If scoring time cannot be instrumented and full-run time is dominated by training, using *te2e* would dramatically understate feasible *wps*.

## Foundational Learning
- **Compute reduction ladders vs. full retuning**  
  *Why needed:* ECoLAD’s innovation is *mechanical* scaling, not per-tier hyperparameter optimization. Engineers must understand that tier differences arise solely from deterministic rules, isolating architectural effects from tuning variance.  
  *Quick check:* If you double *s* from 0.5 to 1.0, what happens to the model’s hidden dimension and why? (Answer: It scales by √*s*, so increases by ~1.414×, because width reduction targets quadratic cost components.)

- **Throughput vs. accuracy trade-offs under constraints**  
  *Why needed:* Deployment imposes hard scoring-rate limits (τ). The engineer shifts from “maximize AUC-PR” to “maximize AUC-PR subject to *wps* ≥ τ.”  
  *Quick check:* Why might a method with higher GPU-tier AUC-PR be less suitable than a lower-accuracy method on CPU-1T for τ=500 wps? (Answer: The high-accuracy method may not have any configuration meeting τ on CPU-1T, while the lower-accuracy method does.)

- **Inference-only vs. full-run throughput semantics**  
  *Why needed:* For streaming inference, only per-window scoring time matters; methods with per-entity fitting (e.g., OmniAnomaly) appear infeasible if full-run time is used.  
  *Quick check:* How do you compute *wps* for a windowed method with 10,000 samples, window size 100, and *tinf* = 2 s? (Answer: *N* = 10,000 − 100 + 1 = 9,901; *wps* = 9,901 / 2 ≈ 4,950.5.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Tier definitions → Scaling engine → Measurement harness → Feasibility analyzer → Audit logger

**Critical path:**  
Baseline config → Apply tier *s* scaling → Run with thread cap → Measure *tinf*/*te2e* → Compute *wps* per entity → For each τ, filter configs with *wps* ≥ τ → Extract best AUC-PR → Aggregate coverage.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Integer-only scaling ensures reproducibility but may miss optimal per-tier configurations.  
- Fixed window length across τ isolates timing effects but prevents window-size tuning for latency.  
- Single platform (M3 Max) simplifies comparison but requires correction factors for target ECU.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Backend-sensitive collapse: GPU→CPU *wps* plummets while AUC-PR stays flat (e.g., TimesNet).  
- Quality-drift-limited: High *wps* but AUC-PR degrades sharply under scaling (e.g., LOF on SMD).  
- Fit-overhead gap: *te2e* much larger than *tinf* (e.g., OmniAnomaly ~55×), misleading if full-run time used.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce Table IV for Telemetry: Run all methods through GPU and CPU-1T tiers; verify AUC-PR and time/1k match (allow minor seed variance).  
2. Throughput sweep on CPU-1T: For one deep (e.g., TranAD) and one classical (e.g., HBOS) method, sweep τ from 100 to 100,000 wps; plot coverage and achievable AUC-PR as in Fig. 3.  
3. Inference vs. full-run gap: For OmniAnomaly on SMD, log *tinf* and *te2e* at CPU-1T; compute ratio and confirm it exceeds 20× as in Table V.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Platform translation:** How can M3 Max throughput measurements be accurately transformed into predictions for specific automotive ECUs?  
  *Basis:* Explicitly stated; M3 Max is not a cycle-accurate ECU emulation. *Why unresolved:* ECU microarchitectures differ; no correction factor provided. *Needed evidence:* Run the ECoLAD ladder on representative ECUs, collect hardware counters, and build a performance model mapping platform-independent metrics to throughput.

- **Scaling vs. tuning:** To what extent does per-tier dedicated hyperparameter tuning improve detection quality and could it change method rankings?  
  *Basis:* Acknowledged limitation of mechanical scaling. *Why unresolved:* Optimal per-tier configurations may differ from monotonic scaled ones. *Needed evidence:* For each detector and tier, perform a thorough hyperparameter search (without scaling constraints) and compare best AUC-PR under the same τ constraints to mechanically scaled results.

- **Benchmark diversity:** Do observed degradation patterns (graceful vs. collapse) hold across a broader, more diverse set of TSAD benchmarks?  
  *Basis:* Inferred from limited dataset selection (3 datasets). *Why unresolved:* Many benchmark collections exist with varied anomaly rates, dimensionality, and label semantics. *Needed evidence:* Replicate ECoLAD on a large, diverse corpus of public TSAD benchmarks to test consistency of the classical/deep split.

- **Additional deployment metrics:** How do latency jitter, memory footprint, and calibration stability vary across tiers and detectors, and could they alter suitability rankings?  
  *Basis:* Introduction mentions these constraints but evaluation only reports AUC-PR and throughput. *Why unresolved:* Real-world deployment requires more than average throughput. *Needed evidence:* Extend ECoLAD to collect latency jitter, peak memory, and score stability metrics, then correlate with feasibility and re-rank methods via multi-criteria analysis.

## Limitations
- Proprietary automotive telemetry is inaccessible; public benchmarks differ in characteristics.
- Mechanical scaling assumes monotonic cost reduction; non-linear hardware effects may break proportionality.
- Results from M3 Max may not transfer to typical automotive ECUs without empirical calibration.
- Fixed window length prevents exploring shorter windows that could improve latency.
