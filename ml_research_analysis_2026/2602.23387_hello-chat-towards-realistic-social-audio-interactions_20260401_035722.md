# Hello-Chat: Towards Realistic Social Audio Interactions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23387
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23387
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves state-of-the-art audio translation (8.93), highest conversational MOS (4.19), and perfect instruction-following (100%) on a strict test.

## Executive Summary
Hello-Chat is an end-to-end audio language model designed to overcome the robotic "read-speech" limitation of existing Large Audio Language Models. It leverages a large-scale dataset of real-life conversations with fine-grained acoustic caption annotations to capture paralinguistic features. A modality-interleaved training strategy aligns perception and generation, enabling realistic social audio interactions. The model demonstrates superior performance on multiple benchmarks, including state-of-the-art audio translation and highest subjective naturalness in conversational speech synthesis.

## Method Summary
The core method of Hello-Chat involves three key components: (1) a large-scale dataset of spontaneous conversations annotated with acoustic captions that describe paralinguistic aspects (e.g., tone, pauses), (2) a modality-interleaved training regimen where audio and text tokens are interleaved in the input sequence to learn cross-modal representations, and (3) an end-to-end architecture that jointly processes and generates speech within a unified transformer framework. This approach aims to model the full spectrum of conversational cues, moving beyond synthetic read-speech patterns.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art audio translation (score: 8.93).
- Highest Conversational Mean Opinion Score (4.19) for speech synthesis naturalness, outperforming IndexTTS2 and F5-TTS.
- Perfect instruction-following accuracy (100%) on a strict stress test.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Hello-Chat addresses the robotic speech problem by explicitly modeling paralinguistic features through acoustic captions and aligning audio-text representations via interleaved training. The use of real conversations provides natural prosody patterns, while the end-to-end design ensures that these cues are coherently integrated into generated speech. The interleaved strategy prevents modality bias and encourages the model to attend to both linguistic and acoustic information, resulting in more human-like interactions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Paralinguistic features**: Non-verbal cues (e.g., intonation, laughter) essential for conveying emotion and intent in conversation. *Why needed*: Standard text-only models ignore these, leading to flat speech. *Quick check*: Presence of acoustic caption annotations in training data.  
- **Modality interleaving**: Interleaving audio and text tokens during training to align representations. *Why needed*: Prevents the model from treating audio as an afterthought and encourages joint understanding. *Quick check*: Multimodal transformer architectures that process mixed sequences.  
- **Conversational dataset**: Large-scale collection of spontaneous dialogues. *Why needed*: Provides natural turn-taking, overlaps, and prosodic variations absent in read speech. *Quick check*: Use of real-life recordings vs. scripted corpora.  
- **End-to-end training**: Jointly optimizing perception and generation within a single model. *Why needed*: Reduces error propagation from separate modules and maintains coherence. *Quick check*: Unified transformer processing input tokens to output audio tokens.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Audio Encoder -> Audio Tokens; Text Encoder -> Text Tokens; Interleaver -> Interleaved Token Stream -> Transformer -> Audio Decoder -> Waveform  
**Critical path**: The interleaving step is the pivot that fuses modalities, enabling the transformer to model cross-modal dependencies essential for social cues.  
**Design tradeoffs**: (1) Acoustic caption annotation cost vs. richness of paralinguistic supervision; (2) Interleaving sequence length vs. computational efficiency; (3) Single-stage generation vs. multi-stage refinement for quality.  
**Failure signatures**: (a) Ignoring instruction commands (low following accuracy); (b) Monotonic or overly expressive prosody (abnormal pitch ranges); (c) Inconsistent turn-taking or inappropriate pauses.  
**First experiments**: (1) Evaluate on a single-speaker read speech dataset to check for overfitting to conversational style; (2) Probe instruction following with simple commands lacking paralinguistic content; (3) Measure prosody variation (e.g., pitch jitter) on a held-out conversational set.
