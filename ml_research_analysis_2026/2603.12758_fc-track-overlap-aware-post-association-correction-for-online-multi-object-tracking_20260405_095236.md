# FC-Track: Overlap-Aware Post-Association Correction for Online Multi-Object Tracking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12758
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12758
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** Achieves competitive MOTA/IDF1/HOTA on MOT17/MOT20 while reducing long-term identity switches to 29.55% of all switches.

## Executive Summary
FC-Track is an online multi-object tracking (MOT) framework that addresses identity switches caused by overlapping objects. It introduces a post-association correction module that identifies and locally reassigns mismatched detections within overlapping tracklet pairs. The method uses Intersection over Area (IoA) to filter out unreliable appearance updates and then reapplies appearance similarity to correct errors. Evaluated on MOT17 and MOT20, FC-Track attains high MOTA, IDF1, and HOTA scores while significantly reducing the duration and frequency of identity switches, all without global optimization or re-identification, thus maintaining real-time performance.

## Method Summary
FC-Track augments an online tracker with a post-association correction stage. Standard association (e.g., Hungarian algorithm with motion and appearance cues) produces initial tracklets. After each association step, FC-Track examines pairs of tracklets whose bounding boxes overlap significantly (IoA above a threshold). For such pairs, it suppresses appearance feature updates for the overlapping detections to avoid contamination. Then, within each overlapping pair, it locally reassigns detections based on appearance similarity to resolve mismatches. This correction is applied iteratively, improving temporal consistency. The framework operates online, requiring no future frames or global optimization.

## Key Results
- MOT17: 81.73 MOTA, 82.81 IDF1, 66.95 HOTA at 5.7 FPS.
- MOT20: 77.52 MOTA, 80.90 IDF1, 65.67 HOTA at 0.6 FPS.
- Reduces long-term identity switches to 29.55% of all switches; mean switch duration 18.33 frames, median 3 frames.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FC-Track works because it directly targets the root cause of many identity switches: overlapping objects that cause appearance feature confusion. By filtering updates when IoA is high, it prevents the tracker from learning from corrupted data. The subsequent local reassignment uses appearance similarity within isolated overlapping pairs to correct mismatches without disturbing the rest of the tracking graph. This two-step correction is both computationally efficient and effective, as it only intervenes when overlap is detected and leverages reliable appearance cues in a constrained setting. The online nature ensures that corrections happen promptly, maintaining identity stability over time.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Detection -> Standard Association -> Initial Tracks -> Overlap Detection -> IoA Filtering -> Local Reassignment -> Corrected Tracks

### Critical Path
1. Detections are obtained per frame (e.g., from a detector like YOLO).
2. Standard association (motion + appearance) matches detections to existing tracks.
3. After association, FC-Track identifies tracklet pairs with IoA above a threshold.
4. For each overlapping pair, it suppresses appearance feature updates for the overlapping detections (to avoid corruption).
5. Within the pair, it recomputes assignment using only appearance similarity, locally swapping IDs if beneficial.
6. The corrected tracks are used for the next frame.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Speed vs. Accuracy**: IoA threshold and limited local reassignment keep computation low, but higher thresholds may miss some overlaps; lower thresholds increase cost.
- **Generality**: The method is agnostic to the underlying association algorithm, but performance depends on the quality of initial appearance features.
- **Local vs. Global**: Local reassignment avoids expensive global optimization but may not resolve conflicts that involve more than two overlapping tracks.
- **Feature Update Suppression**: Prevents error propagation but may also hinder learning in genuinely overlapping cases if misapplied.

### Failure Signatures
- **Missed Overlaps**: If IoA threshold is too high, overlapping objects not flagged, leading to uncorrected switches.
- **False Positives**: Too many overlapping pairs flagged, causing unnecessary reassignment that could introduce new errors or increase computational load.
- **Appearance Ambiguity**: Under similar appearances or viewpoint changes, local reassignment may incorrectly swap IDs, especially if motion cues are ignored during reassignment.
- **Track Fragmentation**: Over-cautious suppression may cause tracks to lose updates and fragment.

### First Three Experiments
1. **Baseline Integration**: Replace the association module of a standard online tracker (e.g., DeepSORT) with FC-Track and evaluate on MOT17/MOT20 test sets using public detections, reporting MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, and FPS.
2. **Ablation Study**: Remove the IoA filtering component and/or the local reassignment component separately to quantify their individual contributions to identity switch reduction and overall metrics.
3. **Sensitivity Analysis**: Vary the IoA threshold across a range (e.g., 0.1 to 0.9) and measure impact on performance, especially identity switches and FPS, to determine robustness and optimal operating point.

## Limitations
- IoA threshold selection lacks justification and may be dataset-specific.
- No ablation studies to isolate the effects of IoA filtering and local reassignment.
- The definition and measurement of "long-term identity switches" are non-standard and not compared to prior work.
