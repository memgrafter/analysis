# GenProve: Learning to Generate Text with Fine-Grained Provenance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04932
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04932
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GenProve achieves state-of-the-art performance in generation-time fine-grained provenance with ROUGE-L 57.25, BLEU 42.22, and provenance F1 51.21, significantly outperforming 14 strong LLMs.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces GenProve to combat LLM hallucinations by requiring models to generate answers with structured sentence-level evidence triples (DocID, SentID, Relation: Quotation, Compression, Inference). It presents the ReFInE dataset, expert-annotated for this task, and a two-stage training method: supervised fine-tuning to learn the output format, followed by group relative policy optimization (GRPO) with a composite reward balancing answer fidelity and provenance accuracy. GenProve sets new state-of-the-art on multiple metrics across 14 LLMs. Analysis reveals a persistent "reasoning gap" where models struggle with Inference-type provenance despite excelling at Quotation.

## Method Summary
GenProve employs a two-stage training pipeline on the ReFInE dataset. First, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) teaches the model to produce syntactically valid outputs interleaving answer sentences with [PROVE] tags containing (DocID, SentID, Relation) triples. Second, reinforcement learning with GRPO optimizes a composite reward: R_sim measures answer content similarity via sentence embedding matching and ROUGE-L, while R_prov evaluates provenance correctness through inverse matching and F1 on the triples. This joint optimization reduces hallucinations by ensuring citations semantically support claims. The method is evaluated on long-form QA with automatic metrics and LLM-as-judge protocols.

## Key Results
- GenProve outperforms 14 strong LLMs, achieving state-of-the-art results: ROUGE-L 57.25, BLEU 42.22, provenance F1 51.21.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both reward components; removing R_sim harms answer quality, while removing R_prov reduces provenance accuracy.
- Diagnostic analysis uncovers a reasoning gap: models excel at Quotation but struggle with Inference, indicating verifiable reasoning remains challenging.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** SFT instills structural adherence to the [PROVE] tag format, providing a stable policy for RL.  
**Mechanism:** Minimizing negative log-likelihood of reference outputs forces the model to learn the pattern of sentence followed by structured citation.  
**Core assumption:** Valid output structure is prerequisite for semantic provenance optimization.  
**Evidence anchors:** [section 4.1] SFT enforces structure adherence; [section 5.3] GRPO boosts over SFT baseline.  
**Break condition:** If format validity is low (<95%), RL cannot optimize semantics.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** GRPO with composite reward effectively balances answer fidelity and provenance accuracy, reducing hallucinations.  
**Mechanism:** Group-based reward normalization within composite R = α*R_sim + β*R_prov penalizes unsupported claims.  
**Core assumption:** Joint optimization prevents degenerate outputs favoring one objective.  
**Evidence anchors:** [section 4.2] composite reward design; [section 5.2] high scores on both answer and provenance; [section 5.3] ablations show trade-off.  
**Break condition:** Poor weight tuning (α, β) leads to overfitting one objective.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Supervising Inference relation exposes the reasoning gap, though performance remains weak.  
**Mechanism:** Dataset and reward require distinguishing Quotation, Compression, Inference, forcing multi-sentence integration learning.  
**Core assumption:** Verifiable reasoning is a distinct skill improvable via direct supervision.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] reasoning gap finding; [section 5.4] Inference F1 significantly lower than Quotation; [appendix] Inference definition requires logical support.  
**Break condition:** If Inference F1 does not improve with scale/data, gap may be inherent.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (Q, D) -> SFT Module (learns output schema) -> GRPO Module (samples, computes R_sim/R_prov, updates policy) -> Output (A with interleaved [PROVE] tags)

**Critical path:**  
1. Construct ReFInE dataset (preprocessing, LLM annotation, expert validation).  
2. Train SFT until format validity >99%.  
3. Initialize GRPO from SFT checkpoint; tune α, β, τ_c, τ_p on validation.  
4. Monitor reward components and relation-specific F1 during training.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Token length vs. transparency: [PROVE] tags increase latency.  
- Reward balance (α vs. β): Heavy β risks degenerate high-F1 but low-fluency answers.  
- Granularity: Sentence-level is strict; Compression relation partially addresses multi-sentence support.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Format validity <95%: SFT failed.  
2. Inference F1 <20% of Quotation F1: Model defaults to copying.  
3. High R_prov but low LLM-as-judge: Gaming provenance F1.  
4. Low Recall in R_prov: Incomplete coverage.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **SFT Format Stress Test:** Generate 100 samples from SFT; parse [PROVE] tags. Target >98% valid tuples.  
2. **Reward Component Ablation:** Train three GRPO models: (a) only R_sim, (b) only R_prov, (c) full composite. Evaluate answer quality, overall F1, relation-specific F1. Expect trade-off patterns.  
3. **Relation-type Sensitivity Analysis:** For 50 Inference reference triples, manually inspect top R_prov-high outputs. Categorize errors: wrong relation, wrong localization, hallucinated triple.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can models be trained to improve verifiable reasoning, specifically to close the performance gap on Inference-type provenance?
2. How can generation-time fine-grained provenance be extended to multilingual and cross-lingual settings while maintaining relation-type consistency?
3. What architectural or decoding modifications can enforce tight synchronization between answer sentence generation and provenance triple emission to reduce unsynchronized outputs?
4. To what extent does the distribution of relation types in ReFInE (dominated by Quotation) bias models against learning Compression and Inference, and how can dataset construction be improved?
5. How reliable are LLM-assisted provenance annotations (Stage 2 of ReFInE construction) for capturing nuanced Inference relationships, and what is the residual annotator bias after expert validation?

## Limitations
- Dataset access: ReFInE is not publicly available, requiring reconstruction of the annotation pipeline.
- Implementation gaps: Exact reward model (sentence transformer), GRPO hyperparameters, and data splits are unspecified.
- Scope generalization: Findings are confined to long-form QA in English; performance on other tasks or languages is unknown.

## Confidence
- **High:** SFT provides necessary syntactic stability; GRPO's composite reward balances answer and citation quality (supported by ablations).
- **Medium:** The reasoning gap (Inference vs. Quotation) is documented in ReFInE but may not generalize to other domains.
- **Low:** Claims about universal benefit for verifiable reasoning lack cross-dataset validation.
