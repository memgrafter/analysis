# When Generative Augmentation Hurts: A Benchmark Study of GAN and Diffusion Models for Bias Correction in AI Classification Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16134
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16134
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** FastGAN augmentation worsens bias for ≤20 images, while Stable Diffusion with LoRA improves macro F1 (0.9125 ± 0.0047) and reduces bias gap by 13.1% versus baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper benchmarks generative augmentation (FastGAN and Stable Diffusion with LoRA) to mitigate class imbalance in fine-grained pet breed classification. Using the Oxford-IIIT Pet dataset with artificially limited minority breeds (20 or 50 images), the study finds that FastGAN severely harms bias for the smallest classes due to mode collapse, whereas diffusion-based augmentation reliably boosts performance and fairness. The results suggest a critical sample-size threshold between 20 and 50 images below which GANs become detrimental.

## Method Summary
The experiments use the Oxford-IIIT Pet dataset (37 breeds). An 80/20 stratified split is performed, then 8 minority breeds are subsampled to 20 (3 breeds) or 50 (5 breeds) training images; the remaining 29 breeds serve as majority classes (~155 images each). A ResNet-50 classifier (ImageNet-pretrained) is fine-tuned for 50 epochs under five conditions: baseline real-only, traditional augmentation (to 500 images), FastGAN (500 synthetic images per minority breed), Stable Diffusion 1.5 with LoRA (500 images), and a hybrid mix (250 each). Generative models are trained from scratch or fine-tuned on each minority class using specified hyperparameters. Evaluation uses macro F1, minority/majority accuracy, bias gap (majority minus minority accuracy), FID, and GPU time.

## Key Results
- FastGAN on ≤20-image classes increased bias gap by +20.7% (Cohen's d=5.03, p=0.013) because generated images formed isolated feature clusters outside the real distribution.
- Stable Diffusion with LoRA achieved the highest macro F1 score (0.9125 ± 0.0047) and reduced bias gap by 13.1% relative to baseline.
- The data indicate a sample-size threshold between 20 and 50 images per class below which GAN-based augmentation becomes harmful in this setting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FastGAN's failure for very small datasets stems from mode collapse: the generator produces a limited set of outputs that fail to capture the full data distribution, creating synthetic outliers that lie far from real images in feature space. When these are added to training, the classifier learns spurious patterns for the minority classes, worsening bias. Stable Diffusion, as a diffusion model, inherently models complex multimodality; LoRA fine-tuning efficiently adapts it to the few minority examples without catastrophic forgetting, preserving diversity and producing high-fidelity samples that align with the real distribution. This helps the classifier generalize better across all classes and reduces bias.

- **Class imbalance**: A distribution skew where some classes have fewer samples; causes models to be biased toward majority classes. *Why needed*: The paper targets bias correction in imbalance. *Quick check*: How would you define bias gap?

- **GAN (Generative Adversarial Network)**: A generative model where a generator creates samples and a discriminator tries to distinguish them from real data. *Why needed*: FastGAN is one of the benchmarked generative approaches. *Quick check*: What is mode collapse, and why does it happen in GANs?
- **Diffusion models**: Generative models that iteratively denoise data from pure noise. *Why needed*: Stable Diffusion is the other benchmarked approach. *Quick check*: How does a diffusion model differ conceptually from a GAN?
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**: A parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that freezes the original model and injects trainable low-rank matrices. *Why needed*: Enables adapting large diffusion models with limited data. *Quick check*: What advantage does LoRA have over full fine-tuning for small datasets?
- **FID (Fréchet Inception Distance)**: Measures similarity between real and generated image distributions using Inception-v3 features. *Why needed*: Quantifies fidelity of synthetic data. *Quick check*: What does a lower FID value mean?

## Architecture Onboarding
The experimental pipeline comprises these sequential components:
Dataset (Oxford-IIIT Pet) → Stratified split → Minority subsampling (20/50) → Generative model training (FastGAN or SD+LoRA per class) → Synthetic image generation → Dataset augmentation (mix real + synthetic) → ResNet-50 fine-tuning → Metric computation (macro F1, bias gap, FID). The critical path flows through synthetic data generation and classifier training; poor-quality samples cascade into biased evaluation metrics.  
**Design tradeoffs**: FastGAN is computationally light and fast to train but highly susceptible to mode collapse on small data. Stable Diffusion yields superior sample quality and diversity but demands significant GPU memory and time. LoRA offers a middle ground for efficient adaptation but risks overfitting if training proceeds too long on scarce data.  
**Failure signatures**: FastGAN: extremely low within-class diversity, FID >200, t-SNE visualizations show synthetic clusters separate from real data; classifier minority accuracy drops versus baseline. SD+LoRA overfitting: FID very low (<50) but generated images are near-duplicates, repeated patterns evident; classifier may overfit to synthetic artifacts, degrading real test performance.  
**First three experiments** to reproduce core findings:  
1. Train classifier on imbalanced real data only (baseline) and record macro F1 and bias gap.  
2. Add FastGAN-generated images (500 per minority class) and compare metrics; observe increased bias gap for ≤20-image classes.  
3. Add Stable Diffusion LoRA-generated images (500 per minority class) and verify improved macro F1 and reduced bias gap versus baseline and FastGAN.

## Limitations
- Exact train/test split seed and Stable Diffusion prompt formatting are unspecified, causing reproducibility variability.
- The sample-size threshold (20–50 images) lacks intermediate data points, making its precise location ambiguous.
- Some results (e.g., SD+LoRA bias reduction percentage) lack statistical significance testing, limiting confidence.

## Confidence
- **FastGAN harm for ≤20 images:** Medium (supported by p=0.013 but only three minority breeds at that size).  
- **SD+LoRA macro F1 improvement:** Medium (consistent across three random seeds but no statistical test reported).  
- **Existence of a sample-size threshold:** Low-Medium (inferred from two sample-size conditions without interpolation).
