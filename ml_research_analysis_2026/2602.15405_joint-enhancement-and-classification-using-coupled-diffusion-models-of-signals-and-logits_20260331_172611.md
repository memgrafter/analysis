# Joint Enhancement and Classification using Coupled Diffusion Models of Signals and Logits

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15405  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15405  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Coupled diffusion methods achieve up to 83.0% accuracy on noisy CIFAR-100 and reduce WER to 3.83% on EARS-Reverb, substantially outperforming separate enhancement-classification pipelines.

## Executive Summary
Robust classification under noise typically suffers when enhancement and classification are separate stages, because enhancement metrics (e.g., SNR, perceptual quality) do not align with the classifier's accuracy objectives and can introduce harmful artifacts. This paper proposes a framework that couples two diffusion models—one for the corrupted input signal and one for the frozen classifier's logits—enabling mutual guidance without retraining the classifier. Three interaction schedules (Parallel, Alternating, Nested) are introduced to coordinate the diffusion processes. Experiments on image classification and automatic speech recognition show consistent and significant improvements over both noisy inputs and separately enhanced solutions across diverse noise conditions.

## Method Summary
The method jointly trains a signal denoiser (e.g., a U-Net for images, SGMSE for audio) and a logits denoiser (e.g., MLP/ResNet) via a noise-prediction objective. Both denoisers are conditioned on the other modality's current estimate (e.g., the signal denoiser receives an intermediate logit estimate, and vice versa). The classifier remains frozen; its clean logits on original data provide training targets for the logits diffusion. During inference, a scheduler orchestrates the interaction: Parallel interleaves single steps, Alternating runs full trajectories alternately, and Nested embeds a full signal diffusion inside each logit step. This mutual guidance iteratively refines both signal and logits toward a joint solution that favors classification accuracy.

## Key Results
- On CIFAR-100 with 30% Gaussian noise, coupled methods reach up to 83.0% accuracy, far exceeding Noisy (10.9%) and Enhanced (60.4%) baselines.
- For ASR with Whisper-base on EARS-Reverb, the Nested strategy reduces WER to 3.83% versus 5.79% (Noisy) and 4.48% (Enhanced).
- All coupled strategies (Parallel, Alternating, Nested) consistently outperform baselines across image and speech benchmarks under various noise types.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Misalignment of Separate Enhancement
**Claim:** Decoupling enhancement and classification misaligns objectives because enhancement metrics (e.g., MSE, SNR) do not correlate with classification accuracy, and aggressive denoising can introduce artifacts harmful to classifiers.
**Mechanism:** The classifier is trained on clean data; a separate enhancement module optimized for signal fidelity may reconstruct features that are perceptually plausible but semantically misleading for the classifier, especially under noise. The downstream task defines the true objective; signal-level reconstruction loss is an imperfect surrogate.
**Break condition:** If the enhancement metric perfectly correlates with classification accuracy (e.g., for simple linear classifiers on certain noise types), coupling may offer less benefit.

### Mechanism 2: Mutual Guidance via Joint Diffusion
**Claim:** Mutual guidance between signal and logit diffusion processes enables iterative refinement toward classification-relevant reconstructions, as the signal provides semantic features and the logits impose discriminative structure.
**Mechanism:** Two coupled diffusion models condition on each other's estimates (ˆx0, ˆy0). The signal diffusion is guided toward regions of the data manifold that yield consistent classifier logits, while the logit diffusion is guided by signal features that support those logits, creating a feedback loop that converges to joint solutions favorable for classification.
**Break condition:** If the classifier's logits are highly uninformative (e.g., extremely overconfident on wrong predictions) or the diffusion models are too weak to model the joint distribution, mutual guidance could propagate errors.

### Mechanism 3: Interaction Schedule Controls Coupling
**Claim:** The interaction schedule (Parallel, Alternating, Nested) controls the strength and stability of mutual guidance by determining when and how often each modality conditions on the other's estimate.
**Mechanism:** 
- Parallel: Interleaved step-wise conditioning; frequent updates but relies on noisy intermediate estimates.
- Alternating: Full-trajectory conditioning; more stable but slower information flow.
- Nested: Hierarchical conditioning; logit steps use full inner signal trajectories for high-fidelity guidance, maximizing semantic alignment at high compute cost.
**Break condition:** If compute budget is severely constrained, Parallel may be the only viable option; if training data is very limited, Nested may overfit.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Diffusion models (DDPM/SDE)**
  - *Why needed here:* The framework builds on modeling reverse-time transitions via learned denoisers; understanding the noise prediction objective, forward/backward processes, and posterior q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x0) is essential.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the theoretical justification for training a neural network to predict diffusion noise ϵ instead of directly predicting x0?

- **Concept: Classifier logits as continuous targets**
  - *Why needed here:* The method treats the classifier's pre-softmax output as a continuous vector to denoise, enabling gradient-based diffusion in semantic space rather than discrete labels.
  - *Quick check question:* Why not denoise the one-hot label or the softmax probability? What property of logits makes them suitable for diffusion?

- **Concept: Conditional generation and guidance**
  - *Why needed here:* Both signal and logit denoisers must effectively incorporate conditioning information (corrupted signal, opposite modality's estimate) to steer generation toward task-relevant samples.
  - *Quick check question:* In the training algorithms, how is cross-modal conditioning simulated during noise prediction training to match inference dynamics?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Frozen Classifier (fϕ) -> Clean logits (y0) & Corrupted logits (y_cor); Signal Denoiser (θ_x) & Logits Denoiser (θ_y) mutually conditioned; Scheduler (Parallel/Alternating/Nested) orchestrates steps.
- **Critical path:** Initialize x_T ~ N(0,I), y_T ~ N(0,I). At each scheduler action: predict ˆx0 or ˆy0 via denoiser conditioned on current states and cross-modal estimate; sample x_{t-1} or y_{t-1} from Gaussian posterior q(·|x_t, ˆx0) or q(·|y_t, ˆy0). Return y_0 after full trajectory.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Coupling strength vs compute:* Nested > Alternating > Parallel in guidance quality, but also in NFE. Parallel fastest but may use noisy intermediate conditioning.
  - *Training stability:* Staged warm-start (pretrain each modality separately) mitigates divergence from premature cross-conditioning.
  - *Modality gap:* Audio uses SGMSE (continuous SDE, complex STFT) while images use DDPM; conditioning injection differs (FiLM, cross-attention).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Training: Loss divergence if cross-modal conditioning introduced before single-modality denoisers are competent.
  - Inference (Parallel): Early-time guidance from noisy ˆy0 or ˆx0 causes semantic drift; mitigate by warming up (conditioning only later).
  - General: Signal denoiser overfits to x_cor, ignoring logit guidance; logit denoiser fails to extract useful signal features.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train signal denoiser alone with x_cor conditioning; verify reconstruction quality but poor classification on noisy inputs.
  2. Train logits-only denoiser conditioned only on fixed x_cor; confirm poor robustness at high noise.
  3. Implement Parallel coupling with T=10 steps; compare accuracy vs. Enhanced baseline on a small noisy dataset (e.g., MNIST 30% GN) to validate mutual guidance.

## Limitations
- Exact training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, warm-start epochs) are undisclosed, hindering reproducibility.
- No direct evidence correlating enhancement metrics (PSNR/SSIM) with classification accuracy to quantify misalignment.
- Generalization beyond tested image/speech tasks and ResNet/Whisper classifiers remains unstudied; computational cost of Nested/Alternating is high.

## Confidence
- Coupled methods outperform baselines: **High**
- Decoupling misaligns objectives: **Medium**
- Mutual guidance improves reconstructions: **High**
- Interaction schedule effects: **Medium**
- Generalizability: **Low**
