# Beyond the Answer: Decoding the Behavior of LLMs as Scientific Reasoners

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28038
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28038
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Prompt optimization via GEPA yields near-perfect performance on scientific benchmarks for the target model (100% algebra, 94.44% GPQA) but the optimized strategies fail to generalize to other LLM architectures.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how prompting shapes the reasoning behavior of large language models on scientific tasks. Using a custom Genetic Pareto (GEPA) algorithm, the authors automatically optimized prompts for two benchmarks: formal algebra (Lean) and graduate-level science (GPQA). Optimized prompts dramatically improved performance for the model used in the optimization loop (DeepSeek-V32), achieving near-perfect scores. However, these gains did not transfer to other models (GPT-5.4-mini, GLM 5, Claude Sonnet 4.6), where simpler, hand-crafted chain-of-thought prompts were often equally or more effective. Analysis revealed that successful prompts evolved to become longer and embed highly specific, model-dependent heuristics—termed "local" logic. The core finding is that effective reasoning strategies in current LLMs are not universal but are brittle, architecture-specific protocols that require precise prompting to elicit, posing challenges for interpretability and safe future AGI collaboration.

## Method Summary
The study employs a custom automated prompt engineering framework called Genetic Pareto (GEPA). GEPA iteratively evolves a population of prompts using an evolutionary loop: a prompt is evaluated on a small sample of problems from the Lean (formal algebra) and GPQA (graduate science) benchmarks; failures are critiqued by an LLM; this critique guides the evolution of a new prompt candidate. The process uses Pareto dominance to maintain a set of non-dominated prompts, balancing performance and potentially other objectives. The target model for optimization was DeepSeek-V3.2. The final optimized prompts were then evaluated not only on DeepSeek but also on three other models (GPT-5.4-mini, GLM 5, Claude Sonnet 4.6) to test transferability. For the Lean benchmark, correctness was determined by formal verification in Lean 4; for GPQA, by answer matching.

## Key Results
- GEPA-optimized prompts for DeepSeek-V3.2 achieved 100% accuracy on the Lean algebra benchmark and 94.44% on GPQA, substantially outperforming simple hand-crafted prompts.
- These optimized prompts showed **severe brittleness**: when tested on other models (GPT-5.4-mini, GLM 5, Claude Sonnet 4.6), they rarely performed best and often underperformed compared to standard hand-crafted chain-of-thought prompts.
- Prompt evolution led to significantly longer prompts that embedded domain-specific knowledge (e.g., specific Lean lemmas like `Eisenstein’s Criterion`) and warnings against common pitfalls, representing a shift from generic instructions to model-specific "coaching."

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Prompt optimization via GEPA discovers and embeds domain-specific heuristics necessary to elicit high-level reasoning from the target model.  
**Mechanism:** The evolutionary loop iteratively critiques failed reasoning, generating new prompts that add corrective instructions and strategic knowledge (e.g., specific proof tactics, structuring tricks). This transforms the prompt from a generic "what to do" into a model-specific "how to do it" coaching script.  
**Evidence:** Successful prompts evolve to be longer and include highly specific, model-dependent heuristics and domain knowledge ("local" logic). Algebra prompts eventually reference strategies like Eisenstein’s Criterion and explicitly warn against common pitfalls.  
**Break condition:** If the model's internal reasoning pathways are fundamentally inaccessible via natural language prompting, or if optimization converges on spurious correlations in the evaluation set.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The extracted reasoning strategies ("local logic") are brittle and architecture-specific, failing to transfer to other models.  
**Mechanism:** GEPA, using one model as optimizer/grader, evolves prompts that exploit idiosyncrasies unique to that architecture (e.g., token distribution, training data remnants, RLHF preferences). Other models lack the corresponding internal circuits to recognize and act on these finely-tuned instructions.  
**Evidence:** Optimized prompts for DeepSeek-V3.2 yield maximal scores for that model but perform poorly on GPT-5.4-mini, GLM 5, and Claude Sonnet 4.6, where hand-crafted CoT often matches or exceeds them.  
**Break condition:** If future architectures converge on more universal reasoning primitives or if prompt optimization finds truly model-agnostic strategies.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Achieving peak performance on complex scientific reasoning requires substantial prompt scaffolding; minimal prompting yields suboptimal reasoning.  
**Mechanism:** The next-token prediction training objective does not incentivize spontaneous generation of rigorous, verifiable reasoning for novel formal tasks. Explicit, lengthy prompting provides the cognitive framework and constraints to channel the model's generative capacity into a correct logical structure.  
**Evidence:** Optimized prompts incorporate extensive domain knowledge and best practices, including sections on handling false statements and tactical warnings. Even hand-crafted CoT baselines outperform simple prompts, showing baseline prompting matters.  
**Break condition:** If models are trained with objectives that directly reward correct reasoning traces (e.g., process-based reward models) to the point where minimal prompting suffices.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
GEPA Loop Controller -> Sample Problems -> Evaluator Module (Lean Verify / Check Answer) -> Accuracy Vector -> Pareto Set Update -> Gather Errors -> LLM Critic -> Critique -> LLM Evolver -> New Prompt -> Population Update -> Next Iteration.  
Model Interface abstraction sits between GEPA and the target LLM (DeepSeek for optimization; others for transfer tests).

**Critical path:**  
1. Initialize with seed prompt.  
2. Sample a batch of problems.  
3. Evaluate current prompt(s) on batch to get success/failure vector.  
4. Update Pareto set of non-dominated prompts.  
5. Gather errors from non-Pareto prompts.  
6. Generate natural language critique of these errors.  
7. Evolve a new prompt from a sampled Pareto prompt + critique.  
8. Add new prompt to population and repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Generality vs. Peak Performance:** Optimization explicitly trades generalizability for maximum score on the optimizer model.  
- **Cost vs. Insight:** Many LLM calls (evaluation, critique, evolution) are required; financial/compute cost is exchanged for extracting detailed, model-specific reasoning protocols.  
- **Automation vs. Human Legibility:** Evolved prompts become long, complex, and potentially "overfitted," effective but inscrutable to humans.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Non-transferability:** Optimized prompt for Model A yields no gain or negative gain on Model B.  
- **Prompt Drift without Gain:** Prompt length and embedding shift but validation accuracy plateaus/declines, suggesting exploitation of spurious patterns.  
- **Critique Degeneration:** The LLM Critic generates vague/repetitive feedback, causing evolutionary stagnation.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate core finding: Run GEPA with DeepSeek-V3.2 on a held-out slice of the Algebra benchmark; confirm final Pareto prompt outperforms hand-crafted CoT *on that model* and parse it for domain-specific elements.  
2. Quantify transfer brittleness: Take top 5 prompts from DeepSeek's final Pareto set; test each on all other models on the same held-out problems; calculate performance drop relative to DeepSeek.  
3. Ablate prompt components: For DeepSeek's best prompt, systematically remove major sections (e.g., "Domain-Specific Strategies," specific lemma names); re-evaluate to identify necessary components for peak performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What are the invariant "reasoning primitives" across LLM architectures?** Future research should work on identifying logical structures invariant across models; current findings suggest reasoning may manifest as model-specific heuristics.
- **Can we build automated tools to decode the reasoning of advanced LLMs when it surpasses human intuition?** If AGI develops logic humans cannot understand, we need automated tools to keep these systems interpretable.
- **What architectural or training factors cause the brittleness of optimized prompts across models?** The paper shows brittleness but does not investigate root causes (e.g., pretraining data, architecture, objectives).
- **How generalizable are optimized reasoning prompts across a wider spectrum of models, tasks, and domains?** Limitations include only two benchmarks and four models; a broader experimental scope is needed to confirm hypotheses.

## Limitations
- Exact GEPA hyperparameters (iterations, sample sizes, Pareto rules) and LLM prompts for critique/evolution are undisclosed, blocking faithful replication.
- Access to three of the four models (GPT-5.4-mini, GLM 5, Claude Sonnet 4.6) may be restricted, and GPQA evaluation prompts are missing.
- Transfer tests are limited to only three alternative models, so the observed brittleness might be over-fitted to a narrow set of architectures.

## Confidence
- **High:** GEPA optimization can substantially improve the target model's performance by embedding domain-specific heuristics (validated by near-perfect scores).
- **Medium:** The resulting reasoning strategies are brittle and fail to transfer to other models; evidence is from a small set of alternatives, so broader generalizability is uncertain.
- **Low:** Extensive prompt scaffolding is an inherent limitation of LLM reasoning; this may be an artifact of current training objectives and could diminish with future process-based rewards.

## Next Checks
