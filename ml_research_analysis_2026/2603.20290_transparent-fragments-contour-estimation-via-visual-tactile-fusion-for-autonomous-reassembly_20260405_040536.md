# Transparent Fragments Contour Estimation via Visual-Tactile Fusion for Autonomous Reassembly

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20290
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20290
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Visual‑tactile fusion framework achieves real‑world reassembly alignment qualities of 93.8% (partial) and 96.7% (complete), with 85% Top‑1 accuracy in multi‑material scenes.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenging problem of contour estimation for transparent fragment reassembly, where refractive distortion, low contrast, and missing depth render standard RGB‑D vision ineffective. The authors propose a visual‑tactile fusion framework that integrates a detection network (TransFragNet), GelSight Mini tactile reconstruction, a fusion material classifier, and a multi‑cue contour matcher. Trained on the TransFrag27K dataset, the system attains high segmentation IoU (up to 0.938 under varying backgrounds) and demonstrates strong real‑world reassembly performance. The work establishes that tactile sensing can reliably complement vision for transparent objects, enabling autonomous reassembly across diverse materials.

Key contributions include (1) a dual‑path decoder with deformable upsampling and anti‑aliased branches to precisely segment irregular boundaries; (2) a photometric stereo pipeline coupled with a frequency‑domain Poisson solver to recover subpixel tactile height maps; (3) a late‑fusion material classifier that mitigates data imbalance; and (4) a contour matching scheme that combines edge, gradient, and height cues with a fallback mechanism. Ablations and evaluations under varying conditions validate the design and set a new benchmark for the task.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of four main components. First, the TransFrag27K dataset provides 27 000 synthetic RGB‑mask pairs (plus real samples) for training. Second, TransFragNet—built on a Swin Transformer backbone and a dual‑path decoder—segments transparent fragments and identifies optimal grasp points. Third, tactile information is captured with a two‑finger gripper equipped with GelSight Mini sensors. Raw tactile images undergo U‑Net contact segmentation, photometric stereo for gradient estimation, and a DCT‑based Poisson integration to yield high‑resolution height maps and edge masks. Fourth, a visual‑tactile fusion classifier (ResNet‑18 visual branch, lightweight CNN tactile branch) classifies materials to restrict matching in multi‑material scenes. Finally, contour matching fuses three cues—Chamfer edge distance, gradient histogram alignment, and height extrema complementarity—and triggers tactile re‑sampling plus material‑based filtering when visual confidence is low.

## Key Results
- **Segmentation:** Mask IoU of 0.8856 (synthetic), 0.9382 (varying backgrounds), 0.8451 (varying illumination).
- **Reassembly:** Alignment quality 93.8% (partial fracture) and 96.7% (complete reassembly) on real objects.
- **Multi‑material matching:** Top‑1 accuracy of 85% in mixed‑material scenes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** TransFragNet's dual‑path decoder with deformable transposed convolution and anti‑aliased upsampling enables accurate segmentation of low‑contrast, irregular transparent fragment boundaries.
- **Mechanism:** The Swin Transformer backbone extracts multi‑scale features with global context. The dual‑path decoder fuses outputs from a deformable branch (adapts sampling to jagged contours) and an anti‑aliased branch (suppresses jagged edges), followed by an attention‑based edge‑sharpening module. This combination preserves thin, irregular edges while remaining robust to refractive distortions.
- **Core assumption:** Global context from transformers and localized edge refinement from the dual‑path design are both necessary; neither a standard CNN decoder nor a single‑path transformer decoder suffices for highly ambiguous fragment edges.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract]: "proposes a visual grasping position detection network named TransFragNet to identify, locate and segment the sampling grasping position."  
  [section IV‑A]: Detailed architecture with Swin backbone, dual‑path decoder, and edge‑sharpening module. Reports mask IoU of 0.8856 (synthetic), 0.9382 (varying backgrounds), 0.8451 (varying illumination).  
  [corpus]: Neighbor papers focus on molecular fragments or document fragments; no direct architectural parallels for transparent object segmentation.
- **Break condition:** Fragments with extreme curvature (e.g., tight curves, sharp corners) or highly ambiguous boundaries under specular reflection may still yield incomplete masks (Section V‑H).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** GelSight Mini‑based tactile reconstruction recovers fine‑scale edge geometry and height maps that are invisible to RGB‑D sensors, providing high‑fidelity local contour details.
- **Mechanism:** Raw GelSight images are processed: (1) A U‑Net segments the contact region (IoU 0.967). (2) Photometric stereo solves for surface normals from RGB channels under known light directions. (3) A frequency‑domain Poisson solver (using DCT) integrates the gradient field into a height map with Neumann boundary conditions, yielding subpixel accuracy.
- **Core assumption:** The elastomeric gel's deformation under contact reliably encodes the fragment's local surface geometry, and the photometric stereo + Poisson pipeline can reconstruct it from the GelSight's patterned illumination.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract]: "use a two‑finger gripper with Gelsight Mini sensors to obtain reconstructed tactile information of the lateral edge of the fragments."  
  [section IV‑B]: Full pipeline description with equations (3)–(6), U‑Net architecture (Fig. 9), and reconstruction results (Fig. 8).  
  [corpus]: One neighbor (ConViTac) explores visual‑tactile fusion but not tactile reconstruction for transparent objects; no direct evidence for the Poisson‑based approach.
- **Break condition:** Slippery, extremely compliant, or highly polished surfaces may not induce sufficient, consistent gel deformation, corrupting the normal estimation and height reconstruction.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Late fusion of visual (ResNet‑18) and tactile (lightweight CNN) features in a classifier improves material classification robustness for transparent fragments, where visual cues are distorted.
- **Mechanism:** Visual branch extracts global context (shape, color) from the fragment's RGB mask. Tactile branch extracts local texture/roughness from the reconstructed tactile height map. Features are concatenated and passed through a dropout‑regularized MLP. The fusion is "visual‑dominant" because tactile data is scarcer (100 vs 300 samples).
- **Core assumption:** Material classification benefits from complementary cues: vision provides holistic appearance; tactile provides surface micro‑texture imperceptible to vision due to refraction. Late fusion prevents tactile noise from dominating the limited tactile training set.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract]: "By fusing this tactile information with visual cues, a visual–tactile fusion material classifier is proposed."  
  [section IV‑C]: Dual‑branch architecture (Fig. 10), augmentation strategies per modality, and classification loss. Implicit evidence: material classification is used to restrict matching in mixed scenes (Experiment 6).  
  [corpus]: ConViTac aligns visual‑tactile representations contrastively but does not address transparent fragment material classification; limited direct support.
- **Break condition:** When tactile contact is partial or off‑edge, the tactile features become unrepresentative, potentially misleading the classifier. The visual‑dominant strategy may still fail if visual distortion is extreme (e.g., heavy refraction on a colored crystal).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input: RGB‑D camera image → TransFragNet (segmentation + grasp points) → Visual mask.  
  Tactile loop: Robot positions gripper (DH‑95 + GelSight Mini) at grasp points → Raw tactile image → U‑Net contact mask → Photometric stereo → Gradient field → DCT Poisson → Height map + edge mask.  
  Fusion: Visual mask + tactile edge → Contour fusion → Database indexing (mask IoU + Chamfer) → If ambiguous (Δscore <5% or IoU <0.70), trigger tactile re‑sampling + material classification (dual‑branch net) → Candidate restriction to same material.  
  Matching: For each candidate pair, compute: sedge (Chamfer on edge points), Sgrad (orientation histogram alignment), Sheight (peak‑trough complementarity) → weighted sum S(T) → highest score → reassembly.

- **Critical path:** Visual detection → Grasp point extraction → Tactile sampling & reconstruction → Fusion/classification → Database match → Fallback (if needed) → Final contour matching → Reassembly. The most critical integration point is the database indexing; a poor visual mask IoU triggers fallback tactile sampling.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Visual vs Tactile reliance:** Visual detection is fast and global but unreliable on transparent fragments; tactile is precise but local and slower. The system defaults to visual‑first, fallback to tactile.  
  - **Dataset diversity vs real‑world gap:** TransFrag27K uses randomized Blender scenes (backgrounds, lighting, fracture) for scale, but may not capture all real‑world material optics or fracture patterns.  
  - **Late vs early fusion:** Late fusion (material classifier) avoids tactile noise dominating the visual‑dominant network given limited tactile data, but discards potentially useful early spatial alignment cues.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - TransFragNet mask IoU < 0.80 on real data (indicating poor segmentation).  
  - Fallback triggered repeatedly (>30% of fragments) in mixed scenes, suggesting database lacks diversity or tactile reconstruction fails on certain materials.  
  - High curvature fragments (vases, goblets) yield low sedge scores even after fusion (Section V‑H limitation).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Experiment 1 (Synthetic detection):** Trains/validates TransFragNet on TransFrag27K. Confirms architecture outperforms EfficientNet/MobileNet/TGCNN on mask IoU (Table I). *Engineer task:* Run inference on provided synthetic test set; expect IoU >0.88.  
  2. **Experiment 2 (Real backgrounds):** Tests TransFragNet on 100 real images with 8 varied backgrounds. Validates generalization to unseen textures/reflections. *Engineer task:* Capture 5 fragments on each of 3 new backgrounds (e.g., wood, metal mesh) and measure IoU drop vs. Table I.  
  3. **Experiment 4 (Partial fracture reassembly):** Vision‑only matching using SAM to extract gap from intact body. Validates contour matching without tactile data for large, nearly complete objects. *Engineer task:* For a notched beaker, run the centroid alignment + rotation sweep pipeline (Section IV‑E); expect IoU >0.74 (Table II).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the visual‑tactile fusion framework be extended to accurately estimate contours for transparent fragments with high curvature or complex intersections (e.g., goblets, sharp corners)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The Limitations section states that performance may degrade for fragments with high curvature or complex intersections, and that expanding the benchmark to cover more diverse fragment shapes is an important next step.
- **Why unresolved:** Current TransFragNet and tactile sampling strategies are optimized for relatively flat fragments; sharp curves and thin edges cause ambiguities in visual mask extraction and incomplete tactile contact.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate on a new dataset of high‑curvature fragments, measuring segmentation IoU and reassembly alignment quality; develop curvature‑aware post‑processing for masks and adaptive tactile probing along curved edges.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Is the longest‑chord‑based tactile sampling strategy optimal for capturing discriminative edge features across all fragment shapes, particularly irregular fragments with concavities or multiple protrusions?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section IV‑A selects sampling points as endpoints of the longest chord to "avoid concavity traps," but acknowledges that concavity traps and edge ambiguities still occur in cluttered scenes.
- **Why unresolved:** The longest chord may not intersect the fracture edges most representative for matching, especially for fragments where the longest chord lies along an intact facet rather than a broken edge.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare contour matching accuracy using longest chord sampling against alternative strategies (e.g., edge saliency, curvature‑based, or farthest point sampling) on a diverse shape set; analyze which sampling points yield the most complementary tactile height maps for matching.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the visual‑dominant fusion strategy in material classification introduce biases that reduce accuracy for materials with subtle visual differences but distinct tactile textures (e.g., frosted vs. clear glass), and can tactile‑only classification be improved with limited data?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section IV‑C notes a "substantial difference in data availability between modalities" (300 visual vs. 100 tactile samples) and adopts visual‑dominant fusion without per‑material performance analysis.
- **Why unresolved:** With only 100 tactile samples per class, the tactile branch may underfit, potentially causing misclassification for visually similar but tactilely distinct materials; the fusion strategy is not validated per material category.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform an ablation study with balanced visual‑tactile data and report per‑class accuracy; collect additional tactile samples for underrepresented materials; explore few‑shot or tactile‑specific data augmentation techniques.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Are the heuristic fallback thresholds (±5% score difference, IoU < 0.70) for triggering tactile re‑sampling optimal, or can they be learned to maximize reassembly success across diverse fragment sets?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section IV‑E defines fallback conditions based on score differences and IoU thresholds but does not justify their values or explore alternatives.
- **Why unresolved:** These thresholds are arbitrarily chosen and may not generalize to scenes with different fragment densities, material mixtures, or fragmentation levels; suboptimal thresholds could cause unnecessary re‑sampling or missed recoveries.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a sensitivity analysis varying the thresholds on multi‑material and cluttered scenes; develop a learned policy (e.g., via confidence calibration or reinforcement learning) that decides when to sample based on matching uncertainty metrics.

## Limitations
- Exact TransFragNet architecture details (Swin variant, deformable transposed convolution, anti‑aliased upsampling) and the precise matching weights/fallback thresholds are undisclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Calibration parameters for the GelSight Mini (light directions, gel properties) are absent, which may limit tactile reconstruction fidelity on slippery or highly polished surfaces.
- Break conditions indicate failures on extreme curvature, specular highlights, and partial tactile contacts, revealing limited robustness for certain fragment geometries and material appearances.
