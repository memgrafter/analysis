# Probabilistic Wind Power Forecasting with Tree-Based Machine Learning and Weather Ensembles
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13010
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13010
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Tree-based machine learning with a weather ensemble reduces errors versus physics-based models, with conditional diffusion (Treeffuser) achieving the best probabilistic performance (normalized MAE 8.0%, CRPS 5.6%).

## Executive Summary
Accurate day-ahead probabilistic wind power forecasts are critical for grid integration but are degraded by errors in weather predictions, spatial mismatches between forecast and farm locations, and turbine wake effects. This study evaluates three tree-based probabilistic machine learning methods—conformalized quantile regression (CQR), natural gradient boosting (NGBoost), and conditional diffusion models (Treeffuser)—using an ensemble of numerical weather forecasts from five providers. Validated on four years of data from all Belgian offshore wind farms, machine learning significantly reduces mean absolute error compared to traditional power curves and a calibrated wake model. The weather ensemble consistently improves point forecast accuracy across all ML methods, with Treeffuser delivering the best overall probabilistic calibration and sharpness.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct a comparative empirical study on four years of historical data from Belgian offshore wind farms. The input features are derived from an ensemble of five numerical weather prediction (NWP) providers, processed to match farm locations. Three tree-based probabilistic regression methods are trained to predict the distribution of next-day total power output: (1) CQR applies quantile regression with conformal calibration for distribution-free uncertainty, (2) NGBoost fits a flexible parametric distribution (typically Gaussian) via gradient boosting, and (3) Treeffuser uses a conditional diffusion model to generate samples from the predictive distribution. Performance is evaluated using deterministic metrics (MAE) and probabilistic scores (CRPS) against baselines including manufacturer power curves and a physics-based wake model.

## Key Results
- Machine learning (ML) methods reduce normalized MAE by up to 53% vs. power curves and 33% vs. a calibrated wake model.
- The five-provider weather ensemble improves point forecast accuracy by up to 23% compared to a single NWP source.
- Treeffuser achieves the best probabilistic performance (normalized MAE 8.0% of capacity, CRPS 5.6%), outperforming NGBoost (MAE ~8.5%, CRPS 6.0%), which degrades at cut-in and above-rated wind speeds due to its Gaussian assumption.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The weather ensemble mitigates systematic biases and spatial displacement errors inherent in any single NWP provider, providing a more robust representation of atmospheric uncertainty. Tree-based ML models capture complex, non-linear relationships between meteorological features and farm-level power output, including aggregated wake effects, without requiring explicit physical formulations. Treeffuser's diffusion-based approach avoids restrictive parametric assumptions (e.g., Gaussianity), better modeling the multi-modal and heteroscedastic nature of wind power distributions, especially at transition wind speeds.

## Foundational Learning
- **Probabilistic Forecasting (CRPS, Quantiles):** Needed to evaluate and produce full predictive distributions, not just point estimates. *Quick check:* Can the model generate calibrated prediction intervals that achieve nominal coverage?
- **Wake Modeling:** Physics-based simulation of turbine interference affecting downstream power. *Quick check:* Does the ML model implicitly learn wake effects from data, and how does its accuracy compare to a calibrated physical wake model?
- **Weather Ensemble (Multi-Model NWP):** Combining forecasts from multiple providers to reduce model-specific error. *Quick check:* How does performance scale with the number of NWP sources, and is there a saturation point?
- **Natural Gradient Boosting (NGBoost):** Boosting algorithm that fits a parametric distribution by optimizing natural gradients. *Quick check:* Does the assumed distribution (e.g., Gaussian) match the empirical residual shape, particularly at distribution tails?
- **Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR):** Distribution-free method that applies conformal calibration to any quantile regressor. *Quick check:* Is the calibration set independent from training to guarantee finite-sample coverage?
- **Conditional Diffusion Models (Treeffuser):** Generative model that learns to denoise a corrupted version of the target conditioned on input features. *Quick check:* Is the sampling process computationally efficient enough for operational day-ahead scheduling?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map: Raw NWP Ensembles + Historical Power -> Feature Engineering -> Model Training (CQR / NGBoost / Treeffuser) -> Probabilistic Forecast Evaluation

Critical Path: Data ingestion (5 NWP providers + SCADA) -> spatial/temporal alignment to farm centroids -> feature extraction (wind speed/direction, shear, etc.) -> train/validation split -> fit each probabilistic model -> evaluate on held-out period using MAE/CRPS.

Design Tradeoffs: Treeffuser offers flexibility (no distributional assumptions) but requires more sampling steps at inference; NGBoost is fast and deterministic but fails under non-Gaussian errors; CQR provides coverage guarantees but depends on the base quantile regressor's quality.

Failure Signatures: NGBoost's CRPS spikes at cut-in and rated speeds (Gaussian mismatch); any model may show degraded performance if NWP ensemble is reduced to a single biased provider; overfitting risk without proper temporal split.

First Three Experiments:
1. Train each model using only the best-performing single NWP provider to isolate the ensemble's marginal value.
2. Replace Treeffuser with a simple neural network (e.g., MLP with dropout) to benchmark non-tree deep learning on the same features.
3. Perform leave-one-farm-out cross-validation to test spatial generalizability across the four Belgian farms.

## Limitations
- Generalizability to other regions (e.g., onshore, complex terrain) is uncertain due to unique Belgian offshore climatology and farm layouts.
- The analysis ignores spatial dependencies and temporal auto-correlations in the time-series data, potentially limiting model performance.
- The comparison is restricted to tree-based methods without justification, omitting relevant deep learning or physics-informed hybrid approaches.

## Confidence
- **High:** The weather ensemble improves point accuracy (consistent with meteorological literature on multi-model ensembles).
- **Medium:** Tree-based methods significantly reduce error vs. power curves/wake model (based on a robust 4-year dataset, but confined to one region).
- **Low:** Treeffuser’s superior performance generalizes to other offshore domains (no out-of-sample geographic validation; single-region study).

## Next Checks
1. **Spatial Cross-Validation:** Re-run analysis using leave-one-farm-out CV to assess robustness to spatial heterogeneity and prevent data leakage from neighboring farms.
2. **Benchmark Against Non-Tree Methods:** Compare Treeffuser and NGBoost against a deep learning ensemble (e.g., LSTM with Monte Carlo dropout) using identical weather ensemble inputs.
3. **Ensemble Sensitivity Analysis:** Systematically vary the number of NWP providers (1–5) to quantify marginal benefit per additional provider and identify saturation.
