# Central Dogma Transformer II: An AI Microscope for Understanding Cellular Regulatory Mechanisms

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08751  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08751  
- **Reference count:** 37  
- **Primary result:** CDT-II predicts CRISPRi perturbation effects (per-gene mean Pearson r=0.84), recovers the GFI1B regulatory network (6.6-fold enrichment, P=3.5×10⁻¹⁷), and aligns cross-attention with ENCODE CTCF sites, enabling pathway analysis of therapeutic targets without clinical data.

## Executive Summary
Central Dogma Transformer II (CDT-II) is an interpretable-by-design neural architecture that mirrors the central dogma—DNA self-attention, RNA self-attention, and DNA-to-RNA cross-attention—to model gene regulation from single-cell CRISPRi perturbation data. Trained on genomic embeddings and raw expression, it achieves high accuracy in predicting perturbation effects and produces biologically meaningful attention maps and gradient-based attributions. Applied to K562 cells, CDT-II recovers known regulatory networks, identifies clinically relevant pathways for TFRC (a target of antibody PPMX-T003), and demonstrates that structured attention emerges only with high-quality, reproducible gene sets. The model functions as an "AI microscope," revealing regulatory mechanisms directly from perturbation experiments.

## Method Summary
CDT-II ingests two inputs per cell: (1) a fixed Enformer-derived DNA embedding for the perturbed locus and (2) raw per-cell RNA expression for a curated gene set (2,361 genes in K562). The DNA tower (2 self-attention layers) contextualizes the sequence; the RNA tower (1 self-attention layer) captures co-expression; cross-attention (RNA queries, DNA keys/values) models transcriptional control. A virtual cell embedder fuses both modalities, and a prediction head outputs log2 fold changes for all genes. The model is trained end-to-end to predict per-cell perturbation effects without being given the baseline state, forcing it to internalize the regulatory network. Attention maps and input gradients (Jacobian of outputs vs. RNA inputs) serve as interpretable readouts of regulatory influence.

## Key Results
- Predicts perturbation effects with high accuracy: mean pseudo-bulk Pearson r=0.84 across five held-out therapeutic genes.
- Unsupervised recovery of GFI1B regulatory network: 6.6-fold enrichment in attention/gradient outputs (P=3.5×10⁻¹⁷).
- Cross-attention enriches for ENCODE CTCF binding sites: mean 7.67× across 28 genes (P<0.001).
- Gradient-based attribution correlates with experimental effect sizes: mean r=0.82 across held-out genes.
- Pathway analysis for TFRC (without clinical data) identifies mechanisms aligned with observed anemia, reticulocyte decrease, and ferroptosis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** CDT-II’s architecture produces attention maps that directly correspond to biological regulatory relationships because each attention mechanism is matched to a specific central dogma flow.
- **Mechanism:** DNA self-attention captures cis-regulatory context; RNA self-attention captures co-regulation; DNA-to-RNA cross-attention models transcriptional control. This forces representations aligned with molecular interactions.
- **Evidence:** Cross-attention significantly enriches for ENCODE regulatory elements (CTCF, DNase sites) across multiple genes.
- **Break condition:** Attention collapse (uniform weights) occurs when trained on a noisy, non-reproducible gene set (9,335 genes → r=0.37).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Predicting per-cell log2FC from raw expression (without baseline) forces the model to internalize the regulatory network.
- **Mechanism:** Using only the perturbed cell’s RNA profile and a fixed DNA embedding, the model must infer the change relative to an implicit control, learning directional influence from the perturbed locus.
- **Evidence:** High pseudo-bulk correlation (mean r=0.84) on held-out genes.
- **Break condition:** Explicit Δexpression as input/output or noisy gene sets degrade performance (r=0.37).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Gradient-based attribution accurately quantifies regulatory influence because it captures task-specific sensitivity across all layers, unlike attention weights.
- **Mechanism:** The Jacobian ∂(predicted output)/∂(RNA input) measures how much a gene’s expression affects predicted changes. Correlation with experimental effect sizes (r=0.82) validates learned causality.
- **Evidence:** Strong gradient–experimental correlation across held-out genes; conventional *in silico* knockdown fails (r≈0.07).
- **Break condition:** Gradients from models not trained on perturbations or out-of-distribution embeddings fail.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Attention as Interpretable Biological Readout**  
  *Why needed:* CDT-II treats attention weights as direct measurements of regulatory strength, rejecting the standard caution that "attention is not explanation."  
  *Quick check:* If an attention head consistently weights genes in the same GO term, does that prove it learned that process? (Answer: No, but cross-validation with ENCODE supports the claim.)

- **Concept: Perturbation-Driven Causal Inference**  
  *Why needed:* Training on CRISPRi data (not observational co-expression) forces the model to approximate a causal graph.  
  *Quick check:* Why would a model trained only on unperturbed data fail to recover GFI1B network cleanly? (Answer: Observational data confounds correlation with causation.)

- **Concept: Gene Set Curation for Biological Resolution**  
  *Why needed:* A curated 2,361-gene set (cross-dataset reproducible) yields structured attention and high accuracy; a noisy 9,335-gene set causes collapse.  
  *Quick check:* If a gene is highly variable in one dataset but not detected in another, what does including it likely do? (Answer: Adds noise, obscuring reproducible signal.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Perturbed locus Enformer embedding (896×3072) → DNA projection (3072→512) → 2× DNA self-attention (8 heads, FFN 2048) → 896×512 DNA representations.  
RNA expression (2361 genes) → RawExpressionEncoder (gene ID embeddings + projected log1p(CPM)) → 2361×512 RNA tokens → RNA self-attention (8 heads) → 2361×512 RNA representations.  
Cross-attention: RNA queries, DNA keys/values → 2361×512 fused representations.  
Virtual Cell Embedder (4-head attention pooling) → fused cell embedding.  
Task head (2-layer MLP) → 2361 log2FC outputs.

**Critical path:**  
1. Input single perturbation’s DNA embedding and a single cell’s RNA profile.  
2. DNA tower builds local regulatory sequence representation.  
3. RNA tower builds gene expression state representation.  
4. Cross-attention: each RNA gene token queries DNA to estimate regulatory influence.  
5. Virtual Cell Embedder integrates all gene→sequence interactions.  
6. Task head predicts log2FC for every gene.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Gene set size vs. interpretability:** Larger sets increase coverage but introduce noise; the 2,361-gene set is optimal for K562 but limits transfer.  
- **Modularity vs. performance:** DNA embedding (Enformer) is swappable, but RNA tower and cross-attention are trained end-to-end on the specific perturbation dataset, limiting cross-cell-type transfer.  
- **Attention depth:** Shallow (2 DNA, 1 RNA) layers risk overfitting without enough data; cross-attention is the critical regulatory mechanism.  
- **Interpretation method:** Attention shows *what the model looks at*; gradients show *what the model uses*. Both are needed.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Training:** Loss plateaus early (cell-level r<0.4) with large gene set → re-filter for reproducibility.  
- **Validation:** Pseudo-bulk r<0.7 → model failed to learn regulatory logic.  
- **Attention:** Uniform weights ("collapse") → check gene set quality.  
- **ENCODE enrichment:** Non-significant (P>0.001) → model failed to align with known elements.  
- **Gradient correlation:** r<0.75 → predictions may not reflect true influence.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Reproduce core K562 results:** Train with 2,361-gene set and 5-gene holdout. Verify cell-level r≥0.64, GFI1B network enrichment ≥6-fold (P<1e-10), cross-attention CTCF enrichment ≥5× across held-outs.  
2. **Test on new held-out target:** For an unseen target (e.g., ITGB1), check CTCF enrichment (>2×) and if gradient top-genes include known partners from literature.  
3. **Attention head ablation:** For GFI1B cross-attention, mask top 5% most-attended DNA bins and re-predict. Dramatic drop (>20% r decrease) confirms critical bins; distributed drop suggests redundancy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does cross-attention enrichment at CTCF sites indicate the model learned 3D chromatin principles from 1D data alone?  
  *Basis:* CTCF enrichment is correlative; without Hi-C, physical interactions are unconfirmed.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Compare attention with Hi-C contact maps in K562.

- **Open Question 2:** How does excluding known GFI1B targets due to the 2,361-gene filter affect network recovery completeness?  
  *Basis:* Explicitly noted in Discussion; trade-off between noise reduction and coverage is unquantified.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Systematically relax filtering and measure recovery against a comprehensive target set.

- **Open Question 3:** Are attention maps directly causal or mostly statistical associations?  
  *Basis:* Paper states attention requires careful interpretation; external validation (ENCODE) is correlative.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Perform CRISPR perturbations on high-attention gene pairs and test predicted effects with independent data.

- **Open Question 4:** Why do gradients succeed (r=0.82) while *in silico* knockdown fails (r≈0.07), and what does this reveal about internal representations?  
  *Basis:* Paper notes gradients query locally within training distribution while knockdown extrapolates; precise mechanisms unanalyzed.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Analyze activation landscapes under realistic vs. extreme perturbations; compare gradient norms and attention shifts.

## Limitations
- **Gene Set Specificity:** The curated 2,361-gene set is optimized for K562; new cell types require re-deriving a reproducible set, limiting generalizability.
- **Attention as Ground Truth:** Enrichment supports but does not prove causality; attention may capture spurious correlations or dataset artifacts.
- **Single-Cell Noise:** Pseudo-bulk metrics are robust, but cell-level correlations (r=0.64) reflect inherent biological variability and measurement noise.

## Confidence
- **Architectural Innovation & Training Stability:** High. Modular design and ablation studies clearly show predicted failure modes (attention collapse, performance drop with noisy genes).
- **Predictive Performance on Held-Out Targets:** High. Pseudo-bulk Pearson r=0.84 is a robust, replication-friendly metric with clear experimental comparison.
- **Biological Interpretability (Network Recovery & Pathway Alignment):** Medium. Enrichment results and TFRC pathway analysis are compelling but rely on the specific K562 dataset and curated gene set; validation on new datasets is needed.
- **Gradient-Based Attribution Accuracy:** Medium. High correlation (r=0.82) is strong evidence, but the orthogonality argument and *in silico* knockdown failure are dataset-bound; independent experimental validation required.

## Next Checks
1. **Independent Cell-Type Test:** Apply the *trained* K562 CDT-II model to a new CRISPRi dataset in a different cell type (e.g., HepG2). Without retraining, measure prediction accuracy and cross-attention enrichment in cell-type-specific ENCODE peaks.
2. **Gradient Prediction Experimental Validation:** For top gradient-identified regulators of TFRC, query independent loss-of-function screens (DepMap, Project Achilles). Test if these genes show significantly stronger dependency in TFRC-dependent contexts (e.g., iron starvation) versus random genes.
3. **Attention Head Functional Specialization:** Systematically annotate the 8 DNA→RNA cross-attention heads. For each head, perform motif enrichment on its top 1% most-attended DNA bins. Hypothesis: distinct heads specialize for motifs like CTCF, GATA1, MYC. Lack of distinct enrichment would challenge structured representations.
