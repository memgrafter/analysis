# Calibrated Adaptation: Bayesian Stiefel Manifold Priors for Reliable Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.17809
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17809
*   **Reference count:** 28
*   **Primary result:** Bayesian adapters on the Stiefel manifold with tangent-space Laplace inference match LoRA/DoRA task performance while reducing Expected Calibration Error by 18–34% and improving selective prediction/OOD detection under domain shift, outperforming deep ensembles at a fraction of the cost.

## Executive Summary
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA often produce poorly calibrated models with unreliable uncertainty estimates, especially under distribution shift. This paper introduces Stiefel-Bayes Adapters (SBA), a Bayesian approach that places Matrix Langevin priors directly on the Stiefel manifold for the orthonormal adapter factors. Posterior inference is performed via a tangent-space Laplace approximation, which avoids the structural variance inflation of projecting flat-space posteriors. Across multiple models (RoBERTa, LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen) and GLUE/SuperGLUE tasks, SBA achieves comparable accuracy to deterministic baselines while significantly improving calibration, selective prediction, and out-of-distribution detection, all with minimal training overhead and a manageable inference cost.

## Method Summary
SBA parameterizes adapter updates as ΔW = UΣVᵀ, where U ∈ St(d_out, k) and V ∈ St(d_in, k) are orthonormal matrices on the Stiefel manifold and Σ is a diagonal matrix. A Matrix Langevin prior is placed on U and V, encoding a preference for well-conditioned, orthogonal subspaces via a concentration parameter κ₀. The posterior mode (MAP) is found by maximizing the task likelihood plus prior terms using Riemannian Adam. Scalable Laplace approximation is then achieved by computing the Hessian of the negative log-posterior restricted to the tangent space at the MAP using a KFAC approximation. Posterior samples are drawn by solving a linear system in the tangent space and mapping samples back to the manifold via QR retraction. Predictions are made by averaging softmax outputs over S samples.

## Key Results
*   SBA matches LoRA/DoRA task performance on GLUE/SuperGLUE while reducing Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by 18–34% over deterministic baselines.
*   Under domain shift (e.g., MNLI→SNLI), SBA improves selective prediction AUROC by 12–25% and outperforms 5-model deep ensembles on OOD detection at a fraction of the parameter cost.
*   The tangent-space Laplace approach theoretically and empirically avoids the "structural variance inflation" inherent in flat-space Gaussian projection methods (Gauss+Proj).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** A Matrix Langevin prior on the Stiefel manifold naturally encodes orthonormality as an inductive bias without hard constraints, regularizing adapter subspaces toward well-conditioned configurations for better calibration.
*   **Mechanism:** The prior's parameter F (via polar decomposition) defines a preferred orthonormal direction U* and a concentration via singular values. The resulting density respects manifold geometry, steering the posterior toward orthogonal, stable subspaces.
*   **Core assumption:** Effective low-rank adaptation primarily involves learning a stable, orthonormal subspace; this structure is captured by the Stiefel manifold.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states the prior encodes the inductive bias for well-conditioned, orthogonal subspaces. [section 2.3] details the Matrix Langevin density and its mode. Corpus work on Riemannian PEFT (e.g., "StelLA") supports orthogonal constraints.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Tangent-space Laplace approximation with geodesic retraction avoids the structural variance inflation of projecting an ambient Gaussian onto the manifold, yielding a more accurate posterior covariance.
*   **Mechanism:** The MAP is found on the manifold. The Hessian is computed in the tangent space. Samples are drawn as Gaussian in the tangent space and mapped back via retraction (e.g., QR), keeping all variance intrinsic.
*   **Core assumption:** A local quadratic approximation is valid on the manifold, and the retraction accurately maps small tangent vectors without extrinsic curvature artifacts.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 4, Theorem 1] proves projecting an ambient Gaussian introduces a strictly positive KL penalty. [section 3.2] describes the 4-step inference procedure. Corpus indicates SBA is the first to formalize this variance inflation problem for Bayesian PEFT.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The manifold-aware posterior yields calibrated predictive uncertainty that reliably indicates prediction hardness under distribution shift, enabling superior selective prediction and OOD detection.
*   **Mechanism:** Posterior samples capture epistemic uncertainty about the adapter subspace. Under domain shift, the learned subspace becomes less adequate, increasing this epistemic uncertainty. Predictive entropy or mutual information from samples then reliably flags OOD or hard inputs.
*   **Core assumption:** Task-specific adaptation involves learning a low-dimensional subspace; domain shift degrades its adequacy, increasing epistemic uncertainty.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] reports 18–34% ECE reduction, 12–25% selective AUROC improvement. [section 5.3, Figure 3] shows accuracy-at-coverage gains under MNLI→SNLI. [section 5.4, Table 4] shows OOD AUROC superiority vs. Deep-Ens-LoRA. [section 5.5, Figure 4] shows rising epistemic uncertainty under shift.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Bayesian Inference (Laplace Approximation)**
    *   **Why needed:** SBA provides calibrated uncertainty by approximating the posterior distribution over adapter parameters. Laplace turns optimization (MAP) into a probabilistic model (Gaussian in tangent space).
    *   **Quick check:** What is the key difference between a point estimate (like LoRA) and a Laplace-approximated posterior in terms of parameter uncertainty representation?
*   **Riemannian Manifolds (Stiefel, Tangent Space, Retraction)**
    *   **Why needed:** Orthonormality constraints define a Stiefel manifold. All operations (gradients, Hessian, sampling) must respect this geometry to avoid variance inflation.
    *   **Quick check:** Why is computing the Hessian in ambient Euclidean space and then projecting inappropriate for parameters constrained to a manifold?
*   **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA/DoRA)**
    *   **Why needed:** SBA is a Bayesian extension of LoRA-style adapters. Understanding the base parameterization (ΔW = BA) and orthogonal variants is essential.
    *   **Quick check:** What is the role of adapter rank k in LoRA, and how does SBA's SVD parameterization (UΣVᵀ) relate to it?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Probabilistic Model (Likelihood + Priors on U, V, Σ) → Inference Engine (Riemannian Adam for MAP → KFAC Hessian in tangent space → Tangent Gaussian sampling + retraction) → Predictive Module (Monte Carlo integration over S samples) → Evaluation (Calibration/Selective/OOD metrics).
*   **Critical path:** Problem (PEFT lacks uncertainty) → Geometric insight (adapters lie on Stiefel) → Prior design (Matrix Langevin) → Inference challenge (scalable manifold posterior) → Solution (tangent-space Laplace) → Theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1 avoids variance inflation) → Validation (cross-model, cross-task reliability gains).
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Prior concentration (κ₀):** Low κ₀ → diffuse prior, higher uncertainty; high κ₀ → anchors near init. Table 5 shows κ₀=1.0 is robust.
    *   **Posterior samples (S):** More samples improve MC integration (lower ECE) but increase inference cost linearly. Table 6 shows S=10 saturates ECE gains, S=5 sufficient for selective prediction.
    *   **Hessian approximation:** Full Hessian is intractable; KFAC balances accuracy (ECE .027) vs. cost (5 min, 50MB). Diagonal is cheaper but degrades ECE (.031).
    *   **Bayesian components:** Table 8 shows making only U or V Bayesian captures ~85% of calibration gain at lower cost than full U+V+Σ.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Poor calibration under shift:** If ECE degrades similarly to Gauss+Proj or LoRA on domain shift (e.g., MNLI→SNLI), the manifold prior may be misspecified (κ₀ wrong) or the Hessian estimate inaccurate.
    *   **High variance across seeds:** If ECE CV exceeds ~10% (Appendix G), the posterior approximation is unstable—check Hessian conditioning or learning rate.
    *   **No OOD detection gain:** If predictive entropy AUROC matches or is worse than Deep-Ens-LoRA, the epistemic component may not be rising on OOD data (check uncertainty decomposition as in Figure 4).
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Replicate Theorem 1's KL gap:** Train a single adapter (e.g., MNLI on LLaMA-2-7B), compute posterior via SBA (tangent) and Gauss+Proj (ambient). Estimate KL divergences to a finely sampled target posterior (from many SBA samples). Expect ΔKL > 0 (tangent better).
    2.  **Calibration benchmark:** Run GLUE/SuperGLUE on LLaMA-2-7B with SBA vs. LoRA, Gauss+Proj, Deep-Ens-LoRA. Verify SBA achieves ECE within 0.1% of best accuracy while reducing ECE by 18–34% over deterministic baselines and 18–33% over Gauss+Proj.
    3.  **Domain shift selective prediction:** Evaluate on MNLI→SNLI (LLaMA-2-7B). Plot accuracy-at-coverage curves. Confirm SBA achieves ≥2% higher accuracy than Deep-Ens-LoRA at 50% and 80% coverage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   Can the tangent space Laplace framework be effectively applied to other Riemannian manifolds (e.g., Grassmannian) for adapter parameterization?
*   How can predictive distillation be extended to preserve the full aleatoric-epistemic uncertainty decomposition from SBA's Bayesian posterior?
*   Does SBA's geometric prior advantage hold for very large language models (e.g., 70B+ parameters)?
*   Can Riemannian MCMC methods improve uncertainty quantification in SBA beyond the local tangent space Laplace approximation?

## Limitations
*   The effectiveness of the Matrix Langevin prior critically depends on the assumption that optimal adapter updates reside near a low-rank orthonormal subspace; misspecification could harm performance.
*   The tangent-space Laplace approximation is a local Gaussian assumption that may underestimate uncertainty for multi-modal or highly non-Gaussian posteriors far from the MAP.
*   Results are validated on models up to 13B parameters; scaling behavior to 70B+ models or different architectures (encoder-decoders) remains an open question.

## Confidence
*   **Claim:** Tangent-space Laplace avoids structural variance inflation. **Label: High** (Theoretical proof in Theorem 1, empirical KL validation).
*   **Claim:** SBA reduces ECE by 18–34% while matching accuracy. **Label: High** (Consistent results across 4 models, 8 tasks in Table 2).
*   **Claim:** SBA improves selective prediction under domain shift. **Label: Medium** (Strong evidence in Table 3, Figure 3, but depends on shift type).
*   **Claim:** Inference overhead is ~10x single forward. **Label: Medium** (Reported in section 5.7, but may vary with implementation and hardware).
*   **Claim:** Framework generalizes to other manifolds. **Label: Low** (Claimed in conclusion, no empirical validation).

2.  **Empirical KL Divergence Test (Theorem 1):** For a single adapter layer (LLaMA-2-7B on MNLI), draw S=1000 posterior samples using both SBA (tangent) and Gauss+Proj. Estimate the true posterior covariance from SBA samples. Compute KL(Gauss+Proj || target) and KL(SBA || target). Confirm KL(Gauss+Proj) > KL(SBA) consistently, especially in deeper layers.
3.  **Scalability Stress Test:** Apply SBA to a 70B-parameter model (LLaMA-2-70B) on a SuperGLUE task with rank k=16 on all linear layers. Measure (a) peak training GPU memory relative to LoRA, (b) total training time overhead, and (c) ECE/selective AUROC gains. A collapse of gains or >20% overhead would challenge the scalability claim.
