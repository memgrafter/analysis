# More Data, Fewer Diacritics: Scaling Arabic TTS

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01622
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01622
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** Scaling non-diacritized training data to ~4,000 hours nearly matches the performance of models trained on 1,000 hours of diacritized text, demonstrating that data volume can compensate for missing diacritics in Arabic TTS.

## Executive Summary
Arabic text-to-speech (TTS) systems are hampered by the frequent omission of diacritics in written text, which creates pronunciation ambiguity. This paper addresses this limitation by building an automated pipeline to curate a large-scale (~4,000 hours) Arabic speech dataset from social media. Training F5-TTS models on subsets of this data reveals that increasing the volume of non-diacritized speech-text pairs significantly narrows the performance gap with models trained on diacritized text. The largest non-diacritized model achieves near-parity with a much smaller diacritized model in objective metrics, and larger data also improves voice cloning quality. The authors plan to release a public Arabic TTS model that operates on raw, undiacritized text.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed a fully automated data curation pipeline to scale Arabic TTS training. Starting from ~20,000 hours of raw social media audio, the pipeline applies: 1) Voice Activity Detection (Silero VAD) to isolate speech segments, 2) Automatic Speech Recognition (Fanar ASR) to generate transcripts, 3) Automatic diacritization (a re-implemented Elmallah et al. model) to produce diacritized text, and 4) filtering for noise (< -30 dB in 1s context) and duration (3-15 seconds). This yielded ~4,000 hours of clean, aligned speech with both diacritized and non-diacritized transcript versions. Three data scales (100h, 1,000h, 4,000h) were sampled, including max/min diversity subsets for the 100h case. Models were trained using the non-autoregressive F5-TTS architecture (flow-matching DiT with ConvNeXt backbone) for 600k steps with a large frame-wise batch size (38,400). A separate test set of 590 items from 59 held-out speakers (each providing a short enrollment prompt and 10 target texts) was used for evaluation with WER (via Fanar ASR) and SpeechBERTScore.

## Key Results
- Increasing non-diacritized training data dramatically reduces the performance gap relative to diacritized models: the 4,000h non-diacritized model (WER 3.18, SpeechBERTScore 0.732) nearly matches the 1,000h diacritized model (WER 2.55, score 0.729).
- Larger data volumes improve voice cloning capability, as evidenced by higher SpeechBERTScore when synthesizing speech in a target speaker's voice using a short prompt.
- Diacritized models consistently outperform non-diacritized models at the same data scale, confirming the value of explicit pronunciation guidance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Increasing the volume of non-diacritized training data significantly narrows the performance gap with diacritized models.
- **Mechanism**: The neural TTS model implicitly learns to disambiguate pronunciation from contextual patterns in large-scale speech-text pairs, reducing reliance on explicit diacritic markers.
- **Core assumption**: The model architecture (F5-TTS) and data distribution provide sufficient inductive bias and contextual coverage for implicit diacritization.
- **Evidence**: Abstract states "larger amounts of training data compensate for the lack of diacritics"; Table 1 shows 4,000h non-diacritized (WER 3.18) nearly matches 1,000h diacritized (2.55); Section 8 notes the model "can implicitly learn the correct pronunciation from context."
- **Break condition**: If non-diacritized model performance plateaus beyond a certain scale while diacritized models continue improving.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The automated pipeline (VAD, ASR, diacritization, filtering) efficiently scales Arabic TTS data curation from noisy raw sources.
- **Mechanism**: Each component automates a traditionally manual step; with sufficient volume, random errors average out, yielding a dataset of adequate quality for training robust TTS models.
- **Core assumption**: Component errors are predominantly random and independent, so their aggregate impact diminishes with data scale.
- **Evidence**: Abstract mentions "robust pipeline... resulting in around 4,000 hours"; Section 3 details pipeline steps and filtering thresholds; Section 6 shows models trained on pipeline data achieve competitive metrics.
- **Break condition**: If components introduce systematic bias (e.g., ASR fails on specific dialects), errors do not average out.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: F5-TTS's diffusion transformer architecture enables effective voice cloning and leverages large-scale data for implicit diacritization.
- **Mechanism**: The non-autoregressive, flow-matching design with ConvNeXt supports stable training on diverse data and captures speaker characteristics from short prompts; model capacity grows with data to learn text-speech mappings without explicit diacritics.
- **Core assumption**: Model capacity and training dynamics allow generalization from few-second enrollment samples and ambiguous text when data is abundant.
- **Evidence**: Section 2.1 describes F5-TTS as state-of-the-art; Section 4 notes large batch sizes (38,400) and 600k steps; Section 6 shows SpeechBERTScore improves with data size.
- **Break condition**: If model fails to clone voices from short prompts at any scale or degrades with more data.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  Raw audio → Silero VAD → Fanar ASR → Elmallah diacritizer → Noise/duration filtering → Train/Test split (speaker-disjoint) → F5-TTS (DiT + ConvNeXt) → Vocos vocoder → Synthesized audio.
- **Critical path**:
  1. Process 20k raw hours: VAD segmentation.
  2. ASR transcription (with diacritics per Elmallah model).
  3. Filter by noise (-30 dB) and duration (3-15 sec).
  4. Split: exclude test speakers (59) via diarization; sample 100h/1k h/4k h subsets.
  5. Train F5-TTS for 600k steps with voice cloning prompts.
  6. Vocode mel-spectrograms to audio.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Public API (Fanar ASR): easier but less control; may introduce bias if ASR errors correlate with diacritics.
  - VAD: Silero is lightweight but may miss dialectal speech; fixed noise threshold (-30 dB) may exclude valid low-SNR content.
  - Diacritizer: Reimplementation of Elmallah; 5.5% WER on WikiNews but unknown on social media data.
  - Data sampling: 100h subsets max/min diversity test tradeoff between coverage and consistency.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High WER on synthesized speech despite low training loss.
  - SpeechBERTScore drop on short enrollment prompts (>3 sec), indicating poor voice cloning.
  - Consistent diacritization errors in training text propagating to TTS output (e.g., wrong vowel length).
  - Degradation on non-MSA dialects if training data is MSA-dominated.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Pipeline validation**: Manually annotate 10h of raw audio; compare automatic pipeline outputs (ASR + diacritics) to ground truth to quantify error propagation.
  2. **Diacritics ablation**: Train two models on identical 1,000h subset—one with diacritics, one without—measure WER/SpeechBERTScore gap to confirm scale effect at intermediate data size.
  3. **Speaker diversity test**: Using 100h max/min diversity subsets, evaluate voice cloning (SpeechBERTScore) on held-out speakers to verify diversity benefit.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: How can reducing errors from ASR and automatic diacritization improve Arabic TTS, and does this benefit vary with data scale?
  - *Basis*: Quality Ceiling: errors from components propagate; they remain a noise source even at large scale.
  - *Why unresolved*: The study used existing components without improvement; impact of error reduction unknown.
  - *What evidence*: Train TTS on manually corrected subsets or with enhanced components; compare across 100h/1k h/4k h.
- **Question 2**: How well do WER and SpeechBERTScore predict human judgments of Arabic TTS naturalness, and does using the same ASR for data and evaluation bias results?
  - *Basis*: Reliance on Objective Metrics: they may not capture perceptual qualities; same ASR family may bias; human evaluation needed.
  - *Why unresolved*: No human evaluation; bias uninvestigated.
  - *What evidence*: Conduct human MOS; compute correlation; recompute WER with another ASR.
- **Question 3**: How do Arabic dialects and domains affect TTS performance, and can specialized corpora improve synthesis for specific varieties?
  - *Basis*: Domain and Dialectal Scope: only MSA evaluated; dialect distribution unanalyzed; suggests genre/dialect-specific corpora.
  - *Why unresolved*: No dialectal evaluation; dataset composition unknown.
  - *What evidence*: Analyze dataset dialects; create dialect test sets; train targeted models; compare to MSA baseline.

## Limitations
- Proprietary data source and unspecified dialect/genre distribution limit generalizability.
- Pipeline components (Fanar ASR, Elmallah diacritizer) lack public checkpoints; substitutions may cause systematic shifts.
- Test set construction (speaker diarization, MSA filtering) is incompletely described, preventing exact replication.
- WER evaluation reuses the Fanar ASR family, risking optimistic bias.

## Confidence
- **Data scale compensates for lack of diacritics**: High. Empirically robust across 100 h, 1 k h, and 4 k h with consistent metric trends.
- **Automated pipeline effectiveness**: Medium. No human-validated quality floor; error accumulation unknown.
- **F5‑TTS architecture enables scaling**: Medium. Claims not contrasted with alternative TTS backbones.

## Next Checks

2. *Cross‑component reproducibility*: Rebuild the 4 k h non‑diacritized corpus using Whisper‑v3 and a public diacritizer (e.g., faras). Train F5‑TTS under identical settings and compare WER/SpeechBERTScore.
3. *Objective speaker similarity test*: Use a pre‑train‑ed speaker encoder (e.g., ECAPA‑TDNN) to measure embedding cosine similarity between generated and reference speech for the 4 k models; correlate with SpeechBERTScore to validate voice cloning claims.
