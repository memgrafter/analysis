# FlexMoRE: A Flexible Mixture of Rank-heterogeneous Experts for Efficient Federatedly-trained Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08818
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08818
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** FlexMoRE reduces parameters by 67% (10.75B vs. 33.27B baseline) while improving average performance by 3.8% (47.18 vs. 45.46) across 120 tasks.

## Executive Summary
FlexMoRE addresses the memory inefficiency of federated mixture-of-experts (MoE) models by introducing rank-heterogeneous experts. It leverages post-hoc low-rank adapters extracted from full-sized domain experts via truncated SVD, without retraining. Evaluated on 120 tasks using six experts across ranks 2⁰–2¹⁴, the approach shows that optimal expert rank is task-dependent: reasoning-heavy benchmarks benefit from higher ranks, while knowledge-heavy ones peak at lower ranks. By selecting per-expert optimal ranks via the MC9 benchmark, a heterogeneous FlexMoRE with 10.75B parameters achieves a higher average score (47.18) than a 33.27B full-size baseline (45.46). Single post-hoc low-rank experts can even surpass their full-size versions by up to 7.13% on certain domains.

## Method Summary
FlexMoRE builds on the federated MoE framework of FlexOlmo, where domain-specific experts are trained independently on private data and anchored to a shared, frozen public base model. The key innovation is replacing some full-size experts with low-rank adapters derived post-hoc from the full experts. For each domain expert, the weight difference Δ = W_domain − W_base is approximated via truncated SVD at a chosen rank *r*, yielding LoRA matrices B and A. The low-rank expert’s output is computed as (W_base + BA)x during forward passes. The router, which has embeddings for each expert, selects the top-*k* experts per token. For a selected low-rank expert, its contribution is added to the output of the shared base expert. The paper explores a wide range of ranks (2⁰ to 2¹⁴) per expert and finds that performance is highly sensitive to rank in a task-dependent manner. The final heterogeneous mixture is constructed by assigning each expert its optimal rank, chosen either to maximize performance on the reasoning-focused MC9 benchmark or on the global average across all benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Heterogeneous FlexMoRE (10.75B parameters) outperforms the full-size FlexOlmo baseline (33.27B) by +3.8% average score (47.18 vs. 45.46) while using 67% fewer parameters.
- Optimal expert rank is task-dependent: reasoning-heavy benchmarks (BBH) prefer higher ranks (median log₂(r*) = 11.5), while knowledge-heavy ones (MMLU) peak at lower ranks (median log₂(r*) = 2).
- Single post-hoc low-rank experts, when evaluated with the public base model, can exceed their full-size counterparts by up to 7.13% on specific domains.
- Selecting expert ranks based on MC9 performance (a reasoning proxy) yields better overall mixture efficiency than selection based on the global average score.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Rank-Heterogeneous Experts Preserve Performance with Fewer Parameters
**Claim:** FlexMoRE achieves memory efficiency without significant performance loss by allowing experts with heterogeneous low-rank adaptations to coexist with full-sized experts within a single MoE routing framework.
**Mechanism:** The architecture uses a shared, frozen public base model. Full-size experts are standard FFN modules. Low-rank experts are adapters (B, A matrices) derived via post-hoc SVD truncation of the difference Δ between a domain expert and the public base. During inference, routing to a low-rank expert activates both the shared base expert and the adapter (output = W_base x + BA x). The router embeddings remain unchanged from the full-expert model.
**Core assumption:** For many domains, the weight update Δ needed to specialize a base model can be adequately captured by a low-rank matrix, preserving task-specific knowledge at a fraction of the parameters.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] Introduces the concept of rank-heheterogeneous experts and states low-rank adapters may be sufficient.
- [section 3.1] Describes the decoupled training and the mechanism for low-rank experts attaching to a base expert.
- [section 3.2] Details the PHLoRA extraction process (truncated SVD on Δ).
**Break condition:** If Δ has high intrinsic rank (many singular values required to capture variance), low-rank approximation causes large reconstruction error and performance drop, as seen for the Math expert at very low ranks.

### Mechanism 2: Task-Specific Rank Sensitivity Drives Heterogeneity
**Claim:** Performance on downstream tasks is non-uniformly sensitive to expert rank, with reasoning-heavy tasks requiring higher ranks than knowledge-heavy tasks, justifying heterogeneous rank allocation.
**Mechanism:** For each expert, performance is measured across ranks 2⁰–2¹⁴ on multiple benchmarks. A linear regression of score vs. log₂(rank) yields a sensitivity slope (β). The peak performance rank r* is recorded. The distribution of r* across benchmark groups reveals a clear pattern: reasoning tasks (BBH) have higher median peak ranks than knowledge tasks (MMLU).
**Core assumption:** The rank-performance relationship can be coarsely summarized by a linear trend in log-space, and the observed peak rank within the tested range indicates the capacity needed for that task type.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [section 3.3] Defines the regression and peak rank computation.
- [section 5.3 & Figure 3] Shows reasoning benchmarks (BBH) have higher median peak ranks than knowledge benchmarks (MMLU).
- [Table 8] Provides per-expert/per-benchmark slopes (e.g., Math on BBH: β=0.0108; News on MMLU: β=-0.0017).
**Break condition:** If the rank-performance curve is non-monotonic with multiple local maxima, a single r* may not represent the true optimum. Linear regression also may mischaracterize complex, non-linear relationships.

### Mechanism 3: MC9-Based Rank Selection Prioritizes Efficient Generalists
**Claim:** Selecting expert ranks based on performance on a reasoning-focused proxy benchmark (MC9) yields a more parameter-efficient mixture than using the global average across all benchmarks.
**Mechanism:** For each expert, the rank that maximizes its MC9 score is chosen (vs. the rank maximizing its average score across all six benchmark groups). These MC9-selected ranks are used to build a heterogeneous FlexMoRE model. This model outperforms both the homogeneous rank-tuned model and the heterogeneous model selected on the global average.
**Core assumption:** MC9 (multiple-choice reasoning tasks) is a sufficient proxy for an expert's general utility in a mixture, particularly for selecting rank-efficient experts that contribute well across diverse tasks. Global average selection favors experts with high but narrow peaks that require higher ranks, which are less efficient under fixed routing budgets.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [section 5.2 & Table 2] The MC9-selected heterogeneous model (10.75B) achieves Avg=47.18, higher than the Avg-selected heterogeneous (46.55) and the full-size baseline (45.46).
- [section 5.2] Explains that MC9 selection "implicitly prioritizes rank-efficient experts" while Avg selection favors "capacity-heavy specialists."
**Break condition:** If the proxy task distribution (MC9) is misaligned with the final deployment task distribution, the selected ranks may be suboptimal. The advantage may diminish if the mixture routing is not sensitive to marginal per-expert efficiency.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)**
  - **Why needed here:** FlexMoRE modifies the standard MoE paradigm by introducing rank-heterogeneous experts trained in a federated manner. Understanding MoE's selective activation mechanism is fundamental.
  - **Quick check:** In a standard MoE, what is the typical trade-off between the number of active experts per token (e.g., top-*k*) and total computational cost (FLOPs)?
- **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**
  - **Why needed here:** FlexMoRE's core efficiency gain comes from representing some experts as low-rank adapters (BA) instead of full matrices. Grasping the LoRA parameterization and the role of rank *r* is essential.
  - **Quick check:** How does increasing the LoRA rank *r* affect the number of trainable parameters and the expressive capacity (ability to approximate the full update) of the adapter?
- **Federated/Decentralized Expert Training**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's motivation is data governance. FlexMoRE extends FlexOlmo, where experts are trained independently on private data using only a shared public base model, without sharing data or model updates.
  - **Quick check:** In the FlexOlmo/FlexMoRE setting, what is the role of the "public base model" during the decentralized training of a domain-specific expert?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Public Base Model (W_public) -> Router (f(x)) -> Top-k Selection -> For selected expert:
    If Full-size: Apply W_expert x
    If Low-rank: Apply (W_public + B_r A_r) x (where B_r A_r approximates Δ = W_expert - W_public)

- **Critical path:**
  1.  Start with pre-trained full-size domain experts (W_expert) and a public base model (W_public).
  2.  For each domain expert, compute Δ = W_expert - W_public.
  3.  Perform SVD on Δ: Δ ≈ U Σ Vᵀ. For target rank *r*, truncate to top-*r* singular values/vectors.
  4.  Split Σ symmetrically: set B_r = U_r √Σ_r, A_r = V_rᵀ √Σ_r. The low-rank expert is defined by (B_r, A_r).
  5.  In the MoE module, register the low-rank expert alongside the router embedding. The router still outputs scores for all experts.
  6.  During inference, router selects top-*k* experts. For each selected low-rank expert, compute its output as (W_public + B_r A_r)x and add it to the output of the activated shared W_public.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Rank vs. Performance:** Higher ranks preserve more of the original expert's capacity but increase memory. Optimal rank is task-dependent (reasoning > knowledge).
  - **Rank Selection Strategy:** Using a single proxy benchmark (MC9) for rank selection outperforms using the global average, favoring rank-efficient generalists over narrow high-peak specialists.
  - **Heterogeneity vs. Complexity:** Managing a registry of experts with varying ranks adds bookkeeping complexity but yields better performance than forcing uniform rank.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Domain-Rank Mismatch:** Assigning a knowledge-heavy expert a very high rank (e.g., 2¹⁴) yields minimal gains for large memory cost.
  - **Routing Inefficiency:** If the router is not retuned after post-hoc adapter extraction, it might allocate high frequency to an expert that now has low rank, missing potential gains from other experts.
  - **Base Model Drift:** The method assumes the public base model is identical to the base used during original expert training. Any mismatch invalidates the Δ computation.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Reproduce PHLoRA Extraction:** Take one full-size FlexOlmo expert, compute Δ vs. public base, perform SVD for ranks 2⁰, 2⁴, 2⁸. Evaluate the resulting low-rank expert (combined with base) on a single downstream task. Verify performance increases with rank up to a point.
  2.  **Validate Rank Sensitivity per Benchmark:** For the Math expert, evaluate performance across ranks 2⁰–2¹⁴ on BBH (reasoning) and MMLU (knowledge). Plot score vs. log₂(rank). Confirm the slope and peak rank are higher for BBH.
  3.  **Test MC9 vs. Average Rank Selection:** For Code and Math experts, find ranks maximizing MC9 and maximizing average over all 6 benchmark groups. Build three 2-expert MoEs: (a) both at MC9-rank, (b) both at Avg-rank, (c) both at fixed high rank (e.g., 2¹¹). Evaluate all on the full 120-task suite. Expect (a) to achieve higher Avg than (b) and (c) with equal or fewer parameters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** What is an optimal, automated strategy for selecting the LoRA rank for each expert in a FlexMoRE model, beyond using a single proxy benchmark like MC9?
  - **Basis in paper:** Future Work section states this is an open direction. The paper only compares two ad-hoc methods (MC9 proxy and Avg performance).
  - **Why unresolved:** No principled, automated rank selection heuristic was developed or evaluated.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic study comparing multiple rank selection heuristics (e.g., based on expert domain, layer depth, validation performance) across diverse expert pools, reporting impact on final MoE performance and parameter efficiency.

- **Question 2:** Does tuning the router's parameters after post-hoc LoRA expert extraction improve the performance of FlexMoRE models, and if so, by how much?
  - **Basis in paper:** Listed as a limitation: router-tuning was not applied. Its effect in FlexOlmo was marginal, but expectation is further improvement here.
  - **Why unresolved:** The effect of router-tuning in the rank-heterogeneous setting is untested.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** An experiment replicating main evaluations with an additional condition where router is fine-tuned for a few steps after ranks are fixed, measuring the delta in Avg score.

- **Question 3:** For a multilingual or multi-domain setup, what is the optimal combination and rank allocation between full-size language-specific experts and low-rank domain-specific experts?
  - **Basis in paper:** Future Work proposes investigating combinations like one full-size expert per language plus low-rank domain experts.
  - **Why unresolved:** Experiments only used a single public full-size base expert and six low-rank domain experts in a monolingual setting.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend experiments to include at least two full-size experts (e.g., English and non-English base models) with attached low-rank adapters, evaluating on cross-lingual and cross-domain benchmarks.

- **Question 4:** Do low-rank experts trained from scratch on their domain data (rather than extracted post-hoc from full fine-tuned experts) achieve comparable or better performance at the same rank, and how does this interact with the router?
  - **Basis in paper:** Methods section mentions scratch training is possible but all experiments used post-hoc extraction (PHLoRA).
  - **Why unresolved:** The efficiency claim is based on post-hoc extraction. Training low-rank experts from scratch is a valid configuration but was not evaluated.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled experiment comparing post-hoc vs. scratch-trained low-rank experts (e.g., Code, Math) at various ranks in both single-expert and MoE settings.

- **Question 5:** Can the layer-wise distribution of expert ranks (instead of a single rank per expert across all layers) yield further efficiency gains without sacrificing performance in FlexMoRE?
  - **Basis in paper:** Future Work suggests investigating more heterogeneity via selective rank distribution among layers (cf. L1RA).
  - **Why unresolved:** The current model applies a uniform LoRA rank per expert across all transformer layers. Recent work (L1RA) shows rank needs vary by layer.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** An experiment where each expert's LoRA rank varies by layer (e.g., lower ranks in earlier layers, higher in later ones), with a search algorithm to determine per-layer rank budget, measuring parameter reduction and Avg score change.

## Limitations
- **Evaluation setup dependency:** Performance claims rely on a specific `2x7B` MoE configuration (public base + one expert per layer) and a particular evaluation harness. Results may not transfer to different model scales or routing setups.
- **Router not tuned:** The router parameters were kept fixed after post-hoc LoRA extraction. While expected to help, router-tuning could alter the optimal rank selection and final performance.
- **Single base model assumption:** The method assumes a perfect match between the public base model and the base used during original expert training. Any divergence invalidates the Δ computation and harms performance.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (rank-heterogeneous experts suffice):** **Medium** – Requires validation that post-hoc extraction accurately preserves expert capability across domains.
- **Mechanism 2 (task-dependent rank sensitivity):** **High** – Strong empirical trend supported by regression analysis across 6 experts and multiple benchmarks.
- **Mechanism 3 (MC9 rank selection optimal):** **Medium** – Supported by one comparison against Avg selection, but needs broader validation across different expert pools and proxy tasks.
- **Overall outcome (67% reduction, +3.8% Avg):** **Medium** – Depends on exact evaluation protocol and access to original checkpoints.
