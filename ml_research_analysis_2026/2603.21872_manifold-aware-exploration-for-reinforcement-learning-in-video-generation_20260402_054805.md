# Manifold-Aware Exploration for Reinforcement Learning in Video Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.21872
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21872
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: SAGE-GRPO stabilizes video GRPO by constraining exploration within the pre-trained model's data manifold, leading to consistent improvements over baselines on multiple quality metrics and user preference.

## Executive Summary
Video reinforcement learning with generative models (GRPO) suffers from unstable exploration due to excessive noise injected during ODE-to-SDE conversion, which degrades rollout quality and reward reliability. This paper introduces SAGE-GRPO, a method that constrains exploration within the data manifold of a pre-trained video generation model. SAGE-GRPO combines three innovations: a precise SDE with logarithmic curvature correction to keep sampling on-manifold; a gradient norm equalizer to balance optimization across timesteps; and a dual trust region with a periodic moving anchor and stepwise KL constraints to prevent long-horizon drift while maintaining plasticity. Evaluated on HunyuanVideo1.5 using the VideoAlign reward, SAGE-GRPO achieves consistent gains over prior video GRPO methods (DanceGRPO, FlowGRPO, CPS) across VideoAlign components, CLIPScore, and PickScore. A user study with 29 evaluators further confirms strong preference for SAGE-GRPO in visual quality, motion quality, and semantic alignment.

## Method Summary
SAGE-GRPO optimizes a diffusion-based video generation policy via group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) with manifold-constrained exploration. The policy is parameterized by a flow matching model, and the training loop generates groups of stochastic rollouts using a precise SDE that incorporates a logarithmic curvature correction to maintain on-manifold sampling. The gradient updates are scaled by a temporal gradient equalizer that normalizes per-timestep gradient norms, mitigating imbalance between high-noise and low-noise steps. A dual trust region regularizer combines a position KL term against a periodically updated anchor policy and a velocity KL term against the previous policy, controlled by an adaptive KL weight that warms up and adjusts based on recent KL statistics. The composite reward from VideoAlign (visual quality, motion quality, temporal alignment) guides the optimization. The method is implemented on top of HunyuanVideo1.5 and demonstrates superior performance over baselines.

## Key Results
- SAGE-GRPO outperforms DanceGRPO, FlowGRPO, and CPS on VideoAlign overall reward and its components (VQ, MQ, TA).
- It also achieves higher CLIPScore and PickScore.
- A user study with 29 evaluators shows strong preference for SAGE-GRPO in visual quality, motion quality, and semantic alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Precise Manifold-Aware SDE
- **Claim**: The precise SDE reduces excess noise injection during ODE-to-SDE conversion, keeping sampling on the video data manifold and improving rollout quality.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of using first-order approximations for noise standard deviation, it integrates the diffusion coefficient over each step and adds a logarithmic correction term `log((1-σ_{t+1})/(1-σ_t))`. This yields a smaller, manifold-aligned exploration region that stays tangent to the flow trajectory.
- **Core assumption**: The pre-trained video generation model defines a valid data manifold; exploration should remain within its vicinity to preserve sample validity and reward reliability.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "We derive a precise manifold-aware SDE with a logarithmic curvature correction... sampling noise stays manifold-consistent"
  - [section 3.2.1] Derivation of Σ_t with log term (Eq. 5–7) and geometric interpretation in Figure 2.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., "Local Manifold Approximation and Projection for Manifold-Aware Diffusion Planning") discuss manifold constraints for diffusion planning, but none directly address video GRPO; evidence is indirect.
- **Break condition**: If the pre-trained model’s manifold poorly captures the target distribution or the task distribution shifts significantly, the precise SDE may still drift off-manifold, especially if the σ schedule is not properly handled (see Appendix A.2).

### Mechanism 2: Temporal Gradient Equalizer
- **Claim**: The gradient norm equalizer normalizes optimization pressure across timesteps, preventing bias toward low-noise phases and stabilizing learning.
- **Mechanism**: Estimates a per-timestep gradient scale proxy `N_t` (proportional to `1/Σ_t^{1/2}`) and normalizes gradients by the median across all timesteps: `S_t = median(N_τ) / (N_t + ε)`. This balances the magnitude of updates so that high-noise and low-noise steps contribute comparably.
- **Core assumption**: Gradient norms vary inversely with noise standard deviation, causing vanishing gradients at high noise (t→1) and exploding gradients at low noise (t→0); balancing them improves update stability and convergence.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "introduce a gradient norm equalizer to stabilize sampling and updates across timesteps"
  - [section 3.2.1] Equation (8)–(9) and empirical validation in Figure 3 and Figure 4.
  - [corpus] No direct neighbor addresses temporal gradient balancing in diffusion RL; the concept relates to gradient clipping/normalization in deep RL but is adapted here to diffusion timesteps.
- **Break condition**: If the median normalization is too aggressive, it may suppress useful gradients; the method assumes the imbalance follows the predicted inverse relationship, which may not hold if the policy network architecture or SDE differs significantly.

### Mechanism 3: Dual Trust Region
- **Claim**: The dual trust region with periodic moving anchor and stepwise KL constraints prevents long-horizon drift while maintaining plasticity.
- **Mechanism**: Combines two KL terms: (1) position control `D_KL(π_θ || π_ref_N)` with a periodically updated reference (every N steps) to bound cumulative displacement; (2) velocity control `D_KL(π_θ || π_{k-1})` to smooth instantaneous updates. Weighted sum `L_KL = β_pos·pos + β_vel·vel`.
- **Core assumption**: Fixed KL anchors (to initial model) are too restrictive for long-term optimization, while stepwise KL alone allows unbounded drift; a dynamic trust region tracks manifold-consistent checkpoints and enables staged exploration.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "dual trust region with a periodic moving anchor and stepwise KL constraints prevents long-horizon drift while maintaining plasticity"
  - [section 3.2.2] Equations (11)–(14) and discussion of position-velocity controller.
  - [corpus] Neighbor "Automatic Intrinsic Reward Shaping for Exploration in Deep Reinforcement Learning" addresses exploration but not trust region dynamics; limited direct corpus support.
- **Break condition**: Poor choice of moving period N or KL weights (β_pos, β_vel) can lead to either excessive drift (if too loose) or underfitting (if too tight). The method also relies on accurate KL estimation from rollout samples, which may be noisy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Flow Matching and Rectified Flow**
  - Why needed: SAGE-GRPO operates on flow matching models; the SDE derivation and noise schedule depend on understanding the ODE trajectory `dxt/dt = v_θ(xt,t)` and linear interpolation `xt = (1-σ_t)x0 + σ_t z1`.
  - Quick check: In rectified flow, what does the noise level σ_t represent? (Answer: It interpolates between data (σ=0) and pure noise (σ=1).)
- **Concept: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
  - Why needed: The overall algorithm is a GRPO variant; group-normalized advantages and stochastic rollouts are central to the update rule.
  - Quick check: How is the group-normalized advantage `A_i` computed? (Answer: `A_i = (r_i - μ_R) / (σ_R + ε)`, where μ_R and σ_R are the mean and std of the group rewards.)
- **Concept: KL Divergence as Trust Region**
  - Why needed: The macro-level constraints use KL to anchor the policy; interpreting KL as a distance in policy space (especially for Gaussians) is key to understanding position vs. velocity control.
  - Quick check: For a Gaussian policy `π(xt-1|xt) = N(µ_θ, Σ_t I)`, what does `D_KL(π_θ || π_ref)` approximate? (Answer: `(µ_θ - µ_ref)^2 / (2Σ_t^2)`.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  1. **Pre-trained video model** (HunyuanVideo1.5): defines the data manifold `M` and provides the velocity field `v_θ`.
  2. **SDE sampler**: implements precise variance with logarithmic correction and gradient equalizer; produces stochastic rollouts.
  3. **Reward model**: VideoAlign computes composite reward from VQ, MQ, TA scores.
  4. **GRPO updater**: computes group advantages, applies temporal gradient equalization, and adds dual KL regularization.
  5. **Moving anchor manager**: periodically updates the reference policy `π_ref` every N steps.
- **Critical path**:
  Prompt → sample group of videos via SDE → compute rewards → compute group advantages → compute policy gradient with equalized scales → add dual KL loss → update policy → every N steps, update reference policy.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Precise SDE**: Minimal computational overhead but requires careful σ schedule handling; clamping at low (1-σ) avoids explosion (Appendix A.2).
  - **Dual trust region**: Parameters β_pos, β_vel, and moving period N require tuning; too tight limits plasticity, too loose allows drift. The two-stage KL warm-up (λ_min to λ_max) helps.
  - **Gradient equalizer**: Median normalization is robust to outliers but may be suboptimal if gradient distribution is highly skewed.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **High temporal jitter/artifacts**: indicates off-manifold exploration; check SDE variance and clamping thresholds.
  - **Reward hacking** (high reward but poor visual quality): suggests trust region too loose or KL weights too small; monitor KL divergence.
  - **Collapse/mode dropping**: may result from over-regularization (large KL weights) or gradient equalizer suppressing useful signals.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Ablate gradient equalizer**: train with/without `S_t` normalization and compare reward curves (Figure 3).
  2. **Compare KL strategies**: run Fixed KL, Step-wise KL, Moving KL, Dual Moving KL; measure mean reward and exploration std (Figure 8).
  3. **Vary KL weight schedule**: test fixed λ, two-stage warm-up (10^{-7}→10^{-5}), and adaptive feedback; observe reward stability (Figure 7).
