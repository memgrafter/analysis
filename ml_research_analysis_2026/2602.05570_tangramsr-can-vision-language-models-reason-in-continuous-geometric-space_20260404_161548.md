# TangramSR: Can Vision-Language Models Reason in Continuous Geometric Space?
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05570
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05570
- **Reference Count:** 10
- **Primary Result:** VLMs fail at continuous geometric reasoning (0.41 single-piece, 0.23 two-piece IoU); a training-free self-refinement loop boosts medium-triangle IoU from 0.63 to 0.932.

## Executive Summary
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) show systematic failures in continuous geometric reasoning tasks like Tangram assembly, achieving low Intersection-over-Union (IoU) scores. This paper introduces **TangramSR**, a training-free test-time framework that combines in-context learning (ICL) with reward-guided feedback loops to iteratively refine geometric predictions without parameter updates. The method uses a verifier to compute a reward signal (IoU minus a positional penalty) that guides the VLM’s subsequent outputs via numeric hints added to the prompt. On medium-triangle single-piece tasks, the loop significantly improves IoU from a baseline of 0.63 to 0.932. However, gains are narrower on other shapes and two-piece compositions, highlighting both the potential and current limits of VLM-based spatial reasoning in continuous domains.

## Method Summary
TangramSR is a test-time self-refinement pipeline that requires no model training. For each test image, it first samples *k* few-shot (image, JSON) pairs to prime the VLM with geometric priors via ICL. The VLM outputs a continuous pose prediction (position, angle, size) in JSON format. A separate geometry verifier renders the predicted polygon and computes IoU against the ground truth, producing a reward `R = IoU - λ·||p̂-p||²/10`. This numeric feedback is appended to the prompt, and the VLM is queried again. The process repeats for *T* iterations, updating the ICL context with past predictions and rewards. The best candidate (highest `R`) is kept. If IoU remains below a threshold `τ` and the task involves position, a deterministic local grid search refines the position. The framework thus creates a recursive proposer-verifier loop that mimics human trial-and-error correction.

## Key Results
- VLMs exhibit baseline failures on continuous Tangram assembly: average IoU of 0.41 (single-piece) and 0.23 (two-piece).
- The proposed loop achieves a **+0.282 IoU gain** on medium-triangle single-piece tasks (from 0.650 to 0.932).
- ICL alone provides a modest gain (+0.145 IoU), while the iterative loop contributes the majority of improvement.
- Performance on non-medium-triangle shapes and two-piece tasks remains low (IoU ≤ 0.23), indicating limited generalizability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: ICL-Enhanced Proposal Generation
**Claim:** In-context learning (ICL) stabilizes and improves the initial pose proposal by providing exemplars of correct geometric arrangements, reducing token-space discretization noise.
**Mechanism:** The framework samples *k* few-shot (image, JSON) pairs to prime the VLM. This injects geometric priors (position, angle, scale relationships) into the prompt, guiding the model to generate outputs in a structured JSON format that aligns better with continuous coordinates than free-form text.
**Core assumption:** VLMs can extract and apply spatial patterns from few-shot examples to novel images, despite being trained on discrete semantic tasks.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "combines in-context learning (ICL) with reward-guided feedback loops"
- [section 3.3]: "build *k* few-shot pairs excluding the current sample; query the VLM with a minimal JSON instruction"
- [section 4.3, Table 3]: ICL-only (k=15) improves IoU from 0.650 to 0.795 (+0.145)
**Break condition:** If few-shot exemplars are geometrically misaligned, noisy, or from a different distribution than the test sample, ICL may provide misleading priors and degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2: Reward-Guided Iterative Correction
**Claim:** The reward function `R(Θ) = IoU(U(Θ), S) - λ·||p̂-p||²/10` provides a scalar feedback signal that enables the VLM to correct small positional and angular errors across iterations without gradient updates.
**Mechanism:** After each VLM output, the verifier computes IoU and L2 position error. The numeric feedback ("previous IoU=x.xx. Try a small correction (Δx,Δy).") is appended to the prompt, invoking the VLM’s ability to interpret numeric critiques and adjust its next proposal accordingly.
**Core assumption:** VLMs can map numeric reward differences to directional corrections in continuous space via natural language hints, effectively simulating a gradient step.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "reward-guided feedback loops... iteratively self-refine predictions based on geometric consistency feedback"
- [section 3.3]: "append a numeric feedback hint of the form 'previous IoU=$x.xx. Try a small correction (Δx,Δy).'"
- [section 4.3, Table 3]: Loop-only (6 iterations, τ=0.9) yields IoU 0.932 vs. baseline 0.65 (+0.282)
- [section 4.3]: "Loop mechanism contributes the majority of improvement"
**Break condition:** If the initial prediction is severely misaligned (IoU < 0.3), the VLM may misinterpret the correction hint, leading to oscillation or divergence rather than convergence.

### Mechanism 3: Recursive Context Adaptation
**Claim:** Updating the ICL context with each iteration’s prediction and reward forms a recursive loop where the model’s own outputs become part of the prompt, enabling progressive refinement akin to human trial-and-error.
**Mechanism:** The context buffer accumulates `(image, JSON, IoU feedback)` tuples from prior iterations. This growing history allows the VLM to observe its own error trajectory and steer subsequent outputs toward higher rewards, effectively creating a closed-loop optimization at test time.
**Core assumption:** VLMs can utilize their own past outputs as in-context examples to adjust future behavior, despite not having internal state between calls.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "recursive verifier-refiner agent iteratively improves predictions"
- [section 3.3]: "each iteration updates the ICL context and feedback signals based on previous outputs"
- [section 4.3]: Ablations show that loop length beyond 6 iterations yields diminishing returns (IoU ~0.93), indicating rapid context utilization.
**Break condition:** If the context window overflows or contains irrelevant/corrupted history, the VLM may attend to less useful examples, causing performance saturation or regression.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Geometric Primitives (position, angle, scale)**
  - *Why needed:* The task decomposes continuous pose estimation into these three orthogonal parameters. Misestimation in any single axis compounds in IoU (section 4.1: "joint prediction aggregates noise").
  - *Quick check:* If a triangle’s angle is off by 30°, but position/scale are perfect, will IoU be high? (Answer: No—angular error destroys overlap even with perfect alignment.)
- **Concept: Intersection-over-Union (IoU) as continuous metric**
  - *Why needed:* IoU measures rasterized polygon overlap, directly aligning with human-like visual judgment and penalizing small geometric drifts (section 3.1).
  - *Quick check:* Does a 5-pixel positional shift on a 100px triangle cause a large IoU drop? (Answer: Yes, especially if edges are thin; IoU is non-linear with displacement.)
- **Concept: Test-time self-refinement loop**
  - *Why needed:* Enables improvement without retraining; leverages VLM as a proposer and a separate geometry verifier as a reward source (section 3.3).
  - *Quick check:* If the loop threshold τ is set too low (e.g., 0.5), what happens? (Answer: Noisy corrections admitted, IoU degrades—Table 4 shows τ=0.5 yields IoU ~0.72 vs. 0.93 at τ=0.9.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Dataset Pipeline -> VLM Generator -> Geometry Verifier -> Loop Controller -> Local Search Module
- **Critical path:** For a test image: (a) load GT JSON for evaluation; (b) sample *k* ICL pairs from training set; (c) initial VLM query → parse JSON → compute IoU; (d) if IoU < τ, append feedback to prompt, requery; (e) update best candidate; (f) repeat until T or IoU ≥ τ; (g) optional local search on position; (h) output best JSON.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Loop depth (T) vs. compute:* More iterations yield marginal gains after 6 (Table 4) but increase latency.
  - *ICL window size (k) vs. noise:* k=15 optimal; k=20-25 slightly degrades (Table 5) due to redundancy.
  - *Threshold (τ) vs. stability:* High τ (0.9) ensures only clear improvements accepted; low τ admits noise.
  - *Temperature:* Zero temperature best; higher temp introduces numerical drift (Table 3, Setting 5).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Stagnation:* IoU does not increase after 2 iterations → likely initial prediction too far; consider fallback to local search earlier.
  - *Oscillation:* IoU alternates up/down → threshold too low or feedback hint ambiguous; tighten τ or clarify hint format.
  - *Context overflow:* Prompt exceeds model limits → prune old ICL examples or reduce *k*.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Run VLM-only on medium triangle subset; record mean IoU (expect ~0.65). Verify JSON parsing and IoU compute pipeline.
  2. **ICL ablation:** Set *k*=15, loop disabled; measure IoU gain vs. baseline. Test *k*=5,25 to confirm sensitivity.
  3. **Loop sensitivity:** Enable loop with T=6, τ=0.9, temp=0; record IoU. Then vary τ to {0.5,0.7,0.8} and observe degradation pattern; finally test T={2,4,6,8} to confirm saturation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the test-time self-refinement framework maintain its performance gains (IoU > 0.9) when scaled to full 7-piece Tangram assembly, or does error accumulation degrade results?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly state: "Our evaluation is limited to one- and two-piece configurations rather than full seven-piece assembly" (Section 5) and list extension to complex tasks as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** Multi-piece composition introduces combinatorial interactions and potential error compounding; the current reward design and loop parameters may not suffice for seven interdependent pieces.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement the same ICL + reward loop on a full 7-piece Tangram benchmark, comparing final IoU, convergence iterations, and failure modes against one/two-piece results.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the simple reward function R = IoU - λ·L2 optimally guide refinement, or would adding geometric constraints (angular consistency, inter-piece non-overlap, edge alignment) improve stability and final accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses a minimal reward without overlap penalties or angle regularizers, noting in limitations that future work may explore "adaptive schedules" (Section 5), suggesting the design is not fully justified.
- **Why unresolved:** Ablations only vary loop parameters and ICL settings; the reward structure itself is not tested. The omission of key geometric terms may limit convergence or final IoU.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically add geometric penalties to the reward (e.g., angular deviation, overlap penalty), measuring impact on convergence speed, final IoU, and robustness across diverse piece shapes and arrangements.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the quantitative failure boundary of the refinement loop? How large an initial error (position, angle, size) can it reliably correct, and how does this threshold vary with piece geometry?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The limitations warn of "regression risk if initial predictions are severely misaligned" (Section 5) but provide no quantitative analysis of correction capacity.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding robustness limits is essential for real-world deployment (e.g., robotics). Current results only show performance starting from VLM baselines, not from systematically degraded initial guesses.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Inject controlled noise (positional offset, angular rotation, scale distortion) into ground-truth poses, then measure success rate (IoU ≥ τ) versus noise magnitude for different Tangram pieces; identify the maximum correctable error per shape.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What specific cognitive mechanisms (e.g., mental rotation strategies, part-whole decomposition, feedback integration) explain the large performance gap between human children (who successfully solve Tangrams) and VLMs (0.23–0.41 IoU), and can these be explicitly incorporated into the refinement framework?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper repeatedly contrasts VLM failure with human success: "far below human performance where children can complete Tangram tasks successfully" (Abstract), but does not analyze the cognitive basis of this gap.
- **Why unresolved:** The work focuses on engineering a fix without dissecting why VLMs initially fail. Identifying missing cognitive components could lead to more targeted improvements beyond generic refinement loops.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct human subject studies (think-aloud protocols, eye-tracking) during Tangram solving to pinpoint strategies; then design VLM modifications (prompting, modular architectures, or training objectives) that emulate those strategies and test for improved baseline performance.

## Limitations
- **Dataset generation ambiguity:** Exact SVG sources, canonical template vertices, and the fitting procedure (Algorithm 1) are unspecified, blocking faithful reproduction.
- **Prompt and ICL sensitivity:** The sampling strategy for few-shot examples, exact prompt template (including feedback hint formatting), and JSON parsing robustness are not detailed, likely causing performance variance.
- **Narrow generalizability:** Significant gains are only demonstrated on medium-triangle single-piece tasks; performance on other shapes and two-piece arrangements remains low (IoU ≤ 0.23), limiting claims about broad continuous geometric reasoning.

## Confidence
- **VLM systematic failures in continuous geometry:** High (consistent across models and tasks in Table 1).
- **Loop-driven improvement on medium triangles:** High (ablations isolate loop contribution in Table 3).
- **Broader geometric reasoning capability:** Low/Medium (poor performance on two-piece and non-triangle shapes).
- **Reproducibility of full pipeline:** Low (critical dataset and prompt details missing).
- **Scalability to 7-piece assembly:** Very Low (no evidence; identified as future work).
