# Total Variation Rates for Riemannian Flow Matching
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05174
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05174
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: First non‑asymptotic total variation convergence analysis for Riemannian flow matching, yielding explicit bounds separating discretization error (step size) and learning error and providing polynomial iteration complexities for hypersphere and SPD manifolds.

## Executive Summary
Riemannian flow matching (RFM) extends generative flow modeling to data supported on Riemannian manifolds. While RFM provides a powerful framework for learning continuous normalizing flows on manifolds, theoretical guarantees for sampling using Euler discretization have been missing. This paper addresses this gap with the first non‑asymptotic total variation (TV) convergence analysis for RFM samplers. The core technical contribution is a differential inequality that governs the evolution of the TV distance between the true population flow and the Euler‑discretized flow; the derivative involves the divergence of the vector‑field mismatch and the score of the true density. By bounding these geometric terms using curvature, parallel transport, and smoothness assumptions on the population field and the learning error ε, the authors obtain explicit error bounds of the form TV ≤ C_Lip h + C_ε ε (with an additional ε² term on compact manifolds). These bounds cleanly separate numerical and learning errors. Instantiations on the hypersphere Sᵈ and the symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrix manifold yield polynomial iteration complexities, demonstrating the feasibility of RFM sampling in practice.

## Method Summary
The paper studies the sampling error of RFM under Euler discretization. Given a data distribution π₁ on a complete Riemannian manifold M, RFM learns a time‑dependent vector field \(\hat{v}(t,x)\) so that the ODE \(\mathrm{d}X_t = \hat{v}(t,X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t\) transports a prior π₀ to approximately π₁ at terminal time T < 1. In practice, the ODE is integrated numerically, e.g., with constant step size h: \(X_{k+1} = \operatorname{Exp}_{X_k}(h\,\hat{v}(t_k,X_k))\). The analysis introduces a continuous‑time interpolation \(\tilde{v}\) that matches the Euler scheme and couples the discretized law \(\hat{\pi}_T\) with the ideal population flow law π_T. By differentiating the TV distance and using the Riemannian continuity equation, the time derivative of TV is expressed as \(\mathbb{E}[|\operatorname{div}(\tilde{v}-v)|] + \mathbb{E}[\|\operatorname{grad}\log p_t\|\,\|\tilde{v}-v\|]\). Under geometric assumptions (curvature bounds, smoothness of v, and a learning guarantee \(\|\hat{v}-v\| \le \varepsilon\)), this derivative is bounded by constants times h and ε. Integrating from 0 to T yields the final TV bound: TV(\(\hat{\pi}_T,\pi_T\)) ≤ C_Lip h + C_ε ε (+ C_{ε²} ε² on compact manifolds). The constants C_Lip and C_ε depend on manifold curvature, the Lipschitz constants of v and its derivatives, the score regularity of the conditional density, and on moments of π₁. Concrete instantiations on Sᵈ (compact) and SPD(n) (Hadamard) give explicit iteration complexities as functions of dimension d, target accuracy ε_target, and early‑stopping time T.

## Key Results
- Derived a differential inequality for the TV evolution between two manifold ODE flows; the time derivative depends on the divergence of the vector‑field mismatch and the score of the reference density.
- Obtained a unified TV error bound: TV ≤ C_Lip h + C_ε ε (plus an ε² term on compact manifolds), separating discretization and learning errors.
- For the hypersphere Sᵈ, the iteration complexity is \(N = \mathcal{O}(d^2/(\varepsilon_{\text{target}}(1-T)^2))\) (improved to \(\mathcal{O}(d^2/(\varepsilon_{\text{target}}(1-T))\) with a step‑size schedule).
- For SPD(n), under a mild moment condition, the complexity is \(N = \mathcal{O}(d^{24} L_R^3 M_{\lambda}^{3/2}/(\varepsilon_{\text{target}} (1-T)^3))\).
- Provided early‑stopping corrections and analysis that applies to both compact and non‑compact (Hadamard) manifolds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The TV distance between the true RFM flow and the Euler‑discretized flow evolves according to a differential inequality involving the divergence of the vector‑field mismatch and the score of the true density.
- **Mechanism**: Direct differentiation of the TV integral, combined with the Riemannian continuity equation \(\partial_t p_t + \operatorname{div}(p_t v)=0\), yields an identity: \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{TV}(p_t,q_t) \le \mathbb{E}[|\operatorname{div}(\tilde{v}-v)|] + \mathbb{E}[\|\operatorname{grad}\log p_t\|\,\|\tilde{v}-v\|]\). This is integrated over time.
- **Core assumption**: The density \(p_t\) and vector fields are sufficiently smooth; the manifold has a well‑defined divergence operator; the derivative formula holds for non‑signed measures.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states the differential inequality; Lemma 5.1 provides the full derivation; Corollary 5.2 uses it to bound TV.
- **Break condition**: If the score \(\|\operatorname{grad}\log p_t\|\) blows up as \(t\to1\) or the divergence term is not integrable, the integrated bound becomes vacuous.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Under curvature bounds and regularity assumptions, the divergence and score terms can be explicitly bounded, yielding a clean error separation.
- **Mechanism**: The score regularity is controlled via analysis of the conditional density \(p_t(x_1|x)\) using geometric tools (Jacobi fields, curvature comparison). The divergence of \(\tilde{v}-v\) is bounded by differentiating the Euler interpolation map, which involves parallel transport and the inverse of \(F_{t,h}(x)=\operatorname{Exp}_x(h\hat{v}(t,x))\).
- **Core assumption**: Manifold satisfies a global curvature bound (Assumption 1); population field \(v\) is \(C^2\) (Assumption 2/4); learned field approximates \(v\) in \(L^\infty\) (compact) or \(L^2\) (Hadamard) with error ε (Assumption 3/6).
- **Evidence anchors**: Theorem 1 (compact case) and Theorem 2 (Hadamard case) present the final TV bound; Propositions 5.3 and 5.4 give score bounds; Lemma C.4 bounds the divergence control.
- **Break condition**: Very negative curvature (or large positive curvature) makes the model functions \(s_{K_{\min}}(r)\) grow exponentially, causing constants to blow up; moment conditions on π₁ are needed to compensate.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The general theory instantiates to concrete manifolds, yielding polynomial iteration complexities.
- **Mechanism**: By verifying the abstract regularity constants on specific manifolds using explicit formulas or comparison theorems, the authors derive concrete bounds: on Sᵈ, the score and divergence terms lead to a complexity polynomial in d and \(1/(1-T)\); on SPD(n), exponential curvature factors require a moment condition to keep constants finite.
- **Core assumption**: On Sᵈ, π₁ has a smooth density bounded away from 0 and above; on SPD(n), π₁ satisfies a moment condition \(\max\{\mathbb{E}[d(X_1,z)^2 e^{\lambda_1 d(X_1,z)}],\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda_1 d(X_1,z)}]\} \le M_{\lambda_1}\) for some λ₁>0.
- **Evidence anchors**: Proposition 3.1 (Sᵈ) and Proposition 4.1 (SPD); Appendices D and E contain detailed calculations of the constants.
- **Break condition**: On SPD(n), the constants involve high polynomial orders in d due to exponential curvature growth; if the moment condition fails, the constants may diverge.

## Foundational Learning
### Riemannian gradient and divergence
- **Why needed**: The flow ODE uses vector fields on the manifold; the continuity equation involves the Riemannian divergence \(\operatorname{div}v = \operatorname{tr}(\nabla v)\), and the TV bound uses both \(\|\operatorname{grad}\log p_t\|\) and \(\operatorname{div}(\tilde{v}-v)\).
- **Quick check**: For a smooth function f: M → ℝ, the Riemannian gradient \(\operatorname{grad}f\) is the metric dual of df, i.e., \(g(\operatorname{grad}f, Y)=df(Y)\) for all vector fields Y. In local coordinates, \([\operatorname{grad}f]^i = g^{ij}\partial_j f\). Given a local orthonormal frame \(\{E_i\}_{i=1}^d\), the divergence of a vector field V is \(\operatorname{div}V = \sum_{i=1}^d \langle \nabla_{E_i} V, E_i\rangle\).

### Exponential map, parallel transport, and cut locus
- **Why needed**: RFM uses geodesic interpolation: \(X_t = \operatorname{Exp}_{X_0}(t\,\operatorname{Log}_{X_0}X_1)\). Euler steps also use \(\operatorname{Exp}\). Parallel transport is needed to define the interpolated vector field \(\tilde{v}\) along the discretized trajectory, ensuring it lives in the correct tangent space.
- **Quick check**: The exponential map \(\operatorname{Exp}_x(v)\) maps \(v \in T_xM\) to the point reached by the geodesic with initial velocity v at time 1. The cut locus of x is the set of points where the exponential map ceases to be a diffeomorphism; near cut points, the interpolated density \(p_t(x_1|x)\) has a singular factor that is smoothed by the sine term on Sᵈ.

### Continuity equation on manifolds
- **Why needed**: The evolution of the density \(p_t\) under the flow is governed by \(\partial_t p_t + \operatorname{div}(p_t v)=0\). This expresses mass conservation and is crucial in the proof of Lemma 5.1 when differentiating the TV integral.
- **Quick check**: For any test function \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(M)\), \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_M \phi\, p_t\,\mathrm{d}V_g = \int_M \langle \operatorname{grad}\phi, v\rangle p_t\,\mathrm{d}V_g\) by the chain rule and definition of divergence; applying this to \(\phi = \operatorname{sign}(p_t-q_t)\) yields the TV derivative identity.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
- **Population vector field \(v(t,x)\)**: The true RFM conditional velocity, defined by \(v(t,x)= \frac{1}{1-t}\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Log}_x X_1 \mid X_t = x]\). Its smoothness (C²) and bounded derivatives are key assumptions (Assumption 2/4).
- **Learned vector field \(\hat{v}(t,x)\)**: A parametric model trained by minimizing the conditional flow matching loss. Analysis assumes \(\|\hat{v} - v\|_\infty \le \varepsilon\) (compact) or \(\mathbb{E}\|\hat{v}-v\|^2 \le \varepsilon^2\) (Hadamard).
- **Euler interpolated field \(\tilde{v}(t,x)\)**: Constructed from \(\hat{v}\) to define a continuous‑time ODE that exactly reproduces the Euler scheme on the discrete grid. Given a step size h, set \(t_k = kh\) and define \(\tilde{v}(t,x) = P_{F_{t,h}^{-1}(x)}^x \hat{v}(t_k, F_{t,h}^{-1}(x))\) for \(t\in[t_k, t_{k+1})\), where \(F_{t,h}(x) = \operatorname{Exp}_x(t\hat{v}(t_k,x))\) and \(P\) denotes parallel transport.
- **Step size condition (Lemma 5.2)**: Ensures that for all \(t\in[t_k,t_{k+1}]\), the map \(F_{t,h}\) is invertible. Requires \(h\) smaller than a constant involving the injectivity radius, a bound on \(\|\hat{v}\|\), and the Lipschitz constant \(\|\nabla \hat{v}\|\); typically \(h < \min\{R / \|\hat{v}\|_\infty,\, 1/(4L_\nabla)\}\).

### Critical path
1. Lemma 5.1: TV derivative bound for two manifold ODE flows.
2. Lemma 5.2: Invertibility of \(F_{t,h}\) establishes a valid continuous interpolation.
3. Propositions 5.3 & 5.4 (or Appendices D/E): Score regularity bounds (i.e., control of \(\mathbb{E}\|\operatorname{grad}\log p_t\|^2\)).
4. Lemma C.4: Bound on \(\mathbb{E}[|\operatorname{div}(\tilde{v}-v)|]\) using curvature and learning error.
5. Theorem 1/2: Integrate TV derivative to obtain final bound \(hC_{\mathrm{Lip}} + \varepsilon C_{\varepsilon}\) (+ ε² term).
6. Propositions 3.1 & 4.1: Instantiation on Sᵈ and SPD(n) to get iteration complexity.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Step size h vs. accuracy**: Smaller h reduces the \(C_{\mathrm{Lip}}h\) term but increases the number of steps N = (T–0)/h. The TV bound suggests choosing \(h = \tilde{O}(\varepsilon)\) to balance both sources.
- **Early stopping time T**: Constants involve factors \(1/(1-T)\)^p; as T → 1 the bound blows up, forcing early stopping and thereby sacrificing exact recovery of π₁.
- **Manifold curvature**: Negative curvature (Hadamard) introduces exponential factors (e.g., \(\sinh(\sqrt{|K_{\min}|}r)\)) that enlarge constants unless compensated by moment conditions on π₁. Positive curvature counterparts behave similarly.
- **Learning error ε**: The analysis requires uniform (compact) or mean‑squared (Hadamard) pointwise control of \(\hat{v}-v\) and its gradient. Achieving small ε may demand larger networks or more data, and the multiplicative constants \(C_{\varepsilon}\) depend on geometric quantities that often scale polynomially with dimension.

### Failure signatures
- **Invertibility violation**: If h is too large, the map \(F_{t,h}\) may fail to be a diffeomorphism (e.g., crossing the cut locus on Sᵈ), breaking the definition of \(\tilde{v}\) and the TV bound.
- **Score blow‑up**: As t → 1, the conditional density \(p_t(\cdot|x)\) may concentrate near a point, causing \(\|\operatorname{grad}\log p_t\|\) to diverge; the score bound in Proposition 5.3/5.4 would then be infinite.
- **Curvature violation**: If the manifold’s curvature lies outside the assumed interval \([K_{\min},0]\), the comparison theorems used to bound Jacobi fields and model functions \(s_{K_{\min}}(r)\) no longer hold, causing constants to become invalid.
- **Large learning error**: If the approximation ε is not sufficiently small relative to h, the bound \(C_{\mathrm{Lip}}h + C_{\varepsilon}\varepsilon\) will be dominated by the ε term and may not decay even with tiny h.
- **Numerical instability**: On SPD(n), inaccuracies in matrix logarithm/exponential or parallel transport can lead to non‑positive‑definite points or loss of orthogonality.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Verify Lemma 5.1 in a Euclidean toy model**: Implement 1‑dimensional FM with known \(p_0, p_1\) (e.g., Gaussian to Gaussian). Numerically approximate the TV derivative via finite differences over a fine time grid and compare against the theoretical expression \(\mathbb{E}[|\operatorname{div}(\tilde{v}-v)|] + \mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\log p_t\|\|\tilde{v}-v\|]\).
2. **Test step size condition on Sᵈ**: For a trained RFM model on the sphere, estimate bounds on \(\|\hat{v}\|\) and \(\|\nabla\hat{v}\|\). Verify that with chosen h (e.g., \(h=0.01\)), the condition \(h < \min\{R/\|\hat{v}\|_\infty, 1/(4L_\nabla)\}\) holds, and that Euler trajectories remain on the manifold and do not exhibit pathological jumps.
3. **Separate discretization and learning errors on Sᵈ**: Use an analytically known population field v (e.g., for a von Mises–Fisher target) to isolate discretization error: vary h while keeping T fixed, plot TV vs. h to check linearity. Then fix a very small h (so discretization error negligible), train \(\hat{v}\) with varying model capacities to achieve different ε (measured as \(\|\hat{v}-v\|_{L^2}\) on a validation set), and plot TV vs. ε. Compare empirical slopes with the theoretical constants \(C_{\mathrm{Lip}}\) and \(C_{\varepsilon}\) (which can be estimated from problem parameters as in Lemma D.5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can the learning error ε be quantified as a function of network architecture and training data size? The paper treats ε as an abstract constant.
  - *Basis*: Assumptions 3 and 6 require ε but do not provide a way to attain it; the analysis ignores approximation theory.
  - *Why unresolved*: Without connecting ε to neural network representation, the total computational cost of RFM (training + sampling) remains uncharacterized.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Approximation rates for manifold‑valued neural networks and generalization bounds expressing ε in terms of width, depth, and sample size, combined with the TV bound, yielding end‑to‑end guarantees.
- **Open Question 2**: Can the dimension dependence d²⁴ on SPD(n) be reduced to a more practical polynomial (e.g., linear or quadratic) via sharper analysis?
  - *Basis*: The paper acknowledges that “although the order of d is polynomial, we expect it could be improved further by more refined computation and additional assumptions.” (after Proposition 4.1).
  - *Why unresolved*: Current proof uses coarse bounds on curvature, derivatives, and divergence, likely inflating the exponent; true dependence is probably much lower.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Sharper geometric estimates—especially for ∇²Exp, parallel transport distortion, and score regularity—that tighten constants and lower the polynomial degree dramatically.
- **Open Question 3**: Does RFM admit direct Wasserstein (e.g., W₁) convergence bounds with polynomial complexity that avoid the diam(M) blow‑up from TV→W₁ conversion?
  - *Basis*: The paper notes that on a compact manifold, TV bound implies a W₁ bound via \(\mathrm{W}_1 \le \mathrm{diam}(M)\,\mathrm{TV}\), which introduces a potentially large factor.
  - *Why unresolved*: That inequality may be loose; a direct analysis could yield tighter, geometry‑intrinsic bounds.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Adapt the TV stability Lemma 5.1 to Wasserstein distance or develop coupling techniques tailored to manifold flows to obtain polynomial W₁ bounds with mild geometric dependence.

## Limitations
- Strong assumptions: requires smooth population vector field v, uniform/L² learning error ε, curvature bounds, and moment conditions on π₁; these may not hold in high dimensions or for heavy‑tailed data.
- Step‑size condition (Lemma 5.2) depends on unknown constants (injectivity radius, bounds on ∇\(\hat{v}\)), often forcing overly small h.
- Constants blow up as T → 1, necessitating early stopping and potentially sacrificing fidelity to π₁.
- SPD(n) complexity scales as d²⁴, which is likely pessimistic and may be prohibitive for moderate dimensions.
- Reliable TV estimation from finite samples on manifolds is challenging and adds statistical error not accounted for in the theory.
