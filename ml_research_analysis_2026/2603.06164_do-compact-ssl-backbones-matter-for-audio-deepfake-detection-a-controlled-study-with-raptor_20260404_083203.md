# Do Compact SSL Backbones Matter for Audio Deepfake Detection? A Controlled Study with RAPTOR

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.06164  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06164  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Iterative multilingual HuBERT pre-training (mHuBERT-Iter2, ~100M parameters) achieves the best cross-domain robustness across 14 benchmarks, matching or outperforming larger wav2vec2-XLSR (300M) and commercial systems on pooled EER.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether compact self-supervised learning (SSL) audio encoders can match the performance of larger models for audio deepfake detection. It does so by fixing the downstream detector (RAPTOR, a pairwise-gated fusion architecture) to isolate the effects of SSL pre-training. Across systematic evaluations on 14 benchmarks, the study finds that an iteratively refined multilingual HuBERT checkpoint (mHuBERT-Iter2, ~100M parameters) delivers the strongest cross-domain robustness, equaling or exceeding wav2vec2-XLSR (300M) and commercial systems under a pooled equal error rate (EER). Additional analysis using test-time augmentation (TTA) reveals that WavLM-based variants suffer from overconfident miscalibration—showing large EER degradation under perturbation while maintaining low aleatoric uncertainty—whereas mHuBERT-Iter2 remains stable. The work concludes that SSL pre-training trajectory, not model scale, is the dominant factor for reliable deepfake detection.

## Method Summary
The study employs a fixed downstream detector called RAPTOR, which fuses all transformer layer outputs of a pretrained SSL encoder via pairwise gating and hierarchical fusion, followed by attention pooling and a binary classifier. The authors compare several SSL families (HuBERT, multilingual mHuBERT at different iterative stages, and WavLM) under two training protocols: Protocol 1 (ASVspoof 2019 only) and Protocol 2 (combined data from ASVspoof 2019, 2024, CodecFake, LibriSeV oc, ADD, and other tracks). All models are fine-tuned jointly using a classification loss and a consistency regularization term (Jensen–Shannon divergence on gate distributions between clean and augmented views). Evaluation covers 14 test sets, reporting both per-dataset and pooled EER. Additionally, test-time augmentation (TTA) with three perturbations (VoIP codec, noise, speed–pitch) estimates aleatoric uncertainty (Uale, mean prediction entropy) and perturbation sensitivity (ΔEER).

## Key Results
- mHuBERT-Iter2 (~100M) achieves the lowest pooled EER (11.72%) among all tested models, outperforming wav2vec2-XLSR (300M, 12.46%) and a commercial system (12.74%).
- WavLM variants exhibit a distinct calibration failure: high ΔEER (e.g., +13.88% on the ITW set) paired with low Uale, indicating overconfident miscalibration under perturbation; mHuBERT-Iter2 remains stable (+0.38% ΔEER, higher Uale).
- mHuBERT-Final shows a pronounced regression on codec-based benchmarks (e.g., 25.68% EER on CodecFake vs. 14.04% for Iter2), suggesting a trade-off between linguistic diversity and sensitivity to low-level acoustic artifacts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Three key mechanisms underpin the findings. First, iterative multilingual SSL pre-training enhances cross-domain robustness by exposing the encoder to diverse phonetic and acoustic patterns across languages, improving generalization to unseen synthesis methods and codecs. This is evidenced by systematic EER improvements from HuBERT-Base to mHuBERT-Iter1 and Iter2 across 14 benchmarks, with a regression at Final indicating a potential sensitivity–diversity trade-off. Second, compact (~100M) SSL backbones can match or exceed larger models (300M–2B) when the pre-training trajectory is optimal. The fixed RAPTOR architecture’s ceiling is set by backbone representation quality, not parameter count; mHuBERT-Iter2 surpasses wav2vec2-XLSR and a 2B system despite being three times smaller. Third, TTA-derived aleatoric uncertainty (Uale) effectively exposes backbone-specific calibration failures: low entropy across perturbed views combined with high ΔEER signals an overconfident model. WavLM’s pattern suggests its representations do not appropriately reflect input variability, while mHuBERT’s stability indicates better-calibrated uncertainty under distribution shift.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** SSL encoder (varying backbone/family/stage) → RAPTOR pairwise-gated fusion (layer-pair softmax gates → hierarchical fusion) → consistency regularization (JS divergence on gate distributions between clean/augmented views) → TTA module (K=3 perturbed views → mean posterior ¯p(x) and Uale) → binary classifier (sigmoid output).  
- **Critical path:** SSL forward pass (all L layers) → pairwise gating (Eq.1) → hierarchical gating → attention pooling → classifier → training loss (L_cls + λL_cons). Inference: clean pass or TTA (augment → forward → aggregate).  
- **Design tradeoffs:** Pairwise gating provides adaptive artifact selection but adds parameters and risks overfitting gate distributions. TTA reveals calibration at the cost of 3× compute. Consistency regularization (λ=0.25) stabilizes gates but may constrain representational capacity.  
- **Failure signatures:** 1) High ΔEER + low Uale (WavLM overconfident miscalibration). 2) Non-monotonic mHuBERT-Final regression on codec sets (loss of low-level sensitivity). 3) Poor cross-domain EER despite good in-domain (failure to generalize).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What specific mechanisms in iterative multilingual pre-training enable mHuBERT-Iter2’s superior robustness compared to both monolingual HuBERT and WavLM with larger pre-training data, beyond the general benefit of multilingual exposure?  
2. Why does mHuBERT-Final regress substantially on codec-based benchmarks (e.g., CodecFake) compared to mHuBERT-Iter2, revealing a non-monotonic pre-training trajectory?  
3. How would incorporating epistemic uncertainty estimation (e.g., Bayesian inference or ensembles) alter the calibration conclusions about WavLM variants’ overconfident miscalibration under perturbation?  
4. Can the qualitative pairwise gate activation patterns in RAPTOR be quantitatively linked to specific synthesis artifact types (e.g., vocoder, codec, or neural synthesis artifacts) across SSL layers?

## Limitations
- RawBoost and TTA augmentation parameters are not fully specified, requiring reconstruction from cited work.  
- The exact composition and file lists for Protocol 2’s multi-dataset training pool are not itemized.  
- The architecture of the pairwise/hierarchical gating modules and attention pooling remains vague (e.g., hidden dimensions, activation functions).

## Confidence
- **Claim 1:** Iterative multilingual SSL pre-training enhances cross-domain robustness. *Confidence: Medium.* Systematic gains from HuBERT-Base to mHuBERT-Iter2, but regression at mHuBERT-Final and missing pre-training details reduce certainty.  
- **Claim 2:** Compact SSL backbones can match larger models given optimal pre-training. *Confidence: Medium.* mHuBERT-Iter2 (~100M) outperforms wav2vec2-XLSR (300M) and a 2B system on pooled EER; conclusions depend on fixed RAPTOR and the evaluation suite.  
- **Claim 3:** TTA-derived Uale exposes calibration failures. *Confidence: Low.* The decoupling is shown for WavLM, but Uale’s predictive validity for unseen shifts remains unverified.
