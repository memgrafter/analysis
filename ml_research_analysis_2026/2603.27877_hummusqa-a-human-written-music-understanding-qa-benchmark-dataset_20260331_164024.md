# HumMusQA: A Human-written Music Understanding QA Benchmark Dataset

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.27877
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27877
*   **Reference count:** 22
*   **Primary result:** Human-authored, peer-validated music QA questions expose that leading LALMs rely on text-only shortcuts; top model (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B) achieves 64.3% accuracy with real audio but maintains >42% with noise/silence.

## Executive Summary
HumMusQA addresses a critical flaw in existing music understanding benchmarks: their reliance on automated generation allows models to answer questions using linguistic shortcuts instead of genuine audio perception. The authors introduce a benchmark of 320 multiple-choice questions, each paired with a Creative Commons music excerpt and authored by music experts who underwent blind peer review to ensure answers require listening. Evaluation of six large audio-language models (LALMs) reveals that while performance improves with real audio, all models retain high accuracy (42-47%) even when provided with noise or silence, confirming pervasive shortcut reliance. Performance degrades significantly with question difficulty and on theory-heavy categories like Harmony and Melody, suggesting models are better at contextual reasoning than analytical music perception.

## Method Summary
The research constructs HumMusQA through a three-stage expert curation process: initial question writing by domain specialists, blind peer review to eliminate questions answerable without listening, and final validation. Each question is linked to a 30-90 second audio excerpt from Jamendo, labeled by one of 13 musical categories and a difficulty level (Low/Medium/High) annotated with GPT-5 and expert-checked. To evaluate LALMs, the authors implement a robust protocol: for each question, models are queried four times with randomly shuffled multiple-choice options. A separate LLM (Gemini-2.5-flash) parses the model's free-form response into an option letter. Primary metrics are average accuracy and consistency (percentage of questions answered identically across all four runs). Crucially, two control conditions replace real audio with Gaussian noise or silence to quantify shortcut reliance.

## Key Results
*   **Qwen2.5-Omni-7B** is the top performer with **64.3% ±2.9** accuracy and **66.6%** consistency on real audio.
*   **Performance drops with difficulty:** Low (73.9%), Medium (58.1%), High (53.2%).
*   **Shortcut vulnerability is severe:** All models maintain **~42-47%** accuracy with noise/silence, well above the 25% random chance baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Expert human annotation and peer-validation create questions that genuinely require audio perception, reducing vulnerability to text-only shortcuts.
*   **Mechanism:** Music experts author questions grounded in specific audio excerpts, then undergo blind peer review to ensure each question has one unambiguous answer resolvable *only* through listening. This process eliminates questions answerable via world knowledge or linguistic cues alone.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "designed to require actual listening"
    *   [section 2.1] "Experts played a dual role... validating each other’s work through iterative peer review... until no further comments or disagreements were raised"
    *   [corpus] Corpus evidence is weak for this specific curation mechanism; neighboring papers (e.g., MMAU-Pro) also use expert curation but do not detail the blind-review protocol's efficacy against shortcuts.
*   **Break condition:** If blind review fails to eliminate questions that text-only models answer near chance (25%), the mechanism is compromised.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Difficulty labeling and category-based analysis expose systematic model weaknesses in music-theory reasoning versus contextual/cultural reasoning.
*   **Mechanism:** Questions are labeled by required knowledge level (Low/Medium/High) and 13 musical categories. Performance degradation from Low→High difficulty and lower scores in "Harmony/Melody/Performance" vs "Genre/Mood" categories indicates models rely more on cultural priors than analytical perception.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "Performance drops with difficulty: low 73.9%, medium 58.1%, high 53.2%. Theory-heavy categories... yield lower scores than contextual ones."
    *   [section 2.2] Describes the LLM-assisted (GPT-5) annotation of difficulty and categories, validated by experts.
    *   [corpus] Neighbors like MUGEN and MMAR also analyze performance by task difficulty, supporting this as a diagnostic approach.
*   **Break condition:** If High-difficulty questions are later found to be answerable by text priors at >50% (as noted in section 3.2), the difficulty labeling is invalid.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Replacing real audio with noise or silence tests for "uni-modal shortcut" reliance, revealing the perception gap.
*   **Mechanism:** If a model's accuracy remains significantly above random chance (25%) when provided noise/silence instead of real audio, it demonstrates the model is exploiting textual or prior knowledge cues rather than auditory perception.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "Models still score above chance with noise/silence audio, confirming vulnerability to language shortcuts."
    *   [section 3.2] Table 2 shows all models maintain ~42-47% accuracy with noise/silence vs ~55-64% with real audio.
    *   [corpus] Zang et al. (2025, neighbor) similarly uses fake audio to test perceptual awareness, establishing this as a community standard.
*   **Break condition:** If future work shows some models can legitimately answer certain questions from text descriptions of audio (e.g., highly formulaic music), the shortcut interpretation becomes nuanced.

## Foundational Learning

*   **Concept: Music Theory Literacy (e.g., harmony, form, notation)**
    *   **Why needed here:** To understand why models fail on "Harmony" and "Melody" categories (section 3, Fig 1) and to interpret high-difficulty questions like "What intervals create dissonance?" or "Which interval doesn't belong to the key?". Without this, one cannot grasp the analytical depth the benchmark demands.
    *   **Quick check question:** What is the difference between a major and minor triad in terms of interval structure, and how would this difference manifest audibly?

*   **Concept: Perceptual vs. Analytical Listening**
    *   **Why needed here:** The core thesis is that models lack *perceptual* grounding. Distinguishing hearing a "flute" (perceptual identification) from analyzing "flutter tonguing" (analytical, technique-specific) is key to understanding the category performance gaps.
    *   **Quick check question:** When listening to a jazz saxophone solo, can you identify the instrument (perceptual) and also detect if the player uses growling or altissimo (analytic)?

*   **Concept: Benchmark Construction & Validity (construct, content, internal validity)**
    *   **Why needed here:** To evaluate whether HumMusQA's design (expert curation, peer review, difficulty labels) truly measures "music understanding" versus "music-related reasoning." This frames the entire paper's contribution and its limitations (e.g., residual shortcut vulnerability).
    *   **Quick check question:** What is a "language shortcut" in a multimodal QA benchmark, and how does randomizing answer order help control for it?

## Architecture Onboarding

*   **Component map:** Audio Encoder -> Projection/Fusion Layer -> LLM Backbone -> Reasoning Mechanism (optional) -> Output Parser.
*   **Critical path:** `Audio file -> (Preprocessing) -> Audio Encoder -> Projection/Fusion -> [Audio representation + Text tokens] -> LLM -> (Parsing) -> Final Answer Letter`. The fusion step is often the bottleneck for true multimodal grounding.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Context Length vs. Computation:** Longer audio (e.g., Qwen2.5-Omni's 32k tokens) enables full-track reasoning but increases memory. HumMusQA uses 30-90s excerpts to manage this.
    *   **Specialization vs. Generalization:** Music Flamingo is music-specialized but may underperform on general audio. Qwen2.5-Omni is a generalist but achieved top music scores.
    *   **Openness vs. Performance:** Open models (Qwen, Music Flamingo) are reproducible but lag behind closed (GPT-Audio, Gemini) in absolute accuracy.
    *   **CoT Depth:** Explicit, structured CoT (AudioSemThinker, Music Flamingo) may improve reasoning on high-difficulty questions but increases latency and may not be used by default.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    1.  **High sensitivity to option order:** Low "consistency" scores (e.g., Music Flamingo 39.1%) indicate the model is not robustly reasoning about the content.
    2.  **Minimal performance drop with noise/silence:** >40% accuracy means the model is not using the audio input meaningfully.
    3.  **Sharp accuracy drop from Low→High difficulty:** If High-difficulty performance is near random, the model lacks the specific theoretical knowledge the benchmark targets.
    4.  **Category-specific failure:** Very low scores in "Harmony" or "Performance" reveal gaps in specific music-theory perceptual skills.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Accuracy with Real Audio:** Run all models on the 320 questions with randomized option order (4 runs). Compute average accuracy and consistency. Goal: Replicate Table 1. Check if Qwen2.5-Omni remains top and if consistency correlates with accuracy.
    2.  **Shortcut Robustness Test:** Repeat experiment 1 with two control conditions: (a) Gaussian noise audio, (b) silent audio. Compute accuracy drop. Goal: Replicate Table 2. Expectation: All models should drop to ~25% if truly perceptual; >35% indicates severe shortcutting.
    3.  **Difficulty & Category Ablation:** Analyze results by difficulty level (Low/Med/High) and primary category (Fig 1). Goal: Validate the paper's finding of theory-heavy category weakness. Use this to diagnose if your own model's weaknesses match known LALM limitations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

*   **Open Question 1:** Why do human-authored music QA questions in HumMusQA remain solvable by text-only language models without audio input?
    *   **Basis in paper:** The authors state in the conclusion: "We leave a more detailed analysis of what makes human-authored questions solvable from text alone to future work."
    *   **Why unresolved:** Understanding the textual cues that enable correct answers without listening is critical for designing benchmarks that genuinely require audio perception rather than exploiting language priors.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation studies where question text is modified to remove identified cues (e.g., statistical likelihood phrases, distractor implausibility), combined with fine-grained model probing (e.g., attention analysis) to isolate which textual features models rely on.

*   **Open Question 2:** Why are high-difficulty questions in HumMusQA still answered correctly by text-only models at approximately 50% accuracy?
    *   **Basis in paper:** The authors note: "follow-up studies could analyse why items labelled as high difficulty are still answered correctly by a text-only model in roughly 50% of cases."
    *   **Why unresolved:** This indicates that even expert-authored, theory-heavy questions contain textual shortcuts, undermining their validity as true tests of music perception; resolving this would improve annotation guidelines.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Correlational analysis between high-difficulty question features (e.g., specificity of terminology, distractor similarity, presence of cultural references) and text-only model accuracy, followed by iterative redesign to balance option plausibility and re-evaluation.

*   **Open Question 3:** What specific deficiencies in LALMs cause systematically lower performance on music theory-heavy categories (e.g., harmony, melody) compared to contextual categories (e.g., genre, mood)?
    *   **Basis in paper:** Results show accuracy drops in theory categories; authors suggest models are "stronger at cultural and contextual reasoning than at formal analytical tasks." This implies a gap in perceptual or analytical capabilities.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Identifying whether the gap stems from insufficient audio perceptual grounding, lack of music theory knowledge in training, or architectural limitations is key to targeted model improvement.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments using simplified audio (e.g., isolated instruments, synthetic examples) or symbolic representations (e.g., MIDI) to decouple perceptual demands from reasoning, combined with analysis of model training data composition and internal representations.

*   **Open Question 4:** What design principles for human-authored music QA questions most effectively eliminate text-only shortcuts while maintaining diversity and difficulty?
    *   **Basis in paper:** The robustness tests reveal models exploit "weak question phrasing and distractors" (e.g., implausible options, statistical cues), and authors note the need to refine annotation procedures.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Current human-authored questions still permit non-perceptual solutions; establishing robust design principles would yield benchmarks that more validly measure auditory intelligence.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Iterative redesign of a question subset with enforced constraints (e.g., all distractors equally plausible from text alone, avoidance of lexical cues), then measuring the reduction in text-only model performance compared to audio-included performance across multiple models.

## Limitations

*   Dataset size (320 questions) may limit statistical power and genre coverage.
*   Peer-review protocol effectiveness against textual shortcuts is not fully verified; above-chance noise performance persists.
*   Difficulty and category labels were GPT-5-assisted; risk of model-driven bias in annotation.

## Confidence

*   **Benchmark design forces genuine listening:** Medium (expert curation helps, but shortcut vulnerability remains).
*   **Models exploit language shortcuts:** High (consistent above-chance accuracy with noise/silence across all LALMs).
*   **Performance drops with difficulty and theory-heavy categories:** High (robust trends in empirical results).
*   **Qwen2.5-Omni-7B is top performer:** High (replicated across runs and metrics).
