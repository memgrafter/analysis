# Trajectory-Optimized Time Reparameterization for Learning-Compatible Reduced-Order Modeling of Stiff Dynamical Systems
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16583
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16583
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: TOTR produces smoother time reparameterizations, reducing τ-MSE by 1–2 orders of magnitude and achieving lower reparameterization‑invariant MSIE across three stiff benchmarks compared to baseline methods.

## Executive Summary
Stiffness in neural ODE reduced‑order models (ML‑ROMs) causes explicit integration to become unstable and implicit methods computationally expensive. This paper introduces Trajectory‑Optimized Time Reparameterization (TOTR), which constructs a time map by optimizing a traversal speed profile in arc‑length coordinates to directly minimize acceleration in stretched time. The resulting reparameterizations yield smoother dynamics that are easier to learn with explicit integrators. TOTR is evaluated on three stiff benchmarks—a parameterized linear system, the Van der Pol oscillator, and the HIRES chemical kinetics model—against solver‑directed and extrema‑based methods. Across all cases TOTR achieves orders‑of‑magnitude reductions in nondimensional τ‑MSE and consistently lower MSIE, particularly in severe stiffness regimes. Moreover, TOTR generates parameter‑stationary clocks that are robust to integration tolerance variations, while TOTR’s arc‑length formulation avoids the ill‑conditioning of direct τ‑space optimization. The results underscore that effective stiffness mitigation for learning requires optimizing time maps for smoothness and learnability, not merely reducing stiffness.

## Method Summary
TOTR transforms physical time to stretched time \( \tau \) by constructing a monotonic time map \( t(\tau) \). The core idea is to represent the augmented state trajectory \( \phi(t) = [y(t), t] \) in arc‑length \( s \) and optimize a traversal speed \( v(s) > 0 \) (with \( d\tau = ds / v \)). The objective \( \int \bigl( v\, v_s^2 + \kappa^2 v^3 \bigr) ds \) penalizes tangential and curvature‑induced acceleration in \( \tau \), subject to the total stretched time constraint \( \tau_f = \int ds / v \). Positivity of \( v \) is enforced via \( v = \exp(a + v^*(s)) \); scalar \( a \) adjusts to meet \( \tau_f \). After optimization, \( \tau(s) = \int_0^s 1/v(\sigma) d\sigma \) is computed and inverted to obtain \( s(\tau) \). The reparameterized trajectories \( \tilde{y}(\tau) = y(s(\tau)) \) and \( \tilde{t}(\tau) = t(s(\tau)) \) are then interpolated onto a uniform \( \tau \) grid. These data train a neural ODE with two networks: \( f_{\text{NN}}(\tilde{z},\tau;\mu) \) for dynamics and \( \alpha_{\text{NN}}(\tilde{z},\tau;\mu) \) (positive via exponential) for the time warp. Training uses the nondimensional τ‑MSE loss with a Latin hypercube search over architectures, learning rates, and prediction horizons. The arc‑length formulation avoids the severe ill‑conditioning of directly discretizing \( \int \| d^2\phi/d\tau^2 \|^2 d\tau \), whose finite‑difference scaling would be \( (\Delta\tau)^{-4} \).

## Key Results
- TOTR reduces nondimensional τ‑MSE by 1–2 orders of magnitude compared to solver‑directed and extrema‑based methods on all three stiff benchmarks.
- TOTR achieves lower reparameterization‑invariant MSIE, especially in severe stiffness regimes (e.g., HIRES τ‑MSE ~\(10^{-5}\) vs ~\(10^{-3}\) for extrema‑based).
- TOTR produces parameter‑stationary time maps robust to integration tolerance variations, while solver‑directed methods are highly sensitive.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Smoothness via Acceleration Minimization
*Claim:* TOTR reduces stiffness in neural ODE training by constructing a time reparameterization that directly minimizes acceleration in stretched time \( \tau \), producing smoother dynamics for explicit integration.  
*Mechanism:* Formulating an optimization problem in arc‑length coordinates \( s \) over a traversal speed profile \( v(s) = ds/d\tau \), the objective \( \int \bigl( v v_s^2 + \kappa^2 v^3 \bigr) ds \) penalizes both tangential acceleration (\( v v_s \)) and curvature‑induced acceleration (\( \kappa v^2 \)). Minimizing this reduces \( d^2\phi/d\tau^2 \), yielding reparameterized trajectories with lower velocity variability and curvature in \( \tau \).  
*Core assumption:* Smooth stretched‑time dynamics improve learnability and stability for explicit integrators used during neural ODE training.  
*Evidence:* Abstract and Section 3.1 state that targeting smoothness of the integrated dynamics produces better‑conditioned time maps.  
*Break condition:* If the arc‑length parametrization is highly irregular, the optimization may produce unrealistic \( v(s) \) or violate the \( \tau_f \) constraint.

### Mechanism 2: Solver‑Independent Stationarity
*Claim:* TOTR decouples time map construction from solver‑specific artifacts, yielding parameter‑stationary clocks that generalize better across parameter variations than solver‑directed methods.  
*Mechanism:* Instead of inheriting \( t(\tau) \) from an implicit solver’s tolerance‑dependent time grid (noisy), TOTR operates purely on the geometric curve \( (y(t), t) \) in arc‑length space. The optimized \( v(s) \) is intrinsic to the trajectory shape, not numerical integration parameters, producing smoother and more consistent \( \tau(s) \) and \( t(\tau) \) across small \( \mu \)‑variations.  
*Core assumption:* A smooth, parameter‑stationary time map leads to more robust neural network learning of \( f_{\text{NN}} \) and \( \alpha_{\text{NN}} \), as target dynamics in \( \tau \) vary coherently across the parameter space.  
*Evidence:* Sections 5.1 and 6 show that solver‑directed reparameterizations change drastically with tolerance, while TOTR and extrema‑based clocks remain stationary.  
*Break condition:* In systems where stiffness topology changes dramatically with \( \mu \), a single smooth \( v(s) \) may be insufficient.

### Mechanism 3: Well‑Conditioned Arc‑Length Formulation
*Claim:* TOTR’s optimization in arc‑length coordinates avoids the ill‑conditioning of directly optimizing \( t(\tau) \) with finite differences, enabling stable gradient‑based solution.  
*Mechanism:* Direct discretization of \( \int \| d^2\phi/d\tau^2 \|^2 d\tau \) introduces \( (\Delta\tau)^{-4} \) factors. Reformulating in \( s \) with \( d\tau = ds/v(s) \) yields the well‑scaled integral \( \int (v v_s^2 + \kappa^2 v^3) ds \), which remains numerically stable even in stiff regions where \( \Delta\tau \) would be tiny.  
*Core assumption:* The arc‑length reformulation preserves the acceleration minimization intent while producing a discretization amenable to gradient‑based optimization.  
*Evidence:* Section 3.1 compares the two formulations and derives the \( s \)‑domain objective via Frenet‑Serret formulas.  
*Break condition:* Poor estimation of \( s \) (e.g., noisy \( y(t) \)) makes \( \kappa(s) \) and \( v_s \) noisy and may reintroduce ill‑conditioning; pre‑smoothing may be required.

## Foundational Learning
**Neural ODEs (NODEs)**  
*Why needed here:* TOTR integrates into the NODE framework; understanding backpropagation through ODE solvers and adjoint methods is essential to see why stiffness and non‑uniform \( \tau \) sampling are problematic.  
*Quick check:* Why would an adaptive‑step implicit solver within a NODE training loop increase computational cost and potentially degrade gradient accuracy?

**Time Reparameterization (TR)**  
*Why needed here:* TR transforms \( t \) to \( \tau \) so stiff physical‑time transients are spread over a larger \( \tau \) interval, enabling stable explicit integration on a uniform grid. Grasping the chain‑rule transformation \( dy/d\tau = \alpha f \) and the role of \( \alpha > 0 \) is key.  
*Quick check:* How does a small \( \alpha(\tau) \) in a stiff region affect the effective eigenvalue magnitudes of the vector field in \( \tau \), and why does this help explicit integration?

**Arc‑Length Parameterization**  
*Why needed here:* TOTR’s optimization is posed in \( s \) to avoid ill‑conditioning. Arc‑length \( s(t) = \int \sqrt{1 + \|dy/dt\|^2} dt \) is a geometric invariant measuring cumulative state variation. Understanding that \( s \) removes dependence on the original time scaling is crucial.  
*Quick check:* If two physical‑time trajectories \( y_1(t) \) and \( y_2(t) = y_1(\lambda t) \) describe the same geometric path but with different speeds, how would their arc‑length parameterizations relate?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Preprocessor → Curvature Estimator → Traversal Speed Optimizer → Time Map Generator → Reparameterized Trajectory Builder → Neural ODE Trainer

**Critical Path:**  
FOM trajectory → arc‑length \( s \) → optimize \( v(s) \) → construct \( \tau(s) \) and \( s(\tau) \) → generate \( (\tau, \tilde{y}, t) \) → train NODE.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- τ_f selection: Larger τ_f yields smoother \( v(s) \) but requires more τ steps for the same physical resolution, increasing training cost. Smaller τ_f may force aggressive stretching, reintroducing stiffness in τ.
- Arc‑length discretization: Finer s‑grid captures curvature better but increases optimization dimension; coarse grid may smooth out critical stiff events.
- Curvature estimation: Sensitivity to noise in the interpolated trajectory \( \bar{\phi}(s) \); pre‑smoothing of \( y(t) \) may be required for noisy full‑order data.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Cusp in \( t(\tau) \): indicates \( dt/d\tau \) has a discontinuity; typical for solver‑directed method (Figures 7, 8).
- Oscillatory \( dt/d\tau) \): extrema‑based method can produce rapid derivative oscillations (Figure 8b).
- Premature τ termination: solver‑directed may cut simulation short if physical‑time acceleration spikes (HIRES, Section 5.4).
- Off‑reference degradation: if \( v(s) \) overfits a single μ, performance drops for new μ.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. Baseline comparison on stiff linear system (SLS): Run TOTR, solver‑directed, and extrema‑based for \( \mu = 10^{3.95} \) (extreme stiffness). Inspect \( t(\tau) \) and \( dt/d\tau \); expect TOTR smooth and monotonic, others with cusps/oscillations. Check τ‑MSE and MSIE tables.
2. Noise robustness test: Generate Van der Pol FOM data with loose integration tolerance (\( 10^{-3} \)). Apply all TR methods; compare \( t(\tau) \) across tolerances (\( 10^{-3} \) vs \( 10^{-6} \)); expect TOTR and extrema‑based to be stationary, solver‑directed to vary wildly.
3. Off‑reference generalization: Train NODEs on a μ‑set \( \{10^{2}, 10^{2.04}, \dots\} \) for each TR method. Test on \( \mu_{\text{test}} = 10^{3.33} \) (Van der Pol) or \( \mu_{\text{test}} = 10^{3.975} \) (HIRES). Compare MSIE; TOTR should degrade least.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How do different time reparameterization strategies impact the learnability and generalization of neural ODE‑based ROMs?**  
   *Basis:* Page 2: “their effect on learnability in neural ODE–based ROMs remains incompletely understood.”  
   *Why unresolved:* TOTR yields lower loss but the causal link between smoothness metrics (e.g., acceleration) and neural network trainability is not quantified; no analysis of optimization landscapes, gradient distributions, or parameter sensitivity.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Empirical studies measuring gradient norms, Hessian spectra, or training convergence rates across TR methods; theoretical work relating time map regularity to condition number of the training problem.

2. **What is the systematic impact of time reparameterization formulation choices (e.g., arc‑length weighting, objective terms) on fidelity and robustness?**  
   *Basis:* Page 2: “the formulation, optimization, and deployment of time transformations—and their impact on fidelity for full‑order dynamics—have not been systematically studied.”  
   *Why unresolved:* TOTR introduces design choices (equal state‑time weighting, acceleration penalty) not compared against alternatives; unclear whether other weightings or penalties could improve performance or generality.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Sensitivity analysis varying arc‑length component weights; comparing alternative smoothness penalties; evaluating on a broader benchmark suite.

3. **Under what conditions can time reparameterizations achieve both smoothness in stretched time and consistent parametric dependence?**  
   *Basis:* Page 10: observed parameter‑stationary clocks for Van der Pol are a special feature of asymptotic relaxation regimes; “in many systems… the stiffness structure evolves with parameters, and reparameterizations that are both smooth and parameter‑independent may not exist.”  
   *Why unresolved:* No analysis of when smooth parameterizations are impossible nor proposals for systems with parameter‑dependent stiffness topology.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Characterization of parameter‑stiffness manifolds; metrics predicting existence of smooth parameterizations; adaptive reparameterization methods that adjust to stiffness changes.

4. **How do spline order and construction details affect the learnability of the extrema‑based method?**  
   *Basis:* Page 24: “For the extrema‑based baseline only the cubic‑spline construction was considered… The quintic‑spline variant… could potentially improve learnability… A systematic comparison between spline orders is an important direction.”  
   *Why unresolved:* Only cubic splines were used; it is unknown whether quintic splines would close the performance gap or if the heuristic fundamentally limits learnability.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Implement and compare both spline orders on the same benchmarks, measuring τ‑MSE, MSIE, and training stability; analyze continuity of \( dt/d\tau \) and its effect on neural network approximation.

## Limitations
- Implementation gaps: arc‑length discretization, curvature estimation, and interpolation details are not fully specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Hyperparameter ambiguity: Latin hypercube ranges are given but exact sampling distributions (e.g., number of s‑points) are omitted.
- Generalization risk: TOTR’s smooth \( v(s) \) may fail for systems with strong μ‑dependent stiffness; this break condition is noted but not broadly tested.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (smoothness via acceleration minimization): High  
- Mechanism 2 (solver‑independent stationarity): Medium  
- Mechanism 3 (well‑conditioned arc‑length formulation): Medium
