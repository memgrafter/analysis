# ExpSeek: Self-Triggered Experience Seeking for Web Agents

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.08605
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08605
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Self-triggered step entropy intervention yields up to 14.6% absolute gain on web agent benchmarks, outperforming passive experience injection.

## Executive Summary
ExpSeek tackles the inefficiency of passive, global experience injection in web agents by introducing a self-triggered, step-level seeking mechanism. It uses step entropy as an intrinsic signal to estimate when an agent is likely to make a mistake, dynamically intervening to retrieve and inject contextual guidance from a stored experience base of (behavior, mistake, guidance) triplets. This adaptive approach improves exploration and efficiency, demonstrating significant performance gains across multiple benchmarks with larger language models.

## Method Summary
The method computes step entropy from the agent's action distribution as an intrinsic uncertainty signal. Thresholds for intervention are estimated per task type using logistic regression on a bootstrapped validation set. An experience base is constructed from paired success-failure trajectories, distilled into guidance triplets. During execution, at each step, the entropy is compared to the threshold. If surpassed, a retrieval module queries the experience base for relevant past mistakes and a guidance generator produces a context-specific corrective suggestion for the agent.

## Key Results
*   Achieves absolute improvements of 9.3% (Qwen3-8B) and 7.5% (Qwen3-32B) over vanilla ReAct on average across GAIA, WebWalkerQA, xbench, and SEAL-HARD.
*   Outperforms passive experience injection baselines by 6.7% and 6.0% on the same models.
*   Shows a peak improvement of 14.6% on the xbench benchmark.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
*   **Entropy as a Mistake Proxy:** Step entropy correlates with decision uncertainty, making it a timely, model-agnostic signal for when intervention is most needed, before errors cascade.
*   **Adaptive Triggering:** Self-estimated thresholds prevent over-correction on easy steps and constant overhead, focusing computational resources on high-risk moments.
*   **Weak-to-Strong Guidance:** A small, specialized experience model can effectively guide a larger, more capable base agent by providing focused, corrective knowledge gaps.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Step Entropy:** Computes uncertainty from the policy's action distribution at each step. *Why needed:* Provides a fine-grained, intrinsic trigger signal without external supervision. *Quick check:* Verify entropy is higher on steps leading to failure vs. success in a held-out set.
*   **Logistic Regression Thresholding:** Fits a binary classifier (intervene/not) on bootstrapped entropy-success pairs to derive a per-task threshold. *Why needed:* Adapts the trigger sensitivity to different task distributions. *Quick check:* Compare threshold stability across multiple bootstrap samples.
*   **Triplet Experience Format:** (Behavior, Mistake, Guidance) structures corrective knowledge. *Why needed:* Separates the faulty pattern from its remedy for precise retrieval. *Quick check:* Human eval: does retrieved guidance directly address the identified mistake?
*   **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** Retrieves relevant past mistakes to condition guidance generation. *Why needed:* Provides contextual, specific advice rather than generic prompting. *Quick check:* Ablate retrieval; does performance drop significantly?
*   **Weak-to-Strong Generalization:** A smaller, tuned model (4B) advising a larger base model (8B/32B). *Why needed:* Leverages specialization to overcome the larger model's lack of targeted experience. *Quick check:* Swap roles; does a larger experience model provide comparable boost to a smaller agent?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Agent Policy -> Entropy Estimator -> Threshold Checker -> (if triggered) Experience Base (Retriever) -> Guidance Generator -> Agent Policy (next step)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Agent generates an action.  
2. Entropy is computed from the action distribution.  
3. Entropy is compared to the task-specific threshold.  
4. If exceeded, the current step's context is used to query the experience base.  
5. Retrieved triplet(s) are formatted into a prompt for the guidance generator.  
6. Generated guidance is injected into the agent's context for the next step.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
*   **Simplicity vs. Accuracy:** Entropy is cheap but a proxy; more complex classifiers might be more accurate but costly.
*   **Generality vs. Specificity:** Global thresholds are simple but per-step adaptive thresholds could be more precise.
*   **Storage vs. Coverage:** The experience base must balance size (retrieval cost) with diversity of covered mistakes.

**Failure Signatures:**  
*   **False Positive Triggers:** Low-entropy, correct steps interrupted, causing inefficiency or degradation.
*   **False Negative Triggers:** High-entropy, mistake-bound steps missed, leading to unrecoverable errors.
*   **Irrelevant Retrieval:** Retrieved guidance is off-topic, confusing the agent or introducing new errors.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. **Ablation - Trigger:** Run with entropy trigger disabled (always intervene) and with a random trigger to isolate the value of adaptive timing.
2. **Ablation - Experience Source:** Compare triplet-based guidance against injecting raw failed trajectories or generic success examples.
3. **Sensitivity - Threshold Estimation:** Compare logistic regression thresholding against a fixed global threshold and a per-step adaptive threshold to validate the design choice.

## Limitations
*   Threshold estimation may overfit to the validation distribution and fail on novel web domains or task types.
*   The experience base construction requires paired success-failure trajectories, which may limit scalability to entirely new tasks.
*   The robustness of entropy as a universal mistake signal is only validated on four specific benchmarks; its effectiveness across diverse architectures and unseen domains is unknown.

## Confidence
*   **Performance gains on evaluated benchmarks:** High (repeated absolute improvements across models and baselines).
*   **Entropy effectiveness as a trigger signal:** Medium (validated on four benchmarks; broader cross-architecture validation needed).
*   **Weak-to-strong guidance (4B -> 32B):** Medium (demonstrated in one setting; scaling to extreme size gaps unverified).
*   **Adaptive intervention improving efficiency:** Low-Medium (claims based on step counts; actual computational overhead not quantified).

## Next Checks
1.  **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** Evaluate ExpSeek on entirely new web domains (e.g., e-commerce checkout, social media navigation) not represented in the experience base or threshold validation to test entropy threshold stability and retrieval relevance.
2.  **Guidance Quality & Actionability Assessment:** Conduct a human evaluation study where annotators rate the correctness and actionability of the generated (behavior, mistake, guidance) triplets, correlating scores with downstream agent success rates.
3.  **Computational Overhead Profiling:** Measure and report the wall-clock time and token consumption of ExpSeek vs. vanilla ReAct, isolating the costs of entropy calculation, experience retrieval, and guidance generation per step and per task.
