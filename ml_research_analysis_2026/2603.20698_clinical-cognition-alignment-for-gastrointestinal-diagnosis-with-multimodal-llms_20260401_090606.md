# Clinical Cognition Alignment for Gastrointestinal Diagnosis with Multimodal LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20698
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20698
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CogAlign-8B achieves 67.67% average accuracy across five GI endoscopy benchmarks, significantly surpassing Qwen3-VL-Plus (41.16%) and demonstrating robust performance under visual noise.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the misalignment between multimodal LLM reasoning and standardized clinical diagnostic pathways in gastrointestinal endoscopy. The proposed CogAlign framework uses a two-stage process: first, hierarchical supervised fine-tuning instill expert diagnostic logic (anatomic location → morphology → micro-details); second, counterfactual-driven reinforcement learning (GRPO) eliminates spurious visual biases by generating normal reference images via lesion masking and optimizing with structured rewards. CogAlign sets new state-of-the-art accuracy on five diverse benchmarks and shows improved robustness to image corruptions.

## Method Summary
CogAlign operates in two stages. Stage 1 (SFT) trains a model (based on Qwen3-VL-8B) on a curated hierarchical dataset D (24,515 samples) to generate a reasoning chain following clinical structure (location → morphology → micro-details) plus a diagnosis. The vision encoder is frozen; LoRA is applied to the LLM. Stage 2 (GRPO) refines the policy using counterfactual images created by Gaussian-blurring lesions (using provided binary masks M). For each sample, eight outputs are sampled and rewarded based on: format compliance (section headers), clinical keyword presence (9 pre-defined terms), and diagnostic consistency with the ground truth. A KL penalty prevents deviation from the SFT model.

## Key Results
- CogAlign-8B averages 67.67% accuracy across CrohnIPI, GastroVision, HyperKvasir, Kvasir-Capsule, and SEE-AI, outperforming Qwen3-VL-Plus (41.16%) and other multimodal LLMs.
- Substantial robustness gain under spot-interference noise; accuracy drops far less than baselines when lesions are masked or corrupted.
- On the multi-label SEE-AI benchmark, CogAlign achieves exact-match accuracy of 62.5%, a large margin over prior work.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hierarchical reasoning structure in Stage 1 forces the model to mimic expert cognitive steps, reducing shortcut learning. Stage 2 uses counterfactual normal images to break association between lesion features and diagnoses, while the multi-component reward explicitly penalizes missing clinical keywords or diagnoses that contradict evidence. This combination aligns model outputs with clinical protocols and removes reliance on spurious visual cues.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Hierarchical Dataset D → SFT Model (Frozen Vision Encoder + LoRA-LLM) → GRPO Policy (Same architecture) → Evaluation Benchmarks

**Critical Path:**  
1. Build/curate D with expert-verified reasoning chains and lesion masks.  
2. Train SFT model to generate `r ⊕ l`.  
3. Generate counterfactual images `x_cf` using masks M.  
4. Run GRPO iterations: sample G=8 outputs, compute rewards (R_fmt, R_cog, R_diag), update policy.  
5. Evaluate on held-out benchmarks, including spot-interference robustness test.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- **Mask Dependency:** GRPO efficacy hinges on accurate lesion masks; noisy masks degrade counterfactual quality.  
- **Computational Cost:** GRPO requires multiple samples per step (G=8) and repeated reward computation, increasing training time vs. standard SFT.  
- **Reward Complexity:** Multi-component reward (`R_fmt + R_cog + 2·R_diag`) may need tuning; over-weighting `R_diag` could neglect reasoning structure.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Low `R_fmt` pass rate → model omits required sections; increase SFT diversity or LoRA rank.  
- Poor robustness (large accuracy drop under noise) → GRPO failed to remove spurious bias; increase GRPO steps or strengthen counterfactual generation.  
- Keyword recall stagnant → reward not guiding; check keyword extraction consistency.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **SFT Sanity Check:** Train for few steps; verify model generates all three reasoning sections and correct diagnosis on 10 random D samples.  
2. **GRPO Convergence:** Run 50 GRPO steps; plot `R_cog` and `R_diag` moving averages; ensure both improve steadily.  
3. **Robustness Probe:** Apply spot interference to 100 test images; compare accuracy drop between SFT-only and CogAlign models; expect significantly smaller drop for CogAlign.

## Limitations
- **Mask generation procedure unspecified:** The pipeline for obtaining binary lesion masks M (initial model + expert refinement) is not detailed, hindering reproducibility of the GRPO stage.
- **Keyword extraction protocol opaque:** The LLM/prompt used to derive the 9 clinical keywords from reasoning chains is not described; variations could affect `R_cog` stability.
- **Benchmark dependency:** Results are on curated in-distribution datasets; generalizability to real-world clinical settings (e.g., diverse endoscope brands, rare pathologies) remains unproven.
