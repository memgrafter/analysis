# Beyond Core and Penumbra: Bi-Temporal Image-Driven Stroke Evolution Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07535
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07535
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Deep embeddings from admission CTP (especially mJ-Net) separate salvaged vs. non‑salvaged penumbra (Δcos=0.19, p=1.5×10⁻⁴) while core tissue shows no outcome‑dependent separation.

## Executive Summary
This study introduces a bi‑temporal framework to characterize ischemic tissue evolution by intersecting admission CT perfusion (CTP) and follow‑up DWI masks into six regions of interest (ROIs) that encode both initial perfusion state and final outcome. Features extracted solely from the admission CTP—including baseline statistics, GLCM radiomics, and deep embeddings from pre‑trained segmentation networks—are aggregated within each ROI and compared across outcome groups. In a cohort of 18 recanalized large‑vessel‑occlusion patients, salvaged penumbra clustered with healthy tissue in feature space, whereas regions destined to infarct were distinct. Critically, mJ‑Net embeddings showed significant separation between salvaged and non‑salvaged penumbra, suggesting that encoder‑derived manifolds capture latent phenotypes predictive of tissue fate. The work demonstrates a novel analysis paradigm for stroke evolution, moving beyond static core/penumbra definitions.

## Method Summary
Manual delineations on admission CTP define core, penumbra, non‑hypoperfused brain (NHB), and contralateral healthy brain (CLB); follow‑up DWI provides final infarct and remaining healthy masks. After rigid registration of DWI to the CTP grid, the two label sets are intersected to create six bi‑temporal ROIs (e.g., penumbra at T1 that infarcted at T2). All features are computed on the admission CTP only and then aggregated within each ROI via max‑pooling. Baseline features (FE1) comprise six intensity statistics from a 3×3×30 sliding window. GLCM radiomics (FE2) yields four texture features per slice. Deep embeddings use pre‑trained mJ‑Net (2D+time) and nnU‑Net (2D) encoders: mJ‑Net processes 16×16×30 patches to produce 256‑dimensional vectors; nnU‑Net extracts a 256×64×64 feature map per slice. Analysis involves t‑SNE visualization, Mann‑Whitney U tests with Bonferroni correction for BL/GLCM features, and a custom separation index (Δcos) for deep embeddings that compares within‑ and between‑group cosine similarity at the patient level.

## Key Results
- Salvaged penumbra (`ROI_b_p`) clustered with healthy tissue (`ROI_b_CLB`), while infarct‑evolving regions (`ROI_fi_*`) formed distinct clusters in feature space.
- mJ‑Net embeddings separated salvaged vs. non‑salvaged penumbra with median Δcos=0.19 (p=1.5×10⁻⁴).
- Core regions (`ROI_b_c` vs. `ROI_fi_c`) showed no significant outcome‑dependent difference (p=0.021, Δcos=0.03), indicating homogeneity regardless of final fate.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Constructing bi‑temporal ROIs by intersecting admission CTP tissue labels with follow‑up DWI outcome labels creates a grounded framework for tracking tissue state transitions.
- **Mechanism:** Manual delineations at T1 (core, penumbra, contralateral healthy) and T2 (final infarct, healthy) are combined via set intersection into six discrete ROIs (e.g., `ROI_fi_p` = penumbra at T1 that infarcted at T2). This converts a continuous evolution problem into a categorical, region‑based analysis where each voxel’s history is defined by its initial perfusion state and final outcome.
- **Core assumption:** Expert manual labels on CTP (T1) and DWI (T2) are accurate and spatially correspondent after registration.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "Manually delineated masks at T1 and T2 are intersected to construct six regions of interest (ROIs) encoding both initial tissue state and final outcome."  
  [section 3.1] Detailed logic for ROI construction: `ROI_{RT2}^{RT1} := RT1 ∩ RT2`.  
  [corpus] Neighbors focus on segmentation/prediction; this paper’s core contribution is the *analysis framework* using predefined ROIs, not a new segmentation model. Corpus evidence for the specific ROI intersection logic is weak; it is a novel methodological choice presented in this paper.
- **Break condition:** Failure in precise spatial co‑registration between CTP and DWI (due to modality differences, head tilt) corrupts the voxel‑wise correspondence, making ROI assignments erroneous.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Extracting complementary feature families (baseline stats, GLCM radiomics, deep CNN embeddings) from *admission CTP only* and assigning them to bi‑temporal ROIs captures distinct aspects of tissue heterogeneity predictive of evolution.
- **Mechanism:** Features are computed on the T1 CTP volume but aggregated *within* each of the six bi‑temporal ROIs. This means a feature vector describes the *aggregate characteristics* of tissue that started as, e.g., "penumbra" and ended as "salvaged brain." Comparing feature distributions across ROIs (e.g., `ROI_b_p` vs. `ROI_fi_p`) tests whether admission signatures differ based on eventual outcome.
- **Core assumption:** The chosen feature families (intensity statistics, texture, deep latent space) are sufficiently sensitive to capture biologically relevant differences in ischemic tissue viability that are latent at admission.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "Features extracted from CTP include baseline statistics, GLCM radiomics, and deep embeddings... Features were aggregated per region and analyzed in feature space."  
  [section 4] Detailed descriptions of FE1 (baseline), FE2 (GLCM), FE3/FE4 (mJ‑Net, nnU‑Net embeddings) and the aggregation (`max pooling`) within each ROI.  
  [section 5, Results] Box plots and t‑SNE show visual separation, and Table 2 shows statistical separation (Δcos, p‑values) for penumbra ROIs but not core ROIs.
- **Break condition:** The feature extraction/aggregation pipeline fails to capture discriminative information. For example, if deep embeddings are invariant to the subtle perfusion differences that predict salvageability, or if ROI aggregation (max pooling) obliterates critical spatial variance within the region.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Encoder‑derived deep feature manifolds (from mJ‑Net, nnU‑Net) reflect an underlying tissue phenotype space where "salvageable" and "non‑salvageable" penumbra are more separable than core tissue subtypes, suggesting the networks learn representations linked to viability.
- **Mechanism:** Pre‑trained segmentation networks (mJ‑Net, nnU‑Net) are used as frozen feature extractors. Their intermediate feature maps (embeddings) are pooled within each ROI. The hypothesis is that the geometry of these learned high‑dimensional spaces separates tissue that ultimately survived from tissue that died, even when both were labeled as "penumbra" at admission. This is quantified via a custom separation index (Δcos) comparing within‑ and between‑group cosine similarity.
- **Core assumption:** The segmentation networks’ encoders are trained on a sufficiently diverse population and task that their internal representations encode more than just the segmentation label (core/penumbra); they encode latent properties related to tissue health and recovery potential.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "mJ‑Net embeddings showed significant separation between salvaged and non‑salvaged penumbra (median Δcos=0.19, p=1.5×10⁻⁴), with core regions showing no significant outcome‑dependent difference."  
  [section 4.1] Definition of Δcos separation index.  
  [section 5, Table 2] Statistical results for mJ‑Net embeddings: Test 1 (`ROI_b_p` vs `ROI_fi_p`) is significant (p=1.5e‑4, median Δcos=0.19); Test 2 (`ROI_b_c` vs `ROI_fi_c`) is weaker (p=0.021, median Δcos=0.03).  
  [section 5, t‑SNE Fig 6] Visual observation that mJ‑Net embeddings show distinct clusters for salvaged vs. infarct‑evolving penumbra, while nnU‑Net shows a core‑to‑healthy gradient but less clear state separation.
- **Break condition:** The observed separation is an artifact of the specific training data or architecture. If the networks were trained only to segment core/penumbra without regard to final outcome, their latent space may not be predictive of salvageability. The small sample size (n=18) also risks overinterpreting patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Perfusion Imaging (CTP) vs. Diffusion Imaging (DWI)**  
  *Why needed here:* The entire analysis hinges on understanding that CTP at T1 estimates *hypoperfusion* (core = severely low blood flow; penumbra = moderately low flow, at risk) while DWI at T2 measures *irreversible injury* (final infarct). Misunderstanding these physiological meanings breaks the ROI logic.  
  *Quick check:* If a voxel is in the "penumbra" mask on admission CTP but the final DWI shows no infarct there, what does that imply about the tissue’s physiological state between T1 and T2?

- **Concept: Radiomics & Feature Aggregation**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper uses radiomics (GLCM) and deep features. Understanding that these are *high‑dimensional descriptors* computed from image intensities/textures/activations, and then *aggregated (max‑pooled)* within anatomically defined ROIs, is key. The analysis compares these aggregated vectors, not voxel‑wise.  
  *Quick check:* Why aggregate (max‑pool) all feature vectors from within a single ROI into one descriptor per slice/patient, instead of analyzing all voxels independently?

- **Concept: CNN Embeddings as Phenotypic Descriptors**  
  *Why needed here:* The central hypothesis is that a network trained for segmentation (core/penumbra) learns a feature space where similar *biological tissue states* are proximal. This is a form of representation learning. One must grasp that the embedding vector is not a prediction but a coordinate in a latent space.  
  *Quick check:* What is the conceptual difference between using a pre‑trained CNN’s embedding as a feature (FE3/FE4) versus using its final segmentation output? What does the embedding represent that the binary label does not?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input Data (CTP 4D, DWI 3D) -> Preprocessing (motion correction, skull‑strip, rigid registration) -> Label Generation (manual T1 masks: core, penumbra, NHB, CLB; T2 mask: final infarct) -> ROI Construction (set intersection: `ROI_{T2}^{T1} = T1_mask ∩ T2_mask`) -> Feature Extraction (FE1: 3×3×30 sliding window -> 6 baseline stats; FE2: 3D GLCM per slice -> 4 texture features; FE3: mJ‑Net encoder -> 256D patch embeddings; FE4: nnU‑Net encoder -> 256×64×64 feature map) -> Analysis (t‑SNE, Mann‑Whitney U/Bonferroni for BL/GLCM; Δcos calculation for deep embeddings).

**Critical path:** The most fragile sequence is **DWI‑to‑CTP registration → ROI intersection**. Any misalignment propagates error to all subsequent feature assignments. Validation of registration quality is a prerequisite for all analysis.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Manual vs. Automatic Labels:** Manual delineation provides ground truth for ROI construction but limits cohort size (n=18). Using automatic segmentation (e.g., from the trained mJ‑Net) would scale but adds model error to the ROI definition itself—a circular risk for this phenotyping study.  
- **2D Slice‑wise vs. 3D Volumetric Analysis:** All feature extraction is done per axial slice (`zi`). This simplifies implementation and matches DWI’s axial orientation but discards inter‑slice context. A 3D aggregation might be more robust but was not explored.  
- **Max‑Pooling Aggregation:** Simple and robust to ROI shape, but discards intra‑ROI feature distribution (e.g., heterogeneity within the penumbra). Mean‑pooling or texture of features could be alternatives.  
- **Architecture Choice (mJ‑Net vs. nnU‑Net):** mJ‑Net is 2D+time, explicitly modeling perfusion dynamics. nnU‑Net is 2D (time as channels). The better separation from mJ‑Net suggests temporal modeling is beneficial, but the architectures/training regimens are confounded.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. **Poor Registration:** Visual inspection of overlaid CTP and DWI shows systematic misalignment, especially at slab boundaries or due to head tilt. *Action:* Check registration metrics; consider slice‑by‑slice refinement.  
2. **Feature Collapse:** t‑SNE shows all six ROIs overlapping completely in all feature spaces. *Action:* Verify ROI masks are non‑empty and correctly assigned; check feature extraction code for bugs (e.g., wrong indexing).  
3. **Unexpected Separation:** `ROI_fi_NHB` (non‑hypoperfused at T1, infarcted at T2) clusters with `ROI_fi_c`/`ROI_fi_p` instead of `ROI_b_CLB`. *Interpretation:* This may indicate latent vulnerability in "normal" CTP tissue, or error in the initial "non‑hypoperfused" label definition. *Action:* Re‑examine T1 labels; consider that "normal" CTP may hide microvascular disease.  
4. **No Statistical Signal:** Mann‑Whitney tests show no significant separation even for `ROI_b_p` vs. `ROI_fi_p` in any feature family. *Action:* With n=18, power is low. Check effect sizes (Cliff’s delta) for meaningful trends even if p>0.05; consider increasing cohort.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Sanity Check Registration & ROI Sizes:** For all 18 patients, visualize the six ROI masks overlaid on CTP/DWI. Compute voxel counts per ROI. Flag patients with extreme imbalances (e.g., `ROI_b_c` = 0 voxels) for exclusion or review.  
2. **Reproduce Key t‑SNE & Δcos for mJ‑Net:** Run the full pipeline on the 18‑patient cohort. Generate the six‑ROI t‑SNE plot for mJ‑Net embeddings and compute the median Δcos for Test 1 (`ROI_b_p` vs `ROI_fi_p`). Confirm it matches the paper’s reported value (~0.19, p=1.5e‑4).  
3. **Ablation: Swap Feature Aggregation Method:** Rerun the analysis for mJ‑Net embeddings using *mean‑pooling* instead of max‑pooling within ROIs before Δcos calculation. Does the separation index (`ROI_b_p` vs `ROI_fi_p`) remain significant?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** Can the bi‑temporal feature characterization framework be generalized to larger, multi‑center datasets, particularly when using automatic core/penumbra segmentations?  
  *Basis:* Authors explicitly state future work will extend cohort size and evaluate on multicenter public datasets; ISLES’24 lacks manual annotations, necessitating automatic segmentation.  
  *Why unresolved:* Current study limited to 18 recanalized patients from a single center with manual annotations.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Validation on a large, diverse multi‑center cohort (hundreds of patients), comparing manual vs. automatic segmentation performance, and demonstrating consistent Δcos and clustering patterns.

- **Question:** How can co‑registration between CTP and DWI be improved to account for axial angulation mismatches caused by head tilt during CTP acquisition?  
  *Basis:* Inferred limitation: CTP often acquired with head tilt to increase coverage; standard 2D registration may not fully compensate.  
  *Why unresolved:* Current rigid 2D similarity transform may not correct for angulation, biasing ROI definitions.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Development/validation of 3D or deformable registration handling angulation; evaluation using spatial correspondence metrics (e.g., Dice of landmark‑based ROIs).

- **Question:** Do ischemic lesions continue to evolve beyond the standard 24–72 hour DWI follow‑up, and how does this affect the reliability of DWI as ground truth for final infarct?  
  *Basis:* Authors caution lesions may remain unstable beyond 24‑72 hours, affecting final infarct definition.  
  *Why unresolved:* Using DWI at 24‑72 hours as definitive outcome may misrepresent true final infarct if lesions expand/contract later.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Longitudinal imaging beyond 72 hours (e.g., 1 week, 1 month); quantify discrepancy between early and late DWI; adjust ROI definitions using extended follow‑up.

- **Question:** Why do ischemic core regions show no significant outcome‑dependent separation in feature space, whereas penumbra does?  
  *Basis:* Results show core separation non‑significant (p=0.021, Δcos=0.03) while penumbra separation is strong; also `ROI_fi_NHB` aligns with core/infarct features.  
  *Why unresolved:* Unclear whether core is inherently homogeneous, embeddings fail to capture subtle differences, or fate is predetermined by non‑imaging factors (e.g., collaterals). Alignment of NHB with infarct suggests latent vulnerability.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Ablation on feature dimensions; correlation with clinical/physiological data (collaterals, reperfusion timing); multi‑timepoint imaging to model core evolution dynamically.

## Limitations
- **Registration fidelity:** Exact DWI‑CTP transform metric and parameters unspecified; no reported spatial accuracy (e.g., Target Registration Error).
- **Model access & reproducibility:** Pre‑trained mJ‑Net/nnU‑Net weights and exact feature extraction layers not released, blocking replication.
- **Sample size & generalizability:** Single‑center cohort of 18 recanalized LVO patients limits statistical power and external validity; high risk of overfitting in high‑dimensional embedding space.
- **Circularity risk:** Using segmentation model embeddings assumes they encode viability beyond the segmentation task; training data/targets for mJ‑Net/nnU‑Net not fully described.
