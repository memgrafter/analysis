# LiteInception: A Lightweight and Interpretable Deep Learning Framework for General Aviation Fault Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2604.01725
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01725
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** A two-stage, lightweight deep learning framework for aviation fault diagnosis that balances high recall detection, accurate classification, and interpretability on edge devices.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of deploying deep learning for general aviation fault diagnosis on resource-constrained edge devices, where both computational efficiency and model interpretability are critical. It proposes LiteInception, a framework with a two-stage cascaded architecture. Stage 1 acts as a high-recall anomaly detector, and only anomalous samples proceed to Stage 2 for fine-grained fault classification. Key innovations include a sensor channel reduction strategy, a compressed "1+1 branch" LiteInception module (derived from InceptionTime), and knowledge distillation to tune the precision-recall trade-off. A dual-level interpretability framework provides traceable evidence. Evaluated on the NGAFID dataset, the system achieves strong detection and identification accuracy while being significantly more efficient than baseline models.

## Method Summary
The method centers on a two-stage cascade. First, a multi-method fusion strategy reduces the original 23 sensor channels to 15 most informative ones. The core LiteInception module compresses the InceptionTime architecture by replacing its 3+1 convolutional branches with a single 3-kernel convolution branch plus a mandatory MaxPool branch, cutting parameters by ~70% with minimal accuracy loss. Stage 1 is a LiteInception stack followed by a Transformer encoder, optimized for high recall in binary anomaly detection. Stage 2 is a pure LiteInception stack for 19-class fault identification, optimized for macro F1-score. Knowledge distillation from a full InceptionTime teacher model, using soft labels with a high temperature, allows the same LiteInception student architecture to be tuned for either higher recall (safety-critical) or higher precision (assisted diagnosis). An interpretability engine aggregates four attribution methods to highlight important sensors and time segments.

## Key Results
*   Achieves fault detection accuracy of 81.92% with 83.24% recall on the NGAFID dataset.
*   Achieves fault identification (19-class) accuracy of 77.00%.
*   The LiteInception module reduces parameters by 70% and accelerates CPU inference over 8x compared to InceptionTime, with less than a 3% F1-score drop.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A two-stage cascaded architecture decouples the competing optimization objectives of high-recall fault detection and high-precision fine-grained classification, enabling on-demand computational resource allocation aligned with maintenance workflows.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 (binary detection) is optimized for maximum recall to minimize missed faults (safety-critical). Only its anomalous outputs feed into Stage 2 (19-class identification), which is optimized for accuracy and interpretability. This separates the "is there a problem?" screening from the "what is the problem?" diagnosis, preventing normal samples (~50% in this dataset, likely higher in reality) from incurring the cost of full multi-class inference.
- **Core assumption:** The operational workflow naturally separates alerting from diagnosis, and the cost asymmetry (missed detection >> false alarm) justifies this decoupling.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Stage 1 performs high-recall fault detection, and Stage 2 conducts fine-grained fault classification on anomalous samples, thereby decoupling optimization objectives and enabling on-demand allocation of computational resources."
  - [section 3.4]: "The two-stage design yields three core advantages. First, objective decoupling—the two stages independently optimize for recall and precision... Second, module independence... Third, workflow alignment..."
  - [corpus]: Weak. Neighboring papers discuss cascading but not the specific decoupling of P-R trade-offs via architecture.
- **Break condition:** If the proportion of normal operational flights in deployment (`p`) is not sufficiently high to make the expected compute cost `C_stage1 + (1-p)*C_stage2` lower than a single-stage `C_full`. If `p` is very low (e.g., <30%), the savings diminish and the cascading latency penalty might dominate.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The 1+1 branch LiteInception module achieves a ~70% parameter reduction with minimal performance loss by pruning redundant multi-scale convolutional branches while preserving the indispensable MaxPool branch for extremal feature extraction.
- **Mechanism:** The original InceptionTime's 3+1 branch parallel structure (conv kernels 1,3,5,7) is reduced to a single 3-kernel convolution branch plus a MaxPool branch. The justification is that (a) a deep stack of small 3-kernel convolutions provides sufficient receptive field for the ~2048-step signals, (b) the 5/7-kernel branches are redundant, and (c) MaxPool uniquely captures peak/trough anomalies (e.g., temperature spikes) critical for aviation faults.
- **Core assumption:** Aviation sensor fault signatures are localized patterns (spikes, trends) best captured by dense non-linear transformations (deep small kernels) and extremum extraction (MaxPool), not by single-layer large-kernel linear mappings.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3.2]: "A 3k=3 convolution can cover this range [tens to hundreds of steps] through 6-layer stacking... the additional receptive field provided by the 5k=5 and 7k=7 branches... exhibits significant redundancy." "The MaxPool operation extracts the maximum value... These features carry direct physical significance in fault diagnosis... this complementarity implies that the information gain... cannot be fully substituted by convolutional branches."
  - [section 4.5.1]: Ablation shows `1+1` (639K) retains ~96.5% F1 vs `3+1` (2159K), while `1+0` (no MaxPool) drops 5pp F1, confirming MaxPool's irreplaceability.
- **Break condition:** If the target time series have fault signatures with very long-range dependencies (> effective receptive field of 6-layer 3-kernel stack) or if extremal features are not diagnostically relevant. The mechanism relies on local pattern dominance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Knowledge distillation, using a pretrained InceptionTime teacher, systematically modulates the student's (LiteInception) precision-recall trade-off via softened labels, enabling the same architecture to be tuned for safety-critical (high recall) or assisted-diagnosis (high precision) scenarios without architectural changes.
- **Mechanism:** High-temperature (`τ`) softmax on teacher logits produces smoother, "softer" probability distributions. The student learns these instead of hard one-hot labels. This smooths the student's decision boundaries, making it more confident on ambiguous/near-boundary samples (improving recall) but also more prone to false positives (reducing precision). The reverse occurs under hard-label-only training.
- **Core assumption:** The teacher's soft-label distribution encodes "dark knowledge" about inter-class similarities and ambiguous sample handling, which directly shapes the student's decision threshold behavior.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.5.2]: "Soft-label learning provides a smooth probability distribution... making the model more willing to render classification decisions on these samples, thereby improving recall... Implicit encoding of inter-class relationships... exposes the teacher model's implicit knowledge of inter-class similarities."
  - [section 4.4.2 & 4.5.3]: Distillation (`τ=8, α=0.7`) yields Precision 76.40%, Recall 75.85% (gap 0.55pp). Direct training yields Precision 77.92%, Recall 73.46% (gap 4.46pp). Distillation improves recall by ~2.4pp. Hyperparameter sweep shows high `τ` correlates with higher recall.
  - [corpus]: Weak. Neighbors discuss distillation for compression, not P-R tuning.
- **Break condition:** If the teacher model itself is poorly calibrated or does not possess superior inter-class relationship knowledge. The adjustment range is limited by `τ` and `α`; extreme targets (e.g., Recall>95%) may require supplementary methods like threshold shifting or cost-sensitive learning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Multi-Scale Temporal Convolution (Inception-style)
  - **Why needed here:** The core LiteInception module derives from InceptionTime, which uses parallel convolutions of different kernel sizes to capture patterns at multiple temporal scales simultaneously. Understanding this is key to understanding what is being pruned (`3+1` -> `1+1`).
  - **Quick check question:** Why might a deep stack of 3-kernel convolutions be sufficient for 2048-step aviation signals, potentially making larger kernels redundant?
- **Concept:** Cascaded/Hierarchical Classification for Imbalanced Workflows
  - **Why needed here:** The entire two-stage architecture is a cascade. The principle is to use a cheap, high-recall first stage to filter the dataset, allocating expensive resources only to the small anomalous subset. This is a classic system design for skewed data distributions.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the expected computational saving formula (`C_expected`) for a cascaded system, and how does it depend on the normal sample proportion `p`?
- **Concept:** Post-Hoc Explainability (XAI) Attribution Methods
  - **Why needed here:** The dual-level interpretability framework relies on four attribution methods (Input Gradient, Occlusion, Grad-CAM, Integrated Gradients). A foundational understanding of how each estimates feature/sensor importance is necessary to interpret the "which sensor x which time" evidence chains.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the key theoretical advantage of Integrated Gradients over simple Input Gradient, and what is its main computational cost?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw 23-channel NGAFID Data -> Channel Selection (Offline MI/Gradient/SE Fusion) -> 15-channel Input -> Stage 1 (LiteInception Stack + Transformer Encoder -> Binary Output) -> [If Anomaly] -> Stage 2 (Pure LiteInception Stack -> 19-class Output). Knowledge Distillation is a training-time switch between the Teacher (InceptionTime) and Student (LiteInception) for Stage 2. Interpretability Engine analyzes Stage 2 outputs.
- **Critical path:** Data preprocessing -> Channel selection -> **Stage 1 Training** (high recall) -> Inference: normal samples exit; anomalous -> **Stage 2 Training/Inference** (high accuracy, with optional Distillation) -> **Interpretability Analysis** on Stage 2 outputs.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **`1+1` Branch vs. Accuracy:** ~3-4pp F1 loss for 70% parameter reduction. Acceptable for edge constraints.
  - **Stage 1 Recall vs. System Missed Detection Rate:** Stage 1 recall is the absolute upper bound on system recall. A false negative in Stage 1 is an unrecoverable miss.
  - **Interpretability Method Choice:** Gradient-based (fast, noisy) vs. Perturbation-based (slow, robust). The paper ensembles them for robustness.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Stage 1 Recall too low (<80%):** Check Channel Selection (excluded key sensors?), Stage 1 architecture (Transformer contribution?), or classification threshold.
  - **Stage 2 Accuracy low (<70%):** Likely `1+1` branch underfitting for a specific fault class. Check class imbalance handling (TimeWarp?), or need for more depth (experiment showed depth 9 sometimes better).
  - **Interpretability Inconsistency:** Low cross-method consensus on key sensors/times for a fault class suggests the fault's signal pattern is genuinely ambiguous or the model is not learning a robust representation.
  - **CPU Inference > 10ms:** Verify `1+1` branch implementation and residual connections. Check for unnecessary operations.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Channel Selection Validation:** Train a strong InceptionTime on **all 23 channels** vs. the **selected 15 channels** (no augmentation) on Stage 2 task. Expected: 15-channel performance should match or exceed 23-channel (paper shows 77.58% vs 69.69% Acc), proving selection removed noise.
  2.  **Branch Ablation:** Train pure `LiteInception` models with `3+1`, `2+1`, `1+1`, `1+0` branches on Stage 2. Expected: F1 drop from `3+1` to `1+1` ~2.75pp; drop from `1+1` to `1+0` ~5pp, confirming MaxPool's critical role.
  3.  **Distillation Parameter Sweep:** Fix teacher (InceptionTime). For student (LiteInception `1+1`), grid search over `τ` (e.g., 2,4,6,8) and `α` (e.g., 0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9). Expected: High `τ` + medium-high `α` yields best F1; low `τ`/`α` yields higher precision/lower recall, demonstrating P-R tunability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does the LiteInception framework generalize to other aircraft types and propulsion systems beyond the Cessna 172?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "The experiments are validated solely on the NGAFID dataset from a single aircraft type (Cessna 172), and the transferability to other propulsion types (turboprop, turbofan engines) and aircraft models remains to be investigated."
- **Why unresolved:** The current evaluation is limited to one aircraft type, so the framework's generalizability and robustness to different data distributions are unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments on multiple real-world datasets from diverse aircraft models and compare performance metrics (accuracy, F1, inference time) to assess transferability.

- **Open Question 2:** What are the error propagation characteristics and overall performance of the two-stage cascaded architecture when integrated end-to-end?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "The current work reports only the independent performance of the two stages separately, and has not yet conducted an end-to-end evaluation of the two stages in series within a unified test pipeline."
- **Why unresolved:** Independent stage metrics do not reveal how false positives/negatives from Stage 1 affect Stage 2 and the final diagnostic reliability in a full system.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a unified pipeline that processes samples through both stages sequentially, measure overall missed detection rate, false alarm rate, and analyze error patterns across stages.

- **Open Question 3:** Can the system detect unknown fault types (open-set recognition) that were not present during training?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "The current system covers only 19 known fault categories and lacks the ability to detect unknown fault types, which in actual operations could lead to novel faults being misclassified as known categories."
- **Why unresolved:** Real-world deployments may encounter previously unseen faults; without open-set capability, such novel faults pose safety risks through misclassification.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduce hold-out fault categories during testing, evaluate the model’s ability to flag them as unknown (e.g., via entropy thresholds or outlier detection), and measure open-set recognition metrics.

- **Open Question 4:** How can the framework be adapted for real-time streaming diagnosis during flight, given it is currently designed for post-flight offline analysis?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "The system is positioned for post-flight offline diagnosis and does not yet support real-time streaming monitoring during flight."
- **Why unresolved:** The offline design processes fixed-length segments after landing, but real-time monitoring requires low-latency processing of continuous data streams with potential windowing trade-offs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Redesign the pipeline for sliding-window streaming inference, test on emulated real-time data streams, and evaluate accuracy-latency trade-offs under edge hardware constraints.

## Limitations
*   **Dataset accessibility:** NGAFID is not publicly available, preventing independent verification of results and full reproducibility.
*   **Teacher model details:** Omitted specifics on InceptionTime teacher training (e.g., augmentation, early stopping) introduce uncertainty in distillation outcomes.
*   **Mechanism assumptions:** Real-world applicability hinges on unstated conditions: high normal flight rate for cascade savings, local fault patterns for 1+1 branch sufficiency, and teacher calibration for effective distillation.

## Confidence
*   **Two-stage cascade decoupling:** Medium. Paper shows theoretical and experimental alignment, but savings depend on deployment-specific normal sample proportion; lacks external validation.
*   **LiteInception compression (1+1 branch):** High. Robust ablation confirms ~70% parameter reduction with <3% F1 loss; MaxPool branch's role is empirically validated.
*   **Knowledge distillation for P-R tuning:** Medium. Experiments demonstrate tunability, but range is limited by hyperparameters and teacher quality; no calibration analysis.
*   **Interpretability framework:** Medium. Multi-method consensus yields traceable evidence, but validation only on correctly classified samples; no ground-truth explanation benchmark.

## Next Checks

3.  **Distillation parameter sweep:** Grid search temperature τ∈{2,4,6,8} and weight α∈{0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9}; expect high τ with α≥0.7 to maximize recall relative to hard-label training, proving tunable P-R trade-off.
