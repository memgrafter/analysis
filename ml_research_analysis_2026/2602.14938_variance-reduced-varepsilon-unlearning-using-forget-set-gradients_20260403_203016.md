# Variance-Reduced $(\varepsilon,δ)-$Unlearning using Forget Set Gradients

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14938
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14938
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: VRU is the first first-order (ε,δ)-unlearning algorithm that directly incorporates forget set gradients via variance reduction, achieving O(1/ε) excess risk rate for strongly convex losses and empirical superiority over baselines.

## Executive Summary
Machine unlearning removes influence of specific data from a trained model while preserving performance on remaining data, ideally with formal privacy guarantees. Existing certified methods avoid direct use of forget set gradients; empirical ones that do lack formal guarantees. This paper introduces Variance-Reduced Unlearning (VRU), a novel first-order (ε,δ)-unlearning algorithm that leverages a variance-reduced stochastic gradient estimator including a term based on the forget set gradient. Under strong convexity and smoothness, VRU achieves an O(1/ε) excess risk convergence rate, improving upon O(1/ε²) of prior certified methods and yielding better scaling with forget fraction and error. Theoretically, VRU asymptotically dominates any forget-set-free algorithm in a low-error regime. Empirically on logistic regression, VRU achieves lower excess risk and reduced membership inference attack success compared to both certified (NFT) and empirical (SCRUB, NegGrad+) baselines, with gains over an order of magnitude when the forget set is small.

## Method Summary
Variance-Reduced Unlearning (VRU) operates given a pre-trained model θ*, retain dataset Dr, forget dataset Df, forget fraction rf = |Df|/(|Dr|+|Df|), and privacy parameters ε, δ. The algorithm: (1) Precompute the full-batch forget gradient ∇∗_f = ∇L(θ*, Df). (2) Set the projection radius either as R = (rf/(1−rf))·(L/μ) (theoretical) or R = (rf/(1−rf))·(‖∇∗_f‖/μ) (practical). (3) For T iterations, sample a mini-batch ξr from Dr, compute the variance-reduced gradient estimator  
 e∇(θ_t) = ∇ℓ(θ_t, ξr) − ∇ℓ(θ*, ξr) − (rf/(1−rf))∇∗_f.  
 Update θ_{t+1} = proj_{B(θ*,R)}(θ_t − (1/(μt)) e∇(θ_t)). (4) After T steps, add Gaussian noise:  
 ˜θ_T = θ_T + (rf/(1−rf))·ν_T·κ_{ε,δ}·Z,  
where Z ∼ N(0,1), κ_{ε,δ}=ε⁻¹√(2log(2.5/δ)), and ν_T = √(2h(T,δ)‖∇∗_f‖(1+κℓ))/(μ√T). The estimator is unbiased and reduces variance by anchoring at θ* and using the forget gradient correction. This separation of variance reduction and privacy noise yields (ε,δ)-DP with improved convergence.

## Key Results
- VRU attains an O(1/ε) excess risk rate for strongly convex losses, outperforming the O(1/ε²) rate of prior certified unlearning methods (e.g., Noise and Fine-Tune) and enabling better computational scaling with forget fraction rf and error ε.
- Theoretically, VRU asymptotically outperforms any first-order unlearning algorithm that neglects forget set gradients in the low-error regime.
- Empirically on logistic regression, VRU consistently achieves lower excess risk and reduced membership inference attack accuracy compared to both certified (NFT) and empirical (SCRUB, NegGrad+) baselines, with gains exceeding an order of magnitude for rf < 0.01.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Variance-Reduced Gradient Estimator with Forget Gradient Anchoring
- **Claim:** Directly incorporating a forget-set gradient term into a variance-reduced stochastic estimator improves convergence under (ε,δ)-unlearning.
- **Mechanism:** At each iteration, compute `e∇(θ, ξr, ξf) = ∇ℓ(θ, ξr) - ∇ℓ(θ*, ξr) - (rf/(1-rf)) ∇ℓ(θ*, ξf)` (using precomputed full-batch forget gradient ∇ℓ(θ*, ξf)). The first two terms provide variance reduction similar to SVRG, anchored at the pre-trained optimum θ*. The third term, derived from the gradient balance condition (1-rf)E[∇ℓ(θ*,ξr)] = -rf E[∇ℓ(θ*,ξf)], has zero expectation at θ*_r and corrects bias without recomputing full-batch gradients. This uses the forget set as an optimization signal while maintaining unbiasedness.
- **Core assumption:** Strong convexity, β-smoothness, and L-Lipschitz loss functions; exact knowledge of θ*; support separation between retain/forget distributions (Assumptions 3.1, 3.2).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "VRU is the first first-order algorithm that directly includes forget set gradients in its update rule, while provably satisfying (ε,δ)-unlearning."  
  - [section 4.1] "The intuition behind this gradient estimator... we add a correction term with null expectation that significantly reduces the variance."  
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers on certified unlearning explore first-order methods but do not incorporate forget gradients directly for variance reduction.
- **Break condition:** If the forget fraction rf is large (>0.1), the theoretical scaling advantage diminishes (Corollary 4.3). High variance in ξr or mismatch between θ* and θ*_r (violating Lemma A.1) degrades variance reduction.

### Mechanism 2: Confined Optimization Ball via Projection
- **Claim:** Projecting iterates onto a small ball centered at θ* ensures optimization stays in a region where the variance-reduced estimator's Lipschitz constant scales with rf, improving convergence.
- **Mechanism:** The algorithm constrains θ_t to B(θ*, R) with R = (rf/(1-rf)) * (L/μ). This radius, derived from the maximum distance between θ* and θ*_r (Lemma A.1), ensures that within the ball, the gradient estimator's norm is bounded by (1+κℓ)(rf/(1-rf))L (Lemma A.2). This local boundedness enables a high-probability sensitivity bound for the subsequent DP noise.
- **Core assumption:** Lemma A.1 holds (strong convexity, Lipschitz gradient at θ*). The ball B(θ*, R) must contain θ*_r.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 4.1] "The projection is done onto the ball B(θ*, rf/(1-rf) L/μ), which is guaranteed to contain the global optimum θ*_r."  
  - [section 4.2, proof sketch] "We start by upper-bounding the Lipschitz constant of e∇ on a small ball around θ*..."  
  - [corpus] Related work studies boundary effects but does not use projection for variance control.
- **Break condition:** If θ* is inaccurate (e.g., from early stopping), the ball may not contain θ*_r, causing projection to hinder convergence or induce bias. Empirical results (Fig. 3) show projection has minimal impact in practice, suggesting robustness.

### Mechanism 3: Noise Calibration Scaled by Forget Fraction and Iteration
- **Claim:** Gaussian perturbation added *after* optimization, with magnitude proportional to (rf/(1-rf)) * ν_T * κ_{ε,δ}, achieves (ε,δ)-unlearning while leveraging fast convergence to minimize noise.
- **Mechanism:** After T PSGD steps with step size 1/(μt), the final iterate θ_T is perturbed: ˜θ_T = θ_T + (rf/(1-rf)) ν_T κ_{ε,δ} Z. The sensitivity Δ scales with the distance from θ_T to θ*_r, which with high probability is bounded by ν_T = √(2h(T,δ)L(1+κℓ)/(μ√T)). Since ν_T decreases with T, and VRU achieves low distance faster (O(1/e) rate), less noise is needed compared to slower methods.
- **Core assumption:** The high-probability sensitivity bound (Eq. 15) holds; the Gaussian mechanism's composition theorem applies.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 4.2, proof] "We can now evaluate the loss of the noised model... applying the Gaussian mechanism... achieving (ε,δ)-unlearning."  
  - [Algorithm 1, lines 7-8] Explicit noise addition after optimization loop.  
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers on DP unlearning use noise during optimization; VRU's separation of optimization and noising is distinct.
- **Break condition:** If T is too small, ν_T is large, requiring excessive noise that harms utility. The high-probability bound fails if Assumptions 3.1/3.2 are violated, potentially breaking the DP guarantee.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Strong Convexity & Smoothness (µ, β)  
  - **Why needed here:** These properties ensure a unique optimum θ*_r and enable linear convergence rates for gradient-based methods. They define the condition number κℓ = β/μ, which directly appears in VRU's complexity (Theorem 4.1). Strong convexity also justifies the distance bound between θ* and θ*_r (Lemma A.1).  
  - **Quick check question:** If a loss is µ-strongly convex and β-smooth, how does the step size 1/(μt) in VRU's PSGD relate to the optimal constant step size for gradient descent?

- **Concept:** (ε,δ)-Differential Privacy / Unlearning  
  - **Why needed here:** Provides the formal privacy guarantee. The noise magnitude is set by κ_{ε,δ} = ε⁻¹√(2log(2.5/δ)). VRU's design minimizes the sensitivity Δ to reduce noise for a given (ε,δ).  
  - **Quick check question:** Why does VRU add noise *after* optimization rather than during each gradient step? How does this relate to the sensitivity bound being high-probability rather than deterministic?

- **Concept:** Variance Reduction (e.g., SVRG)  
  - **Why needed here:** The core innovation is a SVRG-like estimator adapted for unlearning. It reduces the variance of stochastic gradients by anchoring at θ* and using the forget gradient as a low-variance correction term, avoiding expensive full-batch retain gradient recomputation.  
  - **Quick check question:** In the SVRG estimator ∇ℓ(θ,ξ) - ∇ℓ(θ*,ξ) + ∇L(θ*), what is the purpose of the full-batch term? How does VRU replace this with a forget-set term while keeping unbiasedness?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Precomputed ∇∗_f → iteration loop (sample ξr → compute e∇ → update θ_t → project onto B(θ*,R)) → Gaussian noise addition → output ˜θ_T
- **Critical path:** Input θ*, Dr, Df, rf, ε, δ → compute R (or use gradient norm) → precompute ∇∗_f → for t=1 to T: sample ξr, compute e∇, update θ_{t+1} = proj(θ_t - (1/(μt)) e∇) → after loop, compute noise scale (rf/(1-rf))*ν_T*κ_{ε,δ} → perturb θ_T → output
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Stochastic vs. Full-Batch Forget Gradient*: Stochastic is unbiased but higher variance; full-batch (precomputed) reduces variance at cost of one full pass over Df. Paper recommends full-batch if T > 1/rf epochs.  
  - *Projection Radius*: Theoretical radius uses global L, which may be loose; practical radius uses ‖∇∗_f‖, which is tighter and computable.  
  - *Step size schedule*: 1/(μt) ensures convergence; constant step size may diverge under variance reduction.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Excess risk plateaus at high value: Check θ* accuracy (early stopping breaks Lemma A.1). Check projection radius too small.  
  - Noisy output degrades utility: κ_{ε,δ} too large for given T; increase T or reduce privacy budget.  
  - Divergence during optimization: Step size too large or strong convexity violated; VRU assumes locally strongly convex landscape near θ*.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Convergence rate validation**: Run VRU vs. NFT on logistic regression for varying rf, measure excess risk; expect O(1/e) scaling for VRU vs. O(1/e²) for NFT, especially for rf < 0.01 (Fig. 1).  
  2. **Projection ablation**: Compare VRU with and without projection (Fig. 3). Expect minimal difference, indicating robustness.  
  3. **Empirical privacy risk**: Run Membership Inference Attacks (U-LiRA) on VRU vs. SCRUB, NegGrad+ for varying rf. Expect VRU to achieve lower MIA accuracy (closer to 50%) due to (ε,δ) guarantees (Fig. 2).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can VRU be extended to non-strongly-convex settings, such as those satisfying the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition or the neural tangent kernel regime?  
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion explicitly states: "A natural direction for future work is to extend variance-reduced unlearning to relaxed notions of convexity that better capture the behavior of neural networks near local optima, such as the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition (Karimi et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2022) or the neural tangent kernel regime (Jacot et al., 2018)."  
- **Why unresolved:** The current convergence analysis relies heavily on strong convexity, smoothness, and Lipschitz assumptions. Deep neural networks rarely satisfy strong convexity globally.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** New theoretical proofs deriving convergence rates for VRU under weaker conditions (e.g., PL) and empirical validation on deep networks showing improved unlearning performance would confirm feasibility.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does VRU retain its theoretical guarantees when the pre-trained model θ∗ is only approximately available, rather than exact?  
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion notes: "Our analysis relies on strong convexity and, in principle, on the knowledge of the exact pre-trained optimum θ∗, though our experiments suggest robustness to inexact initialization." This acknowledges a gap between theory and empirical observation.  
- **Why unresolved:** The proof of Theorem 4.1 assumes exact θ∗; any error in the initialization could affect the unbiasedness of the gradient estimator and the bound on the distance to θ∗_r. A formal robustness analysis is missing.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A perturbation analysis quantifying how initialization error propagates through VRU updates and impacts the final excess risk would close the gap. Experiments with varying initialization error magnitudes could also support a theoretical bound.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the convergence rate and algorithmic dependence on the Lipschitz constant L be improved by replacing L with the actual forget gradient norm ∥∇L(θ∗,D_f)∥ and potentially removing the projection step?  
- **Basis in paper:** In Section 4.2, the authors write: "In practice the global Lipschitz constant L can effectively be replaced by the magnitude of the average forget set gradient ∥∇L(θ∗,D_f)∥, yielding a tighter bound that depends on the specific samples to unlearn. We discuss implementation details for efficiently exploiting these properties in Section 5.2." The experimental variant (VRU‑exp) uses the full‑batch forget gradient and a tighter radius, but the theoretical analysis still uses L.  
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical bounds remain worst-case due to L, which is often large or intractable to compute. The practical modifications suggest better constants but lack formal justification.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A refined lemma bounding the variance‑reduced gradient directly with ∥∇L(θ∗,D_f)∥, and a proof that the projection is unnecessary (or can be replaced by a cheaper operation), would yield tighter rates. Numerical experiments comparing the empirical iteration complexity against the new bound would validate the improvement.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does VRU maintain its asymptotic advantage over forget‑set‑free methods for excess risk thresholds e outside the specific low‑error regime (e < cκ²_ϵ,δ (rf/(1−rf))² e₀)?  
- **Basis in paper:** Theorem 4.4 establishes a separation only for sufficiently small e. The authors stress that this regime is typical for unlearning, but they do not address larger errors. The condition arises from the proof technique that compares VRU to a generic forget‑set‑free algorithm, and it might be an artifact rather than a fundamental limit.  
- **Why unresolved:** The lower bound for forget‑set‑free algorithms (Theorem A.3) and the upper bound for VRU (Theorem 4.1) are both tight within their assumptions, but their ratio may behave differently for larger e. Without a complete characterization, it is unknown whether VRU’s superiority extends beyond the stated error range.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extending the lower bound for forget‑set‑free methods to hold for all e (or finding a counterexample where they outperform VRU at higher e) would clarify the full complexity landscape. Alternatively, a refined upper bound for VRU that matches or surpasses the forget‑set‑free bound across all e would also answer the question.

## Limitations
- **Theoretical assumptions are stringent:** Strong convexity, β-smoothness, and exact knowledge of θ* are required, but these are unlikely to hold for deep neural networks.
- **Experimental deviation from theory:** The paper uses heuristic noise (fixed κ_ε,δ=0.1) and constant step sizes rather than the theoretically derived ν_T and µ, potentially undermining the claimed (ε,δ)-DP.
- **Limited advantage for large forget fractions:** VRU's theoretical and practical benefits diminish for rf > 0.1, restricting its applicability when deleting a substantial portion of data.

## Confidence
- **VRU as the first first-order certified unlearning method with forget-set gradients:** High
- **Theoretical O(1/ε) excess risk rate for strongly convex losses:** Medium
- **Empirical superiority in excess risk and MIA:** Medium
- **Asymptotic optimality among first-order methods ignoring forget set:** Low
- **Projection ensures bounded Lipschitz constant for the gradient estimator:** Medium
