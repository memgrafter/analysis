# Tensor Network Generator-Enhanced Optimization for Traveling Salesman Problem

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.20175
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20175

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TN-GEO, a framework that uses a matrix product state (MPS) Born machine to generate candidate tours for the Traveling Salesman Problem. The MPS models the probability distribution over permutations via autoregressive sampling with masking, guaranteeing valid tours. A key innovation is the k-site MPS, which captures local consecutive city correlations using a sliding window, improving parameter efficiency. On TSPLIB benchmarks with 14–52 cities, moderate k-values (4,8) consistently find optimal solutions for larger instances, significantly outperforming swap and 2-opt heuristics. The full MPS underperforms, suggesting that local geometric patterns dominate Euclidean TSP structure.

## Method Summary
TN-GEO iteratively refines a population of tours using an MPS generative model. Starting from random tours, each GEO iteration: (1) evaluates costs, (2) builds a finite-temperature softmax target distribution Q(i) ∝ exp(-cost(i)/T) with exponential temperature schedule T(t) from T_init=0.1 to T_final=0.0001, (3) trains the MPS (full N-site or k-site) by minimizing negative log-likelihood on samples drawn from Q, and (4) generates new tours via autoregressive sampling with masking. The k-site variant models k-gram correlations, training on all overlapping k-length subsequences and sampling with a sliding window that rebuilds the left environment from the last k-1 cities. Training uses AdamW with right-canonicalized MPS; sampling proceeds right-to-left, masking visited cities at each step.

## Key Results
- k-site MPS (k=4,8) consistently finds optimal tours for larger TSPLIB instances (att48, eil51, berlin52).
- Outperforms classical swap and 2-opt heuristics, which exhibit gaps up to 30%.
- Full MPS underperforms moderate k-values, indicating local geometric correlations are the primary determinant of tour quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Moderate k-site MPS aligns capacity with local geometric correlations.** The k-site MPS uses a sliding window to model consecutive city subsequences (k-grams), restricting context to local neighborhoods. This reduces parameters and focuses learning on short-range geometric patterns that are key for Euclidean TSP. Full MPS tries to model all N-site correlations with limited bond dimension, underfitting long-range dependencies; k=2 lacks sufficient context. Evidence: abstract and section II.E. Break condition: if optimal tours require significant long-range correlations (non-Euclidean instances), k-site's local prior becomes harmful.

**Mechanism 2: Autoregressive sampling with masking guarantees valid permutation generation.** In the right-canonical MPS, conditional probabilities for the next city are computed given the visited prefix. Masking zeroes probabilities for already-selected cities and renormalizes, forcing each step to extend a partial permutation into a full one. This embeds the permutation constraint into the sampling algorithm. Evidence: abstract and section II.D. Break condition: the mechanism ensures validity but not quality; if the MPS assigns near-zero probability to all valid continuations (dead-end), low-quality tours may result.

**Mechanism 3: Exponential temperature scheduling balances exploration and exploitation.** Starting with high temperature (T_init) flattens the softmax target Q, causing the MPS to learn a diverse set of solutions. Over iterations, temperature decays exponentially to T_final, sharpening Q and concentrating probability on the best solutions found so far. This adapts the training objective as the population improves. Evidence: abstract and section II.B. Break condition: if initial population is extremely poor, high T may cause the model to learn from bad solutions and converge prematurely to a poor local optimum.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
1. MPS Generator (tensors A[k], full N-site or k-site, bond dimension χ)  
2. Sampling Engine (right-canonical MPS with masking, autoregressive)  
3. Softmax Surrogate Builder (computes weights from tour costs and temperature T)  
4. Training Block (NLL minimization via AdamW, right-canonicalization)  
5. Population Manager (stores evaluated tours, tracks best)  
6. Temperature Controller (exponential schedule)

**Critical path:**  
Initial random population → Evaluate costs → Build softmax Q → Train MPS on Q → Sample new tours → Evaluate new tours → Update population & best → Update temperature → Repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Bond dimension (χ) vs. memory/time: Larger χ increases expressiveness but scales memory as O(N χ²).  
- k-site window size (k) vs. locality vs. parameters: Small k is parameter-efficient but may miss medium-range patterns; large k approaches full model cost.  
- Temperature schedule: T_init too low risks premature convergence; decay rate controls exploration-exploitation balance.  
- Full vs. k-site: k-site requires rebuilding left environment during sampling but saves parameters.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Stagnant best cost: temperature schedule too aggressive, bond dimension too low, or k too small.  
- Degenerate distribution: temperature too low early on, causing mode collapse.  
- Invalid samples: bug in masking implementation, allowing duplicates.  
- Memory explosion: Instance N too large for full MPS; switch to k-site or reduce χ/batch size.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Masked Sampling Validation:** Run Algorithm 2 on random MPS for N=5; verify all samples are valid permutations.  
2. **Bond Dimension Sweep:** On a fixed instance (e.g., ulysses16), train MPS with χ=4,16,64,128; compare KL divergence or histogram overlap against softmax Q to assess capacity.  
3. **GEO Loop Sanity Check:** Run one GEO iteration on burma14; confirm new samples have lower average cost than initial random population.

## Limitations
- Scale limited to N≤52; unknown scalability due to population size (2^16) and memory O(N χ²).  
- Missing implementation details for k-site training (k-gram extraction, left environment rebuild) and hyperparameter sensitivity (learning rate, convergence criterion).  
- Assumption that local correlations dominate may not hold for non-Euclidean or constrained TSP variants.

## Confidence
- k-site efficacy: Medium (empirical on limited instances, bond dimension may confound).  
- Autoregressive masked validity: High (algorithmic guarantee).  
- Exponential temperature scheduling: Low (no direct evidence, common design choice).
