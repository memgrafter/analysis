# SocialOmni: Benchmarking Audio-Visual Social Interactivity in Omni Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16859
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16859
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a benchmark revealing significant capability variance and a decoupling between perception and generation in omni-modal LLMs for audio-visual social interactivity.

## Executive Summary
SocialOmni addresses the lack of dynamic conversational evaluation for omni-modal LLMs by operationalizing three dimensions of social interactivity: *who* (speaker identification), *when* (turn timing), and *how* (interruption generation). The benchmark comprises 2,000 perception samples and 209 generation tasks with controls for audio‑visual consistency. Evaluation of 12 models shows modest top performance (e.g., 69.25% accuracy in speaker ID, 67.31% on‑time turn entry, judge score 85.08 for interruptions) and highlights that understanding‑centric metrics alone are insufficient for conversational competence. A pronounced decoupling emerges: models excelling at perception often fail at generation.

## Method Summary
The benchmark uses two tasks: (1) a static perception task where models identify the active speaker at a given timestamp from four options, and (2) an interactive generation task simulating streaming video—models decide whether a designated speaker should enter and then generate an interruption. The dataset includes 2,209 video clips (10–30 s) across 15 dialogue subcategories in 4 domains. Task I inputs video, audio, and a timestamp; models output a single‑letter choice. Task II streams video in 1‑second increments until the model answers “YES” to “Should X speak now?”, then generates a response. Metrics: top‑1 accuracy and macro‑F1 for *who*; on‑time rate and timing phases for *when*; mean LLM‑judge score for *how*. The dataset and evaluation code are public on HuggingFace.

## Key Results
- **Who (speaker ID):** Qwen3‑Omni leads with 69.25% top‑1 accuracy.
- **When (turn timing):** Gemini 3 Pro Preview achieves the best on‑time rate (67.31%).
- **How (interruption quality):** Gemini 2.5 Flash attains a judge score of 85.08, with high coverage but notable judge disagreement on subtle cases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SocialOmni decomposes conversational social competence into three executable subskills, forcing models to integrate auditory and visual cues, reason about discourse structure, and produce context‑appropriate responses. The inclusion of audio‑visual consistency tests (consistent vs. inconsistent clips) distinguishes genuine audio‑speech understanding from visual shortcuts. Streaming simulation in Task II mimics real‑time pressure, revealing gaps that static omni‑modal QA benchmarks overlook. This design exposes critical weaknesses in current OLMs, particularly the decoupling between perceptual accuracy and generation quality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Audio‑Visual Speaker Diarization:** Needed to resolve who is speaking from lip movements and voice. *Quick check:* Does model accuracy drop on clips where the visible speaker is silent?
- **Turn‑Taking Theory:** Models must predict natural turn‑completion points (e.g., gaps < 200 ms). *Quick check:* Does the model often respond too early (Δt < –1 s) or too late?
- **Interruption Pragmatics:** Interruptions must be topically relevant and polite. *Quick check:* Do LLM judges rate model interruptions higher than random baselines?
- **Omni‑Modal Fusion:** Combining modalities is essential; inconsistency testing probes fusion depth. *Quick check:* Is Δcons (accuracy difference between consistent/inconsistent) small for strong models?
- **LLM‑as‑Judge Evaluation:** Scalable but sensitive to prompt engineering and reference handling. *Quick check:* Is the large‑gap rate (judge disagreement ≥20) below 10%?
- **Streaming Inference:** Incremental processing is required for real‑time decisions. *Quick check:* Can the model handle variable‑length prefixes without performance collapse?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Video/Audio Input -> Streaming Simulator -> Speaker Identification -> Turn‑Timing Decision -> Interruption Generation -> LLM Judge Scoring

**Critical Path:** For each clip, the streaming loop (1 s stride) repeatedly queries the model until a “YES” turn‑entry decision; then generation occurs. This loop is the computational bottleneck and determines timing metrics.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Stride size:* Finer strides (0.2 s) improve temporal precision but increase cost; the default 1 s balances practicality.  
- *ASR reliance:* Judges score based on ASR transcripts; errors may penalize otherwise fluent generations.  
- *Multi‑reference aggregation:* The protocol for combining multiple ground‑truth continuations in judging is underspecified, risking score variability.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- *Who:* Large Δcons indicates visual bias (high consistent‑clip accuracy, low inconsistent‑clip accuracy).  
- *When:* High Early rate (Δt < –1 s) suggests over‑reliance on brief silences rather than discourse cues.  
- *How:* Low how‑score despite on‑time entry points to poor context grounding; high large‑gap rate signals ambiguous quality boundaries.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Run both tasks on one model (e.g., Qwen3‑Omni) to verify pipeline functionality and obtain reference scores.  
2. **Consistency gap analysis:** Compare performance on consistent vs. inconsistent clips to diagnose modality reliance (Δcons calculation).  
3. **Stride sensitivity test:** Repeat Task II with 0.2 s and 0.5 s strides to assess impact on on‑time rate and timing distribution.

## Limitations
- ASR transcripts used for judging may contain errors, affecting *how* scores.
- Judge prompts and multi‑reference handling are not fully specified, limiting reproducibility of reported leaderboard numbers.
- The fixed 1‑second streaming stride may miss finer turn‑timing nuances; results could vary with different strides.
