# DeepVision-103K: A Visually Diverse, Broad-Coverage, and Verifiable Mathematical Dataset for Multimodal Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.16742
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16742
- **Reference Count**: 27
- **Primary Result**: Training MiMo-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B with RL on DeepVision-103K achieves SOTA on WeMath (85.11%) and LogicVista (65.62%), with broad improvements across multimodal math and general benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DeepVision-103K, a large-scale dataset of 103K verifiable multimodal math and visual logic problems designed to overcome the limited diversity and coverage of existing multimodal RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) datasets. The dataset spans diverse K12 topics and rich visual categories, curated through automated validity filtering, pass-rate difficulty calibration, and Gemini-driven correctness verification. Reinforcement learning training on DeepVision substantially enhances model performance on multimodal reasoning benchmarks, establishing new state-of-the-art results. Ablation studies confirm the critical roles of visual logic tasks and query correctness verification in driving gains in visual perception, reflection, and reasoning.

## Method Summary
DeepVision-103K is constructed from raw sources (e.g., MM-MathInstruct-3M, MultiMath-300K, Zebra-CoT, GameQA-140K) via a three-stage pipeline: (1) **Validity filtering** removes non-unique-answer or non-vision-dependent questions using rule-based checks and Qwen3-VL-32B; (2) **Difficulty filtering** retains samples with pass-rates between 1/8 and 7/8 (math) or 3/8–4/8 (visual logic) based on 8 rollouts with a base model, ensuring learnable difficulty; (3) **Correctness verification** uses Gemini-3-Flash to discard samples with garbled text, image-text mismatch, or incorrect answers. The final dataset combines 77K math and 26K visual logic samples. Models (MiMo-VL-7B-SFT, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) are then fine-tuned with the GSPO RL algorithm using rule-based rewards for answer correctness under specified hyperparameters.

## Key Results
- Training on DeepVision-103K yields SOTA Pass@1 on WeMath (85.11%) and LogicVista (65.62%), outperforming official thinking models and other open-source RLVR datasets.
- DeepVision-103K200 (200 training steps) exceeds Math-77K200 on math average (70.10% vs 69.02%) and LogicVista (65.92% vs 63.53%), demonstrating the complementary value of visual logic tasks.
- Ablations show that removing correctness verification (Unverified-125K200) degrades performance (math avg 67.93% vs 70.10%), underscoring the necessity of query verification for reliable RLVR.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Visual Diversity Enhances Perception and Reflection
- **Claim**: Training on DeepVision-103K’s visually diverse data improves multimodal reasoning by enhancing visual perception and reflection.
- **Assumption**: Exposing the model to a wide distribution of visual features during RL encourages robust visual encoders; reward signals reinforce correct interpretations.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions "rich visual elements" and "enhanced visual perception, reflection and reasoning capabilities"; Sec. 5.1, Figures 8–10 show DeepVision re-examining visuals while base model fails; neighboring RLVR studies (e.g., SPHINX) focus on synthetic puzzles, making DeepVision’s real-world diversity novel.
- **Break condition**: Reducing visual diversity (e.g., sampling only common categories) likely diminishes perception/reflection gains, as seen in ablations excluding visual logic data.

### Mechanism 2: Visual Logic Tasks Provide Transferable Spatial Reasoning
- **Claim**: Incorporating visual logic tasks (mazes, chess) alongside math yields complementary gains that transfer to both math and general multimodal benchmarks.
- **Assumption**: Visual logic trains spatial reasoning and pattern recognition primitives foundational for interpreting complex diagrams; these primitives augment performance in varied visual domains.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states visual logic "jointly enhancing mathematical and visual logic reasoning"; Sec. 5.2, Table 4 shows DeepVision-103K200 outperforms Math-77K200 on both math and LogicVista; visual-logic-only (26K50) improves over base across benchmarks.
- **Break condition**: Inappropriate mixing ratios or overly domain-specific visual logic tasks may cause negative transfer, as cautioned by other RLVR mixture studies.

### Mechanism 3: Correctness Verification Is Essential for Clean Reward Signals
- **Claim**: Query correctness verification (via Gemini) is necessary for effective RLVR; removing it degrades performance due to noisy rewards.
- **Assumption**: RL updates are sensitive to reward accuracy; corrupted questions or answers produce misleading gradients.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract describes "Gemini-driven correctness verification"; Sec. 5.3, Table 4 shows Unverified-125K200 underperforms DeepVision-103K200; prior work questions verification necessity, but DeepVision provides empirical evidence for its importance in multimodal RLVR.
- **Break condition**: Overly stringent verification loses valid samples (<50% retention); overly lenient verification retains noisy samples (>30% reject rate indicates corruption).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)  
  **Why needed here**: The entire training paradigm relies on RLVR; understanding rule-based rewards (answer correctness) versus learned reward models is key.  
  **Quick check**: What is the main difference between RLVR and standard RL in LLM post‑training? How are rewards computed in DeepVision?  
  Answer: RLVR uses a deterministic verifier (MathVerify) to assign +1 for correct final answers, 0 otherwise, instead of learned reward models.

- **Concept**: Curriculum via Pass‑Rate Difficulty Filtering  
  **Why needed here**: Samples are retained based on model rollouts to ensure data lies in the learnable zone, avoiding extreme difficulty.  
  **Quick check**: Given a sample’s pass rate is 0/8 or 8/8, what action is taken and why?  
  Answer: 0/8 – too hard/unverifiable → discard; 8/8 – too easy → discard; retained in [1/8, 7/8] to provide learning signal.

- **Concept**: Multimodal Query Correctness Verification  
  **Why needed here**: The pipeline uses Gemini‑3‑Flash to filter out garbled text, image‑text mismatches, and incorrect answers, protecting RL from noisy signals.  
  **Quick check**: What three checks does the verifier perform, and what happens to a ‘CORRECTION’ label?  
  Answer: (1) input completeness, (2) image‑text consistency, (3) answer correctness; ‘CORRECTION’ samples are discarded to avoid noise.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Raw sources (MM‑MathInstruct‑3M, MultiMath‑300K, Zebra‑CoT, GameQA‑140K, PuzzleVQA) → Validity filter (rule‑based + Qwen3‑VL‑32B) → Difficulty filter (rollout with MiMo‑VL‑7B‑SFT + MathVerify pass‑rate) → Correctness verifier (Gemini‑3‑Flash) → Final dataset (103K) → Training framework (verl) → RL algorithm (GSPO) → Base models (MiMo‑VL‑7B‑SFT, Qwen3‑VL‑8B‑Instruct) → Evaluation suite (WeMath, MathVision, LogicVista, MMMU, etc.).
- **Critical path**:  
  1. Collect raw multimodal math data.  
  2. Remove proof/descriptive questions via keywords.  
  3. Use Qwen3‑VL‑32B to keep questions with unique answers that require vision.  
  4. Run 8 rollouts with base model, compute pass‑rate via MathVerify; retain math in [1/8, 7/8] (prefer under‑represented knowledge points from [1/8, 4/8]); keep visual logic in [3/8, 4/8].  
  5. Run Gemini‑3‑Flash verification; accept only samples that pass all three checks and have correct answers.  
  6. Combine math (77K) and visual logic (26K) into DeepVision‑103K.  
  7. Train with GSPO for 200 steps using hyperparameters (lr 1e‑6, kl_coef 1e‑3, etc.).  
  8. Evaluate on benchmarks.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - **Pass‑rate window**: wider (e.g., [0, 8]) includes more data but risks too hard/easy samples; narrower (e.g., [2/8, 6/8]) ensures moderate difficulty but reduces volume.  
  - **Rollout sample size**: 8 rollouts is a compromise; 16 increases reliability but doubles cost.  
  - **Verifier choice**: Stronger (Gemini‑3‑Flash) reduces noise but adds cost and potential bias; cheaper models may miss subtle errors.  
  - **Mixing ratio**: Paper uses 3:1 math:visual logic; alternatives might shift transfer balance.  
  - **Training steps**: 200 steps balance learning and overfitting; more steps may yield diminishing returns.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Extremely low retention after validity filtering (<10%): raw data too noisy or filter too aggressive.  
  - Very high retention after difficulty filtering (>80%): base model likely too strong or pass‑rate computation flawed.  
  - Verification rejects >30% of samples: many corrupted inputs; consider improving sources or calibrating verifier.  
  - Training: high reward variance or no increase → remaining noisy samples or hyperparameter mismatch.  
  - Evaluation: no improvement over base model → check dataset mix, reward implementation, or base model capability.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Sanity check**: Train on 500 random samples for 10 steps; verify reward increases and response length grows.  
  2. **Correctness ablation**: Train two models with identical splits, one with verified samples, one without; compare reward curves and benchmark scores to quantify impact.  
  3. **Mixing ratio sweep**: Train with 0%, 25%, 50%, 75% visual logic (total samples constant); evaluate math and general benchmarks to confirm optimal ratio (expected ~25%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Which specific capabilities (visual perception, reflection, mathematical reasoning) are enhanced by RL training on DeepVision-103K, and what are the underlying mechanisms?  
  **Basis**: Section 5.1 analyzes improvements via human annotation, identifying three enhancement types, but the analysis is limited to one model family and small samples; mechanisms are not deeply investigated.
- **Open Question 2**: What is the precise contribution of visual logic tasks (e.g., mazes, chess) to multimodal reasoning, and how do they transfer to mathematical problem‑solving?  
  **Basis**: Section 5.2 shows positive transfer from visual logic mix, but optimal mixture ratio and exact transferred skills (spatial reasoning vs. pattern recognition) remain unresolved.
- **Open Question 3**: Is query correctness verification in the curation pipeline essential for effective RLVR, or could models learn robustly despite a moderate level of noise?  
  **Basis**: Section 5.3 compares verified vs. unverified data, finding verification necessary, but only one unverified type and one model were tested; noise tolerance boundaries and alternative verification strategies are open.

## Limitations
- **Code and model availability**: MiMo-VL-7B-SFT-2508 and GSPO implementation are not public, requiring re‑implementation.
- **Evaluation specification**: Benchmark-specific input formatting and per‑benchmark preprocessing details are underspecified.
- **Hardware constraints**: Training used 32 H20 GPUs; reproducing on limited hardware may necessitate hyperparameter adjustments that affect results.

## Confidence
- **Visual diversity gains**: Medium. Ablations indicate benefit, but diversity effect not isolated.
- **Visual logic transfer**: Medium. Mixed data improves math and logic benchmarks, but optimal ratio/coverage unknown.
- **Correctness verification**: High. Unverified data causes clear performance drop, consistent with RLVR noise sensitivity.

## Next Checks
1. **Diversity ablation**: Create three 30K subsets with low/medium/high visual diversity. Train and compare gains on LogicVista and WeMath.
2. **Transfer isolation**: Train on math‑only, visual‑logic‑only, and combined sets. Evaluate math/logic benchmarks; add control with replaced images to test vision‑specific gains.
3. **Verification sweep**: Vary pass‑rate windows ([0,8]–[3,5]) and verification strictness. Measure performance to find optimal trade‑off.
