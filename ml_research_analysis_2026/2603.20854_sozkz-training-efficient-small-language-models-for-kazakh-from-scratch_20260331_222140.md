# SozKZ: Training Efficient Small Language Models for Kazakh from Scratch

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20854
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20854
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** The 600M-parameter SozKZ model achieves 30.3% accuracy on Kazakh multiple-choice QA and 25.5% on SIB-200 topic classification, outperforming larger multilingual models.

## Executive Summary
The SozKZ paper addresses the severe underrepresentation of Kazakh in existing multilingual LLMs, caused by inefficient tokenization of its agglutinative morphology and minimal parameter allocation in large models. The authors train small, dedicated Llama-architecture models (50M–600M parameters) from scratch on a 9B-token monolingual corpus using a 50K BPE tokenizer trained specifically on Kazakh. Their 600M model nearly matches the performance of Llama-3.2-1B (32.0%) on Kazakh cultural QA (30.3%) despite having half the parameters, and surpasses all evaluated multilingual models up to 2B on the SIB-200 topic classification task (25.5%). The results demonstrate that for low-resource languages, small specialized models with language-appropriate tokenizers can deliver competitive performance at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Method Summary
The training data was assembled from 18 public sources (CulturaX, HPLT 2.0, mC4, MADLAD-400, Kazakh Wikipedia, etc.), yielding ~28.4M raw documents. A 9-stage cleaning pipeline (Unicode normalization, control character removal, length/URL/HTML filtering, script ratio, language identification, deduplication) reduced this to 13.7M documents and ~9B high-quality tokens (48.2% pass rate). A Byte-Pair Encoding tokenizer with 50,257 merges was trained exclusively on this cleaned corpus to capture Kazakh morphemes and minimize fertility. Four model sizes (50M, 150M, 300M, 600M) were built as LlamaForCausalLM with SwiGLU, RoPE, and RMSNorm, using tied embeddings and context lengths of 1024 (150M) or 2048 (others). Training used AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.95, weight decay=0.1), a cosine LR schedule with linear warmup, mixed precision (bfloat16), gradient clipping, and distributed data parallel. The 600M model required 8× H100 80GB GPUs; smaller models used 2× RTX 4090. All models were trained for one epoch over the 9B tokens. Zero-shot evaluation employed full answer text likelihood scoring for multiple-choice tasks and logit-based classification for SIB-200.

## Key Results
- SozKZ-600M reaches **30.3%** accuracy on Kazakh multiple-choice QA, within 1.7 points of Llama-3.2-1B (32.0%) with half the parameters.
- SozKZ-600M achieves **25.5%** on SIB-200 topic classification, surpassing Gemma-2B (20.1%), Llama-3.2-1B (20.1%), and all other multilingual models evaluated up to 2B parameters.
- Performance scales consistently: **50M (22.8%) → 300M (~28.4%) → 600M (30.3%)** on MC QA, with no indication of saturation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dedicated, language-specific tokenizer reduces fertility and improves efficiency
- **Claim:** A tokenizer trained exclusively on Kazakh dramatically improves computational and modeling efficiency by reducing token fragmentation (fertility).
- **Mechanism:** Byte-Pair Encoding merges frequent morphemic units in the agglutinative language, creating subwords that respect suffix boundaries. Lower average fertility means more semantic content per token, allowing more meaningful lexical units within the fixed context window and reducing the number of modeling steps.
- **Core assumption:** Multilingual tokenizers, trained on language mixtures dominated by English, oversegment Kazakh words and destroy morphological cues.
- **Evidence anchors:** Paper reports a 2–3× fertility advantage over multilingual tokenizers; Section 3.2 discusses poor fertility for morphologically rich Cyrillic languages; related work (KazByte, Paramanu) also targets tokenizer inefficiency.
- **Break condition:** If the tokenizer were trained on mixed-language data or with too small a vocabulary, the fertility advantage would diminish.

### Mechanism 2: Monolingual training avoids capacity dilution
- **Claim:** Training a small model from scratch on monolingual data avoids the "capacity dilution" of multilingual models, yielding higher effective capacity per language.
- **Mechanism:** In a multilingual model, a fixed parameter budget is split across representation space for many languages, leaving only a small fraction tuned to Kazakh. A dedicated monolingual model allocates 100% of its parameters to Kazakh, compensating for smaller absolute size.
- **Core assumption:** There is a non-linear relationship between parameter count and performance for a specific language; a fully specialized smaller model can outperform a larger model where most parameters are irrelevant to the target language.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes multilingual models allocate minimal capacity; Section 2.1 discusses mBERT and XLM-R; SIB-200 results show SozKZ-600M outperforming Gemma-2B and Llama-3.2-1B.
- **Break condition:** If monolingual training data were insufficient in quantity or diversity, the specialized capacity would be wasted.

### Mechanism 3: Consistent scaling with parameter increase under controlled conditions
- **Claim:** For a dedicated monolingual model with an efficient tokenizer, performance scales predictably with parameter count within 50M–600M, indicating no premature saturation.
- **Mechanism:** With tokenizer inefficiency and capacity dilution removed, the model exhibits near-ideal scaling. More parameters allow richer representation of Kazakh's morphological and syntactic space. The consistent gains on MC QA suggest the model remains on the pre-asymptotic part of the learning curve.
- **Core assumption:** Chinchilla scaling laws derived from high-resource languages transfer to low-resource morphologically rich languages when tokenizer efficiency is controlled.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports scaling from 22.8% (50M) to 30.3% (600M) on MC QA; Section 5.2 presents Figure 2 showing no saturation; Section 2.3 discusses over-training philosophy relative to Chinchilla optimum.
- **Break condition:** If scaling performance plateaued sharply beyond a certain size, it would indicate limitations from data or architecture.

## Foundational Learning

### Tokenizer Fertility & Agglutinative Morphology
- **Why needed:** The efficiency claim hinges on understanding that Kazakh words often contain many stacked suffixes (e.g., *үйімде* = "in my house"). A poor tokenizer splits such words into many subwords, increasing sequence length and losing morphological cues. This directly impacts model efficiency and performance.
- **Quick check:** How would fertility of a Kazakh sentence compare between a tokenizer trained on English-heavy data versus one trained purely on Kazakh? What is a potential downside of a very large vocabulary for a small model?

### Compute-Optimal vs. Data-Optimal Scaling (Chinchilla Laws)
- **Why needed:** The paper positions its 15:1 token-to-parameter ratio against the ~20:1 Chinchilla optimum. Understanding this trade-off is key to interpreting whether the 600M model is undertrained and whether scaling should increase data or parameters next.
- **Quick check:** If you have 10B high-quality tokens and a 500M parameter budget, should you train for one epoch (10B tokens) or multiple epochs? What does Chinchilla suggest, and what practical constraints might override it?

### Zero-Shot Evaluation with Full Answer Likelihood
- **Why needed:** The evaluation protocol (scoring by sum of log-probs over full answer text) is crucial for fair comparison across models with different tokenizers. Comparing only the first token of each choice would be biased. This concept is about designing a robust, tokenizer-agnostic benchmark.
- **Quick check:** Why is comparing the logit of the first token of each answer choice flawed when models have different tokenizers? How does computing the likelihood of the full answer string mitigate this?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Raw Kazakh sources → Cleaning pipeline → Tokenizer training (50K BPE) → Model initialization (Llama architecture) → Training (AdamW, mixed precision) → Evaluation (full-answer likelihood)

**Critical path:**
1. Clean Corpus → Tokenizer: Tokenizer must be trained only on final cleaned Kazakh corpus; contamination degrades fertility.
2. Tokenizer → Model Inputs: All training/evaluation data must be pre-tokenized with this tokenizer; using a different tokenizer invalidates comparisons.
3. Model Config → Training: Hidden size/intermediate size ratio and context length must match Table 1 exactly for reproducible scaling.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Vocabulary Size (50K): Larger vocab reduces fertility but increases embedding matrix size; for a 600M model, 50K consumes ~20M parameters. Smaller vocab would increase sequence length and hurt throughput.
- Context Length (2K vs 1K): Longer context improves long-range dependencies but increases memory (KV cache) and attention compute O(context²). The 150M model uses 1K likely for memory savings on smaller GPUs.
- Data Filtering Aggressiveness: 48.2% pass rate trade-off; stricter filters improve purity but discard potentially useful low-quality text (e.g., social media).

**Failure signatures:**
- High Fertility: If tokenizer's fertility on a Kazakh test set is not 2–3× better than multilingual tokenizers, the efficiency mechanism failed. Check tokenizer training data diversity and merge operations.
- No Scaling Improvement: If SozKZ-300M and SozKZ-600M have nearly identical MC QA scores, scaling may be capped by data quality/quantity or suboptimal architecture (e.g., too few layers).
- Task-Specific Collapse: If SIB-200 performance is poor while MC QA is good (or vice versa), the model may have specialized to training data distribution or evaluation protocol may be flawed.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Tokenizer Baseline Benchmark:** Compute fertility (tokens/word) on a held-out Kazakh sample for the new tokenizer and for multilingual baselines. Expect at least a 2× gap.
2. **Validation Scaling Curve:** Train small models (50M, 150M) for full 9B tokens. Plot validation loss vs. steps. Expect smooth descending curves with no sharp plateaus.
3. **Zero-Shot Task Sanity Check:** Evaluate a randomly initialized model (should be near random: 25% MC/Belebele, 14.3% SIB-200) and a small model trained for 1B tokens. This validates the harness and confirms early learning signal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does scaling beyond 600M maintain efficiency advantages and close the MC QA gap?
- How does instruction tuning affect performance and the efficiency trade-off?
- Can bits-per-byte (BPB) evaluation more precisely quantify tokenizer efficiency than fertility?
- To what extent is the methodology applicable to other Turkic languages (Turkish, Uzbek, Kyrgyz)?

## Limitations
- Data provenance and accessibility of the 18 aggregated corpora are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Precise training configuration (global batch size, exact number of steps, DDP/FSDP details) is omitted, making compute budget replication ambiguous.
- The demonstrated efficacy is specific to Kazakh; transfer to other language families or scripts requires additional validation.
