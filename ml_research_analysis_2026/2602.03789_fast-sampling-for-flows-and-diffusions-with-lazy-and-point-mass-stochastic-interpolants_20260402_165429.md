# Fast Sampling for Flows and Diffusions with Lazy and Point Mass Stochastic Interpolants

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.03789
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03789
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Accelerates sampling for flows and diffusions by optimizing interpolation schedules without retraining, achieving up to threefold step reduction on a large text-to-image model.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the problem of speeding up sampling from generative models based on flows and diffusions. Its central insight is that different interpolation schedules in stochastic interpolants are pathwise equivalent: any sample path generated under one schedule can be transformed into a path under another schedule via a simple, deterministic reparameterization. This equivalence allows turning a pretrained model that uses a standard linear schedule into one that uses a more efficient "lazy" or "point mass" schedule without any retraining. Under a Gaussian data assumption, the authors show that lazy schedules—zero drift for ODEs and point mass diffusion for SDEs—are optimal in well-defined senses and yield substantial step reductions. For a 1.3B-parameter text-to-image flow model (PRX), lazy ODE cuts required steps by up to three times for ≤64 steps, while lazy SDE achieves even larger gains: 128 lazy steps match the pixel-RMSE of 171 linear steps, with statistically significant improvement. The method is training-free and applies to both flows and diffusions.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is the proof of pathwise equivalence between stochastic interpolant schedules: given two schedules defined by their drift and diffusion coefficients, there exists a time-dependent transformation of the state variable that maps solutions of one SDE to solutions of the other. This transformation is exact and deterministic, preserving the full distribution of samples. The authors extend the framework to point mass schedules, where the base Gaussian collapses to a point at the endpoint. Specializing to Gaussian data, they identify lazy schedules (zero drift for ODEs, point mass for SDEs) that are optimal under variance-preserving and statistical criteria, respectively. For models pretrained with a linear schedule, they derive closed-form formulas to convert the original coefficients into those of the lazy or point-mass schedules. Consequently, one can accelerate sampling simply by plugging the converted coefficients into the existing solver, eliminating any need for retraining.

## Key Results
- Lazy ODE reduces sampling steps by up to three times for step counts ≤64 on the PRX model.
- Lazy SDE achieves comparable pixel-RMSE with 128 steps versus 171 steps for the linear schedule, with statistically significant improvement.
- The approach requires no retraining and works for both flows and diffusions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Pathwise equivalence ensures that any two valid interpolation schedules generate identically distributed samples, differing only by a deterministic transformation of the sample path. By exploiting this, the method converts a slow schedule (e.g., linear) into a fast one (e.g., lazy) through exact reparameterization of the SDE/ODE coefficients. Lazy schedules minimize the deterministic drift (or collapse diffusion to a point), which simplifies the dynamics and reduces the number of numeric integration steps needed without altering the final distribution. The closed-form conversion formulas make this transformation practical for pretrained models, guaranteeing that accelerated sampling produces samples from the same distribution as the original schedule.

## Foundational Learning
- **Stochastic Interpolants**: A unifying framework defining probability paths between data and noise via a base Gaussian process. *Why needed*: It generalizes flows and diffusions, enabling schedule optimization in a common formalism. *Quick check*: Confirm that the interpolant’s marginals at times 0 and 1 recover the data and prior distributions.
- **Pathwise Equivalence**: The property that two schedules produce sample paths related by a deterministic transformation. *Why needed*: It is the theoretical foundation that allows schedule conversion without retraining. *Quick check*: Derive the transformation from the SDE drift/diffusion coefficients and verify it maps solutions correctly.
- **Lazy Schedules**: Schedules with zero drift (ODE) or point‑mass diffusion (SDE). *Why needed*: They minimize deterministic evolution, thus requiring fewer numeric steps for accurate sampling. *Quick check*: For Gaussian data, check that the variance schedule is constant (ODE) or that diffusion vanishes at the endpoint (SDE).
- **Variance-Preserving**: In the lazy ODE case, the variance of the interpolant remains constant over time. *Why needed*: It avoids unnecessary noise injection, simplifying the dynamics. *Quick check*: Compute variance as a function of time and confirm it is constant.
- **Gaussian Assumption**: The theoretical optimality of lazy schedules assumes the data distribution is Gaussian. *Why needed*: It yields closed-form solutions and clear optimality conditions. *Quick check*: Assess data normality (e.g., QQ‑plot) to gauge the assumption’s reasonableness.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Pretrained Generative Model (with linear schedule) → Conversion Module (computes lazy schedule parameters via closed‑form formulas) → Lazy/Point‑Mass Scheduler → SDE/ODE Solver → Generated Samples  
**Critical path**: Given a pretrained model, the conversion module derives the target schedule coefficients from the original ones; the solver then integrates the SDE/ODE using these coefficients, producing samples that follow the pathwise‑equivalent lazy schedule. No model weights are altered.  
**Design tradeoffs**: Lazy schedules drastically reduce step counts but depend on exact knowledge of the original schedule. Under non‑Gaussian data, the theoretical optimality may weaken, potentially requiring more steps to maintain quality. The conversion itself is exact, so there is no inherent quality loss if performed correctly.  
**Failure signatures**: Inaccurate original schedule parameters lead to biased or degenerate samples (e.g., blurriness, mode collapse). Using too few steps with lazy schedules can cause numerical instability, especially for SDEs with near‑zero diffusion. Strong deviations from Gaussian data may cause lazy schedules to underperform relative to the baseline.

## Limitations
- Empirical validation is restricted to a single large text-to-image flow model (PRX); generalizability across architectures (e.g., diffusion U‑Nets) and datasets remains unproven.
- Theoretical optimality of lazy and point‑mass schedules relies on a Gaussian data assumption; real‑world data may deviate, potentially diminishing the gains.
- The proposed conversion formulas require precise knowledge of the original interpolation schedule; robustness to estimation errors is not addressed, limiting practical use with black‑box checkpoints.
