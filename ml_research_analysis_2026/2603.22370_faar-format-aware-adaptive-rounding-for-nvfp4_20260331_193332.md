# FAAR: Format-Aware Adaptive Rounding for NVFP4
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22370
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22370
- **Reference Count:** 31
- **Primary Result:** FAAR with 2FA reduces WikiText-2 perplexity from 14.28 to 12.60 (Llama3-1B) and 23.06 to 21.27 (Qwen3-1.7B) versus RTN, with minimal training overhead.

## Executive Summary
Deploying large language models on edge devices using the NVFP4 format is challenging due to its non-uniform numerical grid, which causes suboptimal rounding errors during weight quantization. This paper introduces FAAR, a learnable rounding strategy that optimizes per-weight rounding by modeling NVFP4's interval structure with continuous variables updated via gradient-based training. This is paired with a two-stage format alignment (2FA) procedure for layer-wise and full-model tuning. Evaluations on Llama3 and Qwen3 models show significant improvements in perplexity, cosine similarity to full-precision, and zero-shot task accuracy over strong baselines like GPTQ, while requiring only about 4 GPU hours for 1B-parameter models.

## Method Summary
FAAR addresses NVFP4's non-uniform grid by treating rounding as a differentiable operation. Each weight is associated with a continuous "rounding offset" that determines its position within the NVFP4 interval. These offsets are learned during a short gradient-based training phase, allowing the model to adapt to local error sensitivities. The two-stage format alignment (2FA) then proceeds: first, layer-wise tuning optimizes offsets per layer; second, a full-model tuning pass refines offsets globally to mitigate inter-layer error accumulation. This combination enables effective quantization-aware adaptation specifically tailored to NVFP4's format constraints.

## Key Results
- Perplexity reductions on WikiText-2: Llama3-1B from 14.28 (RTN) to 12.60, Qwen3-1.7B from 23.06 to 21.27.
- Higher cosine similarity to BF16 (e.g., 98.06% vs 96.08% on Llama3-1B WikiText-2) compared to baselines.
- Minimal training overhead: ~4 GPU hours for 1B-parameter models.
- Gains consistent on C4 dataset and across zero-shot task accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
NVFP4's non-uniform grid creates irregular intervals between representable numbers, making naive rounding (e.g., round-to-nearest) highly suboptimal as it ignores local error sensitivities. FAAR works by making rounding a learned, differentiable parameter. This allows the quantization process to be guided by the model's own loss landscape, effectively "steering" weights into the most beneficial positions within each NVFP4 bin. The two-stage alignment (2FA) then systematically propagates these local improvements through the network hierarchy, first securing per-layer stability and then harmonizing layer-wise adjustments to minimize global error accumulation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Quantization & Non-Uniform Grids:** Maps continuous weights to discrete levels; NVFP4's exponentially spaced levels require format-specific handling.
  - *Why needed:* Standard uniform rounding fails on non-uniform grids like NVFP4.
  - *Quick check:* Compare rounding error distributions on uniform (INT4) vs. NVFP4 grids for a weight tensor.
- **Differentiable Rounding:** Treats rounding as a continuous operation (e.g., via straight-through estimator) to enable gradient-based optimization.
  - *Why needed:* To learn optimal rounding positions within each NVFP4 interval.
  - *Quick check:* Validate gradient flow through a simulated rounding operation in a small network.
- **Quantization-Aware Training (QAT):** Fine-tuning with fake quantization to adapt model weights to low-precision constraints.
  - *Why needed:* FAAR's offset learning is a form of QAT focused on rounding.
  - *Quick check:* Apply basic QAT with uniform 4-bit to a small model and measure accuracy drop.
- **NVFP4 Format:** A 4-bit floating-point format with non-uniform, exponent-based number spacing.
  - *Why needed:* Central to the problem; FAAR is format-aware.
  - *Quick check:* List the representable values in NVFP4 for a given exponent range.
- **Error Propagation in LLMs:** Quantization errors in early layers can amplify through the network.
  - *Why needed:* Justifies the two-stage alignment to manage inter-layer effects.
  - *Quick check:* Measure activation magnitude changes across layers in a quantized vs. full-precision forward pass.
- **Gradient-Based Optimization:** Uses model loss gradients to update rounding offsets.
  - *Why needed:* Core mechanism of FAAR's adaptability.
  - *Quick check:* Implement a toy problem where rounding is parameterized and optimize via SGD.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Activations -> Layer 1 (Quantized Weights + FAAR Offsets) -> Layer 2 ... -> Layer N (Quantized Weights + FAAR Offsets) -> Output Loss. 2FA orchestrates offset updates: first pass (per-layer), second pass (global).

**Critical Path:**  
1. Initialize model in BF16.  
2. For each layer, initialize FAAR offsets for NVFP4 weight quantization.  
3. **Stage 1 (Layer-wise):** Freeze other layers, optimize offsets for current layer using small dataset.  
4. **Stage 2 (Full-model):** Unfreeze all layers, jointly refine all offsets.  
5. Export final quantized weights (offsets applied + NVFP4 rounding).

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Per-layer vs. Joint Optimization:* Stage 1 isolates errors but may miss cross-layer interactions; Stage 2 captures global effects but risks destabilizing per-layer gains. 2FA balances this.
- *Training Overhead vs. Accuracy:* More offset tuning steps yield better perplexity but increase GPU hours; FAAR's ~4 hours is a practical compromise.
- *Format Generality:* FAAR is designed for NVFP4's specific grid; applying to uniform formats yields less benefit.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Diverging loss during Stage 2 (offset updates conflict across layers).  
- No improvement over RTN on early layers (offsets fail to capture local sensitivities).  
- Significant accuracy drop on long-context tasks (error accumulation despite 2FA).

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Ablation of 2FA Stages:** Compare (a) layer-wise only, (b) global only, (c) reversed order (global then layer-wise) on WikiText-2 perplexity.  
2. **Offset Visualization:** For a representative layer, plot histogram of learned FAAR offsets versus NVFP4 bin centers; check for clustering patterns.  
3. **Sensitivity to Dataset Size:** Vary the number of calibration samples used in Stage 1/2 and measure perplexity vs. training time tradeoff.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can FAAR be effectively extended to optimize rounding for activation quantization under NVFP4?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper focuses on weight rounding optimization but acknowledges W4A4 quantization challenges in related work, leaving activation quantization with non-uniform grids unaddressed.
- **Why unresolved:** Activations exhibit dynamic ranges and non-uniform distributions that may require distinct rounding strategies; without format-aware activation quantization, full W4A4 deployment may see degraded performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct W4A4 experiments applying FAAR to both weights and activations, measuring PPL, cosine similarity, and zero-shot accuracy against activation-only baselines.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the FAAR+2FA framework maintain its efficacy and training efficiency when scaling to LLMs beyond 8B parameters?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Evaluation is restricted to Llama3 and Qwen3 models up to 8B; no results on larger models (e.g., 70B) are reported.
- **Why unresolved:** Larger models have different activation distributions, outlier patterns, and error propagation dynamics that could affect rounding optimization and alignment stages; training overhead may also scale unfavorably.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply the method to 70B-class models (e.g., Llama3-70B), report PPL, cosine similarity, and GPU hours, and compare scaling trends against smaller models.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is the two-stage format alignment (2FA) sequence optimal, or could alternative fine-tuning orders (e.g., global then layer-wise) or single-stage approaches achieve comparable or better performance?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper proposes a fixed layer-wise then global order without ablation of stage sequence or comparison to other alignment schedules.
- **Why unresolved:** Inter-layer error accumulation might be mitigated more efficiently with different stage ordering or combined objectives; the current design may not be theoretically optimal.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablate stage order (reverse sequence, single-stage joint optimization) and measure PPL, cosine similarity, and convergence steps to determine the most effective alignment schedule.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to proprietary Llama3 and Qwen3 families without third-party or architectural diversity (e.g., encoder-decoder, MoE).
- No quantitative comparison of NVFP4's non-uniform grid distortion against a uniform 4-bit baseline (e.g., INT4) under identical optimization.
- Hardware assumptions about NVFP4 support may not generalize across all edge devices; real-world deployment constraints unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Quantization accuracy gains (perplexity/cosine similarity):** Medium – Strong internal results but lacking independent verification and broader model diversity.
- **Format alignment (2FA) efficacy:** Medium – Layer-wise tuning is promising but its interaction with full-model tuning needs ablation on deeper models.
- **Minimal training overhead claim:** Low – "~4 GPU hours" is anecdotal; lacks controlled scaling analysis (batch size, hardware specifics, parameter count dependence).

## Next Checks
