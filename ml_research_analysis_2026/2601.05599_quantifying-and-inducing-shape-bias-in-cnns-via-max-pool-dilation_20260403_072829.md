# Quantifying and Inducing Shape Bias in CNNs via Max-Pool Dilation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05599  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05599  
- **Reference count:** 18  
- **Primary result:** A new dataset-level metric (L0-SSIM) predicts shape-dominance; dilating max-pooling layers to 2 while freezing convolutional weights adapts CNNs toward shape bias, improving accuracy on shape-oriented datasets but degrading performance on texture-rich ones.

## Executive Summary
CNNs are known to favor texture over shape, limiting performance on datasets where global structure is more informative (e.g., sketches). This paper introduces L0-SSIM, a metric that quantifies a dataset's shape-texture balance by measuring similarity between an image and its L0-smoothed version. High scores indicate shape-dominance. Building on this, the authors propose a lightweight adaptation: set dilation=2 for all max-pooling layers in a pre-trained CNN, freeze all convolutional weights, and train only the final classifier. Evaluated on six small datasets, L0-SSIM reliably predicts when a shape-biased model will succeed. The adaptation improves accuracy on shape-dominant datasets (e.g., MPEG-7 +0.5 pp, BTSD +1.7 pp) but harms texture-rich ones (e.g., Stanford Dogs -12.8 pp), confirming a clear trade-off. The method is computationally efficient and particularly effective in low-data regimes.

## Method Summary
L0-SSIM computation: For each image, convert to YCbCr and take the luminance (Y) channel. Apply L0 gradient minimization smoothing with λ=0.01 to suppress high-frequency texture while preserving edges. Compute the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) between the original Y and the smoothed Y_smooth. Average SSIM across all images in the dataset to obtain the dataset-level L0-SSIM score.

Adaptation procedure: Start with an ImageNet pre-trained ResNeXt-50. Identify all MaxPool2d layers and set their dilation to 2. Freeze all convolutional layer parameters. Replace the final fully connected layer with a new randomly initialized layer matching the number of target classes. Train only this new classifier using Adam (lr=1e-3) with StepLR decay (factor=0.8 every 10 epochs), batch size 8, up to 100 epochs with early stopping (patience=5), via 5-fold cross-validation.

## Key Results
- L0-SSIM successfully distinguishes shape-dominant datasets (Sketches 0.999, MPEG-7 0.989) from texture-dominant ones (Stanford Dogs 0.699, DTD 0.709).
- Max-pool dilation adaptation (S_maxpool-Model) yields clear gains on shape-oriented datasets: MPEG-7 +0.5 pp, BTSD +1.7 pp, and Sketches +3.1 pp vs. texture-biased baseline. Conversely, it degrades accuracy on texture-rich datasets (Stanford Dogs -12.8 pp) and abstract shape datasets (AnimeFace -9.6 pp).
- The approach requires minimal computational overhead (only classifier training) and shows particular promise in low-data regimes due to its parameter efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: L0-SSIM Quantifies Shape-Texture Balance via Edge-Preserving Smoothing
- **Claim:** L0-SSIM provides a dataset-level metric where high scores indicate shape-dominance.
- **Mechanism:** L0 gradient minimization smooths an image’s luminance channel by suppressing high-frequency texture components while preserving salient edges (global shape). SSIM then measures similarity between the original and smoothed images; high similarity means smoothing did not alter the image much, implying it was already dominated by preserved shape structures.
- **Core assumption:** L0 smoothing selectively removes texture without significantly changing global shape contours.
- **Break condition:** If L0 smoothing fails to preserve edges (e.g., in noisy or low-contrast images), SSIM may not correlate with shape dominance.

### Mechanism 2: Max-Pool Dilation Expands Effective Receptive Field to Promote Shape Bias
- **Claim:** Increasing max-pool dilation to 2 while freezing conv weights enlarges the effective receptive field (ERF), shifting the model’s bias toward global shape.
- **Mechanism:** Dilation in max-pooling causes the pooling operation to cover a larger spatial area (e.g., 2x2 with dilation=2 samples pixels farther apart). This broader aggregation of activations, processed by frozen convolutional filters, effectively increases the ERF of subsequent layers. Larger ERF encourages the network to integrate information over broader spatial contexts, reducing reliance on local texture patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Enlarged ERF inherently favors global shape cues over local texture details.
- **Break condition:** If the task requires fine-grained local information (e.g., texture classification), enlarged ERF may oversmooth and discard crucial cues.

### Mechanism 3: L0-SSIM Predicts Suitability for Shape-Biased Models
- **Claim:** A dataset’s L0-SSIM score reliably indicates whether a shape-biased model (e.g., with larger ERF) will outperform a texture-biased baseline.
- **Mechanism:** Shape-dominant datasets (high L0-SSIM) contain images where global structure persists after L0 smoothing; shape-biased models, which aggregate broader context, better capture this persistent structure. Conversely, texture-dominant datasets (low L0-SSIM) rely on high-frequency details removed by smoothing; shape-biased models lose these cues, underperforming.
- **Core assumption:** There is a systematic correspondence between the dataset’s intrinsic shape-texture statistics (as captured by L0-SSIM) and the optimal inductive bias for classification.
- **Break condition:** If a dataset has mixed or ambiguous shape-texture characteristics not summarized by a single scalar metric, the prediction may be unreliable.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Pre-trained ResNeXt-50 -> MaxPool Layers (dilation=2) -> Frozen Convolutional Layers -> New Classification Layer

**Critical path:**
1. Compute L0-SSIM for the target dataset (one-time, offline).
2. If L0-SSIM > 0.8 (empirical shape-dominant threshold), apply max-pool dilation=2.
3. Freeze all convolutional weights; randomly reinitialize and train only the final classification layer.
4. Deploy the adapted model.

**Design tradeoffs:** Improves accuracy on shape-dominant datasets (e.g., BTSD +1.7 pp, MPEG-7 +0.5 pp) but degrades on texture-rich (Stanford Dogs -12.8 pp) or highly abstract (Sketches -0.9 pp) ones. Minimal compute overhead vs. full fine-tuning, but metric computation adds a preprocessing step. The 0.8 threshold is empirical; may need adjustment for new data distributions.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Accuracy drop on texture-dominant datasets after adaptation.
2. No gain on abstract shape datasets despite high L0-SSIM.
3. Metric instability if L0 smoothing parameter λ is not fixed or data is out-of-distribution (e.g., color images with different statistics).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Metric Computation:** Process a small subset of the new dataset (e.g., 100 images) to estimate L0-SSIM. Verify scores align with visual inspection (shape-dominant images should yield high SSIM after smoothing).
2. **Baseline Comparison:** On a held-out validation set, compare accuracy of standard model (T-Model) vs. max-pool dilated model (S_maxpool) to confirm the expected bias shift for the dataset.
3. **Threshold Sensitivity Test:** Vary the L0-SSIM decision threshold (e.g., 0.75, 0.80, 0.85) and measure validation accuracy to calibrate the point where adaptation switches from beneficial to harmful.

## Limitations
- L0 smoothing implementation details (iterations, solver, tolerance) are unspecified, impacting reproducibility of L0-SSIM.
- Ambiguity in which max-pool layers to dilate; ResNeXt-50 typically has one but "all" suggests more, affecting application.
- Fixed λ=0.01 may not generalize across datasets; sensitivity is unstudied.
- The 0.8 L0-SSIM decision threshold is derived from only six datasets and may not hold universally.
- The binary shape/texture assumption oversimplifies many datasets that may contain mixed cues.
- Evaluation is limited to small datasets; scalability to larger benchmarks is unknown.

## Confidence
- L0-SSIM quantifies shape-texture balance: *Medium* (intuitive and internally validated, but lacks external human-alignment studies).
- Max-pool dilation induces shape bias via ERF expansion: *Medium* (plausible mechanism with empirical support, but direct ERF measurement is absent).
- L0-SSIM predicts model suitability: *Medium-High* (consistent results across six diverse datasets, though sample size is small).
- Adaptation is lightweight and effective in low-data regimes: *Medium* (demonstrated on small datasets, but not benchmarked against full fine-tuning or other efficient methods).

## Next Checks
1. *Human alignment:* Correlate L0-SSIM with human shape/texture judgments on a diverse image set to validate the metric’s perceptual grounding.
2. *Ablation:* Compare max-pool dilation against other ERF-increasing methods (e.g., dilated convolutions, larger kernels) to isolate the unique contribution of modifying pooling layers.
3. *Threshold robustness:* Test the 0.8 decision rule on 10+ new datasets across different domains to determine its generalizability and identify a more robust threshold.
