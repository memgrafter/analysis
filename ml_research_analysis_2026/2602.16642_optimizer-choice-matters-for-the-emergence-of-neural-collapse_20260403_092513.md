# Optimizer choice matters for the emergence of Neural Collapse

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16642  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16642  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Optimizer choice, specifically the coupling of weight decay, determines whether Neural Collapse emerges; decoupled weight decay (AdamW, SignumW) prevents it, while coupled weight decay (Adam, SGDM) enables it.

## Executive Summary
Neural Collapse (NC) is a striking phenomenon in deep classification networks where, near the end of training, the last-layer features and classifiers collapse to a simplex equiangular tight frame. This paper investigates how the choice of optimizer affects the emergence of NC. It introduces a new diagnostic metric, NC0, which measures the row sum of the last-layer weight matrix; full NC requires NC0 to converge to zero. Theoretically, using the Unconstrained Feature Model, the authors analyze stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and SignGD (a simplified Adam) with both coupled (included in the gradient) and decoupled (applied separately) weight decay. They prove that SGD with weight decay drives NC0 to zero exponentially, whereas SignGD with decoupled weight decay (as in AdamW) converges to a positive constant, preventing NC. SignGD with coupled weight decay (as in Adam) can achieve NC0 → 0 when using a decaying learning rate. Extensive experiments covering 3,900 training runs on ResNet9 and VGG9 across MNIST, FashionMNIST, and CIFAR-10 confirm these predictions: decoupled weight decay optimizers consistently fail to exhibit NC, while coupled weight decay optimizers succeed, with momentum further accelerating convergence. The work underscores that the mechanism of weight decay implementation is critical for NC and provides the first theoretical explanation for optimizer‑dependent neural collapse.

## Method Summary
The study combines rigorous theoretical analysis with a large‑scale empirical evaluation. Theoretically, the authors use the Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM), which freezes the last‑layer features to isolate weight dynamics. They derive closed‑form recursions for NC0 under different optimizer updates (SGD, SGD with decoupled/coupled weight decay, SignGD with decoupled/coupled weight decay) and prove conditions for either exponential decay to zero or convergence to a positive constant. Empirically, they train CNN architectures (ResNet9, VGG9) on three balanced image classification datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR‑10) with a wide hyperparameter grid: three initial learning rates per optimizer, six momentum/β₁ values, and six weight decay strengths. For each run, they compute the full suite of NC metrics (NC0–NC4) at every epoch and retain only those runs achieving >99% training accuracy to focus on well‑trained models. The final NC0 values across 3,900 filtered runs systematically validate the theory: optimizers with decoupled weight decay (AdamW, SignumW) never drive NC0 to zero, while coupled weight decay (Adam, SGD, SGDM) does, and momentum shortens the convergence time.

## Key Results
- **NC0 as a necessary diagnostic:** Full Neural Collapse requires the row sum of the last‑layer weights (NC0) to vanish.
- **Theoretical dichotomy:** SGD with weight decay (any coupling) yields exponential NC0 decay; SignGD with decoupled weight decay (AdamW) converges to a positive constant; coupled weight decay in SignGD (Adam) can achieve NC0 → 0 only with a decaying learning rate.
- **Empirical confirmation:** Across three datasets and two architectures, decoupled weight decay optimizers (AdamW, SignumW) consistently produce high final NC0, while coupled weight decay optimizers (Adam, SGD, SGDM) drive NC0 to zero, with momentum accelerating the process.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: SGD with Weight Decay Exponentially Drives NC0 to Zero
- **Claim**: When training with SGD (with or without momentum), applying weight decay (whether coupled or decoupled) causes the NC0 metric to decay exponentially to zero, provided the learning rate and weight decay satisfy specific conditions.
- **Mechanism**: The key is that the row sum of the loss gradient ∇WtLCE transposed multiplied by the all‑ones vector is zero (Lemma E.5). This simplifies the NC0 dynamics to a linear recursion that depends only on weight decay λ and momentum β. For decoupled weight decay (SGDW), mt+1 = (1−ηλ)mt; for coupled weight decay (SGD with weight decay included in the gradient), the recursion involves both β and λ but still yields exponential decay if 0 < ηλ < 2(1+β). This forces the row sums of W to vanish.
- **Core assumption**: The model uses a linear last layer and cross‑entropy loss; the gradient row‑sum property holds.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "prove that SGD with weight decay drives NC0 to zero exponentially"
  - [section] Theorem 3.1 (decoupled) and Theorem 3.2 (coupled) formalize this with conditions on ηλ.
  - [corpus] Neighbors confirm weight decay’s role in NC (e.g., Pan & Cao, 2024; Jacot et al., 2024) but do not distinguish coupling; this paper’s evidence is unique.
- **Break condition**: If weight decay λ = 0, the recursion becomes mt+1 = mt (or with β, mt+1 = (1+β)mt − βmt−1), so NC0 does not decay to zero.

### Mechanism 2: Decoupled Weight Decay in Adaptive Optimizers Prevent NC
- **Claim**: Optimizers like AdamW and SignumW, which use decoupled weight decay, cause NC0 to converge to a positive constant, preventing full Neural Collapse.
- **Mechanism**: In adaptive optimizers such as SignGD (a simplification of Adam), decoupled weight decay adds λW t after the sign operation: Wt+1 = Wt − η(sign(∇WtLCE) + λW t). The signed gradient sign(∇WtLCE) becomes constant for a fixed feature matrix (in UFM), leading to a deterministic update where NC0 increases monotonically to a limit proportional to 1/λ² (Theorem 3.3). This contrasts with SGD, where the gradient row‑sum zero property dominates.
- **Core assumption**: Analysis uses the Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM) with fixed features H = M* to isolate weight updates; the sign gradient is constant in this setting.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "SignGD with decoupled weight decay (AdamW) converges to a positive constant, preventing NC"
  - [section] Theorem 3.3 proves αt → (K−2)²/λ²; Section 3.3 and Figure 9 show empirical evidence with SignGD and AdamW.
  - [corpus] Work on AdamW optimization (e.g., Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019) is cited, but NC implications are not addressed.
- **Break condition**: If weight decay is coupled instead, the signed gradient may flip sign due to λW t term inside sign(), allowing NC0 to decrease under decaying learning rates.

### Mechanism 3: Coupled Weight Decay in Adaptive Optimizers Enables NC with Decaying LR
- **Claim**: When weight decay is coupled (e.g., Adam), the update includes λW t inside the sign/shrinkage operation: Wt+1 = Wt − η(sign(∇WtLCE + λW t)). This can cause NC0 to vanish with an appropriate learning rate decay schedule, though dynamics may be non‑monotonic.
- **Mechanism**: The coupled term λW t alters the sign of the gradient update as weights grow, potentially flipping the direction and enabling αt to decrease after an initial increase (Theorem 3.4). By decaying η towards zero, the impact of the sign flips diminishes, allowing αt → 0. This mimics the behavior of Adam (without decoupled decay) enabling NC.
- **Core assumption**: UFM with fixed features; sign gradient dynamics depend on relative magnitudes of λ and the loss gradient.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "SignGD with coupled weight decay (a special case of Adam) can achieve NC0 with decaying learning rates"
  - [section] Theorem 3.4 shows existence of decay scheme; Figure 8 empirically interpolates between Adam and AdamW, showing NC0 improves with more coupled decay.
  - [corpus] No direct conflict; adjacent work on adaptive optimizers (e.g., Lion, Muon) is emerging but not yet on NC.
- **Break condition**: Without learning rate decay, αt may stagnate or oscillate without convergence to zero.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
Data (balanced classification: MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR‑10) → Model (CNN with linear last layer: ResNet9, VGG9) → Optimizer (SGD, SGDW, Adam, AdamW, Signum, SignumW; varying weight‑decay coupling) → Metrics (NC0, NC1, NC2, NC3, NC4)  
- **Critical path:**  
Model → Choose optimizer (coupling type) → Train with weight decay → Track NC0 each epoch → Compare final NC0 (and other metrics) against baseline; NC emerges iff NC0 → 0 and other metrics are low.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Optimizer selection: Coupled WD (Adam, SGD) promotes NC but may require careful LR decay; decoupled WD (AdamW) is stable for training but suppresses NC.  
  - Weight decay strength: Higher WD accelerates NC0 decay for coupled cases but can over‑regularize.  
  - Momentum: Accelerates NC convergence (Theorems 3.1, 3.2) but may not affect final NC state if WD is insufficient.  
  - Computational cost: NC metrics require full‑batch computation of class means and weights; feasible for small datasets/models.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High final NC0 (> init level) indicates decoupled WD or no WD (AdamW, SignumW, no WD).  
  - NC1‑NC3 decrease while NC0 stays high: partial collapse.  
  - Training accuracy 100% but NC metrics plateau: likely decoupled WD or insufficient WD.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline optimizer comparison: Train ResNet9 on FashionMNIST with Adam and AdamW (fixed LR=0.001, WD=0.0005, momentum=0.9). Plot NC0, NC3 over 200 epochs—expect AdamW NC0 rises, Adam’s decays.  
  2. Weight decay sweep: For SGD on CIFAR‑10, vary WD [5e‑5, 5e‑3] with LR=0.01. Expect NC0 to decay exponentially with rate (1−ηλ)² (Theorem 3.1).  
  3. Coupled vs. decoupled interpolation: Implement hybrid optimizer mixing coupled and decoupled WD (total fixed). Vary coupled fraction; observe NC0 monotonic decrease as coupling increases (Figure 8).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** Theoretical dynamics of NC1–NC3 under realistic optimizers.  
  **Basis:** "A full theoretical understanding of NC1–NC3 under realistic optimization dynamics remains an open challenge."  
  **Why unresolved:** Current theory focuses on NC0 for tractability; NC1–NC3 involve complex geometric relationships that are not fully understood.  
  **What evidence would resolve it:** Prove convergence bounds for NC1–NC3 metrics under various optimizers (SGD, Adam) and validate them with controlled experiments.

- **Question:** Conditions for partial neural collapse (only some NC properties hold).  
  **Basis:** "Understanding the theoretical conditions under which only a subset of NC properties holds remains an intriguing open question."  
  **Why unresolved:** Empirically, e.g., AdamW achieves small NC1/NC2 but NC0 diverges and NC3 fails; theory lacks conditions explaining selective property satisfaction.  
  **What evidence would resolve it:** Identify necessary and sufficient conditions for each NC property separately; demonstrate how weight‑decay coupling affects each.

- **Question:** Effects of novel optimizers (Lion, Mars, Shampoo, SOAP, Muon) on neural collapse.  
  **Basis:** "Their effects on NC remain largely unexplored."  
  **Why unresolved:** These optimizers have updates with preconditioning or different weight‑decay implementations that may interact uniquely with NC.  
  **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically measure NC metrics when training with these optimizers across architectures and datasets.

- **Question:** Does neural collapse occur in intermediate layers and how does it vary with depth?  
  **Basis:** "Due to computational constraints, our study only analyzed NC properties in the last layer... Investigating NC behavior across different depths could provide further insights."  
  **Why unresolved:** Potential layer‑wise collapse is underexplored; prior hints suggest intermediate layers may also collapse.  
  **What evidence would resolve it:** Compute NC metrics for all layers in deep networks (ResNets, ViTs); analyze optimizer‑dependent layer‑wise patterns.

## Limitations
- Reliance on the Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM) may not capture joint feature‑learning dynamics in real networks.
- Experiments are limited to small datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR‑10) and two CNN architectures, leaving generalizability to large‑scale or transformer models uncertain.
- Incomplete hyperparameter details (e.g., exact AdamW learning rates, NC0 normalization) hinder exact replication.

## Confidence
- SGD with weight decay drives NC0 to zero exponentially. **Confidence: High**  
- Decoupled weight decay (AdamW, SignumW) prevents NC by causing NC0 to converge to a positive constant. **Confidence: High**  
- Coupled weight decay (Adam, SGDM) can achieve NC0 → 0 with learning rate decay. **Confidence: High**  
- Momentum accelerates NC convergence. **Confidence: High**  
- NC0 is necessary (but not sufficient) for full Neural Collapse. **Confidence: Medium**
