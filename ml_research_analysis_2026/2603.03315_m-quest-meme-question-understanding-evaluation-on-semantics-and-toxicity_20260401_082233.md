# M-QUEST -- Meme Question-Understanding Evaluation on Semantics and Toxicity

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03315
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03315
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** Introduces a 10-dimension semantic framework and the M-QUEST benchmark (609 human-validated QA pairs for 307 memes); instruction-tuned, reasoning-capable VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieve 86.38% accuracy, while group accuracy (consistent toxicity + reasoning) is systematically lower.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of automatically explaining meme toxicity by identifying its underlying semantic dimensions. It introduces a structured 10-dimension framework ranging from perceptual (e.g., Textual Material) to inferential (e.g., Toxicity Assessment) understanding. Using a neuro-symbolic, semi-automatic pipeline, the authors generate the M-QUEST benchmark from 1,000 memes, yielding 609 human-validated multiple-choice question-answer pairs. Evaluation of eight open-weight vision-language models reveals that performance is driven primarily by instruction-tuning and explicit reasoning capabilities, not scale. While models excel on perceptual dimensions, significant gaps remain on pragmatic inference tasks. The group accuracy metric, requiring consistent answers across linked toxicity and reasoning questions, highlights the difficulty of joint multimodal understanding.

## Method Summary
The authors construct M-QUEST via a two-phase, VLM-driven pipeline. First, a **Knowledge Extraction (KE)** phase sequentially queries a VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) for each of the 10 semantic dimensions. The output for prior, more basic dimensions is injected as context for later, more complex ones, building a structured Knowledge Base (KB) per meme. Second, a **Q&A Generation** phase uses the KB snippets to produce a multiple-choice question, one correct answer, and three distractors per dimension. The generated toxicity question and answer then undergo human validation for plausibility; only memes passing this filter are retained. The final benchmark comprises 307 memes with a paired toxicity and reasoning question, covering 9 dimensions. Models are evaluated zero-shot using a provided prompt template; outputs are parsed to compute accuracy per question and group accuracy (both paired questions correct per meme).

## Key Results
- The 10-dimension framework and M-QUEST benchmark (609 QA pairs, 307 memes) are publicly released.
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct achieves the highest overall accuracy (86.38%); non-reasoning models (e.g., BLIP2-Flan-T5-xl) perform near random (~13%).
- Accuracy is high on perceptual dimensions (Scene: ~98%, Visual Material: ~96%) but lower on pragmatic inference (Toxicity Assessment: ~72%, Overall Intent: ~69%).
- Group accuracy is systematically lower than individual accuracy for all models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL drops from 94.71% on reasoning to 83.25% group), indicating inconsistency in linking toxicity to its semantic cause.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A neuro-symbolic, dimension-sequential prompt design can automatically generate a semantically grounded benchmark (M-QUEST) by building a coherent Knowledge Base (KB) per meme.
- **Mechanism:** A VLM is prompted sequentially for 10 ordered dimensions. Outputs from foundational dimensions (Textual Material, Visual Material) are injected as context into prompts for complex ones (Analogical Mapping, Semiotic Projection), ensuring knowledge builds coherently. The finalized KB is then used to generate multiple-choice questions and distractors.
- **Core assumption:** The predefined ordering and context injection enforce consistency; the VLM can generate plausible questions and distractors from its own extracted snippets.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract, section 4.1] Describes the sequential process and its purpose for consistency.
  - [section 4.1] Explicitly states the prompt order begins with basic dimensions and progresses to complex ones to reuse anchoring points.
- **Break condition:** An erroneous or incomplete extraction for an early, foundational dimension (e.g., misreading on-screen text) propagates inconsistency, leading to invalid or unanswerable questions in later dimensions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Performance on M-QUEST is driven by a model's combination of instruction-tuning and explicit reasoning capabilities, not merely by scale or general vision-language pretraining.
- **Mechanism:** The 10-dimensional questions, especially pragmatic inference ones, require integrating multimodal cues with commonsense and social context. Instruction tuning helps the model follow the specific QA prompt format; reasoning training (often via Chain-of-Thought) enables multi-step inference. Models possessing both (Qwen family) significantly outperform those with only one or neither.
- **Core assumption:** The benchmark's higher-order questions cannot be solved by surface pattern matching; they demand inferential leaps that benefit from structured reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract, section 5.2] Directly correlates performance hierarchy with the presence/absence of instruction tuning and reasoning capabilities.
  - [section 5.2] Notes the synergistic effect of combining both capabilities yields the highest results.
- **Break condition:** If pragmatic inference questions were largely solvable by heuristics (e.g., keyword spotting), the performance gap between reasoning and non-reasoning models would collapse.

### Mechanism 3
- **Human validation acts as a critical quality filter; the semi-automatic generation yields many candidates, but only a minority (~30% of memes) produce fully validated pairs, highlighting the need for costly manual oversight.**
- **Mechanism:** After automatic QA generation, human annotators verify the plausibility of the toxicity "correct" answer and the question's relevance. Only memes passing this stage are kept for the core ToxM-QUEST subset, with further validation on other dimensions. Human judgment filters out VLM hallucinations and misalignments.
- **Core assumption:** Human annotators with relevant expertise serve as a reliable gold standard for plausibility that current VLMs cannot self-assess.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] Reports that only 307/1000 memes (30.7%) had both toxicity and reasoning questions validated positively.
  - [conclusions] Acknowledges the substantial human effort required and the resulting scale limitation.
- **Break condition:** If human annotators are inconsistent or lack a shared understanding (as hinted for abstract dimensions), the validation filter may introduce noise or exclude valid samples, compromising benchmark quality.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Multimodal Semantic Dimensions**
  - **Why needed:** The entire benchmark is built on a 10-dimension framework ordered from perceptual (Textual Material) to inferential (Toxicity Assessment). This ordering structures the Knowledge Extraction process and explains performance variations.
  - **Quick check:** Why is "Scene" (configuration of visual entities) extracted before "Analogical Mapping" (mapping between visual and textual elements)?

- **Concept: Group Accuracy**
  - **Why needed:** This metric (correct on *both* toxicity and paired reasoning question per meme) measures consistent, joint multimodal understanding. It is lower than individual accuracy, showing that a correct toxicity label does not guarantee understanding of the underlying reason.
  - **Quick check:** If a model has 90% accuracy on reasoning and 80% on toxicity, would group accuracy be closer to 80% or 90%? Why?

- **Concept: Instruction Tuning vs. Reasoning Capability**
  - **Why needed:** The paper's central finding is that the *combination* of these architectural features drives performance. Distinguishing them is key: instruction tuning aligns the model to follow prompts; reasoning training enables step-by-step inference.
  - **Quick check:** Could a model with strong instruction tuning but no explicit reasoning training achieve ~83% group accuracy? What evidence in the paper supports your view?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Dimension Framework (static ordered list) -> Knowledge Extraction Module (sequential VLM prompts with KB injection) -> Q&A Generation Module (VLM uses KB to create MCQs) -> Human Validation Interface (annotator plausibility checks) -> Evaluation Harness (zero-shot inference on target VLMs)

- **Critical path:** The sequential Knowledge Extraction is the core innovation. It enforces a dependency graph: `Textual/Visual Material -> Scene -> [Background Knowledge, Emotion, Overall Intent, Target Community, Semiotic Projection] -> Analogical Mapping`. Toxicity Assessment can depend on any. A failure in an early KB entry propagates to all subsequent knowledge for that meme.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Automation Scale vs. Human Labor: Fully automatic generation yields many Q&As, but only ~30% pass human validation. Tradeoff is dataset size vs. quality.
  - Dimension Breadth vs. Depth: The 10-dimension framework covers broad semantic aspects but sacrifices fine-grained sub-types (e.g., kinds of Background Knowledge).
  - Single VLM vs. Specialized Components: Using one VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) for all extraction/generation is simple but may sacrifice accuracy gains from specialized tools (e.g., dedicated OCR, emotion models).

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Very low human validation rates on abstract dimensions (Semiotic Projection: 32%, Target Community: 40%) indicate VLM and/or human struggles with these concepts.
  - Performance drop from "Reasoning only" to "Group (T+R)" accuracy (e.g., Qwen2.5: 94.71% -> 83.25%) signals models cannot consistently link a toxicity judgment to a supporting semantic reason.
  - VLM CoT patterns that force spurious visual-text links (e.g., linking an "未成年" text to a quokka's perceived harmlessness) show failure in pragmatic inference when no intended link exists.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  Replicate Human Validation on a Subset: Take 50 random memes from the 1k candidate set. Run the human validation protocol (check toxicity plausibility, then Q&A plausibility/accuracy). Measure inter-annotator agreement and compare validation rates to Table 1 & Fig 5.
  2.  Dimensional Ablation on KE: For a meme, manually run the KE prompts but intentionally inject an error in the "Textual Material" extraction. Continue the sequential prompts. Check if subsequent dimensions produce contradictions or hallucinations in their KB entries.
  3.  Group Accuracy Diagnostic: Using the final M-QUEST, pick a meme with both Toxicity and Reasoning (e.g., Background Knowledge) questions. Run a strong model (Qwen2.5-VL) and a weak one (BLIP2-Flan-T5-xl). Record answers and CoTs. Manually judge if the strong model's reasoning provides a *principled justification* for its toxicity answer, and if the weak model's answers are inconsistent.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How can the semi-automatic Q&A generation process be improved to increase human acceptance rates while reducing manual validation reliance?
- How can the 10-dimensional semantic framework be formally specified and extended to capture finer-grained distinctions and model explicit interdependencies between dimensions?
- To what extent do excluded dimensions—meme template structure, metadata, morality, ethics, culture, legality—systematically influence toxicity assessment, and how can they be integrated?
- Why do state-of-the-art VLMs exhibit a persistent performance gap between perceptual and pragmatic inference dimensions, even when instruction-tuned and reasoning-capable?
- What specific cognitive and contextual factors make the Semiotic Projection and Target Community dimensions particularly difficult, and how can they be better operationalized for consistent measurement?

## Limitations
- The entire benchmark is constructed using a single VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B), risking model-specific artifacts and limiting diversity in generated QA styles.
- Human annotator expertise is likely specialized (familiar with LLMs/memes), potentially limiting the generalizability of validation results.
- Low human validation rates for abstract dimensions (Semiotic Projection: 32%, Target Community: 40%) suggest definitional fuzziness, with unreported inter-annotator agreement.

## Confidence
- **Neuro-symbolic KE process** (Medium): Sequential dependency is clearly described, but the break condition (early error propagation) is theoretical, not experimentally verified.
- **Reasoning capabilities drive performance** (High): Strong correlation across 8 diverse models; consistent with neighboring knowledge-infusion work.
- **Human validation bottleneck** (High): Directly supported by quantitative validation rates (~30% acceptance) and acknowledged cost limitations.

## Next Checks
1.  **Error Propagation Test:** For 50 memes, deliberately corrupt KB extraction in a foundational dimension (e.g., mis-OCR textual material). Run full KE sequence and quantify contradiction/hallucination rates in later dimensions.
2.  **Cross-VLM Benchmark Stability:** Regenerate M-QUEST QA pairs using multiple diverse VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet). Compare human validation acceptance rates and resulting model performance distributions on a fixed test set.
3.  **Annotator Agreement Study:** Recruit 5+ annotators to independently validate samples from low-validation dimensions. Measure Cohen’s κ; if κ < 0.6, revise dimension definitions and re-validate a subset to isolate disagreement sources.
