# Smooth, Sparse, and Stable: Finite-Time Exact Skeleton Recovery via Smoothed Proximal Gradients

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.18189  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18189  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** SPG-AHOC enables finite-time exact DAG support recovery without post-hoc thresholding by combining the Hybrid-Order Acyclicity Constraint (AHOC) with smoothed proximal gradients, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on sparse graphs (e.g., SHD 49–535 for d=50–500) and superior efficiency.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a key limitation of continuous NOTEARS-style causal discovery: the output is dense and requires arbitrary thresholding due to asymptotic convergence and structural constraint instability (vanishing or exploding gradients). The authors propose SPG-AHOC, which integrates the Hybrid-Order Acyclicity Constraint (AHOC) with a Smoothed Proximal Gradient (SPG) optimizer. AHOC satisfies three axioms (Correctness, L₁‑Synergy, Boundedness) that stabilize the constraint’s gradient. SPG uses a smooth approximation for the constraint while keeping the L₁ penalty non‑smooth, enabling a proximal step that “snaps” weights to exact zero in finite iterations (topological locking). Under standard assumptions—including the Irrepresentable Condition and a lower bound on the L₁ regularization parameter λ₁ ≥ ∥∇L_fit(0)∥∞—the method guarantees finite‑time exact support recovery. Empirical results on synthetic sparse graphs (d=50–500) and the Sachs dataset show superior sparsity, accuracy (SHD), and speed compared to dense baselines.

## Method Summary
SPG‑AHOC minimizes a composite objective: a data‑fitting term (e.g., least‑squares loss for linear SEMs) plus an L₁ regularization, subject to an acyclicity constraint. The constraint is AHOC: h_AHOC(W) = tr(exp( M / (∥M∥_F + ϵ) )) – d, where M = α·(W∘W) + (1–α)·|W| with α∈[0,1). To enable smooth optimization, the absolute value |W| is approximated via smooth_abs(W; δ)=√(W²+δ²). The resulting smoothed objective is smooth in W, but the L₁ penalty remains non‑smooth. An augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) handles the constraint: L̃(W)=L_fit + μ_t·h̃ + (ρ_t/2)·h̃² + λ₁∥W∥₁. The core update is a proximal gradient step: W_{k+1}=prox_{λ₁η}(Wₖ – η∇L̃(Wₖ)), where prox performs element‑wise soft‑thresholding. Backtracking line search adapts the step size η. The algorithm iterates until h_AHOC(W)≈0 and support stabilizes. The theoretical guarantee hinges on strict dual feasibility for zero‑pattern entries: after a finite number of iterations, the proximal operator forces exact zeros, achieving exact support recovery without thresholding.

## Key Results
- Finite‑time exact DAG support recovery under the Irrepresentable Condition and λ₁ ≥ ∥∇L_fit(0)∥∞; no post‑hoc thresholding needed.
- State‑of‑the‑art accuracy on sparse synthetic graphs (d=50–500): SHD ranges 49–535 with >99% sparsity, while baselines (NOTEARS‑EXP, DAGMA) remain dense.
- On Sachs dataset (d=11), achieves SHD=19, outperforming alternatives.
- Computationally efficient: 1.8× faster than DAGMA at d=100.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Topological Locking via Proximal Operator
Once the iterate enters a Geometric Stability Zone defined by strict dual feasibility (|∇L(W)ᵢⱼ| ≤ λ₁ – τ for (i,j) outside the true support, τ>0), the proximal operator’s soft‑thresholding collapses any weight with magnitude below λ₁η to exact zero. This quantization effect overcomes the asymptotic approach‑to‑zero of gradient descent. The condition λ₁ ≥ ∥∇L_fit(0)∥∞ ensures the trajectory starts within the sparse manifold; together with the Irrepresentable Condition, it induces finite‑time identification. Smoothing bias O(δ) is absorbed if δ < τ.

*Evidence:* Section 2.9 introduces “Topological Locking” and Figure 2; Section 3.2, Step 2.1 formalizes strict dual feasibility (Eq 7); Section 2.10 explains smoothing bias absorption.

### Mechanism 2: Axiomatically Complete Constraint (AHOC)
AHOC’s core M(W)=α(W∘W)+(1–α)|W| introduces a linear |W| term that maintains a non‑vanishing gradient near zero (L₁‑Synergy), while normalization by ∥M∥_F+ϵ bounds both constraint value and gradient globally (Numerical Boundedness). This resolves both Type I (vanishing) and Type II (exploding) Structural Constraint Instability that plague standard constraints (e.g., NOTEARS trace‑exp). The constraint remains equivalent to DAG acyclicity (h(W)=0 ⇔ DAG) because M is element‑wise non‑negative.

*Evidence:* Section 2.1 defines the three axioms; Theorem 2.8 proves AHOC satisfies them; Figure 1 visualizes stable gradients vs. standard constraints.

### Mechanism 3: Smoothing‑Non‑Smooth Decoupling
The non‑smooth |W| inside the constraint is replaced by smooth_abs(W;δ), making the augmented Lagrangian part F(W) smooth (Lipschitz‑gradient), while the L₁ penalty stays non‑smooth. This decoupling allows standard proximal gradient convergence rates while preserving exact sparsity via topological locking. The smoothing error is bounded: ∥h̃–h∥∞ ≤ C₁dδ. If δ is smaller than the dual feasibility margin τ/C, the bias remains within the thresholding band, so exact zeros are unaffected.

*Evidence:* Section 2.6 introduces the decoupled paradigm; Proposition 2.14 gives the uniform bound; Theorem 2.12 guarantees SPG convergence for any fixed δ>0; Section 2.7 discusses the δ vs. speed trade‑off.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input data X -> L_fit(W) (least squares) -> smoothed AHOC constraint h̃(W) -> Augmented Lagrangian F(W)=L_fit+μ_t·h̃+(ρ_t/2)·h̃²+λ₁∥W∥₁ -> Proximal Gradient Step (Wₖ – η∇F, then prox_{λ₁η}) -> ALM Update (μ_t, ρ_t) -> Final W (DAG, exact zeros)

### Critical Path
1. Initialize W=0; set λ₁ ≥ ∥∇L_fit(0)∥∞ (critical for stability).
2. Outer ALM loop: increase μ_t, ρ_t until h_AHOC(W) < τ.
3. Inner SPG loop: compute ∇F(Wₖ), backtrack to find η satisfying Armijo condition, apply proximal step W_{k+1}=prox_{λ₁η}(Wₖ – η∇F(Wₖ)).
4. Check convergence: h_AHOC(W)≈0 and support unchanged.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **δ vs. Speed:** Small δ → accurate constraint but tiny η (O(1/δ) iterations); large δ → faster but approximation bias may cause false positives. Backtracking adapts η locally but does not adapt δ.
- **λ₁ vs. Sensitivity:** Large λ₁ ensures stability but prunes weak edges (Beta‑Min condition); small λ₁ (<∥∇L_fit(0)∥∞) causes divergence.
- **α vs. Stability:** α<1 required for L₁‑Synergy; α→1 reverts to standard H‑constraints (vanishing gradients); α→0 makes M≈|W|, possibly degrading smoothness.

### Failure Signatures
- **NaN/Inf:** λ₁ too small (λ₁ < ∥∇L_fit(0)∥∞) → ∥W∥_F explodes.
- **Dense output:** λ₁ too small or δ too large → proximal operator never zeros weights.
- **Stuck constraint (h(W) > 0):** Proximal step yields no update (W_{k+1}=Wₖ) due to over‑regularization (λ₁ huge) or near‑cyclic graphs (ρ≈1) causing ill‑conditioned curvature.
- **Non‑smoothed AHOC failure:** Gradient undefined at zero → optimization barrier.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Gradient Axiom Validation (Exp 1 & 2):** Plot ∥∇h(W)∥_F vs. spectral radius and vs. ∥W∥_F for AHOC and standard constraints. Confirm AHOC is bounded and non‑vanishing; others violate one axiom.
2. **Sparse Recovery Benchmark (Exp 3):** Run on Erdős–Rényi graphs (d=50–500, e=d). Compare SPG‑AHOC (exact zeros, NNZ≈true edges) vs. EXP/DAGMA (dense, NNZ≈d²). Report SHD.
3. **λ₁ Sensitivity & Stability (Exp 7 & H):** Sweep λ₁ around ∥∇L_fit(0)∥∞. Expect: λ₁ too low → divergence; λ₁ adequate → convergence with exact sparsity; λ₁ too high → empty graph.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension beyond Irrepresentable Condition:** Can finite‑time guarantees be weakened to handle strong latent confounders or highly correlated variables? The current theory relies on the Irrepresentable Condition, which is strong and often violated.
2. **Automatic δ selection:** What is the optimal strategy to set the smoothing parameter δ without manual tuning? The trade‑off between approximation error (large δ) and slow convergence (small δ) lacks an automatic schedule.
3. **Dynamic α/δ for near‑cyclic graphs:** Can numerical instability on extreme near‑cyclic graphs (ρ≈1) be mitigated by adjusting α or δ during optimization?
4. **Scalability with approximate matrix exponential:** Is O(d³) a fundamental barrier for d≥1000, or can approximate methods (e.g., Krylov) be integrated without breaking the finite‑time exact recovery theory?
5. **λ₁ lower bound vs. statistical power:** What is the precise relationship between the geometric stabilizer λ₁ ≥ ∥∇L_fit(0)∥∞ and sensitivity to weak edges in high‑dimensional, low‑sample regimes?

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps:** Critical hyperparameters (ALM schedule, proximal gradient Armijo parameters, default α) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- **Condition sensitivity:** The core stability condition λ₁ ≥ ∥∇L_fit(0)∥∞ is dataset‑dependent; violation causes divergence. The required dual feasibility margin τ is not explicitly controlled.
- **Reproducibility risk:** Sachs result (SHD=19) relies on undisclosed hyperparameters; baseline comparisons may use different λ₁ sweeps or stopping criteria.

## Confidence
- **Finite‑time exact recovery:** Medium. Theory is sound under strict dual feasibility and Irrepresentable Condition, but practical sensitivity to λ₁, δ, α is high; empirical validation on sparse graphs supports the claim but lacks ablation on condition violations.
- **AHOC axiom satisfaction:** High. Formal proof (Theorem 2.8) and empirical gradient plots confirm boundedness and non‑vanishing for α<1.
- **Empirical SOTA performance:** Medium. Reported improvements are compelling but depend on unreported hyperparameter sweeps and potential implementation disparities; independent verification needed.
