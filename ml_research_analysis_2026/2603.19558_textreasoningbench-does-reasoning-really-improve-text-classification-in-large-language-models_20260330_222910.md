# TextReasoningBench: Does Reasoning Really Improve Text Classification in Large Language Models?

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.19558
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19558
- **Reference count**: 36
- **Primary result**: Reasoning does not universally improve text classification; moderate strategies like CoT and SC-CoT yield only modest gains, while complex methods often fail or degrade performance, with a 10-100× token overhead.

## Executive Summary
TextReasoningBench presents a comprehensive evaluation of reasoning strategies for text classification using large language models. The study tests seven methods—ranging from simple chain-of-thought to advanced tree- and graph-based search—across ten LLMs and five classification tasks, while also measuring computational costs. The results challenge the prevailing assumption that adding reasoning automatically improves classification accuracy.

The findings indicate that only moderate strategies (CoT and its self-consistency variant) provide small, inconsistent improvements; more elaborate approaches (Tree/Graph-of-Thoughts) frequently underperform the no-reasoning baseline, especially on smaller models. Reasoning incurs a token overhead of 10–100 times with minimal accuracy benefits. Benefits are largely confined to subjective tasks, while objective tasks show no gain. The authors conclude that reasoning should be applied selectively rather than as a default strategy.

## Method Summary
The benchmark comprises five English text classification tasks covering both subjective and objective domains. Ten LLMs of varying sizes and architectures serve as the backbone. Seven reasoning strategies are implemented: (1) zero-shot, (2) few-shot, (3) chain-of-thought (CoT), (4) self-consistency with CoT (SC-CoT), (5) tree-of-thoughts (ToT), (6) graph-of-thoughts (GoT), and (7) a no-reasoning baseline. For each model–task–strategy combination, the system records classification accuracy, token count, and inference latency. A key contribution is the introduction of cost-aware metrics that combine effectiveness and efficiency, enabling direct comparison of the tradeoffs. The experimental design systematically varies one factor at a time to isolate the impact of reasoning, with multiple random restarts to ensure reliability.

## Key Results
- Moderate reasoning strategies (CoT, SC-CoT) offer only modest and inconsistent gains; complex strategies (ToT, GoT) often match or fall below the no-reasoning baseline, particularly on smaller models.
- Reasoning increases token usage by 10–100×, providing minimal accuracy improvements and thus poor cost-efficiency.
- Task type strongly influences outcomes: subjective tasks (e.g., sentiment) sometimes benefit, while objective tasks (e.g., topic classification) show no improvement or even degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Reasoning methods aim to improve classification by breaking the decision into intermediate steps, theoretically allowing the model to allocate more computation, avoid snap judgments, and produce interpretable justifications. This mechanism is most helpful when the task requires multi-step inference or nuanced understanding, as the stepwise approach can focus attention on relevant evidence sequentially.

However, the study reveals that the mechanism often fails in practice. Smaller models cannot generate coherent chains, leading to error propagation. Even large models can produce irrelevant, circular, or noisy reasoning, especially under search-based strategies that explore many branches (e.g., ToT, GoT). The added complexity introduces more points of failure and can amplify mistakes rather than correct them. Moreover, for simple direct-mapping tasks, the extra steps are superfluous and may distract the model from the core classification signal. Thus, the benefits of reasoning are highly conditional on model capability, task complexity, and strategy appropriateness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT)**
  - Why needed: Encourages the model to produce intermediate reasoning steps, improving interpretability and potentially accuracy on tasks requiring decomposition.
  - Quick check: Does the output contain a clear, step-by-step explanation before the final label?

- **Self-Consistency (SC-CoT)**
  - Why needed: Enhances robustness by sampling multiple reasoning chains and taking a majority vote, reducing the impact of occasional erroneous generations.
  - Quick check: Are multiple independent chains generated, and is the final label chosen by voting?

- **Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT)**
  - Why needed: Implements a search over a tree of partial thoughts, allowing backtracking and heuristic-guided exploration to find better solutions.
  - Quick check: Is there evidence of tree expansion (child nodes), state evaluation, and path selection based on a score?

- **Graph-of-Thoughts (GoT)**
  - Why needed: Extends tree search to a graph structure, supporting iterative refinement and non-sequential dependencies among reasoning steps.
  - Quick check: Are reasoning steps connected in a graph with message passing or iterative updates?

- **Cost-aware evaluation**
  - Why needed: Traditional benchmarks ignore the heavy token and compute overhead of reasoning; cost-aware metrics provide a more realistic assessment of practical utility.
  - Quick check: Are token counts and/or latency reported alongside accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: The pipeline consists of: Input Text → Prompt Builder (inserts reasoning instructions) → LLM (executes the chosen reasoning strategy) → (Optional) Aggregator (for SC, ToT, GoT) → Output Parser (extracts final classification label). The core variation lies in the LLM execution phase and the presence of an aggregator.

**Critical path**: For each strategy, the critical execution flow is:
- CoT: Prompt → single generation → parse label.
- SC-CoT: Prompt → multiple generations → majority vote → label.
- ToT/GoT: Prompt → iterative thought generation → search/graph algorithm → best path extraction → label.

**Design tradeoffs**: The primary tradeoff is accuracy versus efficiency. Simple strategies (CoT) add moderate token cost with limited gains; complex ones (ToT/GoT) can theoretically improve accuracy on hard tasks but at exponential cost in tokens and latency. Model size modulates this tradeoff: larger models can exploit complex strategies, while smaller models suffer from instability and overhead. Aggregation (SC) improves robustness but further multiplies cost.

**Failure signatures**:
- Incoherent, repetitive, or circular reasoning chains that fail to reach a clear decision.
- High token consumption with negligible or negative impact on accuracy.
- Complex strategies overwhelming small models, leading to incomplete outputs or search failures.
- Performance drops on simple, direct-mapping tasks where reasoning introduces unnecessary noise.

**Three first experiments**:
1. Baseline evaluation of no-reasoning vs. CoT on a simple, large-model task to measure the raw impact of adding a single reasoning step.
2. Model scaling test: Apply SC-CoT across small, medium, and large models to identify the size threshold where reasoning becomes beneficial.
3. Task-type assessment: Compare the same model and reasoning strategy on subjective (e.g., sentiment) and objective (e.g., topic) classification tasks to quantify task-dependency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
No specific open questions are raised in the paper; the authors focus on reporting findings and limitations.

## Limitations
- The evaluation covers only five classification tasks and ten LLMs, all operating on English text; generalizability to other domains, model families, or languages remains uncertain.
- Cost analysis is restricted to inference time; potential benefits from training-time adaptation or fine-tuning are not considered.
- Benchmark conditions are static and curated; real-world deployment with noisy, dynamic inputs may expose additional failure modes not captured here.

## Confidence
- **Reasoning does not universally improve text classification in LLMs**: High
- **Moderate reasoning strategies provide modest gains; complex strategies often fail or degrade performance**: High
- **Reasoning is frequently inefficient, with high token cost for marginal gains**: High
- **Benefits of reasoning are highly task- and model-dependent**: Medium (strong evidence but limited task/model diversity)
- **Reasoning should be applied selectively, not as a default**: High

## Next Checks
1. Expand evaluation to a wider variety of classification tasks (both subjective and objective) to confirm the observed task dependency and improve generalizability.
2. Test reasoning strategies in multilingual and multimodal contexts to assess robustness beyond English text.
3. Perform ablation studies to separate the effects of reasoning length, strategy complexity, and model capability, and conduct detailed failure analyses to uncover hidden error modes.
