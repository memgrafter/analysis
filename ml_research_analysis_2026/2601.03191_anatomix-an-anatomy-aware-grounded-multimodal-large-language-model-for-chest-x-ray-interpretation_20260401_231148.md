# AnatomiX, an Anatomy-Aware Grounded Multimodal Large Language Model for Chest X-Ray Interpretation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03191
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03191
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** AnatomiX achieves >25% improvement on anatomy grounding over prior SOTA, with robust performance on horizontally flipped images.

## Executive Summary
Existing multimodal medical LLMs for chest X-ray interpretation fail at fine-grained spatial reasoning and lack true anatomical understanding, often relying on superficial cues like orientation markers. AnatomiX addresses this with a two-stage, anatomy-aware architecture. First, an Anatomy Perception Module (APM) explicitly identifies 36 thoracic anatomical structures, predicts their bounding boxes, and retrieves relevant textual descriptions. Second, a MedGemma-based LLM uses these structured outputs in a multimodal prompt to perform downstream tasks like report generation, VQA, and grounding. Across benchmarks, AnatomiX significantly outperforms prior work in anatomical reasoning and robustness, while maintaining competitive performance on standard tasks.

## Method Summary
AnatomiX employs a two-stage pipeline trained on the Chest ImaGenome dataset (237,000+ samples with 36 anatomical objects and localized findings) and eight supplementary datasets for instruction tuning. The Anatomy Perception Module (APM) uses an image encoder and a DETR-style decoder with 36 fixed queries to predict bounding boxes (`ˆybox`) and fine-grained anatomical features (`ˆOA`). During training, `ˆOA` is aligned with textual descriptions via a self-similarity contrastive loss, preserving semantic relationships among co-occurring findings. At inference, a vector database (built from Chest ImaGenome validation sentences) retrieves the most relevant clinical phrase (`ˆSt`) for each anatomical region. These outputs—bounding boxes, features, and retrieved text—are formatted with special tokens into a multimodal prompt for a LoRA-tuned MedGemma-4b LLM, which generates the final response.

## Key Results
- **Anatomy grounding:** 0.73 IoU / 0.66 mAP, a >25% improvement over RadVLM (0.60 IoU / 0.49 mAP).
- **Phrase grounding:** 0.46 IoU / 0.35 mAP, up to 15% better than baselines.
- **Robustness:** Maintains high performance (IoU 0.712) on horizontally flipped images where RadVLM fails (IoU 0.108), demonstrating genuine anatomical understanding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Superior anatomical reasoning stems from the two-stage, anatomy-first decomposition, which separates anatomical structure identification from diagnostic reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** The APM produces a fixed set of 36 structured outputs (`ˆybox`, `ˆOA`, `ˆSt`) that serve as an intermediate, anatomy-anchored representation. The LLM consumes these via dedicated tokens (`<obj_i>`), avoiding the need to implicitly infer anatomy from raw patches.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes the two-stage approach; [section 3.1] details APM outputs; [table 1] shows >25% improvement on anatomy grounding.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The APM's contrastive alignment with a self-similarity loss matrix enables learning nuanced relationships between anatomical regions and their textual descriptions, crucial for co-occurring findings.
- **Mechanism:** The self-similarity matrix `S_self` (from text embeddings) encodes soft similarities among descriptions. KL divergence between `S_self` and the image-text similarity matrix `ˆKA` forces `ˆOA` to preserve this semantic topology, allowing partial matches for overlapping findings.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section S1] explains suitability for overlapping representations; [table S2] shows APM (self-similarity) outperforms APM-CLIP on retrieval; internal ablations validate the loss.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Inference-time vector database (VDB) retrieval injects dataset-derived, authentic clinical language into the prompt, grounding the LLM's generation in actual radiology phrasing.
- **Mechanism:** During training, `ˆOA` aligns with a frozen sentence encoder. At inference, this is replaced by a VDB containing unique sentences per anatomy from Chest-ImaGenome. Nearest-neighbor search retrieves `ˆSt_i` for each `ˆOA_i`, which is injected into the LLM prompt.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.1] describes VDB replacement; [section S2] details VDB construction; [table 1] ablation `AnatomiX-ˆSt` shows severe drop; [fig S3] shows VDB size distribution.

## Foundational Learning

### Transformer-based Object Detection (DETR)
- **Why needed here:** The APM's decoder and bounding box prediction are adapted from DETR. Its permutation-equivariant design and bipartite matching loss enable learning a fixed set of object queries that map consistently to predefined anatomical structures.
- **Quick check question:** How does DETR's set-based prediction and Hungarian matching loss differ from traditional CNN anchor-based detectors, and why is the fixed, non-permutation-invariant design (`O` corresponds to predefined anatomy) crucial for AnatomiX?

### Contrastive Learning with Hard Negative Mining / Soft Targets
- **Why needed here:** The APM's contrastive alignment uses a self-similarity matrix as soft targets instead of one-hot labels. This addresses the issue of false negatives in medical images where multiple findings co-occur.
- **Quick check question:** Why would a standard CLIP loss fail when training on a single chest X-ray with multiple, co-occurring findings (e.g., pleural effusion and atelectasis)? How does `S_self` address this?

### Medical Image-Language Grounding & Anatomy Ontologies
- **Why needed here:** The core problem is "true anatomical correspondence." Standard grounding on natural images often learns dataset bias (e.g., radiology markers) rather than anatomy. Understanding medical semantics—that pathologies are described relative to consistent anatomical locations—is prerequisite.
- **Quick check question:** What is the difference between *spatial* grounding (localizing "the white spot") and *anatomical* grounding (localizing "the right lower lung opacity") in CXR, and why might a model succeed at the first but fail at the second?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input (CXR image `I`, user prompt `U`) -> APM (`ˆybox`, `ˆOA`, `ˆSt`) -> Multimodal Prompt Construction (with tokens `<obj_i>`, `<box>`, `<ref>`) -> MedGemma-4b LLM (LoRA-tuned) -> Output (`T`)

- **Critical path:** `Image -> APM (ˆybox, ˆOA, ˆSt) -> Prompt Construction -> LLM -> Output`. The APM must succeed first; its failures are not recoverable by the LLM.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Two-stage vs. End-to-End:* Sacrifices optimization efficiency for explicit anatomical disentanglement and interpretable intermediates (`ˆybox`); adds inference latency.
  - *Fixed 36 objects vs. Open Vocabulary:* Confines model to Chest-ImaGenome anatomy; lacks flexibility for rare structures but ensures consistent token mapping.
  - *VDB Retrieval vs. Encoder:* VDB is lightweight and injects real phrases, but is a static, non-differentiable lookup from a specific validation set, limiting novelty handling.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - *APM failure:* Low `ˆybox` IoU/mAP (esp. on left/right structures in flipped images); low `ˆSt` retrieval CheXbert F1.
  - *Prompt construction failure:* Mismatch between number of `<obj_i>` tokens and retrieved `ˆSt`/`ˆybox` entries.
  - *LLM failure:* LLM ignores provided tokens/boxes (check attention patterns); ablations `AnatomiX-Ip`/`AnatomiX-ˆOA` show collapse if components removed.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce APM in isolation: Train APM on Chest-ImaGenome only; validate `ˆybox` (IoU/mAP) and `ˆSt` retrieval (CheXbert/RadGraph F1); confirm self-similarity loss beats CLIP loss.
  2. Test APM robustness: Run trained APM on horizontally flipped CXR images; measure degradation in `ˆybox` for left/right symmetric structures; expect near-zero drop vs. catastrophic drop for standard detectors.
  3. Integrate & prompt engineer: Freeze trained APM; build multimodal prompt; run full AnatomiX on anatomy grounding; ablate by removing `ˆSt` and `ˆybox` to see direct impact (compare to `AnatomiX-ˆSt`/`AnatomiX-ˆybox` in Table 1).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Can AnatomiX's anatomy-aware two-stage approach be extended to other modalities like MRI?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Future work could extend anatomy oriented architectures to other modalities such as MRI" (Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** Only chest X-rays are evaluated; generalization is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Build and evaluate an MRI-adapted AnatomiX with modality-specific anatomical objects and demonstrate gains over baselines.

### How can the multimodal prompt be streamlined to reduce redundancy without sacrificing grounding accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "improve the current architecture by reducing the potential redundancy multi-modal prompt" (Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** The prompt combines multiple elements that may overlap; impact of redundancy is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform ablations removing/compressing prompt components, measure performance and efficiency, and design a minimal prompt.

### How can AnatomiX be extended to multi-turn conversations while maintaining grounding accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "extending it to multi turn setups would enhance flexibility and applicability" (Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** The model is single-turn only; multi-turn context tracking and grounding consistency are unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Create a multi-turn medical dialogue dataset, incorporate dialogue history into the architecture, and evaluate on multi-turn VQA/grounding.

## Limitations
- **Implementation details missing:** Exact image encoder configuration (e.g., DINOv2 variant), multimodal prompt template formatting, and vector database indexing algorithm are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **MedGemma accessibility:** MedGemma-4b-it may have restricted access; substitutions could invalidate alignment and require full retraining.
- **VDB static nature:** The vector database is a non-differentiable lookup from a single dataset’s validation set, limiting coverage for novel clinical phrasing and risking retrieval failures.

## Next Checks
1. **Standalone APM replication & failure mode stress test.** Isolate APM training/validation on Chest-ImaGenome. Measure bounding box IoU/mAP per anatomical object, especially left/right symmetric structures. Introduce synthetic occlusions or label noise to quantify brittleness.
2. **Zero-shot cross-dataset anatomy grounding.** Evaluate AnatomiX on a completely external chest X-ray corpus (e.g., MIMIC-CXR-JPG) with *unseen* anatomical phrase annotations. Compare IoU/mAP drop vs. in-distribution; a sharp decline would indicate overfitting to Chest-ImaGenome’s annotation style/VDB.
3. **Vector database coverage audit.** For a held-out sample of Chest-ImaGenome validation sentences, compute nearest-neighbor retrieval rates within the VDB. Correlate retrieval CheXbert/RadGraph F1 with downstream grounding performance per anatomical object to identify coverage gaps for specific findings.
