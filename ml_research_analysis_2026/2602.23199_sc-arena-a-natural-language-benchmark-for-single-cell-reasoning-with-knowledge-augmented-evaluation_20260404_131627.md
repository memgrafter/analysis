# SC-Arena: A Natural Language Benchmark for Single-Cell Reasoning with Knowledge-Augmented Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23199
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23199
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SC-Arena establishes a Virtual Cell abstraction and knowledge-augmented evaluator; top general-purpose LLMs (e.g., Kimi-K2) score only 277.2/500, with perturbation prediction best <38/100, indicating significant gaps in causal reasoning.

## Executive Summary
SC-Arena tackles the fragmented evaluation of large language models (LLMs) in single-cell biology by introducing a Virtual Cell abstraction. This abstraction unifies five natural language tasks: cell type annotation (expression→ontology), captioning (expression→language), generation (ontology/language→expression), perturbation prediction (environment→cell), and scientific QA (cell→environment). It models cells as Knowledge Cell objects with Attributes (intrinsic identity and state) and Methods (extrinsic dynamics), enabling assessment of both static and dynamic reasoning. A knowledge-augmented evaluator grounds model outputs in external ontologies, marker databases, and literature, producing biologically faithful scores (0–100) and interpretable rationales. Benchmarking state-of-the-art LLMs reveals uneven performance: models excel at captioning and QA but struggle with mechanistic tasks, especially perturbation prediction where the best score remains below 38/100. The evaluator strongly aligns with biological hierarchy (Spearman ρ=0.6212, p<0.001) and expert judgment, demonstrating high discriminative capacity. Even top general-purpose models fall far short of a reliable passing threshold, highlighting critical gaps in causal reasoning and the need for biology-aligned models.

## Method Summary
SC-Arena defines five natural language tasks derived from single-cell data. Inputs are standardized "cell sentences" (ranked gene lists) or textual queries. Datasets are drawn from CELLxGENE (for annotation, captioning, generation), perturbation studies (Norman, Adamson for prediction), and PubMed articles (for scientific QA). Each task is formatted as an open-ended QA pair. Model outputs are converted to a unified schema. Evaluation uses a knowledge-augmented LLM-as-judge (GPT-4o-mini) that conditions on retrieved knowledge from the Cell Ontology, CellMarker, Gene Ontology, UniProt, and relevant PubMed abstracts, along with ground truth. The judge assigns a score on a 0–5 scale, which is normalized to 0–100. Scores are aggregated per task and summed to produce a total score out of 500. No model training is performed; the benchmark serves solely as an evaluation suite.

## Key Results
- Top model Kimi-K2 achieves 277.2/500 total score; no model surpasses 300.
- Perturbation prediction remains the most challenging task, with best score <38/100.
- The evaluator aligns with biological hierarchy (Spearman ρ=0.6212, p<0.001) and shows strong agreement with human experts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Virtual Cell Abstraction
- Claim: SC-Arena’s Virtual Cell abstraction unifies evaluation of diverse single‑cell tasks under a coherent object‑oriented paradigm, enabling holistic assessment of both static attributes (cell identity, state) and dynamic methods (interactions with the environment), which is necessary for a model to qualify as a “virtual cell.”
- Mechanism: It defines a Knowledge Cell class with two facets: **Attributes** (expression‑based, text‑based, ontology‑based) and **Methods** (Cell→Environment and Environment→Cell processes). Each benchmark task corresponds to a mapping between these facets (e.g., expression→ontology, ontology→expression, environment→cell), forcing models to handle both representation and reasoning.
- Core assumption: A model that performs consistently across all five tasks has acquired a biologically meaningful, integrated understanding of cellular systems rather than memorizing task‑specific patterns.
- Evidence anchors:  
  [abstract] “SC-ARENA formalizes a virtual cell abstraction that unifies evaluation targets by representing both intrinsic attributes and gene‑level interactions.”  
  [section 3.1] “Attributes represent the intrinsic identity and state of a cell… Methods represent the extrinsic dynamics of a cell, modeling its interactions with the environment…”  
  [corpus] LLM4Cell abstract notes that progress in single‑cell LLMs “remains fragmented across data modalities, architectures, and evaluation standards,” highlighting the need for a unified abstraction like Virtual Cell.
- Break condition: If models achieve high scores by overfitting to task‑specific shortcuts (e.g., memorizing marker genes for annotation but failing generation), the Virtual Cell’s unifying power is weakened; the paradigm would fail to detect lack of integrated reasoning.

### Mechanism 2: Knowledge‑Augmented Evaluation
- Claim: By conditioning evaluation on curated external knowledge (ontologies, marker databases, literature), SC‑Arena produces scores that are biologically faithful, interpretable, and robust to lexical variability, overcoming the brittleness of string‑matching metrics.
- Mechanism: For each model response *r*, the evaluator LLM *E* receives the query *q*, ground truth *g*, and retrieved knowledge *K* (from Cell Ontology, CellMarker, GO, UniProt, PubMed). *E* then assigns a score *s = E(q,r,K,g)* on a scale 0–5, providing rationales. This allows semantic matching, penalization of biologically implausible claims, and partial credit for near‑misses.
- Core assumption: Biologically correct outputs align with consensus knowledge in curated resources, and an LLM judge can reliably compare outputs against that knowledge when supplied with explicit references.
- Evidence anchors:  
  [abstract] “introduce knowledge‑augmented evaluation, which incorporates external ontologies, marker databases, and scientific literature to support biologically faithful and interpretable judgments.”  
  [section 3.3] “Grounding in these verifiable references enables the evaluation to capture semantic coherence, penalize biologically implausible outputs, and assign partial credit to semantically related predictions…”  
  [corpus] CellVerse abstract states that comprehensive evaluation of LLMs on cell biology “remains” insufficient, emphasizing the novelty of knowledge‑grounded scoring.
- Break condition: If external knowledge bases are incomplete/outdated or the judge LLM misinterprets them, scores may become noisy; also, high computational cost may limit scalability.

### Mechanism 3: Open‑Ended Natural Language QA Tasks
- Claim: Reformulating single‑cell benchmarks as open‑ended natural language QA better reflects real‑world scientific practice and reveals reasoning depth, particularly for causal and mechanistic tasks, compared to rigid multiple‑choice or classification formats.
- Mechanism: Each task presents a natural language prompt requiring free‑text generation (e.g., “describe this cell”, “predict genes affected by perturbation”). Models must produce detailed outputs such as ranked gene lists, perturbation responses, or step‑by‑step reasoning. This forces them to construct and articulate their knowledge rather than select from limited options.
- Core assumption: Complex biological reasoning cannot be adequately captured by constrained formats; generative evaluation probes a model’s ability to synthesize and explain biological phenomena.
- Evidence anchors:  
  [abstract] “reformulate single‑cell benchmarks into natural language QA tasks, moving beyond rigid classification or multiple‑choice formats to better reflect real‑world usage.”  
  [section 3.2] “Unlike prior benchmarks… we adopt open‑ended QA to better reflect practical use cases and capture reasoning depth.”  
  [corpus] ChatCell abstract emphasizes that LLMs’ “free‑form dialogue” capabilities can advance single‑cell biology, supporting the shift toward natural language interaction.
- Break condition: Open‑ended generation increases evaluation cost and may suffer from lower inter‑rater reliability even with knowledge‑augmented judging; also, models might generate verbose but incorrect answers that are hard to detect without deep biological knowledge.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Data Sources (CELLxGENE, Norman, Adamson, PubMed) -> Task Generators (CTA, CC, CG, PP, SQA) -> Model Inference -> Unified Schema -> Evaluator (GPT-4o-mini, knowledge from CL/CellMarker/GO/UniProt/PubMed) -> Score (0–100) -> Total (sum) -> Leaderboard

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Load benchmark instance (question, ground truth, relevant knowledge snippets).  
  2. Generate model response (using task‑specific prompt; may involve converting data to cell sentence).  
  3. Preprocess response into unified schema (e.g., `[Predicted_Cell_Type: ...]`).  
  4. For evaluation, pass (q, r, K, g) to evaluator LLM.  
  5. Parse evaluator output to extract numeric score and rationale.  
  6. Aggregate scores across instances/tasks.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - LLM‑as‑a‑judge vs. automated metrics: higher fidelity but slower, costlier, and potentially stochastic; mitigated by using a small judge model (GPT‑4o‑mini) and multiple runs.  
  - Knowledge retrieval: curated static knowledge ensures stability vs. dynamic retrieval which may introduce noise; SC‑Arena uses pre‑selected snippets per task.  
  - Open‑ended tasks: reflect real usage but require robust judging and make Leaderboard noisy; tradeoff between realism and reproducibility.  
  - Unified cell sentence: simplifies cross‑task pipelines but may oversimplify expression distributions; however, it enables consistent formatting.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Evaluator inconsistency: large variance when using different judge models or seeds; manifest as fluctuating scores on identical responses.  
  - Low expert agreement: if human biologists disagree with evaluator scores, indicates misalignment.  
  - Task ambiguity: if model outputs are nonsensical but still receive moderate scores, suggests evaluator is not penalizing errors adequately.  
  - Knowledge gaps: missing marker genes or ontology entries cause false negatives (correct predictions penalized).

- **First experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Benchmarking**: Run all evaluated models (Qwen series, GPT‑4o, DeepSeek‑R1, Kimi‑K2, C2S, scGenePT, etc.) on the full SC‑Arena suite to establish performance tables and identify strengths/weaknesses per task.  
  2. **Validation of Evaluator**: On the Cell Type Annotation task, compute Spearman correlation between evaluator scores and ontology path distance to root to confirm biological grounding; also conduct human preference studies for open‑ended tasks (CC, SQA).  
  3. **Robustness Analysis**: Test evaluator stability across different judge models (e.g., GPT‑4o‑mini vs. DeepSeek‑V3.2‑Exp), random seeds, and response lengths (measure correlation with token count); ensure scores are insensitive to these factors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the Virtual Cell abstraction be extended to spatial transcriptomics, developmental trajectories, and multi-omics integration without fundamental redesign?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 6.3 lists these as future extensions, implying the current abstraction may be adaptable but this is untested.
- Why unresolved: These modalities introduce new dimensions (space, time, multiple molecular layers) that may require additional categories beyond current "attributes" and "methods."
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing prototype tasks for each modality within SC-Arena; demonstrating that existing evaluation metrics and knowledge resources apply; showing that model performance patterns remain interpretable.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What architectural or training changes are needed to close the perturbation prediction gap (<38/100), reflecting fundamental deficits in causal reasoning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Results show best score 37.5/100 on Perturbation Prediction; authors highlight this as revealing "gaps in causal reasoning."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the performance gap but not its cause. It could stem from architecture, data scarcity, lack of explicit causal modeling, or evaluation design.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of model architectures with causal inductive biases; training on larger perturbation datasets; fine-grained error analysis to isolate which sub-task (DEG identification, direction, prediction) is hardest.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can LLMs achieve both linguistic fluency and biological faithfulness, overcoming the dissociation where models perform well on open-ended tasks but poorly on mechanistic ones?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Discussion: "Our results reveal a clear dissociation between linguistic fluency and biological faithfulness... models may speak biology convincingly, yet fail to reason with precision, hierarchy, and causality."
- Why unresolved: The paper diagnoses the gap but does not test solutions. Unknown if this requires architectural changes, pretraining objectives, multi-task learning, or knowledge integration during training.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training interventions that explicitly encode hierarchical biological knowledge (e.g., ontology-aware losses) or causal reasoning objectives; evaluation showing improved correlation between fluency metrics and knowledge-augmented scores across all tasks.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can the knowledge-augmented LLM-as-judge evaluator achieve deterministic, bias‑free scoring while preserving interpretability and biological grounding?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Discussion acknowledges the judge "inherits the probabilistic nature of LLMs and thus exhibits limitations" and suggests ensemble/calibration approaches.
- Why unresolved: Current evaluator (GPT-4o-mini) shows strong correlation with experts but remains stochastic; untested whether proposed mitigations can eliminate variance without losing flexibility.
- What evidence would resolve it: Large-scale evaluation against expert annotations; measuring score variance across judge models and seeds; comparing ensemble/calibrated judges to single-model baseline; demonstrating that scores stabilize while maintaining biological fidelity.

## Limitations
- Incomplete specification of knowledge retrieval and prompt templates may cause reimplementation inconsistencies.
- Evaluator's reliance on an LLM judge introduces nondeterminism and potential biases.
- Benchmark may be vulnerable to superficial heuristics and expert alignment may not generalize across subfields.
