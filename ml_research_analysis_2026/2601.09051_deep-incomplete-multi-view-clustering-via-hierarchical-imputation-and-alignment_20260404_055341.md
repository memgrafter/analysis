# Deep Incomplete Multi-View Clustering via Hierarchical Imputation and Alignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.09051
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09051
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** DIMVC-HIA introduces hierarchical imputation (assignments first, then features) and dual alignment (energy-based + contrastive) to achieve state-of-the-art incomplete multi-view clustering, e.g., on Fashion-MNIST at η=0.7: ACC 95.27%, NMI 92.05%.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC), where some data views are missing. The proposed DIMVC-HIA framework employs a hierarchical imputation strategy that first infers missing cluster assignments via cross-view contrastive similarity and then reconstructs missing features using intra-cluster statistics. Dual alignment modules—an energy-based model for intra-cluster compactness and a contrastive loss for cross-view consistency—are applied to the imputed complete data. Extensive experiments on four benchmarks across missing ratios 0.1–0.7 demonstrate consistent outperformance over ten state-of-the-art methods, with particularly large gains under high missingness.

## Method Summary
DIMVC-HIA uses view-specific autoencoders and a shared clustering predictor. For each sample with missing views, the model hierarchically imputes: (1) missing soft cluster assignments by selecting the most semantically aligned available view via cross-view contrastive similarity; (2) missing latent features by averaging intra-cluster statistics from observed samples. The imputed assignments and features are aligned through two modules: an energy-based model (EBM) that minimizes energy variance within each cluster, and a contrastive assignment alignment (CAA) loss that maximizes agreement between assignment distributions across views while encouraging confident, balanced predictions. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-train autoencoders on reconstruction loss, then fine-tune the full model end-to-end with a combined loss (reconstruction + EBM + CAA).

## Key Results
- DIMVC-HIA achieves superior clustering performance on BDGP, MNIST-USPS, Fashion-MNIST, and Handwritten datasets across missing ratios 0.1–0.7.
- Under high missingness (η=0.7), it attains ACC 95.27% and NMI 92.05% on Fashion-MNIST, and ACC 94.05% and NMI 88.33% on Handwritten.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of contrastive assignment alignment; the model shows stable convergence and parameter robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Assignment-First Imputation
- **Claim:** Recovering missing cluster assignments before feature imputation provides a more reliable semantic prior for reconstruction.
- **Mechanism:** For samples with missing views, the model computes cross-view contrastive similarity scores between co-observed samples (Eq. 8). It then selects the most semantically aligned available view (via a ranked reference list `R_v`) to impute the soft cluster assignment distribution `Q*_v(i)` (Eq. 11). This assignment is subsequently used to impute latent features via intra-cluster statistics (Eq. 14).
- **Core assumption:** Cluster assignment space is a more stable and semantically informative latent space for cross-view propagation than raw or latent feature space, especially under high missingness.
- **Evidence anchors:** The entire "Hierarchical Imputation" subsection (section PAGE 3) details the two-stage process and its label-aware contrastive formulation. Ablation studies show that removing the contrastive alignment (which includes assignment imputation) causes significant performance drop.
- **Break condition:** If cross-view semantic similarity (Eq. 8) is miscalculated due to extremely poor initial cluster predictions, the assignment imputation propagates errors to the feature reconstruction stage.

### Mechanism 2: Dual Alignment for Compactness and Consistency
- **Claim:** Dual alignment (EBM + Contrastive) enforces intra-cluster compactness and cross-view consistency, mitigating representation uncertainty from imputation.
- **Mechanism:** (i) An Energy-Based Model (EBM) per cluster minimizes the energy variance of features within that cluster around a low-energy anchor (Eq. 17), promoting compactness. (ii) A Contrastive Assignment Alignment (CAA) loss maximizes agreement between assignment distributions `Q*_v` and `Q*_v'` for the same sample across views (Eq. 20) while regularizing for confident, balanced predictions (Eq. 22).
- **Core assumption:** Aligning assignments directly is more effective for cross-view consistency than aligning features, and EBMs provide a flexible, non-parametric way to model cluster cohesion beyond simple center-based losses.
- **Evidence anchors:** The "Energy-Based Semantic Alignment" and "Contrastive Assignment Alignment" subsections (section PAGE 4-5) define the losses. Ablation studies (PAGE 6, Figure 2) show that removing `L_CAA` causes the largest performance drop, indicating its critical role. The EBM component is novel but its specific benefit is not isolated in the paper.
- **Break condition:** If the EBM training becomes unstable (e.g., poor estimation of `ε_min` in Eq. 16), it could distort the energy landscape. The CAA loss may fail if the imputed assignments are already highly erroneous and misaligned.

### Mechanism 3: End-to-End Error Mitigation
- **Claim:** An end-to-end framework that jointly optimizes reconstruction, hierarchical imputation, and dual alignment breaks the error propagation cycle of traditional imputation-based IMVC.
- **Mechanism:** The total loss `L = L_REC + α·L_EBM + β·L_CAA` (Eq. 23) couples the reconstruction of *observed* data (stabilizing autoencoders) with the alignment losses applied to the *imputed* (complete) data and assignments. The imputation steps (Eqs. 11, 14) are differentiable operations embedded within the computational graph, allowing gradients from alignment losses to flow back and refine the encoders and predictors.
- **Core assumption:** The alignment losses provide corrective feedback that can counteract noise introduced by the hierarchical imputation, preventing error accumulation.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions "unified architecture addresses the core challenges of error propagation in imputation-based methods". The overall objective and Algorithm 1 (section PAGE 5) show the iterative procedure. Ablation studies confirm the importance of the alignment components.
- **Break condition:** If the weighting coefficients `α` and `β` are poorly tuned, the alignment pressures could overwhelm the reconstruction signal on observed data, leading to degenerate latent spaces.

## Foundational Learning
**Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE)**
- **Why needed:** The assignment imputation (Eq. 8) and CAA loss (Eq. 20) are contrastive objectives that require understanding positive/negative pair construction and the temperature parameter `τ`.
- **Quick check:** How does the label-aware filtering in Eq. 8 (excluding false negatives) modify the standard contrastive objective, and why is this crucial for semantic alignment in clustering?

**Energy-Based Models (EBMs)**
- **Why needed:** The `L_EBM` (Eq. 17) uses an EBM to shape the cluster energy landscape. Understanding that EBM training often relies on Langevin dynamics or contrastive divergence is key, even if this implementation uses a simpler anchor-based loss.
- **Quick check:** Why is minimizing `|Eθk(h) - ε_min|` a valid surrogate for making cluster `k` more compact, and what is a potential pitfall of using a single anchor `ε_min`?

**Hierarchical Imputation Strategy**
- **Why needed:** The core innovation is a two-stage, top-down imputation (assignments → features). This requires understanding that semantic structure is often easier to propagate than precise geometric detail.
- **Quick check:** What is the risk of using a *non-parametric* cluster prototype `C_v(k)` (Eq. 13) for feature imputation, and how might the subsequent alignment losses regularize this risk?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
View-Specific Autoencoders (E_v, D_v) -> Latent H_v -> Shared Clustering Predictor (F) -> Assignments Q_v -> Hierarchical Imputer (Assignment Imputer & Feature Imputer) -> Imputed Q*_v and H*_v -> Dual Alignment Modules (EBM Heads & CAA Module) -> Losses -> Update E_v, F, E_θk. L_REC additionally updates D_v.

**Critical path:**
For a batch: (1) Encode observed views to H_v (via E_v). (2) Predict Q_v via F. (3) Impute missing Q*_v using cross-view similarity (Eq. 8-11). (4) Compute cluster prototypes C_v(k) from observed H_v and imputed Q*_v (Eq. 13). (5) Impute missing H*_v via prototypes (Eq. 14). (6) Compute L_REC on observed reconstructions, L_EBM on all H*_v, and L_CAA on all Q*_v. (7) Backpropagate total loss to update E_v, F, and E_θk; L_REC also updates D_v.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- The two-stage imputation (assignments then features) is computationally cheaper than joint deep generative imputation but risks semantic drift if initial Q_v is poor.
- The non-parametric feature imputation (prototype averaging) is simple but discards within-cluster variation; the subsequent EBM alignment may help refine but cannot recover lost details.
- The model sacrifices potential fine-grained feature recovery for training stability and scalability, which is acceptable for clustering where global structure matters more.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Assignment collapse:** Q*_v becomes nearly one-hot for all samples, making cross-view similarity (Eq. 8) uninformative. Monitor entropy of Q_v.
- **EBM collapse:** All E_θk(h) converge to similar values, trivializing L_EBM. Check per-cluster energy distributions.
- **Imputation failure:** T-SNE of H*_v shows no cluster structure even on observed samples, indicating poor prototypes C_v(k). This may be due to bad initial clustering or empty clusters.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Isolate Assignment Imputation Quality:** With a fixed, high-quality Q_v (from a supervised oracle), evaluate if the cross-view similarity ranking (Eq. 8) correctly identifies semantically aligned views on a synthetic dataset with known view correspondence.
2. **EBM Sanity Check:** Train only the EBM heads on H*_v with known true labels. Verify that within-cluster energy variance decreases and that low-energy samples resemble cluster centroids.
3. **CAA Gradient Flow:** During training, monitor the norm of gradients flowing from L_CAA back into the predictor F. If near zero, the assignment imputation may have saturated, disconnecting the alignment signal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** What is the effect of temperature τ on DIMVC-HIA's performance and what value should be used?
- **Basis:** τ appears in contrastive terms (Eqs.8,20) but is unreported and not studied; the sensitivity analysis (Fig. 5) omitted τ.
- **Why unresolved:** τ affects contrastive sharpness; wrong choice may harm alignment, especially under high missingness.
- **Resolution evidence:** Systematic testing of τ ∈ {0.05,0.1,0.5,1.0} across datasets/missing rates.

**Open Question 2:** Does using a generative model for feature imputation improve over prototype averaging?
- **Basis:** Missing features are imputed by cluster averages (Eq. 13–14). No alternative imputation strategies are evaluated.
- **Why unresolved:** Averaging loses intra-cluster diversity; generative imputation could capture richer structure.
- **Resolution evidence:** Replace prototype imputation with decoder-based generation conditioned on assignments; compare performance.

**Open Question 3:** How does DIMVC-HIA behave when missingness is informative (MNAR) rather than random?
- **Basis:** Experiments use random omission (MCAR/MAR). No evaluation of MNAR where missingness correlates with class or features.
- **Why unresolved:** Informative missingness can bias cross-view similarity and cluster prototypes, which the method relies on.
- **Resolution evidence:** Create synthetic MNAR datasets by making missing probability class-dependent; test DIMVC-HIA and baselines; compare performance drop.

## Limitations
- No direct evaluation of imputation accuracy for assignments/features; benefit inferred solely from downstream performance.
- The EBM component may be unstable (risk of empty clusters or energy collapse) and its specific contribution is not isolated in ablations.
- Critical hyperparameters (temperature τ, loss weights α/β) and optimizer details are omitted, hindering reproducibility; experimental evaluation is limited to four standard datasets; generalizability to larger-scale or structured-missing data is unknown.
- Assumption of random missingness may not hold in real-world scenarios, potentially degrading cross-view similarity estimates.

## Confidence
- **Hierarchical Imputation (top-down assignment→feature):** Medium. Ablation shows performance drop when contrastive alignment is removed, but imputation quality itself is not measured directly; the core assumption remains unverified.
- **Dual Alignment (EBM + Contrastive):** Medium. Contrastive assignment alignment clearly critical per ablation; EBM’s role is less certain due to possible instability and minimal standalone evidence.
- **Overall SOTA Performance & Error Mitigation:** High. Strong, consistent results across four datasets and missing ratios 0.1–0.7 provide robust empirical support, despite limited benchmark diversity.

## Next Checks
1. **Imputation Accuracy Validation:** Create synthetic data with known true cluster assignments and features; compute error rates of imputed Q*_v and H*_v to directly test the hierarchical imputation hypothesis under varying missing ratios.
2. **EBM Isolation Experiment:** Replace L_EBM with a simple cluster-center-based compactness loss (e.g., MSE to class prototypes) and compare performance to quantify the unique contribution of the EBM formulation.
3. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis:** Perform a systematic grid search over τ, α, β on a held-out configuration; report performance variance to confirm the claimed robustness to parameter choices.
