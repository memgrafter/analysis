# Learning Rate Scaling across LoRA Ranks and Transfer to Full Finetuning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06204
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06204
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: μA derives optimal LR scaling for LoRA that depends on initialization and multiplier; Init[B] with α=1 yields rank‑invariant LR matching full finetuning, enabling direct hyperparameter transfer; Init[A] with α=1 yields LR ∝ r⁻¹/². Validated across language, vision, vision–language, RL, diffusion.

## Executive Summary
Maximal-Update Adaptation (μA) provides a theoretical analysis of LoRA dynamics in the joint limit of network width (n) and rank (r). Assuming bounded activations/gradients and a sign‑SGD optimizer, the framework decomposes the LoRA update into three terms and imposes a maximal‑update principle (ΔZ_B = Θ(1)) to determine stable learning rates. This yields explicit scaling laws: for Init[A] (random A, B=0) with constant α, optimal η ∝ n⁻¹/² r⁻¹/²; for Init[B] (random B, A=0) with constant α, optimal η ∝ n⁻¹ independent of r. The latter coincides with full finetuning scaling, enabling direct LR transfer. Empirical experiments on transformers (Llama, Qwen), ViT, diffusion, and RL tasks confirm these predictions: Init[A],α=1 shows η ∝ r⁻¹/²; Init[B],α=1 gives rank‑invariant η aligned with full FT. The work thus establishes a principled foundation for hyperparameter tuning and transfer in efficient adaptation.

## Method Summary
The authors develop μA theory by modeling a single LoRA‑augmented linear layer under asymptotic growth (n, r → ∞). Using random matrix analysis and Stein’s lemma, they compute the leading‑order effects of updating the adapter factors A and B. The sign‑SGD abstraction allows closed‑form expressions for the three update components (δ₁, δ₂, δ₃). The maximal‑update condition ΔZ_B = Θ(1) uniquely determines the optimal learning rate scaling for each initialization (Init[A] vs. Init[B]) and multiplier α. To validate, they run extensive experiments across language modeling (Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-3B on Tulu-3/OpenThoughts), image classification (ViT-Huge on ImageNet), vision–language (Qwen-VL on LLaVA), reinforcement learning (reward models), and diffusion (Stable Diffusion on Naruto captions). For each configuration (rank, init, α) they sweep learning rates on a log₂ grid, using AdamW with a linear‑warmup/cosine‑decay schedule and fixed batch size. The measured optimal learning rates exhibit the predicted trends, supporting μA's theoretical derivations despite the use of AdamW.

## Key Results
- Under **Init[A]** with α = 1, optimal learning rate scales as η ∝ r⁻¹/².
- Under **Init[B]** with α = 1, optimal learning rate is **rank‑invariant** and aligns with the optimal learning rate for full finetuning.
- These scaling laws hold across diverse architectures and tasks, confirming the μA framework.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1 (Initialization changes feature scaling)**  
Init[A] (A∼N(0,1/n), B=0) makes the intermediate feature Z_A scale as Θ(n^{1/2} r^{-(1-γ)/2}) for constant α = r^{-γ}. Since the forward signal through A decays with rank when α is constant, the optimal LR must shrink as r^{-1/2}. Init[B] (B∼N(0,1/r), A=0) keeps Z_A = Θ(1) regardless of rank, leading to LR independent of r.

**Mechanism 2 (Dominant update term)**  
The per‑step LoRA update decomposes into δ₁ (update A with B fixed), δ₂ (update B with A fixed), δ₃ (update both). In the (n, r) → ∞ limit, one term dominates. For Init[A] with constant α, δ₂ dominates, giving ΔZ_B = Θ(η α r Z_A) ⇒ η ∝ n^{-1/2} r^{-(1-γ)/2}. For Init[B] with α=1, δ₁ dominates, giving ΔZ_B = Θ(η α n √r B) ⇒ η ∝ n^{-1}. The maximal‑update condition ΔZ_B = Θ(1) yields the unique scalings.

**Mechanism 3 (Hyperparameter transfer)**  
Init[B] with α=1 produces η ∝ n^{-1}, identical to the full finetuning scaling under the same assumptions. Hence an LR tuned for LoRA at any rank can be directly used for full FT, enabling seamless transfer.

## Foundational Learning
- **Asymptotic Θ‑notation and scaling laws**: Needed to express orders of magnitude (e.g., η ∝ n⁻¹ r⁻¹/²) and guide hyperparameter choices under model growth. Quick check: If η = Θ(n⁻¹ r⁻¹/²), quadrupling r while n unchanged should halve η.
- **LoRA features (Z_A, Z_B) and update decomposition (δ₁, δ₂, δ₃)**: Needed to isolate effects of updating A or B and analyze stability. Quick check: Which δ term corresponds to updating both A and B simultaneously? δ₃.
- **Maximal‑Update Adaptation (μA) principle – ΔZ_B = Θ(1)**: Needed to formalize “stable yet non‑vanishing” feature updates as n,r grow. Quick check: If ΔZ_B = O(1/√n), what happens as n → ∞? Updates vanish, stalling learning.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Pretrained frozen matrix W★ (n×n); trainable LoRA factors A (r×n) and B (n×r) with scalar multiplier α; layer input Z; intermediate Z_A = A Z; LoRA output Z_B = α B Z_A; backward gradient dZ = ∂L/∂Z_B; optimizer (SignSGD) with learning rate η; assumptions: Z, dZ = Θ(1), single‑sample, no momentum.
- **Critical path**: (1) Analyze single LoRA‑augmented linear layer assuming constant Z. (2) Write per‑step update ΔZ_B = α B ΔZ_A + α ΔB Z_A + α ΔB ΔZ_A. (3) Express gradients in sign form using Stein’s lemma. (4) Assume Gaussian initial variance for non‑zero factor (1/n for A, 1/r for B). (5) Recurse scales: Z_A^{(t)} = max(Θ(Z_A^{(t-1)}), Θ(η n)); B^{(t)} = max(Θ(B^{(t-1)}), Θ(η)). (6) Plug recursed scales into asymptotic δ₁, δ₂, δ₃ (Lemmas). (7) Impose ΔZ_B = max(Θ(δ₁), Θ(δ₂), Θ(δ₃)) = Θ(1) to solve for η. (8) Specialize for Init[A] and Init[B] cases. (9) Repeat for full finetuning to compare scaling.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Init[A] yields a wider stable η range but requires η ∝ r^{-1/2} (strong rank dependence). Init[B] gives rank‑invariant η but much smaller (Θ(n^{-1})). Choosing α = r^{-1} with Init[A] can absorb rank dependence but narrows the stability window. Higher rank under Init[A] forces smaller η; under Init[B] (α=1) η stays constant but δ₂ may vanish if r ≪ n, making the adapter behave like a fixed random projection.
- **Failure signatures**: Divergence (loss → NaN) from η too high; reduce LR, check clipping/precision. Stagnation (loss plateau) from η too low; increase LR or check optimizer state. No rank‑dependent trend for Init[A],α=1: widen rank range and densify LR grid. Failed LR transfer: confirm Init[B] α=1, same optimizer, and fixed head LR. Implementation errors: check LoRA output near zero initially; monitor Z_A scaling (should stay O(1) for Init[B]).
- **First 3 experiments**: (1) Init[A] α=1: Vary η across ranks {4,16,64,256,1024} for Llama-3.2-1B on Tulu-3 SFT; expect optimal η shifts left ~1 on log₂ scale per 4× rank increase (η ∝ r^{-1/2}). (2) Init[A] α=r^{-1}: Same ranks; expect η roughly constant. (3) Init[B] α=1 & full FT alignment: Sweep ranks {16,32,64,128,256} for Qwen2.5-3B and full FT; expect optimal η cluster that aligns with full‑FT optimum.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: Do the µA scaling rules accurately describe finite‑width and finite‑rank models, and what are the first‑order correction terms? Basis: Section 6 notes asymptotic sensitivity to noise.
- **Question 2**: What additional conditions, beyond matching width‑dependent learning rate scaling, are sufficient to guarantee LR transfer from LoRA to full finetuning? Basis: Section 6 states current result is necessary, not sufficient.
- **Question 3**: Does the µA learning rate scaling remain valid when using AdamW (experiments use AdamW while theory uses SignSGD)? Basis: Unproven correspondence; observed agreement may be fortuitous.
- **Question 4**: For which architectures or training scenarios do the µA scaling rules break down or require modification? Basis: Assumptions (bounded signals) may fail for non‑transformer architectures or extreme regimes.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis assumes SignSGD and bounded activations/gradients; applicability to AdamW is unproven (Medium confidence).
- Asymptotic regime (n, r → ∞) may not hold for small ranks or narrow models, where finite‑size effects could shift optima (Low confidence).
- Empirical validation across tasks shows high confidence in observed trends, but LR transfer to full finetuning relies on matching width‑scaling η ∝ n⁻¹; deviations likely in practice (Medium confidence).

## Confidence
- **SignSGD vs. AdamW**: Medium
- **Finite‑size effects**: Low
- **Empirical trends across tasks**: High
- **LR transfer sufficiency**: Medium
