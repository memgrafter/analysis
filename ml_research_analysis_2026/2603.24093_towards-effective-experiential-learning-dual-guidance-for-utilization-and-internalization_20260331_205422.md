# Towards Effective Experiential Learning: Dual Guidance for Utilization and Internalization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.24093
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24093
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Dual Guidance Optimization (DGO) consistently outperforms SFT, GRPO, and DAPO on Qwen3 models (4B/8B/14B) across six reasoning benchmarks, achieving up to 39.38% average accuracy with test-time scaling.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles improving LLM reasoning in reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards by better utilizing and internalizing experience, inspired by human dual-mechanism learning. It introduces Dual Guidance Optimization (DGO), an iterative framework with three stages: constructing an experience bank from past trajectories, guiding exploration via external experience and internal knowledge with annealing, and internalizing gains through trajectory rephrasing and policy distillation. Evaluations on Qwen3 models of varying sizes across six benchmarks show consistent improvements over baselines. For instance, DGO (intrinsic inference) on Qwen3-8B-Base yields 32.41% average accuracy, rising to 39.38% with test-time scaling, compared to GRPO's 30.14%. Ablations confirm the necessity of all components, and analysis indicates DGO better leverages relevant experience, resists noisy experience, and internalizes broader reasoning patterns.

## Method Summary
DGO operates in an iterative loop. First, it constructs an experience bank by collecting trajectories (e.g., reasoning chains) generated by the current policy on a set of problems, filtering for correctness. Second, during exploration, the model is guided by a combination of external experience (retrieved from the bank) and internal knowledge (the model's own priors), with an annealing coefficient that gradually shifts weight from external to internal guidance. Third, the improvements are internalized by rephrasing the successful trajectories to increase diversity and then distilling the knowledge into the policy via a standard RL method (e.g., GRPO). This cycle repeats, progressively refining the policy.

## Key Results
- DGO achieves state-of-the-art performance on six reasoning benchmarks for Qwen3 models (4B/8B/14B), with average accuracy gains of +2.27% (intrinsic) and +9.24% (test-time scaling) over GRPO for Qwen3-8B-Base.
- Ablations show all three stages (experience bank, external guidance, internalization) are essential; removing any degrades performance.
- DGO demonstrates superior utilization of relevant experience and resilience to noisy experience compared to standard RL methods like GRPO.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DGO's effectiveness stems from its dual guidance strategy, which mirrors human learning by balancing external demonstrations with internal knowledge. The annealing schedule prevents over-reliance on external examples, encouraging the model to develop its own reasoning patterns. Trajectory rephrasing enhances generalization by diversifying the experience, while policy distillation consolidates gains. This combination allows DGO to better leverage high-quality experiences, filter out noise, and internalize broader reasoning strategies, leading to more robust performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Experience Bank**: A dynamic repository of correct trajectories used for external guidance. *Why needed*: Provides high-quality examples to bootstrap learning. *Quick check*: Verify that trajectories are stored with metadata (e.g., problem ID, reward) and can be retrieved efficiently.
- **Annealing**: A scheduled decay of the external guidance weight over iterations. *Why needed*: Gradually transitions the model from imitation to independent reasoning. *Quick check*: Monitor the annealing coefficient α_k to ensure it decreases as intended.
- **Trajectory Re-phrasing**: The process of rewriting successful trajectories to produce semantically equivalent but varied reasoning chains. *Why needed*: Increases diversity and reduces overfitting to specific phrasings. *Quick check*: Evaluate diversity metrics (e.g., BLEU, BERTScore) among rephrased trajectories.
- **Policy Distillation**: Updating the policy using the rephrased trajectories, typically via an RL objective. *Why needed*: Transfers the refined knowledge into the model's parameters. *Quick check*: Measure policy performance on a held-out set after distillation to confirm knowledge transfer.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Experience Bank → External Guidance (with annealing) → Internalization (rephrasing + distillation) → Updated Policy → (back to Experience Bank construction)
- **Critical Path**: The iterative cycle: (1) generate and store correct trajectories; (2) retrieve relevant experiences and combine with internal knowledge to guide exploration; (3) rephrase successful trajectories and distill into policy; (4) repeat with updated policy.
- **Design Tradeoffs**:
  - External vs. internal guidance: annealing schedule must balance immediate gains and long-term internalization.
  - Experience bank size vs. retrieval cost: larger banks may improve coverage but increase computational overhead.
  - Rephrasing diversity vs. semantic preservation: aggressive rephrasing may distort reasoning steps; a trade-off exists.
- **Failure Signatures**:
  - Rapid performance drop when annealing is too aggressive (model loses external support prematurely).
  - Stagnation if experience bank becomes stagnant (lack of novel solutions).
  - Degradation if rephrasing alters logical flow (checked by manual inspection or consistency metrics).
- **First Three Experiments**:
  1. **Ablate external guidance**: Disable the experience bank (set external weight to zero) and compare performance to full DGO to isolate the contribution of external experience.
  2. **Ablate internalization**: Skip rephrasing and distillation, updating policy directly via RL on raw trajectories; measure impact on final accuracy.
  3. **Annealing sensitivity test**: Run DGO with different annealing schedules (e.g., linear, exponential, constant) to identify a robust schedule and observe convergence speed.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can LLMs autonomously manage and update their experience bank without external supervision?
- **Basis**: The conclusion identifies "enabling models to manage and update their own experience more autonomously" as future work.
- **Why unresolved**: DGO relies on a fixed experience generator and manual quality control via validation performance; full autonomy requires self-evaluation of experience relevance, redundancy detection, and update decisions.
- **Resolution evidence**: A self-supervised mechanism where the policy curates its own experience bank while maintaining or improving DGO's performance gains.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can external and internal experience be more effectively coordinated during training beyond fixed annealing schedules?
- **Basis**: The conclusion states the need to "coordinate external and internal experience more effectively."
- **Why unresolved**: DGO uses predetermined annealing coefficients (𝛼𝑘) and internalization ratios (𝛽𝑘); adaptive coordination based on problem difficulty or model state could improve sample efficiency.
- **Resolution evidence**: A dynamic weighting scheme that adjusts based on real-time metrics (e.g., reward variance, confidence) showing faster convergence or higher final accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What explains DGO's robustness to irrelevant experience compared to standard RL methods like GRPO?
- **Basis**: Figure 5 shows DGO suffers smaller performance drops with noisy experience, but the underlying mechanism is not analyzed.
- **Why unresolved**: It is unclear which components (annealing, rephrasing, distillation) contribute most to noise robustness, limiting mechanistic understanding of effective experience utilization.
- **Resolution evidence**: Systematic ablations isolating each component's effect on noise resilience, or interpretability analysis of trajectory divergence under irrelevant experience.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to Qwen3 (4B/8B/14B) and six benchmarks; generalizability to other model architectures (e.g., Llama, GPT) and broader tasks remains unknown.
- No reporting of training/inference compute costs; the efficiency trade-offs of the iterative DGO process are unclear.
- Internalization components (rephrasing, distillation) contributions are not isolated; ablations confirm necessity but not individual impact.

## Confidence
- **Qwen3 performance gains**: High (consistent across scales/benchmarks, ablations support).
- **Utilization & noise resistance**: Medium (some analysis, but limited stress tests).
- **Internalization of reasoning patterns**: Medium (qualitative evidence, need quantitative pattern metrics).
- **Cross-model generalizability**: Low (no evidence).
- **Efficiency**: Low (no data).
