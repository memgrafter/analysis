# Once-for-All Channel Mixers (HYPERTINYPW): Generative Compression for TinyML
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24916
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24916
- **Reference count:** 2
- **Primary result:** Replaces most pointwise weights with a shared generator, achieving ~6.3× compression (to ~225 kB) while matching a 1.4 MB baseline on ECG tasks and maintaining ≥95% macro-F1 under 32–64 kB budgets.

## Executive Summary
HYPER-TINYPW tackles the flash/SRAM bottleneck in TinyML separable CNNs by replacing stored pointwise (PW) mixer weights with dynamically generated ones. A shared micro-MLP synthesizes PW kernels from tiny per-layer codes at load time; these are cached for standard INT8 inference. The first PW layer is kept stored to stabilize early channel mixing. This generative compression shifts the accuracy–flash Pareto frontier, enabling deployable high-performance models on severely constrained MCUs without per-input overhead or custom kernels.

## Method Summary
The method introduces a generative compressor for pointwise layers beyond the first. A shared generator MLP (`g_ϕ`) maps a low-dimensional per-layer code (`z_l`) to an embedding (`h_l`). A lightweight per-layer head (or factorized `A_l B`) projects `h_l` into the flattened PW kernel, which is reshaped and cached at boot or first use. All generated weights are quantized to INT8. The backbone (depthwise convolutions, stem, classifier) and the first PW layer use standard stored INT8 weights. Training uses a composite loss (focal, soft-F1 auxiliary, spectral regularization, L1 on codes/heads) with optional knowledge distillation. Post-training, generator/heads/codes are quantized to 4/6/8 bits; packed flash size is computed exactly per tensor. Inference runs entirely on cached weights with zero generation calls per input.

## Key Results
- At ~225 kB packed flash, matches a 1.4 MB CNN’s performance on ECG tasks with 6.31× compression (84.15% fewer bytes) and ≥95% macro-F1.
- Under tight 32–64 kB budgets, maintains balanced detection where compact baselines fail.
- Achieves 96.2% test accuracy on Speech Commands (audio).
- Steady-state latency/energy identical to INT8 separable CNN baselines; one-time synthesis overhead is amortized.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Shared generator synthesis reduces cross-layer redundancy in pointwise (PW) mixer storage.
- **Mechanism:** A single micro-MLP (`g_ϕ`) maps tiny per-layer codes (`z_l`) to embeddings, which lightweight heads (`H_l` or factorized `A_l B`) project into full PW kernels. Storing only codes and shared parameters replaces storing each full PW matrix.
- **Core assumption:** Different PW layers share underlying generative factors; a small latent space can express kernel variations across layers.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "replaces most stored PW weights with generated weights: a shared micro-MLP synthesizes PW kernels once at load time from tiny per-layer codes"; [section 3.2] "enforcing a shared latent basis across layers removes cross-layer redundancy".
- **Break condition:** If per-layer transformations are fundamentally orthogonal and cannot be captured by a low-dimensional shared basis, synthesis accuracy collapses.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Keeping the first pointwise (PW1) layer stored as INT8 stabilizes early, morphology-sensitive mixing.
- **Mechanism:** PW1 is excluded from generation and retained in quantized INT8 format, providing a robust, deterministic initial channel transformation before any synthesized mixing occurs.
- **Core assumption:** The earliest channel mixing is most sensitive to raw signal morphology and benefits from uncompromised, stable weights; later layers can tolerate generation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "keeping PW1 in INT8 stabilizes early, morphology-sensitive mixing"; [section 3.2] "we deliberately keep PW1 as a stored INT8 layer and synthesize only PW2:L".
- **Break condition:** If the dataset’s discriminative features rely entirely on late-stage interactions, the stability provided by stored PW1 may not translate to accuracy gains; conversely, if early features are simple, the storage cost of PW1 could be wasteful.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** One-shot, layer-constant synthesis at load time with caching preserves steady-state INT8 inference compatibility and eliminates per-input overhead.
- **Mechanism:** The generator runs exactly once per PW layer during boot or first use; synthesized INT8 weights are cached in flash/SRAM. Subsequent inferences execute standard depthwise and pointwise convolutions using CMSIS-NN/TFLM kernels, with no generation call per input.
- **Core assumption:** MCU workloads require deterministic latency and cannot tolerate per-example branching, SRAM spikes, or jitter from dynamic weight generation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "generation occurs once—at boot or lazily when first needed—then synthesized weights are cached and reused... steady-state latency/energy match INT8 separable CNN baselines"; [section 3.5] "Inference proceeds entirely with standard integer operators... Deployment reduces to a one-time 'weight install' followed by vanilla integer inference."
- **Break condition:** If the target MCU has severe flash write endurance concerns or cannot afford the one-time boot latency, lazy synthesis might be necessary but introduces a first-inference stall; very tight latency budgets might reject the boot synthesis overhead entirely.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Shared Generator (`g_ϕ`) -> Per-Layer Head (`H_l` or factorized `A_l B`) -> Reshape -> Cache (PW kernel); Kept First PW (PW1) and Backbone (DW, stem, classifier) are stored separately; Synthesis & Cache Pipeline triggers at boot/lazy; Inference Engine uses cached weights with standard kernels.
- **Critical path:**
  1. MCU boots or encounters a new layer.
  2. Load `z_l`, fetch `g_ϕ` and `H_l`/`A_l, B`.
  3. Execute `h_l = g_ϕ(z_l)` (integer ops).
  4. Execute `bw_l = H_l h_l` (or `A_l (B h_l)`).
  5. Reshape `bw_l` to PW kernel shape, write to cache (flash/SRAM).
  6. Repeat for all PW2:L.
  7. For each inference: run standard DW+PW (cached) sequence; zero calls to `g_ϕ`.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Generator size (`d_h`) vs. expressivity: Larger `d_h` improves synthesis accuracy but increases `|ϕ|` and `|B|`.
  - Code dimension (`d_z`) vs. per-layer storage: Smaller `d_z` reduces code flash but constrains variation.
  - Head factorization (r): Lower rank `r` saves `|B|` shared storage but may limit capacity.
  - Bit-width (4/6/8-bit): Lower bits reduce flash but introduce quantization noise; 6-bit often optimal.
  - Boot vs. Lazy synthesis: Boot increases startup latency but smooths first inference; lazy reduces boot time but causes first-use stall.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High validation threshold drift on imbalanced datasets (e.g., MIT-BIH) despite high AUC.
  - Generator underfitting: synthesis fails to match stored PW performance, especially near mid-budgets.
  - Codebook collapse: per-layer codes fail to differentiate layers.
  - PW1 ablation failure: removing stored PW1 degrades performance on morphology-sensitive tasks.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline measurement:** Train/evaluate a standard separable CNN (e.g., `regularcnn1d`) to establish ~1.4 MB reference; record macro-F1, AUC, packed flash.
  2. **Synthesis validation (offline):** Run load-time synthesis (Alg. 1) on host; cache PW2:L weights; compare logits/activations against model with stored weights; measure max absolute difference.
  3. **Pareto sweep & elbow detection:** Sweep hyperparameters (`d_z, d_h`, bits, KD); plot macro-F1 vs. packed flash; identify mid-budget elbow (~200-250 kB); confirm HYPERTINYPW dominates frontier there.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can mixed precision be co-designed across HYPERTINYPW's generator, heads, and codes to minimize flash while preserving integer-only inference?  
  *Basis:* Future work (i): "co-design mixed precision for generator/heads/codes under a fixed integer inference path".  
  *Why unresolved:* Current uniform 4/6/8-bit quantization may be suboptimal; optimal bit-width allocation for MCU constraints unknown.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematically vary component bit-widths on ECG datasets, report macro-F1 vs. packed flash, verify on-device integer-only operation.

- **Open Question 2:** Can sharing codebooks across layers or using lower-rank head factorizations further compress HYPERTINYPW without accuracy loss?  
  *Basis:* Future work (ii): "learn shared codebooks or low-rank factors that further tighten packed-byte budgets".  
  *Why unresolved:* Per-layer codes and moderate factorization used; additional sharing/lower rank may improve compression but risk underfitting or instability.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Implement shared codebooks and vary factorization rank r; train on ECG tasks; compare Pareto curves against baseline.

- **Open Question 3:** What are the true cycle-accurate latency and energy costs of HYPERTINYPW on real MCU hardware, including one-time synthesis overhead and SRAM streaming?  
  *Basis:* Future work (iii): "map cycle-acc latency and energy, including streamed synthesis to flash for cold-boot efficiency".  
  *Why unresolved:* Paper uses proxies (virtual models, datasheet estimates); real hardware may differ due to cache effects, interrupts, I/O overhead.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Deploy on Arm Cortex-M boards; measure boot/latency/energy with current probes; compare boot vs. lazy synthesis and weight streaming.

- **Open Question 4:** Can light per-class or subject-specific calibration improve macro-F1 on imbalanced biomedical signals without breaking TinyML constraints?  
  *Basis:* Future work (iv): "add light per-class or subject calibration for imbalance-robust deployment".  
  *Why unresolved:* Global threshold tuning yields high AUC but lower macro-F1 on skewed datasets (e.g., MIT-BIH); per-class thresholds may help but could increase flash/SRAM.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Develop calibration storing minimal extra parameters (e.g., few bytes per class); evaluate on MIT-BIH; report macro-F1 gains and flash overhead.

## Limitations
- Exact backbone architecture (layer counts, channel widths, kernel sizes) not specified, preventing precise reconstruction.
- Training hyperparameters (learning rate schedule, batch size, EMA, loss weights) omitted, increasing replication risk.
- Profiling assumptions (MCU model, clock, energy model, CMSIS-NN version) unknown; absolute latency/energy numbers may not transfer.
- No published ablations isolating generator size, factorization rank, or PW1 contribution; mechanisms remain speculative.
- Evidence solely from authors; no external corroboration in neighbouring corpus.

## Confidence
- **Effectiveness (compression & accuracy):** Medium. Strong empirical numbers across datasets but missing details hinder verification.
- **Mechanisms (shared generator & stored PW1):** Medium. Plausible design but ablations absent to isolate contributions.
- **One-shot generation compatibility:** High. By design, synthesis at load time and standard INT8 inference guarantee zero per-input overhead.
