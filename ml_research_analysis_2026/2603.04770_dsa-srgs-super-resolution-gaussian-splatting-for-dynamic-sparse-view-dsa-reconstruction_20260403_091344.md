# DSA-SRGS: Super-Resolution Gaussian Splatting for Dynamic Sparse-View DSA Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04770
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04770
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** Achieves PSNR up to 34.32 dB and SSIM 0.8563 on DSA-15, significantly outperforming prior SOTA like 4DRGS (33.82 dB PSNR) and qualitatively restoring intricate vessel structures.

## Executive Summary
DSA-SRGS tackles the challenge of reconstructing high-resolution 4D digital subtraction angiography (DSA) sequences from sparse, low-resolution projections. It introduces a super-resolution gaussian splatting framework that combines a DSA-specific super-resolution prior with a dynamic 3D representation. Key innovations include a Multi-Fidelity Texture Learning Module that adaptively blends supervision from low-resolution projections and high-resolution pseudo-labels to mitigate hallucination, and Radiative Sub-Pixel Densification that refines gaussian kernels along high-gradient regions to capture fine vascular details. Evaluated on two clinical DSA datasets, it sets new state-of-the-art results, demonstrating both quantitative and qualitative improvements in vascular detail recovery.

## Method Summary
The method integrates three core components. First, a fine-tuned DSA super-resolution model (CATANet) generates high-resolution images from low-resolution inputs. A confidence-aware strategy computes a per-pixel confidence map by combining SSIM between SR and upsampled LR inputs with a "texture richness" measure, then blends them into a teaching image that guides reconstruction. Second, Radiative Sub-Pixel Densification dynamically splits gaussian kernels where gradient magnitudes exceed a decaying threshold, adding smaller sub-kernels with perturbed positions and scales to densify representation in detailed regions. Third, a Dynamic Neural Attenuation Field (an MLP with spatial and temporal hash features) models time-varying density and attenuation. Training optimizes gaussian parameters with dual losses: one in LR projection space (L_gt) and one in HR space using the teaching image (L_sr), balanced by a weighting factor λ_ssim.

## Key Results
- PSNR up to 34.32 dB and SSIM 0.8563 on the DSA-15 dataset, surpassing 4DRGS (33.82 dB PSNR).
- Significant qualitative recovery of fine vascular structures compared to prior methods.
- Effective mitigation of SR-induced hallucinations via the confidence-aware multi-fidelity learning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by strategically leveraging the SR prior while constraining its unreliability. The Multi-Fidelity Texture Learning adaptively reduces reliance on SR outputs in low-confidence regions (e.g., low-contrast or noisy areas), preventing hallucinations from corrupting the 3D reconstruction. Radiative Sub-Pixel Densification directly addresses the resolution limit of gaussian splatting by concentrating kernel density along high-gradient edges of vessels, which correspond to sub-pixel details in the projections. The Dynamic Neural Attenuation Field provides a compact, time-varying representation that captures the propagation of contrast agent through vessels, enabling consistent reconstruction across sparse views.

## Foundational Learning
- **Super-Resolution Gaussian Splatting:** Combines image SR with 3D GS to bridge sparse-view low-res inputs to high-res 4D output. *Why needed:* DSA projections are inherently low-resolution; GS alone cannot recover sub-pixel details. *Quick check:* Evaluate SR module's PSNR/SSIM on a held-out set of DSA SR pairs before integration.
- **Multi-Fidelity Texture Learning:** Blends SR and upsampled LR signals using a confidence map. *Why needed:* Pure SR supervision risks hallucination; pure LR supervision lacks detail. *Quick check:* Visualize confidence maps and blended teaching images; low-confidence regions should show reduced SR artifacts.
- **Radiative Sub-Pixel Densification:** Gradient-driven splitting of gaussians into sub-kernels. *Why needed:* Fixed gaussian kernels cannot represent fine vascular edges at sub-pixel scales. *Quick check:* Log kernel count vs. iteration; splits should concentrate in high-gradient projection regions.
- **Dynamic Neural Attenuation Field:** MLP with spatial-temporal hash features for time-varying density. *Why needed:* Contrast agent flow changes over time; static density fails to model dynamics. *Quick check:* Render time slices and check temporal coherence of vessel opacities.
- **Confidence-aware Supervision:** Heuristic combination of SSIM and texture richness. *Why needed:* To weight LR vs. SR ground-truth signals per-pixel without requiring labeled uncertainty. *Quick check:* Ablate the confidence term; expect drop in SSIM/PSNR due to increased hallucinations or blur.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input LR Projections → Fine-tuned SR Model (I_SR) + Upsampled LR (I↑LR) → Confidence Calculation (C) → Teaching Image (I_teach = C⊙I_SR + (1-C)⊙I↑LR) → Gaussian Splatting Renderer (with RSD & DNAF) → HR Views.
- **Critical path:** The SR module's quality directly affects I_teach, which supervises the GS optimization; RSD modifies the GS representation during optimization to align with high-gradient regions in I_teach; DNAF ensures temporal consistency.
- **Design tradeoffs:** SR accuracy vs. hallucination risk (tuned by confidence heuristic), kernel count vs. detail (controlled by τ_grad and splitting params), heuristic confidence vs. learned uncertainty (fixed weights may not generalize).
- **Failure signatures:** Visible SR artifacts in low-confidence areas, explosive kernel growth (>200k) from oversplitting, blurry outputs if L_sr weight too low, misaligned projections if camera matrices incorrect.
- **First experiments:** 1) Benchmark SR module alone on DSA SR pairs to establish upper bound. 2) Render a simple geometric phantom (e.g., sphere) using provided projection matrices to validate reprojection consistency. 3) Monitor kernel count and splitting frequency during early training to tune τ_grad decay.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the dependency on paired high-resolution training data for the SR prior be removed by adopting a self-supervised approach?
- **Basis in paper:** Future work will explore self-supervised super-resolution reconstruction.
- **Why unresolved:** Current fine-tuning needs 17,822 paired images, which may be scarce; self-supervision could improve applicability but its effectiveness in DSA is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace the supervised SR model with a self-supervised one (e.g., leveraging physics or unpaired losses) and show similar PSNR/SSIM on DSA-15/28 without HR labels.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can the reconstruction runtime be reduced to clinically acceptable real-time levels (<1 sec/frame) while maintaining accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** Further optimize the reconstruction speed to expand its clinical application.
- **Why unresolved:** The ~15 min per case runtime on RTX 3090 is prohibitive for intraoperative use; bottlenecks in gaussian splatting and sub-pixel densification are not addressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A speed-optimized implementation achieving >10× acceleration with <1% performance drop on benchmark datasets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the handcrafted confidence-aware weighting between LR and SR supervision generalize well, or could a learned confidence map better suppress hallucinations?
- **Basis in paper:** The confidence metric combines SSIM and texture richness with fixed parameters; no learning involved.
- **Why unresolved:** Heuristic may not capture true uncertainty, especially in low-contrast micro-vessels; could lead to either hallucinated textures or loss of real details.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Jointly train a confidence network; a significant improvement in SSIM/PSNR over the static confidence would prove the heuristic suboptimal.

## Limitations
- **SR integration underspecified:** Details on whether CATANet is applied per-frame or shared across time, and its fine-tuning protocol (data split, augmentations, loss) are missing.
- **Rendering pipeline gaps:** Exact X-ray splatting implementation, occlusion handling, and kernel projection via P_v matrices are not fully described, risking geometric inaccuracies.
- **RSD hyperparameters opaque:** Exact values for τ_grad, sub-kernel count K, scale factor β, and decay schedule η_iter are not provided, which could lead to uncontrolled kernel growth or insufficient detail enhancement.

## Confidence
- **Reported metrics (34.32 dB PSNR on DSA-15):** Medium – numbers are stated but without public code or complete hyperparameters, independent verification is limited.
- **Method superiority over 4DRGS:** Medium – comparison is claimed but test set splits and evaluation protocols lack detail.
- **RSD efficacy for vascular details:** Low – qualitative claims rely on unspecified sub-pixel sampling thresholds and residual guidance criteria.
