# Learning Optimal and Sample-Efficient Decision Policies with Guarantees

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17978
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17978
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Introduces DML-CMR and DML-IL achieving Õ(N⁻¹/²) convergence and suboptimality in offline IV bandits and confounded imitation learning; for LTL objectives, counterfactual imagining in product MDPs yields provably optimal policies with improved sample efficiency.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses offline policy learning under hidden confounders and high-level LTL objectives. It proposes DML-CMR, a double/debiased machine learning estimator for conditional moment restrictions, achieving near-optimal statistical rates. This estimator is applied to instrumental variable regression and proximal causal learning. For imitation learning with confounded expert data, DML-IL reformulates the problem as a CMR using trajectory histories as instruments. For LTL specifications, the method constructs a product MDP with an accepting counter and employs counterfactual imagining to learn optimal policies sample-efficiently. Empirical results across IV regression, causal learning, and LTL benchmarks demonstrate state-of-the-art performance and robustness.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is a unified DML framework for conditional moment restrictions (CMR). DML-CMR transforms CMR problems (e.g., E[Y − f(A,Z) | Z] = 0) into orthogonal score functions and uses cross-fitting to debias nuisance estimates, yielding Õ(N⁻¹/²) convergence. This is applied to offline IV bandits, deriving policies with suboptimality bounds scaling as Õ(N⁻¹/²). For confounded imitation learning, DML-IL recasts the problem as a CMR where the expert's action model is the target, using k-step history as an instrument under additive noise and finite confounding horizon assumptions. For temporally extended LTL goals, the approach builds a product MDP from the environment and an LDBA translation of the LTL formula, augmented with an accepting counter. Counterfactual imagining during Q-learning enriches experience without extra environment interactions, establishing optimality guarantees in tabular MDPs.

## Key Results
- DML-CMR achieves Õ(N⁻¹/²) estimation error for causal functions in IV and proximal causal learning, enabling Õ(N⁻¹/²) suboptimality in offline IV bandits.
- DML-IL recovers expert policies with suboptimality bounded by T²·(estimation error + TV drift), outperforming behavioral cloning and residual methods in confounded settings.
- Counterfactual imagining in product MDPs with K-counter yields provably optimal policies for LTL objectives, improving sample efficiency over standard RL in tabular benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Double Machine Learning for CMR (DML-CMR)**
- **Claim:** DML-CMR achieves Õ(N⁻¹/²) estimation error for causal functions in IV regression and PCL, enabling suboptimality bounds of Õ(N⁻¹/²) for derived policies in offline IV bandits.
- **Mechanism:** Transforms the CMR problem into an orthogonal score function. Uses cross-fitting to decouple first-stage nuisance estimation (e.g., E[A|Z], E[Y|Z]) from second-stage parameter estimation, eliminating regularization bias from plug-in estimators.
- **Core assumptions:** Valid instruments satisfy relevance, unconfoundedness, and exclusion; target function is finite-dimensional or satisfies Neyman orthogonality and boundedness.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions Õ(N⁻¹/²) convergence; Section 4.3.1 derives orthogonal score ψ(D;f,(s,g)) = (s(c)−g(f,c))²; neighbor work on DML for IV shares orthogonal score design.
- **Break condition:** Instruments are weak (low correlation with treatment) → ill-posedness increases variance; nuisance parametric rate fails (e.g., overfitting) → cross-fitting cannot fully debias.

**Mechanism 2: Confounded Imitation as CMR (DML-IL)**
- **Claim:** Using trajectory histories as instruments solves a CMR (E[a_t−π(h_t)|h_{t−k}] = 0) and yields an imitator whose suboptimality scales with T²·(estimation error + TV bound on confounder drift).
- **Mechanism:** Recasts IL as a CMR where the expert action model is the target. Instrument = k-step history; learns history-dependent policy π(h_t) by regressing actions on rolled-out histories using DML-CMR.
- **Core assumptions:** Additive noise in expert actions: a_t = π_E(s_t,u_t^o) + u_t^ε; confounding noise u^ε has finite horizon k: u^ε_t ⊥ u^ε_{t−k} for t > k.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions DML-IL reformulates confounded IL as CMR; Section 5.4.1 derives CMR Eq (5.3); neighbor work uses instrumental ideas for quantile criteria under confounding.
- **Break condition:** Confounding horizon k unknown or too large → instrument weakens; expert-observable confounders contain information outside recorded history → π_E not recoverable.

**Mechanism 3: Counterfactual Imagining for LTL in Product MDPs**
- **Claim:** Counterfactual imagining in a product MDP with K-counter and generalized reward yields sample-efficient RL with optimality guarantees for infinite-horizon LTL objectives.
- **Mechanism:** Constructs product MDP = (env MDP) × (LDBA from LTL) × [0..K]. Uses accepting-state counter K to define reward r_n = U·n/K. At each step, imagines being in all possible automaton states from same env state/action, augmenting experience without extra environment interactions.
- **Core assumptions:** Env MDP has finite state and action spaces; LTL specification is translateable to an LDBA.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions product MDP with counter and counterfactual imagining; Section 6.4.3 describes "counterfactual imagining"; neighbor work on sample efficiency for robust MARL.
- **Break condition:** Continuous/massive state spaces → Q-learning fails; nondeterminism in LDBA excessively high → state space blowup.

## Foundational Learning
**Conditional Moment Restrictions (CMR)**
- *Why needed:* Provides a unified framework for IV regression and PCL, allowing causal effect estimation without full confounder observability. Core equation: E[Y−f(X)|C]=0.
- *Quick check:* Does the CMR problem E[R−f(C,A)|Z,C]=0 with instrument Z identify the causal effect E[R|do(A),C]? What additional assumptions (exclusion, relevance) are needed?

**Neyman Orthogonality**
- *Why needed:* Essential for DML’s bias reduction in two-stage nuisance-based estimation. Ensures small errors in first-stage estimates do not propagate to second stage.
- *Quick check:* Why is the score (Y−E[f(X)|Z])² not Neyman orthogonal? What term must be added for orthogonality?

**ω-Automata and Limit-Deterministic Büchi Automata (LDBA)**
- *Why needed:* LDBA translation of LTL specifications enables incorporation into product MDP for RL; limited nondeterminism makes it tractable.
- *Quick check:* How does an LDBA differ from a standard Büchi automaton? Why is determinism after reaching an accepting state crucial for RL in product MDP?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: DML-CMR core (Alg1/2) -> first-stage estimators (E[A|Z], E[Y|Z]) -> second-stage DML estimator; DML-IL: expert trajectories -> history instrument -> rollout model (cM) -> DML-IL (Alg4) -> policy π_h; LTL: LTL formula -> Rabinizer -> LDBA -> product MDP builder -> counterfactual imagining -> Q-learning.

Critical path: 1. Prepare offline dataset D with (context, instrument, action, outcome) or expert trajectories. 2. Train first-stage nuisance estimators. 3. Train second-stage DML-CMR estimator using orthogonal loss with K-fold cross-fitting. 4. For LTL: translate φ to LDBA; construct product MDP; run KC or CF+KC Q-learning. 5. Deploy: for LTL, extract induced policy; for IL, use learned π_h.

Design tradeoffs: Cross-fitting multiplies first-stage training cost by K; K-counter tradeoff: larger K smooths reward shaping but increases product MDP state space; history length in DML-IL: longer captures more u^o but increases dimension and data needs.

Failure signatures: DML-CMR: second-stage loss not decreasing → invalid instrument or misspecification; check first-stage accuracy. DML-IL: high imitation gap despite low CMR error → TV bound δ too large (expert-observable confounders). LTL: policy never visits accepting states → reward shaping hyperparams violate Theorem 6.10; run PRISM to check satisfiability gap.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Synthetic IV regression:** Run DML-CMR on ticket demand dataset (Sec A.3.1) with N=5000. Verify MSE near O(N⁻¹/²) against DeepIV/DFIV. If MSE > 0.1, check instrument strength and first-stage R².
2. **Confounded IL ticket pricing:** Train expert SAC in ticket env with u^o (season) and u^ε (cost noise). Generate confounded trajectories. Compare DML-IL vs BC vs ResiduIL across k=1,5,10. If DML-IL MSE >0.5, check if k exceeds history length used.
3. **LTL probabilistic gate:** Apply CF+KC with K=10, U=0.1, γ=0.99. After 4000 episodes, PRISM satisfaction should be 0.9. If <0.5, verify product MDP transitions and K-counter increments; reward may be too small.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can DML be extended to provide rigorous guarantees for nonparametric CMR problems?
2. Can we automatically identify a valid instrumental variable (or the confounding horizon) from static expert demonstration data in confounded IL?
3. To what extent do confounded IL algorithms improve performance in real robotic systems where hidden confounders are present?
4. Can RL algorithms with optimality guarantees be developed for maximizing LTL satisfaction probability in continuous state and action MDPs?
5. Is it possible to learn policies from offline datasets that maximize LTL satisfaction probability, and what theoretical limits exist?

## Limitations
- DML-CMR depends on valid instruments and first-stage nuisance rates; weak instruments or misspecified models may break the Õ(N⁻¹/²) rate.
- DML-IL assumes additive expert noise and a finite confounding horizon k; unknown or large k invalidates the instrument and inflates the imitation gap.
- LTL learning via product MDP and counterfactual imagining requires a finite (tabular) MDP; scaling to large/continuous spaces risks state explosion and may invalidate optimality guarantees.

## Confidence
- DML-CMR estimation and offline IV bandit suboptimality: **High**
- Confounded imitation learning (DML-IL): **Medium**
- LTL policy learning with counterfactual imagining: **Medium**
