# Milestones over Outcome: Unlocking Geometric Reasoning with Sub-Goal Verifiable Reward
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05073
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05073
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Training with Sub-Goal Verifiable Reward (SGVR) dramatically improves geometric reasoning (Skeleton Rate +37.5 p.p.) and transfers to general math (+8.0%) and reasoning (+2.8%) tasks.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses a core weakness in multimodal LLMs: poor geometric reasoning due to outcome-only supervision that cannot distinguish lucky guesses from sound deduction. It introduces GeoGoal, a benchmark where each problem is paired with a sequence of verifiable numeric sub-goals automatically derived from a formal proof skeleton. The Sub-Goal Verifiable Reward (SGVR) framework uses these sub-goals to provide dense training signals during reinforcement learning. Training Qwen2.5-VL models with SGVR yields large gains on GeoGoal and consistent improvements across geometry, general math, and reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating that dense intermediate verification cultivates more rigorous and transferable reasoning.

## Method Summary
The SGVR framework operates in three stages. First, a formal data engine (TrustGeoGen) generates formally verified geometric proofs represented as sequences of logical predicates. Second, a deterministic mapping translates each predicate into a numeric sub-goal (e.g., a congruence predicate becomes a length equality to check). This creates a training set of (problem, diagram, ordered sub-goal targets) tuples. Third, during RL training (using GRPO), the model generates a structured reasoning trace containing predicted numeric values for each sub-goal. The reward is the Skeleton Rate—the fraction of sub-goals that match their ground truth values exactly. This dense reward incentivizes step-wise correctness, unlike sparse final-answer rewards. The visual encoder is frozen, and only the language model is updated.

## Key Results
- On the GeoGoal benchmark, training with SGVR improves the Skeleton Rate from 50.2% to 87.7% and Skeleton Completion from 2.3% to 15.2% for a 7B model.
- Gains transfer to general mathematics (AMC +8.0%, MATH-500 +4.0% for 7B) and other reasoning tasks (LiveBench-Reasoning +2.8%).
- Process evaluation scores (LLM-as-a-judge) improve across the board, indicating higher-quality intermediate reasoning, not just better final answers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Converting abstract formal proofs into verifiable numeric sub-goals provides a reliable, automated supervision signal that can distinguish correct reasoning steps from errors.
- **Mechanism:** The TrustGeoGen data engine generates formally verified solution skeletons (sequences of logical predicates). A mapping function translates each predicate into a numeric sub-goal (e.g., `cong(A,B,C,D)` → length ratio `|AB|/|CD|` with expected value `1`). This transformation grounds abstract logic in automatically checkable numeric equality, turning the "black box" of a full proof into a verifiable chain of milestones.
- **Core assumption:** Formal geometric proofs can be decomposed into atomic, numeric sub-tasks without loss of logical necessity, and that numeric equivalence is a sufficient proxy for predicate truth.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper details the "proofing-to-solving" transformation and mapping rules in Section 2.1 and Tables 5-9. Ablations show models trained with this dense signal achieve high Skeleton Rate but low Skeleton Completion when using sparse rewards, demonstrating the necessity of the step-wise signal.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Using the aggregated verification of sub-goals (Skeleton Rate) as a dense reward signal for reinforcement learning (GRPO) trains the model to maximize step-wise correctness, not just final-answer luck.
- **Mechanism:** During RL, for each generated reasoning trace, every predicted sub-goal value is compared to its ground truth numeric target. The reward `R(y) = SR = (1/n) Σ I(ŷ_t == y_t)` is the normalized sum of these binary verifications. GRPO then optimizes the policy to increase this cumulative reward relative to a group of peers, providing gradient information for every sub-step.
- **Core assumption:** Maximizing the proportion of correct intermediate steps (SR) is a better training objective for robust reasoning than maximizing a sparse final-answer signal, as it reduces error propagation and discourages lucky guesses.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.1 defines the sub-goal reward. Section 4.3 (Table 4) ablates reward types: models with Final-Answer reward achieve high SR but catastrophically low SC, while SR reward improves both.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Training on geometric reasoning with SGVR cultivates a general **rigorous verification habit** that transfers to non-geometric reasoning tasks, improving process quality and final accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** The dense, step-level verification signal forces the model to maintain logical consistency throughout long derivations. This learned "verification discipline" is not domain-specific; it manifests as reduced hallucination and better constraint adherence in general math and logical reasoning tasks.
- **Core assumption:** The cognitive process of "checking each step against a verifiable truth" is a transferable meta-skill, independent of the specific geometric content used during training.
- **Evidence anchors:** Cross-domain results in Section 4.2 (RQ2) show improvements on AMC, MATH-500, LiveBench-Reasoning, and VisuLogic. Process Evaluation Scores (LLM-as-a-Judge) improve universally (e.g., GeoPQA-Test +16.4%), indicating better reasoning *quality*.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Formal Verification & Proof Skeletons**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire SGVR framework depends on the existence of a formally verified reasoning chain (the "skeleton") to decompose. Understanding that proofs can be machine-checked and represented as predicate sequences is foundational.
  - **Quick check:** If a geometric proof is verified by a theorem prover, what guarantees exist about its logical soundness, and what are the limitations of translating its predicates into numeric sub-goals?
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Feedback (RLVR)**
  - **Why needed here:** SGVR applies RL (GRPO) using a reward derived from automated verification, not human preference. Understanding policy gradient methods, the KL penalty, and group-based advantage normalization is key.
  - **Quick check:** How does GRPO's advantage computation (`(r_i - mean(group)) / std(group)`) differ from standard PPO's value-based advantage, and why might it be more stable for a binary, step-wise reward signal?
- **Concept: Multimodal Model Architecture (Vision Encoder + LLM)**
  - **Why needed here:** The model (Qwen2.5-VL) processes an image (diagram) and text (problem) to output a structured response. Knowing where visual features are injected and how the output is templated is necessary for adaptation.
  - **Quick check:** In a typical MLLM like Qwen2.5-VL, where does the visual feature embedding enter the language model's transformer layers, and how would you modify the generation template to force the model to produce a sequence of `<subgoal>value</subgoal>` tokens?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Engine (TrustGeoGen) -> Decomposition & Mapping Module -> RL Training Loop (GRPO with Policy π_θ, Reward Function R=SR) -> Evaluation Suite
- **Critical path:** Data generation → Mapping → Reward-function implementation → GRPO integration. The most novel and fragile component is the **numeric mapping rules**; errors here poison all downstream training.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Sub-goal Granularity:** Finer steps provide denser signals but increase risk of spurious errors and longer sequences. Coarser steps are more robust but less diagnostic.
  - **Reward Sparsity vs. Fidelity:** `Skeleton Rate` (dense, proportional) vs. `Skeleton Completion` (binary). SR was more effective for training; SC measures full-chain integrity.
  - **RL Algorithm:** GRPO (no value network) was simpler and stable; PPO gave slightly better general reasoning scores for the 7B model.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Training divergence:** If KL penalty (β) is too low, the model collapses to outputting constant sub-goal values that maximize SR but lack meaning.
  - **Poor transfer:** If gains only appear on GeoGoal but not external benchmarks, the model may have overfit to the numeric mapping pattern. Check Process Evaluation Score improvements.
  - **Mapping errors:** Incorrect numeric translation of predicates leads to false-negative rewards. Manually inspect (predicate, T_t, y_t) tuples.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Verify the benchmark pipeline:** For 20 random training GeoGoal instances, confirm every formal predicate's mapped numeric sub-goal is computable unambiguously and that the ground truth matches the formal proof.
  2.  **Implement and sanity-check the reward function:** Using the pre-trained baseline, generate G=8 rollouts per problem. Compute SR for each. Validate that SR scores are spread, partially correct traces get intermediate SR, and a fully correct trace gets SR=1.0.
  3.  **Smoke-test GRPO update:** With 4 problems, run one GRPO update step. Monitor (a) policy loss decreases, (b) new policy's mean SR > old policy's mean SR, and (c) KL divergence stays <0.1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can the subgoal verification mechanism be extended to automatically validate symbolic geometric statements and diagram-based constructions, beyond deterministic numeric equivalence?
- **Question 2:** Can the SGVR framework be successfully adapted to use a general-purpose formal system such as Lean4, enabling it to handle a wider range of mathematical domains beyond plane geometry?
- **Question 3:** Does the SGVR decomposition-and-verification approach improve reasoning in non-mathematical domains, such as commonsense reasoning, code generation, or scientific problem solving, where intermediate subgoals can be automatically checked?
- **Question 4:** How can we construct a geometry benchmark that better captures the diversity of human reasoning, including non-numeric intermediate steps and varied proof strategies?

## Limitations
- **TrustGeoGen dependency:** The entire sub-goal mapping relies on the TrustGeoGen formal engine (Fu et al., 2025), which may be unavailable or contain undetected flaws.
- **Tiny training set:** Only 256 training samples risk overfitting; the paper lacks validation protocol and convergence criteria.
- **Indirect transfer evidence:** Improvements on AMC/MATH are reported, but no ablation isolates whether gains come from "verification habit" or dataset similarity.

## Confidence
- **GeoGoal gains (SR 50.2% → 87.7%):** **High**—abundant internal ablations support SR reward efficacy.
- **Cross-domain transfer (+8.0% AMC, +2.8% general):** **Medium**—results are consistent but mechanistic link to "rigorous reasoning" is inferential.
- **Mapping correctness (predicates → numeric sub-goals):** **Medium**—depends on TrustGeoGen's soundness; no external validation.
