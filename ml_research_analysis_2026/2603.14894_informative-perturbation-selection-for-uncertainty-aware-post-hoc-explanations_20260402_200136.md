# Informative Perturbation Selection for Uncertainty-Aware Post-hoc Explanations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14894
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14894
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** EAGLE reduces perturbation queries by 22–38% while improving explanation stability, via active learning that maximizes expected information gain in a Bayesian linear surrogate.

## Executive Summary
Post‑hoc explanations like LIME rely on random perturbations, which produce unstable and often unreliable feature attributions. This paper introduces EAGLE, an active learning framework that selects perturbations to maximize expected information gain (EIG) for a Bayesian linear surrogate. The surrogate models the black‑box locally with heteroscedastic noise scaled by locality weights. Theoretical analysis shows cumulative information gain scales as *O(d log t)* and provides sample‑complexity guarantees. Empirical evaluation on tabular (COMPAS, German, Adult, Magic) and image (MNIST, ImageNet) datasets demonstrates that EAGLE yields higher reproducibility (Jaccard similarity), better neighborhood stability, and superior perturbation quality compared to baselines such as BayesLIME and GLIME. It achieves 22–38% query savings while maintaining competitive runtime, offering a more efficient and reliable approach to uncertainty‑aware explanations.

## Method Summary
EAGLE begins with a small seed set of perturbations around the instance *x*₀ and fits a Bayesian linear regressor: *f*ₑ(*z*) = *z*⊤*ϕ* + *ε*, where *ε* ~ *N*(0, σ² π(*x*₀)(*z*)). The locality weight π(*x*₀)(*z*) (e.g., RBF kernel) scales the observation variance. The posterior over coefficients *ϕ* has mean *ϕ̂* and covariance *V*ₚₕᵢ. For each batch, a candidate pool of perturbations is generated and scored by the acquisition function *a*(*z*) = π(*x*₀)(*z*) · *z*⊤*V*ₚₕᵢ*z*, which equals the expected information gain. The top‑*B* candidates are selected, the black‑box is queried, and the data and posterior are updated. After a total budget *N* (e.g., 500) the final explanation is *ϕ̂* with uncertainty quantified by *V*ₚₕᵢ. Default hyperparameters: seed size *S*=10, batch size *B*=10, pool size *A*=1000, prior precision λ = *d*, kernel width 0.75√*d*.

## Key Results
- Improves explanation reproducibility and neighborhood stability (higher Jaccard similarity).
- Achieves 22–38% reduction in required perturbations relative to BayesLIME for comparable accuracy.
- Attains lower runtime and better perturbation quality (D‑efficiency, CIG) than uncertainty‑aware LIME variants.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Maximizing expected information gain (EIG) for the Bayesian surrogate's coefficients is mathematically equivalent to maximizing the locality‑weighted posterior predictive variance.
- **Mechanism:** The acquisition function for a candidate perturbation *z* is derived from the expected reduction in entropy of the posterior over coefficients *ϕ*. Using the matrix determinant lemma on the posterior covariance update *V*₊ = (*V*⁻¹ + π(*z*)**zz**⊤)⁻¹, the EIG simplifies to ½ log(1 + π(*x*₀)(*z*)**V*ϕ*z*). Since log is monotonic, maximizing EIG is equivalent to maximizing π(*x*₀)(*z*)**z**⊤*V*ϕ*z*.
- **Core assumption:** The surrogate model is a Bayesian linear regressor with Gaussian noise and conjugate priors. Perturbations are bounded (‖*z*‖₂ ≤ 1) and locality weights π(*x*₀)(*z*) ∈ [0,1].
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "formulates perturbation selection as an active learning problem, maximizing expected information gain to reduce uncertainty in a Bayesian linear surrogate."
  - [section 4] Theorem 1 and its proof in the supplementary material (Section 1) formally derive the equivalence.
  - [corpus] Evidence is weak; nearby papers discuss uncertainty‑aware LIME variants but not this specific EIG derivation.
- **Break condition:** If the surrogate model is non‑linear or the Gaussianity/linearity assumptions fail, the EIG equivalence (Theorem 1) no longer holds. The locality kernel π must also be differentiable and non‑negative.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The cumulative weighted uncertainty reduction over *t* selected perturbations scales as *O*(*d* log *t*), where *d* is feature dimension.
- **Mechanism:** The information matrix *V*⁻¹ₜ = *I* + ∑ₛ π(*z*ₛ)*z*ₛ*z*ₛ⊤ grows with each query. Bounding the trace of *V*⁻¹ₜ ≤ *d* + *t* (since ‖*z*ₛ‖₂ ≤ 1, π ≤ 1) and applying the AM‑GM inequality to the determinant yields log|*V*ₜ| ≤ *d* log(1 + *t*/*d*). This logarithmic growth reflects diminishing returns.
- **Core assumption:** Perturbations are selected by the EAGLE acquisition (maximizing π*z*⊤*V*ₜ₋₁*z*), are bounded, and locality‑weighted. The prior covariance is spherical (*V*₀ = *I*).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5] Lemma 1 and its proof establish the bound ∑ π(*z*ₛ)*z*ₛ⊤*V*ₛ₋₁*z*ₛ ≤ 2*d* log(1 + *t*/*d*).
  - [section 6] Fig. 2 shows D‑efficiency (related to |*V*ₜ|) increasing sublinearly, consistent with the *O*(*d* log *t*) scaling.
- **Break condition:** The bound relies on ‖*z*ₛ‖₂ ≤ 1. If perturbations have unbounded norm or locality weights π can exceed 1, the trace bound fails. Also, the greedy selection may not achieve the theoretical bound if the candidate pool is poorly distributed.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** For a desired ℓ₂‑accuracy ν, the required number of queries *t* scales as *O*((*d* + log(1/δ))/κν²), where κ depends on the minimum eigenvalue of the accumulated information matrix and δ is confidence.
- **Mechanism:** From Theorem 2, with probability 1–δ, ‖∆*ϕ*ₜ‖²_{Vₜ⁻¹} ≤ β_δ + ‖*ϕ*⋆‖_{Vₜ⁻¹}, where β_δ = σ√[*d* + 2√(*d* log(1/δ)) + 2 log(1/δ)]. Using ‖∆*ϕ*ₜ‖₂ ≤ √λ_max(*V*ₜ) · ‖∆*ϕ*ₜ‖_{Vₜ⁻¹} and λ_min(*I*+*S*ₜ) ≥ 1+κ*t*, solving ‖∆*ϕ*ₜ‖₂ ≤ ν gives *t* ≥ (1/κ)((β_δ + ‖*ϕ*⋆‖₂)²/ν² – 1).
- **Core assumption:** The true linear model *y* = *z*⊤*ϕ*⋆ + *ε* holds in the neighborhood. The noise *ε* ~ *N*(0, σ²) is independent. The information matrix *S*ₜ = ∑ π(*z*ₛ)*z*ₛ*z*ₛ⊤ has eigenvalues bounded below by κ*t*.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5] Corollary 1 states the sample complexity bound explicitly.
  - [section 6.2] Table 2 shows empirical query savings (22–38%) versus BayesLIME, supporting that fewer samples achieve similar accuracy.
- **Break condition:** If the local linearity assumption is violated (black‑box model highly non‑linear in the neighborhood), the true *ϕ*⋆ is not well‑defined. The eigenvalue condition λ_min(*S*ₜ) ≥ κ*t* requires that selected perturbations sufficiently span all directions; poor candidate pool design could violate this.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Bayesian Linear Regression with Heteroscedastic Noise
  - **Why needed here:** EAGLE's surrogate model (Eq. 2) uses a Bayesian linear model where each perturbation *z*ᵢ has a noise variance scaled by locality weight π(*x*₀)(*z*ᵢ). This yields posterior covariance *V*ϕ that directly informs the acquisition function.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the weighted design matrix *W* (diagonal with π(*x*₀)(*z*ᵢ)) affect the posterior precision *V*ϕ⁻¹ compared to ordinary Bayesian linear regression?
- **Concept:** Expected Information Gain (EIG) in Active Learning
  - **Why needed here:** EAGLE selects perturbations to maximize EIG, which reduces entropy in *ϕ*. This is the core active learning criterion that distinguishes EAGLE from heuristics like BayesLIME's predictive variance.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does EIG for a linear‑Gaussian model reduce to a quadratic form *z*⊤*V*ϕ*z*, and why is the locality weight π(*x*₀)(*z*) crucial?
- **Concept:** Matrix Determinant Lemma & Concentration Inequalities
  - **Why needed here:** The theoretical analysis (Lemma 1, Theorem 2) uses the matrix determinant lemma to track log|*V*ₜ| and Laurent‑Massart inequality to bound quadratic forms of Gaussian vectors. These provide the *O*(*d* log *t*) and sample complexity bounds.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the AM‑GM inequality applied to eigenvalues of *V*ₜ⁻¹ lead to the logarithmic bound on log|*V*ₜ|?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Black-box model *f*ᵇ (target predictions) → Perturbation generator (sample candidates near *x*₀) → Bayesian surrogate (fit weighted regression) → Acquisition scorer (compute *a*(*z*) = π(*x*₀)(*z*)·*z*⊤*V*ϕ*z*) → Selection loop (greedy selection, query, update)

**Critical path:** Seed perturbations → fit initial surrogate → for each batch: score candidate pool → select top‑*B* → query black‑box → update data and posterior → refit surrogate.

**Design tradeoffs**
- Candidate pool size |*P*|: Larger pools improve selection quality but increase per‑iteration cost; paper shows moderate sizes (e.g., 1000) suffice.
- Prior precision λ: Controls regularization; default λ = *d* balances oversmoothing (Fig. 3a).
- Locality kernel width: Default 0.75√*d*; too narrow or broad harms locality.

**Failure signatures**
- High Jaccard variance across runs → poor acquisition or ill‑conditioned *V*ϕ.
- Slow D‑efficiency convergence → perturbations too similar or mis‑scaled π.
- Wide credible intervals → insufficient queries or irrelevant perturbations.

**First 3 experiments**
1. Convergence diagnosis: plot D‑efficiency and CIG vs. perturbation budget *n* for EAGLE vs. BayesLIME.
2. Stability check: compute Jaccard similarity of top‑*k* features across multiple runs.
3. Ablation – prior λ: run with λ/*d* ∈ {0.1, 1, 10} and observe CCM trends.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does EAGLE's acquisition ensure λ_min(*S*ₜ) ≥ κ *t*?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Corollary 1 requires λ_min(*S*ₜ) ≥ κ *t* but provides no proof.
- **Why unresolved:** Sample complexity hinges on κ; lacking proof, the bound is conditional.
- **What evidence:** Prove λ_min(*S*ₜ)=Ω(*t*) for argmax_*z* π(*z*) *z*⊤*V**z*, or show a counterexample.

#### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can kernel bandwidth be adaptively tuned?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Fixed width 0.75√*d* used (page 10); no adaptation.
- **Why unresolved:** Fixed bandwidth may be suboptimal; adaptivity could improve.
- **What evidence:** Extend EAGLE to optimize bandwidth jointly, demonstrate query savings.

#### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does EAGLE perform under local nonlinearity, and can it use non‑linear surrogates?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Assumes linear surrogate (Sec. 3); experiments omit strong nonlinearity.
- **Why unresolved:** Nonlinearity may degrade linear approximation; theory not extended.
- **What evidence:** Benchmark on synthetic nonlinear black‑boxes; develop non‑linear Bayesian surrogates with bounds.

## Limitations
- Candidate pool distribution unspecified; Gaussian assumption may affect results.
- Ambiguity in observation weighting (precision vs sample weight) requires careful implementation.
- Evaluation protocols (repeat count, top‑*k*) not fully detailed.
- Theoretical assumptions (local linearity, bounded perturbations, spherical prior) may be violated in practice.
- Numerical stability in high dimensions needs ad‑hoc fixes (jitter).
- Hyperparameter sensitivity may demand dataset‑specific tuning.
