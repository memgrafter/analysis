# TerraFlow: Multimodal, Multitemporal Representation Learning for Earth Observation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12762
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12762
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** TerraFlow achieves state-of-the-art performance on GEO-Bench-2 temporal tasks and up to 50% higher F1 score in disaster risk prediction.

## Executive Summary
TerraFlow is a foundation model for Earth Observation that natively handles multimodal, multitemporal data. It extends the TerraMind model by introducing temporal attention through rotary positional embeddings and a Temporal Disjoint Sampling (TDS) pretraining strategy, which forces the model to reconstruct masked tokens from different timestamps, explicitly learning temporal dynamics. Evaluated on the GEO-Bench-2 benchmark, TerraFlow consistently outperforms prior foundation models across all temporal tasks and delivers up to a 50% improvement in F1 score and a 24% reduction in Brier score for disaster risk prediction. Remarkably, the compact 8M-parameter TerraFlow-Tiny matches or surpasses larger 100M-parameter baselines, demonstrating strong parameter efficiency.

## Method Summary
TerraFlow builds upon TerraMind via continuous pretraining on the SSL4EO-S12 v1.1 dataset, which includes Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2 (L1C/L2A), RGB, DEM, and land-cover modalities over ~10k cities with four timestamps (2019–2021). Two key modifications enable temporal reasoning: (i) rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) that encode the absolute time of each token, allowing attention to be conditioned on temporal distance; and (ii) Temporal Disjoint Sampling (TDS), which during pretraining randomly partitions the available timestamps into disjoint input and target sets. The model is trained with a masked multimodal token prediction objective (cross-entropy) using an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture. Downstream, features are extracted from multiple encoder layers and processed with a U-Net decoder following the GEO-Bench-2 protocol. Pretraining used AdamW with a cosine learning rate schedule (1.6e-4 for Base, 6.4e-4 for Tiny), bfloat16 precision, and a total budget of ~75B tokens.

## Key Results
- TerraFlow outperforms the previous state-of-the-art foundation models on all GEO-Bench-2 temporal tasks.
- On the ImpactMesh disaster risk prediction task, TerraFlow achieves up to 50% higher F1 score and 24% lower Brier score compared to baselines.
- The 8M-parameter TerraFlow-Tiny variant matches or exceeds the performance of larger 100M-parameter models, indicating high parameter efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Temporal Disjoint Sampling prevents the model from relying solely on spatial correlations by requiring reconstruction of masked tokens using a different timestamp than the input context. Together with rotary positional embeddings that inject precise temporal information, the model is compelled to integrate information across time. This explicit temporal modeling yields robust representations for tasks that depend on understanding temporal change, such as phenology, land cover dynamics, and risk forecasting.

## Foundational Learning
- **Masked multimodal token prediction** – Pretraining objective that randomly masks tokens across modalities and timestamps and trains the model to reconstruct them. *Why needed:* Enables learning joint representations without manual labels. *Quick check:* Compute reconstruction loss on a held-out set; it should decrease steadily during pretraining.
- **Rotary positional embeddings (RoPE)** – Encodes absolute position/time by rotating query/key vectors. *Why needed:* Provides the model with temporal ordering, crucial for multitemporal analysis. *Quick check:* Shuffle timestamps in a sample; performance should degrade if RoPE is functioning.
- **Temporal Disjoint Sampling (TDS)** – Splits timestamps into non-overlapping input and target subsets. *Why needed:* Forces the model to learn cross-temporal dependencies rather than memorizing single frames. *Quick check:* Compare pretraining loss curves between TDS and random sampling; TDS should show higher initial loss but lead to better downstream gains.
- **Multi-scale feature extraction** – Gathers representations from multiple encoder layers for downstream decoding. *Why needed:* Captures both fine-grained and high-level spatiotemporal patterns for dense prediction. *Quick check:* Visualize feature channels at different layers to confirm hierarchical information.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (multimodal multitemporal tokens) -> Token embedding -> Add Temporal RoPE -> Encoder (Transformer blocks) -> Latent representation -> (Pretraining) Decoder -> Masked token prediction; (Downstream) Feature pyramid (layers [2,5,8,11]) -> U-Net decoder -> Task head

**Critical path:**  
1. Acquire TerraMind checkpoints.  
2. Implement temporal RoPE and TDS.  
3. Continue pretraining on SSL4EO-S12 to reach ~75B tokens.  
4. Extract encoder features and attach U-Net for each downstream task; fine-tune with GEO-Bench‑2 protocol.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *TDS vs. Random Sampling:* TDS improves temporal reasoning but may slow convergence when temporal dynamics are weak.  
- *Attention complexity:* Quadratic in number of tokens; temporal length grows linearly, so memory scales quadratically with time steps.  
- *Model size:* TerraFlow‑Tiny (8M) offers similar performance to larger baselines due to effective pretraining, but may have lower capacity for very complex tasks.

**Failure signatures:**  
- TDS pretraining loss remains high and does not improve, with no downstream gains.  
- Disaster risk predictions are spatially homogeneous, suggesting the model ignores spatial variation.  
- Large discrepancy between reproduced results and GEO‑Bench‑2 leaderboard numbers, often due to data loading inconsistencies.

**First experiments:**  
1. **Baseline pretraining comparison:** Train TerraFlow‑Tiny with TDS and with random sampling; compare masked token reconstruction accuracy and convergence speed.  
2. **PASTIS fine‑tuning:** Follow GEO‑Bench‑2 protocol; record mIoU and verify it aligns with the paper’s value (within 2% absolute).  
3. **ImpactMesh evaluation:** Predict disaster risk maps using pre‑event imagery; compute F1 score and Brier score and confirm improvement over TerraMind.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What causes the performance discrepancies in TerraMind's results on DynamicEarthNet and PASTIS between the original GEO-Bench-2 leaderboard and controlled re-runs?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Supplementary Section D reports that re-running experiments under the same protocol yielded significantly different scores, and authors suspect "additional, unaccounted-for factors."
- **Why unresolved:** The inconsistencies undermine confidence in the benchmark's reliability and fair comparison across models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A fully transparent, community-wide re-evaluation of GEO-Bench-2 with verified data splits, code, and hyperparameters for all baselines.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why does TerraFlow produce spatially undifferentiated wildfire risk maps despite achieving reasonable quantitative metrics?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Page 10–11 and Fig. 4 state that wildfire predictions show "weak spatial differentiation" and are "substantially worse" qualitatively, likely due to unobserved weather/human factors.
- **Why unresolved:** The model may be learning spurious correlations or failing to capture key exogenous drivers not present in satellite imagery.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies incorporating weather or human activity data, or error analysis identifying specific contexts where predictions diverge from ground truth.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does TerraFlow's robustness to temporal shuffling indicate true understanding of temporal dynamics or merely learning of temporal invariants?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section C.1 reports marginal performance drops after shuffling timestamps, suggesting the model "captures temporal agreement" but may not model dynamics.
- **Why unresolved:** Shuffling robustness could mean the model relies on static features or aggregate statistics, not sequential dependencies critical for forecasting.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Performance on tasks requiring strict temporal causality after shuffling, or probing attention patterns for time-dependent relationships.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Under what downstream conditions does Temporal Disjoint Sampling (TDS) improve or degrade performance compared to random sampling (RS)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Table 1 shows TDS sometimes underperforms RS (e.g., TerraFlow‑Tiny‑TDS on Kuro Siwo), despite its design to enforce cross‑timestamp learning.
- **Why unresolved:** The trade-off between forcing temporal reasoning and preserving single‑timestamp context is not characterized; TDS may hurt when temporal dynamics are weak.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic study varying temporal gap between input/target timestamps and task type (e.g., phenology vs. sudden change), with analysis of failure cases.

## Limitations
- Exact source/version (URL/hash) of TerraMind 1.0 Base and Tiny checkpoints is unspecified, hindering exact continuity of pretraining.
- Per-band mean/std normalization statistics for SSL4EO‑S12 and GEO-Bench‑2 are not provided; reproduction must recompute them, risking minor shifts that could affect results.
- Pretraining hyperparameters beyond learning rate (e.g., batch size, masking ratio, number of optimization steps) are absent, limiting precise replication.
