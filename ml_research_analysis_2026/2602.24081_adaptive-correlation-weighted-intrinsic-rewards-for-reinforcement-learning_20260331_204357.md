# Adaptive Correlation-Weighted Intrinsic Rewards for Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.24081
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24081
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** ACWI adapts intrinsic reward scaling via a Beta Network trained on correlation with extrinsic returns, improving sample efficiency and stability in sparse-reward MiniGrid tasks.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning in sparse-reward environments often relies on manually tuned intrinsic rewards, where a fixed scaling coefficient can cause instability or inefficiency. This paper introduces Adaptive Correlation-Weighted Intrinsic rewards (ACWI), which learns a state-dependent scaling factor β(s) using a Beta Network. The network is optimized to maximize the correlation between the scaled intrinsic reward β(s)·I⁺ and the discounted future extrinsic return Gᴱ. On five MiniGrid tasks, ACWI demonstrates better sample efficiency and more stable learning than fixed-β baselines, especially in environments with intermittently informative extrinsic signals. In extremely sparse settings (e.g., Empty-16x16), adaptation is limited due to weak correlation signals, but training remains robust, effectively defaulting to a near-constant scale.

## Method Summary
ACWI augments a standard PPO+ICM setup with a Beta Network that predicts a per-state scaling factor β(s). The training loop alternates between: (1) collecting a rollout batch with the current policy; (2) updating the ICM module (forward and inverse models) on that batch; (3) computing normalized intrinsic rewards I⁺; (4) performing a single update of the Beta Network to maximize the correlation between β(s)·I⁺ and the standardized discounted extrinsic returns Gᴱ, with L2 regularization on log β; (5) forming augmented rewards r̄ = rᴱ + α·β(s)·I⁺; and (6) updating the policy with PPO using GAE. The Beta Network is a multi-layer perceptron; β is constrained to [0.1, 2] via clamped logits. The method introduces minimal computational overhead by updating β once per batch after ICM.

## Key Results
- ACWI achieves higher sample efficiency and more stable learning curves compared to fixed-β baselines on DoorKey-8x8, RedBlueDoors-8x8, and UnlockPickup.
- In the moderately sparse DoorKey-8x8, the learned β(s) adapts meaningfully, decreasing as the agent masters the task.
- In the extremely sparse Empty-16x16, β(s) shows little adaptation due to weak extrinsic signals, yet training remains stable, effectively behaving like a fixed coefficient.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Beta Network's correlation objective aligns the scaled intrinsic reward with the magnitude of future extrinsic returns. This encourages the intrinsic signal to be larger in states where the extrinsic return is predictable and larger, and smaller otherwise. By updating β after the ICM has been refreshed, the scaling adapts to the current quality of the intrinsic module. The per-state prediction allows the agent to modulate exploration intensity locally, avoiding over-exploration in irrelevant regions while maintaining curiosity where it matters. The bounded output and regularization prevent degenerate solutions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Intrinsic Motivation**: Self-generated rewards to drive exploration in reward-sparse MDPs. *Why needed?* External rewards are too delayed for credit assignment. *Quick check:* Does adding any intrinsic reward improve returns in an empty gridworld?
- **PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization)**: A stable policy gradient algorithm using clipped objectives. *Why needed?* Provides reliable policy updates amidst changing reward scales. *Quick check:* Does clipping the probability ratio prevent large policy jumps?
- **ICM (Intrinsic Curiosity Module)**: Uses prediction error of forward dynamics as intrinsic reward. *Why needed?* Generates state-novelty signal without assuming environment structure. *Quick check:* Does forward model loss decrease in frequently visited states?
- **Correlation-based Adaptation**: Learning β(s) to maximize correlation between β·I⁺ and Gᴱ. *Why needed?* Automatically tunes intrinsic scale to match extrinsic signal strength without manual coefficient search. *Quick check:* Is the batch-wise correlation between β·I⁺ and Gᴱ positive and stable during training?
- **Adaptive Exploration**: State-dependent modulation of intrinsic reward. *Why needed?* Fixed coefficients either over-explore (wasting samples) or under-explore (missing rewards). *Quick check:* Does β(s) vary across states in a meaningful spatial pattern?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Environment -> Observations (7x7x3) -> [Policy Network (CNN+FC), ICM (CNN encoder + forward/inverse heads), Beta Network (FC encoder + head)] -> β(s) * I⁺ -> Augmented reward -> PPO update. ICM update happens in parallel using same batch.

**Critical Path:**  
1. Rollout collection with current policy.  
2. ICM update (E epochs, minibatch 64).  
3. Compute normalized I⁺.  
4. Single Beta Network update on full batch (lr=5e-4).  
5. Form augmented reward r̄.  
6. PPO update (K=4 epochs, minibatch 64).

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Beta Network simplicity (3 FC layers) vs. capacity: Too shallow may fail to capture state-dependent patterns; too deep risks overfitting and instability.  
- Correlation objective vs. alternative adaptive criteria: Correlation is simple and directly links to returns but can be noisy on small batches and relies on variance in Gᴱ.  
- Fixed β bounds [0.1,2] vs. learned bounds: Bounds prevent extreme scaling but may clip optimal values; learned bounds add complexity.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- **Beta collapse**: Histogram shows mass at 0.1 or 2.0 → increase λ_reg or lower β learning rate.  
- **Intrinsic explosion**: α·β·I⁺ dominates extrinsic reward magnitude → reduce α or tighten β bounds.  
- **Correlation instability**: Per-batch correlation near zero or highly volatile → check ICM quality, increase batch size, or use a moving-target for Gᴱ.  
- **ICM degeneracy**: Raw intrinsic I_raw does not differ between novel and familiar states → increase ICM capacity or training epochs.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Train ACWI on DoorKey-8x8 and track β(s) distribution over time; expect β to decrease as task is solved.  
2. Ablate β bounds: test [0.01,5] vs. [0.1,2] on DoorKey-8x8 to assess sensitivity to upper bound.  
3. Replace ICM with Random Network Distillation (RND) and repeat; observe whether correlation objective still yields adaptation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Theoretical guarantees**: What convergence and stability guarantees exist for the correlation-based objective? The paper explicitly identifies this as future work; no formal analysis of loss landscape or gradient dynamics is provided.
2. **Generalization to other intrinsic modules**: How does ACWI perform with RND or RIDE instead of ICM? The paper only evaluates with ICM; different intrinsic signals have distinct statistics, so the correlation objective may need modification.
3. **Limits of adaptability under extreme sparsity**: Under what precise environmental conditions (reward frequency, horizon) does the correlation signal become too weak? The paper notes failure in Empty-16x16 but does not characterize the boundary or propose modifications for such regimes.
4. **Task structure and β distribution**: Which structural properties (hierarchies, bottlenecks) determine the qualitative patterns of β(s) (e.g., clustering, decay)? The paper shows visualizations but no causal analysis linking environment graph features to learned scaling patterns.
5. **Extension to multi-task/continual learning**: Can a single β_ψ balance exploration across multiple tasks? The paper lists this as a future direction; interference or the need for task conditioning is unknown.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (ICM loss coefficients α_F, α_I, exact network architectures, update frequencies) are not fully specified, blocking faithful replication.
- Evaluation is confined to discrete MiniGrid tasks; generalization to high-dimensional observations or continuous control is unestablished.
- Adaptation in extremely sparse environments relies on a correlation signal that may vanish; robustness beyond Empty-16x16 is unclear.

## Confidence
- **Adaptive scaling improves sample efficiency**: Medium (supported by benchmarks but hinges on unreported hyperparameters; risk of overfitting to specific task rewards).
- **Beta Network learns meaningful state-dependent scaling**: Low-Medium (correlation objective is plausible, but without full architectural details, learned β(s) may be artifacts).
- **Robustness in extremely sparse environments**: Low (claim based on one task; correlation signal weakness could destabilize training in other sparse settings).
- **Method generality beyond MiniGrid**: Very Low (no evidence in pixel-based or robotic tasks; scalability unknown).

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep**: Systematically vary α_F, α_I, β bounds, and β learning rate on DoorKey-8x8 to quantify impact on ACWI's advantage.
2. **Correlation signal validation**: Compute per-episode correlation between β·I⁺ and Gᴱ during training; verify that learned β(s) scales intrinsic rewards proportionally to actual extrinsic return magnitude.
3. **Generalization test**: Replicate on a distinct sparse-reward suite (e.g., ProcGen “climber” variants or sparse-Mujoco) to assess whether adaptive scaling transfers beyond discrete grid-worlds.
