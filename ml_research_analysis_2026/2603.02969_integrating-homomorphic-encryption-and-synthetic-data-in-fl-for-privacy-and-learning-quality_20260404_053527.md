# Integrating Homomorphic Encryption and Synthetic Data in FL for Privacy and Learning Quality

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02969
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02969
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Alt-FL improves model accuracy by 13.4% vs. Selective HE baseline, reduces HE-related overhead by up to 48%, and maintains robust privacy despite ~20% more convergence rounds.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the privacy-quality-cost trade-off in homomorphic encryption (HE)-based federated learning (FL). It proposes Alternating FL (Alt-FL), which interleaves local training rounds on authentic data (using HE-protected model uploads) with rounds on synthetic data (using plaintext uploads). A tunable interleaving ratio ρ controls the frequency of each round type. Results show Alt-FL significantly improves model accuracy and reduces HE overhead while preserving privacy against gradient inversion attacks, offering a practical compromise for secure and efficient FL.

## Method Summary
Alt-FL modifies standard FedAvg by introducing two round types determined by a schedulers. In authentic rounds (fraction 1−ρ), clients locally train on their authentic data and upload a selectively HE-encrypted model. In synthetic rounds (fraction ρ), clients train on client-specific synthetic data (disjoint from authentic data) and upload the plaintext model. The central server aggregates models without decryption for synthetic rounds and via secure aggregation for encrypted authentic rounds. The selective HE mask encrypts only a subset of sensitive parameters. The interleaving ratio ρ balances the frequency of costly HE operations against the convergence benefits of authentic data updates.

## Key Results
- Model accuracy improved by up to 13.4% compared to the Selective HE baseline.
- HE-related computational overhead reduced by up to 48%.
- Total transmitted ciphertext volume reduced by up to 39.1% despite requiring ~20% more communication rounds to converge.
- Privacy robustness maintained, with DLG attack resistance comparable to or better than Selective HE and the unencrypted baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Overhead Reduction via Interleaving
The core claim is that alternating authentic (HE-protected) and synthetic (plaintext) rounds reduces total HE computational and communication overhead without sacrificing privacy. The mechanism sets an interleaving ratio ρ, so HE operations occur only in the (1−ρ) fraction of authentic rounds. Synthetic rounds involve no encryption/decryption, scaling total HE cost linearly with (1−ρ). The assumption is that synthetic round models do not leak authentic data and that global convergence isn't catastrophically slowed. Evidence includes the reported 48% overhead reduction [abstract] and Algorithm 1's `isAuth` condition controlling encryption [section III]. The break condition is if synthetic data is statistically disjoint enough to cause negative transfer, requiring many more rounds and potentially offsetting savings.

### Mechanism 2: Accuracy Improvement from Synthetic Data Balancing
Integrating synthetic data into local training during synthetic rounds improves final model accuracy by mitigating non-IID data heterogeneity across clients. Synthetic data, generated to be statistically similar but disjoint from authentic data, augments each client's effective training distribution, leading to a more balanced and robust global model. The assumption is that synthetic data is high-fidelity and representative. Evidence includes the stated accuracy gains [abstract] and empirical results in Fig. 4 [section V]. The break condition is low synthetic data quality (e.g., mode collapse), which could introduce bias and degrade accuracy more than the balancing benefit.

### Mechanism 3: Privacy Preservation via Disjoint Synthetic Data and Selective HE
Privacy is maintained because (a) authentic rounds use selective HE to prevent gradient inversion from encrypted parameters, and (b) synthetic rounds use only synthetic data, so any leakage from plaintext models reveals no authentic information. The mechanism relies on synthetic datasets being perfectly disjoint from and statistically independent of authentic datasets. The selective HE mask obscures sensitive parameters during authentic round uploads. Evidence includes DLG attack similarity scores on synthetic rounds being lower than unencrypted baselines [Table II] and the paper's disjointness argument [section III]. The break condition is statistical overlap between synthetic and authentic data (e.g., from generator memorization) or an ineffective selective HE mask missing critical parameters.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Central Server <-> FL Clients (bidirectional communication). Synthetic Data Generator (offline) -> FL Clients (supplies disjoint synthetic datasets). HE Module integrated on FL Clients (encryption/decryption) and Central Server (secure aggregation). Interleaving Scheduler on each FL Client determines round type via `t mod ρ_tot < ρ_tot - ρ_syn`.

**Critical path:**
1. Central Server broadcasts global model.
2. FL Client receives model and decrypts if encrypted.
3. Interleaving Scheduler on client sets `isAuth` for current round t.
4. Client selects dataset: authentic `d_i,a` if `isAuth` true, synthetic `d_i,s` otherwise.
5. Client performs local training on selected dataset.
6. If `isAuth`: client applies selective HE mask M and sends encrypted model; else sends plaintext model.
7. Server collects models from participating clients.
8. Server aggregates models: secure aggregation for encrypted (authentic rounds), simple average for plaintext (synthetic rounds).
9. Server broadcasts new global model; loop repeats from step 1.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **ρ (interleaving ratio):** Higher ρ lowers HE cost and may improve accuracy via synthetic balancing but risks slower convergence or divergence if synthetic data is poor. Lower ρ increases HE cost but provides more authentic updates.
- **η (encryption ratio within authentic rounds):** Higher η improves privacy (stronger DLG resistance) but increases per-authentic-round cost.
- **Synthetic data fidelity:** Higher fidelity improves accuracy but may increase risk of statistical overlap with authentic data, harming privacy in synthetic rounds.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Accuracy degradation:** Final accuracy plateaus below Selective HE baseline; likely cause: synthetic data quality too low or ρ too high (negative transfer).
- **Convergence failure / divergence:** Training loss does not decrease or oscillates; likely cause: ρ too high, causing global model to drift from authentic distribution.
- **Privacy leakage in synthetic rounds:** DLG attack on synthetic round gradients recovers high-similarity authentic images; likely cause: synthetic data not sufficiently disjoint from authentic data.
- **Unexpectedly high HE cost:** Despite high ρ, encryption/decryption times not reduced as expected; likely cause: misunderstanding of mask application timing or aggregation overhead.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline Cost-Accuracy Curve:** Run Selective HE (ρ=0) and Alt-FL with ρ={0.25,0.5} across a range of encryption ratios η. Plot final accuracy vs. total transmitted ciphertext (MB) and total computation time (s) to validate the core trade-off claim.
2. **Convergence Dynamics & ρ Sensitivity:** Fix η=0.2, run Alt-FL with ρ={0,0.25,0.5,0.75}. Plot accuracy vs. communication round and record rounds to convergence. Check if accuracy gain outweighs extra rounds; test for divergence at high ρ.
3. **Privacy Validation via DLG Attack:** For ρ=0.5, η=0.2, perform DLG attacks on models from (a) an authentic round, (b) a synthetic round. Compare maximum MSSSIM/UQI scores to unencrypted baseline (ρ=0, η=0) and Selective HE baseline (ρ=0, η=0.2). Confirm synthetic round models leak no authentic data.

## Limitations
- Disjointness assumption between authentic and synthetic data is not formally verified; statistical overlap could harm privacy or cause negative transfer.
- Synthetic data quality and its impact on convergence are not stress-tested; low-fidelity data may degrade accuracy or require many more rounds.
- The cost model assumes linear scaling with interleaving ratio ρ, ignoring potential overhead from managing two model types and increased total rounds.
- Lack of reproducibility artifacts (code, hyperparameters) hinders independent validation.

## Confidence
- Accuracy improvement (13.4% vs S-HE): Medium (empirically shown but dependent on synthetic data quality and dataset specifics).
- Overhead reduction (48% HE cost, 39.1% ciphertext): High (directly measured under optimal ρ, but may not hold if convergence slows significantly).
- Privacy robustness (DLG resistance): Medium (DLG tests favorable but rely on perfect synthetic/authentic disjointness, which is unproven).
