# CV-18 NER: Augmented Common Voice for Named Entity Recognition from Arabic Speech

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.02209
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02209
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** End-to-end models substantially outperform cascaded pipeline systems on Arabic speech NER, with Whisper-medium achieving 38.0% CVER and AraBEST-RQ 300M achieving 37.0% CoER.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the under-explored task of Named Entity Recognition (NER) directly from Arabic speech. The authors introduce CV-18 NER, the first public dataset for this purpose, created by augmenting Common Voice 18 with 21 entity types following the Wojood schema. Through a comprehensive benchmarking of both cascaded (ASR+NER) and end-to-end (E2E) systems, they demonstrate that E2E models, which jointly transcribe speech and predict entity tags, significantly reduce error rates by avoiding ASR error propagation. Notably, smaller pretrained models (Whisper-medium, AraBEST-RQ 300M) adapt more effectively than their larger counterparts to this low-resource (8-hour) setting. The work also highlights that Arabic-specific self-supervised pretraining yields superior ASR foundations, while multilingual weak supervision (Whisper) transfers more effectively to the joint speech-to-entity learning objective.

## Method Summary
The study compares two main paradigms on the new CV-18 NER dataset: 1) a **cascaded pipeline** where an ASR model first transcribes speech and a separate NER model processes the transcript, and 2) an **end-to-end (E2E)** system where a single model is fine-tuned to directly generate a sequence containing both words and inline BIO-style entity tags from speech. The pipeline uses fine-tuned versions of Whisper and AraBEST-RQ for ASR, paired with fine-tuned Arabic BERT variants (AraBERTv2, AraBERTv0.2, CAMeLbert) for NER. The E2E approach extends the output vocabulary of the same Whisper (sequence-to-sequence) and AraBEST-RQ (CTC) models with special tokens for each of the 21 entity BIO tags and fine-tunes them on speech paired with these enriched transcript sequences. The primary evaluation metrics are Concept Error Rate (CoER) for entity labels and Concept-Value Error Rate (CVER) for combined type and span accuracy.

## Key Results
- End-to-end models substantially outperform the best pipeline configurations (CoER: 37.0% vs. 51.3%; CVER: 38.0% vs. 50.2%).
- Whisper-medium achieves the best CVER (38.0%), while AraBEST-RQ 300M achieves the best CoER (37.0%).
- Larger models (Whisper-large-v3, AraBEST-RQ 600M) fail to adapt in the low-resource 8-hour setting, showing near-random performance.
- AraBEST-RQ achieves lower WER than fine-tuned Whisper models, demonstrating the strength of Arabic-specific SSL pretraining.
- Performance remains poor for rare entity types (e.g., `LAW`, `PRODUCT`) due to severe dataset imbalance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** End-to-end (E2E) models reduce Named Entity Recognition (NER) error rates compared to cascaded pipeline systems by avoiding error propagation from Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR).
*   **Mechanism:** E2E training jointly optimizes acoustic-to-entity mapping, allowing the model to learn semantic patterns directly from speech features. Even if the generated transcription is lexically imperfect, the model can still leverage acoustic cues indicative of entity types (e.g., phonetic patterns for names or locations).
*   **Core assumption:** Entity-relevant acoustic features are sufficiently discriminative and can be aligned with entity tags without an intermediate textual representation.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "E2E systems substantially outperform the best pipeline configuration... reaching 37.0% CoER (AraBEST-RQ 300M) and 38.0% CVER (Whisper-medium)." and "eliminating the intermediate transcription stage reduces error propagation."
    *   [section 5.3] Direct comparison showing lower CoER (37.0 vs 51.3) and CVER (38.0 vs 50.2) for best E2E vs. pipeline.
    *   [corpus] Corpus signal shows related work on spoken NER and error propagation but no direct evidence for this specific dataset's mechanism.
*   **Break condition:** If acoustic features lack sufficient discriminative power for certain entity types (e.g., abstract concepts like `LAW`), the E2E advantage diminishes, as seen in poor performance on rare classes.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Arabic-specific self-supervised learning (SSL) pretraining (e.g., AraBEST-RQ) yields stronger ASR foundation models for low-resource Arabic than massively multilingual weakly supervised models (e.g., Whisper).
*   **Mechanism:** Pretraining on large volumes of diverse Arabic speech (6k–14k hours) with language-specific objectives (BEST-RQ) captures phonetic, morphological, and acoustic patterns unique to Arabic (e.g., consonant clusters, dialectal variation) without the noise of non-Arabic data in Whisper's training.
*   **Core assumption:** The SSL pretraining objective and data domain are aligned closely enough with the target speech (Modern Standard Arabic read speech) that fine-tuning requires less adaptation.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "Arabic-specific self-supervised pretraining yields strong ASR performance."
    *   [section 4.2.1] Table 3: AraBEST-RQ 300M 6k achieves 11.2% validation / 15.1% test WER, outperforming fine-tuned Whisper-medium (14.9% / 22.0%) and Whisper-large-v3 (31.8% / 32.3%).
    *   [section 5.2] "AraBEST-RQ demonstrates a more consistent improvement across both validation and test sets."
    *   [corpus] Corpus includes works on Arabic ASR and SSL (e.g., AraBEST-RQ paper citation) but no direct comparative evidence from this paper's corpus neighbors.
*   **Break condition:** If target speech diverges significantly from pretraining data (e.g., different dialects, noisy environments) or if the pretrained model's capacity is insufficient for the complexity of the downstream task.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** In low-resource settings (8 hours of training data), smaller pretrained models adapt more effectively to the joint speech-to-entity task than larger counterparts.
*   **Mechanism:** Larger models (Whisper-large-v3, AraBEST-RQ 600M) have higher capacity but may overfit or fail to converge when fine-tuned on very limited paired speech-entity data. Smaller models (Whisper-medium, AraBEST-RQ 300M) have a better capacity-data match, allowing more stable joint optimization of transcription and entity tagging.
*   **Core assumption:** The limited 8-hour training set is insufficient to adjust the large parameter space of bigger models without detrimental effects like catastrophic forgetting or poor gradient signal.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "larger models may be harder to adapt in this low-resource setting."
    *   [section 4.3] Table 6: Whisper-medium (20.7% WER, 38.0% CVER) vastly outperforms Whisper-large-v3 (41.9% WER, 59.1% CVER). AraBEST-RQ 300M works (16.0% WER, 37.0% CoER, 43.5% CVER), while 600M variants fail (near-random, e.g., 99.6% WER, 100% CVER).
    *   [section 5.2] "Whisper-large-v3 does not outperform Whisper-medium... attributed to over-parameterization relative to the limited 8-hour training subset."
    *   [corpus] Weak – corpus neighbors discuss data imbalance and model size but not this specific failure mode for Arabic speech NER.
*   **Break condition:** With more training data (e.g., 50+ hours), larger models may surpass smaller ones. The breakpoint depends on model architecture and data diversity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) Loss**
  - **Why needed here:** AraBEST-RQ models use CTC for sequence-to-sequence ASR training. Understanding CTC is crucial to grasp how the model aligns variable-length acoustic sequences with token sequences without explicit segmentation, a core part of the SSL pretraining and fine-tuning pipeline.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does CTC's blank token allow it to handle variable-length alignments between speech frames and output tokens, and how might this property affect learning entity tags that are longer than single words?
- **Concept: BIO Tagging Scheme**
  - **Why needed here:** The CV-18 NER dataset uses BIO markup (e.g., `B-PERS`, `I-PERS`) to annotate entity spans. The E2E models are trained to generate these tags as part of the transcript. This scheme is fundamental to how entity boundaries are represented and predicted.
  - **Quick check question:** If a model generates `B-PERS I-PERS O B-LOC` for the sequence "Ahmed visited Cairo", what are the recognized entities and their types, and what is a common error when converting this tag sequence to a span?
- **Concept: Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) Pretraining Objectives (e.g., BEST-RQ)**
  - **Why needed here:** AraBEST-RQ's strength comes from pretraining on unlabeled Arabic speech with a reconstruction-based objective. This builds a robust acoustic representation before any supervision. Understanding SSL helps explain why AraBEST-RQ achieves lower WER than Whisper when fine-tuned on limited Arabic data.
  - **Quick check question:** How does a contrastive or reconstruction SSL objective (like BEST-RQ) differ from Whisper's weakly supervised speech-text pretraining, and why might the former be more data-efficient for a specific language like Arabic?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  **Audio Frontend:** Raw waveform → 16kHz resampling → feature extraction (log-Mel spectrograms, likely).
  2.  **Encoder:** Two families:
      - *Whisper:* Transformer encoder processing log-Mel spectrogram frames.
      - *AraBEST-RQ:* Conformer-based encoder pretrained with BEST-RQ SSL objective.
  3.  **Decoder/Output Head:**
      - *Pipeline:* Separate text decoder (Whisper) or CTC decoder (AraBEST-RQ) for ASR, then independent text NER model (BERT-based) on transcript.
      - *E2E:* Same architectures, but decoder/head is modified to generate a unified output sequence containing both words and entity tags (e.g., `[speech] Ahmed B-PERS I-PERS went to B-GPE I-GPE`). Tokenizer is extended with special tokens for all entity tags (`<B-PERS>`, `<I-ORG>`, etc.).
  4.  **Loss Function:**
      - *ASR / Pipeline:* Cross-entropy (Whisper) or CTC loss (AraBEST-RQ).
      - *E2E:* Cross-entropy (Whisper) or CTC loss (AraBEST-RQ) over the extended vocabulary including entity tags.
- **Critical path (E2E training):**
  1. Load pretrained model (Whisper-medium or AraBEST-RQ 300M).
  2. Extend tokenizer vocabulary with 21 entity-type BIO tokens (from Wojood schema).
  3. Resize model's embedding layer to match new vocab size.
  4. Prepare training data: For each audio file, the target sequence is the reference transcript with inline BIO tags inserted (e.g., "Mohammed Ali" → "Mohammed B-PERS Ali I-PERS").
  5. Train model (fine-tune) to map audio features to this enriched token sequence.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Pipeline vs. E2E:** Pipeline allows using SOTA ASR and NER models independently but suffers from error propagation. E2E requires a single model capable of both tasks and a modified tokenizer but reduces cascading errors.
  - **Model family:** Whisper (multilingual, weakly supervised) vs. AraBEST-RQ (Arabic-specific, SSL). Trade-off: Whisper has broader linguistic knowledge but may include irrelevant patterns; AraBEST-RQ is linguistically focused but may lack diversity.
  - **Model size:** Smaller (300M/medium) vs. larger (600M/large). Trade-off: Capacity vs. data efficiency. Larger models risk instability on 8-hour fine-tuning.
  - **Vocabulary extension:** Adding entity tags increases output space. Simpler for CTC (character-level in AraBEST-RQ) vs. subword-based (Whisper) where tags must be special tokens.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Large model failure (AraBEST-RQ 600M, Whisper-large-v3):** Divergent training (WER ~99%), random predictions. Sign of over-parameterization for available data.
  - **Poor rare-class performance:** CoER near 100% for `LAW`, `PRODUCT`, `WEBSITE`. Indicates insufficient training examples (<10 in train set) for acoustic-semantic mapping.
  - **High CVER but moderate CoER:** Model gets entity *type* right but span boundaries wrong (substitutions/deletions in entity words), or vice-versa.
  - **Whisper-large-v3 language switch:** Generating English translations or Latin transliterations instead of Arabic, a known issue in multilingual models.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Replicate best pipeline:** Using fine-tuned Whisper-medium ASR + AraBERTv0.2 NER, confirm ~51.3% CoER / 50.2% CVER on test set. Validate data loading and metric computation.
  2.  **Validate E2E entity token injection:** Before full training, run a forward pass on a few samples with the extended Whisper-medium tokenizer to ensure the model can generate sequences containing `<B-PERS>` etc. without crashing. Check that loss decreases on a tiny subset.
  3.  **Ablate model size for E2E:** Train Whisper-medium and Whisper-large-v3 for 1 epoch on the 8-hour train set. Expect Whisper-medium to show clear WER/CoER improvement over zero-shot, while large model shows minimal or negative gain. This confirms the capacity-data mismatch.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Why does the larger Whisper-large-v3 model produce English outputs or transliterations during Arabic ASR fine-tuning, and how can this instability be mitigated?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] "...preliminary analysis of its outputs indicates that Whisper-large-v3 sometimes produces English translations instead of Arabic transcriptions or transliterations into Latin script, which warrants further investigation."  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper notes this behavior but does not diagnose its cause (e.g., tokenizer issues, pretraining data bias, optimization instability).  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments isolating factors like learning rate schedules, tokenizer alignment, and pretraining language distribution; error analysis comparing Whisper-medium vs. large-v3 activation patterns on Arabic phonemes.

- **Open Question 2:** What specific architectural or optimization factors cause the larger AraBEST-RQ 600M variants to fail (near-random predictions) in end-to-end NER fine-tuning, while the 300M variant succeeds?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] "AraBEST-RQ 600M models failed to produce correct outputs in E2E fine-tuning, leading to near-random predictions. However, AraBEST-RQ 300M outperforms all other systems..." and inferred from "this may be attributed to over-parameterization relative to the limited 8-hour training subset."  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper hypothesizes over-parameterization but does not test convergence dynamics, gradient flow, or capacity mismatches between the CTC-based pretraining and the sequence-to-sequence entity tagging objective.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Training curve analyses (loss, accuracy) comparing 300M vs. 600M; ablation on fine-tuning data scale; probing whether the 600M model's representations fail to align entity tags with acoustic features.

- **Open Question 3:** How can the severe performance degradation on low-frequency entity types (e.g., LAW, PRODUCT, QUANTITY) in both pipeline and E2E systems be addressed given the long-tailed distribution of CV-18 NER?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] "Rare classes remain challenging for both paradigms...models are unable to learn stable acoustic-semantic mappings" and the dataset tables show near-zero counts for some types.  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper identifies the imbalance as the "primary bottleneck" but does not evaluate remediation strategies like data augmentation, loss re-weighting, or hierarchical modeling.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Experiments with oversampling/undersampling, focal loss, or few-shot learning techniques on the rarest classes; analysis of whether acoustic signatures of rare entities are distinguishable from contextual clues.

- **Open Question 4:** Why does multilingual weak supervision (Whisper) transfer more effectively to joint speech-to-entity learning than Arabic-specific self-supervised pretraining (AraBEST-RQ), despite AraBEST-RQ's superior ASR performance?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] "multilingual weak supervision transfers more effectively to joint speech-to-entity learning" and Whisper-medium achieves lower CVER (38.0) than AraBEST-RQ 300M (43.5) in E2E, even though AraBEST-RQ has lower WER.  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper suggests semantic robustness but does not dissect whether Whisper's pretraining on diverse languages yields better entity-discriminative features or if its sequence-to-sequence architecture better handles tag injection.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Representation similarity analysis (e.g., CCA) between Whisper and AraBEST embeddings for entity vs. non-entity spans; ablation of pretraining data diversity; testing if Whisper's decoder inherently better models tag sequences.

- **Open Question 5:** To what extent does the single-annotator CV-18 NER annotation pipeline introduce systematic bias, and how does this bias affect model evaluation reliability?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] "relying on a single annotator can introduce bias and aim to mitigate this limitation when additional annotations can be afforded" and no inter-annotator agreement is reported.  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper acknowledges the limitation but cannot quantify its impact without multi-annotator data; potential bias may skew performance estimates, especially for nuanced Wojood tags.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Re-annotation of a subset by multiple annotators to compute agreement (e.g., Cohen's κ); error analysis comparing model mistakes to annotator disagreement points; sensitivity analysis of model performance on high- vs. low-disagreement segments.

## Limitations
- The exact implementation details for modifying the Whisper decoder to generate inline entity tags (e.g., tokenization strategy, alignment) are unspecified, hindering faithful replication.
- The catastrophic failure of larger models (AraBEST-RQ 600M, Whisper-large-v3) is demonstrated but not deeply diagnosed; it is unclear if the cause is purely data insufficiency or architectural/optimization incompatibility.
- The dataset is derived from Common Voice (read speech, MSA). Performance on spontaneous, dialectal, or noisy Arabic speech is unknown. Heavy underrepresentation of 7/21 entity types severely limits conclusions about those categories.
