# CoE: Collaborative Entropy for Uncertainty Quantification in Agentic Multi-LLM Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28360
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28360
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CoE improves uncertainty quantification in multi-LLM systems, achieving AUROC up to 0.772 on TriviaQA and a +39% accuracy gain via a coordination heuristic.

## Executive Summary
Multi-LLM systems suffer from a measurement gap: existing uncertainty quantification methods are single-model-centric and fail to capture cross-model semantic disagreement. This paper introduces Collaborative Entropy (CoE), a unified information-theoretic metric for system-level semantic uncertainty. CoE operates on a shared semantic cluster space, combining average intra-model semantic entropy with weighted inter-model KL divergence to the ensemble mean. Theoretical analysis confirms CoE’s non-negativity, zero-value certainty under perfect consensus, and distinct behavior when models are individually certain yet disagree. A lightweight CoE-guided coordination heuristic adjusts model weights at inference time without training. Experiments on TriviaQA and SQuAD with three instruction-tuned LLMs (LLaMA, Qwen, Mistral) show CoE outperforms entropy- and divergence-based baselines in AUROC and AURAC, with gains scaling with ensemble heterogeneity. On TriviaQA, CoE achieves AUROC 0.683 (two models) and 0.772 (three models), surpassing Semantic Entropy by up to 0.085. The coordination heuristic yields a +39% accuracy improvement.

## Method Summary
CoE operates by having each LLM generate multiple candidate responses, which are clustered into semantic groups via bidirectional entailment to create a shared discrete meaning space. For each model, the cluster probability distribution is estimated from generation probabilities. CoE then sums two components: (1) average semantic entropy across models (intra-model aleatoric uncertainty) and (2) weighted sum of asymmetric KL divergences from each model’s cluster distribution to the ensemble mean (inter-model epistemic divergence). The ensemble mean is a weighted aggregate of individual distributions. Optionally, a coordination heuristic iteratively reweights models to minimize CoE, reducing system uncertainty without training. The method is model-agnostic and inference-time.

## Key Results
- CoE achieves AUROC 0.683 (two-model ensemble) and 0.772 (three-model ensemble) on TriviaQA, surpassing Semantic Entropy by up to 0.085.
- The coordination heuristic improves accuracy by +39% on TriviaQA.
- Performance gains scale positively with ensemble heterogeneity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** System-level uncertainty in multi-LLM collaboration can be decomposed into two interpretable components: average intra-model aleatoric uncertainty and weighted inter-model epistemic divergence.
- **Mechanism:** CoE computes the intra-model component (U_A) as the mean of each model’s semantic entropy over the shared cluster space, and the inter-model component (U_E) as the weighted sum of KL divergences from each model’s cluster distribution to the weighted ensemble mean.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic clustering via bidirectional entailment produces a consistent discrete meaning space across all models, allowing their probability distributions to be compared directly.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "CoE is defined on a shared semantic cluster space and combines two components: intra-model semantic entropy and inter-model divergence to the ensemble mean."
  - [section 3.3]: Formal equations (3) and (4) defining U_A and U_E, with U_CoE as their sum.
  - [corpus]: Corpus neighbors show related work on semantic entropy (e.g., "Kernel Language Entropy") but have 0 citations, indicating limited direct validation of this decomposition in multi-LLM settings.
- **Break condition:** If semantic clustering fails to align meanings across models (e.g., due to model-specific phrasing styles or entailment model bias), the cluster distributions become incomparable, causing both U_A and U_E to reflect artefactual disagreement rather than true semantic uncertainty.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Operating on a shared semantic cluster space, rather than on token sequences or model-specific representations, enables system-level uncertainty quantification that is invariant to surface-form variation.
- **Mechanism:** Each model’s sampled responses are partitioned into semantic clusters using bidirectional entailment. The resulting cluster probability distribution (summing generation probabilities of all sequences within each cluster) serves as the model’s semantic output representation. These distributions are aggregated across models to compute CoE.
- **Core assumption:** Bidirectional entailment is a reliable proxy for semantic equivalence across diverse LLM outputs, even when generated by models with different vocabularies and phrasing tendencies.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2]: Describes semantic entropy (SE) as operating on clusters formed by bidirectional entailment, which CoE inherits.
  - [section 3.3]: Explicitly states CoE is "defined on a shared semantic cluster space."
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "Kernel Language Entropy" also use semantic similarity, but no paper directly validates cross-model clustering reliability; this remains an active research challenge.
- **Break condition:** If entailment models systematically disagree on what constitutes mutual entailment (e.g., on nuanced or ambiguous answers), cluster assignments become model- or prompt-dependent, undermining the comparability required for CoE.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Asymmetric KL divergence (D_KL(p_i || ¯p)) is critical for capturing directional epistemic disagreement: it quantifies how far an individual model’s semantic distribution deviates from the ensemble consensus, treating consensus as the reference.
- **Mechanism:** For each model i, compute D_KL(p_i(c|x) || ¯p(c|x)), where ¯p is the weighted ensemble mean. This penalizes models that assign high probability to clusters underrepresented in the consensus, while models that align with consensus incur low penalty.
- **Core assumption:** The ensemble mean distribution ¯p accurately reflects the system’s collective belief and is a meaningful reference point for measuring individual model divergence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3]: "In our primary formulation we use the asymmetric KL divergence...whose reference-directed asymmetry is critical for capturing directional epistemic disagreement."
  - [section 5.2]: Experimentally, KL-based CoE outperforms symmetric alternatives (JS, Wasserstein, Hellinger) in AUROC and accuracy gain (+39.0% vs. max +27.5% for JS).
  - [corpus]: No neighbor directly compares divergence measures in multi-LLM UQ; evidence is internal to this paper.
- **Break condition:** If the ensemble mean is distorted by outlier models (e.g., due to poor calibration or malicious inputs), D_KL may misattribute disagreement: a model agreeing with ground truth but disagreeing with a biased consensus could be penalized incorrectly.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Semantic Clustering (Bidirectional Entailment)**
  - *Why needed here:* It defines the common discrete space over which all model distributions are compared; without reliable clustering, CoE’s components cannot be computed meaningfully.
  - *Quick check question:* How would you determine if "Paris is France's capital" and "The capital of France is Paris" belong in the same semantic cluster for CoE?
- **Concept: Shannon Entropy & Semantic Entropy**
  - *Why needed here:* U_A is the average semantic entropy; understanding SE (entropy over semantic clusters) is prerequisite to grasping intra-model uncertainty.
  - *Quick check question:* What does SE = 0 tell you about a model’s sampled responses for a given input?
- **Concept: KL Divergence (Asymmetric)**
  - *Why needed here:* U_E relies on KL divergence to measure how each model’s cluster distribution differs from the ensemble mean; asymmetry is key to directional disagreement.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might D_JS (symmetric) fail to capture the same insight as D_KL in the CoE context?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Local Inference & Sampling:** Each LLM generates *m* candidate responses.
  2. **Semantic Clustering:** Responses partitioned into clusters via bidirectional entailment.
  3. **Distribution Estimation:** Compute p_i(c|x) from cluster assignments and generation probabilities.
  4. **Aggregation:** Compute U_A = mean(SE_i), U_E = Σ w_i D_KL(p_i || ¯p), U_CoE = U_A + U_E.
  5. **Optional Coordination Heuristic:** Iterative reweighting to minimize U_CoE.
- **Critical path:** Response generation (slowest) → Clustering → Distribution computation → CoE calculation. The heuristic adds an iterative loop (recompute distributions, update weights, recompute CoE) until convergence.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Sample count (m):* More samples improve cluster stability but increase cost.
  - *Divergence choice:* KL is recommended for directional sensitivity but unbounded; JS is bounded but may underweight disagreement.
  - *Heuristic depth:* More iterations may lower CoE further but with diminishing returns; convergence threshold (ϵ) trades precision for speed.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High U_E with low U_A: Models are individually confident but semantically inconsistent (Quadrant II).
  - High U_A with low U_E: Models broadly agree on a distribution but none is decisive (Quadrant IV).
  - Low U_CoE but incorrect answer: Systemic failure where all models agree on a wrong cluster (theoretical zero CoE possible on error).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline comparison:** Evaluate CoE vs. averaged SE, regular entropy, P_false on two- and three-model ensembles (Table 1, Figures 3–4).
  2. **Divergence ablation:** Test KL, JS, Wasserstein, Hellinger instantiations of CoE on TriviaQA (Figure 7).
  3. **Coordination heuristic:** Measure accuracy gain and residual CoE after applying the heuristic under different divergence measures (Section 5.2, Appendix B.2).

## Limitations
- Reliance on bidirectional entailment for semantic clustering is susceptible to entailment model bias, especially on nuanced answers.
- The ensemble mean distribution used in KL divergence may be distorted by outlier models, misattributing disagreement.
- Evaluations are narrow (three instruction-tuned LLMs, two QA datasets); generalizability to other models, tasks, and languages is unproven, and no independent reproduction exists.

## Confidence
- Theoretical properties (non-negativity, zero under consensus): High
- Empirical superiority over baselines (AUROC/AURAC gains): Medium
- Semantic clustering reliability across diverse models: Low

## Next Checks
1. Stress-test semantic clustering with multiple off-the-shelf entailment models on a diverse set of ambiguous/paraphrased answers to quantify cluster assignment stability and its impact on U_E.
2. Evaluate CoE calibration and error-detection AUROC on adversarial/out-of-distribution data to check if high U_CoE correlates with true errors.
