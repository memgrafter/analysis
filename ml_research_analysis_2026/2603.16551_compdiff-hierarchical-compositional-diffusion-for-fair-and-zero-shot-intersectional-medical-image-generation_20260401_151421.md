# CompDiff: Hierarchical Compositional Diffusion for Fair and Zero-Shot Intersectional Medical Image Generation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16551
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16551
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** CompDiff improves FID and equity metrics on rare/unseen demographic intersections by factorizing demographic conditioning into a hierarchical network, enabling zero-shot compositional generalization.

## Executive Summary
Medical image generators often fail for rare demographic subgroups and unseen intersections due to imbalanced training data. CompDiff introduces a Hierarchical Conditioner Network (HCN) that explicitly models demographic attributes (age, sex, race) as a compositional hierarchy of single-attribute embeddings, pairwise interactions, and a final composed representation. This structure allows the model to recombine learned components to generate images for combinations not seen during training. Integrated with a diffusion model and auxiliary supervision on the projected conditioning token, CompDiff achieves better image quality (FID) and fairness (ES-FID) compared to prior approaches, and improves downstream classifier fairness when trained on its synthetic data.

## Method Summary
CompDiff augments a diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 2.1) with a Hierarchical Conditioner Network (HCN) to process demographic attributes. HCN first embeds each attribute (age, sex, race) separately. It then computes pairwise interaction vectors via MLPs on concatenated attribute embeddings. A composition MLP combines these pairwise vectors into a unified demographic representation `h_demo`. This representation is passed through a variational layer (Gaussian `µ, σ`) and projected to a context token `c`. An auxiliary classification loss is applied directly on `c` to preserve demographic information at the conditioning interface. The token `c` is concatenated with CLIP text embeddings and fed to the diffusion UNet via cross-attention. Total loss combines diffusion, KL divergence, a composition regularization term (`L_comp`) encouraging additive structure, and the auxiliary loss.

## Key Results
- CompDiff achieves lower FID (64.3) than FairDiffusion (75.1) on MIMIC-CXR and up to 21% better FID on held-out intersectional subgroups.
- It yields lower equity-scaled FID (ES-FID), indicating improved fairness across demographic intersections.
- Classifiers trained on CompDiff's synthetic data attain higher AUROC and reduced demographic bias compared to those trained on data from other generators.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*Claim:* The Hierarchical Conditioner Network (HCN) enables zero-shot generalization to unseen demographic intersections by factorizing demographic conditioning into a compositional hierarchy.
*Mechanism:* HCN decomposes demographic attributes into learnable single-attribute embeddings, pairwise interaction vectors, and a final composed representation. This allows constructing representations for rare or unseen combinations by recombining components from more frequent subgroups, without requiring direct training examples for every intersection.
*Core assumption:* Demographic identity is compositional; intersections can be effectively modeled as combinations of constituent attribute representations.
*Break condition:* If pairwise interactions are mislearned due to data sparsity or if attribute interactions are non-compositional (synergistic effects not captured by pairwise sums), recombination may fail.

### Mechanism 2
*Claim:* The hierarchical factorization encourages parameter sharing across subgroups, improving data efficiency for rare intersections without sacrificing performance on common ones.
*Mechanism:* By sharing single-attribute embedding layers and using pairwise MLPs operating on concatenated embeddings, the model reuses parameters across all combinations. This reduces the number of unique parameters needed per intersection, allowing limited samples of rare subgroups to benefit from statistics of more common attribute values.
*Core assumption:* Parameter sharing across the hierarchy is beneficial and does not cause detrimental interference between dissimilar subgroups.
*Break condition:* If subgroups are highly dissimilar (e.g., pathological manifestations differ fundamentally), forced sharing may harm expressivity, leading to poorer FID for some subgroups.

### Mechanism 3
*Claim:* Auxiliary classification applied directly to the projected demographic token `c` ensures demographic information survives projection into the cross-attention space, preserving controllability.
*Mechanism:* After HCN produces `h_demo`, it is mapped to a latent `z` via a diagonal Gaussian, then projected to `c`. The auxiliary loss `L_aux` is applied on `c` using classifiers for age, sex, race. This forces `c` to remain demographically informative at the interface where it conditions diffusion, rather than only preserving information in the pre-projection latent `µ`.
*Core assumption:* Demographic information can be preserved through a projection layer if directly supervised at the output; gradients from diffusion loss alone are insufficient.
*Break condition:* If the projection dimension `d_ctx` is too small or auxiliary loss weights (`λ_aux`) are misconfigured, `c` may become a bottleneck, degrading either demographic fidelity or generative quality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Compositionality in Representation Learning**  
  *Why needed:* CompDiff’s core innovation relies on representing demographic intersections as compositions of simpler attribute units. Without grasping compositionality (building complex concepts from known parts), zero-shot generalization is meaningless.  
  *Quick check:* If a model has seen “elderly”, “Asian”, and “female” separately, should it generate a plausible “elderly Asian female” without having seen that exact combination? Why or why not?
- **Concept: Variational Autoencoding (VAE) for Structured Latents**  
  *Why needed:* HCN uses a diagonal Gaussian (`N(µ, σ²I)`) with a KL loss (`L_KLL`). Understanding VAEs is key to grasping why this stochastic latent might aid generalization and regularization.  
  *Quick check:* What is the purpose of the KL divergence term in HCN’s loss? How might it prevent overfitting to rare subgroups?
- **Concept: Cross-Attention Conditioning in Diffusion Models**  
  *Why needed:* The final `c` token is concatenated with CLIP text embeddings and fed as context to the diffusion UNet’s cross-attention layers. Understanding this mechanism explains how demographic information influences image synthesis.  
  *Quick check:* If `c` were replaced with a random vector, what would happen to generated images’ demographic attributes? What does this imply about the role of `c`?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Demographic Inputs (age, sex, race) -> Single-Attribute Encoder (embedding layers `e_a, e_s, e_r`) -> Pairwise Interaction Module (MLPs `f_a,s`, `f_a,r`, `f_s,r` producing `h_a,s`, etc.) -> Composition MLP (`g`) producing `h_demo` -> Latent Projection (linear to `µ, logσ`) -> Sample `z` (train) or use `µ` (inference) -> Context Projection (`proj_ctx(z)`) -> Demographic token `c` -> Concatenation with CLIP `E_text` -> `E_combined` -> Diffusion UNet cross-attention -> Image.
- **Critical path:** Demographic attributes -> (1-4) -> `h_demo` -> (5) `z` -> (6) `c` -> (7-8) `E_combined` -> UNet cross-attention -> image.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Hierarchy depth: Paper restricts to pairwise (not higher-order) to balance expressivity and overfitting on rare data.
  - Auxiliary supervision placement: Applied on `c` (post-projection) rather than `µ` (pre-projection) to supervise the actual conditioning signal.
  - `L_comp` weight (`λ_comp`): Tuned (default 0.1) to regularize toward additive composition without forcing it; too high harms FID.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Demographic accuracy drops (e.g., sex/race < 0.8): Check auxiliary loss is active and applied to `c`; verify `c` dimension compatibility; ensure demographic embeddings are trainable.
  - Poor FID on rare intersections: Verify HCN is used (not baseline text encoding); check if pairwise MLPs are properly initialized; consider if `λ_comp` is too low (no regularization) or too high (over-constrains).
  - Degraded text-clinical alignment (low disease AUROC): The `c` token may be crowding CLIP’s token budget; inspect if `E_combined` length (78) exceeds model’s expected context; try reducing `d_node`/`d_ctx` to match backbone.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation: Hierarchy vs. Flat. Implement a flat MLP encoder that directly maps concatenated attribute embeddings to `h_demo`. Compare demographic accuracy and FID against HCN. Expect HCN to win on accuracy for rare subgroups.
  2. Zero-shot generalization test. Hold out 2–3 rarest intersectional subgroups entirely from training. Generate images conditioned on these unseen prompts. Compare FID against FairDiffusion and baseline; CompDiff should show relative improvement.
  3. Auxiliary loss placement test. Train two HCN variants: one with `L_aux` on `c` (correct), one on `µ`. Evaluate demographic accuracy and downstream utility (classifier TSTR AUROC). The `c`-supervised version should maintain higher accuracy and better fairness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Do more sophisticated structured representations (e.g., graph-based interaction modeling) outperform the current pairwise MLP hierarchy in HCN for zero-shot intersectional generalization and fairness?
- **Open Question 2:** How does fairness evaluation grounded in clinical expert assessment (vs. automated metrics like ES-FID) change the perceived fairness and utility of CompDiff-generated datasets?
- **Open Question 3:** Can the hierarchical compositional framework be extended to handle continuous (e.g., age as a number) or unstructured demographic attributes (e.g., socioeconomic status inferred from zip code)?
- **Open Question 4:** Why do representation-level solutions (like HCN) still fail to fully eliminate the performance gap between well-represented and rare subgroups, even with improved zero-shot generalization?

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, optimizer, λ_KL, λ_aux) hinder exact replication.
- Ambiguity in `L_comp` implementation and token concatenation with CLIP context may affect results.
- Claims based on only two datasets; generalizability to other modalities unproven.
- Compositionality assumption may fail for non-additive intersectional effects.
- Absolute FID >60 indicates limited image fidelity; equity metric sensitivity not assessed.
- Auxiliary loss on `c` critical but its interaction with diffusion loss remains unclear.

## Confidence
- **Zero-shot compositional generalization:** Medium (Evidence from held-out intersections, but dataset scope and compositionality assumption limit confidence.)
- **Equity and quality improvements:** High (Consistent gains across metrics and datasets; external validation still needed.)
- **HCN design (hierarchy and aux supervision):** Medium (Ablations support necessity, but choices may be dataset-specific.)

## Next Checks

3. **Pairwise interaction ablation:** Remove pairwise MLPs; measure changes in zero-shot FID and demographic accuracy to isolate their contribution.
