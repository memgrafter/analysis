# Learning to Generate Formally Verifiable Step-by-Step Logic Reasoning via Structured Formal Intermediaries

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29500  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29500  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PRoSFI enables a 7B model to achieve ~93% answer accuracy and ~76% reasoning soundness on ProverQA-Hard, with reward hit rate strongly correlated to soundness (0.71), and supports test-time scaling via DTV.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a key limitation in outcome-rewarded reinforcement learning for LLMs: high final-answer accuracy often coexists with unsound or invalid intermediate reasoning. Directly generating formal proofs is infeasible for modest-sized models (e.g., 7B parameters). The proposed **PRoSFI** (Process Reward over Structured Formal Intermediates) method decouples the tasks by having the model output structured, machine-readable reasoning steps (in JSON format with formal conclusions, rules, and dependencies) alongside its natural language reasoning. These steps are verified by external formal provers (e.g., Lean, Prover9). Only chains where *all* steps are formally valid receive full reward during RL training. This process-oriented shaping yields significantly higher reasoning soundness on logical QA benchmarks while maintaining strong answer accuracy, and the structured intermediates enable effective test-time scaling via "Don't Trust; Verify" (DTV).

## Method Summary
PRoSFI operates in two phases. During training, a policy model (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) is prompted to generate a response containing both a natural language reasoning trace and a `<summary>` block with a JSON array of atomic inference steps. Each step includes a `conclusion` (a first-order logic formula), a `rule` (e.g., Modus Ponens), and `dependencies` (IDs of prior steps). After generation, an extraction module parses this JSON. A verification module then invokes a formal prover for each `conclusion` to check its validity from the specified `dependencies`. A GRPO-based reward function assigns: R=1.0 only if the final answer is correct **and** every step verifies; R=0.3 for correct answers with some flawed steps; R=0.1 for correct format but wrong answer; R=0.0 otherwise. The RL algorithm updates the policy based on these rewards, disentangling the generation of verifiable intermediates from the construction of an end-to-end formal proof. At test time, multiple reasoning chains are sampled; any chain whose structured intermediates fully verify is selected, otherwise majority voting decides.

## Key Results
- On ProverQA-Hard, PRoSFI (Qwen2.5-7B) achieves **92.97% answer accuracy** and **76.07% GPT-Soundness**, drastically improving soundness over an outcome-CoT baseline (21.97% soundness, 91.31% accuracy).
- The **Reward Hit Rate** (fraction of fully verified chains during training) correlates at **0.71** with final soundness, far outperforming answer accuracy's correlation (0.47).
- Test-time scaling with DTV (sampling N=16 paths) further increases soundness for PRoSFI, while majority voting fails to improve outcome-CoT. Generalization to Knights&Knaves shows soundness gains of 21–35% across difficulties.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Structured formal intermediates decouple the generation of machine-verifiable steps from full formal proof construction, making stepwise verification accessible to modest-sized LLMs.
- **Mechanism:** The model outputs JSON/YAML blocks containing atomic inference steps (id, dependencies, formal conclusion, rule) aligned with its natural language reasoning. These are parsed and verified individually by external formal provers (e.g., Lean, Prover9), avoiding the need for the model to generate syntactically complete, tactic-rich formal proofs.
- **Core assumption:** 7B-scale models can reliably generate semi-structured JSON with formal propositions even if they cannot produce compilable formal proof scripts.
- **Break condition:** If the model cannot consistently produce parsable JSON with correct FOL expressions, the verification pipeline fails and rewards become noisy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Reward shaping based on full chain verification aligns RL training with reasoning soundness, not just final answer correctness.
- **Mechanism:** A GRPO-based reward function assigns R=1 only when the final answer is correct *and* all intermediate structured steps pass formal verification. Partial rewards exist for correct answers with flawed steps (R=0.3) or format-only success (R=0.1). This provides dense, binary validation feedback at the process level.
- **Core assumption:** Formal provers can reliably judge the logical validity of individual extracted propositions, and a fully verified chain implies a sound overall argument.
- **Break condition:** If the formal prover is incomplete or the extracted propositions do not fully cover the reasoning path, a high reward may not guarantee true soundness.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Verification-ready intermediates enable effective test-time scaling via "Don't Trust; Verify" (DTV) selection, unlike natural language-only CoT.
- **Mechanism:** At inference, multiple reasoning paths are sampled; any path whose structured intermediates pass formal verification is selected, otherwise majority voting is used. The structured format allows reliable automated checking of each path's logical validity.
- **Core assumption:** Formal verification can be performed efficiently at test time and provides a reliable filter for sound reasoning paths.
- **Break condition:** If verification is too slow or brittle, test-time scaling gains are offset by computational cost or low verification pass rates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**  
  *Why needed:* The training algorithm optimizes the policy with the PRoSFI reward signal without a separate value model.  
  *Quick check:* How does GRPO's group-relative advantage computation differ from standard PPO's clipped surrogate objective, and why is this helpful for RL with rule-based rewards?
- **Concept: First-Order Logic (FOL) Formalization**  
  *Why needed:* Structured intermediates express conclusions in FOL (Lean4 syntax) so formal provers can verify them.  
  *Quick check:* Given premises `h1: P → Q` and `h2: P`, what FOL conclusion and rule (e.g., Modus Ponens) would a valid structured step produce?
- **Concept: Process Reward vs. Outcome Reward**  
  *Why needed:* PRoSFI's core innovation shifts from rewarding only the final answer to rewarding a verified reasoning process.  
  *Quick check:* Why might an outcome reward lead to high answer accuracy but low reasoning soundness, and how does a process reward mitigate this?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Prompt -> Generation -> Extraction -> Verification -> Reward -> GRPO
- **Critical path:** Generation produces NL reasoning + JSON summary → Extraction parses JSON and extracts FOL conclusions → Verification checks each conclusion against dependencies using a formal prover → Reward computes score based on verification pass/fail and answer correctness → GRPO updates policy.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - JSON structure richness vs. model formatting errors.
  - Prover expressiveness (Lean) vs. automation speed (Prover9/Z3).
  - Binary per-step verification simplicity vs. potential loss of nuanced error feedback.
- **Failure signatures:** Low Reward Hit Rate (<10%) indicates JSON generation or prover mismatch issues; high answer accuracy but low GPT-Soundness suggests reward misalignment; JSON parse errors point to prompt/schema problems.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate Outcome-CoT baseline on ProverQA-Hard to confirm prior result (~91% accuracy, ~22% soundness).  
  2. Implement PRoSFI pipeline with a simple prover (e.g., Prover9), verify Reward Hit Rate correlates with soundness, and tune JSON schema (dependency limits <5).  
  3. Test DTV scaling (N=1,2,4,8,16) and confirm soundness improves with N for PRoSFI but not for Outcome-CoT.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can PRoSFI be effectively extended to domains beyond first-order logic, such as general natural language reasoning or complex mathematical theorem proving, where formalizing intermediate steps is less straightforward?  
  *Basis:* Conclusion calls for extension to broader domains with more diverse datasets, cold-start strategies, stronger models, and domain-specific tool integration. *Why unresolved:* Current validation is on synthetic logical puzzles; scaling introduces ambiguity, probabilistic reasoning, and lack of canonical formal representations.
- **Open Question 2:** Can more granular reward functions than the current binary/pass-fail verification (e.g., partial credit based on step criticality or solver confidence) further improve RL training stability and final soundness?  
  *Basis:* Conclusion identifies more granular reward functions as a future direction. *Why unresolved:* Current reward is simple but may not provide nuanced feedback; designing informative and feasible partial rewards is unclear.
- **Open Question 3:** How reliable is the GPT-based "Soundness" metric for evaluating reasoning path validity, and to what extent do biases in LLM-as-a-Judge affect the claimed correlation between PRoSFI reward hit rate and true logical soundness?  
  *Basis:* Paper acknowledges use of GPT-OSS-120B as judge and notes potential biases; high correlation (0.71) is central. *Why unresolved:* Human evaluation is expensive; systematic judge biases could artifactually inflate measured improvements.
- **Open Question 4:** To what extent do the structured formal intermediates accurately capture and align with the model's original natural language reasoning process, and could there be a "faithfulness gap" where the formal steps are valid but do not truly represent the model's internal thought chain?  
  *Basis:* Core assumption is that intermediates are a faithful bridge; paper shows correlation but not NL-to-formal alignment. *Why unresolved:* Model may be incentivized to produce verifiable steps that are post-hoc or semantically disconnected from preceding NL.
- **Open Question 5:** Does the decoupling of verification from generation introduce vulnerabilities where the model learns to "game" the verifier by producing formally valid but irrelevant or trivial intermediate steps that satisfy dependencies without capturing genuine reasoning?  
  *Basis:* Paper emphasizes decoupling for efficiency, but reward is based solely on formal validity, not relevance. *Why unresolved:* Success on ProverQA suggests meaningful paths, but in noisy domains model might emit tautologies or circular dependencies to pad chains.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is confined to ProverQA-Hard and Knights&Knaves; generalizability to math, code, or open-ended reasoning is unproven.
- Reliance on external provers introduces latency and potential incompleteness; verification failures may reflect prover limits, not model errors.
- Structured step generation assumes 7B models can consistently output valid JSON with correct FOL syntax; formatting brittleness could break the pipeline.
