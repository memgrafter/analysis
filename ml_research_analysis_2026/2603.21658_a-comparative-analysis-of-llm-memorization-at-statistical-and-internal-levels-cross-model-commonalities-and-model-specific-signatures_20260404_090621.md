# A Comparative Analysis of LLM Memorization at Statistical and Internal Levels: Cross-Model Commonalities and Model-Specific Signatures
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.21658
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21658
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Memorization rate scales log-linearly with model size, but absolute rates vary up to 100× across families due to training data; memorized sequences are highly compressible (compression ratio often ≤0.5), and a small set of attention heads is universally important, yet their layer distribution forms family-specific signatures.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a large-scale comparative study of verbatim memorization in autoregressive language models, examining 20 checkpoints from six families (Pythia, OpenLLaMA, StarCoder, OLMo). The authors combine statistical metrics (memorization rate, compression ratio) with internal probing techniques (noise perturbation, layer decoding, attention head ablation) to characterize both the prevalence and the mechanistic underpinnings of memorization. Key findings reveal that memorization rate grows log-linearly with parameter count, but absolute rates differ drastically (up to 100×) across families due to training data characteristics. Memorized sequences are highly compressible, indicating efficient internal encoding, and a small subset of attention heads is universally critical for accurate recall, though their distribution across layers forms a distinctive fingerprint for each model family.

## Method Summary
The analysis begins by sampling sequences from the known pre-training corpora of each model family. For each sequence, the model generates a 32-token continuation via greedy decoding, and memorization is quantified by an exact token-match score (M=1 if all tokens match). The memorization rate is the proportion of such exact matches. To gauge the depth of memorization, the authors compute a compression ratio: the minimal context length (found by binary search) that still triggers full recall, expressed as a fraction of the original length. For internal probing, three complementary techniques are applied: (1) noise perturbation—Gaussian noise is injected into the first-layer residual stream, and changes in memorization distribution (Wasserstein distance) and attention similarity (cosine) are measured; (2) layer decoding—the logit lens is used to track target token probabilities across layers; (3) attention head ablation—each head’s output is replaced with the average of others, and the resulting disruption (importance score, IM) is recorded. These probes are aggregated across domains and models, enabling cross-family comparisons of head importance distributions and noise sensitivity patterns.

## Key Results
- Memorization rate scales log-linearly with model parameter count, but absolute rates vary up to 100× across families due to differences in training data.
- Memorized sequences are highly compressible, with median compression ratios often ≤0.5, indicating that a small context can fully trigger recall.
- A small set of attention heads is universally important for memorization across domains, yet their layer-wise distribution forms a distinctive fingerprint for each model family.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study’s findings are underpinned by three interconnected mechanisms. First, the log-linear scaling of memorization rate with model size reflects that larger models have greater storage capacity, but the absolute rate is heavily modulated by the training corpus—its statistical properties, duplication, and preprocessing—leading to up to 100× variation across families. Second, the high compressibility of memorized sequences (median ratio ≤0.5) indicates that models encode memories efficiently: a minimal, high-salience pattern within the context acts as a trigger for full recall, suggesting memorization involves low-entropy internal representations rather than rote copying. Third, attention head ablation reveals that a tiny subset of heads (often 1-5%) is necessary for accurate reproduction of memorized content across all domains, demonstrating a shared computational subroutine. However, the specific layers where these heads reside differ systematically between families, implying that the training recipe shapes how this subroutine is routed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Memorization Score & Rate**: Quantifies verbatim recall via exact token match over a fixed length. *Why needed*: It is the core metric for identifying and aggregating memorized sequences across models. *Quick check*: Does a 31/32 token match count as memorized? (No—requires full match.)
- **Compression Ratio**: Ratio of minimal context length to original length needed for full recall. *Why needed*: Distinguishes superficial from firm memorization; low ratios indicate efficient encoding. *Quick check*: Is a ratio of 0.8 considered highly compressible? (No—findings show median ≤0.5, so 0.8 is less compressible.)
- **Attention Head Ablation Importance Scoring**: Measures disruption to output when a head's output is replaced; high IM score denotes necessity. *Why needed*: Isolates the role of individual heads in memorization circuits. *Quick check*: Does ablation causing 20% token change indicate high importance? (Yes—top heads often cause near-total disruption.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Tokenized Context -> Transformer Blocks (Attention + MLP) -> LM Head -> Token Probabilities. Ablation Subsystem: intercepts attention head outputs during forward pass.
**Critical Path**:
1. Sample sequences from known pre-training corpora per domain.
2. For each sequence, greedy-generate continuation; compute memorization score; retain sequences with score 1.
3. Compute memorization rate, compression ratio, domain/frequency distributions.
4. Internal probing:
   - Noise perturbation: add Gaussian noise to first-layer residuals; compare memorization distribution (W1 distance) and attention similarity (cosine).
   - Layer decoding: apply logit lens to hidden states at each layer; plot target token probabilities.
   - Head ablation: for memorized sequences, ablate each head; compute IM score; aggregate top-20% heads per domain/model.
5. Cross-model comparison: align layer-wise head importance distributions (normalized) within/across families (e.g., heatmap in Figure 6).
**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Sample size: Full-corpus analysis infeasible; 300k samples/domain for statistics but only 2.5k-10k memorized examples for head ablation, risking omission of rare heads.
- Metric sensitivity: Hard exact-match threshold may underestimate memory; soft scores could alter trends.
- Probing granularity: Head ablation is coarse; does not isolate head-specific attention patterns or probe MLP neurons directly.
**Failure Signatures**:
- Statistical failure: Non-log-linear scaling for a new family suggests either training difference or artifact of studied families.
- Internal failure: New model's layer importance distribution fails to cluster with its family, contradicting fingerprint hypothesis.
- Noise robustness anomaly: Memorized sequences show higher similarity recovery than unmemorized ones under noise, opposite of expected sensitivity.
**First 3 Experiments**:
1. Replicate Figure 1a: scatter plot of memorization rate vs. model size for a new family; expect log-linear trend with family-specific intercept.
2. Compute compression ratio: binary search minimal context for full recall; expect median ratio ≤0.5.
3. Cross-domain head overlap: for a new model, compute top-20% important heads per domain; pairwise Jaccard similarity; expect within-domain >70%, cross-domain ~20%, with ~1-5% universal heads.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Do cross-model commonalities hold for modern high-capacity models (e.g., Qwen, Deepseek, Llama) with private training data?
2. Which aspects of the training recipe (data distribution, tokenizer, architecture, hyperparameters) drive family-specific memorization signatures?
3. To what extent do statistical patterns depend on the fixed 32-token context and continuation length?
4. What is the precise internal mechanism of the universally important attention heads? (e.g., retrieval, pattern matching, state maintenance)
5. What causes the compression ratio plateau between 7B and 13B parameter scales?

## Limitations
- Memorization score's exact-match threshold may underestimate true memory and bias comparisons.
- Small internal probing samples (2.5k–10k memorized sequences per model) may miss rare but influential heads.
- Different training corpora across families prevent clean separation of architecture vs. data effects.

## Confidence
- Scaling law: High
- High compressibility: Medium
- Universal heads with family-specific fingerprints: Medium

## Next Checks
