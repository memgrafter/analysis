# Do LLMs Encode Functional Importance of Reasoning Tokens?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03066
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03066
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Greedy pruning, a likelihood-preserving deletion procedure, reveals that LLMs internally rank reasoning tokens by functional importance, with symbolic computation tokens preferentially retained; student models trained on pruned chains outperform multiple baselines at matched reasoning lengths.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) encode an internal sense of functional importance for tokens within their reasoning chains. It introduces **greedy pruning**, an iterative procedure that removes tokens while minimally degrading a chosen likelihood objective (answer correctness or reasoning chain probability), thereby inducing a token importance ranking. Through teacher-pruner-student distillation experiments on math reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-Pro), the authors find that students trained on greedily pruned chains significantly outperform those trained on chains pruned by various baseline methods. Analysis of the pruning patterns uncovers a systematic functional hierarchy: tokens involved in symbolic computation are most preserved, while referential and grammatical tokens are pruned early. Furthermore, the pruning ranks are highly predictable from the teacher model’s attention patterns, suggesting that attention weights serve as a proxy for the model’s own importance signals.

## Method Summary
The core method is **greedy pruning**. Starting from a complete, correct reasoning chain generated by a teacher model, the pruner (typically the same model) iteratively evaluates the post-deletion likelihood for each remaining token under a specified objective (e.g., answer likelihood, joint chain likelihood). The token whose removal causes the smallest drop in the objective is pruned first. This process repeats, re-evaluating likelihoods in the shrinking context, until a target keep fraction is reached, producing an importance ranking and a pruned chain. To evaluate whether the pruned chains preserve functional information, the authors use a **distillation framework**: a smaller student model is trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on datasets of pruned chains. Student accuracy on held-out test sets is compared across different pruning strategies at matched reasoning lengths. The functional structure of the retained tokens is analyzed by manually annotating tokens into coarse categories (e.g., symbolic math, meta-discourse, coreference) and tracking their retention rates. Finally, a surrogate MLP is trained to predict the greedy pruning ranks using aggregated future attention scores as features, testing whether attention encodes the importance signal.

## Key Results
- Greedy pruning (using the joint objective) yields student models that consistently outperform baselines like TokenSkip, H2O, Surprisal, and Uniform across GSM8K, MATH, and MMLU-Pro at identical reasoning chain lengths.
- Analysis reveals a stable, systematic functional hierarchy: **SYMBMATH** (symbolic computation) tokens are over-retained (pruned late), while **COREF** (coreference) and **META_DISCOURSE** tokens are under-retained (pruned early); the joint objective sharpens this separation compared to an answer-only objective.
- The token importance ranks produced by greedy pruning are strongly predictable from the teacher model’s attention patterns; an MLP using aggregated future attention as features achieves a **Pearson correlation of 0.88** with the pruning ranks on held-out GSM8K examples from Llama3.1-8B.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Likelihood-Preserving Deletion Reveals Functional Importance
- **Claim:** Greedy pruning uncovers token-level functional importance by iteratively removing the token whose deletion minimally harms a chosen likelihood objective (answer or joint).
- **Mechanism:** The pruner model, under teacher forcing, computes the conditional log-probability of the target (e.g., final answer) given the chain with each candidate token removed. The token with the smallest likelihood drop is deemed least functionally critical at that step and is pruned. This dynamic re-evaluation captures contextual dependencies as the chain contracts.
- **Core assumption:** The model's own conditional log-probability, computed in a teacher-forced setting, is a valid proxy for a token's contribution to generating the correct final output.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Describes greedy pruning as a "likelihood-preserving deletion procedure" that induces a ranking.
  - [section 3.2] Formalizes the objectives (Eq. 1, 2) and presents Algorithm 1 for iterative deletion.
  - [corpus] Limited direct corroboration; related work on pruning uses different criteria (attention, magnitude) but not this specific dynamic, likelihood-based ranking.

### Mechanism 2: Systematic Functional Structure in Pruning Patterns
- **Claim:** The retention patterns from greedy pruning reveal a stable, interpretable hierarchy: symbolic computation tokens are functionally critical, while referential and grammatical scaffolding is less so.
- **Mechanism:** Tokens are manually categorized (e.g., SYMBMATH, COREF, META_DISCOURSE). By plotting the fraction of tokens from each category retained as a function of pruning progress (keep fraction), a consistent ordering emerges. The joint objective (preserving reasoning chain likelihood) sharpens the separation between high- and low-importance categories.
- **Core assumption:** These coarse semantic/functional categories meaningfully map to distinct roles within the reasoning process, and differential retention reflects the model's internal prioritization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.1] Figure 3 shows category retention curves: SYBMATH is persistently above the uniform diagonal (over-retained), while COREF is below until late stages.
  - [section 5.1] Reports annotation scheme stability across different teacher-pruner model pairs.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; neighboring studies on pruning do not report this specific categorical analysis.

### Mechanism 3: Attention Patterns Predict Greedy Pruning Ranks
- **Claim:** The importance ranks from greedy pruning are strongly predictable from the teacher model's attention weights, indicating that attention encodes a signal correlated with functional necessity.
- **Mechanism:** For each token, a feature vector is constructed from the average attention weights paid to it by all future tokens across all layers and heads. A simple MLP is trained to predict the token's post-deletion likelihood rank (from greedy pruning) using these features. High Pearson correlation (0.88) suggests future attention aggregates sufficient information to approximate the model's own importance assessment.
- **Core assumption:** Aggregated future attention to a token serves as a summary statistic for its relevance to subsequent generation and final answer prediction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.3] Details feature construction and reports the 0.88 correlation on a held-out GSM8K set with Llama3.1-8B.
  - [section 5.3] Connects finding to prior work on attention-based attribution but emphasizes the novel prediction of likelihood-based ranks.
  - [corpus] Corpus contains attention-based pruning methods (H2O, FASA), but none establish this specific high correlation with likelihood-preserving deletion ranks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Likelihood-based probing**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire greedy pruning method uses the model's conditional log-probability (under teacher forcing) as the importance metric. Understanding this objective, its connection to model training, and potential biases (e.g., teacher forcing distribution mismatch) is essential.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might a token that is critical for human-interpretable reasoning (e.g., a key inference step) not cause a large drop in the model's log-likelihood when removed?
- **Concept: Token-level functional annotation**
  - **Why needed here:** The analysis interprets pruning behavior by mapping tokens to coarse functional categories (SYMBMATH, METADISC, COREF, etc.). This requires understanding the annotation rules, their purpose (revealing macro-level structure), and limitations (coarseness, manual effort).
  - **Quick check question:** Given the word "calculate", under what circumstances would it be labeled META_DISCOURSE vs. VERBAL_MATH?
- **Concept: Teacher-pruner-student distillation framework**
  - **Why needed here:** This is the experimental paradigm for evaluating if pruned chains preserve functional information. The student's performance gap relative to full-chain training tests the informativeness of the pruning criterion.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is it important that the pruner equals the teacher in the main experiments? What would changing the pruner (e.g., to a different model) test?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Teacher LLM -> Pruner LLM (same as teacher) -> Student LLM; Evaluation: GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-Pro; Baselines: TokenSkip, H2O, Surprisal, Uniform.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Teacher generates a dataset of correct reasoning chains via rejection sampling.
  2. For each chain, the pruner applies greedy pruning (or a baseline) to reach a target keep fraction, producing a pruned chain and a token ranking.
  3. A student model is trained via SFT on the resulting pruned chain dataset.
  4. The student is evaluated on the test set; accuracy vs. keep fraction curves are compared across pruning strategies.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Greedy pruning vs. baselines:** Greedy pruning is computationally heavy (O(n²) likelihood evaluations per chain) but model-internal and dynamic. Baselines are cheaper (single-pass scores) but use external (TokenSkip) or static (attention, surprisal) signals.
  - **Objective choice (JOINT vs. ANS):** JOINT (preserving reasoning chain likelihood) retains more structural tokens and sharpens functional separation; ANS (answer-only) is more aggressive but may weaken reasoning coherence.
  - **Pruner strength:** Using a weaker pruner model distorts the functional structure (e.g., over-retaining entity names) and leads to poorer student performance, highlighting the need for a competent pruner.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Student accuracy after greedy pruning does not significantly exceed baselines at matched keep fractions.
  - Category retention curves collapse to the uniform line; no systematic ordering (e.g., symbolic tokens pruned early).
  - Attention-based predictor correlation is low (<0.7) across models/datasets.
  - Student accuracy drops abruptly at moderate keep fractions, indicating brittle token dependencies not captured by pruning.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate the core distillation curve: For a teacher-pruner-student setup (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B -> Llama2-7B) on GSM8K, generate pruned chains at keep fractions {0.7, 0.8, 0.9} using greedy pruning with the JOINT objective. Train the student and plot accuracy vs. keep fraction. Expect a smooth degradation curve that lies above all baseline curves.
  2. Analyze functional structure: Using the pruned chains from experiment 1, manually annotate a sample of tokens into the six categories. Plot the retention rate of each category as a function of keep fraction. Expect SYMBMATH to be above the diagonal (over-retained) and COREF/META_DISCOURSE below.
  3. Test attention predictability: On a held-out set of 1k GSM8K chains from the same teacher (Llama3.1-8B), compute the aggregated future attention feature for each token. Train the MLP to predict the greedy pruning rank (post-deletion likelihood) and report Pearson correlation on a test split. Expect >0.85.

## Limitations
- The likelihood-based importance measure may not equate to causal necessity; teacher forcing bias could make the model's predictions overly sensitive to its own generated tokens.
- Findings are restricted to evaluated model families (Qwen, Llama) and mathematical reasoning tasks; generalizability to other architectures (e.g., encoder-decoders) and domains (e.g., creative writing) is unknown.
- Token category annotation is coarse, manual, and lacks reported inter-annotator agreement, raising sensitivity concerns around the observed functional hierarchy.

## Confidence
- **Greedy pruning reveals token-level functional importance:** Medium
- **Systematic functional structure (symbolic tokens retained, referential pruned):** Medium
- **Attention patterns strongly predict pruning ranks (correlation ~0.88):** Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Causal validation:** For a set of chains, delete tokens in the greedy rank order (from most to least important) and feed each truncated chain to the model. Measure the drop in final answer accuracy. Test if the correlation between greedy rank and accuracy impact is high.
2. **Cross-model and cross-domain generalizability:** Replicate the attention predictor experiment on different model families (e.g., GPT-4o-mini, Claude Haiku) and on a non-math reasoning benchmark (e.g., LogiQA). Assess stability of the high correlation.
3. **Annotation robustness:** Compute Cohen's kappa for the token category annotation scheme across multiple human annotators. Additionally, explore a fully automated labeling based on POS tags and regex patterns to see if the same retention ordering holds.
