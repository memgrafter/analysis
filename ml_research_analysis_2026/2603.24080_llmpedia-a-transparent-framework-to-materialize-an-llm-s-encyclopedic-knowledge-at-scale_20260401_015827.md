# LLMpedia: A Transparent Framework to Materialize an LLM's Encyclopedic Knowledge at Scale
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24080
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24080
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: LLMpedia generates ~1M articles from parametric memory, revealing a >15-point gap between MMLU scores and verifiable factuality (74.7% on Wikipedia-covered subjects, 63.2% on frontier subjects).

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces LLMpedia, a framework to materialize an LLM's encyclopedic knowledge at scale without retrieval. Using breadth-first expansion from a seed entity and a three-stage entity sanitization pipeline, they generate nearly one million articles across three model families. Evaluations show that standard benchmarks like MMLU overestimate factuality due to availability bias; the true rate on Wikipedia-covered subjects is 74.7% for gpt-5-mini, over 15 points below its MMLU score. On frontier subjects beyond Wikipedia, the true rate drops to 63.2%. Wikipedia covers only 61% of the generated subjects, and model families overlap by a mere 7.3%. In a capture-trap benchmark, LLMpedia achieves higher factuality than Grokipedia at roughly half the textual similarity to Wikipedia, demonstrating its ability to surface non-Wikipedia knowledge. All artifacts are publicly released.

## Method Summary
LLMpedia employs a breadth-first search (BFS) expansion starting from a seed entity (e.g., Vannevar Bush). For each entity, the model generates a Wikipedia-style article and extracts wikilinks. These links undergo a three-stage sanitization: (1) canonical normalization (Unicode, case, punctuation), (2) LLM-based filtering to retain only encyclopedic entities, and (3) embedding-based deduplication (cosine similarity ≥0.90 rejects duplicates). Surviving entities are enqueued for further expansion. Two modes are supported: general-domain (unconstrained) and topic-focused (constrained to a root topic). Evaluation uses a two-tier verification: Tier 1 checks claims against full Wikipedia articles; Tier 2 uses curated web sources for Wikipedia-absent subjects. An LLM judge (gpt-4.1-nano) extracts atomic claims and assigns verdicts. The pipeline is run across three model families (gpt-5-mini, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, DeepSeek-V3) to generate ~1M articles.

## Key Results
- Verifiable true rate for gpt-5-mini: 74.7% on Wikipedia-covered subjects, but only 63.2% on frontier subjects beyond Wikipedia.
- Wikipedia coverage of generated subjects: 61%; overlap between model families: 7.3%.
- In a capture-trap benchmark, LLMpedia achieves higher factuality than Grokipedia at roughly half the textual similarity to Wikipedia.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The BFS expansion taps into the model's parametric memory by prompting it to generate articles and extract links without retrieval. The three-stage sanitization ensures that only high-quality, distinct encyclopedic entities are retained, preventing loops and thin articles. By using a neutral persona and minimal reasoning, the model's internal knowledge is elicited consistently. The two-tier verification provides a robust factuality estimate: Wikipedia serves as a high-precision gold standard for covered subjects, while curated web sources extend evaluation to frontier topics. This design isolates parametric knowledge and measures it against external evidence, revealing the bias in standard benchmarks that rely on readily available (often Wikipedia-aligned) data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Breadth-First Expansion (BFS)**: A graph traversal strategy that systematically explores all entities at the current depth before moving deeper. *Why needed*: Enables exhaustive materialization of an LLM's associative knowledge without retrieval. *Quick check*: Verify that each entity's children are enqueued after sanitization.
- **Entity Sanitization Pipeline**: Three-stage process (normalization, LLM filtering, deduplication) to ensure only valid, unique encyclopedic entities enter the BFS queue. *Why needed*: Prevents degradation from generic terms, loops, and duplicates. *Quick check*: Monitor survival rates per stage; NER filtering should reject >80% of canonical candidates.
- **Verifiable True Rate**: Fraction of claims supported by external evidence (Wikipedia or web). *Why needed*: Provides an objective measure of factuality independent of benchmark availability. *Quick check*: Compute precision on a random sample with gold evidence.
- **Availability Bias**: Tendency of benchmarks to overestimate factuality because they test on widely documented (often Wikipedia) topics. *Why needed*: Explains why MMLU scores are higher than LLMpedia's true rate. *Quick check*: Compare coverage of benchmark subjects vs. LLMpedia's generated corpus.
- **Capture-Trap Benchmark**: An evaluation that measures factuality while controlling for textual similarity to Wikipedia. *Why needed*: Demonstrates that LLMpedia produces high-quality non-Wikipedia knowledge. *Quick check*: Verify that LLMpedia's similarity is lower yet factuality higher than Grokipedia's.
- **Two-Tier Verification**: Uses Wikipedia (Tier 1) for covered subjects and curated web (Tier 2) for frontier subjects. *Why needed*: Extends evaluation beyond Wikipedia's scope. *Quick check*: Ensure Tier 2 sources meet quality scores ≥60 and block listed root domains.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Seed Entity -> Article Generation (LLM) -> Wikilink Extraction -> Stage1 Normalization -> Stage2 NER Filtering -> Stage3 Deduplication -> Queue -> Next Entity. Evaluation runs in parallel: Claim Extraction (LLM) -> Verification (Wikipedia/Web) -> Verdict.

Critical path: BFS loop (generate, extract, sanitize, enqueue) dominates runtime; evaluation is offline on a sample.

Design tradeoffs:
- Using LLM for filtering (Stage2) ensures quality but is slower and costlier than rule-based methods; tradeoff: precision vs. scalability.
- Minimal reasoning reduces hallucination but may limit depth; tradeoff: factuality vs. coverage.
- Embedding deduplication (Stage3) prevents entity explosion but may merge distinct entities with similar descriptions; tradeoff: uniqueness vs. fragmentation.

Failure signatures:
- High survival rate through Stage2 (>90%) indicates weak NER filtering, likely causing quality collapse.
- Sudden drop in queue size after Stage3 suggests over-aggressive deduplication or duplicate link explosion.
- Low Tier 2 retrieval success (<70%) signals web evidence pipeline coverage issues, biasing frontier true rate.

First three experiments:
1. Run BFS for 100 steps from seed "Vannevar Bush" with gpt-5-mini; record survival rates at each sanitization stage and total articles generated.
2. Sample 1000 final articles; compute proportion with Wikipedia coverage and model overlap with Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct.
3. Replicate evaluation on 200 random articles: compare LLM judge (gpt-4.1-nano) verdicts against human annotations to calibrate judge accuracy.

## Limitations
- Underspecified Tier 2 web evidence retrieval logic (search backends, ranking, extraction) may introduce reproducibility bias.
- Stage 2 NER filtering prompt lacks few-shot examples, necessitating iterative tuning for faithful replication.
- Reasoning budget definitions ("minimal" vs. "low") are unquantified, hindering exact ablation replication.
