# Can Blindfolded LLMs Still Trade? An Anonymization-First Framework for Portfolio Optimization

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.17692
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17692
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Anonymization-first portfolio framework achieves annualized Sharpe of 1.40±0.22 on 2025 YTD, with negative control tests confirming signal validity.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces **BlindTrade**, a portfolio optimization framework that addresses LLM agent memorization bias by anonymizing all company identifiers (tickers, names). It employs four specialized LLM agents to generate anonymized stock-specific scores and reasoning. A Graph Neural Network (GNN) then encodes inter-stock relationships from both sector links and the semantic similarity of LLM reasoning. A reinforcement learning policy (PPO-DSR) produces final portfolio weights, conditioned on an interpretable "intent" mode (defensive/neutral/aggressive). Evaluated out-of-sample on 2025 YTD data, the system significantly outperforms benchmarks. Negative control experiments (randomizing GNN outputs) cause performance to collapse, supporting the claim that the signals are valid and not leakage artifacts. However, extended testing reveals strong regime dependency, with notable underperformance in a 2024 bull market.

## Method Summary
The BlindTrade pipeline follows an anonymization-first principle. Input data for stocks (price, fundamentals, news) has all company-specific entities replaced with synthetic identifiers via a knowledge graph. Four distinct LLM agents (Momentum, News-Event, Mean-Reversion, Risk-Regime) process this anonymized data, each outputting a trading score and a textual reasoning JSON. These reasonings are embedded, and node features are constructed from the score and embedding. A Semantic Graph Attention Network (SemGAT) builds a graph using sector connections and semantic similarity between reasoning embeddings. The GNN produces a final stock return distribution. A PPO-based policy with a Differential Sharpe Ratio reward (penalizing turnover) and a Dirichlet action output then allocates capital. The policy's action is modulated by an "Intent Head" that selects a global risk posture (defensive/neutral/aggregressive) based on aggregated LLM and GNN signals.

## Key Results
*   Achieves annualized Sharpe ratio of **1.40±0.22** on 2025 YTD (145 days), outperforming benchmarks.
*   **Negative controls validate signals**: Randomly shuffling GNN outputs collapses the Information Coefficient (IC) to ~0 and Top-K Sharpe from 1.08 to -1.17.
*   Demonstrates **regime dependency**: In volatile 2025 YTD, Sharpe 1.02 beats SPY (0.54); in bull 2024, it lags drastically (0.34 vs SPY 1.70).
*   The RL component drastically reduces turnover (1.7%/day) compared to a naive implementation (139%/day).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Anonymization Blocks Memorization
*   **Claim:** Replacing tickers with synthetic IDs and masking entities prevents LLMs from relying on memorized, ticker-specific patterns (e.g., "buy Tesla on sentiment"), forcing them to generate signals based on market dynamics and anonymized features.
*   **Mechanism:** A pre-processing step maps all tickers and company proper nouns to placeholder IDs (e.g., "AAPL" → "STOCK_0026") using a knowledge graph. LLM agents receive only this anonymized input.
*   **Core Assumption:** Genuine market understanding should yield persistent predictive signals even when ticker-based memorization pathways are severed.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The paper's stated goal is to address "memorization bias from ticker-specific pre-training." The methodology section details the synthetic ID mapping and entity masking. *Corpus evidence for this specific mechanism's effectiveness is weak, and the paper acknowledges no direct ablation comparing anonymized vs. raw inputs.*

### Mechanism 2: GNN Encodes Relational Signals
*   **Claim:** The SemGAT captures predictive inter-stock relationships by combining sector-based links with semantic links derived from the similarity of LLM reasoning embeddings.
*   **Mechanism:** Reasoning text from the four agents is embedded (384-dim). Node features combine the agent scores with these embeddings (394-dim total). Edges are created from (i) sector classification and (ii) cosine similarity >0.75 between reasoning embeddings. Message passing aggregates information from "stocks with similar reasoning."
*   **Core Assumption:** Meaningful, predictive stock relationships are discoverable in the semantic content of LLM-generated reasoning, even under anonymization.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Ablation shows Sharpe drops from 1.40 to 0.62±0.50 without the GNN, indicating its critical role and the instability of isolated LLM signals. The hybrid graph construction is detailed in the methodology. *No corpus evidence directly supports this particular use of semantic reasoning graphs.*

### Mechanism 3: RL Policy Manages Turnover and Intent
*   **Claim:** The PPO-DSR policy optimizes portfolio weights with a cost-sensitive reward and an interpretable intent-mode conditioning, transforming high-frequency LLM signals into a low-turnover, executable strategy.
*   **Mechanism:** The policy outputs Dirichlet distribution parameters for weights. The reward is the Differential Sharpe Ratio minus a linear transaction cost (10bps). An "Intent Head" selects one of three modes (defensive/neutral/aggressive) based on global statistics (e.g., consensus score distribution), which then conditions the policy's exploration.
*   **Core Assumption:** Separating global risk intent from stock-level allocation allows the policy to adapt posture without overfitting to daily noise, and that a cost-penalized Sharpe objective will naturally curb harmful turnover.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Naive equal-weight Top-20 has Sharpe -1.17 after costs due to 139%/day turnover. The RL policy achieves Sharpe 1.08–1.40 with turnover at 1.7%/day, demonstrating the effectiveness of the cost-aware learning. The intent system is presented as a design for interpretability.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Point-in-Time (PIT) Data:** Backtests must use only the stocks that were index constituents on the *specific historical date* to avoid survivorship bias. **Why needed here:** The framework claims to mitigate survivorship bias; using a rolling S&P 500 universe requires strict PIT data. **Quick check:** For each backtest day *t*, is the stock list explicitly restricted to the official constituents on that date?
*   **Information Coefficient (IC):** The Spearman rank correlation between a predicted signal (e.g., LLM score) and future realized returns. Used to validate signal predictive power. **Why needed here:** To verify that the anonymized LLM outputs have genuine, monotonic predictive power and are not spurious. Used for agent/feature selection and leakage audit. **Quick check:** Is IC computed on a strict out-of-sample holdout period, and are values statistically significant (p<0.05)?
*   **Distributional Reinforcement Learning (Dirichlet Policy):** Using a Dirichlet distribution to parameterize the portfolio weight action space. **Why needed here:** It naturally enforces the simplex constraint (weights ≥0 and sum to 1) and allows the policy to output a learned "concentration" reflecting certainty/uncertainty in allocations. **Quick check:** Does the action space output parameters for a Dirichlet distribution, rather than unconstrained logits that are then normalized?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Anonymizer -> LLM Agents -> Reasoning Embeddings (Node Features) -> SemGAT (GNN) -> Global Stats & Market State -> Intent Head -> PPO-DSR Policy (Dirichlet) -> Portfolio Weights
*   **Critical Path:** Raw market data undergoes entity anonymization. Four specialized LLM agents process this data, producing scores and reasonings. Reasonings are embedded to form node features for a graph built on sector and semantic links. The GNN (SemGAT) processes this graph. Its outputs and aggregated statistics feed an Intent Head that selects a risk posture. This conditionsthe PPO policy, which outputs final portfolio weights.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **GNN Complexity vs. Stability:** More complex SemGAT variants (e.g., with volatility-scaled targets) increased variance. Simpler HL-Gauss with ranking loss was more robust.
    *   **Interpretability vs. Performance:** Intent modes offer a high-level, interpretable risk posture. However, the learned mapping from aggregated stats to intent transitions is opaque. Also, no mechanism explicitly limits cash allocation for crash protection.
    *   **LLM Reasoning Cost vs. Signal Quality:** Requiring textual reasoning enables semantic graph construction but increases inference latency and cost (using Gemini 2.5 Flash).
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   IC drops to near zero after anonymization or on holdout → signals are likely memorization artifacts.
    *   Negative control (shuffled GNN outputs) does **not** collapse IC/Top-K Sharpe → indicates potential data leakage or non-predictive signal structure.
    *   Max Drawdown exceeds -30% with sustained high volatility (>40%) → strategy may be unsuitable for risk-constrained mandates.
    *   Persistent underperformance in specific regimes (e.g., strong bull markets) → suggests model is regime-biased and lacks adaptation.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Anonymization Ablation:** Run the full pipeline in parallel on *raw* (non-anonymized) ticker data vs. the proposed *anonymized* data. A significant performance gap (raw >> anonymized) suggests anonymization is ineffective; similar performance suggests memorization may still be present even with anonymization.
    2.  **IC Validation Walk-Forward:** Compute the daily Information Coefficient (IC) for each LLM agent's final selected score on a rolling 6-month validation window. Ablate/retrain any agent showing consistently negative or statistically insignificant (p>0.1) IC in the most recent window.
    3.  **Negative Control Shuffling:** After training the GNN, freeze it and run the RL policy. Introduce a step that randomly permutes the final GNN-predicted scores *cross-sectionally* (across stocks) each day before they enter the RL policy. If the strategy's Sharpe collapses to near zero or negative (as reported: Top-K Sharpe 1.08 → -1.17), it validates that the original cross-sectional signal structure is essential and non-random.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How effective is the anonymization protocol at preventing LLM memorization of ticker-specific patterns?
*   **Basis in paper:** The Limitations section states: "Anonymization effect is not directly validated... we did not perform a direct ablation comparing anonymized vs. raw ticker inputs."
*   **Why unresolved:** Negative control experiments validate the *structure* of the final signal but do not isolate the specific contribution of anonymization. Performance could still be influenced by other biases.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A direct, controlled ablation running the identical pipeline on raw vs. anonymized data. Additionally, probing the LLM's internal representations (e.g., activation analysis) to check for residual strong ticker associations in the anonymized setting.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** What is the relative contribution of sector-based versus semantic reasoning-based edges in the GNN's performance?
*   **Basis in paper:** The Ablation Studies note: "Edge-type ablation—sector-only vs. semantic-only—is left for future work."
*   **Why unresolved:** The hybrid graph's value is asserted but not decomposed. It's unclear if semantic edges add unique predictive power beyond the well-known sector structure or introduce noise.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablations training the GNN with (a) only sector edges, (b) only semantic edges (varying similarity thresholds), and (c) no edges. Compare downstream RL stability (Sharpe mean/variance) and IC persistence.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** What specific market conditions or signals trigger transitions between defensive, neutral, and aggressive intent modes?
*   **Basis in paper:** The Limitations state: "Explaining intent transitions is difficult... explaining why it transitioned to that mode at a specific time using external indicators remains challenging."
*   **Why unresolved:** The Intent Head's decision logic is learned end-to-end and not directly interpretable. While mode distributions are observable, the causal drivers (e.g., volatility spike, drawdown threshold) are unknown, limiting trust and manual oversight.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Post-hoc analysis correlating daily intent mode switches with external market variables (VIX changes, rolling drawdown, market momentum indicators) using statistical models (e.g., logistic regression) or feature attribution methods (SHAP) applied to the Intent Head's input features (global statistics, market state vector).

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Can the framework's regime dependency be mitigated, particularly its underperformance in sustained bull markets?
*   **Basis in paper:** The Discussion and Extended OOS results highlight: "BlindTrade underperforms during strong bull markets (2024: Sharpe 0.34 vs SPY 1.70)... Future work: online adaptation with periodic retraining may improve robustness."
*   **Why unresolved:** The static training period (2020-2023) seems to bias the policy toward volatile regimes. The cause—signal decay, agent bias, or RL objective mismatch—is not diagnosed.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** (a) Retraining the entire framework on data that includes strong bull periods and evaluating generalization; (b) Measuring the IC of the core LLM and GNN signals separately in bull vs. volatile regimes to pinpoint where signal strength drops; (c) Implementing the proposed online adaptation (e.g., monthly walk-forward retraining) and measuring the change in bull-market performance.

## Limitations
*   **Anonymization efficacy** is assumed but not directly validated against a raw-ticker baseline, leaving open the possibility of residual memorization via other features.
*   **Semantic GNN edges** rely on a fixed similarity threshold (cos>0.75) and unproven assumption that LLM reasoning embeddings contain stable, predictive relational signals; corpus support for this is very weak.
*   **RL intent-mode logic** is a black box; the mapping from aggregated stats to defensive/neutral/aggressive transitions is not interpretable, limiting operator trust and potential for manual intervention.

## Confidence
*   **Anonymization prevents memorization bias:** Medium. The mechanism is sound, but evidence is indirect (negative controls). No head-to-head ablation with raw tickers.
*   **GNN captures predictive inter-stock relations:** Medium. The strong ablation effect (Sharpe drop to 0.62) supports its necessity, but the specific value of semantic edges over pure sector edges is unproven and potentially unstable.
*   **PPO-DSR reduces turnover while preserving returns:** Low. Turnover reduction is clear, but the causal role of the intent-mode system is opaque, and the policy's internal logic is not validated.
*   **Regime-dependent performance:** High. Explicit 2024 vs. 2025 YTD comparison shows a stark, repeated performance divergence.

## Next Checks
1.  **Anonymization Ablation:** Re-run the full pipeline with raw tickers (no entity masking) in parallel with the anonymized version. Quantify performance difference.
2.  **Semantic Edge Ablation:** Train the GNN with only sector edges and only semantic edges (thresholded), comparing against the hybrid and no-edge versions. Measure impact on IC stability and Sharpe variance.
3.  **Intent-Mode Correlation Analysis:** Compute the daily correlation between RL intent mode switches (defensive/aggressive) and key external market variables (e.g., VIX change, previous day's market return). Assess if transitions align with recognized risk regimes.
