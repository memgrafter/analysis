# Retrieval-Augmented Anatomical Guidance for Text-to-CT Generation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08305
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08305
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** Retrieval-augmented approach reduces 2.5D FID from 0.527 to 0.303 and improves CT-Net AUC from 0.745 to 0.787 for text-to-CT generation on CT-RATE.

## Executive Summary
Text-to-CT generation suffers from spatial ambiguity due to the absence of explicit anatomical constraints. This work proposes a retrieval-augmented method: given a radiology report, a 3D vision-language encoder retrieves a semantically similar clinical case, and its segmentation mask serves as a coarse anatomical proxy. The proxy is injected into a frozen latent diffusion model via a ControlNet branch, steering the generation toward anatomically plausible volumes without requiring target annotations. On the CT-RATE dataset, the method significantly improves image fidelity (2.5D FID 0.303 vs. 0.527), clinical consistency (CT-Net AUC 0.787 vs. 0.745, precision 0.535 vs. 0.477), and spatial controllability (Dice 0.772, HD95 3.072). Ablations confirm that gains are contingent on retrieval quality: nearest-neighbor proxies yield consistent improvements, while random or farthest selections degrade performance.

## Method Summary
The pipeline starts with dataset preparation: CT-RATE scans are resampled to 512×512×128 voxels, Hounsfield units clipped to [-1000,1000] and normalized to [0,1]; anatomical masks are generated using TotalSegmentator. A frozen 3D vision-language encoder embeds radiology reports; a retrieval index (e.g., FAISS) over training embeddings enables nearest-neighbor search based on cosine similarity. At inference, the nearest neighbor’s mask becomes the anatomical proxy.

The generation model consists of a frozen latent diffusion backbone (VAE + UNet) and a trainable ControlNet branch. The backbone denoises in the VAE latent space conditioned on the report embedding. The ControlNet branch processes the noisy latent, text embedding, and retrieved mask, producing multi-scale feature residuals that are zero‑initialized and added to the backbone’s skip connections and bottleneck. Only the ControlNet branch and projection layers are trained (rectified flow objective); the backbone and vision‑language encoder remain frozen. Inference uses both text and mask conditioning with guidance scales (text: 5.0, ControlNet: 1.0) for 30 steps. Two critical unknowns remain: the exact mechanism for mask integration (e.g., concatenation vs. cross‑attention) and the unavailability of pretrained weights, necessitating expensive retraining.

## Key Results
- **Image fidelity:** 2.5D FID reduced from 0.527 (text‑only) to 0.303 (RAG‑nearest).
- **Clinical consistency:** CT-Net AUC increased from 0.745 to 0.787; precision from 0.477 to 0.535.
- **Spatial controllability:** Dice 0.772, HD95 3.072; retrieval quality is critical (nearest best, random/farthest worse).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
*Mechanism 1: Retrieval quality governs clinical consistency and spatial controllability.*  
- **Claim:** The quality of the retrieved anatomical proxy directly governs the improvement in clinical consistency and spatial controllability.  
- **Mechanism:** A 3D vision‑language encoder maps reports into an embedding space where semantic similarity (cosine) correlates with coarse anatomical patterns. Retrieving the nearest neighbor provides a segmentation mask that acts as a spatial scaffold, biasing diffusion toward globally coherent anatomy while allowing semantic variation.  
- **Core assumption:** Semantic similarity in the joint embedding space reliably proxies for coarse spatial anatomical similarity.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states gains contingent on retrieval quality; section 2 formalizes retrieval; section 4 tables show degradation with farthest/random selections.  
- **Break condition:** If the embedding space misaligns semantically similar reports with anatomically dissimilar volumes, the proxy becomes misleading.

*Mechanism 2: ControlNet injection enables anatomical guidance without retraining the backbone or requiring target annotations.*  
- **Claim:** Injecting the proxy via a ControlNet branch allows guidance without retraining the text‑conditioned diffusion backbone and without needing target annotations at inference.  
- **Mechanism:** A trainable ControlNet branch `E_ψ` processes the noisy latent, text embedding, and retrieved mask `m`. Its multi‑scale features are zero‑initialized and added as residuals to the frozen backbone’s skip connections and bottleneck, modulating denoising toward structures in `m` while preserving text‑driven semantics.  
- **Core assumption:** The frozen backbone’s denoising process can be effectively steered by additive residuals from a coarse mask without catastrophic forgetting.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 2 detail injection; section 3 confirms backbone frozen during ControlNet training.  
- **Break condition:** If backbone representations are not linearly steerable or the mask is too coarse, residual injection may fail to guide global anatomy or distort content.

*Mechanism 3: Retrieval‑augmented generation bridges semantic expressiveness and anatomical precision.*  
- **Claim:** The system bridges the gap between semantic expressiveness (text‑only) and anatomical precision (mask‑only) by treating anatomy as a latent, retrievable prior.  
- **Mechanism:** Decouples semantic conditioning (text → report embedding → backbone) from anatomical conditioning (report → retrieved mask → ControlNet). The retrieved mask is a proxy from a different case, not the target. This allows synthesis of a volume semantically aligned with text and spatially consistent with a plausible anatomical layout.  
- **Core assumption:** A plausible but non‑identical layout from a retrieved case suffices to constrain the space of implausible volumes toward a realistic manifold.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes integration without target annotations; section 1 contrasts semantic vs. spatial control; section 4 shows RAG‑Nearest outperforms text‑only and approaches mask‑only upper bound on spatial metrics.  
- **Break condition:** If the retrieval corpus distribution is narrow or misaligned with test reports (e.g., all healthy but report describes pathology), the proxy may enforce incorrect constraints.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Latent Diffusion Models (LDM)**  
  *Why needed:* The backbone operates in a compressed VAE latent space to make volumetric generation computationally feasible.  
  *Quick check question:* What is the purpose of the VAE encoder/decoder in a latent diffusion pipeline for 3D CT?

- **Concept: ControlNet for Conditional Generation**  
  *Why needed:* The anatomical guidance mechanism relies on ControlNet’s zero‑initialized branching and residual feature injection to a frozen model, avoiding full retraining.  
  *Quick check question:* Why are the ControlNet branch’s weights zero‑initialized at the start of training?

- **Concept: Vision‑Language Pretraining (e.g., CLIP)**  
  *Why needed:* The shared embedding space (`ϕ(·)`) is fundamental for retrieving anatomically relevant cases from text; it assumes reports and volumes with similar clinical content have proximal embeddings.  
  *Quick check question:* What objective function aligns 3D CT volumes and radiology reports in the same embedding space in this work?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Retrieval Module (3D vision‑language encoder `ϕ`, report embedding `c_r`, reference corpus of `(report, mask)` pairs, nearest neighbor search) → ControlNet Branch (trainable encoder `E_ψ` taking `(z_t, t, c_r, m)`, multi‑scale features `(s_ψ^ℓ, b_ψ)`, zero‑initialized projections `g_ℓ(·)`, `g_mid(·)`) → Diffusion Backbone (frozen U‑Net `E_θ, D_θ`, VAE encoder/decoder, text conditioning via `c_r`) → Inference Pipeline (Report → `c_r` → Retrieve `m` → `E_ψ(z_t, t, c_r, m)` → compute residuals → inject into `E_θ` → sample `z_0` → VAE decode → CT volume).

- **Critical path:** Input Report → ϕ(report) → Nearest Neighbor Search → Retrieved Mask m → ControlNet(E_ψ) → Residual Calculation → Injection into Frozen U‑Net → Denoising Steps → Generated CT.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Retrieval corpus size vs speed:* Larger corpus improves proxy quality but increases search latency; approximate nearest neighbor search may be needed.  
  - *Mask resolution vs guidance granularity:* Higher resolution gives precise control but increases memory; downsampled masks are coarser but more efficient.  
  - *Conditioning scale γ:* Higher γ enforces stricter mask adherence (better Dice) but risks overriding text semantics (worse CT‑Net); requires tuning.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Retrieval failure:* Nearest neighbor semantically dissimilar (e.g., normal abdomen for lung nodule report); monitor by visualizing retrieved reports/masks; manifests as low CT‑Net AUC and high HD95.  
  - *Mask‑backbone mismatch:* ControlNet injects features the backbone cannot interpret, causing artifacts; check training loss—if residuals remain near zero, learning may be stuck.  
  - *Semantic drift:* Generated volume reflects retrieved case’s pathology more than input report; if Dice is high but CT‑Net AUC is near text‑only baseline, mask prior overwhelms text condition.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline replication:** Reproduce the text‑only backbone (Text‑to‑CT [22]) on CT‑RATE to establish FID/CT‑Net/Dice baselines; verify pretrained weights load correctly.  
  2. **Retrieval quality ablation:** Implement the three proxy selection strategies (Nearest, Random, Farthest) without training ControlNet (just use mask as conditioning) to isolate the information value of the proxy from the learning capacity.  
  3. **Spatial controllability stress test:** Force retrieval of a mask with severe anatomical variation (e.g., large tumor, missing organ) and evaluate if the generator faithfully copies it (high Dice) but violates the text (low CT‑Net), confirming the scaffold’s influence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What specific characteristics in the vision‑language embedding space define a "semantically aligned" retrieval proxy that leads to improved generation?**  
   *Basis:* The paper states that "semantically nearest proxies yielding the most consistent improvements" but does not disentangle which report terms, anatomical concepts, or pathological descriptions are predictive of high‑quality proxies.  
   *Why unresolved:* They use a single frozen encoder and only ablate retrieval distance, not semantic features.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Probing the embedding space (e.g., concept activation vectors) to identify dimensions correlated with generation success; controlled studies where retrieval is constrained to reports sharing specific keywords/pathologies.

2. **How does the model automatically balance adherence to the retrieved anatomical scaffold against textual semantic constraints when they are in conflict?**  
   *Basis:* The ControlNet branch injects the proxy as guidance with fixed conditioning scale 1.0; the paper notes the proxy is a "coarse scaffold" allowing "local variations," but the mechanism for resolving tension is not studied.  
   *Why unresolved:* No experiments with mismatched report‑proxy pairs; fixed scale assumes optimal trade‑off without characterizing its nature.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Testing with deliberately mismatched pairs and measuring outcome (e.g., lesion segmentation accuracy); ablating conditioning scale per‑sample to see adaptive strategies.

3. **To what extent does the proposed retrieval‑augmented approach generalize to anatomical domains beyond the chest?**  
   *Basis:* All experiments on chest CT‑RATE using TotalSegmentator masks; conclusion mentions "pathology‑specific evaluation" as future work, implicitly acknowledging domain limitations.  
   *Why unresolved:* Chest anatomy has relatively consistent topology; performance on highly variable regions (e.g., abdomen) or different segmentation protocols unknown.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Repeating experiments on other public 3D medical datasets (abdomen CTs, brain MRIs) with reports and segmentation masks; testing whether the vision‑language encoder remains semantically meaningful across body regions.

4. **Can the quality of the anatomical proxy be improved by moving beyond a single nearest‑neighbor retrieval?**  
   *Basis:* The paper uses deterministic nearest‑neighbor retrieval and ablates random and farthest, finding nearest is best; the conclusion notes retrieval quality is key but does not explore enhancements.  
   *Why unresolved:* Nearest‑neighbor is a simple baseline; multi‑retrieval, aggregation, or re‑ranking could provide more robust scaffolds, especially when top match is suboptimal.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Implementing alternative strategies: k‑nearest neighbor masks (averaging/majority vote), cross‑encoder re‑ranker, retrieval from a curated subset of prototype cases; measuring impact on evaluation axes.

5. **Does the anatomical guidance from a retrieved proxy translate to meaningful improvements in downstream clinical tasks compared to text‑only generated CTs?**  
   *Basis:* Evaluation uses surrogate metrics (CT‑Net classifier, automated segmentation); no evaluation of generated data’s utility for real clinical AI tasks or radiologist assessment.  
   *Why unresolved:* The ultimate goal is data augmentation and simulation; FID and CT‑Net AUC are indirect proxies that may not capture clinically relevant details.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Training downstream models (e.g., pathology detectors) on datasets augmented with RAG‑generated vs. text‑only CTs and comparing real‑world performance; conducting a radiologist reader study scoring realism, anatomical correctness, and report alignment.

## Limitations
- Mask integration specifics are ambiguous, hindering exact replication of the ControlNet branch.
- Pretrained weights for the diffusion backbone and 3D vision‑language encoder are unavailable, forcing costly retraining.
- The method critically depends on retrieval quality and is evaluated only on chest CTs, limiting generalizability to other body regions or pathologies.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (retrieval quality governs improvement): **High** (supported by nearest/farthest ablations).
- **Mechanism 2** (ControlNet injection enables guidance without retraining): **Medium** (architectural specifics remain unclear).
- **Mechanism 3** (retrieval‑augmented bridges semantic and anatomical control): **High** (empirical results strongly support).

## Next Checks
- Perform retrieval sensitivity analysis: bin test samples by cosine similarity of retrieved reports and correlate with FID/CT‑Net/Dice to directly quantify impact of retrieval quality.
- Conduct mask‑integration ablation: compare alternative conditioning strategies (e.g., concatenation, cross‑attention) against the reported method to identify the most effective architecture.
- Test cross‑domain generalization: apply the pipeline to a different anatomy (e.g., abdominal CTs from LiTS) using a suitable retrieval corpus to evaluate transferability beyond chest CTs.
