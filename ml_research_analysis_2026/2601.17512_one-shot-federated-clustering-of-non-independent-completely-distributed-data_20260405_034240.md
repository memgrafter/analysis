# One-Shot Federated Clustering of Non-Independent Completely Distributed Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.17512
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17512
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: GOLD consistently outperforms SOTA federated clustering methods, achieving >10% higher Purity/ARI under severe Non-ICD conditions.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of federated clustering with Non-Independent Completely Distributed (Non-ICD) data, where clusters are split across clients in a way that both fragments clusters and aligns them at different granularities. The proposed GOLD framework operates in a single communication round: clients locally apply Fine-grained Competitive Penalized Learning (FCPL) to discover compact micro‑clusters and upload only their centroids to the server. The server then uses Multi‑granular Competitive Penalized Learning (MCPL) to recursively integrate these centroids into a hierarchy of global distributions, followed by Representation Enhancement (REMC) that fuses the multi‑granular structures into the final clustering without needing to specify the number of clusters. Evaluations on ten real‑world datasets using six validity indices show that GOLD significantly outperforms eight state‑of‑the‑art federated clustering methods, with improvements exceeding 10% in Purity and ARI under severe Non-ICD conditions.

## Method Summary
GOLD consists of three sequential phases. On the client side, Fine-grained Competitive Penalized Learning (FCPL) is applied to each local dataset. FCPL starts with a large initial number of clusters and uses a competitive mechanism with reward and penalty terms to gradually eliminate redundant clusters, yielding a set of compact centroids that represent local micro‑clusters. Only these centroids are transmitted to the server, achieving a one‑shot communication pattern. On the server, the received centroids are aggregated into a global dataset. Multi‑granular Competitive Penalized Learning (MCPL) then recursively processes this dataset: at the finest level, it applies the same competitive learning with a large k0 to obtain an initial partition; successive coarser partitions are derived by reinitializing with the number of clusters from the previous level and repeating the process until the cluster number stabilizes and the objective converges. The resulting multi‑granular affiliation matrices are fed into Representation Enhancement (REMC), which encodes each granularity as a separate feature in an enhanced representation and alternately updates feature‑cluster weights and cluster assignments to produce the final global clustering. Notably, GOLD does not require the true number of clusters as input.

## Key Results
- GOLD achieves the highest average rank across six clustering validity metrics (Purity, ARI, NMI, ACC, SC, CH) when compared against eight federated clustering baselines on ten real‑world datasets.
- Under simulated severe Non-ICD conditions (high λ), GOLD maintains robust performance, with less than 5% drop in Purity and ARI, while baselines suffer drops exceeding 15%, resulting in >10% absolute improvement.
- Ablation studies reveal that removing either FCPL or MCPL, or discarding individual granularity levels within REMC, leads to substantial performance degradation, confirming the synergistic design of all three components.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: FCPL for Local Micro-Cluster Discovery
- **Claim**: FCPL discovers compact micro-clusters from local Non-ICD data without prior knowledge of the number of clusters and handles non‑adjacent subclusters.
- **Mechanism**: Starting with a large set of candidate clusters, data points are assigned to winning clusters based on a weighted similarity measure. The winner is rewarded (weight increment) and its nearest rival is penalized (weight decrement proportional to similarity ratio). A feature‑cluster importance matrix H is updated using inter‑cluster Hellinger distance and intra‑cluster similarity, refining subsequent similarity calculations. Clusters with weights near zero are eliminated.
- **Core assumption**: Local data contains dense micro‑clusters separable through competition, and feature importance can discriminatively characterize clusters.
- **Evidence anchors**: The abstract mentions FCPL for micro‑cluster discovery; Section III‑B provides full algorithmic details including competition and feature‑cluster updates. The paper does not provide external validation for FCPL beyond its own experiments.
- **Break condition**: If local clusters are extremely overlapping or features are uninformative, competition may fail to eliminate redundant clusters or incorrectly merge distinct subclusters; performance degrades when the initial k0 is too small to capture micro‑structure.

### Mechanism 2: MCPL for Multi‑Granular Global Aggregation
- **Claim**: MCPL recursively aggregates client centroids into multi‑granular global distributions, automatically determining the number of clusters at each granularity.
- **Mechanism**: MCPL first aggregates all client centroids into a global dataset. It then applies CPL with a large k0 to obtain the finest‑grained partition (yielding k1 clusters). For coarser granularities, it reinitializes with the previous stage’s cluster number but with randomly sampled centroids, then reapplies CPL. Recursion stops when cluster number stabilizes and the objective change falls below a threshold ϵ.
- **Core assumption**: The global distribution exhibits a hierarchical structure where coarser clusters emerge from merging finer ones; reinitialization prevents overfitting to the finer structure and encourages independent discovery at each level.
- **Evidence anchors**: The abstract describes MCPL; Section III‑C details the recursion and convergence criteria (Algorithm 1). The approach is contrasted with prior hierarchical federated clustering methods like FedUC.
- **Break condition**: If the data lacks a clear hierarchical structure, recursion may produce arbitrary partitions without semantic meaning; sensitivity to initialization could lead to inconsistent granularities across runs.

### Mechanism 3: REMC for Representation Enhancement
- **Claim**: REMC encodes multi‑granular cluster structures into an enhanced representation and learns feature‑cluster weights to produce final clustering without requiring the true number of clusters k*.
- **Mechanism**: For each granularity δ, REMC encodes the object‑cluster affiliation matrix Q_δ as the δ‑th feature of the enhanced representation X^(e). It then alternates between: (1) updating a feature‑cluster weight matrix U using inter‑cluster Hellinger distance (α) and intra‑cluster matching rate (β); and (2) reassigning clusters based on weighted similarity to centroids. This process refines the final partition.
- **Core assumption**: Multi‑granular affiliations provide complementary information; weighting features per cluster based on discriminative power and compactness yields a robust representation.
- **Evidence anchors**: The abstract outlines REMC; Section III‑D gives full equations and optimization steps. The concept of feature weighting in clustering is supported by prior work (e.g., reference [51]).
- **Break condition**: If multi‑granular structures are highly conflicting, the encoding may introduce noise; alternating optimization may converge to poor local optima if the initial U or Q is unlucky.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Competitive Penalized Learning (CPL)**
  - *Why needed*: CPL is the core algorithm that enables automatic cluster number determination through competitive reward and rival penalization; both FCPL and MCPL build upon it.
  - *Quick check*: How does rival penalization eliminate redundant clusters? By reducing the weight of clusters that frequently lose competitions, eventually forcing their elimination.
- **Concept: Non-Independent Completely Distributed (Non-ICD) Data**
  - *Why needed*: This term defines the specific challenge of cluster fragmentation and granularity misalignment across clients, which is more severe than standard Non‑IID data and limits existing federated clustering methods.
  - *Quick check*: What are the two conditions that define Non‑ICD data according to Definition 1? 1) Granularity misalignment: different clients hold subclusters of the same global cluster at different granular levels. 2) Non‑adjacent subclusters: subclusters from the same global cluster appear in non‑adjacent regions within a client.
- **Concept: Multi‑Granular Clustering**
  - *Why needed*: GOLD leverages the insight that clusters naturally exist at multiple granularities; MCPL explores this hierarchy and REMC fuses the information to achieve robust global clustering.
  - *Quick check*: Why does MCPL reinitialize centroids at each recursion instead of inheriting the previous centroids? To avoid overfitting to the finer structure and allow each granularity level to independently discover its optimal partition.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
- Client-side: FCPL module (input: local dataset X^(l); output: cluster centroids C^(l))
- Server-side: Aggregation (collects all C^(l) → global dataset X), MCPL module (input: X; output: multi‑granular affiliation matrices {Q_δ}), REMC module (input: {Q_δ}; output: final clustering Q)

### Critical Path
1. Each client runs FCPL on its local data.
2. Clients upload only centroids to the server (one‑shot communication).
3. Server aggregates centroids into a global dataset.
4. Server runs MCPL recursively to obtain multi‑granular affiliation matrices.
5. Server encodes these matrices via REMC and performs alternating optimization to generate final cluster assignments.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **One‑shot communication vs. iterative**: Minimizes privacy leakage and communication cost but eliminates corrective feedback loops that could refine results.
- **Over‑specified initialization (k0 = 0.5n)**: Ensures enough candidate clusters to capture micro‑structures but increases computational overhead.
- **Recursive reinitialization in MCPL**: Promotes exploration of distinct granularities but may require additional iterations to converge.

### Failure Signatures
- **FCPL failure**: Many clusters retain non‑zero weights, or local centroids are highly overlapping, indicating poor local structure discovery (common when λ is high and data is extremely fragmented).
- **MCPL failure**: Recursion yields nearly identical cluster numbers across levels, or the objective does not decrease, suggesting the global distribution lacks hierarchical structure or CPL parameters are unsuitable.
- **REMC failure**: Feature weights U become degenerate (e.g., all but one granularity near zero), signaling conflicting multi‑granular information or dominance by a single level.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Performance comparison**: GOLD vs. eight federated clustering baselines on ten datasets using six validity indices; GOLD achieves highest average ranks and >10% Purity/ARI improvement under severe Non‑ICD.
2. **Impact of Non‑ICD degree λ**: Vary λ; GOLD shows slight performance decline while baselines degrade sharply, demonstrating robustness.
3. **Ablation studies**: (a) Replace FCPL or MCPL with k‑means – performance drops significantly. (b) Remove individual granularity levels from REMC – performance degrades, confirming each level's contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Streaming and continual learning in IoT**  
   *Basis*: The conclusion mentions "the next avenue of this work includes the FC under streaming and continual IoT scenarios".  
   *Why unresolved*: GOLD is designed for static, one‑shot clustering and cannot handle sequential data or evolving cluster structures.  
   *What evidence would resolve*: An extended GOLD that processes data incrementally with mechanisms to update clusters without full retraining, validated on temporal benchmarks.

2. **Federated clustering of heterogeneous modalities**  
   *Basis*: The conclusion identifies "FC of heterogeneous modalities beyond tabular data" as future work.  
   *Why unresolved*: Current GOLD relies on Euclidean distance and feature‑based similarity, which are insufficient for complex data like images, text, or time series; representation learning is needed.  
   *What evidence would resolve*: Extensions of GOLD that incorporate deep neural networks or modality‑specific encoders to extract meaningful features, with experimental validation on multi‑modal datasets.

3. **Higher‑level privacy protections**  
   *Basis*: The conclusion lists "dedicated higher‑level privacy protection in FC" as a future direction.  
   *Why unresolved*: GOLD uses one‑shot communication to reduce leakage but lacks formal privacy guarantees (e.g., differential privacy or secure multiparty computation); integrating such mechanisms may affect clustering quality.  
   *What evidence would resolve*: Implementations of GOLD with DP noise injection or cryptographic protocols, accompanied by privacy budget analysis and empirical studies on privacy‑utility trade‑offs.

4. **Automatic determination of the number of clusters k***  
   *Basis*: The paper claims GOLD "does not require the true number of clusters" but all experiments assume ground‑truth k* for evaluation.  
   *Why unresolved*: The algorithm does not describe how k* is inferred in practice; no evaluation under unknown k* is provided, leaving the automatic selection capability unverified.  
   *What evidence would resolve*: Ablations where GOLD selects k* using internal validity indices (e.g., Silhouette, Calinski‑Harabasz) on the multi‑granular representations, with results compared to methods requiring true k*; also a detailed analysis of the recursion termination condition's relation to true k*.

## Limitations
- GOLD relies on a heuristic initial k0 (0.5n) without theoretical justification; its sensitivity across datasets and Non‑ICD degrees remains unexplored.
- The claim of automatic cluster number determination via REMC is not substantiated by experiments that omit true k*; the mechanism for selecting final k is ambiguous.
- Core Competitive Penalized Learning lacks independent validation; its isolated contribution relative to standard clustering (e.g., k‑means) is unclear, making it difficult to assess the true source of performance gains.

## Confidence
- Overall SOTA performance: High
- FCPL micro‑cluster discovery: Medium
- MCPL multi‑granular integration: Medium
- REMC automatic k determination: Low
- One‑shot communication: High

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis**: Vary k0 (0.3–0.7n), learning rate η (0.01–0.2), penalization λ, and convergence threshold ϵ across synthetic Non‑ICD datasets to determine robust defaults and identify failure thresholds.
2. **Blind k* selection test**: Run GOLD without providing true k*; use internal validity indices (e.g., Silhouette, Calinski‑Harabasz) to select the final partition from the multi‑granular outputs; compare the chosen k to ground truth and measure resulting clustering quality.
3. **CPL core ablation**: Replace the CPL algorithm within FCPL/MCPL with a standard competitive learning approach (e.g., k‑means with a similar penalty scheme) while keeping the rest of GOLD unchanged; evaluate performance drop to isolate the contribution of the specific CPL design.
