# ProMoral-Bench: Evaluating Prompting Strategies for Moral Reasoning and Safety in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13274
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13274
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Compact, exemplar-guided prompting strategies (e.g., Few-Shot) achieve the highest Unified Moral Safety Score (UMSS), outperforming verbose multi-stage reasoning across accuracy, calibration, jailbreak resistance, and token efficiency.

## Executive Summary
ProMoral-Bench introduces a unified benchmark to evaluate prompting strategies for LLM moral reasoning and safety. It assesses 11 strategies across four model families using four datasets, including a new robustness set (ETHICS-Contrast). Performance is measured by the Unified Moral Safety Score (UMSS), which harmonizes moral competency and safety robustness. Findings show that concise, exemplar-based scaffolds (e.g., Few-Shot, Plan-and-Solve) dominate, providing superior accuracy, calibration, adversarial resilience, and efficiency. In contrast, verbose multi-turn strategies (e.g., Thought Experiment) are brittle under minimal perturbations and computationally inefficient.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 11 fixed prompt templates (e.g., Zero-Shot, Few-Shot, Plan-and-Solve) across four datasets: ETHICS (binary acceptability), Scruples (multi-class social dilemmas), WildJailbreak (adversarial safety), and the new ETHICS-Contrast (minimal-edit robustness pairs). Four commercial LLM families (GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet-4, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-V3) are queried with deterministic decoding (temperature=0). Outputs are parsed via regex to extract labels and confidence scores. Metrics include accuracy, F1, expected calibration error (ECE), Brier score, attack success rate (ASR), refusal-to-answer rate (RTA), and token count. The Unified Moral Safety Score (UMSS) is computed as the harmonic mean of a normalized Moral Competence Score (MCS, averaging performance on ETHICS, Scruples, and ETHICS-Contrast) and a normalized Safety Robustness Score (SRS, averaging safe metrics from WildJailbreak). All metrics are min-max normalized across the full model-strategy grid before combination.

## Key Results
- Compact, exemplar-guided scaffolds (Few-Shot, Few-Shot-CoT, Plan-and-Solve) achieve the highest UMSS, balancing high competence and safety.
- These strategies also deliver the best accuracy, calibration (low ECE/Brier), strongest jailbreak resistance (low ASR), and lowest token consumption.
- Verbose multi-stage strategies (Thought Experiment, Self-Correct) show significant performance drops on minimal perturbations (ETHICS-Contrast), poor calibration, high token usage (>10k tokens/example), and an inverse token-UMSS relationship.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Exemplar-guided prompts (Few-Shot, Few-Shot-CoT) improve moral stability and jailbreak resistance by providing clear decision boundaries.
- **Mechanism:** Fixed demonstrations anchor the model's judgment to validated examples, reducing over-reliance on surface-level heuristics and stabilizing output distributions against perturbations.
- **Core assumption:** Models generalize from demonstrated patterns more reliably than from abstract instructions alone, especially under adversarial conditions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Few-shot exemplars consistently enhance moral stability and jailbreak resistance."
  - [section 4.1/4.2/4.4]: Few-Shot/Few-Shot-CoT achieve top or near-top accuracy, calibration (low ECE/Brier), and lowest ASR across ETHICS, Scruples, and WildJailbreak.
  - [corpus]: Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers explore value grounding but do not test exemplar scaffolding comparative efficacy.
- **Break condition:** If demonstrations contain systematic biases or non-representative edge cases, boundary clarification may instead propagate those biases.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Verbose multi-stage reasoning (Thought Experiment, Self-Correct) degrades performance under minimal perturbations due to accumulated noise and hindsight bias.
- **Mechanism:** Extended generation chains increase stochastic variance and self-generated context, causing "reasoning drift" where the model loses alignment with the core moral question across turns.
- **Core assumption:** Moral judgment benefits from constrained, focused deliberation; additional unconstrained reasoning steps introduce compounded errors rather than refinement.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Multi-turn strategies prove brittle under perturbations and computationally wasteful."
  - [section 4.3/4.5]: Verbose strategies show largest accuracy drops (∆Acc) on ETHICS-Contrast and poor calibration (high ECE), despite massive token consumption (>10k tokens).
  - [section 4.6]: Inverse token-UMSS relationship (LSRL slope −4.3×10⁻⁵).
- **Break condition:** If the base model has extreme hallucination or coherence failures in short contexts, multi-stage refinement *might* help, but no evidence suggests this for the evaluated families.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Compact scaffolds (Plan-and-Solve, Role Prompting) improve calibration and efficiency by minimizing decision entropy through structured but concise constraint.
- **Mechanism:** Brief upfront planning or role framing externalizes criteria, narrowing the hypothesis space before commitment without verbose elaboration that introduces hindsight bias.
- **Core assumption:** Moral safety and accuracy scale with *effective* constraint, not verbosity; low-variance prompts yield better probability calibration.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Compact, exemplar-guided scaffolds... outperform verbose multi-stage reasoning in accuracy, calibration, jailbreak resistance, and token efficiency."
  - [section 4.1]: Plan-and-Solve (GPT-4.1: ECE 0.031) and Few-Shot (Claude: ECE 0.091) achieve best calibration among top accuracy runs at moderate token counts (~500-1000).
  - [section 4.6]: Role Prompting achieves UMSS 0.756 at only 572 tokens/example.
- **Break condition:** If the task deeply requires multi-faceted trade-off analysis (e.g., complex legal ethics), overly compact scaffolds may omit necessary considerations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Unified Moral Safety Score (UMSS)
  - **Why needed:** Provides a single, harmonic-mean metric to compare strategies across competence (accuracy on ETHICS/Scruples/Contrast) and safety (WildJailbreak ASR/RTA), penalizing imbalance.
  - **Quick check:** Why use harmonic mean instead of arithmetic mean for UMSS? (Answer: Harmonic mean penalizes extreme imbalance between competence and safety, ensuring robust strategies rank higher.)
- **Concept:** Minimal-edit robustness (ETHICS-Contrast)
  - **Why needed:** Tests consistency under controlled perturbations; a strategy that flips judgments inappropriately on minimal edits is brittle, even if overall accuracy is high.
  - **Quick check:** What does a large negative ∆Acc on ETHICS-Contrast indicate about a prompting strategy? (Answer: High sensitivity to superficial changes, suggesting overfitting to dataset-specific features.)
- **Concept:** Token efficiency as a first-class metric
  - **Why needed:** Verbose strategies consume disproportionate resources; efficiency is crucial for real-world deployment. The paper shows inverse UMSS-token relationship.
  - **Quick check:** According to Figure 1, what is the approximate token threshold above which strategies rarely achieve UMSS > 0.75? (Answer: ~1,500 tokens/example.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - Datasets (ETHICS, Scruples, WildJailbreak, ETHICS-Contrast) -> Models (GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet-4, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-V3) -> Strategies (11 prompt templates) -> Output parsing (regex) -> Metrics (Accuracy, ECE, ASR, tokens) -> UMSS computation (normalization + harmonic mean).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Prepare prompt templates for all 11 strategies and 4 datasets.
  2. For each (model, strategy, dataset) pair, run inference with deterministic decoding, parse outputs via regex.
  3. Compute raw metrics against gold labels/judges.
  4. Min-max normalize all metrics across the full (model, strategy) grid.
  5. Compute MCS (avg over ETHICS, Scruples, Contrast flip accuracy) and SRS (avg of 1-ASR, 1-RTA from WildJailbreak).
  6. Combine MCS and SRS into UMSS with β=1.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Within-distribution demonstrations:** Few-shot exemplars are drawn from the same datasets they evaluate, potentially inflating performance. Tradeoff: controlled comparison vs. generalization realism.
  - **Deterministic decoding (temp=0):** Ensures reproducibility but eliminates exploration of stochastic variance. Tradeoff: stable metrics vs. missing robustness to sampling.
  - **UMSS normalization:** Min-max normalization makes scores relative to the evaluated set; a new model/strategy could change the scale. Tradeoff: comparability within study vs. absolute interpretability.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High token count (>2,000) with low UMSS (<0.5) → verbose strategy failure.
  - Large |∆Acc| on ETHICS-Contrast (>0.05) → brittleness to surface edits.
  - High ECE (>0.2) on classification tasks → poor confidence calibration, regardless of accuracy.
  - For WildJailbreak: ASR > 0.3 *and* RTA > 0.25 → unsafe or over-refusing (context-dependent).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate Table 6 (UMSS by strategy) on a new model (e.g., Llama-3.1) to test cross-family generalizability of "compact scaffolds win."
  2. Run the sensitivity analysis from Appendix B on your top-performing strategy—ablate exemplar diversity or role framing to check robustness to prompt micro-variations.
  3. Evaluate your OwnPrompt™: design a new hybrid strategy (e.g., Few-Shot + Plan-and-Solve) and measure its UMSS and token cost against the paper's top 3 (Few-Shot, Few-Shot-CoT, Role Prompting).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do performance metrics and rankings vary under different sampling strategies or multiple random runs?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section states: "Due to computational constraints ... we report single-run results with deterministic sampling (temperature=0). While this ensures reproducibility, our findings lack error bars or significance tests that would quantify variability across runs or sampling strategies."
- **Why unresolved:** Single-run evaluations cannot capture the stochastic variability inherent in LLM outputs even at temperature=0 due to architectural nondeterminism or minor API fluctuations, making it unclear how robust the observed strategy rankings are.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Re-running key experiments with multiple seeds (if possible) or varying sampling parameters (e.g., top-p) to compute confidence intervals and perform statistical significance tests between strategies.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent do the reported advantages of compact scaffolds generalize to newer model iterations or open-source LLMs?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section notes: "Model and temporal specificity: Results depend on specific model versions accessed via commercial APIs ... Model updates, API changes, or regional variations may affect reproducibility. Our findings may not generalize to newer releases, open-source alternatives, or models with different alignment procedures."
- **Why unresolved:** The study evaluated fixed API versions (GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet-4, etc.) at a snapshot in time; newer models may have different training or safety mitigations that alter the cost–accuracy trade-offs of prompting strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicating the benchmark on the latest versions of the same model families and on representative open-source models (e.g., Llama, Mistral) to compare strategy effectiveness across architectural and alignment differences.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How do the identified optimal prompting strategies perform in non-English linguistic contexts or across diverse cultural moral frameworks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section declares: "Linguistic and cultural scope: All datasets and prompts are in English, and moral judgments reflect predominantly Western, English-speaking norms. Findings may not transfer to non-English languages or culturally distinct moral frameworks."
- **Why unresolved:** Moral intuitions vary significantly across cultures; prompts designed for Western norms may lose efficacy or produce different trade-offs when applied to other linguistic or cultural settings, but this is untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Translating the benchmark datasets (or sourcing culturally specific dilemmas) and evaluating the same set of prompting strategies in multiple languages and cultural contexts to measure cross-lingual/cultural consistency of the UMSS rankings.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does few-shot prompting retain its relative advantage when evaluated on moral dilemmas that are truly out-of-distribution relative to the demonstration exemplars?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section cautions: "Prompt–evaluation coupling: Our few-shot demonstrations are drawn from the same datasets under evaluation. While demonstrations are fixed and non-overlapping with test instances, this within-distribution sampling may inflate few-shot performance relative to truly out-of-distribution scenarios and risks priming effects that favor exemplar-based strategies."
- **Why unresolved:** The few-shot demonstrations were sampled from ETHICS/Scruples themselves, so performance gains might not hold for moral scenarios from entirely different sources (e.g., legal cases, historical narratives) that require generalization beyond the distribution of the exemplars.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Constructing a holdout set of moral dilemmas from a separate source (e.g., philosophical texts, cross-cultural anecdote corpora) and comparing few-shot versus compact non-exemplar strategies on that out-of-distribution test set.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** What underlying mechanisms cause multi-turn reasoning strategies (e.g., Thought Experiment, Self-Correct) to exhibit increased brittleness and calibration degradation under minimal input perturbations?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The Results section notes that verbose pipelines show larger accuracy drops on ETHICS-Contrast and higher ECE/Brier, and hypothesizes "reasoning drift: as models elaborate, they accumulate self-generated context and lose track of core moral question, amplifying stochastic deviations and hindsight bias." However, this mechanistic explanation is not directly tested.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper observes the phenomenon but does not isolate why multi-step reasoning amplifies sensitivity to minor edits—whether due to error propagation, overthinking, or loss of focus—leaving the causal pathway speculative.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablating individual turns in multi-turn strategies on contrast sets, analyzing internal representations (e.g., attention patterns) across steps for perturbation sensitivity, or comparing with human reasoning traces to see if similar drift occurs.

## Limitations
- **Model version drift:** identifiers like GPT-4.1 may be outdated or differ in weights from current API offerings.
- **Unreproduced details:** random seeds for subsampling, exact dev-set for Role Prompting, regex parsing rules, and WildJailbreak judge specifications are not fully disclosed.
- **Evaluation constraints:** within-dataset exemplars may inflate few-shot scores; deterministic decoding ignores stochastic variance; min-max UMSS normalization is relative to the current model-strategy set.

## Confidence
- **High:** Empirical finding that compact, exemplar-guided scaffolds (Few-Shot, Plan-and-Solve) achieve the best UMSS, accuracy, calibration, and token efficiency across four model families.
- **Medium:** Proposed mechanisms (exemplar anchoring, reasoning drift, entropy reduction) that explain *why* these strategies succeed; supported by correlation but not ablation.
- **Low:** Extrapolation of these results to new models, domains, or moral frameworks beyond the tested sets.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the UMSS ranking on a held-out model family (e.g., Llama-3.1) using the same prompts and datasets.
2. Ablate exemplar diversity in Few-Shot by replacing demonstrations with out-of-distribution or adversarially selected examples; measure impact on stability and ASR.
3. Run a sensitivity analysis on the compactness threshold: systematically truncate Plan-and-Solve prompts to token budget increments and re-evaluate UMSS to locate the inflection point for diminishing returns.
