# CDMT-EHR: A Continuous-Time Diffusion Framework for Generating Mixed-Type Time-Series Electronic Health Records

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23719  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23719  
- **Reference count:** 36  
- **Primary result:** Outperforms TimeDiff with only 50 sampling steps, achieving higher downstream task AUC and distribution fidelity on MIMIC-III and eICU.

## Executive Summary
CDMT-EHR introduces a continuous-time diffusion framework to generate synthetic mixed-type time-series electronic health records (EHRs). It addresses key limitations of existing discrete-time diffusion models—finite-step approximation errors, coupled training-sampling steps, and rigid noise schedules—by leveraging a variance-exploding stochastic differential equation (SDE) in continuous time, a bidirectional GRU (BiGRU) backbone for temporal modeling, unified Gaussian diffusion via learnable categorical embeddings, and a factorized learnable noise schedule. Evaluated on MIMIC-III and eICU, the method significantly improves downstream task performance (e.g., AUC 0.751 vs. 0.689 on MIMIC-III), distribution fidelity (e.g., 77% MMD reduction on eICU), and discriminability, while using only 5% of the sampling steps required by the prior state-of-the-art (50 vs. 1000). Classifier-free guidance further enables effective conditional generation to handle class imbalance.

## Method Summary
CDMT-EHR formulates the forward diffusion process as a continuous-time variance-exploding SDE, decoupling training and sampling step counts. A BiGRU-based denoising model approximates the reverse-time score function, processing concatenated feature vectors at each timestep with FiLM conditioning on diffusion time and optional labels. Categorical variables are mapped to L2-normalized, scaled continuous embeddings, enabling unified Gaussian diffusion across all features. A factorized noise schedule ρ_f,l = ρ_global + ρ_feature[f] + ρ_time[l] adapts noise allocation per feature and timestep using only O(F+L) parameters. Training minimizes a weighted data-prediction loss (MSE for numerical, cross-entropy for categorical) with Adam and EMA. Sampling uses an Euler ODE solver with 50 steps. Classifier-free guidance is supported via random label dropout during training.

## Key Results
- Downstream task AUC substantially higher than TimeDiff: 0.751 vs. 0.689 on MIMIC-III; 0.708 vs. 0.642 on eICU.
- Distribution fidelity improved: e.g., 77% MMD reduction on eICU; lower ACF MSE and DTW.
- Sampling efficiency: high-quality generation with only 50 Euler steps vs. 1000 for TimeDiff.
- Conditional generation via classifier-free guidance effectively addresses class imbalance in minority clinical outcomes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Continuous-time diffusion with a BiGRU backbone decouples training and sampling steps while capturing temporal dependencies in mixed-type EHR sequences.
- **Mechanism:** The forward process uses a variance-exploding SDE in continuous time t∈[0,1], avoiding discrete-step approximations. The reverse process is solved by an ODE, where a BiGRU denoiser f_θ approximates the score function. The BiGRU processes concatenated feature vectors, propagating label information and temporal context via hidden states with O(F·L) complexity.
- **Core assumption:** The BiGRU can model sequential dependencies of multivariate EHR time series, and the Euler ODE solver can accurately reverse diffusion with few steps (e.g., 50).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...continuous-time diffusion framework... decouple training and sampling step counts... BiGRU backbone for capturing temporal dependencies." [section III.B] "We therefore adopt a BiGRU... At each time step, the BiGRU receives a feature vector concatenating all variables... allowing label information to naturally propagate via hidden states." [section IV.D] "During sampling... using the first-order Euler method with 50 steps."
- **Break condition:** If BiGRU capacity is insufficient for long sequences (e.g., eICU L=272) or discretization error is prohibitive at very low steps (<20), sample quality degrades.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Mapping categorical variables into a continuous embedding space and applying unified Gaussian diffusion enables joint cross-feature modeling, improving generation coherence.
- **Mechanism:** Each categorical category gets a learnable d-dimensional embedding, L2-normalized and scaled. These and numerical features undergo the same Gaussian forward process x_t = x_0 + σ(t)ϵ. The denoiser operates in a unified continuous space, learning dependencies between numerical values and categorical states at any noise level.
- **Core assumption:** The continuous embedding space is sufficiently rich and structured, and Gaussian noise does not irrecoverably destroy semantic relationships.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "unified Gaussian diffusion via learnable continuous embeddings for categorical variables, enabling joint cross-feature modeling." [section III.C] "By applying unified Gaussian diffusion to both numerical features and categorical embeddings, all features diffuse in a shared continuous space. This enables the model to jointly learn dependencies..." [section IV.F.1] Table V ablation: "Ours-Emb outperforms Ours-Mask... improvement is particularly pronounced with CFG... unified Gaussian diffusion... cross-feature dependencies are maintained during conditional generation."
- **Break condition:** If embeddings collapse or σ_emb_max is mismatched to embedding norm, categorical reconstruction fails, hurting TVD and downstream metrics.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A factorized learnable noise schedule (ρ_f,l = ρ_global + ρ_feature[f] + ρ_time[l]) adapts noise allocation per feature and timestep with O(F+L) parameters, addressing heterogeneous learning difficulties efficiently.
- **Mechanism:** The power-mean parameterization σ_ρ(t) = (σ_min^{1/ρ} + t(σ_max^{1/ρ} - σ_min^{1/ρ}))^ρ controls schedule shape. Factorized ρ_f,l lets the model learn, e.g., that a volatile feature needs more denoising steps in low-noise regions (ρ>1), or that missingness patterns at specific timesteps require different schedules.
- **Core assumption:** Learning difficulty (e.g., signal-to-noise ratio) for a feature varies smoothly over time and decomposes additively into global, feature-specific, and time-specific offsets.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "factorized learnable noise schedule that adapts to per-feature-per-timestep learning difficulties." [section III.D] "This ρ_f,l is substituted into (3)... with only O(F+L) parameters." [section IV.F.2] Table VI ablation: "The learnable schedule improves downstream task performance... with the largest gain in the unconditional setting."
- **Break condition:** If feature-time interactions are highly non-additive, the factorization may not capture local variations, reducing fidelity vs. a theoretical O(F×L) schedule.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Continuous-time diffusion (SDE/ODE)**  
  *Why needed here:* Replaces discrete DDPM's fixed training-sampling step coupling with a continuous-time reverse ODE, enabling high-quality generation with orders of magnitude fewer sampling steps (50 vs. 1000).  
  *Quick check question:* If you double the number of Euler sampling steps for CDMT-EHR, would you expect marginal improvement after 50 steps? Why or why not?

- **Concept: Recurrent neural networks for temporal modeling (BiGRU)**  
  *Why needed here:* Provides an efficient O(F·L) backbone for processing EHR sequences, allowing label information to propagate bidirectionally across time steps, critical for conditional generation (CFG) in imbalanced clinical settings.  
  *Quick check question:* Why might a Transformer backbone be less suitable here than a BiGRU for the L=272 sequence length in eICU?

- **Concept: Unified representation space for mixed-type data**  
  *Why needed here:* EHRs contain interdependent numerical (e.g., heart rate) and categorical (e.g., medication flag) features. Separate diffusion processes prevent joint modeling, while a shared continuous embedding space allows the denoiser to learn cross-type dependencies.  
  *Quick check question:* What is a key risk of using L2-normalized categorical embeddings with a Gaussian diffusion process if the embeddings are not properly scaled?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map:
Input Encoder -> Categorical Embedding Layer -> Noise Scheduler -> Diffusion Forward Process -> Denoising Backbone (BiGRU) -> Output Heads -> Reverse ODE Solver (Euler) -> Post-processing

### Critical path:
(1) Input → (2) Embedding → (4) Forward noising (train) / (7) Reverse Euler steps (sample) → (5) BiGRU denoising → (6) Output → (8) Post-process. The noise schedule (3) must be computed correctly at each Euler step during sampling.

### Design tradeoffs:
- **Parameter Efficiency vs. Fidelity:** Factorized schedule (O(F+L)) reduces overfitting risk vs. full O(F×L), but may underfit complex feature-time interactions, slightly hurting categorical TVD.
- **Sampling Speed vs. Quality:** 50 steps is a sweet spot; fewer steps (<30) likely degrade ACF MSE and downstream AUC; more steps (>100) offer diminishing returns.
- **Unified vs. Separate Diffusion:** Unified embeddings improve cross-feature modeling (higher downstream AUC) but sacrifice exact categorical marginal fidelity (higher TVD) compared to a mask-diffusion hybrid.

### Failure signatures:
- **Training:** Loss NaN (check EMA decay, learning rate, FiLM scaling).
- **Sampling:** C2ST AUC >0.7 with LSTM discriminator → temporal patterns not captured; MMD >> baseline → distribution mismatch; TVD >> 0.1 → categorical marginal collapse.
- **Conditional Generation (CFG):** No improvement on minority-class metrics → guidance weight w too low or label dropout p_drop too high.

### First 3 experiments:
1. **Sanity Check:** Train on a tiny subset (e.g., 100 samples) for 100 epochs. Verify training loss decreases and generate a few sequences; check that numerical values stay within plausible ranges and categorical indices are valid.
2. **Component Isolation:** Ablate the factorized schedule (use fixed ρ=1) and the embedding (use mask diffusion). Measure downstream AUC difference on a validation set to confirm contributions as in Tables V & VI.
3. **Sampling Step Sweep:** Generate data with {10, 20, 50, 100, 200} steps. Plot downstream AUC vs. steps to verify the 50-step optimum and detect under-sampling artifacts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can the categorical marginal distribution fidelity be improved in continuous-time diffusion models for EHR generation?** The unified Gaussian diffusion via embeddings prioritizes cross-feature dependencies over exact marginal reproduction, resulting in higher TVD.
2. **What architectural enhancements are needed to capture complex nonlinear inter-feature dependencies in generated EHR time-series?** The BiGRU backbone may lack capacity, as indicated by C2ST results where an MLP discriminator still achieves AUC ~0.59.
3. **Does CDMT-EHR generalize effectively to other large-scale EHR datasets with heterogeneous structures, such as HiRID?** Evaluation is limited to MIMIC-III and eICU; HiRID has more variables and different preprocessing.
4. **Can the continuous-time diffusion framework be adapted for other temporal clinical data domains beyond EHRs, such as wearable sensor streams or medical imaging sequences?** The current design targets mixed-type tabular EHRs; other domains may require architectural changes.

## Limitations
- Unknown implementation details (FiLM conditioning network, precise EDM preconditioning, Euler solver specifics) block faithful reproduction.
- BiGRU capacity may be insufficient for very long sequences (e.g., eICU L=272).
- Factorized noise schedule may oversimplify non-additive feature-time interactions.
- Categorical embeddings risk collapse if scaling mismatched with numerical features.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Continuous-time diffusion + BiGRU)**: Medium. Strong experimental evidence but dependent on unresolved solver details and possible sequence-length constraints.
- **Mechanism 2 (Unified embedding space)**: Medium. Ablation confirms benefit, but sensitive to embedding initialization and noise scale; collapse risk if misconfigured.
- **Mechanism 3 (Factorized learnable schedule)**: Medium. Ablation shows improvement, but factorization assumption may not capture complex local variations.
