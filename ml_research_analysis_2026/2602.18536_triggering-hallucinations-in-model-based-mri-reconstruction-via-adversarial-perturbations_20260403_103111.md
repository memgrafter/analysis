# Triggering hallucinations in model-based MRI reconstruction via adversarial perturbations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18536
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18536
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Minute, imperceptible perturbations in k-space (L∞ ε=1e-6) trigger severe, targeted hallucinations in deep MRI reconstructions, degrading metrics (e.g., UNet PSNR from 55.60 to 34.24 dB) while standard quality metrics (PSNR, NRMSE, SSIM) fail to detect them due to overlapping distributions with clean reconstructions.

## Executive Summary
Adversarial attacks on deep learning-based magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) reconstruction reveal a critical safety issue: models can be induced to hallucinate false anatomical features (e.g., artificial lines) from tiny, visually undetectable perturbations in raw k-space data. The study demonstrates this vulnerability in UNet and E2E-VarNet models trained on fastMRI data, showing that reconstruction quality metrics drop dramatically despite input perturbations remaining imperceptible. Crucially, traditional reference-free image quality metrics cannot reliably distinguish hallucinated from clean reconstructions because their distributions overlap significantly. These findings underscore the instability of learned reconstruction mappings and the inadequacy of current evaluation practices, calling for more robust architectures and certified detection methods.

## Method Summary
The attack crafting process is a white-box, targeted adversarial method that operates in the k-space domain. It performs an iterative, masked optimization (derived from BIM) on the real component of the k-space data `z`, subject to a tight L∞ constraint (ε=1e-6). The loss function simultaneously encourages the perturbed reconstruction `F(z+δ)` to match a user-defined target `y_t` (e.g., an inserted line) within a designated mask `m`, while penalizing deviations from the original reconstruction `F(z)` outside the mask. After `T=150` iterations with step size `α=1e-7`, the perturbation `δ` is applied to new samples to induce hallucinations. The method is evaluated on UNet and E2E-VarNet models using the fastMRI dataset (single-coil knee, multi-coil knee, and multi-coil brain), with success measured by degradation in PSNR, NRMSE, and SSIM between original and adversarial reconstructions, and by the overlap of these metrics with clean reconstruction pairs.

## Key Results
- For UNet on single-coil knee data, adversarial perturbations cause reconstruction PSNR to drop from 55.60 dB to 34.24 dB, NRMSE to increase from 0.01 to 0.17, and SSIM to fall from 1.00 to 0.95.
- Input perturbations are visually imperceptible (zero-filled PSNR >50 dB), yet they trigger large, structured distortions in the reconstructed image.
- Distributions of PSNR, NRMSE, and SSIM between clean and hallucinated reconstructions overlap heavily, demonstrating that these standard metrics cannot reliably detect the presence of hallucinations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Non-linear instability amplification.**  
The reconstruction neural network `F` implements a highly non-linear, ill-posed mapping from k-space to image space. Small, bounded perturbations in k-space (ε=1e-6) are amplified along directions with high condition number in this mapping, causing large, structured changes in the output. The attack exploits this sensitivity by optimizing a loss that inserts a target feature within a mask while preserving content elsewhere.

**Mechanism 2: Metric insensitivity to localized insertions.**  
Traditional global metrics (PSNR, NRMSE, SSIM) average errors over the entire image. Because the attack only modifies a small masked region and preserves most of the image structure, these metrics remain within the natural variance of clean reconstructions. Their distributions overlap significantly, making them ineffective for detection.

**Mechanism 3: Cascade of learned prior distortions.**  
When presented with an inconsistent k-space measurement, the network’s internal layers—which have learned strong anatomical priors—generate a coherent but false global explanation. This often extends distortions far beyond the inserted artifact, producing biologically plausible but incorrect structures (e.g., altered knee joint geometry) to accommodate the hallucinated feature.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
z (k-space) -> Input Handler (separate real/imaginary, clip) -> z_adv (z+δ) -> Reconstruction Engine F (UNet/E2E-VarNet) -> x̂_adv -> Loss Calculator (masked L1 vs target, L1 vs clean) -> Loss L -> Optimizer (gradient step on δ) -> clip δ to L∞ ball -> repeat for T iterations.  
Mask & Target Generator: creates binary mask `m` and target image `y_t` (with inserted artifact) for Loss Calculator.

**Critical path:**  
Initialize `δ ~ Unif(-ε, ε)`. For `t=1..T`: compute `z_adv = z+δ`; get `x̂_adv = F(z_adv)` and clean `x̂ = F(z)`; compute masked loss `L`; update `δ ← δ - α·sign(∇δL)`; clip `δ` to `∥δ∥∞ ≤ ε`. Return `δ*`.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Perturbation budget `ε` vs. Imperceptibility:** Larger `ε` increases attack success but risks visible corruption in zero-filled k-space. Paper uses `ε=1e-6` for imperceptibility.  
- **Mask size/shape vs. Stealth:** Larger mask eases insertion but forces more of the image to deviate from clean reconstruction, increasing detectability.  
- **Iterations `T` & step size `α` vs. Convergence:** More iterations with smaller `α` better minimize loss but increase cost; too large `α` may overshoot.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Final loss `L` remains large (>> 1e-8), indicating failure to match target or preserve content.  
2. Perturbation `δ*` violates `∥δ∥∞ ≤ ε` after clipping, suggesting `ε` too small.  
3. Metrics (PSNR, NRMSE, SSIM) between `F(z)` and `F(z+δ)` show no significant degradation, staying within clean distribution.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Attack Success:** Run attack on a small labeled subset (e.g., 10 knee images). Visually confirm artificial line insertion in `F(z+δ)` and check that `∥z-(z+δ)∥∞` is tiny.  
2. **Metric Degradation Validation:** For attacked samples, compute PSNR, NRMSE, SSIM on `(F(z), F(z+δ))` pairs. Quantify drop from clean metrics and plot histograms to verify separation (like Fig. 3).  
3. **Detection Attempt (Negative Result):** For same samples, compute metrics between TV reconstruction `TV(z)` and `F(z)`, and between `TV(z+δ)` and `F(z+δ)`. Plot overlapping histograms (like Fig. 5) to confirm standard metrics fail to flag adversarial k-space.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can adversarial training mitigate hallucinations?** The abstract suggests a carefully constructed adversarial training routine may reduce prevalence, but this is not implemented or evaluated. Evidence: Train models with adversarial examples from the proposed attack and measure robustness.
2. **How to develop robust detectors with provable guarantees?** Standard metrics fail; the paper calls for detectors with provable correctness, possibly using certified robustness techniques. Evidence: Design detectors using randomized smoothing or robust width and evaluate against adaptive attacks.
3. **How can compressed sensing principles yield certified defenses?** Existing mathematical results in compressed sensing may enable provable solutions not available in general settings. Evidence: Theoretical analysis linking CS assumptions (e.g., RIP) to robustness certificates for neural MRI reconstructions, with empirical validation.
4. **How do attack parameters and target complexity affect outcomes?** The study uses fixed ε=1e-6, T=150, and simple white lines. Impact of varying ε, T, mask size, and using realistic pathologies (e.g., from fastMRI+) remains unknown. Evidence: Systematic hyperparameter sweeps and target variation studies.
5. **Are vulnerabilities generalizable beyond tested architectures and modalities?** The study is limited to UNet and E2E-VarNet on fastMRI knee/brain. Generalizability to other models (e.g., diffusion) and modalities (e.g., CT) is open. Evidence: Replicate attack on diverse architectures and datasets.

## Limitations
- Exact k-space preprocessing (normalization, scaling, coil combination) is unspecified, potentially affecting reproducibility and metric values.
- Random seeds for perturbation initialization and data splits are unreported, introducing quantitative variability.
- Scope limited to two architectures (UNet, E2E-VarNet) and fastMRI datasets; only one hallucination type (artificial line) tested; white-box attack assumed.

## Confidence
- **Vulnerability:** High (strong empirical evidence; reproducibility confidence Medium due to preprocessing/seed ambiguities).
- **Metric failure:** High (consistently shown across datasets, but limited to these metrics and one hallucination type).
- **Mechanism:** Medium (supported by qualitative figures and reasoning, but lacks ablation isolating architectural components).
