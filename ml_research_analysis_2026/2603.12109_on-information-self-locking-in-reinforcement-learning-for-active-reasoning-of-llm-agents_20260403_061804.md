# On Information Self-Locking in Reinforcement Learning for Active Reasoning of LLM agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12109
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12109
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Identifies "information self-locking" in RL-trained LLM agents for multi-turn active reasoning and proposes AREW, which injects directional critiques to break the feedback loop, improving action selection and belief tracking and increasing final reward by up to 60% across algorithms and models.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning (RL) is increasingly used to train large language model (LLM) agents for active reasoning tasks that require iterative information gathering. However, these agents often exhibit "information self-locking": they cease asking informative questions and fail to incorporate new evidence into their belief state. This paper theoretically decomposes the issue into a detrimental coupling between Action Selection (AS) and Belief Tracking (BT). Weak BT masks the value of informative AS, while limited AS restricts BT improvement, trapping agents in low-information regimes. To address this, the authors introduce the Advantage Reweighting (AREW) method, which uses easily obtained directional critiques—such as whether a query elicited new evidence or a belief updated correctly—to reweight advantages within RL trajectories. Evaluated across seven datasets, AREW consistently breaks self-locking, enhances AS and BT proxies, and boosts final reward by up to 60% under PPO, GRPO, and GSPO with multiple models. The approach is robust to critique noise and fundamentally shifts training dynamics toward effective information-seeking behaviors.

## Method Summary
AREW (Advantage Reweighting) is a training intervention designed to decouple Action Selection and Belief Tracking in RL for active reasoning. It operates by injecting stepwise directional critiques into the RL objective. These critiques are binary signals indicating whether an action (query) was informative (e.g., elicited new evidence) or whether the belief state was updated correctly. During training, AREW reweights the advantages of actions within each trajectory based on these critiques, amplifying gradients for steps that contribute to information gain. This reweighting occurs within standard RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, GSPO), requiring only minimal additional supervision. The critiques are derived from environment responses or internal belief readouts, making them relatively easy to obtain in structured active reasoning tasks. AREW thus provides a simple yet effective mechanism to break the self-locking feedback loop without architectural changes.

## Key Results
- AREW breaks information self-locking across 7 datasets, preventing agents from stagnating in low-information regimes.
- Action Selection (AS) and Belief Tracking (BT) proxy metrics improve significantly with AREW.
- Final task reward increases by up to 60% compared to baselines, consistent across PPO, GRPO, and GSPO algorithms.
- The method is robust to critique noise, with performance remaining stable under up to 50% random critique flips.
- Training dynamics shift toward more frequent and informative questioning behaviors, as evidenced by qualitative trajectory analysis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Information self-locking arises from a vicious cycle between Action Selection and Belief Tracking. When Belief Tracking is weak (e.g., fails to incorporate new evidence), the value of informative queries is masked during RL updates because the resulting belief state does not reflect the information gain. Consequently, the Action Selection policy receives low reward signals for good queries and drifts toward uninformative actions. Simultaneously, with fewer informative queries, the Belief Tracker receives less diverse data to improve, perpetuating its weakness. AREW intervenes by explicitly rewarding steps where informativeness or correct belief updates are detected, via critique-based advantage reweighting. This injects a directional signal that breaks the masking effect: even if BT is poor, the reweighting ensures that informative AS actions receive higher advantages, encouraging their repetition. Over time, this forces both modules to improve jointly—AS generates better queries, providing BT with richer data to learn from, while BT improvements then allow AS to receive clearer reward signals. Thus, AREW decouples the negative feedback loop by substituting environment reward with a proxy that directly reflects information-theoretic progress.

## Architecture Onboarding
The agent follows a modular design with two primary components: Belief Tracking (BT) and Action Selection (AS). BT processes observations to maintain a belief state, which is fed to AS to generate queries. The environment returns responses, which BT uses to update the belief. During RL training, AREW injects critiques that evaluate each step's informativeness or belief update quality. These critiques reweight the advantages computed from environment rewards, altering the policy gradient signal.

**Component map:**  
Input -> Belief Tracking -> Action Selection -> Query -> Environment -> Observation -> Belief Tracking Update  
Critiques (from environment/belief readouts) -> Advantage Reweighting (AREW) -> RL Update (PPO/GRPO/GSPO)

**Critical path:** For each turn: (1) Current belief and context input to AS policy; (2) AS samples a query; (3) Environment responds with evidence; (4) BT updates belief using response; (5) Critiques assess if query was informative or belief update correct; (6) AREW adjusts advantages based on critiques; (7) RL algorithm updates AS and BT parameters using modified advantages.

**Design tradeoffs:** Separating AS and BT allows targeted intervention via AREW but may limit their joint optimization. Using binary critiques is simple and robust to noise but requires some supervision; in tasks without clear informativeness signals, this may not be feasible.

**Failure signatures:** (1) Query repetitiveness or generic questions; (2) Belief state insensitive to new observations; (3) Stagnant AS/BT proxy scores; (4) Plateauing reward despite training.

**First experiments:**  
1. Baseline training without AREW on a canonical active reasoning task (e.g., Twenty Questions) to reproduce self-locking: monitor AS/BT proxies and observe decline in query informativeness over time.  
2. Apply AREW with perfect critiques: verify breakage of self-locking, increased informative queries, and improved belief accuracy.  
3. Test robustness by corrupting critiques with random noise (e.g., 30% flip rate): compare performance to baseline and perfect critique setting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Under what conditions can directional critiques be reliably obtained in active reasoning tasks where ground-truth information gain or belief updates are not easily measurable?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The AREW method relies on easy-to-obtain binary critiques (e.g., whether a query elicited new evidence or whether belief updated correctly). The paper shows robustness to noise up to a 0.5 perturbation rate, but the critiques are still derived from the environment’s responses or internal belief readouts. However, the possibility of obtaining such critiques in open-ended or real-world domains (e.g., conversational agents with subjective user feedback) is not addressed.
- **Why unresolved:** If directional critiques cannot be constructed, AREW cannot be applied. The scalability of the approach depends critically on the availability of stepwise supervision, which may be expensive or impossible in many settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments on tasks with ambiguous or noisy feedback, or define more general surrogate signals for informativeness and belief update quality. Analysis of what minimal signals are necessary to break self-locking would clarify the scope of applicability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does scaling LLM size and capability reduce or eliminate information self-locking, or does the phenomenon persist even with more powerful models?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Experiments are conducted on Qwen2.5-7B and LLaMA‑3.1‑8B; the paper notes self-locking occurs across these families but does not test larger models. The theoretical analysis suggests self-locking arises from structural alignment issues between AS and BT, which might be inherent to outcome-only RL rather than model scale.
- **Why unresolved:** Larger models may have stronger reasoning and belief tracking abilities, potentially reducing the severity of self-locking. Conversely, they might also overfit to low-information regimes more easily.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train and evaluate RL agents using state-of-the-art models (e.g., GPT‑4, Claude 3, or 70B+ open‑weight models) on the same active reasoning benchmarks. Compare the dynamics of AS/BT proxies and the occurrence of self-locking. Ablation studies on model size would reveal whether scaling mitigates the issue.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How general is the theoretical characterization of the self‑locking regime? Can the two‑dimensional analysis (low AS + low BT) be extended to cover non‑locking or mixed‑capability regions?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Theorem 3.4 and Section 3.4 define a locking regime Rδ,ε and prove that updates cannot escape it within K steps under given assumptions. The analysis is rigorously confined to that regime; the behavior outside it (e.g., when AS is high but BT low, or vice versa) is not characterized.
- **Why unresolved:** Practitioners might encounter agents that start with high AS or BT yet still degrade. A full picture of the training dynamics would require understanding how the coupling evolves across the entire AS‑BT space, not just the local locking regime.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend the theory to a global dynamical system model of AS and BT, possibly with piecewise linear or Lyapunov‑based arguments. Empirically, track AS and BT across a wider variety of initializations and tasks to map out the phase diagram of convergence vs. self‑locking.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can alternative training frameworks (e.g., unsupervised auxiliary objectives, policy regularization, or architectural changes) decouple Action Selection and Belief Tracking without explicit critiques?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors propose AREW as a method to inject directional critiques. The conclusion mentions that “this perspective opens up new directions” and they specifically leave “more adaptive or structure‑aware reweighting strategies” for future work. No other decoupling approaches are evaluated.
- **Why unresolved:** AREW depends on critique availability; if critiques are not at hand, alternative mechanisms might still prevent the negative confounding. Understanding whether the core issue is one of credit assignment vs. representation could lead to more general solutions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare AREW with (a) adding an auxiliary loss that directly optimizes AS/BT proxies, (b) modifying the policy architecture to separate query‑generation and belief‑update modules, or (c) using entropy bonuses or curiosity rewards to encourage exploration. Measure whether these methods can also reduce self‑locking when critiques are unavailable.

## Limitations
- Generalization beyond the seven evaluated datasets remains uncertain; tasks with different complexity (e.g., embodied or social environments) may exhibit different self-locking dynamics.
- AREW assumes access to easily obtained directional critiques; its applicability in open-ended settings with ambiguous or subjective feedback (e.g., human conversations) is untested.
- The theoretical analysis assumes separable Action Selection and Belief Tracking modules; monolithic policies might not exhibit the same masking effects, limiting the theory's direct applicability.
