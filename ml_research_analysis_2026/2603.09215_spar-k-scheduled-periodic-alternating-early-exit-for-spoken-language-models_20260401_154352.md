# SPAR-K: Scheduled Periodic Alternating Early Exit for Spoken Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09215
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09215
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** SPAR-K reduces average speech decoding depth by up to 11% on Step-Audio-2-mini and 5% on GLM-4-Voice while preserving task accuracy (max drop 0.82%) and speech quality (negligible MOS/WER change) with no auxiliary computation overhead.

## Executive Summary
Interleaved spoken language models (SLMs) generate text and speech tokens autoregressively, but processing all speech tokens at full transformer depth is computationally expensive. SPAR-K introduces a modality-aware early exit framework that reduces this cost by scheduling most speech tokens to exit at a fixed intermediate layer, while inserting periodic full-depth decoding steps to prevent error accumulation. Evaluated on two state-of-the-art SLMs (Step-Audio-2-mini and GLM-4-Voice) across reasoning, QA, and dialogue tasks, SPAR-K achieves up to 11% average depth reduction on Step-Audio-2-mini and 5% on GLM-4-Voice, with at most a 0.82% drop in task accuracy and no measurable degradation in speech quality (MOS and ASR-WER). The work further shows that confidence-based early exit strategies, common in text LLMs, are ineffective for speech tokens due to fundamental distribution differences.

## Method Summary
SPAR-K comprises two phases: offline training of layer-specific early exit heads, and inference with a scheduled policy. First, for each transformer layer ℓ (1..L), a small linear projection g^(ℓ) is trained to mimic the final layer’s token distribution from the hidden state h^(ℓ)_t, using a frozen backbone and a subset of the VoiceAssistant-400K dataset. Second, during generation, a deterministic scheduler governs the exit decision for each speech token within a block of N_speech tokens. The scheduler alternates between early-exit steps (using a pre-selected intermediate layer ℓ_EE) and full-depth steps (using the final layer L) according to a fixed pattern (e.g., Even: L, ℓ_EE, L, ℓ_EE, ...; period K=2). When an early-exit occurs, the forward pass stops at ℓ_EE and g^(ℓ_EE) produces the token; the KV-cache for layers >ℓ_EE at that position remains incomplete. At the subsequent full-depth step, the backbone processes the entire sequence again, and during this pass the KV-cache manager computes the missing KV entries for all preceding early-exited positions in parallel (akin to prefilling). This design ensures that early-exit steps avoid sequential recomputation overhead, yielding speedup without auxiliary cost.

## Key Results
- Average speech decoding depth reduced by up to 11% (Step-Audio-2-mini) and 5% (GLM-4-Voice).
- Task accuracy preserved with a maximum drop of 0.82% across four evaluation datasets.
- Speech quality unchanged: MOS and ASR-WER differences negligible compared to full-depth baseline.
- Confidence-based early exit strategies perform poorly for speech tokens, contrasting with their success in text LLMs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Fixed intermediate-layer generation with periodic refresh
Speech tokens in interleaved SLMs can be predicted at a fixed intermediate layer ℓ_EE for most positions without significant perceptual loss, as long as the generation schedule includes periodic full-depth steps. The SPAR-K scheduler alternates early-exit steps (ℓ_EE) and full-depth steps (L) within each speech block. The full steps act as "refreshes" that re-anchor the model's internal state and prevent error accumulation from shallow predictions. Core assumption: intermediate layers contain sufficient acoustic-phonetic information for intelligible speech; periodic realignment mitigates cascading errors. Evidence: Token agreement studies show intermediate-layer predictions correlate reasonably with final-layer outputs; however, naive fixed-layer early exit causes catastrophic WER spikes, while SPAR-K's schedule recovers quality. Break condition: if the refresh interval K is too large relative to speech block size N, or if ℓ_EE is too shallow, quality degrades noticeably (e.g., ASR-WER increase >2-3%).

### Mechanism 2: Distribution shift mitigation via periodic full-depth steps
Consistent early exit from a fixed layer causes a distribution shift between training (full-depth) and inference (shallow) conditions, leading to compounding errors in the autoregressive stream. Periodic full-depth steps correct this shift by producing tokens from the intended final-layer distribution and updating the KV-cache for the entire context. This re-synchronizes the generation process and prevents gradual drift. Core assumption: the autoregressive process is sensitive to mismatched token distributions; a single full-depth prediction periodically re-aligns the stream. Evidence: Fixed-layer early exit yields redundant speech tokens and extremely high ASR-WER, while SPAR-K’s refreshes maintain coherence. Break condition: if full-depth steps are spaced too far apart (e.g., triple schedule on long speech blocks), the shift overwhelms the system and quality drops.

### Mechanism 3: Fixed schedule superiority over confidence-based exit
Confidence-based early exit (e.g., using entropy thresholds) works well for text LLMs because semantic coherence correlates with prediction confidence. For speech tokens in interleaved SLMs, this correlation breaks: intermediate-layer speech distributions are often flatter or multi-modal even for correct predictions, making entropy an unreliable signal. A fixed schedule avoids per-token confidence computation and its associated tuning instability. Core assumption: the statistical properties of speech token distributions are inherently different from text token distributions. Evidence: Confidence-based exit causes large accuracy drops on Step-Audio-2 and requires careful thresholding on GLM-4-Voice, whereas SPAR-K’s fixed schedule is robust across models and tasks. Break condition: if the speech token vocabulary or training objective changes to produce peakier intermediate distributions, confidence-based methods might become viable, but the paper suggests the modality difference is fundamental.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
The system consists of:
- **Pretrained Interleaved SLM Backbone:** A frozen transformer stack with L layers that processes interleaved text/speech tokens.
- **Layer-Specific LM Heads (g^(ℓ)):** One linear projection per layer ℓ, trained to mimic the final head’s distribution. Applied only during early exit.
- **SPAR-K Scheduler:** A deterministic state machine that, for each speech token within a block of N_speech tokens, outputs the target exit layer (ℓ_EE or L) based on a fixed pattern (Even/Odd/Triple) and period K.
- **KV-Cache Manager:** Maintains per-layer KV-cache. For early-exit steps, marks the position as having incomplete KV for layers >ℓ_EE. During a full-depth step, it computes missing KV for all such positions in parallel (prefill-style) before generating the current token.
Connections: The backbone receives tokens and processes up to the scheduler-chosen target layer. If the target is ℓ_EE, the corresponding head g^(ℓ_EE) generates the token; the KV-cache manager records the incomplete state. If the target is L, the backbone runs all layers; the KV-cache manager simultaneously fills missing KV for any preceding early-exited positions; then the final head generates the token.

### Critical Path
1. **Offline Head Training:** Using 18K samples from VoiceAssistant-400K, run full-depth generation to collect final-layer distributions and hidden states h^(ℓ) for each layer ℓ. Train each g^(ℓ) via cross-entropy to match the final head’s output. Backbone remains frozen.
2. **Schedule Hyperparameter Selection:** On a held-out validation set, compute oracle fixed-layer metrics (MOS, token agreement) to choose ℓ_EE where isolated quality is acceptable. Then test schedule patterns (Even, Odd, Triple) and period K to minimize quality drop while achieving target speedup.
3. **Inference Loop per Token:** For each new token position t:
   - Determine modality (text or speech) and position within the current speech block.
   - If text: always use full depth (L).
   - If speech: query scheduler → target layer (ℓ_EE or L).
   - Run backbone forward up to target layer.
   - If early exit (ℓ_EE): apply head g^(ℓ_EE) to predict token; do not compute KV for layers >ℓ_EE; mark position t as incomplete.
   - If full depth (L): run backbone to L; before predicting token, use current full pass to compute missing KV for all preceding incomplete positions (t-K+1,..., t-1) in parallel; then predict token for t.
   - Append token, update KV-cache with newly computed entries.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Schedule Pattern (K) vs. Model Cycle Length (N):** Larger K (e.g., Triple) yields higher speedup but risks more error accumulation before a refresh. Models with longer speech blocks (GLM-4-Voice: N=26) are more sensitive to K than those with shorter blocks (Step-Audio-2: N=4).
- **Early Exit Layer (ℓ_EE) vs. Quality:** Deeper ℓ_EE (closer to L) gives less speedup but better quality. A "sweet spot" exists (e.g., ℓ=36 for GLM-4-Voice) where quality is nearly preserved.
- **Fixed Schedule vs. Adaptive:** Fixed schedules avoid per-token confidence overhead and tuning sensitivity but are less flexible than adaptive methods. The paper shows confidence-based exit is unstable for speech.

### Failure Signatures
1. **Catastrophic WER spike:** >10% absolute increase from baseline (e.g., fixed-layer early exit).
2. **MOS drop:** >0.2 absolute drop, indicating unnatural speech.
3. **Task accuracy drop:** >2-3% on QA datasets, signaling semantic degradation.
4. **No speedup or slowdown:** Early exit path still computes unnecessary layers or KV prefilling adds overhead.
5. **Stalling or repetition:** Common when shallow layer distributions are out-of-distribution, leading to low-confidence loops.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Oracle Fixed-Layer Study (Sec 4.1, Table 1):** For a fixed layer ℓ, synthesize only the speech tokens predicted at that layer from a fully-generated sequence (non-autoregressive). Measure MOS and token agreement with final layer. Identifies candidate ℓ_EE where isolated quality is acceptable.
2. **Fixed-Layer vs. Scheduled Early Exit (Sec 5.3):** Implement naive fixed ℓ_EE for all speech positions. Expect severe quality drop. Then implement SPAR-K with same ℓ_EE. Expect quality recovery, demonstrating necessity of periodic refresh.
3. **Schedule Pattern & Period Sensitivity (Sec 5.3):** Test Even, Odd, Triple patterns with same ℓ_EE and K. Also test different ℓ_EE for same pattern. Identifies robust configurations and shows sensitivity to K relative to N.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What specific representational differences between speech and text tokens cause confidence-based early exit to fail for speech while sometimes working for text?** The paper shows empirically that entropy is a poor signal for speech but does not analyze hidden-state distributions or token predictability metrics.
2. **Can SPAR-K's fixed periodic schedule be replaced with an adaptive mechanism that dynamically determines full-depth refreshes based on generation context?** The paper uses handcrafted schedules; adaptive policies might achieve higher efficiency.
3. **What is the precise mechanism by which distribution shift occurs during early exit, and how do periodic full-depth steps prevent it?** The paper attributes failure to distribution shift but does not measure or model the shift dynamics.
4. **How does the text-to-speech token ratio influence the optimal early exit configuration, and what theoretical factors bound the maximum depth reduction?** Current results on two models suggest sensitivity, but no systematic study or theory exists.

## Limitations
- Head architecture (linear only? bias?) and training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, epochs) are unspecified, yet critical for head quality.
- Evaluation pipeline details (vocoder model, GPT-4o-mini prompt version, dataset split handling) are not documented, which could affect reproducibility of MOS, WER, and accuracy numbers.
- Schedule boundary handling (e.g., reset pattern at each speech block, treatment of first token) is unclear; implementation choices may affect effective period K.
- Experiments are limited to two specific SLMs with fixed text:speech ratios; generalizability to other architectures and ratios is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **SPAR-K reduces depth while preserving quality:** High (strong empirical results across multiple datasets and models).
- **Periodic full-depth steps are necessary to mitigate distribution shift:** Medium (ablation evidence is clear but the underlying shift is inferred, not directly measured).
- **Confidence-based early exit is suboptimal for SLMs:** High (demonstrated with large performance gaps and modality analysis).
- **No auxiliary computation overhead:** High (logical design of parallel KV-cache fill; implementation should achieve this if done correctly).

## Next Checks
1. **Reproduce Head Training Fidelity:** Train layer-specific heads on a small validation set and verify that token agreement ratios with the final layer match the paper's Table 1 values for candidate ℓ_EE (e.g., ~46% at ℓ=36 for GLM-4-Voice). Low agreement would explain quality drops.
2. **Validate KV-Cache Parallel Fill Logic:** Instrument the generation loop to confirm that during a full-depth step, the KV-cache for all preceding early-exited positions is computed in the same forward pass (prefill mechanism) and that no sequential recomputation occurs. Measure per-token latency to ensure early-exit steps are faster.
3. **Test Schedule Sensitivity on Varying Block Lengths (N):** Using a held-out set, run SPAR-K with the optimal Even(ℓ_EE) schedule on a modified model where the speech block size N is artificially shortened or lengthened. Observe whether the optimal K=2 period breaks down for very long N, testing the claim that longer speech blocks are more sensitive to K.
