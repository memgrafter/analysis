# Sommelier: Scalable Open Multi-turn Audio Pre-processing for Full-duplex Speech Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.25750
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25750
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Sommelier is a scalable preprocessing pipeline that achieves 7.16% diarization error (DER) on VoxConverse, reduces ASR WER by 37% on LibriSpeech test-other via ensemble, and improves full-duplex SLM benchmark scores after fine-tuning with 83 hours of processed data, all with an RTF of 0.1746 on an A100 GPU.

## Executive Summary
Sommelier addresses the data scarcity challenge for full-duplex speech language models by providing a robust, scalable preprocessing pipeline for multi-speaker, overlapping conversational audio. It integrates a state-of-the-art diarizer (Sortformer), a two-speaker separation module with speaker identity matching, selective background music removal, and an ASR ensemble (ROVER) with a repetition filter. The pipeline yields high-quality, speaker-labeled transcripts and operates efficiently (RTF 0.1746) with concurrency scaling. Downstream, fine-tuning Moshi with 83 hours of Sommelier-processed data demonstrably improves backchanneling, turn-taking, and interruption handling in the Full-Duplex-Bench 1.0.

## Method Summary
Sommelier processes raw multi-speaker audio through a sequential pipeline: (1) audio standardization to 16 kHz mono and loudness normalization; (2) voice activity detection with Silero VAD and speaker diarization using Sortformer; (3) overlap detection and separation via SepReformer, with speaker identity assignment using embeddings extracted from preceding non-overlapping segments (>2 s); (4) optional background music removal using PANNs detection followed by Demucs extraction; (5) ASR ensemble combining Whisper, Canary, and Parakeet with ROVER voting and a 15-gram repetition filter. Outputs are JSON with speaker labels, timestamps, and transcripts, ready for full-duplex SLM fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- Diarization: Sortformer attains 7.16% DER on VoxConverse, outperforming pyannote-3.1 (8.40%) and excelling on segments ≤1.0s and turn-based metrics.
- ASR: ROVER ensemble reduces WER by 37% on LibriSpeech test-other (6.26% → 3.92%) and effectively suppresses repetitive hallucinations.
- Overlap handling: Separation with identity matching yields significant WER, STOI, and UTMOS gains for secondary speakers, especially under full overlap (ρ=1.0) and 0 dB SIR.
- End-to-end: Fine-tuning Moshi with 83 h of Sommelier-processed data enhances Full-Duplex-Bench 1.0 scores; pipeline RTF is 0.1746 on a single A100, scalable via three-way concurrency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Sortformer diarization significantly reduces error rates for very short utterances and rapid turn-taking compared to pyannote-3.1, which is critical for modeling backchannels and interruptions in full-duplex dialogue.
- **Mechanism:** Sortformer employs a permutation-resolved speaker supervision architecture within a speech-to-text framework, enabling it to handle brief, interleaved speech segments without conflating speaker identities—a common failure mode for clustering-based diarizers like pyannote.
- **Core assumption:** The chaotic temporal structure of natural conversation (sub-second backchannels, overlapping turns) requires diarization models that can resolve speaker identity on very short temporal windows, not just long speech regions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Sortformer achieved 7.16% DER vs pyannote3.1's 8.40% on VoxConverse, excelling at short utterances."
  - [section 3.2] Table 2 shows Sortformer's superiority in DER on segments ≤1.0s (16.87% vs 20.21%) and turn-based DER (0.006 vs 0.051).
  - [corpus] VoxConverse dataset (common subset, ≤4 speakers) used for evaluation. Corpus neighbors discuss full-duplex benchmarks (e.g., Audio MultiChallenge, F-Actor) that emphasize turn-taking but do not directly validate Sortformer's architecture—evidence is weak for generalizability beyond this paper's setup.
- **Break condition:** If speaker embedding extracts (used for identity matching in overlap separation) become unreliable for ultra-short (<0.5s) non-overlapping regions, the entire separation pipeline's speaker assignment may degrade. The paper assumes non-overlapping parts >2s are used for reference embeddings, but does not test sensitivity below this threshold.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Systematic handling of overlapping speech via two-speaker separation with speaker identity matching (using reference embeddings from non-overlapping regions) preserves intelligibility and naturalness for both primary and secondary speakers, especially under high overlap ratios.
- **Mechanism:** For overlapped intervals, the pipeline (1) extracts speaker embeddings from preceding non-overlapping segments (>2s), (2) separates the overlap into candidate sources, and (3) assigns each candidate to a speaker via cosine similarity. This "Case 4" strategy (Figure 5) duplicates the overlap into both speakers' streams, avoiding information loss.
- **Core assumption:** Full-duplex SLMs require distinct, separated audio streams per speaker even within overlapped regions, and speaker identity can be robustly transferred from clean reference segments to separated candidates.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Overlap separation significantly improved WER, STOI, and UTMOS, particularly for secondary speakers in high-overlap scenarios."
  - [section 3.3] Tables 6–8 show consistent improvements for Speaker 2 (the interferer) across all metrics at ρ=1.0 (full overlap), e.g., WER drops from 0.444 to 0.138 at 0 dB SIR (Table 6, Speaker 2).
  - [corpus] Synthetic mixtures from LibriSpeech test set. Corpus neighbors (Audio MultiChallenge, F-Actor) highlight overlap handling as a key challenge but do not provide evidence for this specific separation+identity method—weak external validation.
- **Break condition:** If non-overlapping reference segments are too short (<2s) or acoustically dissimilar to the overlapped region (e.g., channel mismatch, noise), speaker embedding similarity scores become unreliable, causing misassignment. The paper does not evaluate failure modes for reference segment duration below 2s.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** ASR ensemble via ROVER (with Whisper as backbone) combined with a repetition filter drastically reduces word error rates and mitigates hallucination artifacts (e.g., repetitive loops) compared to any single model.
- **Mechanism:** Three diverse ASR models (Whisper, Canary, Parakeet) produce word-level hypotheses; ROVER performs alignment and majority voting. A repetition filter discards segments with ≥5 repetitions of 15-grams. Whisper's timestamps are used for alignment.
- **Core assumption:** Hallucinations in large ASR models (especially on silent/noisy segments) are uncorrelated across model families, so consensus voting suppresses spurious outputs while preserving accurate transcriptions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "ASR ensemble reduced WER by 37% on LibriSpeech test-other (6.26% to 3.92%)."
  - [section 3.4] Table 4 confirms ~37% relative WER reduction on test-other; qualitative examples show correction of Whisper's repetitive hallucinations.
  - [corpus] LibriSpeech (clean/other) and TEDLIUM3 test sets. Corpus neighbors do not discuss ASR ensembling for full-duplex data—evidence is limited to standard ASR benchmarks, not necessarily conversational speech with overlaps.
- **Break condition:** If all three ASR models share similar failure modes (e.g., all hallucinate on low-confidence non-speech intervals), ROVER voting may propagate rather than suppress errors. The paper does not analyze correlation of error modes across the three models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Speaker diarization error metrics (DER/JER)**
  - *Why needed here:* To quantify how accurately the pipeline assigns speech turns to speakers, which directly impacts the fidelity of separated streams for full-duplex training.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the difference between DER and JER, and why is JER more sensitive to boundary errors in conversation?
- **Concept: Speech separation evaluation (SI-SDR, STOI, UTMOS)**
  - *Why needed here:* To assess whether separated overlapped speech remains intelligible and natural for training, not just numerically lower WER.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might WER improve while UTMOS (perceptual quality) does not, and what does this imply for SLM training?
- **Concept: Real-Time Factor (RTF) and concurrency scaling**
  - *Why needed here:* The pipeline must process web-scale audio (thousands of hours) efficiently; RTF determines hardware requirements and throughput.
  - *Quick check question:* If RTF=0.1746 on one A100, how does enabling three concurrent processes affect per-GPU effective throughput, and what is the limiting bottleneck?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Audio Standardization → VAD chunking (Silero) → Sortformer diarization → Overlap handling (SepReformer + speaker identity matching) → BGM removal (PANNs + Demucs) → ASR ensemble (Whisper, Canary, Parakeet + ROVER + repetition filter).
- **Critical path:** The pipeline processes audio in a sequential manner: standardization must precede VAD; diarization provides speaker boundaries and overlap detection; separation and BGM removal depend on these boundaries; ASR ensemble consumes the final audio and diarization timestamps. Any failure in diarization cascades to later stages.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Separation fidelity vs. speed:* SepReformer balances quality and resource use; Demucs is used for BGM but slower models like SAM-Audio are excluded due to high RTF.
  - *ASR accuracy vs. latency:* Ensemble improves WER but triples inference cost; mitigated by concurrent processing (3 per GPU).
  - *Overlap preservation vs. artifacts:* The "Case 4" strategy duplicates overlapped content to both speakers, risking ASR errors on mixed signals; separation quality asymmetry yields bigger gains for Speaker 2.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High overlap ratio (ρ=1.0) and low SIR (0 dB) still cause artifacts that degrade Speaker 1 quality.
  - Repetition filter may over-filter meaningful repeated phrases or miss subtle hallucinations.
  - Short non-overlapping reference segments (<2s) lead to unreliable speaker identity matching in overlaps.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Benchmark Sortformer vs. pyannote on VoxConverse, focusing on DER for segments ≤1.0s to validate short-utterance handling.
  2. Stress-test overlap separation by varying overlap ratio (ρ) and SIR, measuring WER/UTMOS gap from oracle to identify failure thresholds.
  3. Ablate the ASR ensemble by comparing ROVER output to individual models on LibriSpeech test-other, counting repetition artifacts before and after filtering.

## Limitations
- The Sortformer diarization model is not publicly released, creating a dependency that hinders independent reproduction and scalability assessment.
- Speaker identity matching relies on an unspecified embedding model and thresholds; robustness to short or acoustically mismatched reference segments is not evaluated.
- End-to-end claims are based on an 83-hour fine-tuning study; the Full-Duplex-Bench 1.0 metrics and code are not provided, limiting external validation.
- Evaluation of separation and ASR improvements uses synthetic mixtures and standard corpora, which may not fully capture the complexity of real full-duplex conversations.

## Confidence
- Sortformer diarization DER improvement: **High** on VoxConverse; **Medium** for general full-duplex scenarios.
- Overlap separation gains for secondary speakers: **Medium** confidence (synthetic evidence; real-world robustness uncertain).
- ASR ensemble WER reduction: **High** on LibriSpeech; **Low** for full-duplex overlap audio.
- End-to-end full-duplex benchmark improvement: **Medium** dependently on the preceding components and unreleased benchmark details.
