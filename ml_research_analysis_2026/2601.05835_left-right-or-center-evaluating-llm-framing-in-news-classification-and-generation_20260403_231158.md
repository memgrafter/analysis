# Left, Right, or Center? Evaluating LLM Framing in News Classification and Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05835
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05835
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** Most LLMs exhibit a systematic "center-collapse" tendency in generated news summaries, producing ideologically neutral (CENTER) outputs even when explicitly prompted for LEFT or RIGHT framing, with Grok 4 being a notable exception.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether an LLM's measured political bias in a classification task predicts its ability to generate text with a specific ideological frame. Evaluating nine models on 12,000 news articles using the AllSides LEFT/CENTER/RIGHT taxonomy, the study finds a pervasive "center-collapse" in generation: models default to CENTER regardless of LEFT or RIGHT steering prompts. While classification accuracy varied (Claude Sonnet 4.5 performed best), generation steerability differed markedly; Grok 4 showed the most ideological expressivity, while open-weight models (Llama 3.1, Qwen 3) struggled severely, especially with RIGHT prompts. The findings suggest safety-aligned behavior induces a conservative, centrist default in generation, and that classification bias signals do not reliably predict framing behavior in generated text.

## Method Summary
The study employs a two-stage evaluation. **Stage 1** measures political bias classification accuracy: each of nine LLMs classifies 12,000 balanced AllSides articles (title+body) into LEFT/CENTER/RIGHT using a fixed few-shot prompt, with results compared to the human AllSides label. **Stage 2** evaluates ideological framing in generation: for a separate 1,000-article subset, each model generates a ~100-token summary under four steering prompts (FAITHFUL, CENTRIST, LEFT, RIGHT). All generated summaries are judged by a single fixed LLM evaluator (Gemini 3 Pro) using a customized prompt to assign a LEFT/CENTER/RIGHT label. The primary analysis compares label distributions, computes generation accuracy (prompt -> judge label), and measures "center-defaulting" rates (LEFT/RIGHT prompts yielding CENTER).

## Key Results
- **Center-collapse is pervasive:** Most models' generated outputs were labeled CENTER across all prompts, indicating a strong default to ideologically neutral framing.
- **Grok 4 is uniquely expressive:** It demonstrated the highest steerability, particularly for right-leaning summaries, resisting the center-collapse seen in other models.
- **Classification vs. generation decoupling:** A model's accuracy in bias classification (e.g., Claude Sonnet 4.5) does not predict its success in ideologically steered generation; open-weight models performed reasonably in classification but failed dramatically in RIGHT-steered generation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Safety alignment in LLMs induces a default toward ideologically neutral (CENTER) framing in generated text, a failure mode termed "center-collapse".
- **Mechanism:** Overly conservative safety training and guardrails prioritize avoiding controversial or partisan language, leading models to hedge and produce outputs that are evaluated as CENTER even when explicitly prompted to adopt LEFT or RIGHT perspectives. This is a form of over-cautiousness that suppresses expressive ideological content.
- **Core assumption:** The evaluator's (Gemini 3 Pro) assessment of CENTER aligns with human perceptions of neutral framing, and the observed collapse is primarily a model behavior artifact rather than an evaluator artifact.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "We find pervasive ideological center-collapse... safety-aligned behavior induces centrist defaulting."
  - [section 5]: "most misclassifications... collapse to CENTER... conservative safety behavior that favors 'safe' centrist language."
  - [corpus]: Contextual support from papers on LLM political bias and safety (e.g., Akiri et al., 2025; Bonaldi et al., 2024).
- **Break condition:** If safety training is optimized for expressivity over harm avoidance, or if the evaluation prompt is redesigned to more effectively activate the intended frame, center-collapse may be reduced.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A model's accuracy in political bias *classification* is not a reliable predictor of its ability to *generate* ideologically steered summaries; these are distinct behavioral modes.
- **Mechanism:** Classification relies on pattern recognition against a fixed label set. Generation involves novel text production conditioned on a perspective prompt, engaging different internal processes for content planning and lexical choice. Success in one does not guarantee control in the other.
- **Core assumption:** The AllSides labels represent a meaningful reference ideology for *source articles*, and the steered generation prompts effectively request a framing shift.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "we study political framing by testing whether LLMs’ classification-based bias signals align with framing behavior."
  - [section 5]: Table 3 shows top classifiers (Claude Sonnet 4.5) are not top generators (Grok 4 is); Llama 3.1 struggles markedly with RIGHT generation.
  - [corpus]: Related work (e.g., Liu et al., 2024 on P3sum) supports generation as distinct from classification.
- **Break condition:** If a unified model architecture or training objective explicitly optimizes for both classification calibration and generation steerability along the same ideological dimension, alignment might improve.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The measurement of ideological framing is heavily dependent on the choice of the fixed evaluator LLM, introducing potential systematic bias.
- **Mechanism:** Using a single LLM judge (Gemini 3 Pro) means all measured "steering success" and "center-collapse" rates are filtered through that model's inherent biases and understanding of the labels.
- **Core assumption:** The Gemini 3 Pro evaluator provides a consistent and meaningful yardstick for *comparative* analysis across models and conditions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4]: "To obtain comparable measurements... we score... with a single fixed LLM evaluator."
  - [section 7 (Limitations)]: "Our conclusions... are evaluated with a single fixed LLM judge... reported shifts... should be interpreted relative to this evaluator."
  - [corpus]: Strong anchor from neighboring papers studying LLM-as-judge biases (e.g., Bavaresco et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024).
- **Break condition:** If multiple, diverse evaluators (human and LLM) with known bias profiles show convergent patterns, confidence in central findings would be less judge-dependent.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: AllSides LEFT/CENTER/RIGHT Taxonomy**
  - *Why needed here:* Foundational label space for the entire study. Its coarse granularity defines what "bias" and "center" mean operationally.
  - *Quick check question:* How might results differ with a finer-grained or multi-dimensional bias label set?
- **Concept: Few-Shot Prompting for Evaluation**
  - *Why needed here:* Core methodology for both stages; choice of examples and phrasing directly influences model behavior and evaluator judgments.
  - *Quick check question:* What is a key risk of using a fixed few-shot set, and how might findings change with different examples?
- **Concept: Center-Collapse**
  - *Why needed here:* Primary empirical phenomenon identified; describes the systematic tendency for outputs to be rated CENTER, a specific steerability failure.
  - *Quick check question:* Is center-collapse better explained as a capability limitation or as a deliberate (over-cautious) design choice?
- **Concept: Ideological Expressivity**
  - *Why needed here:* Contrasting capability highlighted in Grok 4; capacity to generate text strongly aligned with a requested ideological frame.
  - *Quick check question:* What trade-offs might a developer accept to increase expressivity, and what risks could that introduce?
- **Concept: LLM-as-Judge Bias**
  - *Why needed here:* The study's reliance on a single LLM evaluator means all framing judgments are filtered through that model's own ideological lens.
  - *Quick check question:* How could one design an experiment to isolate and measure the judge's bias impact on the center-collapse observation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Source Dataset (AllSides) -> Target LLM (9 models) -> Generation Engine (with 4 steering prompts) -> Fixed Evaluator (Gemini 3 Pro) -> Analysis Module (metrics computation).

- **Critical path:** Balanced article sample from AllSides -> Target LLM Stage 1 Classification (measure bias alignment) -> Target LLM Stage 2 Steered Generation (4 prompts per article) -> Gemini 3 Pro Evaluator Scoring (per summary) -> Analysis (accuracy, center-defaulting, distributions) -> Conclusion on Center-Collapse & Expressivity.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Consistency vs. Validity:* Single evaluator ensures comparability but sacrifices external validity; all judgments inherit Gemini 3 Pro's biases.
  - *Ecological Validity vs. Control:* Real news articles provide realistic input but introduce confounding topic/framing variables.
  - *Open vs. Closed Model Access:* Testing both scenarios highlights different deployment contexts but introduces hard-to-normalize variations in APIs, defaults, and context limits.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - High prevalence of CENTER labels for LEFT and RIGHT generation prompts across most models.
  - Large gap between a model's classification macro-F1 and its generation steering accuracy.
  - For open-weight models, near-zero RIGHT generation accuracy paired with higher CENTER collapse.
  - FAITHFUL prompt summaries receiving CENTER labels at similar rates to CENTRIST prompts.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate Stage 1 confusion matrices for Claude Sonnet 4.5 and Llama 3.1 to verify the "left-wing bias" vs. "centrist tendency" pattern.
  2. For a high-confidence RIGHT article, generate summaries from Grok 4 and Qwen 3 under all four prompts; inspect lexical/structural differences correlating with evaluator's RIGHT vs. CENTER judgment.
  3. Judge-sensitivity check: re-evaluate 50 summaries (25 LEFT-prompt, 25 RIGHT-prompt) with a different LLM judge (e.g., Claude Sonnet 4.5) using the same prompt; quantify label disagreement with Gemini 3 Pro baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **OQ1:** Why do Llama 3.1 and Qwen 3 achieve competitive bias-rating performance but exhibit low inter-model agreement with closed models and different rating distributions?
- **OQ2:** What specific aspects of safety alignment cause the pervasive "center-collapse," and why does Grok 4 resist this tendency more effectively?
- **OQ3:** To what extent do findings depend on the choice of evaluator (Gemini 3 Pro) and the AllSides taxonomy? Would different evaluators or label schemes alter conclusions?
- **OQ4:** Why do open-weight models struggle significantly more with RIGHT-prompted generation compared to LEFT, while commercial models show less asymmetry?
- **OQ5:** Does the misalignment between classification-based bias signals and generation framing behavior indicate that current bias classification tasks are poor proxies for real-world ideological framing in generated text?

## Limitations
- Exact few-shot prompt templates and sample compositions are unreleased, blocking full replication.
- All generation outputs are judged by a single LLM (Gemini 3 Pro), so results reflect that evaluator's biases.
- The AllSides three-way taxonomy may miss nuanced framing differences.
- Decoding parameters and truncation details are unspecified, potentially affecting output style.
- Dataset reconstruction from AllSides may introduce sampling bias.

## Confidence
- **Center-collapse tendency:** Medium confidence. Pattern may be partly an artifact of the single evaluator's centrist bias.
- **Decoupling of classification and generation:** High confidence. Empirical mismatch across models is consistent and clear.
- **Grok 4's relative ideological expressivity:** Medium confidence. Finding depends on single evaluator; robustness unverified.
- **Open-weight models' RIGHT-generation failure:** Medium confidence. Asymmetry is pronounced in results but unexplained.
