# Pantagruel: Unified Self-Supervised Encoders for French Text and Speech

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2601.05911  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05911  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Unified self-supervised framework using data2vec 2.0; speech models outperform LeBenchmark (e.g., WER ↓ 1.2% on Antract), text models match CamemBERT on semantics but lag on syntax.  

## Executive Summary
Pantagruel introduces a unified self-supervised learning framework for French text and speech based on the data2vec 2.0 architecture. It employs modality-specific pre-nets but shares a teacher-student setup with masked latent prediction. For speech, it uses a pure feature-space prediction objective; for text, it augments this with masked language modeling (MLM) to capture fine-grained semantics. Pre-training uses large French corpora, including the newly released 100,000-hour INA-100k broadcast audio dataset. Across speech benchmarks (ASR, SLU, NER, SER, ST), Pantagruel consistently surpasses LeBenchmark baselines. On text tasks (FLUE, Jargon biomedical), it matches strong semantic performers like CamemBERT but shows persistent gaps on syntax-sensitive evaluations, indicating the hybrid objective's partial effectiveness for text.  

## Method Summary
The method adapts data2vec 2.0—a joint embedding predictive architecture—to both text and speech. A teacher network (exponential moving average of the student) processes full input features from a modality-specific pre-net, while a student encoder processes only unmasked features. A lightweight decoder predicts the teacher’s latent representations for masked positions, using an L2 loss. For text, an additional MLM loss is added with a linearly decaying weight \(\lambda\). Models are pre-trained separately: speech on LeBenchmark (14k hours) plus INA-100k (100k hours), and text on Wikipedia/OSCAR/CroissantLLM (302B tokens). Downstream fine-tuning follows standard protocols for each modality.  

## Key Results
- **Speech:** Pantagruel-L-114k reduces WER on Antract from 8.6% to 7.4% and SLU Concept Error Rate from 0.49 to 0.44 versus LeBenchmark. Largest gains on challenging benchmarks like ETAPE (NER NEER ↓ from 55.30 to 44.68) and DyLNet (WER ↓ from 41.23 to 37.48).  
- **Text:** Matches CamemBERT on semantic tasks (e.g., 93.1% vs. 93.7% F1 on classification) but underperforms on syntax-sensitive tasks like verbal substitution and dependency parsing.  
- **Data scale:** INA-100k improves robustness to spontaneous/noisy speech, but large speech models show slight degradation on clean read speech (CommonVoice WER ↑ from 6.95 to 7.07 when scaling from 14k to 114k hours).  

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Unified data2vec 2.0 architecture with modality-specific pre-nets
**Claim:** A shared teacher-student framework with masked latent prediction enables effective cross-modal representation learning.  
**Mechanism:** The student encoder processes a masked subset of features from the pre-net and predicts the teacher’s (EMA) contextualized latent representations for those masks via a decoder. The teacher provides stable targets, forcing the student to capture domain-invariant structural regularities rather than reconstructing surface forms.  
**Core assumption:** Predicting contextualized embeddings in feature space is more effective for continuous signals (speech) and discrete tokens (text) than modality-tailored targets like tokens or units.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the approach “allows modality-specific encoders to capture linguistic and acoustic regularities more effectively.” Section 3.1 details the loss. Strong speech gains (e.g., WER reduction) support effectiveness.  
**Break condition:** If pre-nets extract unstable feature sequences (due to poor tokenization/feature normalization), the latent prediction task becomes ill-defined, causing training collapse.  

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid feature-space + MLM objective for text
**Claim:** Augmenting the data2vec L2 loss with MLM mitigates weaknesses of pure embedding prediction for discrete, sparse textual tokens.  
**Mechanism:** Total text loss \(L_\text{text} = L_{\text{L2}} + \lambda L_{\text{MLM}}\). The decoder predicts both teacher latents (for masked positions) and masked token IDs. \(\lambda\) decays linearly during training, balancing the objectives.  
**Core assumption:** Pure feature-space prediction may under-supervise fine-grained syntactic/semantic boundaries in text; MLM provides necessary token-level supervision.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 1 motivates the hybrid design. Ablations (Table 5) show models with MLM close the semantic gap with CamemBERT but lag on VSD/dependency parsing.  
**Break condition:** Misconfigured \(\lambda\) schedule (too high early or too low late) can cause overfitting to MLM or failure to learn token-level semantics, harming syntax-sensitive tasks.  

### Mechanism 3: Massive diverse pre-training data (INA-100k) for speech robustness
**Claim:** The large, acoustically heterogeneous INA-100k broadcast corpus is critical for speech model robustness to variability and downstream performance.  
**Mechanism:** INA-100k exposes the model to diverse real-world French broadcast speech (news, documentaries, spontaneous speech, noise), enhancing generalization of latent representations to challenging conditions.  
**Core assumption:** Larger, more heterogeneous pre-training data improves self-supervised speech representations, especially for spontaneous/noisy speech.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract highlights INA-100k. Section 4.2 details curation. Tables 8–9 show Pantagruel-L-114k achieves largest gains on ETAPE and DyLNet versus LeBenchmark-L-14k.  
**Break condition:** If INA-100k’s domain (broadcast) diverges from target downstream domains (e.g., clean read speech), benefits may diminish; Table 9 shows slight CommonVoice degradation for large models.  

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) / data2vec  
  **Why needed:** Core objective is predicting contextualized latent representations, not discrete tokens or raw waveforms.  
  **Quick check:** Why does predicting a teacher’s moving-average representation of masked regions encourage meaningful features rather than shortcuts?  
- **Concept:** Teacher-Student Self-Distillation with EMA  
  **Why needed:** EMA teacher provides stable targets, preventing representation collapse.  
  **Quick check:** How would training instability manifest if the teacher updated synchronously with the student?  
- **Concept:** Modality-Specific Pre-nets and Hybrid Objectives  
  **Why needed:** Speech features (log-Mel) and text tokens have different statistical properties; hybrid loss addresses text’s discrete sparsity.  
  **Quick check:** What would be the expected consequence of using the pure speech objective (L2 only) for text pre-training, based on the paper’s results?  

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input → Modality-specific Pre-net → Masking (visible/masked split) → Parallel Teacher Encoder (full) & Student Encoder (visible) → Decoder → Loss (L2 on masked outputs vs. teacher latents; +MLM for text) → Student Optimizer Step → Teacher EMA Update.  
**Critical path:** Input processed by pre-net; masking creates visible/masked subsets. Teacher encodes full sequence; student encodes only visible. Decoder predicts teacher latents for masks (and tokens for text). Loss computed; student updated; teacher EMA applied.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Speech (pure L2): Simpler, lighter, effective for continuous signals but lacks explicit discrete unit learning.  
- Text (hybrid L2+MLM): Aims for best of both worlds but increases complexity, hyperparameter sensitivity (\(\lambda\) schedule), and may still underperform on syntax.  
- Data scale vs. model capacity: Large speech model plateaus/degradates on CommonVoice with 114k hours, suggesting capacity limits or domain mismatch.  
- Tokenizer choice: Custom French-normalizing tokenizer outperforms defaults on XNLI, impacting all downstream text performance.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- Training instability: Loss divergence, NaN values, erratic EMA decay (due to aggressive masking, high LR, insufficient warmup).  
- Representation collapse: Student outputs become uniform (EMA decay too low, ineffective masking).  
- Poor text syntax: Strong semantic scores but weak VSD/dependency parsing (hybrid objective not fully capturing syntax).  
- Speech domain mismatch: Degraded WER on out-of-domain sets (e.g., CommonVoice) after INA-100k training.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Tokenizer impact ablation:** Train Pantagruel-B-Wk with 4 tokenizers (custom, CamemBERT, default, basic) on Wikipedia; evaluate XNLI dev accuracy to confirm custom tokenizer superiority.  
2. **Speech data scale ablation:** Train base (B-1k, B-14k) and large (L-14k, L-114k) models; evaluate WER on CommonVoice dev to reproduce non-linear scaling and diagnose L-114k under/overfitting.  
3. **Hybrid loss schedule sweep:** Fix Pantagruel-B-Osc architecture & data; vary \(\lambda\) start/end and decay length; evaluate FLUE NLI vs. VSD to identify schedules that balance semantic/syntactic performance.  

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Joint multimodal training:** How to train on unaligned or weakly aligned speech–text pairs, and does this improve cross-modal tasks? (Conclusion: multi-modal training in progress, no results yet.)  
2. **Optimal text objective combination:** What is the best way to combine feature-space prediction (data2vec) and token-level objectives (MLM) to close the syntax gap? (Discussion: balance tuning and larger datasets needed; left for future work.)  
3. **Scaling laws and challenges:** What are the scaling laws and practical challenges when increasing model capacity and data diversity for French multimodal encoders, especially for text? (Conclusion: scaling model capacity and corpus diversity listed as future work; large text models unstable.)  
4. **Data mixing for speech:** How does INA-100k affect generalization across speech domains, and what data mixing strategies optimize varied acoustic conditions? (Inferred: INA improves spontaneous speech but may harm clean speech; no systematic mixing experiments provided.)  

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility barriers:** Proprietary INA-100k corpus (100k hours) unavailable; exact French-normalizing tokenizer script not released.  
- **Text objective inadequacy:** Hybrid L2+MLM fails to close syntax gap vs. pure MLM models like CamemBERT on VSD/dependency parsing.  
- **Scaling and stability:** Large text model training unstable; speech model benefits plateau with 114k hours and slightly degrade on clean read speech.  

## Confidence
- **Speech gains:** High (strong multi-benchmark evidence, ablations on data scale).  
- **Unified architecture:** Medium-High (effective for speech, mixed for text due to syntax gaps).  
- **Hybrid text objective:** Medium (improves semantic tasks but not syntax; break condition sensitivity noted).  

## Next Checks
