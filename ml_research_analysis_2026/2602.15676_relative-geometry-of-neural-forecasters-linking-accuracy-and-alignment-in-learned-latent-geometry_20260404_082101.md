# Relative Geometry of Neural Forecasters: Linking Accuracy and Alignment in Learned Latent Geometry
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15676
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15676
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: Anchor-based relative embeddings enable geometry-agnostic comparison of neural forecasters' latent spaces, revealing a positive correlation between alignment and accuracy but a dissociation where transformers achieve high accuracy with low alignment.

## Executive Summary
Neural networks used for forecasting dynamical systems learn internal latent representations that are not directly comparable across models due to arbitrary rotations, scalings, and other linear transformations. This paper introduces anchor-based relative embeddings to transform latent geometries into a common reference frame, making cross-model comparisons possible. The authors train four model families (MLP, RNN, transformer, ESN) on seven canonical dynamical systems and measure alignment via cosine similarity of relative embeddings and accuracy via mean squared error. They find that model families cluster by within-family alignment: MLPs and RNNs maintain high alignment, while transformers and ESNs show weaker alignment. A general positive correlation between alignment and accuracy emerges, but transformers frequently break this trend—achieving high accuracy despite low alignment. This dissociation suggests that minimizing task loss does not guarantee geometrically aligned representations, highlighting the need for separate analysis of latent structure.

## Method Summary
Reproduction notes were not provided explicitly; the method is inferred from the key outcome. The core approach involves training neural forecasters on time series generated from dynamical systems. After training, latent states are extracted for a dataset of input windows. To compare geometries across models, a set of anchor latent states (e.g., from a reference model or a subset of data) is selected. Each latent state is then represented relative to the anchors, for instance by computing differences (latent - anchor) or other invariant transformations. These relative embeddings are invariant to any invertible linear transformation (rotation, scaling) of the latent space, thus providing a geometry-agnostic representation. Alignment between two models is quantified as the average cosine similarity between their corresponding relative embeddings across the same inputs. Forecasting performance is measured by MSE on a held-out set. By aggregating alignment scores across model pairs, the study examines clustering by architecture.

## Key Results
- Relative embeddings provide a robust, geometry-agnostic means to compare latent spaces of neural forecasters.
- Alignment varies by model family: MLPs and RNNs exhibit strong within-family alignment; transformers and ESNs show weaker and more variable alignment.
- A positive correlation between alignment and accuracy is observed overall, but transformers often achieve high accuracy with low alignment, revealing a dissociation between task performance and latent geometry.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The anchor-based relative embedding construction eliminates the effect of arbitrary linear transformations in the latent space. Since any model’s latent geometry can be expressed as a linear transformation of an idealized “true” representation, representing each point relative to anchors (which themselves transform consistently) yields embeddings that are invariant under such transformations. This invariance allows direct comparison across independently trained models. The empirical correlation between alignment and accuracy for some families suggests that models that learn more similar latent structures also generalize better on the forecasting task, possibly because they capture the underlying system’s manifold more faithfully. The dissociation for transformers indicates that their high performance may stem from inductive biases (e.g., attention) that allow accurate prediction without necessarily aligning with the geometry learned by other architectures, or that their latent spaces are more stochastic or less constrained.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Raw time-series input -> Embedding layer (optional) -> Temporal processor (MLP/RNN/Transformer/ESN) -> Latent state -> Prediction head -> Forecast output

**Critical path:** Data is processed sequentially (or in parallel for transformers) through the temporal processor to produce a latent representation that is then read out to generate the next-step (or multi-step) prediction. For analysis, latent states at each timestep are also stored for computing relative embeddings.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- MLPs: simple, no temporal inductive bias; may struggle with long dependencies.  
- RNNs: built-in recurrence, but suffer from vanishing gradients; limited parallelism.  
- Transformers: strong long-range dependencies via self-attention, but require careful positional encoding and may be data-hungry.  
- ESNs: fixed random reservoir with trainable readout; fast training but spectral radius tuning critical.

**Failure signatures:**  
- MLP: high bias, underfitting; latent states may not evolve smoothly.  
- RNN: gradient explosion/vanishing; latent states may become constant or explode.  
- Transformer: attention weights become uniform or focus only on recent tokens; latent states may ignore distant past.  
- ESN: reservoir dynamics become unstable (spectral radius >1) or too contracting (<1); latent states may collapse to fixed point.

## Limitations
- The study is limited to seven canonical dynamical systems; it is unclear whether findings generalize to more complex or real-world systems.
- Only four model families were examined; other architectures (e.g., convolutional, graph neural networks) may exhibit different alignment behaviors.
- The robustness of the anchor-based method to the choice and number of anchors has not been thoroughly evaluated.

## Confidence
- Relative embeddings enable geometry-agnostic comparison: Medium
- Family-level alignment clustering: Medium
- Positive alignment-accuracy correlation with transformer dissociation: Medium-Low
- Task loss insufficient to capture geometry: Low-Medium
