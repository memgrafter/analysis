# SenCache: Accelerating Diffusion Model Inference via Sensitivity-Aware Caching

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.24208
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24208
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: At NFE=21 on Wan 2.1, SenCache achieves LPIPS 0.0540 vs. MagCache 0.0603, with higher PSNR and SSIM.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion model inference is computationally expensive due to its sequential denoising steps. Existing caching methods rely on heuristic, static criteria that require extensive tuning. SenCache introduces a principled, dynamic approach: it formulates caching error through the denoiser’s local sensitivity (Jacobian norms with respect to the noisy latent and timestep) and adapts cache decisions per sample using a first‑order sensitivity score. Evaluated on state‑of‑the‑art video diffusion models (Wan 2.1, CogVideoX, LTX‑Video), SenCache consistently outperforms prior caching methods (TeaCache, MagCache) in visual quality under matched compute budgets, requiring only a small calibration set (8 videos) and no training.

## Method Summary
SenCache precomputes sensitivity coefficients \( \alpha_x(t) \) and \( \alpha_t(t) \) for each reference timestep via directional finite‑differences on a small calibration set. These coefficients approximate the Jacobian norms of the denoiser with respect to the latent and timestep. During inference, the sampler provides incremental changes \( \Delta\mathbf{x} \) and \( \Delta t \). The system maintains accumulators \( d \) (sum of \( \Delta\mathbf{x} \)) and \( \tau \) (sum of \( \Delta t \)) since the last cache refresh, together with a consecutive cache count \( m \). At each step, it computes the sensitivity score \( S = \alpha_x(t_r) \|d\| + \alpha_t(t_r) |\tau| \), where \( t_r \) is the reference timestep of the cached output. If \( S \leq \varepsilon \) and \( m < n \) (where \( \varepsilon \) is an error tolerance and \( n \) a maximum skip length), the cached output is reused; otherwise the denoiser is evaluated, the reference is updated, and accumulators are reset. This process is training‑free and dynamically adapts to the current trajectory’s sensitivity.

## Key Results
- On Wan 2.1 at NFE=21, SenCache yields LPIPS 0.0540 (vs. MagCache 0.0603) and better PSNR/SSIM.
- Consistent improvements observed across CogVideoX and LTX‑Video under matched compute.
- Requires only 8 calibration videos; is training‑free and generalizes across architectures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Caching error bounded by first‑order sensitivity score
- **Claim:** Caching error can be bounded by a first‑order sensitivity score derived from the denoiser’s Jacobians with respect to its inputs; reusing cached outputs is safe when this predicted error stays below a tolerance.
- **Mechanism:** For consecutive denoising steps, output change is approximated by \( f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\Delta t}, t+\Delta t) - f(\mathbf{x}_t, t) \approx \mathbf{J}_x \Delta\mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{J}_t \Delta t \). Taking norms gives \( S_t = \|\mathbf{J}_x\| \|\Delta\mathbf{x}_t\| + \|\mathbf{J}_t\| |\Delta t| \). Cache when \( S_t \leq \varepsilon \).
- **Core assumption:** The first‑order Taylor expansion remains accurate over the interval until the next cache refresh; Jacobian norms at the reference point approximate those along the short trajectory.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.2 show sensitivity as a key predictor of error; neighboring papers lack such theoretical grounding.
- **Break condition:** When accumulated drift or nonlinearities cause the first‑order approximation to diverge, actual output change may exceed \( \varepsilon \), leading to degradation.

### Mechanism 2: Dual latent and timestep sensitivities
- **Claim:** Both noisy latent and timestep sensitivities contribute significantly to caching error; prior heuristics implicitly capture only one component, explaining their failure modes.
- **Mechanism:** The score \( S_t \) combines two terms: \( \|\mathbf{J}_x\|\|\Delta\mathbf{x}_t\| \) (latent drift) and \( \|\mathbf{J}_t\||\Delta t| \) (timestep progression). This explicitly accounts for both sources of variation.
- **Core assumption:** The contributions are additive within the small‑step regime; cross‑terms are negligible.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states both inputs are significant. Section 4.4 shows TeaCache approximates only the timestep term, MagCache only the latent term, explaining artifacts.
- **Break condition:** When one term is negligible, ignoring it may still work; however, if both are moderate, omitting either leads to misprediction.

### Mechanism 3: Efficient estimation via small calibration
- **Claim:** Per‑timestep sensitivity norms can be efficiently estimated using a small calibration set via directional finite‑differences, making SenCache practical without expensive per‑step Jacobian computation.
- **Mechanism:** Offline, for each reference timestep \( t_r \), compute approximations:
\[
\|\mathbf{J}_x\| \approx \frac{\| f(\mathbf{x}_t + \Delta\mathbf{x}, t_r, c) - f(\mathbf{x}_t, t_r, c) \|_2}{\|\Delta\mathbf{x}\|_2}, \quad
\|\mathbf{J}_t\| \approx \frac{\| f(\mathbf{x}_t, t_r+\Delta t, c) - f(\mathbf{x}_t, t_r, c) \|_2}{|\Delta t|},
\]
averaged over a few diverse videos. Store \( \alpha_x(t_r), \alpha_t(t_r) \). At inference, retrieve and compute \( S = \alpha_x \|d\| + \alpha_t |\tau| \).
- **Core assumption:** Jacobian norms are similar across samples at a given timestep; a small, diverse calibration set yields representative estimates. A single perturbation direction suffices to approximate the norm.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.5 and Figure 4 show 8 videos produce nearly identical profiles to a 4096‑video set. Neighbors do not discuss calibration size.
- **Break condition:** If calibration lacks diversity or conditioning strongly affects sensitivity, estimates may be biased, causing mispredictions.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Denoiser (fθ) → Sampler → Decision Module → Cache Storage; Sensitivity Cache (C) → Decision Module; Decision Module ↔ Denoiser (on cache miss)

### Critical Path
1. **Initialization:** Start from \( \mathbf{x}_T \); compute first denoiser output. Set reference \( (\mathbf{x}_r, t_r, \mathbf{y}_r) \). Lookup \( (\alpha_x, \alpha_t) \) for \( t_r \). Reset accumulators \( d=0, \tau=0, m=0 \).
2. **Step loop** (for each solver step): Obtain next latent \( \mathbf{x}_{k-1} \), timestep \( t_{k-1} \), and increments \( \Delta\mathbf{x}, \Delta t \). Accumulate \( d \gets d + \Delta\mathbf{x} \), \( \tau \gets \tau + \Delta t \), \( m \gets m+1 \). Compute \( S = \alpha_x \|d\| + \alpha_t |\tau| \).
   - **Cache hit** if \( S \leq \varepsilon \) and \( m < n \): set \( \mathbf{y}_{k-1} = \mathbf{y}_r \).
   - **Cache miss** otherwise: evaluate denoiser, update reference \( (\mathbf{x}_r, t_r, \mathbf{y}_r) \), reload \( (\alpha_x, \alpha_t) \) for new \( t_r \), reset accumulators \( d=0, \tau=0, m=0 \).
3. Output final sequence \( \{\mathbf{y}_k\} \).

### Design Tradeoffs
- \( \varepsilon \) (tolerance): Larger → more skips, lower NFE, higher quality risk.
- \( n \) (max consecutive skips): Longer skip chains increase speed but risk drift.
- Calibration set size: 8 diverse videos sufficient; larger sets may overfit.
- Finite‑difference step sizes: Should match typical solver step magnitudes for meaningful norms.
- Timestep resolution: Sensitivities precomputed at discrete points; interpolation may be needed if schedule differs.

### Failure Signatures
- Excessive blurring/flickering: \( \varepsilon \) too high or \( n \) too large.
- No speedup (NFE ≈ T): \( \varepsilon \) too low or \( n=1 \).
- Model‑specific failure: Using \( \varepsilon \) tuned for one model on another.
- Calibration mismatch: Test data have unseen sensitivity patterns (e.g., unseen motion).

### First Three Experiments
1. **Calibrate and visualize:** Run 8 diverse videos through denoiser with finite‑difference perturbations; plot \( \alpha_x(t) \) and \( \alpha_t(t) \). Expect patterns similar to paper Figure 4.
2. **Hyperparameter sweep:** Fix \( n \) (e.g., 3), vary \( \varepsilon \) on validation set; record NFE and LPIPS. Confirm monotonic trade‑off and choose operating point.
3. **Baseline comparison:** Set \( \varepsilon \) to match a baseline NFE (e.g., 21 for Wan 2.1). Run SenCache, TeaCache, MagCache; compute LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM. Expect SenCache equal or superior under matched compute.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Higher‑order or learned sensitivity estimators
- **Basis:** Explicit (Discussions and Future Work): “Currently, our implementation relies on a first‑order sensitivity surrogate; an interesting direction would be to find efficient yet richer (higher‑order or learned) estimators that could reduce error in nonlinear regimes.”
- **Why unresolved:** First‑order approximation may be inaccurate in highly nonlinear regions, causing suboptimal cache decisions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark SenCache with alternative estimators (e.g., second‑order Taylor or small neural networks) on video models, measuring LPIPS/PSNR/SSIM under matched NFE.

### Open Question 2: Dynamic ε scheduling across timesteps
- **Basis:** Explicit: “dynamically scheduling ε across timesteps could further accelerate inference while maintaining generation quality: different steps contribute unequally to final fidelity… In this paper, we used a fixed threshold; designing schedules… is left for future work.”
- **Why unresolved:** A static ε may be suboptimal; varying step importance suggests adaptive thresholds could improve efficiency without quality loss.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design ε schedules based on sensitivity norms or predicted output impact, evaluate on VBench prompts for Wan 2.1, compare NFE reductions for equivalent LPIPS/PSNR against fixed‑ε baselines.

### Open Question 3: Generalization to non‑visual diffusion models
- **Basis:** Explicit: “although we validated sensitivity‑aware caching on video diffusion models, the core principle is not limited to the visual domain. Extending this approach to other modalities… represents an exciting avenue for future research.”
- **Why unresolved:** Only evaluated on video DiTs; audio and text diffusion models may have different sensitivity patterns due to architecture and data characteristics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement SenCache on diffusion models for audio (e.g., AudioLDM) or text (e.g., Diffusion‑LM), use domain metrics (FAD, perplexity) to measure quality preservation at reduced NFE.

### Open Question 4: Combining local sensitivity with global error budget optimization
- **Basis:** Inferred from supplementary: “Local sensitivity in SenCache is a proxy for the marginal cost of skipping one more step. Global schedule methods can be seen as doing the same thing but with planning… An interesting future direction is to combine the two.”
- **Why unresolved:** SenCache makes per‑step decisions without considering cumulative error; global methods like LeMiCa optimize overall error distribution; hybrid approaches may better balance adaptivity and stability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a hybrid system where a global optimizer allocates ε(t) based on a total error budget, integrate with SenCache’s local rules, and compare against both baselines on Wan 2.1.

## Limitations
- Exact finite‑difference step sizes (Δx, Δt) and the precise 8‑video calibration set are not specified, hindering exact replication.
- Full hyperparameters (ε, n) for each model are not fully reported; empirical sweep required for reproduction.
- Evaluation is limited to DiT video models; generalizability to other architectures (e.g., UNet) or modalities remains unverified.
