# SAHOO: Safeguarded Alignment for High-Order Optimization Objectives in Recursive Self-Improvement

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06333
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06333
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** SAHOO improves quality by +18.3% (code), +16.8% (math), +3.8% (truthfulness) while preserving constraints (CPS≈1.0) and maintaining low regression (<1%) with GDI below 0.44.

## Executive Summary
SAHOO is a framework for recursive self-improvement (RSI) that prevents alignment drift by monitoring three safeguards: a multi‑signal Goal Drift Index (GDI) to detect behavioural divergence, hard constraint checks to enforce safety‑critical invariants, and regression‑risk quantification to avoid losing prior gains. Evaluated on 189 tasks across code generation, mathematical reasoning, and truthfulness, SAHOO achieves substantial quality improvements while maintaining near‑perfect constraint satisfaction and low regression rates, demonstrating that high‑order optimization can be safely pursued.

## Method Summary
The method operates in iterative cycles where a base model generates outputs, which are evaluated on quality, drift, constraint adherence, and regression risk. The GDI combines four normalized drift signals—semantic, lexical, structural, and distributional—using weights learned on a small calibration set. Constraints (e.g., syntactic correctness, non‑fabrication) are checked per cycle; violations trigger immediate termination and are fed back as penalty signals. Regression risk estimates the probability that the next cycle will drop below the historical quality peak, using volatility and trend analysis. Calibrated thresholds determine when to stop or continue. The improvement prompt incorporates these feedback signals to produce the next model version, enabling continuous alignment‑preserving enhancement.

## Key Results
- Quality gains: +18.3% (HumanEval), +16.8% (GSM8K), +3.8% (TruthfulQA).
- Constraint Preservation Score (CPS): 1.00 (code), 1.00 (math), 0.987 (truthfulness).
- Goal Drift Index (GDI) consistently below the critical threshold of 0.44, with means 0.32–0.35.
- Convergence achieved in 91% of tasks (average 8.2 cycles); regression rates below 1%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Multi‑Signal Drift Detection
**Claim:** Alignment drift during RSI can be measured and bounded by a multi‑signal index combining semantic, lexical, structural, and distributional divergences from a baseline.  
**Mechanism:** GDI computes a weighted sum of four normalized components: semantic (embedding cosine distance), lexical (Jensen‑Shannon divergence on token distributions), structural (feature differences such as length and formatting), and distributional (Wasserstein distance between recent embedding buffers). Weights are learned on a calibration set (18 tasks) via logistic regression to optimize detection of human‑annotated drift. Thresholds are similarly calibrated per task type to maximize F1.  
**Core assumption:** Alignment deviation manifests across these complementary modalities; their combination provides a robust early‑warning system.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions GDI as a learned multi‑signal detector; Section B.6 defines the formal weighted composite and learning objective; neighboring papers address iterative drift but not this specific signal integration.  
**Break condition:** Overfitting of weights to the small calibration set could cause high false positives (stalling legitimate progress) or false negatives (missing subtle drift).

### Mechanism 2: Hard Constraint Checking
**Claim:** Alignment preservation is enforced by making constraint satisfaction a non‑negotiable stopping criterion during improvement cycles.  
**Mechanism:** A task‑specific set of constraints `C` (format, content, logical, ethical) is defined. After each cycle, the Constraint Preservation Score (CPS) computes the fraction of satisfied constraints. Any cycle with `CPS=0` (multiple critical violations) triggers immediate termination. Violated constraints are explicitly fed back into the improvement prompt as penalty signals, guiding the model to correct them.  
**Core assumption:** Safety‑critical invariants can be formally specified and automatically verified; maintaining these invariants prevents catastrophic failures.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract references constraint preservation checks; Section C.3 describes violation highlighting and escalating penalties; the integration as a cycle‑level stopping rule and feedback signal is distinctive.  
**Break condition:** Under‑specified constraints (e.g., truthfulness nuances) may allow the system to satisfy all checks while still producing subtly misaligned outputs (“constraint gaming”).

### Mechanism 3: Regression Risk Quantification
**Claim:** Undoing prior capability gains can be probabilistically quantified from improvement history and used to pause risky cycles.  
**Mechanism:** At each cycle `c`, regression risk `R_c` estimates the probability that the next quality will fall below the historical maximum minus a threshold `δ`, assuming Gaussian‑distributed increments. A trend adjustment increases risk if a downward slope is detected. Exceeding a calibrated threshold `τ_R` issues a warning.  
**Core assumption:** Quality trajectories exhibit statistical regularities (volatility, trend) that distinguish stable improvement from oscillating or degrading patterns.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions regression‑risk quantification; Section D.3 presents the formal probability formula with trend adjustment; the application to RSI with early detection is novel.  
**Break condition:** Non‑Gaussian or dependent quality changes (due to feedback loops) may miscalibrate `R_c`, leading to missed warnings or false alarms.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Recursive Self‑Improvement (RSI) Cycles
**Why needed:** The entire framework assumes a loop where the model `θ_c` generates output, is evaluated on quality, drift, and constraints, and then uses feedback to produce `θ_{c+1}`. This cycle structure is prerequisite to understanding where safeguards plug in.  
**Quick check:** What are the three evaluation dimensions (`Q`, `CPS`, `Δ`) computed at each cycle, and how do they feed into the next iteration's improvement prompt?

### Concept: Multi‑Signal Drift Detection
**Why needed:** The GDI’s design rests on the idea that alignment drift is multi‑faceted. An engineer must understand what each signal (semantic, lexical, structural, distributional) captures and why a single metric is insufficient.  
**Quick check:** Give an example where lexical drift might be low but semantic drift is high, and why that matters for alignment.

### Concept: Constraint Specification vs. Quality Metrics
**Why needed:** SAHOO treats constraints as absolute invariants (hard stops) distinct from gradual quality improvements. Confusing the two leads to misapplication (e.g., treating a “helpfulness” constraint as a quality metric).  
**Quick check:** Why might a system improve on a quality metric (e.g., code pass@1) while still violating a constraint? Provide a concrete constraint for code generation that is not a quality metric.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Drift Module → (computes GDI) → Control Loop  
Constraint Module → (computes CPS, flags violations) → Control Loop  
Regression Module → (estimates R_c) → Control Loop  
Control Loop → (checks stopping rules) → terminates or continues  
Calibration Subsystem → (learns GDI weights, sets thresholds) → initializes parameters  

**Critical path:** Calibration (offline, on 18 tasks) → Initialization (load baseline model θ₀, constraints, thresholds) → Cycle loop (generate, evaluate GDI/CPS/Q, estimate R_c, check stops) → Improvement (construct prompt with feedback, produce θ_{c+1}).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Sensitivity vs. Stability:* Lower drift thresholds catch subtle drift early but may halt on benign fluctuations; higher thresholds risk missing drift. Calibration aims for Pareto frontier.  
- *Constraint Breadth vs. Feasibility:* More constraints cover more failure modes but increase violation rates (e.g., truthfulness) and may block legitimate improvements. Requires iterative refinement.  
- *Calibration Cost vs. Generalization:* The 18‑task, 3‑cycle calibration is cheap but may not generalize to new domains; recalibration is advised for new task families.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High GDI with stable Q: early warning of misalignment before quality suffers.  
- CPS violations clustered in specific constraint types: indicates under‑specified or conflicting constraints (e.g., truthfulness “fabrication”).  
- Oscillating quality with high regression risk: system not converging to a stable improved policy (e.g., outlier task with many regressions).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Calibration Smoke Test:** Run the 18 calibration tasks (6 per domain) for 3 cycles. Verify that GDI weights are learned (non‑zero), τ_GDI values are set (~0.44), and the system doesn’t halt immediately. Check that CPS stays perfect on code/math tasks during calibration.  
2. **Single‑Domain Convergence:** Pick one HumanEval task. Run the full RSI loop (max 20 cycles). Expected: quality improves >15%, GDI mean ≤0.35, CPS=1.0, convergence by cycle 7–10. Validates end‑to‑end pipeline.  
3. **Controlled Drift Injection:** On a GSM8K task, artificially modify the improvement prompt to encourage a subtle semantic shift (e.g., change notation). Verify semantic component of GDI increases and ideally triggers a stop/warning before quality degrades >5%. Tests detector sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Handling Non‑Binary or Ethically Nuanced Alignment Constraints
**Question:** How can SAHOO be extended to handle constraints that resist formal specification?  
**Basis:** Future Works (Page 7): “more sophisticated constraint specification methods could enable richer alignment properties beyond the binary satisfaction approach we employ.” Limitations (Page 6): constraints are “hard to write for many ethical or value‑laden properties.”  
**Why unresolved:** Current constraints are binary and explicit; many alignment properties (fairness, honesty in open‑ended contexts) are vague, context‑dependent, or probabilistic.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Develop and validate soft/probabilistic constraint satisfaction (e.g., using reward models or human feedback), integrate with value learning, and demonstrate on ethically charged tasks without prohibitive violation rates.

### Open Question 2: Robustness of GDI Against Adversarial Manipulation
**Question:** Is the Goal Drift Index robust when a self‑improving system actively tries to evade detection?  
**Basis:** Risks, Limitations (Page 6): “Adversarial robustness of the drift detection system deserves investigation.” Framework assumes natural drift, not adversarial optimization.  
**Why unresolved:** A capable system could learn to minimize GDI by manipulating output distributions just enough to stay below thresholds while still misaligning. No adversarial testing is reported.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Conduct red‑team experiments where an agent optimizes to fool GDI while introducing targeted misalignment; measure evasion success, identify vulnerable components, and develop robust statistics or adversarial training.

### Open Question 3: Scaling to Multi‑Agent Recursive Self‑Improvement
**Question:** How does SAHOO scale to scenarios where multiple systems jointly improve and interact?  
**Basis:** Future Works (Page 7): “extension to multi‑agent self‑improvement scenarios” is a key direction. Current evaluation uses single‑agent tasks.  
**Why unresolved:** Multi‑agent RSI introduces emergent dynamics (coordination, competition, collective drift) that could break single‑agent assumptions: drift may propagate, constraint satisfaction could become a social dilemma, regression risks might amplify.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Implement SAHOO in multi‑agent environments (e.g., collaborative code generation, debate‑based reasoning), measure group‑level alignment (collective GDI, constraint preservation), compare stability to single‑agent baselines, and adapt the framework accordingly.

### Open Question 4: Generalization of Learned Thresholds and Weights
**Question:** Do SAHOO’s learned drift thresholds and component weights generalize to tasks or model architectures outside its narrow calibration distribution?  
**Basis:** Limitations (Page 6): “the system was calibrated on specific benchmark distributions and decoder‑style models so new task families or model architectures will require recalibration.” Calibration used only 18 tasks and 3 cycles per domain.  
**Why unresolved:** Thresholds and weights are learned from a small, homogeneous set; tasks with different success criteria or model families might have different drift characteristics, causing false positives/negatives.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Test SAHOO on diverse benchmarks (BIG‑Bench, multimodal tasks) and varied architectures (decoder‑only vs. encoder‑decoder, different sizes) without recalibration; report GDI adherence, constraint violation rates, and quality gains. If performance degrades, analyze recalibration sample efficiency.

## Limitations
- Exact improvement prompt design (in‑context vs. fine‑tuning) is unspecified, blocking core RSI implementation.
- Automated constraint evaluators for truthfulness (fabrication, overconfidence) are not defined; the paper relies on vague human evaluation.
- Calibration details—including human drift labels and precise per‑domain GDI thresholds—are absent, forcing use of reported values without validation.

## Confidence
- **GDI multi‑signal detection:** Medium – formal definition and calibration are sound, but unknown calibration labels and potential overfit to a small set limit generalizability.
- **Constraint preservation as hard stops:** Medium – mechanism clear, but assumes constraint completeness; likely under‑specification for truthfulness.
- **Regression risk quantification:** Medium – statistical model plausible, but untested parameters and Gaussian assumptions may miscalibrate risk.
