# FormalEvolve: Neuro-Symbolic Evolutionary Search for Diverse and Prover-Effective Autoformalization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19828
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19828
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves semantic hit rates (SH@100) of 58.0% (CombiBench) and 84.9% (ProofNet) under a fixed generator-call budget, producing diverse repertoires that improve downstream proving success compared to strong baselines.

## Executive Summary
FormalEvolve addresses autoformalization—the translation of natural-language mathematics into compilable Lean 4 statements—by recognizing that semantic consistency does not guarantee prover effectiveness. It frames the problem as a budgeted search for a *diverse repertoire* of semantically consistent formalizations rather than a single optimal output. The framework integrates LLM-driven mutation and crossover with bounded patch repair and symbolic AST rewrites (EvolAST) as a diversity fallback, all gated by compilation feasibility. An archive-based evolutionary process with usage-penalized selection prevents mode collapse. On CombiBench and ProofNet with T=100 generator calls, FormalEvolve improves semantic hit rates, reduces concentration of successes across problems, and increases the number of theorems proven under a fixed prover budget.

## Method Summary
FormalEvolve uses an evolutionary loop that maintains an archive of compilation-feasible candidates. Starting from a seed model, it iteratively selects parents (with a usage penalty to avoid collapse), applies one of three LLM-based variation operators (full rewrite, diff patch, cross-patch), and subjects the child to a strict compilation gate. If compilation fails, a bounded repair procedure (limited attempts) uses compiler feedback; if that fails or on exact duplicates, a symbolic AST rewrite (EvolAST) acts as a zero-call fallback. Only compilable candidates are evaluated by an LLM semantic judge. The archive is partitioned into islands to promote diversity, with periodic migration. The final output is the deduplicated set of semantically consistent formalizations found within the total generator-call budget.

## Key Results
- Semantic hit rate (SH@100) of 58.0% on CombiBench and 84.9% on ProofNet.
- Reduced cross-problem concentration: lower Gini coefficient and Top-10% share of semantic successes versus baselines.
- Downstream proving improvement: theorem-complete@64 on CombiBench rises from 8/100 (strongest Kimina baseline) to 13/100 with FormalEvolve.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Budgeted Repertoire Search
Autoformalization is framed as a search for a *diverse set* of semantically consistent statements within a strict generator-call budget, not a single output. The rationale is that two statements can both be semantically faithful yet differ substantially in proof-search difficulty under a fixed prover budget. By constructing a deduplicated archive of compilable candidates and extracting those that also pass an LLM semantic judge, the system hedges against this difficulty variance. Downstream, a prover attempts proofs on this repertoire; success is measured if any variant yields a complete proof within its own attempt budget.

### Mechanism 2: Compilation-Gated Search with Bounded Repair
The feasible space of compilable Lean 4 statements is highly non-convex and sparse. FormalEvolve uses compilation as a hard feasibility gate: only compilable candidates enter the archive. LLM-generated children often fail initially; a bounded repair procedure (default 2 attempts) uses compiler errors to salvage them, with each attempt debiting the generator-call budget to maintain audibility. If repair fails (or on exact duplicates), a symbolic AST rewrite (EvolAST) modifies only binder/goal types as a zero-call fallback to preserve diversity without consuming calls.

### Mechanism 3: Usage-Penalized Selection and Island Model
To prevent mode collapse where a few high-scoring templates dominate, parent selection uses a robust z-score of candidate score (`s(c) = C(c) * (1+J(c))`) combined with a usage penalty that decays with how often a candidate has been reused. The archive is split into `K` islands (default 2); parent sampling and cross-patch inspirations are restricted to the sampled island, with periodic elitist migration. This semi-isolation promotes exploration of different feasible regions, leading to a more uniform distribution of semantic successes across problems.

## Foundational Learning
- **Compilation gating:** In Lean 4, a statement must compile to be a valid formalization. Compilation acts as a hard feasibility gate, defining the search space and providing immediate feedback for repair. *Quick check:* "What happens to a candidate that fails to compile? Does it enter the archive?"
- **Bounded repair:** LLMs often generate near-misses; repair uses compiler errors to fix them. Bounding attempts (default 2) ensures generator-call budget audibility and prevents pathological consumption on a single candidate. *Quick check:* "If a candidate fails compilation, how many repair attempts are allowed, and are they debited?"
- **Semantic judging (LLM-as-judge):** Compilation does not guarantee semantic fidelity. An LLM judge (CriticLean) labels candidates as semantically consistent. The system still builds a repertoire because judge/prover mismatch can occur; diversity hedges against this risk. *Quick check:* "Why build a repertoire after a candidate passes the semantic judge? What risk does this hedge against?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Seed Model -> Archive (partitioned into K islands) -> Parent Selector (with usage penalty) -> Proposal Operators (full/diff/cross) -> Compilation Gate -> Bounded Repair? -> Semantic Judge -> Archive update. EvolAST fallback attached to compile-fail/duplicate path. Periodic Migration between islands.
- **Critical path per problem (budget T):** 1. Seed initial candidates. 2. Loop while t < T: sample island, sample parent (with penalty), sample context/inspirations, propose child (debit call), enforce protocol, if duplicate: EvolAST (no call) -> process; else if compiles: process (may trigger semantic repair); else: EvolAST (no call) -> process, then if budget remains: compile-repair (bounded, debited) -> process. 3. Output deduped set of compilable & semantically consistent candidates.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Strict budget accounting vs. repair completeness (bounding repair may discard fixables); conservative EvolAST (binder/goal types only) vs. diversity gain; island isolation vs. mixing (migration rate 0.1, every 10 gens); usage penalty strength (β=0.05) balances convergence vs. collapse.
- **Failure signatures:** Low SH@T despite high CH@T (judge too strict/operators not diverse); high Gini/top-10% share (mode collapse persists); many EvolAST triggers (LLM struggling to compile); SH@T vs. prover utility discrepancy (judge/prover mismatch); archive size stuck (few candidates compile).
- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Baseline replication: Run Sample, Compile Repair, Compile+Semantic Repair, and FormalEvolve (K=2) on 5 CombiBench problems with T=100. Expect FormalEvolve to have higher SH@100 and lower Gini. 2. Ablation check: Compare FormalEvolve (w/o patch repair) and (w/o EvolAST). Expect removing patch repair to drastically reduce SH@100; removing EvolAST effect smaller. 3. Island effect: Compare K=1 vs K=2. Expect K=2 to have lower Gini (more uniform) but possibly slightly lower SH@100.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Prover-native equivalence checks:** Can BEq/BEq+ replace the LLM semantic judge to reduce noise? The current LLM judge has limited accuracy (~79–81%), and prover-native checks may align better with actual proof utility.
2. **Transfer to other proof assistants:** Does the evolutionary recipe work in Coq or Isabelle with minimal adaptation? The implementation is Lean 4 specific; generalizability to other type theories and libraries is unknown.
3. **EvolAST design optimization:** How should the rule set and triggers be tuned to maximize semantic hit rate without harming search? Current conservative rules sometimes yield many feasible but semantically rejected candidates; optimal balance is unclear.
4. **Generalization across provers:** Does the repertoire benefit generalize across prover architectures (transformer vs. retrieval-augmented) and budgets? Evaluation used a single prover (Goedel-Prover-V2-32B); cross-prover robustness is untested.
5. **Prover-derived fitness during search:** Can cheap prover signals (e.g., first-step attempts, tactic embeddings) guide search toward prover-effective statements? Currently prover feedback is too expensive to use during search; lightweight proxies might help.

## Limitations
- LLM semantic judge may misalign with prover utility (false positives/negatives).
- Bounded repair (2 attempts) may discard fixable candidates, especially for complex errors.
- EvolAST fallback is conservative, potentially limiting diversity gains; rule set is fixed.
- Evaluation on two benchmarks with specific LLMs; generalizability to other domains/models unclear.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (repertoire search improves proving):** High. Strong empirical support from SH@100 and prover metrics showing improved downstream success.
- **Mechanism 2 (compilation-gated search with bounded repair):** High. Ablations confirm repair's critical role; budget audibility is maintained.
- **Mechanism 3 (usage penalty + islands reduce mode collapse):** High. Clear reduction in Gini/top-10% share; island ablations confirm diversification effect.

## Next Checks
1. **Judge-prover alignment study:** Measure agreement between CriticLean verdicts and actual prover success on a held-out subset to quantify judge noise.
2. **Repair budget sensitivity:** Vary bounded repair attempts (0,1,2,5) while fixing total generator calls; record SH@100 and salvage rate to test trade-offs.
3. **Island migration dynamics:** Ablate island count (K=1,2,4) and migration intervals (5,10,20 generations); monitor archive diversity and SH@100 to optimize mixing.
