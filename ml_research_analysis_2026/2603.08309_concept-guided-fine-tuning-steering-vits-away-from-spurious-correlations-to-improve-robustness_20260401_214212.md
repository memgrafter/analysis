# Concept-Guided Fine-Tuning: Steering ViTs away from Spurious Correlations to Improve Robustness

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08309
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08309
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuning a ViT-B with Concept-Guided Fine-Tuning (CFT) on just 1,500 images increases ImageNet-A accuracy from 13.26% to 27.76% while preserving in-distribution accuracy.

## Executive Summary
Vision Transformers (ViTs) often rely on spurious visual correlations, such as backgrounds, leading to severe performance degradation under distribution shift. Concept-Guided Fine-Tuning (CFT) offers a solution by steering the model’s internal reasoning toward fine-grained, class-relevant semantic concepts. The method automatically generates pixel-level concept masks using an LLM to propose attributes and a Vision-Language Model (Grounded SAM) to segment them. During fine-tuning on a small subset of images, the model’s relevance map (via AttnLRP) is aligned with these concept masks while a consistency loss preserves classification accuracy. This approach substantially improves robustness across multiple out-of-distribution benchmarks without requiring full retraining or human annotations.

## Method Summary
CFT operates in three stages: 1) **Concept Discovery**: For a set of classes (500 of 1,000 ImageNet classes), an LLM (GPT-4o-mini) proposes a list of visually discriminative attributes (e.g., "beak" for "bird"). 2) **Validation and Mask Generation**: For each class, a sample of images is processed by Grounded SAM to segment the proposed concepts. Concepts are retained if they appear in ≥15% of samples and cover ≥20% of the object area. For each fine-tuning image, Grounded SAM segments all validated concepts for its class; their masks are combined into a single binary guidance mask. 3) **Fine-tuning**: A pretrained ViT is fine-tuned on 3 images per class (1,500 total) with a dual-loss objective: a relevance alignment loss encourages the model's AttnLRP map to highlight concept pixels and ignore non-concept pixels, and a classification-consistency loss stabilizes predictions. This process steers the model away from background reliance while maintaining in-distribution accuracy.

## Key Results
- CFT improves ViT-B robustness on ImageNet-A from 13.26% to 27.76% (+14.5% absolute).
- Relevance alignment with ground-truth object masks significantly improves (mIoU +5.32% for ViT-B).
- Concept-based masks yield larger robustness gains than conventional foreground-background segmentation masks.
- Performance gains generalize to unseen classes not used during fine-tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** CFT steers ViTs away from spurious correlations by explicitly aligning the model's internal relevance map with fine-grained, class-relevant semantic concepts.  
**Mechanism:** During fine-tuning, the model’s patch-level relevance map (computed via AttnLRP) is optimized to maximize attribution within pixels covered by concept masks and minimize attribution outside these masks. This forces the model to base its decision on semantically meaningful regions rather than background shortcuts.  
**Core assumption:** AttnLRP produces a faithful redistribution of the output prediction signal, making it a suitable optimization target for shaping the model’s reasoning.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract states the approach optimizes internal relevance maps to align with spatially grounded concept masks. Sections 3.1–3.3 detail relevance extraction via AttnLRP and the alignment loss formulation.  
**Break condition:** If the alignment loss weight dominates the classification loss, the model may overfit to the coarse concept masks and suffer a catastrophic drop in classification accuracy (Table 6 shows removal of `L_cls` harms robustness).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Concept masks are generated automatically without human annotation by combining an LLM’s label-free semantic attributes with a VLM’s zero-shot grounding capability.  
**Mechanism:** For each class, an LLM proposes discriminative attributes. For each training image, a VLM (Grounded SAM) segments these attributes wherever they appear, producing binary masks. A validation step filters concepts based on occurrence rate and spatial coverage to ensure quality.  
**Core assumption:** LLM-proposed attributes are semantically relevant and visually groundable in most training images, and the VLM can accurately segment them in a zero-shot manner.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract describes the use of an LLM and VLM for automatic concept mask creation. Section 3.2 covers concept set creation, validation criteria (occurrence rate ≥15%, spatial coverage ≥20%), and mask generation via Grounded SAM.  
**Break condition:** If the LLM generates non-visual or abstract concepts, or if the VLM fails to segment small/thin object parts (<2% area), resulting masks become empty or noisy, degrading the alignment signal (Limitations, Sec. 9.1).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** CFT preserves in-distribution accuracy while improving OOD robustness through a dual-loss objective combining relevance alignment with a classification-consistency constraint.  
**Mechanism:** The total loss combines an alignment component (with separate terms for concept and non-concept regions) and a classification-consistency term (cross-entropy against the original model’s argmax prediction). This prevents the model from sacrificing accuracy to satisfy the alignment constraint.  
**Core assumption:** The original pretrained model’s predictions are sufficiently accurate on the training distribution to serve as a stable anchor for preserving performance.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions suppressing focus on spurious backgrounds while preserving accuracy. Section 3.3 defines `L_cls` as cross-entropy with the original model’s prediction. Table 6 ablation shows removing `L_cls` harms robustness.  
**Break condition:** If `λ_cls` is too low, the model drifts from its original prediction behavior, potentially reducing in-distribution accuracy. If `λ_align` is too low, the alignment effect vanishes and robustness gains disappear.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Attention-aware Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (AttnLRP)**  
  *Why needed:* Provides a transformer-faithful, conservation-satisfying relevance map that reliably indicates which image patches influenced the prediction, unlike gradient-based methods that can be unstable in ViTs.  
  *Quick check:* How does AttnLRP’s propagation rule differ from standard LRP, and why is it more suitable for transformers?

- **Concept: Zero-shot Vision-Language Grounding (Grounded SAM)**  
  *Why needed:* Enables automatic, annotation-free segmentation of LLM-proposed semantic concepts in any image, moving beyond coarse foreground-background masks to fine-grained guidance.  
  *Quick check:* What two models are combined in Grounded SAM, and what is each component’s role?

- **Concept: Label-free Concept Discovery**  
  *Why needed:* Generates class-specific, semantically meaningful attributes without requiring human-labeled concept annotations or segmentation masks, scaling to large datasets like ImageNet.  
  *Quick check:* What two validation criteria (occurrence rate, spatial coverage) are used to filter the initial LLM-generated concept set, and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** LLM (GPT-4o-mini) -> Concept Proposal -> VLM (Grounded SAM) -> Concept Mask Generation -> AttnLRP -> Relevance Computation -> Dual-loss Finetuner -> Optimized ViT.
- **Critical path:** LLM concept generation -> VLM segmentation & mask creation for validation -> AttnLRP relevance computation -> Dual-loss optimization -> OOD evaluation. Concept generation/validation is a one-time preprocessing step; finetuning iterates over relevance computation + loss.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Minimal data (1,500 images) vs. heavy preprocessing cost (running Grounded SAM on 15,000+ images). Finer-grained concepts improve robustness but risk empty masks; validation thresholds balance this. Loss weighting: heavy `λ_non-concept` (1.2) suppresses spurious backgrounds but must balance with `λ_cls` (0.2) to preserve accuracy.
- **Failure signatures:** High rate of empty concept masks → LLM generated non-visual concepts or VLM segmentation failed. No OOD improvement despite stable IN-V accuracy → `λ_align` too low or concept masks not discriminative. Large drop in IN-V accuracy after finetuning → `λ_cls` too low or classification-consistency loss ineffective.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Baseline replication: Implement CFT on ViT-B with 1,500 ImageNet-1K images; verify IN-V accuracy ≈81% and IN-A improvement from 13.3% to ~27.8% (Table 1). 2) Relevance method ablation: Replace AttnLRP with GradCAM/Gradient-Rollout/IIA; expect AttnLRP to yield highest OOD gains (Table 7). 3) Loss component ablation: Remove `L_non-concept`, `L_concept`, or `L_cls` one at a time; predict `L_non-concept` removal to severely hurt IN-A/ObjectNet, and `L_cls` removal to harm all robustness (Table 6).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can dynamic weighting of semantic concepts, based on their discriminative power for specific distribution shifts, further improve CFT's robustness? The paper proposes "Adaptive Concept Weighting" as future work, noting different concepts may contribute unequally to robustness for specific OOD scenarios.
- Does incorporating hierarchical and compositional relationships between concepts enhance both robustness and interpretability compared to independent concept masking? Future work suggests exploring "Hierarchical and Compositional Concepts," as real-world objects exhibit structured semantics beyond independent parts.
- How does CFT's robustness degrade when its underlying Vision-Language Model (GroundedSAM) fails to accurately localize concepts, especially for abstract or fine-grained attributes? The limitations section identifies dependency on GroundedSAM's quality, noting failures for abstract concepts or very small parts, but does not quantify impact.
- To what extent do LLM-generated concepts inadvertently reinforce spurious correlations present in the LLM's training data, rather than promoting true semantic features? The limitations warn that LLMs may "reinforce 'shortcuts'" by suggesting concepts based on co-occurrences in their own data.
- Can CFT's concept-generation and alignment pipeline be effectively transferred to specialized domains (e.g., medical imaging, satellite imagery) where LLM concept vocabularies are mismatched? The limitations note a "domain mismatch" risk and future work suggests exploring "Application to Other Domains" with domain-specific LLMs.

## Limitations
- AttnLRP implementation details for ViTs are underspecified; reproduction may yield different relevance maps.
- GroundedSAM's zero-shot segmentation cost and accuracy for thin/fine-grained concepts are unclear, and the computational overhead is substantial.
- Concept validation thresholds may produce many empty masks for some classes, weakening the guidance signal.

## Confidence
- OOD robustness gains: **High** (consistent improvements across 5 benchmarks).
- Concept masks vs. coarse masks: **Medium** (gains shown but rely on mask quality).
- Relevance alignment mechanism: **Medium** (ablation supports, but AttnLRP faithfulness not independently validated).
