# CBMAS: Cognitive Behavioral Modeling via Activation Steering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.06109  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06109  
- **Reference count**: 37  
- **Primary result**: Introduces continuous activation steering diagnostics (α-sweeps, bias response curves, layer-site sensitivity) to map bias propagation, revealing layer-dependent tipping points and injection-site criticality while preserving fluency.

## Executive Summary
CBMAS addresses the unpredictability of cognitive biases in LLMs by providing a fine-grained diagnostic framework that combines contrastive steering vectors with systematic coefficient sweeps. Applied to GPT-2 Small across sycophancy, reassurance, satisficing, and deference biases, the method uncovers non-linear bias response trajectories and identifies early layers as key leverage points for monotonic steering. The approach maintains output stability (low KL divergence) while shifting targeted behaviors, bridging behavioral evaluation with internal representational dynamics.

## Method Summary
CBMAS constructs steering vectors by averaging the difference in residual stream activations between contrastive prompt pairs (e.g., supportive vs. unsupportive responses) at a chosen layer. During inference, the vector is scaled by a coefficient α and injected into the residual stream at a specified site. The core innovation lies in sweeping α continuously (e.g., from -10 to 10) and measuring metrics (logit differences, KL divergence, perplexity) across readout layers, producing Bias Response Curves (BRCs). This reveals tipping points, layer-specific sensitivity, and fluency trade-offs. Control vectors (random, orthogonal) validate direction specificity.

## Key Results
- Continuous α-sweeps expose latent tipping points and non-linear bias propagation that discrete before/after comparisons miss.
- Early-layer injection (L1) yields sharp, monotonic steering effects; later layers (L11) show attenuated logit shifts but stable KL, indicating fluency preservation.
- Injection site critically influences response monotonicity: L1 produces clean curves while L0 yields noisier behavior; control vectors remain flat, confirming specificity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: α‑Sweeps Reveal Tipping Points
- **Claim**: Continuous α‑sweeps can reveal latent tipping points in bias propagation that discrete before/after snapshots miss.
- **Mechanism**: By systematically varying α and measuring output metrics, smooth bias response curves are generated. Non‑linearities or zero‑crossings indicate thresholds where small intervention changes cause large behavioral flips.
- **Core assumption**: The bias direction is approximately linearly traversable in residual stream space.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions “tipping points”; Section 3.4 describes BRC generation; Section 4.2 shows continuous logit‑difference trajectories with tipping regions around α≈0.
- **Break condition**: If bias direction involves non‑linear feature mixing or superposition, α‑sweeps may yield noisy, non‑monotonic curves.

### Mechanism 2: Layer‑Sensitive Injection
- **Claim**: The layer of injection critically determines steering efficacy and interpretability, with early layers (L0–L1) showing sharper, more monotonic bias encoding.
- **Mechanism**: Injecting at different layers probes where the bias signal is most coherent. Sharp slopes in early‑layer readouts suggest bias is encoded directionally soon after embedding; later layers show attenuation, implying signal integration or dilution.
- **Core assumption**: Bias representation becomes increasingly distributed after initial formation.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 4.1 notes sharp effects at shallow layers (L1) and washed‑out signal at L11; Section 4.3 shows L1 injection produces monotonic Δlogit while L0 is noisier.
- **Break condition**: If bias is only encoded in late layers or via complex interactions, early‑layer injection may be weak or inconsistent.

### Mechanism 3: Fluency Preservation
- **Claim**: Steering can shift behavior while preserving model fluency, as measured by stable KL divergence and perplexity, indicating targeted intervention without destabilizing the overall distribution.
- **Mechanism**: KL divergence between base and intervened distributions separates targeted logit shifts from general corruption. Low, symmetric KL suggests steering moves probabilities between specific outputs while maintaining distribution shape.
- **Core assumption**: Cognitive bias directions occupy a low‑dimensional subspace; moving along it does not require large disruptive changes.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 4.2 reports stable, well‑controlled KL; Section 4.2 notes low symmetric KL at L11; Section 5 mentions lightweight fluency checks.
- **Break condition**: If bias direction is entangled with core linguistic/factual features, steering may increase KL/perplexity, forcing a trade‑off.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Contrastive Activation Steering  
  **Why needed here**: Steering vectors are built by contrasting activations from prompts yielding different behavioral outcomes. Without this, the vector’s semantic meaning is unclear.  
  **Quick check**: Given two prompts differing only in desired output, how would you compute the steering vector from their residual stream activations at a specific layer?

- **Concept**: Mechanistic Interpretability Probes (Logit Lens)  
  **Why needed here**: Logit lens projects intermediate‑layer activations to the output vocabulary, creating “bias curves” that track how the bias signal evolves depth‑wise, linking internal representations to external behavior.  
  **Quick check**: What does a steep slope in a layer’s logit‑difference curve (as α varies) imply about that layer’s role in expressing the targeted bias?

- **Concept**: Distributional Metrics (KL Divergence, Perplexity)  
  **Why needed here**: To distinguish clean steering from output corruption, the framework monitors KL(p₀‖p_α) and per‑token perplexity. High KL would indicate disruption to the model’s core predictive distribution.  
  **Quick check**: Why might a steering method that strongly flips the logit difference between two tokens still be considered “fluent” if its KL divergence remains low?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Dataset Module → Vector Builder → Intervention Runner → Metric Aggregator → Visualization Suite  
  (Each step: loads contrastive prompt pairs, computes mean difference vector v, injects α·v during forward pass, computes metrics per α and read layer, plots BRCs with controls.)

- **Critical path**:  
  1. Choose a bias dataset (e.g., sycophancy).  
  2. Build steering vector v at one layer (e.g., L1).  
  3. Run α‑sweep (default -10 to 10 step 0.5).  
  4. Plot logit diff and KL at multiple read layers.  
  5. Compare injection sites (L0 vs. L1).  
  6. Add control vectors (random, orthogonal) to confirm specificity.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Granularity vs. Cost: Sweeping α finely and across all inject×read layer combinations is computationally expensive; defaults balance coverage for small models.  
  - Specificity vs. Generality: Vector built on one bias dataset may not transfer; assumes bias is linearly separable in chosen layer.  
  - Metric choice: Logit diff is sensitive but noisy; KL/perplexity are stability proxies but may miss subtle semantic drifts.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Non‑monotonic or flat BRCs for all read layers → vector may not capture consistent bias direction; check dataset contrastiveness or try different injection sites.  
  - High KL at moderate α → intervention disrupts core distribution; reduce max α or reconsider layer.  
  - Control vectors producing curves similar to bias vector → direction not specific; re‑verify vector construction or increase dataset size.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Replicate Figure 3 (Reassurance, inject L0): Sweep α, read at L1 and L11, plot Δlogit. Expect steep slope at L1, shallow at L11.  
  2. Injection Site Comparison (Figure 6): Build vectors at L0 and L1, inject each, read at L6. Compare monotonicity and control vector flatness.  
  3. Control Vector Baseline: For a given inject/read pair, run sweeps with random unit and orthogonal vectors. Confirm they yield flat Δlogit curves across α.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How do steering effects persist, decay, or compound over extended autoregressive generation?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors state that "they do not test how steering effects persist, decay, or compound over longer autoregressive sequence‑level generation."
- **Why unresolved**: CBMAS currently evaluates only immediate next‑token predictions; longer‑term dynamics may introduce cumulative effects or feedback loops.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct α‑sweeps while generating multi‑token continuations, tracking bias metrics (e.g., logit differences, KL divergence) as a function of sequence length for multiple injection layers and coefficients.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Which specific circuit components (e.g., attention heads, MLP features, residual pathways) causally mediate bias propagation?
- **Basis in paper**: "causal study is needed: layer‑site α‑sweeps indicate how bias propagates within the model but not which circuit components are responsible."
- **Why unresolved**: Layer‑level sensitivity identifies where bias is expressed but not how it is computed; without pinpointing underlying mechanisms, steering remains coarse.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Combine CBMAS with activation patching on individual heads/MLP layers and sparse autoencoder feature analysis; measure changes in bias response curves to isolate causal components.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can we design metrics that capture coherence, factuality, and discourse‑level quality when steering LLMs?
- **Basis in paper**: "metric coverage can be improved to encompass coherence/factuality, and discourse‑level diagnostics that would reveal downstream effects on longer text."
- **Why unresolved**: Current proxies (perplexity, KL) may fail to detect subtle degradations over extended contexts.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Develop evaluation benchmarks or human studies that explicitly score coherence, factual consistency, and discourse structure for steered generations; correlate with automatic metrics.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Do the layer‑dependent tipping points and injection site effects generalize to larger LLMs and other cognitive biases?
- **Basis in paper**: The study only tests GPT‑2 Small on four synthetic bias datasets; broader generalizability is unknown.
- **Why unresolved**: Architectural differences and model scale can reshape representational geometry; distinct biases may follow different propagation patterns.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Replicate CBMAS on larger models (e.g., LLaMA‑2, GPT‑3) and a wider array of biases (e.g., gender, racial, political); compare layer‑sensitivity maps and tipping points.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability untested**: Claims based solely on GPT‑2 Small and four synthetic bias datasets; unknown if patterns hold for larger models or real‑world biases.
- **Behavioral validity gap**: Interventions evaluated via automatic metrics only; no human assessment confirms qualitative output changes or artifact introduction.
- **Linearity assumption risk**: Method assumes bias directions are linearly traversable; non‑linear feature mixing could produce misleading tipping points or failure.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (α‑sweeps reveal tipping points)**: Medium – Strong empirical support in plots, but break condition (non‑linear mixing) not ruled out for broader biases.
- **Mechanism 2 (layer‑sensitive injection)**: Low – Results are model‑specific (GPT‑2 Small); layer hierarchy may differ in deeper architectures; neighbor papers suggest multi‑behavior steering alters sensitivity.
- **Mechanism 3 (fluency preservation)**: Medium – KL/perplexity are standard, but low KL ≠ preserved task capability; no factual/knowledge retention checks.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑model replication**: Replicate key experiments (inject L1, read L6) on Pythia‑70M and LLaMA‑7B using the same sycophancy dataset. Assess similarity of layer tipping points.
2. **Cross‑bias transfer test**: Build steering vector on *satisficing* dataset, inject into *reassurance* evaluation run. Measure Δlogit for reassurance tokens; a flat curve would confirm bias‑specificity.
3. **Human behavioral audit**: For α values at inferred tipping points (e.g., α≈0), sample 100 model outputs per condition. Have raters blind‑score for target bias presence (e.g., “Does response show undue deference?”). Correlate human scores with automated BRC trends.
