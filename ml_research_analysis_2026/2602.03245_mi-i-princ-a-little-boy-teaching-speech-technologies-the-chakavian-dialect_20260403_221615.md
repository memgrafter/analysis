# Mići Princ -- A Little Boy Teaching Speech Technologies the Chakavian Dialect

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.03245  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03245  
- **Reference count:** 6  
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuned Whisper-large-v3 on the first Chakavian dialect dataset achieves 52.5% relative word error rate reduction and 65.77% relative character error rate reduction, with dataset and model publicly released.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the absence of open dialectal speech resources for Croatian by creating the first Chakavian dialect dataset, derived from a translated version of *The Little Prince*. The dataset provides word-aligned audio and text for 16 micro-dialects, totaling 79 minutes of studio speech. After fine-tuning OpenAI’s Whisper-large-v3 model, the system significantly outperforms the baseline, especially on unseen speakers. The authors release both the dataset and the fine-tuned model, enabling reproducible research in low-resource dialectal ASR.

## Method Summary
The dataset construction involved recording 16 voice actors, each performing a distinct character using a different Chakavian micro-dialect. Raw chapter audio underwent VAD and speaker diarization, then forced alignment (Kaldi) produced word-level timestamps. The resulting audio segments were capped at 30 seconds and paired with normalized text (standard Croatian characters, no punctuation). Training and test splits were defined by chapters: chapters 13 and 15 were held out for testing, containing both in-distribution speakers (Autor, Mići Princ) and out-of-distribution ones (Geograf, Dilavac). The Whisper-large-v3 model was fine-tuned for 80 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-5 and an effective batch size of 16. Evaluation used WER and CER on the normalized transcripts.

## Key Results
- Overall relative reduction: 52.5% WER, 65.77% CER.  
- Unseen speakers: 40–44% WER reduction, CER reduction up to 71.59%.  
- Dataset and fine-tuned model publicly available via CLARIN.SI and HuggingFace.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Word-aligned audio-text pairs
Precise word-level timestamps create unambiguous supervised training examples. This eliminates segmentation errors and lets the model directly learn dialect-specific acoustic patterns. Evidence: 11,591 aligned words with start/end times via Kaldi. Failure if alignment error exceeds a few percent.

### Mechanism 2: Fine-tuning a large pretrained multilingual model
Whisper’s broad pretraining supplies robust feature extraction; low-learning-rate fine-tuning adapts to Chakavian while retaining general knowledge. The dialect shares core phonology with standard Croatian, so only surface-level adjustments are needed. Evidence: strong reductions with only 79 minutes of data. Failure if lexical/phonotactic divergence >20%.

### Mechanism 3: Multiple micro-dialects via distinct voice actors
Using 16 speakers with varied micro-dialects exposes the model to intra-dialectal variation, akin to data augmentation. This improves robustness to unseen speakers. Evidence: actors map to different geographic origins in metadata; studio recordings isolate dialect from noise. Failure if micro-dialect sampling is regionally biased.

## Foundational Learning
- **Transfer learning for low-resource ASR:** Needed because Chakavian lacks large speech corpora; fine-tuning reuses pretrained knowledge. Quick check: Which hyperparameters (learning rate, epochs) balance adaptation and overfitting on tiny data?
- **Word-level speech-text alignment:** Needed to create gold-standard (audio, text) pairs with exact correspondence, removing segmentation ambiguity. Quick check: How do errors from forced alignment (e.g., Kaldi) propagate into final WER/CER?
- **Dialectal variation and speaker generalization:** Needed for real-world deployment across speakers; dataset must sample variation to avoid speaker-specific overfitting. Quick check: Why might a model succeed on training speakers yet fail on new speakers from the same dialect?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data ingestion -> Preprocessing (VAD, diarization) -> Alignment (Kaldi) -> Dataset formatting (ASR-ready segments) -> Model fine-tuning (Whisper) -> Evaluation

**Critical path:** VAD → Diarization → Alignment → ASR segmentation → Fine-tuning → Evaluation. Alignment quality is pivotal; errors cascade.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Segment length: 30-second maximum balances memory limits and contextual retention; shorter segments may lose prosodic cues.  
- Text normalization: Substituting dialect-specific diacritics with standard Croatian improves consistency but discards orthographic richness.  
- Test split: Chapters 13 & 15 are small; results may have higher variance.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Hallucinations in early epochs indicate learning rate too high or insufficient adaptation data.  
2. High WER on unseen speakers suggests inadequate speaker/micro-dialect coverage in training.  
3. Alignment failures (Kaldi) cause skipped words or timestamp mismatches.  
4. Overfitting: training loss decreases while evaluation loss rises; early stopping may be needed despite 80 epochs.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline replication: Run vanilla `openai/whisper-large-v3` on test split; expected WER ~35.43%.  
2. Alignment ablation: Compare fine-tuning on automatically aligned data versus manually corrected alignment to quantify impact.  
3. Speaker held-out test: Train only on Autor and Mići Princ turns; evaluate on Geograf and Dilavac to measure generalization limits.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does incorporating rare Chakavian-specific diacritic characters (¨e, ¨ı, ¨a) improve performance despite their low frequency?  
2. Could more exhaustive hyper-parameter optimization yield significantly better Whisper fine-tuning results?  
3. How well does the fine-tuned model generalize to spontaneous, non-studio Chakavian speech in noisy environments?  
4. What is the prevalence of undetected audio-text mismatches in the Mići Princ dataset beyond the single identified case?  
5. What explains the differing error rate trajectories during fine-tuning across individual speakers, especially the transient hallucinations?

## Limitations
- Dataset split and speaker leakage: Exact test set composition is unspecified; risk that test speakers appear in training if chapter boundaries are mis-assigned.  
- Alignment quality: Word-level timestamps were auto-generated; no reported error rate; misalignments could poison training.  
- Generalization scope: Claims are limited to 16 actors/characters; performance on broader Chakavian-speaking population is unknown.  
- Dialect normalization: Removal of diacritics may omit phonetic cues relevant to Chakavian.

## Confidence
- **High:** Dataset exists and is publicly available; fine-tuned model beats baseline on the provided test split; word-aligned audio-text pairs were created.  
- **Medium:** Reported WER/CER reductions are reproducible under the assumed train/test split and evaluation settings; unseen-speaker gains hold for Geograf/Dilavac.  
- **Low:** Results generalize beyond the 16-actor cohort; alignment errors are negligible; 79-minute dataset is sufficient for robust dialect modeling; normalization does not distort key phonetic contrasts.
