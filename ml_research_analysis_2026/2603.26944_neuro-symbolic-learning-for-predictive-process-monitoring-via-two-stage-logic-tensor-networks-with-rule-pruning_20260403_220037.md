# Neuro-Symbolic Learning for Predictive Process Monitoring via Two-Stage Logic Tensor Networks with Rule Pruning

## Quick Facts
*   arXiv ID: 2603.26944
*   Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26944
*   Reference count: 27
*   Primary result: A novel two-stage neuro-symbolic method using Logic Tensor Networks (LTNs) with rule pruning significantly improves predictive process monitoring, especially in compliance-constrained scenarios with scarce examples, achieving up to 89.96% F1-score.

## Executive Summary
Predictive process monitoring often relies on purely data-driven models that fail to incorporate critical domain constraints (e.g., healthcare protocols, financial regulations), leading to accuracy issues and non-compliance. This paper introduces a neuro-symbolic approach using Logic Tensor Networks (LTNs) to integrate domain knowledge as differentiable logical constraints. A key innovation is a two-stage optimization strategy: first, weighted pretraining prioritizes learning from data over knowledge to avoid catastrophic collapse; second, rule pruning based on validation satisfaction dynamics retains only consistent, contributive axioms. Evaluated on four real-world event logs, the method substantially outperforms data-only baselines and ablations, particularly when compliant training examples are severely limited.

## Method Summary
The method integrates domain knowledge (control-flow, temporal, payload rules) with a neural sequence model (LSTM/Transformer) via Logic Tensor Networks. Training occurs in two stages. Stage 1: Pretraining with a weighted loss function `L = 1 - (α·SatAgg(KD) + β·SatAgg(KP))`, where `α=0.8` prioritizes data axioms (`KD`) over knowledge axioms (`KP`). This stabilizes the neural predictor before strong logical enforcement. Stage 2: After pretraining, rules in `KP` are pruned. For each rule, a gating score `gi = ¯si · e^(-λ·Var(si))` is computed from its mean (`¯si`) and variance (`Var(si)`) of satisfaction scores on a validation set. Rules with `gi ≥ τ` (threshold tuned on validation) form the refined knowledge base `K'P`. The model is then fine-tuned using the standard LTN loss `L = 1 - SatAgg(KD ∪ K'P)`.

## Key Results
*   The two-stage approach significantly outperforms data-only baselines (LSTM, Transformer) and ablations on four event logs, with F1-scores up to 89.96% on the Sepsis dataset's compliance-aware test set.
*   Ablation studies confirm the necessity of the two-stage mechanism: naive knowledge injection without weighting or pruning (`LTN-NoP`) catastrophically degrades performance (e.g., 18.79% F1 on Sepsis).
*   The method excels in scenarios with very few compliant training examples (e.g., 4% in Sepsis), demonstrating its ability to generalize compliance from logical constraints.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** The two-stage optimization (weighted axiom loss followed by rule pruning) prevents the model from satisfying logical constraints at the expense of predictive accuracy, a known failure mode in standard LTNs.
*   **Mechanism:** Stage 1 uses a loss weighting (`α > β`) that prioritizes learning from data axioms (`KD`) over knowledge axioms (`KP`). This stabilizes the neural predicate learning before logical constraints are strongly enforced. Stage 2 prunes rules based on gating scores (`gi = ¯si·e^{-λ·Var(si)}`) that favor rules with high mean satisfaction (`¯si`) and low variance (`Var(si)`), filtering out inconsistent or non-contributive axioms.
*   **Core assumption:** Rules with stable, high satisfaction across samples are more likely to be valid and useful for the prediction task, while rules with low or variable satisfaction are either contradictory to the data or irrelevant.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...without proper control (LTN-NoP) severely degrades performance... Our two-stage approach addresses this fundamental limitation."
    *   [section 4.4] "To address LTNs’ tendency to satisfy logical formulas at the expense of predictive accuracy, we propose a two-stage optimization strategy... weighted loss... followed by rule pruning."
*   **Break condition:** If the gating score threshold (`τ`) is set incorrectly (too high or too low), it may prune away valid contributive rules or retain harmful ones, negating the stabilization benefit.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Rule pruning based on satisfaction dynamics refines the knowledge base to contain only axioms that are both logically consistent with the data and dynamically relevant during training, improving final model performance and interpretability.
*   **Mechanism:** After pretraining, satisfaction scores (`si`) for each rule `ri` are computed on the validation set. The gating function combines the mean (`¯si`) and variance (`Var(si)`) of these scores. High mean indicates general satisfaction; low variance indicates stability across samples. This selects rules that the model can reliably satisfy, discarding those that are often violated or satisfied only on outlier cases.
*   **Core assumption:** A rule's utility for guiding learning correlates with its stable satisfaction across a representative validation set. Unstable rules introduce noise or conflict during fine-tuning.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 4.4] "construct a refined knowledge base `K′P` by selecting rules via gating scores... Rules with `gi ≥ τ` are added... retaining only consistent, contributive axioms."
    *   [table 2] Ablation results: `LTN-NoP` (no pruning) catastrophically fails (e.g., 18.79% F1 on Sepsis), while `Two-Stage-L` (with pruning) achieves 75.82% F1.
*   **Break condition:** If the validation set is not representative of the training distribution, the gating scores may misrepresent rule utility, leading to poor pruning decisions.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Injecting domain knowledge as differentiable constraints provides a strong inductive bias that enables models to generalize to compliance-critical scenarios where compliant training examples are scarce.
*   **Mechanism:** Logical rules (e.g., control-flow, temporal, payload) explicitly encode mandatory domain behaviors. During training, the LTN loss (`1 - SatAgg`) forces the neural predictor's outputs to satisfy these formulas. This encodes "commonsense" or regulatory requirements directly into the model's decision boundary, compensating for the lack of compliant examples in the training data.
*   **Core assumption:** The formalized logical rules accurately capture the true compliance constraints of the domain. The fuzzy semantics of LTNs allow the model to learn a smooth approximation of the constraint boundary even from few examples.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "The approach excels particularly in compliance-constrained scenarios with limited compliant training examples."
    *   [section 5.3] On the compliance-aware split with only 4% compliant examples in training (Sepsis), `Two-Stage-L` achieves 89.96% F1 vs. 73.26% for LSTM.
*   **Break condition:** If the extracted logical rules are incomplete, incorrect, or contradictory, the inductive bias becomes misleading, potentially harming generalization even with pruning.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Fuzzy Logic & Real Numbers in Logic (LTN Core)**
    *   *Why needed here:* LTNs replace binary truth values with continuous `[0,1]` tensors, making logical connectives and quantifiers differentiable. This is essential for gradient-based optimization of a neural network to satisfy logical constraints.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does a `pMean` aggregator for a universal quantifier `∀` in an LTN differ from a standard mean, and why is it stricter?

*   **Concept: Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) for Process Constraints**
    *   *Why needed here:* Process constraints often involve sequencing and timing (e.g., "activity A must eventually be followed by B within 2 hours"). LTL provides a formal syntax to express these, which the paper converts to FOL for LTN integration.
    *   *Quick check question:* What does the LTL formula `□(Rev → ♢Exam)` mean in a business process context, and how might it be grounded as an FOL axiom?

*   **Concept: Knowledge Base Pruning via Dynamic Metrics**
    *   *Why needed here:* Raw rule extraction yields many noisy, redundant, or conflicting axioms. Pruning based on satisfaction dynamics (`mean` and `variance`) is the proposed signal for identifying which rules are "learnable" and stable.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why would a rule with high mean satisfaction but also high variance be a candidate for pruning? What does high variance suggest about that rule's relationship to the data?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component flow:** Feature Extractor -> Rule Extractor -> Knowledge Base Builder (separates KD, KP) -> Pruner -> LTN Classifier (with two-stage training loop)
*   **Critical path:** Raw Event Log -> Feature/Rule Extraction -> Initial `KD, KP` -> **Stage 1 Training (Weighted Loss)** -> Validation Set Satisfaction Calculation -> **Pruning to `K'P`** -> **Stage 2 Fine-Tuning** -> Final Model.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Weighting (`α, β`):* High `α` speeds up data fitting but risks ignoring knowledge; low `α` risks vacuous satisfaction. Paper uses `α=0.8, β=0.2`.
    *   *Pruning Threshold (`τ`):* High `τ` yields very few rules (safe but potentially under-constrained); low `τ` keeps many rules (risking noise). Selected via validation.
    *   *Axiom Formalization:* Converting LTL to FOL can be non-trivial and may lose temporal nuance if done incorrectly.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Catastrophic Collapse:** F1 score plummets (e.g., to ~18% on Sepsis). Signature: `LTN-NoP` variant performance. Cause: Unweighted knowledge injection forces vacuous satisfaction.
    *   **Pruning Failure:** Performance similar to `LTN-Data` or only marginal gain over `LTN-NoP`. Signature: Gating scores either all pass or all fail. Cause: Poor `τ` or uninformative satisfaction dynamics.
    *   **Compliance Failure on Test:** High accuracy but low rule satisfaction on compliance-aware test set. Signature: Disconnect between predictive metric and logical satisfaction on compliant cases. Cause: Overfitting to spurious data patterns; pruned rules insufficient.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Comparison (RQ1):** Run `LSTM`, `TFR`, `Two-Stage-L`, `Two-Stage-T` on temporal split. Expect `Two-Stage` to outperform baselines, especially on smaller datasets (Sepsis, BPIC2012).
    2.  **Ablation Study (RQ2):** Compare `LTN-Data`, `LTN-NoP`, and `Two-Stage`. Expect `LTN-NoP` to perform worst, `Two-Stage` best, proving the two-stage necessity.
    3.  **Compliance-Constrained Evaluation (RQ3):** Evaluate all models on the compliance-aware test set (enriched with compliant traces). Expect `Two-Stage` to show largest relative improvements over data-only baselines, especially on datasets with lowest % of compliant training examples (Sepsis: 4%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How can we formalize an explicit representation with specific templates to automatically connect features with rules?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "In future work, we plan to formalize an explicit representation defining specific templates to automatically connect features with rules." (Conclusion)
*   **Why unresolved:** This requires developing a systematic methodology for mapping extracted features and rules into a formal template structure, which has not been implemented or evaluated yet.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A validated set of templates that can be applied across different process domains, demonstrated to improve automation and accuracy in knowledge integration, ideally tested on additional datasets.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** How does the neuro-symbolic approach generalize to a real-world case study in a domain with critically important domain knowledge, such as healthcare?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Moreover, we aim to evaluate our neuro-symbolic approach on a case study where domain knowledge plays a critical role, such as in healthcare." (Conclusion)
*   **Why unresolved:** The current evaluation uses standard event logs but does not involve a detailed case study in a high-stakes domain where compliance and safety constraints are paramount.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive case study in a healthcare setting, showing that the approach adheres to medical guidelines and improves prediction of clinical outcomes, with validation from domain experts.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** Can the two-stage LTN approach be extended to support more process-aware prediction tasks, such as next event prediction and time-related aspects?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Finally, we plan to extend the approach to more process-aware tasks, such as predictions related to next events and time-related aspects." (Conclusion)
*   **Why unresolved:** The current method is designed for binary classification of process outcomes; extending it to sequential prediction and time estimation requires nontrivial modifications to the LTN framework and loss functions.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** An extended LTN model that outputs next activity or remaining time, with experiments showing improved accuracy and compliance on benchmark datasets for these tasks.

## Limitations
*   Rule extraction details (e.g., Declare mining parameters, LTL-to-FOL mapping) are incomplete, making exact reproduction challenging.
*   Selection of pruning threshold τ is vaguely described; the exact search strategy and relationship to final rule count are unknown.
*   Gating score assumes mean/variance of validation satisfaction reliably predicts rule utility; this may fail if validation distribution shifts or rules interact.
*   Limited analysis of hyperparameter λ sensitivity; the chosen values may be dataset-specific tweaks.

## Confidence
*   Two-stage optimization prevents catastrophic collapse: **High** (consistent ablation gains, failure of unweighted LTN).
*   Rule pruning refines knowledge base: **Medium** (depends on τ and validation; no exhaustive analysis of gating score distribution).
*   Knowledge injection enables generalization with scarce compliant data: **High** (significant improvements on compliance-aware splits, even with 4% compliant training samples).
