# Conditioning Protein Generation via Hopfield Pattern Multiplicity

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20115
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20115
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A single scalar bias added to attention logits shifts protein sequence generation toward user-specified functional subsets without retraining, with conditioning fidelity predicted by a PCA-space separation index.

## Executive Summary
This work introduces a training-free method to steer stochastic attention–based protein sequence generation toward a desired functional subset. By adding a multiplicative bias (multiplicity ratio ρ) to the attention logits of designated patterns, the energy landscape is tilted to increase the representation of those patterns during Langevin sampling. The approach requires no model retraining or architectural changes. Conditioning is exact at the energy level, but decoded phenotype fidelity depends on the geometric separation of functional and background subsets in the PCA space used for encoding. Across five Pfam families, the calibration gap between intended and observed phenotype fractions decreases monotonically with a proposed Fisher separation index (S). For families with high S (>0.3), phenotype transfer is near-perfect; for low S (<0.2), hard curation (training on the designated subset alone) is superior. The method’s utility is demonstrated by amplifying 23 known ω-conotoxin binders into 1,550 candidates preserving a critical pharmacophore at 98.3% fidelity.

## Method Summary
The method conditions stochastic attention (SA) generation by modifying the softmax attention logits. Sequences are encoded via one-hot vectors, centered, and PCA-projected to a low-dimensional space (d, retaining 95% variance). Columns of the projected data form a memory matrix X. A multiplicity vector r is constructed where designated patterns have weight ρ and others 1. During Langevin sampling, the update ξ ← (1−α)ξ + α X softmax(β Xᵀξ + log r) + noise incorporates the bias. The inverse temperature β* is chosen by sweeping for the entropy inflection of the attention distribution. Decoding reverses PCA and takes per-position argmax. Effective designated fraction f_eff = K_desρ/(K_desρ+K_bg) tracks the attention weight on designated patterns, while observed phenotype fraction f_obs measures marker residue fidelity after decoding. The calibration gap Δ = f_eff − f_obs quantifies the loss due to PCA geometry.

## Key Results
- Conditioning via multiplicity bias is exact at the energy level: attention fraction on designated patterns matches f_eff with <0.3% deviation for all ρ.
- Calibration gap Δ decreases monotonically with Fisher separation index S (R² = 0.80 across five Pfam families); high S (>0.3) yields near-perfect phenotype transfer, low S (<0.2) yields large Δ.
- Applied to ω-conotoxins, seeding from 23 Cav2.2 binders produced 1,550 candidates preserving the Tyr13 pharmacophore at 98.3% fidelity and all known binding determinants.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A single scalar bias (multiplicity ratio ρ) added to attention logits shifts generation toward a user-specified functional subset without retraining or architectural changes.
- **Mechanism:** The Hopfield energy's softmax attention distribution is reweighted by assigning higher multiplicity weights (ρ) to designated patterns, equivalent to storing ρ copies of those patterns without memory overhead. The logit bias log(ρ) deepens energy wells around designated sequences, increasing their attraction during Langevin sampling.
- **Core assumption:** The effective designated fraction (f_eff) in the continuous PCA-space attention distribution accurately reflects the intended conditioning strength, as validated by empirical tracking of ¯a_des to f_eff with <0.3% error.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "adds a bias term to these attention scores deepens the energy wells around specific sequences"
  - [section 2] "¯a_des tracked f_eff with <0.3% deviation at all ρ values"
  - [section S1] "f_eff(ρ) = K_desρ/(K_desρ+K_bg)" derived from softmax under equal-similarity assumption
- **Break condition:** When the PCA encoding fails to preserve residue-level variation defining the functional split (low Fisher separation S), the decoded phenotype fraction (f_obs) does not follow f_eff, creating a calibration gap.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The decoded phenotype fidelity is limited by the PCA dimensionality reduction, which may not separate functional subsets geometrically, creating a "calibration gap" between energy-level conditioning and sequence-level outcomes.
- **Mechanism:** The signal chain from multiplicity weights to decoded sequences passes through continuous PCA reconstruction (f_soft) and discrete argmax decoding (f_obs). If designated and background sequences overlap in PCA space, the P1 marker position's variation is not captured by the top d components, causing f_soft to remain near background levels regardless of ρ.
- **Core assumption:** PCA, while preserving global fold structure, can entangle functional residue variation with background variation, especially when functional differences are localized to few positions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2] "f_soft effectively flat at ∼0.27 regardless of ρ" for Kunitz (S=0.20)
  - [section 2] "the PCA encoding mapped designated and background sequences to overlapping regions at the P1 position"
  - [section 3] "When the designated and background subsets overlap in PCA space (low S), the energy-level tilt does not propagate to the decoded residue identity"
- **Break condition:** For families with S < 0.2 (WW, Forkhead), even ρ→1000 yields f_obs far below f_eff, making multiplicity weighting ineffective for phenotype transfer.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The Fisher separation index S, measuring PCA-space separation of functional subsets via pairwise cosine similarities, monotonically predicts the calibration gap Δ.
- **Mechanism:** S quantifies how well the leading principal components separate designated from background patterns. High S means functional variation aligns with top PCA axes, so multiplicity bias in PCA space translates to marker positions during decoding. Low S means functional variation lies in lower-variance components discarded by PCA, blocking signal propagation.
- **Core assumption:** The calibration gap arises purely from geometric entanglement in the fixed PCA basis, not from the multiplicity mechanism itself.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2] "Δ decreased monotonically with increasing separation index: WW (S=0.11) Δ=0.64; Homeobox (S=0.42) Δ=0.04"
  - [section 2] "linear fit across five Pfam families: Δ≈0.72−1.8S, R²=0.80"
  - [abstract] "calibration gap decreases monotonically with S (R²=0.80)"
- **Break condition:** For multi-position phenotypes where functional specificity depends on coordinated residues, S computed from a single marker may not capture the relevant geometry, potentially weakening prediction.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: PCA for sequence encoding**
  - **Why needed here:** Converts high-dimensional one-hot sequences (20L) into a compact continuous space (d ≪ 20L) where the Hopfield energy operates efficiently; retains 95% variance to preserve fold-level structure while discarding noise.
  - **Quick check question:** If the designated functional subset uses positions that are highly variable in the full family, will PCA likely preserve that variation in its top components? Why or why not?

- **Concept: Modern Hopfield energy and stochastic attention**
  - **Why needed here:** Provides a training-free generative mechanism where attention weights define a Boltzmann distribution over stored patterns; sampling via Langevin dynamics produces novel sequences by exploring the energy landscape defined by the alignment.
  - **Quick check question:** What role does the inverse temperature β play in the energy E(ξ) = ½||ξ||² − (1/β) log Σ exp(β m_k^T ξ)? How does β* relate to the number of effective patterns?

- **Concept: Softmax attention as a differentiable weighting**
  - **Why needed here:** The softmax over pattern similarities implements the associative memory retrieval; adding log(r_k) to logits is mathematically equivalent to weighting patterns by r_k in the energy, enabling conditioning without altering the memory matrix.
  - **Quick check question:** If you set ρ=100 for a designated subset of 10 patterns in a family of 100, what is f_eff? How would you compute the required ρ for a target f_eff=0.9?

## Architecture Onboarding
Alignment → PCA → memory matrix → (β* sweep + ρ setting) → sampling loop (N chains × T steps) → decoding → phenotype metrics. The PCA step is irreversible and determines the separation index S before generation begins.

**Component map:**
Input alignment → One-hot encoding → PCA projection (95% variance) → Unit-norm columns (X ∈ ℝ^{d×K}) → Multiplicity vector r (r_k=ρ for designated) → Langevin sampler (ξ update with softmax(β Xᵀξ + log r)) → β* selection (entropy inflection sweep) → Decoding (inverse PCA + argmax) → Validation (f_obs, S, etc.)

**Critical path:**
Alignment cleaning → PCA → memory matrix → β* determination (per ρ) → SA sampling → decoding → phenotype metrics. PCA and S are fixed before generation.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Hard curation (X from designated only) vs. multiplicity weighting (full X + log r): Hard curation guarantees phenotype transfer but loses global fold constraints; multiplicity preserves fold but may suffer calibration gap if S is low.
- β tuning: β > β* increases phenotype fidelity at cost of diversity; β < β* yields more diverse but less conditioned sequences.
- PCA dimensionality d: Higher d preserves more variance but increases sampler cost (O(dK)); lower d risks losing functional signal. 95% variance threshold is pragmatic.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Low S (<0.2): f_obs << f_eff even at high ρ; diversity collapses as β is increased.
2. High KL divergence: Generated amino acid frequencies deviate strongly from stored MSA, indicating poor fold preservation.
3. Low TM-score / pLDDT: Generated sequences fail to fold into native-like structures, often due to extreme conditioning that destroys conserved positions.
4. ρ saturation: f_obs plateaus despite further ρ increases, signaling PCA bottleneck.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline reproduction: Run full-family SA (ρ=1) on a known family (e.g., Kunitz). Verify that generated sequences match stored MSA entropy (r > 0.98) and TM-score >0.8. This confirms the sampler works.
2. Calibration gap quantification: For target family, compute S. Run multiplicity sweep (ρ∈[1,500]) with 30 chains. Plot f_obs vs. f_eff; measure Δ at ρ=500. Compare to S–Δ trend from paper; if point lies far above line, investigate whether phenotype marker is multi-position.
3. Hard curation vs. multiplicity comparison: Build memory from designated subset only (K_des sequences). Generate sequences and measure f_obs. Contrast with multiplicity result at same effective diversity (match D by tuning β). This validates whether hard curation is necessary given S.

## Limitations
- PCA Geometry Bottleneck: The method’s effectiveness hinges on the Fisher separation index S; if functional residue variance aligns with low-variance components discarded by PCA (low S), conditioning fails regardless of ρ.
- Single-Marker Phenotype Assumption: The S index and calibration gap analysis are validated for a single phenotype-defining position; for multi-residue or distributed functional determinants, S may not capture the relevant geometric entanglement.
- β* Selection Stability: The paper does not establish that β* is invariant to ρ; if β* shifts with conditioning strength, the reported calibration gaps could be confounded by temperature effects.

## Confidence
- Multiplicity weighting redefines effective fraction precisely: **High** (empirically validated: attention fraction ¯a_des tracks f_eff with <0.3% deviation).
- Calibration gap Δ monotonically decreases with S: **High** (strong empirical correlation: R² = 0.80 across five families).
- Single-marker S predicts conditioning success: **Medium** (validated only for single-position markers; extrapolation to complex phenotypes is uncertain).
- β* is stable across ρ: **Low** (no evidence provided; critical hidden assumption in experimental design).

## Next Checks
1. Validate β* Stability Across Conditioning Strengths: For a family with moderate S (e.g., Kunitz), perform the β* sweep at multiple ρ values (ρ=1, 10, 100). Compute entropy curves and record selected β*. Hypothesis: β* will remain within 10% across ρ. A larger shift would necessitate re-evaluating the calibration gap analysis.
2. Test Multi-Position Phenotype Conditioning: Choose a family where a functional subset is defined by a motif (e.g., two coupled residues). Compute S separately for each marker and a combined metric. Run multiplicity conditioning for the composite phenotype. Hypothesis: The calibration gap will be larger than predicted by any single-position S, indicating the S–Δ model breaks down for distributed traits.
3. Hard Curation vs. Multiplicity Threshold for Low-S Families: For a family with S < 0.2 (e.g., WW), directly compare hard curation (memory from designated subset only) against multiplicity weighting (full memory + log ρ) at matched diversity levels (via β tuning). Hypothesis: Hard curation will show superior f_obs at the cost of global fold preservation (higher KL), establishing a clear operational boundary for when to abandon multiplicity weighting.
