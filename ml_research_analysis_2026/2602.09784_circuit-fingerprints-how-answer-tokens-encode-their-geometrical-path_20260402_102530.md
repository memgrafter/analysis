# Circuit Fingerprints: How Answer Tokens Encode Their Geometrical Path

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.09784
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09784
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** Answer tokens encode geometric directions that trace their computational pathways, unifying circuit discovery (reading) and activation steering (writing) without requiring gradients or interventions.

## Executive Summary
The Circuit Fingerprint hypothesis posits that answer tokens, when processed in isolation, encode a geometric direction in the residual stream that corresponds to the computational pathway responsible for generating that token. This insight unifies two previously distinct goals: circuit discovery (identifying which model components implement a function) and activation steering (controlling model behavior). By leveraging the linear representation hypothesis, the method measures component importance via geometric alignment of their differential outputs with the answer-derived direction, eliminating the need for gradient-based attribution or expensive causal interventions.

The approach is validated on three benchmark tasks (Indirect Object Identification, Subject-Verb Agreement, Multiple-Choice Question Answering) across four model families (GPT-2 Small, Qwen2.5-0.5B, Llama3.2-1B, OPT-1.3B). Circuit quality metrics (Circuit Performance Ratio, Circuit-Model Distance) are comparable to state-of-the-art gradient methods. For steering, the same geometric directions yield 69.8% emotion classification accuracy, significantly outperforming instruction prompting (53.1%) while preserving factual accuracy (89.6% vs. 90.1%). These results demonstrate that interpretability and controllability are dual operations on the same underlying geometric structure.

## Method Summary
The method consists of two complementary phases: circuit discovery (reading) and activation steering (writing). For circuit discovery, a target direction is constructed by contrasting the final-layer residual activations of answer tokens (e.g., "Paris" vs. "Rome") passed through the model in isolation. This direction is projected into each component's native space (e.g., value vectors for attention heads, output space for MLPs) using the component's linear output matrix. Component importance is then the dot product between this projected direction and the component's differential output on a pair of contrastive prompts (clean vs. corrupted). Edge-level contributions are derived via Shapley value decomposition over the Q/K/V channels of attention heads, and total importance is propagated backward across layers to assemble a circuit.

For steering, an intervention subspace is built from representations of answer tokens associated with the desired feature (e.g., emotion words). Source and target directions are obtained by projecting prototypes onto this subspace. At the attention heads identified by the circuit discovery (or from instruction-based directions), the residual stream is shifted along the vector that replaces the source feature with the target. For known target tokens (factual changes), the intervention replaces the source magnitude with the target magnitude; for stylistic changes (unknown tokens), it transfers the source magnitude onto the target direction. This geometric manipulation directly alters the representational subspace corresponding to the feature, enabling controlled generation.

## Key Results
- Circuit discovery on IOI, SVA, and MCQA yields Circuit Performance Ratio and Circuit-Model Distance scores comparable to gradient-based baselines (EAP, EAP-IG), confirming that geometric alignment can reliably identify task-relevant components.
- Steering using the same directions achieves 69.8% emotion classification accuracy, surpassing instruction prompting (53.1%) and maintaining factual accuracy at 89.6% (vs. 90.1% for the full model), demonstrating effective control with minimal disruption.
- The approach generalizes across diverse architectures (GPT-2 Small, Qwen2.5-0.5B, Llama3.2-1B, OPT-1.3B), indicating robustness to model scaling and architectural differences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Answer tokens processed in isolation encode a geometric direction that corresponds to the computational pathway responsible for generating that token.
- **Mechanism:** When an answer token (e.g., “Paris”) is passed through the model as input, its activations traverse the same internal circuits (attention heads, MLPs) that would be activated if the model were generating that token as output. The difference between activations for contrasting answer tokens (e.g., “Paris” minus “Rome”) at the final layer captures a direction in activation space that is aligned with the circuit's output.
- **Core assumption:** The linear representation hypothesis holds—semantic and computational features are linearly encoded in the residual stream and component-native spaces.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "answer tokens, processed in isolation, encode the directions that would produce them."
  - [Section 3] Hypothesis 3.1: "Answer tokens...trace the computational pathways that produce them."
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on circuit discovery and representation geometry, supporting the premise that circuits have a geometric structure. Direct evidence for the *encoding* mechanism is primarily from the paper's own analysis.
- **Break condition:** If answer token representations are not processed through the same circuits as their generation (e.g., due to strong positional or contextual bias overriding token identity), the direction fails to capture the relevant computational pathway.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Circuit component importance (for a specific task) can be recovered by measuring the geometric alignment between a component's differential output (on contrastive prompts) and the answer token direction transformed into that component's native space.
- **Mechanism:** The answer token direction `∆r(L)` is projected into a component's native space (e.g., value space for an attention head) via its output matrix transpose. A component's task contribution is its dot product with this transformed target. This measures how much of the component's activity is aligned with producing the target answer, without requiring causal intervention or gradients.
- **Core assumption:** A component contributes to a circuit if and only if its output has a non-zero projection onto the geometric signature of the target feature in its native computational space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 4.2] "We measure each component’s task-relevant contribution as its alignment with this transformed target: `Sc = ⟨∆oc, ˆtc⟩`"
  - [Section 6.2] Results show this geometric scoring identifies similar critical heads (e.g., in IOI layers 9-11) as gradient-based EAP-IG (Figure 2).
  - [Table 1] Circuit Performance Ratio (CPR) and Circuit-Model Distance (CMD) are comparable to gradient baselines.
- **Break condition:** The projection method assumes linear readouts from component outputs. Non-linear interactions downstream or significant mixing in the residual stream prior to the final layer could cause misattribution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The same geometric directions used for circuit discovery can be used to steer model outputs (activation steering), demonstrating that reading (identification) and writing (control) are dual operations on the same geometric structure.
- **Mechanism:** Construct an intervention subspace from answer token prototypes for source and target features. At identified circuit locations (e.g., specific heads), shift the activation along the direction separating source and target prototypes (Eq. 12/13). This directly manipulates the representational geometry corresponding to the feature.
- **Core assumption:** The geometric subspace spanned by answer token representations for a feature is causally sufficient to modulate the model's output along that feature dimension.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The same directions that identify circuit components also enable controlled steering."
  - [Section 5] Describes steering intervention using `ds` and `dt` derived from answer token representations.
  - [Figure 5 & Section 6.3] Steering achieves behavioral effects (suppression of correct answer) comparable to full activation patching, and emotion classification accuracy (69.8%) exceeds instruction prompting (53.1%).
- **Break condition:** If features are not linearly disentangled in activation space, adding a direction may have off-target effects (e.g., factual degradation observed for negative emotions) or fail to produce the desired output shift.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Linear Representation Hypothesis**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire method assumes features (like "capital city" or "joy") correspond to directions in activation space. This underpins why comparing directions via dot products is meaningful for both discovery and steering.
  - **Quick check question:** If a feature is linearly represented, what would you expect to see in a PCA plot of activations for sentences containing that feature versus those that do not?
- **Concept: Transformer Residual Stream as a Communication Bus**
  - **Why needed here:** Components (heads, MLPs) write to and read from the residual stream. The method transforms the final-layer answer direction back into component-native spaces, relying on the linearity of the write matrices (`WO`, `Wout`).
  - **Quick check question:** How does the residual stream's additive nature enable the decomposition of a downstream component's input into contributions from upstream components?
- **Concept: Contrastive Prompt Design for Feature Isolation**
  - **Why needed here:** To extract a clean "answer token direction," you need a pair of answers (`a+`, `a-`) that differ only in the feature of interest (e.g., "Paris" vs "Rome" for capitals). The prompts must be minimally distinct to isolate the computational difference.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is it important that the contrastive prompts (e.g., "The capital of France is" vs "The capital of Italy is") are matched in structure except for the query entity?
- **Concept: Geometric Alignment vs. Causal Intervention**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's core innovation is replacing gradient-based or patching-based importance scores with a simple geometric score (`⟨∆oc, ˆtc⟩`). Understanding the difference between correlation (alignment) and causation (intervention) is key to evaluating the method's limits.
  - **Quick check question:** What is a potential failure mode where a component has high geometric alignment with the answer direction but is not causally necessary for the task?
- **Concept: Shapley Value Decomposition for Multi-Channel Components**
  - **Why needed here:** Attention heads have Q, K, V channels. The paper uses Shapley values to apportion a head's total importance to its channels fairly, which then weights the edge attribution ratios.
  - **Quick check question:** In the 3-player cooperative game (Q, K, V), what does the coalition value `v(C)` represent in the context of an attention head's computation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Input/Output Processing:** Token embeddings, final layer unembedding matrix.
  - **Core Computation:** Residual stream (vector sum at each layer), attention heads (Q/K/V/O projections, attention pattern), MLPs (up/down projections).
  - **Data Structures:** Component-specific activation differences (`∆oc`), target directions in native space (`ˆtc`), edge ratios (`Ri→j`), Shapley weights (`ϕQ, ϕK, ϕV`).
- **Critical path:**
  1. For a task, define contrastive answers (`a+`, `a-`) and minimal contrastive prompts.
  2. Compute final-layer answer direction `∆r(L) = r_a+(L) - r_a-(L)`.
  3. For each component, transform `∆r(L)` into its native space: `ˆtc = Wc^T ∆r(L) / ||∆r(L)||`.
  4. For each component, compute its differential output `∆oc` on the contrastive prompts (clean vs. corrupted).
  5. Compute direct importance score `Sc = ⟨∆oc, ˆtc⟩`.
  6. (Optional) For edges to heads: compute per-channel ratios `R(Q/K/V)i→j`, compute Shapley weights `ϕ` via 8-channel-coalition runs, then `Ei→j = Sj * (ϕQ*RQ + ϕK*RK + ϕV*RV)`.
  7. Propagate total importance `Tc` backward from final layer using `Ti += Σ(Ei→j * Tj)`.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Native-space alignment** avoids noise from the residual stream's mixed geometry but assumes the component's write matrix (`Wc`) is the primary linear readout. It may miss interactions that happen *within* a component's internal non-linearities.
  - **Shapley decomposition** provides a principled channel weighting but requires 8 forward passes per head for coalition evaluation, increasing compute.
  - **Final-token focus** simplifies analysis but ignores positional effects and earlier-token contributions that may be crucial for some circuits (acknowledged as a limitation).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Low circuit fidelity:** Recovered circuit CPR/CMD is substantially worse than gradient baselines, especially on harder tasks (e.g., MCQA in larger models?).
  - **Steering degradation:** Steering causes large drops in factual accuracy (>10%) or generates incoherent text (high perplexity) despite on-target feature expression.
  - **Alignment inconsistency:** Component scores (`Sc`) do not correlate with known circuit roles from prior work (e.g., "Name Mover" heads in IOI scoring low).
  - **Cross-model breakdown:** Method fails on a model family where answer token directions are not discriminative (e.g., if answer tokens are represented non-linearly or entangled with context).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce IOI circuit recovery:** Use GPT-2 Small on the standard IOI prompts. Extract answer directions for “John” vs “Mary”. Compute head scores `Sc` and compare the top-ranked heads to the known 15-head circuit from Wang et al. (2022). Expect strong overlap in layers 9-11.
  2. **Validate geometric steering on a factual task:** On the IOI task, intervene using the answer token direction at the final token position. Measure how `P(correct)` degrades with intervention strength `α` (Figure 5). Expect a similar suppression curve to activation patching, though perhaps with a slightly higher endpoint `P(correct)`.
  3. **Test persona circuit discovery via instruction:** Use prompts like “Answer with joy: {neutral QA}”. Extract the direction from the “joy” instruction token representation (or its difference from a neutral instruction). Project all head outputs at the final position onto this direction. Identify the top contributing heads. Then, use those heads and the direction to steer a neutral prompt. Measure emotion classification accuracy and perplexity change against a baseline “Answer normally” prompt.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do contributions from non-final token positions affect the accuracy of circuit fingerprint extraction?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Future work should address position-level effects beyond the final token" (Section 8, Limitations and future work).
- **Why unresolved:** The current method analyzes only the final token position, potentially overlooking circuits that depend on earlier positions or positional interactions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend the edge attribution algorithm to aggregate contributions across all token positions; compare circuit discovery performance (e.g., CPR, CMD) against the final-token-only baseline.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent do LayerNorm nonlinearities distort the linear alignment between answer token directions and component contributions, and can this be corrected?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "ignoring ... LayerNorm nonlinearities in edge attribution" (Section 8, Limitations).
- **Why unresolved:** The method assumes linear additivity in the residual stream, but LayerNorm normalizations may break this linearity, affecting the validity of component scores.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare circuit quality metrics using the current method versus a variant that explicitly incorporates LayerNorm effects (e.g., by adjusting the alignment scores with normalization statistics).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What causes the asymmetry in factual accuracy degradation during emotion steering between positive- and negative-valence emotions, and how can steering methods be improved to preserve factual grounding?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "steering can disrupt factual recall, particularly for negative-valence emotions" and "negative emotion representations may be less disentangled from lexical content" (Section 7.1 and Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** The entanglement between emotional style and factual content in activation space is not understood, leading to trade-offs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze the geometric relationship (e.g., cosine similarity, subspace overlap) between emotion steering directions and factual answer directions; develop steering techniques that project onto orthogonal style subspaces or use regularization to minimize factual drift.

## Limitations
- **Prompt specification gap:** Exact prompts and corruption schemes for SVA and MCQA are not provided, hindering replication.
- **Shapley decomposition underspecification:** Details for channel coalition runs are unclear, risking inconsistent edge attribution.
- **Final-token limitation:** The method only analyzes the final token position, ignoring positional effects and multi-token answers, limiting generalizability.
