# EdgeJury: Cross-Reviewed Small-Model Ensembles for Truthful Question Answering on Serverless Edge Inference

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.00850
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00850
- **Reference count:** 33
- **Primary result:** On TruthfulQA (MC1) EdgeJury achieves 76.2% accuracy (+21.4% relative over a single 8B baseline) and ~55% fewer factual hallucinations, with median end‑to‑end latency of 8.4 s on Cloudflare Workers AI.

## Executive Summary
EdgeJury addresses hallucinations in resource‑constrained edge deployments by orchestrating a four‑stage ensemble of small instruction‑tuned models (3B–8B) via serverless inference. The pipeline first generates parallel role‑specialized answers, then anonymized cross‑review with structured critiques, a chairman synthesis merging the strongest content, and finally a claim‑level consistency labeler. Evaluated on TruthfulQA and an adversarial EdgeCases set, it yields large relative gains over a single strong baseline while maintaining practical latency. The work demonstrates that small‑model ensembles, when carefully coordinated, can deliver truthful QA without retrieval or massive models.

## Method Summary
EdgeJury runs inside a Cloudflare Workers AI worker. Stage 1 calls four model endpoints in parallel, each using a distinct role prompt (Direct Answerer, Edge Case Finder, Step‑by‑Step Explainer, Pragmatic Implementer). Stage 2 sequentially dispatches the same or similar models to review the anonymized Stage 1 outputs, forcing a structured JSON response with rankings, issue flags, and best‑bits excerpts. Stage 3’s chairman model aggregates these reviews and synthesizes a final answer constrained to a JSON object containing a `choice` for multiple‑choice tasks. Stage 4 extracts atomic claims from the final answer and the original candidates, labeling any claim that appears in multiple candidates as “consistent.” The orchestrator handles all calls, validates JSON, and applies fallbacks. Temperature is set to 0 for deterministic outputs (except self‑consistency baselines).

## Key Results
- TruthfulQA (MC1) accuracy: **76.2%**, a +21.4% relative improvement over a single 8B baseline (62.8%).
- Adversarial EdgeCases: **+48.2%** relative gain over the single‑model baseline.
- Manual analysis shows **~55% reduction** in factual hallucination errors.
- Median end‑to‑end latency: **8.4 seconds** on Cloudflare Workers AI.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Role‑specialized generation
Distinct role prompts steer each model toward complementary reasoning, increasing diversity beyond random sampling. This reduces correlated errors because different instructions induce qualitatively different failure modes. Evidence: Ablation (Table 4) shows dropping Stage 1 roles reduces accuracy by several points. Break condition: All models share a systematic misconception, leading to agreement on false claims despite different roles.

### Anonymized cross‑review with structured critique
Models review anonymized peer outputs, producing JSON with rankings, issue flags (e.g., `factual_risk`, `missing_edge_case`), and best bits. This forces explicit error detection and creates actionable feedback for the chairman. Assumption: Models are better at spotting others’ errors than their own. Evidence: Removing Stage 2 costs 7.6 absolute points on TruthfulQA (Table 6). Break condition: Reviewers miss critical errors or produce spurious flags, and the chairman misinterprets the aggregated review.

### Chairman synthesis integrating critiques
A chairman model merges candidates and aggregated reviews, selecting strong segments and resolving contradictions using the critiques. This preserves minority‑correct reasoning while correcting flagged issues. Evidence: Removing Stage 3 leads to the largest single drop (8.8 points). Break condition: Chairman disregards critiques, introduces new errors during reconstruction, or fails to synthesize from conflicting inputs.

### Claim‑level consistency labeling
Stage 4 extracts atomic claims and labels them “consistent” if supported by multiple candidates. This provides interpretable uncertainty but does not guarantee truth—agreement can reflect a shared hallucination.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Ensemble Diversity** – *Why needed:* Small models fail in different ways; diversity in reasoning (via role specialization) increases the chance that at least one candidate is correct. *Quick check:* Why might using the same model with different random seeds be less effective than using different models/roles? (Answer: Sampling yields variations of the same error pattern; distinct roles steer toward different strategies.)

**Concept: Structured Prompting & Constrained Decoding** – *Why needed:* The pipeline relies on models adhering strictly to JSON schemas for machine‑readable downstream processing. *Quick check:* What risks arise if Stage 2’s review output does not conform to the expected JSON schema? (Answer: Parsing failures halt the pipeline or produce incorrect aggregation.)

**Concept: Post‑Hoc Uncertainty Calibration** – *Why needed:* Stage 4 provides claim‑level confidence tags based on inter‑model agreement, offering interpretable but not guaranteed truth signals. *Quick check:* Why can’t Stage 4’s “consistent” label guarantee factual truth, and what does it actually indicate? (Answer: It indicates multiple models independently produced the claim; truth requires external verification, and consensus can be wrong.)

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Orchestrator (Worker) → Parallel Stage1 Generators → Sequential Stage2 Reviewers → Stage3 Chairman → Stage4 Verifier

Critical path:
1. Receive query; orchestrator starts.
2. Launch all four Stage1 generation calls in parallel.
3. Wait for all Stage1 outputs to return.
4. Sequentially send anonymized Stage1 outputs to each reviewer (4 calls).
5. Aggregate reviews (Borda count, issue dedup) into summary.
6. Send summary to chairman; parse final `choice`/`final_answer`.
7. Send final answer plus Stage1 candidates to verifier; receive claim labels.
8. Return final answer and labels.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Latency vs. accuracy:** 10 model calls increase latency (~8.4 s median) but improve truthfulness; could prune stages adaptively for low‑stakes queries.
- **No retrieval:** Avoids complexity but cannot correct shared misconceptions; a hybrid with retrieval might help.
- **Fixed roles vs. dynamic:** Static roles are simple; dynamic assignment per query could improve efficiency but adds routing overhead.
- **Single‑round review:** Limits latency vs. multi‑round debates; may miss deeper critique cycles.

Failure signatures:
- **Parsing failures:** Invalid JSON from any stage crashes the pipeline; need strict validation and fallback (e.g., retry or default to best Stage1 candidate).
- **Correlated hallucination:** All Stage1 models agree on a false claim → Stage4 labels it “consistent” (false confidence); chairman likely propagates it.
- **Chairman override:** Chairman ignores strong critique signals and synthesizes a flawed answer.
- **Latency spike:** One model endpoint slows, stalling pipeline; implement per‑call timeout and partial‑failure fallback.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Baseline sanity check:** Run single 8B model on 20 TruthfulQA questions; verify accuracy (~60%) and parsing.
2. **Diversity check:** Run all four Stage1 models on same 20 questions; inspect output differences and error patterns; ensure complementary failures.
3. **Cross‑review effectiveness test:** For 10 questions where one Stage1 model is correct, check if any reviewer flagged incorrect candidates and whether chairman incorporated the correct bits; record direct correction instances.

## Limitations
- **Model version drift:** Cloudflare Workers AI endpoints may change; exact snapshots are unspecified, harming reproducibility.
- **Unreleased evaluation subsets:** Exact question IDs for MMLU/BBH/NQ subsets (only seed 42 mentioned) are not available, blocking full replication.
- **Implementation gaps:** Borda count tie‑breaking rules and complete Stage 3/4 prompt templates are omitted, risking behavioral divergence.
- **Limited external validity:** Evaluation focused on TruthfulQA and custom EdgeCases; performance on other QA benchmarks remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Ensemble accuracy gain (+21.4% rel on TruthfulQA):** Medium (strong internal ablations; no independent external validation).
- **Mechanism validity (role diversity, cross‑review, chairman):** Medium‑Low (evidence mainly from ablations; theoretical “why” not directly probed; break conditions not stress‑tested).
- **Latency‑practicality (8.4 s median, serverless deployment):** High (directly measured on target platform; timing methodology clear, though variance across time/regions unreported).

## Next Checks
1. **Diversity validation:** Compute pairwise output dissimilarity (BLEU, embedding cosine) and error‑type correlation across the four Stage1 models on a fixed 50‑question TruthfulQA subset; expect low correlation (<0.3).
2. **Chairman critique integration audit:** For 20 questions where cross‑review flags a specific issue (e.g., `missing_edge_case`), inspect the chairman’s final answer to confirm explicit resolution; target >80% success rate.
3. **Claim‑label calibration:** Manually verify 100 random claims labeled “consistent” by Stage 4 against gold answers; compute precision/recall; expect precision >75% but recall likely <60% (per Section 6.6).
