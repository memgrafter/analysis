# InverFill: One-Step Inversion for Enhanced Few-Step Diffusion Inpainting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.23463
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23463
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A one-step inversion network replaces random Gaussian noise initialization in few-step diffusion inpainting, consistently improving text-image alignment metrics (e.g., ImageReward +0.6–1.0) with minimal inference overhead (0.04–0.06s).

## Executive Summary
Few-step diffusion inpainting models suffer from poor harmonization because they initialize the masked region with random Gaussian noise, which lacks semantic alignment with the unmasked context. InverFill introduces a lightweight one-step inversion network trained to predict a noise latent from the masked image that is semantically consistent with the visible regions. Key innovations include a Re-Blending operation to prevent information leakage from masked pixels during training and a Gaussian regularization loss to maintain the expected noise distribution. Trained image-free on synthetic data, InverFill plugs into existing few-step inpainting pipelines without altering the generator. Experiments on SANA-Sprint and SDXL-Turbo show significant gains in alignment and aesthetic scores at 2–4 NFEs, matching specialized multi-step inpainting models while adding negligible latency.

## Method Summary
InverFill trains a one-step inversion network \(F_\theta\) (initialized from a pretrained few-step generator \(G\)) to map a masked image latent \(z^m_0\) and text prompt \(c\) to an inverted noise latent \(\hat{z}_T\). During both training and inference, Re-Blending replaces the masked region of \(\hat{z}_T\) with fresh Gaussian noise \(\epsilon'\) to form \(\hat{z}^\text{blend}_T\), ensuring the masked area adheres to the diffusion prior and preventing leakage of meaningless patterns. The training objective combines: (1) reconstruction losses (\(L_\text{noise}\) on unmasked regions of \(\hat{z}_T\), \(L_\text{image}\) on the generated image), (2) a Gaussian regularization loss \(L_\text{reg}\) matching the first two moments of \(\hat{z}^\text{blend}_T\) to \(\mathcal{N}(0,I)\), and (3) an LADD adversarial loss using a frozen multi-step teacher. At inference, \(\hat{z}^\text{blend}_T\) seeds the few-step generator’s blended sampling loop, where known latents are preserved at each step.

## Key Results
- On SANA-Sprint 0.6B at 2 NFEs, ImageReward improves from 11.02 to 11.65.
- On SDXL-Turbo at 4 NFEs, ImageReward improves from 11.42 to 12.38.
- Gains are consistent across Aesthetic Score and HPS v2, and performance matches multi-step inpainting specialists with only 0.04–0.06 seconds added overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: A one-step inversion network predicts a noise latent semantically aligned with the unmasked context, replacing random initialization to improve few-step inpainting.
- Mechanism: The network \(F_\theta\) (sharing architecture with the generator \(G\)) processes the masked latent \(z^m_0\) and prompt \(c\) to produce \(\hat{z}_T\). This is combined with fresh noise in the masked area via Re-Blending to yield \(\hat{z}^\text{blend}_T\), the starting point for the few-step pipeline.
- Core assumption: The visible part of the masked image contains sufficient semantic and structural information to condition \(\hat{z}_T\) so that the few-step model can generate coherent content in a small number of steps.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Abstract: "InverFill proposes a one-step inversion network that maps a masked image to a semantically aligned noise latent."
  - Section 4.2: \(F_\theta\) shares architecture and pretrained weights with \(G\); training uses synthetic masked images.
  - Corpus: Neighboring works on consistency models address speed for text-to-image but not masked inversion, highlighting a gap.
- Break condition: For very large or ambiguous masks, \(\hat{z}_T\) may not provide adequate guidance, leading to inharmonious artifacts.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Re-Blending prevents information leakage from masked regions during training, ensuring the inverted latent remains near-Gaussian while preserving semantics of the unmasked areas.
- Mechanism: Masked portions of \(\hat{z}_T\) are replaced with fresh Gaussian noise \(\epsilon' \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\) to form \(\hat{z}^\text{blend}_T = \hat{z}_T \odot (1-m) + \epsilon' \odot m\). This restores the expected noise prior in the masked area.
- Core assumption: Masked input pixels carry no useful information for inversion and should be represented by standard Gaussian noise to maintain compatibility with the diffusion prior.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Abstract: "Key innovations include a Re-Blending operation to prevent information leakage."
  - Section 4.4: Detailed description and Figure 5 showing qualitative improvement with Re-Blending.
  - Corpus: Blended latent diffusion uses blending at sampling; Re-Blending specifically targets training-time leakage.
- Break condition: With very small masks, Re-Blending’s benefit is marginal; with very large masks, the generator may still fail to produce high-fidelity content.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: A Gaussian regularization loss explicitly matches moments of \(\hat{z}^\text{blend}_T\) to a standard Gaussian, stabilizing training and improving reconstruction.
- Mechanism: Loss \(L_\text{reg} = \sum_{n\in\{1,2\}} \left\| \frac{1}{D}\sum (\hat{z}^\text{blend}_T)^n - \mu_n^{1/n} \right\|\) penalizes deviations in mean and variance from \(\mathcal{N}(0,I)\). Added to reconstruction and adversarial losses.
- Core assumption: The diffusion noise predictor is optimized for Gaussian inputs; distributional shifts in the initial latent degrade few-step sampling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Abstract: "a Gaussian regularization loss to match the expected noise distribution."
  - Section 4.5: Equation 9 and Figure 6 demonstrate \(L_\text{reg}\) preserves background details.
  - Section 7: Ablation in Table 4 shows tuning \(\lambda_\text{reg}\) improves IR/HPS.
  - Corpus: Moment-matching used elsewhere, but application to inversion for inpainting is novel.
- Break condition: Over-weighting \(L_\text{reg}\) over-constrains the latent to be Gaussian at the expense of semantic content.

- **Few-step sampling (distillation/consistency)**: Targets models like SANA-Sprint and SDXL-Turbo that perform large updates per step. *Why needed*: Initial noise is critical in few-step regimes due to aggressive denoising. *Quick check*: How does a few-step model differ from a traditional multi-step diffusion model in its denoising trajectory?
- **Blended latent diffusion for inpainting**: Sampling merges known latents with generated content at each step (Eq. 4). *Why needed*: InverFeed provides the aligned initial latent for this blending process. *Quick check*: In blended sampling, why is the mask applied in latent space instead of pixel space?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Masked Image \(I_m\) + Prompt \(c\) → VAE Encoder \(E\) → Latent \(z^m_0\) → Inversion Network \(F_\theta\) → Predicted \(\hat{z}_T\) → Re-Blending (with fresh \(\epsilon'\)) → \(\hat{z}^\text{blend}_T\) → Few-step Generator (blended sampling) → Final Latent → VAE Decoder \(D\) → Output Image.
- **Critical path (inference)**:
  1. Encode masked image → \(z^m_0\).
  2. \(F_\theta(z^m_0, c) \rightarrow \hat{z}_T\).
  3. Apply Re-Blending → \(\hat{z}^\text{blend}_T\).
  4. Run few-step blended sampling with \(\hat{z}^\text{blend}_T\) to produce final image.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - One-step inversion vs. iterative: Sacrifices some precision for massive speed gains; acceptable because the few-step model corrects minor residuals.
  - Re-Blending: Introduces randomness in masked area, which could reduce semantic alignment but is necessary for Gaussianity.
  - Regularization strength (\(\lambda_\text{reg}\)): Too low → distribution mismatch; too high → loss of semantic content.
  - Adversarial loss: Improves visual quality but adds training complexity.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Color inconsistency between inpainted region and background.
  - Blurred or low-detail content in the masked area.
  - Loss of background details due to over-regularization.
  - Failure to preserve unmasked content (if \(L_\text{noise}\) is mis-weighted).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Main quantitative evaluation: Compare baseline few-step models (SANA-Sprint, SDXL-Turbo) with and without InverFill on BrushBench and MagicBrush using IR, HPS, AS, CLIP. (Tab. 1)
  2. Component ablation: Remove Re-Blending, then \(L_\text{reg}\), then \(L_\text{adv}\) to show incremental improvements. (Tabs. 7, 8)
  3. Comparison with multi-step specialized inpainting models (e.g., SDXL-Inpainting, BrushNet) and with an iterative inversion baseline (50-step DDIM). (Tabs. 1, 6)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Does InverFill's one-step inversion network generalize to diffusion architectures beyond DiT and UNet (e.g., transformer-based, flow-matching) without retraining?
- Basis in paper: Evaluated only on SANA-Sprint (DiT) and SDXL-Turbo (UNet). Related work notes TurboFill's limitation with UNet architectures, implying architecture sensitivity.
- Why unresolved: Architectural differences may violate assumptions in the inversion network's design. No experiments on newer architectures exist.
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply pretrained InverFill to diverse architectures with standardized benchmarks. Measure performance drop versus architecture-specific training. Identify architectural features affecting transferability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is matching only the first two Gaussian moments theoretically sufficient for stable inversion under masking, or would higher-order constraints improve quality?
- Basis in paper: Gaussian regularization (Eq. 10) matches mean and variance "inspired by [15]" without theoretical justification or ablation on moment order.
- Why unresolved: Masked regions likely induce higher-order distributional deviations. The ad hoc choice leaves open whether more comprehensive matching would help.
- What evidence would resolve it: Derive bounds linking moment matching to reconstruction error. Empirically compare moment orders (1–4) and divergences (KL, JS) on LPIPS and CLIP. Perform hyperparameter search for optimal moment set.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Which mask properties (size, shape complexity, boundary sharpness) most degrade InverFill's performance, and can mask-aware training strategies mitigate these effects?
- Basis in paper: Uses "randomly sampled masks of diverse shapes" but provides no analysis per mask attribute. Failure cases (Fig. 12) suggest mask-dependent artifacts.
- Why unresolved: Different masks pose distinct challenges (large masks require hallucination, thin masks need precise edges). Unknown if training mask distribution covers real-world scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Generate masks with controlled variations in area ratio, perimeter-to-area ratio, curvature. Measure inversion quality (LPIPS, FID) as a function of these attributes. Introduce mask conditioning or curriculum learning and assess improvements on challenging masks.

## Limitations
- Exact mask generation procedure during training is unspecified; reproduction may vary in mask diversity.
- Teacher model for LADD adversarial loss (e.g., specific checkpoint, number of steps) is not detailed, affecting training stability.
- Quantitative improvements demonstrated on only two few-step models; generalizability to other architectures is uncertain.
- Gaussian regularization weight \(\lambda_\text{reg}\) is sensitive; improper tuning can over-constrain the latent and harm semantic content.
- Synthetic training data may not fully represent real-world inpainting scenarios (e.g., complex textures, occlusions).

## Confidence
- **High**: One-step inversion network effectiveness – consistent gains across models and thorough ablations.
- **High**: Re-Blending necessity – ablations show clear degradation without it; visual evidence supports its role.
- **Medium**: Gaussian regularization loss – beneficial in experiments but requires careful \(\lambda\) tuning.
- **Medium**: Generalizability to arbitrary few-step models – limited evaluation scope.
- **High**: Image-free training claim – synthetic pipeline is fully described and reproducible in principle.

## Next Checks
