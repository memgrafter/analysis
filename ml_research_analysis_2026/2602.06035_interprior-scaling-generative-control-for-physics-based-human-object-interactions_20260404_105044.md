# InterPrior: Scaling Generative Control for Physics-Based Human-Object Interactions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06035

## Executive Summary
InterPrior tackles the challenge of scaling physics-based human-object interaction (HOI) controllers beyond limited demonstration data. Its core approach distills a full-reference imitation expert into a goal-conditioned variational policy, which is then fine-tuned with reinforcement learning and perturbation-based data augmentation. This combination enables robust generalization to unseen configurations and sparse goal specifications. The method significantly outperforms the prior state-of-the-art (MaskedMimic) on standard benchmarks and demonstrates strong zero-shot adaptation to novel objects, marking a substantial step toward scalable HOI control.

## Method Summary
The method begins by training an imitation expert that mimics expert demonstrations with full state references. This expert is distilled into a goal-conditioned variational policy, enabling the model to generate diverse actions conditioned on target goals. Subsequently, reinforcement learning fine-tuning is applied, using perturbation-based data augmentation to expose the policy to a wide range of initial conditions and perturbations. This process enhances robustness and generalization, allowing the policy to succeed under sparse goals and recover from failures.

## Key Results
- Snapshot goals: 90.0% success (+25.8% over MaskedMimic)
- Random initialization object lifting: 88.6% success (+74.7% over MaskedMimic)
- Zero-shot adaptation on BEHAVE: success improves from 10.7% to 52.0%

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
InterPrior works by combining complementary techniques: distillation transfers the rich expertise from a full-reference model into a flexible, goal-conditioned variational policy that can sample varied actions; RL fine-tuning then optimizes long-horizon performance and robustness, while perturbation-based data augmentation systematically explores the state space, preventing overfitting to the demonstration distribution. This synergy allows the policy to handle unseen configurations and recover from perturbations, leading to superior generalization.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Imitation Expert -> Distilled Variational Policy -> RL Finetuning (with Perturbation Augmentation) -> Deployment
- **Critical path**: 1) Train imitation expert on demonstrations. 2) Distill into goal-conditioned variational policy. 3) Apply RL fine-tuning with perturbation-based data augmentation. 4) Evaluate on test scenarios.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Distillation offers a strong prior but may limit exploration; RL fine-tuning improves performance but increases compute; perturbation augmentation boosts generalization but may slow training and require careful perturbation design.
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on unseen initial conditions, high variance across seeds, inability to recover from large perturbations, mode collapse in action distribution.
- **First experiments**: (1) Snapshot goal test, (2) Random initialization object lifting, (3) Zero-shot adaptation to novel objects (BEHAVE).
