# Revisiting Text Ranking in Deep Research

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21456
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21456
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Passage retrieval and tuned BM25 outperform neural retrievers; re-ranking boosts accuracy; Q2Q reformulation with reasoning trace improves neural retriever accuracy by up to 7.95%.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates information retrieval (IR) ranking methods within deep research agents that use web search APIs for complex queries. By reproducing ranking pipelines on the fixed-corpus BrowseComp‑Plus dataset, the authors evaluate two open‑source agents (gpt‑oss‑20b and GLM‑4.7‑Flash) with various retrievers and re‑rankers. Key findings show that passage‑level retrieval yields higher accuracy than document‑level, BM25 with proper length normalization surpasses neural retrievers, and re‑ranking (e.g., monoT5) substantially improves results. The Query‑to‑Question (Q2Q) module, which reformulates agent search queries into natural language using the agent’s reasoning trace, closes the performance gap for neural retrievers.

## Method Summary
The study uses BrowseComp‑Plus, comprising 100,195 documents and a derived passage corpus of 2,772,255 passages. Two large language model agents with extensive context windows (131K and 200K tokens) follow an iterative loop: reason about the query, generate a search query, retrieve top‑5 items using a chosen retriever, optionally re‑rank at depths 10/20/50, truncate to 512 tokens, and update the context. The full‑document reader tool can bypass truncation. Retrieval units (document vs. passage) and re‑rankers are varied systematically. For neural rankers, an optional Q2Q component rewrites raw search queries into natural‑language questions, optionally including the most recent reasoning trace. Performance is measured by answer accuracy (LLM judge), evidence recall, and efficiency metrics (search calls, completion rate). All code and data are provided for reproducibility.

## Key Results
- Passage retrieval (e.g., gpt‑oss‑20b) achieves accuracy 0.572 vs. 0.476 with BM25 on documents, enabling more iterations under context limits.
- BM25 with document‑oriented hyperparameters (k1=0.9, b=0.4) outperforms neural retrievers; learned sparse (SPLADE‑v3) and multi‑vector (ColBERTv2) generalize better than single‑vector dense retrievers.
- Re‑ranking consistently improves results; BM25+monoT5‑3B at depth 50 attains 0.716 recall and 0.689 accuracy, while Q2Q (with reasoning trace) boosts SPLADE‑v3 accuracy by 7.95%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Passage‑level retrieval provides finer‑grained context, allowing agents to incorporate more relevant information per search iteration and better utilise the fixed context window. BM25’s effectiveness stems from its explicit length normalization; tuning hyperparameters (e.g., higher k1, b=1) aligns scoring with the corpus’s document length distribution. Neural retrievers, though semantically rich, may miss lexical cues that BM25 captures. Re‑rankers, as cross‑encoders, deeply examine a deeper candidate set, compensating for first‑stage retrieval limits. Q2Q transforms terse, keyword‑heavy web‑search queries into natural‑language questions that better match the training distribution of neural rankers; including the reasoning trace further enriches the query with intent, enhancing performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **BM25**: A probabilistic lexical retriever using TF‑IDF and length normalization. *Why needed*: Strong, interpretable baseline; its performance is sensitive to hyperparameters. *Quick check*: Does BM25 score based on term frequency and inverse document frequency? Yes.
- **SPLADE‑v3**: A learned sparse retriever producing high‑dimensional, sparse term‑weight vectors. *Why needed*: Demonstrates that sparse representations can outperform dense ones in generalisation and efficiency. *Quick check*: Is SPLADE’s output sparse? Yes.
- **ColBERTv2**: A multi‑vector dense retriever using contextual token embeddings. *Why needed*: Shows the benefit of multi‑vector interaction over single‑vector models for ranking. *Quick check*: Does ColBERT compute similarity per token? Yes.
- **monoT5**: A cross‑encoder re‑ranker based on T5. *Why needed*: Refines initial retrieval scores by deeply examining query‑document pairs, boosting recall and accuracy. *Quick check*: Does monoT5 concatenate query and document and generate a relevance score? Yes.
- **Query‑to‑Question (Q2Q)**: A module that rewrites agent search queries into natural language. *Why needed*: Bridges the distribution gap between terse web‑search queries and natural‑language training data of neural rankers. *Quick check*: Does Q2Q use the agent’s reasoning trace to inform the rewrite? Yes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Agent (LLM) -> [Reasoning trace] -> Search Query -> Optional Q2Q -> Retriever -> Optional Re‑ranker -> Truncated context -> Agent. Full‑document reader can bypass truncation.

**Critical path**: Iterative cycle: (1) Agent reasons, (2) issues search query, (3) retrieve candidates, (4) optionally re‑rank, (5) update context with top documents, (6) repeat until termination.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Retrieval granularity: Passages allow more items per context but may lose document coherence; documents preserve context but truncation may drop information.
- Re‑ranking depth: Deeper re‑ranking improves accuracy but increases computational cost and token usage.
- Retriever choice: Lexical (BM25) is fast and benefits from tuning; neural retrievers require GPUs but capture semantics; multi‑vector models balance effectiveness with latency.

**Failure signatures**:
- Low completion rate: context overflow, often from using whole truncated documents instead of passages.
- Poor BM25 performance: likely due to inappropriate length normalization hyperparameters.
- Rank1 underperformance: caused by query distribution mismatch.

**First experiments** (minimum viable reproduction):
1. Install dependencies, download BrowseComp‑Plus, and generate the passage corpus using spaCy (250‑word chunks with document titles).
2. Run baseline evaluation with document‑only corpus and default BM25 (k1=0.9, b=0.4), recording accuracy and completion rate.
3. Switch to the passage corpus, keeping all other settings identical, to compare passage vs. document retrieval.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. *How do the paper’s findings about retriever performance generalise to other deep research benchmarks besides BrowseComp‑Plus?*  
   The authors used only one dataset; augmenting other benchmarks with fixed corpora and relevance judgments is needed to test generalisability.
2. *To what extent do the comparative advantages of different retrievers and re‑rankers hold across diverse LLM‑based deep research agents?*  
   Only two agents were evaluated; additional model families and sizes are required to assess generalisability.
3. *What are the optimal configurations (retriever type, re‑ranker size, re‑ranking depth, retrieval unit) for deep research agents under different computational budgets, and what scaling laws govern these components?*  
   Exploring additional rankers and systematic scaling remains an important direction.
4. *Can more robust and agent‑agnostic query reformulation methods be developed to bridge the mismatch between agent‑issued web‑search queries and the natural‑language queries expected by neural rankers, without relying on agent reasoning traces?*  
   Q2Q currently depends on the agent’s reasoning trace; a trace‑free, universal solution is desirable.

## Limitations
- BM25 hyperparameters (k1, b) are ambiguous; the reported document‑oriented sweep may overfit, hindering fair comparison with neural retrievers.
- Q2Q prompt construction and the “low reasoning‑effort mode” settings for gpt‑oss‑20b are unspecified, risking variability in reformulation quality.
- The study uses only two agents and a single dataset, limiting the generalisability of conclusions.

## Confidence
- Passage retrieval > document retrieval: **High**
- Multi‑vector dense (ColBERTv2) > single‑vector dense: **High**
- BM25 with proper length normalization > neural retrievers: **Medium**
- Q2Q reasoning‑trace reformulation improves accuracy: **Medium‑Low**
- Rank1 re‑ranker underperformance due to query mismatch: **Medium‑Low**
