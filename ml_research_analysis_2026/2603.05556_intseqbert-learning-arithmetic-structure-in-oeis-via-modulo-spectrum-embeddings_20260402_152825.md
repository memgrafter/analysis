# IntSeqBERT: Learning Arithmetic Structure in OEIS via Modulo-Spectrum Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.05556  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05556  
- **Reference count**: 21  
- **Primary result**: Large model (91.5M) achieves 95.85% magnitude accuracy, 50.38% Mean Modulo Accuracy, and a CRT Solver that improves next-term Top-1 accuracy by 7.4× (19.09% vs 2.59%) over a tokenized baseline.

## Executive Summary
OEIS integer sequences often span enormous value ranges and exhibit complex periodic arithmetic. Standard tokenized models struggle with such extreme magnitudes and capture limited arithmetic structure. IntSeqBERT introduces a novel dual-stream Transformer that encodes each integer via a continuous log-magnitude embedding and a sin/cos modulo embedding across 100 residues (moduli 2–101). These streams are fused using Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM), enabling the model to learn scale-invariant magnitude and residue-based periodicities. Trained on 274,705 OEIS sequences, the 91.5M-parameter Large model significantly outperforms a tokenized Transformer baseline on magnitude prediction (+8.9 points) and modulo accuracy (+4.5 points). Moreover, a probabilistic Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) Solver reconstructs integers from the model's residue and magnitude predictions, boosting next-term prediction Top-1 accuracy by 7.4×. Analysis shows composite moduli efficiently aggregate arithmetic structure, evidenced by a strong negative correlation (r = -0.851) between information gain and Euler's totient ratio.

## Method Summary
The task is masked integer-sequence modeling: given a context of integers from an OEIS sequence, predict a masked value's magnitude, sign, and residues modulo 2–101. Each integer is encoded via two parallel streams. The magnitude stream uses the base-10 logarithm of absolute value (or digit count for extremely large numbers) and a one-hot sign indicator. The modulo stream computes sin(2πr/m) and cos(2πr/m) for each residue r = x mod m, across all 100 moduli, yielding a 200-dimensional periodic embedding. These two embeddings are fed into a FiLM layer, where the modulo stream produces scaling (γ) and shifting (β) parameters that modulate the magnitude embedding. The fused representation is processed by a standard Transformer encoder. Three output heads regress the log-magnitude, classify the sign, and predict each residue (binary classification per modulus). During inference, a CRT-based Solver takes the predicted residues (converted to integers) and the magnitude to reconstruct the most probable integer, which is used for next-term prediction. The model is trained on 274,705 filtered OEIS sequences with a combined loss over the three heads.

## Key Results
- Dual-stream architecture yields 95.85% magnitude accuracy and 50.38% Mean Modulo Accuracy, surpassing the tokenized Transformer baseline by +8.9 and +4.5 points respectively.
- Ablation study shows the modulo stream contributes +15.2 points to modulo accuracy and +6.2 points to magnitude accuracy, confirming its additive value.
- The CRT-based Solver achieves a 7.4× improvement in next-term Top-1 accuracy (19.09% vs. 2.59%) over the baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Encoding integers via dual-stream (log-magnitude + modulo residues) allows handling extreme value ranges and capturing periodic arithmetic structure.
- **Mechanism**: The magnitude stream provides scale-invariant representation (log10(|x|) for non-zero), while the modulo stream (sin/cos embeddings for residues mod 2–101) preserves additive/multiplicative congruences independent of magnitude. FiLM fusion lets the modulo stream modulate the magnitude embedding, imparting arithmetic constraints.
- **Core assumption**: Modular residues aggregate structure via Chinese Remainder Theorem—composite moduli encode information from multiple smaller moduli simultaneously.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "modulo embeddings for 100 residues... fused via FiLM... outperforms tokenised Transformer by +8.9pt magnitude accuracy"
  - Section 3.2: Details of fmag, fmod, and FiLM fusion equation.
  - Section 5.1, Table 5: Ablation (no modulo stream) drops MMA by -15.2pt and Mag Acc by -6.2pt (Large).
- **Break condition**: For integers |x| ≥ 10^20 (Huge/Astronomical buckets), Solver accuracy collapses (Table 9) because modulo-prediction errors (MMA ~50%) make CRT reconstruction unreliable.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The sin/cos modulo embedding preserves ring structure of ℤ/mℤ and avoids discontinuities, enabling smoother learning of periodic patterns.
- **Mechanism**: Unlike one-hot residue encoding, sinusoidal embedding respects the cyclic group operation—rotating residues corresponds to adding offsets—reducing spectral complexity for the Transformer to learn modular arithmetic.
- **Core assumption**: The Transformer's spectral bias [12] favors low frequencies; sinusoidal embeddings directly encode periodic frequencies, reducing required depth to learn multiplicative structure.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Section 2.3: "sin/cos embedding is equivariant to the group structure of ℤ/mℤ and avoids discontinuities."
  - Section 5.3, Fig.4: Analysis shows improved learning of modular patterns.
- **Break condition**: Not explicitly stated; fixed set of moduli limits representable frequencies.

## Foundational Learning

#### Logarithmic Magnitude Encoding
**Why needed**: Compresses the vast dynamic range of integer values (from 1 to 10^100) into a manageable continuous scale, facilitating regression.  
**Quick check**: Verify that `log10(1000) = 3` and that sign is handled separately; check that digit count is used for huge numbers.

#### Sinusoidal Modulo Embedding
**Why needed**: Provides a continuous, cyclic representation of residues that respects the group operation of ℤ/mℤ, avoiding boundaries of one-hot encoding.  
**Quick check**: For a given modulus m, compute embeddings for residues r and (r+1) mod m; confirm they are rotated by 2π/m in the 2D plane.

#### Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT)
**Why needed**: Allows reconstruction of an integer from its residues modulo pairwise coprime moduli; the model's residue predictions can be combined to recover the original integer.  
**Quick check**: For residues r_i modulo m_i (coprime), compute x = Σ r_i * M_i * inv(M_i mod m_i) mod M; verify for a small example (e.g., x=23, m={3,5}).

#### FiLM Fusion
**Why needed**: Merges the magnitude and modulo streams by letting the modulo stream produce affine transformation parameters (γ, β) that condition the magnitude embedding, enabling incorporation of arithmetic constraints without heavy cross-attention.  
**Quick check**: Ensure fused = γ(f_mod) * f_mag + β(f_mod); verify shapes match and gradients flow to both streams.

#### Dual-Stream Transformer Architecture
**Why needed**: Separates representation of magnitude (scale) and periodicity (residues) to handle disparate structures in integer sequences; each stream can specialize, and fusion integrates them.  
**Quick check**: Confirm separate embedding layers produce fmag and fmod; ensure they are fused before Transformer encoder.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Integer x → Magnitude Embedding → fmag  
Integer x → Modulo Embedding (sin/cos for m=2..101) → fmod  
fmag, fmod → FiLM (γ, β from fmod) → fused = γ·fmag + β  
fused → Transformer Encoder (stack of self-attention + FFN)  
Transformer output → Prediction heads:  
- Magnitude head (dense → scalar log10)  
- Sign head (dense → 3-way softmax)  
- Modulo head (per-modulus binary classifiers for residues)  
During next-term prediction: predicted residues + magnitude → CRT Solver → reconstructed integer.

**Critical path**  
*Training*: Sequence → dual embeddings → FiLM fusion → Transformer → three heads; combined loss (MSE + cross-entropy + binary cross-entropy) backpropagated.  
*Inference*: Context sequence → model → predictions for masked position → CRT Solver aggregates residue predictions and magnitude → final integer.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Fixed modulo set (2–101): balances coverage vs. embedding dimension (200-d/token). Larger moduli increase dimension but may capture longer periods.  
- Sinusoidal vs. learned embeddings: Sinusoidal is parameter‑free, smooth, and equivariant; learned could adapt but risk discontinuities.  
- Log-magnitude vs. direct integer: log compresses range but loses precision for huge numbers; digit count fallback is heuristic.  
- FiLM fusion: lightweight affine modulation vs. cross‑attention; may limit complex interactions.  
- Exact CRT: deterministic but sensitive to residue errors; approximate CRT could improve robustness.

**Failure signatures**  
- Solver accuracy drops sharply for |x| ≥ 10^20 because residue predictions (~50% correct) cannot uniquely determine the integer.  
- Magnitude prediction errors (>0.5 log scale) enlarge the feasible integer interval, reducing Solver precision.  
- Sequences with periods not dividing any chosen moduli exhibit low modulo accuracy.  
- FiLM may fail to learn meaningful γ, β, leading to no improvement over independent streams.

**First experiments**  
1. Verify sinusoidal cyclic properties: For a fixed m, plot 2D embeddings for residues 0..m‑1; ensure they lie on a circle and adjacent residues are rotated by ~2π/m.  
2. Ablate modulo stream: set fmod = zero (or learnable bias) and compare magnitude/modulo accuracy; expect drops as reported (+15.2 MMA, +6.2 Mag).  
3. Evaluate CRT Solver success vs. mean residue accuracy: sort validation samples by MMA and plot Solver Top‑1 accuracy; expect monotonic decline below threshold ~50% for large magnitudes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can magnitude uncertainty estimation be effectively combined with approximate Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) methods to improve integer reconstruction for large values? (Stated in Future work; current exact CRT fails for huge integers.)  
- **Open Question 2**: Does extending the modulo spectrum beyond moduli 2–101 significantly enhance the model's capture of arithmetic structure and prediction accuracy? (Heuristic choice; higher moduli might capture additional periodicities.)

## Limitations
- Solver accuracy collapses for |x| ≥ 10^20 due to high residue prediction noise.  
- Choice of moduli {2,…,101} is heuristic; optimal CRT‑based coverage is inferred from correlation but not causally validated.  
- Model trained exclusively on OEIS; unknown transferability to other integer sequence domains (e.g., scientific or financial time series).  
- FiLM fusion’s specific contribution vs. alternative fusion mechanisms (e.g., attention, concatenation) is not isolated.

## Confidence
- **Dual-stream architecture efficacy**: High  
- **Sinusoidal modulo embedding ring-structure preservation**: Medium  
- **CRT Solver’s next-term improvement on OEIS**: High  
- **CRT Solver’s next-term improvement external**: Low  

## Next Checks
1. Develop and integrate an approximate CRT algorithm (e.g., allowing a small number of residue mismatches or using Bayesian inference) and evaluate its impact on next‑term Top‑1 accuracy for large integers (|x| ≥ 10^20).  
2. Test generalization by fine‑tuning or zero‑shot evaluating IntSeqBERT on non‑OEIS integer sequence datasets (e.g., scientific time series, financial tick data) and compare to tokenized baselines.  
3. Conduct a comparative ablation of fusion mechanisms: replace FiLM with concatenation, additive fusion, or cross‑attention, and measure changes in magnitude and modulo accuracy to isolate FiLM’s contribution.
