# Cut Less, Fold More: Model Compression through the Lens of Projection Geometry
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.18116
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18116
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Model folding, formalized as low-rank clustering projection, consistently achieves higher post-compression accuracy than structured (magnitude) pruning across diverse architectures and datasets while preserving inference speed and memory.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a geometric perspective on calibration-free neural network compression, framing both structured pruning and model folding as orthogonal projections of weight matrices. While pruning corresponds to axis-aligned projections (e.g., removing entire filters), folding employs low-rank clustering to approximate the weights with a slightly higher rank. Theoretically, folding yields smaller parameter reconstruction error and tighter function approximation bounds under mild smoothness assumptions. Empirical evaluation across over 1000 checkpoints of ResNet, Vision Transformer, and LLaMA models on multiple datasets shows that folding consistently outperforms magnitude pruning, especially at moderate-to-high compression ratios. The advantage persists after lightweight LayerNorm resets and brief fine-tuning, with identical inference characteristics to pruning due to matched architecture.

## Method Summary
The study compares two calibration-free compression techniques: structured pruning and model folding. Structured pruning removes weights according to a magnitude-based criterion, which can be interpreted as an axis-aligned orthogonal projection that zeros out selected rows or columns. Model folding, in contrast, uses low-rank clustering to merge similar neurons or channels, effectively reducing the rank of the weight matrix by one (a negligible increase). Both methods are analyzed within a unified geometric framework where the compression process is an orthogonal projection onto a subspace defined by the chosen structure. The theoretical analysis provides bounds on reconstruction error and approximation error, showing that folding’s low-rank projection better preserves the original function. Empirically, the authors compress various architectures (ResNet, ViT, CLIP, LLaMA) and evaluate accuracy across many training checkpoints, with folding showing consistent gains over pruning.

## Key Results
- Folding achieves higher post-compression accuracy than magnitude pruning across ResNet18, PreActResNet18, ViT-B/32, CLIP ViT-B/32, and LLaMA-60M/130M on CIFAR-10/ImageNet-1K/C4.
- The accuracy gap is largest at moderate-to-high compression ratios (e.g., 50–80% parameter reduction).
- Inference speed and memory usage of folded models match those of pruned models because the resulting architecture is identical; only the weight values differ.
- Folding’s advantage persists after a lightweight LayerNorm reset and brief fine-tuning, indicating robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that folding’s use of low-rank clustering yields a more accurate orthogonal projection than axis-aligned pruning. By clustering weight vectors, folding captures correlations across dimensions, leading to a lower reconstruction error in the parameter space. The theoretical analysis shows that, under smoothness assumptions on the network function, the folded model approximates the original function more tightly than the pruned model, despite a negligible rank increase. This geometric advantage translates into empirical accuracy gains because the projection better preserves the essential features learned by the network.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map** (plain text):
Input Model -> Extract Layer Weights -> Choose Projection Type (Pruning: axis-aligned mask; Folding: low-rank clustering) -> Compute Projection -> Reconstruct Weights -> (Optional) Fine-tune LayerNorm -> Output Compressed Model

**Critical Path**:
1. For folding: (a) flatten each weight matrix into rows (or columns), (b) cluster the rows into a smaller set of cluster centroids, (c) construct a low-rank projection matrix from the centroids, (d) approximate the original weight matrix as the product of the projection and its pseudoinverse, (e) adjust biases accordingly.
2. For pruning: (a) compute magnitude scores (e.g., L1-norm) for structural units, (b) select top-k units to retain, (c) zero out the rest (axis-aligned projection), (d) keep the architecture unchanged aside from reduced width/depth.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Compression ratio vs. accuracy: Both methods trade off between parameter reduction and preservation of performance; folding typically offers better accuracy at the same compression level.
- Computational overhead: Folding involves clustering (e.g., k-means) which adds moderate runtime during compression, whereas pruning is faster (simple sorting).
- Rank increment: Folding may increase the effective rank by one (negligible), while pruning strictly reduces rank.

**Failure Signatures**:
- drastic accuracy drop (>10%) after compression that does not recover with brief fine-tuning, suggesting the projection did not capture important weight correlations.
- extremely high reconstruction error (Frobenius norm) relative to the original weight matrix magnitude.
- clustering fails to converge or yields poorly separated centroids, indicating weight matrix may not have low-rank structure.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is restricted to ResNet, ViT, and LLaMA families; other architectures (e.g., ConvNeXt, large language models) remain untested.
- Theoretical bounds rely on smoothness and orthogonal projection assumptions that may not hold for highly non-linear networks in practice.
- The empirical comparison uses only magnitude pruning as baseline; newer structured pruning methods are not considered.
- Runtime overhead during compression is not quantified.
- Fine-tuning protocol is brief; long-term stability after extensive training is unexplored.
