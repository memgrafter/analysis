# Multi-Agent Comedy Club: Investigating Community Discussion Effects on LLM Humor Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14770
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14770
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** Broadcast community discussion improves LLM-generated stand-up comedy, with 75.6% A/B preference and significant gains in Craft/Clarity (Δ=0.440) and Social Response (Δ=0.422).

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether broadcast community discussion—simulated through a multi-agent sandbox of critics and audience—can enhance the quality of LLM-generated stand-up comedy. Performers generate monologues conditioned on social memory curated from past reception events, while a baseline condition omits discussion. Human evaluation by five experts across 250 paired monologues showed a 75.6% preference for discussion-conditioned outputs, with statistically significant improvements in Craft/Clarity (Δ=0.440) and Social Response (Δ=0.422). The study also notes an increase in aggressive humor, suggesting a trade-off between comedic impact and safety.

## Method Summary
The experiment uses a multi-agent simulation with 35 agents (5 performers, 3 critics, 26 audience members, and 1 host) over 50 fixed comedy topics. In each round, the host assigns a topic and performers generate monologues. The discussion condition adds a threaded interaction phase: critics post structured reviews, audience members reply and engage in free dialogue under adaptive throttling until a stopping rule (150 events or 15 silent steps). From this discussion, a curation filter selects high-signal items (explicit critiques, recurring themes, thread summaries) and stores them in a vector database (social memory). Before the next round, performers retrieve the top-k=30 relevant items (scoring combines embedding similarity, importance, and recency) within a token budget (Bmem=1600) and incorporate them into their context. Performers generate exactly once per round with no within-round revision. Baseline rounds skip the discussion and memory injection. Paired monologues from both conditions (same topics, performers, rounds) are evaluated by five human experts via forced A/B preference and a 15-item rubric covering Craft/Clarity, Social Response, Humor Style, and Moral Pressure.

## Key Results
- Discussion-conditioned monologues won 75.6% of A/B comparisons (189/250, Wilson CI [69.4%, 81.0%]).
- Significant Delta improvements: Craft/Clarity Δ=0.440 (95% CI [0.274, 0.605]), Social Response Δ=0.422 (95% CI [0.261, 0.579]).
- Aggressive humor (Q9/Q10) increased notably; HarmShift index suggests a mild safety trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study proposes three mechanisms by which community discussion enhances humor generation:

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Broadcast community discussion improves LLM-generated humor by providing an aggregated reception signal that performers retrieve and incorporate across rounds.
- **Mechanism:** Reception events (critic reviews, audience posts, dialogue) are curated into high-signal items and stored in a vector database. At the start of each new round, performers retrieve the top-k most relevant past items via embedding similarity, importance, and recency. This retrieved context conditions the generation, biasing material toward patterns that were well-received previously.
- **Core assumption:** The curated reception contains actionable information about comedic craft and social impact, and the performer LLM can effectively integrate this context without within-round revision.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 3.4 (vector database, curation, retrieval), and qualitative similarity to multi-agent feedback systems in other domains.
- **Break condition:** If retrieval fails to fetch relevant feedback (poor matching or miscuration) or the performer misinterprets the context, improvements may not materialize.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The diversity of critic and audience personas in the simulated community generates a richer set of perspectives, leading to more socially aware and craft-improved humor.
- **Mechanism:** The community includes agents with distinct roles and backgrounds (e.g., sharp critic Luna, Lacanian Ethan, theater-oriented Clara, and 26 audience members with varied tastes). Their threaded discussions produce a multiplex of reactions. When curated and retrieved, this diversity exposes performers to multiple viewpoints, helping them calibrate material for broader audiences and refine timing/framing.
- **Core assumption:** Perspective diversity is preserved through curation/retrieval and the performer LLM can synthesize these signals effectively.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 3.2 (agent roster and personas), Appendix C (detailed personas), and analogies to multi-agent discussion improving reasoning.
- **Break condition:** If retrieval favors a narrow subset (e.g., critics over audience) or the performer overfits to a dominant voice, the diversity advantage diminishes.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Community discussion conditions performers toward tighter comedic structures (early premise commitment, single-axis escalation, decisive endings), which raters score higher on craft and clarity.
- **Mechanism:** Social memory retrieves examples and critiques that implicitly emphasize structural patterns. Performers, conditioned on these, tend to commit to a premise early, sustain one personified frame, and escalate cleanly to a punchline. This contrasts with baseline outputs that sometimes contain fragmented frames, weakening landing and language artistry.
- **Core assumption:** The retrieved memory contains structural cues (explicit in critiques or implicit in successful examples) that the performer LLM can abstract and apply.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 5.4 (observation of tightly bundled rhetorical moves), Appendix F (qualitative excerpts showing structural differences).
- **Break condition:** If retrieval emphasizes content over structure or the performer fails to internalize structural patterns, this mechanism may not manifest.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for cross-round conditioning.
  - **Why needed here:** The core architecture relies on storing past reception in a vector store and retrieving relevant snippets to condition future generations without within-round revision.
  - **Quick check:** How does the system’s query for memory retrieval combine topic, persona, and role anchors? What ranking function is used?
- **Concept:** Multi-agent role specialization.
  - **Why needed here:** The sandbox uses distinct agent types (performer, critic, audience) with fixed personas to generate varied signals; understanding this explains why the community produces diverse feedback.
  - **Quick check:** What are the primary differences between critic and audience agents in terms of output format and influence on memory?
- **Concept:** Bounded context construction.
  - **Why needed here:** LLMs have finite context windows; the system builds a context with a token budget, mixing recent dialogue and retrieved memory. This design directly impacts what information performers see.
  - **Quick check:** What are the three components of the bounded context C(a,t,s) and how is the memory block sized?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Host agent (assigns topics) → Performers (generate monologues) → Discussion phase: Critics post reviews → Audience dialogue (threaded, with Adaptive throttling) → Thread manager organizes messages → Curation filter selects items → Social memory (vector DB) stores curated reception → (next round) Retrieval module (queries memory by similarity/importance/recency) → Bounded context builder (assembles context within Bmem token budget) → Performers (conditioned generation).

**Critical path (per round t):**  
1. Host releases topic x_t.  
2. Performers generate monologues in fixed order.  
3. If discussion enabled: critics post reviews; audience posts/dialogue occur (threaded, throttled) until stopping rule (150 events or 15 silent steps).  
4. Curation filter selects high-signal items from discussion; these are written to social memory.  
5. Next round: before performers generate, their context is built with retrieved memory items (top-k=30, scored by λ·cos+ (1-λ)·π + γ·recency) within Bmem=1600 tokens.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Memory budget Bmem: Larger memory gives more context but risks dilution and token overflow; smaller focuses on highest-signal but may miss nuance.  
- Retrieval parameters (k, λ, γ): trade off between semantic relevance, importance, and recency. Tuning affects how strongly past feedback shapes new generations.  
- Curation heuristics: aggressive filtering reduces noise but may discard subtle valuable reactions; lenient filtering may include low-signal chatter.  
- Discussion length: stopping rule (max events or consecutive silences) balances depth of reception against computational cost and redundancy.

**Failure signatures:**  
- No Craft/Clarity gain: retrieval context irrelevant or misleading; performers ignore/misinterpret memory.  
- Over-emphasis on aggressive humor: aggressive content generates high-importance signals, biasing retrieval.  
- Output homogenization: retrieval converges to narrow patterns, reducing performer diversity.  
- Recency bias: newer items dominate due to γ weighting, causing performers to forget earlier lessons.  
- Unbounded discussion: throttling/stopping parameters mis-set lead to runaway dialogue or premature termination.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline replication: Run sandbox with g=0 on 10 topics; measure Craft/Clarity distribution to establish reference.  
2. Memory ablation: Enable discussion but clear retrieved memory (or use random items) to verify necessity of retrieval.  
3. Parameter sweep: Vary retrieval k (e.g., 10, 30, 50) and measure effect on Δ Craft/Clarity to identify optimal context size.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization across LLMs:** Do the observed benefits of broadcast community discussion generalize across different LLM architectures and scales? The study only used GPT-4o-mini; testing other model families (Claude, Llama, Gemini) and scales is needed.
2. **Long-term dynamics:** How do longer simulation horizons (>50 rounds) and more culturally/prompt-diverse topic distributions affect stability and magnitude of the discussion benefit? The study’s snapshot cannot address whether benefits plateau, accumulate, or lead to degeneracy.
3. **Signal decomposition:** What specific aspects of community discussion traces (critique aggregation, audience reaction modeling, shared jargon) drive improvements in Craft/Clarity? The memory filter treats discussion as monolithic; isolating contributions of explicit critique, recurring themes, or thread summaries is necessary.
4. **Style homogenization:** Does persistent feedback exposure lead to homogenization of comedic styles? The ethical note warns of amplification of certain styles; but the study didn’t analyze intra-performer evolution. Tracking stylistic diversity over time would reveal convergence effects.
5. **Community composition:** To what extent are gains dependent on the specific 35-agent roster (counts, roles, personas)? The fixed roster may confound the effect. Variation in community size, critic-to-audience ratio, and persona diversity could show which configuration elements are essential.

## Limitations
- Exact 50-topic list undisclosed; prevents identical condition matching in reproduction.
- Memory curation algorithm (thresholds, importance scoring) underspecified; no quantitative rule provided.
- Decoding configuration (temperature, top_p, max tokens) not reported; output stochasticity unknown.
- Rater qualification details vague; potential evaluation bias.
- Limited prior work on multi-agent comedy; generalizability uncertain.
- Increase in aggressive humor raises safety concerns; HarmShift correlation weak.

## Confidence
- Discussion improves humor (75.6% A/B win): High
- Craft/Clarity and Social Response gains (Δ>0.4): High
- Increase in aggressive humor: Medium
- Aggregated reception signal mechanism: Medium
- Persona diversity mechanism: Medium
- Tighter comedic structure mechanism: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Ablate retrieval: Run discussion condition with random memory retrieval vs curated to test necessity of the curated signal.
2. Vary curation thresholds: Systematically adjust importance π and similarity cutoffs; measure impact on Δ Craft/Clarity.
3. Swap personas: Replace diverse critic/audience agents with homogeneous ones to isolate the contribution of perspective diversity.
