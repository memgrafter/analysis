# Patches of Nonlinearity: Instruction Vectors in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07930
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07930
- **Reference count:** 33
- **Primary result:** Instruction Vectors (IVs) are localized at the final instructional token and exhibit superadditive causal interactions, revealing a circuit-selection mechanism enabled by instruction tuning.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how instruction-tuned language models internally represent and process instructions, addressing a gap in mechanistic understanding. Using causal mediation analysis, the authors localize "Instruction Vectors" (IVs)—representations formed eagerly after processing the instruction at the final instructional token. IVs are linearly separable by task but exhibit superadditive causal interactions, meaning their combined effect exceeds the sum of individual layers. This non-linearity invalidates standard component-based circuit discovery. To overcome this, they propose a novel locally-linear path-tracing method, free from additive assumptions. This reveals that IVs act as circuit selectors: early layers construct task-agnostic representations, while later layers, conditioned on IVs, select task-specific information pathways. Instruction tuning (SFT/DPO) is shown to be necessary for enabling this circuit-selection mechanism.

## Method Summary
The study employs two complementary techniques. First, activation patching (causal mediation) swaps activations between full-instruction and query-only runs to localize IVs and measure causal effects. This reveals superadditivity—where pairs of layers interact synergistically beyond additive sums—which breaks standard patching assumptions. Second, a novel locally-linear path-tracing method approximates non-linearities (e.g., LayerNorm, ReLU) via surrogates within a neighborhood, tracing high-ranking token-to-token paths without interventions. This identifies how IVs condition later layers to activate task-specific subcircuits (attention heads and MLPs). Comparisons between base, SFT, and DPO variants of OLMo-2 isolate the role of instruction tuning.

## Key Results
- IVs are localized at the final instructional token (`T_inst`) and linearly separable by task (>98% probe accuracy).
- IVs exhibit superadditive causal effects: specific layer pairs show improvements exceeding the sum of their individual effects (T-test p < 0.001).
- IVs act as circuit selectors: early layers build task-agnostic representations, while later layers, conditioned on the IV, diverge in head activity to select task-specific pathways; this behavior is absent in base models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Localization and Superadditivity
- **Claim:** Instruction Vectors (IVs) are localized at `T_inst` and exhibit superadditivity—the combined causal effect of two layers exceeds the sum of their individual effects.
- **Mechanism:** Activation patching measures output improvements (rank/logit) when patching source (full instruction) activations into target (query-only) runs. Significant improvements appear in specific *pairs* of layers, not in single layers alone, demonstrating non-additive synergy.
- **Core assumption:** Causal effects can be isolated via patching and quantified by output metrics.
- **Evidence anchors:** Figures 2, 6–11 show localized 2-layer improvements; T-tests confirm superadditivity (p < 0.001).

### Mechanism 2: Circuit Selection
- **Claim:** IVs function as *circuit selectors*: early layers build task-agnostic representations, while later layers, conditioned on the IV, activate task-specific information pathways.
- **Mechanism:** The locally-linear path-tracing method tracks high-ranking paths (contributing to correct answers) from input tokens to output. Head activity patterns diverge in later layers between tasks despite similarity in early layers.
- **Core assumption:** Information flow can be attributed via locally-linear approximations without interventions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Figure 5 shows head activity divergence in later layers; section 6.1 describes conditioned pathway selection.

### Mechanism 3: Necessity of Instruction Tuning
- **Claim:** Instruction tuning (SFT/DPO) is necessary to enable the circuit-selection mechanism; base pretrained models do not exhibit the same structured IV utilization.
- **Mechanism:** Compare base, SFT, and DPO variants of OLMo-2. Only post-trained models show superadditive IV effects and task-specific pathway selection in path-tracing.
- **Core assumption:** Differences between model variants are attributable to post-training methods.
- **Evidence anchors:** Base model patching patterns differ from tuned (e.g., Figure 2); path analysis in section 6.1.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Causal Mediation / Activation Patching**  
  *Why needed:* Primary tool to localize IVs and test superadditivity by swapping activations between runs.  
  *Quick check:* If patching layer L alone gives +0.05 rank improvement and patching layers L and M together gives +0.12, what does the 0.02 difference imply about their interaction?  
  → It implies superadditivity (non-additive synergy).
- **Concept: Linear Representation Hypothesis**  
  *Why needed:* Paper contrasts IVs' linear separability (supporting LRH) with their non-linear causal effects (challenging simplistic LRH-driven circuit discovery).  
  *Quick check:* Can a representation be linearly separable yet exhibit superadditive causal effects? What does this say about "one component, one role" assumptions?  
  → Yes; this shows linear separability of a representation does not imply additive causal effects on outputs, undermining strong LRH interpretations for circuit decomposition.
- **Concept: Circuit Selection**  
  *Why needed:* Core proposed mechanism—IVs don't contain task logic but condition which subcircuits process the query.  
  *Quick check:* How would you test if a representation is a "circuit selector" vs. a "function vector" that directly encodes output?  
  → A selector's effect is contingent on downstream pathways; a function vector's effect is direct. Test by patching the representation into different task contexts: a selector changes pathway usage, a function vector directly biases output regardless of context.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Residual stream at `T_inst` -> IV storage location; All layers (0 to L) -> potential patching targets; Attention heads & MLP blocks -> granular components examined in path-tracing.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Source run: forward on full prompt `[T_inst T_q]` → cache residual at `T_inst` per layer.  
  2. Target run: forward on query-only (with `<s>` filler) → baseline logits.  
  3. Patch: replace `T_inst` activations at layer(s) i (or i+j) with source values; compute Δ reciprocal rank and Δ logit of answer token.  
  4. Build patching heatmap for 1- and 2-layer combos; locate superadditive hotspots.  
  5. For locally-linear path-tracing (1B only): compute local surrogates for non-linearities; build graph with max-attention edges; trace high-ranking paths (answer token rank<100) from input tokens to output.  
  6. Analyze: (i) Paths emitted from `T_inst` vs. other tokens; (ii) Head activity in paths from `T_inst` across tasks.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Patching:* Simple, but assumes additivity; superadditivity breaks it.  
  - *Path-tracing:* No interventions, handles non-linearity via local linearization, but computationally heavier and approximate (depends on "high-ranking" path threshold).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - No localized IV: Patching effects diffuse across layers/tokens.  
  - No superadditivity: 2-layer patching ≈ sum of 1-layer effects.  
  - No circuit selection: Head activity patterns identical across tasks in later layers.  
  - Base model shows same patterns as tuned: Challenges necessity of instruction tuning.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate localization: Run activation patching for a contrastive task pair; verify `T_inst`-centric hot spots in 2-layer patches.  
  2. Verify superadditivity: For top 10 2-layer combos, compute `(effect(L_i ∪ L_j)) - (effect(L_i) + effect(L_j))`; run one-tailed T-test (expect p < 0.001).  
  3. Test circuit selection: For a task pair, run path-tracing; plot head activity heatmaps for `T_inst`-emitted paths; inspect divergence in layers >3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do IV properties (localization, superadditivity) scale with model size beyond 7B?  
  *Basis in paper:* Due to memory limitations, more elaborate patterns may emerge at larger model scales.  
  *Why unresolved:* Patching and path tracing compute-intensive; larger models not tested.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Run experiments on larger OLMo models (13B, 34B); compare heatmaps, superadditivity stats, path sparsity.
- **Open Question 2:** Are IV phenomena general across task domains, or limited to studied tasks?  
  *Basis in paper:* They do not analyze task domain due to few tasks.  
  *Why unresolved:* Only 8 tasks examined; cannot claim generality.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Test on diverse domains (math, code, QA); check consistency of eager IV formation and circuit selection.
- **Open Question 3:** How can linearly separable IVs show superadditive causal effects? Does this challenge the linear representation hypothesis?  
  *Basis in paper:* IVs show "linear separability along with non-linear causal interaction, broadly questioning the scope of the linear representation hypothesis."  
  *Why unresolved:* No theory explains coexistence; hypothesis scope unclear.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Develop formal framework separating representation from computation; test on synthetic tasks with known ground truth.
- **Open Question 4:** What is the mechanistic detail of IVs as circuit selectors? What do early layers encode, and how do later layers use the IV?  
  *Basis in paper:* IVs act as circuit selectors... early layers construct task-agnostic representations, while later layers select task-specific pathways.  
  *Why unresolved:* Path tracing identifies components but not information content or selection algorithm.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Probe early/late layers with SAEs; ablate identified heads/MLPs; analyze how IV modulates attention/MLP activations.

## Limitations
- Scale confined to 1B models; larger architectures may differ.
- Tasks limited to six (two contrastive, four BigBench); broader generalization unclear.
- Path-tracing uses untested local linearization of GELU/RMSNorm and relies on heuristic path thresholds.
- Linear probe depends on subjective rephrasing generation.
- Base vs. tuned comparison only within OLMo-2 family.

## Confidence
- IV localization & superadditivity: **High** (p<0.001 T-tests, consistent heatmaps across tasks).
- Circuit selection mechanism: **Medium** (novel path-tracing lacks independent validation; approximations untested on ground-truth circuits).
- Instruction tuning necessity: **Medium** (controlled single-family comparison; may not generalize to other training regimes).

## Next Checks
1. Test superadditivity and path-tracing on larger models (13B/34B) and diverse families (Llama, Mistral) to assess scalability.
2. Validate path-tracing approximations on toy circuits with known ground truth to measure accuracy and sensitivity to local linearization choices.
3. Expand task diversity to semantic reasoning (e.g., math, code) and probe IV generalizability beyond contrastive tasks.
