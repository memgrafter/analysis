# Phase-Aware Mixture of Experts for Agentic Reinforcement Learning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.17038
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17038
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: PA-MoE+GiGPO with Qwen2.5-1.5B achieves 93.8% success on ALFWorld (+7.7 pp) and 82.3% on WebShop (+14.9 pp) over GiGPO baseline; expert switches reduce from 45 to 8.4 per episode.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a simplicity bias in single-policy reinforcement learning agents, where easy tasks dominate gradient updates and complex tasks are under-trained. Standard Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) with token-level routing fragments temporally coherent behavioral phases, weakening expert specialization. They propose Phase-Aware Mixture of Experts (PA-MoE), which introduces a lightweight phase router that learns latent phase boundaries directly from the RL objective, ensuring consistent expert selection throughout each phase. Experts are implemented as LoRA adapters on a frozen backbone, and training incorporates switching penalties, balance loss, diversity loss, and temperature annealing to encourage stable routing. On ALFWorld and WebShop, PA-MoE+GiGPO with a 1.5B-parameter model significantly outperforms the GiGPO baseline (+7.7 and +14.9 percentage points) and even surpasses 7B baselines. The phase-aware routing drastically cuts expert switches per episode (45 → 8.4), enabling coherent phase-specific expertise and balanced expert utilization.

## Method Summary
PA-MoE replaces token-level MoE routing with a phase router that segments the agent’s trajectory into temporally extended phases and assigns a single expert to each phase. The backbone LLM remains frozen, and each expert is a LoRA adapter applied to the backbone’s activations. The router is trained end-to-end with the RL objective, augmented by auxiliary losses: a switching penalty to discourage frequent expert changes, a balance loss to ensure even expert usage, and a diversity loss to promote specialization. Temperature annealing gradually sharpens the router’s categorical distribution. This design yields stable routing and specialized experts without proportionally increasing inference cost, because only one expert is active per phase.

## Key Results
- ALFWorld: PA-MoE+GiGPO (Qwen2.5-1.5B) reaches 93.8% success, +7.7 pp over GiGPO baseline (86.1%).
- WebShop: achieves 82.3% success, +14.9 pp over baseline (67.4%).
- Even with 1.5B parameters, PA-MoE surpasses 7B baselines; expert switches drop from 45 to 8.4 per episode.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The phase router learns to segment the trajectory into coherent behavioral phases (e.g., navigation, manipulation) and assign a consistent expert to each phase. This prevents the fragmentation of token-level MoE, where independent routing per token causes noisy switches and poor specialization. Stable expert assignment allows each LoRA expert to develop phase-specific strategies. Auxiliary losses (switching penalty, balance, diversity) further stabilize routing and encourage diverse usage. As a result, the limited capacity of the frozen backbone is effectively augmented with specialized adapters that collectively handle tasks of varying complexity, mitigating the simplicity bias.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mixture of Experts (MoE)**: Standard MoE routes each token to one of many experts, enabling conditional computation. *Why needed*: To allocate model capacity dynamically across input parts. *Quick check*: Verify sparsity in gating (e.g., top-1 selection).
- **Phase-aware Routing**: Router operates at temporal-phase level rather than per token. *Why needed*: Maintains temporal coherence of expert assignment, preventing fragmentation and enabling specialization over extended segments. *Quick check*: Measure average switches per episode; phase-aware should yield far fewer than token-level.
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**: Adds low-rank matrices to frozen weights for parameter-efficient fine-tuning. *Why needed*: To create many experts with minimal parameter overhead. *Quick check*: Confirm trainable parameters are a small fraction of the backbone size.
- **Routing Regularization** (switching penalty, balance loss, diversity loss): Auxiliary losses shaping router behavior. *Why needed*: Switching penalty reduces noisy switches; balance loss prevents expert collapse; diversity loss encourages differentiation. *Quick check*: Monitor expert usage distribution (should be balanced) and switch frequency (should be low).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input (state/action history) → Frozen backbone (LLM) → Phase router (lightweight network) → Expert selection (LoRA adapter corresponding to chosen expert) → Policy head (action distribution). The router determines phase boundaries and assigns a consistent expert across each phase.
- **Critical path**: For each timestep, the backbone processes input; the router (conditioned on backbone state) predicts phase membership and expert probabilities; an expert is selected; its LoRA is applied; the modified representation feeds the policy head to produce action probabilities. The RL loss updates router and LoRA parameters; backbone remains frozen; auxiliary losses regularize the router.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Frozen backbone + LoRA: Highly parameter-efficient but limits expressiveness; backbone cannot adapt.
  - Phase router overhead: Adds a small network but reduces average active experts per episode, keeping inference cost comparable to single-expert models.
  - Switching penalty: Too high may prevent necessary switches, harming adaptability; requires careful tuning.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High switch count (>20/episode) despite penalty: router failing to learn stable phases.
  - One expert used >90% of time: balance loss ineffective or router collapse.
  - No improvement on complex tasks: experts not specializing or phase segmentation inadequate.
- **First three experiments**:
  1. Replicate GiGPO baseline on ALFWorld/WebShop to confirm reported numbers without PA-MoE.
  2. Ablate the phase router: replace with random per-timestep expert selection, compare success rates and switch counts.
  3. Sweep the switching penalty weight λ_switch, measuring switch reduction and task success.

## Limitations
- Baseline specificity: The GiGPO baseline is not a standard public benchmark; implementation details and reproducibility are unclear.
- Phase definition: Learned latent phase boundaries are not validated against human annotations or known task structure (e.g., ALFWorld subgoals).
- Generalization: Evaluation limited to two environments (ALFWorld, WebShop); performance on broader agentic RL benchmarks unknown.

## Confidence
- Empirical results (success rates): High
- Causal claim (simplicity bias in single-policy networks): Medium
- Efficiency claim (1.5B vs. 7B): Low
- Phase coherence (switch reduction): High
