# MzansiText and MzansiLM: An Open Corpus and Decoder-Only Language Model for South African Languages

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.20732
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20732
*   **Reference count:** 0
*   **Primary result:** A 125M-parameter decoder-only model achieves 20.65 BLEU on isiXhosa data-to-text generation via monolingual finetuning, competing with 10x larger encoder-decoder baselines, while few-shot reasoning remains near chance.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MzansiText, a curated multilingual pretraining corpus, and MzansiLM, a 125M-parameter decoder-only language model covering all eleven official South African languages. The authors systematically evaluate three adaptation regimes—monolingual task-specific finetuning, multilingual task-specific finetuning, and general multi-task instruction finetuning. Key findings show that monolingual finetuning yields strong generation and sequence labeling performance per language, while multilingual finetuning improves classification for related Bantu languages. However, few-shot reasoning tasks prove challenging at this model scale. The corpus and model are publicly released to establish a reproducible baseline for low-resource South African languages.

## Method Summary
The work specifies a reproducible pipeline: (1) aggregate public multilingual sources (mC4, WURA, etc.) into MzansiText; (2) apply a 7-stage Datatrove filtering/deduplication process; (3) train a 65k BPE tokenizer on the pretraining split; (4) pretrain a MobileLLM-style 125M decoder-only model from scratch for 5 epochs on 3.8B tokens; (5) finetune via LoRA (rank 128, α=256) under three regimes: monolingual per task-language, multilingual per task (pooling languages), and multi-task mixing all tasks/languages; (6) evaluate on standard benchmarks (MasakhaNEWS/NER/POS, T2X, Belebele, IrokoBench). Major unknowns include exact MobileLLM hyperparameters, multi-task sampling weights, and precise Datatrove parameters.

## Key Results
*   Monolingual task-specific finetuning achieves 20.65 BLEU on isiXhosa data-to-text generation, competing with much larger encoder-decoder models.
*   Multilingual task-specific finetuning reaches 78.5% macro-F1 on isiXhosa news classification.
*   Few-shot reasoning performance (e.g., on IrokoBench) remains near chance across all finetuning regimes, highlighting scale limitations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Monolingual task-specific finetuning is most effective for generation and sequence labeling tasks.
*   **Mechanism:** Targeted in-language finetuning allows the model to learn language-specific morphological patterns (e.g., Bantu agglutination) and task structures, compensating for its small parameter count.
*   **Core assumption:** The pretraining corpus contains sufficient high-quality text in each language.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 6.1 highlight strong isiXhosa generation; Table 2 confirms ≥800K tokens even for lowest-resource isiNdebele.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Multilingual task-specific finetuning improves classification for closely related Bantu languages via cross-lingual transfer.
*   **Mechanism:** Pooling examples from related languages exposes the model to shared semantic categories and typological features (e.g., noun-class systems).
*   **Core assumption:** Related languages (e.g., isiXhosa, isiZulu) share sufficient typological similarity.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 6.1 report improved isiXhosa classification; corpus skew (84% English/Afrikaans) still yields benefit, suggesting task-data diversity matters.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Few-shot reasoning tasks remain challenging for the 125M model regardless of finetuning.
*   **Mechanism:** Complex multi-step inference requires emergent capabilities that manifest only at much larger scales (≥70B parameters); the small model lacks capacity for robust in-context learning.
*   **Core assumption:** Reasoning abilities scale predictably with model size.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Table 11 show near-chance accuracy on AfriXNLI/MMLU, matching other small decoder-only models.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Multilingual corpus curation for low-resource languages. *Why needed:* MzansiText's source aggregation (mC4, WURA, etc.), filtering pipeline, and inherent skew (Table 2, Section 3) directly explain downstream performance variation. *Quick check:* If you capped English/Afrikaans to 25% each in pretraining, what downstream impact occurred and why might that be?
*   **Concept:** Decoder-only architecture limitations vs. encoder-based models. *Why needed:* The paper contrasts MzansiLM's generation strengths against encoder dominance on classification/sequence labeling (Tables 6-8); understanding causal attention and next-token prediction constraints is key. *Quick check:* Why might a decoder-only model struggle with token-level labeling (MasakhaPOS/NER) even after finetuning?
*   **Concept:** Adaptation regime trade-offs (monolingual vs. multilingual vs. multi-task). *Why needed:* The paper's core contribution is systematically comparing these strategies, trading off data efficiency, generalization, and task-specific performance. *Quick check:* For a new low-resource language with only 1K labeled examples, which finetuning regime would you prioritize and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
MzansiText (raw curated corpus) -> 65k BPE Tokenizer -> MobileLLM 125M pretraining (5 epochs, 3.8B tokens) -> LoRA finetuning (rank 128, α=256) per regime -> Evaluation on Masakha/T2X/Belebele/IrokoBench.

Critical path: 1. Corpus aggregation & 7-stage Datatrove filtering/deduplication. 2. Pretraining with per-language validation loss averaging. 3. LoRA finetuning with task-specific early stopping. 4. Evaluation with task-specific decoding (greedy/sampling).

Design tradeoffs: Corpus skew (84% EN/AF) maximizes high-quality data volume but limits Bantu representation; forced balance degraded quality (Appendix A). Model scale (125M) enables reproducibility but caps reasoning; larger scales (70B+) are infeasible here. LoRA prevents overfitting on small datasets but may limit complex task capacity.

Failure signatures: Near-chance (<35%) on Belebele/IrokoBench indicates insufficient reasoning capacity. Multi-task finetuning underperforming monolingual on generation/NER suggests task interference. Multilingual finetuning harming distant languages indicates failed cross-lingual transfer. Validation loss dominated by high-resource languages signals mixture issues.

First 3 experiments: 1. Replicate Datatrove pipeline on 10% raw sample; verify token retention matches Table 3 (±2%). 2. Finetune on isiXhosa T2X only; confirm BLEU >20 (Table 5) vs. base 0-shot. 3. Pool isiXhosa, isiZulu, Setswana for MasakhaNEWS; verify isiXhosa macro-F1 >78% (Table 6) while isiNdebele (unpooled) remains low.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Impact of corpus skew:** The authors note the pretraining corpus is heavily skewed toward English and Afrikaans (84% combined). They raise the question of how downstream performance for low-resource Bantu languages would change if the corpus were forced to be more balanced, and whether the observed gains from multilingual finetuning would persist or diminish under such a constraint.
*   **Decoder-only limitations for sequence labeling:** While monolingual finetuning yields strong results, the paper implicitly questions the ultimate ceiling of decoder-only models for token-level tasks (NER, POS) compared to encoder-based architectures, given the inherent mismatch between next-token prediction and label assignment.
*   **Mechanisms of cross-lingual transfer:** The paper shows multinational finetuning helps related Bantu languages but does not deeply analyze *why*—e.g., is it primarily shared vocabulary, similar syntax, or the model's ability to align representations? The authors call for further ablation studies (e.g., by language family) to isolate the drivers of transfer.

## Limitations
*   Exact MobileLLM 125M hyperparameters (layers, heads, dims) are not specified; must be inferred from Liu et al. (2024).
*   Multi-task finetuning sampling weights and specific prompt templates are omitted.
*   The pretraining corpus is heavily skewed toward English/Afrikaans (84% combined), limiting Bantu language representation.

## Confidence
*   Monolingual finetuning effectiveness: **High** (strong empirical results across multiple tasks, reproducibility hinges on hyperparameters).
*   Multilingual finetuning cross-lingual transfer: **Medium** (solid classification scores but pooling all languages mixes high-resource influence; per-language ablations needed).
*   Few-shot reasoning limitations at 125M scale: **High** (near-chance performance aligns with scaling laws and small-model benchmarks; likely intrinsic).
