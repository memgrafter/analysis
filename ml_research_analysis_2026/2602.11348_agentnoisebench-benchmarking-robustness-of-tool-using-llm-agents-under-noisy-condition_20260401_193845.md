# AgentNoiseBench: Benchmarking Robustness of Tool-Using LLM Agents Under Noisy Condition

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.11348
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11348
- **Reference Count:** 36
- **Primary Result:** Under noisy conditions, tool-using LLM agents exhibit a universal average accuracy drop of 20.8%; tool-noise causes significantly greater degradation than user-noise, and reasoning ability shows no strong correlation with robustness.

## Executive Summary
AgentNoiseBench introduces the first systematic benchmark for evaluating the robustness of tool-using large language model (LLM) agents under realistic noise. The work identifies two primary noise families—user-noise (e.g., ambiguous instructions) and tool-noise (e.g., execution failures)—and develops an automated pipeline to inject controllable perturbations into existing agent benchmarks while preserving task solvability. A trajectory-aware evaluation protocol distinguishes truly robust reasoning from chance correct answers. Experiments across 24 models on τ²-Bench, VitaBench, and HotpotQA reveal an average performance drop of 20.8% in noisy settings, with tool-noise proving especially detrimental. Notably, reasoning capability does not predict noise robustness, underscoring the need for targeted robustness engineering in agent systems.

## Method Summary
The core method constructs a controllable noise generator that operates over the agent’s interaction interface. Noise is categorized into two orthogonal families: user-noise (perturbations to natural language instructions) and tool-noise (perturbations to tool outputs). To generate challenging yet solvable perturbations, the authors employ constrained adversarial optimization: a frozen reference agent (o3) is attacked by optimizing a system prompt that produces noisy inputs; the optimization maximizes performance degradation on the reference agent while enforcing a solvability constraint, typically verified by a separate oracle or strong model. The optimized noise prompt is then fixed and applied uniformly to all target agents. Evaluation goes beyond final answer accuracy: a trajectory-aware metric called Stability-Gated Success (SGA) requires that both the final answer be correct and that every intermediate step in the agent’s reasoning chain be valid according to step-wise validity checks, thereby filtering lucky outcomes. The framework is validated on three existing agent-centric benchmarks, adapting them with injection hooks to generate noisy variants.

## Key Results
- **Universal performance drop:** Across 24 models and three benchmarks, average accuracy under noise decreases by 20.8%.
- **Tool-noise dominates:** Tool-noise leads to significantly larger degradation than user-noise, highlighting sensitivity to execution failures.
- **Reasoning vs. robustness disconnect:** No strong correlation is found between an agent’s reasoning ability (measured on clean benchmarks) and its robustness to noise, indicating that current reasoning-focused training does not confer noise resilience.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Systematic noise categorization
A two-type taxonomy—user-noise (ambiguous, inconsistent, redundant, topic-drift, boundary-probing instructions) and tool-noise (execution failures, incomplete, erroneous, misleading, redundant outputs)—enables targeted robustness evaluation. By mapping real-world imperfections to these controlled perturbations, the benchmark avoids arbitrary distortions and covers the most common failure modes in tool-using agents. The taxonomy is derived from analysis of interaction logs and is sufficiently general to apply across multiple benchmarks. **Break condition:** Noise sources outside this dichotomy (e.g., environmental stochasticity not tied to user/tool interfaces) would limit coverage.

### Mechanism 2: Constrained adversarial injection
To ensure noise is both challenging and fair, the authors use an adversarial approach that optimizes a system prompt $\theta$ against a reference agent $A_{\text{ref}}$ (o3). The optimization objective is to maximize degradation $L_{\text{deg}}$ while strictly maintaining solvability $I_{\text{solvable}}=1$. This produces noise prompts $\theta^*$ that are strong yet preserve task solvability; freezing $\theta^*$ for all evaluated agents guarantees a uniform noise condition. The core assumption is that perturbations effective against a state-of-the-art agent transfer to other architectures. **Break condition:** If solvability checking is unreliable, some tasks become unsolvable, compromising fairness.

### Mechanism 3: Trajectory-aware evaluation
The proposed Stability-Gated Success (SGA) metric addresses the limitation of standard accuracy, which can be inflated by agents arriving at correct answers via deviant reasoning under noise. SGA requires: (1) the final answer satisfies the task, and (2) every step in the agent’s trajectory passes a step-wise validity predicate $I_{\text{step}}(s_i, T)$. A trajectory is valid only if all steps are valid, i.e., $I_{\text{traj}}(\tau, T)=1$. This filters “lucky” correct answers and isolates true robustness of the reasoning process. **Break condition:** If step-wise validity labels are noisy or fail to capture legitimate non-monotonic reasoning, robust agents may be penalized.

## Foundational Learning
- **Noise taxonomy (user vs. tool):** Needed to map real-world imperfections to controllable perturbations without arbitrary distortion. *Quick check:* Give one example of user-noise and tool-noise. Why does tool-noise cause greater degradation?  
- **Solvability-preserving injection:** Ensures failures stem from agent fragility, not impossible tasks, enabling fair comparisons. *Quick check:* What mechanism enforces solvability during noise injection? How might it fail?  
- **Trajectory validity gating:** Prevents correct-final-answer artifacts from masking reasoning failures under noise. *Quick check:* What does $S_{G_A}=1$ require that standard accuracy does not?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text flow):**  
Noise Taxonomizer → Adversarial Injector → Solvability Verifier → Trajectory Evaluator → Benchmark Integrator  

**Critical path:**  
1. Define noise taxonomy → 2. Optimize noise prompt $\theta^*$ against reference agent (o3) with solvability constraint → 3. Freeze $\theta^*$ and apply to all benchmarks → 4. Run target agents on clean and noisy splits, logging trajectories → 5. Compute step-wise validity and SGA scores → 6. Aggregate robustness metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Noise intensity vs. solvability:* Stronger noise increases challenge but risks unsolvable tasks, biasing robustness estimates.  
- *Reference agent choice:* Using a state-of-the-art model (o3) aims to produce transferable perturbations; a weaker reference may yield ineffective noise.  
- *Trajectory granularity:* Finer step validation improves diagnosis but increases annotation cost and potential subjectivity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Non-convergence of adversarial optimization → unstable or trivial noise prompts.  
- False negatives in $I_{\text{solvable}}$ → artificially inflated robustness gaps due to unsolvable instances.  
- Low human–validator agreement on $I_{\text{step}}$ → invalid SGA scores.

**First 3 experiments (for onboarding):**  
1. *Taxonomy sanity check:* Inject each noise type individually on a single model; verify expected degradation patterns (e.g., tool-failure severely impacts search tasks).  
2. *Reference agent transfer test:* Optimize $\theta^*$ with $A_{\text{ref}}=o3$, evaluate on non-thinking models; compare degradation to using $A_{\text{ref}}$ of same model family.  
3. *Trajectory gating validation:* Manually inspect 50 trajectories with correct final answers; quantify how many are invalidated by $I_{\text{traj}}$ and confirm via human annotation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Training methodologies for robustness:** What methods (e.g., reinforcement learning with noise-aware objectives) can effectively improve agent robustness? The paper suggests future work on noise-augmented training.  
2. **Planner vs. reactive agents:** How does noise robustness of agents with explicit planning modules compare to reactive LLM-based agents? Current evaluation focuses on reactive architectures; planning-based approaches may handle uncertainty differently.  
3. **Reasoning vulnerability mechanism:** Why are reasoning-capable models more vulnerable to noise despite superior clean performance? The causal link between reasoning training and noise fragility remains unknown.

## Limitations
- The optimized noise prompts $\theta^*$ may not be released, forcing costly re‑optimization with o3 API and a solvability oracle.  
- Step‑wise validity function $I_{\text{step}}$ is unspecified; likely relies on a judge model, introducing subjectivity and potential inconsistency in SGA scores.  
- Solvability verification $I_{\text{solvable}}$ during injection is a single point of failure; unreliability could generate unsolvable tasks that inflate robustness differences.

## Confidence
- Universal 20.8% accuracy drop & tool-noise impact: High  
- No strong correlation between reasoning ability and robustness: High  
- Taxonomy completeness: Medium  
- Adversarial injection fairness: Medium  
- Trajectory gating validity: Low‑Medium  

## Next Checks
1. **Solvability audit:** Randomly sample 100 noisy tasks per benchmark; run a strong agent (e.g., o3) to confirm >95% solvability. Recompute robustness after excluding unsolvable instances.  
2. **Trajectory validity human check:** Annotate 100 trajectories (50 SGA-success, 50 failure) to measure agreement with $I_{\text{step}}$; target Cohen’s $\kappa \ge 0.7$.  
3. **Reference agent transfer test:** Optimize separate noise prompts using different reference agents (e.g., Claude‑3.5, GPT‑4.1); apply to a common target set and measure variance in degradation. Variance >5% absolute suggests limited transferability.
