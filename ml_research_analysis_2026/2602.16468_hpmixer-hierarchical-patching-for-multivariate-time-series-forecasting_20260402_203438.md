# HPMixer: Hierarchical Patching for Multivariate Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16468
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16468
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Hierarchical patching with decoupled periodicity and residual modeling achieves state-of-the-art forecasting accuracy on most benchmarks, ranking first in 23/28 MSE and 21/28 MAE evaluations.

## Executive Summary
HPMixer is a novel architecture for long-term multivariate time series forecasting that addresses two key challenges: modeling heterogeneous periodic patterns and capturing multi-scale residual dynamics. It decouples the input series into periodic and residual components. The periodic branch uses a learnable cycle module enhanced with a channel-wise MLP to model stable, shared periodicities across channels. The residual branch employs a Learnable Stationary Wavelet Transform (LSWT) for shift-invariant, multi-resolution frequency decomposition, followed by a two-level hierarchical non-overlapping patching mechanism that jointly processes coarse and fine temporal scales. An additive combination of the two branch forecasts yields the final prediction. Extensive evaluation on seven standard datasets demonstrates consistent state-of-the-art performance, particularly excelling on datasets with clear periodic structures.

## Method Summary
HPMixer processes input series through two parallel branches. The periodic branch extracts a dominant cycle length via autocorrelation, initializes a learnable cycle matrix to generate a base periodic representation, and refines it with a channel-wise MLP for heterogeneity. The residual branch first computes the difference between the input and the periodic component, then applies a Learnable Stationary Wavelet Transform (LSWT) with trainable filters to obtain approximation and detail coefficients without downsampling. These coefficients are partitioned into coarse patches, mixed via a Channel-Mixing Encoder, further split into fine patches, and processed by a Coarse-Fine Patching Mixer. The transformed coefficients are inverted via an inverse SWT, summed with the periodic branch output, and projected to the forecast horizon. The hierarchical non-overlapping patching enables separate yet integrated modeling of long- and short-term residual variations.

## Key Results
- HPMixer ranks first in 23 out of 28 MSE evaluations and 21 out of 28 MAE evaluations across seven benchmarks and prediction horizons 96–720.
- It significantly outperforms recent baselines like SimpleTM, CycleNet, and iTransformer, with an average MSE reduction of 4.46% on ETTm1 versus SimpleTM.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical contribution of each proposed module: the MLP-enhanced cycle module, LSWT, and hierarchical patching.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Enhanced periodic modeling via a learnable cycle module with channel-wise MLP improves representation of heterogeneous periodicities across channels.
- **Mechanism:** A predefined cycle length (selected via ACF) initializes a recurrent cycle matrix. An MLP refines the resulting periodic representation (`X_period`) across channels, adding non-linearity to capture channels with weak or irregular cycles.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant period length is stable and can be estimated from ACF; channels share a common cycle length but require channel-specific refinement.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "periodic component utilizes a learnable cycle module [7] enhanced with a nonlinear channel-wise MLP for greater expressiveness." [section 3.2] "To address this limitation, we enhance the learnable cycle module with an MLP that increases the model’s capacity to learn periodic structure."
- **Break condition:** If ACF fails to identify a dominant cycle (e.g., in non-periodic or highly irregular series), the periodic branch may inject spurious patterns.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Learnable Stationary Wavelet Transform (LSWT) provides a stable, shift-invariant frequency decomposition of residuals, enabling adaptive separation of trend and high-frequency components.
- **Mechanism:** LSWT convolves the input with trainable low-pass and high-pass filters without downsampling, yielding multi-scale coefficients. Learnable kernels adapt to dataset-specific spectral characteristics, unlike fixed DWT.
- **Core assumption:** Residual dynamics (after period removal) contain meaningful structure better captured in a shift-invariant, multi-resolution frequency representation than in raw time-domain.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "residual component is processed through a Learnable Stationary Wavelet Transform (LSWT) to extract stable, shift-invariant frequency-domain representations." [section 3.2] "The LSWT replaces the fixed wavelet filters with trainable parameters... This produces more informative decompositions than predefined wavelets."
- **Break condition:** If residuals are pure stochastic noise without structure, the LSWT's adaptive filters may overfit or produce unstable coefficients.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Two-level hierarchical non-overlapping patching captures multi-scale temporal dependencies in the residual branch, enabling joint modeling of local variations and long-range context.
- **Mechanism:** Coarse patching creates long segments; each segment is fine-patched into shorter windows. A Channel-Mixing Encoder operates on coarse patches first, then a Coarse-Fine Patching Mixer processes fine patches within coarse windows, mixing information at both scales.
- **Core assumption:** Residual dynamics exhibit both short-term fluctuations and longer-term trends that benefit from separate but integrated processing; non-overlapping patches reduce redundancy and improve efficiency.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "a two-level hierarchical non-overlapping patching mechanism captures coarse- and fine-scale residual variations." [section 3.2] "This hierarchical structure allows the model to learn localized short range patterns from fine patches while still preserving long range temporal context through the coarse patches." [section 2.1] Explicit comparison to overlapping patching (PatchTST) and hierarchical patching inspiration (Swin Transformer).
- **Break condition:** If the optimal temporal scale for a dataset does not align with the fixed coarse/fine split, the model may miss critical scales; also, non-overlapping patches could lose boundary information between segments.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Series X -> Parallel Split: (1) Cycle Module (ACF selects W -> Cycle Matrix -> Channel-wise MLP) -> Y_period; (2) Residual Branch: X_residual = X - X_period -> LSWT (trainable filters) -> Coarse Patching -> Channel-Mixing Encoder -> Fine Patching -> Coarse-Fine Patching Mixer -> Inverse SWT -> Reconstruction -> MLP + Linear -> Y_residual. Final Forecast: Y = Y_period + Y_residual.
- **Critical path:** Residual branch processing (LSWT -> hierarchical patching -> mixer -> ISWT) is most complex; errors here propagate through reconstruction. Periodic branch is shallower but sensitive to cycle length selection.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Non-overlapping patches:** Faster, less redundant, but risks losing cross-patch continuity.
  - **Fixed cycle length (from ACF):** Stable, interpretable, but not adaptive to changing periods.
  - **Separate branches:** Clear decoupling, but assumes additive periodicity+residual decomposition holds.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor performance on high-dimensional, non-periodic datasets (e.g., Traffic as noted in conclusion).
  - Cycle length mismatch: ACF selects wrong length → periodic branch degrades.
  - Stable wavelet filters not learned: Using fixed wavelets (w/o trainable SWT ablation) hurts performance on most datasets.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Visual decoupling check:** Plot X, X_period (output of cycle module), and X_residual (output of LSWT reconstruction) on a known periodic dataset (e.g., ETTm1). Residual should be lower-magnitude, higher-frequency.
  2. **Ablation - remove MLP in cycle module:** Expect drop on datasets with heterogeneous periodicity (per Fig 2 ACF analysis).
  3. **Patch size robustness sweep:** Vary coarse/fine patch sizes on ECL/ETTm1 (per Appendix C); expect U-shaped error curve with clear optimum.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How can the cycle length be made fully adaptive and learnable, rather than predetermined from autocorrelation analysis, to handle highly irregular or non-stationary periodicities?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "relying on a predetermined cycle length limits the model’s adaptability to highly irregular or shifting periodicities... Future work will explore fully adaptive, learnable cycle lengths..."
- **Question:** What architectural modifications are necessary to scale HPMixer’s channel-mixing mechanism to extremely high-dimensional datasets (e.g., Traffic with 862 channels) without performance degradation?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "performance gains diminish on massively high-dimensional datasets like Traffic, suggesting the current channel-mixing mechanism may struggle to fully capture highly complex inter-channel dependencies."
- **Question:** Is the Learnable Stationary Wavelet Transform (LSWT) strictly necessary for the residual branch’s performance, or could a simpler time-domain hierarchical patching achieve comparable results?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Ablation in Table 2 shows "w/o SWT" causes large errors on ETTm1/ETTh1, but the paper emphasizes LSWT's shift-invariance and denoising. However, the success of purely time-domain patching in models like SimpleTM suggests LSWT’s contribution may be dataset-dependent.
- **Question:** Why does the two-level non-overlapping patching mechanism outperform overlapping patching in this frequency-domain setup, given that overlapping patches are widely used in time-domain models like PatchTST?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper advocates non-overlapping patching for "improved computational efficiency and reduced redundancy" but does not analyze why overlapping patches (common in time-domain) are less suitable here. Figure 4’s robustness analysis shows performance peaks at specific patch sizes, indicating sensitivity.

## Limitations
- Stable dominant period assumption may fail on non-periodic/irregular data; ACF cycle selection could be wrong.
- LSWT may overfit pure-noise residuals; trainable filters might become unstable.
- Fixed coarse/fine patch split may miss optimal temporal scales; non-overlapping patches risk boundary information loss.
- Performance drop on high-dimensional, non-periodic datasets (e.g., Traffic).
- Additive period+residual decomposition may break under cross-talk/non-linearities.
- Additional parameters reduce efficiency vs simpler models.

## Confidence
- **Periodic modeling (cycle+MLP):** High (ablation confirms MLP benefit).
- **LSWT decomposition:** Medium (ablation vs fixed DWT shows gains, but shift-invariance effect not isolated).
- **Hierarchical patching:** Medium (better than single-level in some cases, but scale sensitivity unclear).
- **Overall SOTA results:** High (consistent top rankings across 7 datasets, 28 metrics).
