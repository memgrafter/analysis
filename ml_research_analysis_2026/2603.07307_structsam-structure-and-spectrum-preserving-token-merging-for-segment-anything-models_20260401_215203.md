# StructSAM: Structure- and Spectrum-Preserving Token Merging for Segment Anything Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07307
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07307
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Reduces encoder FLOPs by 25-30% (up to 40%+ with prompt-aware merging) while incurring only minor mIoU/Dice drops across eight benchmarks, consistently outperforming baseline token merging methods.

## Executive Summary
StructSAM is a token merging technique designed to accelerate the Segment Anything Model (SAM) without sacrificing segmentation quality, particularly at object boundaries. Existing merging methods degrade SAM's performance because they fail to account for SAM's mixed windowed/global attention and the dense feature requirements of its mask decoder. StructSAM introduces three key ideas: (1) a gradient-based energy score that identifies and protects boundary-critical tokens; (2) grid-based flatness screening to confine merging within spatially coherent, low-gradient cells; and (3) explicit token recovery to restore full resolution after each layer. Across eight natural and medical segmentation benchmarks, StructSAM reduces encoder FLOPs by 25-30% with negligible mIoU loss, and up to 40%+ with prompt-aware merging, while consistently outperforming five recent token merging methods. On the INbreast medical dataset, it achieves 347.8 GFLOPs with 74.81 Dice (vs. baseline 486.4/75.43), and the prompt-aware variant reaches 283.0 GFLOPs with 74.72 Dice.

## Method Summary
StructSAM performs inference-time token merging on SAM's image encoder without any retraining. For each transformer block, the token grid is reshaped into a 2D feature map. A first-order gradient operator (e.g., Sobel) computes an energy map highlighting high-frequency variations. Tokens are partitioned into non-overlapping grid cells aligned with SAM's attention windows. Cells are ranked by flatness (negative maximum gradient within the cell). To achieve a target merge rate r, the flattest cells are selected for merging. Within each mergeable cell, the token with minimal energy is chosen as the destination; all tokens in the cell are averaged into this destination. After self-attention (windowed or global as per SAM's design), token recovery copies the destination feature back to every original position in the cell, restoring the full-resolution grid for subsequent layers and the final decoder. A prompt-aware variant lowers the merge rate inside regions defined by box prompts. The process is applied independently at every encoder layer.

## Key Results
- Reduces encoder FLOPs by 25-30% (up to 40%+ with prompt-aware) while maintaining segmentation quality (minor mIoU/Dice drops).
- Outperforms ToMe, PiToMe, ToMeSD, VidToMe, and ALGM at equal compute across eight benchmarks.
- On INbreast (MedSAM), achieves 347.8 GFLOPs / 74.81 Dice vs. baseline 486.4 / 75.43; prompt-aware variant: 283.0 GFLOPs / 74.72 Dice.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Gradient-based energy preserves boundaries
StructSAM computes first-order feature gradients (e.g., via Sobel) to assign an energy score to each token. Tokens with high gradient indicate strong local variation and are likely near object boundaries; these are protected from merging. The core assumption is that high gradient magnitude correlates with semantically important boundaries, while flat regions are redundant. Evidence includes visualizations (Figure 7) showing energy maps aligning with object edges, and an ablation (Table 4) where a less accurate gradient estimator (central difference) leads to a significant drop in Boundary IoU.

### Grid-based flatness screening maintains spatial coherence
The token grid is partitioned into non-overlapping cells aligned with SAM's attention windows. Cells are scored by flatness, defined as the negative maximum gradient within the cell. Only the flattest cells are selected for merging, and merging occurs only within each cell by averaging tokens into the lowest-energy destination. This confines merging to spatially coherent, low-gradient regions, preventing cross-boundary token aggregation. Ablation studies (Table 4) demonstrate that removing cell partitioning (“No-Cell”) or randomizing cell selection (“Rand-Cell”) both degrade performance, proving that spatially coherent, gradient-sorted cells are essential.

### Score-guided merging bounds spectral distortion
Viewing tokens as graph nodes and merging as graph coarsening, StructSAM's gradient-guided destination selection within coherent cells yields bounded change in Laplacian eigenvalues (spectral discrepancy). This spectral stability explains why StructSAM preserves segmentation quality at high merge rates while baselines degrade. The paper provides a theoretical framework with Theorem 1 and Theorem 2, establishing bounds under conditions such as within-region feature similarity concentration (S1) and margin across regions (S2). Although the assumptions may not always hold in practice, empirical results across datasets support the bound's practical relevance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
SAM image encoder: Patch embedding → Transformer blocks (interleaved windowed and global self-attention) → multi-scale features.  
SAM mask decoder: Cross-attends prompt tokens and image features to generate masks.  
StructSAM injection: Inside each encoder block, between attention layers: compute gradient energy → cell partition → merge → attention → token recovery.

**Critical path:**  
1. After patch embedding, for each transformer block ℓ, reshape tokens to a 2D feature map.  
2. Compute gradient magnitude map G(ℓ) using a Sobel operator (or central differences).  
3. Partition the grid into non-overlapping s×s cells within each attention window.  
4. Score cells by flatness (ϕ = -max(G in cell)), sort, and select mergeable cells to achieve target merge rate r.  
5. For each mergeable cell, choose destination token with minimal G and average all tokens' features into it.  
6. Perform self-attention on the merged token set (windowed or global as per SAM).  
7. Token recovery: Copy the updated destination feature back to all original token positions in the mergeable cell; protected cells retain original features.  
8. Output full-resolution token grid to next block and eventually to the mask decoder.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Cell size s:** Larger cells enable higher merge rates but risk crossing boundaries; smaller cells reduce savings. Should align with patch size and typical object scales.  
- **Gradient operator:** Sobel is robust to noise but slightly costlier than central differences; both are cheap compared to graph-based scores.  
- **Merge aggressiveness:** Higher r gives more speedup but narrows safety margin; prompt-aware variant mitigates by lowering r in prompted regions.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Boundary erosion:** Gradual blurring or shrinkage of thin structures; detected by dropping Boundary mIoU.  
- **Prompt information leakage:** If merging occurs inside prompted regions (without prompt-aware variant), decoder may lose conditional cues.  
- **Spectral drift:** Unexpected overall mIoU drop not explained by boundary loss; may indicate cross-region merging violating Assumption 1.  
- **Token count mismatch:** After unmerging, token grid shape must exactly match original [H,W]; mismatch breaks windowed attention.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Gradient estimation ablation:** Replace Sobel with central difference → observe larger drop in Boundary IoU (Table 4), confirming gradient accuracy matters for edge preservation.  
2. **Cell structure test:** Remove cell partitioning (“No-Cell”) or use random cells (“Rand-Cell”) → performance degrades (Table 4), proving spatially coherent cell-based merging is critical.  
3. **Baseline comparison at high merge rates:** Run ToMe, PiToMe, etc. at r≥0.55 → their mIoU plummets while StructSAM remains stable (Figures 6, Table 3), demonstrating robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Learned gradient operators:** Can hand-crafted gradient operators be replaced with adaptive ones to better handle highly textured or domain-specific imagery? The paper acknowledges that fixed operators (Sobel/central differences) may be suboptimal for complex regions, and the trade-off between simplicity and adaptability is unexplored. Resolving this would require experiments comparing lightweight neural modules for adaptive gradients against fixed operators on textured datasets like DIS5K, measuring boundary preservation and FLOP overhead.
2. **Generalization to non-box prompts:** The prompt-aware variant only addresses box prompts; SAM also supports points and masks. How can merging policies be generalized to multi-modal prompts? Extending beyond boxes requires rethinking how prompt relevance maps to token-level decisions.
3. **Empirical validation of theoretical assumptions:** The spectral guarantees rely on conditions (within-region similarity concentration, margin across regions, gradient score separation) that may not always hold in real-world datasets. How do violations affect spectral distortion and segmentation performance? Evidence would come from analyzing token feature similarities within/across semantic regions across diverse datasets and correlating with spectral discrepancy and boundary mIoU drops.
4. **Adaptive cell partitioning:** The use of predefined fixed grid cells introduces a design choice that may need tuning for different resolutions or architectures. Can cells be made adaptive per layer or input content to further optimize accuracy-efficiency trade-offs? This would require ablation studies with content-aware or learned partitioning on multi-resolution datasets.

## Limitations
- **Cell size and alignment ambiguity:** Exact cell size `s` and its alignment with SAM's window size are not reported, hindering exact reproduction and affecting trade-offs between merge savings and boundary protection.
- **Gradient operator specification gaps:** Sobel kernel coefficients, normalization, and edge token handling are unspecified, leading to variability in energy maps and protection decisions.
- **Prompt-aware variant underspecification:** Inner/outer merge rates and pixel-to-token mapping for box prompts are not provided, preventing faithful replication of the strongest result (283.0 GFLOPs / 74.72 Dice on INbreast).
