# Friends and Grandmothers in Silico: Localizing Entity Cells in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01404
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01404
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Language models use sparse, early-layer MLP neurons as entity-specific access points; negative ablation causes entity-specific amnesia and injection restores recall, strongest in Qwen2.5-7B.

## Executive Summary
This paper tests the "grandmother cell" hypothesis—whether individual neurons serve as dedicated access points for entity-specific factual knowledge—in language models. Using Qwen2.5-7B, the authors localize candidate neurons via stability of activations across templated prompts about the same entity. They validate causality through negative ablation (which induces entity-specific amnesia) and controlled injection (which restores recall). Results show localized cells concentrate in early layers (0–5), persist across aliases and languages, and often suffice as single-neuron handles. However, the phenomenon is strongest in Qwen2.5-7B, with weaker signals in other architectures like OLMo and Llama.

## Method Summary
The study uses PopQA-200 (200 popular entities) with 100 attribute templates for localization. Activations from MLP down-projections are extracted at the entity token, normalized using statistics from 399 generic prompts, and ranked by stability score \( S = (\mathbb{E}[z])^2 / (\text{Std}[z] + \epsilon) \). The top neuron per entity is selected. Trustworthiness is assessed via negative ablation (scaling activations by \(\alpha < 0\)), measuring relative knowledge score drops. Sufficiency is tested by injecting the localized neuron at a placeholder token with mean-entity initialization, comparing pass@5 to controls. Interventions are implemented with NNsight on a single GPU.

## Key Results
- **Early-layer localization and robustness:** Neurons concentrate in layers 0–5 for 99.0% of entities and persist across aliases, misspellings, and multilingual variants.
- **Causal necessity:** Negative ablation (α = -3) reduces Obama's relative knowledge score from 1.0 to 0.123 while Trump remains near baseline.
- **Single-neuron sufficiency:** Injection achieves 63.3% pass@5 vs. 1.8% for mean-entity and wrong-cell controls; 41 of 79 entities succeed with one cell. Effect is robust in Qwen2.5-7B but weaker in OLMo, Llama, Mistral, OpenLLaMA, and Qwen3-8B.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Entity-specific factual recall is anchored by sparse, stable MLP neurons ("entity cells") concentrated in early layers (0–5).
- **Mechanism:** These neurons act as access points to a canonical entity identity representation formed early in the forward pass. The stability score identifies neurons that respond consistently to any surface form of the same entity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Localization strongly non-uniform: 99.0% of entities peak in layers 0–5; same neuron persists across aliases, acronyms, misspellings, and multilingual variants.
- **Break condition:** If localization is not early-layer concentrated or the same neuron does not persist across surface forms, the canonical identity hypothesis weakens.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Suppressing a localized neuron causes entity-specific amnesia—selective failure to recall facts about the target entity while preserving fluency and other entities.
- **Mechanism:** The neuron's output directionally gates access to distributed factual circuits; negative scaling disrupts this signal, dropping answer probability for the target entity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Negative ablation: Obama recall drops substantially as α decreases (to 0.123 at α=-3), while control (Trump) remains near baseline.
- **Break condition:** If negative ablation affects control entities equally or causes no drop for the target, the neuron is not causally necessary.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Activating a single localized neuron at a placeholder token is sufficient to restore entity-consistent factual recall.
- **Mechanism:** The neuron's output steers the model's next-token distribution toward the entity's factual manifold, even with generic context.
- **Evidence anchors:** Controlled injection: pass@5 is 1.8% for mean-entity control, 63.3% for correct-cell injection, and 1.8% for wrong-cell injection.
- **Break condition:** If correct-cell injection shows no separation from controls or requires many neurons (k >> 1) for most entities, single-neuron sufficiency fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: MLP neurons as key-value memories**  
  Why needed: Entity "cell" is a pair of vectors (down-projection detector and up-projection writer) within an MLP block, aligning with MLP storage/retrieval interpretations.  
  Quick check: How does the down-projection activation \( a_{\ell j}(x) \) relate to the neuron's write operation?

- **Concept: Stability-based ranking**  
  Why needed: Localization ranks neurons by cross-prompt stability of normalized activations, favoring high mean and low variability.  
  Quick check: Why is stability score \( S_{\ell j} = (\mathbb{E}[z])^2 / (\text{Std}[z] + \epsilon) \) preferred over mean activation alone?

- **Concept: Causal interventions (ablation/injection)**  
  Why needed: To test whether localized neurons are correlational or causally involved in factual recall.  
  Quick check: What is the key difference between negative ablation (scaling by α < 0) and controlled injection (setting activation to a target value)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Tokenizer -> Residual stream (with layer norms) -> Transformer blocks (self-attention + MLP) -> Logits; intervention targets MLP down-projection activations at specific token positions.
- **Critical path:** 1) Localize candidate cells via stability ranking across templated prompts for an entity. 2) Filter trustworthy cells via negative ablation (entity-specific amnesia test). 3) Validate sufficiency via controlled injection at a placeholder with mean-entity initialization. 4) Test robustness across surface forms.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Sparsity (top-1 neuron) vs. distributed codes (top-k): paper finds marginal gains with top-k, favoring sparsity.  
  - Per-entity α selection (optimizes sensitivity) vs. fixed-α protocol (more generalizable): paper uses per-entity sweep.  
  - First-token metrics vs. full answer sequence: first-token may understate multi-token competence.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Localization: Top neuron not in early layers (< layer 6) for most entities.  
  - Necessity: Negative ablation does not produce a steeper drop for target vs. control entities.  
  - Sufficiency: Correct-cell injection does not significantly exceed mean-entity/wrong-cell baselines.  
  - Robustness: Top neuron changes across aliases/multilingual variants.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Localization smoke test:** For a high-popularity entity (e.g., "Barack Obama"), run stability ranking on K=2 templated prompts. Verify top cell is in early layer (0–5) and activation is stable (low coefficient of variation) across prompts.  
  2. **Ablation sanity check:** For the localized cell, apply negative ablation (α = -3) on an entity-present QA prompt. Measure relative answer score; expect substantial drop for target entity but not for a control entity (e.g., "Donald Trump").  
  3. **Injection directional test:** On a placeholder prompt ("Fact: the spouse of X is named"), inject the localized cell at X with mean-entity initialization. Compare pass@5 against mean-entity-only and wrong-cell injections; expect clear positive delta for correct cell.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Why is the entity cell phenomenon model-specific, with robust effects in Qwen2.5-7B but weaker signals in other architectures like OLMo and Llama?  
  Basis: Explicitly reported cross-family results show weaker consistency.  
  Why unresolved: No investigation of pretraining data, model size, or architectural differences.  
  Resolution evidence: Systematic comparison across matched-scale models; analysis of correlations with layer norms, activation distributions, or data coverage.
- **Open Question 2:** What factors determine whether an entity admits a reliable single-neuron handle, and why are popular entities more likely to have one?  
  Basis: Coverage higher for popular entities; not every entity has a reliable handle (131/200 pass trust filter).  
  Why unresolved: Popularity is a correlate, not dissected for causal attributes like training frequency or relational diversity.  
  Resolution evidence: Correlational study between entity attributes (frequency, number of relations) and localization metrics; controlled experiments across popularity ranges.
- **Open Question 3:** Are there distributed or multi-cell codes for entity representation that complement single-neuron handles, and does the sparse localization method miss them?  
  Basis: Limitations note sparse ranking may miss distributed codes; only one entity required multiple cells.  
  Why unresolved: Methodology prioritizes sparsity for interpretability, potentially overlooking collective encoding.  
  Resolution evidence: Apply distributed probing (linear probes, matrix factorization) to MLP activations; test interventions on ranked neuron sets beyond top-1; compare with entities lacking single-neuron handles.
- **Open Question 4:** Does the entity cell mechanism support full multi-token answer generation, or is it limited to first-token prediction in narrow relational contexts?  
  Basis: Metrics mostly first-token based; interventions narrow in relation coverage.  
  Why unresolved: Entity cells might only influence initial token selection, not sequential generation.  
  Resolution evidence: Extend interventions to generate complete answers (sequence-level pass@k); evaluate on diverse multi-token QA datasets; track activation propagation during generation.

## Limitations
- Template dependency: Localization uses templated prompts; full list unreleased, so phrasing may bias stability scores.
- Injection value estimation: Entity-specific value \(v_j\) is "estimated from entity-present prompts" without a defined formula (mean? max?).
- Cross-model generality: Strong effects appear only in Qwen2.5-7B; other models show weaker signals, possibly due to architectural or training differences.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Early-layer localization): High
- Mechanism 2 (Negative ablation amnesia): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (Single-neuron sufficiency): Medium
- Cross-model replication: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Prompts diversity stress test:** For 50 PopQA entities, rerun localization with K=10 templated prompts (including paraphrases). Check whether top-neuron layer distribution (0–5 concentration) holds and whether the same neuron persists across expanded prompt sets.
2. **Injection α standardization:** On the trusted 131 entities, fix α=32 for all injection experiments, removing per-entity optimization. Recompute pass@5 gaps (correct vs. wrong-cell) with bootstrapped confidence intervals; requires ΔRelProb ≥ 0.30 for ≥80% of entities to support sufficiency.
3. **OLMo 7B ablation replication:** Using the exact localization protocol (same templates if available, else closest equivalents), identify top neurons for 20 high-popularity PopQA entities. Perform negative ablation with α sweep (−3 to 1) and measure relative knowledge score degradation compared to 20 control entities.
