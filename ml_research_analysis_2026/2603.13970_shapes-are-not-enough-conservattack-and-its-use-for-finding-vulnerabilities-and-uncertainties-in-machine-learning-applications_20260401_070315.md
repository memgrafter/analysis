# Shapes are not enough: CONSERVAttack and its use for finding vulnerabilities and uncertainties in machine learning applications

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13970  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13970  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** An adversarial attack (CONSERVAttack) can induce misclassification in HEP deep learning models while keeping dataset-level Jensen-Shannon Distance and linear correlation changes within statistical uncertainties, thus evading standard validation checks.

## Executive Summary
High-energy physics (HEP) relies on deep learning models trained on simulated data. Standard validation—comparing marginal distributions and linear correlations between simulation and real data—fails to detect subtle, high-dimensional mismodeling that can invalidate analyses. This paper introduces CONSERVAttack, an adversarial attack that perturbs input events while constraining dataset-level statistical metrics (Jensen-Shannon Distance and Frobenius norm of correlation matrix changes) to remain within uncertainty bounds. The attack achieves high fooling ratios on Higgs and jet-tagging tasks while remaining statistically invisible. Defenses such as adversarial training and an adversarial detector significantly reduce vulnerability. The authors propose a workflow to systematically quantify and mitigate such hidden systematic uncertainties in HEP.

## Method Summary
CONSERVAttack generates adversarial examples by iteratively perturbing input features while enforcing constraints on dataset-level statistics: Jensen-Shannon Distance (JSD) between marginal distributions and relative Frobenius norm (ΔFN) of Pearson correlation matrix changes. For each input, it computes gradient w.r.t. the loss, considers candidate perturbations per feature, and selects the candidate that minimizes a combined loss `L = α·JSD + β·ΔFN` over the current batch. Already-fooled events are masked from further updates but still contribute to batch statistics. The attack operates under the assumption that the model depends on high-dimensional, non-linear feature interactions not captured by marginals or pairwise correlations. Two defenses are evaluated: adversarial training (retraining on a mix of clean and adversarial examples) and an adversarial detector (binary classifier trained to separate clean from adversarial events).

## Key Results
- On the Higgs dataset (30 features), CONSERVAttack achieves a fooling ratio of ~0.89 while keeping average JSD < 0.02 and ΔFN < 0.2, evading standard sanity checks.
- On the TT vs. WW jet-tagging dataset (87 features), the attack reaches a fooling ratio of ~0.675 with average JSD ~0.045 and ΔFN ~0.182.
- Adversarial training and the adversarial detector reduce the corrected fooling ratio to ~0.05–0.20, demonstrating effective mitigation.
- In a restricted-region attack, the method can cause near-perfect misclassification in a signal region while keeping statistics unchanged in a control region, highlighting insidious vulnerability.
- Adversarial augmentation in a low-data regime improves clean test AUROC by ~1 percentage point on Higgs, suggesting potential for model regularization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Traditional HEP validation fails because it checks only marginal distributions and linear correlations, missing high-dimensional adversarial mismodeling that exploits non-linear feature interactions. CONSERVAttack perturbs input samples while constraining dataset-level Jensen-Shannon Distance (JSD) and relative Frobenius norm of Pearson correlation matrix changes (ΔFN). This keeps low-dimensional statistics within uncertainty bounds, evading standard sanity checks, while the perturbation moves events across the model’s decision boundary via high-dimensional dependencies the model relies on. The core assumption is that the downstream deep learning model depends on feature relationships beyond pairwise linear correlations. Evidence: The paper states that comparing marginals and linear correlations "does not guarantee that all possible sources of deviations have been accounted for" and notes that neural networks often rely on high-dimensional and non-linear correlations. A break condition would be if the model is a linear classifier or uses only explicitly provided marginals and pairwise correlations.

### Mechanism 2
The iterative gradient-based candidate search efficiently finds adversarial perturbations that balance misclassification against statistical invisibility under HEP validation metrics. For each input, compute sign of gradient w.r.t. loss; for each feature, generate multiple candidate perturbations (based on `p_min`, `ϵ_step`). For each candidate, compute batch-level JSD and ΔFN; select candidate that minimizes `L = αJSD + βΔFN`. Iterate `n_it` times, skipping updates for already-fooled events while still including them in batch statistics. The core assumption is that a greedy per-feature perturbation search over a limited candidate set, guided by batch-level statistics, can converge to globally effective adversarial examples under the constraints. Evidence: The paper details the algorithm with loss function (Eq. 4), candidate generation, and dynamic masking; empirical results show high fooling ratios with low JSD/ΔFN. A break condition is if the true adversarial manifold requires coordinated, non-local changes across many features that cannot be reached by independent per-feature steps within the candidate budget.

### Mechanism 3
Adversarial examples generated by CONSERVAttack can improve model performance on clean data when used as data augmentation, particularly in low-data regimes. Train a baseline model on a reduced dataset (inducing underfitting). Generate adversarial examples on that training set. Augment the original training set with these adversaries (1:1 ratio). Retrain a new model from scratch on the augmented set. The adversarial samples expose the decision boundary to regions not covered by clean data, improving generalization. The core assumption is that adversarial samples occupy regions near the decision boundary that are underrepresented in the clean training distribution, and that retraining on them acts as a regularizer. Evidence: The paper shows an explicit pipeline and results: Higgs AUROC improved by ~1 pp on clean test data after adversarial augmentation. A break condition is if the baseline model is already sufficiently trained; adversarial augmentation may then degrade clean performance due to distribution shift or overfitting.

## Foundational Learning
- **Jensen-Shannon Distance (JSD):** Quantifies change between marginal distributions of features; attack constrains JSD to keep histograms within expected statistical fluctuations. *Quick check:* How does JSD differ from KL divergence, and why is it symmetric and bounded?
- **Pearson Correlation Matrix & Frobenius Norm (ΔFN):** Measures preservation of linear pairwise feature relationships; ΔFN normalizes the matrix change by the clean matrix magnitude. *Quick check:* Why use the relative Frobenius norm (ΔFN) instead of an absolute matrix norm?
- **Distance Correlation:** Extends correlation preservation to non-linear dependencies; used in Section 10 to show attack robustness under stricter constraints. *Quick check:* What property makes distance correlation zero if and only if two variables are independent?
- **Fooling Ratio (FR):** Fraction of inputs whose predicted label changes under attack; primary success metric. *Quick check:* Why is FR more informative than average perturbation size in adversarial robustness?
- **Adversarial Detector:** Binary classifier trained to distinguish clean from adversarial events; used to filter adversaries post-attack. *Quick check:* How does the detector’s performance depend on the diversity of attack strategies in its training set?
- **Controlled Region Attack:** Attack that constrains statistics only in a predefined control region, allowing large changes elsewhere. *Quick check:* Why is this a severe threat to analysis integrity?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Baseline MLP Training -> CONSERVAttack (Candidate Generation, Batch Statistic Computation, Iterative Optimization) -> Adversarial Examples -> (Adversarial Training Loop OR Adversarial Detector Training) -> Evaluation (Fooling Ratio, JSD, ΔFN)

Critical path:
1. Train baseline MLP on clean simulated HEP data.
2. Generate adversarial examples for test set via CONSERVAttack using fixed hyperparameters.
3. Compute initial fooling ratio (FR), average JSD, average ΔFN.
4. For mitigation: either perform adversarial training (iteratively augment training set with generated adversaries and retrain) or train an adversarial detector on clean+adversarial labels.
5. Compute corrected fooling ratio by filtering adversaries through the detector.
6. Compare initial vs. corrected FR; if corrected FR falls within known physical systematic uncertainties, no extra uncertainty needed.

Design tradeoffs:
- Attack strength vs. detectability: α, β hyperparameters weight JSD vs. ΔFN; stricter bounds yield lower FR but more invisible attacks.
- Pearson vs. Distance Correlation: Pearson is cheap (O(d) per candidate via incremental update); Distance Correlation captures non-linearities but is expensive (O(d²n²)), limiting sample size.
- Detector threshold: High threshold reduces false positives (clean events flagged) but may miss adversaries; low threshold does opposite.

Failure signatures:
- High JSD (>0.05) or high ΔFN (>0.3): Attack visible; hyperparameters too loose.
- Low fooling ratio (<0.3): Attack ineffective; model robust or hyperparameters too strict.
- Detector AUROC ~0.5: Cannot distinguish clean vs. adversarial; insufficient training data or detector architecture inadequate.
- Clean efficiency drop on real data: Detector or baseline suffers from simulation-to-real mismatch; may need domain adaptation.

First 3 experiments:
1. Reproduce attack on Higgs dataset: Use hyperparameters from Table A2. Verify FR ≈ 0.89, JSD < 0.02, ΔFN < 0.2 over 10 runs.
2. Restricted region attack: Define control region via optimal single-variable cut (e.g., DER mass MMC). Constrain JSD/ΔFN only in control region. Show FR ≈ 1 in signal region while control region JSD/ΔFN remain small.
3. Adversarial detector pipeline: Train detector on clean+adversarial Higgs events. Apply to test adversaries from other runs. Achieve corrected FR ~0.05–0.08. Verify detector AUROC > 0.9 on test set.

## Limitations
- Unknown baseline training details (optimizer, LR, epochs) may affect model robustness and attack transferability.
- Unclear TT vs. WW feature construction and splits hinder exact replication; reliance on [19] leaves preprocessing ambiguous.
- Attack algorithm pseudocode missing; candidate selection and convergence logic must be reverse-engineered, risking implementation drift.
- Generalizability beyond Higgs/TT vs. WW and to real (non-simulated) data remains unevaluated; real-data systematic shifts may break constraints.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (α, β, `p_min`, etc.) not fully explored; reported values may be tuned for optimal presentation.

## Confidence
- **Attack evades marginal/correlation checks:** High (direct experimental evidence with low JSD/ΔFN and high FR).
- **Defenses reduce fooling:** Medium (detector/adversarial training results shown, but dependence on clean training data quality and hyperparameters unclear).
- **Attack improves generalization in low-data regimes:** Low (single-dataset result; benefit may not hold for other architectures or data regimes; risk of overfitting to adversarial artifacts).
