# TeamHOI: Learning a Unified Policy for Cooperative Human-Object Interactions with Any Team Size

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.07988
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07988
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** A single decentralized policy achieves 97.5–99.2% success on cooperative table-carrying with 2–8 agents, zero-shot generalizes to 16 agents and unseen object geometries, and maintains 81.1% success under 5× weight.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses cooperative human-object interaction (HOI) with a variable number of agents. It proposes learning a single decentralized policy that can coordinate teams of any size, removing the need to train separate policies for each team configuration. The core approach combines a Transformer-based network with teammate tokens for scalable coordination, a masked Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) to ensure motion realism using only single-human reference data, and a geometric formation reward that is agnostic to team size and object shape. The unified policy demonstrates high success rates, robust zero-shot generalization to larger teams and new objects, and stable performance under heavy loads, significantly outperforming baselines designed for fixed team sizes.

## Method Summary
The method learns a single decentralized policy for cooperative HOI that generalizes to any number of agents. Each agent’s policy is a Transformer that processes its own observation tokens and a variable set of teammate tokens, enabling scalable coordination. Motion realism is enforced via a masked AMP strategy, which uses a discriminator trained on single-human reference motions but masks out the object-interacting body parts (e.g., arms), allowing task rewards to directly shape those motions while the lower body adheres to realistic styles. Stable object transport is promoted by a formation reward that incentivizes agents to distribute support along the object’s principal axes, computed from its projected convex hull and inertia. The policy is trained in parallel environments with randomly sampled team sizes (2–8 agents) and object shapes.

## Key Results
*   The unified policy achieves 97.5–99.2% success on table-carrying with 2–8 agents under normal weight, and 81.1% success for 8 agents at 5× weight.
*   It significantly outperforms baselines with policies fixed to specific team sizes and demonstrates zero-shot generalization to teams of 12–16 agents and unseen table geometries.
*   Learned formations are coherent and stable, with the formation reward successfully dispersing agents to balance the object across diverse shapes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** A Transformer-based policy with teammate tokens enables decentralized coordination across variable team sizes with a single policy.
*   **Mechanism:** The policy processes the observing agent's observation tokens and a variable set of teammate tokens via alternating self-attention (on self) and cross-attention (on teammates). This architecture natively handles a variable number of teammate inputs, removing the fixed-size limitation of MLPs. The teammates' states (position, heading, relative angle) are expressed in the observer's local frame, providing a consistent relational representation regardless of total team size.
*   **Core assumption:** The relevant coordination cues for any agent can be captured by a fixed-dimensional representation of each teammate (9D in the paper). The self-attention mechanism can effectively weigh the relevance of different teammates dynamically.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "Each agent operates using local observations while attending to other teammates through a Transformer-based policy network with teammate tokens, allowing scalable coordination across variable team sizes."
    *   [section 3.2] Details the token structure, transformer layers, and the variable set of teammate tokens.
*   **Break condition:** Performance degrades if the maximum number of teammates in the positional encoding or attention mechanism is exceeded (e.g., >16 agents in this study), or if the local frame representation fails to capture necessary global coordination geometries for extremely large N.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** A masked Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) strategy broadens the diversity of feasible cooperative behaviors from single-human reference data by decoupling full-body style realism from object-interaction constraints.
*   **Mechanism:** Two discriminators are trained: `D_full` evaluates full-body motion realism against reference data. `D_mask` evaluates only non-interacting body parts (e.g., legs, torso), masking out hands/forearms. During object interaction, the style reward is blended toward `D_mask`, freeing the hand-object interaction from the constraints of single-person reference motions. Task rewards (e.g., hand proximity, lifting) then directly shape the masked hand/arm motions.
*   **Core assumption:** The lower-body locomotion style (the "masked" regions) can be effectively learned from single-human walking data and remains realistic when the upper-body interaction deviations are guided only by task rewards. The blending schedule (`σ(α_t)`) provides a smooth transition.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "To enforce motion realism while addressing the scarcity of cooperative HOI data, we further introduce a masked Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) strategy that uses single-human reference motions while masking object-interacting body parts during training."
    *   [section 3.2] Provides the formal reward blending equation (Eq. 4) and contrasts with part-wise motion priors (PMP).
*   **Break condition:** If the task rewards are poorly shaped or insufficiently dense, the hand-object interactions guided solely by rewards may become physically implausible or unstable, causing the overall motion to diverge despite a realistic lower body.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** A team-size- and shape-agnostic formation reward promotes stable object transport by encouraging agents to distribute support along the object's principal axes of rotational stability.
*   **Mechanism:** The reward combines two terms: 1) `r_ang` (angular spread) provides a continuous signal to avoid clustering by rewarding agents for achieving target angular spacing (`2π/m`). 2) `r_cov` (principal-axes coverage) measures the support polygon's extent along the object's principal axes (computed via inertia tensor), rewarding formations that span the center of mass. The convex hull projection handles irregular shapes. The combination ensures early dispersion (`r_ang`) leads to balanced, stable support (`r_cov`).
*   **Core assumption:** Stability for a rigid object on a horizontal plane is primarily determined by the convex hull of contact points spanning the principal axes through the center of mass. Agents naturally prefer locomotion aligned with these axes.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "Finally, to promote stable carrying, we design a team-size- and shape-agnostic formation reward."
    *   [section 3.3.1] mathematically defines `r_ang` (Eq. 5) and `r_cov` (Eq. 6, Figure 3) and their combination.
*   **Break condition:** Fails for object geometries where the principal axes are not aligned with the preferred human locomotion directions (e.g., a very long, narrow object where the short axis is the stable one), or if the mass distribution is highly non-uniform and not captured by the 2D planar inertia calculation.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with Reward Shaping**
    *   **Why needed here:** The core training paradigm for the decentralized policies. The complex cooperative HOI task has a sparse primary success signal (reaching the target), so dense, shaped sub-rewards (walking, formation, lifting, transport) are essential for credit assignment and stable learning.
    *   **Quick check question:** If the `r_form` reward were removed, would agents still learn to spread out and form a stable support polygon, or would they cluster and fail to lift?
*   **Concept: Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP)**
    *   **Why needed here:** To ensure the humanoid motions remain physically plausible and human-like despite learning complex cooperative interactions from a scarcity of multi-human data. It regularizes the policy using single-human reference motions.
    *   **Quick check question:** If `λ_AMP` (style reward weight) were set to zero, would the agents learn to carry the table successfully, and would their locomotion gaits look natural?
*   **Concept: Decentralized Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)**
    *   **Why needed here:** The application requires a single policy that generalizes to any number of agents. Each agent must act solely on its local observation (including teammate tokens) without a centralized controller during execution. Training uses parallel environments with different team sizes to expose the policy to this variability.
    *   **Quick check question:** If the teammate tokens were removed from the observation, would the policy still achieve high success rates for 4+ agents, or would cooperation degrade into uncoordinated pushing?

## Architecture Onboarding
Observation Tokenizer -> Transformer Backbone -> Action Head
AMP Discriminators (`D_full`, `D_mask`) train in parallel on policy and reference transitions.

*   **Critical path:** Agent Observation -> Tokenization (MLPs) -> Transformer (Self/Cross-Attention stacks) -> Action Head (MLP) -> PD Control Commands -> Simulator Step. AMP discriminators process state transitions from the policy replay buffer and reference motion dataset.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Transformer vs. MLP:* Transformer adds compute/memory overhead but is necessary for variable-length teammate input. MLPs are faster but limited to fixed team sizes.
    *   *Masked AMP:* Allows diverse hand motions from limited data but risks unrealistic trajectories if task rewards (`r_hand`, `r_lift`) are poorly tuned.
    *   *Formation Reward (`r_cov`):* Geometry-agnostic and smooth but relies on accurate contact point projection and convex hull calculation, which can be sparse for small teams.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Policy collapse:** Agents stand still or fall immediately. Check reward scaling, especially `r_vel_walk` and termination conditions.
    *   **Unrealistic locomotion:** Jagged, non-humanoid gaits. Check `λ_AMP` weight and reference motion diversity.
    *   **Uncoordinated pushing:** Agents pull/push in different directions. Check teammate token correctness (local frame?) and team-size advantage normalization.
    *   **Table rotation/tumbling:** Agents form unbalanced clusters. Check `r_cov` implementation for the specific object geometry and mass distribution.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Sanity Check:** Train the unified policy for 2 agents only. Expect ~99% success. Verifies core task pipeline (tokenization, rewards, AMP) works before scaling.
    2.  **Ablation: Masked AMP:** Train with `r_style` sourced *only* from `D_full` (no masking). Expect lower success rates, especially in lifting, as hand motions are constrained by single-human upper-body references.
    3.  **Zero-Shot Team Size:** Load the 2-8 agent trained policy and evaluate on 12 or 16 agents with a large table. Expect competent but degraded coordination (lower `t_coop`, slightly higher jerk). Validates the scalability claim of the teammate token mechanism.

## Limitations
*   The Transformer's variable-length handling has an empirical ceiling (tested up to 16 agents); scalability beyond that is unknown.
*   Masked AMP assumes dense task rewards can plausibly shape hand-object interactions; it may fail with sparse rewards.
*   The formation reward's geometric heuristic (convex hull along principal axes) may not hold for objects with extreme aspect ratios, highly non-uniform mass, or non-convex shapes.

## Next Checks
1.  *Scalability Stress Test:* Evaluate the policy (trained on 2–8 agents) on team sizes 9–32 with a large object. Measure success rate, formation metrics (angular dispersion, convex hull coverage), and computational cost to pinpoint the scalability threshold.
2.  *Geometric Robustness Probe:* Test on objects with (a) high aspect ratio (e.g., 10:1 rectangle), (b) off-center mass distribution, and (c) non-convex shape (L-shape). Compare stability and success against standard rectangles to expose limitations of the principal-axis heuristic.
3.  *Masked AMP Sensitivity:* Ablate by training with full AMP (no masking) and no AMP, while varying lifting reward density (sparse vs. dense). Assess success rates and upper-body motion realism (via discriminator or human evaluation) to test reliance on dense task rewards for hand interactions.
