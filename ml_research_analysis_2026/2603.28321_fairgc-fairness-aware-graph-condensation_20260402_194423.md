# FairGC: Fairness-aware Graph Condensation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28321
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28321
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** FairGC reduces fairness disparity (e.g., ΔEO from 7.59% to 0.42% on Pokec-n-G) while maintaining comparable accuracy to prior non-fair graph condensation methods.

## Executive Summary
Graph condensation compresses large graphs into smaller synthetic ones for efficient training but often amplifies demographic bias by ignoring fairness during reduction. FairGC introduces a fairness-aware framework that directly integrates fairness objectives into the condensation process. It combines distribution-preserving condensation, spectral structure retention, and a fairness-enhanced neural architecture. Evaluations on four real-world datasets demonstrate a superior accuracy-fairness trade-off, significantly lowering disparities in Statistical Parity (ΔSP) and Equal Opportunity (ΔEO). This approach effectively decouples predictive signals from sensitive attributes, enabling equitable synthetic graphs for sensitive applications.

## Method Summary
FairGC addresses bias amplification through three integrated components. First, distribution-preserving condensation synchronizes label and sensitive attribute distributions between original and synthetic graphs, ensuring demographic parity is maintained. Second, spectral encoding using Laplacian eigen-decomposition retains global graph structure, which is critical for preserving relational information that might inform fairness. Third, a fairness-enhanced neural architecture employs multi-domain fusion to separate predictive and sensitive feature representations, coupled with a label-smoothing curriculum to stabilize training. These components are optimized jointly, allowing the synthetic graph to balance utility and fairness from the outset.

## Key Results
- On Pokec-n-G, FairGC achieves 70.87% accuracy with ΔEO of 0.42%, compared to GCond’s ΔEO of 7.59% with similar accuracy.
- Across four datasets, FairGC significantly reduces ΔSP and ΔEO disparities while maintaining competitive node classification accuracy.
- The method successfully decouples prediction from sensitive attributes, yielding synthetic graphs that promote equitable performance across demographic groups.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FairGC’s mechanism hinges on embedding fairness constraints at every stage of condensation. The distribution-preserving objective forces the synthetic graph to mirror the original graph’s demographic makeup, preventing under/over-representation of groups. Spectral encoding preserves global topology, ensuring that structural patterns correlated with fairness (e.g., homophily across groups) are not inadvertently distorted. The multi-domain fusion architecture explicitly learns domain-invariant representations, reducing the model’s reliance on sensitive attributes for prediction. Together, these ensure the synthetic graph is a fair summary of the original, not a biased subset.

## Foundational Learning
- **Graph Condensation:** The process of reducing a large graph to a smaller, synthetic representative for efficient model training. *Why needed:* Scalability for massive graphs. *Quick check:* Does the condensed graph preserve performance on the original task?
- **Demographic Bias in Graphs:** Unfair disparities in model performance across groups defined by sensitive attributes (e.g., gender, race). *Why needed:* Ethical deployment of graph-based systems. *Quick check:* Are predictions correlated with sensitive attributes?
- **Statistical Parity (ΔSP):** Difference in positive prediction rates between groups. *Why needed:* Measures outcome fairness. *Quick check:* Do groups have equal likelihood of favorable predictions?
- **Equal Opportunity (ΔEO):** Difference in true positive rates between groups. *Why needed:* Measures fairness in correct positive identifications. *Quick check:* Are qualified individuals equally likely to be identified across groups?
- **Laplacian Eigen-decomposition:** Spectral method to analyze graph structure via eigenvectors of the Laplacian matrix. *Why needed:* Captures global connectivity patterns. *Quick check:* Do low-frequency eigenvectors encode community structure?
- **Multi-domain Fusion:** Learning representations that are invariant across domains (e.g., sensitive attribute domains). *Why needed:* Prevents domain-specific (biased) features from dominating. *Quick check:* Are feature distributions aligned across sensitive groups?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Graph -> Distribution-Preserving Condensation -> Spectral Encoding -> Fairness-Enhanced Architecture -> Synthetic Graph
- **Critical path:** The distribution-preserving and spectral modules jointly generate an initial synthetic graph structure, which the fairness-enhanced architecture then refines by learning decoupled representations, ensuring the final output is both structurally sound and fair.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Computational overhead from spectral decomposition and multi-domain fusion vs. fairness gains; potential slight accuracy trade-off for substantial fairness improvement.
- **Failure signatures:** High ΔEO/ΔSP despite similar overall accuracy; performance drop for minority groups; unstable training curves due to competing objectives.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to four datasets; generalizability to larger or structurally different graphs is unknown.
- No reported statistical significance or variance across random seeds, leaving results potentially sensitive to initialization.
- Computational overhead from spectral encoding and multi-domain fusion is not quantified.
- The claim of "decoupling predictive signals" lacks interpretability analysis (e.g., feature attribution, counterfactuals).
- Fairness is only assessed via Statistical Parity and Equal Opportunity; other metrics like Individual Fairness are not considered.
