# Bielik 11B v3: Multilingual Large Language Model for European Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.11579
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11579
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves SOTA among 11B models: 65.93 on Open PL LLM, 71.83% on PLCC, and 82.98 on Belebele; outperforms larger models on Polish tasks and remains competitive in English.

## Executive Summary
Bielik 11B v3 addresses the scarcity of high-performance language models for underrepresented European languages, particularly Polish. It scales the Mistral 7B v0.2 architecture to 11B parameters via depth up-scaling and employs a four-stage training pipeline (continuous pre-training, SFT, DPO, RL) on a 1.1-trillion-token multilingual corpus. The model achieves state-of-the-art results among 11B-class models, scoring 65.93 on the Open PL LLM Leaderboard, 71.83% on the Polish Linguistic and Cultural Competency Benchmark (PLCC), and 82.98 on Belebele reading comprehension across European languages. It consistently outperforms models with 2-6× more parameters on Polish-specific tasks while maintaining competitive English performance (72.45 average on the Open LLM Leaderboard, 85.60 on GSM8K) and efficient deployability via extensive quantization.

## Method Summary
The architecture starts from Mistral 7B v0.2, duplicates its 32 layers to 64, and removes the first 8 and last 8 layers to yield a 50‑layer, 11B‑parameter model. Pre‑training occurs in three stages: (1) >1 trillion tokens at 8 k context, (2) ~50 billion tokens at 32 k context (long documents only), and (3) >1 billion tokens at 65 k context using YaRN to extend to 131 k. The 1.1 T corpus is 54.25% Polish, 20.50% English, with rigorous quality filtering and ethical processing. Post‑training includes supervised fine‑tuning on 20 M Polish/European instructions, Direct Preference Optimization on 114 k reasoning‑focused pairs, and reinforcement learning (GRPO) on 143 k verifiable problems (math, logic, tool‑use). Training uses AdamW, cosine learning‑rate schedules, bf16 precision, gradient clipping, and checkpoint merging. The model uses a standard Transformer decoder with GQA, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, RoPE (θ=1e6), and a 32 k‑token tokenizer.

## Key Results
- State‑of‑the‑art among 11B‑class models: 65.93 on Open PL LLM, 71.83% on PLCC, 82.98 on Belebele.
- Outperforms models with 2‑6× more parameters on Polish‑specific tasks.
- Competitive English performance: 72.45 average on Open LLM Leaderboard, 85.60 on GSM8K; efficient deployment with quantization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Scaling the Mistral 7B v0.2 architecture to 11B parameters via depth up-scaling preserves the original model's learned representations while increasing capacity, enabling high performance on consumer‑grade hardware.
- **Mechanism:** The process duplicates existing layers of a 32‑layer base model and strategically removes the first 8 and last 8 layers from the central junction, resulting in a 50‑layer model. This method aims to initialize the deeper network with useful, pre‑trained weights rather than random initialization, potentially easing optimization and retaining the base model's knowledge structure.
- **Core assumption:** The knowledge and feature hierarchies learned in the intermediate layers of the original model are general enough that duplicating them provides a beneficial starting point for a larger model, and the removed layers are the least critical for preserving core functionality.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.1] Describes the exact depth up‑scaling procedure: "we initiated the process with the original n=32‑layer architecture... duplicating the model’s existing layers, followed by the strategic excision of the final eight and initial eight layers... culminating in a s=50‑layer model."
  - [abstract] States the model "scales the Mistral 7B v0.2 architecture to 11B parameters via depth up‑scaling" and achieves "state‑of‑the‑art results among 11B‑class models."
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (Bielik 11B v2, Bielik v3 Small) validate this approach within the same model family, showing consistent performance gains from this scaling method. *Assumption:* The success in v3 is directly attributable to this specific up‑scaling implementation.
- **Break condition:** If the up‑scaled model fails to converge during continued pre‑training or shows significant degradation on early validation benchmarks compared to the base model, the hypothesis that duplicated layers provide a useful initialization would be undermined.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A four‑stage post‑training pipeline (continued pre‑training, SFT, DPO, RL) progressively aligns the model with human preferences and enhances complex reasoning, particularly in Polish and European contexts.
- **Mechanism:** 1) **Continued Pre‑training:** Expands linguistic knowledge on a massive 1.1 T multilingual corpus. 2) **SFT:** Imprints structured dialogue patterns, instruction following, and tool‑use capabilities using a Polish‑curated dataset. 3) **DPO/DPO‑P:** Refines preferences using contrastive pairs, with a focus on logical/mathematical tasks and multi‑turn dialogues. 4) **RL (GRPO/Dr. GRPO):** Optimizes for verifiable reward signals (e.g., correct math answers, proper function calls), improving token efficiency and analytical rigor.
- **Core assumption:** Each stage builds upon the previous one in a complementary manner; the synthetic and human‑curated data for later stages is of sufficient quality to provide a consistent training signal that generalizes beyond the specific examples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4] Details the entire pipeline: "four‑stage training pipeline: continuous pre‑training, supervised fine‑tuning (SFT), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and reinforcement learning." It specifies dataset enhancements (e.g., "expanded the dataset to over 114,000 examples" for DPO) and RL's focus on "logic, STEM, mathematics, and tool‑use domains."
  - [section 4.4.1] Notes the RL phase used "143k curated problems" with "definitive, checkable solution," providing the verifiable reward foundation.
  - [corpus] The "Making Bielik LLM Reason (Better)" neighbor paper explicitly investigates reasoning enhancements, supporting the efficacy of the alignment stages.
- **Break condition:** Degradation in performance on general language benchmarks (e.g., Open PL LLM) after the RL stage would indicate that the reward optimization is causing overfitting to narrow tasks or catastrophic forgetting of broader linguistic capabilities.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A pre‑training corpus heavily skewed towards Polish (54.25%) and other European languages, with rigorous quality control and ethical filtering, installs foundational linguistic and cultural knowledge specific to the target region, which later fine‑tuning stages specialize.
- **Mechanism:** The 1.1 trillion token dataset prioritizes Polish legal, academic, and cultural sources (e.g., Polish Constitution, Parliamentary Corpus, regional Wikipedias). Pre‑processing includes deduplication, anonymization (PESEL, URLs), and rights‑reservation filtering. This creates a high‑signal foundation that the subsequent SFT/DPO/RL stages can efficiently specialize for Polish tasks and cultural nuances.
- **Core assumption:** High‑quality, region‑specific data in the pre‑training phase is more critical for performance on specialized regional benchmarks (like PLCC) than sheer data volume from generic web crawls. The filtering and blending strategies successfully reduce noise without removing essential knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1.1] Describes data processing: "rigorous preprocessing pipeline... anonymization mechanism... deduplication and quality‑filtering heuristics." It also notes monitoring of "robots.txt files, meta‑tag‑based TDM reservations, and Terms of Service."
  - [section 3.1] & [Table 3] Quantify the Polish‑centric distribution: "Polish 428.6M 54.25%" and lists sources like "Parliamentary Discourse Corpus" and "Kashubian Wikipedia editions."
  - [section 5.5] The exceptional PLCC score (71.83%) is presented as direct evidence of this corpus's effectiveness for cultural knowledge.
- **Break condition:** If ablation studies (not presented) showed that removing non‑Polish European languages from pre‑training did not harm Polish‑specific performance, the claim about the *multilingual* European blend being necessary would weaken. A decline in general European reading comprehension (Belebele) without the multilingual mix would support the claim.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Transformer Autoregressive Pre‑training**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire model is based on the standard decoder‑only Transformer architecture. Understanding next‑token prediction, causal masking, and self‑attention is essential to grasp how the model learns from the 1.1 T token corpus.
  - *Quick check question:* How does causal masking in the attention mechanism ensure the model cannot "see" future tokens during training, and why is this property critical for a language model?
- **Concept: Depth Up‑Scaling vs. Width Scaling**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's key architectural novelty is scaling from 7B to 11B not by widening layers (increasing `model_dim`), but by adding more layers (`Layers` from 32 to 50). This is a non‑standard approach that impacts training dynamics and parameter efficiency.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the potential advantages and disadvantages of increasing model depth (number of layers) instead of width (hidden dimension) when trying to scale model capacity while targeting a specific parameter budget?
- **Concept: Alignment Pipeline (SFT → DPO → RL)**
  - *Why needed here:* The model's strong performance on instruction‑following and reasoning tasks is not from pre‑training alone but from this specific sequence of fine‑tuning stages. Each stage addresses a different aspect of alignment.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the primary difference between what Supervised Fine‑Tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) aim to learn from their respective datasets?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
50 layers, 4096 model_dim, 32 attention heads / 8 KV heads (GQA), SwiGLU activation, RMSNorm, RoPE (θ=1e6). Tokenizer: 32,128 vocab (Mistral base + chat tokens). Context: Native 32k, extended to 131k via YaRN.

### Critical path
1. **Init:** Load Mistral 7B v0.2 checkpoint and tokenizer.
2. **Up‑scale:** Duplicate layers to reach 50, excise as described.
3. **Pre‑train (3 stages):** Baseline (8k ctx, 1T tokens) → Full Context (32k ctx, 50B tokens) → Long Context (65k ctx, 1B tokens, with YaRN).
4. **Post‑train:** SFT (20M samples) → DPO‑P (114k pref.) → RL (143k problems).
5. **Merge:** Checkpoint averaging at stage ends.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Tokenizer:** Retained Mistral's 32k vocab for multilingual efficiency over Polish‑specific tokenization efficiency (Table 2 shows it's suboptimal for Polish CpT). Tradeoff: Slightly higher token count for Polish vs. broader language support.
- **Context Length:** Extended to 131k via YaRN, but primary training on 32k/65k. Tradeoff: The value of extreme context length for typical European tasks vs. significant compute/memory cost and potential data sparsity for very long documents.
- **Pre‑training Corpus:** Heavily Polish‑biased (54.25%) for target performance. Tradeoff: Potential risk of slightly lower English performance compared to a perfectly balanced corpus, though results show competitive English ability.

### Failure signatures
- **Pre‑training:** A steady decline on both `Open PL LLM` and `Open LLM` benchmarks during the Baseline stage (Fig 2) indicates corpus quality issues. Action: Halt, inspect recent data batches for contamination/low‑quality text.
- **Post‑training:** A large drop in Polish benchmarks after RL stage suggests over‑optimization for narrow reward signals (e.g., math format) at the expense of general fluency. Action: Reduce KL penalty coefficient or rebalance RL dataset.
- **Deployment:** Poor generation quality on long‑context tasks (>32k) despite YaRN training suggests failure in the Long Context pre‑training stage or incorrect YaRN application during inference. Action: Validate YaRN scaling factors and test on a known long‑document benchmark.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Depth Up‑scaling Validation:** Train a shallow control (e.g., 32‑layer) model on the *same* first stage pre‑training tokens as the up‑scaled 50‑layer model. Compare learning curves and final Open PL LLM scores to isolate the benefit of the up‑scaling initialization.
2. **Ablation – Polish Data Ratio:** Run a small‑scale pre‑training ablation varying the percentage of Polish data in the mix (e.g., 30%, 54%, 70%). Measure impact on PLCC vs. general European (Belebele avg) performance to quantify the trade‑off between Polish specialization and multilingual competency.
3. **RL Reward Sanity Check:** Before full RL training, run a small probe: generate 100 samples from the DPO‑P model for a subset of RL problems. Manually verify that the automatic verifiers (LaTeX box extractor, multiple‑choice selector, function call parser) correctly label ~95% of samples. Mis‑labeling would poison the RL signal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do individual post‑training stages (SFT, DPO, RL) contribute to Polish versus multilingual performance?
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion states they aim to conduct "more extensive ablation studies to better understand the individual contributions of each training stage."
- **Why unresolved:** No ablation has been performed; thus, resource allocation for future training is not optimized and stage interdependencies remain unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train models with subsets of stages (e.g., pre‑training+SFT, pre‑training+SFT+DPO) and evaluate on Polish‑specific (Open PL LLM, PLCC) and multilingual (INCLUDE, Belebele) benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can a tokenizer specifically optimized for Polish and other morphologically complex European languages reduce token count and improve efficiency without degrading multilingual performance?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors retained the Mistral tokenizer due to a "satisfactory trade‑off between tokenization efficiency for Polish and its performance across other supported languages," noting it is not Polish‑optimized.
- **Why unresolved:** The potential for a more efficient tokenizer remains unexplored; this directly impacts inference speed and memory usage on consumer hardware, a key design goal.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop tokenizers with extended Polish‑specific vocabulary, compare tokens‑per‑word for Polish and other languages, and measure downstream task performance to ensure no regression.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the optimal distribution of pre‑training data across European languages to maximize performance on both high‑resource and low‑resource languages within the target set?
- **Basis in paper:** The pre‑training corpus is 54.25% Polish, but the paper does not analyze how this imbalance affects per‑language outcomes across the 32 supported languages.
- **Why unresolved:** The current distribution may inadvertently limit capabilities on less‑represented languages, conflicting with the model's multilingual ambitions; the ideal proportion is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct controlled experiments varying language proportions (e.g., reducing Polish share, increasing low‑resource languages) and evaluate comprehensively on benchmarks like INCLUDE‑base‑44 and Belebele for a wide range of European languages.

## Limitations
- Depth up‑scaling implementation is ambiguously described ("remove first 8 and last 8" vs. 7), preventing exact replication.
- Training pipeline efficacy cannot be independently verified due to unreleased SFT/DPO/RL datasets.
- Causal attribution of the Polish‑centric corpus to the model's specialization lacks ablation; the optimal multilingual mix is unknown.

## Confidence
- Architectural scaling (depth up‑scaling): Medium
- Training pipeline efficacy (SFT→DPO→RL): Medium
- Multilingual/Polish specialization: High for reported benchmark scores; Medium for causal attribution

## Next Checks
1. Depth up‑scaling replication test: implement both layer‑removal interpretations (7 vs. 8) and train 1B tokens from the same Mistral 7B baseline, comparing convergence speed and Open PL LLM performance to isolate the benefit of the up‑scaling initialization.
2. Polish data ratio ablation: pre‑train three small models (1B params) with Polish corpus ratios at 30%, 54%, and 70% (all else equal). Measure impact on PLCC vs. average Belebele across European languages to quantify trade‑offs.
3. RL reward verifier audit: manually label 500 samples from the RL problem set. Compute precision/recall of the automatic verifiers (math box extraction, MCQ exact match, tool‑call schema validation). Error rate >5% would invalidate the RL signal.
