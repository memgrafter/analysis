# Beyond Correlations: A Downstream Evaluation Framework for Query Performance Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17339  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17339  
- **Reference count:** 32  
- **Primary result:** QPP-weighted fusion improves AP@100 and nDCG@10 by over 4.5% relative to unweighted fusion on TREC Deep Learning datasets.

## Executive Summary
Standard query performance prediction (QPP) evaluation relies on set-level correlation (e.g., Kendall’s τ) between predicted and actual query effectiveness across a query set. This approach does not measure per-query effectiveness or downstream utility. This paper introduces a downstream-aware framework where QPP estimates from multiple rankers serve as weights for information retrieval fusion methods (CombSUM, RRF). On TREC Deep Learning 2019 and 2020, QPP-weighted fusion yields more than 4.5% relative improvement in AP@100 and nDCG@10 compared to unweighted fusion. However, the ranking of QPP models by downstream effectiveness shows only moderate correlation (Kendall’s τ = 0.48–0.68) with rankings based on standard correlation, indicating a misalignment between traditional evaluation and practical utility. Per-query analysis further reveals moderately positive Pearson correlations (0.28–0.57) between a QPP model's accuracy in predicting true ranker preferences and the metric gain achieved by using it as a fusion weight.

## Method Summary
The study implements a downstream evaluation framework that integrates QPP estimates into retrieval fusion. Using TREC Deep Learning 2019 and 2020 topics and eight pre-retrieved ranked lists from diverse rankers (BM25, RM3, Splade, E5, ColBERT variants, MonoT5), per-query QPP scores are computed for each ranker’s top-100 results. These scores weight document scores in CombSUM (linear sum) or reciprocal ranks in RRF via the scoring function: θf(q,d;Θ,Φ) = ∑θ∈Θ ϕ(q,Lθ(q)) σ(q,d;θ), where ϕ is the QPP model and σ is the original ranker score. The fused rankings are evaluated against relevance judgments with AP@100 and nDCG@10. The framework also calculates per-query accuracy of QPP models in predicting true ranker preferences (Kendall’s τ over rankers) and correlates these with downstream metric gains. No QPP model is trained on fusion outcomes; existing QPP implementations are used.

## Key Results
- QPP-weighted fusion (using models like RSD) achieves >4.5% relative improvement in AP@100 and nDCG@10 over unweighted CombSUM/RRF on TREC Deep Learning datasets.
- The ranking of QPP models by downstream fusion gains shows low-to-moderate correlation (Kendall’s τ = 0.48–0.68) with rankings based on standard set-level correlation, evidencing a misalignment between traditional QPP evaluation and downstream utility.
- Per-query analysis reveals moderately positive Pearson correlations (0.28–0.57) between a QPP model’s accuracy in predicting true ranker preferences and the actual metric gain from using it as a fusion weight.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** QPP estimates used as priors in IR fusion (e.g., CombSUM) improve retrieval effectiveness over uniform-prior fusion.
- **Mechanism:** A QPP model ϕ outputs a distribution of predicted retrieval qualities across multiple rankers Θ. These predictions ϕ(q, Lθ(q)) replace uniform priors P(Lθ(q)|d)=1 in a weighted fusion scoring function θf(q,d;Θ,Φ) = ∑θ∈Θ ϕ(q,Lθ(q)) σ(q,d;θ), where σ is the ranker's score. The intuition is that a QPP distribution closer to the true ranker qualities will yield a more effective fused ranking.
- **Core assumption:** The relative correctness of a QPP model's ranker preference estimates for a given query is preserved when used as multiplicative weights in a linear fusion framework.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "QPP estimates from multiple rankers serve as weights for IR fusion... On TREC DL datasets, QPP-weighted fusion improves AP@100 and nDCG@10 by over 4.5% relative to unweighted fusion."  
  - [section 2.2] Formalizes Equation 3: θf(q,d;Θ,Φ) = ∑θ∈Θ ϕ(q,Lθ(q))σ(q,d;θ). States: "If the predicted likelihoods closely approximate the true retrieval performance, incorporating them as priors in the fusion process should enhance overall retrieval effectiveness."  
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers ("Breaking Flat...", "On Coherence-based Predictors...") focus on per-rank prediction, supporting the assumption that predicting *which* ranker is best for a query is a distinct, valuable goal.
- **Break condition:** If the QPP model's estimated ranker preferences are largely uncorrelated with true ranker preferences on a per-query basis (low EQ[τΘ]), using them as weights will degrade or fail to improve fusion performance compared to uniform weighting.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Standard set-level QPP evaluation (e.g., average Kendall's τ) is misaligned with downstream utility, as a model high in correlation may not be best for fusion weighting.
- **Mechanism:** Standard QPP eval measures correlation between predicted and true scores *across a set of queries* (EΘ[τQ]). This is a set-level, relative measure. The downstream framework measures utility by the actual retrieval metric gain when predictions are used as fusion weights. These two orderings of QPP models can differ because the former prioritizes overall distributional similarity, while the latter prioritizes accurate per-query ranker preference.
- **Core assumption:** A QPP model's ultimate value lies in its ability to make correct *query-specific* decisions (e.g., choosing a ranker), not just in its global score distribution fidelity.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "The downstream effectiveness of QPP shows low correlation with standard correlation-based rankings, with Kendall's τ between 0.48 and 0.68, indicating a misalignment."  
  - [section 4] "the correlation between the relative ordering of the QPP models between downstream retrieval effectiveness... and EΘ[τQ] evaluation... for AP is τ=0.4805 for DL’19 and τ=0.6798 for DL’20."  
  - [corpus] "Uncovering the Limitations of QPP..." and "On the Feasibility and Robustness of Pointwise Evaluation..." directly investigate limitations of set-level correlation, providing context for this misalignment.
- **Break condition:** If EΘ[τQ] perfectly correlates (τ=1) with downstream fusion gains across diverse QPP models and datasets, the misalignment claim is falsified.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** There is a moderately positive correlation between a QPP model's per-query accuracy in predicting ranker preferences (per-query τΘ) and the downstream IR metric gain achieved when its estimates weight fusion for that specific query.
- **Mechanism:** For a fixed QPP model (e.g., RSD), for each query q, compute 1) its per-query ranker prediction accuracy τΘ (correlation between predicted and true ranker scores for that q across all rankers) and 2) the gain in AP@100 or nDCG@10 from using QPP-weighted fusion vs. unweighted fusion. Plotting these pairs shows a positive trend (Pearson ρ ~0.28-0.57), meaning queries where the QPP model better identifies the best ranker also see larger improvements from QPP-weighted fusion.
- **Core assumption:** The benefit of QPP-weighted fusion is primarily driven by the QPP model's accuracy on a per-query basis in ranking the available retrieval systems.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 4] "Figure 1 shows the per-query correlations... against the downstream IR effectiveness gains... We observe a moderately positive correlation (values reported in the caption)... between the per-query effectiveness of ranker prediction and the downstream fusion gains."  
  - [section 2.1] Defines the per-query distribution output: ϕ(q, LΘ(q)) ↦ R^m, enabling per-query τΘ calculation.  
  - [corpus] Weak. Neighbor papers focus on evaluation frameworks or selective processing, not specifically on this per-query-to-downstream-gain link. Evidence is primarily internal (Figure 1).
- **Break condition:** If the Pearson correlation ρ between per-query τΘ and fusion gains is zero or consistently negative across datasets and metrics, the proposed causal mechanism for fusion gains is invalidated.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Query Performance Prediction (QPP)**  
  *Why needed here:* The entire paper re-contextualizes QPP. You must understand that QPP's standard goal is to predict a scalar retrieval metric (AP/nDCG) for a query *without* relevance judgments, typically evaluated by correlating predictions with true metrics across a query set.  
  *Quick check question:* What is the primary difference between predicting a query's absolute performance (standard QPP) and predicting its relative performance across multiple rankers (this paper's starting point)?

- **Concept: Information Retrieval Fusion (CombSUM, RRF)**  
  *Why needed here:* The downstream application is fusion. CombSUM linearly combines retrieval scores from multiple rankers; RRF combines reciprocal ranks. Both are baseline aggregation strategies. Understanding their standard, unweighted form is necessary to see the innovation of adding QPP-derived weights.  
  *Quick check question:* In CombSUM, how are scores from different rankers combined before final ranking? How does RRF differ?

- **Concept: Correlation Metrics (Kendall's τ, Pearson's ρ)**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper contrasts set-level correlation (Kendall’s τ over queries) with per-query correlation (Kendall’s τ over rankers for a single query) and with Pearson’s correlation between two continuous variables (per-query τΘ vs. fusion gains). Knowing what each metric measures is crucial to interpreting results.  
  *Quick check question:* What does Kendall's τ measure, and how does its interpretation change when computed "over queries" vs. "over rankers"?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input → QPP Module → Fusion Engine → Evaluation Harness → Datasets  
  (Input: query q, multiple ranked lists Lθ(q) from rankers Θ={θ1..θm}. QPP Module: outputs QPP score ϕ(q, Lθ(q)) per ranker. Fusion Engine: implements weighted fusion (Equation 3). Evaluation Harness: computes standard QPP correlation (EΘ[τQ]) and downstream metric (AP@100, nDCG@10); also computes per-query τΘ and gain ΔP for analysis. Datasets: TREC DL 2019 & 2020 topics with pre-computed runs from 8 rankers.)

- **Critical path:** The pipeline's core logic flows from **QPP Module** → **Fusion Engine** → **Evaluation Harness**. The innovation is in step 2's output feeding step 3. A misconfiguration in the QPP model choice or its interpretation (e.g., expecting higher scores for better performance but a model outputs inverse) will break the entire downstream benefit.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Fusion Method:** CombSUM (score-weighted) generally outperformed RRF (rank-weighted) with QPP weights. Choose based on whether QPP model outputs well-calibrated scores.  
  - **QPP Model Family:** Score-based unsupervised models (NQC, RSD, UEF) consistently outperformed neural supervised models (BERT-QPP, NQA-QPP) in this downstream task. This suggests robustness/calibration may be more important than absolute prediction accuracy for this fusion use-case.  
  - **Metric Target:** Improvements were larger for AP@100 than nDCG@10. The fusion benefit may be sensitive to the depth of the ranked list being optimized.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. **Negative correlation between EΘ[τQ] and fusion gains:** If your best-correlating QPP model (by standard eval) yields worse fusion results than a poorer-correlating one, you are observing the core misalignment. This is expected and indicates you should not rely on standard eval for this application.  
  2. **Low or negative per-query ρ:** If your per-query τΘ vs. gain scatter plot shows no trend or negative trend for your best model, its predictions are not useful for this specific fusion task. Re-evaluate the QPP model's output scale/calibration.  
  3. **Failure to beat unweighted baseline:** If no QPP-weighted fusion outperforms plain CombSUM/RRF, either your QPP model is extremely poor, or the set of rankers Θ is too homogenous (no meaningful preference to predict).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can QPP models be specifically designed to accurately predict the best ranker for each individual query?  
  *Basis:* The authors state: "In future, we plan to develop novel QPP approaches for addressing this fine-grained task of predicting the best ranker on a per-query basis."  
  *Why unresolved:* Current QPP models are optimized for set-level correlation, not per-query ranker preference. The downstream framework shows that standard models do not always excel at this fine-grained task.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* New QPP models trained with a per-query ranker selection objective, evaluated on their ability to improve fusion gains across multiple datasets.

- **Open Question 2:** Can QPP estimates be leveraged to build adaptive retrieval pipelines for RAG systems that dynamically choose retrievers or halt retrieval?  
  *Basis:* The authors mention: "we also plan to apply the QPP estimates for developing an adaptive pipeline for RAG systems."  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper only explores QPP for fusion; its utility in RAG's retrieve-generate loop is unexplored. QPP could help decide when retrieved documents are sufficient for generation.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Implementing a RAG system that uses QPP to control retrieval (e.g., skip retrieval, select retriever) and measuring generation accuracy and efficiency gains.

- **Open Question 3:** Why does standard QPP evaluation (set-level correlation) poorly align with downstream fusion performance, and what model properties drive downstream success?  
  *Basis:* The paper reports Kendall's τ between 0.48 and 0.68 between the two evaluation approaches and moderate per-query correlations (0.28-0.57), indicating misalignment.  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper does not analyze why some QPP models (e.g., RSD) excel downstream despite moderate standard correlation, while others (e.g., NQC) do not. Understanding this could guide better QPP design.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Error analysis: correlate QPP model features (e.g., score variance, robustness) with downstream gains; study query characteristics that moderate the relationship.

- **Open Question 4:** Does the QPP-weighted fusion framework generalize to other IR collections and fusion methods beyond TREC DL and CombSUM/RRF?  
  *Basis:* Experiments are limited to two TREC Deep Learning datasets, eight rankers, and two fusion methods.  
  *Why unresolved:* The diversity of rankers and the nature of the collection may affect the observed improvements. Generalizability to web search, different languages, or more rankers is unknown.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Replicate the study on additional benchmarks (e.g., MS MARCO, Robust04) and with other fusion techniques (e.g., CombMNZ, Borda count) to assess consistency.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is restricted to two TREC Deep Learning datasets and a fixed set of eight rankers, limiting generalizability to other collections or more diverse ranker pools.
- No statistical significance testing is reported for the main gains (e.g., 4.5% AP@100 improvement); it is unclear whether these improvements are robust across query set variations.
- Per-query correlation claim (Pearson ρ ∼0.28–0.57) lacks raw data and confidence intervals; outliers or non-linear relationships could distort the observed trend.
- Critical hyperparameters and implementation details for QPP models (e.g., RSD subsampling, neural model checkpoints) are not fully specified; exact reproduction may require reverse-engineering.
- Only two fusion methods (CombSUM, RRF) were explored; other fusion strategies (e.g., Borda, learned fusion) may yield different conclusions.

## Confidence
- **Cluster 1 (QPP-weighted fusion improves over unweighted):** Medium – empirical improvement clearly stated but depends on correct implementation and lacks significance testing.
- **Cluster 2 (Misalignment between standard correlation and downstream utility):** Medium – moderate Kendall’s τ (0.48–0.68) suggests misalignment, but evidence base is narrow (two datasets, no error bars).
- **Cluster 3 (Per-query correlation between QPP accuracy and fusion gains):** Low – reported Pearson correlation derived from a single figure without statistical validation; high risk of spurious or query-specific effects.
