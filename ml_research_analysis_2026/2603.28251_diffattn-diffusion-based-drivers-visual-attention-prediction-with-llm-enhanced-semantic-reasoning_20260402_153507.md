# DiffAttn: Diffusion-Based Drivers' Visual Attention Prediction with LLM-Enhanced Semantic Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.28251
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28251
- **Reference count**: 37
- **Primary result**: DiffAttn achieves state-of-the-art drivers' visual attention prediction, setting new bests on four datasets with KLD as low as 0.24 (TrafficGaze), CC up to 0.95, SIM up to 0.82, NSS up to 6.11, and AUC‑J up to 0.99, outperforming video‑based, top‑down‑feature‑driven, and other LLM‑enhanced baselines.

## Executive Summary
DiffAttn addresses the challenge of accurately predicting drivers’ visual attention to anticipate latent hazards in complex driving scenes. The approach frames attention prediction as a conditional diffusion denoising process: given a single RGB image, a Swin Transformer encoder extracts multi‑scale features, which are refined by a Feature Fusion Pyramid (FFP). The deepest feature is enhanced through a frozen LLaMA3.2‑1B layer to inject top‑down semantic reasoning. Four independent diffusion U‑Nets, conditioned on the fused features and coarser‑scale predictions, generate saliency maps at multiple scales. Training combines binary cross‑entropy, Kullback–Leibler divergence, and diffusion denoising losses. Evaluated on TrafficGaze, DADA‑2000, BDD‑A, and DrFixD‑rainy, DiffAttn consistently surpasses prior work across standard metrics (KLD, CC, SIM, NSS, AUC‑J). The model uses a Swin‑Base encoder, processes 192×320 images, and is trained with AdamW (lr=1e‑5, batch=18) on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090.

## Method Summary
DiffAttn employs a Swin Transformer (ImageNet‑pretrained) as an encoder that yields four feature maps X₀…X₃ at 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, and 1/32 resolutions. A Feature Fusion Pyramid (FFP) applies Channel Attention (CA) and Cross‑Layer Attention (CLA) to integrate cross‑scale context, producing three refined outputs (out0 at 1/4, out1 at 1/8, out2 at 1/16) and a deep feature `deep = CA(X₃)` at 1/32. The `deep` tensor is reshaped into a sequence, projected to the LLM hidden size, and passed through a frozen 15th layer of LLaMA3.2‑1B; the output is projected back and reshaped, injecting high‑level semantic cues.

Conditional diffusion is performed independently at each scale. For scale *s*, a small U‑Net takes as input a noisy saliency map S_τ (obtained by forward diffusion on the ground‑truth Gaussian‑blurred fixation map) along with a timestep embedding and a conditioning tensor c_s. The conditioning tensor is built by upsampling FFP outputs (and the LLM‑enhanced deep feature) to the resolution of scale *s*, concatenating them, and applying an ELU + convolution; for coarse scales (s<3) the prediction from the next finer scale is also upsampled and added. The diffusion model is trained to predict the added noise ε, using the loss L_DD = ||ε − ε_θ(S_τ, τ, c_s)||². The total training loss aggregates BCE, KLD, and DD losses over all scales with weights λ₁=1, λ₃=0.001, and λ₂ dataset‑dependent. Inference uses a DDIM‑like deterministic sampler with a small number of steps (12–16 depending on dataset) to produce the final high‑resolution saliency map.

## Key Results
- State‑of‑the‑art performance on all four evaluated datasets (TrafficGaze, DADA‑2000, BDD‑A, DrFixD‑rainy).
- Significantly lower KLD and higher CC, SIM, NSS, AUC‑J than video‑based, top‑down‑feature‑driven, and LLM‑enhanced baselines.
- Notable metrics on TrafficGaze: KLD 0.24, CC 0.95, SIM 0.82, NSS 6.11, AUC‑J 0.99.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The conditional diffusion formulation models the inherent uncertainty and multi‑modality of human gaze, allowing the generation of diverse plausible saliency patterns. The multi‑scale diffusion decoders recover both coarse contextual guidance and fine‑grained details. The Feature Fusion Pyramid aggregates cross‑layer information, ensuring that high‑level semantics and low‑level edges are jointly available. Injecting a frozen LLM layer on the deepest feature provides top‑down semantic reasoning, highlighting safety‑critical objects (e.g., pedestrians, traffic signs). The combined loss aligns the generated distribution with the ground‑truth fixation distribution while preserving spatial precision. This synergy of diffusion probabilistic modeling, multi‑scale processing, and semantic reasoning explains the superior results over deterministic or single‑scale approaches.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conditional Diffusion Models**  
  *Why needed*: To capture the stochastic nature of human attention and generate high‑quality saliency maps through iterative denoising.  
  *Quick check*: Verify that training minimizes the denoising loss L_DD and that the forward diffusion process correctly adds noise to ground‑truth saliency.

- **Swin Transformer**  
  *Why needed*: Provides hierarchical feature representations with shifted windows, balancing global context and local detail for multi‑scale encoding.  
  *Quick check*: Ensure the encoder outputs features at the expected down‑sampling rates (1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32) and that the pretrained weights are loaded.

- **Feature Fusion Pyramid (FFP)**  
  *Why needed*: Integrates information across encoder layers via Channel Attention (CA) and Cross‑Layer Attention (CLA), refining features for better conditioning of the diffusion decoders.  
  *Quick check*: Visualize or print the shapes of `out0`, `out1`, `out2`, and `deep` to confirm they match the intended resolutions; test CA/CLA by passing a dummy batch.

- **LLM for Semantic Reasoning**  
  *Why needed*: Leverages large language model knowledge to inject high‑level, safety‑related concepts (e.g., “pedestrian”, “stop sign”) that may not be evident from low‑level features alone.  
  *Quick check*: Load the frozen LLaMA3.2‑1B layer, pass the reshaped `deep` feature, and verify that the LLM output differs from its input (e.g., check norm change).

- **Saliency Map Ground Truth via Gaussian Blur**  
  *Why needed*: Converts sparse fixation points into smooth density maps that match the continuous output of the diffusion model.  
  *Quick check*: Generate a saliency map for a sample image and ensure it is a smooth blur centered on fixation coordinates with plausible spread.

- **Multi‑Scale Loss Weighting**  
  *Why needed*: Balances the influence of each scale’s prediction; λ₂ varies per dataset to adapt to different scene complexities.  
  *Quick check*: Confirm that the total loss decreases during training and that the contribution of KLD (λ₂) is adjustable.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  `Image → Swin Encoder (X0,X1,X2,X3) → FFP (out0,out1,out2,deep) → LLM(deep) → Condition construction (upsampling + concat) → Diffusion Decoders (4 scales) → Predicted Saliency S0`

- **Critical path**:  
  During inference, the input image flows through the encoder, then the FFP. The `deep` feature is processed by the LLM. For each scale (starting from the coarsest), the diffusion decoder runs T_e reverse steps conditioned on the upsampled FFP outputs and, for finer scales, the prediction from the next coarser scale. The finest‑scale output is upsampled to the original image size as the final saliency map.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  *Independent diffusion per scale*: Allows scale‑specific modeling but increases parameter count; a shared decoder would reduce complexity but might sacrifice performance.  
  *Frozen LLM*: Saves training compute and prevents forgetting, but may limit adaptation to driving‑specific semantics; fine‑tuning could help.  
  *Conditioning via concatenation + convolution*: Simple but may not fully exploit cross‑scale interactions; alternative attention‑based fusion could be explored.  
  *Number of diffusion steps*: More steps improve quality but slow inference; the per‑dataset T_e values represent a practical trade‑off.  
  *Loss weighting*: λ₂ is dataset‑dependent, requiring tuning for optimal results.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  *NaN loss*: Often caused by dimension mismatches in condition addition, time embedding broadcast errors, or incorrect reshaping of the LLM input/output.  
  *Poor metric scores (high KLD, low CC)*: Could arise from incorrect Gaussian blur σ, missing FFP connections, LLM layer not loaded or frozen incorrectly, or insufficient training.  
  *Out‑of‑memory*: The LLM layer (≈44 M parameters) is memory‑intensive; reduce batch size, use gradient checkpointing, or employ mixed precision (AMP).

- **Three first experiments**:  
  1. **Single‑scale sanity check**: Train only the diffusion decoder at scale 0 (without FFP/LLM) to verify basic loss convergence and gradient flow.  
  2. **LLM ablation**: Remove the LLM branch and compare validation KLD/CC against the full model to quantify its contribution.  
  3. **Conditioning visualization**: Upsample and plot the condition tensors c_s for a few samples to ensure they contain meaningful structures (e.g., object outlines) and not noise.

## Limitations
- The U‑Net architecture for diffusion decoders is under‑specified (exact depth, channel widths, attention placement, normalization type).  
- The Gaussian blur σ used to generate saliency ground truth is not disclosed; σ likely varies across datasets and must be guessed.  
- The diffusion β schedule (linear? cosine?) and the exact sampler (DDPM vs. DDIM) are not described.  
- For DADA‑2000 the train/val/test splits are not defined; the authors adopt splits from the original paper, which may differ across implementations.  
- LLM integration details (handling of dimension mismatch, positional encoding scheme) are ambiguous, raising doubts about the reproducibility of claimed benefits.
