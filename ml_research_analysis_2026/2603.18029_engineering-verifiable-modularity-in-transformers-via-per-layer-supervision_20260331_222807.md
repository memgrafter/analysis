# Engineering Verifiable Modularity in Transformers via Per-Layer Supervision

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18029
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18029
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: Per-layer supervision increases ablation effect standard deviation from 0.63% to 6.32%, exposing hidden modularity and enabling 4× greater control leverage.

## Executive Summary
Standard Transformers exhibit a "Hydra effect" where ablating critical attention heads yields minimal behavioral change due to distributed redundancy via residual connections, making interpretability illusory. This paper introduces per-layer supervision (PLS), an architectural intervention that adds independent gradient signals at each network depth alongside dual-stream processing and gated attention. This combination exposes hidden modularity: PLS models show ablation effects 5–23 times larger than controls, with near-zero variance in controls versus substantial variance in supervised models. The work enables predictable steering of targeted behaviors like capitalization, demonstrating that modularity can be engineered and verified in Transformers.

## Method Summary
The core method modifies Transformer architecture in three ways: (1) Dual-stream decomposition splits the residual into a frozen token identity stream (xt) and an accumulating contextual stream (xe), with CASCADE mode freezing xt after initialization. (2) Gated attention multiplies each head's output by a learned gate (sigmoid from query) to regularize toward discrete activation. (3) Per-layer supervision (PLS) adds auxiliary cross-entropy losses at every intermediate layer (weighted sum with λ=0.1), forcing each layer to make independent progress. Two models are compared: PLS (with all three modifications) and control C2 (identical but without PLS). Both are trained on grade-school instructional text with identical hyperparameters. Post-training, features are extracted to validate functional specialization, and causal interventions (ablation, scaling) assess modularity on three tasks.

## Key Results
- Ablation effect standard deviation increases from 0.63% (control) to 6.32% (PLS), a 10× rise, indicating exposed sensitivity.
- Identified attention heads produce 5–23× larger behavioral changes when ablated in PLS models.
- Steering (scaling head outputs) yields 4× greater control leverage, with smooth, predictable curves for capitalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Standard Transformers exhibit a "Hydra effect" where ablating a seemingly critical attention head produces minimal behavioral change due to distributed redundancy via the residual stream.  
**Mechanism:** The residual stream functions as a shared communication channel. When one pathway is damaged, other components compensate through residual connections, maintaining output fidelity. This creates silent backup systems.  
**Core assumption:** Gradient-based optimization without specific pressure for modularity will naturally develop redundant, distributed representations because they minimize loss robustly.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "distributed redundancy compensates for damage"; [section 1] "The residual stream functions as a shared communication channel... When one pathway is damaged, others compensate"; corpus weak/absent.  
**Break condition:** Removing or severely constraining residual connections should diminish the Hydra effect, but training may become unstable.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Per-layer supervision (PLS) exposes hidden modularity by providing independent gradient signals at each depth, preventing lazy accumulation of redundant backup circuits.  
**Mechanism:** Standard training only supervises the final layer, allowing early and middle layers to defer corrections and develop overlapping functionality. PLS adds auxiliary losses at every layer, forcing each layer to make distinct, measurable progress toward the target, converting silent compensation into exposed sensitivity.  
**Core assumption:** Each layer has the capacity to make meaningful, independent contributions if incentivized by direct training signal.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "per-layer supervision providing independent gradient signal at each depth"; [section 3.3] "Per-layer supervision encourages each layer to make independent progress... Without it, layers can defer to later corrections, creating redundant pathways"; [section 5.2] Ablation effect std dev jumps from 0.63% to 6.32%.  
**Break condition:** Removing PLS while keeping dual-stream and gated attention should revert to control-like state with low ablation variance.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The combination of frozen dual-stream processing and gated attention with PLS creates conditions where functional specialization emerges, making modularity verifiable.  
**Mechanism:** (1) Dual-stream (CASCADE) separates and freezes token identity (xt) from contextual accumulation (xe), eliminating a primary channel for hidden redundancy and forcing context processing into a single stream where specialization is more detectable. (2) Gated attention modulates head output via learned gates, regularizing toward discrete activation patterns (heads specialize or stay inactive). (3) PLS provides training pressure that, within constrained dual-stream architecture, pushes different tasks to route through different head groups.  
**Core assumption:** Architectural constraints that limit compensation channels (frozen xt) combined with regularization (gating) and training pressure (PLS) will induce functional specialization rather than distributed redundancy.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "dual-stream processing... per-layer supervision... gated attention"; [section 3.1-3.4] details each component's role; [section 5.3] shows task-specific routing ("diagonal sparsity") in PLS vs entangled routing in controls; corpus weak/absent.  
**Break condition:** Removing either dual-stream (using standard residual updates) or gated attention should reduce clarity of task-specific routing and decrease ablation effect magnitude/variance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Gradient Starvation (Pezeshki et al., 2021)**  
  Why needed: Explains why standard training naturally yields redundant representations—once a dominant feature minimizes loss, gradients for alternative features vanish, leading to lazy, overlapping solutions (substrate of Hydra effect).  
  Quick check: If a network can solve a task using multiple similar features, which features will SGD preferentially develop, and why does this promote redundancy?

- **Concept: Causal Abstraction (Geiger et al., 2021)**  
  Why needed: Provides the validation framework. A component is causally responsible if intervening on it changes target behavior. Distinguishes accessible information (probing) from used information (causal role).  
  Quick check: What is the difference between finding that an attention head correlates with a linguistic property (probing) and showing that ablating that head changes model behavior (causal validation)?

- **Concept: Permutation-Invariant Feature Design**  
  Why needed: Engineered features must capture computational dynamics independent of token identity or absolute position. The three design invariances (Token Identity, Permutation, Topology Preservation) are critical for discovering structure orthogonal to vocabulary.  
  Quick check: Why would clustering raw activations likely rediscover token-type clusters, and how do the paper's invariances prevent this?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Standard Transformer -> Dual-Stream (CASCADE) -> Gated Attention -> Per-Layer Supervision
- **Critical path**: Start with a standard transformer. Add dual-stream separation. Implement CASCADE (freeze xt). Add gated attention to heads. Finally, add PLS auxiliary losses. The control model (C2) is identical but without PLS.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Frozen token stream vs. expressivity: Limiting xt forces all context integration into xe, potentially reducing capacity but making computation in xe more traceable.
  - PLS weight (λ) vs. final performance: High λ may force early layers onto suboptimal intermediates, harming final accuracy; paper uses λ=0.1.
  - Feature dimensionality vs. clustering noise: High-dimensional structural features (163D) risk noise; PCA to 30D used for HDBSCAN.
- **Failure signatures**:
  1. Hydra effect persists: Ablation std dev remains low (~0.6%) in supervised model.
  2. Feature vocabulary leakage: ARI between engineered and raw activation clusters >0.1.
  3. No task-specific routing: Ablation effect matrix shows smeared effects, not diagonal sparsity.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Replicate ablation sensitivity: Ablate identified head groups on capitalization, gender, Winograd tasks; compute Δ. Expect PLS σ(Δ) ~6%, control σ(Δ) ~0.6%.
  2. Feature clustering validation: Cluster 10k tokens using Tier 2 features. Compute ARI vs. raw activation clustering; expect ~0.008. Inspect clusters for functional coherence.
  3. Steering curve construction: Scale entity head outputs (0× to 1.5×) for capitalization task; plot probability vs. scale. Expect 4× greater slope range than control.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Question**: Do modularity signatures persist in larger models (e.g., >1B parameters)?  
   Basis: Paper explicitly notes limitation of 6-layer, 6-head models.  
   Why unresolved: Larger models might develop stronger compensation; supervision signal may weaken.  
   Evidence needed: Train models of increasing scale with/without PLS; compare ablation variance and routing.

2. **Question**: Does per-layer supervision produce modularity on natural, diverse corpora?  
   Basis: Paper acknowledges grade-school text may not reflect natural language complexity.  
   Why unresolved: Additional noise and tasks could alter effect or introduce confounds.  
   Evidence needed: Train PLS and control on large heterogeneous corpus (e.g., The Pile); evaluate ablation variance across tasks.

3. **Question**: How do engineered relational features compare to sparse autoencoder (SAE) features in revealing causal circuit structure?  
   Basis: Authors note no direct comparison; features capture dynamics vs. semantics.  
   Why unresolved: Unclear if SAE features predict ablation sensitivity or overlap with relational clusters.  
   Evidence needed: Apply SAEs to same model, derive features, cluster, correlate with relational feature clusters; test prediction of ablation outcomes.

## Limitations
- Exact training corpus composition, preprocessing, and size are unspecified.
- Precise test cases for capitalization (8) and gender (16) tasks are not provided; analogies may not match difficulty.
- Head identification for "entity" and "anchor" lacks deterministic criteria; indices used in paper not justified.
- Training duration and auxiliary loss scheduling (e.g., decay) are unclear; may affect modularity induction.
- Implementation details of gated attention (e.g., gate computation scope) are ambiguous.

## Confidence
- Hydra effect in standard transformers: Medium (plausible mechanism but direct replication blocked by missing protocols).
- PLS exposure of modularity: Medium (quantitative results clear but depend on unresolved specifics).
- Dual-stream + gating enabling specialization: High (architectural description sufficient if implemented correctly).
