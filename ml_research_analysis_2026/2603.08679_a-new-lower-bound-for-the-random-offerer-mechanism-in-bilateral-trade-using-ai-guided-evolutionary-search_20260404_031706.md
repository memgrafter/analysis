# A New Lower Bound for the Random Offerer Mechanism in Bilateral Trade using AI-Guided Evolutionary Search

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08679
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08679
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** AI-guided evolutionary search discovers a seller distribution yielding a worst-case approximation ratio of 2.0749 for the Random Offerer mechanism, improving the previous lower bound of ~2.02.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the open problem of finding the worst-case approximation ratio for the Random Offerer (RO) mechanism in bilateral trade. Instead of analytical derivation, the authors employ an AI-guided evolutionary framework called AlphaEvolve to synthetically search for pathological value distributions. By fixing the buyer distribution to the Discrete Equal Revenue distribution and evolving the seller's cumulative distribution function (CDF) via program synthesis, the search discovers a novel, non-intuitive seller structure: a mixture of power laws with a sinusoidally modulated exponent. This instance achieves an approximation ratio of 2.0749, demonstrating a larger efficiency gap between the RO mechanism and the first-best benchmark than previously known.

## Method Summary
The method reformulates the worst-case instance search as a program synthesis problem. An LLM-based agent iteratively mutates Python code that defines the seller's discrete CDF. Each candidate is evaluated on a large discrete domain (H=20000) by computing the exact approximation ratio ρ = GFT_FB / GFT_RO. To ensure numerical rigor, probabilities are rounded to multiples of ε=10⁻¹⁵, enabling exact integer arithmetic. The fitness function is strictly the achieved ratio, driving evolution toward higher values. Crucially, the buyer distribution is fixed to the Discrete Equal Revenue distribution, which focuses the search and accelerates discovery compared to jointly evolving both sides.

## Key Results
- Discovered a seller CDF: mixture weight w=0.2, base exponent 4.0, and a modulated exponent `α_eff(z) = 0.15 + 0.05 sin(2πz)` where `z=(m+1)/(H+1)`.
- Achieved approximation ratio ρ ≈ 2.0749 on the discretized instance.
- First-Best Gains from Trade (GFT_FB) ≈ 1.2322, Random Offerer GFT (GFT_RO) ≈ 0.5939.
- Improved upon the prior best lower bound of approximately 2.02.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** AI-guided evolutionary search can discover non-intuitive functional forms for value distributions that widen the worst-case efficiency gap.
- **Mechanism:** The search treats distribution code as evolvable programs. An LLM agent proposes mutations to a Python function returning the seller's CDF. Each candidate is evaluated by computing ρ on a discrete grid with high-precision arithmetic. Fitness-driven selection favors higher ρ.
- **Core assumption:** The program space includes expressive structures like sinusoidal modulation, and the fitness landscape has a peak corresponding to a worst-case instance.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the framework identified a new worst-case instance with ρ=2.0749. Section 4.2 describes the iterative mutation and fitness-defined loop. The corpus shows neighbor papers focus on regret bounds, not AI-driven discovery.
- **Break condition:** If LLM mutations fail to generate or sustain sinusoidal forms, or if evaluation has hidden numerical instabilities creating artificial peaks.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A seller distribution as a power-law mixture with a sinusoidally modulated exponent creates a larger efficiency gap than smooth power laws.
- **Mechanism:** The structure `F_s(m) = w·z^{α_eff(z)} + (1-w)·z^{4}` likely exploits regions where both sub-mechanisms (Seller-Offering, Buyer-Offering) are weak, while keeping First-Best relatively high.
- **Core assumption:** Sinusoidal modulation specifically disrupts optimal pricing in both sub-mechanisms in a correlated way a single exponent cannot.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 5.2.1 defines the mixture and modulation parameters (w=0.2, α2=4, a1_base=0.15, a1_amp=0.05, a1_freq=2.0). Section 5.1 reports component GFTs yielding ρ≈2.0749, improving on ~2.02. No direct corpus evidence.
- **Break condition:** If small parameter perturbations cause ρ to drop significantly, the peak may be narrow and non-robust.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Fixing the buyer to the Discrete Equal Revenue distribution catalyzes search by reducing the space and focusing pressure on the seller side.
- **Mechanism:** Holding the buyer's survival function `Pr(b≥m)=1/m` constant avoids the combinatorial difficulty of a two-sided search. Evolution solely optimizes the seller CDF against this fixed buyer.
- **Core assumption:** A worst-case pair exists where the buyer is exactly (or very close to) the Discrete ER distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 follows Babaioff et al. by fixing the buyer to Discrete ER. Section 4.4 notes simultaneous evolution was slower and did not surpass 2.07. Neighbor papers study fixed-price settings, not this fixed-buyer approach.
- **Break condition:** If future joint/alternating evolution surpasses ρ=2.0749, the fixed-buyer constraint prematurely capped the achievable ratio.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bayesian Incentive Compatibility (BIC) & Budget Balance (BB)**  
  *Why needed:* The RO mechanism is studied because it is simple, BIC, IR, and BB. These constraints explain why its GFT can be far from first-best (Myerson-Satterthwaite impossibility) and define the approximation ratio.  
  *Quick check:* What trade-off forces does BIC impose on the seller's optimal reserve price in the Seller-Offering sub-mechanism?

- **Gains from Trade (GFT) & First-Best Benchmark**  
  *Why needed:* The objective is maximizing ρ = GFT_FB / GFT_RO. Accurate GFT computation requires understanding efficient allocation (trade iff b≥s) versus the RO mechanism's probabilistic pricing.  
  *Quick check:* For discrete distributions, how do you compute expected First-Best GFT from the PMFs of seller costs and buyer values?

- **Power Law Distributions & Tail Behavior**  
  *Why needed:* The worst-case seller distribution is based on power laws (z^α). Exponent α controls tail heaviness, affecting how often extreme costs/values appear and the surplus captured by sub-mechanisms.  
  *Quick check:* If a seller's cost distribution has a heavier tail (smaller α), how might that impact Seller-Offering GFT relative to First-Best GFT?

- **Program Synthesis & Evolutionary Search**  
  *Why needed:* AlphaEvolve uses program synthesis to explore a broad space of distribution structures (like sinusoidal modulation) beyond handcrafted parametric families.  
  *Quick check:* What are the risks of evolving full code (vs. tuning parameters) in a fitness-driven loop?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** AlphaEvolve Agent (LLM coder) -> Fitness Evaluator (GFT calculator) -> Search Loop (elitism/selection)
- **Critical path:** Code mutation generates seller CDF -> Rounding to integer PMF -> For each (s,b) compute FB surplus and RO-optimal prices -> Aggregate GFTs -> Compute ρ -> Selection.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Precision vs Computation:* Larger H (20000) allows finer distributions but makes evaluation O(H²). Rounding to ε ensures exact rational comparison but discretizes the continuous problem.  
  - *Search Focus vs Generality:* Fixing the buyer accelerates discovery but may miss a globally worst pair where the buyer is not ER. Evolving both sides is more general but combinatorially harder.  
  - *Code Space vs Parameter Space:* Evolving full code enables novel structures (sinusoidal modulation) but risks invalid CDFs (non-monotonic), requiring safety checks.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - ρ plateaus near 2.02 without improvement.  
  - Evolved code produces non-monotonic CDFs or probabilities outside [0,1].  
  - Fitness scores become noisy/inconsistent across runs, indicating numerical instability.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline validation:** Run with uniform seller distribution against fixed ER buyer. Expect ρ near known bounds (~2.0). Confirms evaluator correctness.  
  2. **Evolve simple power law:** Initialize agent with `z^alpha` seller (alpha tunable). Verify if evolution can rediscover a known worse-case alpha from prior work (e.g., alpha≈0.2).  
  3. **Introduce modulation:** Seed population with code containing `sin` in exponent (e.g., `alpha_eff = base + amp * sin(freq * z)`). Monitor if evolution refines `amp` and `freq` toward discovered values (0.05, 2.0).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What is the exact worst-case approximation ratio ρ* for the Random Offerer mechanism?  
  *Basis:* The paper provides a new lower bound of 2.0749, leaving the supremum unknown.  
  *Why unresolved:* No matching upper bound exists; the true supremum might exceed 2.0749.  
  *Resolution evidence:* A tight upper bound matching the lower bound or a substantially higher lower bound.

- **Open Question 2:** For the discovered mixture of modulated power laws seller distribution, what is the exact approximation ratio in the continuous (unrounded) setting?  
  *Basis:* The 2.0749 bound applies only to the rounded discrete instance; the continuous case is open.  
  *Why unresolved:* Rounding could alter the ratio; the continuous distribution might yield a different value.  
  *Resolution evidence:* High-precision numerical evaluation with error guarantees or an analytic derivation.

- **Open Question 3:** Can alternating optimization of seller and buyer distributions lead to a higher approximation ratio?  
  *Basis:* Simultaneous evolution was slower and did not surpass 2.07; the paper suggests alternating as future work.  
  *Why unresolved:* It is unknown whether alternating could escape local optima and achieve ρ > 2.0749.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Implement an alternating evolutionary search and observe convergence to a pair with ratio exceeding 2.0749.

- **Open Question 4:** Why does sinusoidal modulation in the exponent improve the ratio? Is this structure necessary, or can other forms achieve even higher ratios?  
  *Basis:* The AI discovered a non-intuitive sinusoidal modulation that human analysis might overlook, implying explaining its advantage is an open challenge.  
  *Why unresolved:* No theoretical analysis explains why this structure yields a higher ratio or whether it is optimal.  
  *Resolution evidence:* A theoretical characterization of the worst-case seller distribution (e.g., solving the optimization problem or proving necessity of oscillatory exponents).

## Limitations
- AlphaEvolve hyperparameters (population size, mutation ops, LLM prompts) are not described, preventing replication of the discovery process.
- The ratio 2.0749 is for a discretized instance (H=20000, rounding ε=10⁻¹⁵); the continuous case remains unknown.
- The fixed Discrete Equal Revenue buyer may not be truly worst-case; joint/alternating evolution could surpass 2.0749.
- Sinusoidal modulation parameters appear fine-tuned; robustness to perturbations is unverified.

## Confidence
- *AI-guided search finds worst-case:* Medium (single run, opaque search dynamics).
- *Specific seller distribution yields ρ≈2.0749 (discrete):* High (exact arithmetic, reproducible instance).
- *Fixed ER buyer catalyzes search:* Medium (joint evolution was lower/slower but not exhaustive).
- *Sinusoidal modulation is key to improvement:* Low (no theoretical justification; could be an artifact of search).
- *Continuous ratio > 2.0749:* Low (rounding could inflate or deflate; unknown).
