# A Quantitative Characterization of Forgetting in Post-Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12163  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12163  
- **Reference Count:** 40  
- **Primary Result:** Forward-KL training on new-only data inevitably causes mass forgetting (old weight β* = 0), while reverse-KL preserves mass (β* = α) with old-mean drift exponentially small in mode separation; replay must alter the training distribution for forward-KL but only stabilizes minibatches for reverse-KL.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of quantifying forgetting during post-training of generative models. It introduces a clean abstraction: a two-component Gaussian mixture representing old and new behaviors. Using this, it analyzes forward-KL (supervised fine-tuning) and reverse-KL (regularized RL) objectives, showing they induce fundamentally different dynamics. Forward-KL trained solely on new data collapses the old component’s mixture weight to zero, with the gradient driven by exponentially small leakage probabilities. Reverse-KL exactly preserves the target mixture weight and, if the old mean is initially correct, its drift is controlled by the overlap between modes (Bhattacharyya coefficient). The paper extends these insights to replay strategies and three near-on-policy algorithms (SDFT, TTT-Discover, OAPL), deriving conditions under which they avoid catastrophic forgetting.

## Method Summary
The core method is a theoretical analysis using a parametric two-mode Gaussian mixture model with equal covariances. The old and new data distributions are \( p_o = \mathcal{N}(\mu_o, \Sigma) \) and \( p_n = \mathcal{N}(\mu_n, \Sigma) \), with target mixture \( p_\alpha = \alpha p_o + (1-\alpha)p_n \). The learner model is \( q_{\beta,m}(y) = \beta \phi_\Sigma(y; m_o) + (1-\beta)\phi_\Sigma(y; m_n) \). For forward-KL SFT, the objective is \( \text{KL}(p_n \| q_\beta) \); for reverse-KL RL, it is \( \text{KL}(q_{\beta,m} \| p_\alpha) \). The authors derive closed-form gradients and population optima, express responsibilities and misassignments in terms of normal CDFs, and bound these quantities using the Mahalanobis separation \( \delta = \|\mu_n - \mu_o\|_{\Sigma^{-1}} \) and the Bhattacharyya coefficient \( \text{BC} = \exp(-\delta^2/8) \). Replay variants are modeled by mixing old samples either into the data distribution (numerator) or the model/behavior distribution (denominator/sampling). For near-on-policy methods, the paper maps algorithmic parameters (e.g., demonstrator strength, KL anchor) to effective mixture weights and derives stability conditions.

## Key Results
- Forward-KL with new-only data forces the old mixture weight to zero; the gradient \( \dot\phi = \mathbb{E}_{p_n}[r_o(Y)] - \beta \) is negative for all \( \beta>0 \) because \( \mathbb{E}_{p_n}[r_o] \le \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\beta/(1-\beta)}\exp(-\delta^2/8) \). Only numerator replay (old samples in training data) changes the population optimum to \( \beta^* = \lambda \).
- Reverse-KL preserves mass exactly (\( \beta^* = \alpha \)). When \( m_o = \mu_o \), the gradient on \( m_o \) is \( \nabla_{m_o} L = \beta \Sigma^{-1} \big( \varepsilon_q (m_n-\mu_o) - \varepsilon_p (\mu_n-\mu_o) \big) \), where misassignment probabilities \( \varepsilon_q, \varepsilon_p \) are bounded by \( \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(1-\beta)/\beta}\,\text{BC} \) and \( \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}\,\text{BC} \), respectively—exponentially small in \( \delta^2 \). Locally the objective satisfies a Polyak–Lojasiewicz condition, yielding exponential convergence.
- For SDFT (with demonstrator strength \( \lambda>0 \)), the old mass remains nonzero and total drift is finite (exponentially small if modes separated). TTT-Discover’s entropic reward induces mass collapse unless its KL anchor \( \lambda_{\text{ref}} \) exceeds a critical threshold \( \lambda_{\text{crit}} \); however, its old-mean drift is still overlap-controlled. OAPL can preserve only modes already present in its frozen reference policy, and its drift is also exponentially small in mode separation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Forward-KL Drives Mass Forgetting via Gradient Incentive on Mixture Weight
- **Claim:** Training with forward-KL on new-only data inevitably collapses the old mixture weight to zero, even if the model perfectly represents both components.
- **Mechanism:** The loss \( L_{\text{SFT}}(\beta) = \text{KL}(p_n \| q_\beta) \) is strictly increasing in \( \beta \). The logit gradient is \( \frac{d}{d\phi} L_{\text{SFT}}(\sigma(\phi)) = \beta - \mathbb{E}_{p_n}[r_o(Y)] \). Because new data never samples the old mode, \( \mathbb{E}_{p_n}[r_o(Y)] \) is bounded by \( \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\beta/(1-\beta)}\exp(-\delta^2/8) \), which is exponentially small for separated modes. Hence the gradient is effectively negative, pushing \( \beta \) downward.
- **Core assumption:** Data distribution is purely \( p_n \); the old component in the model is already correct (\( m_o = \mu_o \)); modes are distinct and have limited overlap.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Theorem 2.1; Equation (2.4); analogy to catastrophic forgetting in FedAvg under drift (arxiv:2601.13456).
- **Break condition:** Numerator replay (mixing old samples into the data distribution) shifts the optimum to \( \beta^* = \lambda \).

### Mechanism 2: Reverse-KL Aligns with Target Mixture and Limits Drift via Overlap-Gated Gradients
- **Claim:** Reverse-KL with target \( p_\alpha \) preserves old mass (\( \beta^* = \alpha \)) and, if \( m_o = \mu_o \), the old-mean drift is driven solely by exponentially small misassignment probabilities.
- **Mechanism:** The global optimum is \( (\beta^*, m_n^*) = (\alpha, \mu_n) \). The gradient w.r.t. \( m_o \) decomposes as \( \nabla_{m_o} L = \beta \Sigma^{-1} \big( \varepsilon_q (m_n-\mu_o) - \varepsilon_p (\mu_n-\mu_o) \big) \), where \( \varepsilon_q = \mathbb{E}_{p_o}[1 - r_o(Y)] \) (model misassignment) and \( \varepsilon_p = \mathbb{E}_{p_o}[1 - s_o(Y)] \) (target misassignment). Both are bounded by \( \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(1-\beta)/\beta}\,\text{BC}(p_o,p_n) \) and \( \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}\,\text{BC} \), respectively, with \( \text{BC} = \exp(-\delta^2/8) \). Thus drift is overlap-gated and exponentially small.
- **Core assumption:** Target mixture retains old mode (\( \alpha>0 \)); model contains correct old component; equal covariances; well-separated modes.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Theorem 2.3; Theorem 2.2; alignment with trust-region methods (arxiv:2602.02417).
- **Break condition:** If \( m_o \neq \mu_o \) initially, or modes heavily overlap (small \( \delta \)), drift can be significant.

### Mechanism 3: Replay Modifies Population Optimum Only for Forward-KL; Stabilizes Minibatches for Reverse-KL
- **Claim:** Replay (mixing old samples) has distinct effects: for forward-KL it must change the training distribution to avoid forgetting; for reverse-KL it leaves the population objective unchanged but prevents finite-batch old-mode starvation via bounded importance sampling.
- **Mechanism:**
  - *Forward-KL*: The objective depends on the data distribution. New-only data gives \( \beta^*=0 \). Denominator replay (mixing into model) reparameterizes the family but still minimizes at \( \beta^*=0 \) (with apparent retention only due to a hard floor \( \lambda \)). Numerator replay changes the data to \( (1-\lambda)p_n + \lambda p_o \), shifting optimum to \( \beta^* = \lambda \).
  - *Reverse-KL*: The population objective does not involve sampling. However, minibatches drawn solely from \( q_{\beta,m} \) may contain zero old samples when \( \beta \) is small, causing stochastic forgetting. Sampling from a replay mixture \( b_{\lambda,\beta,m} = (1-\lambda)q_{\beta,m} + \lambda p_o \) yields an unbiased estimator with importance weights bounded by \( 1/(1-\lambda) \). Moreover, the probability a minibatch of size \( N \) contains at least \( \lambda N/2 \) old samples is \( \ge 1 - \exp(-\lambda N/8) \), independent of \( \beta \).
- **Core assumption:** Replay rate \( \lambda \in (0,1) \); gradient estimator correctly approximates the reverse-KL gradient (e.g., via reparameterization).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Lemma 2.2 (numerator vs denominator replay); Lemma 2.3 (unbiasedness, bounded weights, Chernoff bound); note that improper replay (denominator for forward-KL) does not prevent forgetting (arxiv:2506.04377).
- **Break condition:** For forward-KL, if only denominator replay is used, collapse still occurs. For reverse-KL, if no replay mixture is used and \( \beta \) is small, minibatch starvation leads to stochastic forgetting.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Two-component Gaussian mixture model**  
  Why needed: The entire analysis reduces continual post-training to learning a mixture with two components representing old and new behaviors. This allows exact gradients and responsibilities.  
  Quick check: What does the mixture weight \( \beta \) represent in the learner model \( q_{\beta} \)? How is the responsibility \( r_o(y) \) defined?

- **Concept: Forward vs. reverse KL divergence**  
  Why needed: Forward-KL corresponds to supervised fine-tuning on new data, reverse-KL to KL-regularized policy improvement. Their gradient signs regarding \( \beta \) are opposite.  
  Quick check: Why does \( \text{KL}(p_n \| q_\beta) \) increase with \( \beta \) while \( \text{KL}(q_\beta \| p_\alpha) \) is minimized at \( \beta = \alpha \)?

- **Concept: Bhattacharyya coefficient and mode overlap**  
  Why needed: Overlap between old and new modes determines misassignment probabilities that drive drift in reverse-KL. For equal-covariance Gaussians, \( \text{BC} = \exp(-\delta^2/8) \).  
  Quick check: How does the Mahalanobis separation \( \delta \) affect the probability that an old-mode sample is assigned to the new component?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map (plain text):**
  Old distribution \( p_o \) and New distribution \( p_n \) -> Target mixture \( p_\alpha = \alpha p_o + (1-\alpha)p_n \)
  Learner model \( q_{\beta,m_n} = \beta \phi_\Sigma(\cdot; m_o) + (1-\beta) \phi_\Sigma(\cdot; m_n) \) -> Objectives:
    * SFT: \( L_{\text{SFT}} = \text{KL}(p_n \| q_\beta) \) (potentially with numerator replay data \( \tilde p_\lambda = (1-\lambda)p_n + \lambda p_o \))
    * RL: \( L_{\text{RL}} = \text{KL}(q_{\beta,m} \| p_\alpha) \) (with optional replay sampling distribution \( b_{\lambda,\beta,m} = (1-\lambda)q_{\beta,m} + \lambda p_o \))
  Near-on-policy algorithms (SDFT, TTT-Discover, OAPL) map to modified dynamics over \( (\beta, m_n) \).

- **Critical path:**
  1. Define \( (\mu_o, \Sigma), (\mu_n, \Sigma), \alpha \) and initial \( m_o = \mu_o \).
  2. Choose training paradigm (SFT vs RL) and corresponding objective.
  3. If SFT and retention needed: set numerator replay rate \( \lambda \) and mix old data; otherwise expect \( \beta^* = 0 \).
  4. If RL: optionally use replay mixture \( b_{\lambda,\beta,m_n} \) for minibatch sampling to avoid old-mode starvation.
  5. Optimize \( \beta \) (and \( m_n \) for RL) via gradient descent/SGD, tracking \( \beta(t) \) and \( \|m_o - \mu_o\| \).
  6. For SDFT/TTT-Discover/OAPL: ensure hyperparameters (demonstrator strength \( \lambda \), KL anchor \( \lambda_{\text{ref}} \), reference policy \( q_0 \)) satisfy derived thresholds (e.g., \( \lambda_{\text{ref}} > \lambda_{\text{crit}} \)).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Forward-KL (SFT)*: Simple likelihood training; but mass forgetting inevitable unless old data explicitly included. Including old data (numerator replay) dilutes new signal by \( \lambda \).
  - *Reverse-KL (RL)*: Naturally preserves old mass and yields local updates; but requires careful KL estimation and may suffer from old-mode starvation in finite batches. Replay mixture with bounded importance weights stabilizes minibatches without altering population target.
  - *SDFT vs. TTT-Discover vs. OAPL*: SDFT uses a demonstrator to anchor the teacher, guaranteeing finite total drift if \( \lambda>0 \). TTT-Discover balances reward-driven discovery with a KL anchor; insufficient anchor causes mass collapse. OAPL can only preserve modes already present in its frozen reference \( q_0 \).

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Mass forgetting:** Under forward-KL with new-only data, \( \beta(t) \to 0 \). Monitor \( \beta \); rapid drop indicates collapse.
  - **Old-mean drift:** In reverse-KL, if \( m_o \) drifts steadily despite initial \( m_o=\mu_o \), likely high overlap (small \( \delta \)) or poor initialization. Drift per step is \( O(\exp(-\delta^2/8)) \).
  - **TTT-Discover mass collapse:** Compute \( \lambda_{\text{crit}}^{\text{(new)}} \) from (3.14). If \( \lambda_{\text{ref}} \le \lambda_{\text{crit}} \) and new reward is higher, \( \beta^* = 0 \). Track \( \beta \); collapse indicates anchor too weak.
  - **OAPL inability to recover old mode:** If frozen reference \( q_0 \) has \( \beta_0 = 0 \), the OAPL target will also have zero old mass (Equation 3.18). Ensure \( \beta_0 > 0 \).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce Mechanism 1 (mass forgetting).** Set \( \mu_o=0, \mu_n=\Delta, \Sigma=I_d \) with \( d=1 \) or 2. Fix \( m_o=\mu_o, m_n=\mu_n \). Compute \( L_{\text{SFT}}(\beta) \) analytically or via MC. Vary \( \delta \) (e.g., 1.0, 2.0, 3.0). Simulate gradient flow on logit \( \dot\phi = \mathbb{E}_{p_n}[r_o(Y)] - \beta \) and plot \( \beta(t) \). Expect \( \beta \to 0 \) in all cases, faster with larger \( \delta \).
  2. **Reproduce Mechanism 2 (overlap-controlled drift).** With \( m_o=\mu_o \), vary \( m_n \) and \( \beta \). Compute empirical gradient \( \nabla_{m_o} L_{\text{RL}} \) via numeric differentiation or exact formula. Compare with bound from Theorem 2.3. Vary \( \delta \) and measure \( \|\nabla_{m_o} L\| \); should decay exponentially in \( \delta^2 \).
  3. **Test replay interaction (Mechanism 3).** *Forward-KL:* Compare three settings: (a) new-only data, (b) numerator replay (\( \lambda=0.2 \) mixed into data), (c) denominator replay (model becomes \( (1-\lambda)q_\beta + \lambda p_o \)). Track \( \beta(t) \). Expect (a) \( \beta\to0 \), (b) \( \beta\to\lambda \), (c) \( \beta\to0 \) but with apparent floor due to reparameterization. *Reverse-KL:* Simulate SGD with minibatches drawn purely from \( q_{\beta,m_n} \) vs. from replay mixture \( b_{\lambda,\beta,m_n} \) with \( \lambda=0.1 \). When \( \beta=0.01 \), pure on-policy will frequently yield batches with zero old samples; replay mixture stabilizes. Validate the Chernoff bound \( 1 - \exp(-\lambda N/8) \) on probability of at least \( \lambda N/2 \) old samples.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Under what precise conditions (e.g., reward structure, KL anchor strength \( \lambda_{\text{ref}} \), mode separation \( \delta \)) does TTT-Discover avoid mass forgetting of old modes in multi-modal or non-Gaussian settings?
- **Basis in paper:** The analysis derives an explicit critical threshold only for the equal-covariance Gaussian case with piecewise-constant rewards. Real-world applications involve richer reward landscapes, non-Gaussian data, and many modes. The condition for mass retention in such settings is not characterized.
- **Why unresolved:** No generalization beyond the Gaussian two-mode setting is provided. The mapping from algorithmic parameters to mixture dynamics may not extend straightforwardly.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend analysis to general log-concave or mixture-of-experts settings; derive tight bounds on optimal \( \beta^* \) for arbitrary reward functions; identify a universal measure (beyond BC) predicting when the KL anchor overcomes reward-driven mode-seeking.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How robust is the reverse-KL's overlap-controlled drift guarantee against approximate (rather than exact) on-policy sampling and finite-batch stochasticity in deep generative models?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper shows pure on-policy reverse-KL can suffer old-mode starvation in finite batches and proposes replay-mixing to guarantee old-mode visibility. However, the analysis assumes exact gradient expectations and a model family that contains the target exactly.
- **Why unresolved:** Practical LLMs use biased estimators (REINFORCE, PPO) and function approximation. The interaction between estimator bias, optimization noise, and drift bounds is not analyzed. The gap between the mixture abstraction and real neural networks is unaddressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis of SGD (or Adam) for reverse-KL objectives in overparameterized settings, accounting for estimator bias and representational error. Empirical large-scale experiments measuring drift across model scales and architectures.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the mixture-model insights be leveraged to design new post-training algorithms that provably guarantee both retention of old behaviors and efficient acquisition of new ones, with explicit quantifiable trade-offs?
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion explicitly proposes designing "principled post-training algorithms that explicitly balance exploration of new behaviors with retention of past capabilities using theoretically grounded sampling or memory mechanisms."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper provides a diagnostic framework but no new unified algorithm. Existing near-on-policy methods have limitations (SDFT needs demonstrations, TTT-Discover needs careful tuning, OAPL limited by frozen reference). A method combining the best properties with theoretical guarantees remains an open design problem.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Propose a new algorithm (e.g., adaptive reverse-KL with replay) and prove under the mixture model (or extensions) that it achieves both bounded mass forgetting and exponentially small drift. Validate on standard continual learning benchmarks against existing methods.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** To what extent do the Gaussian mixture results (exponential decay of drift with \( \delta^2 \)) predict catastrophic forgetting behavior in large language models post-trained on new tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** The entire analysis rests on the two-mode Gaussian abstraction. The authors argue it captures the essence of forgetting by representing old and new behaviors as distinct modes. However, real LLM behaviors are complex and may intertwine in ways not captured by symmetric Gaussians.
- **Why unresolved:** No empirical validation links the theoretical \( \delta^2 \) exponent to actual forgetting in LLMs. The abstraction may oversimplify; real "modes" are not symmetric, and the model's representations may not separate cleanly. The paper acknowledges this as a key limitation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical studies defining meaningful "modes" in LLM output space (e.g., via clustering or task-specific prompts), measuring a geometric separation metric (e.g., embedding-space distance), and correlating it with observed forgetting after various post-training methods. Alternatively, richer theoretical models (e.g., hierarchical mixtures) deriving analogous drift measures estimable from real model trajectories.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How does the stochastic optimization process (e.g., minibatch size, learning rate, optimizer) interact with the population-level findings on forgetting? Can optimization noise itself cause forgetting not captured by population analysis?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors note that for reverse-KL, replay prevents finite-batch old-mode starvation but do not analyze other hyperparameters. The paper focuses on population objectives and simple gradient flow dynamics.
- **Why unresolved:** Real training uses adaptive optimizers, learning rate schedules, and finite batches. The interaction between optimization noise and geometric quantities (\( \delta \), BC) is not characterized. Stochasticity could cause drift beyond the overlap-gated signal.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Stochastic approximation theory analyzing SGD (or Adam) for reverse-KL objectives, showing that under appropriate stepsize and batch size conditions, iterates stay within the local PL region and accumulated drift remains bounded by overlap quantities. Empirical ablations varying batch size and learning rate while measuring drift in the Gaussian mixture setting.

## Limitations
- The two-Gaussian mixture abstraction with equal covariances is a significant simplification; real LLM loss landscapes are unknown and likely more complex.
- Mapping practical algorithms (SFT, PPO, SDFT, etc.) to the mixture parameters (\( \alpha, \lambda, \eta \)) is not rigorously derived; the correspondence may be approximate or mismatched.
- The local Polyak–Lojasiewicz condition and required step-size for exponential convergence are not specified for stochastic training; the analysis assumes idealized gradient flow.
- Importance-weight estimators for reverse-KL replay may be biased in high dimensions; the analysis assumes exact expectations.

## Confidence
- **Forward-KL collapse:** High (Theorem 2.1 proof is solid).  
- **Reverse-KL mass preservation (population optimum):** High (Theorem 2.2).  
- **Reverse-KL overlap-gated drift (Gaussian case):** High (Theorem 2.3, explicit bounds).  
- **Replay mechanism distinction:** High within the mixture model (Lemmas 2.2, 2.3).  
- **SDFT/TTT-Discover/OAPL stability:** Medium (additional assumptions; mapping to real algorithmic hyperparameters is not unique).
