# Mixture-of-Experts as Soft Clustering: A Dual Jacobian-PCA Spectral Geometry Perspective

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.11616
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11616
- **Reference count**: 33
- **Primary result**: MoE routing induces functional decomposition into low-overlap expert subspaces, reduces local sensitivity (flatter Jacobians), and redistributes representation variance (increasing effective rank on isotropic data but decreasing on natural language).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Dual Jacobian-PCA spectral probe to characterize how Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing shapes the geometry of learned functions and representations. In controlled MLP-MoE experiments on synthetic Gaussian data, experts exhibit consistently smaller leading Jacobian singular values and faster spectral decay than dense baselines, indicating reduced local sensitivity. Cross-expert Jacobian alignment is near zero, suggesting a functional decomposition into low-overlap subspaces. Weighted PCA reveals expert-local representations distribute variance across more principal directions (higher effective rank), but this trend inverts when training a transformer on WikiText, implying an interaction between routing and the natural language manifold. The study provides a geometric lens for interpreting MoE specialization and its dependence on data structure.

## Method Summary
The authors train MLP-MoE models (8 experts, 64–128–64 architecture) and dense baselines on synthetic isotropic Gaussian data (dim 64, batch 512) using MSE loss and Adam (lr 1e‑3). Routing strategies include Top‑k (k=2) and fully‑soft. After training, expert-local Jacobians Jₑ(x)=∂fₑ/∂x are computed via autograd and aggregated using routing weights; singular value spectra and pairwise cosine similarities are analyzed. Weighted PCA on hidden states h(x), weighted by routing scores gₑ(x), estimates effective rank (components needed for 90% variance). Dense baselines use standard PCA. A validation experiment replaces the FFN in a 3‑layer transformer with the MoE layer and repeats probes on the first MoE layer after training on WikiText‑2.

## Key Results
- Cross-expert Jacobian alignment averages near zero (typically –0.01 to 0.015), indicating functional decomposition into low-overlap subspaces.
- Experts exhibit 1.5–1.7× smaller leading singular values and faster spectral decay than the dense baseline, reflecting reduced local sensitivity.
- On synthetic data, MoE experts require >20 PCA components for 90% variance vs. ~3 for dense (higher effective rank); on WikiText, dense needs ~20 while experts need ~3, inverting the trend.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Functional decomposition via soft clustering**  
Routing softly partitions the input domain, causing each expert to dominate a distinct region. Their local Jacobians align with the sensitivity structure of that region, leading to near-orthogonal average transformations. Evidence: Near-zero cross-expert Jacobian cosine similarities in Figure 1B. Break condition: Uniform routing weights or strong architectural coupling (e.g., shared normalization).

**Mechanism 2: Reduced local sensitivity**  
Experts process restricted input subsets under the router; within these subsets the mapping can be smoother, yielding smaller leading singular values and faster spectral decay. Evidence: Smaller σ₁ and steeper decay in Figure 1C vs. dense baseline. Break condition: Poor routing exposing experts to diverse inputs or insufficient expert capacity.

**Mechanism 3: Variance restructuring by data manifold**  
On isotropic data, clustering spreads variance across more principal directions (higher effective rank). On a low-dimensional natural‑language manifold, clustering aligns with intrinsic charts, allowing simpler per‑expert subspaces (lower rank) while the dense model mixes heterogeneous contexts. Evidence: Inversion in Figure 2B (synthetic) vs. Figure C.3 (WikiText). Break condition: Data already isotropic or routing fails to create well-separated clusters.

## Foundational Learning
- **Jacobian singular-value spectrum**  
  Quantifies local curvature and sensitivity; faster decay implies smoother mappings across input directions. *Why needed*: Core to Mechanism 2 (reduced sensitivity). *Quick check*: “What does a faster decay in singular values imply about an expert’s behavior across input directions?”

- **Weighted PCA with routing weights**  
  Computes covariance of hidden states as perceived by each expert under the routing distribution; weights modify sample contributions vs. standard PCA. *Why needed*: Captures expert‑local representation geometry (Mechanism 3). *Quick check*: “How do routing weights as sample weights change the PCA compared to standard PCA over all inputs?”

- **Effective rank via cumulative variance threshold**  
  Summarizes intrinsic dimensionality; more components needed for 90% variance indicates higher effective rank. *Why needed*: Distills representation geometry into a single comparable metric. *Quick check*: “If an expert needs 10 components to explain 90% variance vs. 2 for another, what does that suggest about their effective ranks?”

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Router (linear+softmax/Top‑k) → Experts (MLPs) → Output aggregation (weighted sum). Probes: Jacobian (autograd) and PCA (weighted covariance) attached post‑training.

**Critical path**:  
1. Input x enters MoE layer.  
2. Router computes g(x).  
3. Each expert fₑ(x) activates according to gₑ(x).  
4. For analysis: collect h(x) and Jₑ(x); aggregate Jₑ weighted by gₑ; compute weighted PCA on h(x) with weights gₑ.  
5. Spectral analysis (SVD, eigenvalues, cosine similarities) on aggregated matrices.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Synthetic vs. real data: synthetic enables exact Jacobian/stable PCA but lacks linguistic structure; real data requires approximate Jacobians and shows different patterns.  
- Top‑k vs. soft routing: sharper specialization with Top‑k lowers effective rank and cross‑alignment; soft increases overlap.  
- Exact Jacobians tractable only in small MLPs; transformers need power‑iteration estimates.

**Failure signatures**:  
- High cross‑expert cosine similarity (>0.1) → insufficient decomposition.  
- Dense Jacobian norm grows without plateau → routing fails to constrain sensitivity.  
- No PCA rank difference between dense/expert, or missing inversion on language → ineffective routing or confounded representation.  
- Unnormalized weighted PCA weights (sum ≠ 1) → biased covariance.

**First experiments**:  
1. Baseline spectral comparison: Train dense vs. Top‑k MoE on synthetic; verify smaller σ₁ and faster decay for experts.  
2. Routing sharpness: Compare Top‑k (k=2) vs. fully‑soft MoE; expect lower effective rank and cross‑alignment for Top‑k.  
3. Transfer to transformer: Train 3‑layer transformer with MoE on WikiText; confirm curvature reduction and rank inversion in first MoE layer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Do the geometric signatures (flattened Jacobians, near‑orthogonal experts, rank modulation) persist in large‑scale transformer MoEs (>70B) and correlate with hallucination rates, expert counts, and routing diversity?
- What causes the effective rank inversion on natural language, and does it reflect a general duality between function flattening and representation compression?
- How do standard transformer components (attention, layer norm, residuals) interact with MoE routing to shape spectral geometry?
- In deep MoE stacks, do these effects compound, average, or change qualitatively across layers?

## Limitations
- Exact training schedule (steps, early‑stopping) unspecified; synthetic replication may diverge if timing differs.
- Ambiguity regarding dense PCA representation (raw isotropic input vs. dense block output) affects reported effective rank.
- Possible use of an auxiliary load‑balancing loss for MoE training is undisclosed, potentially influencing routing sharpness.
- Transformer experiment details (tokenization, context length, train vs. validation split) are sparse, hindering full reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **Functional decomposition (low cross‑expert alignment)**: High for synthetic MLP‑MoE; Medium for transformer (limited to one layer, inversion may reduce orthogonality).
- **Reduced local sensitivity (flatter Jacobians)**: High – consistently observed across synthetic and transformer settings.
- **Variance restructuring (effective rank inversion)**: Medium on synthetic; Low–Medium on WikiText due to lack of mechanistic explanation and potential representation confounders.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate load‑balancing loss**: Train Top‑k MoE with/without auxiliary load‑balancing term; measure routing entropy, cross‑expert Jacobian cosine, and PCA effective rank. Expect higher entropy and weaker specialization without the loss.
2. **Dense representation control**: For synthetic data, apply PCA to (a) raw isotropic input, (b) dense block’s output before MoE comparison point, and (c) MoE‑aggregated output. This isolates whether rank changes arise from routing or architectural depth.
3. **Span routing sharpness**: Sweep router temperature (or k for Top‑k) across a wide range; for each, compute average expert σ₁, spectral decay slope, cross‑expert alignment, and expert effective rank to confirm monotonic relationships with sharpness.
