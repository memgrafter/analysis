# A Geometric Probe of the Accuracy-Robustness Trade-off: Sharp Boundaries in Symmetry-Breaking Dimensional Expansion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17948
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17948
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Symmetry-Breaking Dimensional Expansion (SBDE) boosts CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 clean accuracy to 95.63% from 90.47% but reduces ℓ∞ robust accuracy to ~0%; test-time projection of auxiliary pixels restores robustness to ~82%.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the geometric origin of the accuracy-robustness trade-off using Symmetry-Breaking Dimensional Expansion (SBDE). By inserting constant-valued pixels into input images, SBDE breaks convolutional weight permutation symmetry, reducing parameter degeneracy and improving clean accuracy. However, this creates a highly anisotropic loss landscape with steep gradients along the new auxiliary dimensions. Gradient-based ℓ∞ attacks exploit these steep directions, causing catastrophic robustness failure. A diagnostic test-time projection that resets auxiliary pixels to their training constant neutralizes attacks and recovers robustness, localizing the vulnerability to the auxiliary subspace. The trade-off is thus framed as a sharpening of loss curvature off the natural data manifold in the auxiliary dimensions.

## Method Summary
The method modifies the input preprocessing: each image is spatially expanded by inserting rows and columns of a constant value `c` at regular intervals defined by an expansion factor `F`. A ResNet-18 is adapted by adjusting its first convolutional layer (stride 2, replacing initial max-pool with identity) to handle the larger spatial dimensions. The model is trained on the expanded images using standard CIFAR-10 augmentation and SGD. Robustness is evaluated in the expanded input space using ℓ∞ Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) attacks. For diagnosis, a test-time mask projection operator `Π` is applied to adversarial examples before inference, resetting the inserted constant pixels to `c`.

## Key Results
- SBDE training increased clean accuracy on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 from 90.47% to 95.63%.
- Under ℓ∞ PGD attack in the expanded space, the SBDE model’s robust accuracy collapsed to approximately 0%.
- Applying the test-time mask projection `Π` restored robust accuracy to an average of ~82%, demonstrating that attacks exploit steep gradients exclusively along auxiliary dimensions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Mechanism 1:** SBDE breaks translational symmetry of convolutional weights by inserting constant pixels, reducing parameter degeneracy and guiding optimization toward a deeper, more constrained basin for natural data, thereby increasing clean accuracy.
- **Mechanism 2:** The loss landscape becomes anisotropic: flat along signal directions (natural image variations) but steep along auxiliary directions. Gradient-based attacks greedily follow the steepest ascent, concentrating perturbations on auxiliary pixels for maximal loss increase per unit budget.
- **Mechanism 3:** The test-time mask projection `Π` resets auxiliary pixels to their constant training value `c`, collapsing displacement along steep auxiliary directions and returning the example to the flatter basin around the data manifold, thereby neutralizing the attack.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw CIFAR-10 Image -> SBDE Preprocessor -> Expanded Image -> ResNet-18 (adapted first layer) -> Logits. Attack Engine receives Expanded Image -> Adversarial Expanded Image -> (optional) Projection Module Π -> Projected Image -> ResNet-18 -> Robustness Score.
- **Critical path:** For clean evaluation: Image -> SBDE -> Model. For robust evaluation: Image -> SBDE -> Attack -> Adversarial Expanded Image -> (Π?) -> Model -> Accuracy.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Expansion factor `F` should not be an integer multiple of the first-layer stride for maximal robustness after projection; constant `c` can be any value in the data range; projection Π is a diagnostic, not a deployable defense against aware attackers.
- **Failure signatures:** (1) SBDE model shows near-100% clean accuracy but ~0% robust accuracy without projection. (2) Post-projection robustness is much lower than clean accuracy, indicating attack budget leaked into signal subspace. (3) No robustness recovery after Π contradicts the core localization claim.
- **First 3 experiments:** (1) Train ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 with and without SBDE (e.g., `F=5`, `c=0`); compare clean and PGD-robust accuracy. (2) For SBDE model, evaluate robust accuracy with test-time projection Π applied; expect recovery to ~80-85%. (3) Ablate `F` (2–9) and first-layer stride (2,3,4); measure post-Π robustness, expecting best when `F` not divisible by stride.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does SBDE’s effect generalize to other architectures (e.g., Vision Transformers) and datasets (e.g., ImageNet)?
- Is the vulnerability along auxiliary dimensions exclusive to ℓ∞ white-box attacks, or does it extend to ℓ2, black-box, and corruption-based robustness evaluations?
- What is the precise mathematical relationship between the first-layer stride and the SBDE expansion factor `F` that determines the sharpness of auxiliary boundaries?
- Can models be trained with SBDE that avoid creating steep auxiliary boundaries while still gaining clean accuracy (e.g., via regularization on auxiliary gradients)?

## Limitations
- Findings are limited to CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18; generalization to other settings is unproven.
- The diagnostic projection Π is not a practical defense against an attacker with knowledge of it.
- The exact spatial insertion pattern of constant pixels and first-layer adaptation details are not fully specified, requiring assumptions for reproduction.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (SBDE reduces degeneracy → higher clean accuracy): Medium (supported by accuracy gains but degeneracy not directly measured).
- **Mechanism 2** (Steep auxiliary gradients exploited by attacks): Medium (attack trajectories and robustness collapse suggest anisotropy, but gradient magnitudes per direction are not reported).
- **Mechanism 3** (Π restores robustness → vulnerability localized to auxiliary subspace): High (robust accuracy jumps from ~0% to ~82% after projection, strongly confirming localization).

## Next Checks
- **Insertion alignment ablation:** Train SBDE models with different starting indices for constant pixel insertion and evaluate whether clean accuracy gains depend on alignment.
- **Direct gradient anisotropy measurement:** For a trained SBDE model, estimate loss gradient norms along random unit vectors in the auxiliary vs. signal subspaces (e.g., via finite differences); expect significantly larger norms in auxiliary directions.
- **Generalization of projection effect:** Apply Π to adversarial examples from SBDE-trained models on a different dataset (e.g., SVHN); if robustness recovery holds, it supports that the geometric trade-off is dataset-agnostic.
