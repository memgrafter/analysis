# Safety-Guided Flow (SGF): A Unified Framework for Negative Guidance in Safe Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13300  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13300  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** SGF unifies prior heuristic negative guidance methods under an energy-based MMD potential and proves, via control-barrier analysis, that safety interventions must be concentrated in an early denoising window and decay to zero afterward to balance safety and quality.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the absence of a unified, theoretically grounded safety framework for diffusion and flow-based generative models. Existing approaches—such as control barrier functions from robotics and heuristic negative guidance in image generation—lack clear criteria for *when* to intervene, often degrading output quality. The authors propose Safety-Guided Flow (SGF), a probabilistic framework that steers the generative trajectory away from unsafe data using a Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) potential. SGF recasts prior methods (Shielded Diffusion, Safe Denoiser) as specific instances of this energy-based guidance. A core theoretical contribution uses control-barrier function analysis to justify a critical insight: negative guidance must be strong early in the reverse process and decay to zero after a specific time window to ensure safety without compromising generation quality. Empirically, SGF achieves state-of-the-art results across nudity-safe generation, class diversity, and memorization mitigation, demonstrating both superior safety and preserved or improved output quality.

## Method Summary
SGF modifies the reverse-time dynamics of diffusion or flow models by adding a guidance term derived from an energy potential. The potential `E(xt) = MMD²_k({xt}, D⁻)` measures the distributional discrepancy between the current sample (or its clean prediction `zt`) and an unsafe reference dataset `D⁻` using a kernel (typically RBF). Its gradient `∇E` provides a repulsive force steering the sample away from regions of high similarity to `D⁻`. The framework prescribes a time-dependent guidance strength `λ(t)` that is non-zero only within a critical early window `[ts, te]` (e.g., `[1.0, 0.78]`) and zero thereafter. The kernel bandwidth `σ` is set adaptively, often using the median of the top-3 pairwise distances between a batch and `D⁻`. During sampling, for each timestep `t` in the window, the clean estimate `zt` is perturbed: `z'ₜ = zₜ + λ(t)∇E(zt)`, and this adjusted value is used in the subsequent ODE/SDE step.

## Key Results
- On nudity-safe generation against adversarial prompts, SGF reduces Attack Success Rate (0.051 vs. 0.127 for SAFREE) and Toxic Rate while maintaining COCO FID ~23.7.
- For class-of-image diversity, early-stopped guidance (`[1.0, 0.78]`) achieves better FID (31.81) and CLIP (30.78) than full-guidance SPELL (FID 48.50, CLIP 28.17).
- For memorization mitigation, SGF reduces the 95th-percentile Gen–Train similarity from 0.437 to 0.328 and improves FID from 41.19 to 32.44.
- Ablation studies confirm that applying guidance in the earliest time window (e.g., `[1.0, 0.78]`) is optimal across all tasks; extending the window harms quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The gradient of the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) potential provides a unified repulsive force that recasts prior heuristic negative guidance methods as specific instances of an energy-based steering mechanism.
- **Mechanism:** The potential `E(xt) = MMD²_k({xt}, D⁻)` with an RBF kernel yields a closed-form gradient `∇E(xt) = (2/σ²) * Σ_{y∈D⁻} k(xt,y)(xt - y) / (|D⁻| * mean_k)`. This is a weighted, kernel-based repulsion field pushing samples away from unsafe references, with weights proportional to kernel similarities.
- **Core assumption:** The RBF kernel is characteristic; `D⁻` adequately covers unsafe regions; the model’s clean prediction `zt` is a stable query point.
- **Evidence anchors:** Propositions 1 and 2 formally equate the MMD gradient to the Safe Denoiser’s weighted kernel repulsion and Shielded Diffusion’s radial force via bandwidth/radius matching.
- **Break condition:** Kernel bandwidth `σ` poorly matched to data scale; `D⁻` is unrepresentative; guidance applied in pixel space instead of clean space.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Safety guidance must be concentrated in a critical early time window of the reverse process and decay to zero afterward to avoid degrading generation quality.
- **Mechanism:** Control-barrier function analysis on the forward-time dynamics `dx/ds = f~(s,x) + β(s)∇E(x)` yields a safety certificate: a weighted integral of `β(s)` over a window `[0, sc]` must exceed a threshold. The weight `w~L(u) = exp(∫_u^{sc} L(τ)dτ)` favors earlier intervention because the base drift `f~` is bounded by `L(s)` and is typically smaller later. Setting `β(s)=0` after `sc` preserves safety if the unguided flow is forward-invariant in the safe set.
- **Core assumption:** Near the safe/unsafe boundary, the base drift `f~` has limited influence (`|∇h·f~| ≤ L(s)|h|`) and the guidance direction aligns with the barrier gradient (`∇h·∇E ≥ µ > 0`).
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorem 2 proves shifting guidance earlier strictly improves the safety bound for fixed total guidance effort. Ablations show lowest Attack Success Rate for early windows like `[1.0, 0.8]`.
- **Break condition:** Base drift `f~` is strong and misaligned near the boundary; unsafe set is time-dependent; model uses non-monotonic step sizes (e.g., accelerated text diffusion).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The radial repulsion in Shielded Diffusion is equivalent to the MMD-gradient field with an RBF kernel after matching force magnitude at a chosen distance `d0`.
- **Mechanism:** For a single unsafe reference `y`, the MMD gradient yields a Gaussian radial force `FG(d;σ) = (2λ/σ²) ||d|| exp(-||d||²/(2σ²)) (d/||d||)`. Shielded Diffusion uses `Frad(d) = α(r - ||d||)_+ (d/||d||)`. By solving `||Frad(d0)|| = ||FG(d0;σ)||` via the Lambert W function, the forces match in magnitude at `d0` and share radial direction. With multiple references and thresholding, SPELL becomes a radius-thresholded instance of MMD guidance.
- **Core assumption:** Unsafe set approximates a union of balls; kernel is RBF; bandwidth `σ` can be tuned to match `r`, `α`, and `d0`.
- **Evidence anchors:** Proposition 2 derives the σ-d0 matching condition. The paper notes SPELL’s empirical observation of strong early interventions aligns with the derived critical window.
- **Break condition:** Unsafe set is non-radial (e.g., textured surfaces); bandwidth mismatch; guidance applied outside the calibrated window `[ts, te]`.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** model (pretrained diffusion/flow) -> clean prediction `zt` -> MMD potential `E(zt, D⁻)` -> gradient `∇E` -> scaled by `λ(t)` (active in early window) -> added to drift -> ODE integrator (Euler, Midpoint) -> next sample `xt-1`
- **Critical path:** At each reverse step `t`: compute `zt` from model; if `t` within window `[ts, te]`, compute `∇zt E(zt, D⁻)` using batched pairwise RBF distances; perturb `z'ₜ = zₜ + λ(t)∇E`; feed `z'ₜ` into sampler step to produce `xt-1`.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Guidance magnitude `λ`: higher → safer but lower quality; needs calibration per task.  
  - Time window `[ts, te]`: early (`ts` near 1.0, short) → preserves quality (empirically `[1.0, 0.78]` optimal); late/long → distortion.  
  - Bandwidth `σ`: too small → overfitting to nearest negative, hyper-repulsion; too large → weak guidance. Adaptive per-batch estimation recommended.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High ASR/toxicity when window is too short/narrow or `λ` too small.  
  - Quality drop (FID↑, CLIP↓) when window extends beyond `te≈0.6` or `λ` is excessive.  
  - No change if `D⁻` is poorly aligned (wrong modality, low coverage) or `σ` is extreme.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Nudity safety:** Use SAFREE + SGF with `λ=0.0015`, window `[1.0,0.6]`, adaptive `σ`, `D⁻`=515 nudity images. Expect ASR < 0.2, FID ~24.  
  2. **Diversity (ImageNet):** Use SD-v3, `λ=1.0` (high) or `0.03` (low), early-stop `[1.0,0.78]`. Expect higher Vendi vs. full guidance, small FID penalty.  
  3. **Memorization mitigation:** Fine-tune SD-v2.1 on ImageNette; apply SGF at inference with training images as `D⁻`, `λ=0.03`, window `[1.0,0.8]`. Expect @Sim95% ↓ from 0.44 to ~0.33, FID improvement ~20%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the theoretical guarantee be extended when the MMD gradient does not perfectly align with the control barrier field near the unsafe set boundary?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state: "A limitation is that our proofs assume the gradient of the MMD guidance aligns with the ideal control barrier field... As future work, we will investigate ways to relax this assumption by quantifying guidance mismatch."
- **Why unresolved:** Current analysis relies on Assumption 1 (alignment and drift bounds), which may not hold for all kernels or distributions. Misalignment could cause safety failures or quality degradation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A metric for gradient misalignment; theoretical bounds for safety under imperfect alignment; empirical validation showing how safety guarantees weaken with misalignment.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the critical window principle extend to diffusion models with non-monotonic step sizes, such as those used in accelerated language model sampling?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper notes: "However, there are diffusion language models where these conditions do not hold... In such cases, the assumptions used in our theorem are violated, and one would need a more general analysis tailored to these acceleration schedules."
- **Why unresolved:** The theorem requires decreasing guidance impact over time (`L(s)` nonnegative), but token-based unmasking may increase update magnitude later, invalidating "earlier is better".
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis of unmasking schedules in text diffusion models; a new theorem for non-monotonic `L(s)`; experiments comparing guidance timing in text diffusion.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the critical time window `[1.0, sc]` be derived analytically from model and task parameters instead of via heuristic ablation?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper empirically finds early windows optimal (e.g., `[1.0,0.78]`) but provides no formula for `sc`. Theorem 2 gives a sufficient condition but does not invert it to find minimal `sc`.
- **Why unresolved:** Practitioners must search over windows; a closed-form `sc` would improve practicality and explain task-specific variations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A closed-form expression for `sc` in terms of barrier properties, drift bounds, and `β` budget; validation that predicted `sc` matches empirical optima across datasets/models.

## Limitations
- Missing prompt lists and scaling factors (e.g., η in Safe Denoiser) hinder exact replication.
- CBF assumptions (bounded drift, forward invariance) may fail for stochastic samplers or large step sizes.
- MMD gradient is highly sensitive to `D⁻` coverage and kernel bandwidth; poor choices cause misdirected repulsion.
- Reliance on automated metrics (NudeNet, CLIP, FID) without human evaluation.
- Static potential cannot handle time-varying safety constraints.

## Confidence
- **Unified framework equivalence:** High. Theorems and Propositions rigorously recast Shielded Diffusion and Safe Denoiser as MMD-gradient instances.
- **Early-window guidance:** Medium. CBF derivation uses strong assumptions; ablations confirm trend but optimal window may vary across models/samplers.
- **Empirical superiority:** Medium. Results are comprehensive but lack statistical tests and could be hyperparameter-sensitive.
