# GUI-Libra: Training Native GUI Agents to Reason and Act with Action-aware Supervision and Partially Verifiable RL
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.22190
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22190
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: GUI‑Libra introduces a three‑stage training pipeline (curated 81K reasoning dataset, action‑aware SFT, conservative RL) that significantly improves open‑source native GUI agents on long‑horizon tasks (e.g., +15.6% on AndroidWorld for 4B) and matches proprietary agents.

## Executive Summary
GUI‑Libra addresses two key shortcomings of open‑source native GUI agents: a lack of action‑aligned reasoning data and standard post‑training pipelines that neglect GUI‑specific challenges. It proposes a three‑part recipe: (1) a high‑quality 81 K reasoning dataset assembled from multiple GUI sources and rigorously filtered; (2) action‑aware supervised fine‑tuning that mixes reasoning‑then‑action and direct‑action samples with token reweighting to preserve grounding accuracy; (3) a conservative reinforcement learning stage using GRPO with KL regularization and success‑adaptive gradient scaling to cope with partially verifiable rewards and align offline metrics with online success. Evaluations across Android and web benchmarks show substantial improvements in step accuracy and task completion, often outperforming much larger models and rivaling proprietary systems.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds in two stages. First, **Action‑aware SFT** trains the base VLM on a mixture of reasoning‑augmented and direct action trajectories. Token importance weights (`αa` for action tokens, `αg` for grounding tokens) are applied to prevent the model from over‑emphasizing long chain‑of‑thought outputs at the expense of precise element localization. Second, **Conservative RL** optimizes the policy via GRPO with a KL penalty toward the ASFT checkpoint and employs Success‑Negative Gradient Scaling (SNGS) to mitigate reward hacking arising from partial verifiability. The RL training uses a balanced 40 K‑step subset of the curated data and adapts the learning rate to a low value (1e‑6) for stability.

## Key Results
- On AndroidWorld, GUI‑Libra yields **+15.6%** (4B) and **+12.2%** (8B) absolute improvements in task success.
- On Online‑Mind2Web, it achieves **+4.0%** (4B) and **+8.7%** (8B) gains.
- Pass@1 scores on AndroidControl‑v2 and MM‑Mind2Web‑v2 surpass larger models and approach proprietary agents.
- Ablations confirm each component’s contribution and show that KL regularization strengthens the correlation between offline step accuracy and online success.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GUI‑Libra succeeds because its design directly counteracts the two failure modes of existing agents. **Action‑aware SFT** ensures that the model does not ignore grounding when generating long reasoning traces, maintaining step‑level precision. **Conservative RL** with SNGS stabilizes updates under sparse, partially observable rewards by scaling negative gradients according to group success, thus avoiding reward hacking and better transferring offline pretraining to online performance. The combination yields a policy that effectively reasons, grounds, and adapts to diverse GUI environments.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: `Raw GUI data → Unification & Reasoning Augmentation → ASFT (SFT Trainer + reweighting) → RL Trainer (GRPO + KL + SNGS) → Eval`  
- **Critical path**: Start with a pretrained VLM (base model). Apply ASFT on the full 81 K‑step reasoning dataset to obtain a checkpoint that balances reasoning and grounding. Then perform conservative RL on the 40 K‑step RL subset to adapt the policy to online success signals. Finally evaluate on held‑out benchmarks.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Mixing ratio*: Higher direct‑action proportion improves grounding but may reduce reasoning capability; tuned via validation.  
  - *KL penalty*: Too high hinders exploration; too low allows reward hacking. β is set per model scale (0.001 for 3B, 0.005 for 7B).  
  - *SNGS parameters*: λ0 and κ control aggressiveness; larger κ amplifies adaptation but risks instability.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Grounding accuracy (ScreenSpot‑v2) drops → increase αg or direct‑action mix.  
  - RL reward rises but offline step accuracy falls → reduce KL or SNGS scaling.  
  - Web benchmarks lag → RL data likely mobile‑biased; rebalance domain distribution.  
  - Out‑of‑memory errors → reduce batch size, increase gradient accumulation, or enable FSDP.
- **First three experiments**:  
  1. **ASFT mix ablation**: Train with 0% direct actions vs. 50% mix; compare grounding accuracy.  
  2. **SNGS ablation**: Disable SNGS in RL while keeping KL; evaluate online success on AndroidWorld.  
  3. **Domain shift test**: Evaluate final policy on a held‑out web‑only test set (e.g., WebArena‑Lite‑v2) to assess generalization.

## Limitations
- The data pipeline relies on proprietary models (GPT‑4.1, Qwen3‑VL‑32B), limiting accessibility.
- GUI‑Libra‑81K is not yet publicly released; reproduction without it requires re‑building the filtering pipeline, which may not match quality.
- Training was conducted on high‑end B200 GPUs; porting to more common hardware necessitates untuned batch‑size and parallelism adjustments.
- The SNGS mechanism lacks extensive theoretical grounding and has not been validated outside this work.

## Confidence
| Claim | Confidence |
|-------|------------|
| Performance gains on long‑horizon GUI benchmarks (AndroidWorld, Online‑Mind2Web) | Medium |
| Action‑aware SFT mitigates grounding degradation from long CoT | Medium |
| Conservative RL (GRPO + KL + SNGS) stabilizes learning under partial verifiability | Low |
| Generalization to unseen GUI layouts or domains | Low |
- Medium evidence: Ablations and comparisons are internal; no independent external validation yet.
- Low evidence: SNGS is novel; limited ablation depth and potential overfitting to specific datasets.

## Next Checks
1. **Exact ASFT replication**: Using the released GUI‑Libra‑81K, train Qwen2.5‑VL‑3B from scratch with the ASFT recipe; achieve AndroidControl‑v2 test step accuracy within ±1% of reported value.
2. **SNGS ablation**: Remove SNGS (keep KL only) from RL for the same ASFT checkpoint; measure absolute drop in AndroidWorld success rate; expect >5% decrease.
3. **Domain shift validation**: Evaluate final policy on a fresh subset of WebArena‑Lite‑v2 that was not included in any training stage; compare success rate against base model to quantify out‑of‑distribution performance.
