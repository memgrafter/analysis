# Free-RBF-KAN: Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks with Adaptive Radial Basis Functions for Efficient Function Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.07760  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07760  
- **Reference count**: 33  
- **Primary result**: Proposes Free-RBF-KAN with learnable RBF centroids and smoothness, achieving accuracy comparable to B-spline KANs while significantly reducing training and inference time on regression, PDE, and operator learning tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Free-RBF-KAN, a variant of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks that replaces B-spline activations with adaptive radial basis functions (RBFs). Unlike prior RBF-KANs with fixed grids, Free-RBF-KAN makes both the RBF centroids and smoothness parameters (`σ`) trainable via reparameterization, allowing the network to dynamically align its basis functions with the data distribution. The authors provide a universal approximation proof for RBF-KANs and empirically demonstrate that Free-RBF-KAN matches or exceeds the accuracy of standard KANs while being much faster to train, particularly on physics-informed and operator learning problems.

## Method Summary
Free-RBF-KAN constructs a network where each layer computes a sum over input nodes and a set of Gaussian RBFs: `xᶫ⁺¹ᵢ = Σⱼ Σₘ ωᵢⱼₘ * K((xⱼ - cᵢⱼₘ)/σᵢⱼₘ)`. The centroids `c` are constrained to the input domain via `c = xₗ + (xᵣ - xₗ)/(b-a)(tanh(˜c)-a)` with `a=-1, b=1`, and `σ` is expressed as `σ = exp(˜σ)` to ensure positivity. These parameters are optimized jointly with network weights using Adam (often followed by LBFGS). The architecture includes optional residual connections and uses SiLU activations in hidden layers with sigmoid/output identity. Training employs PINN-style losses for PDEs and standard MSE for regression.

## Key Results
- On a 2D nonsmooth function, Free-RBF-KAN achieves test MSE 2.74e-4 vs. KAN’s 3.54e-3 with similar training time.
- For the 2D Helmholtz equation, Free-RBF-KAN attains L2-loss 3.35e-2, far below KAN’s 1.58, while training much faster.
- The method shows no spectral bias, with NTK eigenvalue spectra decaying slower than MLPs, similar to B-spline KAN.
- On MNIST, Free-RBF-KAN (error 8.79e-2) still lags behind MLP (6.70e-2) but uses comparable parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Adaptive Meshing via Learnable Centroids
Learning RBF centroid positions allows the network to align basis functions with regions of high activation variance, improving approximation for complex functions without extra compute. Fixed uniform grids are suboptimal; adaptive centering concentrates basis functions where needed. Evidence: Paper’s abstract and Section 4.1 detail the reparameterization and contrast with inflexible B-spline knots. Break condition: centroids collapsing or becoming poorly conditioned (overly sparse/clustered).

### Mechanism 2: Adaptive Smoothness via Trainable Scale Parameters
Treating `σ` as a trainable variable (`σ = exp(˜σ)`) lets each RBF adjust its width, balancing detail capture and noise suppression. A fixed `σ` is insufficient for multiscale features. Evidence: Abstract and Section 4.2 emphasize joint optimization and expressiveness; Section 5.1 shows Free-RBF-KAN outperforms fixed-σ RBF-KAN on nonsmooth data. Break condition: `σ` → 0 (spikes, gradient explosion) or → ∞ (flat functions, vanishing gradients).

### Mechanism 3: Theoretical Universality and Absence of Spectral Bias
RBF-KANs are universal approximators for continuous functions on compact domains and do not suffer spectral bias. The proof extends classical RBF network universality to the KAN’s superpositional structure. NTK analysis empirically shows a flatter eigenvalue spectrum vs. MLP. Evidence: Section 4.3 provides Theorem 4.5 and Corollary 4.6; Section 5.2’s Fig. 3 compares NTK spectra. Break condition: Using a polynomial RBF kernel or degenerate `σ` optimization that makes the kernel effectively polynomial.

## Foundational Learning
- **Kolmogorov-Arnold Representation Theorem**: Guarantees any multivariate continuous function can be expressed as a finite superposition of continuous univariate functions. This justifies KAN’s layered univariate activation structure. Quick check: What is the number of univariate outer/inner functions required for a `d`-variable target?
- **Radial Basis Functions (RBFs)**: Gaussian RBFs `exp(-(x-c)²/σ²)` provide localized, smooth bumps; `c` (center) controls location, `σ` (width) controls smoothness. Quick check: How does increasing `σ` affect the RBF shape? What if `σ` is very small?
- **Universal Approximation Theorem for RBFs**: A single layer of RBFs with appropriate centers and widths can approximate any continuous univariate function if the RBF kernel is non-polynomial. Quick check: What kernel property makes `{K((x-c)/σ)}` dense in `C([a,b])`?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input → RBF-KAN Layer (sum over RBFs: Σ ω * K((x - c)/σ)) → Optional scaling/activation (W_rbf ⊙ Φ + W ⊙ ρ) → Activation ρ_o (SiLU/sigmoid) → Next layer → Output.
- **Critical path**: Forward pass evaluates many 1D Gaussian RBFs per node; adaptive `c` and `σ` are part of the computational graph. Training alternates between Adam and LBFGS with exponential LR scheduling.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Grid size `G` increases capacity linearly with parameters/compute; adaptivity (`c`, `σ` learnable) boosts accuracy but risks instability; depth enables hierarchical composition but requires careful optimization.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Loss divergence: check for `σ` → 0/∞ or centroid collapse causing NaN gradients.  
  - High error: may indicate insufficient `G`, poor `c` initialization, or unsuitable `σ` range.  
  - Slow convergence: likely mismatched learning rates for adaptive parameters or optimizer choice.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Nonsmooth 2D function regression (MSE comparison vs. KAN/RBF-KAN).  
  2. Spectral bias check via NTK spectrum on multiscale 1D function.  
  3. 2D heat equation PINN (L2-error and training time vs. MLP/KAN).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Mathematical dualities with Gaussian-activated MLPs**: Can formal equivalences be established to transfer MLP optimization advances (e.g., second-order methods) to Free-RBF-KAN? *Basis*: Conclusion mentions future exploration. *Unresolved*: No such duality studied. *Resolution*: Prove equivalence under overparameterization or show hybrid training gains.
- **Scalability to high-dimensional unstructured data**: Can Free-RBF-KAN match MLP accuracy on datasets like ImageNet while keeping efficiency on scientific tasks? *Basis*: Paper notes MNIST gap (8.79e-2 vs. 6.70e-2). *Unresolved*: No architectural modifications for such tasks proposed. *Resolution*: Systematic scaling experiments with convolutional/attention adaptations.
- **Optimization dynamics of adaptive parameters**: What are convergence guarantees for learning `c` and `σ`? Do issues like unit splitting or poor local minima arise? *Basis*: Paper uses Adam/LBFGS without analysis. *Unresolved*: No theoretical or hyperparameter sensitivity study. *Resolution*: Convergence proofs under simplifying assumptions; empirical studies of initialization/optimizer effects.
- **Approximation of discontinuous functions**: Does Free-RBF-KAN universally approximate discontinuous functions, and how do kernel choices (e.g., Matérn) affect this? *Basis*: Universality proof assumes continuity and non-polynomial RBF; experiments use continuous (nonsmooth) functions only. *Unresolved*: Theory and experiments for discontinuities lacking. *Resolution*: Extend UAT to discontinuous bounded functions; test on hyperbolic PDEs with shocks.

## Limitations
- **Centroid initialization**: Exact strategy (uniform, perturbed, tied) unspecified; poor initialization may cause collapse.
- **Baseline reproducibility**: Precise KAN/MLP configurations (grid ranges, B-spline order, scaling) ambiguous, risking unfair comparisons.
- **Optimization stability**: Learnable `σ` and centroids may diverge without regularization; break conditions noted but not thoroughly validated.
- **Scalability claims**: Speed advantage over B-spline KAN depends on grid size `G` and implementation; not compared to all RBF variants (e.g., BSRBF-KAN).
- **Theoretical assumptions**: Universality requires non-polynomial RBF; if `σ` adapts to polynomial-like behavior, guarantees may not hold in practice.

## Confidence
- **Adaptive RBF mechanism (learnable centroids & σ)**: Medium. Empirical gains on nonsmooth tasks, but robustness to hyperparameters and long-term stability unverified.
- **Universality & absence of spectral bias**: High for theory (formal proof under kernel conditions). Medium for empirical spectral bias (limited NTK spectra in Fig. 3).
- **Overall superiority on regression/PDE tasks**: Medium. Results promising but rely on specific optimizer settings; reproducibility concerns due to missing seeds/baseline specs.

## Next Checks
