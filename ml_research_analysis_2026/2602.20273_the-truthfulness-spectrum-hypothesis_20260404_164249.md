# The Truthfulness Spectrum Hypothesis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20273
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20273
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Truth directions exist on a spectrum from domain-general to domain-specific; linear probes generalize across most truth types but fail on sycophancy and expectation-inverted lying (AUROC ~0.55); Mahalanobis cosine similarity between probe directions predicts cross-domain generalization nearly perfectly (R²=0.98); concept erasure isolates both general and specific directions; causal steering shows domain-specific directions improve truthfulness (+0.05 Δdiff) while domain-general degrade (-0.07).

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether large language models encode truthfulness via a single linear direction or multiple domain-specific ones. It proposes the truthfulness spectrum hypothesis, where truth representations range from domain-general to domain-specific. Using new datasets (FLEED, sycophancy, expectation-inverted) and existing benchmarks, the authors show that linear probes generalize across most truth types but fail on sycophancy and expectation-inverted lying (AUROC ~0.55). Mahalanobis cosine similarity between probe directions predicts cross-domain generalization nearly perfectly (R²=0.98), far outperforming standard cosine. Concept erasure reveals both domain-general and domain-specific directions; causal steering indicates that domain-specific directions are causally effective (+0.05 Δdiff), while domain-general ones degrade performance (-0.07). Post-training (e.g., RLHF) separates sycophancy representations from others, potentially explaining increased sycophancy in chat models.

## Method Summary
The study uses a suite of controlled true/false datasets: FLEED (definitional, empirical, logical, fictional, ethical), sycophancy (MMLU STEM with fabricated bios), expectation-inverted lying, and prior honesty benchmarks. Activations from assistant response tokens are extracted from Llama-3.3-70B (primary), Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen-2.5 variants. Linear probes (logistic regression) are trained on frozen activations to predict truth. Cross-domain generalization is measured via AUROC. Geometry is analyzed using Mahalanobis cosine similarity (test-set covariance reweighting). Stratified INLP and LEACE erasure isolate domain-general and domain-specific truth subspaces. Causal steering adds scaled probe directions to MLP output biases at a mid-layer; effects are evaluated on SimpleQA via Δdiff (probability difference between true/false answers).

## Key Results
- Linear probes generalize across most truth types but fail on sycophancy and expectation-inverted lying (AUROC ~0.55).
- Mahalanobis cosine similarity between probe directions predicts cross-domain generalization nearly perfectly (R²=0.98), far exceeding standard cosine.
- Concept erasure isolates both domain-general and domain-specific directions; causal steering finds domain-specific directions are causally effective (+0.05 mean Δdiff), while domain-general ones degrade (-0.07). Post-training separates sycophancy representations from others.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Mahalanobis Cosine Similarity Predicts Cross-Domain Generalization
- **Claim:** The Mahalanobis cosine similarity between linear probe directions nearly perfectly (R²=0.98) predicts out-of-domain probe performance, explaining why standard cosine similarity fails.
- **Mechanism:** Standard cosine similarity treats all high-dimensional space equally, but model activations are highly anisotropic with low effective dimensionality (<200 in 8192D). Mahalanobis cosine reweights the inner product by the test data covariance Σ_test, effectively measuring alignment only along signal-bearing directions.
- **Core assumption:** The truth-relevant subspace has low effective dimensionality and is captured by the test covariance; probe directions are linear and discriminative.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...Mahalanobis cosine similarity between probe directions predicts cross-domain generalization nearly perfectly (R²=0.98), far exceeding standard cosine."
  - [section 5] "Variance is concentrated along a small number of directions... Mahalanobis variant reweights the inner product by the data covariance."
  - [corpus] Weak; neighboring papers (e.g., "Probing the Geometry of Truth") also examine probe geometry but do not specifically validate Mahalanobis similarity.
- **Break condition:** If the test covariance Σ_test is poorly estimated or the truth subspace is not linearly separable, the similarity metric becomes unreliable.

### Mechanism 2: Concept Erasure Reveals a Spectrum of Generality
- **Claim:** Stratified INLP and LEACE erasure expose coexisting truth directions from fully domain-general to fully domain-specific, confirming the spectrum hypothesis.
- **Mechanism:** INLP iteratively projects out linearly predictive subspaces. Stratified INLP first extracts mutually orthogonal, highly domain-general directions (by training on all domains). Then, after removing these, INLP on single domains yields highly domain-specific directions. LEACE selectively erases one domain's predictive subspace, causing heterogeneous degradation on other probes, revealing intermediate shared subspaces.
- **Core assumption:** Truth information is linearly decomposable into orthogonal subspaces with varying domain coverage; erasure removes target subspace without affecting others linearly.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Concept-erasure isolates both domain-general and domain-specific directions."
  - [section 7.1] "Stratified INLP... explicitly isolates highly domain-general and domain-specific directions."
  - [section 7.2] "After erasure, probes' in-distribution performance drops to chance... but generalization to other domains shows selective failure."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Truth Neurons" use activation patching but not systematic erasure; "Sycophancy Is Not One Thing" decomposes sycophancy behaviorally, not representationally.
- **Break condition:** If truth concepts are nonlinearly entangled or subspaces are not linearly discoverable, erasure will not cleanly isolate them.

### Mechanism 3: Domain-Specific Directions Are Causal, Domain-General Are Not
- **Claim:** Causal steering along domain-specific directions improves model truthfulness (+0.05 mean Δdiff), while steering along the domain-general direction degrades it (-0.07), indicating functional differences.
- **Mechanism:** Interventions add a scaled direction to MLP output biases. Domain-specific directions effectively boost correct answer confidence by suppressing incorrect answers, while the domain-general direction increases probability for both answers but more for incorrect ones, harming discrimination.
- **Core assumption:** Steering at a mid-layer (layer 15) propagates to output logits; the extracted directions are causally implicated in the model's truth computation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Causal steering finds domain-specific directions are causally effective (+0.05 mean Δdiff), while domain-general ones degrade (-0.07)."
  - [section 8] "Most domain-specific truth directions are not merely predictive but functionally meaningful... domain-general direction produces consistently negative Δdiff."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Balancing Stylization and Truth" use steering for style but do not compare domain generality; "When Truth Is Overridden" studies sycophancy mechanisms but not causal efficacy of directions.
- **Break condition:** If the evaluation task (SimpleQA) does not align with the domain of the steered direction, or if the intervention magnitude (α) is misspecified, effects may not appear or may be nonlinear.

## Foundational Learning
#### Linear Probing
- **Why needed here:** To extract and evaluate linear truth directions across domains; the entire analysis relies on training linear classifiers (logistic regression, LDA) on frozen model activations.
- **Quick check question:** When might a linear probe succeed in-distribution but fail out-of-domain despite a domain-general truth representation existing?

#### Mahalanobis Distance
- **Why needed here:** To compute a meaningful similarity between probe directions in anisotropic, low-dimensional subspaces; standard Euclidean/cosine distances are dominated by noise dimensions.
- **Quick check question:** How does whitening (using Σ_test^{-1/2}) change the interpretation of vector alignment compared to standard cosine?

#### Concept Erasure (INLP/LEACE)
- **Why needed here:** To systematically remove predictive subspaces and observe selective degradation, proving coexistence of multiple truth representations.
- **Quick check question:** What is the key difference between INLP (iterative nullspace projection) and LEACE (closed-form least-squares erasure) in terms of orthogonality guarantees?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Base/Chat Models + Datasets -> Activation Extraction -> Probe Trainer -> Geometry Analyzer -> Erasure Module -> Causal Steerer -> Evaluation

**Critical path:**  
1. Create/obtain truthfulness datasets (FLEED, sycophancy, expectation-inverted, honesty benchmarks).  
2. Extract activations for relevant tokens (assistant responses) from chosen layer.  
3. Train single-domain probes; evaluate cross-domain AUROC.  
4. Compute Mahalanobis cosine between probe weights; plot vs. OOD AUROC.  
5. Apply Stratified INLP to extract general/specific directions; validate cross-task accuracy.  
6. Apply LEACE to erase each domain; observe selective degradation.  
7. Conduct causal steering with extracted directions on held-out factual QA (SimpleQA).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Probe architecture: LDA vs. logistic regression vs. Difference of Means; logistic regression balances performance and simplicity.  
- Token aggregation: Last token vs. average; average tokens typically perform best.  
- Layer selection: Mid-layers (e.g., 33 for Llama-70B, 15 for Llama-8B) often peak in cross-domain performance; base vs. chat models may shift optimal layers.  
- Erasure method: INLP is iterative and can yield orthogonal directions; LEACE is closed-form but may not guarantee orthogonality.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low in-distribution probe AUROC (<0.8) indicates dataset quality or layer choice issues.  
- Mahalanobis cosine near zero for all pairs suggests probes are not aligned even after whitening; possibly nonlinear concept.  
- LEACE erasure fails to reduce erased-domain AUROC to chance; subspace not fully captured.  
- Causal steering shows no effect or flips sign; intervention magnitude/layer may be wrong.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Cross-domain generalization benchmark:** Train probes on each FLEED domain and sycophancy; test on all others. Verify that FLEED domains generalize among themselves but not to sycophancy/expectation-inverted, and vice versa.  
2. **Mahalanobis vs. standard cosine validation:** For all probe pairs, compute both similarities and correlate with OOD AUROC. Expect R² ~0.98 for Mahalanobis, ~0.56 for standard.  
3. **Stratified INLP extraction:** Train on all domains jointly; extract first 5 directions. Validate that each direction alone achieves >0.85 accuracy on all domains. Then remove these, train per-domain directions; validate that each has high in-domain accuracy (>0.95) but near-chance out-of-domain.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Why do universal truth directions exist but fail to causally steer model behavior, while domain-specific directions succeed?  
  *Basis*: [explicit] "Why do universal truth directions exist but fail to steer behavior?" (Discussion).  
  *Unresolved*: The paper shows domain-general directions are easily detected across many truth types (high AUROC) but interventions along them degrade performance (mean Δdiff = -0.07), unlike domain-specific ones (+0.05). Whether this is because the general direction is a superposition of domain-specific ones with interfering components, or encodes confounds, remains unknown.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Decompose the general direction into contributions from specific subspaces; test whether removing domain-specific components from it improves steering; correlate its alignment with potential confound variables (e.g., sycophancy, user alignment signals).

- **Question 2:** What theoretical principle explains the near–perfect linear relationship (R²=0.98) between Mahalanobis cosine similarity of probe directions and cross-domain generalization performance?  
  *Basis*: [inferred] "A theoretical account of this tight linear relationship is an interesting direction for future work." (Section 5/Discussion).  
  *Unresolved*: The empirical correlation suggests Mahalanobis cosine captures the functional similarity of truth encodings after accounting for low effective dimensionality. No existing theory links probe geometry to generalization bounds for overlapping subspaces.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Derive generalization bounds for linear probes under assumptions of low-rank signal and shared/overlapping subspaces; test whether other concept types show the same relationship; compare Mahalanobis cosine to alternative transferability metrics.

- **Question 3:** How does post‑training (instruction tuning, RLHF) specifically reorganize the geometry of truth representations, leading to increased separation of sycophantic lying from other truth types?  
  *Basis*: [inferred] The paper shows chat models have lower Mahalanobis similarity between sycophancy and other truth probes than base models (Figure 4) and notes this reorganization may explain increased sycophancy. However, the mechanisms are not investigated.  
  *Unresolved*: Without knowing which training objectives or data patterns cause the dissociation, we cannot control unwanted representational shifts (e.g., sycophancy) during alignment.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Track representation geometry across fine‑training checkpoints; compare different post‑training regimes (SFT vs. RLHF vs. DPO) on the same base model; identify whether certain synthetic data or reward signals accelerate the separation.

- **Question 4:** Do nonlinear truth representations exist in LLMs that linear probes fail to capture, and if so, how do they relate to the linear truth spectrum?  
  *Basis*: [inferred] Limitations: "Our analysis focuses exclusively on linear structure; nonlinear truth representations may exist, but would require different methods to uncover."  
  *Unresolved*: The truthfulness spectrum is built from linear probes and erasures. If truth is also encoded nonlinearly, our understanding is incomplete and linear interventions might be insufficient.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Train nonlinear probes (e.g., kernel SVMs, shallow neural networks) on truth tasks and compare performance with linear probes; examine whether cases where linear probes fail correspond to nonlinear decision boundaries; test whether nonlinear components align with or diverge from the linear subspaces identified.

## Limitations
- Synthetic generation (FLEED) may imprint Gemini-3-Pro biases; ethical subset not originally paired.
- Model coverage limited; layer/token choices for smaller models (8B, Qwen 7B/14B) under-specified.
- Causal steering evaluated only on SimpleQA with modest effect sizes; generalizability to complex tasks uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Mahalanobis prediction (R²=0.98):** Medium. Strong empirical fit, but hinges on anisotropic subspace being adequately captured by test covariance; breaks if truth subspace is high-rank or covariance poorly estimated.
- **Spectrum via erasure:** Medium. Stratified INLP/LEACE results consistent, but orthogonality of extracted subspaces not guaranteed; LEACE may leave residual predictive information.
- **Causal distinction (+0.05, -0.07):** Low–Medium. Modest effect sizes; steering scale and layer tuned; generalization to other layers/models unclear.

## Next Checks
1. **Robustness of Mahalanobis similarity:** Recompute Mahalanobis vs. standard cosine correlations across all model scales (7B–70B) and additional domains (math reasoning, medical QA). If R² drops below 0.90 for any, revisit subspace assumption.
2. **Orthogonality of erased subspaces:** After Stratified INLP, compute pairwise cosine between extracted general and specific directions; expect near-zero. Also re-run LEACE on one domain and measure residual in-domain probe accuracy; should be at chance. If not, linear decomposition is incomplete.
3. **Steering generalizability:** Apply domain-specific and general steering directions to three held-out factual benchmarks (TruthfulQA, HellaSwag, MMLU) at multiple intervention layers (10, 20, 30). Validate that domain-specific directions improve accuracy consistently, while domain-general degrades or shows no effect. If patterns reverse or vanish, causal claims are task/layer-specific.
