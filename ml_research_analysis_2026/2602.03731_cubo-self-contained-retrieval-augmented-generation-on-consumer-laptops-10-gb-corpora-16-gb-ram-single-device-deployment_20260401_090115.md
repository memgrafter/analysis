# CUBO: Self-Contained Retrieval-Augmented Generation on Consumer Laptops 10 GB Corpora, 16 GB RAM, Single-Device Deployment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03731
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03731
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Enables retrieval-augmented generation on consumer laptops (≤16 GB RAM) with 10 GB corpora, achieving Recall@10 0.48–0.97 and 185 ms median retrieval latency using streaming ingestion, tiered hybrid retrieval, and aggressive quantization, within a 15.5 GB RAM ceiling.

## Executive Summary
CUBO addresses the need for GDPR‑compliant, local retrieval‑augmented generation on consumer laptops where cloud‑based AI risks data exfiltration and existing local systems require 18–32 GB RAM. It integrates streaming ingestion with O(1) buffer overhead, a tiered hybrid retrieval system (hot in‑memory HNSW + cold disk‑mapped IVFPQ), and hardware‑aware orchestration that aggressively unloads unused models and disables expensive features. On standard BEIR benchmarks, CUBO achieves competitive recall (0.48–0.97) and a median retrieval latency of 185 ms on an i5‑1135G7 laptop while staying under a 15.5 GB RAM limit, making air‑gapped deployment practical for sensitive professional archives where competitors fail.

## Method Summary
CUBO’s pipeline consists of ingestion, indexing, retrieval, and generation. During ingestion, documents are streamed in small batches; each batch is parsed, chunked, flushed to Parquet files on disk, and followed by an explicit `gc.collect()` to maintain constant memory overhead. The indexing manager then builds two indices: a hot HNSW index holding the 500 K most recent vectors in RAM for low‑latency search, and a cold IVFPQ index (8‑bit, m=8) that is memory‑mapped from disk, reducing storage to ~8 bytes per vector. Quantization‑Aware Routing applies a global β=0.2 correction to dense scores to compensate for systematic recall loss. At query time, dense (HNSW) and sparse (BM25) retrievals run in parallel and are fused via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (k=60). Optional reranking and enrichment are disabled in “laptop mode” to conserve memory. Generation uses a quantized 3B LLM. Orchestration auto‑detects resource constraints, enforces a 15.5 GB RAM ceiling, and lazily unloads the embedding model after 300 seconds of inactivity.

## Key Results
- **Recall@10** on BEIR: 0.48–0.97 across domains (e.g., SciFact 0.97, TREC‑COVID 0.48)
- **Median retrieval latency**: 185 ms on a consumer laptop (i5‑1135G7) in laptop mode
- **Peak RAM during ingestion**: 14.2 GB for a 9.8 GB corpus; steady‑state index size: 8.2 GB

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### 1. Streaming ingestion
Documents are processed in batches; after each flush to disk, Python’s garbage collector is invoked explicitly. This prevents accumulation of transient objects, yielding a sawtooth memory pattern with a buffer delta consistently below 50 MB regardless of corpus size. Evidence: empirical validation across 0.05–10 GB corpora (Table 10, Appendix J). Break conditions: parser leaks or failure to collect circular references could cause linear memory growth; OS file‑cache pressure might affect RSS measurements.

### 2. Tiered hybrid retrieval
A hot HNSW index keeps 500 K vectors in RAM for sub‑millisecond dense search, while a cold IVFPQ index (8‑bit, m=8) is memory‑mapped, providing disk‑backed storage at ~8 bytes/vector. Query results are fused via Reciprocal Rank Fusion; Quantization‑Aware Routing downweights dense scores by a factor β=0.2 to offset the 1–3% recall loss from quantization. Evidence: Recall@10 0.48–0.97 on BEIR within 15.5 GB RAM; steady‑state memory 8.2 GB; on SciFact, 8‑bit IVFPQ even exceeds FP32 recall by +2.1% (Appendix O). Break conditions: domain shift may invalidate β; hot index growth beyond 500 K vectors threatens the RAM budget; slow disk I/O can spike latency.

### 3. Hardware‑aware orchestration
On systems with ≤16 GB RAM or ≤6 cores, “laptop mode” is auto‑detected. The embedding model is lazily loaded and unloaded after 300 s of idle time; indices are memory‑mapped; reranking and enrichment are disabled; a global async lock prevents concurrent model swaps; CPU thread counts are tuned to avoid UI lag. Evidence: Peak RAM 14.2 GB achieved on target hardware; median latency 185 ms (Table 16). Break conditions: multiple queries within 300 s may prevent unload and risk OOM; extremely slow disks (HDD) could cause mmap page‑fault latency outliers beyond 295 ms p95.

## Foundational Learning
- **Product Quantization (PQ)**: Compresses vectors to ~8 bytes each (384× vs FP32), enabling the cold index to fit in ~200 MB for a 10 GB corpus. Despite compression, recall can improve due to reduced noise (e.g., SciFact +2.1%). Quick check: Why does 8‑bit IVFPQ sometimes exceed FP32 recall?
- **Hybrid fusion invariance (RRF)**: Reciprocal Rank Fusion converts ranks to normalized scores (k=60), avoiding scale mismatch between BM25 (unbounded) and dense scores (unbounded). This eliminates the need for per‑domain α tuning, crucial for air‑gapped deployments. Quick check: What property of Convex Combination necessitates domain‑specific α, and how does RRF avoid it?
- **Memory‑mapped I/O**: The cold index is memory‑mapped, so only active pages reside in RAM; the OS handles paging. Assumes sequential access patterns; a cold page cache initially increases latency but amortizes over queries. Quick check: How does mmap affect first‑query latency after reboot, and why is that acceptable in laptop mode?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: DeepIngestor -> Indexing Manager -> Retrieval Router -> Generation Pipeline. Resource Monitor oversees all components.
- **Critical path**: Document arrives → DeepIngestor streams chunks to Parquet → Indexing Manager builds hot HNSW (bounded) and cold IVFPQ → Query: embed → parallel HNSW+BM25 → QAR adjustment → RRF fusion → optional reranker → LLM generation → answer.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Recall vs. Memory: chose m=8 PQ over m=16 (better recall but double memory). Disabled reranking (saves 1 GB) at cost of 6% Recall@10.
  - Latency vs. Throughput: single‑threaded ingestion avoids memory spikes; concurrency increases throughput but raises peak RAM.
  - Zero‑config vs. Optimal: fixed β=0.2 and k=60 for air‑gap usability; calibrated QAR improves nDCG by +1.3–2.1% but needs 50–100 dev queries.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - OOM during ingestion: buffer delta >50 MB → check parser leaks or reduce batch size.
  - Latency >500 ms: embedding cold start (~70 ms), disabled cache, hot index not loaded, or disk slowness.
  - Recall drop in new domain: QAR mis‑calibration → run `QAR.calibrate()`.
  - Concurrency lock contention: SQLite WAL busy → reduce worker count.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Validate O(1) ingestion buffer: run `tools/validate_memory_scaling.py` on 0.05–10 GB synthetic corpora; confirm delta <150 MB.
  2. Measure tiered retrieval latency: warm up 10 queries then 100 queries; target p50 ≤185 ms.
  3. Test QAR calibration: on SciFact, compare β=0.2 vs. calibrated β; expect +1.3–2.1% nDCG gain; disabling QAR should drop Recall@10 by ~1.3%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Local RAGAS judge reliability**: Evaluation uses an uncalibrated Ollama‑hosted LLM (likely Llama‑3.1) with unspecified prompts and no inter‑judge agreement. This risks systematic bias, especially on low‑precision domains like FiQA. Resolution requires human evaluation or comparison with a GPT‑4 judge.
- **Cross‑lingual retrieval**: Multilingual embedding models (e.g., e5‑multilingual) exceed the 16 GB memory budget. Current stemming only handles morphological variation; semantic cross‑linguality remains unsupported within constraints. Evidence needed: benchmark multilingual models on MIRACL within CUBO, quantify memory/accuracy trade‑offs, and explore tiering adjustments.
- **Automatic QAR β inference**: Zero‑config β=0.2 yields suboptimal recall; manual calibration is infeasible for private corpora. An automatic method to infer β from corpus statistics (e.g., vector variance) or an online adaptation scheme is needed.

## Limitations
- Exact composition of the 9.8 GB BEIR corpus is undisclosed, risking variability in ingestion size and recall.
- Local RAGAS judge specification (model, prompts) is omitted, preventing faithful recreation of context metrics.
- Memory/latency results are validated only on a specific Windows‑11 laptop (i5‑1135G7, SSD); HDD/Linux may experience higher mmap latency or RAM overhead.
- Fixed QAR β=0.2 assumes stable quantization error; domain shifts may degrade recall without auto‑calibration.
- Streaming ingestion parser leak risk remains unproven for exotic file types.
