# AILS-NTUA at SemEval-2026 Task 10: Agentic LLMs for Psycholinguistic Marker Extraction and Conspiracy Endorsement Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04921
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04921
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** An agentic LLM pipeline achieved a 0.24 Macro F1 (100% improvement) for marker extraction and 0.79 Macro F1 (49% gain) for conspiracy detection on the development set.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a dual-system solution for SemEval-2026 Task 10 on conspiracy text analysis. For **marker extraction** (S1), it introduces a Dynamic Discriminative Chain-of-Thought (DD-CoT) with a Self-Refine loop to resolve semantic ambiguity and a deterministic verifier for character-accurate span localization. For **conspiracy endorsement detection** (S2), it proposes an Anti-Echo Chamber architecture: a Forensic Profiler computes psycholinguistic signals, a Parallel Council of adversarial jurors deliberates, and a Calibrated Judge synthesizes votes with conservative rules to avoid the Reporter Trap (misclassifying neutral reporting as endorsement). The system ranks 3rd on the S1 leaderboard and shows substantial gains over zero-shot baselines.

## Method Summary
The method is a prompting-based, no-fine-tuning agentic pipeline orchestrated via LangGraph. **S1 (Marker Extraction):** Input text triggers contrastive retrieval for few-shot examples. A Generator proposes labeled spans via DD-CoT, forcing explicit counter-arguments against plausible alternative labels. A Critic audits for frame leakage and boundary issues; a Refiner executes fixes; a final Deterministic Verifier anchors spans to exact character offsets using a tiered matching cascade, decoupling semantic reasoning from brittle localization. **S2 (Stance Detection):** A Forensic Profiler computes metrics like attribution density and agency gap. Four persona-based jurors (Prosecutor, Defense, Literalist, Profiler) independently vote using identical inputs (document, S1 markers, precedents, profiler warnings). A Calibrated Judge applies conservative rules (default to "non" on ambiguity, require structural assertion of malice) and uses forensic priors as tie-breakers to output the final binary label.

## Key Results
- **S1 Marker Extraction:** Achieved 0.24 Macro Overlap F1 on the development set, a 100% improvement over a zero-shot baseline.
- **S2 Stance Detection:** Achieved 0.79 Macro F1 on the development set, a 49% gain over baseline, with contrastive retrieval halving the false positive rate.
- **Ablation Evidence:** The DD-CoT Self-Refine loop contributed +6.7% F1 for S1; removing the Prosecutor juror caused an 11% F1 drop for S2.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dynamic Discriminative Chain-of-Thought (DD-CoT) for Marker Extraction
- **Claim:** DD-CoT improves psycholinguistic marker extraction by forcing explicit semantic discrimination between confusable label categories (e.g., ACTOR vs. VICTIM).
- **Mechanism:** The Generator produces candidate spans and must articulate (i) evidence for the chosen label and (ii) a counter-argument against at least one plausible alternative. This is embedded in a Self-Refine loop (Generator → Critic → Refiner → Deterministic Verifier). The Critic audits for frame leakage, span bloat, and reporter trap errors. The Refiner executes surgical fixes. The Verifier anchors all spans to exact character offsets via a tiered matching cascade, decoupling semantic reasoning from brittle structural localization.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states DD-CoT "forces explicit counter-arguments" and "decouples semantic LLM reasoning from deterministic span localization." Section 3.2 details the four-node loop. Section 5.1 reports a +2.7 point gain in ACTOR F1, linking to disambiguating agency in passive constructions.

### Mechanism 2: Deterministic Span Verification with Cascaded Matching
- **Claim:** Character-accurate span extraction is achieved by decoupling semantic identification (LLM) from structural localization (deterministic verifier).
- **Mechanism:** After the DD-CoT loop outputs verbatim text strings with labels, a non-LLM verifier maps each string to the source document using a five-tier cascade: (i) exact match, (ii) case-insensitive, (iii) normalized (smart quotes/whitespace), (iv) fuzzy (Levenshtein ≤15% for spans >4 chars), (v) SequenceMatcher alignment (LCS-based, ≥60% coverage). Overlaps are deduplicated.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 explicitly states the decoupling: "We explicitly decouple semantic identification from span indexing... LLMs can justify category assignments but are brittle at character-accurate localization." Appendix B provides the full verifier cascade. Section 5.1 attributes S1's doubled F1 partly to this separation.

### Mechanism 3: Anti-Echo Chamber (Parallel Council + Calibrated Judge) for Stance Detection
- **Claim:** The Reporter Trap (misclassifying neutral reporting/analysis as conspiracy endorsement) is overcome by structuring S2 as an adversarial multi-agent deliberation with a conservative adjudicator.
- **Mechanism:** A Forensic Profiler computes deterministic linguistic signals (attribution density, shouting score, JAQing, agency gap, epistemic intensity). Four persona-based jurors (Prosecutor, Defense, Literalist, Profiler) independently vote on the document using identical inputs (document, S1 markers, retrieval precedents, profiler warnings). The Calibrated Judge synthesizes votes *and* forensic signals using conservative rules: defaults to "non" on ambiguity, requires structural assertion of malice for conviction, and uses forensic priors (uncertainty_ratio, epistemic_intensity) as tie-breakers.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract introduces the "Anti-Echo Chamber" to overcome the "Reporter Trap." Section 3.3 details the council architecture and Judge's calibration rules. Table 2 shows contrastive retrieval halves the false positive rate (0.160→0.080) and the council boosts recall (+16.4%). Section 5.1 notes baseline suffers from Reporter Trap; the council "successfully disentangled conspiracy topics from conspiratorial stance."

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contrastive Few-Shot Retrieval**
  - *Why needed here:* Provides discriminative in-context examples that define decision boundaries, especially for S2 where hard negatives (non-conspiracy texts containing markers) are crucial to teach the Reporter Trap distinction.
  - *Quick check question:* Why does retrieving *similar* documents harm stance detection, and how does hard negative mining address this?
- **Concept: Multi-Agent Deliberation with Role Specialization**
  - *Why needed here:* Complex psycholinguistic judgments (agency, stance) require complementary perspectives (prosecution, defense, literal text analysis, profiling) to avoid single-perspective biases like confirmation bias or false positive over-penalty.
  - *Quick check question:* What specific failure mode does the Literalist juror mitigate that the Prosecutor might miss?
- **Concept: Psycholinguistic Forensic Signaling**
  - *Why needed here:* Quantitative text metrics (attribution density, agency gap, epistemic intensity) provide objective, context-invariant anchors to calibrate subjective LLM judgments, especially for ambiguous cases.
  - *Quick check question:* How does a high "agency gap" (passive voice proxy) inform a conspiracy endorsement decision?
- **Concept: Prompt Schema Engineering with XML/LLM Constraints**
  - *Why needed here:* Complex multi-agent workflows require strict output schemas (Pydantic) to ensure deterministic state transitions and prevent cascading errors from malformed agent outputs.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is schema-constrained generation more critical for the Refiner and Judge nodes than for the Generator?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (Document + Metadata) -> Pre-process (Contrastive Retrieval) -> S1 Pipeline (DD-CoT Generator -> Critic -> Refiner -> Deterministic Verifier) -> S2 Pipeline (Forensic Profiler -> Parallel Council (4 Jurors) -> Calibrated Judge) -> Output (Labeled Spans, Conspiracy Label)
- **Critical path:** 1. Document retrieval yields discriminative precedents. 2. S1: Generator proposes spans -> Critic flags errors -> Refiner corrects -> Verifier produces final offsets. 3. S1 markers + forensic profile + precedents feed Council. 4. Council jurors vote independently -> transcript + synthesis to Judge. 5. Judge applies forensic priors and tie-breakers to output final label.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Latency vs. Accuracy:* Adding agents increases latency 2.9× (S1) and 6.4× (S2) but improves F1 and reduces false positives.
  - *Generality vs. Specialization:* Persona-based jurors improve robustness but require careful prompt engineering; the Literalist provides rigor but may hurt recall on ironic texts.
  - *Retrieval Cost:* Hard negative mining for S2 requires 4× over-retrieval and reranking, increasing compute but essential for teaching the stance boundary.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **S1:** High-context irony where marker spans are ambiguous; extreme span boundary fuzziness (>15% edit distance) breaking verifier; rare marker types (EVIDENCE, VICTIM) still underrepresented.
  - **S2:** Council deadlock (2-2) on Poe's Law/sarcasm cases; Judge defaulting to "non" on ambiguous but potentially conspiratorial texts; subreddit context misclassification (e.g., r/conspiracy posts treated as analysis).
  - **Systemic:** Latency makes real-time use challenging; Qwen-8B portability suffers from reduced schema fidelity.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline vs. DD-CoT:** Run S1 with/without the Critic/Refiner loop on a balanced dev sample to isolate the +6.7% F1 gain from Self-Refine.
  2. **Council Ablation:** Run S2 with Leave-One-Out jurors to confirm the Prosecutor's primary role (11% F1 drop when removed) and Defense's precision role.
  3. **Retrieval Contrast:** Compare standard similarity-based retrieval vs. hard-negative contrastive retrieval for S2, measuring false positive rate on a held-out set of reporter-trap examples.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1  
- Question: How can discourse-level context (e.g., thread structure, user history) be integrated into the agentic LLM pipeline to improve detection of high-context irony and implicit stance?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "we did not incorporate discourse-level context such as thread structure, parent comments, or user posting history, which could help disambiguize irony, sarcasm, and implicit stance, our primary failure modes." (Limitations section)  
- Why unresolved: The current system processes only single-document text, missing conversational and user-level cues that are crucial for interpreting irony and nuanced stance.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation experiments adding thread-parent features or user posting histories to the input, evaluated on irony/implicit stance subsets of the dataset, with statistical comparisons of F1 scores.  

### Open Question 2  
- Question: Does supervised fine-tuning on task-specific data improve marker boundary precision and stance classification accuracy compared to pure prompting?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "we did not fine-tune any model on the task data, which could improve both marker boundary precision and stance classification through task-specific supervision." (Limitations section)  
- Why unresolved: The authors speculate fine-tuning could help but provide no empirical evidence; the relative benefits of prompting vs. fine-tuning for this dual-task setting remain unknown.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Fine-tune the base LLM (e.g., GPT-5.2) on the SemEval training set for both S1 (span extraction) and S2 (classification), then compare macro F1 to the agentic prompting baseline using paired significance tests.  

### Open Question 3  
- Question: What is the impact of systematic human evaluation on the interpretability and reliability of the extracted psycholinguistic markers?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "we did not perform systematic human evaluation of extracted markers; such analysis could quantify interpretability gains beyond automated overlap metrics." (Limitations section)  
- Why unresolved: The system’s claims of interpretability rely solely on character-level IoU; human judgment of whether spans are semantically coherent and psychologically meaningful is lacking.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct a crowdsourcing or expert annotation study where humans rate the validity and clarity of extracted spans (vs. baselines) and correlate these ratings with automated metrics.  

### Open Question 4  
- Question: Can ensemble or distillation strategies maintain the agentic pipeline’s performance while reducing dependence on proprietary large models?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "we did not explore ensembles of heterogeneous models or distillation strategies to reduce dependence on a proprietary backbone while preserving agentic reasoning." (Limitations section)  
- Why unresolved: The current system uses GPT-5.2, limiting accessibility and scalability; portability to smaller open-weights models (e.g., Qwen-3-8B) showed performance drops, but ensembles/distillation were not tested.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement heterogeneous ensembles (e.g., combining outputs from multiple 8B models) or distill the multi-agent workflow into a single smaller model, then evaluate whether S1/S2 performance approaches the GPT-5.2 system on dev/test sets.

## Limitations
- Proprietary GPT-5.2 dependency; open-model portability suffers significantly.
- Known failures on high-context irony, implicit stance, and reporter ambiguity; conservative Judge lowers recall.
- Significant latency (×2.9 S1, ×6.4 S2) limits real-time use.
- Reddit-only corpus; cross-domain robustness untested.

## Confidence
- **High:** Deterministic verification (explicitly credited for boundary gains), Anti-Echo Chamber (contrastive retrieval halves false positives; council ablation >10% F1 impact), overall dev results.
- **Medium:** DD-CoT (self-ablation +6.7% F1; mechanism inferred), Forensic profiler (used but not isolated).
- **Low:** Portability to open models (Qwen-8B drop noted but not systematically explored).
