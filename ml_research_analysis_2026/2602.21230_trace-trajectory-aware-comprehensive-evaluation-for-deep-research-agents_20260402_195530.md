# TRACE: Trajectory-Aware Comprehensive Evaluation for Deep Research Agents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21230
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21230
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: TRACE reveals that high Pass@1 deep research agents often have lower overall utility due to poor efficiency and cognitive quality, while more balanced agents achieve higher utility despite lower Pass@1.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the evaluation crisis for Deep Research Agents, where outcome-only metrics like Pass@1 create a "high-score illusion" by ignoring process quality, and static benchmarks fail to measure robustness and latent capability. It introduces TRACE, a trajectory-aware evaluation framework featuring a Hierarchical Trajectory Utility Function that holistically scores accuracy, process efficiency, and cognitive quality (evidence grounding, robustness). TRACE also includes Scaffolded Capability Assessment (via minimum hint rate λ_min) and a purpose-built benchmark (DeepResearch-Bench) with embedded information traps. Evaluations on state-of-the-art agents show a critical divergence: high Pass@1 scores often correspond to lower overall utility due to inefficiency and untrustworthiness, while agents with better process quality achieve higher utility. TRACE's diagnostic metrics uncover trade-offs entirely missed by Pass@1, demonstrating the necessity of multi-dimensional evaluation.

## Method Summary
TRACE evaluates deep research agents by analyzing their full trajectories (sequences of actions and observations) rather than just final answers. The core is a Hierarchical Trajectory Utility Function U(H) that multiplies an accuracy gate by a weighted geometric mean of Process Efficiency E(H) and Cognitive Quality C(H). E(H) measures how efficiently the agent gains relevant information via Marginal Information Gain (MIG) and penalizes redundant exploration. C(H) combines Evidence Grounding G_E (using NLI to score claim-evidence entailment) and Reasoning Robustness R_R (measuring recovery latency from embedded information traps). Additionally, Scaffolded Capability Assessment determines the minimum hint rate λ_min needed for an agent to achieve a target success rate, formalizing its Zone of Proximal Development. The DeepResearch-Bench benchmark provides 650 tasks with oracle trajectories and strategically placed traps for robustness testing. The evaluation pipeline generates trajectories, computes metrics, and for scaffolding iteratively tests varying hint fractions.

## Key Results
- Agents like DeepSeek-V3.1-671B achieve high Pass@1 (65.8%) but lower overall utility (0.65) due to poor efficiency and cognitive quality.
- More balanced agents like AgentFounder-30B achieve higher utility (0.81) despite lower Pass@1 (60.1%).
- TRACE metrics (E, C, G_E, R_R, λ_min, TRS) successfully uncover trade-offs in efficiency, trustworthiness, self-correction, and latent potential entirely missed by Pass@1.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Hierarchical Trajectory Utility Function \(U(H)\) counteracts the "high-score illusion" by holistically scoring the entire problem-solving process, not just the final answer.
- **Mechanism:** \(U(H)\) multiplies an accuracy gate \(I(J(A_{\text{final}}, A_{gt}))\) by a weighted geometric mean of Process Efficiency \(E(H)\) and Cognitive Quality \(C(H)\). The geometric mean ensures that a severe deficiency in any single component (e.g., very low evidence grounding) disproportionately reduces the overall score, enforcing a "weakest link" principle.
- **Core assumption:** A high-quality research trajectory must be simultaneously accurate, efficient, and cognitively sound; a fatal flaw in any one dimension renders the overall process untrustworthy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "To counter the 'high-score illusion', we propose a Hierarchical Trajectory Utility Function that quantifies process efficiency and cognitive quality, including evidence grounding, alongside accuracy."
  - [section 3.1] "The geometric mean formulation is chosen... because it reflects the principle that a research process is only as strong as its weakest link."
  - [corpus] *Assumption: The provided corpus shows related work on evaluation benchmarks but does not directly anchor the specific mathematical formulation of the geometric mean utility. The evidence is from the paper itself.*
- **Break condition:** If the geometric mean fails to produce a sufficiently distinct ranking compared to a weighted arithmetic mean when components vary widely, the "weakest link" principle would not be operationalized.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Scaffolded Capability Assessment (via \(\lambda_{\text{min}}\)) quantifies an agent's latent problem-solving potential by measuring the minimum external guidance required to achieve a target success rate.
- **Mechanism:** For a given difficult task, \(\lambda_{\text{min}}\) is the smallest fraction \(\lambda\) of an oracle trajectory provided as a hint such that the agent's expected success rate meets or exceeds a threshold \(\theta_{\text{succ}}\). It formalizes a "Zone of Proximal Development" for AI agents.
- **Core assumption:** An agent's intrinsic capability is better revealed by its performance with minimal, structured support rather than by its unassisted success rate on extremely hard tasks where it may always fail.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "To measure deeper attributes, TRACE introduces a Scaffolded Capability Assessment protocol, quantifying an agent's latent ability by determining the minimum guidance needed for success."
  - [section 3.2.1] "The Minimum Hint Rate (\(\lambda_{\text{min}}\)) is the solution to the following constrained optimization problem... \(\lambda_{\text{min}}\) offers a continuous measure of an agent’s intrinsic capability."
  - [corpus] *Assumption: Corpus papers discuss scaffolding for pedagogy [3] and benchmarks, but TRACE's specific operationalization via hint fraction and oracle trajectories is novel to this work.*
- **Break condition:** If the hint function \(h(\lambda)\) (e.g., providing the first \(\lambda\)-fraction of steps) does not correlate with genuine improvement in the agent's success, the metric would not measure latent potential but merely sensitivity to specific prompt engineering.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The DeepResearch-Bench benchmark, with strategically embedded "information traps," enables the measurement of an agent's Reasoning Robustness (\(R_R\)), i.e., its ability to self-correct after encountering misleading information.
- **Mechanism:** Certain tasks contain plausible but incorrect information (traps). \(R_R\) is computed as an exponential decay of the average recovery latency—the number of steps an agent takes after a trap encounter to find a productive path (positive Marginal Information Gain).
- **Core assumption:** Robustness is a distinct capability from accuracy; it is demonstrated by an agent's critical thinking and ability to escape from erroneous information states, which static benchmarks cannot provoke or measure.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "To systematically evaluate attributes like robustness, we constructed the DeepResearch-Bench. This benchmark features tasks... with strategically embedded 'information traps'... enabling the measurement of these previously hard-to-quantify characteristics."
  - [section 3.1.2] "The robustness score is an exponential decay function of the average recovery latency... directly quantifies an agent’s critical thinking and self-correction capabilities."
  - [section 4.3] "DeepResearch-Bench... strategically embed 'information traps' for robustness testing."
  - [corpus] *Assumption: Corpus papers mention robustness in benchmarks (e.g., GAIA, WebWalker) but do not describe the controlled embedding of traps with a defined recovery latency metric.*
- **Break condition:** If trap information is not sufficiently plausible or if the definition of "productive recovery" (via MIG) does not accurately reflect a return to a valid reasoning path, the \(R_R\) score would be noisy and unreliable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Weighted Geometric Mean & "Weakest Link" Principle**
  - *Why needed here:* Understanding why \(U(H)\) uses a geometric mean (Eq. 1) is critical to grasp how TRACE penalizes catastrophic failures (e.g., hallucinations) more severely than a linear score would.
  - *Quick check question:* Why would an agent with 100% accuracy but 0% evidence grounding receive a utility score of 0 under TRACE's design?
- **Concept: Marginal Information Gain (MIG)**
  - *Why needed here:* MIG \(g_t\) is the core signal for detecting redundant exploration (the REP penalty) and defining recovery from traps. It operationalizes "novel relevance."
  - *Quick check question:* How does the formula \(g_t = \max(0, \cos(\Phi(o_t), \Phi(A_{gt})) - \sup_{i<t}\{\cos(\Phi(o_i), \Phi(A_{gt})))\) ensure that only *new* relevant information is rewarded?
- **Concept: Natural Language Inference (NLI) for Evidence Grounding**
  - *Why needed here:* \(G_E\) uses an NLI model to score entailment between atomic claims and cited evidence. This is a technical prerequisite for implementing the trustworthiness component.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is using an NLI model's entailment probability more sensitive to hallucinations than a simpler ROUGE or BLEU score between claim and evidence?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Trajectory Generator (agent interacting with environment) produces trajectory H; Utility Calculator computes E(H), C(H), G_E, R_R and combines with accuracy gate via geometric mean to yield U(H); Scaffold Assessor (using oracle trajectories) finds λ_min; Benchmark Suite (DeepResearch-Bench) provides tasks, ground-truth answers, and oracle trajectories. Flow: Trajectory Generator -> Utility Calculator -> Metrics Storage. Scaffold Assessor operates in a separate loop.
- **Critical path:** The primary evaluation flow is: Generate Trajectory -> Compute Utility -> Store Metrics. The Scaffolded Assessment runs in a separate, more expensive loop that requires oracle trajectories.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Geometric Mean Sensitivity: Ensures failure visibility but can make scores very low if any single claim is weakly grounded. Tradeoff: extreme sensitivity vs. robustness to minor noise.
  - Trap Design: Traps must be plausible enough to deceive but not so common that tasks become unsolvable. Controlled complexity C(q) is needed to isolate robustness from raw difficulty.
  - Oracle Requirement: Scaffolded Capability Assessment requires perfect oracle trajectories for each task, increasing benchmark construction cost but enabling precise λ_min calculation.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High Pass@1 + Low U(H): Classic "high-score illusion." Agent gets correct answers via inefficient, costly, or untrustworthy trajectories.
  - Very High λ_min: Agent is fundamentally brittle; requires extensive guidance even on hard tasks, indicating weak latent reasoning.
  - Low R_R Score: Agent repeatedly falls into traps and shows poor recovery, indicating lack of critical thinking or self-correction.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Comprehensive Re-evaluation (RQ1): Run full TRACE suite on SOTA agents (e.g., DeepSeek-V3.1, AgentFounder). Goal: Demonstrate divergence between Pass@1 and U(H) rankings to validate the "illusion."
  2. Ablation on TRACE-Robustness Subset (RQ2): Compare R_R scores and Pass@1 drop rates on trap-heavy tasks for agents with different training data (e.g., WebSailor-V2 vs. others). Goal: Show R_R provides granular insight into robustness that Pass@1 misses.
  3. Controlled Training Paradigm Study (RQ3): Apply different post-training methods (AgentFounder, ReSum, WebSailor-V2) to the same base model (Qwen-30B-Base) and evaluate. Goal: Use TRACE metrics (E, G_E, R_R, λ_min) to fingerprint the specific capabilities injected by each method.

## Limitations
- Lack of public DeepResearch-Bench dataset prevents full reproduction.
- Insufficient specification of trap design and annotation hinders R_R replication.
- Undocumented oracle trajectory format, hint mechanism, and judge prompts lead to implementation guesswork.

## Confidence
- High: The "high-score illusion" exists (data divergence in paper).
- Medium: U(H) and submetrics (E, C, G_E, R_R) are valid (theory sound but benchmark-dependent).
- Low: λ_min as latent capability metric (oracle reliance, high variance risk).
