# When Fusion Helps and When It Breaks: View-Aligned Robustness in Same-Source Financial Imaging

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11020
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11020
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Early fusion suffers negative transfer under label ambiguity; late fusion becomes reliable after label stabilization and degrades more gracefully under view-constrained attacks, yet both are vulnerable to tiny perturbations and joint attacks; consistency regularization improves alignment but its robustness effect is conditional.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates multi-view financial image prediction using same-source OHLCV charts and technical indicator matrices. It introduces an ex-post minimum-movement filter to mitigate label ambiguity from near-zero price movements and compares early (channel-stacked) and late (dual-branch) fusion under white-box ℓ∞ attacks. Key findings indicate that fusion benefits are regime-dependent: early fusion can suffer negative transfer in noisier settings (low threshold τ), while late fusion becomes reliably beneficial once labels are stabilized (sufficiently high τ). Both fusion types are highly vulnerable to minuscule pixel perturbations (≤1/255). Late fusion leverages redundancy to degrade more gracefully under view-constrained attacks (one perturbed view), but joint attacks on both views still cause severe degradation. Cross-view consistency regularization improves branch alignment but its impact on robustness is conditional. Evaluation employs leakage-resistant time splits and view-aligned threat modeling to isolate fusion effects.

## Method Summary
The authors derive two aligned 256×256 grayscale images from daily SGE gold spot data (2005–2025): an OHLCV candlestick chart with volume subpanel (`ohlcv`) and a 15×15 technical indicator matrix upsampled to 256×256 (`indic`). To reduce label noise, they filter samples where the absolute next‑day return is below a threshold τ (minimum‑movement filter). For training and evaluation, they use leakage‑resistant time‑block splits with an embargo to prevent temporal contamination. Models compare early fusion (single encoder processing stacked channels) versus late fusion (dual encoders feeding a fusion head) with optional cross‑view consistency regularization (symmetric KL divergence between branch logits). Adversarial robustness is assessed using white‑box ℓ∞ FGSM/PGD attacks in raw pixel space, with view‑constrained (perturb one view) and joint (perturb both) threat models. Performance is measured via Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) across seeds.

## Key Results
- Fusion effectiveness is regime‑dependent: early fusion can suffer negative transfer under noisy label regimes (low τ), while late fusion becomes more reliable once labels stabilize (higher τ).
- Both fusion architectures are highly vulnerable to ℓ∞ attacks, with performance degrading sharply even at budgets ≤1/255.
- Under view‑constrained attacks, late fusion degrades more gracefully due to an intact evidence path, but joint attacks on both views overwhelm redundancy and cause rapid performance drops.
- Cross‑view consistency regularization improves cross‑branch alignment, but its effect on clean performance and robustness is conditional and non‑monotonic with respect to τ and λ.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Fusion effectiveness is regime‑dependent, determined by label ambiguity via `min_move`.
- **Mechanism:** The `min_move` (τ) threshold filters samples with near‑zero next‑day returns, reducing label noise. In noisy regimes (low τ), ambiguous labels cause early fusion (channel stacking) to suffer negative transfer—combining noisy views harms performance. Once labels stabilize (sufficiently high τ), complementary signals in both views become exploitable, making multi‑view learning beneficial.
- **Core assumption:** Near‑zero price movements create weakly defined direction labels that are easily overwhelmed by noise, harming multi‑view representation learning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "fusion is regime dependent: early fusion can suffer negative transfer under noisier settings, whereas late fusion is a more reliable default once labels stabilize."
  - [section 6.1]: Figure 2 shows MCC near zero for τ ∈ {0, 0.002, 0.004}, then rises sharply for τ ≥ 0.006; early fusion (`lite-cnn | both`) is negative at τ=0.004 but positive at τ=0.006.
  - [corpus]: Corpus neighbors discuss multimodal fusion broadly but do not provide direct evidence for this regime‑dependent mechanism; evidence is anchored in the paper's own experiments.
- **Break condition:** Overly strict filtering (e.g., τ = 0.010) reduces sample size drastically, increasing variance and destabilizing training, as noted in Section 6.1 ("drop at τ=0.010 aligns with reduced effective sample size").

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Late fusion (dual‑branch) provides robustness advantages under view‑constrained attacks by preserving an unperturbed evidence path.
- **Mechanism:** Under attacks that perturb only one view (e.g., `indic`), the unperturbed branch in late fusion can still contribute predictive signal through the fusion head, leading to more graceful degradation. Early fusion mixes signals at the input level, so a perturbation in one channel directly corrupts the combined representation.
- **Core assumption:** The two views contain partially independent predictive signals; corrupting one does not destroy all useful information if the other remains intact.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "late fusion often degrades more gracefully under view‑constrained attacks, leveraging redundancy."
  - [section 6.2]: Figures 3 and 4 show late‑fusion curves (`*-cons`) are flatter than early fusion under single‑view attacks; Table 2 shows `Lite‑CNN‑Cons (λ=1)` has `∆worst = -0.150` for `ohlcv` attack vs. early fusion's `-0.193`.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "Defending multimodal fusion models against single‑source adversaries" [20] support the concept of view‑constrained threat modeling but do not provide domain‑specific evidence.
- **Break condition:** Joint attacks perturbing both views simultaneously overwhelm the redundancy, causing rapid degradation even for late fusion, as shown in Table 2 and Appendix J.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Cross‑view consistency regularization primarily improves branch alignment, with conditional and non‑monotonic effects on robustness.
- **Mechanism:** The consistency term (`L_cons`) forces branch logits to be similar, aligning representations. This can act as a regularizer that stabilizes training under certain label regimes (τ), but its impact on adversarial robustness is indirect and secondary to the fusion architecture itself.
- **Core assumption:** Predictive signals for the same label should be coherent across views; enforcing this coherence at the logit level improves generalization and may indirectly affect robustness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "consistency regularization primarily improves cross‑branch alignment while its effects on clean performance and robustness are conditional."
  - [section 6.1 & Appendix H]: Table H.7 shows increasing λ sharply increases `agree_ab` and reduces `sym_kl`, but fused MCC improvements are modest and τ‑dependent (e.g., for Lite‑CNN, λ=0.5 best at τ=0.006, λ=1 best at τ=0.008).
  - [section 6.2]: Robustness rankings among λ values shift with τ (Appendix I), indicating conditional effects.
- **Break condition:** Mismatched λ for a given operating point (τ) can hurt clean performance without providing robustness gains, as seen in the non‑monotonic MCC trends.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Ex‑post Minimum‑Movement Filtering (`min_move`)
- **Why needed here:** Controls label ambiguity from near‑zero price moves, which dominate the unconditional next‑day direction task and make learning nearly impossible. It defines a conditional evaluation regime, not an inference rule.
- **Quick check question:** If τ is increased, what happens to sample size and label clarity, and why might performance drop at very high τ?

### Concept: View‑Aligned Threat Modeling
- **Why needed here:** In multi‑view inputs (channel‑stacked), attacks must be mapped to specific views (`ohlcv` vs. `indic`) to assess whether fusion provides robustness via redundancy when only one view is corrupted.
- **Quick check question:** How does the view–channel mapping (Section 3.3) enable comparing a single‑view model's robustness to a two‑view model under a targeted attack on the same view?

### Concept: Leakage‑Resistant Time Splits with Embargo
- **Why needed here:** Sliding window inputs cause look‑ahead leakage in random splits. Embargoed time‑block splits ensure temporal generalization by preventing data contamination across train/val/test boundaries.
- **Quick check question:** Why is an embargo necessary even after blocking by time, and how does it differ from simple random splitting?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
1. **Dual‑view generator:** Takes a rolling window of OHLCV data and outputs two aligned 256×256 grayscale images: `ohlcv` (candlestick+volume) and `indic` (15×15 indicator matrix upsampled).
2. **Channel mapper:** For two‑view inputs, stacks views as `[v_ohlcv; v_indic] ∈ ℝ²×256×256`. Single‑view models place their view in channel 0.
3. **Fusion backbones:**
   - *Early fusion:* Single encoder (e.g., ResNet18‑P adapted for C=2) processes stacked channels.
   - *Late fusion:* Dual encoders (each C=1) process separate views; features concatenated before fusion head. Optional consistency term enforces branch logit similarity.
4. **Training protocol:** AdamW, leak‑avoiding normalization (stats from train only), early stopping on validation MCC, leakage‑resistant time‑block split with embargo.
5. **Robustness evaluation:** White‑box ℓ∞ attacks (FGSM/PGD) on raw pixel space; view‑constrained (perturb one view) vs. joint (perturb both views).

### Critical path
Data generation → Filtering by `min_move` → Time‑block split with embargo → Model training (with/without consistency) → Clean test evaluation → Adversarial attack per view‑channel mapping → MCC degradation analysis.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Early vs. late fusion:** Early fusion is simpler (single encoder) but prone to negative transfer under label noise. Late fusion adds parameters and complexity (two encoders + fusion head) but offers robustness under view‑constrained attacks and is more reliable once labels stabilize.
- **Consistency regularization (λ):** Increases cross‑branch alignment but its benefit is τ‑dependent and non‑monotonic; tuning is required per operating point.
- **Backbone choice:** Lite‑CNN shows cleaner trends; ResNet18‑P (pretrained) exhibits higher variance and severe vulnerability to `indic` attacks, possibly due to inductive biases.

### Failure signatures
- **Early fusion collapse:** Negative MCC under weak `min_move` filtering (e.g., τ=0.004 for Lite‑CNN) indicating negative transfer.
- **Saturated view failure:** ResNet18‑P robustness curves for `indic` attacks drop to MCC ≈ -1.0 even at 0.25/255, showing near‑total predictive signal loss in that view.
- **Joint attack overwhelm:** Late fusion still suffers large `∆worst` under joint perturbations (e.g., `Lite‑CNN‑Cons` `∆worst ≈ -0.192`), limiting worst‑case guarantees.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Clean baseline sweep across τ:** Train all models (single‑view, early/late fusion) for τ ∈ {0, 0.002, ..., 0.010}. Plot MCC vs. τ to identify the operating point where multi‑view becomes beneficial (e.g., τ≥0.006 for Lite‑CNN). This establishes the label‑stabilization prerequisite.
2. **View‑constrained PGD attack at chosen τ (e.g., 0.006):** For each two‑view model, run PGD (ϵ=1/255) separately on `ohlcv` channel and `indic` channel. Compare degradation to single‑view baselines. Expect late fusion to show flatter curves under single‑view attacks.
3. **Joint PGD attack on two‑view models:** Perturb both channels simultaneously at same budget. Verify that degradation is worse than view‑constrained case for all fusion types, confirming redundancy limits.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do Transformer‑based architectures perform in same‑source multi‑view financial imaging, and do the findings on fusion regime dependence and robustness generalize beyond convolutional backbones?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly state in the Contributions and Discussion that extending the benchmark to Transformer‑based architectures remains an important future direction, as their study is limited to convolutional backbones (Lite‑CNN and ResNet18‑P).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only evaluates CNN‑based models, which may have different inductive biases for processing spatial patterns in financial images. Transformers could capture long‑range dependencies or global context differently, potentially altering the trade‑offs between early/late fusion and adversarial robustness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train Vision Transformers or similar architectures on the same dual‑view dataset, apply early and late fusion with optional consistency, and conduct view‑aligned ℓ∞ robustness evaluations under varied τ regimes; compare results to CNN baselines to assess generalizability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Beyond ℓ∞ pixel‑space attacks, what is the robustness profile of multi‑view financial image models under alternative threat models (e.g., ℓ2 attacks, spatial transformations, or feature‑space attacks), and do late fusion advantages persist under these scenarios?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note that their threat model is restricted to pixel‑space ℓ∞ perturbations and that broader threat models (e.g., other norms or realistic financial manipulations) are valuable extensions, as mentioned in the Contributions and limitations (Section 7).
- **Why unresolved:** The study focuses solely on small ℓ∞ attacks as a representation‑level stress test, but financial adversaries might operate in other domains (e.g., altering raw price sequences or applying semantic changes), which could expose different vulnerabilities or mitigate the observed view‑dependent asymmetries.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement and evaluate adversarial attacks under ℓ2 norms, spatial corruptions (e.g., rotations, scaling), or attacks on time‑series inputs before rendering; measure degradation curves for fusion strategies and compare to ℓ∞ results to determine if late fusion’s graceful degradation under view‑constrained attacks holds universally.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What precise factors (e.g., τ, backbone architecture, consistency weight λ) govern the conditional effectiveness of cross‑view consistency regularization on adversarial robustness, and what mechanism underlies its non‑monotonic impact?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper reports that consistency regularization improves cross‑branch logit alignment but its robustness effects are conditional and can shift with τ and λ (Section 6.2, Appendix H and I). For instance, optimal λ varies between τ=0.006 and τ=0.008, and branch diagnostics show alignment without clear robustness gains.
- **Why unresolved:** While consistency is shown to reduce branch disagreement (e.g., lower sym_KL), the link to robustness is unclear—it may enhance redundancy or smooth decision boundaries, but the paper does not isolate why benefits materialize only in certain regimes or architectures.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a controlled study varying τ, λ, and model types, logging intermediate features, gradient magnitudes, and certified robustness metrics; analyze whether consistency induces feature‑level redundancy that cushions view‑constrained attacks or if it inadvertently increases sensitivity to joint perturbations.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter details missing:** Critical learning rates, weight decay, and batch sizes are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **Single‑dataset evaluation:** Results are limited to SGE gold spot data; generalizability across instruments and markets is not established.
- **Inconsistent exploration of consistency regularization:** The λ weight is swept only sparsely (λ ∈ {0,0.5,1}) and its interaction with τ is not systematically studied, weakening conditional robustness claims.

## Confidence
- **Regime‑dependent fusion effectiveness:** Medium (strong internal evidence but limited to two backbones and one dataset).
- **Late‑fusion robustness under view‑constrained attacks:** High (repeatedly flatter degradation curves and smaller Δ_worst across models).
- **Conditional impact of cross‑view consistency:** Low (non‑monotonic, τ‑specific effects without comprehensive analysis).
