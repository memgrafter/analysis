# Towards Batch-to-Streaming Deep Reinforcement Learning for Continuous Control

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.08588
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08588
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Streaming versions of SAC and TD3 (S2AC, SDAC) match state-of-the-art streaming baselines on MuJoCo and DM Control without per-environment hyperparameter tuning, and enable batch-to-streaming finetuning via SGDC critic pre‑training.

## Executive Summary
Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) traditionally relies on replay buffers and batch updates for stable learning. Streaming RL eliminates these buffers, enabling on‑device training but existing streaming methods are incompatible with standard DRL algorithms like SAC and TD3, hindering practical finetuning. This paper proposes Streaming Soft Actor-Critic (S2AC) and Streaming Deterministic Actor-Critic (SDAC), which adapt SAC and TD3 to purely online updates. Key modifications include eligibility traces, the Overshooting‑bounded Gradient Descent (ObGD) optimizer, online state normalization, reward scaling, sparse initialization, and LayerNorm. S2AC dynamically scales its entropy coefficient by the running reward standard deviation, while SDAC injects target noise for stability. Both algorithms achieve competitive performance on MuJoCo Gym and DM Control Suite without per‑environment tuning. Furthermore, the authors reveal that switching from Adam (used in batch pre‑training) to ObGD during finetuning often fails due to enlarged critic weight norms. Pre‑training the critic with SGDC—an optimizer resembling ObGD—keeps norms small, enabling successful batch‑to‑streaming finetuning and even surpassing from‑scratch streaming performance in some tasks with fewer samples.

## Method Summary
S2AC and SDAC are streaming adaptations of SAC and TD3, respectively, designed for continuous control without replay buffers. They share a core architecture: a stochastic (S2AC) or deterministic (SDAC) actor updated with Adam, and a critic updated with ObGD using eligibility traces (TD(λ)) to smooth the noisy online gradient signal. Both algorithms employ online normalization of observations and scaling of rewards using running statistics (Welford’s algorithm) to maintain stable input distributions. S2AC additionally scales its entropy coefficient by the reward standard deviation, while SDAC adds Gaussian noise to the critic’s target actions to regularize learning. For batch‑to‑streaming finetuning, the paper recommends pre‑training the critic with SGDC (a clipped SGD variant) instead of Adam, as this yields smaller weight norms and retains plasticity when switching to ObGD. Hyperparameters (e.g., learning rates, λ, κ) are largely environment‑agnostic, though S2AC requires a small set of environment‑specific entropy coefficients. The method is evaluated on MuJoCo and DM Control Suite benchmarks, comparing against previous streaming baselines and batch counterparts.

## Key Results
- S2AC and SDAC achieve performance comparable to state‑of‑the‑art streaming baselines on MuJoCo and DM Control without per‑environment hyperparameter tuning.
- Batch‑to‑streaming finetuning succeeds when the critic is pre‑trained with SGDC, leading to stable learning and sometimes outperforming from‑scratch streaming with fewer samples.
- Switching the critic optimizer from Adam to ObGD during finetuning causes failure due to large critic weight norms; SGDC pre‑training effectively mitigates this issue.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Streaming stability is achieved by combining eligibility traces with the ObGD optimizer, which prevents overshooting in single‑sample updates.
- **Mechanism:** Eligibility traces (TD(λ)) extend credit assignment across timesteps, smoothing the noisy gradient signal inherent to online updates. ObGD constrains the update magnitude based on the observed TD error, avoiding large destabilizing steps common with Adam in non‑stationary streaming settings.
- **Core assumption:** The optimization landscape for the value function is sufficiently smooth that constraining step size relative to temporal difference error prevents divergence.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions inclusion of eligibility traces and ObGD; Section 2.2 describes Stream AC(λ) using these components; prior work (Elsayed et al., 2024) empirically validates the combination.
- **Break condition:** If environment dynamics change faster than the trace decay (λ), credit assignment becomes misaligned, potentially destabilizing learning.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Online state normalization and reward scaling mitigate activation distribution shifts and maintain consistent gradient scales across streaming updates.
- **Mechanism:** Running estimates of state mean/variance (Welford, 1962) normalize inputs to each layer, reducing internal covariate shift. Reward scaling by a running standard deviation keeps the magnitude of TD errors and policy gradients in a stable range, preventing the optimizer from acting on wildly different scales as reward statistics evolve.
- **Core assumption:** The non‑stationarity in streaming RL is primarily due to evolving data distributions, which normalization statistics can track adequately online.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract lists state normalization and reward scaling; Section 3 details the use of LayerNorm, observation normalization, and reward scaling; Section 4.2 ablations show performance boost when adding these techniques to batch SAC/TD3.
- **Break condition:** Abrupt, non‑Gaussian shifts in state or reward distributions could cause the running statistics to lag, resulting in poorly scaled inputs and unstable updates.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Successful batch‑to‑streaming finetuning requires architectural compatibility in the critic’s optimizer; pre‑training with SGDC (which resembles ObGD) yields smaller weight norms and retains plasticity for adaptation.
- **Mechanism:** Adam during pre‑training leads to large critic weight norms, which prior work associates with reduced plasticity in networks with LayerNorm. SGDC’s clipping behavior acts as an implicit regularizer, keeping norms smaller. Smaller norms preserve the network’s ability to adapt when switching to ObGD‑based streaming updates.
- **Core assumption:** Critic weight norm magnitude is a reliable proxy for network plasticity under distribution shift, and optimizer choice causally influences this norm.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3 shows switching optimizers causes finetuning failures due to large weight norms; pre‑training with SGDC reduces norms and enables finetuning; Figure 5 visualizes the norm difference; external literature (Pascanu et al., 2025; Lyle et al., 2024) provides theoretical context.
- **Break condition:** If the pre‑training task is too dissimilar to the finetuning target (e.g., different state‑action manifold), even small weight norms may not suffice for adaptation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
Environment → Online Normalizer (state & reward) → Actor → Critic → (ObGD for critic, Adam for actor) → repeat.

**Critical path:**  
1. Environment step  
2. Normalization (state, reward)  
3. Actor selects action  
4. Action executed  
5. TD error computed  
6. Critic trace updated  
7. Critic ObGD update  
8. Actor Adam update  
9. Repeat

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **S2AC vs. SDAC:** Maximum entropy (S2AC) provides exploration but requires adaptive temperature tuning (α/σ₍ᵣ₎); deterministic (SDAC) is simpler but needs explicit target noise for stability.  
- **Traces (λ):** Higher λ improves credit assignment but increases bias if transitions are non‑Markovian.  
- **ObGD κ:** Larger κ allows larger steps but risks overshooting; generally robust across environments.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Sudden performance drop after N steps → likely optimizer instability or exploding weight norms; monitor critic L2 norm.  
- Policy becomes deterministic/pessimistic (S2AC) → fixed α with drifting σ₍ᵣ₎ causes imbalance; monitor normalized reward magnitude.  
- No learning in SDAC → missing target noise (ϵ₂) in critic target; verify implementation includes N(0,0.1²) as per Table 8.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **From‑scratch replication:** Train S2AC and SDAC on HalfCheetah‑v4 for 2 M steps; verify curve matches Figure 1/9; track σ₍ᵣ₎, α (if S2AC), and critic weight norm.  
2. **Adaptive α ablation:** Run S2AC with fixed α=0.2 vs. α/σ₍ᵣ₎; expect divergence or poor final performance when σ₍ᵣ₎ drifts.  
3. **Optimizer compatibility test:** Pre‑train TD3‑norm critic with Adam vs. SGDC for 1 M steps, measure critic L2 norm; then switch to SDAC finetuning. SGDC should keep norms low and learning stable, Adam should cause collapse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### How to improve SDAC finetuning on quadruped‑run?
- **Basis:** Explicitly stated as an important open challenge.
- **Why unresolved:** Current modifications (SGDC, noise) yield limited improvement; underlying failure modes remain unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Systematic comparison of modifications (architectures, traces, exploration) on quadruped‑run, identification of predictive metrics.

### Why is S2AC finetuning unstable and entropy‑sensitive?
- **Basis:** Explicitly noted that S2AC finetuning results are not competitive and the entropy coefficient is sensitive.
- **Why unresolved:** Interaction of reward scaling, adaptive entropy, and streaming updates is not understood.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Ablation of entropy scheduling, scaling, and update frequency; correlate entropy drift with performance.

### Why does Adam pre‑training cause large critic weight norms and finetuning failure?
- **Basis:** Inferred from empirical results showing Adam produces larger norms than SGDC, hypothesized to affect plasticity.
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical mechanism linking optimizer dynamics to weight norm growth under LayerNorm is lacking.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Compare weight trajectories, gradients, and curvature between Adam and SGDC; model optimizer dynamics.

## Limitations
- **ObGD and normalization implementation details are not fully specified**, requiring external code or educated guesses for faithful reproduction.
- **Theoretical justification for weight‑norm‑plasticity relationship is weak**, as cited papers are not in the immediate neighbor set, limiting independent verification.
- **Claims of environment‑agnostic hyperparameters may not hold under large distribution shifts**, and hidden tuning might exist for specific tasks.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (traces + ObGD):** Medium (empirical support but implementation opacity)
- **Mechanism 2 (online normalization + reward scaling):** High (standard techniques, ablations confirm effect)
- **Mechanism 3 (SGDC pre‑training for critic):** Medium (strong empirical results, theoretical backing weak)
- **Overall reproducibility (given missing details):** Medium
