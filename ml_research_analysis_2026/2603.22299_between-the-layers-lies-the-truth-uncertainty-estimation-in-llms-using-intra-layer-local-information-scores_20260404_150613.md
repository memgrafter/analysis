# Between the Layers Lies the Truth: Uncertainty Estimation in LLMs Using Intra-Layer Local Information Scores
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22299  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22299  
- **Reference Count:** 22  
- **Primary Result:** A compact KL-divergence signature between layer activations enables uncertainty estimation that matches in-distribution linear probing while consistently outperforming it under cross-dataset transfer and 4-bit quantization.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel, compact uncertainty estimator for large language models (LLMs) that uses internal representations. Instead of relying on output distributions or high-dimensional raw activations, the method computes pairwise Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergences between post-MLP activations at task-relevant tokens across all layers, forming an L×L "signature map." This structured, low-dimensional representation is fed to a lightweight gradient-boosted decision tree (GBDT) to predict whether the model's output is correct. Evaluated across multiple models and datasets, the approach achieves parity with linear probing on in-distribution data while demonstrating superior robustness to cross-dataset transfer and 4-bit weight-only quantization. The signature’s extreme compactness (L² features vs. high-dimensional probes) enables efficient, generalizable uncertainty estimation without repeated forward passes or model modifications.

## Method Summary
Given an input, the method first selects a task-relevant token (e.g., the token where the answer is generated). For that token, it extracts post-MLP activations from every layer. Each activation vector is normalized into a probability distribution via temperature-scaled softmax. All pairwise directed KL divergences are computed between these distributions, producing an L×L matrix (the signature). After an optional contrast transform, the matrix is flattened into an L²-dimensional feature vector. A pre-trained LightGBM classifier consumes this vector to output a probability of correctness; the uncertainty score is one minus this probability. Training requires labeled data (correct/incorrect) to fit the GBDT, but the LLM itself remains unmodified. The entire pipeline runs in a single forward pass of the model followed by lightweight post-processing.

## Key Results
- **In-Distribution Parity:** On same-dataset evaluation, the method’s performance is close to that of a linear probe trained on raw hidden states, with minor drops in AUPRC (at most −1.8 percentage points) and Brier score changes (at most +4.9 points).
- **Cross-Dataset Transfer:** When the GBDT is trained on one dataset and tested on another, it consistently outperforms the linear probe, with AUPRC improvements up to +2.86 points and Brier score improvements up to +21.02 points.
- **Quantization Robustness:** Under 4-bit weight-only quantization, the signature method shows average improvements of +1.94 AUPRC points and +5.33 Brier points over the linear probe, indicating resistance to degradation from low-precision weights.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
The L×L KL signature compresses high-dimensional, noisy activation vectors into a structured, lower-dimensional representation that retains information relevant for uncertainty estimation. By normalizing activations into distributions and computing pairwise divergences, the method summarizes cross-layer (dis)agreement patterns. The core assumption is that these patterns correlate with model correctness and are preserved through the dimensionality reduction. Evidence: The signature is defined as S(t)ₖⱼ = D_KL(pₖ^(t) ‖ pⱼ^(t)) [section 3.3], and the abstract notes it forms a "signature map." The break condition would be if the correctness signal is fundamentally nonlinear in ways a shallow GBDT cannot capture, or if softmax normalization destroys critical signal for some architectures.

### Mechanism 2
Directed KL divergences capture asymmetric, layer-wise information flow or disagreement, which is predictive of correctness. A confidently wrong model may exhibit consistent, high divergences in specific directional patterns (e.g., early to late layers) compared to a correct prediction where information stabilizes. The core assumption is that directionality contains discriminative signal that generalizes better than absolute activation geometry. Evidence: The abstract emphasizes "pairwise Kullback-Leibler divergences" and section 2.5 states the method "emphasizes cross-layer agreement and disagreement." Ablation in Appendix C shows D_KL performs comparably or better than symmetric Jensen-Shannon. Break condition: If the predictive signal is primarily symmetric or in absolute magnitudes, or if the selected tokens are poor proxies for decision-making.

### Mechanism 3
The structured, low-dimensional signature enables a simple classifier to learn general "correctness" patterns from internal dynamics, avoiding overfitting to dataset-specific cues present in high-dimensional raw probes. Because features represent relationships between layers, they are less likely to encode spurious, task-specific features. This improves cross-dataset transfer. Evidence: The abstract notes the representation is "orders of magnitude more compact" and "enabling efficient and transferable uncertainty estimation." Section 5.2 finds "predominantly positive off-diagonal differences" indicating robust transfer. Section 2.5 claims the signatures "capture task-agnostic properties." Break condition: If correctness heavily depends on absolute high-dimensional geometry for a given task, or if the GBDT’s capacity is insufficient.

## Foundational Learning
- **Kullback-Leibler Divergence as a Measure of Distributional Disagreement:** Needed as the core operation transforming activation vectors into scalar similarity scores. *Quick check:* Why use directed KL (D_KL(P||Q)) instead of a symmetric alternative like Jensen-Shannon?
- **Information Bottleneck (IB) Principle:** Provides theoretical framing; the method seeks a compressed, structured representation of internal states that retains task-relevant information (correctness signal). *Quick check:* How does this method’s per-instance KL divergences differ from estimating mutual information I(X;T) in the standard IB objective?
- **Probing Classifiers for Internal Representations:** Understanding linear probing’s limitations (high-dimensional, task-specific, poor transfer) motivates the signature approach. *Quick check:* What are two practical challenges of using a linear probe on raw, per-token hidden states for uncertainty estimation that the signature method addresses?
- **Contrastive Transformations:** The optional 1−exp(−αS) operation enhances distinguishability of divergences. *Quick check:* How does this nonlinear contrast affect the distribution of feature values fed to the GBDT?

## Architecture Onboarding
Activation Extractor -> Distribution Normalizer -> Signature Generator -> Feature Flattener -> Uncertainty Classifier -> Scoring. Critical path: Forward LLM pass -> Extract token activations -> Softmax-normalize -> Compute full L×L KL matrix -> Flatten -> LightGBM inference -> Output uncertainty. No multiple forward passes or weight changes.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Compactness vs. In-Distribution Nuance:** L² features are vastly smaller than high-dimensional probes but may discard fine-grained cues useful when train/test distributions are identical.
- **Structure vs. Flexibility:** The fixed KL-signature structure imposes an interpretable prior on cross-layer relationships but may miss other patterns in raw activations that attention-based processors could capture.
- **Supervision & Token Selection:** Requires labeled correctness data for GBDT training and a heuristic to pick task-relevant tokens (e.g., last token). Performance is sensitive to both.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **Poor Token Selection Heuristic:** If the chosen token does not contain decision-relevant information (e.g., first token in QA), the signature will be irrelevant.
2. **Extreme Distribution Shift:** The method was tested on common NLP tasks and 4-bit quantization; failure likely under novel domains (e.g., biomedical text for a web-trained model) where layer dynamics change qualitatively.
3. **Model Architecture Mismatch:** Assumes a standard Transformer with clear per-layer post-MLP activations; may not translate to MoE models without clear per-layer aggregation or state-space models.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline Parity (In-Distribution):** Train/test GBDT on same dataset (e.g., MMLU for Llama-3.1-8B). Compare AUPRC/Brier against linear probe on raw hidden states. Expect near-parity.
2. **Cross-Task Transfer:** Train GBDT on Task A (e.g., TRIVIAQA), test on Task B (e.g., WINOGRANDE). Repeat for all pairs. Expect positive off-diagonal AUPRC/Brier deltas vs. linear probe.
3. **Quantization Robustness:** Train GBDT on full-precision activations, evaluate on 4-bit quantized model activations. Expect maintained/improved AUPRC and Brier vs. linear probe degradation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Can we train a single uncertainty estimator that generalizes across tasks without per-task labels? *Basis:* Future work mentions multitask and cross-domain training, semi-supervised or unsupervised variants. *Why unresolved:* Current method requires supervised correctness labels per dataset, limiting scalability and transfer. *What evidence would resolve it:* A unified model trained on multiple tasks (or via self-supervision) matches task-specific probe performance on unseen tasks.

### Open Question 2
How can we automatically select the most informative tokens for computing layer-layer signatures? *Basis:* Performance depends on accurate selection of task-relevant tokens; richer token-level strategies are needed. *Why unresolved:* Heuristic choices (e.g., last token) may miss relevant signals and vary in effectiveness across tasks. *What evidence would resolve it:* An automated token selection method (e.g., gradient- or attention-based) yields robust uncertainty estimates across diverse tasks.

### Open Question 3
What do the patterns in KL signature maps reveal about model information flow and correctness? *Basis:* Signature maps offer a structured perspective; a comprehensive interpretability study is left for future work. *Why unresolved:* Preliminary feature importance maps exist, but causal mechanisms and common motifs for correct vs. incorrect predictions are unknown. *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic cross-model analysis, correlation with other interpretability metrics, and causal interventions (layer ablation, patching) identify reliable links.

### Open Question 4
Can the signature approach be theoretically connected to information-theoretic concepts like mutual information or the Information Bottleneck? *Basis:* A deeper theoretical treatment connecting signature maps to broader information-theoretic quantities is needed. *Why unresolved:* The method uses local KL divergences without formal justification tying them to global information measures. *What evidence would resolve it:* Derivation of analytical relationships (e.g., signatures bounded by mutual information) and empirical validation of these predictions.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter Blackbox:** Critical values (temperature τ, contrast α, LightGBM params) are unreported, risking non-replicable performance.
- **Token Selection Dependency:** Efficacy hinges on the task-relevant token selector I(x), which defers to an external, unavailable method; using a naive fallback may degrade results.
- **Aggregation Ambiguity:** The method for combining per-token signatures into an instance-level feature vector (mean, sum, max?) is unspecified, introducing a significant degree-of-freedom.

## Confidence
**High:** The core claim of compact representation (L² vs. high-dimensional probes) is mathematically straightforward and well-supported.
**Medium:** Claims of matched in-distribution performance and superior cross-dataset transfer/quantization robustness are based on reported empirical results but depend on unstated hyperparameters and token selection.
**Low:** The proposed mechanisms (e.g., directed KL capturing asymmetric information flow as the key signal) are plausible but not definitively proven; ablation (vs. JS) is suggestive but not conclusive.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity Sweep:** Systematically vary τ, α, and LightGBM complexity on a validation set for one model/dataset (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B/MMLU) to bound performance variation and identify a robust default.
2. **Token Selector Sanity Check:** Compare the reported I(x) against a simple "last token" baseline on a multi-token QA dataset (e.g., TRIVIAQA). Measure delta in AUPRC/Brier to quantify the contribution of sophisticated token selection.
3. **Aggregation Method Ablation:** Test mean, max, and sum pooling across all task-relevant tokens for a fixed model/dataset. Determine if the choice significantly impacts final uncertainty metrics and if one method consistently dominates.
