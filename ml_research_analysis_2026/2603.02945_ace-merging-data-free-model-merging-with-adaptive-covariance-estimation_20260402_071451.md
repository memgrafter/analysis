# ACE-Merging: Data-Free Model Merging with Adaptive Covariance Estimation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.02945
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02945
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: State-of-the-art data-free model merging with adaptive covariance estimation; on GPT-2 (GLUE) average accuracy 74.1% (+4+ points), on RoBERTa-base normalized average score 90.4% (+5+ points), with consistent vision gains (e.g., 90.6% on ViT-B/16).

## Executive Summary
ACE-Merging introduces a novel data-free model merging framework that operates solely on the weights of a pretrained model and its fine-tuned experts, without access to any training data. The core theoretical insight is that the task-specific input covariance is proportional to the covariance of the fine-tuning parameter changes (task vectors). This enables a closed-form optimal merging solution that adaptively normalizes task contributions to handle scale heterogeneity and applies spectral refinement for numerical stability. Extensive experiments on language (GLUE) and vision benchmarks demonstrate substantial improvements over prior state-of-the-art, with gains of over 4 points on GPT-2 and 5 points on RoBERTa-base, and consistent improvements across vision architectures and task counts.

## Method Summary
The method processes each linear layer independently. Given a pretrained weight matrix \(W_0\) and \(T\) fine-tuned experts \(\{W_t\}\), compute task vectors \(\Delta W_t = W_t - W_0\). The empirical covariance of the fine-tuning updates serves as a proxy for the task input covariance \(\hat{\Sigma}_t \propto (\Delta W_t - \mathbf{1}\mu_t^\top)^\top (\Delta W_t - \mathbf{1}\mu_t^\top)\), where \(\mu_t\) is the column mean. A heterogeneity metric \(\gamma = \mathrm{Var}_t[\log \|\Delta W_t\|_F^2] / (\mathbb{E}_t[\log \|\Delta W_t\|_F^2])^2\) decides whether adaptive scaling is needed. If \(\gamma > \tau\) (default 0.3), each \(\hat{\Sigma}_t\) is trace-normalized and regularized with \(\epsilon \cdot \mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_t)^{-1} I\); otherwise raw \(\hat{\Sigma}_t\) with a fixed \(\epsilon\) is used. A collective prior \(C_{\mathrm{agg}}\) is formed from the aggregated regularized covariances. The preliminary merged weights are \(\bar{W}_{\mathrm{pre}} = \big(\sum_t W_t \hat{\Sigma}_{t,\mathrm{reg}}\big) \big(\sum_t \hat{\Sigma}_{t,\mathrm{reg}} + C_{\mathrm{agg}}\big)^{-1}\). For high-\(\gamma\) settings, spectral refinement adds a low-rank correction derived from the top-\(k\) singular vectors of a residual matrix, flattening the singular value spectrum and improving robustness. The final merged model \(\bar{W}\) is obtained after this optional step. Unknowns include exact regularization strengths for some architectures, precise layer selection criteria, and bias merging.

## Key Results
- On GPT-2 (GLUE), average accuracy reaches 74.1%, an absolute improvement of over 4 points versus prior methods.
- On RoBERTa-base, normalized average score is 90.4%, over 5 points higher than previous state-of-the-art.
- Vision benchmarks: e.g., 90.6% on ViT-B/16 with 8 tasks (2+ points above SOTA), with robust scaling to 20 tasks and larger architectures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The task-specific input covariance matrix \(\Sigma_t\) can be estimated from the fine-tuning parameter displacement (task vector) \(\Delta W_t\) without access to the original data.
- **Mechanism**: Under a linearized model and small fine-tuning updates, the gradient updates during fine-tuning are proportional to \((W_0 x - y) x^\top\). Summing these over the dataset yields \(\Delta W_t \propto \mathbb{E}_x[xx^\top] = \Sigma_t\). The covariance of the observed \(\Delta W_t\) (treated as a sample from the fine-tuning stochastic process) is thus proportional to \(\Sigma_t\).
- **Core assumption**: Fine-tuning updates are small and centered, such that the mean update \(\mathbb{E}[\Delta W_t] \approx 0\), making the covariance of the displacement reflective of the second-order structure of the inputs.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract] "theoretically showing that task input covariance is proportional to the covariance of fine-tuning parameter changes."
  - [section 3.2/Theorem 1] Derives the closed-form optimal merging solution \(\bar{W} = (\sum W_t \Sigma_t)(\sum \Sigma_t)^{-1}\) and shows \(\Sigma_t \propto \mathrm{Cov}_{\mathcal{D}_t}[\Delta W_t]\).
  - [corpus] Neighbors (e.g., WUDI-Merging [23], TSV-M [19]) also explore task vector properties but do not establish this direct proportionality link; ACE-Merging's novelty is the theoretical grounding and closed-form use of this estimate.
- **Break condition**: If fine-tuning updates are large or exhibit strong directional bias (\(\mathbb{E}[\Delta W_t] \neq 0\)), the covariance estimator (\(\Delta W_t^\top \Delta W_t\)) may capture systematic shifts rather than input geometry, degrading merger quality.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Adaptive normalization of estimated task covariances mitigates domination by high-energy tasks during aggregation.
- **Mechanism**: A heterogeneity metric \(\gamma = \mathrm{Var}_t[\log \|\Delta W_t\|_F^2] / (\mathbb{E}_t[\log \|\Delta W_t\|_F^2])^2\) detects large scale divergence across tasks. If \(\gamma > \tau\) (threshold), each estimated covariance \(\hat{\Sigma}_t\) is trace-normalized (\(\hat{\Sigma}_{t,\mathrm{scaled}} = \hat{\Sigma}_t / \mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_t)\)) and regularized with an energy-adjusted \(\epsilon\). This equalizes task contributions before aggregation.
- **Core assumption**: The Frobenius norm of the task vector \(\|\Delta W_t\|_F\) is a reliable proxy for the total "energy" or scale of the task-specific update, and scaling covariances by their trace balances their influence in the summed matrix \(\sum \hat{\Sigma}_t\).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.2] Defines \(\gamma\) and the adaptive scaling policy. Figure 1 shows \(\gamma\) varies significantly across architectures (e.g., RoBERTa vs. ViT), justifying the need for adaptivity.
  - [section 5.3/Table 4] Ablation shows the adaptive regularization (E2) provides the largest gain on RoBERTa-Large (~8 points), confirming its importance in heterogeneous settings.
- **Break condition**: The metric \(\gamma\) may be insensitive to subtle but harmful interference if task scales are similar but directional covariances conflict. In such cases, even with low \(\gamma\), normalization might be needed but is skipped.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Spectral refinement corrects the ill-conditioned spectrum of the preliminary closed-form solution \(\bar{W}_{\mathrm{pre}}\) by preserving its principal subspace but redistributing singular values toward isotropy.
- **Mechanism**: For high-\(\gamma\) cases, after computing \(\bar{W}_{\mathrm{pre}}\), a residual based on task covariance disagreement (\(\Delta_{\mathrm{res}}\)) is fused. SVD of this fused matrix yields top-\(k\) singular vectors (\(U_{:,:k}, V_{:,:k}\)). These are reweighted by the mean of the top-\(k\) singular values (\(\sigma_{\mathrm{iso}}\)) and added back: \(\bar{W} = \bar{W}_{\mathrm{pre}} + \sigma_{\mathrm{iso}} U_{:,:k} V_{:,:k}^\top\).
- **Core assumption**: The principal subspace (leading eigenvectors) of \(\bar{W}_{\mathrm{pre}}\) is correct, but its singular values are unevenly distributed (some directions over-amplified, others suppressed), causing sensitivity. Flattening the spectrum within the top-\(k\) subspace improves robustness without losing the primary merged structure.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.4] Figure 2 shows \(\bar{W}_{\mathrm{pre}}\) has extreme spectral concentration (top 5% singular values >99% energy) while \(\bar{W}\) has a flatter spectrum, yet their leading eigenvectors are nearly identical.
  - [section 5.3/Table 4] Adding spectral refinement (E4) yields the final best scores, indicating it resolves residual instability after adaptive normalization and CSP.
- **Break condition**: If the preliminary solution \(\bar{W}_{\mathrm{pre}}\) already has a well-conditioned spectrum (unlikely in high-\(\gamma\) settings), this step may unnecessarily add noise or dilute task-specific information. The \(k_{\mathrm{frac}}\) parameter controls this trade-off.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Task Vector (\(\Delta W_t\))**
  - **Why needed here**: ACE-Merging operates solely on model weights. The task vector is the fundamental unit of information from each expert, representing the discrete parameter shift induced by fine-tuning.
  - **Quick check question**: Given a pretrained model \(W_0\) and a fine-tuned model \(W_t\), how would you compute the task vector, and what does its magnitude indicate about the fine-tuning process?
- **Concept: Input Covariance (\(\Sigma_t = \mathbb{E}[xx^\top])\)**
  - **Why needed here**: The optimal linear merging solution (Eq. 3) requires \(\Sigma_t\) to weight each expert appropriately, accounting for the distribution of its training data. ACE-Merging's core contribution is estimating this from \(\Delta W_t\).
  - **Quick check question**: In the quadratic merging objective (Eq. 2), what role does \(\Sigma_t\) play, and why would equal-weight averaging (simple mean) be suboptimal?
- **Concept: Linearization & Local Linearity**
  - **Why needed here**: The theoretical derivation (Theorem 1) relies on approximating the fine-tuning gradient updates and the forward pass linearly (\(f(W,x) \approx Wx\)). This makes the optimization problem tractable and links \(\Delta W_t\) to \(\Sigma_t\).
  - **Quick check question**: What are the practical implications if the linear approximation breaks down (e.g., for large fine-tuning steps or highly nonlinear architectures)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Pretrained model \(W_0\) and expert models \(\{W_t\}\) → Compute task vectors \(\Delta W_t\) → Compute heterogeneity metric \(\gamma\) → If \(\gamma > \tau\): trace-normalize \(\hat{\Sigma}_t\) and add regularization \(\epsilon\); else use raw \(\hat{\Sigma}_t\) → Compute collective prior \(C_{\mathrm{agg}}\) → Compute preliminary merged weights \(\bar{W}_{\mathrm{pre}} = \big(\sum_t W_t \hat{\Sigma}_{t,\mathrm{reg}}\big) \big(\sum_t \hat{\Sigma}_{t,\mathrm{reg}} + C_{\mathrm{agg}}\big)^{-1}\) → If \(\gamma > \tau\): perform spectral refinement (top-\(k\) SVD reweighting) to obtain final \(\bar{W}\) → Output merged model \(\bar{W}\).

**Critical path**:  
Task vectors → \(\gamma\) detection → adaptive \(\hat{\Sigma}_t\) estimation → aggregation (+CSP) → [if high \(\gamma\): spectral refinement]

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Adaptive scaling vs. simplicity**: The \(\gamma\) gate adds complexity but prevents unnecessary normalization on homogeneous tasks (e.g., ViT-B/16 in 8-task setting), which could over-regularize.
- **Spectral refinement cost**: Adds an SVD per high-\(\gamma\) layer, increasing compute (O(\(n^3\)) vs. O(\(n^2\)) for matrix inversion). However, it's skipped for low-\(\gamma\) layers, making it efficient overall.
- **Global hyperparameters**: \(\epsilon\) (regularization strength) is architecture-dependent (larger for GPT-2, smaller for RoBERTa). \(\tau\) (γ threshold) is fixed at 0.3. \(k_{\mathrm{frac}}\) (rank fraction) controls refinement strength.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Performance collapse on heterogeneous tasks**: If \(\gamma\) is underestimated (threshold too high), adaptive scaling and refinement are disabled, leading to interference. Monitor \(\gamma\) distribution across layers.
- **Over-regularization**: If \(\epsilon\) is too large, \(\hat{\Sigma}_{t,\mathrm{reg}} \approx \epsilon \cdot \mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_t)^{-1} I\), causing \(\bar{W}\) to converge to a weighted average and underfit task specifics.
- **Numerical instability**: Matrix inversion \((\sum \hat{\Sigma}_{t,\mathrm{reg}} + C_{\mathrm{agg}})\) can be ill-conditioned if \(\epsilon\) is too small or if \(\hat{\Sigma}_t\) are rank-deficient. Ensure \(\epsilon\) provides a minimal eigenvalue floor.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Validate heterogeneity detector**: Compute \(\gamma\) for different architectures and task set sizes (Figs. 1, 4, 5, 6, 7). Confirm \(\gamma\) increases with task diversity and differs across model families (RoBERTa high, ViT low).
2. **Component ablation**: Run ACE-Merging with sequential disabling of modules (E1–E4 in Table 4). Expect largest drop when removing adaptive regularization (E1→E2) on high-\(\gamma\) models (RoBERTa, GPT-2).
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Vary \(\tau\) (γ threshold) and \(k_{\mathrm{frac}}\) (Tables 5, 6). Identify stable ranges (e.g., \(\tau \in [0.1,0.3]\), \(k_{\mathrm{frac}} \in [0.1,0.5]\)) where performance plateaus.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the regularization strength \(\epsilon\) be automatically determined from model statistics without manual tuning?
- **Basis in paper**: In the sensitivity analysis (Sec. 5.3), the authors state: "A possible direction for future work is to design an automatic procedure to estimate an optimal \(\epsilon\) from model statistics."
- **Why unresolved**: The current method requires setting \(\epsilon\) per model family (e.g., 4e-2 for GPT-2, 2e-4 for RoBERTa-Base), which limits scalability and optimality in new settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Propose an estimator for \(\epsilon\) based on properties of task vectors (e.g., norm, condition number) and validate across a wide range of architectures and task sets, showing matching or improved performance with reduced tuning.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: To what extent does the linear approximation of fine-tuning updates (\(f(W,x) \approx Wx\)) affect the validity of the theoretical guarantee and practical performance of ACE-Merging?
- **Basis in paper**: The derivation in Sec. 3.2 explicitly linearizes the forward pass to obtain a closed-form solution. The paper assumes "fine-tuning updates are typically small in magnitude" to justify this approximation.
- **Why unresolved**: If fine-tuning induces large parameter changes or involves non-linear layers where the linearization is inaccurate, the relation \(\Sigma_t \propto \mathrm{Cov}[\Delta W_t]\) may break, leading to suboptimal merging. The paper does not analyze this regime.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled experiments varying the fine-tuning learning rate or number of steps to produce larger \(\Delta W\), comparing merging performance against data-dependent baselines that have access to true \(\Sigma_t\); or extend the theory to second-order approximations.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Is the empirical covariance estimator \(\hat{\Sigma}_t \propto (\Delta W - \mathbf{1}\mu^\top)^\top(\Delta W - \mathbf{1}\mu^\top)\) statistically consistent for the true input covariance \(\Sigma_t\), and how does estimation error propagate to the merged model?
- **Basis in paper**: Theorem 1 relates \(\Sigma_t\) to \(\mathrm{Cov}[\Delta W_t]\), but the estimator in Eq. (5) uses a single task vector as a proxy, treating rows as independent samples. The paper does not provide statistical guarantees.
- **Why unresolved**: Without consistency or error bounds, the robustness of ACE-Merging to noise or limited data during fine-tuning is unclear, especially for tasks with few training samples where \(\Delta W\) may be high-variance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Theoretical analysis deriving bias/variance of the estimator under different fine-tuning regimes; empirical validation on synthetic data where true \(\Sigma_t\) is known; or experiments with varying numbers of fine-tuning samples to measure stability.

## Limitations
- The linearized small-update assumption may fail for large fine-tuning steps, breaking the \(\Sigma_t\)-\(\mathrm{Cov}[\Delta W_t]\) link.
- Hyperparameters (\(\epsilon\), \(\tau\), \(k_{\mathrm{frac}}\)) are architecture-specific; values for many models (ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, LLaMA) are unspecified.
- The algorithm omits details on which layers to merge and how to handle biases.

## Confidence
- **Covariance estimation**: High (Theorem 1 provides solid theoretical basis, validated by consistent gains across NLP and vision tasks)
- **Adaptive normalization**: Medium (ablation shows significant improvements but the \(\gamma\) metric could miss subtle interference and the fixed \(\tau=0.3\) may not be universal)
- **Spectral refinement**: Medium (stabilizes high-γ merges but is heuristic; \(k_{\mathrm{frac}}\) tuning required and possible over-smoothing)
