# Tracing Moral Foundations in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.05437  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org.org/abs/2601.05437  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Moral foundations (Care, Fairness, Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity) are encoded as distinct linear directions in LLM activation spaces, with separability peaking in late layers and aligning with human moral perception.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) genuinely internalize human-like moral structures or merely perform superficial mimicry. Using Moral Foundations Theory (MFT) as a framework, the authors conduct a multi-level analysis of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. They find that moral foundations are represented as linearly separable directions in the residual stream, with separability increasing in later layers and correlating with human judgments. Sparse autoencoder (SAE) decomposition reveals coherent sub-features (e.g., physical vs. emotional care). Crucially, causal steering along these directions predictably shifts the model’s responses on a standardized moral questionnaire (MFQ-2) without significantly harming general capability (MMLU). Cross-model differences suggest that alignment training can compress some moral representations.

## Method Summary
The study employs a pipeline combining geometric validation, mechanistic decomposition, and causal intervention. First, "concept vectors" are constructed for each moral foundation by contrasting mean residual activations (last token) of expanded moral vignettes against a "Social Norm" baseline. Separability is validated by projecting human-labeled Reddit posts onto these vectors and measuring distributional separation via Signed Wasserstein Distance (SW1). Second, pretrained sparse autoenciders (SAEs) are applied to decompose activations; features aligning with each foundation’s vector are identified and semantically validated using top-activating contexts from a large corpus. Third, activation steering is performed: residual streams are modified by adding scaled versions of either the dense foundation vectors or sparse SAE feature directions. The effect on MFQ-2 scores is measured, with MMLU accuracy serving as a capability control.

## Key Results
- Moral foundations are encoded as distinct linear directions; separability (SW1) peaks in late layers and aligns with human-labeled data.
- SAE features decompose foundations into coherent subcomponents (e.g., Care splits into physical suffering and emotional distress).
- Steering along both dense and sparse directions predictably shifts MFQ-2 scores (linear response, high R²), without degrading MMLU accuracy; micro-steering can rescue efficacy for compressed foundations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Linear Separability of Moral Foundations
- **Claim:** Moral foundations are encoded as distinct, linearly separable directions in the residual stream, aligning with human perception.
- **Mechanism:** For each foundation `k`, a unit concept vector `v_k` is formed by normalizing the difference between mean activations of foundation-tagged vignettes and a "Social Norm" baseline. Separability is validated by projecting held-out Reddit posts onto `v_k` and computing SW1 between distributions of moral and non-moral posts.
- **Core assumption:** High-level moral distinctions manifest as geometric clusters/directions in activation space; linear readouts in late layers are behaviorally actionable.
- **Evidence anchors:** Layer-wise SW1 increases in final layers (e.g., Llama Layer 31: Care SW1=1.71). Human-labeled Reddit data shows aligned separation.
- **Break condition:** Consistently low/negative SW1 across late layers, or collapse of multiple foundations into a single direction.

### Mechanism 2: Mechanistic Decomposition via Sparse Autoencoders
- **Claim:** Coarse moral vectors are composed of granular, interpretable SAE features that provide a mechanistic decomposition.
- **Mechanism:** Pretrained SAEs (BatchTopK, expansion factor ~32–36×, k=64) decompose residual activations. For foundation `k`, top-K SAE decoder directions `d_i` are selected by highest cosine similarity with `v_k`. Semantics are grounded via top-activating contexts from FineWeb, interpreted by an LLM and human-validated.
- **Core assumption:** SAEs disentangle superposed directions into monosemantic features; alignment (cosine similarity) indicates a feature’s contribution to the foundation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Layer-wise alignment peaks (e.g., Llama Layer 16); features yield coherent sub-concepts (Care → physical/emotional harm; Authority → governance/roles).
- **Break condition:** Top-aligned features lack semantic coherence or fail to exceed random baseline similarity.

### Mechanism 3: Causal Steering along Moral Directions
- **Claim:** The identified directions play a causal role in generating moral judgments; intervention predicts MFQ-2 score shifts without capability loss.
- **Mechanism:** Activation addition: `h'_l = h_l + α * v_steer` at selected layers `l`. Macro-steering uses `v_k`; micro-steering uses SAE `d_i`. Steering coefficient `α` is swept; change in MFQ-2 subscale score (ΔScore) and MMLU accuracy are measured.
- **Core assumption:** Representations are functionally used in output generation; linear interventions isolate causal contributions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Linear ΔScore–α curves (R² >0.95 often); MMLU remains stable (±1.5 points for Llama macro-steering). Micro-steering boosts efficacy for weak macro-effects (e.g., Llama Authority: β_macro=0.041 → β_micro=0.234).
- **Break condition:** Non-monotonic/inversely signed ΔScore, or severe MMLU drop (>10%) at moderate `|α|`.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Moral Foundations Theory (MFT)**
  - *Why needed*: Provides the psychological framework (Care, Fairness, Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity, Liberty) defining target moral dimensions.
  - *Quick check*: List the five/six MFT foundations and give a one-sentence defining example for each.
- **Concept: Linear Representation Hypothesis**
  - *Why needed*: Justifies extracting concepts via difference-in-means and steering with linear directions.
  - *Quick check*: Given two activation sets ("care" vs. "social norm"), how do you mathematically find a distinguishing direction?
- **Concept: Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**
  - *Why needed*: Enables mechanistic decomposition of abstract foundations into interpretable, monosemantic features.
  - *Quick check*: What is the purpose of the sparsity-inducing function (e.g., BatchTopK)? What does "overcomplete" mean?
- **Concept: Causal Intervention / Activation Steering**
  - *Why needed*:Tests whether representations are functionally used, not just correlated.
  - *Quick check*: In `h' = h + α*v`, what are `h`, `v`, `α`? What outcome indicates a causal effect?

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Pipeline -> Analysis Backbone -> Decomposition Engine -> Metrics Engine

Critical path: 1) Build foundation vs. Social Norm vectors from vignettes. 2) Validate separability on held-out Reddit (SW1 peak in late layers). 3) Load SAEs; find top-K aligned features per foundation. 4) Semantically validate features via FineWeb contexts. 5) Select best steering layer per foundation (max positive slope). 6) Run macro & micro steering on MFQ-2; measure ΔScore and MMLU.

Design tradeoffs:
- SAE layer stride (every 4th) trades coverage for compute; may miss transient dynamics.
- Single steering layer simplifies but ignores distributed effects; multi-layer could be more powerful.
- "Foundation vs. Social Norm" isolates moral signal but may conflate compliance; cross-foundation contrasts are more sensitive.
- Evaluation limited to MFQ-2 and MMLU; open-ended behavior not measured.

Failure signatures:
- Projection: Low/negative SW1 across late layers; sign inversions.
- SAE alignment: Top-K features indistinguishable from random; incoherent semantics.
- Steering: Non-monotonic/inverse ΔScore; MMLU drop >5% at moderate `|α|`.
- Cross-model: One model shows alignment inertia (e.g., Llama Authority) while another does not.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Replicate Projection Validity**: Compute layer-wise SW1 for all foundations (Foundation vs. Social Norm) for both models; verify terminal peak pattern; check Fairness sign inversion in Llama vs. clean separation in Qwen.
2. **SAE Feature Fingerprinting**: For Llama at Layer 16 (peak), extract top-10 features per foundation; run semantic validation (sample 50k FineWeb docs, GPT-5.1 interpretation); manually verify ≥3/10 features for Care/Authority match expected semantics.
3. **Macro-Steering Baseline**: Sweep `α` using optimal layer’s `v_k`; plot ΔScore vs. α; confirm linearity (R²>0.9) for ≥4 foundations in Llama; note attenuated Authority slope (~5× smaller than Care); check MMLU at `α=±2.0` for >2% drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions regarding the nature and stability of moral representations in LLMs:
1. **Representation Format**: Are moral foundations truly *atomic* linear directions, or are they distributed patterns that the linear probe approximates?
2. **Cross-Model Invariance**: Why do models like Llama show "alignment inertia" (e.g., weak Authority separability/steering) while Qwen does not? Is this due to pretraining data, alignment objectives, or architectural differences?
3. **Generalization Beyond MFQ-2**: Do steering effects on a standardized questionnaire translate to more naturalistic, open-ended moral reasoning or behavior? Could steering induce brittle or context-sensitive changes?
4. **SAE Dependency**: How dependent are the findings on the specific SAE architecture (BatchTopK, stride=4, k=64)? Would different SAEs yield different decompositions or steering efficacy?
5. **Developmental Trajectory**: At what point during training do these moral directions emerge? Do they form gradually or appear abruptly during alignment?

## Limitations
- **Dataset constraints**: The expanded moral vignettes used to construct concept vectors are not publicly released. Reproduction may require using the original 130-vignette set or regenerating expansions, which reduces signal and may introduce variability.
- **SAE specificity**: Results depend on the specific SAELens autoencoders (stride 4, BatchTopK, k=64). Different SAE training regimes, expansion factors, or missing intermediate layers could alter the identified features and their steering efficacy.
- **Narrow evaluation**: Effects are measured only on the MFQ-2 questionnaire and MMLU. Steering's impact on open-ended moral dilemmas, conversational behavior, or real-world task performance is unknown. The MFQ-2 itself may not capture full moral complexity.
- **Cross-model analysis**: Differences between Llama and Qwen (e.g., Llama's compressed Authority representation) are documented but not causally explained. The role of reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) versus supervised fine-tuning (SFT) remains speculative.
- **Steering scope**: Interventions are applied at a single "best" layer per foundation. Distributed representations across layers are not fully captured, and multi-layer steering might yield different effects.
- **Interpretation caution**: Semantic validation of SAE features relies on LLM-assisted labeling and limited human review. Coherence does not guarantee monosemanticity or exhaustive coverage of a foundation's semantics.

## Confidence
- **Linear separability**: High. The pattern of increasing SW1 in late layers, alignment with human-labeled Reddit data, and consistent cross-model replication (despite sign differences) provide strong geometric evidence. **Assumption**: The "Social Norm" baseline adequately isolates moral from non-moral social content.
- **SAE decomposition**: Medium. Features show coherent sub-concepts (e.g., physical/emotional care) and align with foundation vectors, but results are SAE-specific. **Unknown**: Generalizability to other SAE families or models without publicly available SAEs.
- **Causal steering**: Medium. Predictable, linear MFQ-2 shifts without MMLU degradation suggest functional use. However, the narrow probe (MFQ-2) and single-layer intervention limit claims about broad behavioral causality. **Assumption**: MFQ-2 score changes reflect genuine shifts in moral judgment rather than superficial questionnaire manipulation.
- **Cross-model differences**: Medium. The pattern (e.g., Llama Authority's weak separability/steering vs. Qwen's) is reproducible but mechanistic explanations are post-hoc. **Unknown**: Whether this reflects fundamental differences in training data, alignment pressure, or innate architectural biases.

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset robustness**: Compare results using the original 130 vignettes versus the expanded set; recompute foundation vectors, SW1, and steering sensitivity to quantify signal loss.
2. **Model generalization**: Apply the entire pipeline (vector construction, SW1 validation, SAE decomposition, steering) to other aligned models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) to test separability and steering transferability.
3. **Behavioral impact**: Evaluate steering effects on out-of-distribution moral benchmarks (e.g., ETHICS dilemmas, Moral Stories) and in open-ended conversational settings; correlate ΔMFQ-2 with changes in these more naturalistic tasks.
4. **SAE variation**: Rerun key analyses using different SAEs (e.g., different sparsity penalties, architectures like JumpReLU) for the same models to assess decomposition stability.
5. **Multi-layer steering**: Implement steering interventions distributed across the top 3–5 layers per foundation to test for additive or synergistic effects on MFQ-2 shifts and potential capability trade-offs.
6. **Developmental analysis**: If training checkpoints are available, trace the emergence of moral direction separability (SW1) across pre-training and alignment phases to pinpoint when these representations form.
