# Sparsity is Combinatorial Depth: Quantifying MoE Expressivity via Tropical Geometry

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03204
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03204
- **References:** 13
- **Core result:** Top‑k MoE routing equals the *k*-th elementary symmetric tropical polynomial; decision boundaries are the Normal Fan of a hypersimplex, yielding combinatorial capacity Θ(\(\binom{N}{k}(kH)^{d_{\text{in}}}\)) in ambient space and Θ(\(\binom{N}{k}(kH)^{d_{\text{eff}}}\)) on a *d*<sub>eff</sub>-dimensional manifold, while dense networks suffer capacity collapse.

## Executive Summary
This paper employs tropical geometry to analyze the expressive power of Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) architectures with Top‑*k* routing. The authors establish that the routing decision is algebraically equivalent to the *k*-th elementary symmetric tropical polynomial, whose decision boundaries form the Normal Fan of a (*k*,*N*)‑hypersimplex. This characterization yields a geometric capacity that scales combinatorially as Θ(\(\binom{N}{k}(kH)^{d_{\text{in}}}\)) in the ambient input space. Under the manifold hypothesis, where data lies on a lower‑dimensional manifold of intrinsic dimension *d*<sub>eff</sub>, the MoE retains the combinatorial factor, achieving effective capacity Θ(\(\binom{N}{k}(kH)^{d_{\text{eff}}}\)) in expectation. In contrast, dense ReLU networks exhibit only polynomial scaling Θ(\(H^{d_{\text{eff}}}\)) and suffer from capacity collapse on low‑dimensional data. The results reveal that sparsity in MoE acts as “combinatorial depth,” dramatically increasing model expressivity without a proportional increase in computational cost.

## Method Summary
The analysis reduces the MoE output to a piecewise linear function driven by the router’s Top‑*k* selection. Router logits are affine in the input, and the optimal expert coalition maximizes the sum of those logits; this optimization is exactly the *k*-th elementary symmetric tropical polynomial. Its tropical hypersurface consists of hyperplanes where two coalitions tie, and these hyperplanes define the normal fan of the hypersimplex Δ<sub>*k*,*N*</sub>. Inside each cone (a fixed active set), the model becomes a standard ReLU network of width *kH*. The number of linear regions per cone is given by Zaslavsky’s theorem applied to the *kH* expert hyperplanes, yielding Φ(*kH*,*d*<sub>in</sub>) regions. Summing over all \(\binom{N}{k}\) cones (assuming general position and that every coalition appears) provides the total capacity. For data lying on a *d*<sub>eff</sub>-dimensional manifold, the effective number of regions is estimated by the proportion of cones whose intersection with the manifold has positive spherical measure. Under isotropy of router weights and transversality of expert hyperplanes to the manifold, each intersected cone contributes Φ(*kH*,*d*<sub>eff</sub>) regions, thus preserving the \(\binom{N}{k}\) combinatorial factor. Dense networks lack this multiplier, resulting in the well‑known capacity collapse on low‑dimensional manifolds.

## Key Results
- Top‑k routing ≡ *k*-th elementary symmetric tropical polynomial; decision boundaries = Normal Fan of (*k*,*N*)‑hypersimplex.
- Ambient geometric capacity: Θ(\(\binom{N}{k}(kH)^{d_{\text{in}}}\)).
- On a *d*<sub>eff</sub>-dimensional manifold, expected effective capacity: Θ(\(\binom{N}{k}(kH)^{d_{\text{eff}}}\)) vs. dense networks: Θ(\(H^{d_{\text{eff}}}\)) (capacity collapse).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Top‑k Routing Is a Tropical Polynomial
- **Claim:** The Top‑k routing mechanism is algebraically isomorphic to the *k*-th elementary symmetric tropical polynomial.
- **Mechanism:** Router logits \(z_i(x)=w_i^\top x+b_i\) are affine. The selected expert set \(I\) maximizes \(\sum_{i\in I}z_i(x)\), which is exactly the tropical sum over all \(\binom{N}{k}\) such sums, i.e. \(\text{SymTrop}_k(\mathbf{z}) = \max_{|I|=k}\sum_{i\in I}z_i\). Softmax preserves the argmax, so the isomorphism holds.
- **Core assumption:** Router logits are affine functions of the input; ranking is deterministic (no stochastic noise).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the isomorphism; Section 2.4 derives Equation (2) and the reduction to a tropical polynomial.
- **Break condition:** Non‑affine router transformations or noise that alters the ranking of sums break the correspondence.

### Mechanism 2: Normal Fan of a Hypersimplex Drives Combinatorial Capacity
- **Claim:** The routing partition coincides with the Normal Fan of the (*k*,*N*)‑hypersimplex, leading to a total number of linear regions Θ(\(\binom{N}{k}\Phi(kH,d_{\text{in}})\)).
- **Mechanism:** Ties between two coalitions \(I,J\) define hyperplanes \(\sum_{i\in I}z_i = \sum_{j\in J}z_j\). These form the tropical hypersurface of \(\text{SymTrop}_k\); its covectors are exactly the normal fan of the hypersimplex Δ<sub>*k*,*N*</sub>. Within each cone, the model is a ReLU network of width *kH*; by Zaslavsky’s theorem, its hyperplanes produce Φ(*kH*,*d*<sub>in</sub>) regions. Summing over all \(\binom{N}{k}\) cones yields the bound. Theorems 3.15 and 3.17 give matching upper and lower bounds.
- **Core assumption:** Router and expert hyperplanes are in general position, and all \(\binom{N}{k}\) coalitions are realized (non‑empty cells).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract highlights the Normal Fan and binomial scaling; Section 3.2 contains Theorem 3.15 and Lemma 3.16.
- **Break condition:** Degeneracies (parallel hyperplanes, dependent intersections) reduce region count; routing collapse (some coalitions never active) lowers the effective \(\binom{N}{k}\) factor.

### Mechanism 3: Combinatorial Resilience on Low‑Dimensional Manifolds
- **Claim:** Under the manifold hypothesis (*d*<sub>eff</sub> ≪ *d*<sub>in</sub>), MoE maintains effective capacity Θ(\(\binom{N}{k}(kH)^{d_{\text{eff}}}\)) in expectation, while dense networks collapse to Θ(\(H^{d_{\text{eff}}}\)).
- **Mechanism:** Router cones \(V(I)\) are polyhedral cones in ℝ<sup>*d*<sub>in</sub></sup>. Their intersection with the data manifold *M* (dimension *d*<sub>eff</sub>) yields a submanifold. Assuming isotropic router weights (random orientation) and that expert hyperplanes intersect *M* transversally almost surely (Assumptions 3.19, 3.23), the proportion of cones intersecting *M* is proportional to the spherical measure of the projection \(\pi(M)\). Each intersected cone contributes Φ(*kH*,*d*<sub>eff</sub>) regions. Summing gives the expected effective capacity, retaining the combinatorial factor. Theorems 3.23 and 3.24 provide matching bounds; Corollary 3.26 shows the ratio of upper bounds is \(\binom{N}{k}k^{d_{\text{eff}}}\).
- **Core assumptions:** Router weights i.i.d. isotropic; \(kH \ge d_{\text{eff}}\); *M* smooth and compact; transversal intersections hold.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions combinatorial resilience and capacity collapse; Section 3.3 derives Theorems 3.23/3.24.
- **Break condition:** If *M* occupies a set of zero spherical measure, or router weights are biased (non‑isotropic), the number of intersected cones may drop. Non‑transversal intersections (e.g., hyperplanes tangent to *M*) can also cause collapse.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Input *x* → Router (linear layer) → Logits *z*(*x*) → Top‑*k* operator → Active expert set *I*<sub>*x*</sub> → Expert *E*<sub>*i*</sub>(*x*) for *i*∈*I*<sub>*x*</sub> (ReLU networks) → Gating *G*<sub>*i*</sub>(*x*) = Softmax(*z*<sub>*i*</sub>) → Weighted sum Σ*G*<sub>*i*</sub>(*x*)*E*<sub>*i*</sub>(*x*) → Output *y*.

**Critical path:**
1. Input *x* arrives.
2. Router computes logits *z*(*x*).
3. Top‑*k* operator identifies active expert set *I*<sub>*x*</sub>.
4. Forward pass runs only for experts in *I*<sub>*x*</sub>; others skip.
5. Compute gating weights via Softmax on active logits.
6. Combine expert outputs *E*<sub>*i*</sub>(*x*) with weights *G*<sub>*i*</sub>(*x*).
7. Produce final output.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Active experts *k*: Larger *k* increases combinatorial capacity \(\binom{N}{k}\) and FLOPs; smaller *k* improves efficiency but risks under‑expressive model.
- Total experts *N* vs. expert width *H*: *N* multiplies number of routing regions; *H* controls per‑expert polynomial capacity. Total parameters ≈ *NHd*<sub>in</sub> + *Nd*<sub>in</sub> (router).
- Router simplicity: A linear router preserves the tropical isomorphism; non‑linear routers may break the guarantee.
- Load balancing: Without auxiliary losses, some experts may be rarely used, reducing effective \(\binom{N}{k}\).

**Failure signatures:**
- Routing collapse: Almost all inputs select the same *k* experts → effective \(\binom{N}{k}\) collapses.
- Expert under‑utilization: Many experts have near‑zero activation frequency.
- Degenerate hyperplane arrangement: Weights not in general position, reducing region count below bound.
- Manifold misalignment: Router cones align with data manifold, shrinking intersection volume and effective capacity.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ambient capacity validation:** Sample inputs uniformly from ℝ<sup>*d*<sub>in</sub></sup>, enumerate distinct linear regions (activation patterns), verify scaling \(\binom{N}{k}(kH)^{d_{\text{in}}}\) versus dense with same active FLOPs.
2. **Manifold resilience test:** Generate data from a low‑dimensional manifold (e.g., *d*<sub>eff</sub>=2 curve in ℝ<sup>10</sup>), compute effective capacity (regions intersecting manifold), confirm MoE retains \(\binom{N}{k}\) factor while dense scales only with \(H^{d_{\text{eff}}}\).
3. **Router isotropy and load balancing:** Train an MoE on a simple synthetic task, measure activation entropy over coalitions, and observe degradation when router initialization is biased (anisotropic).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Does MoE's combinatorial resilience extend to non‑smooth data manifolds?**  
   The theory assumes a smooth compact submanifold; real data may be irregular, and the spherical projection argument might fail.
2. **How does training the router affect the combinatorial partition and effective capacity?**  
   The analysis treats router weights as isotropic random variables, but in practice routers are learned, potentially biasing cone volumes and reducing the number of active coalitions.
3. **Can tropical MoE extend to deep stacks and other routers?**  
   The paper analyzes only a single Top‑k layer; deep MoE stacks or continuous routers lack a discrete hypersimplex structure, requiring new theory.

## Limitations
- General position of router and expert hyperplanes may be violated in finite‑size initializations or due to weight sharing, leading to fewer linear regions than the theoretical bound.
- The combinatorial factor \(\binom{N}{k}\) requires every size‑*k* expert coalition to be active; routing collapse or biased initialization can leave many coalitions unused, eliminating the gain.
- The combinatorial resilience proof relies on smooth manifolds with positive spherical projection and isotropic router weights; real data manifolds often violate these assumptions, possibly collapsing the advantage.
