# Hiding in Plain Text: Detecting Concealed Jailbreaks via Activation Disentanglement

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19396  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19396  
- **Reference count:** 31  
- **Primary result:** FrameShield achieves state-of-the-art detection of goal-preserving framing jailbreaks (e.g., 0.81 accuracy, 0.79 F1 on Vicuna-7B) and generalizes robustly to out-of-distribution goals.

## Executive Summary
Goal-preserving framing (GPF) jailbreaks hide malicious intent by rephrasing harmful requests while preserving their underlying goal, evading standard heuristic defenses. This paper introduces a self-supervised disentanglement framework: ReDAct extracts goal and framing representations from frozen LLM activations using contrastive learning on the constructed GoalFrameBench corpus. FrameShield then operates on the disentangled framing signals as an anomaly detector, flagging prompts whose framing deviates from the benign distribution. The approach yields significant improvements across multiple LLM families with minimal computational overhead and demonstrates strong out-of-distribution generalization.

## Method Summary
ReDAct is a lightweight two-headed module attached to a frozen LLM layer. It comprises two MLP encoders that produce a goal representation \(v_g\) and a framing representation \(v_f\), plus a decoder that reconstructs the original hidden state. The module is trained on GoalFrameBench—a corpus of contrastive goal–framing prompt pairs generated by GPT-4.1 augmentation—with a composite loss: InfoNCE contrastive loss (sufficiency), an orthogonality penalty (controlled leakage), and a reconstruction loss (completeness); gradient reversal may be added. After training, FrameShield builds a reference subspace from the framing representations of benign prompts: it computes a whitening transform and PCA retaining components that explain 80% of the variance. For a test prompt, the anomaly score is the squared residual norm after projecting its \(v_f\) onto this subspace; prompts exceeding the 95th percentile threshold are flagged as jailbreaks. FrameShield-Crit selects the layer with the largest Cohen’s \(d\) between benign and harmful framing scores on a calibration set.

## Key Results
- FrameShield-Crit on Vicuna-7B reaches 0.81 accuracy and 0.79 F1, substantially outperforming JBShield (0.60/0.63) and other baselines (LlamaGuard, SelfEx, GradSafe).
- Consistent gains are observed across multiple LLM families (LLaMA-2, Vicuna, GPT-3.5, Gemini), confirming model-agnostic effectiveness.
- Out-of-distribution generalization is strong: performance drops only slightly on unseen jailbreak goals, indicating robustness to novel framing variations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GPF attacks preserve the underlying goal while altering the linguistic framing, implying that goal and framing are separable latent factors in model activations. ReDAct forces the two representations to be (1) **sufficient** for their respective factors via contrastive learning, (2) **orthogonal** to minimize leakage, and (3) **complete** through reconstruction, ensuring no essential information is discarded. The resulting framing representation becomes sensitive to phrasing changes but invariant to the specific harmful goal. FrameShield then models the distribution of benign framing vectors; jailbreak framings, being statistically atypical, yield large residual norms under a whitened PCA projection. This mechanism directly targets the core vulnerability of GPF: framing manipulation, though subtle, is detectable as an anomaly relative to the benign linguistic manifold.

## Foundational Learning
- **Activation Disentanglement**  
  *Why needed:* To isolate framing from goal so that framing anomalies can be detected without goal interference.  
  *Quick check:* Compute ANOVA η² on a held-out set; η²(goal | \(v_g\)) and η²(framing | \(v_f\)) should be high while cross-effects are low.

- **Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE)**  
  *Why needed:* Encourages each representation to exclusively encode its assigned factor by pulling together positives and pushing apart negatives.  
  *Quick check:* Nearest-neighbor retrieval in each representation’s space should match factor labels on a validation set.

- **Orthogonality Constraint**  
  *Why needed:* Prevents one representation from inadvertently leaking information about the other factor.  
  *Quick check:* Mean cosine similarity between \(v_g\) and \(v_f\) across batches should be near zero.

- **Reconstruction Loss**  
  *Why needed:* Guarantees completeness—no necessary information is lost during disentanglement.  
  *Quick check:* Reconstruction mean squared error on validation should be low (e.g., < 1e-4 for normalized activations).

- **Whitened PCA Anomaly Detection**  
  *Why needed:* Compresses benign framing variations into a compact subspace; large residuals indicate out-of-distribution framing.  
  *Quick check:* Residual norms of benign samples should approximately follow a χ² distribution with degrees of freedom equal to the reduced dimensionality.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**  
  Input prompt → Frozen LLM (layer L) → hidden state \(h(L)\)  
  \(h(L)\) → Encoder G → goal rep \(v_g\)  
  \(h(L)\) → Encoder F → framing rep \(v_f\)  
  (\(v_g, v_f\)) → Decoder → reconstruction \(\hat{h}(L)\)  
  FrameShield: collect \(v_f\) from benign prompts → compute mean \(\mu\), whitening \(W\), PCA (retain \(r\) dims, 80% variance).  
  Test: \(s(x) = \|W(v_f - \mu) - P P^\top W(v_f - \mu)\|^2\); flag if \(s > \chi^2_{d-r,0.95}\).

- **Critical path**  
  1. Construct GoalFrameBench (contrastive pairs).  
  2. Extract activations for all prompts from the frozen LLM at chosen layer.  
  3. Train ReDAct with composite loss; monitor disentanglement (ANOVA η²).  
  4. Compute \(v_f\) for calibration benign set.  
  5. Fit whitening \(W\) and PCA onto those \(v_f\).  
  6. For test prompts: compute \(v_f\), score \(s\), compare to threshold.

- **Design tradeoffs**  
  - *Frozen vs. fine-tuned LLM*: Frozen simplifies training and avoids alignment degradation but may limit adaptability; fine-tuning could improve representations but risks catastrophic forgetting.  
  - *Layer selection*: Different depths capture different abstraction levels; Crit method picks layer with maximal goal/framing separation but depends on calibration diversity.  
  - *Dimensionality reduction*: Too little retained variance (\(r\) small) increases false positives; too much reduces sensitivity.  
  - *Threshold choice*: 95th percentile balances precision/recall but may need tuning for specific deployment constraints.

- **Failure signatures**  
  - Off-diagonal ANOVA η² values comparable to diagonal → leakage; reconsider orthogonality weight or pair coverage.  
  - Overlapping \(v_f\) distributions between benign and harmful → FrameShield scores not separable; verify layer choice, PCA variance retention, and calibration set representativeness.  
  - ReDAct training fails to converge (loss plateaus, vanishing gradients) especially for Mistral/Llama3; may require per-model hyperparameter adjustment.  
  - Crit layer selection yields no clear winner → likely due to weak goal/framing separation; fall back to a fixed layer with historically good performance.  
  - OOD novel framings produce scores within benign range → false negatives; consider expanding benign calibration diversity.

- **First three experiments**  
  1. **Disentanglement validation**: After training ReDAct, compute ANOVA η² on a held-out set. If cross-effects are high (> 0.2), increase λ_orth or ensure balanced pair coverage.  
  2. **Anomaly score separation**: Compute FrameShield scores for 500 benign and 500 harmful prompts; plot histograms or compute AUC. AUC < 0.8 indicates need to adjust layer or PCA variance retention.  
  3. **Orthogonality loss ablation**: Train ReDAct with λ_orth = 0 and compare FrameShield performance. Expect a significant drop, confirming the constraint’s importance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the disentanglement framework be extended to handle multiple latent factors beyond two?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 7 states: "Our framework currently focuses on binary semantic factor pairs, and extending to richer multi-factor decompositions is a natural direction."  
- **Why unresolved:** The current contrastive and reconstruction losses are designed for pairwise disentanglement; multiple factors may require new objectives or architectural modifications to disentangle without interference.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Construct a dataset with three or more semantic factors (e.g., goal, framing, sentiment), design a multi-head disentangler, and evaluate whether each head captures its target factor via metrics like ANOV A η².

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Would end-to-end training of the disentanglement module alongside the LLM weights improve factor representations and detection performance?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 7: "Future work can also explore end-to-end integrations that interact with model weights for improving performance."  
- **Why unresolved:** Using a frozen LLM simplifies training but may limit representation adaptability; updating weights could align disentanglement better with model behavior but risks alignment degradation.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiment with jointly training ReDAct and a subset of LLM layers, comparing disentanglement metrics and FrameShield accuracy against the frozen baseline.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the disentanglement approach be effective for other safety-critical semantic factors beyond goal and framing?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 7: "apply semantic factor disentanglement to additional safety-relevant factors beyond goal and framing."  
- **Why unresolved:** Goal and framing were chosen based on framing theory; other factors (e.g., sentiment, topic) may have different statistical properties or interactions that challenge disentanglement.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Identify candidate factors, create contrastive datasets similar to GoalFrameBench, train ReDAct, and measure disentanglement and detection for those factors.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What causes the convergence difficulties during ReDAct training for certain models (e.g., Mistral, Llama3) leading to ineffective critical layer selection?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.3: "Critical layer selection does not improve performance in Mistral and Llama3. This is likely driven by convergence difficulties during ReDAct’s training on a subset of layers for these models, which compromised the identification of truly critical layers."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper speculates but does not investigate; could be due to model-specific activation statistics, optimization landscapes, or data distribution mismatches.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze training loss curves and gradient norms per layer for these models, perform hyperparameter sensitivity studies, or visualize activation distributions to identify convergence bottlenecks.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** What underlying mechanisms in LLM architectures cause goal and framing signals to concentrate at different network depths, and how universal is this pattern?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 6: "These observations are not only insightful for our approach but also relate to the broader mechanistic interpretability literature... a thorough and rigorous investigation in this direction is beyond the scope of this paper and remains an open direction."  
- **Why unresolved:** They observe layer-wise separation but do not explain why; factors like training objectives, residual connections, or attention patterns might influence factor distribution.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct controlled experiments varying model architecture (depth, width), training data, and objectives, then measure goal/framing association (ANOV A η²) across layers to identify causal factors.

## Limitations
- **Dataset construction underspecification:** Minor variations in GoalFrameBench generation (GPT-4.1 temperature, augmentation templates) may affect ReDAct training stability and disentanglement quality.  
- **Per‑model hyperparameter tuning:** Optimal values for λ_orth, representation dimensions, etc., appear model‑specific; the paper provides no tuning protocol, risking suboptimal FrameShield performance on new LLMs.  
- **Layer‑selection robustness:** The Crit heuristic’s stability under distribution shift is unclear; it may fail for models where goal/framing signals are not well separated or calibration sets are unrepresentative.
