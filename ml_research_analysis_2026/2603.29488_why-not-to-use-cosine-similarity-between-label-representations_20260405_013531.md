# Why not to use Cosine Similarity between Label Representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29488
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29488
- **Reference count:** 2
- **Primary result:** Cosine similarity between label unembeddings in a softmax classifier is mathematically disconnected from output probabilities and cannot be used to infer label co-occurrence or semantic relationships.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a fundamental mismatch between a common interpretative tool—cosine similarity between label representations (unembeddings)—and the actual behavior of softmax classifiers. It proves that the probabilities output by such a classifier are invariant to adding any fixed vector to all unembeddings, a transformation that can arbitrarily alter the cosine similarity between any pair of unembeddings. Consequently, models with identical predictive behavior can exhibit any possible cosine similarity value. Even when standard constraints like centering or unit norm are imposed, experiments show that high or low cosine similarity does not reliably correspond to high or low joint probabilities for the same inputs. The core message is that the geometric relationships encoded in the unembedding matrix are not tied to the model's probabilistic outputs and should not be used to explain or predict label co-occurrence.

## Method Summary
The paper conducts a purely theoretical analysis. It begins by formalizing the standard softmax classifier over dot products (Eq. 1). The core method is algebraic proof: first establishing translation invariance of probabilities (Lemma 2.1), then constructing explicit translation vectors that force pairwise cosine similarity to exactly 1 or -1 (Lemmas 2.2, 2.3). Combining these yields the main disconnect theorem (Theorem 2.4). To address potential constraints, the authors provide constructive counterexamples showing that even when unembeddings are centered or normalized, cosine similarity fails to predict joint probabilities. No empirical validation on trained models is presented.

## Key Results
- Softmax classifier probabilities are invariant under adding the same vector to all label unembeddings.
- For any two distinct unembeddings, a translation exists that makes their cosine similarity exactly 1 or -1 while preserving all probabilities.
- Even with centered or unit-norm unembeddings, high/low cosine similarity does not guarantee correspondingly high/low joint probabilities for any input.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Translation Invariance of Probabilities
- **Claim:** Adding a fixed vector **v** to every unembedding **g(y)** leaves all output probabilities **p(y|x)** unchanged.
- **Mechanism:** The logit for label *y* becomes **f(x)⊤(g(y) + v) = f(x)⊤g(y) + f(x)⊤v**. The additive term **f(x)⊤v** is identical for all labels. In the softmax numerator and denominator, this common term cancels out during normalization, preserving the original probability distribution.
- **Core assumption:** The model strictly follows Eq. (1): logits are dot products **f(x)⊤g(y)** without additional per-label biases or non-dot-product interactions.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] States the preservation property (Lemma 2.1).
  - [section 2.1] Provides the full algebraic derivation (Eqs. 2–6).
- **Break condition:** If the architecture includes label-specific biases *b_y* (logits = **f(x)⊤g(y) + b_y**), adding **v** to all **g(y)** adds **f(x)⊤v** to every logit, which does not cancel and thus changes probabilities.

### Mechanism 2: Arbitrary Cosine via Translation
- **Claim:** For any two distinct unembeddings **a** and **b**, there exists a translation **v** that forces **cos(a+v, b+v) = 1** (parallel) or **-1** (anti-parallel).
- **Mechanism:** The proof constructs **v** by solving linear equations that make the translated vectors scalar multiples (for cos=1) or sum to zero (for cos=-1). For cos=-1, choose **v = -(a + ½(b−a))** so that (a+v) + (b+v) = 0. For cos=1, choose **v = -a + ½(b−a)** so that (b+v) = 3(a+v).
- **Core assumption:** The two original unembeddings are distinct (**a ≠ b**); if **a = b**, cosine is already 1 and the construction for -1 is undefined.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 2.2] Contains the constructive proofs for Lemmas 2.2 (cos=1) and 2.3 (cos=-1).
  - [section 3.1] Visualizes the effect in 2D, showing an original cos≈0.8 becoming exactly -1 or 1 after translation.
- **Break condition:** If **a = b**, Lemma 2.2's construction fails (division by zero in the scalar factor), and cos is trivially 1.

### Mechanism 3: Mathematical Disconnect
- **Claim:** Because probabilities are translation-invariant (Mechanism 1) but cosine is translation-variant (Mechanism 2), the two quantities are disconnected. Models with identical **p(y|x)** for all **x** can have any pairwise cosine similarity.
- **Mechanism:** Apply the same translation **v** from Mechanism 2 to all unembeddings. By Mechanism 1, probabilities are preserved. By Mechanism 2, the cosine for a chosen pair becomes exactly 1 or -1. Since **v** can be chosen independently for each pair (by adding multiple translations sequentially), any set of pairwise cosine values is achievable.
- **Core assumption:** At least two labels have distinct unembeddings originally; otherwise all cosines are trivially 1.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] Summarizes the disconnect: "it is possible to make another model which gives the same probabilities... but where the cosine similarity... is now either 1 or -1."
  - [section 2.3] States Theorem 2.4 and uses Lemmas 2.1–2.3 in its proof.
  - [section 3.2–3.3] Shows that even after constraining unembeddings (centering, unit norm), high/low cosine does not predict joint probabilities.
- **Break condition:** If we restrict the equivalence class by fixing a canonical representation (e.g., forcing the unembeddings to have zero mean), then not all cosine values can be realized while keeping probabilities identical—but the paper shows that even within such constrained geometries, cosine still fails to predict joint probabilities.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Softmax classifier mechanics**  
  Model outputs are **p(y|x) = exp(f(x)⊤g(y)) / Σ_{y'} exp(f(x)⊤g(y'))**.  
  *Why needed:* This form reveals that probabilities depend only on *differences* between dot products **f(x)⊤g(y)**, not on absolute values. A common additive shift to all **g(y)** adds the same constant to every logit, which cancels in the softmax normalization.  
  *Quick check:* If you add **v** to every **g(y)**, each logit gets **+ f(x)⊤v**. Why does the softmax output not change? (Because the same constant is added to numerator and denominator of every class probability, preserving ratios.)

- **Concept: Cosine similarity and its invariances**  
  Cosine is defined as **cos(a,b) = (a·b) / (∥a∥∥b∥)**. It is invariant to uniform scaling of either vector but *not* to translation (adding a constant vector).  
  *Why needed:* The disconnect arises because softmax is translation-invariant while cosine is translation-variant. Understanding which transformations affect which measure explains why they can be decoupled.  
  *Quick check:* Given vectors **a** and **b**, can you find a **v** such that **cos(a+v, b+v) = 0**? What if **a** and **b** are already orthogonal? (Yes, often possible by solving for **v** that makes (a+v)·(b+v)=0.)

- **Concept: Translation vs. scaling transformations**  
  Softmax probabilities are invariant to translating all unembeddings by **v** (adds same constant to all logits). They are also invariant to scaling **f(x)** by *c* and all **g(y)** by *1/c* (dot products unchanged). Cosine is invariant to scaling either vector but not to translation.  
  *Why needed:* Recognizing these distinct invariance groups clarifies that the absolute positions of unembedding vectors are unidentifiable from probabilities, while their relative angles (cosine) are a separate, non-invariant property.  
  *Quick check:* If you multiply all **g(y)** by 2 and all **f(x)** by ½, what happens to probabilities? To cosine between two **g(y)**? (Probabilities unchanged; cosine between **g(y)** vectors unchanged because both scaled equally.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input encoder -> f(x) -> Label lookup table (unembedding matrix G) -> Dot products f(x)⊤g(y) -> Softmax -> p(y|x)
- **Critical path:**  
  1. For input *x*, compute representation **f(x)**.  
  2. For each label *y*, compute logit *z_y* = **f(x)⊤g(y)**.  
  3. Apply softmax to logits *z* to obtain probabilities **p(y|x)**.  
  *Key insight:* The dot product step depends only on differences between **g(y)** vectors. Adding a constant **v** to all **g(y)** adds **f(x)⊤v** to every logit, which cancels in step 3. Thus, the absolute placement of the columns of **G** does not affect outputs.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Centering constraint* (mean of **g(y)** = 0) removes translation ambiguity for cosine, but does not guarantee that cosine reflects joint probabilities (Section 3.2).  
  - *Unit-norm constraint* (∥g(y)∥=1) still fails to link cosine to probability (Section 3.3).  
  These constraints do not alter the fundamental invariance: multiple equivalent **G** matrices with different pairwise cosines yield identical **p(y|x)**.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **High cosine** between **g(y₁)** and **g(y₂)** does *not* imply that for many inputs *x*, **p(y₁|x)** and **p(y₂|x)** are both high or positively correlated.  
  - **Low/negative cosine** does *not* imply that **y₁** and **y₂** are rarely predicted together or have disjoint decision regions.  
  In both cases, one can always find an **f(x)** that makes the probabilities for that pair extreme or tied, regardless of cosine.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Reproduce Lemma 2.1:** Create a random softmax classifier with **f(x)** and **g(y)**. Pick random **v**, add to all **g(y)**, and verify that **p(y|x)** is unchanged for a batch of **x**.  
  2. **Test Theorem 2.4:** For a specific pair of distinct unembeddings from a toy model, compute the translation **v** from Lemma 2.2 (cos=1) and Lemma 2.3 (cos=-1). With the same **f(x)** samples, confirm probabilities are identical to the original while cosines become exactly 1 and -1.  
  3. **Counterexample hunt:** Use the paper's construction for centered (Section 3.2) or unit-norm (Section 3.3) unembeddings. For a label pair with high cosine, try to find an **f(x)** that yields very different probabilities for the two labels. For a pair with low cosine, try to find an **f(x)** that makes their probabilities equal or nearly equal. This demonstrates that even constrained geometries do not couple cosine to joint probability.

## Limitations
- **Biases not covered:** The invariance proof assumes no per-label biases. Adding a common **v** to unembeddings would change probabilities if biases exist, limiting the generality of the disconnect claim.
- **Empirical gap:** All evidence is synthetic; no analysis of models trained on real data shows whether optimization implicitly avoids translations that create extreme cosine values.
- **Training dynamics unknown:** The paper proves existence of equivalent models with arbitrary cosine, but does not address whether gradient-based training ever produces such translations without adjusting the encoder **f(x)**.

## Confidence
- **Lemma 2.1 (translation invariance):** High. The algebraic cancellation is straightforward and easily verified.
- **Lemmas 2.2 & 2.3 (forcing cos=±1):** High. Constructive proofs are correct and explicit.
- **Theorem 2.4 (cosine–probability disconnect):** High for the statement that cosine is not determined by probabilities. The claim that *any* cosine value can be realized is strongly supported by achieving extremes and continuity, but not rigorously proven for every intermediate value.
- **Constraint experiments (centered/unit-norm):** High. The provided counterexamples clearly show that constraints do not rescue the predictive link between cosine and joint probabilities.

## Next Checks
1. **Bias sensitivity test:** Implement a softmax with per-label biases. Apply a translation **v** to all unembeddings and verify that probabilities change, establishing the boundary of Lemma 2.1.
2. **Real-model correlation study:** Train a softmax classifier (e.g., on CIFAR-10). Compute pairwise cosine similarities among unembeddings and empirical co-occurrence statistics (e.g., probability vector correlations, top‑k overlaps). Expect minimal correlation, consistent with the theoretical disconnect.
3. **Translation sweep for a pair:** Take unembeddings from a trained model. For a fixed label pair, sample many random translation vectors **v** and compute **cos(g(y₁)+v, g(y₂)+v)**. Verify that the resulting cosines span a wide range (including values far from the original and both ±1), confirming arbitrary manipulability.
