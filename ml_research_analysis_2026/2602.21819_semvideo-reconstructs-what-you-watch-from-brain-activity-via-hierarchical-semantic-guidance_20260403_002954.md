# SemVideo: Reconstructs What You Watch from Brain Activity via Hierarchical Semantic Guidance
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21819
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21819
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves state-of-the-art video reconstruction from fMRI, winning 8 of 10 metrics with 2-way retrieval 0.865, VIFI 0.608, hue-pcc 0.849, and low EPE 4.788.

## Executive Summary
SemVideo tackles persistent challenges in fMRI-to-video reconstruction: inconsistent object appearances and poor temporal coherence. It introduces SemMiner, a hierarchical semantic guidance module that uses a multimodal large language model to decompose videos into three textual description levels—static anchor, motion narratives, and holistic summaries. These descriptions guide three core components: a Semantic Alignment Decoder maps fMRI signals to CLIP embeddings; a Motion Adaptation Decoder with tripartite attention reconstructs motion latents; and a Conditional Video Render progressively synthesizes the final video. Evaluated on CC2017 and HCP datasets, SemVideo sets new benchmarks, achieving top scores on the majority of metrics and demonstrating significant gains in semantic alignment, pixel fidelity, and spatiotemporal consistency. Ablation studies confirm that all three semantic levels are essential, with motion guidance being particularly critical for temporal smoothness. ROI analyses further show that distinct brain regions (e.g., MT for motion) correspond to each semantic stream, offering neuroscientific validation.

## Method Summary
The SemVideo pipeline starts with SemMiner, which takes a training video and, via a multimodal LLM, produces three textual descriptions: a static anchor (key objects and scenes), motion narratives (describing movements), and a holistic summary (overall context). These descriptions are encoded (e.g., with CLIP text encoder) into semantic embeddings. The fMRI signals are processed by two specialized decoders. The Semantic Alignment Decoder aligns fMRI to CLIP image embeddings that represent the static anchor. The Motion Adaptation Decoder employs tripartite attention to integrate fMRI data, static anchor embeddings, and motion narrative embeddings, producing a motion latent code. Finally, the Conditional Video Render conditions on all hierarchical cues (static CLIP embeddings, motion latent, holistic summary) to generate video frames progressively, ensuring coherence. Training uses paired fMRI-video data with losses for embedding alignment, motion prediction, and frame reconstruction.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art performance on CC2017 and HCP, winning 8 of 10 evaluation metrics.
- Strong semantic alignment: 2-way video retrieval score of 0.865.
- Excellent spatiotemporal fidelity: VIFI-score 0.608, hue-pcc 0.849, and low Endpoint Error (EPE) of 4.788.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hierarchical separation of video content into static, motion, and holistic components aligns with the nature of fMRI signals, which have low temporal resolution and encode high-level semantics. By explicitly modeling these three aspects, SemVideo reduces ambiguity and enforces consistency across appearance, motion, and context. The Semantic Alignment Decoder anchors object appearances via CLIP embeddings, while the Motion Adaptation Decoder uses tripartite attention to fuse fMRI patterns with motion narratives and static features, producing coherent motion latents. The Conditional Video Render then combines all cues to synthesize frames progressively, mitigating temporal artifacts. Ablations demonstrate that removing any semantic level degrades performance, confirming that each contributes uniquely. Moreover, ROI activation maps reveal that the brain processes these semantics in partially distinct regions (e.g., MT for motion), supporting the conceptual split and indicating that the model captures meaningful neural representations.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
SemMiner (video) → {Static Anchor, Motion Narratives, Holistic Summary}  
fMRI → Semantic Alignment Decoder → Static CLIP Embeddings  
fMRI + Motion Narratives + Static Anchor → Motion Adaptation Decoder → Motion Latents  
Static CLIP Embeddings + Motion Latents + Holistic Summary → Conditional Video Render → Video Frames

**Critical path**: The pipeline begins with hierarchical textual descriptions (from SemMiner) and fMRI input. The Semantic Alignment Decoder produces static semantic embeddings; the Motion Adaptation Decoder, using tripartite attention, generates motion latents; finally, the Conditional Video Render synthesizes the video by attending to all hierarchical cues.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Reliance on an external multimodal LLM (SemMiner) provides rich semantics but introduces computational overhead and potential bias.  
- The three-branch decoder design allows specialized processing but increases model complexity.  
- Progressive rendering improves video quality at the cost of slower inference.  
- Aligning fMRI to CLIP embeddings focuses on semantics, which may sacrifice low-level pixel details; the renderer compensates for this.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Inaccurate static anchor → object appearance mismatches and wrong identities.  
- Missing or corrupted motion narratives → temporal jitter and high EPE.  
- Distorted holistic summary → incoherent scene context and overall semantic drift.  
Additionally, poor fMRI signal-to-noise ratio can degrade alignment across all components.

**First experiments**:  
- Component isolation: Verify that each decoder produces outputs consistent with its intended semantics (e.g., static anchor embeddings correlate with ground-truth CLIP embeddings).  
- Ablation replication: Remove each semantic level and measure metric drops (retrieval, VIFI, EPE) to confirm contributions.  
- ROI validation: Conduct group-level ROI analysis to ensure expected activations (e.g., MT for motion) across subjects with statistical testing.

## Limitations
- Generalizability: Evaluation restricted to CC2017 and HCP; performance on diverse or clinical data remains unknown.  
- LLM dependency: SemMiner’s potential biases or inaccuracies may propagate to reconstruction.  
- Lack of human evaluation: Automated metrics may not fully capture subjective video quality or semantic fidelity.

## Confidence
- Resolution of appearance mismatches and motion misalignment: Medium  
- Necessity of all three semantic levels (static, motion, holistic): Medium‑High  
- State‑of‑the‑art performance on CC2017/HCP: High  
- Neuroscientific validation via ROI activation maps: Medium  

## Next Checks
- Cross-dataset generalization: Test on additional datasets (e.g., Noble, BOLD5000) to evaluate robustness.
- Human perceptual evaluation: Conduct within-subject studies rating semantic accuracy, temporal smoothness, and overall fidelity.
- Rigorous ROI statistical testing: Use representational similarity analysis (RSA) or encoding modeling with permutation tests to quantitatively link semantic levels to fMRI patterns.
