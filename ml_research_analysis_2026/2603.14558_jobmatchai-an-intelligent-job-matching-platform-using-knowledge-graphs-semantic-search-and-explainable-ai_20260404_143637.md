# JobMatchAI An Intelligent Job Matching Platform Using Knowledge Graphs, Semantic Search and Explainable AI

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14558  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14558  
- **Reference Count:** 2  
- **Primary Result:** Hybrid retrieval with reranking achieves NDCG@10 of 0.81 (7% relative gain over BM25) with median latency under 100 ms.

## Executive Summary
JobMatchAI addresses the limitations of keyword-based job matching, such as failure to handle skill synonyms and opaque scoring, by integrating a hybrid retrieval stack with transparent, multi-factor reranking. The system combines BM25 lexical search, semantic vector search, and Neo4j knowledge graph traversal for skill expansion, followed by a user-adjustable utility function and grounded LLM explanations. On the JobSearch-XS benchmark, it achieves high ranking quality (NDCG@10=0.81) and low latency, while a pilot user study (N=20) rated the AI-generated explanations as highly helpful (4.17/5) and noted the value of factor-level control.

## Method Summary
The pipeline ingests job postings and resumes, indexing text/vectors in Elasticsearch and skill relationships in a Neo4j knowledge graph built from ESCO-style synonyms. For a query, it extracts skills and enriches them via depth-2 graph traversals. Three retrievers (BM25, ANN, KG) run in parallel (k=150/150/75), and their results are merged using query-adaptive Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) with a union cap of 400. After hard filtering, a white-box utility scorer computes six normalized factor scores (skill fit, experience, location, salary, semantic similarity, company fit) and combines them with user-adjustable weights. Finally, a generative LLM narrates the precomputed scores and supporting KG paths to produce auditable explanations.

## Key Results
- Hybrid retrieval + reranking yields NDCG@10=0.81, a 7% relative improvement over the BM25 baseline.
- Median end-to-end latency remains under 100 ms (P50=82 ms).
- Pilot user study (N=20) rated explanation helpfulness at 4.17/5 and weight-adjustment usefulness at 3.83/5.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The hybrid retrieval stack improves recall for skill synonyms and semantic generalization while maintaining sub-100ms latency.
- **Mechanism:** Parallel execution of BM25, semantic (ANN), and knowledge graph retrievers, followed by query-adaptive RRF fusion. Short queries (≤2 tokens) prioritize graph expansion (w_kg=0.7); longer queries favor text channels. Graph traversal uses `RELATED_TO` edges to expand skills (e.g., "Kubernetes" → "container orchestration").
- **Core assumption:** Each channel surfaces distinct candidate sets with limited overlap, and graph edges correctly associate synonymous skills.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] hybrid stack description; [section 2.3] parallel retrieval parameters and adaptive weights; [section 4.1] Table 2 (NDCG@10=0.81, P50=82ms).
- **Break condition:** Sparse or noisy `RELATED_TO` edges introduce irrelevant candidates, harming precision (evidenced by low NDCG for KG-only baseline). The recall gap (Recall@100=0.35 vs BM25's 0.57) suggests fusion may over-prune.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The multi-factor utility reranker produces interpretable, user-controllable rankings by decomposing match quality into six normalized, audit-friendly components.
- **Mechanism:** Each candidate-job pair receives six factor scores ϕ_f ∈ [0,1] (e.g., skill Jaccard + KG bonus, experience level distance). Final score U(c,j)=Σ w_f·ϕ_f uses user-adjustable weights via frontend sliders.
- **Core assumption:** Each hiring dimension can be quantified from structured features without learned parameters, and linear weights adequately express user preferences.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] multi-factor utility with adjustable weights; [section 2.4] Table 1 (factor definitions and default weights); [section 4.2] user study weight-adjustment rating (3.83/5).
- **Break condition:** Ill-defined normalizations (e.g., salary ratio for jobs lacking midpoints) destabilize ϕ_f. User study feedback on "more granular skill filtering" indicates factor definitions may be too coarse.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The LLM explanation layer generates faithful, auditable narratives because it receives only precomputed factor scores and KG paths, not raw documents.
- **Mechanism:** Post-reranking, the six ϕ_f scores and top N KG paths (e.g., `(Candidate)-[:HAS_SKILL]->(Python)`) are fed to an LLM with a prompt instructing it to narrate only the provided evidence, preventing hallucination.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM can synthesize coherent explanations from numerical scores and triples without adding unsupported claims, and KG paths provide sufficient evidence.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "explained via a grounded LLM"; [section 2.4] grounding claim; [section 4.2] audit: 100% avoided unsupported claims, 94.5% surfaced weaknesses (<0.5 scores).
- **Break condition:** Miscalculated scores or irrelevant KG paths yield misleading narratives. The 70.5% rate for mentioning the top factor (C1) suggests omissions, possibly due to prompt design or LLM focus on negatives.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Knowledge Graph Traversal for Skill Expansion**
  - *Why needed:* Resolves vocabulary mismatch (e.g., "k8s" vs "Kubernetes") by following `RELATED_TO` edges to surface adjacent skills absent from the original query.
  - *Quick check:* If a candidate has "Docker" and a job requires "container orchestration," what KG path connects them, and how does this affect retrieval recall?

- **Concept: Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) for Multi-Source List Merging**
  - *Why needed:* Combines three heterogeneous ranked lists (BM25, ANN, KG) without score normalization. Query-adaptive weighting prioritizes channels based on query length.
  - *Quick check:* Why might fixed RRF weights (w_lexical=w_semantic=w_kg=1/3) underperform the query-adaptive version for short, skill-focused queries?

- **Concept: Normalized Utility Scoring with User-Adjustable Weights**
  - *Why needed:* Transforms incomparable signals (skill overlap count, salary ratio) into a single ranking score while letting users reweight priorities (e.g., salary over location). Each ϕ_f is independently normalized to [0,1].
  - *Quick check:* If a user sets "Skill" weight to 0.6 and "Location" to 0.1, how does this change the ranking compared to default weights, and what happens to the LLM explanation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Query Enricher -> ThreadPool (BM25/ES, ANN/HNSW, KG/Neo4j) -> RRF Merger -> Hard Filter -> Reranker (Utility Calculator) -> LLM Explainer. Data stores: Elasticsearch (text+vectors), Neo4j (skill graph), Redis (caching?). Ingestion pipeline separately populates stores from crawled jobs and resume uploads.

**Critical path:** Query → Skill extraction → Parallel retrieval (150+150+75) → Union/RRF (cap 400) → Hard filters → Compute 6 factor scores per candidate → Top-N reranked → Generate explanations via LLM. Latency dominated by parallel retrieval (~80ms).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Recall vs. Precision:* High k-per-channel (150) + union cap 400 boosts recall but fusion and filters reduce final Recall@100 to 0.35 vs BM25's 0.57. Tradeoff intentional for precision-focused interactive search.
- *Deterministic vs. Generative:* Scoring is white-box math; LLM only narrates. Ensures auditability but limits explanation depth to precomputed factors (no counterfactuals).
- *Graph coverage vs. Freshness:* Skill graph built from ESCO synonyms; misses emerging skills (limitation §4.3). Requires manual curation.

**Failure signatures:**
- *Low NDCG@10 (<0.7):* Check graph edge density, embedding quality, or RRF weight misconfiguration.
- *High recall drop from BM25 baseline:* Inspect fusion stage—union cap too low or RRF weights undervaluing lexical channel for long queries.
- *Explanation mismatches:* Audit LLM input contains correct ϕ_f scores and relevant KG paths; prompt enforces grounding constraint.
- *Latency >200ms:* Profile parallel retrieval; likely ANN or graph query bottleneck (check HNSW parameters, Neo4j indexes).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Channel isolation ablation:** Run pipeline with only one retriever active (BM25-only, semantic-only, KG-only). Measure NDCG@10 and Recall@100 against Table 2 to validate each channel's contribution and diagnose hybrid gains.
2. **RRF weight sensitivity:** Fix k-values, vary query-adaptive weights (e.g., test w_kg from 0.3 to 0.9 for short queries). Plot NDCG@10 vs. weight to confirm adaptive strategy outperforms fixed weights, especially on skill-synonym queries.
3. **Explanation faithfulness audit:** For 100 random results, compare LLM narrative to computed ϕ_f values and KG paths. Check (a) top factor mentioned, (b) any factor <0.5 flagged as weakness, (c) no claims unsupported by input. Use keyword matching as in §4.2.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling JobSearch-XS:** How can the benchmark be scaled to a larger, multi-domain set with denser human judgments to improve evaluation reliability and coverage? The current small, single-domain benchmark (30 queries) yields high metric variance and limited generalization assessment.
2. **Improving Fusion Recall:** Can the hybrid retrieval fusion (RRF) be modified to improve Recall@100 without sacrificing the precision gains that drive high NDCG? The current union cap and weights cause a recall gap (0.35 vs BM25's 0.57).
3. **Graph Freshness for Emerging Skills:** To what extent does the static ESCO-based skill graph limit performance on emerging or niche skills, and how can the graph be maintained or learned to stay current? The reliance on curated synonyms misses new skills and jargon.
4. **Bias in Explainable Scoring:** Does the explainable scoring and user-adjustable weights inadvertently introduce or amplify biases against non-traditional candidates (e.g., career changers, bootcamp graduates)? Factor scores and graph topology may disadvantage nonlinear resumes, and weight controls could encode discriminatory preferences.
5. **Explanation Faithfulness in Practice:** Are the AI-generated explanations truly faithful and sufficiently detailed for high-stakes hiring, given 29.5% of cases omitted the top contributing factor? Omissions could mislead users and undermine trust/compliance.

## Limitations
- **Recall gap:** Hybrid fusion yields Recall@100=0.35, below BM25's 0.57, indicating over-pruning in the interest of precision.
- **Static knowledge graph:** ESCO-based synonym table lacks coverage for emerging skills, requiring manual curation and limiting recency.
- **Small benchmark:** JobSearch-XS (30 queries) is too limited for reliable metrics and cross-domain evaluation, as acknowledged by authors.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid retrieval stack improves recall for skill synonyms while maintaining latency:** High (supported by ablation metrics and latency logs).
- **Multi-factor utility reranker enables interpretable, user-controllable rankings:** Medium (user study validates control but notes coarse granularity).
- **LLM explanations are faithful and auditable:** Medium-High (audit shows high avoidance of unsupported claims, but 70.5% top-factor mention rate suggests omissions).

## Next Checks
1. **Channel Isolation Ablation:** Run the pipeline with only one retriever active (BM25, semantic, or KG) on JobSearch-XS. Measure NDCG@10 and Recall@100 to quantify each channel's unique contribution and validate the hybrid gain claim.
2. **RRF Weight Sensitivity Sweep:** Fix retrieval parameters, vary the query-adaptive KG weight (e.g., 0.3–0.9 for short queries) and text weight for longer queries. Plot NDCG@10 to confirm the adaptive strategy outperforms fixed weights, especially on skill-synonym queries.
3. **Explanation Input-Output Audit:** For 100 sampled results, programmatically compare the LLM's narrative against the exact input ϕ_f scores and KG paths. Check: (a) top factor by score is mentioned, (b) any factor <0.5 is flagged as a weakness, (c) no claims fall outside the provided evidence.
