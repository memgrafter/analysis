# Instructor-Aligned Knowledge Graphs for Personalized Learning
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.17111
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17111
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** InstructKG achieves mean node significance scores above 0.94 and triplet accuracy up to 0.577 (18-34% gains) over recent LLM-based KG baselines across three CS courses.

## Executive Summary
The automatic construction of fine-grained, instructor-aligned knowledge graphs from lecture materials remains a challenging problem. Existing methods often rely on surface-level data or ignore pedagogical signals, failing to capture prerequisite and compositional learning dependencies.

InstructKG addresses this by synergizing temporal teaching order with semantic pedagogical role classification (Definition, Example, Assumption) and LLM reasoning over clustered evidence chunks. Across three computer science courses, it achieves mean node significance scores above 0.94 and triplet accuracy up to 0.577 with Qwen-14B, representing 18-34% improvements over recent LLM-based KG baselines. Human evaluation confirms graph quality, and a student error-mapping application demonstrates diagnostic utility.

## Method Summary
InstructKG is a three-phase pipeline that transforms lecture PDFs into a directed knowledge graph of learning concepts. First, documents are preprocessed into text chunks (max 8192 tokens). An LLM then extracts concepts from each chunk, which are deduplicated and normalized to canonical IDs. Next, the same LLM classifies each (chunk, concept) pair into pedagogical roles: Definition, Example, Assumption, or NA. Meanwhile, all chunks are embedded and clustered thematically using UMAP and HDBSCAN; representative chunks are selected per cluster. For every candidate concept pair that co-occurs either within a chunk or inside the same cluster, an evidence packet is assembled containing the relevant chunks, role labels, and temporal first-introduction order. A final LLM judge determines the relation type (depends_on, part_of, null) under DAG constraints. The resulting edges and nodes form the final knowledge graph.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Temporal teaching order provides a strong, low-cost prior for inferring prerequisite (`depends_on`) relationships.
**Mechanism:** The framework tracks the first introduction (`first(𝑣)`) of each concept across the ordered lecture sequence. Concepts appearing earlier are treated as candidate prerequisites for concepts introduced later, especially when combined with role signals.
**Core assumption:** An instructor's sequencing reflects the intended learning progression, such that foundational concepts are presented before those that build upon them.
**Evidence anchors:** 
- [abstract] "recursion is taught before merge sort"
- [section 3.1] Equation (1) formalizes `first(𝑣)` using `lecture_index` and `chunk_index`.
- [section 5.1] Ablation "*No Clustering*" (which retains temporal signals but removes cross-chunk aggregation) still achieves triplet accuracy within ~3–4% of full InstructKG on Algorithms (0.456 vs. 0.487 with Qwen-14B), showing temporal order alone captures substantial prerequisite signal.
**Break condition:** Fails for courses with non-linear topic progression (e.g., interleaved modules) or where instructor sequencing does not align with logical dependency (e.g., motivational examples early).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Pedagogical role classification (`Definition`, `Example`, `Assumption`) disambiguates `depends_on` vs. `part_of` edge types beyond mere co-occurrence.
**Mechanism:** During relation judgment, the LLM receives role labels for each concept in relevant chunks. A concept appearing as an `Assumption` while another is `Defined` signals a prerequisite (`A depends_on B`). A concept appearing as an `Example` alongside another's `Definition` suggests composition (`A part_of B`).
**Core assumption:** These roles are consistently interpretable by an LLM and predictive of the directional relationship type.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "recursion is mentioned in the definition of merge sort"
- [section 3.2] Explicitly defines the three roles and gives the mapping rule: `Assumption` in context of another's `Definition` → `depends_on`.
- [section 5.1] Ablation "*No Roles*" causes the largest triplet accuracy drop on Algorithms (0.357 vs. 0.487 with Qwen-14B), indicating role classification is most critical for accurately typing edges in that course.
**Break condition:** Fails if role classification is noisy (e.g., confusing `Example` with `Definition`) or if pedagogical language in lectures is ambiguous/inconsistent.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Thematic clustering of semantically related chunks surfaces implicit dependencies between concepts that do not co-occur within a single chunk.
**Mechanism:** Chunks are embedded, reduced (UMAP), and clustered (HDBSCAN). For a candidate concept pair, if both appear in the same cluster (even in different chunks), separate representative chunks for each are aggregated into an evidence packet, allowing the LLM to infer relationships from broader thematic similarity.
**Core assumption:** Related educational concepts tend to appear in thematically coherent segments of the corpus, even if separated by chunk boundaries.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [section 3.3] Describes the clustering pipeline and evidence packet construction from cluster co-occurrence.
- [section 5.1] Ablation "*No Clustering*" shows mixed effects: triplet accuracy drops on Algorithms (0.456 vs. 0.487) but is nearly identical or slightly better on NLP/SQL (e.g., NLP: 0.591 vs. 0.577; SQL: 0.595 vs. 0.576). This suggests clustering's benefit is course-structure-dependent.
**Break condition:** Fails if chunk embeddings poorly capture semantic similarity, if clustering parameters produce fragmented or overly broad clusters, or if course topics are highly discrete with little thematic overlap.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Document Processor -> Concept Extractor -> Role Classifier -> Context Clusterer -> Evidence Aggregator -> Relation Judge -> KG Builder

### Critical Path
1. **Document Processor** converts PDFs to sorted text chunks (with lecture_id, chunk_index, pages).
2. **Concept Extractor** runs LLM prompts per chunk to extract raw concept strings, which are deduped and normalized to canonical IDs.
3. **Role Classifier** assigns pedagogical roles (`Definition`, `Example`, `Assumption`, `NA`) to each (chunk, concept) pair.
4. **Context Clusterer** embeds all chunks, applies UMAP (15 components, 15 neighbors) and HDBSCAN (min_cluster_size=5) to find thematic clusters; selects representative chunks per cluster.
5. **Evidence Aggregator** builds evidence packets for candidate concept pairs (from chunk co-occurrence or shared cluster) using roles, temporal first-introduction metadata, and up to 3 representative chunks.
6. **Relation Judge** uses an LLM to decide `depends_on`, `part_of`, or `null` for each pair, with DAG constraints and alphabetical normalization.
7. **KG Builder** assembles the final graph `(V, E)` from all non-null judgments.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Chunk size:** Smaller chunks increase granularity but risk splitting related content; larger chunks provide context but introduce noise and higher LLM cost.
- **Clustering parameters:** `min_cluster_size` and `n_neighbors` control cluster granularity; too small yields noisy micro-clusters, too large over-merges topics.
- **LLM choice:** Larger models (e.g., Qwen-14B) improve reasoning but increase compute; smaller models may suffice for simpler courses.

### Failure Signatures
- **Node significance low (<0.8):** Concept extractor pulling non-concepts (e.g., variable names). Check prompt specificity and deduplication.
- **Triplet accuracy low (<0.3):** Role classifier mislabeling or evidence packets poorly constructed. Inspect role distribution and evidence relevance.
- **Many null relations from judge:** Evidence packets lack clear support; adjust candidate pair generation thresholds or evidence selection.
- **Clustering noise (high -1 label):** Embedding model or HDBSCAN misconfiguration; visualize UMAP projections.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Sanity check pipeline:** Run full flow on 1–2 lecture PDFs with Llama-3B. Verify chunks, concepts, roles, clusters, and edges; catch preprocessing bugs.
2. **Ablation: Role classification:** Disable role classifier (assign all `NA`). Rerun relation judgment. Expect significant triplet accuracy drop on theory-heavy courses (like Algorithms), confirming role signal importance.
3. **Manual spot-check:** Sample 20 candidate pairs from evidence aggregator. For each, read the provided chunks and judge relationship; compare to LLM output. Reveals if evidence selection or LLM judgment is the bottleneck.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability:** Results are from only three CS courses; uncertain if the method transfers to other domains or non-lecture materials (e.g., textbooks, labs).
- **Clustering Sensitivity:** Thematic clustering shows inconsistent benefits; performance highly sensitive to hyperparameters and course-specific topic coherence.
- **Role Classification Ambiguity & Evaluation Biases:** Pedagogical role definitions may not capture all instructive language; errors propagate to edge typing. Evaluation relies on LLM-as-judge metrics with limited human validation; no downstream student learning impact measured.

## Confidence
- Temporal order as prerequisite prior: Medium-High
- Role classification for edge typing: High
- Thematic clustering utility: Medium
- Overall performance gains (18–34% over baselines): Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Chunking heuristic sensitivity test:** Re-run InstructKG with alternative chunking strategies (fixed-size, slide-boundary, semantic similarity threshold) on one course; measure impact on triplet accuracy and cluster quality.
2. **Role label calibration study:** Manually annotate 200 (chunk, concept) pairs for roles. Compute inter-annotator agreement and LLM role classifier F1. If <0.7, role signal reliability is questionable.
3. **Cross-domain transfer test:** Apply the trained InstructKG pipeline (without fine-tuning) to a non-CS course (e.g., introductory biology). Report node significance and triplet accuracy; a drop >15% would confirm limited domain generalizability.
