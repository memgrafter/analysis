# Probing the Lack of Stable Internal Beliefs in LLMs
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.25187
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25187
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: All tested LLMs exhibit 100% dialogue drift occurrence; per-turn drift rates range from 17.37% to over 50%; KL divergence regularization in supervised fine-tuning significantly reduces drift while preserving external consistency.

## Executive Summary
This work investigates LLMs’ ability to maintain implicit consistency—stable adherence to unstated goals—in multi-turn interactions, a key requirement for persona-driven systems. Using a 20‑question guessing game with numerical indexing, the authors track internal belief states via Token‑based Drift Rate and KL Divergence. Results show pervasive goal drift: every tested model experiences at least one drift per dialogue, with high per‑turn instability. KL divergence regularization during fine‑training substantially reduces drift without harming externally observable answer consistency, highlighting a fundamental limitation in internal state stability and the need for explicit anchoring mechanisms for reliable persona modeling.

## Method Summary
The authors implement a 20‑question guessing game where a Proposer (LLM) selects a secret target from 10 candidates and answers yes/no questions from a Guesser. After each turn, a “SUDO USER” probe forces the Proposer to output the index (0‑9) of its current internal selection, yielding a probability distribution over candidates. Drift is measured by Token Drift Rate (discrete argmax changes) and average KL divergence between consecutive distributions; external consistency is verified by an LLM judge. For fine‑tuning, the training loss combines cross‑entropy with a KL regularization term: L = α·D_KL(P_t_probe ∥ P_initial) + β·L_CE, which incentivizes the belief distribution to remain close to the initial one.

## Key Results
- Pervasive drift: All tested LLMs show 100% dialogue drift occurrence; per‑turn drift rates range from 17.37% to over 50%.
- KL divergence regularization drastically reduces drift (e.g., Qwen‑2.5‑14B‑Instruct Drift Rate drops from 36.83% to 13.99%) while maintaining 100% external consistency.
- Reasoning‑enhanced model variants often exhibit increased drift, suggesting “overthinking” destabilizes simple goal maintenance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Standard LLM architectures lack a persistent, anchored internal representation for an implicitly chosen goal across multi‑turn interactions.
- **Mechanism:** The model’s next‑token prediction is conditioned on the immediate conversational context but does not maintain a stable, latent "goal state" vector that resists drift from new information or reasoning paths. Each turn re‑computes a belief distribution over candidates based on the累积 history, but without an explicit mechanism to privilege the initial selection, the distribution can shift.
- **Core assumption:** Autoregressive generation without explicit state tracking leads to context‑dependent belief recomputation rather than stateful goal maintenance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "LLMs struggle to preserve latent consistency: their implicit 'goals' shift across turns unless explicitly provided their selected target in context."
  - [section 2] Formalizes implicit inconsistency as `arg maxe∈E bk = e′ ≠ et` despite externally consistent answers.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers on "stable personality trait evaluation" and "maintaining a persona in discourse" implicitly acknowledge this instability as an open problem.
- **Break condition:** If the model’s initial target is re‑provided in the context at each turn (bypassing the need for internal persistence), drift ceases.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Implicit goal drift is exacerbated in reasoning‑enhanced models for simple tasks due to "overthinking"—the model generates intermediate reasoning chains that inadvertently reframe or重新weight candidate properties mid‑dialogue.
- **Mechanism:** The model’s internal chain‑of‑thought or extended reasoning process introduces new contextual constraints or interpretations that conflict with the originally selected target’s properties, causing the belief distribution to shift even though the final yes/no answers remain externally consistent for both the old and new implied target.
- **Core assumption:** Reasoning processes are not goal‑anchored; they freely explore hypotheses that can overwrite the latent selection.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1] "+ Reasoning" variants show increased drift rates in Number Guessing (e.g., Deepseek‑v3.1 from 38.46% to 54.25%), suggesting reasoning destabilizes simple goal maintenance.
  - [abstract] "reasoning‑enhanced model variants" often exacerbate the problem.
- **Break condition:** If reasoning is constrained or directed to reference the initial selection (e.g., via a system prompt reminder), drift may reduce.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** KL divergence regularization on the belief state distribution during training can mitigate implicit drift by penalizing deviation from the initial belief state, effectively anchoring the implicit goal.
- **Mechanism:** The training objective `L = α·D_KL(P_t_probe ∥ P_initial) + β·L_CE` explicitly incentivizes the model’s turn‑wise belief distribution (probed via the index question) to remain close to its distribution at turn 0. This creates a pressure to maintain the initial probability mass on the originally selected index.
- **Core assumption:** The internal belief state, as approximated by the probing question’s output distribution, is a trainable signal that can be regularized.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.4] Defines the combined loss with KL term.
  - [section 4.2] KL‑only fine‑tuning reduces Drift Rate from 36.83% to 13.99% for Qwen‑2.5‑14B‑Instruct.
  - [abstract] "KL divergence regularization in supervised fine‑tuning significantly reduces drift."
- **Break condition:** If the KL penalty weight (α) is zero or too small, the effect disappears; CE‑only training fails to reduce drift.

## Foundational Learning
### Implicit vs. External Consistency
- **Why needed here:** The paper’s core distinction separates observable answer contradictions (external) from hidden belief shifts (implicit). Understanding this is crucial to diagnose the specific failure mode.
- **Quick check question:** Can a dialogue have no external contradictions but still show implicit inconsistency? (Yes, if the model silently changes its internal target to another entity that satisfies all previous answers.)

### Probing Internal Belief States via Branching
- **Why needed here:** The method relies on inserting a isolated, identical probe after each turn to extract a probability distribution over candidate indices without contaminating the main dialogue. This is the primary measurement tool.
- **Quick check question:** Why use numerical indexing (0‑9) instead of full entity names in the probe? (To ensure a single‑token, fixed‑vocabulary prediction that yields a clean, comparable probability distribution across all candidates and tasks.)

### KL Divergence as a Regularizer for State Stability
- **Why needed here:** The key intervention that reduces drift. Understanding it as a measure of distributional change from an anchor (P_initial) is essential to grasp the training mechanism.
- **Quick check question:** In the loss `α·D_KL(P_t ∥ P_initial)`, what does a low value indicate about the model’s goal persistence at turn t? (It indicates the belief distribution at turn t is close to the initial distribution, i.e., goal is stable.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Dialogue Engine (Proposer) -> Guesser -> [after each turn] Probing Interface -> Metrics Calculator -> Training Modifier; External Evaluator (LLM‑as‑judge) assesses main dialogue.

### Critical path
1. Implement the 20‑question game loop with separate Proposer/Guesser roles.
2. Integrate the branch‑based probe after every turn.
3. Collect probe distributions to compute baseline drift metrics.
4. For fine‑tuning, modify the loss to include D_KL(P_t_probe ∥ P_initial).
5. Re‑evaluate drift and external consistency.

### Design tradeoffs
- Probing frequency vs. dialogue naturalness: Every turn provides fine‑grained data but may disrupt flow; coarser probing reduces overhead but misses intermediate drift.
- Indexing vs. natural language probing: Indexing is clean but artificial; natural language yields multi‑token distributions harder to normalize.
- KL regularization strength vs. task performance: High α may force excessive stability, potentially harming legitimate belief updates.

### Failure signatures
- High Drift Rate (>20%) and non‑zero average KL Divergence in baseline (no‑KL) models.
- KL‑only training reducing drift but possibly harming External Consistency Verification if over‑regularized (though paper shows KL‑only maintained 100% ECV).
- CE‑only training failing to reduce drift, indicating standard cross‑entropy on answers doesn’t address internal state stability.

### First 3 experiments
1. Replicate baseline prompting with models like GPT‑4o or Seed‑1.6; confirm pervasive drift.
2. Implement and validate the probe: manually inspect index probe outputs; verify argmax can change despite externally consistent answers.
3. Test KL regularization: fine‑tune Qwen‑2.5‑14B‑Instruct with combined loss; expect significant drop in Drift Rate and KL Div vs. base and CE‑only baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can memory mechanisms or explicit belief state tracking architectures effectively reduce implicit goal drift in LLMs during multi‑turn interactions?
- **Basis in paper:** (explicit) The conclusion states "Future work should explore memory mechanisms, explicit belief state tracking, and alternative training objectives to anchor implicit goals over time."
- **Why unresolved:** Current experiments only demonstrate that KL divergence regularization provides partial mitigation; the underlying mechanisms for sustaining internal goal representations remain unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement models with external memory buffers or state tracking modules and evaluate their implicit consistency (Drift Rate, KL Divergence) against baselines in the 20‑question paradigm, showing significant reduction in drift.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why do reasoning‑enhanced models exhibit increased goal drift in simple tasks, and to what extent does "overthinking" destabilize internal goal representations?
- **Basis in paper:** (inferred) The authors hypothesize that reasoning‑enhanced models increase drift due to overthinking but provide no empirical validation.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper reports patterns (e.g., Deepseek‑v3.1 + Reasoning increases drift) but does not analyze reasoning processes or internal states to confirm the overthinking explanation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze reasoning traces (e.g., chain‑of‑thought) for drift episodes, correlate reasoning length/depth with drift, and possibly intervene to limit reasoning steps to see if drift decreases.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do architecture‑specific differences (e.g., Claude vs GPT‑4/Deepseek) account for varying patterns of implicit inconsistency, and can these be linked to reasoning module integration?
- **Basis in paper:** (explicit) "Claude’s reversed pattern may suggest architecture‑specific differences in how reasoning modules interact with goal maintenance; we keep it as our future work."
- **Why unresolved:** The observation is based on a few models; no systematic study across architectures explains the mechanism.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct controlled experiments comparing models with and without built‑in reasoning capabilities, measuring drift rates and analyzing internal representations to identify architectural factors contributing to stability or drift.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does implicit goal drift generalize beyond the controlled 20‑question game to more complex, open‑ended multi‑turn dialogues?
- **Basis in paper:** (inferred) The limitations note that the evaluation tasks may not fully capture real‑world conversational complexity.
- **Why unresolved:** All experiments used a narrow game paradigm; real dialogues involve more varied interactions, goals, and contextual shifts that could affect stability differently.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design new probing tasks with natural dialogues, perhaps involving persona‑driven role‑play, and measure implicit consistency using similar belief probing techniques across longer, more diverse conversations.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How does model scale affect implicit consistency, and can larger models inherently maintain stable internal beliefs without specialized training?
- **Basis in paper:** (inferred) Fine‑tuning was limited to a 14B model; scaling effects are unknown.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only studies 14B models due to compute constraints; larger models might have different capacities for state stability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate a range of model sizes (e.g., 7B, 14B, 70B, etc.) on the implicit consistency tasks using prompting alone, to see if drift rates decrease with scale, indicating natural improvement.

## Limitations
- Probing relies on single‑digit token output, which may oversimplify the belief state; multi‑token or distributional shifts could be missed.
- Entity Guessing task details (entity list, attributes) are unreported, hindering exact replication.
- Guesser's question generation algorithm (tree expansion) is unspecified, possibly influencing drift rates.
- KL regularization evaluation is limited to one 14B model and one task; generalizability to other scales/domains remains open.
- External consistency assessment via an LLM judge may introduce systematic bias.
