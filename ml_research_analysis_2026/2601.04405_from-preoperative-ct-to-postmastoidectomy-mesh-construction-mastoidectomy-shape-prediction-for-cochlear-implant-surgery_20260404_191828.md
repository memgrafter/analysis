# From Preoperative CT to Postmastoidectomy Mesh Construction: Mastoidectomy Shape Prediction for Cochlear Implant Surgery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04405
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04405
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieved mean Dice 0.721 and HD95 16.159, outperforming state-of-the-art models.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of predicting mastoidectomy shapes from preoperative CT scans for cochlear implant surgery, eliminating the need for manual annotations. The authors propose a hybrid two-stage framework. The first stage is self‑supervised: a SegMamba network learns to predict a binary removal mask such that applying it to the preoperative CT yields a volume resembling the actual postoperative CT. A multi‑scale similarity loss (MS‑SSIM with spatial correlation) enables training despite metal artifacts and noise in postoperative images. The second stage uses the first stage’s thresholded predictions as weak labels to train a fresh SegMamba model with a novel 3D T‑Distribution loss, which is robust to label noise. Evaluated on 751 CT scans, the method achieves a mean Dice of 0.721 and HD95 of 16.159, significantly surpassing U‑Net and transformer baselines.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with rigid registration, ear-region cropping, and intensity normalization of paired preoperative and postoperative CTs. In Stage 1, a frozen SAM‑Med3D encoder extracts general 3D features, and a trainable SegMamba decoder (with Tri‑orientated Mamba modules) predicts a probability mask δ. The mask is applied to the preoperative volume ρ to synthesize a postoperative-like scan ρ⊗δ, which is compared to the true postoperative CT ω using a multi‑scale similarity loss (L_msssim_cscc) plus a smoothing regularizer. After training, δ is thresholded to generate weak labels K for all preoperative training scans. Stage 2 trains a new SegMamba model on the same preprocessed preoperative CTs, using K as supervision and replacing standard losses with the 3D T‑Distribution loss (L_TD). This loss, derived from the Student‑t distribution, adaptively down‑weights voxels with large residuals relative to K, making the second stage robust to noise. The final segmentation is obtained by thresholding the Stage 2 output.

## Key Results
- Mean Dice score 0.721 and HD95 16.159 on a manually annotated test set of 32 cases.
- Outperforms strong baselines (U‑Net, UNet++, UNETR, SwinUNETR) in both self‑supervised and weakly‑supervised settings.
- The 3D T‑Distribution loss shows markedly better robustness to noisy weak labels compared to CE, BCE, Focal, MSE, and MAE losses.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Reconstruction‑based self‑supervision** – The visual difference between a preoperative CT and its paired postoperative volume is mainly the removal of mastoid air cells and bone, creating a cavity with distinct intensity statistics. By training the network to predict a mask that, when applied to the preoperative scan, yields a volume resembling the postoperative scan, the model learns the mastoidectomy shape. The multi‑scale similarity loss (MS‑SSIM with spatial correlation) is inherently robust to metal artifacts and low signal‑to‑noise ratios in postoperative images, enabling effective learning without manual masks.

2. **Robustness to noisy weak labels via T‑Distribution loss** – The masks produced by Stage 1 are imperfect (e.g., fragmented boundaries). The 3D T‑Distribution loss treats residuals between the prediction Y and weak label K as samples from a Student‑t distribution. The heavy tails of this distribution assign lower likelihood (higher loss) to large residuals, effectively down‑weighting outlier voxels. The loss learns per‑voxel variances and a degrees‑of‑freedom parameter r during training, dynamically adapting to the noise level and preventing overfitting to the weak labels.

3. **SegMamba with Tri‑orientated Mamba for global context** – The large, irregular mastoid cavity benefits from integrating information across the entire 3D volume. SegMamba replaces some U‑Net blocks with a Tri‑orientated Mamba (ToM) module, which processes feature maps along three orthogonal directions (axial, sagittal, coronal). Each voxel’s representation is updated by a selective state‑space model (Mamba) that attends to long‑range dependencies in all dimensions with linear complexity. Combined with a powerful pre‑trained SAM‑Med3D encoder, this architecture captures the global shape context necessary for accurate segmentation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self‑Supervised Learning via Image Reconstruction**  
  *Why needed*: Manual segmentation of mastoidectomy cavities is extremely time‑consuming; self‑supervision using postoperative CTs as targets removes the annotation requirement.

  *Why needed*: The self‑supervised model outputs are noisy weak labels; the second stage must train on these without catastrophic overfitting.

  *Why needed*: Global context is crucial for 3D mastoid segmentation; Mamba provides an efficient alternative to quadratic self‑attention.  
  *Quick check*: How does the Tri‑orientated Mamba adapt a 1D sequence model to process a 3D feature volume?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Preprocessor (registration, cropping, normalization) → Stage1 Encoder (frozen SAM‑Med3D) → Stage1 Decoder (SegMamba with ToM) → Loss1 (L_msssim_cscc + L_smooth) → Weak Label Generator (thresholding) → Stage2 Model (SegMamba) → Loss2 (3D T‑Distribution) → Final segmentation.

**Critical path**:  
Preoperative CT is preprocessed and used in Stage 1 (trained with paired postoperative CT) to produce a mask δ; thresholded δ becomes weak labels K; Stage 2 is then trained on the same preprocessed preoperative CTs supervised by K; at inference, the trained Stage 2 model processes a new preoperative CT to output the final mask.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Self‑supervised loss complexity (MS‑SSIM + SCC) provides robustness to postoperative noise but is computationally heavy.  
- Stage 2 performance is capped by the quality of Stage 1 weak labels; if Stage 1 is poor, L_TD’s robustness may be insufficient.  
- Tri‑orientated Mamba gives global context but increases sequential processing steps, trading off accuracy vs. training time/memory.  
- The T‑Distribution loss uses a diagonal covariance for efficiency, which may under‑model spatial correlations compared to a full matrix.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Fragmentary outputs: insufficient smoothing or incorrect rigid registration in Stage 1.  
- Systematic under‑/over‑segmentation: possibly due to intensity bias in the reconstruction loss or L_TD down‑weighting.  
- Training instability in Stage 2: r or σ² parameters diverge, causing loss explosion.  
- Out‑of‑memory errors when processing full 3D volumes; may require reduced batch size or checkpointing.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Loss ablation (Stage 1)**: Train SegMamba with L_msssim_cscc versus L_msssim (without SCC) and compare Dice/HD95 to quantify the SCC component’s contribution.  
2. **Weak label noise assessment**: Compute Dice between Stage 1 predictions (thresholded) and the 32 manual GTs; then train a standard U‑Net with CE loss and with L_TD on these weak labels, measuring the performance gap as an indicator of robustness.  
3. **Architecture comparison (Stage 2)**: Replace SegMamba in Stage 2 with a vanilla 3D U‑Net and SwinUNETR, train each with L_TD on identical weak labels, and evaluate to confirm the advantage of the Mamba‑based design.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalizability to broader clinical settings** – The dataset originates from Vanderbilt University Medical Center and a few collaborators; performance across diverse scanners, reconstruction algorithms, and patient populations remains unvalidated. A multi‑center study across 5–10 hospitals is needed.
2. **Adaptation to other bony structures** – The framework’s applicability to resections of other structures (e.g., maxillary sinus, facial recess) is uncertain due to differing anatomy and tissue properties. Testing on at least two additional tasks with minimal modifications would clarify required adaptations.
3. **Texture synthesis for navigation meshes** – Current meshes lack realistic surface textures, limiting intraoperative navigation. Developing a texture synthesis pipeline (e.g., generative models, atlas mapping) and evaluating via surgeon user studies and phantom experiments is required.
4. **Alternative weak label generation** – Dependency on self‑supervised weak labels may restrict robustness and scalability. Comparing alternative sources (pre‑trained segmentation models, probabilistic atlases) on external data without postoperative CTs could improve accuracy and eliminate the need for paired scans.

## Limitations
- Implementation specifics for SegMamba, the 3D T‑Distribution loss, and MS‑SSIM hyperparameters are not fully disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Preprocessing steps (rigid registration, ear‑cropping, intensity normalization) rely on external models (e.g., Zhang et al.12) with insufficient description, creating reproducibility bottlenecks.
- Weak labels from Stage 1 have not been quantitatively evaluated on the full training set; potential systematic bias may explain the observed precision‑sensitivity trade‑off and cap Stage 2 performance.

## Confidence
- Self‑supervised reconstruction via appearance matching: **Medium** (well‑motivated, supported by ablation, but artifact tolerance untested).
- 3D T‑Distribution loss for noisy labels: **Medium‑High** (clear improvement over standard losses, but stability of learned parameters not thoroughly analyzed).
- SegMamba with Tri‑orientated Mamba: **Medium** (outperforms baselines, but exact contribution beyond the strong SAM‑Med3D encoder is unclear).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the Stage 1 loss ablation: train SegMamba with L_msssim_cscc and without the SCC component, measuring Dice/HD95 on a held‑out set to confirm the SCC’s impact.
2. Quantify noise in Stage 1 weak labels: compute Dice between thresholded predictions and the 32 manual GTs; then train a standard U‑Net with CE loss and with L_TD on these labels, comparing performance to gauge robustness.
3. Test registration sensitivity: introduce controlled translations (1–3 mm) into pre/post‑CT pairs and evaluate the resulting change in Stage 1 reconstruction similarity (MS‑SSIM) and final segmentation Dice to bound expected failure modes.
