# Uncertainty-Aware Mapping from 3D Keypoints to Anatomical Landmarks for Markerless Biomechanics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.26844
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26844
- **Reference count**: 20
- **Primary result**: Epistemic uncertainty shows strong monotonic correlation with landmark error (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.63), enabling selective retention of reliable frames (error reduced to ~16.8 mm at 10% coverage) and accurate outlier detection (ROC-AUC ≈ 0.92).

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the lack of frame-wise quality control in markerless biomechanics by modeling predictive uncertainty for the mapping from 3D pose keypoints to anatomical landmarks. Using an LSTM with Monte Carlo dropout to estimate epistemic uncertainty and heteroscedastic regression for aleatoric uncertainty, they evaluate on AMASS motion capture data. Epistemic uncertainty correlates strongly with error (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.63), allowing reliable frames to be selected for lower error (≈16.8 mm at 10% coverage) and severe failures to be detected (ROC-AUC ≈ 0.92). The ranking remains stable under Gaussian noise degradation, while aleatoric uncertainty contributes minimally, indicating model limitations are the dominant failure source.

## Method Summary
The work formulates frame-wise mapping from a temporal window of 3D keypoints (K=20) to anatomical landmarks (L=43) as a sequence-to-sequence prediction problem. An LSTM processes input windows of T=60 frames, with outputs per frame being landmark coordinates. To estimate uncertainty, the model incorporates two components: epistemic uncertainty via Monte Carlo dropout (rate 0.1) at inference, sampling M=50 stochastic forward passes and computing empirical variance across predictions; and aleatoric uncertainty via heteroscedastic regression, where an auxiliary head predicts per-frame log-variance trained with Gaussian negative log-likelihood loss alongside auxiliary smoothness and consistency terms. The model is trained with a warm-start strategy: first train a deterministic LSTM to convergence, then initialize the heteroscedastic model from those weights and fine-tune with the full loss. Frame-wise reliability is assessed by aggregating per-landmark variances into a scalar uncertainty score, which is used for risk-coverage analysis and outlier detection.

## Key Results
- Epistemic uncertainty exhibits a strong monotonic correlation with per-frame landmark error (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.63).
- Selective retention of the 10% lowest-uncertainty frames reduces mean error to ~16.8 mm (34% reduction).
- Outlier detection (error > 50 mm) achieves ROC-AUC ≈ 0.92.
- Aleatoric uncertainty contributes minimally; combining it with epistemic uncertainty does not improve reliability metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Epistemic uncertainty (model uncertainty) provides a robust, monotonic ranking signal for frame-wise biomechanical error.
- **Mechanism:** Monte Carlo (MC) dropout applied during inference stochastically samples different sub-networks. The empirical variance across these samples (`σ²_epi,t`) captures the model's parameter uncertainty for a given input configuration. For inputs representing rare, ambiguous, or out-of-distribution motion patterns (e.g., occlusions, extreme postures), the sampled predictions diverge more, yielding higher epistemic uncertainty. This divergence correlates with larger errors because the model lacks confident, generalized knowledge for such cases.
- **Core assumption:** Failure modes in keypoint-to-landmark mapping are primarily due to the model's inability to generalize to specific input patterns (i.e., lack of training data coverage or representational gaps), rather than irreducible noise in the input keypoints.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Epistemic uncertainty showed a strong monotonic correlation with landmark error (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.63)... Reliability ranking remains stable under controlled input degradation."
  - [section 2.4] Describes MC dropout for epistemic variance estimation: "σ²_epi,t = (1/M) Σ (µ(m)_t - ¯µt)²."
  - [section 4.2 & 4.3] Reports the strong ρ=0.63 correlation and stability of this correlation under Gaussian noise (ρ remains ~0.592–0.597).
- **Break condition:** The ranking property would degrade if the model's training distribution is severely mismatched to the test distribution (e.g., entirely new motion types or body morphologies not represented in AMASS), or if MC dropout is insufficient to capture model uncertainty (e.g., for very deep or deterministic architectures).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Aleatoric uncertainty (input-dependent observation noise) contributes minimally as a reliability signal in this specific mapping task.
- **Mechanism:** Heteroscedastic regression learns to predict an input-dependent variance (`σ²_ale,t`). This is designed to model noise inherent to the observation (e.g., jitter in keypoint positions). However, the experiments show that adding this component does not improve the correlation between uncertainty and error, nor does it enhance risk-coverage or outlier detection beyond using epistemic uncertainty alone. This suggests the dominant errors are not from random input noise but from systematic model limitations.
- **Core assumption:** The primary 3D keypoint inputs (from a pose estimator or motion capture) have relatively low, non-heteroscedastic observation noise compared to the model's own errors on ambiguous inputs. The mapping task's failure is epistemic, not aleatoric.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Aleatoric uncertainty contributed minimally, indicating model limitations are the dominant failure source."
  - [section 4.2] "Combining heteroscedastic regression with MC Dropout does not improve reliability metrics compared to epistemic uncertainty alone... Total uncertainty closely overlapped with epistemic uncertainty."
  - [section 5] "Dominant errors are driven by model generalization rather than sensor-level variability."
- **Break condition:** This mechanism would fail (i.e., aleatoric uncertainty would become informative) if the input keypoints were corrupted by substantial, input-dependent noise (e.g., varying levels of marker occlusion or sensor dropout patterns not uniform across poses).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Frame-wise uncertainty estimates can be used for risk-controlled inference, selectively filtering frames to achieve lower average error on retained data.
- **Mechanism:** Frames are ranked by ascending uncertainty. The `Risk(c)` metric computes the expected error on the top `c` fraction of frames (lowest uncertainty). A well-ordered ranking (where low uncertainty consistently corresponds to low error) yields a risk-coverage curve where risk increases slowly as coverage decreases. The paper shows this, achieving a 34% error reduction (to ~16.8mm) by retaining only the 10% most confident frames.
- **Core assumption:** The uncertainty ranking is stable and generalizable; a frame ranked as low-uncertainty on the validation/test set will reliably have low error in deployment, assuming similar data distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "enabling selective retention of reliable frames (error reduced to ≈16.8 mm at 10% coverage)."
  - [section 4.2] Reports the risk-coverage curve (Figure 1) and the 16.8mm @ 10% coverage result.
- **Break condition:** This utility breaks down if the uncertainty ranking is not monotonic with error (low correlation) or if the ranking is not stable under relevant distribution shifts (e.g., different camera angles, clothing, or environments not seen in AMASS), causing the selective set to include high-error frames.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Aleatoric vs. Epistemic Uncertainty Decomposition**
  - *Why needed here:* To correctly interpret the source of prediction unreliability. Mistaking aleatoric for epistemic (or vice versa) leads to misapplied quality control (e.g., trying to "denoise" a fundamentally model-limited prediction).
  - *Quick check question:* If a model's error on a specific gait frame is high because the subject is wearing baggy pants (obscuring keypoints), is this primarily aleatoric or epistemic? (Answer: Epistemic, as the model was not trained on such inputs; the input noise is systematic, not random.)
- **Concept: Temporal Sequence Modeling with RNNs/LSTMs**
  - *Why needed here:* The mapping `f_θ` operates on a temporal window of keypoints (`x_1:T`). Biomechanical landmarks are influenced by motion dynamics; a single frame is often ambiguous. The LSTM leverages past/future context to resolve ambiguity (e.g., inferring ankle position during foot occlusion from leg swing trajectory).
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a deterministic LSTM still produce high error on a frame within a smooth walking sequence? (Answer: Even with context, some poses are inherently ambiguous from keypoints alone, or the training data lacks examples of that specific joint configuration.)
- **Concept: Risk-Coverage Analysis for Selective Prediction**
  - *Why needed here:* Provides a principled, quantitative framework to evaluate and use uncertainty for quality control. It moves beyond average error to ask: "If I only trust X% of my predictions, what is my expected error?"
  - *Quick check question:* What does a flat risk-coverage curve indicate about the model's uncertainty estimates? (Answer: Poor ranking; uncertainty does not correlate with error, so filtering by it is no better than random selection.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Input Preprocessing:** 3D keypoint sequences (`x_1:T`), shape `[Batch, T=60, K=20, 3]`, flattened to `[Batch, T, 3K]`.
  2. **Temporal Core:** LSTM (hidden size unspecified) processes sequence. Dropout layers (rate=0.1) are embedded for MC sampling.
  3. **Heads:**
     - *Mean Head:* Linear layer outputs landmark means `µ_t` (`[Batch, T, L=43, 3]`).
     - *Aleatoric Head (optional):* Linear layer outputs log-variance `logσ²_t` (`[Batch, T, L, 3]`), trained with NLL loss.
  4. **Inference Engine:** For a given sequence, perform `M=50` stochastic forward passes with dropout active. Compute per-frame epistemic variance across samples. Combine with aleatoric variance (if enabled) for total uncertainty.
  5. **Reliability Scorer:** Aggregate per-landmark/coordinate variances to a frame-wise scalar `u_t` (mean used).
- **Critical path:** Raw keypoint sequence → (Preprocessing) → LSTM with dropout → (Mean & Variance heads) → MC sampling loop → Variance aggregation → Frame ranking → Risk-coverage/outlier metrics.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Temporal window size (T=60):* Larger `T` provides more context but increases memory/computation and may include irrelevant distant frames. Smaller `T` risks losing temporal disambiguation.
  - *MC Dropout samples (M=50):* More samples reduce estimation variance but increase inference latency quadratically. `M=50` is a practical balance.
  - *Aleatoric head:* Adds minimal parameters but requires careful warm-start training (from deterministic baseline) to avoid destabilizing the mean prediction. The paper finds it adds negligible utility here.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low Spearman ρ (<0.4) between uncertainty and true error → ranking is weak; quality control ineffective.
  - Aleatoric variance dominates total variance → suggests input noise is the main issue, not model limitation; may need input sanitization instead of model-centric fixes.
  - Risk-coverage curve is flat or rises very steeply early → indicates either poor uncertainty calibration or that a small set of frames is catastrophically bad but *not* ranked as high-uncertainty.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Deterministic Training:** Train LSTM without uncertainty heads to establish MPJPE baseline (~21.14mm). Validate model can learn the mapping.
  2. **Uncertainty-Error Correlation:** With MC dropout (+ optional aleatoric head), compute frame-wise epistemic uncertainty and error on test set. Calculate Spearman ρ. Target: ρ > 0.6.
  3. **Degradation Robustness:** Inject Gaussian noise (σ=10, 20, 30mm) into validation inputs. Re-compute ρ and outlier ROC-AUC. Expect stable correlation (Δρ < 0.01) and ROC-AUC > 0.85.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the strong monotonic correlation between epistemic uncertainty and landmark error generalize to non-LSTM temporal architectures, such as Transformers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge a limitation: "uncertainty behavior was evaluated using a single temporal architecture (LSTM)" and propose investigating Transformer-based models in future work (Section 5, Conclusion).
- Why unresolved: The uncertainty-error relationship may be influenced by architectural inductive biases; different sequence models could yield distinct uncertainty characteristics or calibration.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replicate the study using Transformer-based sequence-to-sequence models on the same keypoint-to-landmark task, comparing Spearman rank correlation, risk-coverage curves, and outlier detection metrics for epistemic uncertainty.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What underlying factors cause aleatoric uncertainty (heteroscedastic regression) to provide minimal additional benefit beyond epistemic uncertainty in this mapping task?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper reports that combining aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty "does not improve reliability metrics" and that aleatoric "contributed minimally" (Section 4.2), implying model uncertainty is dominant, but does not analyze the root causes.
- Why unresolved: Without understanding whether this stems from the low observation noise in AMASS keypoints, model misspecification, or task-specific properties, it is unclear if aleatoric modeling could be beneficial in other settings (e.g., with raw video input).
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct controlled experiments varying input noise distributions (e.g., synthetic vs. real pose estimator errors), model capacities, and loss formulations, measuring changes in aleatoric uncertainty's contribution to error correlation and risk-coverage.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust is the uncertainty-based frame reliability ranking under realistic pose estimation degradations such as occlusions, motion blur, or joint detection failures?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Degradation tests only involved "Gaussian noise and simulated missing joints" (Section 3.4), which are simplistic compared to complex, structured errors from video-based pose estimators.
- Why unresolved: Real-world markerless pipelines use imperfect 2D/3D pose models; uncertainty may not maintain a monotonic error relationship under such natural corruptions, limiting practical utility.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluate the framework on pose keypoints corrupted by realistic synthetic occlusions/blur or on datasets with natural occlusion failures, assessing stability of Spearman correlation and risk-coverage curves as degradation increases.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does predictive uncertainty propagate and evolve through an end-to-end markerless biomechanics pipeline (from video to final biomechanical outputs)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The Conclusion states: "Future work will... extend the analysis to end-to-end markerless pipelines to study uncertainty propagation across stages."
- Why unresolved: The current work isolates the keypoint-to-landmark mapping; uncertainty from upstream pose estimation may compound, cancel, or distort, affecting downstream inverse kinematics and musculoskeletal analysis reliability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Integrate the uncertainty-aware mapping into a full pipeline (e.g., video → pose estimation → landmark mapping → inverse kinematics), quantifying uncertainty at each stage and correlating with final biomechanical metric errors (e.g., joint moments).

## Limitations
- Missing architectural details (e.g., LSTM layers, hidden units) hinder exact replication.
- Undefined auxiliary loss terms (`L_vel`, `L_acc`, `L_angle`, `L_pos`) and their precise weights impede faithful reproduction of heteroscedastic training.
- Evaluation limited to AMASS synthetic motion capture data; lacks validation on real markerless keypoints or out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Confidence
- Epistemic uncertainty ranking: Medium (relies on MC dropout setup not fully detailed).
- Noise robustness: Medium (architecture missing may affect stability).
- Aleatoric uncertainty minimal contribution: Medium-Low (possible training issues from missing loss definitions).
