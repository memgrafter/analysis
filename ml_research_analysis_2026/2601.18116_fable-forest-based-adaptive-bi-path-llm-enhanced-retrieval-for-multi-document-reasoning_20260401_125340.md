# FABLE: Forest-Based Adaptive Bi-Path LLM-Enhanced Retrieval for Multi-Document Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.18116
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18116
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Outperforms SOTA structured RAG (e.g., +8.0 EM on 2WikiMultiHopQA) while using up to 94% fewer tokens than full-context LLMs.

## Executive Summary
FABLE tackles the inefficiencies of long-context LLMs and the semantic noise of flat-chunk RAG for multi-document reasoning. It introduces a two-phase approach: first, it constructs LLM-generated hierarchical "forest" indexes that preserve document structure and discourse coherence; second, it employs a budget-adaptive bi-path retrieval strategy that combines LLM-guided hierarchical navigation with structure-aware propagation. This design consistently achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on multi-hop QA benchmarks while drastically reducing token consumption, demonstrating that intelligent structured retrieval remains critical even as LLM context windows expand.

## Method Summary
FABLE operates in two main phases. During offline indexing, an LLM semantically chunks each document into coherent units and recursively builds a hierarchical tree (TOC, summaries, children) up to a depth of 4. All nodes (internal and leaf) are embedded for vector search. At query time, a budget-adaptive bi-path retrieval begins with document-level fusion: it combines results from depth-adaptive LLM selection (over non-leaf nodes) and standard vector document retrieval. If the total tokens of these candidate documents fit within a budget (𝐵max), they are returned directly. If not, a node-level bi-path activates: an LLM navigates within the selected document trees, while a propagation algorithm (TreeExpansion) diffuses relevance scores through the tree structure based on similarity, ancestor inheritance, and child aggregation. The results from both paths are fused with a priority order (LLM nodes first), deduplicated (preserving ancestor context), and position-sorted. A final greedy selection extracts chunks until 𝐵max is reached.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on multi-hop QA: **63.87% F1** on 2WikiMultiHopQA (+8.0 vs. HippoRAG2) and **52.5% EM** on HotpotQA (+7.0).
- Matches full-context LLM inference completeness (**~91%**) while reducing token usage by **up to 94%** (to ~31K tokens).
- Bi-path retrieval demonstrates complementary strengths, with ablations showing significant performance drops when either path is removed, especially under tight token budgets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: LLM-Constructed Hierarchical Forests Reduce Semantic Noise
- **Claim:** LLM-generated hierarchical trees align chunk boundaries with discourse coherence and enable multi-granularity reasoning, reducing noise from flat semantic matching.
- **Mechanism:** An LLM segments documents into semantically self-contained chunks and recursively builds a tree with TOC and summaries. This preserves topical continuity across chunks and creates navigable abstractions (e.g., section → subsection). Retrieval can reason over high-level summaries to locate relevant sub-hierarchies.
- **Core Assumption:** LLMs can reliably infer document-level semantic boundaries and hierarchical relations during offline indexing.
- **Evidence Anchors:**
  - [abstract] "constructs LLM-enhanced hierarchical forest indexes with multi-granularity semantic structures"
  - [section 3.1] "Semantic-Aware Document Chunking... preserving discourse coherence" and "Tree Construction... LLM-based structuring module"
  - [corpus] Weak—neighbors discuss long-context or multi-doc QA but do not directly validate LLM-driven hierarchical indexing.
- **Break Condition:** Indexing fails for highly unstructured documents (e.g., legal boilerplate) or if LLM segmentation produces inconsistent boundaries.

### Mechanism 2: Bi-Path Retrieval Combines Semantic Precision and Structural Coverage
- **Claim:** The bi-path strategy (LLM-guided navigation + structure-aware propagation) jointly optimizes for deep semantic relevance and document-level structural integrity, especially under budget constraints.
- **Mechanism:** Two parallel paths run at document and node levels: (1) an LLM selects relevant nodes via hierarchical reasoning over summaries; (2) TreeExpansion propagates relevance through the tree using embedding similarity, ancestor-inheritance, and child-aggregation scores. The final fusion prioritizes LLM-identified nodes before supplementing with propagated ones.
- **Core Assumption:** LLM symbolic reasoning and vector/graph-based propagation capture complementary relevance signals.
- **Evidence Anchors:**
  - [abstract] "bi-path strategy combining LLM-guided hierarchical traversal with structure-aware propagation"
  - [section 3.2] "Bi-Path Retrieval... ensures that retrieval both captures deep semantic relevance and preserves document-level structural integrity"
  - [corpus] Weak—methods like MuDAF/CAFE address long-context distraction differently; no direct comparison evidence.
- **Break Condition:** LLM path fails on ambiguous queries without clear navigation cues; propagation adds noise if trees are shallow or poorly constructed.

### Mechanism 3: Budget-Adaptive Routing Optimizes Granularity
- **Claim:** Dynamic routing between document-level and node-level retrieval avoids unnecessary fine-grained traversal when coarse results suffice, improving efficiency without sacrificing recall.
- **Mechanism:** After document-level bi-path fusion, the system checks if the total token size of candidate documents fits 𝐵max. If yes, it returns full documents immediately. Only if the budget is exceeded does it proceed to node-level selection and greedy chunk extraction.
- **Core Assumption:** Many queries have limited relevant scope; early termination at document-level preserves efficiency.
- **Evidence Anchors:**
  - [abstract] "budget-adaptive bi-path retrieval strategy... with explicit budget control for adaptive efficiency trade-offs"
  - [section 3.2] "Budget-Adaptive Routing... if the total content size of Dfusion falls within budget constraint 𝐵max, the framework terminates at document-level"
  - [corpus] Weak—works on context budgets exist but not adaptive retrieval granularity.
- **Break Condition:** Queries requiring synthesis across many documents often exceed 𝐵max at document-level, forcing node-level retrieval; mis-estimation of document content size could cause premature termination or overflow.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hierarchical Knowledge Indexing**: Replacing flat chunks with LLM-built semantic trees preserves cross-chunk coherence and enables top-down navigation. *Why needed:* Without grasping how hierarchies reduce "lost-in-the-middle" noise, the core indexing innovation is missed. *Quick check:* How does a multi-level ToC help avoid middle-chunk degradation compared to linear chunk lists?
- **Bi-Path Fusion Strategy**: Combines LLM symbolic reasoning (precision) with vector/tree propagation (coverage) via ordered fusion. *Why needed:* Misunderstanding their complementary roles leads to incorrect fusion implementation. *Quick check:* Why prioritize LLM-selected nodes before TreeExpansion results? What if reversed?
- **Context Budget as First-Class Constraint**: Token limits drive the entire retrieval architecture, trading depth for breadth. *Why needed:* Budget-awareness is not an afterthought but a core design driver. *Quick check:* At which two decision points does the system explicitly check the remaining budget, and what alternatives does it take?
- **TreeExpansion Propagation**: Diffuses relevance through hierarchical structure using similarity, ancestor-inheritance, and child-aggregation. *Why needed:* Explains how structural context supplements semantic matching. *Quick check:* How does ancestor-inheritance differ from standard parent-child propagation in trees?
- **Depth-Adaptive LLM Selection**: LLM navigates trees but limits depth (≤ L) to avoid overwhelming the model. *Why needed:* Balances LLM reasoning capability with recall. *Quick check:* What is the trade-off when increasing the depth threshold L?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** Semantic Chunker -> Tree Builder -> Vector Indexer -> Document-Level Bi-Path (LLM selection + Vector retrieval) -> Budget Router -> (if needed) Node-Level Bi-Path (LLM navigation + TreeExpansion) -> Node Fusion & Ordering -> Budget Control -> Retrieved Chunks.
- **Critical Path:** Query -> Document-Level Bi-Path -> Budget Router -> (conditional) Node-Level Bi-Path -> Node Fusion -> Budget Control -> Output.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - *Indexing cost vs. query efficiency:* Offline tree construction is expensive but amortized; not suitable for highly dynamic corpora.
  - *LLM path vs. vector/tree path:* LLM path is precise but costly; vector/tree is cheap but noisy. Bi-path balances both but requires careful fusion logic.
  - *Granularity:* Finer node levels improve precision but increase fusion complexity and fragmentation risk.
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - High hallucination: LLM path may have selected misleading subtrees; inspect summary quality.
  - Low recall on multi-hop: TreeExpansion depth decay or ancestor inheritance may prune relevant branches; check score distribution.
  - Budget overflow: Document-level fusion set too large; adjust depth threshold L or vector top-K.
  - Poor performance on unstructured text: Semantic chunking may produce arbitrary trees; consider fallback to fixed chunking.
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. **Indexing strategy ablation:** Compare LLM semantic chunks vs. fixed-length chunks. Expected: LLM-chunks achieve higher completeness at lower budgets due to preserved discourse coherence.
  2. **Bi-path necessity:** Compare full FABLE (docs/nodes) vs. single-path variants (FABLE(llm-docs), FABLE(treexp-nodes)). Expected: Bi-path fusion improves completeness at moderate budgets (e.g., +1.6 pts at 4K tokens).
  3. **Budget adaptation test:** Vary 𝐵max from 1K to 128K tokens and measure completeness/token usage. Expected: FABLE matches full-context LLM performance at ~8K tokens (vs. 517K).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is FABLE's performance on unstructured text corpora and keyword-centric queries?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section explicitly states: "Its advantages diminish on highly unstructured corpora or queries relying solely on keyword matching."
- **Why unresolved:** Evaluation is restricted to structured multi-hop QA and agent tasks; no experiments on unstructured data or keyword queries validate or quantify the degradation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate FABLE on corpora with minimal semantic structure (e.g., social media posts, legal briefs) and on keyword-dominated query sets, comparing accuracy and efficiency against traditional RAG (BM25, BGE-M3).

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Are the fixed uniform weights (1/3 each) in TreeExpansion's composite score optimal across diverse document hierarchies, or do they require adaptation?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Authors assign uniform weights without tuning, claiming robustness "across diverse document structures," but this is not systematically validated.
- **Why unresolved:** No ablation or sensitivity analysis explores weight variations; the assumption of universal robustness remains untested against different structural characteristics (depth, branching factor, domain formats).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments varying weights on datasets with contrasting hierarchical properties (e.g., deep scientific papers vs. shallow narrative texts), measuring impact on recall, completeness, and hallucination.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the quality of the LLM used during indexing (forest construction) affect the overall performance of FABLE?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method relies on capable LLMs (GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek-V3.2) for semantic chunking and tree generation, but the impact of indexing LLM quality is never studied.
- **Why unresolved:** Unstated assumption; errors in generated semantic forests could propagate to retrieval, but no analysis quantifies this dependency or establishes a minimum capability threshold.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Repeat indexing with LLMs of varying sizes/capabilities, evaluate tree quality metrics (semantic coherence, structural accuracy), and correlate with downstream QA performance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Is the position-preserving ordering in NodeFusion (prioritizing LLM-selected nodes before TreeExpansion nodes) universally optimal, or should the fusion strategy be query-adaptive?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The fusion uses fixed priority: "explicitly identified relevant content precedes structurally inferred context." This design choice is not ablated; its universality is assumed.
- **Why unresolved:** The rationale assumes LLM selections are always more precise, but for queries where structural context is crucial (e.g., comparative analysis), this ordering may suppress relevant inferred information. No experiments test alternative ordering.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies comparing fixed priority against adaptive strategies (e.g., interleaving by relevance score, pure document order) across query types, measuring changes in completeness and hallucination.

## Limitations
- Exact LLM prompts and core hyperparameters (TreeExpansion weights, deduplication thresholds) are undisclosed, blocking faithful replication.
- LLM-based semantic chunking may fail on highly unstructured documents (e.g., legal contracts, boilerplate-heavy text).
- Bi-path fusion logic and budget thresholds are likely tuned to evaluated datasets; generalizability to other domains is unproven.
- Dependence on proprietary LLMs (GPT-OSS-120B) limits accessibility and introduces result variability.

## Confidence
- **FABLE significantly outperforms HippoRAG2:** High (consistent EM/F1 gains on two multi-hop QA benchmarks).
- **Hierarchical forest indexing reduces semantic noise:** Medium (ablation suggests benefit, but LLM-inferred boundaries are not independently validated).
- **Budget-adaptive retrieval matches full-context accuracy with far fewer tokens:** High (quantitative 94% token reduction and comparable completeness).
- **Bi-path retrieval is complementary:** Medium (single-path ablations degrade performance, yet synergy might be dataset-specific).
