# A Diffusion-Based Generative Prior Approach to Sparse-view Computed Tomography

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10722  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10722  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** The proposed RD-DGP method matches or outperforms state-of-the-art diffusion-based techniques on sparse‑view CT reconstruction (PSNR/SSIM) and demonstrates that FBP‑based initialization and cosine annealing are essential for stable, high‑quality results.

## Executive Summary
Sparse‑view computed tomography (CT) suffers from severe artifacts because limited projection angles make the reconstruction ill‑posed. This paper presents a deep generative prior (DGP) approach using a Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model (DDIM) as the generator. The method optimizes a latent variable to fit the measured sinogram while regularizing with a Tikhonov term on the latent and Total Variation on the image. Key innovations are: (i) initializing the latent via filtered backprojection (FBP) followed by DDIM inversion to avoid poor local minima, (ii) a cosine annealing learning rate schedule for stable convergence, and (iii) the combination of latent and image regularization.

On the Mayo chest CT dataset, RD‑DGP achieves PSNR/SSIM comparable to or better than DDRM, DPS, and DiffPIR for 30, 45, and 60 views. Ablations confirm the FBP+DDIM initialization is indispensable; without it, optimization fails. The cosine annealing schedule reduces noise and improves contrast. Qualitatively, RD‑DGP preserves anatomical structures better than DDRM. The authors note high computational cost and limited evaluation as remaining challenges for clinical adoption.

## Method Summary
The pipeline has three stages: training, inversion, and optimization. A UNet denoiser is trained with the DDPM objective on augmented chest CT slices, using a cosine noise schedule over 1000 timesteps. The trained model serves as the generator \(G\) within a DDIM framework. Given a sparse‑view sinogram \(y_\delta\), an FBP reconstruction \(x_{\text{FBP}}\) is first computed. This image is then deterministically inverted through the DDIM diffusion process (i.e., running the diffusion sampler backwards) to obtain an initial latent \(z_0\). The reconstruction phase then optimizes \(z\) to minimize  
\[
F(z) = \frac{1}{2} \|K G(z) - y_\delta\|^2 + \lambda_1 \|z\|^2 + \lambda_2 \operatorname{TV}(G(z)),
\]  
where \(K\) is the forward CT operator implemented via the ASTRA Toolbox. Optimization uses Adam with a cosine‑annealed learning rate. Each iteration requires generating the image \(G(z)\) via the full DDIM reverse process, computing the loss, and back‑propagating to update \(z\). After convergence, the final image is \(x = G(z)\).

## Key Results
- RD‑DGP attains PSNR/SSIM comparable to or exceeding DDRM, DPS, and DiffPIR on Mayo chest CT for 30, 45, and 60 projection angles.  
- Ablation: Removing the FBP+DDIM initialization causes complete failure (no meaningful reconstructions), proving its necessity.  
- The cosine annealing learning schedule and TV regularization effectively reduce noise and enhance contrast in the outputs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RD‑DGP leverages the strong inductive bias of a diffusion model trained on natural (CT) images, which defines a manifold of plausible anatomies. Latent‑space optimization confines the search to this manifold, improving stability and output plausibility. The FBP reconstruction provides a rough solution in image space; projecting it back to latent space via DDIM inversion yields a sensible starting point close to a true solution, avoiding the many poor local minima that trap random initializations. The Tikhonov penalty on the latent discourages large deviations from the prior, while Total Variation on the image promotes piecewise smoothness characteristic of CT scans. Together they regularize the ill‑posed problem. The cosine annealing schedule gradually reduces the step size, preventing oscillations and helping the optimizer settle into a balance between data fidelity and regularization.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)** – A generative model that learns to reverse a Gaussian noising process. *Why needed:* Supplies the generator \(G(z)\) that captures the data distribution. *Quick check:* After training, iterative denoising of random noise yields diverse, realistic samples.
2. **DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model)** – A deterministic, faster variant of DDPM with an explicit inversion formula. *Why needed:* Enables exact mapping of a real image to a latent and efficient generation. *Quick check:* Using a trained DDPM, apply the DDIM update to sample in fewer steps without stochasticity.
3. **Deep Generative Prior (DGP)** – Using a generator (trained or untrained) as a prior for inverse problems by optimizing its input. *Why needed:* Regularizes ill‑posed reconstruction without hand‑crafted priors. *Quick check:* Optimize the latent of a pre‑trained generator to match a corrupted measurement and inspect reconstruction quality.
4. **Sparse‑view Computed Tomography** – CT reconstruction from a limited number of projection angles, leading to severe streaking artifacts. *Why needed:* The target application. *Quick check:* Simulate sparse projections from a CT volume and reconstruct with FBP to observe artifacts.
5. **Total Variation (TV) Regularization** – A variational method that promotes piecewise smooth images, preserving edges while removing noise. *Why needed:* Provides complementary image‑space regularization to the generative prior. *Quick check:* Apply TV denoising to a noisy image and verify edge preservation.
6. **Latent Space Optimization** – Optimizing over the input of a generator rather than the image pixels directly. *Why needed:* Reduces the search space and exploits the learned manifold for stable convergence. *Quick check:* Train an autoencoder, then reconstruct an image by optimizing its latent code with gradient descent.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**  
  FBP reconstruction → DDIM inversion → Initial latent \(z_0\) → Optimization loop:  
  Generate image \(G(z)\) (full DDIM) → Compute loss (data fidelity + Tikhonov + TV) → Back‑propagate → Update \(z\) with Adam (cosine LR) → repeat → output final \(G(z)\).

- **Critical Path**  
  The two dominant computational costs are: (1) the full DDIM sampling inside each optimization iteration, and (2) the forward projection operator \(K\) (ASTRA) required for data fidelity. These steps drive runtime and memory consumption.

- **Design Tradeoffs**  
  • *DDIM steps vs. quality/speed*: More steps improve fidelity but increase per‑iteration cost.  
  • *TV weight \(\lambda_2\)*: Higher values yield smoother images but risk oversmoothing edges; lower values preserve edges but may leave noise.  
  • *Learning rate schedule*: Cosine annealing stabilizes convergence but requires tuning of max/min rates; a fixed LR may diverge.  
  • *Initialization*: FBP+DDIM gives a strong start at extra cost; random initialization could be tried for speed but often fails.

- **Failure Signatures**  
  • Blurry or anatomically implausible outputs: indicates poor \(\lambda\) choices or an underfitted diffusion model.  
  • Oscillatory or diverging loss: learning rate too high or too few DDIM steps.  
  • Staircasing artifacts: TV regularization excessive (\(\lambda_2\) too high).  
  • Complete lack of structure: missing FBP+DDIM initialization or severe model underfitting.

- **3 First Experiments**  
  1. **Initialization impact**: Compare RD‑DGP with random latent vs. FBP+DDIM initialization (PSNR/SSIM, visual inspection).  
  2. **LR schedule ablation**: Replace cosine annealing with a constant learning rate and monitor convergence stability and final noise levels.  
  3. **TV weight sensitivity**: Vary \(\lambda_2\) to observe the tradeoff between edge preservation and residual noise/artifacts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Reducing computational cost** – The authors acknowledge that substantial effort is needed before generative models can be reliably adopted in clinical practice, particularly regarding runtime. No complexity analysis or speed‑quality trade‑offs are provided.  
2. **Robustness under extreme sparsity** – At very few projection angles (≤30), performance degrades, with occasional shape distortions and noise. The paper calls for systematic evaluation across ultra‑sparse regimes and robustness metrics.  
3. **Generalization of medical diffusion models** – Training solely on chest CT slices raises questions about applicability to other body regions and pathologies. The authors suggest investigating multi‑dataset generalization and the benefit of natural‑image pre‑training.

## Limitations
- **Undisclosed hyperparameters**: Values of \(\lambda_1\), \(\lambda_2\), and the number of optimization iterations are not reported, forcing ad‑hoc tuning and hindering exact replication.  
- **Limited dataset diversity**: Evaluation is restricted to Mayo chest CT slices; generalization to other anatomies (abdomen, head) and scanning protocols remains unverified.  
- **High computational demands**: Full DDIM inversion and iterative latent optimization are expensive, raising scalability concerns for 3D or high‑resolution clinical use.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter recovery and sensitivity analysis** – Conduct a systematic grid search over \(\lambda_1\), \(\lambda_2\), and the number of iterations on a held‑out validation set; report optimal values and performance variance to establish reproducibility bounds.  
2. **Cross‑dataset generalization** – Apply the trained diffusion model to at least two additional public CT datasets (e.g., AAPM Low‑Dose CT, abdomen/pelvis scans) and compare PSNR/SSIM to evaluate robustness across anatomies and dose levels.
