# Over-Searching in Search-Augmented Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05503
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05503
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Search-augmented LLMs over-search on unanswerable queries, reducing abstention and wasting computation despite improving accuracy on answerable queries.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates over-searching in search-augmented large language models (LLMs), where models unnecessarily invoke external search tools even for queries that are unanswerable. Through systematic evaluation across model architectures, retrieval conditions, and multi-turn dialogs, the authors reveal a critical trade-off: search boosts answer accuracy on answerable queries by an average of 24.0% but harms abstention on unanswerable ones by 12.8%. Over-searching is especially severe in reasoning-oriented models and under noisy retrieval, and it compounds across conversation turns. The study introduces Tokens Per Correctness (TPC) to quantify the cost-performance balance and releases the OverSearchQA benchmark. While query-level prompts and retrieval-level corpus augmentation yield modest improvements, they fail to resolve the core irrationality, pointing to the need for alignment interventions.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate over-searching using the newly introduced OverSearchQA dataset, which contains 1,188 balanced answerable and unanswerable queries spanning categories like Answer Unknown, False Premise, and Underspecified Context. They test various search-augmented LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o-mini, o4-mini, Kimi-K2, Qwen3-235B-Instruct) with a search loop that retrieves documents from a corpus (Wikipedia-Latest or C5) using the E5-base retriever (top‑k=3 chunks) and allows up to 10 search calls. For each run, they log generated tokens, input tokens, and search calls to compute TPC = total cost / total correct, where cost = generated tokens + 0.25·input tokens + 500·searches. Answer accuracy (on answerable queries) and abstention accuracy (on unanswerable queries) are judged by GPT-4o-mini. The study systematically varies query type, model reasoning effort, retrieval quality, and dialog turns, and also tests mitigation strategies such as abstention‑aware prompts and synthetic negative document augmentation.

## Key Results
- Search improves answer accuracy by +24.0% on average but harms abstention by -12.8% across models.
- Over-searching is most pronounced in reasoning/deep research models and under noisy retrieval (e.g., C5 yields 3.6× higher TPC than Wikipedia); it compounds in multi-turn dialogs when prior turns are answerable.
- The presence of negative evidence (e.g., statements of uncertainty) in retrieved documents dramatically improves abstention, though such documents comprise only 13–22% of results.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Search augmentation improves answer accuracy on answerable queries but harms abstention on unanswerable ones.
- **Mechanism:** When search is available, the model invokes it for any query. For answerable queries, retrieved documents contain relevant information, leading to correct answers. For unanswerable queries, retrieved documents may include misleading positive evidence (plausible but incorrect snippets) or fail to provide clear signals of unanswerability, causing the model to attempt answers or continue searching unnecessarily instead of abstaining.
- **Core assumption:** The presence of any retrieved content, even if irrelevant, creates an illusion of answerability that undermines the model's intrinsic abstention capability.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "search generally improves answer accuracy on answerable queries but harms abstention on unanswerable ones"; [section] §5.1, Table 2 shows average answer accuracy ↑24.0% with search but abstention accuracy ↓12.8% across models; [corpus] indirect.
- **Break condition:** If retrieved documents consistently contain explicit negative evidence (e.g., statements of uncertainty or contradictions), abstention improves. The paper finds that negative evidence presence dramatically boosts abstention.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Over-searching is more severe in reasoning models and under noisy retrieval.
- **Mechanism:** Reasoning models (e.g., o4-mini, Qwen-Thinking) are trained for extended deliberation and tool use, encouraging aggressive search policies that invoke many calls even when unnecessary. Noisy retrieval (e.g., C5 corpus) yields low-relevance documents, forcing the model to perform additional searches to compensate, thereby increasing TPC. Interestingly, consistently poor retrieval may sometimes help abstention because the model eventually fails to find supporting evidence, but cost remains high due to excessive searches.
- **Core assumption:** The search policy is sensitive to both the model's reasoning architecture and retrieval quality; suboptimal conditions lead to inefficient tool use.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "over-searching is more pronounced in complex reasoning models and deep research systems, is exacerbated by noisy retrieval"; [section] §5.1, Table 3 shows increasing reasoning effort on o4-mini improves answer accuracy but degrades abstention and raises TPC monotonically. Table 4 shows C5 yields 3.6× higher TPC than Wikipedia-Latest; [corpus] indirect.
- **Break condition:** Improved retrieval quality or explicit constraints on search calls could reduce unnecessary searches and lower TPC.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Over-searching compounds in multi-turn conversations.
- **Mechanism:** The model's search decisions are stateful across dialogue turns. If earlier turns involve answerable queries that trigger searching, the model develops a "search habit" that persists to subsequent turns, even when the current query is unanswerable. This leads to accumulated costs and degraded abstention. Conversely, if earlier turns are consistently unanswerable, abstention remains stable.
- **Core assumption:** The search policy is not sufficiently conditioned on the current query's answerability when conversation history contains answerable queries.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "compounds across turns in multi-turn conversations"; [section] §5.3, Figure 6 for GPT-4o-mini shows that with answerable or mixed history, abstention accuracy decreases and TPC increases as turns grow; unanswerable history maintains stable abstention; [corpus] indirect.
- **Break condition:** Resetting search behavior per turn or explicitly incorporating turn context into the decision could prevent carry-over of over-searching.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** User Query -> Search-Augmented LLM (with tool-calling) -> Retriever (E5-base over corpus) -> Retrieved Documents -> LLM Response; Evaluation: LLM Response + Ground Truth -> LLM Judge -> Accuracy Metrics; Cost Tracking: Input/Output Tokens + Search Calls -> TPC Calculator.

**Critical path:**
1. Load query from OverSearchQA.
2. Run search-augmented LLM: model may initiate up to 10 search calls, each retrieving top‑k documents.
3. Model generates final response.
4. LLM judge evaluates response for correctness (answerable) or abstention (unanswerable).
5. Log tokens and search calls.
6. Aggregate to compute answer accuracy, abstention accuracy, and TPC.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Search budget vs accuracy: More search turns improve answer accuracy but increase cost and harm abstention.
- Retrieval source: High-quality (Wikipedia) reduces TPC but may still harm abstention; noisy (C5) increases TPC but sometimes improves abstention.
- Model type: Reasoning models yield higher accuracy at expense of TPC and abstention.
- Multi-turn: Enabling search across turns compounds over-searching but may be needed for complex interactions.

**Failure signatures:**
- High TPC with plateaued accuracy: diminishing returns from additional searches.
- Abstention accuracy lower than the base model (without search): search induces overcommitment.
- In multi-turn, TPC ↑ and abstention ↓ as turns increase for answerable/mixed histories.
- Large TPC increase when switching to noisy retrieval corpus.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline with/without search: Evaluate models (e.g., GPT-4o-mini, o4-mini) on OverSearchQA both with and without search. Compute dual accuracy and TPC to confirm the trade-off.
2. Search turn ablation: For a fixed model (o4-mini), vary maximum search turns (0–19) and plot answer accuracy, abstention accuracy, and TPC. Identify the point where additional searches no longer improve accuracy but increase TPC.
3. Retrieval source comparison: Test a model with Wikipedia-Latest, C5, and Web Search. Measure TPC and abstention to validate the impact of retrieval quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Which alignment techniques can teach search-augmented LLMs to stop searching when further searches are unlikely to improve correctness? (Basis: The paper suggests post-training or alignment interventions; current training-free mitigations are insufficient.)
- **Open Question 2**: How can retrieval systems be modified to consistently retrieve negative evidence (e.g., statements of uncertainty or unanswerability) to improve model abstention? (Basis: Negative evidence is rare in natural retrievers; augmentation yields only modest gains.)
- **Open Question 3**: What mechanisms can prevent the snowball effect of over-searching in multi-turn conversations, where prior searches bias subsequent ones toward unnecessary tool use? (Basis: The study identifies the phenomenon but no mitigation.)
- **Open Question 4**: How does over-searching behavior differ on real-world unanswerable queries from deployment, compared to the curated OverSearchQA benchmark? (Basis: OverSearchQA's unanswerables are synthetic; real-world queries may have different patterns.)

## Limitations
- Reproduction fidelity: Exact model prompting, search decision thresholds, and retrieval preprocessing are unspecified, risking configuration drift.
- Internal model behavior: The "search habit" mechanism is inferred but not directly probed; we cannot verify if the model maintains an explicit search state.
- Benchmark generalizability: OverSearchQA's construction may not capture real-world user query distributions or dynamic retrieval systems.

## Confidence
- Core phenomenon (search improves accuracy but harms abstention): High
- Mechanistic explanations (reasoning model bias, negative evidence impact): Medium
- Proposed mitigations (query/retrieval-level) yield only modest gains: Low
