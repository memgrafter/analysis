# GPT-NL Public Corpus: A Permissively Licensed, Dutch-First Dataset for LLM Pre-training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00920
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00920
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: A 36B-token Dutch-first corpus (plus 207B English, 232B Code, 48B German/Danish) all under CC-BY, newly aggregated and curated for commercial use.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces the GPT-NL Public Corpus, a large-scale, permissively licensed dataset aimed at enabling lawful commercial training of Dutch language models. To address the scarcity of high-quality Dutch text with clear licensing, the authors combine existing permissively licensed corpora with newly collected Dutch content from institutional partnerships and synthetically generated data from Wikidata. A custom HTML parser (C5) identifies CC-0/CC-BY licensed web content from Common Crawl. The final corpus contains 36 billion preprocessed Dutch tokens, all newly aggregated and not overlapping with other major LLM corpora, alongside substantial English, code, and German/Danish tokens. Rigorous curation includes language detection, quality filtering, harm reduction, and manual verification for legal compliance. The entire corpus is released under a CC-BY license on the Hugging Face Hub.

## Method Summary
The construction employs a multi-source curation pipeline. Existing permissively licensed subsets (e.g., from Common Corpus) are incorporated. New Dutch collections are obtained through partnerships with governmental, archival, and research organizations. Synthetic data is generated from CC-0 Wikidata by converting triples to Dutch sentences and paraphrasing with the permissively licensed Phi-4 model. Web content is processed via C5, which structurally parses HTML to detect permissive licenses in header/footer, followed by domain-level, positional, and manual verification filters. All sources undergo Unicode normalization, whitespace cleaning, and basic deduplication. A sample of each dataset is evaluated for language purity (GlotLid), heuristic quality (minimum characters, duplication), and scored across dimensions. Manual inspection informs threshold adjustments before full filtering. Post-processing removes personal data (automated + manual spot-check) and harmful content (heuristics + blocklists). Final deduplication is applied within collections; cross-collection deduplication is omitted. Each dataset is assigned metadata (source, license, risk level) and the corpus is packaged for release.

## Key Results
- 36 billion preprocessed Dutch tokens, all newly aggregated and not present in other LLM corpora.
- Additional tokens: 207B English, 232B Code, 48B German/Danish, all permissively licensed.
- All data distributed under CC-BY license.
- Curation filters ensure language quality, reduce duplication, and mitigate harmful content; manual verification applied to high-risk domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Institutional Partnerships Provide High-Quality Dutch Text
- **Claim:** Partnering with Dutch/Belgian institutions directly yields large volumes of high-quality, legally clear Dutch text that is otherwise unavailable at scale.
- **Mechanism:** Formal collaborations with archives, governments, and research organizations (e.g., Nationaal Archief, Tweede Kamer, Naturalis) enable the curated harvesting of public domain and permissively licensed content that is natively Dutch and domain-diverse (legal, parliamentary, archival, scientific). This bypasses the licensing ambiguity and noise of web crawls.
- **Core assumption:** Institutional partners hold or can provide access to substantial text corpora with well-defined rights status suitable for commercial redistribution.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...newly created Dutch-specific collections. Most newly created Dutch collections consist of content collected in collaboration with organisations..." [section 4.1] "Most of the new data... was collected in collaboration with governmental, research, archival and judicial organisations." [corpus] Table 3 shows 21 distinct "Collected Data" sources (e.g., Officiële bekendmakingen, De Rechtspraak) contributing ~24B Dutch tokens, representing the core newly aggregated Dutch content.
- **Break condition:** If key institutional partners withdraw cooperation, revoke access, or their holdings are smaller than anticipated, the primary source of novel, high-quality Dutch data collapses.

### Mechanism 2: Synthetic Wikidata Augmentation for Factual Coverage
- **Claim:** Transforming structured, permissively licensed knowledge graphs (Wikidata) into fluent Dutch text synthetically augments factual coverage without copyright risk.
- **Mechanism:** A two-step pipeline: (1) deterministic rule-based mapping of Wikidata triples (CC-0) to raw Dutch sentences, followed by (2) LLM-based (Phi-4, MIT) paraphrasing for naturalness. Final filtering removes non-notable person entries and trivial facts.
- **Core assumption:** LLM paraphrasing of structurally generated text preserves factual accuracy from the source graph while improving linguistic quality, and the CC-0 license of Wikidata cleanly transfers.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...synthetically augmented content." [section 4.2.1] Detailed pipeline for "Wikidata-Synth" from triples to filtered Dutch sentences. [corpus] Table 3 lists "Wikidata-Synth" as a source contributing 1.3B Dutch tokens.
- **Break condition:** If LLM paraphrase step introduces systematic factual drift (hallucination) not caught by filters, the resulting data becomes a source of misinformation rather than reliable knowledge.

### Mechanism 3: Structural License Detection with C5
- **Claim:** A structurally-aware HTML parser (C5) can identify domain-level permissive licenses (CC-0, CC-BY) in Common Crawl with sufficiently low false positive rates for commercial LLM training compliance.
- **Mechanism:** Unlike regex-based methods, C5 parses HTML into a DOM, locates license metadata (meta tags, JSON-LD, footer/header links), and ranks license signals by location/context. For the Public Corpus, outputs are conservatively filtered: only domains with verified headers/footers and manual review of top contributors are retained.
- **Core assumption:** License declarations in header/footer elements are intended to cover the entire page’s content, and manual verification can reliably distinguish true from false positive annotations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...developing the C5 tool for precise license detection." [section 4.3.1] "C5 performs full HTML parsing and structural license extraction... This structural approach allows careful post-filtering to minimize false positives." [section 4.3.2] Describes the three-step filtering (domain-level, license position, manual verification) applied to C5 data for inclusion. [corpus] Table 3 shows "C5 Filtered Web content" contributed 40M Dutch tokens (0.04B).
- **Break condition:** If legal interpretation evolves to require opt-in consent rather than permissive license + TDM exception (Art. 4 CDSM), or if manual verification misses contextual mis-licensing (e.g., footer license applies only to icons), the licensed web portion becomes legally non-compliant.

## Foundational Learning
### Copyright License Compatibility for LLM Training
**Why needed here:** The entire corpus construction is governed by a restrictive license policy (only CC-0, CC-BY, PD). Understanding the distinction between NC, SA, ND clauses and why they are excluded (Sec 3.2) is fundamental to the paper's value proposition: enabling lawful commercial model development.  
**Quick check:** If a dataset is CC-BY-SA, why can it not be included in a corpus for a commercially licensed LLM under the paper's assumptions?

### Synthetic Data Lineage & Hallucination Risk
**Why needed here:** The paper explicitly limits synthetic data to "Type 1" (traceable, source-compliant) to mitigate hallucination. This reflects a specific design philosophy: synthetic data is for reformatting/translating *existing* facts, not generating *new* facts.  
**Quick check:** Why is using an LLM to paraphrase Wikidata triples (Type 1) considered lower risk than prompting an LLM to "write a paragraph about X" (Type 2)?

### Curation as a Differentiated Filtering Process
**Why needed here:** The curation pipeline (Fig 1) is not one-size-fits-all. Filter thresholds (language, duplication, quality) are tuned per dataset based on manual inspection of edge cases (e.g., dialects may score lower on "Dutch" metrics). This acknowledges that source data requires customized treatment.  
**Quick check:** Why might the same minimum character threshold be inappropriate for a collection of short social media posts versus a collection of legal judgments?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Source Acquisition (APIs/scrapers, digitization, synthetic generators, CC crawls) -> Preprocessing & Normalization (Unicode, whitespace, basic deduplication) -> Core Curation Engine (sample -> Language ID -> Heuristic filtering -> Scoring) -> Human-in-the-Loop Interface (visualize, adjust thresholds) -> Post-processing (PII removal, harmful filtering, final deduplication) -> License Verification & Metadata Attachment -> Release

### Critical Path
Raw Source → Normalization → Sample for Evaluation → Threshold Tuning (Manual Inspection) → Full Filtering → Post-Processing → Risk Analysis → Metadata Attachment → Release

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Scale vs. Compliance:** Full Common Crawl (~149B tokens) was rejected for uncertain licensing; only a verified 37M-word slice (C5 filtered) was included.
- **Automation vs. Labor:** Heavy reliance on manual inspection of threshold boundaries and top-risk domains. This is slow but deemed necessary for legal certainty and quality control.
- **Dutch-First vs. Utility:** Inclusion of English/Code/German/Danish was accepted only if it demonstrably aids Dutch capability (multilingual transfer, reasoning) and meets license standards, not for general web-scale coverage.

### Failure Signatures
- **Token Shortfall:** Inability to hit target Dutch token counts due to overly strict filtering or failed partnerships.
- **License Contamination:** False positive CC license annotation in C5 leading to inclusion of NC/SA/ND content.
- **Harmful Bias Leakage:** Archival content (e.g., 19th-century newspapers) containing severe bias/propaganda passing filters and being upsampled.
- **Synthetic Drift:** Wikidata-Synth containing facts not present in the source triples due to LLM paraphrasing errors.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **C5 Validation:** Run C5 on a manually labeled set of 1,000 known CC-BY/CC-0/PD/NON-COMPLIANT websites. Measure precision/recall at domain and document level. Does the header/footer heuristic hold?
2. **Synthetic Data Sanity Check:** Sample 500 Wikidata-Synth sentences. For each, trace back to the source triple(s). Does the sentence factually correspond? Are notable-person PII rules followed?
3. **Filter Threshold Sensitivity:** For a problematic dataset (e.g., archival OCR with noise), run the curation pipeline with varying `min_chars` and `lang_score` thresholds. Plot the trade-off between retained token volume and estimated noise/harmful content (via manual sampling of low-score buckets).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Historical Harm vs. Cultural Utility Trade-off
- **Question:** How does the inclusion of historically harmful content in archival collections trade off against the perceived utility of historical cultural knowledge for model capabilities, and what is the measurable impact of this trade-off on downstream model performance and safety?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state: "Some collections are included despite having a questionable result in terms of the harm and bias requirement... for example: in the archival collections there is a lot of harmful content, but these collections have a lot of historical context about Dutch culture, so the usefulness perspective made us decide to include them."
- **Why unresolved:** The decision was made qualitatively based on a "usefulness perspective," but the paper provides no quantitative analysis of how this specific harmful content affects model outputs (e.g., toxicity, bias) or whether the historical knowledge gain justifies the potential risk. The risk analysis is described as a high-level process for up-sampling decisions, not a measured impact study.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled ablation study training identical model architectures on (a) a version of the corpus with these archives removed and (b) the full corpus, followed by rigorous evaluation of (i) performance on tasks requiring historical/cultural Dutch knowledge and (ii) metrics for harmful, biased, or toxic output generation. Correlational analysis between specific archive-derived tokens in training and problematic generations would also help.

### Open Question 2: Residual PII Prevalence and Privacy Risk
- **Question:** What is the true rate of residual personally identifiable information (PII) in the final GPT-NL Public Corpus after automated replacement and manual verification, and does this residual PII pose a quantifiable privacy risk in potential model memorization?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors report on the PII removal step: "The automated replacement of personal identifiable information was manually verified with a sample of 100 documents. In this manual verification, some false positives were found... No false negatives were found in the sampled documents, but due to the limited number of documents, there could still be some non-replaced personal information in the GPT-NL Public Corpus."
- **Why unresolved:** The sample size (n=100) is small and not designed to estimate a population-level false negative rate. The "could still be" statement acknowledges a significant knowledge gap. The risk of this residual PII is not assessed—it is unknown if it is frequent enough or structured in a way that makes memorization by LLMs a concrete privacy threat.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A larger, statistically robust manual audit (e.g., stratified sampling across all collection types) to estimate the prevalence of missed PII. Combined with a memorization evaluation protocol (e.g., membership inference attacks or extractive memorization tests) on a model trained on the corpus to determine if any residual PII can be reliably extracted.

### Open Question 3: Cross-Collection Duplication Impact
- **Question:** How does the lack of cross-collection deduplication across similar governmental document sets (e.g., 'Openraadsinformatie', 'Woogle', 'Officiële Bekendmakingen') affect data diversity, training efficiency, and potential model overfitting to repetitive governmental jargon?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The limitation states: "There has been no cross collection deduplication of documents. This is especially relevant for the collections of ‘Openraadsinformatie’, ‘Woogle’, and ‘Officiële Bekendmakingen’, which might include the same document multiple times intended for different audiences."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the issue but does not measure the extent of duplication or its consequences. It is unclear if duplication is minor (same notice posted in multiple places) or severe (nigh-identical content inflating token counts). The impact on model training—such as reduced data diversity leading to poorer generalization or wasted compute on redundant examples—is purely speculative.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A full-corpus similarity analysis (e.g., using minhash or document embedding clustering) to quantify the volume and nature of near-duplicate documents across these collections. Training ablation experiments comparing a deduplicated vs. non-deduplicated version of the corpus, measuring (a) validation loss curves and (b) performance on out-of-distribution Dutch text tasks to see if duplication harms generalization.

### Open Question 4: Opportunity Cost of Strict Licensing Policy
- **Question:** What is the opportunity cost of the strict permissive licensing policy (excluding CC-BY-SA, NC, etc.) in terms of missing high-quality, domain-specific Dutch content (like Wikipedia) that is legally unavailable, and could alternative legal frameworks (e.g., TDM exception with robust opt-out respect) safely incorporate such data to improve model coverage?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors justify the policy: "This means that commonly used sets like Wikipedia are not included due to it being licensed under CC-BY-SA." They also discuss the TDM exception: "no readily available crawling tools able to identify all possible versions of machine-readable opt-outs... solely relying on Common Crawl’s adherence to opt-outs... might not be sufficient... we have not included the full Common Crawl dataset but only portions..."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper establishes the policy but does not evaluate the linguistic or domain coverage gap left by excluding CC-BY-SA data like Wikipedia. The dismissal of the TDM exception is based on technical feasibility (tooling), not a fundamental legal conclusion. This raises the question: is the conservative legal stance unnecessarily limiting corpus quality, and could technical/legal innovation bridge this gap?
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comparative analysis of the domain and factual coverage of the GPT-NL Public Corpus vs. a hypothetical corpus that included CC-BY-SA Dutch Wikipedia (e.g., using entity/topic distribution metrics). Legal and technical research to prototype a robust, high-recall opt-out detection system for Common Crawl data that could satisfy the authors' conservative standards, followed by a pilot incorporation study to measure quality gains vs. perceived legal risk.

### Open Question 5: Synthetic Data Lineage and LLM Licensing Complexities
- **Question:** For the Type 1 synthetic data pipeline (e.g., Wikidata-Synth), what are the bounds of factuality and hallucination in the generated text, and how does the use of an LLM (Phi-4) for rewriting introduce its own proprietary licensing and "data lineage" complexities that may conflict with the corpus's permissive goal?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors define Type 1 data: "Synthetic data constructed from permissible and traceable sources... Only Type 1 data is included." For Wikidata-Synth: "An LLM, Phi-4 (MIT-licensed)... was used to paraphrase these outputs... the output, while less repetitive, remained below human parity." On licensing: they note Phi-4 is MIT-licensed, implying compatibility.
- **Why unresolved:** "Below human parity" suggests known quality limitations, but no systematic evaluation of factual accuracy vs. the source triples is reported. More critically, while Phi-4's license is permissive, the act of using an LLM to generate training data creates a new "synthetic" layer. The paper assumes this is clean but does not analyze whether the rewriting step introduces novel hallucinations not present in the source triples or whether the MIT license of Phi-4 fully covers the derivative output for commercial LLM training (a debated legal area).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A grounded accuracy evaluation of a random sample of Wikidata-Synth sentences against the source Wikidata triples to measure the rate of unsupported or contradictory statements. A legal analysis of the MIT license in the context of using an LLM's outputs as training data for another commercial LLM, potentially identifying hidden terms or precedents. A comparison of models trained on raw triple-to-text vs. LLM-rewritten text to measure any performance delta from the rewriting step.

## Limitations
- License verification rigor: C5's manual review protocol (domains >250k words) lacks defined criteria for "correct annotation" and acceptable false positive rates, creating legal compliance uncertainty.
- Curation threshold opacity: Dataset-specific filter thresholds (language scores, heuristic buckets) are undisclosed, preventing exact replication of the 36B Dutch token distribution.
- Synthetic drift risk: LLM paraphrasing (Phi-4) may introduce factual inaccuracies not fully mitigated by the described filtering; no hallucination rate is reported.

## Confidence
- Partnership Data Volume/Quality: Medium
- Synthetic Data Factuality: Low
- C5 Legal Compliance: Medium
