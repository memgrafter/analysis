# Pseudo-Invertible Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06042
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06042
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Introduces SPNNs for zero-shot inversion of non-linear degradations, achieving 92.3% mean binary attribute agreement on semantic restoration.

## Executive Summary
The paper proposes Surjective Pseudo-invertible Neural Networks (SPNNs) to perform zero-shot inversion of non-linear operators, a task where traditional linear back-projection fails. SPNNs combine a surjective forward mapping with an auxiliary network to learn a unique non-linear pseudo-inverse. This enables Non-Linear Back-Projection (NLBP), a closed-form update that projects samples onto the pre-image of the operator while preserving null-space details. Applied to semantic restoration on CelebA-HQ, the method reconstructs faces from 40 binary attributes with 92.3% agreement, and allows precise attribute control over diffusion model outputs without retraining.

## Method Summary
SPNNs employ a multi-scale architecture built from affine surjective coupling blocks and orthogonal mixing via Cayley 1x1 convolutions. Training proceeds in two phases: first, the forward network g (image → attributes) is optimized on the classification objective; second, an auxiliary network r is trained to satisfy the “Natural” non-linear pseudo-inverse condition, minimizing L_natural = E_y[‖G(g†(y)) - G(0)‖²] together with stability losses. The bijective completion G embeds the operator into a higher-dimensional space, making the pseudo-inverse a proper inverse. During diffusion sampling, NLBP updates the denoised estimate x̂₀|ᵗ via: x̂₀|ᵗ ← x̂₀|ᵗ + λ[G⁻¹(G(g†(y)) - G(g†(g(x̂₀|ᵗ)))].

## Key Results
- Achieves 92.3% mean binary attribute agreement on CelebA-HQ semantic restoration.
- Reconstructs key attributes like eyeglasses and hats with >97% accuracy.
- Enables precise zero-shot attribute control over diffusion model outputs without retraining.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SPNNs embed a mathematically well-defined pseudo-inverse through surjective coupling and an auxiliary network, ensuring that for any attribute vector y there exists a pre-image g†(y). The bijective completion G maps the original input into an extended space where g becomes injective; the pseudo-inverse then equals the inverse of G. The NLBP update performs a gradient step that minimizes the distance between the current sample’s G-feature and the target’s G-feature, effectively projecting onto the constraint set while staying on the data manifold modeled by the diffusion prior. Stability losses and orthogonal mixing keep the inverse well-conditioned.

## Foundational Learning
- **Surjective functions**  
  Why needed: Guarantees every attribute vector has at least one pre-image under g, a prerequisite for the pseudo-inverse to be defined everywhere.  
  Quick check: Is a function f: X→Y surjective if its range equals Y? Yes.
- **Pseudo-inverse (linear)**  
  Why needed: Serves as the linear foundation; Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse gives the minimum-norm solution and inspires the non-linear extension.  
  Quick check: For full-column-rank matrix A, A⁺ = (AᵀA)⁻¹Aᵀ.
- **Null-space projection / back-projection**  
  Why needed: In linear inverse problems, solutions lie in an affine space x̂ = A⁺y + z with z∈null(A); the back-projection idea is extended to non-linear case via NLBP.  
  Quick check: The smallest-norm solution to Ax=y is orthogonal to which space? null(A).
- **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)**  
  Why needed: Provides a powerful generative prior over natural images; combined with NLBP it yields high-quality reconstructions.  
  Quick check: What objective does DDPM train? Predicting the noise added during forward diffusion.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Image x -> SPNN_Forward (g) -> y
y -> AuxNet (r) -> latent z -> SPNN_Inverse -> x̂
Diffusion: x̂₀|ᵗ -> g(g†(y)) - g(g†(g(x̂₀|ᵗ))) -> G⁻¹ -> δ; x̂₀|ᵗ ← x̂₀|ᵗ + λδ

Critical path:
1. Train g on attribute classification (cross-entropy).
2. Freeze g, train r to minimize L_natural and stability losses (~50 epochs).
3. Load pre-trained DDPM.
4. Run reverse diffusion with NLBP update at each timestep (constant λ for reconstruction).

Design tradeoffs:
- Surjective coupling vs invertibility: auxiliary r avoids needing g invertible, but r must accurately capture the null-space distribution.
- Orthogonal mixing (Cayley) improves stability but may limit expressive power.
- Bijective completion G choice influences pseudo-inverse; “natural” q is assumed but not unique.
- NLBP adds per-step overhead and requires careful λ selection.

Failure signatures:
- High surjectivity loss (‖y - g(g†(y))‖²) indicates g does not cover the attribute space.
- Poor quality from r alone suggests r failed to learn the manifold; L_natural may be insufficiently weighted.
- Divergence during NLBP sampling may stem from excessive λ or weak stability regularization; reduce λ or increase λ_surj/λ_stab.
- Low attribute agreement despite stability may reflect mismatch between G and diffusion prior.

First experiments:
1. Validate surjectivity: Compute mean ‖y - g(g†(y))‖² on validation; target near zero.
2. Test inverse plausibility: Sample random y, compute x̂ = r(y) (no diffusion); inspect for semantic coherence.
3. Execute NLBP reconstruction: Run diffusion with fixed λ (e.g., 1) on 100 test images; measure mean binary attribute agreement; sweep λ ∈ {0.1,0.5,1,2,5} to locate optimum.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does the capacity and architecture of auxiliary network r affect realism and fidelity of reconstructed pre-images? The training framework optimizes a geometric loss (L_natural), but the relationship between r’s representational power and its ability to model the true data manifold is not analyzed.
2. Can the proposed non-linear pseudo-inverse definition and NLBP be extended to non-surjective operators where the measurement y lies outside the range of g? The current framework assumes surjectivity to ensure a unique minimal-norm solution; for out-of-range y, multiple valid pre-images exist and the selection principle is unclear.
3. Is the choice of bijective completion G (the mapping defining the latent space for the PInv) unique, and how does it influence the properties and performance of NLBP? Different completions could lead to different minimal-norm solutions and thus different NLBP updates; the paper assumes a “natural” completion but provides no analysis of its optimality or sensitivity.
4. Does the NLBP framework generalize to non-binary or probabilistic semantic degradations, such as classifier confidence scores or multi-label distributions, without modification? The SPNN is trained on deterministic binary targets, and the NLBP update assumes a fixed y; probabilistic measurements would require rethinking the training objective and possibly the PInv definition.

## Limitations
- λ guidance scale for semantic reconstruction is not reported; performance is sensitive to this hyperparameter.
- Bijective completion G is incompletely described; the formation of q(x) is omitted, hindering exact replication of Eq. (22).
- Missing training details for the pre-trained DDPM (iterations, optimizer, learning rate) prevent faithful recreation of the generative prior.
