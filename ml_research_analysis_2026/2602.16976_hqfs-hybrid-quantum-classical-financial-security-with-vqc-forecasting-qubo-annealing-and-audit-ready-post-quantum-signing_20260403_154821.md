# HQFS: Hybrid Quantum Classical Financial Security with VQC Forecasting, QUBO Annealing, and Audit-Ready Post-Quantum Signing

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.16976
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16976
*   **Reference Count:** 14
*   **Primary Result:** Integrates VQC forecasting, QUBO annealing, and post-quantum signing to improve portfolio metrics (Sharpe +9.4%, MDD -11.7%) and cut solve time (-28%) while providing an immutable audit trail.

## Executive Summary
HQFS proposes a unified hybrid quantum-classical pipeline for financial portfolio management that addresses the fragmentation between prediction and optimization. It uses a Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC) to jointly forecast returns and volatilities, maps the resulting mean-variance problem with discrete budget constraints to a QUBO for quantum annealing (with a classical fallback), and cryptographically signs each allocation decision using a post-quantum signature for full auditability. On S&P 500 data, the system demonstrates improved forecasting accuracy and superior out-of-sample portfolio performance compared to tuned classical baselines, while reducing optimization solve time and creating a verifiable record of every decision.

## Method Summary
The HQFS pipeline operates in four stages. First, a preprocessing module ingests market data, computes log-returns and rolling volatilities, and constructs normalized sequential feature windows. Second, a VQC processes each feature window to produce a joint forecast of next-step return and volatility; a small classical head maps quantum measurement outcomes to the two outputs, trained with a combined loss. Third, at each rebalance date, these forecasts define the mean μ_t and covariance Σ_t of a standard mean-variance objective. This continuous problem with a budget constraint is converted into a binary QUBO via a normalized bit encoding and quadratic penalties. The QUBO is dispatched to a quantum annealer or a classical heuristic solver. The binary solution is decoded back into continuous asset weights. Finally, a cryptographic hash of the model parameters (θ, ϕ), the resulting weight vector w_t, and the timestamp is signed with a post-quantum private key, creating an immutable, verifiable allocation record.

## Key Results
*   Forecasting: VQC reduces return prediction error by 7.8% and volatility error by 6.1% versus tuned classical models (LSTM/Transformer).
*   Portfolio: Out-of-sample Sharpe ratio improves by 9.4% and maximum drawdown is reduced by 11.7%.
*   Optimization: QUBO-based solve time is reduced by 28% compared to a mixed-integer programming baseline.
*   Audit: The signing process generates fully traceable, signed allocation records with negligible (<5ms) overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Joint VQC Forecasting
*   **Claim:** A VQC-based joint forecaster improves the consistency and accuracy of return and volatility signals versus separate classical models, stabilizing downstream optimization.
*   **Mechanism:** The VQC encodes a sequence of features into a quantum state via parameterized gates. Measurements on this state yield differentiable features z_q, which a classical head f_ϕ maps to joint predictions (r_hat, σ_hat). A combined loss function L_pred (MSE on return + β·MSE on volatility) forces the VQC to learn representations useful for both moments.
*   **Core Assumption:** A carefully trained, small-scale VQC canrepresent temporal dependencies in financial returns more effectively than a classical recurrent network of comparable size, avoiding the mismatch of separately trained models.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [Abstract] Reports 7.8% lower return error and 6.1% lower volatility error.
    *   [Section 3.2] Details the VQC embedding (Eq. 10) and joint loss (Eq. 11).
    *   [Corpus] General evidence for quantum advantage in time-series is limited and architecture-specific; this paper's results are self-contained.

### Mechanism 2: QUBO for Constrained Optimization
*   **Claim:** Mapping the mean-variance problem with discrete budget constraints to a QUBO and solving via annealing reduces solve time while maintaining solution quality.
*   **Mechanism:** The continuous allocation problem is transformed into a binary QUBO. A normalized bit encoding converts weights w_i into binary variables x_{i,b}. Quadratic penalty terms enforce the budget constraint Σ_i w_i = 1 and non-negativity. This unified quadratic form is natively solvable by quantum annealers and efficiently by classical QUBO heuristics, bypassing the iterative projections or branch-and-bound of mixed-integer programming.
*   **Core Assumption:** The binary encoding and penalty-based constraints produce allocation solutions close enough to the continuous optimum that portfolio metrics are preserved, while the solve time is significantly reduced.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [Abstract] States a 28% reduction in average solve time versus a mixed-integer baseline.
    *   [Section 3.2] Provides the QUBO formulation (Eq. 16) and the classical fallback design.
    *   [Corpus] Related work explores constrained quantum optimization, but evidence for this specific financial QUBO's speedup is internal.

### Mechanism 3: Post-Quantum Audit Trail
*   **Claim:** Signing each rebalance output with a post-quantum signature creates an immutable, verifiable audit trail linking the allocation to the exact model parameters and timestamp, satisfying regulatory traceability.
*   **Mechanism:** A cryptographic hash is computed over the tuple (θ, ϕ, w_t, t), where θ/ϕ are model parameters, w_t is the weight vector, and t is the timestamp. This hash is signed with a private key (sk) via a post-quantum algorithm (e.g., lattice-based). The public key (pk) allows any verifier to confirm the allocation's provenance and integrity.
*   **Core Assumption:** The chosen post-quantum signature scheme is secure against classical and quantum attacks, and the hash uniquely binds all components, invalidating the signature upon any undetected change.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [Abstract] Explicitly mentions "audit-ready post-quantum signing" and "fully traceable, signed allocation records."
    *   [Section 3.2] Describes the audit step in Algorithm 1 and notes the overhead is negligible.
    *   [Corpus] No direct empirical validation of the audit layer's security or binding properties is provided.

## Architecture Onboarding
Preprocessing -> VQC Forecaster -> QUBO Builder -> Solver Interface -> Audit Signer

*   **Critical Path:** Data ingestion & windowing -> VQC inference on current window -> Construct µ_t, Σ_t from forecasts -> Formulate QUBO -> Solve (annealer or classical fallback) -> Decode binary solution to w_t -> Compute hash and sign allocation -> Store record.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Quantum Annealer vs. Classical Fallback:** Annealer may offer speed but introduces hardware dependency, queue times, and embedding noise. The fallback ensures deployability but forfeits potential quantum advantage.
    *   **VQC Depth (D) vs. Trainability:** Deeper circuits increase expressivity but risk barren plateaus and noise, making training unstable. The "small classical head" compensates.
    *   **Binary Bits (B) vs. Precision:** More bits per asset (higher B) improves allocation granularity but linearly increases QUBO size (M·B), raising solve time. B=6 is a chosen compromise.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   **VQC:** Forecast correlations collapse; prediction loss plateaus at a high value. Check for vanishing quantum gradients.
    *   **QUBO:** Decoded weights violate constraints (Σw_i ≠1, w_i<0) or have extremely high objective value. Indicates poorly tuned penalty weight ρ_c or ill-conditioned QUBO matrix.
    *   **Audit:** Verification fails for stored records. Possible causes: corrupted storage of (m_t, σ_t), mismatched public key, or post-signing modification of model parameters (θ, ϕ).
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **VQC Sanity Check:** Train on synthetic sine-wave data with known volatility. Verify joint prediction quality and non-vanishing parameter-shift gradients.
    2.  **QUBO Mapping Validation:** For a known continuous optimum w*, encode to binary x*, build QUBO, solve, decode to ŵ*, and measure ||w* - ŵ*||₂. Should be small for adequate B and ρ_c.
    3.  **Audit Loop Test:** For one rebalance date, generate w_t, compute hash m_t, sign with sk to get σ_t. Verify using pk. Then tamper slightly with w_t and confirm verification fails.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How does HQFS perform during high-volatility stress periods, and can drift-aware retraining improve model stability under market regime changes?
    *   **Basis:** Section 5.2 states intent to test stress regimes and use drift-aware retraining.
    *   **Why Unresolved:** Evaluation uses a standard chronological split without specific stress-period analysis; robustness in turbulent markets is unknown.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** Run HQFS on historical high-volatility periods (e.g., 2008, 2020) and compare with/without drift detection/retraining; measure prediction error, Sharpe, and drawdown.
*   **Open Question 2:** How do shrinkage and factor-based covariance estimation techniques affect the conditioning of the QUBO matrix and the scalability of HQFS to larger asset universes?
    *   **Basis:** Section 5.2 plans to strengthen covariance estimation with shrinkage and factor updates.
    *   **Why Unresolved:** Current covariance construction may become ill-conditioned as asset count grows; no evidence on this scalability limit.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** Implement shrinkage (e.g., Ledoit-Wolf) and factor models (e.g., PCA); compute condition numbers and solve times for varying M.
*   **Open Question 3:** What is the impact of incorporating additional trading-realistic constraints (e.g., sector caps, lot sizes, slippage) on the QUBO size and solve time in HQFS?
    *   **Basis:** Future work notes adding trading-realistic constraints and measuring their impact.
    *   **Why Unresolved:** The presented QUBO includes only basic budget/non-negativity; adding real-world constraints affects problem dimension and solver efficiency.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** Extend the QUBO to include these constraints; report resulting binary variable count (M·B) and average solve time.
*   **Open Question 4:** Which post-quantum signature algorithms are most suitable for the audit layer in terms of security, signature size, and computational overhead?
    *   **Basis:** Section 5.2 proposes evaluating more post-quantum signature choices.
    *   **Why Unresolved:** The paper does not specify the algorithm nor compare alternatives; the optimal auditable choice is unknown.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** Integrate several schemes (e.g., CRYSTALS-Dilithium, Falcon, SPHINCS+) and measure generation/verification latency, storage size, and security strength.

## Limitations
*   **Hyperparameter Gaps:** Critical parameters (sequence length L, window W, bits B, asset count M, penalty ρ_c, VQC depth D, etc.) are omitted, hindering exact replication and full scalability assessment.
*   **Evidence Base:** All performance claims are internal; no external validation or comparison with broader state-of-the-art classical methods is provided.
*   **Audit Layer:** The post-quantum signing component is a design claim without empirical validation of security, binding correctness, or detailed overhead analysis.

## Confidence
*   **Forecasting (VQC) – Medium:** Internal MAE/MSE reductions are promising, but VQCs are prone to barren plateaus and hardware noise. Evidence is self-contained; no comparison with recent quantum forecasting architectures.
*   **Optimization (QUBO) – Medium:** Reported 28% solve-time reduction is internal only. The QUBO mapping's sensitivity to penalty weight and binary precision for larger universes is a known break condition not stress-tested.
*   **Audit (Post-Quantum Signing) – Low:** This is a design proposal without empirical validation of the scheme's security, implementation correctness, or operational overhead. The unspecified algorithm and key management raise practical concerns.
