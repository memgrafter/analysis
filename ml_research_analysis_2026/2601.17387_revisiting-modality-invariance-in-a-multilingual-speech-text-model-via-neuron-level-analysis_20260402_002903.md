# Revisiting Modality Invariance in a Multilingual Speech-Text Model via Neuron-Level Analysis
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17387
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17387
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** Multilingual speech-text model (SeamlessM4T v2) exhibits incomplete modality invariance, with modality-specific signals localized in decoder cross-attention and speech-conditioned decoding relying on fewer, more concentrated neurons.

## Executive Summary
This paper probes modality invariance in the SeamlessM4T v2 model, a multilingual speech-to-text (S2T) and text-to-text (T2T) system using a shared decoder. Through neuron-level analysis, the authors find that both speech and text encoders compress inputs into language-agnostic representations, placing a heavy reconstruction burden on the shared decoder. This burden is asymmetric, making speech-to-text translation particularly challenging. Critically, modality-specific information is not broadly distributed but is sharply localized in the cross-attention key and value projections of the shared decoder. Furthermore, speech-conditioned decoding and processing of non-dominant scripts show higher activation concentration, suggesting reliance on a smaller, less redundant neuron subset, which may contribute to brittleness.

## Method Summary
The study uses the SeamlessM4T v2 Large model and the FLEURS benchmark (six languages; 1000 utterances each). Synthetic speech for non-English languages is generated via XTTS v2 to control speaker variability. The core method identifies language- and modality-selective neurons by computing Average Precision (AP) scores for each neuron's activation pattern against binary class labels (e.g., "is this input language X?"). Functional roles are probed via *median-replacement intervention*: during inference, a targeted neuron's activation is replaced by its dataset median, abating its specific signal while preserving activation scale. The impact on task performance (translation: BLEU+chrF; ASR: WER/CER) is measured against random-neuron ablation baselines. Complementary analysis quantifies *activation magnitude inequality* (mean activation per layer, Gini coefficient) to assess computational distribution and concentration.

## Key Results
- Encoders produce language-agnostic representations, forcing the shared decoder to recover language identity; this is harder when the decoder conditions on speech encoder outputs (S2T) than text encoder outputs (T2T).
- Nearly all modality-selective neurons are located in the decoder's cross-attention key and value projections, confirming this sublayer as the primary site for modality reconciliation.
- Speech-conditioned decoding and non-dominant scripts (Chinese, Japanese) exhibit higher activation concentration (larger mean magnitudes, higher Gini coefficients), indicating heavier reliance on a small neuron subset.
- Ablating modality-specific neurons causes a catastrophic performance drop in T2T translation (e.g., combined score collapse), proving their functional necessity; random ablation of an equal number of neurons does not.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The encoder progressively compresses inputs into language-agnostic representations, but this creates a recovery burden for the shared decoder, especially when mapping speech-conditioned representations to text output.
- **Mechanism:** Language-specific information is processed and largely eliminated in the encoders (speech and text). The shared decoder must then reconstruct language-specific output from these compressed, language-neutral representations. The difficulty of this reconstruction is modulated by the input modality, with speech-conditioned decoding being more challenging than text-conditioned decoding.
- **Core assumption:** A shared decoder can effectively generate target language text from a common, language-agnostic latent space, regardless of whether that space originated from speech or text input.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "encoder representations become increasingly language-agnostic, this compression makes it more difficult for the shared decoder to recover the language of origin when constructing modality-agnostic representations, particularly when adapting from speech to text."
  - [section 5.1] Unimodal language-specific neurons in the encoders are concentrated in mid (speech) and early (text) layers, indicating language processing is completed before the final encoder outputs.
  - [corpus] Neighboring work on text-only LLMs supports the existence of language-specific neurons but does not address the speech-text adaptation burden on a shared decoder.
- **Break condition:** If the decoder's cross-attention mechanism were perfectly aligned, the modality of the encoder input would not differentially impact the decoder's ability to recover language identity. The observed performance asymmetry between S2T and T2T translation indicates this break.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Modality-specific information is not broadly distributed but is sharply localized within the cross-attention key (K) and value (V) projection layers of the shared decoder.
- **Mechanism:** The decoder's cross-attention sublayers, which attend to the encoder's output, contain nearly all identified modality-selective neurons. These neurons in the K/V projections likely modulate how the decoder attends to and processes the modality-conditioned encoder states, enabling coherent text generation from either speech or text origins.
- **Core assumption:** Modality differences between speech and text encoder outputs are primarily resolved at the point of cross-attention within the shared decoder, rather than through widespread adjustments throughout the decoder's self-attention or FFN layers.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "we further observe sharply localized modality-selective structure in cross-attention key and value projections."
  - [section 5.1] "modality-specific neurons are concentrated almost entirely in attention rather than feed forward submodules...nearly all modality-selective neurons occur in cross-attention key and value projections."
  - [section 5.2] Interventions ablating modality-specific neurons cause severe performance collapse in T2T translation, confirming their critical, non-redundant role.
- **Break condition:** If modality differences were handled by a more distributed mechanism, ablating the small subset of neurons in cross-attention K/V would not cause catastrophic failure. The localized concentration and high intervention sensitivity define this mechanism's narrow operational envelope.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Speech-conditioned decoding and processing of non-dominant scripts are associated with higher activation concentration, meaning computation relies on a smaller, more highly activated subset of neurons, which may indicate less robust, more brittle internal representations.
- **Mechanism:** Activation magnitudes are not uniform across neurons. Conditions with higher "activation concentration" show a Gini-like inequality where fewer neurons carry most of the activation signal. This suggests these processing paths have lower representational redundancy, making them more vulnerable to perturbations or noise.
- **Core assumption:** Greater dispersion of activation magnitudes across neurons indicates a more robust, distributed computation, while concentration indicates reliance on specialized, potentially fragile mechanisms.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "speech-conditioned decoding and non-dominant scripts exhibit higher activation concentration, indicating heavier reliance on a small subset of neurons, which may underlie increased brittleness across modalities and languages."
  - [section 5.3] "speech-conditioned decoding generally exhibits larger activation magnitudes...languages written in non-dominant scripts...tend to show higher activation values."
  - [section 5.2] Notes higher Gini coefficients for modality-specific and speech-modality neurons, quantitatively supporting the concentration claim.
- **Break condition:** This mechanism would be invalidated if concentration did not correlate with performance degradation. The paper links concentration to hypothesized brittleness but does not establish a direct causal performance drop *due to concentration*; the evidence is correlational.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Average Precision (AP)-based Neuron Ranking**
  - **Why needed here:** This is the core method for identifying language- and modality-selective neurons. It treats neuron activation as a scoring function for a binary classification task (e.g., "is this input German?"). A high AP means a neuron's activation reliably ranks examples of the target class higher than others. This operationalizes "selectivity" without needing a threshold.
  - **Quick check:** If a neuron has a high AP score for the "speech" modality label, what does that imply about its activation pattern across speech and text inputs?
- **Concept: Median-Replacement Intervention**
  - **Why needed here:** This is the primary causal analysis technique. By replacing a targeted neuron's activation with its dataset median during inference, the authors "ablate" that neuron's specific, potentially selective signal while preserving the overall activation distribution's scale and central tendency. This allows testing the *functional necessity* of the identified selective neurons compared to a random control.
  - **Quick check:** Why is median-replacement preferred over zero-ablation (setting activation to zero) for testing the role of selective neurons in this study?
- **Concept: Activation Magnitude Inequality**
  - **Why needed here:** This complementary analysis quantifies *how much* neurons activate, not just *which* ones are selective. Measures like layer-wise mean activation or Gini coefficient reveal whether computation is spread evenly (low inequality) or concentrated in few units (high inequality). This helps explain differences in robustness between conditions (e.g., speech vs. text, dominant vs. non-dominant scripts).
  - **Quick check:** What would a higher Gini coefficient for activation magnitudes across neurons in a layer suggest about that layer's computational strategy?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** SeamlessM4T v2 Large has three main modules: (1) A Conformer-based **speech encoder** processing 16kHz log-mel features. (2) A Transformer-based **text encoder** processing tokenized text. (3) A shared **Transformer text decoder** that performs cross-attention on *either* encoder's output. The speech generation path (decoder -> speech-specific components -> vocoder) is excluded from analysis.
- **Critical path:** Input (speech waveform or text tokens) -> Modality-Specific Encoder (produces modality-conditioned sequence representations) -> Shared Decoder (cross-attends to encoder output, generates target text tokens). The point of modality reconciliation is the decoder's cross-attention layers.
- **Design tradeoffs:** The architecture uses a **single shared decoder** for both speech and text inputs, trading off potential modality-specialized efficiency for parameter sharing and unified text-generation logic. This design assumes the decoder can treat both encoder output types as similar "context." The paper's findings suggest this assumption is partially violated, as modality-specific signals persist and are handled by a small, localized subset of decoder cross-attention parameters.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1.  **Performance collapse in T2T when ablating modality-specific neurons:** Indicates the shared decoder's text-generation path critically depends on the small set of neurons that distinguish text-input history from speech-input history.
  2.  **Higher activation concentration for speech & non-dominant scripts:** Predicts increased brittleness (e.g., larger performance drops under noise or distribution shift) for these conditions, as computation is less redundantly distributed.
  3.  **Limited overlap in unimodal language-selective neurons between speech- and text-conditioned decoding:** Signals that the decoder does not use a common "language module" but instead has modality-dependent language processing pathways.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Neuron Identification:** For each component (speech encoder, text encoder, shared decoder) and for four neuron-type definitions (unimodal language, multimodal language, modality, language-modality), compute AP scores for all neurons. Select top/bottom-k as selective/inhibitory sets. Map their distribution across layers and submodules (FFN, Attention, Convolution).
  2.  **Functional Intervention:** During text generation (ASR, S2T, T2T), replace activations of selected neuron sets (k=500,1000,2500,5000) with their median values. Compare performance degradation (BLEU+chrF for translation, WER/CER for ASR) against random-neuron ablation baselines of the same size.
  3.  **Activation Inequality:** Record mean-pooled activations for each layer during generation. Compute average magnitude across neurons per layer. Compare these layer-wise magnitudes across modalities (S2T vs. T2T) and languages (dominant Latin-script vs. non-dominant), and calculate Gini coefficients for different neuron types to quantify concentration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Do the neuron-level patterns of language- and modality-selectivity generalize to other multilingual speech-text models and a broader set of languages?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Our analysis is limited to a single model... prevents systemic assessment of whether the observed neuron-level findings generalize across architectures or training paradigms."
- **Why unresolved:** The study examined only one model and six languages; it is unknown if patterns are universal.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct identical neuron identification and intervention analyses on other publicly available models (e.g., Whisper, SALMONN) and on diverse languages, especially low-resource ones.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does using natural speech inputs (with speaker and acoustic variability) affect internal representations compared to synthetic speech?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "all speech inputs are synthetically generated... does not fully capture the variability present in natural speech."
- **Why unresolved:** Synthetic speech may introduce artifacts and lacks the acoustic diversity of real speech, potentially biasing results.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Repeat the experiments with real speech recordings (e.g., original FLEURS audio) and compare neuron selectivity distributions and activation magnitudes.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can training strategies improve cross-modal alignment between speech and text encoders, reducing reliance on decoder cross-attention for modality reconciliation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "future work should explore training strategies that encourage greater cross-modal alignment between speech- and text-conditioned encoder representations, enabling the shared decoder to operate on more modality-invariant inputs."
- **Why unresolved:** The current model exhibits incomplete modality invariance, with decoder cross-attention shouldering most of the conversion burden.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train modified models with alignment objectives (e.g., contrastive losses, shared latent regularization) and measure changes in encoder selectivity, decoder cross-attention usage, and cross-modal performance.

## Limitations
- Synthetic speech artifacts: XTTS v2 synthesis for non-Latin script languages (zh, ja) may introduce acoustic distortions affecting neuron selectivity.
- Data split ambiguity: Paper states 1000 utterances per language but not exact FLEURS split, requiring arbitrary choices for reproduction.
- Activation pooling details: Mean-pooling for Conformer modules is described only in passing; dimensional assumptions could alter AP rankings.
- Limited language coverage: Only six languages (three scripts) were studied; conclusions may not generalize broadly.
- Correlational evidence: Link between activation concentration and brittleness remains hypothesis-driven, not causally proven.
- Non-determinism: Transformer generation nondeterminism could subtlely influence intervention outcomes despite seed control.
