# Learning to Collaborate: An Orchestrated-Decentralized Framework for Peer-to-Peer LLM Federation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17133
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17133
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** KNEXA-FL improved Pass@1 by ~50% relative (from 8.89% to 13.33%) versus random P2P on a heterogeneous code generation task, while a centralized baseline catastrophically collapsed.

## Executive Summary
Federated learning for heterogeneous large language models faces a dilemma: centralized aggregation risks privacy and single-point failures, while naive peer-to-peer (P2P) collaboration is inefficient and prone to negative transfer. This paper introduces KNEXA-FL, a hybrid framework that resolves this trade-off. It employs a non-aggregating Central Profiler/Matchmaker (CPM) that formulates optimal teacher-student pairing as a contextual bandit problem (LinUCB), orchestrating direct, secure knowledge distillation between clients. On a federation of six diverse small LLMs fine-tuning for code generation, KNEXA-FL achieved stable convergence and a significant performance gain over random P2P, whereas a strong centralized knowledge distillation baseline suffered catastrophic forgetting. Large-scale simulations further validated the CPM's learned policy at scale.

## Method Summary
KNEXA-FL combines a lightweight central orchestrator with fully decentralized model updates. Clients (heterogeneous LLMs with PEFT modules like LoRA) send privacy-preserving abstract profiles to the CPM. The CPM treats each directed teacher-student pair as a contextual bandit arm, using LinUCB to select disjoint pairs that maximize an upper confidence bound score, balancing known good collaborations with exploration. Paired agents then engage in Adaptive Knowledge Distillation (AKD): the teacher generates text predictions on a shared transfer set; the student re-encodes this text with its own tokenizer and updates its local model using a combined loss of its standard task objective and a cross-entropy KD term. After the exchange, the student reports a normalized reward to the CPM, which updates its bandit model. This cycle repeats, with the CPM learning a policy to maximize collaboration utility without ever accessing raw data, model parameters, or gradients.

## Key Results
- On a 6-client heterogeneous code generation federation, KNEXA-FL achieved a Pass@1 of ~13.33%, a ~50% relative improvement over random P2P collaboration (~8.89%).
- In contrast, a centralized logit-aggregation knowledge distillation baseline (Central-KD) peaked at 18.33% before catastrophically collapsing to 2.00% Pass@1.
- Synthetic simulations scaling the federation to 64 clients showed the CPM's LinUCB policy learned to outperform random pairings by up to 48.5% relative gain, demonstrating scalability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Learned Pairing via Contextual Bandits
The CPM formulates the selection of teacher-student pairs as a combinatorial linear contextual bandit problem (LinUCB). It constructs a context vector from the concatenated abstract profiles of a potential directed pair (static model family, dynamic performance metrics, data distribution embeddings). By maximizing an upper confidence bound (UCB) score, it selects Kₚ pairs per round that balance exploiting known beneficial collaborations and exploring new ones, guided by a reward signal (γ·loss_reduction - δ·communication_cost) reported by the student. This learns a policy that maximizes the expected utility of decentralized knowledge exchange, overcoming the inefficiency of random matching. The core assumption is that abstract profiles contain sufficient signal to predict pairing efficacy as a linear function of context features. Evidence is the significant gain over random and heuristic baselines in simulations.

### Mechanism 2: Architecture-Agnostic Knowledge Transfer via Text
Direct P2P knowledge exchange uses Adaptive Knowledge Distillation (AKD), where teachers send decoded text predictions (not logits) to students. The student re-encodes this text with its own tokenizer, creating soft token targets. This text-level approach sidesteps tokenizer/vocabulary mismatches and output head differences, enabling effective collaboration between architecturally heterogeneous LLMs. The core assumption is that the semantic intent in the teacher's text is a robust transfer signal, even for diverse models. Evidence is that AKD-based KNEXA-FL works where centralized logit-aggregation (Central-KD) fails due to catastrophic interference from heterogeneity.

### Mechanism 3: Non-Aggregating Orchestration for Privacy & Efficiency
The hybrid architecture separates concerns: the CPM is a *non-aggregating* orchestrator that only sees abstract profiles and scalar rewards, never raw data or model updates. Actual knowledge transfer happens directly over secure P2P channels. This removes the central point of failure/aggregation (mitigating inversion attack risks) while adding intelligent coordination to avoid random P2P's inefficiency and negative transfer. The core assumption is that a central entity learning only from such abstract signals does not create a significant new privacy attack surface. Evidence is the design description and the achievement of stable gains without a central model repository.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Non-IID Data & Model Heterogeneity in FL**
  - **Why needed:** The problem is driven by statistical heterogeneity (non-IID private data) and architectural heterogeneity (different LLM backbones/PEFT configs). These make naive aggregation (FedAvg-style) prone to catastrophic forgetting and random P2P statistically inefficient.
  - **Quick check:** If all clients had identical data distributions and architectures, would the CPM's policy still be necessary? (Likely minimal; paper's synthetic experiments show gains diminish in low-heterogeneity settings.)

- **Concept: Contextual Multi-Armed Bandits**
  - **Why needed:** The CPM's decision problem—selecting optimal pairs from agent profiles and past outcomes—is a combinatorial contextual bandit. LinUCB provides a sample-efficient algorithm with theoretical guarantees under linear reward assumptions.
  - **Quick check:** What if the CPM used a greedy algorithm (always pick highest-rewarded seen pair) instead of LinUCB's UCB exploration? (Likely slower convergence to optimal policy, trapped in local optima.)

- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) & Knowledge Distillation (KD)**
  - **Why needed:** Full fine-tuning of LLMs is prohibitive. PEFT (e.g., LoRA) makes local updates and small package exchange viable. KD via teacher predictions enables cross-architecture transfer without sharing gradients or full models.
  - **Quick check:** Why use text-based AKD instead of sharing logits? (Logits require tokenizer/vocabulary alignment; text-based approach is robust to tokenization differences, which is critical for heterogeneous agents.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** LLM Agent (gateway + frozen base LLM + PEFT module + Guardrail Filter) -> (sends profile to) CPM (LinUCB matcher) <- (receives reward from) Student Agent. Teacher Agent -> (sends AKD package via) Secure P2P Channel -> Student Agent. Shared Resources: Public Transfer Set Xᵤ, Global Test Set.
- **Critical path (one communication round):**
  1. All Agents: Perform local PEFT update, generate abstract profile pᵢ and (if selected as teacher) knowledge package κᵢ, send profile to CPM.
  2. CPM: On trigger (quorum/timer), run LinUCB matchmaking on latest profiles to form K disjoint pairs Eₜ, dispatch pairing directives (K_rec, teacher_id) to each student.
  3. Paired Agents: Teacher sends κ (text predictions) to Student over secure channel. Student updates its PEFT module using AKD loss (Eq. 2).
  4. Student: Computes normalized reward rₙₒᵣₘ and context xᵢⱼ, reports to CPM. CPM updates bandit matrices (A, b).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **CPM Trust vs. Aggregation Risk:** CPM is a trusted orchestrator but non-aggregating. Trade-off: introduces a central point for policy learning vs. removing central aggregation point.
  - **Profile Expressivity vs. Privacy/Overhead:** Richer profiles could improve matchmaking but increase privacy leakage and communication. Paper uses modest 32-dim vectors.
  - **AKD Fidelity vs. Efficiency:** Text-based KD is robust but coarser than logits, potentially losing nuanced knowledge. Logits are more informative but require alignment and are larger.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Catastrophic Collapse:** Sharp drop in all clients' performance (like Central-KD), indicating destructive interference.
  - **CPM Stagnation:** KNEXA-FL performance tracks Random-P2P closely, suggesting LinUCB failed to learn.
  - **Hetero-Greedy Underperformance:** Performance worse than random, indicating heuristic pitfalls.
  - **High Variance Across Runs:** Large swings between seeds, possibly from unstable reward normalization.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Sanity Check:** Run `LocalOnly` (no collaboration) to establish low, stable baseline (e.g., ~2% Pass@1).
  2. **P2P Primitive Test:** Run `Random-P2P` to confirm P2P KD can work heterogeneously (e.g., ~9% Pass@1) and set the bar.
  3. **CPM Ablation:** Run `Heuristic-P2P` (e.g., greedy JS divergence). If it underperforms random, it motivates learned matchmaking.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does the CPM's LinUCB-based matchmaking policy scale in terms of performance and computational overhead when the federation size increases to hundreds or thousands of clients under realistic wide-area network (WAN) latency conditions?
- **Open Question 2:** What are the formal convergence guarantees for the end-to-end KNEXA-FL system, given that the CPM's learned matchmaking policy dynamically alters the collaboration graph over time?
- **Open Question 3:** Can more expressive, non-linear contextual bandit models (e.g., neural networks) within the CPM further improve matchmaking efficacy and robustness compared to the linear LinUCB model?
- **Open Question 4:** How robust and generalizable is the KNEXA-FL framework and the CPM's learned policy when the underlying data heterogeneity departs from the Dirichlet-distributed, category-based partition used in the code-generation task?
- **Open Question 5:** What is the precise privacy-utility trade-off when integrating rigorous differential privacy (DP) or zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) mechanisms into the KNEXA-FL protocol?

## Limitations
- The LinUCB exploration constant β is unspecified; its value is critical but its sensitivity is unevaluated.
- The Guardrail Filter's design for filtering sensitive teacher predictions is not detailed and its impact is unmeasured.
- The system's vulnerability to adversarial reward poisoning by malicious agents is not addressed in the threat model.
- Evaluation is limited to small LLMs (~500M) on a single task (code generation) with a maximum of 64 clients in simulation; scalability to larger, more diverse federations is unproven.
- The CPM's state maintenance and reward normalization details (µᵣ, σᵣ tracking) are not fully specified.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid Orchestration Trade-off Resolution:** High. Strong architectural evidence and stark result contrasts (KNEXA-FL stability vs. Central-KD collapse).
- **LinUCB Matchmaking Efficacy:** Medium. Supported by synthetic gains (up to 48.5% relative), but linear context assumption may not hold for complex, non-linear agent interactions in wild settings.
- **Text-Based AKD Robustness:** Medium. Validated on the studied heterogeneity spectrum, but break condition (excessive semantic gap) is plausible and not stress-tested.
- **Privacy Guarantee:** Low. Relies on design principle (non-aggregation) without formal privacy accounting (e.g., ε-δ) or analysis of inference risks from profiles/rewards.

## Next Checks
1. **Parameter Sensitivity Sweep for LinUCB β:** Systematically vary β across multiple seeds in the 6-client setup. Monitor policy convergence speed, final Pass@1 stability, and pair quality to identify robust operating ranges.
2. **Profile Feature Ablation Study:** Zero out or randomize groups of the 32-dimensional profile features (e.g., model family, data histogram bins) and measure the performance drop relative to Random-P2P. This quantifies each component's contribution and tests feature sufficiency.
3. **Adversarial Reward Poisoning Simulation:** Introduce a fraction of malicious clients that report manipulated rewards to the CPM. Evaluate degradation in honest clients' performance and the system's resilience to such poisoning attacks.
