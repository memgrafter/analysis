# Near-Optimal Regret for KL-Regularized Multi-Armed Bandits

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02155
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02155
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** KL‑UCB achieves regret Õ(ηK log²T) for strong regularization and Õ(√(KT)) for weak regularization, with matching lower bounds establishing near‑minimax optimality across all η.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the open problem of characterizing regret for multi‑armed bandits with a KL penalty to a reference policy. It introduces the KL‑UCB algorithm, which combines optimism under 1‑sub‑Gaussian noise with a softmax policy biased by the reference. A novel martingale peeling technique yields high‑probability bounds with linear dependence on the number of arms. The algorithm attains Õ(ηK log²T) regret for small η (strong regularization) and Õ(√(KT)) for large η (weak regularization), matching the standard minimax rate. Matching lower bounds confirm near‑optimality in all regimes.

## Method Summary
KL‑UCB proceeds as follows: for each arm maintain a count \(N_t(a)\) and empirical mean \(\bar{r}_t(a)\); compute a confidence bonus \(b_t(a)=\sqrt{2\log(TK/\delta)/(N_t(a)\vee1)}\); form an optimistic reward \(\tilde{r}_t(a)=\operatorname{clip}(\bar{r}_t(a)+b_t(a),0,1)\); and set the policy \( \pi_{t+1}(a) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(a)\exp(\eta\tilde{r}_t(a))\). The analysis decomposes the KL‑regularized regret into \(\eta\sum_t \mathbb{E}\big[(\tilde{r}_t(a_t)-r^*(a_t))^2\big]\), splits the sum into a deterministic harmonic term \(I_1\) and a martingale‑difference term \(I_2\), and applies a peeling argument (truncating \(I_2\) and using Freedman’s inequality) to handle \(I_2\) while preserving logarithmic scaling for small η. For large η the bound transitions to \(\sqrt{KT}\). Reproduction involves implementing the algorithm, sweeping η across regimes, and verifying the scaling on synthetic environments.

## Key Results
- **Strong regularization (η ≤ √(T/K))**: Regret upper bound \( \tilde{O}(\eta K \log^2 T) \) with high probability.
- **Weak regularization (η ≥ √(T/K))**: Regret upper bound \( \tilde{O}(\sqrt{KT}) \), matching the classical minimax rate.
- **Lower bounds**: \( \Omega(\eta K \log T) \) for high regularization and \( \Omega(\sqrt{KT}) \) for low regularization; together the results prove KL‑UCB is near‑minimax optimal in all η regimes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Optimism via inflated rewards and softmax
Construct the policy using an optimistic reward estimate \(\tilde{r}_t(a)\) (empirical mean plus a Hoeffding bonus, clipped to \([0,1]\)) and a softmax over the reference: \( \pi_{t+1}(a) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(a) \exp(\eta\tilde{r}_t(a))\). The bonus inflates uncertain arms, ensuring that with high probability \(\tilde{r}_t(a) \ge r^*(a)\) (optimism). The exponential tilt then promotes exploration while respecting the reference bias. The event \(E(\delta)\) (all concentration inequalities hold) guarantees optimism.

### Mechanism 2: Regret decomposition into biased squared error
Lemma A.1 shows that for any policy and the optimal one, the KL‑regularized suboptimality is bounded by \(\eta\) times the expected squared error of the optimistic estimator: \( J(\pi^*) - J(\pi_t) \le \eta\, \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_t}\big[(\tilde{r}_t(a)-r^*(a))^2\big] \). This converts the original objective into a sum amenable to concentration and peeling. The bound requires optimism and knowledge that \(\tilde{r}_t\in[0,1]\).

### Mechanism 3: Peeling to control martingale differences
The sum of squared biases splits into \(I_1\) (deterministic harmonic sum \(\le 4K\log T\)) and \(I_2\) (a martingale‑difference sequence). To bound \(I_2\) in high probability without degrading to \(O(\sqrt{T})\), define events \(E_i = \{ \sum_{s=1}^t \mathbb{E}[1/N_{s-1}(a_s)] \le 2^i \}\). On each \(E_i\), truncated martingale differences have conditional variance at most \(2^i\). Apply Freedman’s inequality to each truncated sequence, union‑bound over \(i \le \log T\), and solve a quadratic to obtain \(I_1+I_2 = O(K\log T)\) for small η (where \(I_1\) dominates) and \(O(\sqrt{KT})\) for large η.

## Foundational Learning
- **Stochastic multi‑armed bandits (MAB)**  
  *Why needed:* The KL‑regularized problem extends the classical stochastic bandit model; we rely on bounded, independent reward noise and the cumulative regret objective.  
  *Quick check:* What is the minimax regret rate for standard stochastic K‑armed bandits with bounded rewards? (Answer: \(\Theta(\sqrt{KT})\))
- **KL‑regularized objective and optimal policy**  
  *Why needed:* The learning goal is \(J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[r(a)] - \eta^{-1}\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|\pi_{\text{ref}})\). Its closed‑form optimum \(\pi^*(a) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(a)e^{\eta r^*(a)}\) appears in the regret definition and lower bounds.  
  *Quick check:* How does increasing η affect the sparsity of \(\pi^*\) relative to \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\)? (Answer: Larger η makes \(\pi^*\) more concentrated on arms with high \(r^*\))
- **Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) principle**  
  *Why needed:* KL‑UCB builds an optimistic estimate via \(\bar{r}_t + b_t\); optimism under uncertainty is a standard exploration mechanism.  
  *Quick check:* In UCB1, why is the bonus divided by the number of pulls? (Answer: To ensure confidence intervals shrink with more samples)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Algorithm: \(N_{t-1}(a) \rightarrow \bar{r}_t(a) \rightarrow b_t(a) \rightarrow \tilde{r}_t(a) \rightarrow \pi_t(a) \rightarrow a_t\).  
Analysis: Regret decomposition \(\rightarrow \eta \sum_t \mathbb{E}[(\tilde{r}_t(a_t)-r^*(a_t))^2] \rightarrow\) split into \(I_1\) (harmonic) and \(I_2\) (martingale) \(\rightarrow\) peeling events \(E_i(t) \rightarrow\) Freedman’s inequality \(\rightarrow\) quadratic bound.

**Critical path**  
1. Maintain counts \(N(a)\) and sums \(S(a)\) for each arm.  
2. Compute empirical mean \(\bar{r}(a)=S(a)/(N(a)\vee1)\) and bonus \(b(a)=\sqrt{2\log(TK/\delta)/(N(a)\vee1)}\).  
3. Form optimistic reward \(\tilde{r}(a)=\operatorname{clip}(\bar{r}(a)+b(a),0,1)\).  
4. Set policy \(\pi(a) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(a) \exp(\eta\tilde{r}(a))\).  
5. Sample action \(a\) from \(\pi\) and observe reward.  
6. Update \(N(a)\) and \(S(a)\).  
On the event \(E(\delta)\), the regret is bounded by \(\eta\) times the sum of squared biases; the peeling argument then yields the final rates.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Confidence parameter \(\delta\): Smaller \(\delta\) widens the bonus, increasing optimism but reducing failure probability; it hides \(\log(1/\delta)\) in bounds.  
- Bonus choice: The standard \(\sqrt{\log(TK/\delta)/N}\) ensures sub‑Gaussian concentration; a KL‑based bonus could tighten constants but complicates peeling.  
- Clipping: Guarantees \(\tilde{r}\in[0,1]\) for optimism but may slightly under‑estimate optimistic values.  
- Peeling vs. Azuma: Direct Azuma on \(I_2\) gives \(O(\sqrt{T})\) and destroys the logarithmic rate; peeling is essential for fast rates but introduces extra \(\log\) factors.

**Failure signatures**  
- Violation of sub‑Gaussianity (e.g., heavy‑tailed rewards) invalidates Hoeffding, breaking \(E(\delta)\).  
- Zero reference probability on an optimal arm leads to linear regret (that arm is never selected).  
- Numerical overflow in \(\exp(\eta\tilde{r})\) for large \(\eta\) if not handled in log‑space.  
- Off‑by‑one indexing when updating counts can distort the empirical means.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. *Regime validation*: Run KL‑UCB with small η (e.g., 0.1) and large η (e.g., 10) on Bernoulli bandits; plot cumulative regret vs. \(T\) to verify \(\eta K \log^2 T\) and \(\sqrt{KT}\) scaling respectively.  
2. *Peeling necessity*: Replace the peeling step with a direct Azuma bound on \(I_2\); observe that regret degrades to \(O(\sqrt{T})\) even for small η.  
3. *Reference policy sensitivity*: Use a \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) that assigns zero probability to one optimal arm; confirm that cumulative regret grows linearly in \(T\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Tightening the high‑regularization bound** – The current upper bound is \(\tilde{O}(\eta K \log^2 T)\) while the lower bound is \(\Omega(\eta K \log T)\); closing the \(\Theta(\log T)\) gap remains open.  
2. **Minimax regret for KL‑regularized contextual bandits** – Extending the analysis to context‑dependent rewards and potentially infinite action spaces is an open direction.  
3. **Function approximation** – The results are limited to finite arms; extending to linear or general function approximation (e.g., using eluder dimension) is not resolved.  
4. **Adversarial setting** – How KL regularization interacts with adaptive adversaries and whether \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{KT})\) can be achieved under worst‑case arrangement is unknown.

## Limitations
- The analysis assumes 1‑sub‑Gaussian reward noise; heavy‑tailed distributions break the confidence guarantees.  
- The algorithm requires \(\pi_{\text{ref}}(a) > 0\) for every arm; assigning zero mass to any optimal arm causes linear regret.  
- The effect of clipping and the choice of \(\delta\) on finite‑time performance is not fully analyzed in the paper.

## Confidence
- **Upper bounds (both η regimes):** High (rigorous decomposition and martingale peeling under stated assumptions).  
- **Lower bounds:** Medium (adaptation to KL‑regularization is sketched but not fully detailed).  
- **Near‑minimax optimality:** Medium (the small‑η upper bound has an extra logarithmic factor, so exact optimality holds only asymptotically).

## Next Checks
1. **Scaling test:** Simulate KL‑UCB for \(K=10\), \(\eta=0.1\) and \(\eta=10\) over \(T\in[10^2,10^5]\) with ≥100 runs; verify empirical regret matches \(\eta K \log^2 T\) and \(\sqrt{KT}\) trends.  
2. **Support test:** Set \(\pi_{\text{ref}}(a)=0\) for one optimal arm and confirm that cumulative regret becomes linear in \(T\).  
3. **Heavy‑tailed test:** Replace Gaussian noise with Cauchy‑distributed noise; check whether empirical regret exceeds the predicted bounds, exposing the sub‑Gaussian assumption.
