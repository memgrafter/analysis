# Recurrent Reasoning with Vision-Language Models for Estimating Long-Horizon Embodied Task Progress

## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2603.17312
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17312
- References: 40
- Result: R2VLM achieves SOTA progress estimation (ALFRED MAE 2.19, Ego4D MAE 19.25) and improves downstream policy and reward modeling.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents R2VLM, a recurrent reasoning vision-language model for estimating progress on long-horizon embodied tasks. R2VLM processes video snippets sequentially while maintaining a global Chain of Thought (CoT) that records task decomposition and step completion, thereby reducing computational cost and enabling reasoning over temporal dependencies. The model is trained on automatically generated datasets from ALFRED and Ego4D via a two-stage procedure: supervised fine-tuning followed by reinforcement learning with a composite reward. R2VLM sets new state-of-the-art results on both benchmarks and demonstrates significant gains in downstream applications such as policy learning and reward modeling.

## Method Summary
R2VLM is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. Given a task description and a video streamed as short snippets (4 frames per snippet at 1 fps for ALFRED, 2 fps for Ego4D), the model iteratively consumes each snippet together with the current history CoT and task query, producing an updated CoT (including a decomposition of steps and completed steps) and an integer progress value (0–100). The updated CoT is fed back as history for the next snippet, forming a recurrent loop. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) Supervised fine-tuning on automatically generated dialogue tuples (124k from ALFRED, 128k from Ego4D) using full-parameter fine‑tuning (LR=1e-5, batch=64, 8 epochs). (2) Reinforcement learning with PPO (KL=0) starting from the cold‑start checkpoint; rewards combine Format, Bin, MAE, Improvement (error reduction), and Finish multiplicatively. Frameworks used include LLaMA‑Factory for SFT and Verl for RL, with DeepSpeed ZeRO-2/3 for efficiency. The datasets and code are publicly released on HuggingFace.

## Key Results
- On ALFRED: progress MAE 2.19, step-bin accuracy 0.917, task completion accuracy 0.988.
- On Ego4D: progress MAE 19.25, step-bin accuracy 0.318, task accuracy 0.761.
- Downstream: progress‑enhanced policy learning improves goal completion by 2.2%; reward modeling raises step completion to 2.45% from a 2.02% baseline; the system also enables effective step‑wise proactive assistance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
R2VLM’s effectiveness stems from its recurrent reasoning design that balances local processing with global context via an evolving Chain of Thought. By handling short video snippets sequentially, the model avoids the computational burden of processing entire long videos, while the CoT accumulates step decompositions and completions, providing a persistent global state. This setup allows the model to reason about temporal dependencies and correct earlier mistakes as new information arrives. The two‑stage training further boosts performance: SFT equips the model with basic reasoning and progress prediction from large‑scale automatic data; RL with a composite reward (Format, Bin, MAE, Improvement, Finish) fine‑tunes the model to optimize directly for the target metrics, encouraging precise incremental updates and successful task completion.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map** (plain text flow):  
Task Query + Initial CoT → Loop over video snippets:  
  History CoT + Current Snippet + Task Query → R2VLM (Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B‑Instruct) → (New CoT, Progress)  
  New CoT updates History CoT for next iteration.  
Training pipeline:  
Raw trajectories → Auto CoT/Progress Annotation → SFT Dataset → SFT Trainer → Cold‑start Checkpoint → RL Trainer (Verl + PPO) with Reward Calculator → Final Model.

**Critical Path**:
1. Collect trajectory data (ALFRED/Ego4D).
2. Generate automatic CoT and progress labels.
3. Perform supervised fine‑tuning.
4. Conduct reinforcement learning with designed rewards.
5. Deploy recurrent inference at test time.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- *Snippet duration*: Longer snippets capture more context but increase memory and risk attention dispersion; shorter snippets are efficient but may miss critical actions.
- *Recurrent vs. global*: Recurrent reduces compute but may lose very long‑range cues; CoT mitigates by maintaining a summary.
- *Automatic vs. human data*: Automatic scaling vs. potential label noise (e.g., 74% retention on Ego4D).
- *Two‑stage training*: SFT provides a stable initialization; RL adds complexity but yields metric‑driven refinement.

**Failure Signatures**:
- SFT loss does not decrease: likely data format mismatch or image loading error.
- RL shows no improvement: possibly incorrect reward computation (e.g., Improvement always zero) or unstable PPO hyperparameters.
- CoT becomes incoherent over long sequences: context length exceeded or insufficient global state representation.

**First Experiments**:
1. **Data loading sanity check**: Load a small batch (8 samples) and confirm the model produces outputs with the required `<think>` and `<answer>` blocks.
2. **SFT on a subset**: Fine‑tune on 1k samples; verify loss decreases and the model begins to generate meaningful CoT and progress values.
3. **Recurrent inference validation**: Using the SFT checkpoint, run iterative snippet inference on 2–3 full trajectories; manually compute progress MAE to ensure the recurrent loop functions correctly.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Single‑frame R2VLM for online RL reward modeling**  
   *Basis*: Authors suggest training a dedicated single‑frame model to avoid duplicating frames. *Unresolved*: No evaluation yet. *Evidence*: Train a single‑frame R2VLM and compare step/task completion on the EVAL INSTRUCT benchmark.

2. **Adapting GRPO for multi‑turn VLM reasoning**  
   *Basis*: PPO is used because GRPO requires identical inputs for multiple candidates, which conflicts with the multi‑turn setting. *Unresolved*: A suitable GRPO variant is unexplored. *Evidence*: Design a GRPO that groups trajectories by similar CoT histories and compare training stability and final progress metrics against PPO.

3. **Impact of noise in automatically generated CoT**  
   *Basis*: Training data are automatic; manual review of Ego4D samples yields 74% retention, indicating label noise. *Unresolved*: The effect of such noise on accuracy and robustness is unknown. *Evidence*: Train models on human‑verified vs. low‑quality subsets and analyze performance differences and error types.

4. **Optimal video snippet duration and frame rate**  
   *Basis*: Snippets are fixed at 4 s (ALFRED) and 2 s (Ego4D) without justification. *Unresolved*: The trade‑off between accuracy and computational cost is unclear. *Evidence*: Ablate over durations (1 s, 2 s, 4 s, 8 s) and frame counts, measuring MAE, bin accuracy, and inference latency.

5. **Alternative Chain‑of‑Thought structures**  
   *Basis*: CoT format is strictly prescribed; no alternatives compared. *Unresolved*: Different layouts may better suit VLMs or downstream needs. *Evidence*: Evaluate templates (flat list, hierarchical, with/without explicit decomposition) on automatic metrics and human interpretability.

## Limitations
- Dataset format and frame extraction details are incompletely documented, making exact data loading difficult.
- The custom multi‑turn rollout and reward implementation for RL (especially the Improvement reward) are not open‑sourced, requiring reimplementation.
- Automatically generated CoT may contain noise, particularly on real‑world Ego4D, which could limit robustness and contribute to the performance gap between the two benchmarks.
