# AdditiveLLM2: A Multi-modal Large Language Model for Additive Manufacturing

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.22017
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22017
*   **Reference Count:** 35
*   **Primary Result:** A domain-adapted multi-modal LLM (Gemma 3-12B) achieves over 90% accuracy on general AM knowledge tasks and top performance on vision-based anomaly identification and machine recognition via a three-stage, LoRA-based tuning pipeline on a 50M token AM dataset.

## Executive Summary
AdditiveLLM2 is a specialized multi-modal large language model (VLM) for the additive manufacturing (AM) domain, built by adapting the Gemma 3-12B model. The development employs a three-stage training pipeline: (1) Domain Adaptive Pretraining (DAPT) on a 29M token text corpus from AM journals, (2) DAPT on paired figure-caption data to adapt the vision encoder, and (3) Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT) on ~20k generated conversational examples. This approach efficiently imbues a general-purpose model with deep AM expertise. Evaluated on the proprietary Additive-Manufacturing-Benchmark, the final model demonstrates state-of-the-art proficiency, particularly in language-based multiple-choice questions (>90% accuracy) and vision-based tasks like laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) anomaly identification and AM machine recognition, significantly outperforming both the base model and variants without full adaptation.

## Method Summary
The method involves sequential, parameter-efficient fine-tuning of a base multi-modal model (Gemma 3-12B-IT). First, the language components are adapted via LoRA on a large text corpus of open-access AM journal articles. Second, the vision encoder is adapted (with language weights frozen) using image-caption pairs extracted from the same journal figures. Third, both language and vision components are jointly fine-tuned using LoRA on a dataset of visual question-answering and descriptive conversations generated from the captions. This staged approach aims to first build foundational domain knowledge (DAPT) and then shape it into a useful, instruction-following assistant (VIT). All adaptation uses Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) with rank `r=16` for computational efficiency.

## Key Results
*   **Language Tasks:** Achieves >90% accuracy on general additive manufacturing knowledge multiple-choice questions.
*   **Vision Tasks:** Attains top performance on the Additive-Manufacturing-Benchmark for LPBF anomaly identification (F1 score) and AM machine recognition.
*   **Efficacy:** The full three-stage pipeline (DAPT-text → DAPT-images → VIT) significantly outperforms the base Gemma 3 model and models that skip either DAPT stage.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Domain Adaptive Pretraining (Text)
Continued pretraining on ~29M tokens of AM-specific text adjusts the model's self-attention weights to better represent domain terminology (e.g., "LPBF", "melt pool") and factual relationships between process parameters and outcomes. This builds a strong parametric knowledge base for language-only tasks. Evidence is found in the described corpus construction and the reported high accuracy on general knowledge benchmarks following this stage. A break condition would be degradation in general knowledge after this stage, indicating catastrophic forgetting.

### Mechanism 2: Domain Adaptive Pretraining (Vision)
The vision transformer is adapted on paired AM figure-caption data while the language tower is frozen. This trains the vision encoder to align visual features (e.g., keyholing defects, machine components) with the already domain-specialized language representations, creating a joint embedding space for AM-specific visual concepts. Evidence includes the focus on figure-caption pairs and the target vision tasks. A break condition would be a drop in vision task performance after this stage, suggesting poor alignment or inadequate data.

### Mechanism 3: Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT)
Supervised fine-tuning on ~20k conversational and descriptive examples, generated from the captions, uses LoRA to update both language and vision weights. This shapes the model's behavior to follow complex instructions, perform step-by-step reasoning, and generate useful responses across modalities. Evidence is the explicit use of "instruction tuning" and multi-turn conversation tasks. A break condition would be no improvement in vision-based task accuracy after VIT compared to DAPT-images alone, indicating misaligned training data.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Transformer Architecture (Decoder-only)**
    *   **Why needed here:** Gemma 3's core is a decoder-only transformer. Understanding next-token prediction and self-attention is key to grasping how Domain Adaptive Pretraining (DAPT) updates the model's internal representations for AM concepts.
    *   **Quick check question:** How does updating only specific LoRA-injected layers during DAPT constrain the model's adaptation compared to full fine-tuning, and what knowledge might be preserved?
*   **Concept: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**
    *   **Why needed here:** Full fine-tuning a 12B parameter multi-modal model is infeasible for most. LoRA enables efficient, modular domain adaptation by freezing original weights and injecting small, trainable rank-decomposition matrices into attention projections.
    *   **Quick check question:** What are the practical trade-offs (memory footprint, speed, final performance) when choosing a LoRA rank `r=16` versus a higher rank like `r=64` for this three-stage pipeline?
*   **Concept: Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT)**
    *   **Why needed here:** VIT is distinct from continued pretraining. It is supervised learning on (image, instruction, response) triples to teach the model to interpret a user's query in context and generate relevant, structured answers, which is crucial for a useful AM assistant.
    *   **Quick check question:** Why is the loss in a multi-turn conversation often computed only on the assistant's tokens, and how does prompt masking ensure the model learns from its own generation context?

## Architecture Onboarding
Base Model: Gemma 3-12B-IT (multi-modal with frozen SigLIP vision encoder)
Data Pipeline: PDF extraction (PyMuPDF) → text corpus (~29M tokens) + image-caption pairs (~24k) → VIT examples generated via GPT-OSS 120B
Adaptation Engine: Three-stage sequential LoRA training: DAPT-text → merge adapters → DAPT-images → merge adapters → VIT. Adapters applied to `q,k,v,o` projections (`r=16, alpha=32`).
Evaluation Harness: `additive-manufacturing` Python package with 6 benchmark tasks.

Critical path: Data curation (extraction & cleaning) → DAPT-text → merge adapters → DAPT-images → merge adapters → VIT → benchmark evaluation. Stage failure halts progression.

Design tradeoffs:
*   **Dataset Size vs. Compute:** 50M tokens enables rapid iteration (~36h/stage on 3xA6000) but risks underfitting the broad AM domain; larger corpora would require more resources.
*   **LoRA Rank vs. Capacity:** `r=16` saves memory but may limit adaptation depth; higher ranks risk overfitting or memory overflow.
*   **Freezing Strategy:** Freezing language weights during DAPT-images protects language knowledge but may limit cross-modal learning; unfreezing risks catastrophic forgetting of the text-based domain adaptation.

Failure signatures:
*   **Catastrophic forgetting:** Sudden drop in multiple-choice accuracy after DAPT-images or VIT.
*   **Vision tower collapse:** Vision task scores (e.g., LPBF F1) fall to random chance after any stage.
*   **Benchmark contamination:** Near-perfect scores on all tasks immediately suggest training data leakage into the benchmark.

First 3 experiments:
1.  **Baseline Establishment:** Run the full Additive-Manufacturing-Benchmark on the unadapted `gemma-3-12b-it` base model to establish a performance floor.
2.  **DAPT-text Isolation:** Train **only** the first stage (DAPT-text). Evaluate. Expect a large boost in general knowledge multiple-choice accuracy but no improvement (or a drop) in vision tasks.
3.  **VIT Ablation:** Take the best model from experiment 2 (DAPT-text-only) and **skip** DAPT-images, applying VIT directly. Compare vision task scores (LPBF F1, machine ID) against the full pipeline model. This isolates the contribution of the DAPT-images stage for vision specialization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How can AdditiveLLM2 be effectively integrated into an agentic system for real-time AM process monitoring and defect mitigation?
*   **Basis in paper:** The Future Work section explicitly proposes investigating AdditiveLLM2 within agentic systems, similar to LLM-3D Print, to evaluate its efficiency in making tool calls based on domain knowledge.
*   **Why unresolved:** The current work only evaluates AdditiveLLM2 on static benchmark tasks; its dynamic decision-making and tool-use capabilities in a closed-loop agentic framework remain untested.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement AdditiveLLM2 as the VLM component in an agentic system (e.g., for FDM defect correction), then measure improvements in mechanical performance, defect resolution rate, and tool-call accuracy compared to non-specialized LLMs.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** What is the primary cause of the observed performance degradation during the DAPT image training stage, and how can it be mitigated?
*   **Basis in paper:** Results show a noticeable drop in performance (e.g., in general knowledge and LPBF anomaly tasks) after DAPT image training across both IT and PT base models. The authors speculate this could be due to frozen language weights, missing caption pairs, or catastrophic forgetting, but do not isolate the cause.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the trend but does not perform ablations to pinpoint the dominant factor, leaving the mechanism of this degradation unclear.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct controlled experiments: (a) train with language weights unfrozen vs. frozen, (b) train on images with/without associated captions, (c) measure task-specific knowledge retention before and after image DAPT to quantify forgetting. Performance recovery methods (e.g., reversible fine-tuning) could then be tested.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** To what extent does the initial instruction tuning of the base model (Gemma-3-12b-it vs. -pt) influence the effectiveness and efficiency of subsequent domain adaptive pretraining in additive manufacturing?
*   **Basis in paper:** The paper compares models built from the instruction-tuned (IT) and pretrained-only (PT) variants of Gemma 3, consistently finding the IT-based models perform better. However, it does not analyze *why* the IT base confers such a significant advantage for domain adaptation.
*   **Why unresolved:** The superior performance of IT-based models is empirically shown but not deconstructed; it is unclear whether this stems from better prompt following, preservation of general reasoning, or other inherent properties of the IT base.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform a comparative analysis using identical DAPT/VIT pipelines on IT and PT bases. Probe internal representations (e.g., via attention visualization or probing tasks) to measure retention of general capabilities vs. acquisition of AM-specific knowledge. Evaluate if the IT base requires less domain data to reach parity.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Would a significantly larger and more comprehensive AM-domain dataset lead to proportional performance gains in AdditiveLLM2, or is there a saturation point?
*   **Basis in paper:** The Future Work section states that a "larger and more encompassing dataset" is expected to yield "greater performance gains." The paper currently uses ~50M tokens from 1,704 articles, which is small compared to general pretraining corpora.
*   **Why unresolved:** While the authors hypothesize benefits from more data, they do not test scaling laws specifically for AM domain adaptation. The relationship between dataset size/diversity and task performance remains empirical.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Train multiple AdditiveLLM2 variants with progressively larger AM datasets (e.g., 50M, 200M, 500M tokens) from a wider range of sources (patents, standards, technical reports). Plot performance on the Additive-Manufacturing-Benchmark tasks against dataset size to identify scaling trends and potential saturation.

### Open Question 5
*   **Question:** Can continual learning techniques be effectively applied to AdditiveLLM2 to sequentially adapt to new AM sub-domains (e.g., moving from LPBF to vat photopolymerization) without catastrophic forgetting of previously learned tasks?
*   **Basis in paper:** The Future Work directly proposes investigating continual learning to "alleviate the effects of catastrophic forgetting" and build a framework where the model integrates new fine-tuning cycles without losing prior abilities.
*   **Why unresolved:** The current model is trained in distinct stages (DAPT text → DAPT images → VIT) but not in a truly sequential, lifelong manner where new sub-domain data is introduced later. The risk of forgetting when expanding the domain scope is unaddressed.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Simulate a continual learning protocol: first train on LPBF-focused data, then fine-tune sequentially on data from other AM processes (e.g., DED, material extrusion). Evaluate performance on all sub-domain tasks (using the benchmark) after each step, and compare strategies like elastic weight consolidation or experience replay against baseline sequential fine-tuning.

## Limitations
*   **Benchmark Validity:** Evaluation relies on a proprietary `Additive-Manufacturing-Benchmark` of unclear public availability and validation, risking overfitting to a single, non-standard suite.
*   **Data Contamination:** The 50M token training corpus from open-access journals may overlap with benchmark questions or common test sets, potentially inflating reported scores.
*   **Generalization Scope:** Demonstrated performance is limited to specific AM processes (LPBF, FDM) and task types; transfer to other processes (e.g., DED, SLM) or truly out-of-distribution real-world images is unproven.

## Confidence
*   **>90% accuracy on general AM knowledge (multiple-choice):** **Medium**. Relies on a single, non-public benchmark; no external validation or human expert comparison is reported.
*   **Top performance on vision-based tasks (anomaly ID, machine recognition):** **Low-Medium**. Vision task evaluation is limited in scope and number; no statistical significance testing against multiple baselines is provided in the excerpt.
*   **Efficacy of the three-stage pipeline (DAPT-text → DAPT-images → VIT):** **Medium**. While the pipeline is clearly described, the causal contribution of each stage is inferred from results; formal ablation studies isolating each component are not presented in the provided summary.

## Next Checks
1.  **Independent Benchmark Replication:** Obtain or construct a separate, publicly available AM QA dataset (e.g., from textbooks, certification exams) to re-evaluate the final model, testing generalization beyond the proprietary benchmark.
2.  **Contamination Audit:** Manually inspect a random sample of benchmark questions and images against the source 50M token training corpus to quantify potential text and image leakage.
3.  **Cross-Process Vision Test:** Assess the LPBF-trained model's zero-shot performance on defect images from a different AM process (e.g., Material Jetting or Vat Photopolymerization) without any fine-tuning to measure true visual concept transfer versus memorization.
