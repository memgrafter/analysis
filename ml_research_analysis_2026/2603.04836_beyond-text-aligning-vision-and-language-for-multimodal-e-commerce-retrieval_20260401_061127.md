# Beyond Text: Aligning Vision and Language for Multimodal E-Commerce Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04836
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04836
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** Adding images improves NDCG@1 by up to 4.86% for desirability and 2.36% for relevance over text-only baselines.

## Executive Summary
E-commerce retrieval systems typically rely solely on textual information, ignoring influential visual signals from product images. This paper proposes a multimodal two-tower model that integrates both text and image cues. The approach uses CLIP-based encoders with domain-specific fine-tuning, a two-stage alignment where queries are separately matched to product titles and images, and a lightweight Mixture-of-Modality-Experts fusion enhanced by a bilinear interaction to capture cross-modal relationships. Training incorporates self-adversarial negative sampling and a multi-objective loss combining engagement and relevance signals.

On large-scale e-commerce datasets, the model yields substantial gains: up to 4.86% higher NDCG@1 for desirability and 2.36% for relevance compared to text-only baselines. Ablation studies confirm the critical role of domain fine-tuning and query alignment, and the MoE+Bilinear fusion consistently outperforms alternative fusion strategies.

## Method Summary
The system employs a two-tower architecture. The query tower uses a CLIP text encoder to embed user queries. The item tower has two parallel branches: a CLIP text encoder processes product titles, and a CLIP image encoder processes product images. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) domain-specific fine-tuning of both CLIP encoders on a large corpus of title–image pairs; (2) explicit alignment of queries with each item modality separately; (3) fusion of the two item modality embeddings via a Mixture-of-Modality-Experts (MoE) gate and a multi-head bilinear interaction to produce a final item representation. Relevance scoring is performed via cosine similarity between the query embedding and the fused item embedding. The loss function combines self-adversarial negative sampling with a weighted sum of hinge-based engagement and relevance objectives.

## Key Results
- Incorporating product images yields absolute NDCG@1 gains of up to 4.86% for desirability and 2.36% for relevance compared to text-only retrieval.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that domain-specific CLIP fine-tuning and the two-stage query alignment are essential components.
- The proposed MoE+Bilinear fusion module consistently outperforms alternative fusion strategies (e.g., MoE alone, attention, MLP).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Domain-specific fine-tuning of CLIP encoders on e-commerce title–image pairs is necessary to adapt general multimodal representations to commercial relevance signals.
- **Mechanism:** The pretrained CLIP model, while broadly aligned, does not capture e-commerce-specific semantics (e.g., product attributes, styling nuances). Fine-tuning via contrastive learning on large-scale product data (4M pairs) adjusts the encoders' embedding spaces to better reflect how text descriptions and product images co-occur in this domain.
- **Core assumption:** The distribution of text–image pairs in e-commerce differs sufficiently from generic web data to require explicit adaptation; otherwise, the model underfits domain-specific relevance patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "we show that domain-specific fine-tuning is essential for aligning multi-modal representations with commercial relevance."
  - [section 2.5 Stage I]: "we perform domain-specific fine-tuning of the CLIP encoders on a large-scale title–image dataset... to adapt pretrained visual-text representations to e-commerce semantics."
  - [corpus]: VL-CLIP [neighbor 45848] identifies "weak object-level alignment and ambiguous textual representations in product data," supporting the need for domain adaptation. However, direct performance causality for *this* paper's fine-tuning protocol is not cited in neighbors—corpus evidence here is theoretical alignment.
- **Break condition:** If domain fine-tuning is omitted, performance regresses toward the pretrained CLIP baseline (Table 2: "+ FT" vs. "CLIP" shows smaller gains than "+ FT + Align"), indicating failure to inject domain knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Explicit, staged alignment of user queries with individual product modalities (text and image) before fusion is crucial for learning modality-specific relevance signals.
- **Mechanism:** Queries are separately trained to match product titles (*query–text alignment*) and product images (*query–image alignment*) in Stage II. This decoupled learning allows the model to first grasp how user intent maps to each modality's feature space before combining them, reducing interference and ensuring each modality's evidence is properly grounded in query semantics.
- **Core assumption:** User relevance judgments decompose into modality-specific components (e.g., style via image, specification via text); learning these separately before fusion yields more robust multimodal representations than end-to-end fusion from random initialization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "explicitly align user queries with both product text and product images... are both crucial."
  - [section 2.5 Stage II]: "we explicitly align user queries with individual item modalities... This curriculum step encourages the model to learn modality-specific relevance signals."
  - [section 3.6.1 Table 2]: Adding alignment ("+ FT + Align") on top of fine-tuning yields the strongest gains over fine-tuning alone or pretrained CLIP, confirming the staged approach.
  - [corpus]: neighbor "MAKE" [197629] uses a similar two-phase retrieval (retrieval then ranking) but does not explicitly study staged query-modality alignment; evidence is indirect. Corpus does not provide direct ablation for this paper's alignment stages.
- **Break condition:** Skipping Stage II (directly training on fused representations) leads to a clear performance drop (Table 2: "CLIP" and "+ FT" underperform "+ FT + Align"), suggesting the model fails to learn distinct query-modality correspondences.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The proposed Mixture-of-Modality-Experts (MoE) with bilinear interaction fusion outperforms other strategies by jointly enabling adaptive modality weighting and fine-grained cross-modal feature interactions.
- **Mechanism:** The MoE module (gating network) dynamically weights the text and image embeddings per instance, capturing modality importance. The bilinear interaction layer simultaneously models element-wise multiplicative interactions between projected text and image features across multiple heads, capturing subtle cross-modal relationships (e.g., a specific color mentioned in text appearing in the image). The residual design preserves unimodal signals while enhancing cross-modal synergies.
- **Core assumption:** E-commerce relevance often depends on interactions between textual and visual cues (e.g., "red leather sofa" requires matching both color text and material texture). A fusion that only linearly combines or attends post-hoc is insufficient to capture these pairwise feature dependencies.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "propose a noval modality fusion network... with bilinear interaction captures cross-modal relationships."
  - [section 2.2 Equation 3]: The bilinear block explicitly computes cross-modal products `W(k)_t h_t ⊙ W(k)_i h_i` for multiple heads.
  - [section 3.6.2 Table 3]: "MoE+Bilinear" consistently outperforms "MoE" (adaptive weighting only), "Attention," and "MLP" fusions on both desirability and relevance metrics.
  - [corpus]: neighbor "OMGM" [100205] considers multi-granularity and multi-modality orchestration, suggesting the community explores complex fusion. However, no neighbor directly validates bilinear interaction's superiority for this specific e-commerce retrieval setting.
- **Break condition:** Replacing bilinear interaction with simpler fusion (e.g., MoE alone or MLP fusion) degrades performance (Table 3). If bilinear heads fail to learn meaningful interactions (e.g., near-zero activations), the model would revert to MoE-only behavior, losing the cross-modal gains.

## Foundational Learning
- **Contrastive Representation Learning (CLIP pretraining)**  
  *Why needed:* Provides a pretrained, semantically aligned text-image embedding space. The two-tower retrieval architecture fundamentally relies on cosine similarity in a shared space; CLIP initializes both query (text) and item (text & image) towers with strong cross-modal grounding.  
  *Quick check:* Is the CLIP text encoder used for query encoding? Is the CLIP image encoder one branch of the item tower?

- **Curriculum/Staged Training**  
  *Why needed:* The multimodal alignment problem is complex. Staging from domain adaptation (modality-agnostic) → modality-specific query alignment → fused alignment prevents catastrophic forgetting and shapes representations progressively toward user decision signals.  
  *Quick check:* Are the three training stages (Domain FT, Query Alignment, Fusion Alignment) run sequentially with optimizer reset or fine-tuning?

- **Self-Adversarial Negative Sampling**  
  *Why needed:* In-batch negatives include highly distracting items. Weighting negatives by similarity (top-K off-diagonals) exposes the model to challenging confusable products within the batch, improving discriminative power for fine-grained e-commerce distinctions (e.g., similar styles/brands).  
  *Quick check:* For each query in a batch, are negatives selected from other batch items based on current similarity scores (top-K), rather than random?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
1.  **Query Tower:** CLIP text encoder → query embedding `h_q`.
2.  **Item Tower Branches:** CLIP text encoder (title `t` → `h_t`) and CLIP image encoder (image `v` → `h_v`).
3.  **Fusion Module:** Gating network (predicts weight α) → linear combination `h_f = α h_t + (1-α) h_v`.
4.  **Cross-Modal Interaction:** Multi-head bilinear projections (`W(k)_t`, `W(k)_i`) → element-wise products → concatenation → MLP → residual + LayerNorm → final item embedding `h_x`.
5.  **Scoring:** Cosine similarity `s(q,x)=cos(h_q, h_x)`.
6.  **Training Loss:** Two three-part hinge losses (engagement, relevance) weighted (`λ_eng`, `λ_rel`), with self-adversarial negative sampling.

### Critical Path
1.  Initialize both towers with pretrained CLIP weights.
2.  **Stage I:** Fine-tune *both* CLIP encoders on title–image contrastive loss (domain adaptation).
3.  **Stage II:** Freeze encoders? (Implied by "alignment" focus; paper doesn't specify but ablations suggest encoders continue learning). Train with query vs. `h_t` and query vs. `h_v` losses separately (modality-specific alignment).
4.  **Stage III:** Activate fusion (MoE + Bilinear) and interaction. Train with query vs. `h_x` loss (multimodal alignment).
5.  Throughout: Use self-adversarial negatives from current batch, compute dual hinge losses, backpropagate `L_total`.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **MoE+Bilinear vs. MoE alone:** Bilinear adds ~K×`d_model` parameters and computation (projection heads + MLP) for interaction. Tradeoff: increased expressiveness for cross-modal cues vs. slight latency/inference cost. Paper shows consistent metric gains (Table 3), suggesting favorable tradeoff for e-commerce where nuanced matching matters.
- **Two-stage alignment vs. end-to-end fusion from start:** Staging requires managing multiple training phases and potential data loading complexity. Tradeoff: more stable/effective representation learning vs. simpler but riskier single-phase training. Ablations (Table 2) strongly favor staging.
- **Three-part hinge loss vs. standard cross-entropy:** Hinge loss with ordered margins (`ϵ+ > ϵ- > ϵ0`) explicitly models graded engagement/relevance. Tradeoff: more hyperparameters (margins, exponent `m`) to tune, but better exploitation of ordinal supervision.

### Failure Signatures
- **Gating collapse:** MoE weight α consistently near 0 or 1 for all instances, indicating the gating network failed to learn modality importance, rendering fusion ineffective.
- **Bilinear inactivity:** Outputs of the bilinear interaction heads (`W(k)_t h_t ⊙ W(k)_i h_i`) have near-zero variance or norm across batch, suggesting the projection heads did not learn meaningful cross-modal interactions.
- **Convergence to text-only bias:** Final `h_x` is dominated by `h_t` embeddings (average α >> 0.5) even in visually distinctive categories, or relevance metrics improve minimally over text-only baseline (<1%), indicating visual signals are not being integrated.
- **Stage interference:** Performance drops when moving from Stage II to Stage III if fusion is introduced, suggesting destructive interference rather than complementary learning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the proposed multimodal retrieval framework be extended to support image-based search queries, where the user query is an image rather than text, while maintaining the efficiency of two-tower retrieval?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "Future work includes extending the framework to support image-based search by incorporating visual encoders into the query tower, enabling unified text and image queries within the same retrieval framework."
- **Why unresolved:** The current model only processes text queries; incorporating image queries requires designing a query-side visual encoder and aligning it with the item tower, which introduces new challenges in representation learning and scalability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Developing a variant where the query tower uses a visual encoder (e.g., CLIP image encoder) and training on a dataset with image queries and relevance labels; evaluating retrieval performance (nDCG) and latency compared to the text-only query model.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the proposed MoE+Bilinear fusion approach compare, in terms of both retrieval effectiveness and computational efficiency, to recent domain-specific multimodal retrieval methods such as VL-CLIP, EI-CLIP, and FashionKLIP on large-scale e-commerce benchmarks?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper compares its fusion module only to simple baselines (MLP, attention, etc.) and does not include comparisons to these more specialized methods that address e-commerce-specific challenges like entity-aware modeling or external knowledge integration. This omission leaves unclear whether the gains are due to the fusion design or simply from CLIP fine-tuning.
- **Why unresolved:** Without direct comparisons, it is difficult to assess the true contribution of the proposed fusion relative to the state-of-the-art in e-commerce multimodal retrieval. The domain-specific methods might offer better accuracy or efficiency trade-offs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Reimplementing or obtaining implementations of VL-CLIP, EI-CLIP, and FashionKLIP, training them on the same e-commerce dataset (or a standard benchmark), and measuring retrieval metrics (nDCG@K) along with computational costs (training time, inference latency, memory usage) for indexing and querying at scale.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the optimal trade-off between optimizing for user engagement signals (e.g., clicks, purchases) and semantic relevance in the multi-objective training framework, and does this optimal weighting vary across product categories or query types?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors fix λeng=0.7 and λrel=0.3 without any systematic exploration or justification. The relative importance of engagement versus relevance may depend on the category (e.g., fashion vs electronics) or query specificity.
- **Why unresolved:** A fixed weighting may not generalize well; some categories might benefit from more relevance supervision, while others might require more engagement signals. The current model may be suboptimal in certain segments.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting experiments with a range of weight combinations (e.g., λeng from 0.1 to 0.9) and evaluating per-category performance on both desirability and relevance metrics. Additionally, analyzing whether a dynamic weighting scheme (e.g., learned per category) improves overall results.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Is the proposed three-stage curriculum training strategy (domain adaptation -> modality-specific alignment -> fusion alignment) necessary for achieving the reported performance gains, or could an end-to-end training approach that jointly optimizes all components yield comparable or better results with reduced training complexity?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors adopt curriculum training based on the intuition that it helps the model gradually acquire multimodal relevance patterns, but they do not provide an ablation study comparing it to alternative training schedules (e.g., starting directly with multimodal fusion alignment, or random order of stages). The paper does not isolate the contribution of the curriculum itself.
- **Why unresolved:** Curriculum training increases training time and pipeline complexity. If similar performance can be achieved with simpler end-to-end training, it would simplify deployment and potentially allow for better convergence. Moreover, the relative importance of each stage is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Training the same model architecture without curriculum (e.g., initializing from pretrained CLIP and directly training with multimodal fusion and full objective) and comparing retrieval performance, convergence speed, and robustness to hyperparameter changes. Additionally, experimenting with different stage orders or intermediate checkpointing.

## Limitations
- **Domain generalizability:** The evaluation is restricted to a single e-commerce domain; it remains unclear how well the model adapts to new product categories, rare items, or queries requiring complex multimodal reasoning.
- **Efficiency trade-offs:** The bilinear interaction and MoE gating introduce additional computational overhead; the paper does not analyze inference latency or memory usage at scale, which are critical for production systems.
- **Incomplete comparative evaluation:** The model is not benchmarked against recent e-commerce-specific multimodal retrieval methods (e.g., VL-CLIP, EI-CLIP), leaving the relative contribution of the proposed fusion design uncertain.

## Confidence
- Domain Adaptation Necessity: High
- Staged Alignment Efficacy: High
- MoE+Bilinear Superiority: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Encoder Role Verification:** Confirm the Query Tower uses the CLIP *text* encoder and the Item Tower uses both CLIP *text* (for titles) and *image* encoders. Validate that similarity scoring (`cos(h_q, h_x)`) is between the query embedding and the *fused* item embedding `h_x`.
2. **Bilinear Interaction Sanity:** On a small trained checkpoint, extract the output norms of each bilinear head (`W(k)_t h_t ⊙ W(k)_i h_i`). Check for non-zero variance across the batch to confirm the layer is active and learning cross-modal products, not passing through zero gradients.
3. **Modality Bias Test:** Compute the average gating weight α over a held-out set, stratified by product categories (e.g., "fashion" vs. "electronics"). A consistent α ≫ 0.5 across all categories would indicate the model collapsed to text-only reliance, contradicting the claimed visual benefit.
