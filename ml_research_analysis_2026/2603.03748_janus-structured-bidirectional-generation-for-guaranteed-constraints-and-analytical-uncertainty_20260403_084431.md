# JANUS: Structured Bidirectional Generation for Guaranteed Constraints and Analytical Uncertainty

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03748
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03748
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: JANUS achieves state-of-the-art fidelity (Detection Score 0.497) with 100% constraint satisfaction and 128× faster uncertainty estimation than Monte Carlo methods.

## Executive Summary
JANUS introduces a framework for high-stakes synthetic tabular data generation that simultaneously attains fidelity, constraint satisfaction, reliable uncertainty, and efficiency—addressing the Quadrilemma. It uses a causal DAG of Bayesian Decision Trees with two key innovations: Reverse-Topological Back-filling for guaranteed constraint propagation without rejection sampling, and Analytical Uncertainty Decomposition via Dirichlet priors for closed-form aleatoric/epistemic separation. Evaluated on 15 datasets and 523 constrained scenarios, JANUS outperforms baselines in fidelity and mode collapse while handling complex inter-column constraints and providing fast uncertainty estimates.

## Method Summary
JANUS learns a causal DAG over the data variables. For each node, a Bayesian Decision Tree is trained using a hybrid splitting criterion that combines supervised loss (predicting the child) with an unsupervised term (organizing parent features) to enable bidirectional sampling. Trees store at each leaf a Dirichlet posterior for forward conditional probabilities and backward histograms for inverse probabilities. Constrained generation follows a two-phase algorithm: first, constraints are propagated backward through the DAG by intersecting backward histograms of affected children; second, forward top-down sampling uses masked distributions to ensure all constraints are met. Uncertainty is computed analytically from the Dirichlet parameters via digamma functions, separating aleatoric and epistemic components without Monte Carlo sampling.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art fidelity: Detection Score 0.497 and strong correlation preservation across 15 datasets.
- 100% Constraint Satisfaction Rate on feasible constraint sets, handling complex inter-column constraints where baselines fail.
- Fast analytical uncertainty estimation 128× faster than Monte Carlo methods, eliminating mode collapse (MCS 0.946).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Reverse-Topological Back-filling
- **Claim**: Achieves 100% constraint satisfaction without rejection sampling by propagating constraints backward through the causal DAG using stored parent histograms.
- **Mechanism**: For a constrained child node, the algorithm identifies tree leaves predicting valid output ranges (Line 10 of Algorithm 1) and then samples parent values exclusively from the backward histograms `H[l][p]` within those leaves (Line 11). For multiple constrained children, it computes the elementwise product of individual child histograms to find the joint valid domain.
- **Core assumption**: Joint feasibility (Assumption 1)—the constraint set has at least one valid configuration within the observed data distribution, and for any parent with multiple constrained children, the intersection of their valid parent domains is non-empty.
- **Break condition**: If the histogram intersection `H_joint` is empty due to mutually exclusive constraints, generation fails.

### Mechanism 2: Analytical Uncertainty Decomposition
- **Claim**: Provides closed-form, 128× faster separation of aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty via Dirichlet-Multinomial conjugacy.
- **Mechanism**: Each leaf stores Dirichlet posteriors `α` from observed counts. For a leaf with total concentration `S = Σ α_k`, aleatoric uncertainty is computed via Equation (4) using digamma functions `ψ`. Epistemic uncertainty is the residual `H_total - H_aleatoric` (Equation 5). This leverages the fact that the Dirichlet posterior's entropy decomposes cleanly under multinomial sampling.
- **Core assumption**: Discretized data follows a multinomial distribution with a Dirichlet prior; uncertainty is captured at the leaf level by posterior counts.
- **Break condition**: Coarse binning (small `K`) may poorly approximate continuous distributions, distorting uncertainty estimates. The decomposition does not cover structural uncertainty about the DAG.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid Splitting Criterion & Dual Storage
- **Claim**: Enables bidirectional sampling (`P(Y|X)` and `P(X|Y)`) essential for back-filling by forcing trees to learn structure in both output and input feature spaces, even for pure output nodes.
- **Mechanism**: The splitting objective `S_split` (Equation 2) combines a supervised term `log P(Y|split)` with an unsupervised term `λ_unsup * log P(X|split)` and a diversity term. The unsupervised term splits pure output nodes if organizing the parent feature distribution `X` improves, creating finer-grained leaves with concentrated backward histograms `H[l][p]`. Leaves store both forward Dirichlet `α` and backward histograms `H`.
- **Core assumption**: Even when `Y` is pure, the feature distribution `X` within that node has meaningful structure worth partitioning for reverse sampling.
- **Break condition**: If parent features `X` are statistically independent of `Y` *and* have a uniform distribution within any `Y`-slice, the unsupervised term cannot find beneficial splits, leading to diffuse histograms that may fail constraint propagation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bayesian Decision Trees & Dirichlet-Multinomial Conjugacy**  
  *Why needed*: Trees provide the DAG factorization; leaves store Dirichlet posteriors for closed-form uncertainty and fast set operations.  
  *Quick check*: How does a Dirichlet posterior at a leaf allow computing both a predictive distribution `P(X|parents)` and its uncertainty in closed form?

- **Causal DAGs & Topological Ordering**  
  *Why needed*: The back-filling algorithm requires a DAG to define parent-child relationships and a reverse topological order to propagate constraints from effects to causes.  
  *Quick check*: Why must the constraint propagation pass go in reverse topological order, while the final sampling pass goes forward?

- **Discretization via Quantile Binning**  
  *Why needed*: Transforms continuous constraints into fast, deterministic bin intersections. Without discretization, continuous range constraints would need numerical optimization or rejection sampling.  
  *Quick check*: What is the trade-off introduced by global quantile binning with a fixed `K` (e.g., 50 bins) for features with heavy-tailed distributions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Structure Learner -> Discretizer -> Tree Builder -> Constraint Engine -> Back-filling Sampler -> Uncertainty Calculator. Optionally, the Uncertainty Calculator can be used after generation.
- **Critical path**: For constrained generation: Structure Learning -> Discretization -> Tree Building -> Back-filling Algorithm (Reverse pass then Forward pass) -> Output. For uncertainty estimation: Tree Building -> Uncertainty Calculator.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Discretization granularity `K`*: Higher `K` preserves continuous fidelity but slows bin intersections and risks sparse leaves; lower `K` speeds computation but may blur constraints.  
  - *Tree complexity*: Controlled by `min_samples_leaf` and `λ_unsup`. Deeper trees capture complex conditionals but risk overfitting; shallower trees generalize but may fail back-filling if histograms are diffuse.  
  - *Structure learning*: Exact causal discovery (PC/GES) is slower but needed for valid interventions; Random Forest is faster and sufficient for conditional generation.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - *Empty histogram intersection*: Indicates infeasible constraints. Check feasibility with a pre-sampler.  
  - *High epistemic uncertainty everywhere*: Suggests insufficient data or too deep trees. Reduce `max_depth` or increase `min_samples_leaf`.  
  - *Poor correlation preservation*: Likely due to excessive discretization (`K` too low) or weak unsupervised splitting (`λ_unsup=0`).  
  - *Slow generation*: Many constrained nodes with complex dependencies can increase histogram intersection cost.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Constraint Satisfaction Smoke Test: Generate 100 samples with a simple range constraint; verify 100% CSR and binning compliance.  
  2. Uncertainty Decomposition Validation: Train on dataset with artificially flipped labels; verify epistemic uncertainty increases in noisy/sparse regions while aleatoric remains stable.  
  3. Baseline Fidelity Comparison: Run unconditional generation on one dataset (e.g., Adult); compare Detection Score and Feature Correlation against TabDDPM/CTGAN.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Under what specific conditions does the greedy domain intersection in Reverse-Topological Back-filling fail to find feasible solutions for complex inter-column constraints, even when a joint feasible configuration exists in the data?
2. How does global quantile discretization with a fixed number of bins (K=50) impact the fidelity of synthetic data generation for features with heavy-tailed distributions or extremely high cardinality categorical variables?
3. Can JANUS's analytical uncertainty decomposition be extended to provide per-sample prediction-level uncertainty (e.g., confidence in a generated row) alongside the current data-level uncertainty, and how would this impact high-stakes decision-making?
4. How can JANUS replace global discretization with continuous density estimators (e.g., Gaussian Processes or normalizing flows in tree leaves) while preserving the closed-form analytical uncertainty decomposition and constraint satisfaction guarantees?

## Limitations
- **Dirichlet prior hyperparameter** (`α_prior`): Value unspecified; default assumptions may affect uncertainty calibration.
- **Tree termination logic**: Exact conditions beyond `min_samples_leaf` are not detailed, impacting model capacity and back-filling success.
- **Discretization granularity trade-off**: Fixed `K=50` may blur fine-grained constraints in heavy-tailed features or inflate epistemic uncertainty in sparse bins.
- **Scalability to very high-dimensional DAGs**: Histogram intersection complexity in back-filling is not analyzed for worst-case scenarios.
- **Uncertainty scope**: Decomposition captures leaf-level data uncertainty, not structural uncertainty about the learned DAG itself.

## Confidence
- **High**: Mechanism 1 (Back-filling) and Mechanism 3 (Hybrid Splitting) empirical claims (100% CSR on feasible sets, ablation results).
- **Medium**: Mechanism 2 (Uncertainty Decomposition) closed-form speedup (128×) and separation validity; theoretical derivations rely on discretization assumptions.
- **Medium**: Generalizability to unseen constraint types beyond those evaluated (e.g., non-linear, multi-node cycles not in DAG).
- **Low**: Claims about "guaranteed constraints" — depends critically on Assumption 1 (joint feasibility) being met by the user's constraint set and data distribution.
