# GeM-VG: Towards Generalized Multi-image Visual Grounding with Multimodal Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04777
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04777
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: Achieves state‑of‑the‑art on multi‑image grounding with +2.0% MIG‑Bench, +9.7% MC‑Bench, +9.1% ODINW over Qwen2‑VL‑7B, while preserving general multi‑image understanding.

## Executive Summary
GeM‑VG addresses the limited scope of current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in generalized multi‑image visual grounding, which can only handle single‑target localization and narrow task varieties. The authors propose a systematic taxonomy for multi‑image grounding tasks and construct MG‑Data‑240K, a dataset with 240K samples covering multiple target instances and rich cross‑image relations (semantic, temporal, spatial). A hybrid reinforcement finetuning strategy combines chain‑of‑thought and direct answering, using a multi‑metric rule‑based reward and dynamic reward adjustment to balance reasoning modes throughout training. Built on Qwen2‑VL‑7B, GeM‑VG yields substantial absolute improvements on three key benchmarks (MIG‑Bench, MC‑Bench, ODINW) and retains strong performance on general multi‑image understanding tasks.

## Method Summary
GeM‑VG employs a three‑stage training pipeline on top of the Qwen2‑VL‑7B model. Stage 1 performs supervised fine‑tuning with short answers using a mixture of multi‑image grounding data (including the newly created MG‑Data‑240K) and multi‑image understanding samples. Stage 2 introduces chain‑of‑thought (CoT) traces by mixing in CoT and direct‑answer data, teaching the model to output either style. Stage 3 applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a hybrid reward that penalizes invalid format, checks image selection, and evaluates localization precision and recall. A reward adapter dynamically balances CoT and direct modes: early training encourages a 0.5 ratio, while later stages add a length bonus to inaccurate CoT outputs. The reward components are summed to produce a scalar score, and advantages are computed within groups of eight sampled completions per prompt.

## Key Results
- Absolute improvement of 2.0% on MIG‑Bench, 9.7% on MC‑Bench, and 9.1% on ODINW compared to the base Qwen2‑VL‑7B model.
- State‑of‑the‑art performance across all three benchmarks; notably, MC‑Bench APref50 increases from 27.0% to 36.7%.
- Maintains robust general multi‑image understanding scores on MuirBench and BLINK, indicating no catastrophic forgetting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Comprehensive task coverage:** The systematic taxonomy and MG‑Data‑240K provide diverse multi‑image grounding scenarios, including multiple targets and explicit semantic, temporal, and spatial relations. This unified training distribution mirrors real‑world complexity, enabling the model to learn both fine‑grained perception and cross‑image reasoning.

**Balanced reasoning modes:** The hybrid reinforcement finetuning with a multi‑metric reward and dynamic adjustment prevents collapse to a single output style. Early stage balancing encourages exploration of both CoT and direct answers, while later stage length bonuses promote longer reasoning when initial attempts fail. This yields robustness across perception‑heavy and reasoning‑intensive tasks.

**Progressive learning scaffold:** The three‑stage pipeline first establishes basic localization with short‑answer SFT, then introduces reasoning structure via CoT‑based SFT, and finally refines with GRPO using multimodal feedback. This ordering builds foundational skills before the harder RL objective, reducing catastrophic forgetting and stabilizing exploration.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Vision encoder (Qwen2‑VL visual backbone) → Projection layer → LLM (Qwen2‑VL language model). Additional components: Reward function (format, image, precision, recall), Reward adapter (dynamic adjustment), GRPO trainer (group sampling, advantage calculation, KL‑penalized update).

**Critical path:**
1. Receive input: a sequence of images V = {V₁,…,Vₘ} and instruction T.
2. Encode each image into visual tokens H_v.
3. Concatenate H_v with text tokens H_ins and feed to the LLM.
4. LLM generates completion o, optionally containing a `<think>` block.
5. Parser extracts bounding boxes and associated image indices from o.
6. Compute reward components (format, image‑level, precision, recall) and apply adapter adjustments.
7. For a group of N=8 completions per prompt, compute normalized advantages.
8. Perform policy update via the GRPO objective with KL penalty.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- CoT vs direct answer: CoT aids reasoning but may blur fine localization; direct answers are precise but lack interpretability.
- Reward equal weighting: The sum of four components may implicitly equal‑weight them; risk of over‑ or under‑emphasizing one aspect.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: Dynamic adjustment parameters (α, θ, γ, δ) require careful tuning; too strong adjustment overrides accuracy signals, too weak fails to balance modes.

**Failure signatures:**
- Mode collapse early in RL (all direct answers) if early balancing disabled.
- Low recall on dense or multi‑instance scenarios when recall reward is omitted.
- Format errors leading to zero reward if regex does not match.
- Deficiencies on cross‑image subtasks (OT, MV, Co‑Re) due to insufficient relation coverage.
- Training instability using excessively high α (as shown in ablations).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Evaluate Stage 1 SFT on a single‑image grounding benchmark (e.g., ODINW subset) to verify retained perceptual ability.
2. Ablate the recall reward component and test on MC‑Bench; expect drop in recall‑heavy metrics.
3. Monitor the CoT/direct answer ratio during early RL; confirm that with balancing the ratio hovers near 0.5 and final performance exceeds the no‑balancing baseline.

## Limitations
- Key implementation details for GRPO (KL coefficient β, reference model update rule) and stage transition thresholds are omitted, making exact replication difficult.
- The dataset’s focus on predefined semantic/temporal/spatial relations may not encompass real‑world scenarios such as causal reasoning, possibly limiting broader generalization.
- The dynamic reward adjustment is sensitive to hyperparameters; mis‑configuration can cause mode collapse or reward hacking.

## Confidence
- Dataset taxonomy & MG‑Data‑240K coverage: Medium
- Hybrid RL finetuning with reward balancing: Medium‑High
- Progressive three‑stage training pipeline: Medium
- Overall SOTA claims across benchmarks: High

## Next Checks
1. Ablate reward adaptation: Remove early‑stage balancing and vary α; measure the frequency of mode collapse and its impact on reasoning‑heavy MIG‑Bench subtasks (OT, MV, Co‑Re).
2. Generalization to novel relations: Create a held‑out set requiring part‑whole, functional, or causal reasoning across images; evaluate zero‑shot accuracy to assess transfer beyond the original taxonomy.
3. Data scaling & bias analysis: Train on 50% and 100% random subsets of MG‑Data‑240K to identify saturation points; also analyze per‑category representation statistics and correlate failures on MC‑Bench splits with under‑represented object types.
