# Optimize Wider, Not Deeper: Consensus Aggregation for Policy Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.12596
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12596
- **Reference count**: 12
- **Primary result**: CAPO outperforms PPO and compute-matched deeper baselines by up to 8.6× on Gymnasium continuous control by redirecting optimization compute from depth (epochs) to width (parallel replicas) and aggregating via consensus, reducing KL waste by 2–46% with 19–72% wall-clock overhead.

## Executive Summary
PPO’s standard use of multiple epochs on a fixed batch accumulates Fisher-orthogonal “waste” that consumes the trust region budget without improving the surrogate objective, creating an optimization-depth dilemma. CAPO addresses this by replacing sequential depth with parallel width: it runs K independent PPO replicas on the same batch with different minibatch orders, then aggregates their updates into a consensus policy via parameter averaging or logarithmic opinion pool (natural parameter averaging). The consensus provably reduces waste while preserving learning signal. On six Gymnasium continuous control tasks, CAPO achieves higher returns on five tasks—up to 8.6× improvement over PPO—and reduces waste by 2–17% (parameter averaging) or up to 46% (LogOP on Humanoid), with modest additional compute cost (19–72% wall-time). Results empirically confirm that widening optimization beats deepening.

## Method Summary
CAPO modifies the standard PPO loop. First, a batch of transitions is collected using the current policy πₜ and advantages are computed. Then, instead of performing E epochs of minibatch SGD on this single copy, CAPO initializes K independent PPO experts from πₜ (and optionally the incumbent optimizer state). Each expert performs E epochs of updates on the same batch but with a unique random minibatch shuffle. After all experts complete, their policies are aggregated: for CAPO-Avg, parameters are averaged (θₜ₊₁ = θₜ + (1/K)∑(θₖ−θₜ)); for CAPO-LogOP, natural parameters are averaged and then distilled into a new policy via KL minimization. The value function is trained once per generation on the shared batch. The key innovation is the consensus aggregation step, which cancels waste across experts while preserving the shared natural gradient signal.

## Key Results
- CAPO (both variants) outperforms PPO and compute-matched deeper PPO (higher E) on five of six Gymnasium continuous control tasks, with up to 8.6× higher returns.
- Waste reduction: CAPO-Avg reduces Fisher-orthogonal waste by 2–17%; CAPO-LogOP reduces waste by up to 46% on Humanoid (though it can amplify waste on very low-dimensional tasks).
- Compute overhead: Wall-clock time increases by 19–72% depending on K and task, deemed acceptable for the performance gains.
- Width vs. depth: Increasing K (width) consistently improves or maintains performance, while increasing E (depth) past ~10 degrades returns due to waste accumulation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: PPO’s Epochs Accumulate Waste
**Claim**: PPO’s multiple epochs on a fixed batch accumulate Fisher-orthogonal waste that consumes the trust region budget without improving the surrogate objective.
**Mechanism**: Each minibatch SGD step decomposes the update into a Fisher-projected signal (the natural gradient direction) and a Fisher-orthogonal residual (waste). Sequential epochs on the same batch cause waste to accumulate monotonically while the signal saturates after a few epochs.
**Core assumption**: The Fisher information matrix accurately approximates local trust region geometry; PPO’s clipping is a noisy proxy for the KL constraint.
**Evidence anchors**: Proposition 1 formalizes the decomposition; Table 1 shows waste KL grows ~11× from E=2 to 40 while signal KL plateaus.

### Mechanism 2: Parallel Experts Cancel Independent Waste
**Claim**: Running parallel PPO replicas on the same batch with different minibatch orders produces independent waste vectors that partially cancel under averaging, while the shared signal aligns and is preserved.
**Mechanism**: Each expert’s update ∆ₖ = cₖ·d̂ + ϵₖ contains the same projected signal direction d̂ (natural gradient) but different waste ϵₖ due to random minibatch sequencing. Averaging updates yields ¯∆ = ¯c·d̂ + ¯ϵ, where the variance of ϵₖ is large relative to its mean, so ∥¯ϵ∥₂ is much smaller than the mean ∥ϵₖ∥₂.
**Core assumption**: Waste vectors across experts are approximately independent and zero-mean in the Fisher-orthogonal subspace; signal vectors are highly correlated.
**Evidence anchors**: Table 5 shows waste correlation ρ_ϵ ∈ [0.77, 0.97] (partial independence), while signal correlation ρ_c > 0.99.

### Mechanism 3: LogOP Consensus Provably Improves KL-Penalized Surrogate
**Claim**: Aggregating experts in natural parameter space via the logarithmic opinion pool (LogOP) yields a consensus policy that provably achieves a higher KL-penalized surrogate and tighter trust region compliance than any individual expert.
**Mechanism**: For exponential family policies (e.g., Gaussians), LogOP averages natural parameters η̄ = (1/K)∑ηₖ. Theorem 2 proves this consensus maximizes a lower bound on the KL-penalized surrogate and satisfies the trust region constraint if all experts do. For Gaussians, this corresponds to precision-weighted averaging of means.
**Core assumption**: The policy belongs to a minimal exponential family; the surrogate is linearized around the incumbent.
**Evidence anchors**: Theorem 2 and Corollary 3 provide formal guarantees; Section 5.2 shows CAPO-LogOP outperforms CAPO-Avg on high-dimensional tasks (e.g., Humanoid), where precision-weighting amplifies benefits.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fisher information geometry and natural gradients**: Needed to decompose PPO updates into signal (Fisher-projected natural gradient) and waste (orthogonal residual); defines the trust region geometry. *Quick check*: Why is the natural gradient direction (F⁻¹g) the steepest ascent direction per unit KL cost?
- **Trust region policy optimization (TRPO/PPO)**: Provides the constrained optimization framing; PPO’s clipped surrogate approximates the KL constraint, leading to the optimization-depth dilemma. *Quick check*: How does PPO’s clipping differ from TRPO’s exact KL constraint, and why does this lead to waste accumulation?
- **Exponential families and natural parameters**: Enables closed-form LogOP aggregation and precision weighting; the consensus theorem holds exactly in this space. *Quick check*: For a Gaussian policy, what are the natural parameters, and how does averaging them differ from averaging means and variances?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Batch collector -> Parallel experts (K PPO replicas) -> Aggregator (parameter avg or LogOP) -> Value function trainer -> Next generation.
- **Critical path**: Data collection → parallel expert updates → consensus aggregation → value update → next generation. The consensus step is the pivotal innovation; failing to aggregate wastes the parallel compute.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Width vs. depth*: Increasing K (width) consistently improves or maintains performance; increasing E (depth) past ~10 degrades returns (Fig. 4). CAPO trades sequential epochs for parallel replicas.
  - *Parameter vs. natural parameter averaging*: LogOP excels on high-dimensional tasks (e.g., Humanoid +8.6×) via precision-weighting; parameter averaging is simpler and robust on low-dimensional tasks (e.g., Hopper). LogOP adds distillation cost.
  - *Optimizer state carry*: Carrying incumbent Adam state (“incumbent” mode) is default; “reset” is comparable; “best-expert” transfer can help parameter averaging.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - *Catastrophic collapse*: If K=1 (reverts to PPO) and E>10, waste dominates and returns drop (Table 1).
  - *LogOP distillation failure*: If neural network capacity is too low, KL(q∥πₜ₊₁) may not approximate q well, breaking the theoretical guarantee; monitor surrogate drop after distillation.
  - *Poor warmup*: With 0% warmup, early generations may start from a low-quality incumbent, slowing convergence; Table 12 suggests 5–20% warmup is task-dependent.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Waste reduction validation**: Run PPO with E=10, compute Fisher decomposition of final update. Then run CAPO-Avg (K=4) and verify ∥ϵ¯∥₂² / (mean ∥ϵₖ∥₂²) < 1 (Table 6). Confirms waste cancellation.
  2. **Width scaling ablation**: Fix E=10, vary K∈{1,2,4,8,16} for CAPO and CAPO-Avg. Expect monotonic improvement or plateau; degradation indicates over-aggregation (Section C.2).
  3. **LogOP vs. parameter averaging**: On Humanoid (high dₐ), LogOP should significantly outperform CAPO-Avg; on Hopper (low dₐ), they should be comparable (Table 4). If LogOP underperforms, check precision-weighted mean implementation and distillation step.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How to generate CAPO experts with diversity that preserves Theorem 2’s guarantees?** Theorem 2 requires experts optimize the same surrogate; introducing heterogeneity (e.g., diverse GAE λ) breaks linearity. Unresolved: theoretical extension for heterogeneous objectives; empirical validation of KL-penalized surrogate improvement.
- **Does CAPO’s waste reduction benefit apply to discrete action spaces?** Theory covers exponential families, but discrete case untested. Unresolved: implementation on discrete tasks (e.g., Atari); measurement of waste reduction and returns vs PPO.
- **What determines whether LogOP reduces or amplifies waste across tasks?** Table 6 shows LogOP reduces waste 46% on Humanoid (376 params) but amplifies 5–10× on ≤17-param tasks. Unresolved: no model explains dimensionality dependence. Needed: derive expected waste reduction under LogOP as function of expert covariance and dimension; controlled experiments varying action space size.

## Limitations
- Relies on Fisher geometry approximations and linearized trust region; may not hold for complex neural policies.
- Waste cancellation assumes independent zero-mean waste; correlations in Table 5 (0.77–0.97) indicate partial dependence, potentially limiting gains.
- LogOP requires exact exponential family policies; distillation step introduces approximation error that could violate the surrogate guarantee if network capacity is insufficient.
- Evaluation limited to six continuous control tasks; unknown for image-based, discrete-action, or real-world domains.
- Wall-clock overhead of 19–72% may scale poorly; no analysis beyond K=16.
- Low citation count suggests limited independent verification.
