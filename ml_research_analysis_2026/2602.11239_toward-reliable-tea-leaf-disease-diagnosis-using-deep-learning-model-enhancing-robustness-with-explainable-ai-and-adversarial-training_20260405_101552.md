# Toward Reliable Tea Leaf Disease Diagnosis Using Deep Learning Model: Enhancing Robustness With Explainable AI and Adversarial Training
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11239
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11239
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** EfficientNetB3 achieves 93% classification accuracy on the teaLeafBD dataset (7 classes, 5,278 images).

## Executive Summary
This study develops a deep learning system for automated tea leaf disease classification, addressing reliability through robustness and interpretability. Using the teaLeafBD dataset (5,278 images, 7 classes), the pipeline combines data augmentation, adversarial training, and Grad-CAM explainability. EfficientNetB3 outperforms DenseNet201, reaching 93% accuracy. Adversarial training at ε=0.12 improves validation accuracy (84.83%) and speeds convergence, while Grad-CAM visualizations confirm the model focuses on relevant diseased regions. The approach offers a practical, interpretable solution for agricultural disease management.

## Method Summary
The task is multi-class classification of tea leaf diseases. The teaLeafBD dataset (5,278 RGB images, resized to 224x224) is split 70% train, 20% validation, 10% test. Two pre-trained models—DenseNet201 and EfficientNetB3—are fine-tuned using Adam (lr=1e-4, batch=32) with categorical cross-entropy loss and early stopping. Data augmentation (flipping, rotation, scaling) is applied only to training data. Adversarial training integrates perturbed examples during training, testing ε values from 0 to 0.2. Model interpretability is provided via Grad-CAM heatmaps overlaid on input images.

## Key Results
- EfficientNetB3 achieves 93% test accuracy, surpassing DenseNet201 (91%).
- Adversarial training with ε=0.12 boosts validation accuracy to 84.83% and accelerates convergence.
- Grad-CAM visualizations validate that the model attends to diseased leaf regions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Adversarial training improves robustness by exposing the model to noisy inputs, forcing it to learn more resilient features. Grad-CAM enhances interpretability by generating coarse localization maps that highlight image regions influencing predictions, building user trust. The combination addresses both technical performance and practical deployability in real-world agricultural settings where noise and model transparency are critical.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input Image -> Resize to 224x224 -> Augmentation (Train Only) -> DenseNet201/EfficientNetB3 -> Softmax Output -> Grad-CAM Visualization. Critical path: Preprocessed image flows through the convolutional backbone to final classification, with Grad-CAM applied post-hoc using gradients from the target layer. Design tradeoffs: EfficientNetB3 prioritizes accuracy but may be computationally heavier; adversarial training improves robustness at potential cost to clean accuracy. Failure signatures: Excessive train-validation accuracy gap (overfitting), poor minority-class F1-scores (imbalance), sharp accuracy drops under ε>0 (fragile robustness). First three experiments: (1) Train without augmentation to quantify overfitting, (2) Introduce adversarial training at ε=0.12 and measure validation gains, (3) Compare both architectures on clean and adversarially perturbed validation sets.
