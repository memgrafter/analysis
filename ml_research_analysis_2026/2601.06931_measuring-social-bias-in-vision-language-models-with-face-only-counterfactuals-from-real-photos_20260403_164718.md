# Measuring Social Bias in Vision-Language Models with Face-Only Counterfactuals from Real Photos

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06931  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06931  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Face-only counterfactuals reveal persistent demographic bias in VLMs, with task design critically shaping bias expression.

## Executive Summary
Visual confounding in real photographs impedes causal attribution of demographic bias in vision-language models (VLMs). This paper introduces **FOCUS**, a dataset of 480 face-only counterfactual images derived from real photos, where only race and gender are edited while all other scene factors (background, clothing, pose, lighting) remain fixed. Using the **REFLECT** benchmark—comprising pairwise comparisons (2AFC), multiple-choice questions (MCQ), and continuous salary recommendation—the authors evaluate five VLMs (GPT-5, Gemini, Qwen, DeepSeek, Llama). Bias persists even under strict visual control, and its magnitude and direction vary substantially across task formulations. The study concludes that counterfactual evaluation with face-only edits is essential for unbiased auditing, and that task design fundamentally influences measured bias.

## Method Summary
**FOCUS construction:** 48 high-quality source photos (8 per occupation: CEO, doctor, cook, nurse, teacher, lawyer) are edited into 10 race–gender variants (5 races × 2 genders) using a unified prompt with `gemini-3-pro-image-preview`. This yields 480 images where non-facial elements are preserved. Quality control employs MediaPipe face masks to verify edit localization (mean pixel diff_face=0.141 vs. diff_nonface=0.022) and GPT-4o for demographic label accuracy (97.9%).  

**REFLECT tasks:**  
1. **2AFC:** Paired images from the same source photo; model chooses A/B based on scenario (Income, Education, Perceived Safety).  
2. **MCQ:** Single image; model selects salary bracket (A–F) or education level (A–D).  
3. **Salary Recommendation:** Image + standardized biography; model outputs integer USD salary.  

Models are queried with deterministic decoding (temperature=0) and strict output parsing; invalid responses are discarded. Metrics include win rates (2AFC), mean gaps and Jensen–Shannon divergence (MCQ), and salary gaps per occupation (recommendation).

## Key Results
- Bias persisted across all five VLMs despite perfect visual control of non-facial context.
- Task formulation dramatically influenced bias: in 2AFC, gender preference reversed by scenario (Income: male-favoring; Safety: female-favoring), while MCQ showed stable male and White advantages in salary.
- Racial hierarchies (White-high, Black-low) were pronounced in 2AFC win rates but varied in other tasks; CEO occupation amplified racial disparities in salary recommendation.
- FOCUS yielded lower group-wise Jensen–Shannon divergence than the uncontrolled VisBias benchmark, confirming reduced spurious context bias.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Face-only counterfactual editing isolates causal effects.** By editing only facial attributes (skin tone, features) while preserving background, clothing, pose, and lighting, the method creates within-photo variants where the sole systematic difference is the face's demographic presentation. This allows attribution of output disparities to facial cues rather than spurious correlations. Evidence includes the editing prompt specification, quality control showing edits concentrated on the face (mean diff_face=0.141 vs diff_nonface=0.022), and high label accuracy. The approach breaks if edits inadvertently alter non-facial regions or perceived age.

**Mechanism 2: Task formulation shapes bias expression.** Different task structures (comparative 2AFC, categorical MCQ, numeric salary recommendation) engage distinct reasoning pathways in VLMs, revealing that bias signals are not monolithic but modulated by prompt structure and output format. For instance, gender preference flips between Income (male-favoring) and Safety (female-favoring) in 2AFC, while MCQ shows consistent male/White advantages. This variability is the evidence; if all tasks showed consistent ordering, the mechanism would be falsified.

**Mechanism 3: Real-photo sourcing preserves ecological validity while enabling control.** Starting from real photographs ensures dataset similarity to natural deployment data, avoiding distribution shift from fully synthetic images. The face-only constraint provides a middle ground: tighter control than uncontrolled benchmarks (like VisBias) where race/gender correlate with background, and higher realism than synthetic setups. Evidence includes the JSD comparison showing FOCUS has lower group-wise divergence than VisBias, indicating reduced confounding from visual context.

## Foundational Learning
- **Counterfactual reasoning for bias auditing:** Creates "what-if" scenarios to isolate demographic effects. Quick check: What distinguishes an *interventional* counterfactual (as used here) from an *observational* one in fairness literature?
- **Visual confounding:** Co-variation of demographic attributes with background/clothing in real images impedes causal attribution. Quick check: Give an example of a visual confound that might correlate with a demographic group in uncontrolled photos.
- **Task design and elicitation format:** Output constraints and prompt phrasing shape how bias manifests in model decisions. Quick check: Why might a model show different gender preference in a 2AFC "who earns more" vs. an MCQ "salary bracket" task for the same image?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Source photos -> Face-only editing (NanoBanana Pro) -> Quality control (MediaPipe, GPT-4o) -> REFLECT task prompts -> Batch model inference -> Output parsing -> Metric calculation (win rates, gaps, JSD)

**Critical path:** Curate source photos → Generate counterfactuals → QC verification → Prepare task-specific inputs → Run inference with strict constraints → Validate and parse outputs → Compute demographic metrics → Statistical analysis.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Realism vs. Control: real photos + edits offer attribution and realism but limited scale (480 images) and potential editor artifacts.  
- Task Diversity vs. Interpretability: multiple tasks provide varied bias signals but complicate overall assessment.  
- Scale vs. Rigor: 8 source photos per occupation sufficient for signal detection but not population inference.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High discard rate (>30%) in 2AFC due to invalid outputs.  
- MediaPipe QC shows diff_nonface approaching diff_face, indicating edit leakage.  
- FOCUS JSD similar to VisBias, suggesting control failure.  
- Salary outputs non-integer or verbose, showing prompt adherence issues.  
- Consistent win rates ~50% across all pairs, implying either no bias or complete control failure (all variants identical to model).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Validation of Face-Localization:** Compute pixel-wise difference inside vs. outside MediaPipe face mask for same-source pairs; expect mean(diff_face) >> mean(diff_nonface) and ratio ~8–12.  
2. **2AFC Position Bias & Consistency:** Run each pair twice with swapped labels; compute % of trials where same image is chosen both times. Low consistency indicates position bias; high discard rate suggests instability.  
3. **FOCUS vs. VisBias Confounding Check:** For a model (e.g., Gemini), compute group-wise JSD on MCQ for both datasets; FOCUS JSD should be lower, confirming reduced spurious context.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does task design shape bias direction?** The paper shows variability but doesn't explain why a model's bias flips across task formats under identical visual inputs. Evidence needed: controlled format variations with internal probing.
2. **Do unintended visual changes from editing confound attribution?** Face-only edits may alter non-facial regions (hair, expression) or perceived age, potentially influencing decisions. Evidence needed: human or model-based assessment of non-face changes, or ablation with stricter masks.
3. **Why does CEO amplify racial disparities?** Occupation strongly moderates salary gaps; CEO yields strongest amplification. Unclear if due to occupational stereotypes in the model, biography content, or numeric output. Evidence needed: decouple title from biography, analyze model justifications.
4. **Which spurious correlations distort uncontrolled benchmarks?** FOCUS vs. VisBias JSD differs, but specific confounding factors (clothing, background) remain unidentified. Evidence needed: incremental reintroduction of contextual variations to FOCUS to isolate drivers.
5. **Do patterns generalize beyond limited occupations?** FOCUS covers only 6 occupations and 8 source photos each; broader diversity might alter bias patterns. Evidence needed: larger-scale replication with expanded occupations and photo styles.

## Limitations
- **Unintended edits:** Face-only editing may still alter non-facial attributes (hair, expression) or perceived age, confounding attribution despite QC.
- **Task and occupation scope:** Results are limited to three tasks and six occupations; may not generalize to other decision contexts or job roles.
- **Scale and diversity:** Only 8 source photos per occupation limits capture of within-occupation variability (lighting, pose, accessories).
- **Model and editing accessibility:** Evaluated five VLMs; smaller/older models may behave differently. Editing pipeline relies on a specific, potentially inaccessible model (`gemini-3-pro-image-preview`).

## Confidence
- **Face-only isolation claim:** Medium-High (strong QC metrics but perfect control unverifiable without full human assessment).
- **Task-dependence claim:** High (consistent reversal patterns across scenarios and tasks).
- **Reduced confounding claim (vs. VisBias):** High (direct JSD comparison shows lower spurious context influence).
- **Bias persistence claim:** Medium (evidence across five models but limited model diversity and task reliance).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model replication:** Test additional VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, GPT-4 variants) using exact FOCUS prompts to verify bias pattern generalizability.
2. **Human edit assessment:** Conduct a study where annotators judge whether only facial demographics changed between pairs and rate naturalness, validating isolation beyond automated metrics.
3. **Extended occupation/source diversity:** Expand FOCUS to more occupations (e.g., STEM vs. service) and increase source-photo count per occupation (e.g., 30+), then re-run key tasks to check stability of bias patterns.
