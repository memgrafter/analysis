# Diffusion Models for Joint Audio-Video Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16093  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16093  
- **Reference count:** 29  
- **Primary result:** A sequential video-then-audio pipeline using CogVideoX and MMAudio achieves superior fidelity (FAD 5020.37, FVD 206.49) compared to training a joint MM-Diffusion model from scratch (FAD 9260.94, FVD 251.62) on new paired datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles joint text-to-audio-video generation, a task where most existing models are single-modal. The core contribution is a modular, sequential pipeline: first generate video with the text-to-video model CogVideoX, then condition a separate video-to-audio model (MMAudio) on that video and the original prompt to produce synchronized sound. This approach outperforms the authors' own from-scratch training of a joint multimodal diffusion (MM-Diffusion) model, which is found to be computationally expensive and unstable. The work is supported by the release of two new paired audio-video datasets (concerts and gaming) and highlights a key design trade-off: leveraging powerful, pre-trained single-modality models versus the complexity of training a unified model from scratch.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates three approaches to joint audio-video generation. **Approach 1 (Primary Contribution):** A sequential pipeline where CogVideoX generates a video latent from text, which is then fed to MMAudio along with the text prompt to generate synchronized audio. **Approach 2 (Baseline):** Training an MM-Diffusion model from scratch, which uses two coupled U-Nets with cross-attention to denoise video and audio latents jointly. **Approach 3 (Negative Result):** An attempt at latent joint diffusion by combining mismatched, pre-trained VAEs (Stable Diffusion for video, Music2Latent for audio), which failed due to inconsistent decoding. The sequential method is shown to be more resource-efficient and produces higher-fidelity outputs as measured by Fréchet distances.

## Key Results
- The sequential CogVideoX→MMAudio pipeline achieves a **Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD) of 5020.37** and **Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 206.49** on the new concert dataset.
- The from-scratch MM-Diffusion model yields significantly worse metrics: **FAD 9260.94** and **FVD 251.62**.
- Training MM-Diffusion is described as prohibitively resource-intensive and produces noisy loss curves, contrasting with the efficient inference-only nature of the sequential approach.
- The paper releases two new paired datasets: **64 hours of concerts** and **13 hours of gaming** footage, each segmented into 34-second clips.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A sequential video-first, then audio pipeline produces high-fidelity, synchronized outputs more efficiently than training a single joint model from scratch.
- **Mechanism:** A strong pre-trained video generator (CogVideoX) creates a coherent, temporally precise visual latent. This video output is then used as conditioning input for a separate pre-trained video-to-audio model (MMAudio), which is explicitly designed to align audio with visual features. The audio model’s diverse training on real video-audio pairs allows it to generalize to the *generated* video from the first stage.
- **Break condition:** The pipeline fails if the first-stage video has poor motion semantics, artifacts, or perspectives outside the second-stage audio model's training distribution, leading to uncorrelated audio.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Training a joint multimodal diffusion model (MM-Diffusion) from scratch can learn coherent audio-video associations but is unstable and extremely resource-intensive.
- **Mechanism:** Two modality-specific U-Nets (video, audio) are trained with a shared noise schedule and loss. Cross-attention layers condition each modality's denoising on the other’s noisy state at every timestep, forcing the model to learn the joint distribution directly.
- **Break condition:** Training collapses or yields poor alignment if the paired dataset lacks diversity, hyperparameters are poorly tuned, or computational limits restrict model capacity/batch size.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Naïvely combining mismatched, pre-trained single-modality encoder-decoders for joint latent diffusion leads to poor-quality outputs.
- **Mechanism:** Video and audio are compressed into latent spaces using independently trained VAEs (Stable Diffusion's, Music2Latent). A joint diffusion process is applied to the combined latent space. However, the individual decoders—never trained on outputs from the other modality's latent manifold—fail to reconstruct coherent signals.
- **Break condition:** Decoding fails due to incompatible latent manifolds and decoder architectures, causing phase inconsistencies and artifacts regardless of joint latent diffusion quality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Diffusion Probabilistic Models**  
  - **Why needed:** All generation approaches are built on denoising diffusion (forward/noise schedule, reverse process).  
  - **Quick check:** If βₜ is increased at timestep t, what is the effect on `q(xt|xt-1)`? *(Answer: Increases the variance of the forward process, making xt noisier relative to xt-1.)*
- **Concept: Cross-Modal Attention & Conditioning**  
  - **Why needed:** Alignment is achieved by conditioning. MM-Diffusion uses cross-attention between subnets; CogVideoX uses an Expert Transformer; MMAudio uses a multimodal transformer and sync module.  
  - **Quick check:** In cross-attention where Query is from modality A and Key/Value from modality B, what is learned? *(Answer: The model learns to retrieve and aggregate relevant information from modality B to inform the representation of modality A.)*
- **Concept: Latent Space Compression & VAEs**  
  - **Why needed:** All models operate in compressed latent spaces for efficiency. The failed latent MM-Diffusion experiment hinges on VAE compatibility.  
  - **Quick check:** If a VAE is trained only on clean concert videos, what happens when its encoder processes a generated video with synthetic artifacts? *(Answer: The encoder may produce an out-of-distribution latent, leading to poor reconstruction.)*

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Text Prompt -> CogVideoX (3D VAE Encoder -> Latent Diffusion U-Net/Transformer -> VAE Decoder -> Video Frames) -> MMAudio (Synchformer Feature Extractor -> Multimodal Transformer + Conditional Sync Module -> Flow Matching Denoiser -> Audio Latent -> Mel-Spectrogram -> BigVGAN Vocoder -> Audio Waveform)
- **Critical path:** Text Prompt -> CogVideoX (success) -> Valid Video Output -> MMAudio Feature Extraction (success) -> MMAudio Conditioning -> Valid Audio Output.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Modularity vs. Error Propagation:* Sequential design leverages SOTA single-modal models but compounds errors (bad video → inevitably bad audio).
  - *Quality vs. Resource Cost:* From-scratch MM-Diffusion aims for intrinsic joint learning but requires massive compute; sequential uses inference-only calls.
  - *Control:* Sequential pipeline offers two explicit conditioning points (text→video, then text+video→audio); joint training has one entangled signal.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Stage 1:** Video has semantic drift, poor motion, low resolution. *Check:* Visual inspection of video output.
  - **Bridge:** Valid video but feature extraction fails (e.g., resolution/FPS mismatch). *Check:* MMAudio preprocessing logs.
  - **Stage 2:** Audio is asynchronous, noisy, or semantically unrelated despite good video. *Check:* Manual alignment review; spectrogram vs. motion comparison.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Isolate Stage Quality:** Generate videos with CogVideoX; evaluate FVD alone. Generate audio from *real* videos using MMAudio; evaluate FAD and sync.
  2. **End-to-End Smoke Test:** Run full pipeline on 3 simple high-action prompts (drumming, clapping); verify basic sync.
  3. **Failure Mode Isolation:** Run on prompts likely to cause Stage 1 (dynamic camera) or Stage 2 (rare sound) issues; log intermediates to correlate final failure with specific stage output.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can latent MM-Diffusion be modified to achieve SOTA performance while significantly reducing computational resources?** The authors note decoding instability and propose improving the latent approach as future work.
- **What specific architectural or training inconsistencies cause multimodal decoding instability in latent joint audio-video diffusion?** The paper identifies mismatched decoder architectures as the cause of poor performance but does not diagnose root causes.
- **Does the sequential pipeline generalize to diverse domains beyond concerts and gaming, and how does its alignment compare to end-to-end joint models?** Evaluation is limited to two custom datasets; comparisons with other joint SOTA on matched data are absent.
- **Can the sequential framework be optimized for real-time or interactive applications?** The paper reports only quality metrics, not latency or throughput, which are critical for deployment.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters for CogVideoX and MMAudio (guidance scales, inference steps) are unreported, hindering metric reproducibility.
- Evaluation protocol details (feature extractor variants, real-data subset size, preprocessing) are unspecified, potentially invalidating FAD/FVD comparisons.
- The new datasets lack diversity benchmarks; prompt generation via LLaMA 3.2 may introduce linguistic biases not reflective of real-world queries.
- The from-scratch MM-Diffusion comparison is only against the authors' own implementation, not against other published joint models.

## Confidence
- **Sequential pipeline superiority (FAD 5020 vs 9260):** High. Clear comparative numbers and mechanistic explanation.
- **From-scratch MM-Diffusion resource instability:** High. Explicitly stated training difficulties and resource costs.
- **Latent joint diffusion decoder failure:** Medium. Identified as a negative result but lacks systematic ablation of individual components.
- **Dataset contribution (64h/13h):** High. Quantified and released, though curation process is opaque.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Re-run sequential pipeline with ±20% variation in CogVideoX/MMAudio guidance scales and steps; report FAD/FVD delta to establish robustness bounds.
2. **Decoder incompatibility isolation:** Replace MM-Diffusion's audio decoder with MMAudio's BigVGAN (keeping latent space) and vice versa; measure reconstruction quality to isolate which decoder contributes most to failure.
3. **Cross-dataset generalization:** Test sequential pipeline on an external paired benchmark (e.g., AIST++, AudioSet) without fine-tuning; compute FAD/FVD drop versus in-distribution (concert) performance to quantify dataset overfitting.
