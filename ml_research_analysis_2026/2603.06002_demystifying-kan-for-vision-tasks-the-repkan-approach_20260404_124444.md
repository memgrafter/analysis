# Demystifying KAN for Vision Tasks: The RepKAN Approach
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06002
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06002
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: RepKAN_Grid3 achieves 98.78% accuracy on EuroSAT (+0.37% over baseline) and 79.17% on NWPU-RESISC45 (+5.36% over baseline) while providing intrinsically interpretable spectral reasoning.

## Executive Summary
RepKAN introduces a hybrid vision module that combines spatial convolutions with spectral non-linear splines to address the black-box nature of CNNs/Transformers in remote sensing classification. The design uses a dual-path architecture: a Spatial Linear Path preserves local textures via multi-branch convolutions, while a Spectral Non-linear Path employs one-dimensional KAN splines to model band interactions and automatically discover meaningful spectral indices. Through structural reparameterization, the spatial path is fused into a single convolution at inference, maintaining efficiency. Experiments on EuroSAT (multispectral) and RESISC45 (RGB) show significant accuracy gains and reveal physically interpretable spline curves that refine traditional indices like NDVI.

## Method Summary
The RepKAN layer is inserted into a CNN backbone at three stages. Each layer contains two parallel paths: (1) Spatial Path: a multi-branch convolutional block (1×1 and 3×3 convs with BatchNorm) that captures local spatial context; (2) Spectral Path: channel‑wise 1D B‑spline functions that learn nonlinear mappings between input bands. The two path outputs are added element‑wise. During training the spatial branches benefit from multi‑branch regularization; at inference they are structurally re‑parameterized into a single 3×3 convolution (in the spirit of RepVGG), while the spectral splines remain unchanged. The model is trained with AdamW, cosine learning rate scheduling, and standard augmentations. The spectral path’s splines are scaled to the [0,1] range and parameterized by grid size G (number of knots), which controls nonlinear capacity.

## Key Results
- On EuroSAT (13‑channel): RepKAN_Grid3 reaches 98.78% overall accuracy, a 0.37% absolute improvement over the baseline CNN.
- On NWPU‑RESISC45 (RGB): RepKAN_Grid3 achieves 79.17% accuracy, a 5.36% absolute gain over the baseline.
- Learned splines provide intrinsic interpretability: curves and 2D interaction landscapes reveal class‑specific spectral fingerprints and can be symbolically regressed to approximate equations that refine known indices (e.g., NDVI).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Spatial Linear Path preserves essential local spatial context (e.g., textures, edges) that is lost when flattening image data for vanilla KANs.
- **Mechanism:** A multi‑branch convolutional structure (1×1 and 3×3 convolutions with BatchNorm) processes the 2D feature map. This captures hierarchical spatial patterns and structural relationships critical for land‑cover analysis.
- **Core assumption:** Local spatial structures (e.g., building shapes, field boundaries) are primary discriminative features for remote sensing scenes and must be explicitly modeled.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “Spatial Linear Path (convolutions) for local context”; Section 2.3.1 states the multi‑branch design; neighboring work (e.g., KCN) also combine KAN with CNNs, suggesting a recognized gap.
- **Break condition:** If spatial textures are not the dominant discriminative feature (e.g., purely spectral classification on non‑textured water bodies), the added spatial complexity may be unnecessary overhead.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Spectral Non-linear Path, via learnable 1D B‑spline functions applied channel‑wise, autonomously models complex, non‑linear band interactions and discovers class‑specific spectral indices.
- **Mechanism:** For each output channel, the response is a sum over input channels of a learned spline function ϕ applied to the value at each spatial location. The spline shape is a weighted combination of B‑spline basis functions, optimized during training to create non‑linear mappings between spectral band values and the output.
- **Core assumption:** Spectral signatures of land‑cover materials involve non‑linear relationships between bands (e.g., soil moisture effects on Red‑NIR space) that are not fully captured by linear combinations or fixed indices like NDVI.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “Spectral Non‑linear Path (1D KAN splines) to model band interactions”; Section 2.3.2 describes the 1‑D B‑splines; Sections 3.4 & 3.5 provide visualizations of spline curves and 2D interaction landscapes differentiating classes.
- **Break condition:** If spectral relationships are predominantly linear, the non‑linear spline path may overfit or learn redundant functions without performance gain, as suggested by the performance drop with higher grid sizes (Grid5/7) in Table 1.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Structural reparameterization fuses the multi‑branch spatial convolutional path into a single, equivalent 3×3 convolution at inference, decoupling training‑time multi‑path benefits from deployment‑time efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** Following RepVGG, the parameters (weights and biases) of the 1×1 and 3×3 branches are mathematically combined into the weights and bias of a single deployable 3×3 convolution kernel. The spectral spline path remains unchanged.
- **Core assumption:** The expressive benefit of the multi‑branch spatial path during training can be fully encoded into a single convolutional kernel without loss, enabling standard CNN‑level inference speed.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.2 and Figure 1(b) illustrate the reparameterization; the inference equation shows fused convolution plus spline term.
- **Break condition:** If the training‑time multi‑path objective leads to parameters that cannot be perfectly merged into a single kernel, a slight accuracy drop at conversion could occur (not reported).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Kolmogorov‑Arnold Representation Theorem & KANs**  
  - *Why needed:* RepKAN integrates KANs, which place learnable univariate functions on edges, to model spectral interactions. Understanding that KANs approximate multivariate functions via sums of 1D splines is essential to grasp the spectral path’s mechanism.  
  - *Quick check:* How does a KAN’s learnable spline on an edge differ from a neuron with a fixed activation (like ReLU) in an MLP?

- **Concept: Spectral Remote Sensing Indices**  
  - *Why needed:* The interpretability claims center on discovering equations that refine classical indices (e.g., NDVI). Knowing what a spectral index is and why non‑linear combinations might be physically meaningful is necessary.  
  - *Quick check:* What is the physical rationale behind NDVI, and what limitation might a learned non‑linear function address?

- **Concept: Multi‑Scale/Spectral Remote Sensing Data**  
  - *Why needed:* The datasets (EuroSAT 13‑band, RESISC45 RGB) have specific characteristics; the design choices are responses to these data properties.  
  - *Quick check:* Why might a 13‑band multispectral image offer different classification cues than a 3‑band RGB aerial photo?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input → Stem → [RepKAN Stage1 → RepKAN Stage2 → RepKAN Stage3] → Global Average Pooling → Linear Classifier → Output.  
  Each RepKAN Stage contains: Spatial Path (BN(Conv1×1) + BN(Conv3×3)) ⊕ Spectral Path (∑ ϕ_{o,c}(x) per channel) → Add.
- **Critical path:** Data flows sequentially through the three stages. At each stage, the input feature map is processed by both paths in parallel; their outputs are summed and passed to the next stage. After training, the spatial branches are fused into a single convolution per stage, leaving the spectral path intact.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Grid size G:* Larger G increases spline nonlinear capacity but risks overfitting and higher compute; paper finds G=3 optimal.  
  - *Spatial vs. Spectral balance:* The spectral path contributes heavily (>77% in early stages); channel widths control trade‑off.  
  - *Dataset suitability:* Benefit is strongest on multispectral data; on pure RGB, gains arise from non‑linear channel interactions, not physical bands.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. Over‑reliance on spectral path (e.g., >90% contribution in Stage 1 for some classes) may underutilize spatial context, harming performance on spatially distinct but spectrally similar classes.  
  2. Poor spline learning: low R² in symbolic regression (e.g., <0.5) indicates splines are not converging to smooth, meaningful functions.  
  3. Reparameterization mismatch: accuracy drop >0.1% after fusing spatial branches suggests multi‑branch parameters cannot be perfectly merged.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate baseline CNN on EuroSAT and RESISC45 to obtain reference accuracies (Table 1, 2).  
  2. Integrate RepKAN blocks into the baseline, sweep grid sizes {3,5,7}, and reproduce reported gains (+0.37% EuroSAT, +5.36% RESISC45).  
  3. Validate interpretability: extract Stage 1 splines for NIR band, fit cubic polynomials (R² >0.7 for key classes), and regenerate Figure 2c Red–NIR interaction landscape.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does RepKAN’s accuracy degrade when increasing spline grid size beyond 3, as observed on EuroSAT?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper reports RepKAN_Grid3 98.78% OA, while Grid5 and Grid7 drop to 98.59% and 98.56% respectively, and notes this aligns with prior work [18] but provides no mechanistic explanation.
- **Why unresolved:** The sensitivity to grid size—a key hyperparameter—remains unexplained, hindering theoretical understanding and optimal configuration.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled ablation analyzing spline coefficient distributions, training dynamics (loss curvature), and validation gaps across grid sizes, potentially with explicit regularization on spline smoothness.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can RepKAN’s spectral path scale to hyperspectral data (100+ bands) while preserving performance and interpretability?
- **Basis in paper:** Experiments are limited to EuroSAT (13 bands) and RESISC45 (RGB), yet the paper emphasizes spectral discovery, implicitly assuming moderate band counts without validation on high‑dimensional data.
- **Why unresolved:** The computational cost of the spectral path and the feasibility of visualizing high‑dimensional band interactions are unaddressed, leaving scalability uncertain.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmarking on hyperspectral datasets (e.g., Indian Pines), reporting accuracy, parameter growth, inference time, and assessing whether dimensionality‑reduced spline weights retain physically meaningful interactions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is RepKAN’s intrinsic interpretability empirically superior to post‑hoc XAI methods like Grad‑CAM for remote sensing tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper claims “intrinsic transparency” over post‑hoc saliency maps but provides only qualitative case studies (Figs. 4‑5) without quantitative comparison or expert evaluation.
- **Why unresolved:** Without standardized metrics (e.g., fidelity, stability) or user studies, the interpretability advantage remains anecdotal and unverified for practical adoption.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled experiment with remote sensing experts comparing explanation quality (e.g., believability, actionability) for RepKAN’s spectral reasoning maps versus Grad‑CAM on misclassification cases, along with fidelity scores against known spectral indices.

## Limitations
- Architectural and spline specifications are incompletely described, hindering exact replication.
- No empirical runtime measurements are provided to substantiate the claimed inference‑time efficiency.
- Generalisation to other datasets (e.g., hyperspectral) remains unevaluated.
