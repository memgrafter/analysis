# Probing the Latent World: Emergent Discrete Symbols and Physical Structure in Latent Representations
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20327
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20327
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: V-JEPA 2's latent space encodes physical structure; a passive vector quantization probe (AIM) yields discrete symbols whose distributions shift significantly across semantic categories (e.g., grasp angle, object geometry, motion temporal structure).

## Executive Summary
JEPA models learn predictive latent representations but lack an inspectable output layer, creating an interpretability gap. The authors introduce the AI Mother Tongue (AIM) framework—a vocabulary‑free vector quantization probe attached to a frozen V‑JEPA 2 encoder—to convert continuous latents into discrete symbols without modifying the encoder. Category‑contrast experiments on Kinetics‑mini reveal statistically significant symbol distribution shifts (χ² p < 10⁻⁴; mutual information 0.036–0.117 bits; Jensen‑Shannon divergence up to 0.342) with 62.5% codebook utilization. The results indicate that V‑JEPA 2’s latent space is compact: diverse actions share a core representation, and semantic differences are encoded as graded distributional variations, particularly along temporal structure.

## Method Summary
AIM is a passive, vocabulary‑free probing framework. A pretrained, frozen V‑JEPA 2 encoder processes input videos and outputs continuous latent vectors for each spatial–temporal location. These vectors are quantized via a learned codebook (vector quantization) to produce discrete symbols. No encoder weights are updated during probing. The authors conduct category‑contrast experiments on Kinetics‑mini, selecting videos that vary in grasp angle, object geometry, and motion temporal structure. For each category, they collect symbol distributions generated by the probe and compute statistical metrics (χ² test, mutual information, Jensen‑Shannon divergence) to quantify the association between symbols and semantic properties. Codebook utilization is reported to assess how fully the discrete vocabulary is used.

## Key Results
- Statistically significant symbol distribution shifts across physical categories (χ² p < 1e-4; MI 0.036–0.117 bits; JSD up to 0.342).
- 62.5% of the codebook entries are actively used, indicating moderate utilization.
- V‑JEPA 2’s latent space is compact: diverse actions share a core representation, with semantic differences encoded as graded distributional variations, especially along temporal structure.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
V‑JEPA 2 is trained to predict future representations, forcing it to capture underlying physical factors (e.g., geometry, motion). This objective organizes the latent space such that vectors reflecting similar physical attributes lie near each other. The AIM probe applies vector quantization, partitioning this already structured space into discrete regions (codebook entries). Because the latent space is semantically aligned, the resulting discrete symbols inherit meaningful correlations with physical properties. The probe is passive (no encoder updates), so it reveals the encoder’s inherent organization. The observed category‑dependent symbol distributions demonstrate that the encoder encodes these factors; the graded nature of the differences suggests that categories are separated probabilistically rather than by hard boundaries, consistent with the continuous variation present in the physical world.

## Foundational Learning
- **Predictive Latent Representations**: Self‑supervised objective where a model predicts future states; extracts factors of variation without labels. *Why needed*: Provides a representation that captures physical structure for downstream tasks. *Quick check*: Does the representation better predict future frames/actions than random features?
- **Vector Quantization (VQ)**: Maps continuous vectors to discrete codebook entries via nearest‑neighbor search. *Why needed*: Enables extraction of discrete symbols for interpretability and potential symbolic processing. *Quick check*: Is the quantization operation differentiable (straight‑through) or does it block gradient flow?
- **Interpretability Gap**: Discrepancy between continuous latent spaces and human‑understandable discrete concepts. *Why needed*: Bridging it allows inspection of what the model has learned. *Quick check*: Can we associate latent dimensions or clusters with specific semantic attributes?
- **Category‑Contrast Analysis**: Statistical comparison of symbol distributions across predefined categories (e.g., χ², mutual information). *Why needed*: Quantifies the association between discrete symbols and semantic properties. *Quick check*: Are the chosen metrics appropriate for categorical data and sensitive to distributional shifts?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
V‑JEPA 2 Encoder -> AIM Probe (Codebook) -> Discrete Symbols  

**Critical Path:**  
Input video → frozen V‑JEPA 2 encoder → per‑frame (or per‑patch) continuous latent vectors → nearest‑codebook lookup → discrete symbol sequence → distributional analysis per semantic category.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Codebook size*: Larger sizes enable finer discretization but risk lower utilization and overfitting; smaller sizes may merge distinct concepts.  
- *Probe passivity*: Preserves the original encoder but prevents the probe from adapting to better capture semantics.  
- *Vocabulary‑free*: Avoids imposing human labels, but makes direct interpretation of codes ambiguous.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Very low codebook utilization (e.g., <20%) suggests mismatch or oversized codebook.  
- Non‑significant χ² tests or near‑zero mutual information indicate the probe fails to capture semantic structure.  
- High‑entropy, nearly uniform symbol distributions across categories imply lack of discriminative power.

**First Experiments:**  
1. Vary codebook size (e.g., 128, 512, 2048) and measure utilization and category‑discriminability metrics.  
2. Replace AIM with a baseline random projection followed by k‑means clustering (trained on the same latents) to test if observed patterns are probe‑specific.  
3. Probe intermediate layers of V‑JEPA 2 (early vs. late) to determine at which depth physical properties become discretely separable.

## Limitations
- **Probe design & justification:** Key choices (codebook size, quantization objective) lack ablation studies; results may reflect probe artifacts rather than true latent structure.  
- **Causal inference gap:** The study shows correlation between symbol distributions and categories, but cannot confirm that V‑JEPA 2 uses these distinctions for prediction.  
- **Dataset & task specificity:** Experiments are limited to Kinetics‑mini video actions; findings may not generalize to other physical domains (e.g., robotics) or modalities (e.g., images, audio).  
- **Baseline deficiency:** No comparisons to alternative discretization methods (e.g., k‑means, VAEs) or other self‑supervised models (e.g., MAE, SimCLR) weaken claims about V‑JEPA 2’s uniqueness.

## Confidence
- AIM converts continuous latents into discrete symbols without modifying the encoder. → High  
- Statistically significant symbol distribution shifts exist across categories. → High  
- V‑JEPA 2's latent space is compact; diverse actions share a core representation with semantic differences encoded as graded distributional variations. → Low  
- Differences are especially encoded along temporal structure. → Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Probe Ablation & Baselines:** Test different codebook sizes; use alternative discretization (e.g., random projection + k‑means); train a probe on shuffled latents to isolate Whether patterns stem from the latent space or probe specifics.  
2. **Cross‑Dataset Generalization:** Apply the frozen AIM probe to latents from a different video dataset (e.g., Something‑Something V2) and a non‑video physical dataset (e.g., robotic manipulation trajectories). Significant drop in symbol‑category correlation would indicate overfitting to Kinetics‑mini biases.  
3. **Layer‑Wise Probing & Causal Intervention:** Probe intermediate layers of V‑JEPA 2, then perturb latent dimensions suspected to encode temporal structure (based on probe analysis) and measure the impact on the model’s video prediction performance. A strong performance drop would support the functional encoding of temporal structure.
