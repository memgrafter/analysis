# Conditional Factuality Controlled LLMs with Generalization Certificates via Conformal Sampling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.27403  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27403  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: CFC provides conditional factuality control with provable coverage guarantees and smaller prediction sets than marginal conformal baselines.

## Executive Summary
Large language models suffer from hallucinations, requiring reliable test-time factuality control. Existing conformal prediction methods offer only marginal coverage, failing to equally protect hard and easy prompts. This paper introduces Conditional Factuality Control (CFC), a post‑hoc conformal framework that learns a feature‑conditional acceptance threshold via augmented quantile regression on a latent success score. CFC yields theoretical conditional coverage under exchangeability and a PAC‑style finite‑sample certificate. Empirical evaluation on synthetic data, reasoning/QA benchmarks (GSM8K, TriviaQA), and a vision‑language task (Flickr8k) shows that CFC and its PAC‑certified variant achieve near‑target coverage across difficulty subgroups while using smaller prediction sets than marginal baselines, all without retraining the base generator.

## Method Summary
CFC operates in a split conformal setting. A calibration set of prompts provides latent success scores \(S_i\) (the minimum verifier score among correct candidates). For a test prompt \(x\), CFC computes a feature vector \(\Phi(x)\) (e.g., quadratic in mean verifier loss) and solves an *augmented quantile regression* that jointly considers calibration scores and a candidate test score \(s\). The threshold \(\hat{\lambda}(x)\) is defined as the largest \(s\) such that \(s \leq \Phi(x)^\top \beta_s\), where \(\beta_s\) is the regression solution for that \(s\). Candidates \(y\) are retained if verifier score \(V(x,y) \leq \hat{\lambda}(x)\).

The PAC variant (CFC‑PAC) adds ridge regularization to the regression and shrinks the nominal significance level to \(\alpha_{\text{eff}} = \max(0, \alpha - \varepsilon_N(\delta))\) with \(\varepsilon_N(\delta) = O(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/N})\). This adjustment, derived from a stability analysis, yields a high‑probability bound on conditional miscoverage deviation.

## Key Results
- **Conditional coverage**: CFC attains coverage close to the target \(1-\alpha\) across difficulty‑defined subgroups, while marginal ICP undercovers hard prompts and overcovers easy ones.
- **Efficiency**: CFC produces smaller average prediction set sizes (APSS) than ICP and Learnt CP baselines at comparable coverage.
- **PAC guarantees**: CFC‑PAC provides a finite‑sample certificate \( \varepsilon_N(\delta) = O(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/N}) \) and empirically improves worst‑group coverage (GSC) with modest APSS increase.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Feature‑Conditional Threshold via Augmented Quantile Regression
- **Claim**: CFC learns a data‑dependent threshold that adapts to prompt difficulty, enabling conditional coverage.
- **Mechanism**: For test \(x\), compute \(\hat{\lambda}(x)\) as the largest fixed point of \(g_x(s) = \Phi(x)^\top \beta_s\), where \(\beta_s\) solves the augmented quantile regression objective that includes calibration scores and the candidate test score \(s\). The prediction set contains all candidates with \(V(x,y) \leq \hat{\lambda}(x)\).
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract; Section 4.1; related work on conformal factuality.
- **Break condition**: If \(\Phi(x)\) fails to encode difficulty or calibration/test exchangeability is violated, conditional coverage may degrade to marginal behavior.

### Mechanism 2: PAC‑Style Finite‑Sample Certificate via Stability
- **Claim**: CFC‑PAC guarantees that conditional miscoverage deviates from the target by at most \(\varepsilon_N(\delta) = O(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/N})\) with high probability.
- **Mechanism**: Add ridge regularization to the quantile regression objective; replace \(\alpha\) by \(\alpha_{\text{eff}} = \max(0,\alpha - \varepsilon_N(\delta))\). The slack \(\varepsilon_N(\delta)\) comes from a bounded‑differences inequality applied to the Lipschitz stability of the threshold under single‑sample replacement.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract; Section 4.2; Theorem 4.2 and Appendix B.2.
- **Break condition**: Violations of the bounded feature norm or Lipschitz assumptions, or overly strong ridge regularization, can invalidate the certificate or harm efficiency.

### Mechanism 3: Efficiency Gains from Conditional Thresholding
- **Claim**: An oracle conditional threshold (true conditional quantile) yields smaller expected prediction‑set size than any marginal threshold at the same coverage; CFC approximates this oracle and inherits the gain asymptotically.
- **Mechanism**: The oracle sets \(\lambda^*(x) = q_\alpha(x)\), the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of the latent success score given \(x\). Under assumptions (strictly increasing CDFs, monotonicity in difficulty, convexity of certain functions), the expected set size under the oracle is strictly lower than under any constant threshold. CFC’s uniform consistency ensures its expected set size converges to the oracle’s.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 4.3 (Proposition 4.3, Theorem 4.4); Appendix B.3–B.4.
- **Break condition**: If assumptions (e.g., convexity of \(C_t\)) are violated or the feature map is misspecified, the conditional rule may not outperform marginal CP.

## Foundational Learning
- **Split Conformal Prediction**: CFC builds on the split (inductive) conformal framework, which uses a held‑out calibration set to construct distribution‑free prediction sets with marginal coverage.  
  *Quick check*: In split conformal prediction, what is the role of the nonconformity score, and how is the prediction set formed?
- **Quantile Regression and the Pinball Loss**: CFC’s conditional threshold is obtained by solving an augmented quantile regression problem that uses the pinball loss \(\rho_\tau(u) = u(\tau - \mathbb{1}\{u<0\})\). This loss is proper for quantile estimation and handles asymmetric penalties.  
  *Quick check*: What is the pinball loss, and why is it suitable for quantile estimation?
- **Fixed‑Point Threshold Construction**: Instead of evaluating the quantile regression fit on a grid, CFC defines the threshold as the largest \(s\) satisfying \(s \leq g_x(s)\). This fixed‑point rule ensures the threshold corresponds to a valid quantile and avoids repeated optimization.  
  *Quick check*: Why can’t we simply take \(\hat{\lambda}(x) = g_x(\hat{\lambda}(x))\) directly? What does the fixed‑point construction guarantee?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Base generator \(\pi\) produces candidate set \(C(x)\); verifier \(V\) scores candidates; feature map \(\Phi(x)\) encodes prompt difficulty; calibration set \(\{(x_i,S_i)\}\) is stored. At test time, given \(x\), compute \(\Phi(x)\), then solve augmented quantile regression for various \(s\) using calibration data to obtain \(\beta_s\); find \(\hat{\lambda}(x) = \sup\{s : s \leq \Phi(x)^\top \beta_s\}\); output \(\{y \in C(x) : V(x,y) \leq \hat{\lambda}(x)\}\). CFC‑PAC adds ridge regularization and uses \(\alpha_{\text{eff}}\).

- **Critical path**:
  1. **Calibration**: For each calibration prompt, compute latent success score \(S_i\) and store.
  2. **Test‑time per prompt**:
     a. Sample candidates \(C(x)\) from \(\pi\).
     b. Compute feature vector \(\Phi(x)\).
     c. Binary search over \(s\): for each \(s\), solve the augmented quantile regression (with ridge if PAC) to get \(\beta_s\) and evaluate \(g_x(s) = \Phi(x)^\top \beta_s\).
     d. \(\hat{\lambda}(x)\) = largest feasible \(s\) (i.e., \(s \leq g_x(s)\)).
     e. Filter candidates: keep \(y\) with \(V(x,y) \leq \hat{\lambda}(x)\).
  3. **Output**: The filtered set.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Feature engineering*: Simpler \(\Phi\) (e.g., quadratic) is cheap but may miss complex patterns; more expressive \(\Phi\) (neural) risks overfitting and higher compute.
  - *Candidate budget \(M\)*: Larger \(M\) increases chance of correct answers but can increase set size when threshold is loose; paper finds small \(M\) (e.g., 5 on GSM8K) often suffices.
  - *PAC adjustment*: Shrinking \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha_{\text{eff}}\) improves worst‑group coverage but typically increases average set size; tradeoff controlled by confidence \(\delta\).
  - *Ridge regularization*: Stabilizes PAC variant but introduces hyperparameter \(\lambda\); too large oversmooths and hurts efficiency.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Under‑coverage on hard prompts if \(\Phi(x)\) does not capture difficulty.
  - Numerical instability in fixed‑point search if \(g_x(s)\) is not monotonic; check \(g_x(0) \geq 0\) and \(g_x(1) \geq 1\).
  - Exchangeability violation between calibration and test distributions leads to silent guarantee failure.
  - Over‑conservative thresholds in CFC‑PAC when \(\varepsilon_N(\delta) \ge \alpha\) (small \(N\) or high‑dimensional \(\Phi\)).

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Synthetic validation**: Generate prompts with known difficulty \(T \in [0,1]\), with \(S\) and \(V\) distributions depending on \(T\). Show CFC’s threshold monotonic in \(T\) and flat groupwise miscoverage, while ICP undercovers hard bins.
  2. **GSM8K reasoning**: Generator: LLaMA‑3‑8B‑Instruct; verifier: Qwen2.5‑Math‑RM‑72B; \(\Phi(x)=[1, T_{\text{proxy}}, T_{\text{proxy}}^2]\) with \(T_{\text{proxy}}\) = mean verifier score over 5 samples. Compare CFC variants to ICP and Learnt CP on coverage (ECR), worst‑group coverage (GSC), and APSS. CFC achieves target coverage with smaller APSS and higher GSC.
  3. **Flickr8k vision‑language**: Use Qwen2‑VL‑7B‑Instruct for image captioning, up to 2 cached candidates per image, same feature map. CFC‑PAC‑FULL attains near‑target coverage with improved subgroup reliability, demonstrating post‑hoc transfer without base‑model finetuning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Non‑linear features**: Can CFC be extended to non‑linear feature representations while retaining its conditional coverage guarantee? The current theory assumes linear \(\Phi(X)^\top \beta\); an extension is non‑trivial.
2. **Robustness to exchangeability violations**: How robust is CFC to distribution shift? The guarantees explicitly require exchangeability; in practice data may drift, and a weighted or adaptive version is needed.
3. **Verifier imperfections**: What is the effect of verifier noise on CFC’s coverage, and how can we account for it? The latent success score \(S\) assumes a perfect verifier; real verifiers may misrank candidates, jeopardizing the coverage link.

## Limitations
- **Feature sensitivity**: Performance hinges on the choice of \(\Phi\); misspecification can degrade conditional coverage and efficiency.
- **Calibration size dependence**: The PAC slack \(\varepsilon_N(\delta) = O(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/N})\) becomes large for small \(N\), leading to loose certificates and overly conservative thresholds.
- **Computational overhead**: The fixed‑point computation solves an LP at each binary search step, which can be expensive for large calibration sets or many test prompts.

## Confidence
- **Conditional coverage guarantee**: Medium  
- **PAC finite‑sample certificate**: Medium  
- **Efficiency gains (smaller sets)**: Medium‑High  

## Next Checks
1. **Feature map ablation**: Vary \(\Phi\) (linear, quadratic, neural) on synthetic data with known difficulty. Measure conditional coverage by difficulty bins and APSS. Expected: expressive \(\Phi\) improves coverage/efficiency until overfitting with small \(N\).
2. **Calibration size scaling**: For a fixed dataset (e.g., GSM8K), run CFC with \(N \in \{1k, 5k, 10k, 50k\}\). Plot worst‑group coverage vs. \(N\) and compare to theoretical \(\varepsilon_N(\delta)\). Expected: coverage converges at rate \(1/\sqrt{N}\).
3. **Distribution shift experiment**: Split calibration and test by topic (e.g., algebra vs. geometry in GSM8K). Compare conditional coverage degradation of CFC vs. ICP. Expected: both degrade, but CFC may be more sensitive if the \(\Phi\)-coverage relationship shifts.
