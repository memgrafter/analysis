# Match or Replay: Self Imitating Proximal Policy Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27515
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27515
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** SIPP integrates self-imitation into PPO via two modes—MATCH (optimal transport prioritization for dense rewards) and REPLAY (trajectory replay for sparse rewards)—yielding faster convergence and higher success rates across diverse tasks with a single trade-off parameter ε.

## Executive Summary
Self-Imitating Proximal Policy Optimization (SIPP) enhances the sample efficiency of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) by self-imitation without modifying rewards or adding auxiliary models. For dense-reward tasks, MATCH uses optimal transport (Sinkhorn) to prioritize transitions in PPO updates that are distributionally similar to the best historical trajectory. For sparse-reward tasks, REPLAY uniformly replays stored successful full trajectories to reinforce critical behaviors. A single parameter ε controls the balance between imitation and exploration. Evaluated on MuJoCo, PointMaze, and Animal-AI Olympics, SIPP consistently outperforms PPO and related baselines, demonstrating broad applicability across reward densities and partial observability.

## Method Summary
SIPP modifies PPO’s data sampling with an imitation buffer (BI) and a sampling controller governed by ε. In MATCH (dense rewards), BI holds the single best trajectory by return. After collecting a new rollout in buffer D, SIPP computes a Sinkhorn distance between the state distribution of each state in D and BI’s trajectory, assigning priorities. During PPO’s minibatch sampling, with probability ε a batch is drawn uniformly; otherwise, it is drawn proportionally to these priorities, biasing updates toward high-value regions. In REPLAY (sparse rewards), BI stores up to L successful trajectories (sorted by return). While filling D, with probability ε a full successful episode is sampled from BI and added; otherwise, a fresh episode is collected with the current policy. Standard PPO updates then proceed on uniformly sampled minibatches from D. After collection, newly collected episodes are added to a candidate pool, and BI is updated to retain the top-L by return.

## Key Results
- MATCH surpasses PPO and baselines (SIL-PPO, SVPG-PPO, PER-PPO) on all 10 dense-reward MuJoCo tasks, achieving faster convergence and higher final returns.
- REPLAY yields large improvements in success rates on sparse-reward PointMaze navigation and partially observable Animal-AI Olympics tasks.
- Ablation studies identify ε ∈ {0.1, 0.3} as optimal across dense, sparse, and POMDP settings; extending SIPP with RND further improves performance on hard Atari games (e.g., +11.7% on Gravitar).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Optimal Transport-Based Prioritization for Dense Rewards
MATCH accelerates learning on dense-reward tasks by prioritizing PPO updates on state-action transitions whose state distribution is similar (via Sinkhorn optimal transport) to the best past trajectory. The core assumption is that in dense reward settings, nearby states yield similar returns (reward smoothness), so the best trajectory’s state distribution proxies high-value regions. For each state in the current buffer, a priority is computed as the negative average Sinkhorn cost to the best trajectory’s states. During PPO’s minibatch sampling, with probability ε a uniform draw occurs; otherwise, sampling is weighted by these priorities, biasing gradients toward promising regions. Evidence is provided by the algorithm description ([Section 4.1]) and experimental superiority over non-prioritized baselines.

### Mechanism 2: Trajectory Replay for Sparse and Binary Rewards
REPLAY dramatically improves success rates in sparse-reward and partially observable environments by uniformly replaying stored successful full trajectories. The assumption is that successful episodes contain necessary state-action sequences to achieve the goal, and repeated exposure via replay allows the policy to incorporate these patterns through gradient updates despite rare external rewards. The mechanism: with probability ε, a full successful episode from BI is sampled into the data buffer; otherwise, fresh data is collected. Standard PPO updates then occur on uniformly sampled minibatches, effectively mixing imitation and exploration. Evidence includes strong results on PointMaze and Animal-AI ([Section 5.4.2]) where vanilla PPO struggles.

### Mechanism 3: Imitation-Exploration Trade-off via ε
The single hyperparameter ε balances imitation and exploration in both MATCH and REPLAY. In MATCH, ε controls the probability of priority-based sampling versus uniform; in REPLAY, it controls the probability of sampling a replayed trajectory versus collecting new experience. This allows SIPP to adapt across reward regimes without structural changes. An ablation study ([Section 5.5]) shows ε ∈ {0.1, 0.3} often yields the best performance, indicating a narrow optimal range that depends on task difficulty and reward sparsity.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Policy network (actor-critic) → Data buffer D → Imitation buffer BI → [OT module (MATCH) / Replay sampler (REPLAY)] → Sampling controller (ε) → PPO update module → Updated policy
- **Critical path:**
  - *MATCH:* (1) Collect rollout τ with π_θ → store in D. (2) Update BI if τ’s return > current best. (3) Compute Sinkhorn coupling between D states and BI states → priorities dOT. (4) Inner loop: for each minibatch, with prob ε sample uniformly; else sample with probability ∝ dOT. (5) Perform PPO update → new θ. (6) Repeat outer loop.
  - *REPLAY:* (1) While filling D (2048 steps): with prob ε sample a full episode from BI → add to D; else collect episode with π_θ → add to D. (2) Uniformly sample minibatches from D. (3) Perform PPO updates → new θ. (4) Add newly collected episodes to candidate pool; update BI to keep top-L by return. (5) Repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - MATCH vs. REPLAY: MATCH uses distributional similarity (more targeted) but incurs O(TT') OT compute; REPLAY is cheaper but may need larger BI (L>1). MATCH suits dense rewards; REPLAY excels in sparse/POMDP.
  - Imitation buffer size: MATCH uses size 1 (memory-efficient, less diverse); REPLAY uses size L (stores multiple successes).
  - ε: Higher ε accelerates convergence on known behaviors but risks stagnation; lower ε encourages exploration but may slow learning. Ablation suggests ε=0.1–0.3 often optimal.
  - On-policy purity: SIPP reuses data within an epoch (multiple gradient steps), slightly off-policy but stable.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - ε too low: negligible gain over PPO; too high: performance plateau or drop.
  - OT numerical failure: Sinkhorn diverges if regularization too low or cost matrix extreme; monitor distance values.
  - Empty BI in REPLAY: no successful episode found → no imitation signal; may need longer exploration or adjust ε.
  - Partial observability pitfalls: MATCH may struggle on high-dimensional pixels without feature abstraction; REPLAY is more robust.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Dense-reward evaluation: Compare SIPP-MATCH vs. PPO and baselines on 10 MuJoCo tasks; observe consistent improvement in sample efficiency and returns.
  2. Sparse-reward evaluation: Run SIPP-REPLAY on PointMaze navigation tasks; show large success-rate gains.
  3. Partially observable evaluation: Apply SIPP-REPLAY to Animal-AI Olympics (POMDP); demonstrate mitigated policy instability and higher success than PPO.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does bootstrapping policy learning with few past experiences enhance sample efficiency and hard exploration across diverse tasks? *Basis:* Section 5 (page 5). *Why unresolved:* Prior self-imitation methods are off-policy or limited to specific reward structures; it is unclear whether an on-policy approach like SIPP generalizes across dense, sparse, and POMDP settings with minimal past data.
- **Open Question 2:** Can a single past successful behavior be sufficient to guide policy learning in complex sequential continuous control tasks? *Basis:* Section 5 (page 5). *Why unresolved:* Existing methods typically aggregate multiple trajectories; sufficiency of one trajectory for long-horizon or hierarchical tasks is uncertain.
- **Open Question 3:** Is replaying past successful trajectories sufficient for policy learning in multi-goal and partially observable, sparse reward settings? *Basis:* Section 5 (page 5). *Why unresolved:* Traditional self-imitation assumes full observability; REPLAY shows promise but lacks theoretical justification for POMDPs.

## Limitations
- Sinkhorn optimal transport parameters (entropy regularization ε_reg, iterations) are unspecified, affecting numerical stability and computational cost.
- The transformation from OT scores to sampling probabilities (e.g., linear shift vs. softmax) is ambiguous and may impact prioritization bias.
- Replay buffer composition is unclear: whether the rollout buffer contains only the latest episode or multiple episodes, and whether replayed episodes contribute to updating BI.

## Confidence
- MATCH mechanism (OT prioritization improves dense-reward learning): Medium
- REPLAY mechanism (trajectory replay improves sparse-reward success): Medium
- Single-parameter ε trade-off: Medium
- Overall superiority over baselines: High
