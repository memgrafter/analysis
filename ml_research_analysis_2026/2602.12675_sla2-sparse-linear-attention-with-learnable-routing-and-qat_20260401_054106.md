# SLA2: Sparse-Linear Attention with Learnable Routing and QAT
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12675
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12675
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** SLA2 achieves 97% attention sparsity with an 18.6× attention speedup while matching or improving video generation quality over full attention.

## Executive Summary
SLA2 addresses limitations in accelerating diffusion video models via sparse-linear attention. The prior SLA method used heuristic, suboptimal routing between sparse and linear attention branches and suffered from a misalignment in their combination formulation. SLA2 introduces three key improvements: (1) a learnable router that dynamically selects computations based on Q and K, (2) a direct combination using a learnable per-row scaling factor α that correctly aligns the sparse branch output with the full attention decomposition, and (3) quantization-aware training (QAT) with low-bit attention in the forward pass to reduce quantization error. Together, these enable high sparsity (up to 97%) with significant speedups (18.6×) while preserving or enhancing generation quality across standard video benchmarks.

## Method Summary
SLA2 replaces standard attention in a pre-trained video diffusion model (Wan2.1) through a two-stage fine-tuning process. First, a lightweight router R and a scaling vector α are trained to approximate full attention. R uses mean-pooled Q/K representations passed through learnable linear projections; a SoftTop-k operator (training) or hard Top-k (inference) produces a sparse mask M. The attention output is computed as O = α ⊗ Oₛ + (1-α) ⊗ Oₗ, where Oₛ is quantized sparse attention computed only on masked positions, and Oₗ is linear attention computed via per-block accumulation. During Stage 1, R and α are optimized to minimize MSE against full attention outputs. In Stage 2, R is frozen, and the entire model (including α) is fine-tuned end-to-end on the diffusion loss, with low-bit quantization applied only in the forward pass of Oₛ to train robustness while maintaining FP16 gradients.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 97% attention sparsity and an 18.6× attention kernel speedup.
- Matches or surpasses full-attention baseline video quality (VBench metrics) at high sparsities, and outperforms prior SLA and other sparse attention baselines at lower sparsities.
- The QAT scheme enables effective low-bit (e.g., INT8) inference without quality degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Learnable Router
A learnable router R dynamically assigns each attention computation to the sparse or linear branch. R compresses Q and K via mean pooling (reducing O(N²) routing cost), applies learnable projections (`projq`, `projk`) to produce a score matrix, and uses SoftTop-k (training) or hard Top-k (inference) to select the top *k*% positions per row as the sparse mask *M*. This routing is trained via gradient descent to minimize the attention approximation error. The core assumption is that adjacent tokens have similar distributions, making pooled representations sufficient for routing decisions, and that the optimal split depends on transformed Q-K interactions, not raw scores.

**Break condition:** If token distributions are highly non-smooth or projection learning fails to converge, routing may misassign computations.

### Mechanism 2: Direct α Combination
SLA2 uses a learnable per-row scaling factor α to combine outputs as O = α ⊗ Oₛ + (1-α) ⊗ Oₗ. This directly corrects the row-normalization mismatch between the renormalized sparse attention output Oₛ and the desired sparse component P₁V of full attention. In prior SLA, a compensating linear projection was needed; α eliminates this by aligning Oₛ with P₁V via element-wise scaling and ensuring proper row-normalized combination.

**Break condition:** If α varies significantly across rows within a head, a single vector per layer may be insufficient.

### Mechanism 3: Quantization-Aware Training (QAT)
During fine-tuning, the forward pass quantizes Q, K, P, and V to low-bit (e.g., INT8) per block to compute Oₛ, then dequantizes. The backward pass uses full-precision (FP16) to compute gradients, avoiding quantization-induced noise. This exposes the model to simulated low-bit operations, teaching robustness while preserving training stability.

**Break condition:** Very aggressive low-bit (e.g., 4-bit) may cause irreparable forward error; kernel implementations might not perfectly match simulated quantization (e.g., per-token vs. per-block scales).

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse-Linear Attention Decomposition:** Needed to understand the original SLA motivation (P = P₁ + P₂) and the row-normalization issue (α scaling) that SLA2 corrects. Quick check: Why does sparse attention output Oₛ need scaling by α? (Answer: Sparse attention renormalizes row probabilities to sum to 1; P₁ sums to α < 1 per row.)
- **Block-wise FlashAttention:** SLA2's efficient implementation uses block-wise processing to avoid full N² matrices; the router operates on pooled blocks (N/bq × N/bk), and kernels iterate blocks to skip computations. Quick check: How does block-wise processing reduce router cost? (Answer: Router operates on a compressed score matrix of size N/bq × N/bk instead of N×N.)
- **Quantization-Aware Training for Attention:** QAT adapts model parameters to low-bit forward operations during training, unlike post-training quantization. Quick check: Why does SLA2 perform low-bit quantization only in the forward pass during training? (Answer: To maintain stable FP16 gradients, avoiding noise from quantized gradients.)
- **Differentiable Mask Learning (SoftTop-k):** SoftTop-k enables gradient flow during Stage 1 while preserving hard Top-k structure for inference. Quick check: What is the purpose of temperature τ in SoftTop-k? (Answer: To control mask softness; lower τ approximates hard Top-k.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (Q,K,V) -> Router R (pooling -> proj_q/proj_k -> SoftTop-k/Top-k) -> compressed mask M_c (expanded to M) -> Sparse Branch (block-wise quantized matmul -> masked softmax -> quantized matmul -> dequantize Oₛ) and Linear Branch (per-block accumulate K^φ^T V and K^φ^T 1 -> normalize Oₗ) -> Combiner (α ⊗ Oₛ + (1-α) ⊗ Oₗ) -> Output O. Parameters: α (vector), proj_q, proj_k; shared φ activation.
- **Critical path (inference):** 1) Pool Q,K to ¯Q,¯K. 2) Compute P_c = proj_q(¯Q) proj_k(¯K)^T / √d. 3) Apply hard Top-k to P_c to get M_c. 4) For each block (i,j): if M_c[i,j]=1 compute quantized sparse attention block (Q_i,K_j,V_j) -> Oₛ_i; else accumulate K^φ_j^T V_j and K^φ_j^T 1 for Oₗ. 5) Normalize Oₗ_i per block. 6) Combine O = α ⊗ Oₛ + (1-α) ⊗ Oₗ.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Router pooling reduces cost but may lose fine-grained relationships; projections add parameters but enable adaptivity. α as a vector is efficient but may not capture per-row variability. Forward-only QAT preserves backward stability but requires careful kernel-train alignment. Block sizes (bq,bk) trade router resolution for overhead.
- **Failure signatures:** Quality drop at high sparsity (α/ routing mislearned); router collapse (M_c all-0/1); quantization artifacts (noise/banding); memory explosion (failure to skip computations).
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) **Sparsity-quality curve:** Fine-tune Wan2.1 with SLA2 at sparsities {85%,90%,95%,97%}; compare VBench metrics (IQ,OC,AQ,MS,SC,VR) vs. full attention and SLA. Expect SLA2 to match/exceed full attention up to 97%. 2) **Router ablation:** Replace learnable router with SLA's heuristic (Top-k on pooled QK^T) keeping α and QAT; compare quality at fixed sparsity (e.g., 95%). Expect significant drop, validating learnable routing. 3) **QAT ablation:** Train SLA2 without low-bit forward (full-precision forward) but infer with low-bit INT8; compare against full QAT training at same sparsity. Expect QAT to preserve quality, non-QAT to degrade.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Does SLA2 generalize to non-video models (e.g., LLMs, image DiTs)? The paper only evaluates video diffusion models; the locality assumption may not transfer.

### Open Question 2
Could a non-linear router (e.g., MLP) improve upon linear projections? The router uses simple linear proj_q/proj_k; alternatives with higher complexity might yield better splits but increase overhead.

### Open Question 3
Is the two-stage training with fixed router necessary, or would end-to-end training work? The paper uses two-stage training without comparing to joint training; joint training could adapt the router but may cause instability or train-inference mismatch.

## Limitations
- **Private dataset specifics:** Undisclosed video sources, preprocessing, and captioning details prevent exact replication of fine-tuning data distribution.
- **Low-bit implementation vagueness:** Exact bit-width (INT8/FP8), quantization scheme (per-tensor/channel), and kernel alignment with SageAttention2++ are unspecified, risking implementation mismatch.
- **Mechanism assumptions:** Pooling efficacy relies on adjacent token smoothness; α’s per-row constancy may fail if sparse probabilities vary widely within a head; QAT stability assumes forward-only quantization error is correctable via FP16 backward.

## Confidence
- **Learnable routing (Medium):** Evidence from section 4 and SoftTop-k design, but break conditions if distributions are non-smooth or projections fail.
- **α formulation (High):** Strong theoretical foundation from section 2.2 and Equations 16–17, directly correcting row-normalization mismatch.
- **QAT scheme (Medium):** Builds on established Attn-QAT work, but kernel-level details and potential train/inference drift require validation.
