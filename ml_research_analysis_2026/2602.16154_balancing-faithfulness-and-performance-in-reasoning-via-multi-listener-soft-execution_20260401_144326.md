# Balancing Faithfulness and Performance in Reasoning via Multi-Listener Soft Execution

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16154
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16154
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** REMUL simultaneously improves reasoning faithfulness (e.g., hint attribution +3.1%, early answering AOC +4.6) and task accuracy (up to +3.2%) by combining multi-listener reinforcement learning with masked supervised finetuning.

## Executive Summary
REMUL addresses the inherent trade-off between reasoning faithfulness and task accuracy in large language models. It introduces a two-stage framework: first, a speaker model generates reasoning traces that are evaluated by a diverse pool of listener models via "soft execution"—truncated prefixes are completed by listeners, and speaker rewards are based on consensus among listener answers. This encourages executable, faithful chains. Second, a masked supervised fine-tuning phase trains only the final answer tokens to recover and boost accuracy without overwriting the improved reasoning structure. Evaluated on multiple challenging benchmarks, REMUL achieves statistically significant gains in both faithfulness metrics (hint attribution, truncated/mistake AOC) and end-task accuracy, while producing more legible, concise, and linear reasoning traces.

## Method Summary
REMUL trains a speaker model through two decoupled phases. Phase 1 uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for reinforcement learning. For each prompt, the speaker generates a full chain-of-thought (CoT) and answer. The CoT is split by newlines into segments, and prefixes are created at approximately 25%, 50%, and 75% of the reasoning. Each prefix is fed to a fixed pool of heterogeneous listener models (Ministral-3, Phi-4-reasoning-plus, Qwen3), which continue generation to produce answers. The speaker’s reward `rmatch` is the sum of indicators where each listener’s answer matches the speaker’s original answer across all truncations. Phase 2 applies masked supervised fine-tuning: a LoRA adapter is trained on the speaker checkpoint, but the loss is computed only on the final answer tokens (all reasoning tokens are masked). This recovers correctness while preserving the reasoning patterns incentivized by the consensus reward. The LoRA is merged into the speaker for deployment.

## Key Results
- Hint attribution improved by up to 3.1% (BBEH).
- Early answering area over curve (AOC) increased by up to 4.6 points (ZebraLogicBench).
- Mistake injection AOC improved.
- End-task accuracy increased by up to 3.2% (FOLIO).
- Reasoning traces became more legible, shorter, and exhibited less backtracking.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Multi-Listener Consensus as a Faithfulness Signal
**Claim:** Training the speaker to generate reasoning traces that are consistently executable by a diverse pool of listener models improves reasoning faithfulness.
**Mechanism:** The speaker’s reward `rmatch` aggregates listener answer consensus across multiple truncated prefixes. A high consensus indicates the reasoning prefix is logically sound and interpretable enough for other capable models to follow and reach the same conclusion, even without seeing the final answer. Unfaithful traces (e.g., with leaps or post-hoc rationalizations) are less likely to be replicable.
**Core assumption:** Faithful reasoning is generally executable across model architectures; listener diversity tests against model-specific quirks.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract links clarity to listener followability; Section 2.2 defines `rmatch`; ablations (Table 4) show reduced gains with homogeneous listeners.
**Break condition:** If listeners are not diverse enough (e.g., all same architecture), the consensus signal weakens. If listeners are too weak relative to the speaker, they may fail even on faithful traces, yielding noisy rewards.

### Mechanism 2: Decoupled Correctness Regularization via Masked SFT
**Claim:** Optimizing for faithfulness via RL alone harms task accuracy; this trade-off is mitigated by separately regularizing for correctness using masked SFT applied only to final answer tokens.
**Mechanism:** After RL training, a LoRA adapter is trained with a loss computed solely on answer tokens. This teaches the model to correct its final decision boundary without altering the reasoning trace structure optimized by the consensus reward.
**Core assumption:** The faithfulness and correctness objectives conflict when combined in a single reward (as shown in Figure 3). Isolating the correctness signal to the answer channel allows it to improve accuracy while leaving the reasoning chain intact.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states correctness is maintained by finetuning only answer tokens; Section 2.3 defines masked loss; Section 3.4/Figure 3 show joint reward underperforms.
**Break condition:** If masking is faulty (answer tokens misidentified) or LoRA capacity is insufficient, accuracy may not recover. If RL introduces harmful reasoning patterns not confined to the answer head, masked SFT might not reverse them.

### Mechanism 3: Heterogeneous Listener Pool Necessity
**Claim:** Using multiple, architecturally diverse listener models (rather than multiple samples from a single model) is crucial for robust gains in both faithfulness and accuracy.
**Mechanism:** The listener pool consists of different model families (Ministral-3, Phi-4-reasoning-plus, Qwen3). The speaker’s reward aggregates consensus across these heterogeneous listeners, exposing its reasoning to varied execution styles and failure modes. This encourages generally executable and legible traces.
**Core assumption:** Different architectures have different inductive biases and knowledge gaps. A trace executable across a diverse set is more likely fundamentally sound.
**Evidence anchors:** Section 2.2 specifies the listener set; Section 4 (Table 4) ablations: homogeneous listeners reduce hint usage gains; single listener eliminates gains.
**Break condition:** If all listeners share a common weak knowledge area (e.g., domain-specific facts), consensus may remain low regardless of speaker faithfulness. Listeners must be similarly capable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: *Why needed:* The core RL algorithm that updates the speaker policy based on relative advantage within a group of rollouts, suited for multi-output reward settings like consensus. *Quick check:* How does GRPO’s advantage calculation differ from PPO, and why might it be more stable here?
- **Masked Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: *Why needed:* To inject correctness without altering RL-learned reasoning patterns by computing loss only on answer tokens. *Quick check:* What would happen if SFT loss were applied to the entire sequence (reasoning + answer)?
- **Soft Execution & Cross-Model Reasoning**: *Why needed:* The paradigm where a model’s reasoning trace is treated as a program executed by another model, forming the basis of the faithfulness reward. *Quick check:* How does model-based “soft execution” differ from “hard execution” (e.g., code interpreter), and what are the trade-offs for measuring faithfulness?
- **Faithfulness Metrics (Hint Attribution, AOC)**: *Why needed:* Quantitative measures to evaluate whether reasoning traces are actually used. Hint attribution tests sensitivity to inserted hints; AOC tests sensitivity to truncation or error injection. *Quick check:* What does a lower AOC for “Truncated CoT Answering” imply about the model’s reasoning process?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Speaker Model -> Truncation Module -> Listener Pool -> Execution & Reward Module -> RL Trainer (GRPO) -> Masked SFT Module -> Model Merger
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Initialize speaker `S` (pre-trained with CoT capability).  
  2. For each training step: sample prompts; generate full `(t, a)` from `S`.  
  3. Split `t` by newlines; create prefixes at ~25%, 50%, 75% lengths.  
  4. For each prefix, have every listener `L_i` continue to get `a'_j`.  
  5. Compute `rmatch = Σ Σ I[a'_j = a]`; use GRPO to update `S` (full-rank).  
  6. After RL, train LoRA on `S` with masked SFT loss (only on answer tokens).  
  7. Merge LoRA into `S` → final REMUL model.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Listener diversity vs. compute cost: More heterogeneous listeners improve faithfulness but increase rollout cost linearly; three models is a pragmatic balance.  
  - RL vs. SFT decoupling: Joint RL reward fails (Figure 3); decoupling adds a second training phase but is empirically necessary.  
  - Truncation granularity: Newline-level splits are used; sentence-level similar (Table 8). Finer splits (token-level) may be noisier; coarser splits may not test intermediate steps well.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Reward conflict:** If monitoring `rmatch` and accuracy during joint RL, both may stagnate/decrease (Figure 3). Signal need for decoupled training.  
  - **Accuracy drop from RL-only:** If after RL-only training, validation accuracy drops significantly (>2-3%), model overfit to consensus. Proceed to masked SFT.  
  - **Single listener ablation:** Using only one listener (or same-model listeners) diminishes or eliminates hint usage/AOC improvements (Table 4). Indicates diversity is key.  
  - **Listener-model mismatch:** If listeners are much weaker than speaker, `rmatch` may be near random, yielding no learning signal. Check baseline listener solo performance.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Comparison:** Train REMUL (RL + masked SFT) and all baselines on BBH subset (1250 samples). Evaluate on BBEH, ZLB, MuSR, FOLIO for accuracy and faithfulness metrics (hint usage, truncated/mistake AOC). Expect REMUL to show balanced gains (Tables 1, 2).  
  2. **Listener Diversity Ablation:** Train REMUL variants: (a) with 3 instances of the same listener type, (b) with only one random listener from the pool. Compare to full REMUL with 3 heterogeneous listeners. Expect (a) reduced faithfulness gains, (b) loss of all gains (Table 4).  
  3. **Domain Transfer Test:** Train REMUL on BBH (default) and on FOLIO training split. Evaluate both on FOLIO test. Expect BBH-trained model to show faithfulness gains with moderate accuracy; FOLIO-trained model to show larger accuracy gain but no faithfulness improvement (Table 7). Tests generalizability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Why does in-domain training improve accuracy but not necessarily faithfulness, and how can training data be designed to simultaneously boost both?**  
   *Basis:* The authors compare REMUL trained on BBH vs. FOLIO, noting FOLIO-trained REMUL yields higher accuracy on FOLIO but lower hint attribution and similar AOC. They suggest diverse training data improves robustness but not task accuracy. *Why unresolved:* The underlying mechanism linking data diversity to faithful reasoning execution is unclear; no ablation on data mixture diversity is provided.

2. **What specific properties of listener diversity (e.g., architecture, scale, training data, prompting style) are most crucial for the multi-listener reward to improve faithfulness?**  
   *Basis:* Ablations show heterogeneous listeners (Ministral, Phi-4, Qwen3) outperform homogeneous or single listeners. *Why unresolved:* The listeners differ along multiple axes (architecture, size, training data), so the driving factor of diversity is not disentangled.

3. **Can we design a single, unified training objective that eliminates the trade-off between faithfulness and correctness, removing the need for separate RL and masked SFT stages?**  
   *Basis:* Jointly optimizing `rmatch` (faithfulness) and answer correctness in one GRPO reward (“balanced rewards”) leads to competing signals and degraded performance. *Why unresolved:* The paper does not explore alternative unified objectives (e.g., multi-objective RL, adaptive weighting) or analyze why the conflict occurs (e.g., gradient interference).

4. **How does the truncation strategy (fixed percentage vs. adaptive or content-aware truncation) affect the quality of the soft execution signal and overall faithfulness gains?**  
   *Basis:* The method uses fixed truncation at 25%, 50%, 75% of reasoning lines. This choice is not justified, and alternatives are not tested. *Why unresolved:* Different reasoning structures may require truncation at logical breakpoints rather than fixed percentages to produce meaningful listener execution signals.

5. **To what extent do REMUL’s improvements in automated legibility metrics (e.g., autorater scores, backtracking reduction) correlate with human judgments of explanation clarity and usefulness?**  
   *Basis:* The authors measure legibility using an autorater and find improvements, but acknowledge faithfulness and legibility are related yet do not validate with humans. *Why unresolved:* Autoraters may capture superficial qualities (fluency) that do not align with human interpretability; human evaluation is needed to verify that gains translate to perceived clarity.

## Limitations
- **Listener bias risk:** If listeners share common biases (e.g., from similar training data), the consensus reward may not capture general executability and could reinforce shared errors.
- **Evaluation scope:** Results are primarily on multiple-choice logical reasoning benchmarks (BBEH, ZLB, MuSR, FOLIO); generalizability to open-ended, creative, or knowledge-intensive tasks is unproven.
- **Implementation underspecification:** Critical details (exact listener prompts, GRPO normalization, AOC computation) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication and possibly obscuring sensitivity to these choices.
