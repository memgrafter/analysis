# Speech-Worthy Alignment for Japanese SpeechLLMs via Direct Preference Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12565
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12565
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Preference-based alignment via DPO combined with SFT yields an 18% relative improvement in speech-worthiness (SpokenElyza: 3.44 vs 2.91) while preserving written-style performance (Elyza: 3.78 vs 3.97).

## Executive Summary
Japanese SpeechLLMs, which couple a frozen speech encoder (Whisper) with a textual LLM (Sarashina-2-7B), tend to generate written-style text unsuitable for TTS because of the stark divergence between spoken and written Japanese. This paper proposes a solution: align the model to speech-worthy outputs using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on preference pairs that contrast spoken and written responses, combined with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to maintain completeness. A spoken system prompt steers style, and only key-query and LayerNorm (KQ-LN) parameters are tuned globally to preserve general instruction-following. Evaluation on the new auditory-verified benchmark SpokenElyza shows an 18% relative gain, while scores on the written-style Elyza benchmark remain robust. Surface-form analyses confirm reductions in verbosity and non-vocalizable artifacts, yielding more TTS-friendly text.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds in three stages. **Modality alignment** first trains a shallow projector between a frozen Whisper-large encoder and the Sarashina-2-7B LLM on 100k speech-text pairs (e.g., ReazonSpeech), with only the projector updated. **Preference data construction** translates existing corpora (SpeechPref, InstructS2S-200K, DeepDialog) to Japanese, generates multiple model rollouts per instruction, scores each for speech-suitability using `gpt-oss-120B`, and filters pairs where the chosen response exceeds the rejected by a margin of 1.5×; TTS synthesis and auditory verification finalize the triples. **Preference fine-tuning** then starts from the pretrained model, trains the projector plus all KQ-LN parameters in the LLM, and uses a combined loss: \(L = w \cdot L_{\text{DPO}} + (1-w) \cdot L_{\text{SFT}}\) with \(w=0.99\). A spoken system prompt is prepended to every instruction during training and inference. Training runs for 2 epochs (LR=5e-6) on 8×H100. Performance is measured via LLM-as-judge (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) on Elyza and SpokenElyza, supplemented by surface-form metrics (word count, dependency depth, non-vocalizable characters).

## Key Results
- The proposed DPO+SFT with KQ-LN tuning and spoken prompt achieves a relative 18% improvement on SpokenElyza (3.44) over the pretrained baseline (2.91), while written-style performance on Elyza remains stable (3.78 vs 3.97).
- KQ-LN parameter selection outperforms top-layer fine-tuning, yielding higher SpokenElyza (3.44 vs 3.38) and less degradation on Elyza (3.78 vs 3.61).
- Surface-form metrics show significantly shorter sentences, lower dependency depth, and fewer non-vocalizable characters, confirming more TTS-friendly output.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: DPO Aligns via Preference Pairs
DPO directly optimizes the policy by increasing the likelihood of chosen (spoken) responses over rejected (written) ones while applying a KL constraint to a reference policy. This avoids training a separate reward model and efficiently steers outputs into the speech-suitable region. Evidence includes the explicit DPO loss (Equation 1) and ablations showing DPO+SFT outperforms either alone. Risk: noisy preferences or weak KL regularization can misalign the model.

### Mechanism 2: KQ-LN Tuning for Global Style Transfer
Tuning key-query matrices and LayerNorm parameters across all layers outperforms top-layer tuning because spoken-style differences (brevity, syntactic simplicity) are reflected in attention patterns and activation distributions rather than factual knowledge in feed-forward layers. KQ adjustments globally influence context selection; LayerNorm shapes activation distributions to support cohesive style change without disrupting semantics. Supported by KQ-LN’s smaller Elyza drop (3.78 vs 3.61) and higher SpokenElyza (3.44 vs 3.38). Limitation: if style gaps involve deep lexical reformulations, KQ-LN may be insufficient.

### Mechanism 3: Spoken Prompt Synergy with DPO+SFT
The spoken system prompt immediately activates the model’s latent capacity for conversational output. DPO then refines this signal using preferences, correcting over‑truncation; SFT ensures completeness. The triple combination yields the largest gains (PT+Prpt 2.94 → DPO+SFT+Prpt 3.44). Risk: a poorly worded prompt could conflict with preference data, or the base model may lack sufficient spoken‑style knowledge for the prompt to be effective.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Audio → Whisper-large (frozen) → Projector (trainable in pretraining) → Sarashina-2-7B LLM (frozen except KQ-LN parameters). Preference pipeline: instructions → model rollouts → `gpt-oss-120B` scoring → margin‑filtered pairs. Training loss: \(L = w·L_{\text{DPO}} + (1-w)·L_{\text{SFT}}\). Inference prepends spoken system prompt.

### Critical Path
1. Modality alignment: Train projector on ReazonSpeech (100k steps, LR=1e-4), freeze Whisper and LLM.
2. Preference dataset construction: Translate corpora, generate rollouts, score with LLM, filter on 1.5× margin, TTS‑synthesize for verification.
3. Preference fine‑tuning: From pretrained checkpoint, tune projector + KQ‑LN, set \(w=0.99\), add spoken prompt, train 2 epochs (LR=5e-6, 8×H100).
4. Evaluation: LLM‑as‑judge on Elyza/SpokenElyza; compute surface‑form metrics.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **DPO weight (w)**: Higher w boosts speech‑worthiness but risks harming written‑style capability.
- **Parameter selection**: TopLayers (fewer parameters) computationally cheaper but causes larger Elyza drop; KQ‑LN (more parameters) preserves knowledge better.
- **SFT inclusion**: Pure DPO (w=1) can yield incomplete responses; adding SFT ensures accuracy and completeness.
- **Prompt alone vs. combined**: Spoken prompt alone drastically truncates outputs; DPO+SFT balances brevity with richness.

### Failure Signatures
- Sharp Elyza score drop (>0.3 absolute) when w ≥ 0.99 signals over‑specialization.
- Word count < 50 with consistently high dependency depth suggests superficial shortening without structural simplification.
- KQ‑LN training with LR > 1e-5 or >2 epochs causes instability and regression.
- Preference pairs with small score margins (Δ < 0.3) yield negligible gains.
- Omitting spoken prompt at inference leads to inconsistent style adoption.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline characterization**: Evaluate the pretrained model on Elyza and SpokenElyza to quantify the initial performance gap and surface-form issues.
2. **DPO vs SFT ablation**: Train two models with identical hyperparameters but (a) w = 1 (DPO only) and (b) w = 0 (SFT only); compare to isolate each method’s contribution.
3. **Parameter selection study**: With w = 0.99 and spoken prompt, fine‑tune either the top 4 layers or KQ‑LN; measure both benchmarks to confirm KQ‑LN’s advantage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
To what extent does the proposed speech-worthy alignment approach generalize to languages with different magnitudes or types of written-spoken divergence? The paper focuses on Japanese due to its pronounced divergence; it is unknown if the same DPO-based recipe (KQ-LN tuning, high DPO weight) is optimal for typologically distinct languages where the style gap lies in different linguistic dimensions.

### Open Question 2
Is the KQ-LN parameter tuning strategy universally optimal for style transfer in SpeechLLMs? The paper compares KQ-LN only to top-layer tuning and hypothesizes that style transfer requires adjusting attention and normalization, but its performance relative to other efficient fine‑tuning methods (LoRA, IA³, etc.) remains unknown.

### Open Question 3
How do synthetic, translated preference data and the model’s self‑rolled preferences affect the fidelity and cultural authenticity of the learned spoken style for Japanese? The training data come from translated corpora and are scored by an LLM; a single‑expert auditory verification was used for the test set. The alignment between these automatically derived preferences and native Japanese spoken preferences is not fully validated.

### Open Question 4
What is the precise trade‑off curve between speech‑worthiness and general instruction‑following across the DPO loss weight (w) hyperparameter, and is the optimal w dataset‑ and task‑specific? The paper experiments with a few discrete w values; the full Pareto frontier and potential dataset‑specific optima are uncharted.

### Open Question 5
Can the auditory verification step in SpokenElyza construction be standardized and scaled beyond a single‑researcher correction, and how does the TTS system’s quality interact with the final speech‑worthiness of model outputs? The verification relied on one native researcher and one in‑house TTS, introducing potential bias and scalability limits; robustness to different TTS quality is unknown.

## Limitations
- **KQ-LN specification ambiguity**: The exact set of parameters included in "KQ-LN" (e.g., whether only k_proj/q_proj or also v_proj/o_proj, and which LayerNorms) is not precisely defined, risking inconsistent replication.
- **Undisclosed spoken prompt**: The exact wording of the spoken system prompt used during training and inference is omitted, making exact reproduction of the synergy effect difficult.
- **Missing DPO β value**: The KL penalty coefficient β (or its equivalent) is not reported, leaving the precise DPO loss scaling unclear and requiring guesswork during replication.

## Confidence
- **DPO alignment effectiveness**: High (empirical gains and ablations strongly support the mechanism).
- **KQ-LN superiority**: Medium (empirical advantage over top layers but no broader comparison or theoretical justification).
- **Synergy of prompt+DPO+SFT**: Medium (observed improvement but the prompt was always present during DPO+SFT training; a true isolation test is lacking).

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt dependency test**: Train DPO+SFT (w=0.99, KQ-LN) without the spoken prompt; evaluate with and without the prompt at inference to determine whether the prompt is essential for the learned style or only for steering.
2. **Parameter scope ablation**: Compare true KQ-LN (k/q + LayerNorm) against ablations with only k/q or only LayerNorm to isolate which component drives Elyza preservation and SpokenElyza improvement.
3. **β sensitivity sweep**: Fix w=0.99 and vary β (e.g., 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0) to monitor SpokenElyza, Elyza, and KL divergence; identify the β that maximizes the trade‑off between speech‑worthiness and instruction‑following.
