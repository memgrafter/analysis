# Quantifying LLM Attention-Head Stability: Implications for Circuit Universality

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16740
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16740
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Attention-head stability across model seeds varies with depth, is lowest in middle layers, and is improved by the AdamW optimizer; the residual stream remains consistently more stable than individual heads.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether mechanistic "circuits" discovered in transformer models are universal across independently trained instances (seeds) or are specific to a particular initialization. To quantify robustness, the authors introduce a stability metric that compares flattened attention-score matrices of identically configured attention heads across different model seeds using cosine similarity. Their large-scale study across numerous architectures and optimizers reveals that head stability is not uniform: it dips sharply in middle layers, worsens with model depth, and correlates with query-weight norm growth. Notably, the residual stream is far more stable than any single head, and using the AdamW optimizer substantially enhances head stability without sacrificing performance. These findings suggest that discovered circuits in less stable, mid-depth heads may be seed-specific artifacts rather than universal mechanisms, with significant implications for the reliability of mechanistic interpretability and AI safety monitoring.

## Method Summary
The core method quantifies the *cross-seed stability* of individual attention heads. For a given architecture, multiple model instances (seeds) are trained from different random initializations. For a fixed evaluation prompt set, the post-softmax attention score matrix `[seq_len, seq_len]` is extracted for every head in every layer of every seed. The stability score `S_hi` for a specific head `h_i` in an anchor model is computed by comparing its vectorized attention matrix to those of all heads in the *same layer* of every other seed. For each comparison seed, the maximum cosine similarity is taken (best-match). The average of these best-match similarities across all other seeds yields `S_hi`. Layer-level stability is the average of `S_hi` over all heads in that layer. This within-layer constrained matching isolates the consistency of a head's *functional role* across instances. The study extends to cross-layer matching, residual stream stability (measured via CKA on activations), and correlates stability with functional importance (post-ablation perplexity change) and internal properties like query-weight norms.

## Key Results
- Mid-layer heads (e.g., layer 5 in an 8-layer model) are the least stable (stability ~0.70) yet are the most representationally distinct from other layers.
- Instability peaks at mid-depth and intensifies with greater model depth.
- Unstable heads in deeper layers exhibit higher functional importance as measured by the increase in perplexity after ablation.
- The AdamW optimizer significantly improves head stability, especially in 8- and 12-layer models, with no performance penalty.
- The residual stream remains significantly more stable than individual attention heads across all tested architectures and optimizers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Larger mean ℓ₂ norms of attention-head query-weight matrices in a layer correlate with lower cross-seed stability for those heads.
- **Mechanism:** Norm growth during training reflects less constrained optimization paths, leading to divergent attention distributions across random initializations.
- **Core assumption:** Query weights directly shape the attention score distribution; uncontrolled norm expansion increases the variability of solutions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "A negative correlation between query-weight norm and head stability suggests norm growth contributes to instability."
  - [section 4.4.1] "We observe a clear negative correlation—layers with larger mean query-weight norm tend to exhibit lower stability."
- **Break condition:** If weight decay (e.g., AdamW) suppresses norm growth, the correlation weakens or disappears.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Longer input sequences reduce attention-head stability across seeds due to increased token-token interaction complexity.
- **Mechanism:** Extended prompts create more pairwise attention relationships, amplifying minor representation divergences between refits into larger dissimilarities in flattened attention matrices.
- **Core assumption:** Attention matrices encode pairwise token affinities; more tokens yield higher-dimensional, more variable interaction patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Instability findings are derived from evaluating on prompt sets; length effect is quantified.
  - [section 4.4.2] "Stability decreases sharply with the prompt length, indicating that longer prompts induce greater token-token interaction variability."
- **Break condition:** Short prompts (e.g., ≤5 tokens) maintain higher stability; the effect scales with length.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The residual stream remains more stable than individual attention heads because skip connections aggregate multiple heads, averaging out head-level divergences.
- **Mechanism:** Residual additions enforce a consistent representational baseline; even if specific heads vary, the summed output path remains functionally aligned across seeds.
- **Core assumption:** The residual stream’s role is to preserve information flow; its stability indicates that overall layer functionality is robust despite head idiosyncrasies.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The residual stream remains significantly more stable than the individual attention heads."
  - [section 4.7] "Across architectures and optimizers, the residual stream is consistently more stable than the corresponding attention heads."
- **Break condition:** If residual connections are altered (e.g., removed in attention-only models), the stability gap between residual and heads may narrow, but paper shows residual still more stable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Transformer attention heads**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper’s unit of analysis is the attention head; understanding its internal computation (query/key/value matrices, softmax over token pairs) is essential to grasp how stability is measured via attention score matrices.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the attention score matrix for a head depend on its query and key weight matrices?
- **Concept: Mechanistic interpretability & universality hypothesis**
  - *Why needed here:* The work questions whether discovered circuits are universal across model instances; this frames the stability investigation as a test of universality.
  - *Quick check question:* What does it mean for a circuit to be “universal” across different model seeds?
- **Concept: Optimization dynamics (Adam vs. AdamW, weight decay)**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper identifies AdamW as a key factor improving stability; understanding decoupled weight decay and its effect on norm growth is necessary to interpret results.
  - *Quick check question:* How does AdamW’s weight decay differ from L2 regularization in Adam, and why might it affect norm growth?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Multiple independently trained model refits (same architecture, different seeds) -> each model’s attention heads (per layer) -> attention score matrices (per head per prompt) -> residual stream activations (per layer) -> optimizer configuration (Adam/AdamW).
- **Critical path:** 1) Train N refits per architecture. 2) For each refit, compute attention score matrices for all heads on a fixed prompt set. 3) For an anchor refit, compare each head to all heads in the same layer of every pair refit using cosine similarity of flattened matrices. 4) Average best-match similarity across all pair refits to get head stability score. 5) Aggregate head scores to layer stability.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Attention score matrices vs. activations: Scores are directly comparable across seeds without alignment issues; activations may require permutation-invariant metrics (CKA) but can capture richer representations.
  - Layer-constrained vs. cross-layer matching: Constrained matching tests within-layer universality; cross-layer relaxes it but may conflate functional role shifts.
  - Prompt set selection: Must be diverse and fixed; length and content affect stability magnitudes.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High variance in stability scores across anchor refit choices (should be low given averaging).
  - AdamW not reducing norm growth as expected (verify activation norms).
  - Residual stream stability not exceeding heads in deeper models (contradicts paper’s consistent finding).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate the middle-layer stability dip in a 8-layer, 8-head MLP (Adam) using the paper’s cosine-similarity pipeline on 100 prompts; expect layer 5 stability ≈0.70 vs. layer 1 ≈1.0.
  2. Compare Adam vs. AdamW on a 12-layer GPT2-small; verify AdamW increases mid-layer stability without changing validation perplexity.
  3. Measure residual stream stability via CKA vs. head stability for the same architecture; confirm residual is consistently higher across layers.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does a more comprehensive set of training hyperparameters (beyond optimizer choice) systematically affect attention-head stability?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section states: "we did not attempt an exhaustive hyperparameter sweep... a study exploring and analyzing the relation between seed stability and a more comprehensive set of hyperparameters is left for future work."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper isolates architecture and optimizer but acknowledges many other factors (learning rate schedules, warm-up, etc.) could influence stability. Without this, the full "stability landscape" across training configurations is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A large-scale study systematically varying key hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, warm-up steps, dropout) across multiple seeds of fixed architectures, then measuring head stability to build a predictive model of stability from hyperparameters.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the mechanistic explanation for the observed negative correlation between attention-head query-weight norm and stability?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper reports a "clear negative correlation" between layer-wise mean query-weight ℓ2 norm and stability, and links this to prior work on norm growth and training instabilities, but does not explain *why* larger query norms specifically lead to less consistent head representations across seeds.
- **Why unresolved:** The correlation is correlational; the causal pathway (e.g., norm growth leading to sharper loss landscapes, reduced mode connectivity, or permutation sensitivity) is not investigated. This limits actionable interventions beyond using AdamW.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments manipulating query norms (e.g., via explicit regularization, initialization scaling) while keeping performance constant, then measuring stability. Analysis of loss landscape geometry (mode connectivity) or permutation invariance patterns between high- and low-norm heads could also clarify the mechanism.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why is the residual stream substantially more stable than individual attention heads, and what does this imply for the "universal" locus of circuit discovery?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Result (5) states the residual stream is "comparatively stable." The Conclusion suggests the shared residual pathway may nudge representations toward robustness, but the precise reason is not explained.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper speculates the residual connection provides a "shared pathway" but does not test whether stability arises from (a) the additive aggregation stabilizing representations, (b) the residual stream learning higher-level features less sensitive to low-level head variations, or (c) other components (e.g., MLPs) dominating stability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare stability of (a) pre-attention residual streams, (b) post-attention residual streams, (c) MLP outputs, and (d) final layer representations across seeds. Interventions that disrupt residual addition (e.g., ablating it in specific layers) could test its causal role in stabilizing downstream representations.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Do the "unstable" mid-layer heads, despite being functionally important in deeper models, correspond to semantically interpretable or human-understandable circuits, or are they simply high-dimensional, non-monosemonic features?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper finds unstable heads in deeper layers have higher functional importance (via ablation) and are most unique (Fig. 4). However, it does not probe whether these unstable-but-important heads are *interpretable*. The abstract questions whether circuits are "universal or seed-specific," implying interpretability may be fleeting if unstable.
- **Why unresolved:** A head can be functionally important but its mechanism might be complex, distributed, or not correspond to a human-understandable concept. Stability may be a prerequisite for reliable interpretability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply current interpretability methods (e.g., activation atlases, probing, causal scrubbing) to the unstable, important mid-layer heads across multiple seeds. If consistent, human-interpretable features/patterns emerge across seeds, they are universal; if each seed yields different explanations, the circuits may be epiphenomenal.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Does the mid-depth instability pattern observed in 8- and 12-layer models persist, intensify, or diminish in much larger transformers (e.g., 24+ layers, billions of parameters)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section cite "budget and resource considerations" as limiting model size and depth, preventing generalization of the "stability dip" to larger scales.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper shows instability peaks at mid-depth and intensifies with depth up to 12 layers. Transformer scaling laws may change internal representations; deeper models might develop more hierarchical or modular structures, potentially altering the stability profile.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train or obtain multiple seeds of larger decoder-only models (e.g., 24, 36 layers) at comparable compute budgets. Replicate the head-wise and layer-wise stability analysis to see if the mid-depth instability dip remains, shifts, or disappears. Correlate with other scaling trends (e.g., feature dimensionality, attention entropy).

## Limitations
- Exact evaluation prompt sets are not publicly released in the main paper, hindering direct numerical replication of stability magnitudes.
- Training hyperparameters for the 26 architecture/optimizer configurations are partially inferred from templates; exact seeds, learning rate schedules, and warm-up steps are not enumerated.
- Findings are restricted to relatively small decoder-only transformers (≤12 layers); generalization to larger models or different architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder, MoE) is unknown.
- The primary stability metric (flattened attention matrix cosine similarity) may miss functional nuances captured by other representation similarity measures.

## Confidence
- **Stability patterns (mid-layer dip, depth intensification, residual superiority):** High. Consistently observed across multiple architectures and metrics.
- **AdamW effect on stability:** High. Robust across model sizes and clearly demonstrated.
- **Query-weight norm correlation:** Medium. Correlation is clear and theoretically plausible, but causal mechanism is not established.
- **Prompt length effect:** Medium. Effect is quantified and logical, but not deeply mechanistically explored.
- **Functional importance of unstable heads:** Medium. Correlation is shown, but potential depth confounds and lack of interpretability analysis weaken causal claims.
- **Generalizability to larger models:** Low. Explicitly noted as a limitation due to resource constraints.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt diversity validation:** Replicate the stability analysis across different prompt corpora (e.g., varying domain, length distribution, syntactic complexity) to confirm the robustness of the mid-layer dip pattern and quantify sensitivity to prompt characteristics.
2. **Scale generalization test:** Apply the same stability pipeline to publicly available multi-seed checkpoints of larger models (e.g., 24-layer GPT-2, 30+ layer LLaMA variants) to verify if the mid-depth instability peak persists, shifts, or disappears at scale.
3. **Norm causality experiment:** Isolate the effect of query-weight norm by training identical architectures with Adam (high norm growth) while applying explicit regularization (e.g., explicit ℓ2 constraint on query matrices) to suppress norm growth, then measure resulting stability. A restoration of stability would strengthen the causal claim.
