# EmDT: Embedding Diffusion Transformer for Tabular Data Generation in Fraud Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13566
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13566
- **Reference Count:** 35
- **Primary Result:** EmDT achieves an F1-score of 0.849 on a credit card fraud dataset by generating synthetic fraud samples to address severe class imbalance.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the critical challenge of severe class imbalance in fraud detection, where traditional classifiers are biased toward the majority non-fraud class. The proposed EmDT framework introduces a cluster-guided diffusion process: it first uses UMAP to identify distinct fraud patterns, then trains separate Transformer-based diffusion models per cluster to generate high-fidelity synthetic fraudulent transactions. These synthetic samples are used to augment the training data for an XGBoost classifier. On a real-world credit card fraud dataset, EmDT sets a new state-of-the-art in downstream classification performance (F1=0.849) while maintaining a comparable privacy risk to other generative models and better preserving feature correlations.

## Method Summary
EmDT's pipeline begins with preprocessing a highly imbalanced transaction dataset (e.g., 0.172% fraud). From the training set's minority fraud samples, it applies UMAP for dimensionality reduction and KMeans clustering to discover multiple underlying fraud patterns (3 clusters in the paper). For each cluster, a dedicated diffusion model is trained. The core of each model is a Transformer denoiser that uses sinusoidal positional embeddings for both feature indices and diffusion timesteps. The forward diffusion process employs a linear β-schedule over 1000 timesteps. After training, synthetic fraud samples are generated proportionally to each cluster's size to double the original fraud count in the training set. This augmented dataset, balancing real and synthetic minority samples, is then used to train a final XGBoost classifier, with hyperparameters tuned via validation F1-score.

## Key Results
- Downstream classification: **F1-score 0.849**, recall 0.791, precision 0.916, balanced accuracy 0.895, outperforming multiple generative baselines.
- Privacy: Achieves a **Distance to Closest Record (DCR) score of 0.555**, indicating a comparable privacy risk to other examined generative models.
- Data Fidelity: Better preserves feature correlation structure compared to baselines like CTGAN and TVAE.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method fundamentally addresses the "needle in a haystack" problem of fraud detection. Standard oversampling (e.g., SMOTE) often creates unrealistic samples by interpolating between the tiny minority and vast majority. EmDT circumvents this by **clustering the scarce real fraud cases first**, ensuring synthetic generation is confined to learned, coherent fraud manifolds. The Transformer-based denoiser, with its attention mechanism, is well-suited for modeling complex, non-local dependencies in tabular data. Training separate models per cluster prevents mode collapse across dissimilar fraud patterns. Finally, using a powerful tree-based classifier (XGBoost) on this enriched dataset effectively learns a more robust decision boundary. The UMAP clustering is the critical first step that scopes the synthetic generation to realistic subpopulations.

## Foundational Learning
- **UMAP for Manifold Learning & Clustering:** Reduces high-dimensional tabular data to a lower-dimensional space to reveal cluster structure. *Why needed:* To identify distinct, separable patterns within the scarce fraud samples before generation. *Quick check:* Visualize 2D UMAP embedding of fraud samples; clusters should be visually distinct.
- **Diffusion Models for Tabular Data:** Iterative noising/denoising process to learn a data distribution. *Why needed:* To generate new, high-fidelity samples that match the complex distributions of real fraud data. *Quick check:* Train a single-cluster EmDT and generate samples; compare marginal histograms of key features (e.g., `V28`) between real and synthetic.
- **Transformer Denoiser:** Uses self-attention to denoise data at each diffusion step. *Why needed:* Captures feature interactions better than MLPs for complex tabular relationships. *Quick check:* Ablate the Transformer (replace with MLP) and measure degradation in synthetic data correlation preservation.
- **Sinusoidal Positional Embeddings:** Encodes timestep and feature index information. *Why needed:* Provides the model with a sense of sequence/order, which is crucial for the denoising trajectory and feature-wise processing. *Quick check:* Replace with learned embeddings and compare training stability/convergence.
- **XGBoost for Imbalanced Classification:** Gradient boosted trees with built-in handling of non-linearities and scale_pos_weight. *Why needed:* A strong, robust final classifier suited for tabular data that can effectively leverage the augmented training set. *Quick check:* Train on original (highly imbalanced) data vs. augmented data; recall should increase significantly without catastrophic precision loss.
- **Distance to Closest Record (DCR) for Privacy:** Measures whether synthetic samples are closer to training or test set members. *Why needed:* Quantifies membership inference risk; lower DCR (~0.5) suggests synthetic data is not simply memorizing training points. *Quick check:* Compute DCR for randomly generated noise vs. EmDT samples; EmDT should be notably higher than noise.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Real Fraud Training Data -> UMAP (2D) -> KMeans (3 clusters) -> [Per-Cluster EmDT Model 1, 2, 3] -> Synthetic Fraud Samples (proportional) -> Augmented Training Set -> XGBoost Classifier -> Evaluation Metrics (F1, DCR)

**Critical Path:** 1. UMAP clustering quality directly determines the diversity and fidelity of generated samples. 2. Individual EmDT model training must converge for each cluster without overfitting. 3. Synthetic sampling strategy must correctly proportion samples to avoid skewing the augmented dataset.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- *Clustering vs. Generation Complexity:* More clusters (higher k in KMeans) may capture purer patterns but require more generative models and risk unstable training on smaller per-cluster data.
- *Diffusion Timesteps (T):* Higher T (e.g., 1000) improves sample quality but slows generation. The paper's linear β-schedule is a standard tradeoff between noising speed and signal preservation.
- *TransformerSize:* Larger Transformer (more heads/layers) increases capacity to model complex interactions but raises overfitting risk on limited cluster-specific data.

**Failure Signatures:**
- *Cluster Failure:* UMAP+KMeans produces overlapping clusters or fewer than 3 stable clusters across runs, leading to less targeted generation.
- *Generation Failure:* Synthetic samples have wildly different marginal distributions (e.g., `Amount` all near zero) or correlation structures (e.g., all features uncorrelated) compared to real fraud.
- *Downstream Failure:* XGBoost fails to improve recall on the test set after augmentation, or precision collapses, indicating synthetic samples are either useless or harmful.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. **Cluster Stability Test:** Run UMAP+KMeans 10 times with different random seeds on the fraud training set. Compute Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) between cluster assignments. Target: ARI > 0.8 for consistent 3-cluster solution.
2. **Synthetic Data Fidelity Audit:** For a chosen cluster, generate samples. Compute Frobenius norm of the difference between real and synthetic feature correlation matrices. Target: Norm < 0.3 (relative to baseline methods). Also, perform Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests on key features (`V28`, `Amount`).
3. **Downstream Sanity Check:** Train XGBoost on (a) original imbalanced data, (b) data oversampled with SMOTE, (c) data augmented with EmDT synthetic samples (using default/hand-tuned params). Compare F1 and recall on validation set. Target: EmDT significantly outperforms (a) and (b) in recall with limited precision drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization to Other Domains:** Does EmDT generalize effectively to other high-stakes domains beyond credit card fraud, such as healthcare diagnostics, insurance fraud, or network intrusion detection? The paper only evaluates on one dataset.
- **Perp-Neg Integration:** Can incorporating Perpendicular Negative Prompting (Perp-Neg) into the diffusion process improve the fidelity of synthetic minority samples by preventing them from drifting toward the majority class distribution? This is proposed but untested.
- **Hybrid with Gaussian Copula:** Does combining EmDT's cluster-based diffusion with Gaussian Copula models enhance preservation of feature relationships and privacy compared to using either approach alone? This potential synergy is highlighted for future work.

## Limitations
- **Implementation Ambiguity:** Key parameters for UMAP (n_neighbors, min_dist), KMeans initialization, and final hyperparameters for EmDT/XGBoost are not specified, hindering exact replication.
- **Single-Dataset Evaluation:** Claims are based solely on one credit card fraud dataset, limiting evidence for generalizability across different data distributions and imbalance ratios.
- **Metric Reporting:** Performance metrics (F1, recall, etc.) lack statistical significance (e.g., standard deviation over multiple runs/splits), making the reported gains' robustness uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Core Methodology Description:** **Medium**. The high-level pipeline (UMAP -> cluster -> per-cluster diffusion -> XGBoost) is clear, but critical implementation details (hyperparameters, seeding, exact allocation) are missing.
- **Reported Performance Numbers:** **Low**. Values like F1=0.849 are presented without confidence intervals or variance across folds. The specific configuration that achieved them is unknown.
- **Privacy Claim (DCR=0.555):** **Medium**. The metric is defined and reported, but details like the number of nearest neighbors (k) and distance metric are unspecified, making comparison difficult.
- **Generalizability Statement:** **Very Low**. The paper explicitly states the limitation of single-dataset evaluation; any claim beyond credit card fraud is purely speculative.
