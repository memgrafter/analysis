# Probing Ethical Framework Representations in Large Language Models: Structure, Entanglement, and Methodological Challenges

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23659
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23659
- **Reference count:** 25
- **Primary result:** LLMs internally represent distinct ethical frameworks in partially entangled subspaces; conflict between deontological and utilitarian signals predicts behavioral instability, but probe interpretations are complicated by miscalibration and potential lexical confounds.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether large language models (LLMs) internally differentiate five major ethical frameworks—deontology, utilitarianism, virtue, justice, and commonsense morality. Using linear probes on hidden states across six LLMs (4B–72B parameters), the authors find evidence of structured but entangled representations. Cross-framework transfer is asymmetric (e.g., deontology→virtue transfers well, commonsense→justice fails), ruling out a single "acceptability" dimension. However, probes are highly confident yet inaccurate on out-of-framework tasks, indicating miscalibration. Crucially, the measured disagreement between deontological and utilitarian probes correlates with the entropy of model choices across multiple generations, suggesting that representational tension manifests as behavioral instability. These patterns persist across model scales and architectures, though dependence on surface lexical features cautions against over-interpretation.

## Method Summary
The core methodology involves training linear logistic regression probes on frozen hidden states (final non-pad token at a selected depth) to decode framework-specific signals from the ETHICS dataset. For each of the five frameworks, a probe is trained on a held-out subset. The primary analyses are: 1) cross-framework transfer evaluation, measuring accuracy and confidence when a probe trained on one framework is tested on another; 2) calibration assessment via expected calibration error (ECE); and 3) behavioral correlation, where the conflict between deontological and utilitarian probes (computed as |p_d−p_u|×min(c_d,c_u)) is correlated with the Shannon entropy of ten sampled generations per scenario at temperature 1.2. All patterns are replicated across six models of varying size and architecture.

## Key Results
- **Asymmetric transfer patterns** between ethical frameworks (e.g., deontology→virtue accuracy ~0.67 vs. commonsense→justice ~0.51) indicate differentiated, not collapsed, representational subspaces.
- **Probe miscalibration**: Cross-framework evaluations show uniformly high confidence despite low accuracy, with severe ECE, suggesting representations share superficial features that trigger confident but incorrect predictions.
- **Conflict-entropy correlation**: The disagreement score between deontological and utilitarian probes predicts choice entropy in model generations (e.g., Mistral-7B: r=0.362, p=0.004), implying internal representational tension leads to behavioral inconsistency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLMs encode ethical frameworks in differentiated but entangled representational subspaces, as evidenced by asymmetric cross-framework probe transfer.
- **Mechanism:** Linear probes trained on one framework’s activations partially transfer to related frameworks (e.g., deontology → virtue) but fail catastrophically on orthogonal ones (e.g., commonsense → justice). This indicates that frameworks share overlapping features for conceptually adjacent norms but remain distinct for divergent ones.
- **Core assumption:** Linear probes can decode framework-specific signals from frozen hidden states, and transfer accuracy reflects geometric similarity between representational subspaces.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "asymmetric transfer (e.g., deontology→virtue succeeds, commonsense→justice fails) rules out a single acceptability dimension."; [section 4.2] Transfer matrices show non-uniform accuracy; e.g., deontology→virtue ~0.67 while commonsense→justice ~0.51.
- **Break condition:** If transfer were uniform across all framework pairs, it would support a collapsed "good/bad" dimension instead of differentiated subspaces.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Probes maintain high confidence despite poor accuracy on out-of-framework tasks, indicating representations share surface-structural features that trigger confident but incorrect predictions.
- **Mechanism:** Probe confidence (distance from 0.5 probability) remains high even when accuracy drops on cross-framework evaluation. This miscalibration (high ECE) suggests that different frameworks activate similar activation patterns that the probe misinterpret as familiar, preventing reliable detection of framework mismatch.
- **Core assumption:** Probe confidence reflects representational similarity to training distribution; overconfidence under shift indicates insufficient abstraction.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.2] "Probes maintain uniformly high confidence even during severe transfer failures... representations for different frameworks share structural features sufficient to trigger confident (but incorrect) predictions."; [section 3.2] ECE analysis confirms severe miscalibration on off-diagonal transfers.
- **Break condition:** If probes were well-calibrated (confidence ≈ accuracy) across transfers, it would indicate cleanly separated subspaces with reliable OOD detection.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Disagreement between deontological and utilitarian probes predicts higher behavioral entropy in model generations, suggesting internal representational tension manifests as output instability.
- **Mechanism:** Conflict score C = |p_d − p_u| × min(c_d, c_u) captures scenarios where both frameworks make confident opposing predictions. Higher C correlates with greater choice entropy across multiple samples from the same prompt, implying that competing internal signals cause stochastic final outputs.
- **Core assumption:** Probe-detected conflict is behaviorally relevant; i.e., representational disagreement at 90% depth propagates to generation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Conflict between deontological and utilitarian probes correlates with choice entropy (Mistral-7B: r=0.362, p=0.004; similar effects in Qwen-7B and Llama-70B)."; [section 4.3] Replication across architectures with medium effect sizes (d≈0.54–0.58).
- **Break condition:** If correlation vanished after controlling for scenario difficulty (e.g., perplexity), it would suggest conflict merely co-occurs with difficulty rather than causing instability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Linear probing methodology**
  - *Why needed:* The entire analysis relies on training linear classifiers to decode framework-specific signals from hidden states; misunderstanding probe limitations (e.g., capacity, causality) would invalidate structural claims.
  - *Quick check:* What does high cross-framework transfer accuracy imply about representational geometry? (Answer: shared features between frameworks.)
- **Concept: Ethical framework taxonomy (deontology, utilitarianism, virtue, justice, commonsense)**
  - *Why needed:* Asymmetric transfer patterns hinge on conceptual adjacencies (e.g., deontology and virtue both focus on rules/character vs. justice/commonsense divergence).
  - *Quick check:* Why might deontology probes transfer to virtue but not to justice? (Answer: overlapping focus on non-consequentialist norms.)
- **Concept: Behavioral entropy as inconsistency metric**
  - *Why needed:* The conflict-entropy link uses Shannon entropy over 10 sampled choices per scenario to quantify output variability; confusing this with lexical diversity would misalign evidence.
  - *Quick check:* What does an entropy of 1.0 indicate for binary choices? (Answer: perfectly split decisions, maximum inconsistency.)
- **Concept: Probe calibration (ECE)**
  - *Why needed:* High confidence with low accuracy indicates miscalibration, a key finding that signals representational overlap not captured by accuracy alone.
  - *Quick check:* What does a high ECE value signify? (Answer: gap between predicted confidence and actual accuracy, indicating over- or under-confidence.)
- **Concept: Cross-framework transfer**
  - *Why needed:* The asymmetry of transfer matrices is the primary evidence for differentiated subspaces rather than a single moral dimension.
  - *Quick check:* If framework A probes transfer perfectly to framework B but not to C, what does that suggest about B vs. C relative to A? (Answer: B is representationaly closer to A than C is.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input → Tokenization → Frozen LLM (all layers) → Hidden state extraction (final non-pad token) → Linear probe (logistic regression, L2) → Probability p, confidence c=2|p−0.5| → Conflict score C. Behavioral branch: same prompt → 10 generations (T=1.2) → Choice extraction → Entropy H.
- **Critical path:** 1) Extract layer-wise activations for all scenarios. 2) Train probes per framework on training split. 3) Evaluate cross-framework transfer at 65% depth (accuracy/confidence/ECE). 4) Train deontology/utilitarianism probes at 90% depth. 5) Compute conflict scores, bin scenarios, generate responses, compute entropy. 6) Correlate conflict vs. entropy.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Depth selection (65% for transfer maximizes generalization; 90% for conflict maximizes specialization). Linear probes favor interpretability over expressivity; may miss non-linear signals. Temperature T=1.2 balances diversity/fluency; higher T may inflate entropy artificially.
- **Failure signatures:** 1) Uniform transfer matrix (collapsed dimension). 2) Low conflict scores across all scenarios (failed differentiation). 3) No correlation between conflict and entropy (confound dominance). 4) Paraphrase sensitivity in probe predictions (surface reliance).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce cross-framework transfer matrix for one model (e.g., Mistral-7B) at layer ⌊0.65×L⌋; verify asymmetric patterns (deontology→virtue > commonsense→justice).
  2. Compute ECE for off-diagonal entries; confirm high miscalibration (ECE > 0.4) despite within-framework good calibration.
  3. For deontology/utilitarianism probes at 90% depth, bin scenarios by conflict quartiles; run generation (10 samples each) and measure entropy; test correlation (should match r≈0.3–0.36).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1  
- **Question:** Does the correlation between probe-defined ethical framework conflict and behavioral entropy persist after statistically controlling for scenario difficulty?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly note in the Limitations and Conclusion that "partial correlation analyses controlling for perplexity, logit margin, and output-level uncertainty are needed" to isolate the effect of representational conflict.  
- **Why unresolved:** The reported correlation (r≈0.36) could be explained by difficulty-related factors that simultaneously increase both probe disagreement and choice entropy; without controlling for these, the claim that internal conflict causes behavioral inconsistency remains speculative.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform regression analyses or partial correlation tests that include measures of scenario difficulty (e.g., perplexity, length, ambiguity) as covariates. A significant residual effect of conflict would support its unique contribution.  

### Open Question 2  
- **Question:** Can activation interventions based on probe-derived ethical framework directions causally influence model behavior to confirm the functional relevance of these representations?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that "future work should validate the conflict-behavior link causally through activation interventions," noting that their preliminary steering experiments showed changes in position preference but not ethical accuracy.  
- **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether the lack of causal effect in initial steering attempts reflects a fundamental limitation of linear probe directions or merely suboptimal intervention methods. Without successful causal manipulation, the probes may only capture task artifacts rather than behaviorally relevant representations.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically apply activation steering (or other interventions) using probe directions across layers and measure effects on ethical accuracy and consistency. If steering can reliably shift judgments in a way that aligns with the targeted framework (e.g., increasing utilitarian choices when steering along utilitarian probes), it would establish a causal link.  

### Open Question 3  
- **Question:** Do the representational entanglement patterns (asymmetric transfer, miscalibration) generalize to other moral reasoning benchmarks and diverse cultural contexts beyond the Western-centric ETHICS dataset?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors acknowledge in Limitations that their dataset scope is limited and call for validation on "diverse moral reasoning benchmarks and culturally varied scenarios to assess true generality."  
- **Why unresolved:** The ETHICS benchmark may embed specific framing and cultural assumptions; different datasets or cultures could yield different entanglement structures. Without cross-dataset validation, the findings risk being dataset-specific artifacts.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the probing pipeline on multiple moral reasoning benchmarks (e.g., Moral Foundations, MCT) and on culturally diverse scenarios. Consistent patterns across sources would support generality; systematic variations would provide insights into cultural or dataset-specific effects.

## Limitations
- Linear probes may primarily capture lexical or stylistic correlates rather than genuine ethical representation.
- Asymmetric transfer could be driven by dataset artifacts (e.g., overlapping scenario content) not fully ruled out.
- The conflict-entropy correlation is vulnerable to confounding variables such as scenario difficulty or inherent moral ambiguity.

## Confidence
- **Differentiated subspaces (asymmetric transfer):** Medium
- **Probe miscalibration (high confidence/low accuracy):** Medium-High
- **Conflict predicts entropy:** Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Lexical-orthogonality test:** Construct a version of the dataset where each ethical framework is expressed in a distinct, non-overlapping vocabulary (via expert paraphrasing or controlled substitution). Re-evaluate cross-framework transfer; loss of asymmetry would implicate lexical features.
2. **Scenario-difficulty adjustment:** Obtain a difficulty estimate per scenario (e.g., model perplexity or human disagreement) and compute the partial correlation between probe conflict and entropy after removing difficulty effects. A significant residual would support a direct link.
3. **Non-linear probe robustness:** Train small MLP probes (1–2 hidden layers) on the same frozen states and repeat all core analyses. Persistence of the three patterns would alleviate concerns that linearity artificially shapes the results.
