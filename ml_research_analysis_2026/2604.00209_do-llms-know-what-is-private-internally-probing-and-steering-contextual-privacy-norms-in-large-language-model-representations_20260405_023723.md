# Do LLMs Know What Is Private Internally? Probing and Steering Contextual Privacy Norms in Large Language Model Representations
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2604.00209
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00209
- **References:** 34
- **Result:** CI-parametric steering reduces private information leakage from 42.5% to near‑zero (0.0%–2.6%) on benchmarks, while monolithic steering often increases leakage.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) internally encode contextual privacy norms as defined by Contextual Integrity (CI) theory. By probing four instruction‑tuned models, the authors find that the three key CI parameters—information type, recipient, and transmission principle—are represented as linearly separable, functionally independent directions in the model’s residual stream. Despite this strong internal signal, models leak private information in up to 42.5% of open‑ended scenarios, revealing a gap between representation and behavior. To bridge this gap, the authors introduce CI‑parametric steering, which independently intervenes along each CI axis. On synthetic data, it lowers leakage from 42.5% to 5% (PPI 98.8%). On two challenging benchmarks, CONFAIDE and PrivaCI‑Bench, it achieves near‑zero leakage (0.0%–2.6%) and transfers robustly across models, whereas monolithic steering often increases leakage. Subspace selectivity tests confirm the representational independence of the CI dimensions, demonstrating that structured, compositional intervention yields more reliable privacy control.

## Method Summary
The authors generate synthetic scenarios grounded in CI theory, systematically varying one of the three norm‑determining parameters while holding others constant. From these, they extract last‑token residual activations of LLMs and fit linear probes (logistic regression and PCA) to obtain a direction for each CI parameter. They validate that these directions lie in near‑orthogonal subspaces via a cross‑projection selectivity test. For behavioral control, they apply activation steering: at inference, they add a scaled sum of the three directional vectors (α · ∑ v(p)) to the activations at the top‑k layers identified by probe accuracy. The effectiveness is evaluated by prompting the model with privacy‑sensitive queries and using a GPT‑4o‑mini judge to classify outputs as leaked, refused, or appropriate. Performance is measured by leakage rate, norm compliance rate (NCR), and privacy protection improvement (PPI).

## Key Results
- The three CI parameters are encoded as linearly separable, functionally independent directions in LLM activation space, confirmed by subspace selectivity (diagonal accuracy > 90%, off‑diagonal ≈ 20%).
- Models exhibit a “privacy awareness gap”: strong probe accuracy (near > 95% AUROC) coexists with high behavioral leakage (up to 42.5%).
- CI‑parametric steering reduces leakage to near‑zero (0.0%–2.6%) on CONFAIDE and PrivaCI‑Bench and transfers robustly across models, while monolithic steering often fails or increases leakage.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**1. Independent Linear Subspaces**  
*Claim:* The three CI parameters are encoded as functionally independent linear directions in the residual stream.  
*Mechanism:* Contrastive probe data isolates each parameter; linear models trained on this data separate variations for that parameter while others are held constant, yielding near‑orthogonal subspaces as measured by cross‑projection accuracy.  
*Evidence:* Abstract and section demonstrate near‑perfect classification on own categories but ~20% on others; related work on concept subspaces supports non‑monolithic encoding.  
*Break condition:* If off‑diagonal selectivity exceeds 50%, subspaces are entangled and parametric steering loses its advantage.

**2. Privacy Awareness Gap**  
*Claim:* Despite strong internal privacy representations (high probe AUROC), models leak private information in up to 42.5% of scenarios.  
*Mechanism:* The privacy signal fails to be properly amplified during autoregressive generation, creating a misalignment between static representation and dynamic decision‑making.  
*Evidence:* Abstract and results show high probe accuracy yet high leakage; analogous to factuality‑behavior gaps in prior work.  
*Break condition:* If behavioral compliance approaches probe accuracy (>95% with <5% probe error), the gap is closed.

**3. Compositional Steering Advantage**  
*Claim:* CI‑parametric steering reduces leakage more robustly than monolithic steering by intervening independently along each CI axis.  
*Mechanism:* A monolithic privacy direction compresses the multi‑dimensional CI structure and may overfit to specific parameter interactions; parametric steering recombines independent axes to handle diverse violation patterns.  
*Evidence:* Abstract and ablations show monolithic steering often increases leakage, while combining all three axes reduces it to 2.6% on CONFAIDE and transfers well.  
*Break condition:* If monolithic steering consistently matches or outperforms parametric steering across all α and datasets, the compositional hypothesis fails.

## Foundational Learning
### Linear Probing
**Why needed:** Extracts linearly decodable directions for abstract concepts from frozen model activations, enabling identification of the privacy signal without modifying the model.  
**Quick check:** If a linear probe achieves high AUROC on a concept, what does that imply about the geometry of that concept in the model’s representation space?

### Contextual Integrity (CI) Theory
**Why needed:** Provides the theoretical framework of privacy as appropriate information flow governed by five parameters; focusing on the three norm‑determining ones (info type, recipient, transmission principle) allows decomposition of the privacy representation.  
**Quick check:** Which three CI parameters jointly determine whether an information flow is appropriate, and why are the other two (data subject, sender) treated differently in the steering design?

### Inference‑time Activation Steering
**Why needed:** Serves as the intervention method: by adding a pre‑computed direction to activations during inference, it allows rapid, reversible hypothesis testing and deployment without retraining.  
**Quick check:** How does additive steering (h′ = h + α·v) differ from weight‑based methods like LoRA in modification scope and transferability?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Scenario templates → LLM (extract last‑token residual activations) → Probe module (fit linear directions per CI parameter) → Steering module (add α·∑v(p) at top‑k layers) → Generated output → Evaluator (GPT‑4o‑mini judge)

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate synthetic contrastive probe data isolating each CI parameter.  
2. Extract layer‑wise last‑token activations and fit per‑parameter directions (via PCA or supervised probe).  
3. Validate subspace independence via cross‑projection selectivity test.  
4. Apply CI‑parametric steering to behavioral prompts; evaluate leakage/NCR on held‑out benchmarks (CONFAIDE, PrivaCI‑Bench).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Parametric vs. monolithic: Parametric steering adds complexity (3 directions × layers) but gains robustness; monolithic is simpler but can catastrophically increase leakage on transfer.  
- Steering strength (α): Higher α reduces leakage but risks utility loss (lower NCR, coherence). CI‑parametric is more stable across α.  
- Layer selection: Using top‑k probe‑accuracy layers is effective; using all layers may add noise.  
- Parameter scope: Including all five CI parameters dilutes signal; stick to the three norm‑determining parameters.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Subspace selectivity test shows >40% off‑diagonal accuracy → subspaces not independent → parametric steering likely fails.  
- Steering increases leakage on synthetic data → α too high or directions contaminated by non‑privacy signals (e.g., sentiment).  
- High leakage on CONFAIDE despite low synthetic leakage → directions overfit to synthetic templates; need more diverse probing data.  
- NCR drops sharply with small α → utility collapse; adjust α downward or consider per‑parameter tuning.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replicate subspace selectivity:** On your target model, generate CI‑parametric probe examples, fit per‑parameter LDA subspaces, and compute cross‑projection accuracy. Expect diagonal >90%, off‑diagonal ~20%.  
2. **Basic steering on synthetic:** Apply monolithic and CI‑parametric steering to 200 synthetic behavioral scenarios. Plot leakage vs. α. Expect CI‑parametric to dominate the Pareto frontier and show monotonic improvement.  
3. **Transfer to CONFAIDE:** Apply directions extracted from synthetic probes to CONFAIDE Tier 3 without further tuning. Expect monolithic to increase leakage at low α, CI‑parametric to reduce it; if both fail, probing data diversity may be insufficient.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**1. Adversarial robustness**  
*Question:* How does CI‑parametric steering perform under adversarial and dynamically evolving attack settings?  
*Why unresolved:* Only static benchmarks were evaluated; adversarial robustness remains untested.  
*Resolution:* Conduct adaptive red‑team simulations that iteratively probe for leakage while adjusting attack strategies to measure stability and effectiveness.

**2. Mechanism of the privacy awareness gap**  
*Question:* What mechanism causes the privacy awareness gap (strong internal encoding but frequent leakage)?  
*Why unresolved:* The paper does not analyze the generation process linking internal privacy signals to output.  
*Resolution:* Ablate specific layers/heads during generation, probe output distributions, or correlate internal privacy signals with generation choices to locate the breakdown.

**3. Incorporating sender and data subject parameters**  
*Question:* Can data subject and sender parameters be incorporated into CI‑parametric steering without causing interference that increases leakage?  
*Why unresolved:* Five‑parameter expansion raised leakage (e.g., Llama‑2 from 52% to 88%) and harmed transfer; no exploration of alternatives.  
*Resolution:* Systematically vary inclusion and scaling of these parameters across models, measure leakage and utility, and analyze their representation.

**4. Automatic tuning of per‑parameter steering strengths**  
*Question:* How can we automatically tune per‑parameter steering strengths (αₚ) to balance privacy protection and output utility?  
*Why unresolved:* The paper notes combining three axes reduces leakage but incurs utility loss, suggesting per‑parameter tuning but leaves it unexplored; the privacy‑utility trade‑off is uncharacterized.  
*Resolution:* Perform a grid or Bayesian search over αₚ on a validation set, evaluating both leakage and utility (e.g., helpfulness scores) to derive a Pareto‑optimal operating region.

## Limitations
- Dynamic top‑layer selection depends on probe accuracy and unreported random seeds, harming reproducibility.
- Evaluation relies on GPT‑4o‑mini as a judge without quantified agreement, introducing potential bias.
- Synthetic probe data may not capture the complexity of real‑world privacy contexts, limiting generalisation.

## Confidence
- Independent linear subspaces: **High** (strong subspace selectivity evidence)
- Privacy awareness gap: **High** (consistent behavioral leakage despite probe accuracy)
- CI‑parametric steering effectiveness: **High** (robust leakage reduction across benchmarks)
