# MealRec: Multi-granularity Sequential Modeling via Hierarchical Diffusion Models for Micro-Video Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01926
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01926
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MealRec outperforms ten baselines on four micro-video recommendation datasets, achieving up to 17.27% relative improvement in Hit Ratio and NDCG.

## Executive Summary
MealRec introduces a hierarchical diffusion framework for micro-video sequential recommendation, addressing two key challenges: preference-irrelevant content within videos and inherent modality conflicts in user interactions. It proposes Temporal-guided Content Diffusion (TCD) to refine intra-video frame representations using temporal dynamics and user-specific visual context, and Noise-unconditional Preference Denoising (NPD) to recover clean user preferences from noisy sequential signals without diffusion timestep conditioning. Extensive experiments on Microlens and Shortvideo datasets show state-of-the-art performance, with ablations confirming the complementary roles of TCD and NPD. The approach also demonstrates robustness to visual feature noise and generalizes across multiple Transformer-based backbones.

## Method Summary
MealRec processes user interaction sequences of micro-videos, each preprocessed to extract multimodal features: frame sequences (`F_i`) via VideoMAE, global visual embeddings (`v_i`), and text embeddings (`h_i`) via BERT or GloVe. For each video in a sequence, TCD applies a diffusion-based U-Net to denoise frame-level features. The forward diffusion gradually corrupts `z0` (encoded frames); the reverse process predicts clean frames conditioned on a recency-weighted aggregation of the user’s prior visual embeddings (`c_v`). The refined frame representation (`ˆz0`) is pooled to obtain an item embedding (`e'_k`). These embeddings form a sequence fed into a sequential encoder (e.g., SASRec) to produce an initial user preference representation (`x0`). NPD then denoises `x0` using a self-attention network that does **not** receive the diffusion timestep `t`. Instead, it conditions on the latest item’s TCD output (`ˆz0^L`) and its text (`h^L`). The cleaned user representation (`ˆx0`) scores candidate items via dot product with their multimodal embeddings. Training minimizes a hybrid loss: next-item prediction cross-entropy (`L_R`), TCD reconstruction loss (`L_T`), and NPD reconstruction loss (`L_N`), combined as `L_total = L_R + λ_T L_T + λ_N L_N`.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art performance on Microlens-small/big and Shortvideo-small/big, surpassing ten competitive baselines.
- Top scores in Hit Ratio (H@10, H@20) and NDCG (N@10, N@20), with relative improvements up to 17.27%.
- Ablation studies confirm both TCD and NPD are individually effective and their combination yields complementary gains.
- Robustness experiments show resilience to added visual feature noise.
- Universality tests demonstrate consistent gains when plugging into SASRec, CL4SRec, and TedRec backbones.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MealRec’s effectiveness arises from its hierarchical diffusion design that disentangles noise at two granularities.

**Temporal-guided Content Diffusion (TCD)** mitigates preference-irrelevant content within a video. It treats irrelevant frames as noise and uses a diffusion process guided by the user’s historical visual context (`c_v`). The forward diffusion corrupts frame features; the U-Net denoiser predicts clean features conditioned on `c_v` and the noise level. This aligns intra-video representations with the user’s interests. Evidence: Section 4.3 defines `c_v` (Eq.7) and the objective `L_T` (Eq.11); ablation in Section 5.3 shows removing TCD harms performance.

**Noise-unconditional Preference Denoising (NPD)** addresses noisy inter-video interactions (e.g., accidental clicks). It takes the sequential encoder’s output (`x0`) as a noisy preference embedding, corrupts it, and recovers the clean embedding with a self-attention denoiser **without** seeing the diffusion timestep. Instead, it conditions on the latest item’s TCD output (`ˆz0`) and text (`h_L`), using cross-modal consistency as a stable prior. Evidence: Section 4.4 describes the blind denoising and loss `L_N` (Eq.14); ablation confirms NPD’s additive contribution with TCD.

**Hierarchical multi-granularity denoising** allows specialization: TCD handles fine-grained temporal patterns within items, while NPD cleans coarse-grained user preferences across the sequence. Their joint optimization yields complementary benefits, as shown by the full model’s superiority over ablations (Section 5.3). Limitations include sensitivity to mismatched multimodal content and reliance on sufficient historical context.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion Probabilistic Models**  
  *Why needed:* TCD and NPD rely on diffusion processes for representation denoising; understanding forward diffusion, reverse denoising, and training objectives (e.g., predicting noise vs. data) is essential.  
  *Quick check:* In a diffusion model, what is the purpose of the noise schedule `{β_t}`, and how does it affect the distribution of `x_t`? Alternatively, what does the reverse process typically predict (`ε` or `x0`), and why might one choose one over the other?

- **Sequential Recommendation with Self-Attention**  
  *Why needed:* The backbone (e.g., SASRec) models user behavior sequences using self-attention; knowledge of positional encoding and masked attention is necessary to understand how preferences are encoded.  
  *Quick check:* In SASRec, why is positional encoding added to item embeddings, and how does the masked self-attention ensure autoregressive modeling?

- **Multimodal Feature Extraction for Videos**  
  *Why needed:* MealRec uses pre-trained encoders (VideoMAE for frames, BERT/GloVe for text) to obtain multimodal features; understanding their output dimensions, frame sampling, and fusion is important.  
  *Quick check:* What are the advantages of using a pre-trained video encoder like VideoMAE over training from scratch for micro-video recommendation? How does uniform sampling at 1 fps affect temporal resolution?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Preprocessor -> TCD Module -> Sequential Encoder -> NPD Module -> Recommendation Head

**Critical path (training):**  
1. Sample a batch of user sequences.  
2. For each position `k`, compute recency-weighted visual context `c_v^k` from prior items.  
3. Run TCD on all items to obtain refined frame representations `ˆz0^k` and pooled embeddings `e'_k`.  
4. Feed the sequence `[e'_1,…,e'_L]` into the sequential encoder to get initial user representation `x0`.  
5. Apply NPD to `x0` using the latest item’s `ˆz0^L` and its text `h^L` to get clean user representation `ˆx0`.  
6. Compute losses: `L_R` (next-item cross-entropy), `L_T` (TCD reconstruction), `L_N` (NPD reconstruction).  
7. Combine: `L_total = L_R + λ_T L_T + λ_N L_N`.  
8. Backpropagate and update TCD, NPD, sequential encoder, and projection layers.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Diffusion steps increase training time/memory but improve representation quality.  
- TCD’s per-item U-Net is parameter-heavy but captures fine-grained temporal patterns.  
- NPD’s timestep-agnostic design reduces parameters and avoids schedule tuning but may limit flexibility.  
- Hierarchical separation allows independent optimization but introduces hyperparameters (`λ_T`, `λ_N`, `T`).  
- Precomputed features lower cost but prevent visual encoder adaptation; end-to-end training could help.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Loss imbalance:** If `λ_T` or `λ_N` are too large, denoising losses dominate; if too small, diffusion modules contribute little. Monitor individual losses and validation metrics.  
- **Weak multimodal conditioning:** NPD relies on `ˆz0` and `h^L`; poor TCD output or dimension mismatch degrades denoising. Check TCD convergence (`L_T`) and model capacity.  
- **Degradation on longer sequences:** May indicate sequential encoder bottleneck or insufficient context modeling.  
- **Ablation anomaly:** If removing TCD or NPD does not hurt performance, the module may be improperly integrated.  
- **Noise sensitivity:** Under severe non-Gaussian noise (e.g., corrupted modalities), performance may drop sharply; consider stronger data augmentation.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Train MealRec with SASRec backbone on Microlens-small; report H@10 and N@10, verifying they match Table 2 (e.g., H@10 ≈ 0.0897).  
2. **Ablation study:** Implement variants MealRec‑TCD (without TCD), MealRec‑NPD (without NPD), and MealRec‑TCD‑NPD (without both); expect performance drops as in Figure 3.  
3. **Parameter sensitivity:** Sweep `λ_T` and diffusion steps `T` for TCD, and `λ_N` and `T` for NPD; reproduce the stable trends shown in Figure 4.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does the choice and training strategy of the pre-trained visual encoder (e.g., VideoMAE) affect TCD’s effectiveness, and would end-to-end fine-tuning with the diffusion process improve performance?
2. To what extent does NPD’s reliance on the latest interacted item’s content (`ˆz0` and `h_L`) assume it is a reliable anchor for preference recovery, and how does performance degrade when the last item is noisy, unrepresentative, or from an exploratory session?
3. Is the hierarchical separation of TCD (intra-video) and NPD (inter-video) diffusion processes necessary, or could a unified diffusion model jointly capture both granularities, and what are the trade-offs in performance, complexity, and interpretability?
4. How universally does MealRec apply to sequential recommenders beyond Transformer-based models (e.g., SASRec, CL4SRec, TedRec), particularly to RNN/GRU-based or large language model (LLM)-based backbones, and does the diffusion integration require architectural adaptations?
5. Does MealRec’s hierarchical diffusion inadvertently overfit to dataset-specific patterns in micro-video platforms (Microlens vs. Shortvideo), given varying contributions of TCD and NPD across datasets, and how can its design be made more robust to platform heterogeneity?

## Limitations
- Missing architectural details (exact U-Net configuration, self-attention layout) and hyperparameter values (e.g., `λ_T`, `λ_N`, diffusion schedule) hinder exact reproduction.
- Core assumptions (recency-weighted context, cross-modal consistency, Gaussian noise) may fail in edge cases such as very short videos, modality misalignment, or non-Gaussian interaction noise.
- Reliance on frozen precomputed features limits the model’s ability to adapt the visual encoder to the recommendation task.
