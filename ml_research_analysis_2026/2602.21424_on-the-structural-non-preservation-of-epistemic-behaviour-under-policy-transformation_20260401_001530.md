# On the Structural Non-Preservation of Epistemic Behaviour under Policy Transformation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.21424
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21424
- **Reference count**: 27
- **Primary result**: Reinforcement learning policies that encode internal state-dependent (epistemic) behavior are not closed under convex aggregation, and such behavior systematically erodes under gradient-based optimization when trained with skewed latent priors.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates how epistemic behavior—variation in a policy’s action distribution conditioned on internal memory while observations are held fixed—is affected by common policy transformations. It formalizes behavioral distance as a within-policy metric and proves that policies exhibiting non-trivial behavioral dependency cannot be combined convexly without loss. Furthermore, optimizing a policy under a biased latent prior induces gradient-driven contraction of behavioral distance, leading to failure when the deployment prior shifts. Controlled experiments in a partially observable gridworld and bandit tasks empirically confirm these theoretical mechanisms, showing that behavioral degradation precedes observable performance drops under prior shift.

## Method Summary
The study uses an Abstract Epistemic MDP with a 5‑dimensional binary observation space. Episodes begin with a probe revealing a latent mode \(m \in \{0,1\}\), followed by four delay steps with random distractor bits and a matching reward, and end with an evaluation phase requiring recall of \(m\). A recurrent Advantage Actor‑Critic (A2C) agent with a GRU backbone is trained in two stages: first with a uniform latent prior until behavioral distance \(d(\pi) > 1.9\), then with a skewed prior (e.g., \(P(m=0)=0.98\)) and reduced success reward. Behavioral distance is computed as the \(\ell_1\) distance between action distributions at the evaluation observation under each latent mode, and hidden‑state separation is measured to assess memory retention.

## Key Results
- **Non‑closure under convex aggregation**: Mixing policies with non‑trivial behavioral dependency via convex combination strictly reduces behavioral distance.
- **Gradient‑driven contraction**: Under a skewed prior, policy gradient descent on a mixture objective contracts behavioral distance when the dominant‑mode gradient aligns with \(-\nabla d\).
- **Empirical validation**: In gridworld and bandit experiments, Stage2 training with a biased prior lowers behavioral distance and hidden‑state separation, while performance remains high under the training prior but drops under a reversed prior—erosion of \(d\) precedes the performance collapse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Convex aggregation averages action probabilities across internal states, eliminating the variation that constitutes epistemic behavior. Under a skewed latent prior, the policy gradient is dominated by gradients from frequent‑mode experiences, which pulls the policy toward ignoring the rare mode’s internal state. This reduces behavioral distance because the policy becomes less sensitive to the latent mode. The sufficient local condition—alignment of the dominant‑mode gradient with \(-\nabla d\)—ensures that each update step contracts \(d\), explaining the systematic erosion observed during optimization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Epistemic MDP**: An MDP where the optimal policy must condition actions on latent information not present in the current observation. *Why needed*: Captures partial observability with persistent hidden state. *Quick check*: Can an observation‑only agent achieve near‑optimal return? If yes, memory is unnecessary.
- **Behavioral Distance (d(π))**: \(\ell_1\) distance between action distributions at a fixed probe observation conditioned on each latent mode. *Why needed*: Quantifies epistemic behavior within a single policy. *Quick check*: From rollouts, compute \(2|p_1 - p_0|\); values near 2 indicate strong mode dependence.
- **Convex Aggregation**: Mixing policies by \(\pi = \sum_i \alpha_i \pi_i\) with \(\sum \alpha_i = 1\). *Why needed*: Standard technique for ensembles, distillation, and safe policy improvement. *Quick check*: Mix two high‑\(d\) policies; the mixture’s \(d\) should be lower.
- **Gradient Contraction**: Reduction in \(d\) under gradient updates when the update direction aligns with \(-\nabla d\). *Why needed*: Explains why optimization under distribution shift degrades epistemic behavior. *Quick check*: Project policy gradient onto \(-\nabla d/\|-\nabla d\|\); a positive inner product predicts decreasing \(d\).
- **Latent Prior Shift**: Change in the distribution of latent modes between training and deployment. *Why needed*: Models real‑world non‑stationarity. *Quick check*: Train with uniform prior, test with biased prior; a performance drop indicates overfitting to the training prior.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Observation → Linear(64) → GRU(hidden=64) → [Policy Logits, Value Head]. GRU state persists across timesteps within an episode.
- **Critical Path**: During evaluation at \(t=5\), the GRU hidden state encodes the latent mode from \(t=0\); the policy head’s action distribution depends on this state, enabling behavior that varies with \(m\) despite identical observations.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: GRU provides memory for the delay period but introduces recurrence; A2C offers stable actor‑critic updates with entropy regularization but may be less sample‑efficient than PPO. A feedforward network would fail the task due to lack of memory.
- **Failure Signatures**: \(d(\pi)\) remains low (\(<1.5\)) after Stage1; hidden‑state separation (L2 distance between mode‑specific states) is small; under biased prior, reversed‑prior return degrades while \(d\) declines slowly or not at all.
- **First Three Experiments**: 1) Train with uniform prior until \(d>1.9\) to obtain a policy with strong epistemic behavior. 2) Switch to skewed prior (\(\delta=0.98\)) and continue training; track \(d\), biased‑prior return, and reversed‑prior return. 3) Convex‑aggregate two independently trained high‑\(d\) policies and verify that the mixture’s \(d\) is significantly lower.

## Limitations
- The environment uses a highly abstract 5‑dimensional binary observation and a short 4‑step delay, which may not capture the complexity of real‑world partial observability.
- A2C hyperparameters (e.g., GAE \(\lambda\), value loss weight, number of epochs per batch) are unspecified, potentially affecting reproducibility and numerical results.
- The sufficient condition for gradient‑driven contraction is not necessary; its prevalence across tasks, architectures, and non‑convex objectives remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **High**: Structural proof that the set of policies with non‑trivial behavioral dependency is not closed under convex aggregation.
- **Medium**: Empirical demonstration of behavioral distance erosion in gridworld and bandit tasks under skewed priors.
- **Low**: Generalization to diverse architectures (e.g., transformers) and non‑convex transformations (e.g., policy distillation).

## Next Checks
1. **Environment stress test**: Extend the memory horizon (more delay steps) and use continuous observations to verify whether behavioral distance still contracts under biased priors.
2. **Non‑convex aggregation**: Test policy combinations via product of experts or knowledge distillation to see if non‑preservation extends beyond convexity.
3. **Architectural robustness**: Replace the GRU with a transformer‑based recurrent module and repeat Stage2 training to confirm gradient‑driven contraction holds without recurrence.
