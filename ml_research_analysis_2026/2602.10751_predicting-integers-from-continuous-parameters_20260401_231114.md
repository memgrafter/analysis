# Predicting integers from continuous parameters

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10751  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10751  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Dalap and Bitwise distributions achieve state-of-the-art negative log-likelihood (bits) across tabular, sequential, and image tasks, with Dalap offering the best balance of likelihood and regression error.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the prediction of integer-valued labels (e.g., upvotes, bike counts, MIDI ticks) using neural networks. Traditional regression treats integers as continuous, discarding discrete probabilistic semantics. The authors introduce novel discrete probability distributions—Dalap (a discrete analogue of Laplace) and Bitwise (a bitwise Bernoulli representation)—that parameterize probabilities using continuous values (e.g., location, decay, bit probabilities) to enable gradient-based learning. Evaluations on diverse tasks show these distributions achieve superior bits (negative log-likelihood) compared to discretized continuous baselines and continuous regression. Dalap particularly excels, providing competitive RMSE alongside significant bits improvements, while Bitwise suits high-entropy data where likelihood is prioritized over absolute error.

## Method Summary
The core method replaces continuous output layers with discrete distributions defined over integers. Dalap models probabilities via a geometric tail: \(p(n|\mu,\gamma) \propto \gamma^{|n-\mu|}\), where \(\mu\) (location) and \(\gamma\) (decay) are continuous neural network outputs. Its partition function is computed in closed form via geometric series sums, yielding a differentiable NLL with a linear penalty on absolute error. Bitwise represents integers as independent Bernoulli variables on each bit (sign-magnitude), with per-bit probabilities \(\pi_i\) output by the network. Both distributions avoid the rounding artifacts of discretized continuous densities (e.g., discretized Laplace) and support end-to-end training via negative log-likelihood minimization.

## Key Results
- Dalap and Bitwise achieve the best bits (negative log-likelihood) across tabular (Bicycles, Upvotes, Migration), sequential (MIDI ticks), and image (PixelCNN++) tasks, often outperforming discretized logistic mixtures.
- Dalap provides the best balance when both likelihood (bits) and regression error (RMSE) matter, remaining competitive with continuous regression on RMSE while superior on bits.
- Bitwise yields strong bits scores but suffers large RMSE due to bit error propagation, limiting its use where absolute error is critical.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Dalap as a discrete analogue
Dalap defines a probability mass function \(p(n|\mu,\gamma) \propto \gamma^{|n-\mu|}\) with continuous parameters \(\mu\) (location) and \(\gamma\) (decay). The partition function \(z\) is computed via a geometric series sum, giving a closed-form NLL: \(\ell = -\log z + |n-\mu|\log\gamma\). This yields a linear penalty on absolute error, tempered by uncertainty \(\log\gamma\) and regularization \(\log z\). As \(\gamma \to 0\), the distribution concentrates around \(\mu\), but \(\mu\) is continuous, allowing gradient flow. Evidence: derivation in section 3.4; consistent bits superiority across tables. Break conditions: numerical instability if \(\gamma\) approaches 0 without clipping; non-integer \(\mu\) heavily penalized as \(\gamma \to 0\).

### Bitwise Bernoulli representation
Bitwise models an integer as a bitstring (k bits in sign-magnitude), with independent Bernoulli distributions per bit parameterized by \(\pi_i \in [0,1]\). The probability is the product of Bernoulli terms. The mean depends on the weighted sum of bit contributions, and variance vanishes as \(\pi_i\) match the true bits. This enables direct gradient flow through bit-level predictions and parallel computation. Evidence: formalization in section 3.8; strong bits on high-entropy data. Break conditions: errors in high-order bits cascade into large absolute errors, inflating RMSE; support bounded by \(k\) bits.

### Discrete analogues vs. discretized distributions
Discrete analogues (Dalap, Danorm) directly define PMFs on integers, avoiding rounding-induced distribution shifts that plague discretized continuous densities (e.g., dlaplace, dnormal). Discretized approaches require integrating continuous densities over integer intervals, which distort probabilities and harm likelihood-based training. Discrete analogues offer closed-form or efficiently computable partition functions (Dalap via geometric series; Danorm via finite sum), yielding better bits and stable gradients. Evidence: conceptual distinction in section 2.2; empirical bits advantage in section 4. Break conditions: Danorm’s O(n) partition sum is memory-prohibitive for large supports (e.g., images); Dweib unstable on bounded intervals.

- **PMFs vs. PDFs**  
  Why needed: Distinguishing discrete (PMF sums to 1, yields bits) from continuous (PDF integrates to 1, yields density) is central to the paper’s motivation; mixing them in losses loses interpretability.  
  Quick check: Why can’t you directly sum a continuous PDF’s negative log-density with a discrete PMF’s negative log-probability in a loss function?

- **Negative log-likelihood (NLL) and bits**  
  Why needed: Bits (base-2 NLL) measure compression and distribution fit; the paper prioritizes bits over RMSE for discrete models.  
  Quick check: How does minimizing NLL in bits relate to maximizing predictive probability, and why is it preferred for discrete labels?

- **Gradient-based learning with continuous parameters**  
  Why needed: Neural networks require continuous parameters for backpropagation; the paper’s distributions must have continuous location (\(\mu\)) and dispersion (\(\gamma\)) parameters with well-behaved gradients.  
  Quick check: What happens to backpropagation if a distribution’s PMF has discontinuities or zero gradients in the parameter space?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input features (tabular: 58 features; MIDI: embedded events; image: pixels) → Neural network backbone (MLP for tabular, LSTM for MIDI, PixelCNN++ for images) → Distribution output layer (e.g., two neurons for Dalap: \(\mu\), \(\gamma\); \(k\) sigmoid neurons for Bitwise) → Loss computation: \(-\log p(n|\text{params})\) (with masking/bounds if needed) → Backpropagation and Adam optimizer.

Critical path: Ensure distribution parameters are continuous and bounded (\(\gamma \in (0,1)\), \(\pi_i \in [0,1]\) via sigmoid). Compute partition functions efficiently: Dalap uses closed-form geometric sums; Bitwise requires no partition; Danorm uses finite sum with log-sum-exp trick. For bounded supports (e.g., pixels [0,255]), use conditional PMFs (Dalap/Danorm) or clip Bitwise’s support.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Dalap: Best bits, linear loss in \(|n-\mu|\), low memory; slight RMSE penalty vs. continuous regression.  
- Bitwise: Simple, parallelizable; high RMSE due to bit error propagation; support grows exponentially with \(k\).  
- Danorm: Tempered squared error; but partition sum memory-heavy (excluded from images).  
- Discretized distributions (dlaplace, dnormal): Simpler but inferior bits; dnormal suffers from heavy tails.

Failure signatures:  
- Non-convergence or NaN losses: Check \(\gamma\) clipping (avoid \(\gamma \le 0\)), Danorm’s tail cutoff (set max \(\gamma\), finite sum range).  
- Poor bits but good RMSE: Model under-dispersed; increase mixture components or adjust distribution (e.g., Dalap’s \(\gamma\)).  
- Memory overflow in PixelCNN: Danorm requires summing over ~1000 integers per pixel; reduce \(k\) or avoid Danorm.

First 3 experiments:  
1. **Bicycles tabular regression**: MLP with Dalap/Danorm/Bitwise output; compare bits and RMSE to squared error baseline. Verify Dalap achieves competitive RMSE and superior bits (Table 2).  
2. **MIDI ticks prediction**: LSTM predicting next tick duration; isolate tick distribution only. Confirm Dalap/Bitwise outperform discretized baselines in bits (section 5.1 MAESTRO results).  
3. **CIFAR10 PixelCNN**: Compare Dalap mixture (K=10) to Dlogistic on bits/dim and FID. Validate Dalap matches Dlogistic bits (3.02 vs. 3.02) with better sampling FID (Table 7).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What is the optimal number of mixture components K for discrete distributions in image generation tasks, and does fine-tuning this hyperparameter yield statistically significant improvements in bits per dimension and FID scores?  
   Basis: Limited K search (1,2,4,8); authors acknowledge potential for further improvement.  
   Evidence needed: Systematic K study (1–32) on CIFAR10, MNIST, FashionMNIST with proper validation, measuring bits/dim and FID.

2. Can the partition function computation in Danorm be optimized (e.g., via approximation schemes, caching, or closed-form alternatives) to reduce its memory complexity from O(n) to something suitable for high-dimensional generative modeling, while preserving its theoretical advantages?  
   Basis: Danorm required >90GB VRAM for images, exceeding hardware; excluded from image experiments.  
   Evidence needed: Memory-efficient implementation (<5% VRAM vs. current, <1% bits degradation on CIFAR10 relative to Dalap).

3. What specific numerical issues cause Dweib to fail on bounded intervals (like [0,255] for pixels), and can targeted stabilization techniques (e.g., reparameterization, probability floors, or gradient modifications) make it a viable competitor to Dalap for image generation?  
   Basis: Dweib exhibited numerical instability on [0,255], leading to convergence failures.  
   Evidence needed: Diagnostic analysis (gradient norms, probability mass) + stabilization methods demonstrating stable convergence with competitive bits/FID.

4. Does the Bitwise distribution's sensitivity to higher-order bit errors fundamentally limit its applicability to long-sequence generation (e.g., full MIDI compositions), and can architectural modifications (e.g., hierarchical modeling, bit group conditioning) mitigate catastrophic error propagation?  
   Basis: Bitwise showed high RMSE despite decent bits; only evaluated on single-tick prediction; error propagation mentioned but unaddressed for sequences.  
   Evidence needed: Full MIDI generation model; sequence-level metrics (coherence); test interventions (loss weighting, hierarchical priors) to reduce long-horizon degradation.

## Limitations
- Dalap’s continuous location \(\mu\) leads to non-integer optima; \(\gamma\) clipping to avoid partition instability may bias gradients, especially in deep architectures like PixelCNN++.
- Bitwise distribution’s high RMSE due to bit error propagation is empirically confirmed, limiting use to high-entropy, bits-focused tasks where absolute error is secondary.
- Danorm’s O(n) partition sum makes it impractical for high-dimensional settings (e.g., images); scalability to large supports remains unproven without approximation.

## Confidence
- Dalap as discrete analogue with gradient-friendly closed form: **High** (rigorous derivation in sec 3.4; consistent bits superiority across tasks).
- Bitwise Bernoulli representation: **Medium** (theoretically sound and parallelizable, but high RMSE reveals poor magnitude calibration).
- Discrete analogues (Dalap/Danorm) outperforming discretized (dlaplace/dnormal): **High** (clear empirical bits advantage and conceptual distinction; Danorm instability noted but Dalap robust).

## Next Checks
1. **Dalap γ-clipping stress test**: In PixelCNN++, sweep \(\gamma_{\text{min}}\) thresholds; track NLL, RMSE, and gradient norms to detect clipping-induced bias or instability.
2. **Bitwise decoding mitigation**: Replace naive sign-magnitude decoding with Hamming-distance or error-correcting codes; measure RMSE reduction vs. bits cost.
3. **Danorm partition approximation**: For image data, implement truncated series or cached lookup; compare bits/dim and memory footprint against exact (tabular) and Dalap (image) results.
