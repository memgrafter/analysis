# The Discrete Charm of the MLP: Binary Routing of Continuous Signals in Transformer Feed-Forward Layers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10985
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10985
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: MLPs in GPT-2 Small implement binary routing of continuous signals via a consensus of seven default-ON neurons and one exception handler, with near-binary activations and monotonic functional importance; polynomial approximations fail (max R²=0.26), while binarized activations preserve routing accuracy (79.2% vs 78.8%).

## Executive Summary
The paper reexamines the role of feed-forward networks in transformer models, challenging the view that MLPs act as smooth function approximators. Analyzing GPT-2 Small, the authors uncover a discrete routing mechanism: a small set of neurons fires in a near-binary, mutually exclusive pattern, forming a consensus architecture that decides whether nonlinear processing is needed. Removing the MLP for tokens where the consensus breaks causes a >4× larger perplexity increase than when consensus holds. This suggests MLPs are hybrid systems where a binary routing logic gates the application of nonlinear transformations, while continuous activation magnitudes modulate correction size. The findings prompt a reevaluation of how transformer MLPs process information and scale with model size.

## Method Summary
The study uses a probing approach without retraining. For each MLP layer in GPT-2 Small, they collect input (residual stream) and output pairs for 500k tokens from WikiText-103. They compute a linear approximation (least-squares fit) and define the residual δ as the nonlinear component. Tokens are categorized by the norm of δ (linear-default, barely-nonlinear, highly-nonlinear). Neuron firing rates are measured by thresholding the GELU activation (typically >0.1). They rank neurons by the absolute difference in firing rates between the linear-default and highly-nonlinear regimes. The top-k neurons are examined for co-activation patterns to identify mutually exclusive groups. Additionally, they fit polynomial functions (degrees 2–7) on PCA-reduced inputs (50 dimensions) to predict δ, using Ridge regression (α=1.0) with cross-validation. Causal importance is assessed by ablating the MLP output for tokens grouped by consensus level (number of default-ON neurons firing) and measuring the change in perplexity.

## Key Results
- Near-binary neurons: In Layer 11 a set of seven default-ON neurons and one exception handler (N2123) exhibit 93–98% mutual exclusivity.
- Polynomial failure: Max cross-validated R² for predicting δ with polynomials is only 0.26 (most layers <0.06), indicating no smooth structure.
- Binarized routing accuracy: Using binary neuron activations to predict the MLP’s decision (high vs. low δ) yields 79.2% accuracy, close to 78.8% from continuous activations.
- Functional gradient: Ablating the MLP increases perplexity by 43.3% when consensus breaks (0/7 default-ON active) but only 10.1% at full consensus (7/7 active)—a >4× difference. The consensus gradient is perfectly monotonic.
- Developmental arc: Across layers, quorum size grows from 1 to 7 in decision layers, and exception handler output norm strengthens with depth.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The MLP implements a binary routing decision—whether a token requires nonlinear processing—via near-binary activations of specific neurons, even though routed signals remain continuous.
- **Mechanism:** Certain neurons (e.g., seven "default-ON" and one exception handler in Layer 11) exhibit near-binary, mutually exclusive firing patterns. The exception handler (N2123) fires predominantly when the default-ON consensus is broken, triggering full nonlinear computation. When consensus holds, the neuron outputs approximately cancel, and the MLP's net effect is minimal.
- **Core assumption:** The mutual exclusivity and consensus gradient are learned weight properties, not activation-function artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "seven 'default-ON' neurons and one exception handler (N2123) that are 93–98% mutually exclusive." Section 4.4: N2123 fires 94.7% when 0/7 consensus neurons fire vs. 0.5% when all 7 fire; MLP output norm is 2.8× higher at breakdown. Related work: "Half the Nonlinearity Is Wasted" confirms nonlinearity is only needed for a subset of tokens, aligning with the routing decision framing.
- **Break condition:** The clean 7+1 consensus structure does not replicate in larger GPT-2 variants (Medium/Large), suggesting capacity-dependent emergence or different organizational principles at scale.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The binary routing decision is causally linked to functional importance; tokens routed to the nonlinear path (consensus breakdown) rely critically on the MLP, while consensus tokens do not.
- **Mechanism:** Ablating the entire MLP for tokens at different consensus levels shows a monotonic perplexity increase: 43.3% at consensus breakdown (0/7) vs. 10.1% at full consensus (7/7)—a >4× difference. The MLP's effect is primarily distributional (high KL divergence) rather than a targeted boost to the correct token.
- **Core assumption:** Consensus level, derived from binarized activations, is a reliable proxy for whether the MLP's nonlinear computation is essential.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.8, Table 7: Perplexity delta gradient across consensus levels, with bootstrap-confirmed monotonicity. Section 4.9: KL divergence drops monotonically with consensus, while correct-token boost falls below 1.0× at high consensus, indicating noise-like output. Related work: "Intelligent Neural Networks" and "Neural Inhibition" discuss dynamic routing, supporting the idea that routing decisions modulate computational pathways.
- **Break condition:** If the consensus gradient is not monotonic in other architectures (e.g., SwiGLU-based), the causal link between binary routing and functional importance may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Binary routing structure emerges developmentally through depth, from single gateway neurons in early layers to full quorum consensus in late layers, paralleling increasing computational complexity.
- **Mechanism:** Layers L1–L3 use isolated gateway neurons to route exceptions without quorum; L4–L6 show diffuse, unstructured processing; L7–L11 develop consensus/exception architectures with growing quorum size (1→3→7). The exception handler's firing rate and output norm gradient strengthen with layer depth.
- **Core assumption:** The consensus/exception architecture is not universal but emerges where the nonlinearity delta (∥δ∥) is highest, reflecting task-demanded complexity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.7, Table 6: Three-phase developmental arc across all 12 layers of GPT-2 Small, with increasing quorum size and monotonic consensus gradients in decision layers. Section 2: Nonlinearity delta (∥δ∥) used to define regimes; higher deltas in later layers correlate with consensus emergence. Related work: "Distributed Specialization" finds rare-token neurons, suggesting different layers may develop different specialization strategies.
- **Break condition:** The arc may not hold for architectures with different depth-to-width ratios or normalization schemes; scaffold/diffuse/decision phases could be GPT-2 Small-specific.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Piecewise-linear activations and spline theory.
  - *Why needed here:* GELU's near-zero/linear regimes enable binary-like behavior; spline theory (Balestriero & Baraniuk) provides the baseline "how" of piecewise-affine computation, which this work complements with a "what" (routing).
  - *Quick check:* How does GELU's smooth transition region affect the sharpness of binary routing decisions in practice?
- **Concept:** Residual stream as a representational bottleneck.
  - *Why needed here:* The residual's fixed dimension (768) forces the MLP to make discrete routing decisions to resolve superposition and ambiguity, as suggested in Elhage et al. (2022).
  - *Quick check:* Why might a continuous bottleneck encourage discrete routing rather than smooth interpolation?
- **Concept:** Nonlinearity delta (∥δ∥) as a regime metric.
  - *Why needed here:* The paper uses the norm of the least-squares residual to define "linear-default" vs. "highly-nonlinear" tokens, enabling the identification of routing-relevant neurons.
  - *Quick check:* What are the limitations of using a scalar residual norm to capture complex, multi-dimensional nonlinear effects?

## Architecture Onboarding
Transformer MLP: residual input x → W_in → GELU → W_out → residual addition. Analysis hooks capture pre-activation (W_in x + b_in), post-GELU activations, and MLP output. Routing logic derived from binarizing top-k neurons by firing-rate shift between linear and nonlinear regimes.

Critical path:
1. Collect input-output pairs for each MLP layer over a large corpus (e.g., 500K tokens).
2. Compute linear approximation (least-squares Wx + b) and residual δ to define nonlinearity regimes.
3. For each neuron, compute firing rate (GELU > threshold) in linear-default vs. highly-nonlinear regimes; rank by absolute difference.
4. Binarize top neurons, analyze co-activation patterns for mutual exclusivity and consensus gradients.
5. Validate causally: ablate MLP for tokens at each consensus level and measure perplexity impact.

Design tradeoffs:
- Binary vs. continuous features: Binarization preserves routing decision accuracy (79.2% vs. 78.8%) but loses magnitude information needed for predicting output norm (R² 0.22 vs. 0.36).
- Threshold selection: Binary routing structure is robust across GELU thresholds (0.01–1.0), but firing rates and exclusivity percentages vary.

Failure signatures:
- Polynomial fits (degrees 2–7) consistently yield low cross-validated R² (<0.06) in nonlinear layers, indicating absence of smooth structure.
- Random weight control: No consensus gradient or mutual exclusivity in untrained model.
- Scale mismatch: Clean consensus structure absent in larger GPT-2 variants (Medium/Large).

First 3 experiments:
1. Replicate binary routing detection in Layer 11: compute firing-rate shifts, identify 7+1 neuron set, verify mutual exclusivity >93% and monotonic N2123 fire rate vs. consensus count.
2. Test polynomial failure: fit degree-3 Ridge on PCA-compressed residuals (k=50) for a high-nonlinearity layer (e.g., L9); expect R² < 0.07 even with clustering.
3. Perform causal ablation: for tokens grouped by consensus level, remove MLP output and compute perplexity delta; expect >4× difference between 0/7 and 7/7 consensus.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How does the binary routing consensus architecture scale with model size, and what determines its emergence or degradation across layers?
- **Question 2:** Is the clean binary routing structure in small models an artifact of limited capacity forcing discrete decisions, or does it reflect a general computational principle that persists despite increased capacity?
- **Question 3:** Does the binary routing characterization generalize to transformer MLPs with non-GELU activations, such as SwiGLU or GeGLU, which incorporate explicit gating mechanisms?

## Limitations
- The routing pattern is only robustly observed in GPT-2 Small; it degrades in larger GPT-2 variants, limiting claims of universality.
- Detection pipeline relies on several hyperparameters (clustering method, PCA dimensions, GELU threshold) not fully specified, making replication fragile.
- Causal ablation removes the entire MLP, conflating the routing decision with the continuous signal modulation.
- Analysis uses single-token context; longer contexts may alter the consensus structure (briefly tested but not reported).

## Confidence
- **Binary routing existence (Mechanism 1):** Medium – Strong in one model but fails to generalize; detection method sensitivity.
- **Functional importance gradient (Mechanism 2):** High – Large, monotonic, bootstrap-validated effects within GPT-2 Small.
- **Developmental arc (Mechanism 3):** Medium – Clear layer-wise pattern in GPT-2 Small but may not generalize to other architectures.
- **Polynomial failure:** Medium – Consistently low R², though alternative smooth models (e.g., splines) were not tested.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model replication:** Run the full pipeline on GPT-2 Medium (355M) and Llama 2 7B, re-identifying exception handlers per layer; test for significant mutual exclusivity (>90%) and a monotonic consensus gradient in ablation.
2. **Activation-agnostic robustness:** Replace GELU with SwiGLU in a comparable model (e.g., Pythia-70M) and repeat binary routing detection; check if discrete consensus structure persists despite threshold shifts.
3. **Controlled context stress-test:** For GPT-2 Small Layer 11, compute consensus gradients using ±3-token sliding windows vs. single-token context; if the perplexity delta ratio (0/7 vs. 7/7) drops from >4× to <2×, it suggests context interference limits stability.
