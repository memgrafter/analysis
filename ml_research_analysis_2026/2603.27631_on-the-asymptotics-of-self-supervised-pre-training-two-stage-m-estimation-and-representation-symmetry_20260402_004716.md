# On the Asymptotics of Self-Supervised Pre-training: Two-Stage M-Estimation and Representation Symmetry

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27631
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27631
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Asymptotic characterization of the downstream test risk in self-supervised pre‑training via two‑stage M‑estimation on quotient manifolds, yielding a decomposition into a well‑specified OLS term and a pre‑training interaction term proportional to the inverse pre‑training data ratio.

## Executive Summary
Self‑supervised pre‑training (SSP) is widely used to learn representations that improve downstream task performance. However, a rigorous understanding of how pre‑training sample size influences downstream risk—especially when the pre‑training objective induces symmetry (e.g., rotation or permutation invariance)—remains incomplete. This paper fills that gap by developing an asymptotic theory for SSP as a two‑stage M‑estimation problem. The first stage estimates a representation that is identifiable only up to a Lie group action; the authors resolve this non‑identifiability by introducing a descriptor map that projects onto a quotient manifold. Under an orthogonal equivariance condition linking the pre‑training representation to the downstream linear predictor, they derive the limiting distribution of the scaled excess risk when both pre‑training and fine‑tuning sample sizes grow large with a fixed ratio α = m/n. The limit consists of two terms: (i) σ²·d_eff(Ω⋆), where d_eff is the effective dimension of the population descriptor, and (ii) α⁻¹·∥L(Z)∥² capturing the interaction between pre‑training error and downstream loss. The theory yields sharp rates and tighter constants for concrete cases such as spectral contrastive learning, factor models, and Gaussian mixtures.

## Method Summary
The analysis models SSP as a sequential estimation procedure. Pre‑training minimizes a loss ℓ_pre that is invariant under a compact group G acting on the raw parameter w (e.g., orthogonal rotations). Because of this symmetry, ŵ_m is not uniquely defined; instead, the authors consider a descriptor Ω = R(w) lying on the quotient manifold M = W/G. A smooth local lift s: M → W yields a well‑defined feature map ϕ(x,Ω)=ψ(x,s(Ω)). For downstream fine‑tuning, they assume a linear model y = f_⋆(x)+ε and use ordinary least squares (OLS) with features ϕ(·,Ω). Crucially, if the representation is orthogonally equivariant (ψ(x,g·w)=ρ(g)ψ(x,w) with ρ(g)∈O(p)), then the minimum‑norm OLS solution depends only on the descriptor Ω, not on the specific representative w. This orbit‑invariance allows the downstream risk to be expressed as a function of Ω alone. The authors then combine (i) a central limit theorem for the descriptor estimator on the Riemannian manifold, (ii) a delta method for the downstream projector Π_Ω and related quantities, and (iii) concentration of variance terms, to obtain the joint asymptotic distribution of the scaled excess risk. The result cleanly separates the well‑specified OLS contribution (proportional to σ²·d_eff) from a term that decays as 1/α, quantifying the benefit of more pre‑training data.

## Key Results
- The paper provides a rigorous two‑stage M‑estimation framework that characterizes the limiting distribution of the conditional downstream test risk under joint scaling of pre‑training and fine‑tuning samples.
- The asymptotic decomposition: n(R - σ²) → σ²·d_eff(Ω⋆) + α⁻¹·∥L(Z)∥², where Z∼N(0,V) and L encodes the pre‑training error projected onto the downstream loss.
- Applications to spectral contrastive learning, factor models, and Gaussian mixtures yield instance‑optimal convergence rates and improve constant factors compared to prior bounds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The two‑stage M‑estimation framework characterizes the joint limiting distribution of the downstream conditional test risk, separating a well‑specified OLS term and a pre‑training interaction term.
- **Mechanism:** Pre‑training estimates a representation parameter (possibly orbit‑unidentifiable). Downstream OLS uses this representation. Under joint scaling m,n→∞ with m/n→α, the scaled excess risk converges to a sum: σ²·d_eff(Ω⋆) + α⁻¹·∥L(Z)∥², where Z∼N(0, V).
- **Core assumption:** Pre‑training loss is C², has a unique minimal descriptor orbit, and its Hessian on the tangent space is positive definite; downstream moments of order 4+ exist; well‑posedness of the empirical least‑squares problem holds.
- **Break condition:** If the first‑stage estimator does not attain the √m rate (e.g., due to degenerate Hessian) or the design matrix in downstream OLS does not concentrate, the joint CLT fails.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Symmetry‑induced non‑identifiability is resolved by moving to a quotient manifold via G‑invariant descriptor maps, enabling a Riemannian M‑estimation CLT.
- **Mechanism:** A Lie group G acts on the representation space; the loss is invariant under G. One constructs a C² local lift s from a neighborhood of the descriptor manifold M=R(·). The intrinsic estimator Ω̂_m = R(ŵ_m) lies on M. Using normal coordinates and local quadratic expansion, √m·log_{Ω⋆}(Ω̂_m) follows a multivariate normal law with covariance V = H_⋆⁻¹ Σ_⋆ H_⋆⁻¹.
- **Core assumption:** The group action is free and proper on a neighborhood; a C² local section separating orbits exists; the population descriptor Ω⋆ is unique and the Riemannian Hessian H_⋆ on T_{Ω⋆} M is invertible.
- **Break condition:** If the group action has stabilizers at Ω⋆ or no smooth local lift exists, the descriptor manifold may fail to be smooth, breaking the CLT.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Orthogonal equivariance guarantees that the downstream minimum‑norm OLS predictor is orbit‑invariant, making the conditional risk a function of the quotient parameter alone.
- **Mechanism:** Assume ψ(x, g·w) = ρ(g) ψ(x, w) with ρ(g)∈O(p). For any w and g, the OLS solution satisfies ŵ_{g·w} = ρ(g) ŵ_w, hence the predictor ˆf_{g·w} = ˆf_w. This allows defining intrinsic quantities Π_Ω, Rep(Ω), etc., solely on the quotient.
- **Core assumption:** The equivariance condition (4.4) holds; downstream linear model is well‑specified (f_⋆∈H_{Ω⋆}); noises are i.i.d. with finite variance.
- **Break condition:** If equivariance fails, different orbit representatives may yield different OLS solutions, and the leakage term no longer simplifies to Π_{Ω,n}e_Ω, preventing its convergence to zero.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Pre‑training loss ℓ_pre → representation ψ(x,w) → symmetry group G → descriptor map R(w)=Ω (quotient manifold M) → local lift s(Ω) → feature map ϕ(x,Ω) → downstream OLS (empirical projector Π_{Ω,n}, population Π_Ω) → risk decomposition (Rep(Ω), Var_n(Ω), Leakage_n(Ω)) → asymptotic modules (manifold CLT for Ω̂_m, Fréchet derivatives of Π_Ω, variance concentration, leakage vanishing).

**Critical path:**
1. Establish CLT for √m·log_{Ω⋆}(Ω̂_m) (Theorem 4.1).
2. Show Fréchet differentiability of Ω↦Π_Ω and Ω↦Rep(Ω) (Prop. E.6, Cor. E.7).
3. Prove n·Var_n → σ²·d_eff(Ω⋆) (Prop. E.14) and n·Leakage_n → 0 (Prop. E.16).
4. Combine with pre‑training fluctuations via delta method to obtain Theorem 5.3.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Descriptor choice*: Linear descriptors (e.g., M=A⊤A) yield closed‑form but may not capture nonlinear symmetries.
- *Equivariance strength*: Strong orthogonal equivariance gives exact orbit‑invariance; weaker forms require extra bias corrections.
- *Well‑posedness*: Assumes full‑rank downstream designs; in high‑dimensional regimes one might need ridge, which alters the limit.

**Failure signatures:**
- Degenerate Hessian H_⋆ on tangent space → no CLT for Ω̂_m.
- Absence of a C² local lift → descriptor coordinates not smooth → derivative of Π_Ω undefined.
- Rank instability of Σ(Ω̂_m) → variance term does not concentrate (Lemma E.10 fails).
- Violation of equivariance → Leakage_n does not vanish and contributes a persistent bias.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Linear spectral contrastive model** (Section 6.1). Generate data matching Assumption 6.1; verify that scaled excess risk converges to the mixture of χ² distributions given by Corollary 6.3 for several α=m/n.
2. **Variance term concentration**. For the same model, plot n·Var_n versus n and confirm convergence to σ²·k (d_eff(Ω⋆)=k) as n grows.
3. **Break equivariance**. Modify the downstream feature map by injecting a fixed nonlinear transform that destroys ψ(x,g·w)=ρ(g)ψ(x,w). Then increase m,n and observe that Leakage_n no longer →0, demonstrating the necessity of Condition 4.4.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Are the asymptotic characterizations tight?** The authors provide an asymptotic limit but do not prove matching lower bounds. An information‑theoretic lower bound that matches the derived distribution would confirm optimality.
2. **Extension beyond linear downstream models?** The analysis heavily relies on linear OLS and orthogonal equivariance. Generalizing to non‑linear regression (e.g., GLMs) or other estimators remains an open challenge.
3. **Finite‑sample bounds?** The results are asymptotic; deriving non‑asymptotic high‑probability bounds that reflect the leading terms is an important future direction.
4. **Necessity and sufficiency of equivariance?** The paper assumes Condition 4.4 (orthogonal equivariance) but does not characterize broader families of pre‑training objectives for which the downstream predictor remains well‑defined on the quotient. A representation theorem or counterexamples are needed.

## Limitations
- The Riemannian CLT requires a C² local lift and a non‑degenerate Hessian on the tangent space; verifying these conditions for complex SSL losses may be difficult in practice.
- The downstream OLS analysis assumes full‑rank design matrices; this well‑posedness can break in high‑dimensional or over‑parameterized settings, and regularized extensions are not covered.
- Orthogonal equivariance is a strong condition that may only approximately hold for many self‑supervised objectives; the robustness of the theory to violations and the size of the resulting leakage term are not quantified.

## Confidence
- **Two‑stage decomposition (Theorem 5.3):** Medium (rigorous for the case studies; general validity hinges on hard‑to‑verify assumptions about the descriptor CLT and OLS well‑posedness).
- **Manifold CLT (Theorem 4.1):** Medium (depends on free/proper group action and existence of a smooth local section; must be checked model‑by‑model).
- **Equivariance → orbit‑invariance (Lemma 4.2):** High (pure algebraic argument; easily verified when the representation map satisfies the stated transformation rule).
