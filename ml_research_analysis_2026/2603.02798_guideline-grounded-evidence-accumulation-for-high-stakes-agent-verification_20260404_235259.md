# Guideline-Grounded Evidence Accumulation for High-Stakes Agent Verification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02798
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02798
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** GLEAN achieves AUROC >0.94 and Brier <0.10 with active verification, improving AUROC by 12% over the best baseline.

## Executive Summary
High-stakes AI agents, such as those used for clinical diagnosis, suffer from unreliable verification due to limited domain knowledge and poor confidence calibration. This paper introduces GLEAN, a framework that operationalizes expert clinical guidelines into step-by-step alignment scores, aggregates them via discounted accumulation, and calibrates them into correctness probabilities using Bayesian logistic regression. When uncertainty is high, GLEAN actively expands guideline coverage and performs differential checks to gather more evidence. Evaluated on three diseases from MIMIC-IV, GLEAN outperforms the best baseline by 12% AUROC and reduces the Brier score by 50%, reaching >0.94 AUROC and <0.10 Brier with active verification. An expert clinician study rated its interpretability and clinical utility highly (average 4.3/5).

## Method Summary
GLEAN verifies agent decisions by grounding them in domain-specific guidelines. For a given diagnostic trajectory, it first retrieves the top-K relevant clinical guidelines (K=3) using keyword and embedding-based search. Each step of the trajectory is then scored by an LLM judge (the same backbone as the agent) for alignment with each guideline; the multi-guideline scores are aggregated into a feature (e.g., minimum and average). The step-wise alignment scores are transformed to log-odds and accumulated with a discount factor β=0.5 to produce a total evidence score S_T. This score is fed into a Bayesian logistic regression calibrator (trained on 100 labeled trajectories with MCMC sampling) to output a calibrated correctness probability p̂_T and its entropy. If the entropy exceeds a threshold (0.5), active verification is triggered: one additional relevant guideline and two competing diagnosis guidelines are retrieved; the original alignment scores are rectified in logit space (α=0.2) and the accumulation-recalibration loop is repeated. The final confidence guides risk-aware actions such as Best-of-N selection.

## Key Results
- **Discrimination:** AUROC >0.94, a +12% improvement over the strongest baseline.
- **Calibration:** Brier score reduced by 50%, falling below 0.10; ECE also significantly improved.
- **Clinical usefulness:** Expert clinicians rated GLEAN’s interpretability and utility at 4.3/5 on average.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Sequential Log-Odds Accumulation
- **Claim:** Discounted summation of log-odds from step-wise guideline alignment approximates Bayesian evidence, producing well-calibrated probabilities after logistic regression.
- **How:** For each step, compute alignment score s_{t,g} ∈ [0,1] with each guideline g. Transform to log-odds log(s/(1−s)). Accumulate with discount factor β: S_t = Σ_{i=1}^t β^{t-i} log(s_i/(1−s_i)). The Bayesian logistic regressor (with Gaussian priors) maps S_T to a probability p̂_T, yielding calibrated confidence.
- **Assumption:** The accumulated log-odds S_T is linearly related to the logit of true correctness; step scores are sufficiently informative.
- **Evidence:** The theoretical derivation (Equation 2) and empirical logit-linearity (R² ≈ 0.94) support this; ablation shows accurate calibration when log-odds assumption holds.

### Mechanism 2: Guideline-Grounded Discrimination
- **Claim:** Explicit clinical guidelines ground the step-wise signal, making it highly discriminative and approximately logit-linear, which justifies a simple linear calibrator.
- **How:** An LLM judge rates alignment of each trajectory step with the top-K retrieved guidelines. Scores from multiple guidelines are aggregated (e.g., min and average). The resulting per-step feature exhibits strong separation between correct and incorrect prefixes and a near-linear relationship with the correctness logit.
- **Assumption:** Guidelines are relevant and accurately retrieved; the LLM judge can interpret them correctly.
- **Evidence:** Guideline-grounded signals show significant discrimination (p < 1e−3) and high R² (0.943) versus uninformed signals. Ablation confirms the importance of using multiple guidelines.

### Mechanism 3: Uncertainty-Triggered Active Verification
- **Claim:** For high-uncertainty cases, actively expanding guideline coverage and performing differential checks supplies additional evidence, enhancing verification under budget constraints.
- **How:** If the entropy H(p̂_T) > ε_u (0.5), GLEAN (i) adds one more relevant guideline (expansion) and (ii) retrieves two competitive diagnosis guidelines to rectify scores against alternatives (rectification factor α=0.2). The modified scores are re-accumulated and re-calibrated, yielding improved discrimination (Table 2) and reduced error.
- **Assumption:** High entropy indicates insufficient, non-specific evidence that can be resolved by gathering more targeted information.
- **Evidence:** Ablation study (Table 2) shows both expansion and differential checks contribute; their combination yields the best AUROC and Brier improvements.

## Foundational Learning
### Bayesian Inference for Sequential Updating
- **Why needed:** GLEAN relies on a Bayesian decomposition of correctness probability into a sequence of incremental log-odds. Understanding priors, likelihoods, and posterior updates is essential to grasp why the accumulated evidence yields calibrated probabilities.
- **Quick check:** In Equation 2 (log evidence decomposition), what does the term `log(P(o_t, a_t | Z=1, τ_{1:t-1}) / P(o_t, a_t | Z=0, τ_{1:t-1}))` represent, and why is it additive?

### Probability Calibration Metrics (Brier Score, ECE)
- **Why needed:** For high-stakes deployment, confidence estimates must match empirical correctness. Brier score and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) quantify this calibration; GLEAN explicitly targets their reduction.
- **Quick check:** If a verifier predicts 90% confidence on 100 instances of which 80 are correct, what are the approximate Brier score and ECE? What do they indicate about the verifier’s calibration?

### Discounted Accumulation in Trajectory Models
- **Why needed:** Agent trajectories contain variable-quality steps; early steps often carry noise due to limited information. Discounting down-weights early evidence to prevent it from degrading the accumulated signal.
- **Quick check:** How does setting β=0.1 versus β=0.9 affect the influence of the first step on the final accumulated evidence S_T? What are the trade-offs between these choices?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Guideline Retriever → Step-wise Scorer → Evidence Accumulator → Bayesian Calibrator → [Active Verifier on high entropy → loop to Evidence Accumulator] → Final Confidence

### Critical path
Input trajectory → retrieve initial guidelines → score each step with judge → aggregate multi-guideline scores → compute discounted log-odds sum S_T → calibrate to p̂_T → if entropy > 0.5 → trigger active verification (expand/differential) → recompute S_T and p̂_T → output final confidence.

### Design tradeoffs
- **K (guidelines per step):** More guidelines increase robustness but raise LLM call cost; paper finds K=3 optimal.
- **β (discount factor):** Lower β (e.g., 0.3) emphasizes recent steps; higher β (e.g., 0.7) weights earlier history. β=0.5 balances noise vs. informativeness.
- **α (differential rectification):** Larger α penalizes competitive diagnoses more, improving discrimination but harming calibration; α=0.2 chosen for conservative correction.

### Failure signatures
- Low AUROC despite high step-wise accuracy → indicates poor accumulation/calibration; check log-odds linearity.
- High ECE after calibration → suggests miscalibrated step scores or insufficient calibration data; validate with reliability diagrams.
- Active verification degrades performance → likely due to irrelevant expanded/differential guidelines; assess retrieval relevance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*Can GLEAN generalize to diverse clinical settings and other high-stakes domains?* The paper only evaluates three diseases in MIMIC‑IV; broader validation across various healthcare contexts and non‑medical domains (e.g., legal, financial) is needed to confirm robustness.

### Open Question 2
*How robust is GLEAN to flawed, incomplete, or outdated guidelines?* The framework assumes guidelines are authoritative, but real‑world guidelines may contain errors or lag behind current practice. Stress tests with corrupted or outdated guidelines, plus consistency‑checking mechanisms, are required.

### Open Question 3
*Can calibration be made agent‑agnostic?* The paper notes cross‑agent calibration drifts when a verifier trained on one agent is applied to another. Developing invariant calibration techniques (e.g., unsupervised adaptation) would enable flexible deployment across different agent models.

## Limitations
- The logit‑linearity assumption for evidence accumulation may not hold universally; performance is sensitive to the discount factor β and aggregation function Φ.
- Active verification’s effectiveness hinges on the quality of retrieved guidelines; in rare or complex diseases, expanded or differential guidelines may be irrelevant, limiting gains.
- Key calibration details (prior precision, MCMC iterations, convergence diagnostics) are omitted, hampering exact replication and stability assessment.

## Confidence
- **Sequential accumulation & Bayesian calibration:** Medium
- **Guideline grounding & signal logit-linearity:** High
- **Uncertainty‑triggered active verification:** Medium
