# MMKU-Bench: A Multimodal Update Benchmark for Diverse Visual Knowledge

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.15117
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15117
*   **Reference Count:** 33
*   **Primary Result:** Current multimodal models (InternVL2.5-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) struggle to revise outdated knowledge without catastrophic forgetting and fail at reliable cross-modal knowledge transfer.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MMKU-Bench, a large-scale benchmark designed to systematically evaluate how well large multimodal models (LMMs) can update their knowledge. It addresses a critical gap: most prior work focuses on injecting *new* facts, but real-world updates often require *revising* existing, entrenched facts. The benchmark features over 25,000 knowledge instances across two scenarios: "updated" knowledge (counterfactually edited from originally correct facts) and "unknown" knowledge (new, post-training-cutoff information). Experiments reveal that standard fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RLHF) methods perform better on unknown knowledge but cause severe catastrophic forgetting of general capabilities. In contrast, dedicated knowledge editing methods preserve general skills but fail to scale to the benchmark's size. A core finding is a pronounced lack of cross-modal consistency; knowledge learned via one modality (text or image) does not reliably transfer to the other.

## Method Summary
MMKU-Bench constructs a dataset of multimodal question-answer pairs covering two knowledge types. "Updated" knowledge is created by taking originally correct Visual Question Answering (VQA) pairs and using GPT-4o to generate counterfactual answers, simulating a factual correction. "Unknown" knowledge is sourced from temporal Wikipedia diffs, identifying entities with new information after a model's training cutoff. The benchmark supports three evaluation settings that permute the modality of knowledge injection (during training) and evaluation: Multimodal-to-Multimodal (M→M), Multimodal-to-Text (M→T), and Text-to-Multimodal (T→M). This design isolates the challenges of revising old facts versus learning new ones and measures the consistency of a fact's representation across vision and language pathways. The benchmark's scale (~25k instances, 49k images) enables testing of continual, large-scale updates.

## Key Results
*   SFT and RLHF achieve higher performance on *unknown* knowledge than *updated* knowledge but induce severe catastrophic forgetting, significantly degrading performance on OCR, math, and hallucination benchmarks.
*   Knowledge editing methods (GRACE, WISE) best preserve general capabilities but show negligible success on large-scale, continuous updates required by the benchmark.
*   Cross-modal knowledge transfer is poor; all methods perform best in the M→M setting, with significant drops in M→T and T→M settings, indicating brittle, modality-specific fact representations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** MMKU-Bench enables systematic evaluation by creating a controlled dataset with two distinct knowledge types via counterfactual editing and temporal extraction.
*   **Mechanism:** The benchmark constructs paired data where the same visual entity has different textual knowledge states. "Updated" instances are made by editing correct answers to new values. "Unknown" instances come from new facts in later Wikipedia snapshots. This isolates the learning challenge of adding versus overwriting.
*   **Core Assumption:** Counterfactual editing of existing correct answers is a valid proxy for real-world knowledge updates, and temporal Wikipedia diffs represent genuine unknown knowledge.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [Abstract] Explicitly states the goal of revising existing outdated knowledge vs. only injecting new knowledge, and describes the two scenarios.
    *   [Section 3.2] Details the pipeline: counterfactual editing for updated knowledge and temporal Wikipedia diff extraction for unknown knowledge.
*   **Break Condition:** If counterfactual edits introduce unnatural semantic shifts (e.g., changing entity types) or if temporal extractions include non-visual/irrelevant facts, the benchmark's validity for *visual* knowledge updating breaks.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Models face fundamentally different dynamics for "updated" vs. "unknown" knowledge due to interference with entrenched parametric memory, making updated knowledge more challenging and destructive.
*   **Mechanism:** For "unknown" knowledge, the model has no prior conflicting representation, so learning is additive. For "updated" knowledge, the new fact conflicts with a strongly encoded old fact in the model's parameters, requiring suppression of the old association, which causes representational instability and catastrophic forgetting.
*   **Core Assumption:** Pretrained parametric knowledge is stable and resistant to change; updating requires overwriting existing weights, which is more disruptive than adding new ones.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [Section 4.2, Obs 2 & 6] Notes stronger adaptability for updated knowledge in open-ended QA due to semantic priors, but concludes updated knowledge causes more severe degradation of general capabilities due to conflicts with pre-existing knowledge.
    *   [Section 4.3, Obs 6] Directly states "updating existing knowledge (Updated) tends to result in more severe degradation of general capabilities" compared to injecting unknown knowledge.
*   **Break Condition:** If models show no performance difference between updating existing facts and learning new facts (equal CEM/F1 and equal degradation on general benchmarks), the assumed mechanism of "parametric conflict" would be invalid.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Current multimodal models fail at cross-modal knowledge transfer; knowledge acquired in one modality does not reliably generalize to the other for the same fact.
*   **Mechanism:** The model's internal representations for a fact are not modality-agnostic. Knowledge injected via text-only training does not create robust image-grounded associations, and knowledge trained on image-text pairs does not create a strong enough text-only representation, indicating brittle cross-modal alignment.
*   **Core Assumption:** True multimodal understanding requires a shared, modality-invariant representation for facts. Current LMMs learn primarily modality-specific pathways or shallow alignments.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [Section 4.4, Cross-modal Transfer] "Knowledge injected in a single modality does not readily generalize to other modalities... all methods achieve relatively better performance under the setting of multimodal knowledge injection with multimodal evaluation (M→M)."
    *   [Section 4.4] Text-based injection enables more direct modification of entity knowledge compared to multimodal injection, implying separate pathways.
*   **Break Condition:** If M→T and T→M performance approaches M→M performance (within a small margin, e.g., <5%) across all methods and knowledge types, the claim of fundamental cross-modal inconsistency would be falsified.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Parametric Knowledge in LMMs**
    *   **Why needed:** The entire paper hinges on the idea that models store facts in their weights from pre-training. "Knowledge updating" means modifying these weights, not just retrieving, which is fundamentally hard.
    *   **Quick Check:** If a model knows "The Eiffel Tower is in Paris" from pre-training, and you fine-tune it on data saying "The Eiffel Tower is in London," what must happen to its parameters for it to answer correctly, and why might it forget other facts?
*   **Concept: Catastrophic Forgetting**
    *   **Why needed:** The key negative result is that SFT and RLHF cause severe degradation on general benchmarks. This is the classic continual learning problem now observed in multimodal knowledge update scenarios.
    *   **Quick Check:** Why does fine-tuning a model on a new set of facts tend to hurt its performance on unrelated tasks like math or OCR?
*   **Concept: Cross-modal Alignment**
    *   **Why needed:** The benchmark's third evaluation setting (M→T, T→M) tests the consistency of a fact's representation across vision and text. Poor performance indicates the model hasn't learned a unified, modality-independent concept.
    *   **Quick Check:** If a model is trained that "this animal (image of a jaguar) lives in the rainforest," and then tested with the text-only question "Where does a jaguar live?" without an image, why might it fail?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    Data Source Layer (EVQA dataset, Wikipedia dumps, image search APIs) -> Construction Pipeline (GPT-4o for editing/QA, CLIP for filtering, human annotators for QC) -> Evaluation Harness (Settings: M→M, M→T, T→M; Metrics: CEM, F1, 11 general benchmarks) -> Test Models & Methods (InternVL2.5-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B; Baselines, SFT, RLHF, KE).
*   **Critical Path:**
    1.  **Data Collection:** Select entities with images. For updated: filter for QA pairs both target models get correct -> edit answer. For unknown: find new entities in later Wikipedia -> collect images.
    2.  **QA Generation & Paraphrasing:** Generate initial QA from facts -> paraphrase to create separate injection/evaluation sets -> replace entity references with hypernyms ("this person") for multimodal data.
    3.  **Cross-modal Split:** For text-only evaluation/injection, remove images and replace hypernyms with entity names.
    4.  **Quality Control:** Human verification of answer correctness, question clarity, image-text consistency.
    5.  **Experiment Execution:** Train/inject using each method on the injection set -> evaluate on the corresponding evaluation set under the three modality transfer settings -> run general capability benchmarks.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Scale vs. Depth:** 25k+ instances is large for multimodal editing, but uses counterfactual editing instead of waiting for real-world updates, which may not perfectly simulate true knowledge evolution.
    *   **Generalization vs. Control:** Using diverse entities (plants, celebrities) tests broad applicability, but focuses on descriptive Q&A (short answers) which may not capture complex reasoning updates.
    *   **Injection Cost vs. Fidelity:** Full-FT gives best updated knowledge performance but destroys capabilities; KE preserves capabilities but fails at scale. The benchmark quantifies this tradeoff.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   **Knowledge Inertia:** High "Outdated" accuracy (model still gives old answer) despite training on updated data.
    *   **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Large drops (>20-30%) on general benchmarks like MME, OCRBench, MathVision after injection.
    *   **Cross-modal Collapse:** M→T or T→M performance drops to near-random or below the baseline, while M→M works. Indicates no shared fact representation.
    *   **Parameter Editing Limits:** KE methods (GRACE, WISE) show negligible update performance (CEM < 10%) when scaling to thousands of edits.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Establishment:** Run Vanilla model and RAG-with-Golden-Context on MMKU-Bench. Confirm: (a) RAG closes most gap for unknown knowledge but not for updated, (b) Vanilla has 100% outdated accuracy on updated tasks.
    2.  **Update vs. Unknown Comparison:** Train a single method (e.g., Full-FT) on both Updated and Unknown subsets. Measure: (a) CEM/F1 on respective test sets, (b) degradation on a lightweight general benchmark. Expect: Better CEM on Unknown, worse forgetting on Updated.
    3.  **Cross-modal Transfer Sanity Check:** For one method (e.g., LoRA), train only in M→M setting. Then evaluate in all three settings (M→M, M→T, T→M). Expect: M→M > M→T and T→M, confirming cross-modal inconsistency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** Why do LMMs struggle to overwrite entrenched parametric memories with updated knowledge, even when provided with explicit contradictory evidence?
*   **Basis in paper:** Obs 1 notes RAG with golden context still yields worse performance on updated vs. unknown knowledge. Obs 5/6 show updated knowledge causes more severe capability degradation.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates the symptom but does not probe internal mechanisms (e.g., conflicting subnetworks, layer resistance, semantic priors). No representational analysis (probing, activation patching) is conducted.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments using linear probes, activation patching, or representational similarity analysis (RSA) to compare how updated vs. unknown knowledge are encoded across layers. Success would show whether updated knowledge co-exists with old knowledge in superposition or if certain pathways remain fixed.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** What explains the paradoxical finding that models show better adaptability to updated knowledge in open-ended QA yet worse performance in MCQ settings with distractors, and how does this relate to “semantic entanglement”?
*   **Basis in paper:** Obs 2 states models adapt better to updated knowledge due to semantic priors, but Obs 5/6 and Fig. 4 Right show updated knowledge accuracy is lower than unknown knowledge in MCQ. The paper suggests semantic entanglement in updated tasks triggers residual memory biases.
*   **Why unresolved:** The link between helpful semantic priors (generation) and harmful distractor-triggered biases (choice) is hypothesized but not tested. It is unclear whether the issue stems from residual activation of old facts, distractor design, or output formatting constraints.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze model confidence distributions, probability mass on distractors vs. correct answers, and how question phrasing (open-ended vs. forced-choice) changes reliance on parametric memory. Ablate distractor similarity to old facts to test entanglement strength.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** Can we develop a knowledge updating method that scales to large-scale, continuous updates without catastrophic forgetting or prohibitive computational cost, surpassing the limitations of SFT/RLHF (forgetting) and KE (scaling)?
*   **Basis in paper:** Conclusion and Obs 4 state SFT/RLHF cause catastrophic forgetting, while KE fails under large-scale continual updates (beyond ~3k edits). The authors call this an “open challenge.”
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper benchmarks existing methods but does not propose or evaluate new scalable algorithms. The failure modes of KE at scale are noted but not analyzed mechanistically. No hybrid or novel approaches are tested.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduce and test a new method (e.g., modular adapters, sparse editing, momentum-based regularization) on MMKU-Bench with thousands of sequential edits, measuring both update accuracy and retention on general benchmarks.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Why is cross-modal knowledge transfer so poor (M→T, T→M in Fig. 4), and what architectural or training modifications could improve modality-agnostic knowledge representations?
*   **Basis in paper:** Fig. 4 (Left) shows M→M consistently outperforms cross-modal settings. The paper concludes “cross-modal knowledge alignment remains a major challenge.”
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the phenomenon but does not investigate whether the issue lies in vision-language alignment during pretraining, modality-specific bottlenecks, or the update process itself (e.g., whether edits are stored in modality-specific parameters).
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct probing experiments to compare representation similarity of the same fact in multimodal vs. unimodal contexts after injection. Test architectural changes (e.g., tying vision-language projections, multimodal contrastive losses during fine-tuning) and measure transfer improvements.

### Open Question 5
*   **Question:** Why does RLHF (CPO/ORPO/SimPO) only partially reduce reliance on outdated facts (Obs 3), and what reward design or data configuration could fully suppress outdated knowledge without harming new knowledge acquisition?
*   **Basis in paper:** Obs 3 states RLHF is more effective than SFT at weakening reliance on outdated knowledge but improvements are “limited and unstable.” The paper does not explain why RLHF succeeds partially or how to optimize it.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper uses preference pairs where outdated/incorrect answers are rejected, but the reward model is implicit (reference-free). It is unknown whether the failure stems from insufficient contrast, the nature of the rejected samples, or the reinforcement objective itself.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze the learned reward function or policy gradients to see which features drive rejection of outdated answers. Experiment with varying distractor strengths, temperature, or adding explicit consistency penalties between modalities.

## Limitations
*   **Benchmark Construction:** GPT-4o-generated counterfactuals may not fully mimic real-world updates; undisclosed parameters (e.g., CLIP thresholds, forbidden answer list) affect dataset fidelity.
*   **Scope:** Evaluation limited to two LMM families (InternVL, Qwen) and short-answer VQA tasks; findings may not generalize to other architectures or complex reasoning updates.
*   **Knowledge Editing:** GRACE/WISE shown ineffective at the benchmark's scale; broader evaluation of editing methods and their scaling limits is absent.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (Benchmark Validity):** Medium – The pipeline is detailed, but the proxy nature of counterfactual editing and hidden parameters limit certainty.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Updated vs. Unknown Dynamics):** High – Supported by consistent evidence across multiple metrics, methods, and model evaluations.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Cross-modal Transfer Failure):** High – Robust performance gaps observed in all experiments across both model families.

## Next Checks
1.  Conduct a human evaluation of a stratified sample of MMKU-Bench's updated/unknown QA pairs to verify answer plausibility and image-text alignment, validating the counterfactual and temporal extraction processes.
2.  Replicate the core experiments on at least two additional model families (e.g., LLaVA, a GPT-4o class model) to test the generalizability of the key findings on updated/unknown dynamics and cross-modal transfer.
3.  Perform a representational analysis (e.g., CCA, linear probes) on the vision and text encoders of a fine-tuned model to diagnose the cause of cross-modal transfer breakdown and the encoding difference between updated versus unknown knowledge.
