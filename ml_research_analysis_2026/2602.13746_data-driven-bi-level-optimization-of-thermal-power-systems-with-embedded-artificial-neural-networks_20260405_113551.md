# Data-driven Bi-level Optimization of Thermal Power Systems with embedded Artificial Neural Networks
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.13746
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13746
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ANN-KKT framework solves bi-level thermal power optimization within 0.22–0.88 s, achieving optimal set-points (coal: 583 MW, 7337 kJ/kWh; gas: 402 MW, 7542 kJ/kWh) and robust envelopes maintaining >42% gas turbine efficiency.

## Executive Summary
Thermal power system optimization involves hierarchical objectives (e.g., maximize power while minimizing heat rate), leading to computationally challenging bi-level problems. This study introduces an ANN-KKT framework where artificial neural networks approximate both upper- and lower-level objectives, and the lower-level problem is embedded via Karush-Kuhn-Tucker optimality conditions, reformulating the problem as a single-level optimization. Validated on standard benchmarks and two industrial plants—a 660 MW coal-fired unit and a 395 MW gas turbine—the method matches traditional bi-level solutions with marginal computational overhead (0.22–0.88 s). Optimal operations produce 583 MW at 7337 kJ/kWh for the coal plant and 402 MW at 7542 kJ/kWh for the gas turbine. The framework also identifies robust operating envelopes that hedge against uncertainty, maintaining thermal efficiency above 42% for the gas system.

## Method Summary
The approach replaces upper- and lower-level objectives with differentiable ANN surrogates trained on historical plant data. The lower-level problem is eliminated by embedding its KKT conditions—stationarity, primal/dual feasibility, and complementarity—as constraints in a single-level mathematical program with equilibrium constraints (MPEC). Complementarity is smoothed using the Fischer-Burmeister function to avoid numerical instability. A Mahalanobis distance constraint, derived from the training data covariance, ensures the solution stays within the historical operational envelope. The resulting nonlinear program is solved with IPOPT, achieving near-instantaneous solutions. The framework also extends to robust optimization by introducing worst-case perturbations within a stability radius, providing envelopes that guarantee performance under uncertainty.

## Key Results
- Computationally efficient: bi-level problems solved with 0.22–0.88 s overhead, matching traditional methods.
- Coal plant optimum: 583 MW output at turbine heat rate 7337 kJ/kWh.
- Gas turbine optimum and robustness: 402 MW at 7542 kJ/kWh; robust envelopes maintain thermal efficiency >42% under uncertainty.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Replacing both upper- and lower-level objective functions with smooth ANN surrogates and embedding the lower-level problem via KKT conditions reformulates a computationally intractable bi-level problem into a single-level optimization solvable with standard nonlinear programming solvers.
- **Mechanism**: The hierarchical problem is transformed by expressing the lower-level optimality conditions (stationarity, primal/dual feasibility, complementarity) as constraints. ANN surrogates provide continuously differentiable replacements, enabling gradient-based solvers to navigate the combined search space.
- **Core assumption**: Trained ANNs accurately approximate the true lower-level response over the relevant domain; KKT conditions are sufficient for a local optimum (constraint qualifications hold).
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states ANN-KKT reformulation; Section 2.2.1 gives the mathematical formulation with ANN-based objectives and KKT constraints.
- **Break condition**: If ANN surrogates introduce spurious local minima or non-smooth regions, the solver may converge to suboptimal or infeasible points violating the original hierarchy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Embedding Mahalanobis distance constraint \((y-\mu)^T \Sigma^{-1} (y-\mu) \leq \tau^2\) ensures optimal solutions respect the historical data distribution, yielding physically plausible set-points.
- **Mechanism**: The ellipsoidal constraint, defined by training data covariance \(\Sigma\), penalizes correlated deviations from the data manifold; \(\tau\) controls stringency.
- **Core assumption**: Multivariate Gaussian assumption adequately captures operational correlations; training data covers safe regimes.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 3.2.1 describes the constraint’s role; Figure 5b illustrates effect of varying \(\tau\) on feasible envelope and solution.
- **Break condition**: If data contains outliers or lacks coverage of safe states, the envelope may exclude truly optimal points or include unsafe regions; \(\tau\) tuning is non‑trivial.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Reformulating complementarity slackness using the Fischer-Burmeister function with a small perturbation \(\varepsilon\) mitigates numerical instability from discrete products.
- **Mechanism**: Replace \(\mu \cdot g = 0\) with smooth \(\Phi^*_{FB}(\mu,g,\varepsilon) = \sqrt{\mu^2+g^2+\varepsilon} - \mu - g\), enabling gradient-based solvers to handle complementarity as a smooth equality.
- **Core assumption**: \(\varepsilon\) small enough to preserve feasible set geometry near the solution but large enough to ensure smoothness.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 2.2.2 introduces the function; Appendix B.4 shows IPOPT convergence with this reformulation.
- **Break condition**: Poor \(\varepsilon\) choice (too large or too small) distorts feasible region or fails to smooth, causing solver failure.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
1. **Data Pipeline**: Collect historical plant data (operating variables, performance metrics), clean, and split (train/val/test). Normalize inputs to [0,1].
2. **ANN Training Module**: For each performance variable (Power, THR), use Hyperopt+PyTorch to search hyperparameters (neurons, learning rate, λ regularization) and train a shallow feed‑forward network with SiLU activation. Validate via R² and RMSE.
3. **Reformulation Engine**: Construct the single‑level optimization problem. Embed trained ANN objectives. Formulate KKT constraints for the lower‑level problem (stationarity using symbolic gradients ∇ᵧf_ANN, primal feasibility, box constraints). Apply Fischer‑Burmeister reformulation to complementarity conditions if numerical violations occur.
4. **Domain Constraint Integrator**: Compute mean (µ) and covariance (Σ) from training data. Add Mahalanobis ellipsoid constraint \((y-µ)^T Σ^{-1} (y-μ) ≤ τ²\) with tunable τ.
5. **Solver Interface**: Export the Pyomo model to GAMS/IPOPT (or BARON). Configure solver tolerances, iterate, and monitor convergence (objective progress, primal infeasibility).
6. **Post-processing & Validation**: Extract optimal (x*, y*). Check constraint violation and LICQ. Compare against benchmarks or historical operating points. For robust optimization, generate worst‑case perturbations δ within the stability radius ρ.

### Critical path
Data Collection → ANN Training → Reformulation → Solver Convergence.

### Design tradeoffs
- **ANN Capacity vs. Optimization Stability**: Deeper/wider ANNs may fit data better but can have noisier second‑order gradients, harming solver convergence. The paper opts for shallow nets.
- **Mahalanobis Tolerance (τ)**: Low τ yields highly domain‑compliant but potentially suboptimal solutions; high τ risks infeasible or unrealistic set‑points. Requires iterative tuning.
- **Solver Choice**: IPOPT (local, fast, open‑source) vs. BARON (global, slower). Paper uses IPOPT for real‑time applicability but notes BARON for validation.

### Failure signatures
- **Solver fails to converge**: Often due to non‑smooth ANN gradients, poor scaling, or violated LICQ. Remedy: Check surrogate smoothness, tighten bounds, use Fischer‑Burmeister, or scale variables.
- **KKT constraints persistently violated**: The optimal point does not satisfy stationarity or complementarity. Indicates ANN surrogate quality is insufficient in critical regions or τ is too loose.
- **Solutions lie outside historical data cloud**: τ is set too high. Lower τ and retest.
- **"Infeasible" solutions in results**: The combination of τ and the plant’s nonlinear response makes the MPEC infeasible. Reduce τ or relax constraints.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the Mahalanobis distance threshold τ be systematically tuned to balance feasibility and optimality in the ANN‑KKT framework for thermal power plant optimization?
- **Basis**: The authors state in the Limitations and Future Work section: “We have embedded Mahalanobis distance‑based constraint that adjust the search space… The tuning of τ is challenging and it affects the feasibility of solutions, rendering it one of the limitations of this study.”
- **Why unresolved**: The current approach relies on ad hoc selection of τ percentiles (e.g., 81%–95%) without a principled method to determine the optimal trade‑off between staying within the training data manifold and expanding the search space. This manual tuning may not generalize to other plants or data distributions.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An adaptive τ‑selection algorithm (e.g., based on Bayesian optimization, cross‑validation, or real‑time constraint violation feedback) that can automatically determine τ for a given plant. Validation on multiple independent plant datasets showing a higher success rate of feasible optimal solutions compared to fixed percentiles.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can ANN surrogate models be convexified or otherwise structured to improve numerical convergence and guarantee global optimality in the KKT reformulation?
- **Basis**: The authors write: “ANN model introduce non‑convexity in the optimization problem and may complicate the solution convergence profiles; future research may consider convexification of ANN models that may expedite the numerical convergence performance of the optimization solver.”
- **Why unresolved**: The non‑convex nature of standard feed‑forward ANNs can lead to local minima and solver instability, especially for non‑convex lower‑level problems. No concrete convexification method (e.g., input convex neural networks) was implemented or tested within the ANN‑KKT framework.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Development and integration of convex neural network architectures or training schemes that enforce convexity of the learned objective functions. Benchmarking on standard bi‑level test problems and the industrial case studies, demonstrating faster convergence, reduced solver iterations, and elimination of infeasible solutions due to numerical instability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: To what extent does the ANN‑KKT framework generalize to other thermal power configurations (e.g., combined cycle, multi‑unit coordination) and larger‑scale systems?
- **Basis**: The paper only applies the method to a single 660 MW coal unit and a 395 MW gas turbine. Scalability is claimed in the abstract and conclusions, but not tested on more complex systems with multiple interacting units, additional performance metrics (e.g., emissions), or larger numbers of decision variables.
- **Why unresolved**: The computational benefits observed on two relatively small case studies may not hold as the problem size grows or when additional nonlinear constraints and coupling are introduced. The structure of the bi‑level problem might change (e.g., mixed‑integer decisions) and affect solver performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Application of the ANN‑KKT framework to at least one additional complex system, such as a combined cycle gas turbine with heat recovery steam generator or a multi‑unit power plant optimization. The study should report solution quality, computational time, and memory usage as the number of variables and constraints increases, providing clear evidence of scalability or identifying breakpoints.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Can the ANN‑KKT framework be integrated into a dynamic model predictive control (MPC) scheme to handle real‑time optimization under changing operating conditions and disturbances?
- **Basis**: The paper focuses exclusively on steady‑state optimal operating points. There is no discussion of closed‑loop control, time‑varying inputs, or the ability to provide fast solutions repeatedly in a receding horizon fashion.
- **Why unresolved**: Industrial power plants operate in dynamic environments where conditions (e.g., ambient temperature, demand, fuel quality) change frequently. A static optimization may become outdated quickly, and the ANN‑KKT framework’s computational time (though small) must be evaluated within an MPC loop that requires solving the problem every few minutes.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implementation of a pilot or software‑in‑the‑loop MPC that calls the ANN‑KKT solver at each control interval using real‑time sensor data. Performance metrics such as constraint satisfaction, economic objective improvement, and average solve time should be reported over a simulated or real campaign, demonstrating the framework’s suitability for dynamic operation.

## Limitations
- **Data and hyperparameter transparency**: Raw industrial data and full ANN training details (λ₁, λ₂, random seeds) are not provided, hindering independent verification and exact reproducibility.
- **Surrogate and domain constraints**: ANN accuracy is critical; Mahalanobis distance constraint depends on a Gaussian assumption and careful τ tuning; poor choices lead to infeasible or unrealistic solutions.
- **Numerical stability and optimality**: Fischer‑Burmeister smoothing parameter ε requires careful selection; IPOPT is a local solver with no global optimality guarantee and may converge to suboptimal points.
