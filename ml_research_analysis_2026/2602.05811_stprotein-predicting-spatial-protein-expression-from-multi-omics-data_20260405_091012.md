# STProtein: predicting spatial protein expression from multi-omics data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05811
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05811
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** STProtein predicts spatial protein expression from transcriptomics via a graph attention autoencoder with multi-task learning, achieving lower RMSE and superior clustering metrics than four baselines across three datasets.

## Executive Summary
STProtein addresses the scarcity of matched spatial proteomics data by predicting protein expression from spatial transcriptomics. It constructs a K-nearest neighbor (KNN) graph based on transcriptional similarity in latent space and employs a graph attention autoencoder trained with a combined RNA and protein reconstruction loss. Evaluated on mouse spleen, mouse thymus, and human lymph node datasets, it outperforms totalVI, scArches, Dengkw, and cTp net in protein prediction accuracy (RMSE) and downstream spatial domain clustering (NMI, ARI). Ablation studies confirm the importance of its KNN graph construction and multi-task learning objective. A case study on mouse spleen reveals novel macrophage subsets, suggesting utility for biological discovery.

## Method Summary
STProtein processes spatial transcriptomics (RNA) and spatial proteomics (protein) data from co-assayed spots. RNA is log-normalized, the top 4000 highly variable genes are selected, and PCA is applied, retaining a number of components equal to the protein feature count. Protein data undergoes CLR normalization. A KNN graph (k=3) is built from the PCA-reduced RNA embeddings, where nodes represent spots and edges connect spots with similar transcriptional profiles regardless of physical distance. A Graph Attention Network v2 (GATv2) autoencoder processes this graph: the encoder generates a latent embedding that serves as the predicted protein expression, while the decoder reconstructs the input RNA. The model is trained with a multi-task loss combining RNA reconstruction (MSE) and protein prediction (MSE) losses, weighted (β1=5, β2=3). For downstream analysis, the predicted protein matrix is clustered using mclust to define spatial domains.

## Key Results
- STProtein achieves lower RMSE (e.g., 0.95 on mouse spleen) than four baseline methods (totalVI, scArches, Dengkw, cTp net) for upstream protein expression prediction.
- In downstream spatial domain clustering, STProtein yields superior NMI, ARI, and FMI scores across all three evaluated datasets.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that the KNN feature graph and the combined multi-task loss are critical for performance; removing either degrades both prediction and clustering metrics.
- A mouse spleen case study shows STProtein's predicted protein patterns reveal macrophage subsets (MZMΦ, MMMΦ) not detected in the original annotation, suggesting potential for novel biological insights.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** STProtein predicts protein expression by modeling relationships between spots based on feature similarity in a latent space, not just physical proximity.
- **Mechanism:** It constructs a K-nearest neighbor (KNN) graph from PCA-reduced RNA embeddings. Each spot (node) is connected to its `k` most similar spots in this embedding space, creating edges that represent potential functional or phenotypic similarity regardless of physical distance. This graph structure is fed into a Graph Attention Network (GATv2) to propagate and aggregate information.
- **Core assumption:** Spots with similar transcriptional profiles (in reduced PCA space) are likely to share similar protein expression states, even if spatially distant in the tissue. This captures broader biological relationships beyond immediate neighborhood.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - The section "KNN Feature Graph" explicitly states the construction method and rationale: "spots with identical cell types or states may not be spatially adjacent... To capture the relationship of such spots in a latent space, we explicitly model their relationships using a feature graph."
  - The ablation study (Table 5) shows the KNN graph ("STProtein") outperforms the spatial neighbor graph ("SN Feature Graph") on RMSE (0.95 vs. 0.96) and all clustering metrics (e.g., ARI 40.43% vs. 21.31%).
  - Related work (e.g., SpatialGlue) also uses KNN graphs for semantic relationships, supporting the approach’s prevalence in spatial omics.
- **Break condition:** Performance would degrade in tissues where spatial adjacency (e.g., contiguous tissue structures, immediate cell-cell contact) is the dominant driver of molecular similarity, and where transcriptional similarity alone fails to capture critical spatial dependencies (e.g., strict gradient morphogens).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Multi-task learning with combined RNA and protein reconstruction losses stabilizes training and improves representation learning for both modalities.
- **Mechanism:** The autoencoder is trained to simultaneously reconstruct the input RNA expression (`L_rna`) and the target protein expression (`L_protein`). The total loss `L_total = β1 * L_rna + β2 * L_protein` forces the shared latent embedding (the protein prediction output) to preserve information relevant to both data types.
- **Core assumption:** The shared latent space is constrained by both modalities, preventing the model from learning spurious correlations specific to only one data type and encouraging a more biologically coherent representation that captures cross-modal relationships.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - The section "Loss Function" defines the multi-task objective (Eq. 12-14).
  - The ablation study (Tables 5 & 6) demonstrates that using either `L_protein` alone or `L_rna` alone yields worse upstream (RMSE 0.97/1.02) and downstream (ARI 19.12%/9.23%) performance than the combined loss (RMSE 0.95, ARI 40.43%).
  - The abstract states the framework employs a "multi-task learning strategy."
- **Break condition:** If RNA and protein expression are governed by entirely independent biological mechanisms in the studied tissue, or if one modality is extremely noisy or sparse, the multi-task constraint could introduce harmful bias or prevent fitting the dominant signal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The GATv2-based autoencoder architecture effectively integrates spatial feature graph topology with node attributes to generate robust protein embeddings.
- **Mechanism:** The encoder uses two stacked GATv2 layers with multi-head attention to learn spot embeddings. The attention mechanism dynamically weights neighbor contributions based on feature similarity, allowing the model to focus on the most relevant spatial context for each spot. The decoder reverses this process to reconstruct RNA. The protein embedding (encoder output) is used directly for prediction.
- **Core assumption:** The attention mechanism in GATv2 can learn meaningful, data-dependent edge weights on the KNN graph, effectively distinguishing which latent neighbors are most informative for predicting a given spot’s protein profile.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - The section "Graph Attention Layer" details the GATv2 implementation, including the dynamic attention score computation (Eq. 2).
  - The section "Graph Convolutional Layer Model Comparison" (Tables 9 & 10) shows GATv2 outperforms GCN, SAGE, Transformer, and original GAT on both upstream (lowest RMSE 0.95) and downstream (highest ARI 40.43%) tasks on the mouse spleen dataset.
  - The section "Method" states the encoder output "is considered as the reconstructed protein normalized expression."
- **Break condition:** Would fail if the pre-constructed KNN graph is inherently flawed (e.g., wrong `k`, PCA discarded critical dimensions), leaving the attention mechanism with a poor structural prior. Also, if the biological relationship between RNA and protein is not well-captured by message-passing over this graph type.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map (plain text):** Input RNA/Protein Matrices & Coordinates -> Preprocessing (log-normalize RNA, select HVGs, PCA; CLR protein) -> KNN Graph Construction (from PCA-RNA) -> GATv2 Autoencoder (Encoder: 2x GATv2+ReLU+Linear; Decoder: symmetric with tied weights) -> Loss Calculation (β1·L_RNA + β2·L_protein) -> Encoder Output Z (Predicted Protein) -> Downstream Clustering (mclust).
- **Critical path:** Preprocess RNA & protein -> Build KNN graph from PCA-RNA -> Train GATv2 autoencoder (minimize combined loss) -> Freeze encoder -> Apply to new RNA-only data to obtain Z for protein prediction and clustering.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **KNN vs. Spatial Neighbor Graph:** KNN captures latent similarity but ignores physical adjacency; spatial neighbor captures local structure but misses distal functional similarity. Tradeoff depends on tissue biology.
  - **Loss Weights (β1, β2):** Paper uses β1=5, β2=3 (favoring RNA reconstruction). Ablation shows insensitivity, but optimal weights likely dataset-dependent.
  - **GATv2 vs. Other GNNs:** GATv2 allows dynamic attention; simpler GCNs/SAGE are faster but less expressive. Tradeoff: accuracy vs. computational cost.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Upstream (Prediction):** High RMSE despite low training loss → likely graph construction failure (wrong `k`, PCA lost signal) or severe modality mismatch between train/test platforms.
  - **Downstream (Clustering):** Low NMI/ARI with ground truth → embedding `Z` may not separate known cell types. Check if clustering algorithm (mclust) matches data distribution; try leiden/louvain. Could indicate multi-task loss mis-weighting or that predicted proteins lack discriminative markers.
  - **General:** If training is unstable or loss doesn’t converge, check normalization (CLR for protein, log-normal for RNA) and PCA dimension consistency.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Reproduce Table 1 (Upstream Benchmarking):** On provided Zenodo datasets, train STProtein on a mouse spleen replicate, predict protein for the other replicate, compute RMSE vs. totalVI/scArches/Dengkw/cTp net. Validate the claimed RMSE advantage (~0.95).
  2.  **Ablation - Loss Function (Table 5/6):** Train models with `L_rna` only, `L_protein` only, and combined loss. Confirm combined loss minimizes RMSE and maximizes ARI/NMI, justifying MTL.
  3.  **Parameter Sensitivity - K Value (Table 11/12):** Systematically vary `k` in {1,2,3,4,5} for KNN graph. Confirm optimal performance at `k=3` on mouse spleen for both RMSE and ARI, establishing a default.

## Limitations
- Generalizability is limited to three lymphoid tissue datasets; performance on other tissues (e.g., brain, tumor) or newer platforms (e.g., Xenium, MERFISH) is unverified.
- The core assumption that transcriptional similarity in PCA space predicts protein co-expression may break in tissues with strong spatial gradients (e.g., morphogen-driven development), where physical adjacency matters more.
- The PCA dimension is rigidly fixed to the protein count, which may discard discriminative RNA variance.
- The ablation favors KNN over spatial graphs, but hybrid approaches (e.g., combining physical and feature edges) remain unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Protein prediction accuracy (RMSE):** Medium. Validated on three datasets vs. four baselines, but baseline implementations may vary; reproduction sensitivity to seeds/hyperparameters is unknown.
- **Clustering improvement (NMI/ARI):** Medium. mclust-dependent; performance may drop with alternative clustering algorithms or unknown cell type numbers.
- **KNN graph efficacy:** Medium. Ablation shows benefit over spatial neighbors, but optimal `k` is dataset-specific (paper uses `k`=3).
- **Multi-task loss benefit:** High. Consistent ablation gains across upstream/downstream tasks strongly support MTL.
- **GATv2 advantage:** Medium. Outperforms other GNNs on one dataset; may not hold with different graph constructions or larger `k`.
