# Extended Empirical Validation of the Explainability Solution Space
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01235
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01235
- **Reference count:** 3
- **Primary result:** ESS generalizability is supported across high-stakes, substitution-governed tabular domains, yielding a consistent hybrid recommendation (SHAP always-on, Counterfactuals selective, Rule Extraction periodic) mirroring the prior HR attrition case.

## Executive Summary
This report validates the Explainability Solution Space (ESS) framework in a novel, high-stakes domain: real-time European bank fraud detection. The study applies ESS's stakeholder-weighted evaluation (Compliance, User, Developer) to five XAI techniques, incorporating a substitution deployment context and a 200ms latency constraint. Results produce differentiated, non-dominated technique profiles, leading to a hybrid deployment strategy that closely matches the pattern found in a prior HR attrition case. This provides initial empirical evidence for ESS's cross-domain generalizability in governed, real-time tabular settings, while highlighting remaining uncertainties regarding the framework's fixed weight assignments and sensitivity to contextual multipliers.

## Method Summary
The ESS pipeline was applied to an XGBoost fraud classifier trained on engineered transaction, geospatial, device, and behavioral velocity data. Five XAI techniques were profiled across seven intrinsic properties (1–5 scale). Baseline stakeholder scores (C, U, D) were computed via fixed linear aggregations of these properties. Contextual multipliers for a "substitution" deployment (γC=1.15, γU=1.10, γD=1.00) were applied to adjust scores before clipping. A composite utility score (Ut) and a resource cost proxy (Rt = 1/Eff.) were calculated, with the U/R ratio used to rank techniques for a tiered recommendation (always-on, selective, periodic) under the latency constraint.

## Key Results
- SHAP achieved the highest efficiency-adjusted utility (U/R = 15.3) with strong Compliance and Developer scores, securing the always-on tier.
- Counterfactuals maximized adjusted User recourse (U' = 5.00) for adverse decisions, earning a selective/dispute tier role.
- Rule Extraction attained the highest raw utility (U = 3.90) but was excluded from real-time tiers due to latency, recommended for periodic offline audits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The ESS framework converts abstract XAI characteristics into stakeholder-aligned scores via weighted aggregation of intrinsic properties.
- **Mechanism:** Each technique is assigned a 7-dimensional property vector (Auditability, Traceability, Comprehensibility, Actionability, Fidelity, Debuggability, Efficiency). Fixed linear weights aggregate these into three stakeholder axes—Compliance (C), User (U), Developer (D)—producing a baseline coordinate.
- **Core assumption:** The defined linear aggregation weights and property dimensions sufficiently capture the core, irreducible needs of each stakeholder group across diverse tabular domains.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] presents aggregation functions (Equations 2–4) and applies them to Table 2; [section 3.2] defines the seven intrinsic properties.
- **Break condition:** If the fixed aggregation weights (e.g., 0.6 for Audit. in C) do not represent a specific organization's priorities, derived rankings may misalign with operational requirements. The paper acknowledges these are "first-order approximations pending formal elicitation studies."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The framework adapts baseline scores to the specific risk context via *contextual multipliers* (γ_C, γ_U, γ_D) tied to deployment modality (e.g., substitution).
- **Mechanism:** For a "substitution" deployment, pre-defined multipliers (e.g., γ_C=1.15) amplify axes tied to heightened accountability (Compliance) and individual impact (User) before clipping to a [1,5] range.
- **Core assumption:** A finite set of *usage situation classifications* with associated multiplier values can systematically adjust technique profiles across domains.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 2.3] classifies the system as "Substitution" and lists applied multipliers; [section 3.4] shows final adjusted coordinates (Table 3) and notes non-trivial adjustments.
- **Break condition:** The multiplier scheme may be too coarse. Unforeseen domain-specific risks (e.g., adversarial fraud drift) might require new classification types or non-linear adjustments not captured by current multipliers.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The framework enables a principled hybrid recommendation by combining *resource-aware multi-objective optimization* with tiered deployment logic.
- **Mechanism:** A composite utility score (U_t) weights adjusted C', U', D' scores (e.g., Ut = 0.4C' + 0.4U' + 0.2D'). A resource cost proxy (R_t = 1/Eff._t) inversely relates to computational efficiency. The efficiency-adjusted ratio (U/R) identifies Pareto-optimal techniques under latency constraints. Techniques are assigned to tiers (Always-on, Selective, Periodic) based on scores, cost, and feasibility.
- **Core assumption:** The specific utility weighting (0.4, 0.4, 0.2 for substitution) and linear cost proxy are adequate for making operational trade-offs in high-stakes, real-time deployments.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4] defines Ut (Equation 6) and Rt (Equation 7); Table 4 calculates U/R and assesses latency fit. [section 5] locks the final hybrid recommendation to the U/R and feasibility analysis.
- **Break condition:** If utility weights do not match the organization's true trade-offs (e.g., they value Developer input more than 0.2), the recommended hybrid may allocate resources sub-optimally. Fixed weights are a methodological choice, not a validated model.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Stakeholder Axes (C, U, D)**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire ESS structure rests on the premise that explainability must serve three irreducible, sometimes conflicting, stakeholder needs. Understanding each axis' composition (e.g., Compliance = Auditability + Traceability) is essential to interpret any technique's profile.
  - **Quick check:** For a fraud system, why would "Traceability" be part of the Compliance axis and not the User axis?

- **Concept: Substitution Deployment Context**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the *contextual modifier* that adjusts baseline scores. It defines the domain as "most demanding" per the ESS taxonomy, justifying amplification of C and U scores and driving the hybrid tier design (e.g., needing recourse for automated blocks).
  - **Quick check:** How would the ESS ranking likely differ for a "human-in-the-loop" advisory system compared to this substitution fraud system?

- **Concept: Efficiency-Adjusted Utility (U/R) Ratio**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the key *decision metric* for resource-constrained, real-time deployment. It prevents selecting the highest raw utility (U) technique if its computational cost (R) is prohibitive under the 200ms latency budget.
  - **Quick check:** In Table 4, why does Rule Extraction have the highest U (3.90) but the *lowest* U/R (7.8)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Property Assignment -> Baseline Aggregation -> Contextual Adjustment -> Multi-Objective Scoring -> Tier Assignment
- **Critical path:** Property Assignment -> Baseline Aggregation -> Contextual Adjustment -> Multi-Objective Scoring. The quality of the final recommendation is most sensitive to the initial property vector assignments and the correctness of the usage classification.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Fixed Weights vs. Elicited Preferences:* Universal fixed weights ensure reproducibility but risk misalignment; obtaining real stakeholder weights via elicitation is costly and domain-specific.
  - *Linear Aggregation vs. Non-linear Interactions:* The additive model assumes stakeholder needs are independent. A technique strong on both Comprehensibility and Actionability might still fail if explanations are too technical for customer service agents.
  - *Efficiency Proxy vs. Hard Constraints:* Using `Rt = 1/Eff.` treats cost as a continuous trade-off. However, the 200ms latency is a *hard feasibility gate*. Rule Extraction is excluded not just for a low U/R but because it's fundamentally offline.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Single-point failure in property assignment:** An incorrect property score can cascade into a completely wrong tier placement.
  - **Mis-classified usage situation:** Applying "Advisory" multipliers instead of "Substitution" multipliers would invalidate the adjustment rationale and likely the hybrid design.
  - **Ignored hard constraints:** Recommending Rule Extraction for real-time use because its raw utility is highest, without checking its Eff. score and offline nature, would cause system failure.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Reproduce the Core Pipeline:** Implement the ESS calculations (Equations 2-7) for the fraud case using Table 2. Verify exact C', U', D' in Table 3 and U/R in Table 4.
  2.  **Test Sensitivity of Usage Classification:** Rerun assuming a "Human-in-the-loop" (Advisory) context with hypothetical multipliers (e.g., γ_U=1.05, γ_C=1.0). Observe tier assignment changes. Does SHAP remain dominant? Does CF's advantage shrink?
  3.  **Simulate Hard Constraint Violation:** Take the recommended "always-on" SHAP. Estimate its actual 99th-percentile latency on production-scale features. Compare to 200ms. If it approaches the limit, experiment with reducing SHAP's `Eff.` score and observe impact on U/R and potential tier demotion.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How should the stakeholder-weighted aggregation coefficients (e.g., 0.6 for Audit in Compliance) be formally elicited from domain experts to replace current heuristic assignments?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work prioritizes "user studies ... to validate the assumed property weights."
- **Why unresolved:** Current aggregation weights are first-order approximations without formal stakeholder elicitation, potentially misaligning with actual governance priorities.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct structured elicitation sessions with compliance officers, fraud analysts, and data scientists to derive empirically grounded weights, then re-run ESS analyses to compare ranking changes.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How sensitive are ESS technique rankings to variations in contextual multipliers (γC, γU, γD) across different substitution scenarios?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work prioritizes "sensitivity analyses of the multiplier scheme."
- **Why unresolved:** Multipliers are fixed heuristically based on substitution classification, but their quantitative impact on final technique scores and recommendations is unvalidated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically vary multipliers within plausible ranges (e.g., 0.9–1.2) and compute rank correlations (e.g., Spearman’s ρ) between resulting technique orderings to assess stability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can the ESS contextual modifier system be extended to represent hard operational constraints like latency budgets and user population heterogeneity as first-class constructs?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work prioritizes "extension of the contextual modifier system to capture latency constraints and dual-user-population heterogeneity."
- **Why unresolved:** Latency is only indirectly captured via the continuous Efficiency property, not as a binary feasibility gate; the User axis aggregates distinct sub-populations (analysts vs. agents) into a single score, possibly obscuring trade-offs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduce online/offline mode flags and decompose User axis into expert/lay sub-scores; re-evaluate the fraud detection case study to observe changes in technique feasibility and tiered recommendations.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What property dimension(s) should be added to the ESS to account for temporal stability of explanations in adversarially non-stationary domains like fraud detection?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Discussion notes "the ESS currently does not model temporal robustness ... incorporating a temporal fidelity property would be particularly relevant."
- **Why unresolved:** The seven-dimensional property vector lacks any measure of explanation stability over time, which is critical in domains where data distributions shift due to adversarial adaptation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Define and measure temporal fidelity metrics (e.g., variance of feature importance scores over rolling time windows) for XAI techniques under simulated distribution shift; integrate the best-performing metric as a new property and re-profile techniques to assess impact on Pareto optimality.

## Limitations
- The intrinsic property vectors (Table 2) lack transparent derivation; scores cannot be independently verified without the hidden expert calibration process.
- The exact fraud dataset, feature engineering, and model training details are undisclosed, preventing end-to-end replication.
- Fixed stakeholder weightings and usage multipliers are first-order approximations; their applicability to other regulatory regimes or user populations is unknown.
- The efficiency proxy (1/Eff.) may not capture real-world latency variance; the 200ms constraint is treated as a soft filter, but a single outlier transaction could violate SLAs.

## Confidence
- **Framework Validity:** Confidence: Medium. Internal equations are clear, but property assignments and weight justification rely on unpublished elicitation.
- **Domain Generalizability:** Confidence: Medium. Supported by two case studies; broader validation needed.
- **Optimality of Recommendation:** Confidence: Low. Optimality hinges on untested utility weights, coarse multipliers, and the assumption of linear stakeholder aggregation.
