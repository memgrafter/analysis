# Moonshine v2: Ergodic Streaming Encoder ASR for Latency-Critical Speech Applications

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.12241
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12241
- **Reference count**: 7
- **Primary result**: Moonshine v2 achieves state-of-the-art word error rates while reducing streaming latency by 3–11× on edge hardware, using a novel ergodic encoder with bounded time-to-first-token independent of utterance length.

## Executive Summary
Latency-critical on-device automatic speech recognition (ASR) is fundamentally limited by full-attention encoders, which incur quadratic computational cost and cause time-to-first-token (TTFT) to grow linearly with audio duration. Moonshine v2 resolves this with an ergodic streaming encoder that employs sliding-window self-attention and deliberately omits positional embeddings. This design yields a bounded TTFT that plateaus after a short initial context window, enabling responsive real-time transcription. Models ranging from 33M to 245M parameters match the accuracy of much larger systems like Whisper Large v3 while offering dramatically lower end-to-end latency and compute load on devices such as the Apple M3.

## Method Summary
Moonshine v2 addresses low-latency streaming ASR by redesigning the encoder for incremental processing. The system processes 16 kHz raw audio through a causal preprocessor (CMVN, asinh nonlinearity, two stride-2 convolutions) to produce a ~50 Hz feature stream. The core innovation is an ergodic Transformer encoder that applies sliding-window self-attention with a fixed local context (e.g., 16 past, 4 future frames) and contains no positional embeddings, making its computations translation-invariant. This allows the encoder to emit usable representations as soon as the local window is filled, without waiting for the full utterance. A lightweight adapter then adds learned positional embeddings to the encoder outputs, bridging the position-free encoder with the position-aware decoder. The decoder is a standard causal Transformer with rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) that performs cross-attention and autoregressive token generation. The models are trained on approximately 300 K hours of speech using a Schedule-Free optimizer.

## Key Results
- Bounded TTFT: Sliding-window attention ensures time-to-first-token plateaus after ~80 ms of audio, independent of utterance duration.
- Accuracy: Moonshine v2 models (33M–245M) achieve word error rates comparable to Whisper models up to 6× larger (e.g., Whisper Large v3).
- Latency & efficiency: On Apple M3, response latency ranges from 50–258 ms versus 289–11 286 ms for Whisper, with compute load reduced by 3–11×.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Sliding-window self-attention in the encoder bounds time-to-first-token (TTFT) independently of utterance length.
- **Mechanism**: By restricting each frame's attention to a fixed local neighborhood (e.g., 16 past, 4 future frames), the computational cost of attention mixing becomes linear in sequence length (O(T·w) vs. O(T²)). The encoder can incrementally emit usable representations as soon as the local context window is filled, without waiting for the full utterance.
- **Core assumption**: Local acoustic and phonetic context within the window is sufficient for high-quality intermediate representations; any ambiguity requiring longer-range context can be resolved later by the decoder.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "sliding-window self-attention to achieve bounded, low-latency inference while preserving strong local context."
  - [section 2.2] "With a fixed window size w, the attention mixing cost becomes linear in sequence length... the encoder can emit usable representations incrementally as soon as the required local context has arrived."
  - [section 3.2] Details window configuration (16,4) and its 80 ms algorithmic lookahead.
- **Break condition**: If the model's accuracy degrades significantly on utterances requiring long-range coarticulation or lexical disambiguation that cannot be resolved by the decoder's cross-attention, the local window size may be insufficient.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Removing positional embeddings from the encoder (making it "ergodic") ensures that encoder computations are translation-invariant, simplifying the streaming computation graph and removing dependence on absolute sequence position.
- **Mechanism**: Without absolute or relative positional embeddings, the encoder applies the same function to any local window of audio features regardless of its temporal location in the utterance. This eliminates the need to track or recompute position-dependent states as audio streams in, enabling a simpler, more predictable incremental processing pipeline.
- **Core assumption**: The necessary temporal structure for transcription can be introduced later (via positional embeddings added in the adapter and decoder) and does not need to be encoded in the initial feature extraction stage.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "ergodic streaming encoder... using sliding-window self-attention and no positional embeddings."
  - [section 3.2] "We do not use any absolute or relative positional embeddings in the encoder. As a result, encoder computations are translation-invariant in time... the encoder is ergodic in the sense that it has no explicit notion of absolute position."
- **Break condition**: If the decoder's added positional embeddings are insufficient to establish the temporal ordering required for coherent sequence generation (e.g., for very long utterances or specific linguistic structures), the lack of encoder position awareness may harm final accuracy.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: An adapter layer re-introduces positional information to the encoder outputs before cross-attention in the decoder, bridging the position-free encoder and the position-aware decoder.
- **Mechanism**: The adapter adds a learned positional embedding to the sliding-window encoder's output features and projects them to the decoder's dimension. This provides the decoder with the absolute position signals it expects via its rotary positional embeddings (RoPE), while allowing the encoder to remain position-free and streaming-efficient.
- **Core assumption**: The positional information required by the decoder can be added as a simple, post-hoc embedding without disrupting the optimized, incremental encoder output stream. The decoder's RoPE can effectively utilize these position-augmented features for accurate token prediction.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.3] "The adapter bridges the ergodic encoder and the decoder. It adds a learned positional embedding to the encoder outputs... the encoder remains position-free, while the decoder receives position-aware inputs."
  - [section 3.4] Mentions the decoder uses "rotary positional embeddings (RoPE)."
- **Break condition**: If the adapter's simple addition of a positional embedding is a bottleneck or fails to provide sufficiently accurate positional cues for the decoder's cross-attention, transcription quality may drop compared to a model with fully integrated positional encoding.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Audio Preprocessor -> Ergodic Encoder -> Adapter -> Decoder
- **Critical path**: Audio Stream → Preprocessor (causal, no wait) → Encoder (incremental, bounded by window) → Adapter (adds position) → Decoder (autoregressive loop) → Text Tokens. The first usable encoder state for the bottom layer is available after `wright` frames (80 ms) have arrived.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Accuracy vs. Latency**: Smaller window → lower latency but potential loss of long-range context disambiguation. Config (16,4) is a chosen trade-off.
  - **Encoder vs. Decoder Complexity**: Encoder is streamlined for streaming (sliding window, no pos emb); decoder remains a full autoregressive Transformer with cross-attention, holding most parameters.
  - **Provisional vs. Finalized States**: Using right-context layers means the newest encoder states are provisional and will be revised as more audio arrives. Systems must handle transcription corrections.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High WER on lexically ambiguous sentences where resolving homophones or distant word dependencies relies on context beyond the window, *and* the decoder fails to correct via cross-attention.
  - Non-decreasing TTFT with audio length in empirical tests, indicating the streaming implementation is not truly incremental (e.g., waiting for whole chunks).
  - Jumpy or inconsistent transcriptions in live streaming, suggesting the provisional/finalized state update mechanism is not handled correctly in the serving pipeline.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **TTFT vs. Audio Duration**: Measure TTFT for Moonshine v2 and a full-attention baseline across varying input lengths (e.g., 1s to 30s). Expect Moonshine v2's TTFT to plateau, while baseline grows linearly.
  2. **WER Benchmarking**: Evaluate Moonshine v2 Tiny/Small/Medium on LibriSpeech clean/other and other Open ASR leaderboard datasets. Compare against Whisper models of similar and larger parameter counts.
  3. **Response Latency on Target Hardware**: Measure end-to-end latency (VAD end to transcript return) and compute load on an Apple M3 for Moonshine v2 vs. Whisper Tiny/Small/Large v3. Expect order-of-magnitude latency reduction for larger Whisper models.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed training data sources and acoustic conditions for the 300K hours (especially the 100K internal dataset), prohibiting independent retraining.
- Missing critical hyperparameters (learning rate schedule, dropout, etc.) and tokenizer training corpus details.
- Potential discrepancies in sliding-window attention implementation between training and inference backends affecting streaming accuracy.
- No external validation: neighboring papers show low similarity and zero citations; claims lack third-party testing.

## Confidence
- Bounded TTFT via sliding window: **High** (mathematically guaranteed O(T·w) complexity and demonstrated incremental emission).
- Ergodic encoder (no positional embeddings): **Medium** (design simplifies streaming but potential long-range context loss not ruled out).
- Overall accuracy-latency trade-off: **Medium** (strong self-reported results, yet unverified by independent benchmarks and hardware-specific).

## Next Checks
