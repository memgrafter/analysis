# Interpretable Analytic Calabi-Yau Metrics via Symbolic Distillation

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method to derive interpretable analytic approximations for Calabi-Yau metrics, which are essential in string theory but traditionally require expensive numerical computations. By applying symbolic regression to neural network surrogates, the authors obtain a compact five-term formula that maintains high fidelity over a significant moduli range. The expression not only reduces computational complexity but also reveals geometric structure through invariant scaffolds and hierarchical coefficient modulation. Validations on physical benchmarks, such as volume integrals and Yukawa couplings, demonstrate its utility for exact and approximate calculations in ways previously intractable.

## Method Summary
The approach involves training neural networks to approximate Calabi-Yau metrics across a moduli range, then using symbolic regression to distill these surrogates into sparse analytic expressions. The process identifies power sums and symmetric polynomials as key geometric features, resulting in a five-term formula with smoothly varying coefficients. No detailed reproduction notes were provided, but the core innovation lies in transforming black-box neural approximations into transparent, low-parameter analytic forms.

## Key Results
- Distilled a five-term analytic formula achieving R²=0.9994 fidelity versus the neural surrogate, with a 3,000× parameter reduction (5 parameters vs. 15,000).
- The formula's functional scaffold remains invariant across ψ∈[0,0.8]; coefficients modulate smoothly with singular terms reversing sign and symmetric terms strengthening monotonically.
- Physics benchmarks show volume integrals computed with ~2% relative error to the surrogate and exact reproduction of the Fermat-point Yukawa coupling κ₁₁₁=5.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism likely arises from the geometric structure of Calabi-Yau metrics, which often admit representations in terms of symmetric polynomials and power sums of the moduli. Symbolic regression, when applied to the neural surrogate, discovers a sparse combination of these features that captures the metric across the training range. The invariant five-term scaffold suggests the formula respects underlying symmetries (e.g., monodromy or coordinate transformations), while the smooth variation of coefficients—especially the sign reversal of singular terms and monotonic strengthening of symmetric terms—encodes higher-order corrections in a hierarchical manner. This explains the high R² despite a drastic reduction in parameters. The paper does not elaborate on the symbolic search process or why this particular scaffold emerges; those details remain opaque.

## Limitations
- Generalizability is restricted to a specific Calabi-Yau hypersurface (likely the quintic) over ψ∈[0,0.8]; extrapolation to higher ψ or other manifolds is unverified.
- Physical scope validation is partial: volume integral error is relative to the surrogate, and cumulative errors in full moduli space or other observables are unknown.
- Discovery bias may exist: the symbolic search could overfit to the neural surrogate's training distribution and prescribed functional forms, potentially misidentifying "essential" features.

## Confidence
- **High**: Empirical fidelity (R²=0.9994) and parameter reduction within the tested ψ range; smooth coefficient variation and sign patterns are directly observed.
- **Medium**: Invariant five-term scaffold and hierarchical modulation as geometric principles; volume integral accuracy of ~2% based on surrogate comparison.
- **Low**: Generalizability to arbitrary Calabi-Yau manifolds; broader utility for exact physical predictions; fundamentality of identified features versus dataset artifacts.
