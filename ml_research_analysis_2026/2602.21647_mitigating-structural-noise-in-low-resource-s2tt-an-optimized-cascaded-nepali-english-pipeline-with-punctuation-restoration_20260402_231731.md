# Mitigating Structural Noise in Low-Resource S2TT: An Optimized Cascaded Nepali-English Pipeline with Punctuation Restoration

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.21647
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21647
*   **Reference count:** 3
*   **Primary result:** Inserting an mT5-based punctuation restoration module between ASR and NMT mitigates structural noise, yielding a 4.90 BLEU point gain over a direct ASR-to-NMT baseline for Nepali-English translation.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical source of degradation in cascaded speech-to-text translation (S2TT) for low-resource languages: the loss of punctuation and related structural information during automatic speech recognition (ASR). The authors demonstrate that this "structural noise" causes a significant 20.7% relative drop in BLEU score for Nepali-English translation. Their core solution is an optimized pipeline that inserts a multilingual T5 (mT5)-based punctuation restoration (PR) module directly after the ASR component (Wav2Vec2-XLS-R-300m) and before the neural machine translation (NMT) component (a fine-tuned MarianMT). This configuration outperforms alternative placements and a direct ASR-to-NMT connection, achieving a 4.90 BLEU improvement. Human evaluation confirms the gains, with higher ratings for adequacy and fluency.

## Method Summary
The study employs a cascaded S2TT architecture. The ASR foundation is a Wav2Vec2-XLS-R-300m model fine-tuned on Nepali speech. The NMT component uses a MarianMT model, multi-stage fine-tuned first on general Nepali-English text and then on a pseudo-parallel corpus generated from the ASR training data. The key intervention is a dedicated punctuation restoration module based on the mT5 model, fine-tuned on punctuated Nepali text. The central experimental manipulation is the placement of this PR module: it is tested on the raw ASR output (unpunctuated), on the gold reference transcript (oracle), and at other stages. The primary evaluation uses BLEU score on a test set, supplemented by a human evaluation measuring adequacy and fluency on a sample of translations.

## Key Results
*   Punctuation loss from ASR output causes a 20.7% relative BLEU degradation compared to using gold punctuated text as NMT input.
*   The optimal pipeline, with PR applied directly to raw ASR output, achieves a 4.90 BLEU point gain (36.38 vs. 31.48) over the baseline cascaded pipeline without PR (ASR -> NMT).
*   Human evaluation validates the automatic metric improvement, showing higher adequacy (3.673) and fluency (3.804) scores for the optimized pipeline, with strong inter-annotator agreement (Krippendorff’s α ≥ 0.723).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed method works by explicitly addressing a known weakness in cascaded S2TT: ASR systems for low-resource languages are typically not trained to predict punctuation, generating long, unsegmented text strings. This "structural noise" severely disrupts the NMT model, which is trained on properly punctuated, syntactically segmented sentences. The mT5-based PR module acts as a syntactic and discourse-level repair mechanism. It reintroduces crucial boundary markers (periods, commas) that segment the text into proper sentence units and signal clause boundaries, thereby restoring the structural format the NMT model expects. This reduces the distribution shift between the ASR output and the NMT training data, leading to more accurate translation. The finding that PR on raw ASR output is optimal suggests the PR model is robust enough to correct ASR's structural errors without being confused by the underlying word errors, which the NMT must handle separately.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Cascaded S2TT:** Decomposes translation into sequential ASR and NMT steps. *Why needed:* Allows leveraging mature monolingual speech and text models for low-resource pairs where direct speech-translation data is scarce. *Quick check:* Does the system latency and error propagation make it unsuitable for real-time applications?
*   **Punctuation Restoration (PR):** A text-normalization task that predicts punctuation from raw, unpunctuated text. *Why needed:* ASR hypotheses lack punctuation, creating a mismatch with NMT training data and harming translation quality. *Quick check:* Is PR a solved problem for high-resource languages, and does its performance drop significantly for low-resource ones like Nepali?
*   **Low-Resource NMT:** Machine translation for language pairs with limited parallel text. *Why needed:* Direct end-to-end S2TT models require large amounts of speech-translation data, which is unavailable for most language pairs. *Quick check:* What are the primary techniques (e.g., transfer learning, pseudo-parallel data) to adapt high-resource NMT models to low-resource settings?
*   **mT5 (Multilingual T5):** A text-to-text transformer pre-trained on 101 languages. *Why needed:* Provides a strong, transferable foundation for the PR task without requiring massive amounts of labeled Nepali punctuation data. *Quick check:* How does the performance of a language-specific PR model compare to a multilingual one like mT5 on a truly low-resource language?
*   **Wav2Vec2-XLS-R:** A self-supervised speech model pre-trained on vast unlabeled speech audio across many languages. *Why needed:* Delivers robust acoustic representations for low-resource ASR with minimal labeled speech data. *Quick check:* What is the difference between XLS-R and its predecessors (e.g., XLS-53), and why is the 300m parameter variant chosen?
*   **MarianMT Fine-Tuning:** A sequence-to-sequence NMT model. *Why needed:* Marian is an efficient, open framework for NMT. Multi-stage fine-tuning (general then in-domain) adapts it to the noisy, ASR-induced text distribution. *Quick check:* What risks does multi-stage fine-tuning pose (e.g., catastrophic forgetting) and how are they mitigated?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Raw Audio -> Wav2Vec2-XLS-R-300m (ASR) -> mT5 (Punctuation Restoration) -> Fine-tuned MarianMT (NMT) -> English Text

**Critical Path:** The audio signal's journey is linear. The most computationally intensive steps are the ASR acoustic model and the NMT decoder. The PR module is a lightweight text-based transformer that operates on the ASR hypothesis.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Cascaded vs. End-to-End:** The cascaded design allows independent optimization and use of state-of-the-art ASR/NMT models but introduces latency and error propagation. The paper does not compare against an end-to-end S2TT baseline.
*   **PR Placement:** Testing PR on raw ASR output vs. gold text vs. other configurations. The optimal choice (on raw ASR) balances correcting structural errors while not interfering with the NMT's job of handling lexical errors.
*   **Model Choice:** Using a large multilingual model (mT5) for PR on a single low-resource language trades potential over-parameterization for strong transfer learning benefits from other languages.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **ASR Failure:** High word error rate (WER) or completely garbled output will provide poor input to both PR and NMT, leading to meaningless translations.
*   **PR Failure:** Over-punctuation (adding spurious breaks) or under-punctuation (missing critical boundaries) will create syntactically invalid inputs for NMT, reducing fluency and adequacy.
*   **NMT Failure:** Domain mismatch if the NMT model (fine-tuned on pseudo-parallel ASR data) has not seen enough natural, punctuated Nepali text. This could result in "translationese" or awkward output even with correct punctuation.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Ablation on PR Placement:** Systematically compare BLEU scores for pipelines where the PR module is placed: a) on raw ASR output, b) on gold transcript (oracle), c) after NMT (post-editing punctuation in English), d) omitted entirely. The paper's main result is the superiority of (a).
2.  **PR Model Size & Capacity:** Evaluate different mT5 variants (e.g., small, base, large) or a smaller Nepali-specific model to determine if the large multilingual model's capacity is necessary or if a more efficient model suffices for this task.
3.  **Direct End-to-End Baseline:** Implement or obtain a state-of-the-art end-to-end S2TT model (e.g., a fine-tuned Whisper or SpeechLLM) and evaluate it on the same Nepali-English test set. Compare its BLEU and human evaluation scores directly to the optimized cascaded pipeline to assess the practical value of the cascaded+PR approach.

## Limitations
*   Evaluation is confined to a single language pair (Nepali-English) and a fixed set of models, limiting generalizability to other low-resource scenarios or architectural advances.
*   Human evaluation details are sparse; the number of annotators, the number of segments evaluated per system, and the blinding procedure are not described, making it hard to assess the robustness of the human results.
*   The study lacks a comparison to modern end-to-end S2TT models (e.g., Whisper), so the trade-off between the proposed cascaded approach's performance and its inherent latency/error-propagation risks is not quantified.
