# Transport Clustering: Solving Low-Rank Optimal Transport via Clustering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03578
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03578
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Transport Clustering (TC) reduces low-rank optimal transport to generalized K-means via a Monge registration step, achieving polynomial-time constant-factor approximations (1+γ for negative-type metrics and 1+γ+√(2γ) for kernels) and outperforming state-of-the-art LR-OT solvers on synthetic, CIFAR-10, and large single-cell benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Low-rank optimal transport (LR-OT) enhances robustness and reveals latent structure but solving it exactly is NP-hard and existing methods often lack solid theoretical guarantees. This paper introduces Transport Clustering (TC), which reduces LR-OT to a generalized K-means problem via a transport registration step: compute the full-rank Monge permutation, then warp the cost matrix accordingly. This reduction enables polynomial‑time algorithms with constant‑factor approximation guarantees—specifically (1+γ) for negative-type metrics and (1+γ+√(2γ)) for kernel costs, where γ is the ratio of full-rank to low-rank optimal costs. Empirically, TC demonstrates consistent advantages over state-of-the-art LR-OT methods: it reduces transport cost by up to 23% on synthetic data, achieves superior co‑clustering scores on CIFAR-10, and scales to single‑cell datasets with 131k cells where competitors fail, while delivering better alignment metrics.

## Method Summary
Transport Clustering follows a simple two‑stage pipeline. First, it solves a full‑rank optimal transport problem (with uniform marginals) to obtain the Monge permutation \(P_{\sigma^\star}\). This can be done exactly via the Hungarian algorithm or approximately using entropic Sinkhorn iterations; for very large datasets, hierarchical refinements are employed. Second, the original cost matrix \(C\) is registered as \(\tilde{C} = C P_{\sigma^\star}^\top\). The registered matrix is then fed to a generalized K‑means solver, which minimizes \(\langle \tilde{C}, Q \text{diag}(1/Q^\top\mathbf{1}_n) Q^\top \rangle_F\) over binary cluster indicators \(Q\). The resulting \(Q\) together with \(R = P_{\sigma^\star}^\top Q\) constitute the low‑rank transport factors. The entire procedure is non‑amortized and leverages well‑established subroutines.

Theoretically, the reduction is justified by showing that the cost of the generalized K‑means solution is at most a constant factor (depending on γ and the cost structure) above the optimal low‑rank transport cost. For costs of negative type the factor is \(1+\gamma\); for kernel costs it is \(1+\gamma+\sqrt{2\gamma}\). This analysis hinges on bounding the Monge map cost relative to the low‑rank optimum and exploits the reassignment form of the low‑rank OT problem.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical guarantee:** TC yields a \((1+\gamma)\)-approximation for negative‑type metrics and a \((1+\gamma+\sqrt{2\gamma})\)-approximation for kernel costs, where \(\gamma = \text{OPT}_{\text{full}}/\text{OPT}_{\text{rank-}K} \in [0,1]\).
- **Synthetic benchmarks:** TC reduces transport cost by up to 23% compared to LOT, FRLC, and LatentOT across 2M‑8G, shifted Gaussians, and stochastic block model datasets.
- **Real‑world alignment:** On CIFAR-10 co‑clustering, TC achieves lower OT cost and higher AMI/ARI/class‑transfer accuracy. On single‑cell data (up to 131k cells), TC scales where competitors fail and provides superior alignment metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Transport Registration via Monge Map
- **Claim:** The full‑rank optimal transport plan (the Monge permutation \(P_{\sigma^\star}\)) provides a stable registration of the cost matrix \(C\) that aligns the source and target datasets, enabling a reduction to clustering.
- **Mechanism:** The algorithm computes \(P_{\sigma^\star} = \arg\min_{P\in\Pi(\mathbf{u}_n,\mathbf{u}_n)} \langle C, P \rangle_F\) (the exact Monge map for uniform marginals). It then registers the cost matrix as \(\tilde{C} = C P_{\sigma^\star}^\top\), warping the original costs so that a standard generalized K‑means clustering on \(\tilde{C}\) yields a low‑rank factor \(Q\).
- **Core assumption:** The Monge map exists and is efficiently computable (e.g., via Hungarian or Sinkhorn), and its cost ratio \(\gamma = \text{OPT}_{\text{full-rank}} / \text{OPT}_{\text{rank-}K}\) is typically much less than 1 for intrinsically low‑rank structure.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states TC "first compute the full‑rank optimal transport plan (Monge permutation), then cluster the registered cost matrix." Section 3 formalizes the "Monge registration" \(\tilde{C} = C P_{\sigma^\star}^\top\) and shows the reduction. Prior works (FRLC, LOTFormer) also use transport couplings, supporting the viability of registration.
- **Break condition:** When the Monge map is unstable—small perturbations in cost or data change \(P_{\sigma^\star}\) dramatically—the registered cost \(\tilde{C}\) becomes meaningless for clustering. Proposition 4.2 constructs examples where \(\gamma \to 1\) and the Monge map yields a poor approximation.

### Mechanism 2: Reduction to Generalized K‑means
- **Claim:** Low‑rank optimal transport with hard assignments is equivalent to a generalized K‑means problem applied to the registered cost matrix \(\tilde{C}\).
- **Mechanism:** Using the reassignment form, fixing \(P_{\sigma^\star}\) converts the co‑clustering objective \(\min_{Q,R} \langle C, Q \text{diag}(g^{-1}) R^\top \rangle_F\) with \(R = P_{\sigma^\star}^\top Q\) into \(\min_Q \langle \tilde{C}, Q \text{diag}(1/Q^\top\mathbf{1}_n) Q^\top \rangle_F\). This is exactly generalized K‑means (Definition 2.1) on the rows of \(\tilde{C}\).
- **Core assumption:** The hard assignment constraints \(Q,R \in \Pi_\bullet(\mathbf{u}_n, \mathbf{u}_n)\) are sufficient to capture low‑rank structure, as full‑rank OT solutions are permutations with flat spectra, while hard low‑rank factors reflect latent, interpretable clusterings.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2 derives generalized K‑means from the mean‑free form of K‑means, showing equivalence to low‑rank OT when \(Q=R\). Section 3 explicitly states: "Transport clustering reduces low-rank OT (co‑clustering) to clustering a single set of Monge registered correspondences using generalized K‑means."
- **Break condition:** The reduction relies on the existence of a Monge permutation that aligns datasets globally. For metrics not of negative type or with high asymmetry, the approximation guarantee weakens and the equivalence to K‑means may break down (Section A.5).

### Mechanism 3: Constant‑factor Approximation Guarantees
- **Claim:** Solving generalized K‑means on \(\tilde{C}\) yields a transport plan with cost at most \((1+\gamma)\) (negative‑type metrics) or \((1+\gamma+\sqrt{2\gamma})\) (kernel costs) times the optimal low‑rank OT cost.
- **Mechanism:** The proof (Appendix A.1) bounds the partition cost \(J(X, \sigma(X))\) in terms of the intra‑cluster costs of the optimal low‑rank solution \(\mathcal{X}^\star, \mathcal{Y}^\star\) and the Monge map cost \(M_\sigma\). For negative‑type costs, Lemma A.3 gives \(M_\sigma + J(\mathcal{X}^\star, \mathcal{Y}^\star) \leq (1+\gamma)\text{OPT}_K\). For kernel costs, Lemma A.6 with optimized \(t\) yields the \((1+\gamma+\sqrt{2\gamma})\) factor.
- **Core assumption:** The cost matrix \(C\) is induced by a metric of negative type, a kernel, or a metric satisfying the triangle inequality. The ratio \(\gamma = \text{OPT}_n / \text{OPT}_K \in [0,1]\) is typically \(\ll 1\) for low \(K\).
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states the approximation factors. Section 4 (Theorem 4.1) gives precise bounds and defines \(\gamma\). Proposition 4.2 shows the bounds are tight in worst‑case constructive examples.
- **Break condition:** The guarantee does not hold if \(\gamma \approx 1\) (low‑rank provides no benefit) or if the cost is neither metric nor kernel (e.g., arbitrary asymmetric \(C\)). The paper does not address non‑metric costs.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Full‑rank OT solver (Hungarian or Sinkhorn) → Cost registration (\(\tilde{C} = C P_{\sigma^\star}^\top\)) → Generalized K‑means solver (mirror descent or SDP) → Post‑processing (reconstruct factors).

### Critical path
The pipeline is sequential and non‑amortized: full‑rank OT → registration → clustering. Runtime is dominated by OT (Step 1) and clustering (Step 2). For \(n \sim 10^5\), OT is the bottleneck; scalable approximate OT (e.g., Hierarchical Refinement) and low‑rank GKMS are required.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Permutation vs. Entropic coupling:** Hungarian gives exact \(P_{\sigma^\star}\) but costs \(O(n^3)\); Sinkhorn is faster but yields a soft matrix. Ablation shows lower entropy \(\epsilon\) yields better TC cost.
- **K‑means solver quality:** Mirror descent GKMS is fast but local; SDP relaxation is global but heavier. For \(n > 10^4\), GKMS with K‑means++ initialization is practical.
- **Hard vs. soft OT:** The paper focuses on hard assignments. Soft low‑rank OT (using Kantorovich registration) is more flexible for unbalanced marginals but lacks guarantees.

### Failure signatures
1. **Unstable Monge map** (Proposition 4.2): When \(\gamma \to 1\) or the Monge map is non‑unique, registration produces a misleading cost matrix and TC cost degrades.
2. **Generalized K‑means stuck in bad local optimum:** Since GKMS is local, poor initialization leads to high final cost. Mitigate with transport‑registered initialization or multiple restarts.
3. **Asymmetric dataset sizes (\(n \neq m\)):** Monge registration requires a square cost matrix; use Kantorovich registration for unbalanced cases, but guarantees may weaken.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Synthetic benchmark (Section 5.1):** Compare TC to LOT/FRLC/LatentOT on 2M‑8G, shifted Gaussians, and stochastic block models. Measure relative OT cost across ranks \(K\). Expected: TC achieves up to 23% lower cost.
2. **Co‑clustering on CIFAR‑10 (Section 5.2):** Run TC with \(K=10\) on two stratified splits of 60k images. Report OT cost, AMI/ARI vs. ground‑truth labels, and class‑transfer accuracy. Expected: TC yields lowest OT cost and highest AMI/ARI/CTA.

## Limitations
- **Monge map instability:** When the full‑rank transport cost ratio \(\gamma \approx 1\) or the Monge map is non‑unique, the registration step yields a misleading cost matrix, degrading clustering quality.
- **Cost assumptions:** Theoretical guarantees hold only for negative‑type metrics or kernels; for arbitrary asymmetric or non‑metric costs the approximation bounds weaken or may not apply.
- **Computational bottlenecks:** The first stage requires solving a full‑rank OT problem, which in the worst case is \(O(n^3)\) and memory‑intensive, limiting scalability beyond approximately 131k points.

## Confidence
