# Uni-RS: A Spatially Faithful Unified Understanding and Generation Model for Remote Sensing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17673
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17673
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** Uni-RS increases Spatially Faithful Rate (SFR) from 47.61% to 80.15% and lowers FID on RSIEval, while maintaining strong performance on understanding tasks.

## Executive Summary
Unified remote sensing vision-language models suffer from a "Spatial Reversal Curse": they accurately understand spatial relations in images but fail to generate scenes that faithfully reflect spatial descriptions in text. Uni-RS resolves this asymmetry through three innovations: (1) Spatial-Layout Planning, which converts user instructions into explicit, structured layout plans; (2) Spatial-Aware Query Supervision, which biases a subset of learnable queries toward encoding spatial relations; and (3) Image-Caption Spatial Layout Variation, which trains on geometry-consistent rotated image-caption pairs. On RSIEval, Uni-RS raises SFR by over 32 absolute points and improves FID, demonstrating a spatially faithful unified model without sacrificing understanding capabilities.

## Method Summary
Uni-RS adopts a two-stage training pipeline within a frozen MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) to diffusion (SANA-1.6B) architecture connected by 256 learnable queries. Stage 1 trains the connector and diffusion on a large general RS dataset (Git10M). Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes on understanding datasets (RSICap, VRSBench) and a new RS-Spatial dataset providing structured layout plans, spatial relation labels, and rotation-augmented pairs. The three core components address spatial information loss: (i) Spatial-Layout Planning rewrites instructions into `<plan>` tags (SCENE, OBJ, REL) to make spatial constraints explicit; (ii) Spatial-Aware Query Supervision adds an auxiliary loss on the last 32 queries to predict 79 predefined spatial relations; (iii) Image-Caption Spatial Layout Variation creates training pairs from 90°/180°/270° rotations with LLM-rewritten captions that update directional terms.

## Key Results
- Spatially Faithful Rate (SFR) on RSIEval improved from 47.61% (baseline) to 80.15% (Uni-RS).
- FID on RSIEval reduced from 13.47 to 13.04; on VRSBench from 4.91 to 4.51.
- Performance on understanding tasks (captioning, VQA, grounding) remains comparable to existing unified models.
- Ablation studies confirm all three components contribute additively to mitigating the spatial asymmetry.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Spatial-Layout Planning
- **Claim:** Mitigates overshadowing of spatial constraints by object-centric descriptions by converting textual instructions into an explicit, structured spatial plan.
- **Mechanism:** The MLLM rewrites the user instruction into a `<plan>` tag containing global scene context (SCENE), object instances with coarse locations (OBJ), and explicitly stated spatial relations (REL). This restructures the instruction to make spatial layout information salient and separable from object semantics, helping the conditioning queries pass spatial constraints more faithfully to the diffusion generator.
- **Break condition:** If spatial relations in the original caption are ambiguous or not parsed into the structured plan, the layout specification becomes incorrect or incomplete, propagating errors.

### Mechanism 2: Spatial-Aware Query Supervision
- **Claim:** Biases a subset of learnable queries toward encoding spatial relations explicitly mentioned in the instruction, reducing information loss in the MLLM-to-diffusion pathway.
- **Mechanism:** A predefined set of `Q_spa` (e.g., last 32 queries) is supervised via an auxiliary head to predict a multi-hot vector of spatial relation labels (from a closed vocabulary of 79 relations) extracted from the instruction. This auxiliary loss (BCE) encourages these queries to retain spatial relation features during training.
- **Break condition:** If the spatial relation vocabulary is incomplete or the instruction uses compositional/non-standard phrasing not captured by the 79 labels, the supervision signal becomes noisy or misaligned.

### Mechanism 3: Image-Caption Spatial Layout Variation
- **Claim:** Improves model robustness to spatial layout changes by training on geometry-consistent transformations, helping associate spatial descriptions with relative layouts rather than absolute positions.
- **Mechanism:** For each training image, rotated variants (90°, 180°, 270°) are generated. An LLM rewrites the caption to update orientation/direction-dependent spatial expressions (e.g., "North of" → "East of" for 90° rotation) while preserving invariant relations and object semantics. The model learns that the same scene with different absolute layouts shares the same underlying spatial semantics.
- **Break condition:** If LLM-based caption rewriting introduces inconsistencies (e.g., failing to update all directional terms), the training pairs become noisy, teaching incorrect spatial associations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Unified MLLM-Diffusion Architecture via Learnable Queries**  
  Why needed: Uni-RS builds on a paradigm where a frozen MLLM's output is converted into diffusion conditioning via a fixed set of learnable queries. Understanding this bottleneck explains why spatial information degrades.  
  Quick check: If the MLLM's final hidden states contain rich spatial information, why might that information be lost when projected into 256 fixed queries?

- **Concept: Spatial Relation Taxonomy for Remote Sensing**  
  Why needed: Spatial-Aware Query Supervision relies on a closed vocabulary (79 labels) of spatial relations (directional, topological, etc.). This taxonomy defines what "spatial" means for supervision.  
  Quick check: Why might "surrounded by" be considered "distributional" in RS, and how would its representation differ from "to the north of"?

- **Concept: Geometric Invariance in Overhead Imagery**  
  Why needed: The Image-Caption Spatial Layout Variation technique assumes that rotating an RS image by multiples of 90° preserves scene semantics and many spatial relations, justifying the augmentation.  
  Quick check: For a 90° clockwise rotation, why is "east of" preserved (just relabeled) while "north-facing" becomes "east-facing"? What does this say about rotation-invariant vs. dependent spatial aspects?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Text Instruction → MLLM + Spatial-Layout Planning → Structured Plan → MLLM hidden states + Plan → Transformer Connector → Learnable Queries (with `Q_spa` biased) → Diffusion Model conditioning → Generated Image

Critical path: The spatial plan is attended to by the queries, and `Q_spa` carries an explicit spatial signal via auxiliary supervision.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Query Count vs. Spatial Detail:** Fixed 256 queries must encode all semantics; allocating 32 to `Q_spa` limits capacity for other signals.
- **Closed Vocabulary vs. Expressivity:** The 79-label vocabulary ensures tractable supervision but may miss nuanced RS captions.
- **Rotation Augmentation Scope:** Only 90° increments are used because RS overhead views have 4-fold rotational symmetry; arbitrary angles would break category and relation invariances.

Failure signatures:
- **Spatial Reversal Curse persists:** SFR remains low (~47-50%) while SUA is high (>80%), indicating queries are not carrying spatial layout.
- **Ablation of SLP drops SFR:** Removing spatial planning causes a significant SFR drop.
- **Ablation of SQS has marginal FID/CLIP impact:** SQS primarily boosts SFR but not image quality metrics.
- **Ablation of ISV reduces final SFR:** Removing rotation variation lowers final SFR from 80.15% to 63.40%.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Reproduce the Spatial Reversal Curse:** Train the base unified architecture on a remote sensing dataset. Measure SUA (spatial understanding accuracy) and SFR (spatially faithful rate). Expect a large gap (Δ > 25%).
2. **Component-wise Ablation:** Starting from the base model, incrementally add (a) Spatial-Layout Planning, (b) Spatial-Aware Query Supervision, (c) Image-Caption Variation. Evaluate SFR and FID after each addition.
3. **Spatial Relation Vocabulary Analysis:** Sample 100 RS captions, extract all spatial expressions, and compute coverage by the 79-label vocabulary. If coverage <90%, identify frequent missing relations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does Uni-RS generalize to non-optical remote sensing modalities (e.g., SAR, hyperspectral)?
- How does Spatial-Layout Planning perform on scenes with many objects (>20) and nested spatial relations?
- Is the Spatial Reversal Curse an inherent property of the MLLM-diffusion architecture, or could alternative unified architectures close the spatial gap without extra supervision?
- Does the LLM-generated caption rewriting for rotation-based augmentation introduce systematic caption errors that degrade performance?

## Limitations
- **Dataset Access:** The RS-Spatial dataset (20k+ plans, 37k rotated pairs) is not public, blocking exact replication of the spatial supervision and augmentation pipeline.
- **Implementation Gaps:** Exact 79-label vocabulary, prompt templates for planning/caption rewriting, and loss weighting are unspecified.
- **Generalization Scope:** Evaluations are limited to two RS benchmarks; applicability to other domains (e.g., natural images) where rotation invariance is weaker is unclear.

## Confidence
- **High:** The spatial reversal curse is real and Uni-RS improves SFR (47.61% → 80.15%). Ablations support additive contributions.
- **Medium:** Proposed mechanisms are theoretically sound and ablations are positive, but causality is inferred from the paper’s own experiments without external validation.
- **Low:** Claims about broader applicability beyond RS or robustness to arbitrary geometric transforms lack evidence.

## Next Checks
1. **Rotation Augmentation Integrity Check:** Sample 100 rotated RS image-caption pairs. Use an LLM judge to verify that all direction/orientation terms are correctly updated according to the rotation. Fail if >5% of pairs have inconsistent spatial descriptions.
2. **Spatial Query Probing:** After training, extract activations from the designated `Q_spa` on a held-out set of RS captions with known spatial relations. Train a linear classifier to predict the 79 relation labels. Target probe accuracy >70%.
3. **Baseline Replication of Spatial Reversal Curse:** Re-train the base unified architecture (without Uni-RS modules) on Git10M+RSICap. Measure SUA and SFR on the same evaluation sets. Confirm a significant gap (Δ > 25%).
