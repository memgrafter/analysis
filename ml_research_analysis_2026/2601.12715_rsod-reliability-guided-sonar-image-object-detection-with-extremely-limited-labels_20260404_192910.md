# RSOD: Reliability-Guided Sonar Image Object Detection with Extremely Limited Labels

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.12715
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12715
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** With only 5% labeled data, RSOD achieves 34.7% mAP on UATD and 67.4% mAP on FSOD, competing with fully supervised baselines and significantly improving small object detection.

## Executive Summary
RSOD addresses the challenge of object detection in sonar images under extreme label scarcity. It introduces a teacher‑student semi‑supervised framework that leverages unlabeled data through pseudo‑labeling enhanced by reliability assessment. The key innovations include a Pseudo‑Label Reliability Assessment (PLRA) that scores pseudo‑labels based on multi‑view consistency across mirror and scale augmentations, and an Object Mixed Pseudo‑Label (OMPL) strategy that mixes high‑reliability boxes to enrich training for small and multiple objects. Additionally, a reliability‑guided adaptive constraint weights the unsupervised loss by reliability and adds a corner‑point regression for precise localization. On the UATD dataset, RSOD with 5% labeled data obtains 34.7% mAP, matching a fully supervised model trained on 100% labels. On the FSOD dataset, it achieves 67.4% mAP with 5% labels, outperforming prior semi‑supervised methods. The approach also notably improves small object detection, achieving 12.6% mAP(small) on UATD under the same low‑label regime.

## Method Summary
RSOD employs a teacher‑student detection architecture (based on Faster R‑CNN). The teacher, initialized from the student and updated by exponential moving average, processes weakly augmented unlabeled images to generate initial pseudo‑labels. The Pseudo‑Label Reliability Assessment (PLRA) computes a reliability score for each pseudo‑label by comparing the teacher’s predictions on the original image with two augmented views (mirrored and scaled). High‑reliability pseudo‑labels (score > γ) are then used in two ways: (1) the Object Mixed Pseudo‑Label (OMPL) strategy selects these boxes, applies random rotation and scaling, and pastes them onto other images in the batch if they do not overlap (IoU < δ) with existing boxes, thereby increasing the occurrence of small and multiple objects; (2) the student is trained on unlabeled images with a reliability‑weighted unsupervised loss (PLRW) that multiplies each matched pseudo‑label’s classification/regression loss by its reliability, and a corner‑point regression loss (CPRL) for boxes with high reliability. For labeled images, the student computes standard supervised loss. The total loss combines these: L = L_sup + λ_u · (L_PLRW + L_CPRL). Alternating between labeled and unlabeled batches, the model is trained for 180K iterations. This design effectively filters noisy pseudo‑labels, focuses learning on trustworthy predictions, and mitigates the scarcity of small/multi‑object examples.

## Key Results
- **UATD (5% labels):** 34.7% mAP, competing with fully supervised baseline (100% labels).
- **FSOD (5% labels):** 67.4% mAP, significantly outperforming state‑of‑the‑art semi‑supervised detection methods.
- **Small object improvement:** 12.6% mAP(small) on UATD under 5% label setting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Pseudo‑Label Reliability Assessment (PLRA)
**Claim:** Calculating a reliability score via multi‑view prediction consistency improves pseudo‑label quality under sonar image noise and label scarcity.  
**Mechanism:** For an unlabeled image, the teacher predicts on the original view and two augmented views (mirrored, scaled). Each predicted box in the original view is matched to its highest‑IoU box in each augmented view. The reliability score `Rj` combines the IoU match, class confidence consistency, and category match (β) across views, scaled by a factor `H`.  
**Core assumption:** Pseudo‑labels that are geometrically and categorically consistent across benign augmentations (mirroring, scaling) are more likely to be correct, while inconsistent ones are noise.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "...calculates a reliability score by assessing the consistency of the teacher’s predictions across different views."  
- [PAGE 3‑4] Detailed equations (3,4,5) and description: "we propose a pseudo‑label reliability assessment scheme based on multi‑view consistency… comprehensively assess the reliability… by jointly considering both the positional consistency and the category consistency."  
- [corpus] Corpus signals related to noisy pseudo‑labels in special domains (e.g., "Learning Camouflaged Object Detection from Noisy Pseudo Label") provide contextual support for the general problem but not direct evidence for RSOD's specific multi‑view scoring mechanism. Evidence is weak/indirect.  
**Break condition:** If augmentations (e.g., scaling) distort sonar‑specific features (speckle noise, shadow geometry) such that consistent boxes correspond to common artifacts rather than true objects, the reliability score may not correlate with correctness.

### Mechanism 2: Object Mixed Pseudo‑Labels (OMPL)
**Claim:** Augmenting and mixing high‑reliability pseudo‑label boxes across samples enriches training data for small and multiple objects, which are otherwise under‑represented in scarce sonar labels.  
**Mechanism:** Pseudo‑labels with `Rj > γ` are selected. These boxes are randomly rotated/scaled and pasted onto the first image of a batch only if they do not overlap (`IoU < δ`) with existing boxes in that image. This synthetically increases the density of small/multiple objects in the training batch.  
**Core assumption:** Sonar images often have few small/multi‑object instances in limited labeled sets. Mixing verified object regions (high‑reliability boxes) into new contexts provides valid training examples for these challenging cases without introducing excessive background noise.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "...introduce an object mixed pseudo‑label method to tackle the shortage of labeled data… enhance the model’s ability to recognize challenging samples (such as multiple small objects)."  
- [PAGE 4‑5] "This strategy involves rotating and scaling the pseudo‑label bounding boxes… mixing them with pseudo‑labels from other samples… These pseudo‑label bounding boxes can cover a substantial amount of irrelevant noise… maximizing the utilization of valuable high reliability object pseudo‑label bounding boxes."  
- [corpus] Corpus mentions small object detection in sonar (e.g., "Detection of weak and small targets in forward‑looking sonar image") supports the problem but not the specific mixing strategy. Evidence is contextual.  
**Break condition:** If pasted high‑reliability boxes are placed in sonar image regions with dramatically different acoustic textures or shadow patterns, they may become contextually inconsistent, serving as misleading training examples.

### Mechanism 3: Reliability‑Guided Adaptive Constraint
**Claim:** Weighting the unsupervised loss by reliability scores and adding a corner‑point regression loss for high‑score boxes focuses student learning on high‑quality pseudo‑labels and refines localization.  
**Mechanism:** The unsupervised loss `L_unsup` comprises two parts: 1) PLRW: each student prediction is matched to a teacher box, and the classification/regression loss is multiplied by that teacher box’s reliability `Rj`. 2) CPRL: For teacher boxes with `Rj > γ`, an L2 loss is applied between teacher and student corner coordinates, normalized by the diagonal length.  
**Core assumption:** Higher reliability scores indicate pseudo‑labels where the teacher’s geometric and categorical prediction is stable. These should be trusted more heavily (higher weight in loss) and provide sharper localization signals (corner points).  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "...optimize the performance of the student by implementing a reliability‑guided adaptive constraint."  
- [PAGE 5‑6] Equations (8,9,10) and descriptions: "We use reliability scores to differentiate the extent to which different pseudo‑labels impact the loss function… enhance the precision of the student’s bounding box localization by introducing a corner point regression loss."  
- [corpus] Related work on loss weighting in semi‑supervised detection (e.g., "Soft Teacher") is cited, but RSOD’s specific combination of reliability‑weighting and corner regression is novel. Corpus evidence is generic.  
**Break condition:** If the reliability score `Rj` is miscalibrated (e.g., high for geometrically consistent but semantically wrong boxes due to sonar texture ambiguities), the student will be reinforced on wrong predictions, harming performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
1. **Input:** Labeled batch `D_l`, Unlabeled batch `D_u`.
2. **Teacher Network:** Receives weakly augmented unlabeled images. Outputs initial pseudo‑labels (boxes, scores, classes).
3. **PLRA Module:** Takes teacher’s predictions on original and augmented (mirror, scale) views. Computes reliability score `Rj` per box.
4. **OMPL Module:** Filters boxes by `Rj > γ`. Selects boxes from other batch images, augments them (rotate, scale), and pastes onto the first image of the batch if `IoU < δ`.
5. **Student Network:** Receives (a) labeled images (standard supervised loss `L_sup`), (b) weakly augmented unlabeled images with teacher pseudo‑labels (weighted by `Rj` via PLRW), and (c) for high‑`Rj` boxes, applies CPRL using corner coordinates.
6. **Loss Aggregation:** `L_total = L_sup + λ_u · (L_PLRW + L_CPRL)`.
7. **Teacher Update:** Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of student weights.

### Critical Path
The training loop for unlabeled data proceeds as: Teacher weak prediction → PLRA scoring → high‑reliability box selection → (optional OMPL mixing) → student strong prediction → loss computation (PLRW + CPRL for high‑reliability boxes) → student update → teacher EMA update.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Reliability threshold γ:** Higher γ yields cleaner pseudo‑labels but fewer (risk of under‑training). Lower γ uses more data but risks more noise. Tuned empirically (paper uses 0.5).
- **Augmentations for PLRA:** Mirroring/scaling are simple and expected to preserve semantics, but aggressive scaling could make small objects disappear or create artifacts, breaking the consistency assumption.
- **OMPL IoU threshold δ:** Lower δ (e.g., 0.2) allows denser mixing but risks overlapping objects; higher δ ensures separation but may not sufficiently increase object density.

### Failure Signatures
- **High noise sensitivity:** If teacher’s initial pseudo‑labels on 5% labeled data are very poor, PLRA’s multi‑view consistency may not distinguish bad from good boxes, propagating noise.
- **Small object stagnation:** If OMPL mixing is disabled or γ is too high, the model may fail to learn small object features, showing low `mAP(small)`.
- **Training instability:** If `λ_u` is too high or reliability scores are poorly calibrated, the student may diverge from the teacher, breaking the consistency cycle.
- **Corner point misalignment:** If CPRL corner coordinates are extracted incorrectly (e.g., axis‑aligned vs. rotated), the localization constraint may not align with the detector’s output, harming convergence.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline SSL replication:** Implement a standard teacher‑student SSOD (e.g., Soft Teacher) on sonar data with 5% labels. Establish the performance ceiling of generic SSOD without RSOD’s modifications. Expected: moderate improvement over fully‑supervised 5% baseline, but gap to 100% supervised remains large.
2. **Ablation - Add PLRA & PLRW:** Introduce the reliability scoring and loss weighting (without OMPL and CPRL). Measure change in mAP and pseudo‑label precision/recall. Expected: significant noise reduction, improved student stability, mAP increase.
3. **Ablation - Add OMPL:** Enable object mixing on top of (2). Analyze performance on small objects (`mAP(small)`) and on images with multiple objects. Expected: notable boost in small/multi‑object metrics, demonstrating OMPL’s targeted effect.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How well does RSOD generalize to sonar images from diverse underwater environments, sensors, and noise conditions not represented in UATD and FSOD?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors only evaluate on two datasets (UATD and FSOD) collected with specific sonars (Oculus M750d) in controlled settings (Bohai Bay). The introduction emphasizes sonar image variability due to illumination, turbidity, and noise, but RSOD’s robustness to such variations is untested.
- **Why unresolved:** Sonar image characteristics can vary widely with water clarity, depth, and sonar type; performance on these unseen conditions could differ, limiting real‑world applicability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Testing RSOD on additional sonar datasets from varied environments (e.g., deep sea, turbid waters) and sensors, with quantitative mAP comparisons to assess degradation or stability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the multi‑view consistency‑based reliability scoring in PLRA remain effective under extremely high noise or texture‑sparse scenarios where augmented views may not align well?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] PLRA relies on mirrored and scaled augmentations to compute consistency, assuming these transformations preserve object structure. However, sonar images are noted for sparse textures and noise, which might cause consistency measures to fail for severely degraded images.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not evaluate RSOD under controlled noise injection or varying texture levels; thus, the boundary conditions for PLRA’s efficacy are unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies with synthetically added noise or blur on UATD/FSOD, measuring mAP changes as noise increases, and correlating with reliability score distributions to identify failure points.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can RSOD effectively handle long‑tailed class distributions in sonar datasets, particularly for rare object categories with very few labeled examples?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] FSOD exhibits long‑tail distribution (e.g., diver category with only 81 images), but the main results report only overall mAP without per‑class analysis. The OMPL strategy aims to boost small objects but does not explicitly address class imbalance.
- **Why unresolved:** Semi‑supervised methods may struggle with rare classes if pseudo‑labels are biased toward majority classes; this is critical for rescue scenarios like diver detection but unverified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Per‑class precision/recall metrics on FSOD and other imbalanced sonar datasets, comparing RSOD to SOTA SSOD methods in low‑label settings for tail classes, and analyzing pseudo‑label distribution across categories.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does the object mixed pseudo‑label (OMPL) strategy impact model performance for densely packed or overlapping objects, and does it introduce artifacts that harm detection?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] OMPL mixes high‑reliability pseudo‑labels via rotation and scaling to augment small/multiple objects, but the paper only shows qualitative improvements on simple scenes. The paste operation uses an IoU threshold to avoid overlaps, but complex scenes with cluttered objects are not discussed.
- **Why unresolved:** In sonar images, objects can overlap due to perspective; OMPL’s pasting might create unrealistic samples or miss true positives, but this trade‑off is unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments on synthetic or curated datasets with varying object densities, measuring mAP and false positive rates; visual analysis of mixed pseudo‑labels to check for unnatural compositions.

## Limitations
- **Missing implementation details:** Exact augmentation magnitudes for PLRA (mirror probability, scale range), OMPL rotation/scaling ranges and paste blending, and the precise formulation of corner‑point extraction for CPRL are not specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** The reliability threshold γ, scaling factor H, and EMA decay are tuned empirically but their sensitivity across different sonar domains is not thoroughly explored; optimal values may be dataset‑specific.
- **Limited evaluation scope:** Experiments are conducted on only two sonar datasets (UATD, FSOD) captured with a specific sensor (Oculus M750d) in controlled waters. Generalization to diverse underwater environments, sensors, and noise conditions remains untested.
