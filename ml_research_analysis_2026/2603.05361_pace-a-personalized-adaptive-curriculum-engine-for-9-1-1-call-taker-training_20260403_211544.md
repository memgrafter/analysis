# PACE: A Personalized Adaptive Curriculum Engine for 9-1-1 Call-taker Training

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.05361
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05361
*   **Reference Count:** 17
*   **Primary Result:** A co-pilot system achieving 19.50% faster time-to-competence and 10.95% higher terminal mastery versus state-of-the-art baselines.

## Executive Summary
PACE is an AI co-pilot designed to address the critical shortage of trainers for 9-1-1 call-takers, who must master over 1,000 interdependent skills. It creates personalized adaptive curricula by maintaining a probabilistic model of each trainee's skill mastery, modeling their individual learning and forgetting dynamics, and selecting training scenarios via a contextual bandit. The system propagates evidence across a structured skill graph to efficiently estimate mastery across thousands of skills. In simulation, PACE significantly accelerates learning and improves final mastery. In operational studies with real training logs, its recommendations align closely with expert judgments and dramatically reduce per-call turnaround time for reviewing trainee performance.

## Method Summary
PACE constructs a directed skill graph (V, E) linking 1,053 procedural knowledge nodes. It uses an LLM to extract noisy outcome observations (⊤, ⊥, ∼, ⊘) from trainee interaction transcripts. A Belief Tracker maintains a Beta posterior per skill, updating it with weighted evidence and propagating mastery estimates to similar nodes via a precomputed semantic and positional similarity index Φ. An Dynamics Estimator fits individual learning pace (λ) and forgetting rate (ψ) parameters from performance history. A contextual bandit, using Thompson Sampling with a linear reward model, selects batches of scenarios from a filtered pool of 297. The context vector (xt) aggregates coverage, belief variance, and estimated dynamics to balance exploitation of known deficits with exploration of uncertain skills. The system operates after a cold-start observation phase (N0=15 sessions).

## Key Results
*   **Learning Efficiency:** Achieves 19.50% faster time-to-competence (Z2H) and 10.95% higher terminal mastery (RE) compared to state-of-the-art adaptive and non-adaptive baselines in simulation.
*   **Operational Alignment:** In a co-pilot study with 923 historical training logs, PACE's scenario recommendations align with expert next-session assignments in 95.45% of cases.
*   **Trainer Workload Reduction:** Reduces average per-call turnaround time for debriefing from 11.58 minutes to 34 seconds (95.08% reduction) by automating transcript analysis and recommendation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Graph-Based Inductive Belief Propagation
*   **Claim:** PACE accelerates mastery estimation across 1,000+ skills by propagating evidence through a structured skill graph, reducing the need for direct observation of every node.
*   **Mechanism:** When a skill is directly observed, evidence is shared with semantically and procedurally similar skills via a precomputed similarity index Φ. The Beta posterior belief for an unobserved skill v′ is updated as the mean of its current belief and the observed mastery of a related skill v, weighted by similarity ϕ(v, v′).
*   **Core Assumption:** Skills with similar textual descriptions and similar depth in the procedural hierarchy exhibit positive transfer; mastery in one provides evidence for mastery in another.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Propagation is stated to accelerate diagnostic coverage. Ablation removing propagation ("PACE w/o propagation") shows substantially worse coverage speed (e.g., Z2H: 46.13 vs. 22.19 for Fast Learner), confirming its critical role.

### Mechanism 2: Individual Learning and Forgetting Dynamics Modeling
*   **Claim:** Personalizing the curriculum to a trainee's specific learning pace (λ) and forgetting rate (ψ) prevents skill decay and optimizes practice scheduling.
*   **Mechanism:** λ is estimated as the average marginal mastery gain per practice opportunity. Forgetting is modeled with a power-law decay: θ(τ+∆τv) = θ(τ) · (1+κ·∆τv)^−ψ, where ψ is fitted from observed retention after variable temporal gaps. These parameters are features in the bandit's context.
*   **Core Assumption:** Forgetting follows a power law over real time, and individual differences in λ and ψ are stable enough within a training program to be estimated from early performance.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The paper explicitly models individual dynamics. Ablation removing dynamics ("PACE w/o dynamics") shows significant performance drops for archetypes with non-average dynamics (e.g., Quick Forgetter RE: 80.16 vs. full PACE 91.04), validating personalization.

### Mechanism 3: Contextual Bandit for Adaptive Scenario Selection
*   **Claim:** Formulating scenario selection as a contextual bandit allows PACE to dynamically balance exploiting believed skill deficits and exploring uncertain regions, leading to efficient curriculum progression.
*   **Mechanism:** At each session, a context vector xt (including belief variance ¯σ², coverage c, estimated λ/ψ) is fed to a Thompson Sampling policy. The policy samples from a Bayesian linear regression model of expected reward (learning gain ∑ ∆θv) and selects a batch of K scenarios. This naturally balances exploitation (targeting low-mastery skills) with exploration (reducing uncertainty).
*   **Core Assumption:** The immediate learning gain is a useful, noisy proxy for long-term mastery improvement, and maximizing it session-by-session leads to faster time-to-competence.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The method uses a contextual bandit with Thompson Sampling. Behavioral analysis shows the explore:exploit ratio adapts to belief uncertainty (¯σ²), shifting toward exploration as beliefs consolidate, suggesting the mechanism operates as designed.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Bayesian Knowledge Tracing with Beta Posteriors
    *   **Why needed here:** PACE maintains probabilistic beliefs for 1,053 skills using Beta distributions, updating them with noisy observation evidence. Understanding conjugate prior updating is key to interpreting belief dynamics.
    *   **Quick check:** If a skill starts with Beta(1,1) and receives a single successful observation (⊤) with weight w+=1, what are the new α and β? (Ans: α=2, β=1).
*   **Concept:** Skill Graph Representation and Transfer
    *   **Why needed here:** The propagation mechanism depends on a directed graph (V, E) encoding procedural dependencies and implications. Without this, fine-grained interdependent skill modeling is impossible.
    *   **Quick check:** What type of edge connects a condition node ("patient unconscious") to a question node ("check for breathing")? (Ans: entailment link—the condition activates/mandates the question).
*   **Concept:** Contextual Multi-Armed Bandits (Linear Posterior)
    *   **Why needed here:** The core decision engine is a linear Thompson Sampling bandit. Engineers must understand how context features map to expected reward and how Bayesian updating of the coefficient matrix works.
    *   **Quick check:** In Thompson Sampling for a linear bandit with Gaussian prior, what is sampled and how is it used? (Ans: Sample a coefficient vector B_S from the posterior Gaussian; compute expected reward as B_Sᵀx_t; pick action with highest sample).

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Belief Tracker -> Dynamics Estimator -> Contextual Bandit -> Scenario Selection
    Pre-runtime Similarity Module -> Belief Tracker (propagation)
*   **Critical path:**
    1. Cold Start (N0=15 sessions): PACE observes only. Beliefs and dynamics are initialized.
    2. Adaptive Phase Start: Bandit policy activates.
    3. Per-session loop: (a) Get trainee performance on last batch → (b) Belief Tracker updates posteriors + propagates → (c) Dynamics Estimator updates λ, ψ → (d) Bandit constructs xt → (e) Sequentially selects K scenarios → (f) Scenarios are played → (g) Repeat.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Propagation Fidelity vs. Noise: Larger similarity threshold (ϕ) reduces noise but limits inferential reach; smaller increases coverage risk but may spread incorrect beliefs. The ϵ parameter in the exponential depth penalty controls this.
    *   Bandit Context Size vs. Data Efficiency: More context features could improve policy but require more data to learn. The current ~7-dimensional xt is a compromise.
    *   Cold Start Duration vs. Early Utility: N0=15 is based on logistics. Shorter cold start risks poor initial beliefs; longer reduces perceived usefulness.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   δ (approximation gap) does not decrease across sessions. Indicates belief tracker is not converging, possibly due to poor propagation or LLM extraction errors.
    *   Terminal coverage (C@50) plateaus far below 100% for all archetypes. Suggests insufficient graph connectivity or a stuck bandit policy.
    *   High variance in Z2H across runs for same archetype. Points to instability in bandit exploration or sensitive dynamics estimation from limited early data.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Belief Convergence Test:** Run PACE on a single Fast Learner agent. Plot δ and ¯σ² vs. session. Expect δ to decrease and ¯σ² to trend down, with point color (explore:exploit) shifting as ¯σ² drops.
    2.  **Dynamics Estimation sanity check:** After 15 cold-start sessions, compare PACE's estimated ˆλ and ˆψ against the agent's ground-truth values. Expect reasonable correlation; significant bias indicates power-law fit failure.
    3.  **Ablation - Bandit vs. Greedy:** Replace Thompson Sampling with a greedy policy (always exploits by minimizing sum(1− ˆθv) over VSt). Compare coverage progression. Expect greedy to converge faster initially but to a lower terminal coverage due to insufficient exploration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Generalization:** Can PACE be effectively generalized to other high-stakes procedural training domains beyond 9-1-1 call-taking? Domain-specific variations in skill interdependencies and dynamics may require substantial adaptations. Resolving this requires deployment and validation in a new domain (e.g., medical triage).
*   **Real-World Human Validation:** How does PACE perform with real human trainees over extended periods, considering factors like motivation, stress, and transfer to real-world calls? The paper's evaluation used simulated agents due to logistical constraints of human studies, leaving a gap in real-world validation. Resolution requires a longitudinal study with human trainees, tracking both simulation metrics and real-world outcomes.
*   **Graph Robustness:** What is PACE's robustness to inaccuracies or incompleteness in the underlying skill dependency graph? The graph's accuracy is vital for inductive tracing and scenario filtering, but the paper does not evaluate sensitivity. Resolution requires introducing controlled perturbations into the graph and measuring performance degradation.

## Limitations
*   **External Validity:** Efficacy claims rely on LLM-based simulated agents, raising questions about performance with real humans exhibiting stress, motivation, and complex behavioral patterns.
*   **Operational Metrics:** The reported 95.08% turnaround time reduction is an extraordinary magnitude that requires cautious interpretation; the 95.45% expert alignment lacks a controlled study design.
*   **Hyperparameter & Data Gaps:** Exact values for critical hyperparameters (similarity ϵ, belief update weights w⁺/w⁻, bandit priors), LLM prompts, skill graph construction rules, and scenario filtering criteria are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism Soundness (Propagation):** High. The ablation shows a drastic impact, and the graph-based reasoning is clearly defined.
*   **Mechanism Soundness (Dynamics):** Medium. The power-law model is justified, but identifiability of ψ from limited early data with irregular gaps is uncertain.
*   **Mechanism Soundness (Bandit):** Medium. Thompson Sampling behavior aligns with theory, but the linear reward model may underfit complex learning patterns.
*   **Simulation Results:** Medium. Gains are large and consistent across archetypes, but dependence on simulated agent fidelity is a major caveat.
*   **Operational Co-Pilot Study:** Low for turnaround reduction (magnitude), Medium for expert alignment (observational, no control).

## Next Checks
1.  **External Human Trial:** Conduct a randomized controlled study with actual 9-1-1 trainees, comparing PACE to a standard curriculum on certification pass rates and objective call-handling quality metrics.
2.  **Sensitivity Analysis:** Systematically vary propagation threshold ϵ, belief-update weights, and bandit context features; measure stability of coverage and Z2H across trainee archetypes to identify robust parameter regimes.
3.  **LLM Extraction Audit:** Manually annotate a stratified sample of simulation transcripts, compute agreement with PACE's LLM-based observations, and quantify how extraction errors propagate into belief error (δ) and final mastery.
