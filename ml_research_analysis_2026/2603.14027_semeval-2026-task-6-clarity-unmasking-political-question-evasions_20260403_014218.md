# SemEval-2026 Task 6: CLARITY -- Unmasking Political Question Evasions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.14027
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14027
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Top macro-F1 scores: 0.89 on clarity classification and 0.68 on evasion strategy classification, with hierarchical LLM prompting pipelines yielding the best performance.

## Executive Summary
The CLARITY shared task introduces a benchmark for detecting political question evasion in U.S. presidential interviews. It defines two subtasks: a 3-way clarity classification (Clear Reply, Ambivalent, Clear Non-Reply) and a 9-way evasion strategy classification based on an expert-grounded taxonomy. The competition attracted 124 teams. Results show a large performance gap: clarity classification is relatively well-solved (top macro-F1 0.89 vs 0.68 baseline), while the evasion subtask remains challenging (top 0.68 matching the baseline). The most effective approach used hierarchical LLM prompting that first predicted fine-grained evasion types and then mapped them to clarity labels, demonstrating the benefit of exploiting the taxonomy structure.

## Method Summary
The CLARITY task provides a curated corpus of U.S. presidential interview transcripts annotated with both clarity labels and detailed evasion strategies. An expert panel developed a taxonomy of nine evasion tactics, which informed the annotation guidelines. Participants received training and development sets and were evaluated on held-out test data using macro-F1. Baseline models included traditional encoder-only transformers and LLM-based prompting methods. The evaluation protocol measured performance separately for the two subtasks, encouraging the development of systems that could handle both fine-grained strategy detection and broader clarity assessment.

## Key Results
- Best macro-F1 on clarity classification reached 0.89, substantially outperforming the 0.68 baseline.
- For evasion strategy classification, the top score was 0.68 macro-F1, identical to the baseline, with the next best around 0.666.
- Hierarchical LLM prompting pipelines that first predict evasion types and then derive clarity labels consistently outperformed systems that treated the subtasks independently.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hierarchical pipeline works because the fine-grained evasion classification provides intermediate, semantically rich cues that directly correlate with the final clarity label. For example, specific tactics like "answer avoidance" or "topic shift" are strong indicators of a clear non-reply. By separating the tasks, the system can leverage specialized representations and reduce label noise. Additionally, LLM prompting allows the model to incorporate the expert taxonomy definitions without extensive fine-tuning, benefiting from the model’s pre-trained knowledge and instruction-following capabilities. The pipeline structure also simplifies error analysis and supports incremental improvements.

## Foundational Learning
- **Macro-F1 evaluation**: Needed to fairly assess performance across imbalanced classes; quick check: compute per-class precision/recall and average.
- **Expert-grounded taxonomy**: Ensures domain-relevant evasion categories; quick check: verify inter-annotator agreement to validate categories.
- **Hierarchical classification**: Exploits correlations between coarse and fine labels to boost accuracy; quick check: compare joint vs. pipeline models.
- **LLM prompting**: Leverages pre-trained knowledge for low-resource tasks; quick check: compare zero-shot prompting with supervised fine-tuning.
- **Political question evasion detection**: Critical for analyzing democratic discourse; quick check: manually annotate a sample to understand evasion patterns.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input Text -> LLM Prompt Module -> Fine-Grained Evasion Classifier -> Clarity Label Mapper -> Output Labels

**Critical path**: The main inference flow runs from the input through the LLM-based evasion predictor and then to the mapping module; any error in the evasion prediction directly contaminates the final clarity label.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Hierarchical vs. joint multitask*: Hierarchical simplifies training and allows use of different models per stage, but errors propagate. Joint multitask can learn cross-task dependencies but may suffer from optimization complexity.  
- *Prompting vs. fine-tuning*: Prompting is flexible and requires minimal task-specific data but is slower at inference; fine-tuning yields faster, potentially more accurate models but needs more annotated data and risks overfitting.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Misclassification of evasion tactics (e.g., labeling an ambivalent reply as a clear non-reply).  
- Ambiguous mapping from evasion types to clarity labels (some tactics may map to multiple clarity categories depending on context).  
- LLM ignoring taxonomy definitions or hallucinating non-standard evasion categories.

**First experiments**:
1. Baseline: Directly prompt the LLM to predict clarity labels without hierarchical decomposition.
2. Hierarchical pipeline: Use LLM prompting to predict fine-grained evasion types, then apply a rule-based mapping to clarity labels.
3. Oracle ablation: Replace the predicted evasion types with gold-standard labels to measure the upper bound of the hierarchical approach.

## Limitations
- The benchmark is restricted to U.S. presidential interviews, limiting generalizability to other political contexts or media formats.
- The paper lacks expert annotation details such as inter-annotator agreement, making it hard to assess data quality.
- The evasion subtask’s top score matches the baseline, and neighboring system scores are lower, raising reproducibility concerns.
