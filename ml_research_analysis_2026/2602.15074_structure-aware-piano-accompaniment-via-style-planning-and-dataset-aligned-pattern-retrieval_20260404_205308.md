# Structure-Aware Piano Accompaniment via Style Planning and Dataset-Aligned Pattern Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15074
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15074
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** Generates controllable long-form piano accompaniments with small datasets, achieving near-saturated pattern diversity (unique ratio ~0.998) and strong style isolation via decoupled style planning and dataset-aligned retrieval.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a system for generating piano accompaniments that are both controllable and structurally coherent, despite training on a relatively small dataset (POP909). The approach decouples the problem into two stages: a lightweight Transformer predicts a per-measure style plan based on harmonic and structural context; a retrieval module then selects and reharmonizes patterns from the corpus using an optimization that enforces harmonic feasibility, voice-leading continuity, and diversity. The method achieves robust performance with only 3.3% of measures requiring relaxation, and produces highly diverse outputs with strong adherence to style prompts. Limitations include corpus-bound creativity and lack of acoustic expressiveness.

## Method Summary
The system operates on a lead sheet input (melody, chords, structure) and produces a MIDI accompaniment. First, the lead sheet is preprocessed: key normalized to C major/A minor, chords represented functionally as Roman numerals with factorized quality, and measure-level structure vectors (section/phrase role) extracted. A four-layer Transformer encoder then predicts six categorical style slots (dynamics, articulation, rhythm, tension, texture, register) for each measure, conditioned on a context window of past and future measures. To guarantee realizability, the raw slot predictions are projected onto an inventory of style vectors that actually appear in the training corpus, possibly after applying section-level anchor penalties and keyword constraints. 

Next, a pattern retriever constructs a candidate pool for each target measure by querying a pre-indexed corpus (POP909 measures) using style slots and chord-quality sequences. If the strict pool is empty, staged relaxation (e.g., wildcard matching, quality-family matches, Hamming-distance style neighbors) yields feasible candidates. For each candidate, the retriever computes an energy score comprising penalties for chord-quality mismatches, voice-leading discontinuities (e.g., large leaps, parallel fifths), style drift from the plan (subject to slack budgets), and repetition of pattern signatures. The highest-scoring candidate is selected, then reharmonized by mapping its pitches to the target chord tones while preserving rhythm and voicing topology. The reharmonized pattern is appended to the output, and the process repeats sequentially for all measures.

## Key Results
- **Diversity:** Near-saturated pattern diversity (unique ratio ~0.998) and minimal repeats (repeat ratio ~0.000) on held-out test songs.
- **Style Isolation:** Strong separation between different style prompts; active pairwise match mean ~0.02 indicates low cross-style contamination.
- **Robustness:** Only 3.3% of measures required staged relaxation from empty strict pools, showing effective corpus coverage for most contexts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Style Planning with Corpus-Inventory Constraints
A lightweight Transformer predicts per-measure style slots (dynamics, articulation, rhythm, tension, texture, register) conditioned on harmonic and structural context. The model is trained with a masked-slot objective over a context window of 32 past measures and one future measure. Its outputs are projected onto an inventory of style vectors that actually appear in the POP909 corpus, ensuring that every requested style has at least one realizable pattern. This projection, combined with section-level anchors and limited slack budgets, yields a coherent plan that is feasible given the dataset. Evidence: the inventory covers 77,638 measures; only 3.3% of test measures needed relaxation, indicating high coverage.

### Mechanism 2: Constrained Retrieval with Staged Relaxation
Pattern retrieval is formulated as an energy minimization over a candidate pool. The pool is built via staged relaxation: starting from exact matches on style and chord-quality sequences, then relaxing to quality-family matches, quality-only matches, and finally bounded Hamming distance in style space. The energy function explicitly penalizes chord-quality mismatches, voice-leading discontinuities (e.g., large leaps, parallel fifths), style drift beyond allowed slack, and repetition of recently used patterns. This multi-objective scoring produces selections that are harmonically appropriate, smoothly connected, and diverse. Evidence: median strict pool size is 23.2 measures, and the 3.3% miss rate shows most target contexts have feasible strict matches.

### Mechanism 3: Robust Coupling via Slack and Diversity Controls
Long-form coherence is maintained by coupling the planner and retriever through inventory constraints and limited deviation budgets. The planner's outputs are anchored within each section to encourage macro-contrasts, while a phrase-level and song-level slack budget permits a small number of style deviations only at harmonically justified points (typically annotated transitions). A drift penalty monitors continuous features (velocity, staccato, register) to prevent gradual accumulation of unintended changes. Diversity is enforced via a repetition penalty on pattern signatures with a cooldown window. Together, these mechanisms prevent mode collapse and ensure varied outputs without sacrificing structural integrity. Evidence: the extremely low repeat ratio (0.000) and high unique ratio (0.998) confirm effective diversity control; ablation studies show statistically reliable style motion and section contrast.

## Foundational Learning
### Functional Harmony & Transposition-Invariant Representation
Chords are represented by Roman numerals (e.g., V7) and factorized quality (major, minor, dominant, etc.) rather than absolute pitch classes. This allows patterns to be matched across keys based on harmonic function, drastically reducing sparsity in a small corpus. *Quick check:* Why would matching on absolute chords (e.g., G7) be less effective than functional roles for a retrieval system with only 909 songs?

### Structure-Aware Modeling (Sections & Phrases)
The system explicitly uses section labels (verse, chorus) and phrase-role tags (start, mid, end) as conditioning. These long-range form cues enable the planner to anticipate structural boundaries (e.g., building tension before a chorus) and the retriever to select patterns appropriate for their position (e.g., cadential patterns at phrase ends). *Quick check:* How does a phrase-role label like "end" differ in expected accompaniment style from "mid", and why can't this be captured by looking at just the current chord?

### Retrieval-Based Generation with Adaptive Matching
The approach reuses real pianist performances for idiomatic voicing and articulation. The retriever is not a simple nearest-neighbor lookup; it performs a constrained optimization that gracefully degrades feasibility via staged relaxation when strict matches are unavailable. ## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input (lead sheet) → Preprocessor (normalization, functional harmony, structure extraction) → Style Planner (Transformer) → Projection onto inventory → For each measure: Retriever (index lookup → staged relaxation → energy scoring → selection) → Reharmonization → Output (MIDI concatenation)

### Critical Path
The style planner runs once over the entire song to produce a per-measure style plan. Then the retriever processes measures sequentially, using the previously generated pattern to compute voice-leading costs for the next measure. Slack budgets and diversity penalties depend on this sequential state.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Planner size vs. data:** A compact 4-layer Transformer avoids overfitting on POP909; larger models would likely overfit.
- **Retriever exhaustivity vs. speed:** Indexing by length, style, and quality enables fast lookup; staged relaxation balances feasibility with search space growth.
- **Corpus creativity vs. quality:** The system cannot invent new patterns, limiting diversity to the corpus's stylistic scope (mostly 4/4 Chinese pop). However, it gains idiomatic realism.
- **Interpretability vs. nuance:** Six discrete style slots simplify planning and indexing but sacrifice intra-measure dynamics and articulation details.

### Failure Signatures
- **Empty strict pool:** After style+QUAL match, candidate pool empty. Triggers relaxation; if fully exhausted, fallback may break coherence.
- **Reharmonization rejection:** Candidate's required pitch shifts exceed threshold or chord-tone ratio too low, forcing a less optimal selection.
- **Slack budget exhausted:** Too many required style deviations in a phrase/song cause subsequent measures to strictly adhere to plan, risking repetition or poor matches.
- **Planner uncertainty:** Moderate slot accuracy (~0.636) means many planned styles deviate from dataset mode; projection and slack act as stabilizers but may filter out desirable prompts.
- **Drift:** Continuous features (velocity, staccato) may gradually leave the target band implied by planned labels, causing subtle incoherence.

### First Three Experiments
1. **Train style planner:** Use masked-slot objective on POP909 training split. Monitor slot accuracy (expect ~0.6-0.7 for dyn/rhythm, lower for texture/register) and loss convergence. Validate that predictions respect structure (e.g., higher texture in choruses).
2. **Build retrieval index:** Preprocess POP909 measures into indexed records (length, style, quality sequence, role, signature, MIDI). Test lookup for typical style+quality pairs; ensure median pool size is adequate (>5).
3. **End-to-end generation with AUTO prompts:** Run on a held-out test song. Check diversity metrics: unique ratio should be ~0.998, repeat ratio ~0.000. If unique ratio drops, inspect pool sizes and relaxation stages.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Beat-level dynamics and intra-measure texture:** Can the style planner predict finer-grained controls? Current output is measure-level only.
2. **Neural editing component:** Would a lightweight neural decoder improve harmonic adaptation while maintaining pianistic realism beyond rule-based reharmonization?
3. **Expanding the retrieval corpus:** Would integrating additional datasets (e.g., MAESTRO) increase pattern diversity and reduce empty candidate pools for rare styles?
4. **Robust reharmonization for edge cases:** How to handle altered chords and non-chord tones without artifacts in the pitch mapping?
5. **Perceptual evaluation:** How does the generated output compare to human performances and commercial AI systems in listening tests?

## Limitations
- **Dataset narrowness:** POP909 is limited to Chinese pop piano (4/4), raising questions about genre and meter generalization.
- **Inventory dependence:** The system is constrained by the corpus's style inventory; rare or novel prompts may trigger relaxation or fallback, risking coherence.
- **Coarse style representation:** Six discrete slots sacrifice intra-measure nuance, limiting fine-grained control.

## Confidence
- **Style planning (Transformer):** Medium (slot accuracy ~0.636, moderate predictive power).
- **Dataset-aligned pattern retrieval:** High (near-perfect diversity, low miss rate).
- **Inventory-constraint coupling:** High (ablations show reliable contrast, slack budgets prevent collapse).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-corpus evaluation:** Apply the trained system to a different piano dataset (e.g., MAESTRO) to assess generalization; measure inventory coverage, miss rate, and style transferability.
2. **Structure error robustness:** Inject synthetic errors into section/phrase boundaries and measure degradation in style coherence and retrieval success.
