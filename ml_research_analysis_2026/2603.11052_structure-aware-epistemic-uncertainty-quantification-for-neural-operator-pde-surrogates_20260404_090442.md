# Structure-Aware Epistemic Uncertainty Quantification for Neural Operator PDE Surrogates

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.11052
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11052
*   **Reference Count:** 27
*   **Primary Result:** A method that restricts epistemic uncertainty sampling to the lifting module of a neural operator, yielding uncertainty bands with superior spatial alignment to residuals, high coverage (e.g., 99.9% on 2D Darcy), and tighter bandwidths than unstructured baselines while maintaining practical inference cost.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses epistemic uncertainty quantification (UQ) for neural operator surrogates of partial differential equations (PDEs). It introduces a *structure-aware* principle that exploits the standard lifting-propagation-recovering modular architecture. Instead of applying stochasticity (e.g., dropout) across the entire network, the method injects noise *only* into the lifting module, which maps the input field to an initial latent feature representation. The subsequent propagation (learned solver dynamics) and recovering (output readout) modules are treated as deterministic. On challenging benchmarks with discontinuities (2D Darcy) and geometry shift (3D ShapeNet Car), this approach produces uncertainty bands that are more tightly aligned with the true error residual field, leading to higher and more reliable coverage rates compared to baselines like MC Dropout, Laplace Approximation, and Deep Ensembles, with significantly lower computational overhead than ensembles.

## Method Summary
The method operates on a trained neural operator with a modular structure: an input field is first passed through a **lifting module** (typically a linear layer) to create an initial latent feature tensor. This tensor is then processed by a **propagation module** (e.g., Fourier layers or attention) that implements learned solver dynamics, and finally a **recovering module** (usually linear) produces the output prediction. For epistemic UQ, the core idea is to treat the propagation and recovery as a fixed, deterministic operator **T**. Epistemic uncertainty is modeled by injecting channel-wise stochasticity *only* into the output of the lifting module. Two instantiations are proposed: (A) multiplicative Bernoulli dropout applied per channel (with rescaling), and (B) additive Gaussian noise with variance matched to the feature magnitude. For a given input, *T* stochastic forward passes are run with different noise samples. The predictive mean and standard deviation across these samples form the surrogates for the output mean and epistemic uncertainty band. A scalar calibration factor *k* is tuned on a validation set to achieve a target coverage rate (e.g., 99.9%).

## Key Results
*   Achieves near-perfect total coverage rate (99.9%) on the challenging 2D Darcy benchmark with discontinuities, significantly outperforming MC Dropout (91.7%) and matching Deep Ensembles (99.9%) but with ~30x lower inference cost.
*   Produces substantially tighter normalized average bandwidths than all baselines on both Darcy and 3D ShapeNet Car, indicating more efficient uncertainty estimates.
*   Demonstrates markedly improved spatial alignment between the uncertainty band field and the absolute residual field, as shown qualitatively in Figures 3–5, compared to unstructured baselines where bands appear smeared or in irrelevant smooth regions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Lifting as the Primary Epistemic Subspace
*   **Claim:** Restricting epistemic uncertainty sampling to the lifting module yields uncertainty bands that better align with localized residual structures compared to sampling across the full network or other modules.
*   **Mechanism:** The lifting module maps inputs to an initial latent feature field. Perturbing this module injects uncertainty into the initial conditions of the deterministic propagation-recovery operator **T**, which then propagates that uncertainty through the learned solver dynamics. This isolates uncertainty to the representation of input-dependent features rather than distorting the fixed solver dynamics (propagation) or output combination (recovery).
*   **Core Assumption:** The dominant epistemic error in a well-trained neural operator stems from imperfect representation of the input field in the lifted feature space, not from uncertainty in the learned evolution (propagation) or readout (recovery) mechanisms.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] states the method "restrict[s] Monte Carlo sampling to a module-aligned subspace by injecting stochasticity only into the lifting module". [section 3.1] explains that sampling recovering or propagation produces uninformative or outlier samples. Figures 3–5 visually confirm superior alignment.
*   **Break Condition:** If the primary epistemic error source shifts from lifted feature representation to the propagation operator's learned dynamics (e.g., due to severe distribution shift in PDE physics), lifting-only perturbations will underestimate uncertainty in regions where the fixed solver fails.

### Mechanism 2: Channel-Wise Zero-Mean Feature Perturbation
*   **Claim:** Channel-wise multiplicative noise (dropout-style) and matched-variance Gaussian perturbation on lifted features provide efficient, zero-mean sampling that preserves feature structure while generating diverse predictive samples.
*   **Mechanism:** Both instantiations apply per-channel noise broadcast across space. This respects the spatial coherence of features while allowing each channel's magnitude to scale its perturbation, maintaining a linkage between signal strength and uncertainty scale. The noise is calibrated to have unit mean, preventing systematic bias in the predictive mean after MC averaging.
*   **Core Assumption:** Perturbations should be applied in the feature space (after lifting) rather than directly to weights, and should respect channel-wise statistics to avoid destroying informative feature patterns.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [section 3.2] defines both noise forms and notes they are "zero-mean around the deterministic embedding". The derivation for dropout shows `Var(E_i,j) = V0,i,j² · p/(1-p)`, demonstrating heteroscedastic noise proportional to feature magnitude.
*   **Break Condition:** If lifted features are highly sparse or binary, multiplicative dropout could zero-out entire channels excessively, collapsing sample diversity. The Gaussian alternative may be more robust but could add noise where it is not epistemically justified.

### Mechanism 3: Deterministic Propagation Transforms Initial Uncertainty into Residual-Aligned Bands
*   **Claim:** Structure-aware sampling produces uncertainty bands with improved spatial alignment to the true residual field (|r(x)|), leading to more reliable coverage and tighter bandwidths than unstructured methods.
*   **Mechanism:** Because samples are generated by perturbing only the initial lifted features and then applying the *same* deterministic operator **T**, the resulting prediction variability across samples primarily reflects how uncertainty in initial feature representations propagates and amplifies through **T**. This variability pattern is more likely to match the true residual patterns (which also arise from imperfect feature representations) than variability from perturbing later modules, which can introduce artificial error modes.
*   **Core Assumption:** The deterministic remainder operator **T** (trained propagation+recovery) is a sufficiently accurate surrogate for the true PDE solver on the test distribution, so that differences between samples arise mainly from lifted feature perturbations, not from **T**'s internal instability.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] claims "uncertainty bands should align with the localized residual structures". [section 1.3] provides the operational interpretation of converting weight uncertainty into initial condition uncertainty. [section 4, Figures 3, 4, 5] show visual comparisons with tighter bands that track discontinuities/geometric features better than baselines.
*   **Break Condition:** Under severe distribution shift (e.g., new PDE physics in inputs), the fixed operator **T** may produce large, spatially coherent errors that are *not* captured by lifting perturbations, leading to under-coverage. The mechanism relies on **T** being locally accurate.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Neural Operator Modular Anatomy (Lifting-Propagation-Recovering)
    *   **Why Needed:** The entire method depends on identifying the lifting module as the correct subspace for perturbation. Without understanding that lifting creates the initial latent field and that propagation/recovery implement the learned solver dynamics, the design rationale is opaque.
    *   **Quick Check:** In a standard FNO, which component maps the input coefficient field to the initial latent feature tensor, and what is its typical parameter count relative to the whole model?
*   **Concept:** Epistemic vs. Aleatoric Uncertainty
    *   **Why Needed:** The method targets epistemic uncertainty (from limited data/optimization), not aleatoric (inherent noise). Misidentifying the uncertainty type would lead to misapplying the technique.
    *   **Quick Check:** For a deterministic PDE with noiseless data, what type of uncertainty dominates a neural operator's errors on a held-out test set?
*   **Concept:** Uncertainty Quantification Metrics (Coverage Rate, Bandwidth)
    *   **Why Needed:** The paper’s evaluations hinge on these metrics. Understanding that "better" means high coverage *with* low bandwidth, and that spatial alignment is an additional qualitative criterion, is essential to interpreting results.
    *   **Quick Check:** If an uncertainty band field exactly matches the absolute residual field at every point, what would the coverage rate and normalized average bandwidth be (approximately)?
*   **Concept:** Calibration of Uncertainty Bands
    *   **Why Needed:** The raw standard deviation from MC samples must be scaled by a constant *k* to achieve nominal coverage (e.g., 99.9%). This post-hoc calibration step is critical for reliable intervals.
    *   **Quick Check:** If the uncalibrated uncertainty bands systematically under-cover on a validation set, should the calibration multiplier *k* be set to a value greater than or less than 1?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    Input discretized field (A) -> Lifting module (P_θP) -> Lifted feature field (V0) -> *[Noise Injection Here]* -> Propagation module (M_θM) -> Final latent field (VL) -> Recovering module (Q_θQ) -> Output prediction (û). The deterministic operator T = Q_θQ ∘ M_θM.
*   **Critical Path:**
    1.  Identify the lifting layer(s) in the existing NO codebase (typically the first layer(s) that increase channel dimension).
    2.  Modify the lifting forward pass: For method A (dropout), sample a channel mask ξ ~ Bernoulli(1-p)/(1-p) and multiply feature output V0 by ξ. For method B (Gaussian), add V0 ⊙ ϵ where ϵ ~ N(0, p/(1-p)). Use `torch.no_grad()` for noise sampling.
    3.  In the inference loop, run T forward passes with *different* noise samples, collecting outputs.
    4.  Compute raw uncertainty band as standard deviation across T outputs.
    5.  Apply the calibrated scalar multiplier *k* (tuned on validation set) to the raw band.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Dropout (A) vs. Gaussian (B):** Dropout is discrete, zeroes out entire channels at rate *p*. Cheaper but can collapse diversity if *p* is too high. Gaussian is continuous, smoother, but may require slightly more samples (T) for similar variance estimation.
    *   **Dropout rate (*p*):** Too small → samples nearly identical → under-coverage. Too large → most channels zeroed → biased mean, over-conservative bands. Paper finds 0.1–0.3 works well.
    *   **Sample count (T):** Larger T reduces Monte Carlo noise in band estimate but increases inference cost linearly. Paper shows T=20–100 sufficient; diminishing returns beyond T=1000.
    *   **Calibration necessity:** Raw std from samples must be scaled by *k*>1 to achieve nominal coverage. *k* is data-dependent and must be tuned on a validation set.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   **Biased predictive mean:** MC average of samples (û) differs significantly from deterministic prediction. Indicates noise injection is too aggressive or mis-scaled.
    *   **Inflated bandwidth, poor coverage:** Bands are uniformly too wide and/or still contain high residuals despite width. Suggests wrong sampling subspace (e.g., applied to propagation) or *p* is too large.
    *   **Spatially smeared bands:** Uncertainty is high everywhere, not concentrated where residuals are high. Classic MC Dropout symptom; indicates unstructured perturbations.
    *   **OOD collapse:** On geometry-shifted test data (e.g., ShapeNet Car), coverage drops severely even with high *k*. Suggests fixed **T** is not modeling OOD error modes; may need OOD-aware recalibration.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline replication:** On 2D Darcy (FNO), implement naive MC Dropout (dropout in *all* layers) and record Total C.R., Normalized Avg. B.W., and inference time. Compare against paper’s Table 1 row for MC Dropout. Expected: high bandwidth, decent coverage but spatially poor.
    2.  **Method A (dropout in lifting only):** Apply channel-wise dropout *only* in the lifting layer. Use *p*=0.3, T=20. Perform validation set tuning to find scalar *k* that yields ~99% coverage on validation. Evaluate Total C.R. and Avg. B.W. on test. Expected: comparable/better coverage than Deep Ensemble with much lower bandwidth and faster inference.
    3.  **Ablation - sampling location:** Apply the same channel-wise dropout (*p*=0.3, T=20, same *k*) to (a) propagation module only, (b) recovering module only, (c) lifting+recovery. Visualize uncertainty bands and compute coverage. Expected: (a) and (b) produce wider, less aligned bands; (c) similar to lifting-only. Confirms lifting is the critical subspace.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   What richer *z* distributions improve UQ? (Only two simple perturbations evaluated.)
*   How extend structure-aware UQ to multi-physics for coupled uncertainty? (Current method handles single-field only.)
*   Can the method incorporate aleatoric uncertainty from solver errors? (It currently does not.)
*   Can calibrated uncertainty be maintained under distribution shift? (OOD inputs degrade calibration; current methods lack robust adaptation.)
*   What are theoretical bounds for lifting-subspace sampling? (No formal guarantees on coverage or convergence.)

## Limitations
*   Missing training hyperparameters, full architecture specifications, and precise calibration procedure for *k* hinder exact replication of results.
*   The method's effectiveness relies on the propagation operator **T** being locally accurate; under severe distribution shift (e.g., new PDE physics), coverage may degrade.
*   The evaluation focuses on two specific PDE benchmarks; generalizability to other operator architectures or problem classes is not extensively validated.

## Confidence
*   **Lifting-only superiority:** Medium-High (strong experimental results but replication limited by missing details).
*   **Channel-wise noise design:** Medium (theoretically sound, but limited external validation; depends on feature statistics).
*   **Inference efficiency:** High (theoretical cost low and empirically confirmed).
