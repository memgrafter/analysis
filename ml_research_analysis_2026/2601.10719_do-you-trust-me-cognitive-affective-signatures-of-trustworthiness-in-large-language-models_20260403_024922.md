# Do You Trust Me? Cognitive-Affective Signatures of Trustworthiness in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.10719
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10719
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Trust signals are linearly accessible in attention-head activations of instruction-tuned LLMs (peak accuracies: 61–67%), aligning with psychological dimensions (fairness, certainty, accountability-self).

## Executive Summary
This paper explores whether large language models (LLMs) implicitly encode perceived trustworthiness in a manner consistent with human cognitive-affective processes. The authors use the PEACE-Reviews dataset of autobiographical narratives, each annotated with reader trust ratings and author-reported appraisals, emotions, and intentions. They analyze three instruction-tuned models (Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen 2.5 7B, Mistral 7B) by extracting attention-head activations and applying linear probes to decode trustworthiness. Results demonstrate that trust cues are linearly decodable with accuracies between 61% and 67%, and that the most informative features correspond to key psychological constructs such as fairness, certainty, and accountability-self—central to human trust formation. The trust signal emerges predominantly in mid-to-late layers, varies across architectures, and is sharpened by LoRA fine-tuning without being relocated. Nonlinear probing offers only marginal improvements, underscoring the linear separability of trust representations. This work suggests that LLMs capture social-semantic structures related to trust during pretraining, and that these representations can be accessed and refined with minimal supervision.

## Method Summary
The study leverages the PEACE-Reviews dataset, comprising 1,400 autobiographical narratives with binary trust labels (derived from 5-point Likert ratings) and comprehensive annotations of cognitive appraisals, emotions, and intentions. Three state-of-the-art instruction-tuned models—Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3—are evaluated. For each narrative, the authors perform a forward pass with frozen models and extract the final-token pre-projection attention-head outputs across all layers and heads. This yields activation tensors of shape [num_layers, num_heads, head_dim] per sample. 

To probe trust representations, they compute two main analyses: (1) the normalized activation magnitude difference between high- and low-trust groups for each layer-head pair (RQ1), and (2) a logistic regression probe trained per head to classify trustworthiness (RQ2). For RQ3, they train probes for all other psychological variables to assess alignment with trust. They also fine-tune each model on the trust labels using LoRA (rank r=8, alpha=32, dropout=0.1) and repeat the probing to examine representational changes. Additional robustness checks include nonlinear MLP probes and probing of post-attention and post-MLP residual streams.

## Key Results
- Systematic activation differences between high- and low-trust texts are observed across layers and heads, with peak linear probe accuracies ranging from 61% to 67%.
- Trust signals are linearly decodable, concentrate in mid-to-late layers (with architecture-specific patterns), and strongly correlate with psychological dimensions of fairness, certainty, and accountability-self.
- LoRA fine-tuning sharpens the existing trust signal without fundamentally restructuring its layer/head distribution; nonlinear probes yield only marginal gains over linear ones.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Trustworthiness is implicitly encoded in LLM activations during pretraining.
- **Mechanism:** Exposure to diverse narratives during pretraining leads the model to internally represent patterns correlated with human trust judgments (e.g., fairness, certainty) without explicit supervision.
- **Core assumption:** Linguistic cues in text have stable statistical relationships with perceived trustworthiness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "trust cues are implicitly encoded during pretraining."
  - Section 4.1: Systematic layer-head activation differences between high/low trust texts across all models.
  - Corpus: Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers study trust perception but not pretraining encoding mechanisms.
- **Break condition:** If pretraining corpora lack sufficient trust-relevant narratives or the model's objectives do not encourage capturing such social-semantic structure.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Trust signals occupy linearly decodable subspaces in intermediate representations.
- **Mechanism:** Features relevant to trust (e.g., fairness appraisals) are represented in directions in activation space that a linear classifier can isolate.
- **Core assumption:** Psychological constructs central to trust map onto near-linear manifolds in LLM representations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "Trust signals are linearly accessible."
  - Section 4.2: Linear probe accuracies peak at 61–67% across models.
  - Section G.1: Nonlinear probes yield only marginal gains.
- **Break condition:** If trust judgments require complex nonlinear integration of many disparate features that do not align with a single linear direction.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Supervised fine-tuning sharpens existing trust representations without relocating them.
- **Mechanism:** LoRA adapters adjust weights to amplify trust-sensitive activations already present in specific layers/heads from pretraining.
- **Core assumption:** Pretrained models already contain a robust, distributed trust signal that supervision refines rather than invents.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "Fine-tuning with LoRA sharpens but does not restructure these pretrained representations."
  - Section 4.2 & Figure 2: Layer/head-level activation patterns and probe curves remain structurally similar post-fine-tuning.
  - Section F: Fine-tuning configuration details (LoRA on specific projection layers).
- **Break condition:** If fine-tuning on trust labels causes a significant shift in which layers/heads are most predictive, indicating restructuring rather than sharpening.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Appraisal Theory (e.g., fairness, certainty, accountability-self)
  - *Why needed here:* Provides the psychological vocabulary to interpret *what* trust-related dimensions the models encode. The paper links model activations to these specific human cognitive-affective variables.
  - *Quick check question:* How would you distinguish an appraisal of "fairness" from "certainty" in a text sample, and why might both matter for trust?
- **Concept:** Linear Probing
  - *Why needed here:* The core methodological tool to test if a latent representation (trust) is accessible in a simple, interpretable way. It operationalizes "linearly decodable."
  - *Quick check question:* What does a successful linear probe tell you about the geometry of the target concept in the model's representation space? What does it *not* tell you?
- **Concept:** Residual Stream Dynamics
  - *Why needed here:* Trust signals evolve through the transformer layers. Understanding whether they emerge early, consolidate mid-stack, or persist to the end is key to interpreting model processing. The paper tracks this via post-attention/post-MLP residuals.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a socially complex judgment like trustworthiness be expected to emerge in the middle-to-late layers rather than the first few?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. Data: PEACE-Reviews dataset (narratives + reader trust labels + author appraisals/emotions).
  2. Models: Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen 2.5 7B, Mistral 7B (all instruction-tuned).
  3. Extraction Pipeline: Forward passes with frozen models; extract attention-head output (pre-projection) at final token; also extract post-attention and post-MLP residuals.
  4. Analysis Suite: (a) Activation magnitude difference (RQ1), (b) Logistic regression probe per layer-head (RQ2), (c) Multi-construct probing (RQ3), (d) Nonlinear & residual robustness checks (Appendix G).
  5. Fine-tuning: LoRA adapters on base models, repeat probing.
- **Critical path:** Data Preparation → Base Model Activation Extraction → RQ1 Heatmaps → RQ2 Linear Probes → RQ3 Multi-Construct Probes → Robustness Checks (Nonlinear/Residual) → Fine-tune & Repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Final token vs. all tokens: Focusing on final token simplifies analysis but risks losing spatially distributed signals. The paper's choice assumes narrative-level summary.
  - Head outputs vs. residual stream: Head outputs isolate specific attention mechanisms; residuals capture integrated, post-transformation state. Paper uses both for robustness.
  - Linear vs. nonlinear probes: Linear probes offer interpretability; nonlinear (MLP) probes may capture complex features but risk overfitting. Findings show strong linear separability.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - 1. No layer-head pattern: Activation difference heatmaps (RQ1) show no red/blue distinction, suggesting trust signal is absent or extremely diffuse.
  - 2. At-chance probe performance: All layer-head probe accuracies hover near 50% (binary), indicating no linearly decodable trust information.
  - 3. Inconsistent construct alignment: Probing for core appraisals (fairness, certainty) performs no better than for theoretically irrelevant ones (e.g., disgust), breaking the central psychological claim.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Baseline Separation Test: For a single model (e.g., Mistral), compute and visualize the normalized activation magnitude difference (Δℓ,ℎ) for all layer-head pairs. Identify if any regions show systematic separation.
  2. Single-Layer Probe: Choose the layer-head with max Δ from Exp 1. Train a logistic regression probe *only* on that head's activations. Report accuracy/F1. This tests if local signal is sufficient.
  3. Construct Correlation: Using the best-performing layer from Exp 2, train probes for *all* psychological variables (Table 1). Correlate probe performance (F1) with theoretical relevance to trust (e.g., fairness > disgust).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Why do different LLM architectures (Llama, Qwen, Mistral) exhibit distinct layer-wise patterns for trust signal emergence?
- **Open Question 2:** How would alternative fine-tuning regimes (e.g., full parameter updates, instruction-tuning on diverse tasks) modify the location and structure of trust representations compared to LoRA?
- **Open Question 3:** Why are certain psychological constructs (e.g., disgust, difficulty) less linearly decodable from trust-related activations than others (e.g., fairness, certainty)?
- **Open Question 4:** What is the causal direction between trustworthiness judgments and specific cognitive appraisals (e.g., accountability-self) in LLM representations?

## Limitations
- Unspecified dataset split and binarization details risk misaligned baselines or data leakage.
- Activation extraction details (e.g., tensor indices, pre- vs. post-projection) are ambiguous, potentially affecting reproducibility of observed head patterns.
- Probe training hyperparameters (regularization, solver, class weighting) are not reported, leaving performance estimates vulnerable to overfitting or underfitting.

## Confidence
- Implicit encoding during pretraining: Medium
- Linear accessibility of trust signals: Medium
- LoRA sharpening without restructuring: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Extraction robustness:** Repeat probing using both final-token and mean-pooled token activations; compare layer-head patterns and peak accuracies to confirm signal is not an artifact of the final-token assumption.
2. **Statistical rigor:** Conduct 5‑fold cross‑validation with multiple random seeds, sweep logistic regression regularization (`C`), and report confidence intervals to ensure performance is reliably above chance.
3. **Cross‑model generalization:** Apply the same probing pipeline to the base (non‑instruction) variants of each model (e.g., Llama‑3.1‑8B) to disentangle whether trust encoding is present after pretraining or emerges from instruction tuning.
