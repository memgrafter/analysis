# Arcee Trinity Large Technical Report

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17004
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17004
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** Sparse MoE LLMs (up to 400B total params) trained stably on 10–17 trillion tokens with zero loss spikes, achieving 82.58 MMLU, 65.20 MATH500, and 0.994 MK-NIAH @256K.

## Executive Summary
The Trinity family introduces a series of highly sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models (Nano 6B/1B, Mini 26B/3B, Large 400B/13B) trained on 10–17 trillion tokens. Key architectural innovations include an interleaved hybrid local/global attention pattern with gated attention, the SMEBU load balancer for MoE, and the use of the Muon optimizer for hidden layers. These components collectively enabled stable, loss-spike-free training at unprecedented scale. The Trinity Large Base model achieves strong performance (82.58 MMLU, 65.20 MATH500) and demonstrates exceptional long-context generalization (0.994 MK-NIAH @256K), with inference throughput benefiting from extreme sparsity.

## Method Summary
The Trinity architecture is a decoder-only Transformer featuring depth-scaled sandwich normalization, QK-normalization, and gated attention (sigmoid modulation). Its signature hybrid attention interleaves three local layers (sliding window attention with RoPE) per one global layer (full attention without positional embeddings). MoE routing uses a sigmoid-based router with a shared expert; Nano/Mini rely on standard aux-loss-free balancing, while Large introduces SMEBU (Soft-clamped Momentum Expert Bias Updates) and a small sequence-wise auxiliary loss for stability. Training employs Muon for hidden-layer updates (with fanout-adjusted learning rates) and AdamW for embeddings/logits. The data pipeline uses a custom 200K BPE tokenizer and a Random Sequential Document Buffer (RSDB) to reduce batch heterogeneity during large-scale training. Context extension for Large involved continued training at 256K sequence length using a length-biased data blend.

## Key Results
- Trinity Large Base scores 82.58 on MMLU (5-shot) and 65.20 on MATH500.
- Achieves 0.994 MK-NIAH at 256K context after extension.
- All models completed training with zero loss spikes, indicating high stability.
- Inference throughput is enhanced by 4–8 active experts and efficient architecture.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Local/Global Attention with Gating
- **Claim:** The 3:1 ratio of local (SWA + RoPE) to global (NoPE) layers, combined with gated attention (sigmoid modulation), enables efficient long-context performance while maintaining training stability.
- **Mechanism:** Local layers capture fine-grained, position-aware relationships within windows. Global layers integrate information across the full context without positional bias. Gating applies element-wise sigmoid scaling to attention outputs before projection, reducing activation magnitudes and preventing "attention sinks" that destabilize training, especially with Muon.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Interleaved local/global attention and gated attention are listed as core architecture features.
  - [section 2.2] Gated attention is detailed as reducing attention sinks and improving long-sequence generalization; the 3:1 pattern follows prior work.
  - [section 3.5] Context extension experiments show rapid loss recovery when adjusting only global layers' positional embeddings, confirming their primary role in length generalization.
  - [corpus] Weak external signal: Similar hybrid patterns appear in Yang et al. and NVIDIA Nemotron, but direct comparative evidence is lacking.
- **Break condition:** If gate activations saturate (all near 0 or 1), the model loses expressivity. Monitor gate distribution for bimodality or extreme skew.

### Mechanism 2: SMEBU for MoE Load Balancing
- **Claim:** SMEBU (Soft-clamped Momentum Expert Bias Updates) prevents MoE router collapse during trillion-token training by enabling expert bias to converge rather than oscillate indefinitely.
- **Mechanism:** Standard aux-loss-free methods use discrete ±γ updates based on load violation, causing persistent oscillation. SMEBU replaces this with `tanh(κ * violation)` (soft-clamped) plus momentum buffering, allowing the bias to settle while still correcting imbalance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] SMEBU is introduced specifically for Trinity Large.
  - [section 2.3] Derivation shows how discrete updates prevent convergence; SMEBU's momentum dampens noise near equilibrium.
  - [section 6] Direct attribution: after applying SMEBU with other fixes, "MaxVio stopped diverging, expert utilization remained balanced."
  - [corpus] Weak signal: DeepSeek-V3 and GLM-4.5 use aux-loss-free plus sequence-wise loss but do not report SMEBU; no independent validation yet.
- **Break condition:** If clamping constant κ is too low, updates become negligible; too high reverts to unstable linear steps. Monitor MaxVio trend and expert load variance.

### Mechanism 3: Muon Optimizer with Adjusted Learning Rate
- **Claim:** The Muon optimizer, with a fanout-adjusted learning rate, enables stable large-batch training and higher sample efficiency than AdamW for hidden layers.
- **Mechanism:** Muon applies orthogonal updates to weight matrices, preserving gradient norm magnitudes across matrix multiplications. The rule `lr_adjusted = lr * sqrt(max(1, fanout/fanin))` ensures optimal transfer when scaling model width, supporting larger critical batch sizes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We train the models using the Muon optimizer."
  - [section 3.4.2] Explicit learning rates and the adjustment rule are specified; Muon is used for hidden layers, AdamW for embedding/output.
  - [section 1] Claims Muon enables larger critical batch size and higher sample efficiency than AdamW.
  - [corpus] Strong support: Muon's original paper (Jordan et al., 2024) provides theory and evidence; adoption by DeepSeek-V3 and Kimi indicates industry validation.
- **Break condition:** Without QK-normalization, max attention logits can grow and couple with Muon's dynamics, causing instability. Monitor max logits and gradient norms.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing:** Needed because Trinity uses sparse MoE to scale total parameters while keeping FLOPs low. Understanding router collapse (all tokens going to one expert) and how aux-loss-free or SMEBU balancing corrects it is central.
  - *Quick check:* What happens if the router sends all tokens to a single expert? How does aux-loss-free balancing attempt to correct this?
- **Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) vs. NoPE:** Local layers use RoPE for relative position within windows; global layers use NoPE to avoid positional interference at very long contexts. The coexistence explains the hybrid pattern's design.
  - *Quick check:* Why might NoPE be preferred for global layers when extending to very long contexts (e.g., 256K)?
- **Gradient norm stability & batch heterogeneity:** The paper identifies imbalanced minibatches (from sequential packing) as a hidden instability source. RSDB was invented to mitigate this. The link between data packing and gradient statistics is key.
  - *Quick check:* How can the distribution of document lengths in a minibatch affect the variance of gradient updates even if the overall data distribution is IID?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Tokenizer (200k BPE, digit isolation, script-aware) -> Embedding (×√d scaling) -> [x60 layers: sandwich norm -> (local: GQA+QK-norm+RoPE+SWA+gated) or (global: GQA+QK-norm+NoPE+gated) -> MoE (sigmoid router, 1 shared + 256 routed experts, SMEBU)] -> Final RMSNorm -> LM head (tied).
- **Critical path:** (1) Tokenizer with digit chunking; (2) Model scaffold with GQA and QK-norm; (3) Implement local vs. global attention switch; (4) Add gated attention; (5) Integrate MoE with sigmoid routing; (6) Add SMEBU or standard aux-free balancing; (7) Apply depth-scaled sandwich norm; (8) Configure Muon+AdamW optimizers.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Coarse-grained experts (256 routed, 4 active) for Large vs. fine-grained (128 routed, 8 active) for Smaller: trades router granularity for throughput and expert parallelism efficiency.
  - Increasing initial dense layers (2→6 for Large) stabilizes early representations but raises early FLOPs.
  - Extending only global attention windows keeps local SWA efficient; however, local windows become relatively smaller at extreme lengths.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Router collapse: rising MaxVio, plateauing loss, uneven expert utilization. Fix: Apply SMEBU + sequence-wise aux loss + small z-loss.
  - Loss spikes: often from batch imbalance. Monitor BatchHet metric; implement RSDB.
  - Muon instability: watch max attention logits; ensure QK-norm active. Check LR adjustment formula if diverging.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement basic transformer with Muon on small synthetic dataset; verify `lr_adjusted = lr * sqrt(max(1, fanout/fanin))` yields smooth loss decrease.
  2. Add gated attention and hybrid 3:1 local/global pattern; run sequence-length sweep (2K, 4K, 8K) to confirm no loss spikes when increasing context.
  3. Integrate MoE with standard aux-loss-free balancing; deliberately reduce router dimension to provoke collapse; verify SMEBU reduces MaxVio oscillation vs. sign updates.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** What is the relative contribution of each stability intervention (SMEBU, z-loss, intra-doc masking, increased dense layers) to Trinity Large's success?
  - *Basis:* Six changes applied simultaneously without controlled ablations.
  - *Why unresolved:* No systematic isolation of individual effects.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Ablation study varying one intervention at a time, measuring MaxVio, utilization balance, loss stability, final benchmarks.
- **Question:** Why did SuperBPE tokenizer (better compression) fail to improve downstream performance?
  - *Basis:* Explicitly reported null result; cause not investigated.
  - *Why unresolved:* No analysis of tokenizer-model scaling interactions.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Compare standard BPE vs. SuperBPE across scales (Nano, Mini, Large), analyzing loss curves, gradient norms, representation quality.
- **Question:** How does RSDB independently affect stability and final quality vs. other data/architecture choices?
  - *Basis:* RSDB reduced BatchHet 4.23x but introduced during phase 3 data shift.
  - *Why unresolved:* Hard to disentangle from distribution changes; causal link to final performance not established.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Train matched models with/without RSDB from start, measuring BatchHet, gradient kurtosis, sample efficiency, and final evaluation scores.
- **Question:** To what extent does model scale independently facilitate context extension beyond trained length?
  - *Basis:* Larger models (Mini, Large) extended better than Nano; Large scored 0.42 MK-NIAH @1M after training at 256K.
  - *Why unresolved:* Correlational; no controlled scaling study isolates scale as variable.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Train scaled-down Trinity Large architecture (same depth/width ratios) at 256K, evaluate at 512K/1M; compare to full 400B and smaller models.
- **Question:** Is SMEBU's tanh clamping and momentum fundamental, or could simpler adaptive bias updates work?
  - *Basis:* SMEBU adopted based on intuition; discrete sign updates hypothesized to cause oscillation.
  - *Why unresolved:* No comparison to alternative schemes (e.g., decaying λ, AdamW-style bias updates).
  - *Resolving evidence:* Compare SMEBU to variants: sign updates with decaying λ, linear updates with momentum, tanh without momentum; track MaxVio, entropy, final loss in medium-scale MoE models.

## Limitations
- Exact composition and sourcing of the 10–17 trillion token corpora and synthetic data generation recipes are undisclosed.
- Precise hyperparameter schedules (global batch size ramp, aux-loss coefficients, RSDB buffer details) and evaluation protocols (few-shot prompts, post-processing) are not fully specified.
- Weak external validation for SMEBU and gated attention; no independent replications yet.

## Confidence
- **Muon optimizer:** High (strong theory, empirical support, industry adoption).
- **Hybrid local/global + gated attention:** Medium (solid internal evidence, limited comparative ablations).
- **SMEBU load balancing:** Low–Medium (self-attributed, no external validation).
- **Zero loss spikes claim:** Medium (no public training logs, possible underreporting).

## Next Checks
