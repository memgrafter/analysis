# When Less is More: The LLM Scaling Paradox in Context Compression

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09789  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09789  
- **Reference count:** 13  
- **Primary result:** Scaling LLM compressors from 0.6B to 90B parameters improves training loss but degrades reconstruction faithfulness, revealing a Size-Fidelity Paradox where larger models yield higher BLEU scores but lower QA accuracy (e.g., 90B drops to 0.55 FaithEval accuracy vs. 0.71 for 4B at 16× compression).

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates a counterintuitive scaling phenomenon in lossy context compression using large language models. While larger compressor models achieve lower training loss, they reconstruct source text with reduced factual and structural fidelity. Two distinct failure modes are isolated: **knowledge overwriting** (source facts replaced by model priors) and **semantic drift** (subtle restructuring of relationships). Theparadox is mechanistically linked to increased effective rank and conditional entropy in larger models, properties beneficial for open-ended generation but detrimental to verbatim reconstruction. Findings challenge the assumption that scaling universally improves quality in compression tasks.

## Method Summary
The study employs a compressor-decoder framework. A compressor model maps an input sequence of length *L* to *M* memory tokens (a latent tensor *Z* ∈ ℝ<sup>*M*×*d*</sup>), where the compression ratio is *L*/*M*. A fixed decoder (Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct in main experiments) autoregressively reconstructs the original text from *Z*. Training uses a hybrid loss combining prefix reconstruction (*L*<sub>re</sub>) and next-token prediction (*L*<sub>nt</sub>). Compressor models span the Qwen-3 and LLaMA-3.2 families (0.6B to 90B parameters). Evaluation uses surface metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) and fidelity-focused QA tasks: FaithEval and ConflictQA test for knowledge overwriting by probing counterfactual recall; custom FineWeb-based QA pairs test for semantic drift across seven structural dimensions (e.g., role binding, modifier scope).

## Key Results
- Scaling compressor size improves training loss and surface similarity (BLEU) but consistently degrades reconstruction fidelity as measured by QA accuracy.
- At 16× compression, a 90B compressor yields 0.55 FaithEval accuracy versus 0.71 for a 4B model—demonstrating a non-monotonic fidelity curve.
- Two failure modes are cleanly separated: larger models show stronger negative correlation of effective rank with knowledge overwriting (r = −0.931) and of conditional entropy with semantic drift (r = −0.823).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Knowledge Overwriting via Increased Effective Rank
- **Claim:** Larger compressors exhibit knowledge overwriting due to increased effective rank of memory embeddings.
- **Mechanism:** Higher effective rank indicates more dispersed, distributed representations across semantic subspaces. This dispersion creates broader inference pathways where the model’s parametric priors can more easily intrude and override source-specific facts.
- **Core assumption:** Representational distribution width (effective rank) directly modulates susceptibility to prior interference during reconstruction.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “increased effective rank ... facilitate prior knowledge intrusion”; [section 5.1] “effective rank increases monotonically with model scale” and “negative correlation (r=−0.931) between effective rank and QA accuracy”.
- **Break condition:** If effective rank is constrained (e.g., via explicit low-rank regularization during training), knowledge overwriting may decrease even for large models, decoupling scale from fidelity loss.

### Mechanism 2: Semantic Drift via Higher Conditional Entropy
- **Claim:** Larger compressors exhibit semantic drift due to higher conditional entropy in token prediction distributions.
- **Mechanism:** Elevated entropy reflects greater generative uncertainty—the decoder’s predictions are less peaked, making it more likely to sample among multiple fluent paraphrases that subtly alter relational or structural elements (e.g., role reversal).
- **Core assumption:** The decoder’s predictive uncertainty, conditioned on the compressed representation, drives restructuring instead of verbatim copying.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “higher entropy over token prediction distributions promotes rewriting”; [section 5.2] “entropy ... rises from 4B to 90B” and “strong negative correlation (r=−0.823) between entropy and QA accuracy”.
- **Break condition:** If the training objective or decoding strategy is modified to reduce output entropy (e.g., via token-level consistency losses), semantic drift may be suppressed regardless of model scale.

### Mechanism 3: Inherent Trade-off Between Semantic Capacity and Fidelity
- **Claim:** The Size-Fidelity Paradox emerges from an inherent trade-off between semantic capacity (beneficial for reasoning) and fidelity preservation (required for lossy compression).
- **Mechanism:** Scaling amplifies properties—high effective rank and high entropy—that enhance abstract reasoning and generative flexibility. However, faithful reconstruction demands low-rank, deterministic representations that rigidly preserve source content. The same properties that empower complex generation interfere with the conservative copying needed for compression.
- **Core assumption:** The capabilities optimized for open-ended generation (scaling benefits) are misaligned with the constraints of verbatim reconstruction.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “the very properties that enable complex reasoning interfere with the rigid fidelity required”; [section 1] “excessive semantic capacity and amplified generative uncertainty ... accompany scaling”.
- **Break condition:** Architectural designs that separate reasoning and compression pathways (e.g., distinct modules or training phases) could preserve scaling benefits while mitigating fidelity loss.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Effective Rank**
  - *Why needed here:* Measures how distributed the memory embeddings are across semantic dimensions; higher rank means broader representational capacity that enables prior intrusion.
  - *Quick check question:* How is effective rank computed from singular values, and why does a higher value indicate greater vulnerability to knowledge overwriting?
- **Concept: Conditional Entropy**
  - *Why needed here:* Quantifies the uncertainty in the decoder’s token predictions given the compressed representation; higher entropy increases the likelihood of semantic drift through paraphrasing.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is averaging entropy over reconstruction tokens a better indicator of drift risk than single-step entropy?
- **Concept: Compressor-Decoder Paradigm**
  - *Why needed here:* Defines the setup where a compressor maps a long sequence to memory tokens and a decoder reconstructs; fidelity is assessed against verbatim source, not topical similarity.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the reconstruction objective here differ from abstractive summarization in terms of tolerance for deviation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Compressor → Memory tokens *Z* → Decoder → Reconstructed text
  Training objective: Hybrid loss *L* = *L*<sub>re</sub> + *L*<sub>nt</sub>
  Evaluation suite: Surface metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) vs. fidelity QA tasks (FaithEval, ConflictQA, custom FineWeb QA)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Input text → compressor → memory tokens *Z*.
  2. *Z* → decoder (autoregressive) → reconstructed text.
  3. Compare reconstruction against source using both n‑gram overlap and QA accuracy probes.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Capacity vs. fidelity:* Larger compressors improve training loss but increase effective rank and entropy, harming literal preservation.
  - *Compression ratio vs. information loss:* Higher compression (e.g., 64×) exacerbates both failure modes.
  - *Decoder scale:* Larger decoders may amplify drift; smaller decoders can suppress but not eliminate the paradox.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Knowledge overwriting:* Replacement of source facts with model’s internal knowledge (e.g., “white strawberry” → “honey bee”) despite correct surface fluency.
  - *Semantic drift:* Structural distortions such as role reversal, modifier loss, or relation flip (e.g., “Alice hit Bob” → “Bob hit Alice”) while maintaining topical coherence.
  - *Metric divergence:* High BLEU/ROUGE paired with low QA accuracy indicates superficial reconstruction hiding fidelity degradation.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate scaling paradox:** Train compressors across scales (0.6B–90B) at fixed compression rates (4×, 16×, 64×). Plot BLEU and QA accuracy vs. parameter count to confirm non‑monotonic fidelity.
  2. **Diagnostic QA isolation:** Evaluate on FaithEval/ConflictQA (knowledge overwriting) and custom FineWeb QA (semantic drift). Compare performance curves across scales to isolate failure modes.
  3. **Representation probing:** Compute effective rank (from *Z* singular values) and conditional entropy (from decoder predictions) for each model. Correlate with QA accuracy to verify mechanistic links.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can we modify compression training or architecture to maintain faithfulness as model scale increases? The conclusion highlights that scaling demands "fundamentally different design principles" for faithful compression, but no modifications are tested.
- Are increased effective rank and conditional entropy causal drivers of fidelity loss, or merely correlates? The study is correlational; intervening on these properties while holding scale constant is needed for causal proof.
- Does the Size-Fidelity Paradox manifest in other compression paradigms, such as single-model autoencoders or discrete latent representations? All experiments use two-stage compressor-decoder with continuous memory tokens; other designs remain unevaluated.

## Limitations
- Missing training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, schedule) block full replication; reported results may be sensitive to these choices.
- Diagnostic QA datasets for semantic drift (prompt design, generation, filtering) are underspecified; independent construction could yield different question sets, altering measured drift.
- Decoder fixed to Llama-3-8B; interactions with decoder scale/architecture remain unexplored, potentially confounding the observed paradox.
- Mechanistic evidence is correlational (effective rank, entropy); no causal interventions (e.g., rank regularization) are tested.

## Next Checks
1. **Rank regularization ablation:** Train compressors with explicit low-rank constraints (e.g., orthogonality, bottlenecks) to test if controlling effective rank preserves fidelity at large scales.
2. **Entropy control ablation:** Apply token-level consistency losses or constrained decoding (low temperature, nucleus) to reduce decoder entropy; evaluate whether semantic drift decreases.
3. **Dataset release and cross-check:** Publish the FaithEval/ConflictQA and drift QA benchmarks; conduct independent audits and test on alternative long-context corpora (e.g., GovReport, arXiv).
