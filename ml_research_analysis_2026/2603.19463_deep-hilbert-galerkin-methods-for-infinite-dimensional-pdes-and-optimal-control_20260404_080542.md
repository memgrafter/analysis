# Deep Hilbert--Galerkin Methods for Infinite-Dimensional PDEs and Optimal Control

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19463
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19463
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces Hilbert-Galerkin Neural Operators (HGNOs) with novel universal approximation theorems for functions and derivatives on Hilbert spaces, enabling deep learning solutions for fully nonlinear second-order infinite-dimensional PDEs and HJB optimal control equations.

## Executive Summary
This paper develops deep learning methods for solving fully nonlinear second-order partial differential equations (PDEs) on infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, with a focus on Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equations from optimal control. The core contribution is the Hilbert-Galerkin Neural Operator (HGNO), a neural architecture that represents functions on H by projecting onto a finite set of basis coordinates and processing them with a standard neural network. The authors prove universal approximation theorems demonstrating that HGNOs can approximate any classical solution and its first two Fréchet derivatives in compact-open topologies or weighted Sobolev norms, even when the PDE involves unbounded operators. This justifies solving the PDE by minimizing the L²_μ residual over the whole Hilbert space. Two algorithms are proposed: Deep Hilbert-Galerkin Method (DHGM) for direct residual minimization, and Hilbert Actor‑Critic for optimal control problems, which alternates between updating a critic (value function) and an actor (feedback control). Numerical experiments on Kolmogorov and HJB PDEs linked to heat and Burgers equations achieve mean errors below 0.01 and relative errors around 1%, validating the approach.

## Method Summary
The method uses a coordinate trunk to reduce the infinite-dimensional input to a finite number *d* of basis projections. The unknown function *v(x)* is approximated by *v_{d,θ}(x) = ˜f_{d,θ}(⟨x,e₁⟩,…,⟨x,e_d⟩)*, where {*e_i*} is a fixed orthonormal basis and *˜f_{d,θ}* is a feedforward neural network. Derivatives *Dv* and *D²v* are obtained by automatic differentiation. To evaluate the PDE residual at a point *x*, one uses a higher resolution *N ≫ d* to accurately represent *x* itself, ensuring correct application of unbounded operators and evaluation of nonlinearities. The training loss is the L²_μ norm of the residual, estimated by sampling points from a reference measure *μ* (a Gaussian with suitable covariance). Two gradient schemes are used: DHGM directly minimizes the squared residual, while QHPDE employs a biased gradient that often converges faster for discounted HJB equations. For optimal control, the Hamiltonian contains an infimum over controls. The Hilbert Actor‑Critic algorithm alternates: (i) critic step: fix current control *u*, update *v* to reduce the HJB residual; (ii) actor step: fix *v*, update *u* to reduce the expected Hamiltonian.

## Key Results
- Novel universal approximation theorems for HGNOs enabling dense approximation of functions, their Fréchet derivatives up to second order, and even under action of unbounded operators, in compact‑open topologies or weighted Sobolev norms.
- Theoretical guarantee that minimizing the L²_μ residual of the PDE yields approximate solutions on the whole Hilbert space, with error bounds relating residual to solution error for dissipative PDEs.
- Numerical validation on Kolmogorov and HJB PDEs (heat and Burgers) achieving mean errors < 0.01 and relative errors ~1%; successful application of Hilbert Actor‑Critic to stochastic optimal control problems.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of the approach is underpinned by three main mechanisms.

**Mechanism 1: Density of Derivative‑Informed HGNOs in Infinite‑Dimensional Space.** By truncating the input to the first *d* basis coordinates and using a finite‑dimensional neural net, the HGNO encodes a function on *H*. Universal approximation theorems for finite‑dimensional networks, together with uniform convergence of the cylindrical projections *P_d* on compact subsets, yield density of such representations in C²(H) with the compact‑open topology (Theorem 3.3) and in weighted Sobolev norms (Theorem 3.6). The basis {*e_i*} must lie in the domain of the unbounded operator *L* and satisfy continuity conditions (34)/(36). Evidence: The paper’s Theorems 3.3 and 3.6 establish the theoretical foundation. If *Dv* has singularities not regularized by *P_d* or if *L* is highly oscillatory, the conditions may fail. Moreover, the compact‑open topology on S(H) is essential; convergence in operator norm is generally impossible.

**Mechanism 2: Solving PDEs by Minimizing L²_μ Residual.** Given the density result, the PDE ⟨*L Dv*, *x*⟩ + *F*(*x*,*v*,*Dv*,*D²v*)=0 can be solved by training an HGNO *v_{d,θ}* to minimize the L²_μ norm of the residual *R(x)=F v_{d,θ}(x)*. The gradient (DHGM) or the QHPDE gradient (which incorporates a bias term) are used with samples from a reference measure *μ* that has finite *q*-moments. Theorem 4.1 shows that under the density and continuity assumptions, the residual can be made arbitrarily small. For dissipative PDEs, Theorem 4.5 bounds the solution error by the residual error. Evidence: Numerical results report residuals with mean errors <0.01. The finite‑dimensional deep Galerkin literature [143719, 212919] supports gradient‑based residual minimization. Break conditions include failure of sequential continuity of *F* in the compact‑open topology of S(H), heavy‑tailed *μ* causing high variance, and QHPDE’s reliance on an approximate monotonicity property.

**Mechanism 3: Actor‑Critic Learning of Optimal Feedback Controls.** For optimal control, the HJB equation involves an infimum over controls, whose minimizer defines the optimal feedback Φ(*x*). The Hilbert Actor‑Critic alternates: (i) critic step: fix current actor *u_{d,φ}*, update critic *v_{d,θ}* to satisfy the HJB equation with *u_{d,φ}* substituted; (ii) actor step: fix *v_{d,θ}*, update *u_{d,φ}* to minimize the expected Hamiltonian ∫ *F_cv*(*x*,*v_{d,θ}*,*Dv_{d,θ}*,*D²v_{d,θ}*,*u*) d*μ*. Theorem 5.3 guarantees that if *v*, *Dv*, *D²v* are uniformly continuous on bounded sets and other conditions hold, the induced feedback Φ_{d,θ}(*x*)=argmin_*u* *F_cv*(*x*,*Dv_{d,θ}*,*D²v_{d,θ}*,*u*) converges to the true Φ in L²_μ or uniformly on compacts. Evidence: Experiments on stochastic heat control show that alternating updates lower both residual and expected cost relative to a non‑alternating baseline. The alternating scheme itself lacks a convergence proof; if *F_cv* is not convex in *u* or if the derivatives are poorly approximated, instability may occur.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Universal Approximation Theorems for Infinite‑Dimensional Functions & Derivatives
- **Why needed:** Standard UATs apply only to finite‑dimensional domains and target C⁰ norms. To solve a PDE on *H* we must simultaneously approximate a function *v* and its first two Fréchet derivatives over the entire infinite‑dimensional space, in a topology that accommodates unbounded operators (compact‑open or weighted Sobolev).
- **Quick check:** For *v(x)=∑ (x_i)²* on ℓ², compute *D²v* (identity) and its *d*-th cylindrical projection *D²v_d* (identity on first *d* coordinates, zero elsewhere). Show that ‖*D²v*(x)−*D²v_d*(x)‖ does not converge for *x* with infinite support, but sup_{|x|≤R,|h|≤R'}|⟨(*D²v*−*D²v_d*)h⟩| does converge as *d*→∞.

### Concept: Continuity of the Nonlinear Operator *F* in Weak Topologies
- **Why needed:** The UATs yield convergence of *D²v_{d,θ}* only in the compact‑open topology of S(H). To conclude *F*(...,*D²v_{d,θ})→*F*(...,*D²v*), *F* must be sequentially continuous when S(H) carries that topology. This is a non‑standard, weaker continuity condition that must be verified case‑by‑case.
- **Quick check:** For *F*(*x*,*v*,*p*,*Z*)=⟨*Z x*, *x*⟩ with *Z∈S(H)*, check sequential continuity on a bounded set *K*⊂*H*: if *Z_n→Z* uniformly on compacts (i.e., sup_{h∈K'}|(*Z_n*−*Z*)h|→0 for all compacts *K'*), then ⟨*Z_n x*, *x*⟩→⟨*Z x*, *x*⟩ uniformly for *x*∈*K*.

### Concept: Unbounded Operators and Cylindrical Approximations
- **Why needed:** The PDE term ⟨*L Dv*, *x*⟩ involves an unbounded *L*. Even if *Dv_{d,θ}→Dv* uniformly on compacts, we need ⟨*L*(*Dv_{d,θ}−*Dv*), *x*⟩→0 uniformly (or in L²_μ). This is nontrivial because ‖(*I*−*P_d*)*x*‖ may not decay fast enough for *x* in the domain of *L*^*. The basis {*e_i*} must satisfy *e_i*∈*D*(*L*) and regularity conditions on *Dv* (e.g., entering a Sobolev space) are required.
- **Quick check:** Let *L*=∂/∂ξ on L²([0,2π]) with periodic BCs, basis *e_n*(ξ)=sin(*n*ξ). For *v*(*x*)=∑ *x_n²*/n³ with *x_n*=⟨*x*,*e_n*⟩, we have *Dv*(*x*)=∑ 2*x_n*/n³ *e_n*. Compute ⟨*L*(*Dv*−*Dv_d*), *x*⟩ = ∑_{n>d} 2*x_n²*/n³. This converges to zero uniformly on compacts if the *x_n* decay sufficiently fast. For convergence in L²_μ, we need *μ* to have moments decaying faster than 1/*n³.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input *x*∈*H* → Encoder *E_d(x)* = (⟨*x*,*e_i⟩_{i=1}^d*) → Bottleneck neural net *˜f_{d,θ}* → Output (scalar for *v*, or vector *p*∈ℝ^*p* for control) → (Optional) Decoder *Ê(p)* = ∑ *p_j g_j* to embed into *U*.

### Critical Path
1. **Design**: Choose orthonormal basis {*e_i*} of *H* that aligns with *L* (e.g., eigenbasis of *L*^*L*). Set hyperparameters *d* and network architecture (layers, width, activation).  
2. **Sample Generation**: Draw a batch of points {*x*^(j)} from reference measure *μ*. For each *x*^(j), compute its first *N ≫ d* basis coefficients ⟨*x*^(j),*e_i*⟩ for *i*=1..*N*. The remaining coefficients are effectively zero‑padded in the decoder when needed.  
3. **Residual & Derivatives**: For each *x*^(j) (represented by its *N*-dimensional truncated coefficients):
   - Compute *v* = *˜f*(*E_d(x*^(j)*)*.
   - Compute *p* = ∇_*y* *˜f*(*E_d(x*^(j)*)*) ∈ ℝ^*d*, then *Dv(x*^(j)*)* = ∑_{i=1}^d *p_i e_i*.
   - Compute *Z* = ∇²_*y* *˜f*(*E_d(x*^(j)*)*) ∈ ℝ^{d×d}, then *D²v(x*^(j)*)h* = ∑_{i,j=1}^d *Z_{ij} h_j e_i* for any *h*.
   - Evaluate PDE term ⟨*L Dv*, *x*⟩ using full *x*: ⟨∑ *p_i L e_i*, *x*^(j)*⟩ = ∑ *p_i* ⟨*L e_i*, *x*^(j)*⟩ (precomputed *L e_i*).
   - Assemble residual *F*(*x*^(j)*,*v*,*Dv*,*D²v*).
4. **Loss & Gradient**:
   - **DHGM**: Loss = (1/*M*) ∑_*j* (*F v_{d,θ}(x*^(j)*)*)². Compute ∇_*θ* Loss via autograd.
   - **QHPDE**: Loss = (1/*M*) ∑_*j* (*F v_{d,θ}(x*^(j)*)*)(−*v_{d,θ}(x*^(j)*)*). Compute ∇_*θ* Loss.
5. **Parameter Update**: Use ADAM optimizer with learning rate schedules *α_t* (and *β_t* for actor). Iterate until convergence. For Hilbert Actor‑Critic, alternate between critic updates (using current actor *u*) and actor updates (using current critic *v*).

### Design Tradeoffs
- ***d* vs *N***: *d* controls expressivity of derivatives; *N* (evaluation resolution) should be ≫ *d* to accurately represent *x* and *L* actions while keeping network size manageable. Typical values: *d*≈25–100, *N*≈250–500.
- **Basis Choice**: Must satisfy *e_i*∈*D*(*L*) and ideally diagonalize *L*; otherwise *L e_i* may have large norms, causing gradient explosion. For elliptic operators, eigenbases of *A*^*A* are natural.
- **Network Depth vs Width**: The UATs assume a single hidden layer with sufficient width, but practical implementations use moderate depth (2–4 layers) for better optimization. Deeper nets risk vanishing gradients.
- **DHGM vs QHPDE**: DHGM is general but may converge slowly. QHPDE incorporates a bias (−*v*) that exploits monotonicity in discounted HJB equations, often accelerating convergence but requiring careful learning rate tuning.

### Failure Signatures
1. **Actor‑Critic Divergence**: Critic loss or actor objective grows after alternating updates. Remedy: reduce actor learning rate *β_t*, increase critic updates per actor step.
2. **Poor Hessian Approximation**: Even if |*v*−*v*_analytic| and |*Dv*−*Dv*_analytic| are small, residual remains large. Indicates *D²v_{d,θ}* not captured well; increase *d* or adjust basis.
3. **Gradient Explosion**: Very large ∇_*θ* Loss. Check ‖*L e_i*‖; if growing with *i*, choose a better‑aligned basis or scale down inputs.
4. **Measure Mismatch**: Heavy‑tailed *μ* leads to high variance in loss; consider truncating tails or using a lighter‑tailed measure that still covers relevant dynamics.
5. **Unsatisfied Assumptions**: If *F* lacks sequential continuity in the compact‑open topology of S(H) (e.g., *F*(*x*,*p*,*Z*)=‖*Z x*‖), the residual may not vanish. Reexamine PDE formulation or topology.

Monitor critic residual, expected cost ∫ *J*(*x*;*u*) d*μ*, and actor gradient norm. Expect B to achieve lower residual and cost than C, and more stable convergence than A.

## Limitations
- Infinite‑dimensional sampling requires truncating to *N* basis elements; the impact of this truncation error on convergence is not quantified theoretically or numerically.
- Architectural details for the Burgers equation’s LSTM‑like network are incomplete (e.g., exact GRU connectivity, initialization), requiring reverse‑engineering of reference [114].
- The alternating Hilbert Actor‑Critic scheme lacks a convergence proof; only a single‑step approximation error is established.
- The treatment of boundary conditions for domains other than the whole Hilbert space (e.g., [0,2π]) is not discussed; the theory assumes *O*=*H*.

## Confidence
- UATs and residual minimization theory: Medium (proofs rely on compact‑open topologies and restrictive assumptions; convergence not in operator norm).
- Actor‑critic control approximation: Low‑Medium (Theorem 5.3 guarantees a good actor for a fixed critic, but alternating updates are unanalyzed).
- Numerical results: Medium (reported errors are plausible but not independently verified; implementation details for Burgers are vague).
