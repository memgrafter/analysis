# Generalizable Hyperparameter Optimization for Federated Learning on Non-IID Cancer Images

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.12664  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12664  
- **Reference count:** 5  
- **Primary result:** Combined hyperparameter configuration (averaging learning rates, modal optimizer and batch size) achieved highest mean F1-score (0.909) across six CNN architectures in non-IID federated learning on ovarian and colon cancer histopathology images.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies hyperparameter transferability in federated learning (FL) for non-IID cancer histopathology images. Using ovarian and colon cancer datasets, the authors first obtain dataset-specific optima via centralized Bayesian hyperparameter optimization (TPE). They then propose a cross-dataset aggregation heuristic: average the learning rates and take the mode (most frequent value) for optimizer and batch size, with a validation-score tie-breaker. In a non-IID FL simulation with FedAvg and six CNN architectures, the combined configuration surpasses both dataset-specific optima in mean F1-score. The results indicate that this simple heuristic yields a robust, generalizable hyperparameter strategy for heterogeneous federated cancer image analysis, particularly benefiting lighter models.

## Method Summary
The methodology comprises two stages. First, Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) is applied centrally on each cancer dataset to identify optimal hyperparameters (learning rate, optimizer, batch size) for a given model architecture, producing λ⋆_ov and λ⋆_co. Second, a combination heuristic constructs a unified configuration ¯λ by averaging the two learning rates (¯η = (η_ov+η_co)/2) and selecting the mode of the optimizer and batch size (with a validation F1 tie-breaker). The authors then simulate a non-IID FL scenario using FedAvg, where clients hold heterogeneous partitions of the two datasets. They train six diverse CNNs (AlexNet, ResNet variants, SqueezeNet, EfficientNet-B0) using the three hyperparameter sets (ovary-optimized, colon-optimized, combined) and evaluate global model performance on a held-out test set, comparing mean F1-scores across architectures.

## Key Results
- The combined configuration ¯λ achieved a mean F1-score of 0.909 across all six CNN architectures, exceeding ovary-optimized (0.899) and colon-optimized (0.900).
- Deeper architectures (ResNet50, ResNet34) performed best with ovary-optimized hyperparameters, while lighter models (ResNet18, EfficientNet-B0) gained the most from the combined approach.
- The heuristic demonstrates that cross-dataset hyperparameter aggregation can provide a robust, generalizable configuration for heterogeneous FL, avoiding overfitting to a single dataset’s distribution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Averaging Learning Rates
- **Claim:** Averaging the learning rates from dataset-specific optima produces a robust, generalizable step size for heterogeneous FL clients.
- **Mechanism:** Learning rate controls update magnitude; averaging (¯η = (η_ov+η_co)/2) mitigates the risk of selecting a rate too aggressive or too conservative for the mixed non-IID loss landscape, seeking a middle ground suitable for both datasets.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal learning rates for the two cancer tasks lie on a continuum where their arithmetic mean is a viable compromise for the combined non-IID scenario.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.5 (Eq. 7) defines ¯η as the mean; the abstract and results show the combined config achieves highest mean F1.
- **Break condition:** If the two optimal rates differ by orders of magnitude (e.g., 1e-5 vs 1e-3), the mean may be ineffective for both, causing divergence or stagnation.

### Mechanism 2: Mode Selection for Categorical Hyperparameters
- **Claim:** Selecting the mode (most frequent value) for optimizer type and batch size captures a common, stable configuration choice across tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Optimizer (Adam vs. SGD) and batch size are categorical with non-linear effects; the mode selects the choice preferred by at least one dataset, assuming that if both datasets independently choose the same option, it is inherently more robust to distribution shifts. Ties are broken by higher centralized validation F1.
- **Core assumption:** The categorical hyperparameters that perform best on individual homogeneous datasets are indicative of stable choices when those datasets are mixed in non-IID FL.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.5 (Eq. 8) defines ¯o = mode{o_ov, o_co} and ¯b = mode{b_ov, b_co}. Section 4.1 shows optimizer preferences vary (colon uses Adam exclusively; ovary uses Adam except for SqueezeNet).
- **Break condition:** If the two optima always choose different optimizers and batch sizes, the tie-breaker (validation score) may not reliably predict federated performance.

### Mechanism 3: Regularization via Configuration Averaging
- **Claim:** The combined configuration acts as a regularizer against model-specific overfitting to a single dataset’s distribution, leading to higher mean performance across diverse architectures in non-IID FL.
- **Mechanism:** The heuristic synthesizes a configuration not optimally tuned for any single (dataset, model) pair, but one that avoids pitfalls of a config highly specialized for one data distribution (e.g., ovary-optimized LR may be too low for ResNet18 on mixed data). This “averaged” config provides more conservative, general learning dynamics that benefit models with varying capacities and inductive biases.
- **Core assumption:** A configuration that is not the best for any specific (dataset, model) pair can outperform pair-specific optima when evaluated across a distribution of models in a federated, non-IID setting.
- **Evidence anchors:** The combined config achieved the highest mean F1 (0.909). Per-model breakdown (Section 4.2, Table 5 & Figure 3) shows combined wins for ResNet18 and EfficientNet-B0; ovary-optimized wins for deeper ResNets (50, 34). Mean benefit is driven by improvement on lighter models.
- **Break condition:** If client partitions become extremely heterogeneous (e.g., some clients have only ovarian, others only colon data), the combined config may be suboptimal compared to client-specific or dataset-specific tuning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bayesian Hyperparameter Optimization (TPE)**  
  *Why needed:* To efficiently find high-quality dataset-specific hyperparameter optima (λ⋆_ov, λ⋆_co) within a limited budget; random or grid search would be less sample-efficient.  
  *Quick check:* How does TPE’s modeling of “good” vs. “bad” hyperparameter densities differ from random search or grid search in efficiency for small-budget HPO?

- **Non-IID Federated Learning (FedAvg)**  
  *Why needed:* The core problem is that FL performance is sensitive to hyperparameters under non-IID data; the experiment simulates statistical heterogeneity by partitioning two datasets into clients with varying sample sizes and label proportions.  
  *Quick check:* Why does non-IID data (statistical heterogeneity) make hyperparameter tuning more critical and transfer more difficult in FL compared to IID data?

- **Model Architecture Variability as a Stress Test**  
  *Why needed:* Evaluating six CNNs with different depths/widths tests the generalizability of the hyperparameter heuristic; if a strategy works across architectures, it is more likely robust.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a hyperparameter configuration optimized for ResNet-50 fail for EfficientNet-B0 or AlexNet in the same FL task, even with the same data?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Centralized HPO Module (TPE) → λ⋆_ov, λ⋆_co → Combination Heuristic (average LR, mode optimizer/batch) → ¯λ → FL Simulation Server (Flower, FedAvg) → Client Workers (local training) → Evaluation Aggregator (test metrics).

**Critical path:** Run TPE on ovarian dataset to obtain λ⋆_ov; run TPE on colon dataset to obtain λ⋆_co; apply combination heuristic to produce ¯λ; conduct FL simulation with FedAvg using each configuration (λ⋆_ov, λ⋆_co, ¯λ) across six CNN architectures; compare mean F1-scores on held-out test set.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Simplicity vs. Adaptivity:* The heuristic is transparent and requires no extra training, but is static and does not adapt to client-specific non-IID patterns (e.g., a client with only ovarian data).  
- *Generality vs. Optimality:* The combined config sacrifices peak performance on specific model/dataset pairs (e.g., ResNet50) for higher mean performance across diverse architectures, trading robustness for best-case accuracy.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. All three schemes (ovary, colon, combined) achieve similar mean F1, indicating the heuristic provides no benefit and hyperparameter sensitivity is low.  
2. The combined config is consistently the worst across all models, disproving the main claim.  
3. High variance in per-model performance under the combined config (e.g., works for some models, catastrophically fails for others) suggests averaging disrupts model-specific needs.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **TPE Convergence Validation:** Increase TPE evaluation budget (more trials) on both datasets to ensure λ⋆_ov and λ⋆_co are stable optima, not noise.  
2. **Non-IID Partition Sensitivity:** Vary the degree of non-IIDness (label skew, quantity skew) in the FL simulation to determine at what point the combined config’s advantage diminishes.  
3. **Architecture Ablation:** Test the combined config on a wider range of architectures (e.g., Vision Transformers) to assess if the benefit generalizes beyond CNNs.

## Limitations
- Limited to only ovarian and colon cancer histopathology datasets; generalizability to other cancer types or imaging protocols is unknown.
- FL simulations use synthetically created non-IID partitions from two datasets; real-world hospital heterogeneity (scanner variations, staining differences) may invalidate the heuristic.
- The heuristic’s mode-based tie-breaker for optimizer and batch size lacks theoretical justification and was not validated against alternative aggregation rules.

## Confidence
- Combined config achieves higher mean F1-score across architectures: **Medium** (backed by experiments but limited to 6 CNNs and 2 datasets).
- Averaging learning rates yields a robust step size for heterogeneous FL: **Low** (mechanism plausible but no ablation on learning-rate distribution gaps; break condition untested).
- Heuristic generalizes beyond tested architectures: **Low** (no evaluation on vision transformers or non-CNN models).

## Next Checks
1. Extend dataset diversity: Replicate the study with ≥3 additional cancer histopathology datasets (e.g., breast, lung) and include multi-institution external validation.
2. Stress-test non-IID extremes: Simulate FL with pathological client skew (e.g., some clients exclusively ovarian, others colon) and quantify performance decay of combined config versus client-specific tuning.
3. TPE stability sweep: Increase TPE evaluation budget (2×–5× more trials) on both datasets to confirm dataset-specific optima are stable.
