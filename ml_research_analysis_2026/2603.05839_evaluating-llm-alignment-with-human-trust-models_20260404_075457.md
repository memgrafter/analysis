# Evaluating LLM Alignment With Human Trust Models

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.05839
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05839
*   **Reference Count:** 4
*   **Primary Result:** GPT-J-6B's internal trust representation aligns most strongly with the Castelfranchi socio-cognitive model (average cosine similarity 0.7303).

## Executive Summary
This study probes how the GPT-J-6B language model represents the abstract concept of trust internally. The authors generate latent vectors for "trust" and 45 related concepts (e.g., risk, benevolence, honesty) using a contrastive prompting technique. These vectors are then compared against the structural relationships defined in five established human trust models (Castelfranchi, Marsh, Mayer, etc.) via cosine similarity. A significance threshold of 0.6 is established using 60 general emotional concepts. The LLM's trust geometry shows the highest average alignment with the Castelfranchi model, while exhibiting negative similarity scores for key concepts like "risk" and "benevolence" within Mayer's model, indicating a potential structural divergence from that particular human framework.

## Method Summary
The investigation employs a probing methodology based on contrastive prompting. For each target concept (trust and 45 related terms), the model is prompted to generate a vector representation in its embedding space. The cosine similarity between the "trust" vector and each related concept's vector is computed. These similarity scores are then correlated with the expected relationships (positive/negative, strength) specified by each of the five human trust theories. Alignment is quantified as the average similarity for concepts predicted to be positively associated within a given model. A baseline significance threshold of 0.6 is derived from the average maximum similarity between 60 unrelated emotional concepts, above which a relationship is considered meaningfully represented.

## Key Results
*   The LLM's trust representation aligned best with the **Castelfranchi socio-cognitive model** (average similarity 0.7303), with eight concept similarities exceeding the 0.6 threshold.
*   Alignment was second-highest with the **Marsh model** of trust.
*   Several theoretically central associations in **Mayer's model** (notably "risk" and "benevolence") exhibited **negative cosine similarity**, suggesting a fundamental mismatch with that model's structure.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide a detailed explanatory mechanism for why the LLM's latent geometry aligns with certain human trust models. The observed alignment is presented as an empirical measurement of representational similarity, not as an outcome of a designed process. The underlying cause—whether it stems from the model's training data distribution, architectural inductive biases, or the specifics of the probing method—is not investigated.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Contrastive Prompting**
    *   **Why Needed:** To extract stable, directional vectors for abstract concepts (like trust) from a frozen LLM without fine-tuning.
    *   **Quick Check:** Consistency of generated vectors across different prompt paraphrases for the same concept.
*   **Concept: Castelfranchi Socio-Cognitive Trust Model**
    *   **Why Needed:** Serves as one of the benchmark human theories against which LLM representation is compared.
    *   **Quick Check:** Model predicts positive associations between trust and concepts like "reliability," "confidence."
*   **Concept: Cosine Similarity in Embedding Space**
    *   **Why Needed:** The primary metric for quantifying geometric alignment between concept vectors.
    *   **Quick Check:** Similarity score ranges from -1 (opposite) to 1 (identical).
*   **Concept: Significance Threshold Derivation**
    *   **Why Needed:** To distinguish meaningful conceptual relationships from random noise or spurious correlations.
    *   **Quick Check:** Threshold (0.6) is the average maximum similarity among 60 conceptually unrelated emotional terms.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Prompt Template Library -> LLM (GPT-J-6B) -> Embedding Extraction -> Vector Arithmetic -> Similarity Calculator -> Model-Theory Comparator

**Critical Path:** The fidelity of the final alignment scores depends critically on the **Prompt Template Library**. Poorly constructed or inconsistent prompts will yield noisy concept vectors, corrupting all downstream measurements.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
*   **Threshold Choice:** A higher threshold reduces false positives but may miss subtle relationships; the chosen 0.6 uses emotional concepts as a baseline, which may not be ideal for abstract social constructs.
*   **Model Specificity:** Results are tied to GPT-J-6B. Larger, instruction-tuned models may represent concepts differently due to alignment training.
*   **Linear Assumption:** Using cosine similarity assumes the relationships between concepts are linearly mapped in the embedding space, which may not hold.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  Negative or near-zero similarity for core theoretical concepts (as seen with Mayer's "risk").
2.  Low reproducibility of concept vectors across prompt variations.
3.  No clear peak alignment with any single trusted human model, indicating a poor fit overall.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Prompt Robustness Test:** Generate vectors for "trust" using 20 semantically diverse prompt templates (e.g., "The concept of trust means...", "Trust is...", "Can you define trust?") and measure intra-concept vector variance.
2.  **Baseline Concept Audit:** Replace the emotional concept threshold baseline with a set of 60 physically unrelated nouns (e.g., "table," "orbit," "quartz") and recompute the significance threshold to see if key results persist.
3.  **Nearest Neighbor Sanity Check:** For each target concept vector, extract its top-10 nearest neighbors in the embedding vocabulary. Manually inspect if these neighbors are semantically coherent and trust-related, validating the probe's semantic capture.

## Limitations
*   **Threshold Arbitrariness:** The 0.6 significance threshold was derived from 60 *general emotional concepts*, not a theoretically grounded set of non-trust baseline concepts, potentially weakening the definition of meaningful alignment.
*   **Limited Scope:** Findings are confined to a single, older model (GPT-J-6B) and one specific probing technique (contrastive prompting), with unknown generalizability to modern LLMs or other methods.
*   **Interpretation of Negative Similarity:** Negative scores for key theoretical concepts (e.g., risk in Mayer's model) are methodologically puzzling and could indicate probing flaws or fundamental representational divergence.

## Confidence
*   **Claim:** "LLM trust representation aligns with Castelfranchi model" → **Label:** Medium. Clear statistical result, but dependent on a single model and a potentially fragile threshold.
*   **Claim:** "Divergence from Mayer's model structure" → **Label:** Medium. Negative similarities are a strong signal, but the root cause (representation vs. probing error) is uncertain.
*   **Claim:** "Results provide generalizable insights about LLM trust" → **Label:** Low. Based on one model and method; broad claims are unsupported.

## Next Checks
1.  **Multi-Model Replication:** Repeat the exact probing pipeline on at least three modern, instruction-tuned LLMs (e.g., Llama 3 70B, GPT-4, Claude 3) to test if the Castelfranchi alignment pattern holds.
2.  **Threshold Methodology Audit:** Re-derive the significance threshold using a large, curated set of *conceptually unrelated* baseline concepts (e.g., "table," "justice," "velocity") and assess robustness of results to this change.
3.  **Alternative Probing Validation:** Employ a different, causal probing method (e.g., activation patching or linear probes on diverse trust-related texts) to verify if the same concept relationships are detectable independently of contrastive prompting.
