# Diffusion Reinforcement Learning via Centered Reward Distillation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.14128
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14128
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CRD achieves 0.93 GenEval (vs. 0.63 baseline) and 0.92 OCR accuracy, improving aesthetic scores and human preference metrics while converging faster and exhibiting reduced reward hacking compared to forward-process baselines.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models fine-tuned with reinforcement learning (RL) often suffer from distribution drift and reward hacking due to an intractable prompt-dependent normalizer in the KL-regularized objective. This paper introduces Centered Reward Distillation (CRD), which cancels this normalizer by centering rewards within each prompt group, converting an ill-posed absolute reward matching problem into a well-posed difference-matching problem. CRD stabilizes training through three practical innovations: (1) decoupling the sampler from a moving reference to prevent the log-density ratio signal from collapsing, (2) anchoring the KL penalty to a CFG-guided version of the pretrained model to control long-run drift and align with inference semantics, and (3) using a reward-adaptive KL strength. On text-to-image tasks, CRD achieves state-of-the-art results (GenEval 0.93, OCR 0.92) with faster convergence and less reward hacking than prior forward-process RL methods.

## Method Summary
CRD formulates diffusion RL as a KL-regularized optimization with an external reward. The core loss matches centered external rewards to centered implicit rewards estimated via an ELBO-based difference between the current model and a moving reference. Centering per prompt cancels the unknown log normalizer. The sampler (used to generate samples) and moving reference are maintained as separate exponential moving averages (EMAs) to keep a meaningful learning signal: a fast EMA for the sampler stays on-policy, while a slow EMA for the reference prevents ratio collapse. Additionally, a KL penalty anchors the model to a fixed, CFG-guided version of the pretrained distribution, with its coefficient scaled adaptively by the raw reward. Training uses LoRA adapters, group size K=24, β_old=1.0, β_init=0.1, CFG scale 4.5 for the anchor, and EMA decay rates (e.g., η_samp=0.999, η_old=0.9). The algorithm alternates between sampling, reward computation, implicit reward estimation, centering, loss computation, parameter updates, and EMA updates.

## Key Results
- GenEval score: 0.93 (vs. 0.63 baseline)
- OCR accuracy: 0.92
- Improved aesthetic scores and human preference metrics, faster convergence, and reduced reward hacking.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Within-Prompt Reward Centering Resolves the Intractable Normalizer
- **Claim:** Centering rewards within each prompt group cancels the unknown, prompt-dependent normalizer βlogZ(c), turning an ill-posed absolute reward-matching problem into a well-posed matching of *differences*.
- **Mechanism:** The KL-regularized optimum implies r(c,x) = R_θ*(c,x) + βlogZ(c). For samples {x_i} from the same prompt c, subtracting a weighted group mean (Eq. 5) removes βlogZ(c). The CRD loss (Eq. 7) then matches the *centered external reward* Δ_i^r,w to the *centered implicit model reward* Δ_i^bR,w (estimated via diffusion ELBO).
- **Core assumption:** The reward model's normalizer is constant across samples from the same prompt c. This is mathematically derived from the exponential tilt form of the KL-regularized optimum.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...canceling the intractable prompt-dependent normalizer via within-prompt reward centering, yielding a well-posed reward-matching objective."
  - [section 3.2]: Derives Eq. (5) showing the normalizer cancels under centering and defines Δ_i^r,w and Δ_i^bR,w.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors discuss "diversity collapse" and "reward hacking," which centering aims to mitigate by providing a stable learning signal, but do not directly evidence the centering derivation.
- **Break condition:** If samples within a group are not drawn from the same prompt c, the normalizer does not cancel and the objective remains ill-posed.

### Mechanism 2: Decoupled Sampling Prevents Ratio-Signal Collapse
- **Claim:** Maintaining separate EMA models for sampling (p_samp) and as the moving reference (p_old) prevents the log-density ratio signal from collapsing to zero when p_old drifts too close to the current model p_θ.
- **Mechanism:** The implicit reward estimator bR_θ depends on the ratio between p_θ and p_old. If p_old is updated too aggressively (high η_old), it becomes similar to p_θ, making the log-ratio vanish and gradients become uninformative. Decoupling (Fig. 2) lets p_samp (with faster EMA η_samp) stay on-policy for data collection, while p_old (with slower EMA η_old) provides a stable reference for the ratio calculation.
- **Core assumption:** Gradient informativeness in forward-process RL is proportional to the divergence between p_θ and p_old. This is implied by the form of the ELBO-based estimator (Eq. 4).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "decoupling the sampler from the moving reference to prevent ratio-signal collapse."
  - [section 3.3]: "If p_old is updated too aggressively, it can drift too close to the current model p_θ, collapsing the log-ratio signal... We therefore further decouple sampling..."
  - [corpus]: Neighbor "Beyond the Dirac Delta" discusses diversity collapse in RL fine-tuning, which this mechanism addresses by maintaining a meaningful reference distribution.
- **Break condition:** If η_old is not sufficiently slower than η_samp, or if updates are too frequent, p_old may still collapse toward p_θ, killing the signal.

### Mechanism 3: CFG-Anchored KL Regularization Controls Long-Run Drift
- **Claim:** Adding a KL penalty that anchors p_θ to the *CFG-guided* version of the *initial* pretrained model (p_CFG_ϕ) prevents reward hacking by controlling distribution shift and aligning the regularization target with inference-time semantics.
- **Mechanism:** Online RL with a moving reference accumulates reward tilt (Appendix E), leading to over-concentration on reward-hacked modes. A fixed reference anchor (Eq. 9) provides a constant pull toward the base model's manifold. Using the CFG-guided version (rather than the pure conditional model) matches the typically stronger, more aligned distribution used at inference, avoiding a mismatch that would pull the model toward lower-quality regions.
- **Core assumption:** The CFG-guided pretrained distribution p_CFG_ϕ is a better proxy for "desired" semantics at inference time than the unconditional-CFG model p_ϕ. The adaptive scaling (Eq. 10) that ties KL strength to raw reward r_raw is effective.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "KL anchoring to a CFG-guided pretrained model to control long-run drift and align with the inference-time semantics of the pre-trained model."
  - [section 3.3]: Explains the problem with anchoring to non-CFG p_ϕ and introduces p_CFG_ϕ as the reference. Presents reward-adaptive KL strength (Eq. 10).
  - [corpus]: Direct evidence is weak. Neighbors focus on reward hacking and diversity but do not specifically address CFG anchoring. The assumption about CFG-guided anchor quality is paper-specific.
- **Break condition:** If the CFG scale used for p_CFG_ϕ is mis-specified (too high or too low) relative to inference, the anchor may be misaligned. If adaptive scaling (Eq. 10) is poorly calibrated (e.g., r_raw not in [0,1]), it may over- or under-regularize.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Forward-Process vs. Backward-Process RL for Diffusion**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper positions CRD within the forward-process family (vs. Flow-GRPO). Understanding this dichotomy is key to grasping CRD's efficiency advantage and its specific drift challenges (moving reference).
  - *Quick check question:* What is the primary computational trade-off when using a forward-process objective compared to a backward-process (trajectory-based) one?
- **Concept: ELBO as a Surrogate for Intractable Log-Density Ratios**
  - *Why needed here:* The core estimator bR_θ (Eq. 4) is an ELBO-based difference that approximates the log-ratio β log(p_θ/p_old). This surrogate makes the otherwise intractable ratio accessible for training.
  - *Quick check question:* Why can't we directly compute the exact log-density ratio p_θ(x|c)/p_old(x|c) for a diffusion model, and what property of the ELBO allows its use as a *difference* estimator?
- **Concept: Accumulated Tilting and Reward Hacking**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's motivation for the fixed KL anchor stems from the theoretical insight (Appendix E) that repeated online KL-regularized updates with a moving reference compound the reward inverse temperature, causing distribution collapse onto reward-model loopholes.
  - *Quick check question:* In the idealized recursion (Eq. 25), what happens to the effective inverse temperature β_eff as the number of online update epochs K increases, and what is the practical consequence?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  **Sampler Model (p_samp):** EMA of current θ (fast rate η_samp). Generates clean samples {x_i}.
  2.  **Moving Reference (p_old):** EMA of current θ (slow rate η_old). Fixed during an epoch. Provides the baseline velocity v_old in the ELBO difference (Eq. 4).
  3.  **Fixed CFG Reference (p_CFG_ϕ):** The initial pretrained model ϕ with Classifier-Free Guidance applied. Provides v_CFG_ϕ for the anchoring KL loss (Eq. 11).
  4.  **Reward Model (r_ψ):** External, black-box scorer (GenEval, OCR). Provides r(c, x_i).
  5.  **Training Model (p_θ):** The trainable copy of ϕ. Its velocity field v_θ is optimized to minimize L_CRD_KL (Eq. 12).
- **Critical path (per training iteration, Algorithm 1):**
  1.  Sample prompts c and random noise.
  2.  Generate group {x_i} using ODE solver with p_samp.
  3.  Compute external rewards r(c, x_i), apply group normalization.
  4.  For each (x_i, t, ϵ), compute x_t and estimate bR_θ(c, x_i) using v_θ and v_old (Eq. 4).
  5.  Compute centered terms Δ_i^r,w and Δ_i^bR,w (Eq. 6).
  6.  Compute L_CRD (Eq. 7).
  7.  Compute per-sample adaptive KL coefficient ˆβ_init(c, x_i) = r_raw(c, x_i) * β_init.
  8.  Compute L_KL using v_θ and v_CFG_ϕ (Eq. 11).
  9.  Update θ with gradient of L_CRD_KL.
  10. Update p_samp (η_samp) and p_old (η_old) via EMA.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **EMA rates (η_samp vs. η_old):** Faster η_samp keeps samples on-policy; slower η_old preserves a meaningful ratio signal. Too fast η_old causes signal collapse.
  - **CFG scale for anchor:** Higher CFG yields a sharper, more aligned reference but risks oversaturation. The paper uses 4.5 (Table 3).
  - **Adaptive KL:** Reward-dependent ˆβ_init accelerates learning on low-reward samples but could under-regularize them early on. The linear scaling assumes r_raw ∈ [0,1].
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Ratio-signal collapse:** KL divergence between p_θ and p_old becomes very small (<0.01 nats) early in training; gradients vanish.
  - **Reward hacking / visual collapse:** High reward scores on task metric (GenEval/OCR) but severe degradation on held-out preference metrics (ImageReward, aesthetics). Visually: uniform backgrounds, text artifacts, mode collapse (e.g., all zebras brown).
  - **Anchoring failure:** KL divergence to p_CFG_ϕ spikes or plateaus at a high value, indicating the model is being pulled too strongly or is incompatible with the anchor.
- **First 3 experiments (to validate setup):**
  1.  **Ablation: Old-model decay rate (η_old).** Replicate Fig. 4a: compare fast (0.999) vs. slow (0.9) decay. Expect fast decay to show initial speed but high final KL and potential reward hacking; slow decay to be more stable.
  2.  **Ablation: Initial KL strength (β_init).** Replicate Fig. 4b: try β_init=0.0001, 0.05 with CFG=1.0 and 3.0. Expect very low β_init to converge faster but risk hacking; higher β_init to be slower but produce better aesthetics. Observe the trade-off in evaluation reward curves and sample quality.
  3.  **Ablation: Adaptive KL (ˆβ_init).** Replicate Fig. 4c/7: compare fixed β_init=0.05 vs. adaptive. Expect adaptive to have similar final reward but faster early progress and potentially better late-stage stability (lower KL).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Provide convergence and stability guarantees for CRD under its practical approximations (ELBO surrogates, moving references, finite-sample centering).
- **Basis in paper:** Appendix A notes that “a more rigorous theoretical analysis remains open including convergence and stability guarantees under the practical approximations such as ELBO-based log-ratio surrogates, moving references, and finite-sample within-prompt centering.”
- **Why unresolved:** The approach is currently justified empirically; without formal guarantees, understanding failure modes, hyperparameter sensitivity, and long-term behavior remains limited.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Mathematical proofs deriving convergence rates and conditions (e.g., step sizes, KL coefficients), supported by experiments that validate these conditions across diverse tasks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can CRD overcome the coverage limitations of the pretrained reference model, which may rarely generate high-reward samples, to prevent diversity collapse?
- **Basis in paper:** Appendix A states that “CRD is fundamentally constrained by the coverage… if the base model rarely produces trajectories that exhibit a desired behavior, the reward signal provides little leverage to bootstrap it… A natural mitigation is to inject real data… mixing RL updates with SFT or adding a data-anchoring regularizer.”
- **Why unresolved:** The suggested mitigations are untested; it is unclear how effective they are, how to balance data mixing, and whether they preserve both reward gains and diversity.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation of CRD with added curated data or SFT objectives, measuring both reward improvement and diversity metrics (e.g., concept coverage, intra-set variance), plus theoretical analysis of the regularizers’ effect on the stationary distribution.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can CRD be efficiently scaled to high-resolution video diffusion models without prohibitive computational overhead from full sampling during training?
- **Basis in paper:** Appendix A mentions that “although CRD is substantially more efficient than Flow-GRPO variants, it still incurs non-trivial computational overhead… for video diffusion models… Future work could reduce training costs through integration with faster samplers or diffusion distillation techniques.”
- **Why unresolved:** The experiments are limited to image generation; video generation is qualitatively more expensive, and it is unknown whether the proposed efficiency improvements can retain reward optimization quality.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementation of CRD on a video diffusion benchmark, comparing training time and video quality with and without amortization strategies (e.g., faster ODE solvers, knowledge distillation), and analyzing the trade-offs between computational cost and reward gains.

## Limitations
- **Reward model ambiguity:** Exact implementations and normalization of GenEval and OCR reward models are unspecified, potentially affecting reproducibility and performance.
- **Sensitive hyperparameters:** CFG scale for the anchoring reference, adaptive KL scaling, and group size K require careful tuning; deviations from paper settings may weaken theoretical guarantees or cause failure.
- **Coverage limitation:** CRD is constrained by the coverage of the pretrained model; rare high-reward concepts may not emerge without additional data injection.
