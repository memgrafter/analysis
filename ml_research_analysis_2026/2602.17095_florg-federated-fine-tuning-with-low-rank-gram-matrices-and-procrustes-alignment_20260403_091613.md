# FLoRG: Federated Fine-tuning with Low-rank Gram Matrices and Procrustes Alignment
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17095
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17095
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Outperforms five federated fine-tuning baselines by up to 1.52% (MNLI) and 1.13% (WNLI) on GLUE while reducing communication by up to 2041×.

## Executive Summary
Federated fine-tuning with LoRA suffers from aggregation error when separately averaging the two low-rank matrices and from decomposition drift when recovering matrices from their product. FLoRG addresses these by replacing the LoRA product with a single low-rank matrix whose Gram matrix is linearly averaged on the server, eliminating aggregation error and cutting communication. After decomposition, Procrustes alignment reparameterizes the matrix to match the previous round’s subspace, minimizing drift. This yields improved downstream task accuracy and a tighter theoretical convergence bound.

## Method Summary
FLoRG reparameterizes the LoRA update ΔW = BA as ΔW = L Aᵀ A R, where L and R are fixed semi-orthogonal matrices. Clients compute local updates only on A. The server aggregates the Gram matrices Q = Aᵀ A from all clients via a linear average. It then decomposes the aggregated Q into a canonical low-rank matrix Ã = Λ^(1/2) P. To mitigate decomposition drift, the server applies Procrustes alignment, solving for the orthogonal matrix S that minimizes ||SÃ - A^t||_F, yielding A^(t+1) = S* Ã, which is broadcast back to clients. This process linearizes aggregation and stabilizes the subspace across rounds.

## Key Results
- Achieves higher accuracy on GLUE benchmarks (e.g., MNLI, WNLI) compared to five baselines, with gains of up to 1.52%.
- Reduces communication overhead by up to 2041× by transmitting only one matrix per client instead of two.
- Theoretical analysis confirms a tighter convergence bound, with a drift term eliminated by Procrustes alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
FLoRG replaces the separate aggregation of LoRA's B and A matrices with the linear aggregation of the Gram matrix Q = Aᵀ A. This directly approximates the true average update (1/N)∑ B_n A_n, avoiding the bias from (∑ B_n / N)(∑ A_n / N). The linear operation preserves the positive semi-definite structure and cuts communication by half. The core assumption is that ΔW = L Aᵀ A R with shared semi-orthogonal L, R can effectively approximate fine-tuning updates. Evidence includes the derivation of the aggregation formula and related works that highlight the separate-aggregation error. A break condition occurs if Q becomes numerically rank-deficient (near-zero eigenvalues), making decomposition unstable.

### Mechanism 2
Procrustes alignment selects the decomposition of the aggregated Gram matrix that is closest (in Frobenius norm) to the previous round's A matrix. After eigendecomposition Q^(t+1) = Pᵀ Λ P, the canonical decomposition Ã^(t+1) = Λ^(1/2) P is non-unique. FLoRG solves min_{S: SᵀS=I} ||S Ã^(t+1) - A^t||_F, with closed-form solution S* = UVᵀ from the SVD of A^t (Ã^(t+1))ᵀ. This stabilizes the gradient direction for the next round. The assumption is that subspace drift can be minimized by this alignment. Evidence is the theoretical formulation and the ablation showing performance drops without alignment. Alignment may fail if the minimum singular value of Ã^(t+1)(A^t)ᵀ is very small, causing numerical instability.

### Mechanism 3
The combination yields a tighter convergence bound for non-convex federated fine-tuning. The analysis models global update error and shows that Procrustes alignment controls the drift term Δ_proc^(t+1), which vanishes when S_t = S*_t. The bound also accounts for residual bias from the single-matrix approximation. Assumptions include L-smooth loss, bounded gradients, and bounded matrices. Evidence is Theorem 2's bound and empirical outperformance of baselines. The guarantee weakens under extreme non-IID data (e.g., Dirichlet ρ → 0) if bounded gradients are violated or alignment cannot compensate for divergent local updates.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Client local update (A_n^(t+1/2)) → Server aggregates Gram (Q^(t+1) = (1/N)∑ (A_n^(t+1/2))ᵀ A_n^(t+1/2)) → Server decomposes (Ã^(t+1) = Λ^(1/2) P) → Server aligns (S*_t from SVD of A^t (Ã^(t+1))ᵀ) → Server broadcasts A^(t+1) = S*_t Ã^(t+1) → Clients update with new A^(t+1)
- **Critical path:** The alignment step is crucial for stabilizing the next round’s gradients by minimizing drift between consecutive A matrices.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Communication vs. compute:* Transmission is halved (one r×k matrix vs. two), but server adds O(k³) eigendecomposition and O(k r' r) Procrustes alignment.
  - *Expressivity vs. stability:* Single-matrix form may limit representational flexibility; alignment trades optimality for convergence stability.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - Rank collapse in Q^(t+1): many near-zero eigenvalues cause unstable decomposition/alignment.
  - Misaligned subspaces: alignment failure leads to sudden accuracy drop after a round.
  - High heterogeneity: extreme non-IID (ρ ≪ 1) makes aggregated Gram unrepresentative, slowing convergence.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline comparison on GLUE:** Run FLoRG vs. FedIT, FeDeRA, etc., on OPT-125M/RoBERTa with r=4, ρ=0.5; expect FLoRG higher accuracy, especially on MNLI/WNLI.
  2. **Ablation of Procrustes alignment:** Disable alignment (use canonical Ã^(t+1) directly); expect significant drop (e.g., 3-5% on MRPC/QQP).
  3. **Rank sensitivity study:** Vary r ∈ {2,4,8}; expect FLoRG’s relative improvement to persist or increase with higher rank.

## Limitations
- Single-matrix reparameterization may reduce expressiveness compared to independent LoRA matrices.
- Procrustes alignment can become numerically unstable if the aggregated Gram matrix is near rank-deficient.
- Theoretical convergence bound relies on standard assumptions (L-smoothness, bounded gradients, heterogeneity) that may not hold under extreme non-IID data.
- Experiments are limited to medium-scale models (≤125M parameters); scalability to larger foundation models is unverified.

## Confidence
- **Aggregation error elimination & communication reduction:** High. Mathematically sound linear aggregation; communication savings empirically confirmed.
- **Procrustes alignment drift minimization:** Medium. Strong theoretical derivation and ablation evidence, but confidence drops under ill-conditioned matrices.
- **Overall convergence and accuracy gains:** Medium. Consistent GLUE improvements, but theoretical guarantees are conditional on assumptions that may not always hold.
