# Riemannian Motion Generation: A Unified Framework for Human Motion Representation and Generation via Riemannian Flow Matching

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15016
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15016
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art text-to-motion generation, with FID 0.043 on HumanML3D (best) and FID 5.6 / R@1 0.86 on MotionMillion.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Riemannian Motion Generation (RMG), a unified framework that factorizes human motion into a compact product manifold—global translation (ℝ³) and per-joint unit-quaternion rotations ((S³)ʲ)—to respect the intrinsic non-Euclidean geometry of valid motions. Instead of using redundant Euclidean representations, RMG learns generative dynamics via Riemannian flow matching, employing geodesic interpolation, tangent-space vector field supervision, and manifold-preserving ODE integration. The approach yields high-fidelity, scalable motion generation, setting new records on HumanML3D and MotionMillion while demonstrating superior stability under classifier-free guidance. Ablations confirm that the minimal translation-plus-rotation (T+R) representation is both sufficient and geometrically optimal.

## Method Summary
RMG represents a motion sequence as a path on a product manifold M = ℝ³ × (S³)ʲ, where each frame consists of a root translation and unit quaternions for global orientation and joint rotations. A prior distribution (wrapped Gaussian around a rest pose) is transformed to the data distribution via a learned continuous normalizing flow. Training uses Riemannian flow matching: conditional paths are constructed along geodesics (xt = Exp_x₀(t Log_x₀(x₁))), and the network predicts a velocity vector that is projected onto the tangent space at xt (Π_TₓₜM v_θ). The loss supervises this projected vector to match the geodesic tangent (vt = (1/(1-t)) Logₓₜ(x₁)). During sampling, an ODE solver integrates dxt/dt = Π_TₓₜM v_θ(xt, t) using manifold-preserving Euler steps (xt+h = Exp_xt(h Π_TₓₜM v_θ(xt, t))). The text condition is fused via a large language model encoder (Qwen3) and injected into a Diffusion Transformer backbone that processes manifold-valued tokens.

## Key Results
- On HumanML3D, RMG achieves an FID of 0.043, ranking first on all metrics under the MotionStreamer format.
- On MotionMillion (1M motions), it surpasses baselines with an FID of 5.6 and R@1 of 0.86.
- Ablations show the compact T+R representation yields the lowest FID and most stable performance across guidance scales, outperforming variants that include pre-shape (P) or temporal differences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Representing motion on a product manifold (translation on ℝ³, rotations on (S³)ʲ) removes redundant degrees of freedom present in Euclidean encodings, leading to a scale-free, intrinsically normalized representation that improves optimization stability and model performance.
- **Mechanism:** The paper factorizes an articulated motion frame into a minimal set of geometrically natural components: global translation (ℝ³), global orientation, and per-joint rotations (unit quaternions on S³). This avoids the high-dimensional, correlated ambient space (e.g., concatenating 6D rotations or joint positions) that lies *around* the true motion manifold. By operating directly on the manifold M = ℝ³ × (S³)ʲ, the model’s predictions are guaranteed to be valid (e.g., unit norm quaternions) without post-hoc correction, and the loss landscape is free of flat directions introduced by redundant coordinates (Proposition 1).
- **Core assumption:** Valid human motions lie on or near a lower-dimensional manifold whose intrinsic geometry is captured by the product of translation and rotation groups. Euclidean coordinates introduce artificial complexity that harms learning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "factorizes motion into several manifold factors, yielding a scale-free representation with intrinsic normalization... RMG achieves state-of-the-art results."
  - [section 3.1] "We adopt this decomposition but cast each factor on its natural Riemannian manifold... The rotation component lies on the product manifold (S³)ʲ." Table 1 shows RMG has the lowest ambient dimension (4J+3 vs. 12J+12 in DART).
  - [corpus] Weak direct corpus evidence; neighboring papers (e.g., DisCoRD, Riemannian MeanFlow) explore continuity/discretization trade-offs but not the specific T+R factorization. The claim is primarily anchored in the paper’s own theory and ablation.
- **Break condition:** If a strong Euclidean baseline (e.g., using 6D rotations with re-orthogonalization) matched RMG’s performance across all guidance scales and datasets, the necessity of the manifold representation would be undermined.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Learning the generative dynamics via Riemannian flow matching (using geodesic interpolation, tangent-space vector field supervision, and manifold-preserving ODE integration) ensures that the learned probability path respects the underlying geometry, resulting in more stable training and higher-fidelity samples compared to Euclidean flow matching or diffusion.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of linear interpolation in ℝᴰ, RMG constructs conditional paths along geodesics on M. The training target velocity is the geodesic tangent (vt = (1/(1-t)) Logₓₜ(x₁) ∈ TₓₜM), and the neural network’s output is projected onto the tangent space (Π_{TₓₜM} vθ). During inference, ODE integration uses the Riemannian exponential map (x_{t+h} = Expₓₜ(h Π_{TₓₜM} vθ)) to stay exactly on M. This geometry-aware pipeline prevents the model from learning trajectories that leave the valid motion manifold, which in Euclidean space would require implicit regularization or post-processing.
- **Core assumption:** The true data distribution p_data is supported on M, and the most efficient way to transport a prior to p_data is along geodesics of M. Disregarding this structure introduces modeling error.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "learns dynamics via Riemannian flow matching... using geodesic interpolation, tangent-space supervision, and manifold-preserving ODE integration."
  - [section 3.3] Equation (4) shows the loss with projection; Equation (5) shows the ODE. "This preserves manifold constraints by construction."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Riemannian Flow Matching Policy for Robot Motion Learning" (arxiv:2403.10672) apply similar principles to robot policies, providing external validation of the approach on other manifold-valued problems.
- **Break condition:** If sampling from the learned ODE (5) consistently produced invalid motions (e.g., non-unit quaternions) despite the projection step, or if Euclidean flow matching with heavy regularization (e.g., projection loss) matched RMG’s stability and FID.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The compact T+R representation (omitting pose coordinate and temporal difference factors) is sufficient for high-quality, guidance-stable motion generation, as evidenced by superior and more robust FID scores across guidance scales compared to representations including P (pre-shape) or d· (temporal differences).
- **Mechanism:** The ablation (Fig. 3) shows T+R yields the lowest FID and best stability under varying classifier-free guidance scales. Adding P (T+R+P) degrades performance unless converted back via rotations ("Recovered by R"), suggesting pose coordinates introduce noise or misalignment. Temporal differences (dT+R, T+dR) attenuate absolute state and harm long-horizon modeling, leading to worse FID at higher guidance. The mechanism is that translation and rotation fully determine articulated kinematics; other factors are either redundant (P is determined by T+R in a kinematic chain) or destabilizing (d· emphasizes local variations over global structure).
- **Core assumption:** For text-to-motion generation at moderate horizon lengths (~10s), the full articulated state is sufficiently specified by global translation and joint rotations. Additional factors like per-frame joint positions or velocities are either derivable or detrimental.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] "T+R is consistently the best and most stable setting... T+P degrades monotonically as guidance increases... T+R+P... recovering with R is consistently better than recovering with P... temporal differencing does not improve performance."
  - [section 3.1] Theoretical justification: "global translation (T), global orientation, and joint rotations (R) are sufficient to capture articulated motion, which are verified through empirical studies and theoretical analysis (Section B)."
  - [corpus] Limited; neighboring works like UniHM incorporate object interactions which might necessitate P, but RMG’s scope is pure text-to-motion without scene context.
- **Break condition:** If a representation including P (with proper manifold handling) consistently outperformed T+R across all guidance scales and metrics, especially on tasks requiring precise joint positioning (e.g., contact modeling), the sufficiency of T+R would be falsified.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Riemannian Manifolds (Hypersphere S³, Product Manifolds)
  - **Why needed here:** The entire representation (unit quaternions) and flow matching (geodesics, tangent spaces, exponential/log maps) rely on manifold calculus. Without understanding curved spaces, the “why” of using quaternions and geodesic interpolation is opaque.
  - **Quick check question:** On S³, what is the geodesic distance between two antipodal points, and why must the interpolation formula handle the zero denominator?

- **Concept:** Flow Matching on Manifolds
  - **Why needed here:** RMG’s training objective and ODE integration are direct applications of Riemannian flow matching. Understanding the conditional velocity field (vt) and its supervision via log maps is central to the method.
  - **Quick check question:** In Euclidean flow matching, vt = (x₁ - x₀). What is the manifold analogue, and why does it reduce to the Euclidean form on ℝⁿ?

- **Concept:** Unit Quaternions and Rotation Representation
  - **Why needed here:** The rotation factor uses (S³)ʲ. Knowing that q and -q represent the same rotation, and that S³ is a double cover of SO(3), explains the need for the upper hemisphere restriction and the advantage over 6D continuous rotations (no redundancy, smooth geodesics).
  - **Quick check question:** Why are unit quaternions preferable to Euler angles or 6D vectors for geodesic interpolation on the rotation manifold?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Text Encoder -> Riemannian Representation Head -> Time-Conditioning -> Vector Field Network -> Manifold Integration -> Conversion Module

- **Critical path:**
  Text → Encoded features → Fused with time → Transformer processes manifold-valued tokens → Tangent-space velocity prediction → Exponential map integration → Convert to output format.
  *Key invariant:* All motion states (xt) must always lie exactly on M; any deviation indicates a bug in the Exp/Log or projection step.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Compactness vs. Expressivity:** T+R is most stable but discards explicit pose parameters. If downstream tasks need precise joint positions (e.g., interaction), P may be needed but requires careful conversion (Fig. 6) and may harm guidance stability.
  - **Guidance Scale:** Higher ω improves text alignment (R@1) but can degrade fidelity (FID). T+R shows the most graceful degradation (Fig. 3a).
  - **Model Scale:** RMG scales to 0.5B flow model + 1.7B LLM on MotionMillion, suggesting the representation’s efficiency allows larger text encoders without proportionally increasing motion network size.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Training instability/loss spikes:** Check gradient norms (Fig. 5). Likely cause: incorrect tangent space projection or Log/Exp implementation.
  - **Poor FID at high guidance (ω > 8):** Overfitting to conditioning, possibly from insufficient data augmentation or too strong guidance. Switch to T+R if using T+R+P.
  - **Invalid quaternions in samples:** The Exp map or projection step is broken; outputs must be unit norm. Use ‖q‖₂ as a diagnostic.
  - **Low diversity (Div) or MModality:** Prior distribution µ (rest pose) may be too narrow; consider increasing prior variance Σ in RN(µ, Σ).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation on Representation:** Train T+R, T+P, T+R+P (with both conversion variants) for 50k steps on HumanML3D. Sweep ω ∈ [2.5, 9.5]. Expect T+R to show lowest FID and flattest FID vs. ω curve. Validate that converted samples are valid (no joint dissociation).
  2. **Manifold Integrity Check:** After every 10k training steps, sample 1000 xt from the model (t~U[0,1]) and compute: (a) min quaternion norm (should be ~1.0), (b) max distance from manifold (e.g., ‖q‖²-1). Should be ~0. If not, debug Exp/Log.
  3. **Guidance Scaling on MotionMillion:** Train RMG-base (Table 7) on MotionMillion subset (e.g., 100k clips). Evaluate at ω=2.0, 3.0, 4.0. Expect FID to be competitive (≈10) and R@1 to increase with ω but with diminishing returns and possible FID degradation beyond ω=3.5.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can the RMG framework's compact T+R representation be formally proven sufficient for all articulated motion modeling tasks, or are there specific motion types where the omitted pose/differential factors are theoretically necessary?
- How does Riemannian Flow Matching scale in terms of computational complexity and sample efficiency compared to Euclidean diffusion when applied to very high-dimensional product manifolds (e.g., for hand/face modeling with hundreds of joints)?
- Can the Riemannian representation and training pipeline be extended to conditional generation modalities beyond text (e.g., audio-driven co-speech gestures, video-to-motion, scene constraints) without compromising geometric benefits?
- What are the failure modes or instability conditions of Riemannian flow matching on highly curved submanifolds (e.g., S³ for quaternions) when using large step sizes in the ODE integrator, and how do they compare to Euclidean diffusion instabilities?

## Limitations
- Inverse mapping from RMG’s T+R representation to HumanML3D/MotionStreamer formats is unspecified, risking evaluation inconsistencies.
- No direct ablation isolating Riemannian flow matching; claimed geometry benefits may be entangled with other architectural choices.
- Incomplete hyperparameter details (EMA decay, dropout, exact text preprocessing) hinder exact replication.
