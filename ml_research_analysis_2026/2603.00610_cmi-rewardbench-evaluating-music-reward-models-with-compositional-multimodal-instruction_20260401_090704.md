# CMI-RewardBench: Evaluating Music Reward Models with Compositional Multimodal Instruction
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.00610
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00610
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CMI-RM achieves 78.2% preference accuracy on CMI-Pref, surpassing Gemini 3 Pro (65.8%) and SongEval-RM (72.4%), and reaches 82.4% on text+lyrics+audio conditions.

## Executive Summary
Music generation models increasingly support complex multimodal inputs (text, lyrics, audio prompts), yet their evaluation remains fragmented across disparate datasets and tasks. This paper introduces a unified ecosystem: CMI-Pref-Pseudo (110k pseudo-labeled preference pairs) and CMI-Pref (4,027 expert-annotated pairs) for compositional multimodal instruction alignment; CMI-RewardBench, which consolidates prior benchmarks (PAM, MusicEval, Music Arena) with CMI-Pref to evaluate reward models across five tasks (musicality, text-music alignment, lyric-music alignment, audio-music alignment, full compositional instruction preference); and CMI-RM, a specialized reward model (~30M parameters) using a two-tower architecture with frozen MuQ-MuLan encoders and a two-stage training regime (pseudo-label pre-training with label smoothing, fine-tuning on human preferences). CMI-RM sets a new state-of-the-art on CMI-Pref (78.2% accuracy) and demonstrates strong generalization, while best-of-N reranking with it improves objective metrics for existing music generators. The work establishes a comprehensive framework for benchmarking and improving music reward models in the multimodal era.

## Method Summary
CMI-RM employs a two-tower architecture: frozen MuQ-MuLan encoders process each input modality (text, lyrics, reference audio, evaluation audio). The encoded prompt components (text, lyrics, reference audio) are concatenated and passed through a small Prompt Transformer (4 layers) to fuse multimodal prompt information. The resulting representation is combined with the evaluation audio embedding via a Joint Transformer (1 layer), and a shared MLP outputs two scalar scores: musicality and alignment. Training proceeds in two stages: first, pre-training on 110k pseudo-labeled pairs from CMI-Pref-Pseudo using the Bradley–Terry loss with label smoothing (ε=0.2) to establish a robust prior; second, fine-tuning on the high-quality CMI-Pref dataset (4,027 pairs) and MusicEval, combining pairwise (Bradley–Terry) and scalar (MSE with tanh) losses. The CMI-RewardBench benchmark unifies four existing datasets (PAM, MusicEval, Music Arena, CMI-Pref) under consistent evaluation protocols for five distinct tasks, enabling comprehensive assessment of reward models across diverse multimodal conditions.

## Key Results
- CMI-RM achieves 78.2% preference accuracy on CMI-Pref, surpassing Gemini 3 Pro (65.8%) and SongEval-RM (72.4%).
- On the challenging text+lyrics+audio condition, CMI-RM reaches 82.4% accuracy.
- Best-of-N reranking with CMI-RM yields consistent improvements in objective metrics (e.g., MuQ-MuLan similarity) for MusicGen-small and Stable-Audio-Open-small.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Pre-training on large-scale pseudo-labeled preference data with label smoothing yields a robust prior that enhances fine-tuning on limited human annotations and improves cross-benchmark generalization.
**Mechanism:** The model is first trained on 110k pseudo-labeled pairs from CMI-Pref-Pseudo using the Bradley–Terry loss with label smoothing (ε=0.2) to mitigate overconfidence from noisy pseudo-labels. This establishes an initial preference representation. Then, fine-tuning on 4k high-quality human pairs (CMI-Pref) and MusicEval refines the model. Label smoothing prevents the model from becoming overly confident on incorrect boundaries in the pseudo-data.
**Core assumption:** Pseudo-labels, despite noise, contain a useful signal that can be harnessed with regularization; label smoothing effectively reduces overfitting to spurious patterns.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "trained in two stages: pseudo-label pre-training with label smoothing, then fine-tuning on human preferences."
- [section 3.3.2] "To mitigate over-confident decision boundaries induced by noisy pseudo labels, we apply label smoothing with a ratio of 0.2 during this stage."
- [corpus] Ablation (Section 4.2, Table 4) shows Distill+CMI-Pref outperforms Distill only and Scratch+Both.
**Break condition:** If pseudo-label noise is too high or structurally biased, label smoothing may dilute correct signal. If human data distribution is extremely mismatched, the prior may harm fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** A frozen two-tower encoder design with small trainable transformers efficiently handles compositional multimodal conditions and achieves strong performance with limited training data.
**Mechanism:** The architecture uses frozen MuQ-MuLan encoders for each modality (text, lyrics, audio prompt, evaluation audio). The prompt components are concatenated and processed by a Prompt Transformer (4 layers) to fuse multimodal prompt information. The evaluation audio embedding is combined with the fused prompt via a Joint Transformer (1 layer). An MLP yields musicality and alignment scores. Only the transformers and MLP are trained (~30M parameters), while the large MuQ-MuLan encoders remain frozen.
**Core assumption:** Pre-trained MuQ-MuLan encoders provide domain-appropriate representations for music and text, and the small trainable components suffice to learn reward-specific interactions.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "CMI-RM employs a two-tower architecture with frozen MuQ-MuLan encoders, a prompt transformer, and a joint transformer."
- [section 3.3.1] Detailed diagram and equations.
- [section 4.1] CMI-RM (30M) outperforms much larger MLLMs (Gemini 3 Pro) on CMI-Pref.
**Break condition:** If frozen encoders are not well-aligned with the reward task, performance may plateau. Limited capacity of small transformers might restrict modeling complex interactions.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Unified evaluation across diverse datasets and tasks via CMI-RewardBench reveals that even strong MLLMs fail to reach high agreement with human experts on compositional multimodal music evaluation, highlighting the need for specialized reward models.
**Mechanism:** CMI-RewardBench integrates PAM, MusicEval, Music Arena, and CMI-Pref into a single benchmark covering five tasks: (1) absolute musicality scoring, (2) text-music alignment, (3) lyric-music alignment, (4) audio-music alignment, and (5) full compositional instruction preference. Models are evaluated via correlation (LCC, SRCC) or accuracy. This composite framework tests generalization across modalities and instruction complexities.
**Core assumption:** Performance across this heterogeneous suite is a robust indicator of a reward model's real-world utility for music generation alignment.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "CMI-RewardBench unifies prior datasets ... to evaluate reward models on five tasks including musicality, text-music alignment, and full compositional instructions."
- [section 3.2] Defines evaluation protocols and baseline models.
- [section 4.1] Frontier MLLMs achieve only ~65-78% on CMI-Pref, while CMI-RM reaches 78.2%, demonstrating a gap.
**Break condition:** If benchmark tasks are not truly representative or if there is leakage between training and test sets, conclusions may be inflated. Dataset-specific biases might not generalize.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Input modalities (text t, lyrics l, reference audio a_ref, evaluation audio a_eval) are encoded by frozen MuQ-MuLan encoders: text encoder for t and l, audio encoder for a_ref and a_eval. The encoded prompt components (E_t, E_l, E_a_ref) are concatenated and processed by a Prompt Transformer (4 layers) to produce h_prompt. h_prompt is concatenated with E_a_eval and fed to a Joint Transformer (1 layer) to produce joint representation h_eval. Temporal pooling over h_eval yields a pooled vector, which is passed to an MLP that outputs two scalars: s_MUS (musicality) and s_ALI (alignment). Only the Prompt Transformer, Joint Transformer, and MLP are trained (~30M parameters); MuQ-MuLan encoders remain frozen.

### Critical path
1. **Data collection:** Generate audio from diverse models/APIs for multimodal prompts; collect human annotations on CMI-Pref; generate pseudo-labels on CMI-Pref-Pseudo via Qwen3-Omni with position consistency.
2. **Pre-training:** Train model on pseudo-labeled pairs with label smoothing for ~2k steps.
3. **Fine-tuning:** Combine CMI-Pref train split and MusicEval train split; train for early-stopped ~250 steps, using both pairwise (Bradley–Terry) and scalar (MSE with tanh) losses.
4. **Evaluation:** Test on CMI-RewardBench (PAM, MusicEval, Music Arena, CMI-Pref test) for musicality and alignment tasks. Optionally use for best-of-N reranking.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Frozen encoders vs. full fine-tuning:** Frozen MuQ-MuLan reduces trainable parameters and training cost, but may limit adaptation to music reward specifics. Evidence: CMI-RM still outperforms larger MLLMs, suggesting encoders generalize well.
- **Pseudo-label scale vs. quality:** Using 110k pseudo samples provides a strong prior but introduces noise. Label smoothing helps, but there is a tradeoff; ablations show diminishing returns beyond 64k pseudo samples.
- **Two-stage training:** Pre-training on pseudo-data then fine-tuning on human data yields better generalization than training only on human data. But requires careful hyperparameters (e.g., smoothing, steps).
- **Multi-head vs. single-head:** Predicting two separate scores (MUS and ALI) allows disentanglement but increases complexity; they share most backbone and are trained jointly.

### Failure signatures
- **Poor performance on audio-only or lyric-only conditions:** Degradation on certain modalities (e.g., lyrics) may indicate the frozen text encoder does not process lyric structures well.
- **Overfitting to pseudo-labels:** If validation loss on real human data increases during pre-training (without smoothing), the model becomes overconfident. Use label smoothing and early stopping.
- **Temporal drift on Music Arena:** Results degrade on later months, indicating lack of robustness to distribution shifts. Monitor performance over time.
- **High confidence but low agreement:** If model confidence scores do not correlate with human agreement, the model may be miscalibrated.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline evaluation on CMI-RewardBench:** Run the trained CMI-RM on all five tasks (PAM musicality & alignment, MusicEval musicality, Music Arena preference, CMI-Pref musicality & alignment) and compare against baselines (PAM, audiobox, SongEval, Gemini, Qwen). Confirm state-of-the-art on CMI-Pref (78.2%) and competitive on others.
2. **Ablation study on training data:** Compare variants: Distill only, Distill+CMI-Pref, Distill+MusicEval, Distill+Both, Scratch+Both. Measure aggregated scores (mean SRCC/Acc) across PAM, MusicEval, Music Arena, CMI-Pref. Expect Distill+Both to be optimal.
3. **Test-time scaling:** Use CMI-RM to rerank candidates for MusicGen-small and Stable-Audio-Open-small with N=1,3,10. Evaluate improvement on objective metrics (MuQ-MuLan, AudioBox, SongEval). Expect monotonic gains for MusicGen-small.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can music reward models reliably incorporate long-range temporal structure and text–music relationships for full-length compositions?** The paper notes that current CMI-RM uses fixed windows or simple averaging, and accuracy varies with duration, suggesting incomplete capture of musical development.
2. **How can lyrical content be effectively integrated into reward models without performance degradation?** The paper observes a 4% drop in "Text + Lyrics w/o Audio" and attributes it to the frozen MuQ-MuLan text encoder struggling with raw lyric structures without acoustic grounding.
3. **Does context-aware musicality evaluation primarily reflect genuine human cognitive anchoring or exploitation of spurious correlations?** The paper shows gains from adding context but cannot disentangle whether these gains reproduce human bias or exploit deterministic links between prompt and generation distribution.
4. **How can music reward models achieve robustness against temporal distribution shifts?** Results on Music Arena show performance decay over months, indicating sensitivity to evolving generation quality and user preferences.

## Limitations
- Pseudo-label noise from Qwen3-Omni may bias learning; label smoothing partially mitigates but noise structure and impact are not fully quantified.
- CMI-Pref's 4k expert-annotated pairs may not cover global music diversity; annotator backgrounds are undisclosed, risking sample bias.
- CMI-RewardBench merges datasets with different protocols (pairwise, scalar); potential scale mismatches and hidden train/test leakage could inflate results.
- Frozen MuQ-MuLan encoders might lack reward-specific features; small transformer capacity may limit modeling of complex cross-modal interactions.
- Performance degradation on later Music Arena months indicates sensitivity to distribution shifts, limiting real-world robustness.
- No public code or detailed training logs, hindering independent reproduction.

## Confidence
- Pseudo-label pre-training with label smoothing yields a robust prior: **High**
- Frozen two-tower architecture efficiently handles multimodal inputs: **High**
- CMI-RewardBench reveals a gap between MLLMs and specialized reward models: **Medium**
- Best-of-N reranking with CMI-RM improves generation metrics: **High**

## Next Checks
1. **Noise sensitivity analysis:** Systematically corrupt a clean validation set of preferences to measure performance degradation with increasing label noise; estimate upper bound if pseudo-labels were perfect.
2. **Cross-domain zero-shot test:** Evaluate CMI-RM on music genres or cultural styles absent from training (e.g., traditional non-Western music) to probe generalization limits.
3. **Encoder unfreezing ablation:** Fine-tune the MuQ-MuLan encoders on the reward task and compare performance, training stability, and computational cost against the frozen baseline.
