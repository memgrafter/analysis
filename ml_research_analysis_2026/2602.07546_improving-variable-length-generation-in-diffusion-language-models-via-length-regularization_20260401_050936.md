# Improving Variable-Length Generation in Diffusion Language Models via Length Regularization

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.07546
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07546
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LR-DLLM achieves 51.3% Pass@1 on HumanEval-Infilling (+13.4% vs DreamOn) and 51.5% average Pass@1 on McEval (+14.3%) via a training-free length regularization framework.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion language models (DLLMs) exhibit an intrinsic bias: as the length of a masked span increases, average confidence scores decay logarithmically, independent of semantic fit. This makes variable-length generation unreliable when the target span is unknown, often causing under-generation or redundant outputs. The proposed LR-DLLM framework corrects this bias by introducing a length regularization term \(k \log L\) that decouples semantic compatibility from length-induced uncertainty. It employs a two-stage, training-free inference process: exponential probing to estimate the regularization coefficient \(k\) and select an initial mask length, followed by greedy bidirectional refinement that dynamically adjusts the span during generation. On code and math infilling benchmarks, LR-DLLM substantially outperforms prior methods, with ablations confirming the necessity of both the regularization and the refinement stage.

## Method Summary
LR-DLLM addresses the length-bias problem by modeling the decay in average confidence \(\overline{S}(L)\) as approximately \(a \log L + b\) due to increasing predictive entropy with mask length \(L\). It subtracts an estimated term \(k \log L\) from the raw confidence to produce a length-regularized score \(C_L(L)\) that better reflects semantic fit. The coefficient \(k\) is inferred in Stage I by probing geometric lengths (e.g., 1, 2, 4, ...) and performing linear regression. The length \(L_{\text{rem}}\) that maximizes \(C_L(L)\) is chosen as a starting point. In Stage II, the algorithm iteratively evaluates neighboring lengths (\(L_{\text{rem}} \pm 1\)) and moves to a neighbor if it yields a higher \(C_L\); otherwise, it commits the leftmost token and updates the context. This bidirectional refinement continues until all masked positions are filled or a budget is exhausted, requiring no model retraining.

## Key Results
- **HumanEval-Infilling:** LR-DLLM attains 51.3% Pass@1, a +13.4% absolute improvement over DreamOn.
- **McEval ( multilingual math):** Achieves 51.5% average Pass@1 across four languages (+14.3%).
- **Ablations:** Removing the \(\log L\) regularization or disabling the bidirectional refinement stage both cause significant performance drops, confirming their necessity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1:**  
**Claim:** DLLMs suffer an intrinsic bias where average confidence decays logarithmically with mask length \(L\) due to increasing predictive entropy.  
**Mechanism:** The number of admissible token sequences grows roughly as \(L!\) under a power-law assumption for choice growth with distance, leading entropy \(S \propto \log L\).  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions "intrinsic length-induced bias"; Appendix E derives \(\overline{S}(L) \approx a \log L + b\); supporting corpus paper "Diffusion LLM with Native Variable Generation Lengths" also addresses fixed-length issues.  
**Break condition:** If entropy growth is super-logarithmic or context constraints dominate at large \(L\), the \(\log L\) model fails.

**Mechanism 2:**  
**Claim:** Subtracting \(k \log L\) from raw confidence yields a length-regularized signal \(C_L(L)\) that isolates semantic compatibility for cross-length comparison.  
**Mechanism:** The term removes the bias component \(R(L)\) from \(\text{AVGconf}(L)\), leaving \(S(L)\) as a fit proxy; \(k\) is estimated via linear regression on exponentially probed lengths.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes "explicit length regularization"; Section 3.2 defines \(C_L(L) = \text{AVGconf}(L) - k \log L\); corpus paper "Beyond Fixed: Training-Free Variable-Length Denoising" uses confidence-based length selection.  
**Break condition:** Poor \(k\) estimation (e.g., insufficient probing lengths or rapidly varying \(S(L)\)) leaves residual bias.

**Mechanism 3:**  
**Claim:** A two-stage process (exponential probing → greedy bidirectional refinement) dynamically determines the correct length without retraining.  
**Mechanism:** Stage I probes geometric lengths to fit \(k\) and pick initial \(L_{\text{rem}}\); Stage II greedily adjusts \(L_{\text{rem}}\) by local moves and commits tokens when stable.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] outlines both stages; Algorithms 1 and 2 detail the procedures; corpus paper "\(ρ\)-EOS: Training-free Bidirectional Variable-Length Control" uses similar bidirectional adjustment.  
**Break condition:** Noisy or multimodal \(C_L(L)\) may cause Stage II oscillations or suboptimal convergence; sparse probing may miss global optimum in Stage I.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
DLLM backbone -> Confidence estimator -> Length regularizer -> Probing module -> Refinement module

**Critical path:**  
Input context \((p, s)\) → Stage I: probe geometric lengths \(\{1, 2, 4, \dots\}\) → fit \(k\) and select \(L_{\text{rem}} = \arg\max C_L(L)\) → Stage II loop: evaluate \(C_L\) for \(\{L_{\text{rem}}-1, L_{\text{rem}}, L_{\text{rem}}+1\}\) → if neighbor improves, move \(L_{\text{rem}}\); else commit leftmost token and update \(p, L_{\text{rem}}\) → repeat until \(L_{\text{rem}}=0\) or budget exceeded.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Training-free vs. overhead: Avoids retraining but adds \(O(\log L_{\text{max}} + N H)\) forward passes (probing + refinement).
- Bidirectional adjustment vs. simplicity: Supports expansion and contraction, increasing robustness over one-sided methods.
- Probing budget vs. \(k\) accuracy: More probe lengths improve \(k\) estimation but increase initial cost.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Poor \(k\) fit: Stage I estimate drifts if probe lengths are insufficient or bias deviates from \(\log L\).
- Oscillating \(L_{\text{rem}}\): Stage II may loop if \(C_L\) is flat/noisy around current length.
- Stalled commitment: Algorithm may get stuck if no neighbor improves but \(L_{\text{rem}} > 0\) (boundary handling mitigates).
- Inefficient tail: Long sequences incur high refinement cost if local optima shift rapidly.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Reproduce bias validation:** Plot \(\text{AVGconf}(L)\) vs. \(\log L\) for diverse contexts to confirm logarithmic trend.
2. **Ablate log L regularization:** Run LR-DLLM with \(k=0\) on HumanEval-Infilling and McEval; measure Pass@1 drop.
3. **Test Stage II necessity:** Disable local refinement (use only Stage I's \(L_{\text{rem}}\) for full generation) and compare to full LR-DLLM.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Is \(\log L\) the optimal regularization function, or could alternatives (e.g., polynomial) improve calibration?
- Can the instance-specific coefficient \(k\) be predicted from context features without probing, reducing inference overhead?
- Does greedy bidirectional refinement converge to globally optimal lengths, or could beam search yield better performance?
- How does LR-DLLM generalize to non-code, non-math text generation where length desiderata differ?
- Does the length-induced bias and LR-DLLM's overhead scale with model size, and can overhead be made sublinear for very large contexts?

## Limitations
- Entropy model assumes independent, power-law token choice growth; real DLLMs may violate this, yielding inaccurate bias characterization.
- Fitting \(k\) from few probe lengths risks error if the bias deviates from \(\log L\) or semantic fit \(S(L)\) varies rapidly.
- Experiments are restricted to code/math benchmarks; transfer to open-ended text or dialogue tasks remains untested.

## Confidence
- **Bias existence (logarithmic decay):** High (theoretical derivation and strong empirical fit).
- **Regularization correctness (\(k\log L\) removes bias):** Medium (depends on accurate \(k\) estimation and slow-varying \(S(L)\)).
- **Two-stage inference benefit:** High (large benchmark gains and ablations).
- **General training-free applicability:** Medium (architecture-agnostic in principle but needs broader validation).
