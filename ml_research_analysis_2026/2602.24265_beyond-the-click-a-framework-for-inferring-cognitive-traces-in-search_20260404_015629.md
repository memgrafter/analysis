# Beyond the Click: A Framework for Inferring Cognitive Traces in Search

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.24265
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24265
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A multi-agent LLM framework annotates search logs with cognitive traces (based on Information Foraging Theory), which improves prediction of user behavior outcomes by up to 6.6% F1 on datasets with sparse behavioral signals, with gains diminishing when click patterns are already highly predictive.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework to infer cognitive traces—such as confusion, goal clarity, and satisfaction—from user behavioral logs (queries, clicks, ratings). It uses a multi-agent LLM system grounded in Information Foraging Theory (IFT) to produce consistent, theory-based labels. The schema is validated by human experts and applied to three public datasets (AOL search, Stack Overflow, MovieLens), yielding over 530,000 annotations. Cross-dataset evaluation shows that augmenting behavioral data with these cognitive labels improves performance on downstream prediction tasks, particularly where raw actions are ambiguous or sparse. A shuffled-label control confirms the gains reflect genuine signal, not mere regularization.

## Method Summary
The core method is a multi-agent LLM pipeline (Analyst, Critic, Judge) that debates and decides on an IFT-based cognitive label for each action in a user session. The six labels (e.g., `FollowingScent`, `PoorScent`) are derived from Information Foraging Theory. A human-annotated gold standard (500 sessions, Krippendorff’s α=0.78) calibrates the system to 92.4% accuracy. Active learning flags the most ambiguous cases (top 1% disagreement) for expert review. For evaluation, a Transformer classifier compares three input variants: (1) behavioral features (action types + text), (2) behavioral + cognitive labels, and (3) behavioral + shuffled cognitive labels. Training uses user-based splits to prevent leakage, with early stopping and class balancing.

## Key Results
- Cognitive labels boost F1 by up to 6.6% over behavioral-only baselines on MovieLens tasks (e.g., predicting positive outcome) and by 1.8% above the shuffled control.
- On AOL search, where click sequences are already highly predictive, cognitive traces provide minimal additional signal, demonstrating they are most valuable when behavioral data is sparse or uninformative.
- The multi-agent system achieves 92.4% agreement with human experts on the calibration set, supporting the fidelity of large-scale annotation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A multi-agent debate system (Analyst, Critic, Judge) reduces individual LLM biases and improves label fidelity.
- **Mechanism:** The Analyst proposes labels, the Critic challenges inconsistencies, and the Judge synthesizes to a final decision. This adversarial, iterative process forces explicit reasoning and curbs hallucination.
- **Core assumption:** Different LLM personas produce qualitatively different error profiles; debate surfaces and corrects them.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions multi-agent LLM system; [section 3.3] describes agent roles, model assignments (Claude Analyst, GPT-4o Critic/Judge), and 92.4% accuracy against human gold standard.
- **Break condition:** If the Critic/Judge consistently fail to correct systematic Analyst errors (e.g., domain-specific misinterpretations), or if debate introduces new inconsistent heuristics.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Grounding the schema in Information Foraging Theory (IFT) provides a task-agnostic vocabulary for cross-domain cognitive annotation.
- **Mechanism:** IFT concepts (scent, patch, foraging) are operationalized into six concrete labels describing user-information environment relationships, not subjective emotions.
- **Core assumption:** Fundamental cognitive processes (assessing cue validity, persistence decisions) are similar across web search, Q&A, and recommender systems.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states framework is IFT-grounded and annotates three datasets; [section 3.2] defines the six labels and provides cross-domain examples.
- **Break condition:** If label definitions prove irreconcilable for a new domain (e.g., social media feeds), requiring a different theoretical basis.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Cognitive traces add predictive signal specifically when behavioral sequences are informationally insufficient.
- **Mechanism:** Cognitive label embeddings distill user goals, confidence, and satisfaction, clarifying ambiguous actions (e.g., a `RATE` in MovieLens). Benefit is greatest when raw actions are sparse or ambiguous; minimal when actions are already predictive (e.g., `QUERY`→`CLICK` in AOL).
- **Core assumption:** Inferred cognitive states are causally upstream of discrete behavioral outcomes.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes gains are near zero on AOL; [section 4.3] Finding 1 and Table 3 show MovieLens has few action types encoding content quality, making cognitive labels essential.
- **Break condition:** If shuffled-control experiments show no improvement over behavioral baseline across multiple datasets/tasks where behavioral features are weak, indicating labels add only noise/regularization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Information Foraging Theory (IFT)**
  - *Why needed:* The entire annotation schema interprets "cognitive traces" via IFT constructs (patches, scents, diet). Understanding IFT clarifies what the labels mean and why they were chosen.
  - *Quick check:* How would you translate "information scent" into an observable label for a user issuing three similar queries without clicking?
- **Concept: Multi-Agent LLM Systems for Consensus**
  - *Why needed:* The annotation pipeline relies on structured debate between specialized LLM agents to scale high-quality labeling beyond single-prompt approaches.
  - *Quick check:* What is a failure mode if the Critic is weaker than the Analyst? How might the Judge mitigate it?
- **Concept: Shuffled-Label Control for Causal Validation**
  - *Why needed:* The key claim—that cognitive labels have genuine predictive utility—rests on defeating the null that any extra embedding (even random) helps. The shuffled control is the essential experiment.
  - *Quick check:* In MovieLens "Positive Outcome", Cognitive F1=0.709, Shuffled=0.703 (+0.6% ΔS). What does ΔS tell you? What would negative ΔS imply?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Schema Definition (6 IFT labels) -> Multi-Agent Engine (Analyst/Critic/Judge with structured prompts) -> Input Processor (parse logs to JSON) -> Human-in-the-Loop Loop (gold standard creation, calibration, active learning QA) -> Output Formatter (JSON per event with label/justification) -> Evaluation Harness (Transformer classifier comparing Behavioral/Cognitive/Shuffled)
- **Critical path:** Define schema -> Build multi-agent prompt chain -> Create 500-session human gold standard -> Calibrate agents to ≥92.4% accuracy -> Run large-scale annotation with active learning QA -> Validate via cross-dataset shuffled-control experiments.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Model Choice:* Analyst uses faster/cheaper model (Claude Sonnet) for throughput; Critic/Judge use more capable/expensive (GPT-4o) for reasoning. Tradeoff: cost vs. quality.
  - *Schema Granularity:* Six discrete labels vs. continuous cognitive state. Tradeoff: interpretability/annotation ease vs. nuance.
  - *Active Learning Threshold:* Flagging top 1% disagreement. Tradeoff: expert workload vs. catching majority of errors.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low inter-annotator agreement (α < 0.7) on gold standard indicates schema ambiguity.
  - Calibration accuracy < 85% indicates multi-agent system cannot replicate expert judgment.
  - Cognitive model fails to exceed shuffled control on tasks with weak behavioral signal (contradicts Finding 1).
  - Extreme label distribution imbalance (e.g., >95% `FollowingScent`) suggests schema misses domain dynamics.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Schema Piloting:** Have 2 experts apply 6-label schema to 50 diverse sessions from one dataset. Measure Krippendorff’s α; if <0.7, refine definitions with concrete examples.
  2. **Agent Calibration Run:** Using 500-session gold standard, run full multi-agent chain. Tune system prompts until classifier test accuracy ≥92.4%. Log which agent makes most corrections.
  3. **Shuffled Control Sanity Check:** On 1,000 sessions of one dataset (e.g., MovieLens), run evaluation for one task (e.g., Positive Outcome). Verify Shuffled-Cognitive F1 ≤ Behavioral F1. If Shuffled > Behavioral by >0.5%, inspect for data leakage or feature engineering bug.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can continuous cognitive embeddings capture nuanced user states better than discrete IFT labels? Basis: authors propose exploring "continuous vector in a learned cognitive space". Why unresolved: six-label schema may oversimplify; continuous could model subtle gradients. Evidence: train embedding models from agent justifications; validate against fine-grained self-reports/physiological signals.
- **Open Question 2:** How do cognitive strategies evolve as users gain expertise or shift interests? Basis: authors call for studying "how a user’s cognitive patterns change over time" with longitudinal data. Why unresolved: temporal dynamics unexplored despite multi-session data; hypothesized expertise effects unmeasured. Evidence: analyze cognitive trajectories across years in Stack Overflow/MovieLens, correlate with expertise metrics (reputation, rating consistency).
- **Open Question 3:** Can real-time cognitive inference enable proactive interventions that measurably improve search outcomes? Basis: authors envision systems detecting states like repeated `PoorScent` to intervene before abandonment. Why unresolved: validated offline; real-time feasibility, latency, and causal impact untested. Evidence: integrate into live platform, A/B test interventions (query reformulation suggestions), measure effects on success/abandonment/satisfaction.

## Limitations
- Full multi-agent prompts and decoding parameters are unreleased, hindering exact replication of the annotation process.
- The mapping from raw logs to the discrete action-type vocabulary is not enumerated; researchers must infer it from provided label files.
- The six IFT-based labels may not generalize to domains with different interaction patterns (e.g., social feeds), where cognitive states could be qualitatively different.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Multi-agent debate):** Medium – Calibration accuracy high, but no ablation of individual agents or alternative architectures.
- **Mechanism 2 (IFT grounding):** Medium – Cross-domain demonstration strong, but scope limited to three datasets with structured logs.
- **Mechanism 3 (Sparse-behavioral boost):** High – Shuffled-control experiments consistently show gains where behavioral signal is weak, across multiple tasks.
