# Differential Attention-Augmented BiomedCLIP with Asymmetric Focal Optimization for Imbalanced Multi-Label Video Capsule Endoscopy Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.17879  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17879  
- Reference count: 9  
- Primary result: Temporal mAP@0.5 = 0.2456, mAP@0.95 = 0.2353 on RARE-VISION test set (161,025 frames) with ~8.6 minutes inference on a single GPU  

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses extreme class imbalance in multi-label video capsule endoscopy (VCE) classification, where pathological findings constitute less than 0.1% of frames. The authors enhance BiomedCLIP by replacing its standard self-attention with differential attention to suppress noise and amplify rare signals. They mitigate imbalance through a multi-level strategy: sqrt-frequency weighted sampling, asymmetric focal loss (γ⁻=4), mixup, and per-class threshold optimization. Temporal post-processing (median filtering, gap merging) converts frame-level predictions into coherent clinical events. On the RARE-VISION test set (161,025 frames from 3 NaviCam exams), the method achieves temporal mAP@0.5 of 0.2456 and mAP@0.95 of 0.2353, with a narrow gap indicating precise event boundary localization. Inference takes approximately 8.6 minutes on a single GPU.

## Method Summary
The model builds on BiomedCLIP (ViT-B/16) by replacing all 12 transformer block self-attentions with differential attention. This mechanism splits heads into two groups, computes separate softmax attention maps, and subtracts a λ-scaled second map from the first to cancel common-mode noise. The classification head includes an excitation block (Squeeze-and-Excitation, reduction 16), dropout (p=0.4), and a linear layer outputting 17 logits. A parallel contrastive path normalizes image features and computes dot products with frozen PubMedBERT text embeddings (prompts like “a video capsule endoscopy image showing [label]”) scaled by a temperature τ. The total loss is L = L_cls + 0.4·L_con. L_cls is asymmetric focal loss with γ⁺=1, γ⁻=4 and class-positive weights derived from frequency; L_con is BCE on contrastive logits with the same weighting. Imbalance is addressed via sqrt-frequency weighted sampling (each frame weighted by 1/√f_c where f_c is the frequency of its rarest positive label) and per-class threshold optimization on validation to maximize F1. Regularization includes mixup (α=0.3), label smoothing (ε=0.05), strong augmentations, and EMA (β=0.999). Training uses AdamW (weight decay 5e-4) with OneCycleLR (max_lr backbone 9e-5, head 3e-4) for 5 epochs at batch size 128. At inference, per-class thresholds binarize sigmoid outputs; temporal post-processing (median filter window 7, gap merging ≤5 frames, removal of events <3 frames) yields the final event JSON.

## Key Results
- Temporal mAP@0.5 = 0.2456 and mAP@0.95 = 0.2353 on the held-out RARE-VISION test set (161,025 frames).  
- Inference time: ~8.6 minutes on a single GPU.  
- The small difference between mAP@0.5 and mAP@0.95 (0.0103) indicates precise temporal boundary localization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Differential attention suppresses noise
- **Claim:** Replacing standard self-attention with differential attention amplifies signal from rare pathological regions by suppressing common-mode noise.  
- **Mechanism:** For input X, two separate attention distributions are computed from partitioned heads: softmax(Q₁K₁ᵀ/√dₕ) and softmax(Q₂K₂ᵀ/√dₕ). Their difference, scaled by a learnable λ, forms the attention output. Subtraction cancels uniform or low-rank attention maps (noise) arising from uninformative query-key similarities, leaving stronger gradients for distinctive spatial patterns.  
- **Core assumption:** Noise manifests as similar, diffuse attention patterns across head groups, while pathogenic signals generate distinct, high-weight patterns.  
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract describes the differential attention mechanism; Section 2.1 elaborates that subtraction cancels common-mode noise and amplifies rare signals. Related work on attention augmentation exists, but direct validation of this specific noise-cancellation effect in the biomedical imbalanced context is weak.  
- **Break condition:** If noise patterns are not common-mode across groups (e.g., highly specific to each group), subtraction could cancel signal and degrade performance relative to standard attention, especially on rare classes.

### Mechanism 2: Coordinated multi-level imbalance strategy
- **Claim:** A combination of sqrt-frequency sampling, asymmetric focal loss, and per-class threshold optimization effectively mitigates extreme class imbalance (<0.1% pathological frames).  
- **Mechanism:** (i) sqrt-frequency sampling assigns weight 1/√f_c to frames based on their rarest active label, providing moderate oversampling of rare events without instability; (ii) asymmetric focal loss with γ⁻=4 heavily down-weights gradients from easy negatives, preventing the model from being overwhelmed by abundant “no pathology” frames; (iii) per-class threshold optimization on validation adjusts decision boundaries per label to maximize F1, compensating for calibration skews across the frequency spectrum.  
- **Core assumption:** No single technique suffices—sampling influences gradient frequency, loss influences gradient magnitude, and thresholds influence decision boundaries; together they address imbalance at training, optimization, and inference levels.  
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract lists all components; Section 2.3 explicitly states the multi-level strategy. Neighbors show similar needs for long-tailed recognition and VLMs, but the exact combination is not directly evidenced in the corpus.  
- **Break condition:** If imbalance is not driven primarily by easy negatives (e.g., rare classes are visually ambiguous), asymmetric focal loss may help little. Also, small validation sets may yield unstable per-class thresholds.

### Mechanism 3: Temporal post-processing for coherent events
- **Claim:** Applying median filtering and gap merging along the temporal axis converts noisy frame-level predictions into clinically plausible contiguously occurring events, ensuring precise temporal boundaries.  
- **Mechanism:** After per-class thresholding, a median filter (window 7) removes isolated false positives; gaps of ≤5 frames between same-class events are merged; events shorter than 3 frames are discarded. This exploits the fact that pathological findings in VCE generally persist over contiguous frames.  
- **Core assumption:** Clinically relevant events are temporally contiguous; isolated false positives are improbable.  
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions temporal coherence via median-filter smoothing and gap merging; Section 2.6 details the parameters; Section 3.1 notes the narrow mAP@0.5 vs. mAP@0.95 gap as evidence of precise localization.  
- **Break condition:** If true pathological events are extremely brief (<3 frames) or highly fragmented, the filters would delete true positives. Also, fixed parameters may not generalize across VCE devices with differing frame-to-frame consistency.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map (plain text):
Input pipeline (sqrt-frequency weighted sampler + mixup & strong augmentations) → Differential BiomedCLIP (ViT-B/16 with differential attention in all 12 blocks) → Dual heads: (a) Classification: Excitation Block (reduction r=16) → Dropout(p=0.4) → Linear(17 logits); (b) Contrastive: normalized image features dot frozen PubMedBERT text embeddings (17 prompts) + temperature τ → Combined loss = asymmetric focal (γ⁻=4, class-weighted) + 0.4·BCE(contrastive) → AdamW with OneCycleLR, EMA(β=0.999). Validation: per-class threshold optimization (search to maximize F1). Inference: thresholds → median filter(w=7) → gap merging(≤5 frames) → min-length filter(3) → event JSON.

Critical path:
The training pipeline: data loading (with sqrt-sampling) → forward pass through differential BiomedCLIP → compute classification and contrastive losses → total loss backpropagation → optimizer step → EMA update. Separately, after each epoch, per-class thresholds are tuned on the validation set. During test inference, thresholds are applied and temporal post-processing is performed to produce final events.

Design tradeoffs:
- Differential attention adds ~2× attention FLOPs (two attention computations per block) but is accepted for noise suppression.  
- Per-class threshold optimization requires a validation set with enough rare-class examples; otherwise rare thresholds may be unreliable.  
- EMA (β=0.999) stabilizes evaluation but increases storage (separate weights for inference).

Failure signatures:
- Rare-class collapse: Macro AP substantially lower than common-class AP, e.g., active bleeding AP near 0.  
- Temporal fragmentation: Many short events (<3 frames) after thresholding, leading to large mAP@0.5 vs. mAP@0.95 gap.  
- Domain shift performance drop: Test mAP on NaviCam (480px) much lower than validation (mixed cameras), indicating resolution/color mismatch.

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline: Train standard BiomedCLIP (no differential attention) with only BCE loss and no sampling/threshold optimization.  
2. Ablation 1: Add only differential attention to Baseline; measure change in rare-class validation AP.  
3. Ablation 2: Apply full imbalance strategy (sampling, asymmetric loss) but without any temporal post-processing; compare frame-level vs. temporal mAP.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can temporal modeling (e.g., recurrent or attention-based sequence modules) improve detection of rare pathological events in VCE by leveraging the sequential frame context and temporal persistence?  
2. To what extent does the domain shift between NaviCam test videos and primarily Olympus/PillCam training data degrade performance, and can domain adaptation techniques mitigate this?  
3. Are the current multi-level imbalance strategies sufficient for classes with extremely low support (<200 frames), or are specialized few-shot learning approaches necessary?  
4. Does differential attention provide a consistent advantage over standard self-attention in BiomedCLIP for imbalanced VCE classification, particularly for rare classes? (The paper lacks a direct ablation isolating its effect.)

## Limitations
- The specific dataset splits and label format are not publicly released, hindering exact reproduction of experiments.  
- Differential attention implementation details (head partition, value handling) and contrastive prompt strings are ambiguous; the provided description leaves room for interpretation.  
- Per-class threshold optimization on a small validation set may lead to overfitting and unstable thresholds, especially for rare classes.  
- Fixed temporal post-processing parameters (median window=7, gap merging ≤5 frames) may not generalize across different VCE devices or pathologies with short durations.

## Confidence
- Differential attention amplifies rare signal: Medium  
- Multi-level imbalance strategy necessary: Medium  
- Temporal post-processing yields precise boundaries: Medium  
- Test set metrics (temporal mAP@0.5=0.2456, mAP@0.95=0.2353): High  

## Next Checks
1. Ablation: Train BiomedCLIP with only differential attention (remove imbalance/temporal steps) on a balanced subset; measure change in rare-class AP on validation.  
2. Threshold stability: Repeatedly sample validation splits (e.g., 5 random 80/20 splits) and re-optimize per-class thresholds; compute variance per class, focusing on rare ones.  
3. Post-processing sensitivity: Vary median filter window (5, 7, 9) and gap merging length (3, 5, 7) on validation; report mAP@0.5 and mAP@0.95 to assess robustness.
