# Tool-Aware Planning in Contact Center AI: Evaluating LLMs through Lineage-Guided Query Decomposition
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14955
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14955
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LLMs struggle with compound queries and plans exceeding 4 steps; highest metric-wise score 84.8% (Claude-3-7-Sonnet), top one-shot A+ match 49.75% (o3-mini).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating large language models (LLMs) on tool‑aware planning for contact center queries, which requires decomposing natural‑language questions into executable steps with correct tool selection and dependency wiring. The authors propose a dual‑perspective evaluation framework: a metric‑wise evaluator scoring seven dimensions (e.g., tool‑prompt alignment, executability) and a one‑shot evaluator measuring structural closeness to a reference plan. Additionally, they introduce an evaluator‑optimizer loop that iteratively refines plans, creating a lineage of corrections. Testing across 14 LLMs reveals persistent weaknesses with compound queries and longer plans (beyond 4 steps). The best metric‑wise score reaches 84.8% (Claude‑3‑7‑Sonnet), while the top one‑shot A+ match is only 49.75% (o3‑mini). Plan lineage yields mixed benefits but improves step executability for several models. The work highlights significant gaps in tool‑prompt alignment and tool‑usage completeness, and demonstrates that shorter plans are markedly easier for current LLMs.

## Method Summary
The core method comprises three contributions. First, a **dual‑perspective evaluation** framework: the metric‑wise evaluator rates plans on seven orthogonal dimensions (tool‑prompt alignment, format, executability, query adherence, dependencies, redundancy, tool‑usage completeness) using learned weights (70% Effectiveness, 30% Efficiency). The one‑shot evaluator compares a generated plan to a human‑crafted reference, computing step‑level precision/recall/F1 and checking format, dependencies, and placeholders, then mapping the result to a seven‑point quality tier. Second, an **evaluator‑optimizer loop**: a step‑wise evaluator tags defects (e.g., INCORRECT TOOL, COMPLEX PROMPT), and a plan optimizer applies conservative edits (local fixes and global coherence updates) to produce a revised plan; iterations continue until no changes or a four‑pass cap, generating a plan lineage. Third, **lineage prompting**: the planner’s prompt includes example lineages to teach common repair patterns, hypothesizing that the model will avoid similar errors in one‑shot generation. Plans are expressed as JSON sequences with a depends_on field, requiring a valid directed acyclic graph (DAG). The authors generate a stratified query set with GPT‑4o, obtain human‑verified gold plans, and evaluate 14 LLMs with and without lineage examples.

## Key Results
- LLMs perform poorly on compound queries and plans longer than 4 steps; the highest one‑shot A+ match is 49.75% (o3‑mini) and the best metric‑wise score is 84.8% (Claude‑3‑7‑Sonnet).
- The evaluator‑optimizer loop demonstrably improves plan quality for Nova‑Lite: after refinement, “Extremely Good” plans increase by 3.6 percentage points and “Very Good” by 5.6 points.
- Lineage prompting produces mixed outcomes: 6 of 14 models achieve a higher A+ rate, and 9 show improved step executability, but gains are inconsistent and may not justify additional prompt overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Iterative Refinement via Evaluator‑Optimizer Loop
- **Claim**: The feedback loop systematically improves plan quality by diagnosing and repairing errors in tool selection, prompt design, and dependencies.
- **Mechanism**: A step‑wise evaluator tags issues per step (e.g., INCORRECT TOOL, COMPLEX PROMPT, REPEATED DETAIL). A plan optimizer then applies Change 0 (local fix) and Change 1 (global coherence) edits, producing a revised plan; distinct revisions are stored as a lineage and the loop stops after a full pass with no changes or up to 4 passes.
- **Core assumption**: LLMs can accurately diagnose plan defects and apply conservative, structural repairs when guided by a deterministic rubric.
- **Evidence**: Section 3 details the loop and tags; Section 6.2 reports quality uplift (e.g., Extremely Good + 3.6pp, Very Good + 5.6pp). Related work lacks such a textual, non‑executing refinement loop.

### Mechanism 2: Dual‑Perspective Evaluation for Comprehensive Scoring
- **Claim**: Combining metric‑wise (seven orthogonal dimensions) and one‑shot (plan‑to‑reference structural match) evaluation yields a robust, multi‑faceted assessment.
- **Mechanism**: The metric‑wise evaluator scores discrete aspects with learned weights (70% Effectiveness, 30% Efficiency). The one‑shot judge computes step‑level precision/recall/F1 plus format/dependency/placeholder checks, mapping F1 to a seven‑point quality rating.
- **Core assumption**: No single evaluation captures all failure modes; decomposing correctness helps identify specific weaknesses, while an overall closeness rating reflects end‑to‑end usability.
- **Evidence**: Section 4 defines both modes and the weighted aggregation (Equation 1); Section 5 presents per‑metric scores and tier distributions; Section D.4 confirms learned weights are stable (ρ > 0.84) across random draws.

### Mechanism 3: Lineage Prompting as In‑Context Error‑Avoidance
- **Claim**: Providing planners with plan lineages (sequences of progressively corrected plans) improves their initial outputs by teaching common repair patterns.
- **Mechanism**: For each query, the prompt includes examples where a weak plan is iteratively refined; the target LLM is expected to internalize corrections (e.g., splitting complex steps, fixing tool mismatches, adding missing dependencies) and generate a stronger plan in one shot.
- **Core assumption**: LLMs can generalize from a few demonstration lineages to avoid typical planning pitfalls on new queries of similar structure.
- **Evidence**: Section 3 describes lineage construction and inclusion; Section 5 compares performance with vs. without lineage (mixed gains: 6/14 models improve A+, 9/14 improve step executability); the abstract notes mixed benefits but gains for several top models.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Tool‑Aware Planning in Contact Centers**  
*Why needed*: The domain uses three specialized tools (T2S for structured Snowflake queries, RAG for transcript analysis, LLM for synthesis). Correct tool assignment and prompt design are fundamental to plan executability.  
*Quick check*: *When should a plan use RAG instead of T2S?* (Answer: RAG for within‑call temporal dynamics and subjective content; T2S for aggregates, counts, and QA scores on structured data.)

**Concept: DAG‑Based Plan Representation**  
*Why needed*: Plans are JSON sequences with `depends_on` fields that must form a directed acyclic graph (DAG) to enable safe parallel execution. Breakages in dependency wiring cause runtime failures.  
*Quick check*: *What two conditions guarantee a valid dependency graph?* (Answer: All edges point from a lower‑indexed step to a higher‑indexed step, and the graph must be acyclic.)

**Concept: Multi‑Metric Evaluation of Plans**  
*Why needed*: A single score obscures weaknesses; the seven metrics (tool‑prompt alignment, format, executability, query adherence, dependencies, redundancy, tool‑usage completeness) diagnose specific defects.  
*Quick check*: *Name two metrics under the Efficiency (30 pt) group.* (Answer: Dependencies, Redundancy, Tool‑Usage Completeness – any two.)

## Architecture Onboarding
*Component map*: Query Generator (GPT‑4o) → Planner (14 LLMs) → (optional) Feedback Loop (Step‑wise Evaluator + Plan Optimizer, both GPT‑4o) → Evaluation Suite (Metric‑wise Evaluator and One‑shot Evaluator, both Claude‑Sonnet‑4) → scores. Reference Plan DB stores gold plans and lineages curated by human annotators; it is consumed by the Evaluation Suite.

*Critical path*:  
1. Generate query (GPT‑4o)  
2. Produce initial plan (target LLM, with or without lineage prompt)  
3. (Optional) Run feedback loop on Nova‑Lite to create new lineage  
4. Evaluate plan via both evaluators  
5. Aggregate scores and analyze.

*Design tradeoffs*:  
- **Metric weighting**: Learned 70/30 Effectiveness/Efficiency vs. equal‑weight averages. Learned weights are stable across random draws (ρ > 0.84) but require validation triplets.
- **Evaluator design**: *Deconstructed* (one prompt per metric) > *Single* in human agreement (Table 21) but increases calls. *Reference‑based* prompts (with gold plan) boost accuracy over reference‑free.
- **Lineage prompting**: Adds prompt length; benefits strong models (e.g., o3‑mini) but can hurt others. Mixed cost‑benefit; only applied to test‑time inference for Nova‑Lite to measure lift.

*Failure signatures*:  
- Low Tool‑Prompt Alignment and Tool‑Usage Completeness scores across models (Table 2) indicate persistent difficulty in selecting the right tool and covering dual‑tool cases.
- Steep drop in one‑shot A+ rate for best‑possible plans exceeding 4 steps (Table 9).
- Some models (e.g., Llama3‑2‑1B) consistently score near zero, revealing capacity limits.
- Fidelity gap between metric‑wise and one‑shot rankings (e.g., Claude‑3‑7‑Sonnet top in metric‑wise but mediocre in one‑shot) suggests metrics capture different aspects.

*First 3 experiments*:  
1. Baseline one‑shot generation: For all 14 LLMs, generate plans w/ and w/o lineage prompts; compute both evaluator scores (Sec 6.1).
2. Feedback loop efficacy: Run the evaluator→optimizer loop on Nova‑Lite initial plans; compare pre‑loop vs. post‑loop quality using the one‑shot evaluator (Sec 6.2, Table 4).
3. Judge robustness: Replace Claude‑Sonnet‑4 with GPT‑5 as judge; measure Spearman rank correlation on planner rankings (Sec E.4, Table 24).

## Limitations
- Corpus weakness: Related work cites low‑FMR, low‑impact neighbors, questioning novelty anchoring.
- Evaluation noise: Dependence on Claude‑Sonnet‑4 as judge; limited human validation (only 105 triplets for weight learning).
- Mixed lineage gains: Benefits are model‑specific and inconsistent; prompt overhead may outweigh gains for weaker models.

## Confidence
- Dual‑perspective evaluation design: **High**
- Evaluator→optimizer loop improves plan quality: **Medium**
- Lineage prompting helps avoid planning errors: **Low/Medium**
- Persistent gaps in tool‑prompt alignment and completeness: **High**

## Next Checks
1. **Human eval scaling**: Run human annotations on 500+ plans (diverse step lengths) to validate metric‑wise and one‑shot judge agreement beyond the 105‑triplet subset.
2. **Cross‑domain transfer**: Test the trained evaluator/optimizer loop on a different tool‑rich domain (e.g., API orchestration) using held‑out tools to measure generalizability.
3. **Sensitivity analysis**: Ablate the 70/30 Effectiveness/Efficiency weight split and the 4‑pass loop cap; measure impact on final plan quality and failure modes.
