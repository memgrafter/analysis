# PolyFrame at MWE-2026 AdMIRe 2: When Words Are Not Enough: Multimodal Idiom Disambiguation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18652  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18652  
- **Reference count:** 10  
- **Primary result:** Zero-shot transfer achieved 60.0% Top-1 accuracy on English and Portuguese; multilingual blind test (15 languages) averaged 0.35 Top-1 / 0.73 NDCG@5 (image+text) and 0.32 / 0.71 (text-only).

## Executive Summary
PolyFrame tackles multimodal idiom disambiguation—a task requiring interpretation of figurative language across text and images—by combining frozen large-scale encoders with lightweight, trainable modules. The system leverages CLIP-style and multilingual BGE M3 encoders to obtain robust multimodal representations without expensive fine-tuning. A pipeline of idiom substitution, sentence-type prediction, distractor-aware scoring, and Borda rank fusion boosts performance. On the MWE-2026 AdMIRe 2 benchmark, PolyFrame substantially improves over a CLIP baseline (26.7% to 60.0% Top-1 on English development) and yields competitive results in zero-shot transfer and multilingual settings. Ablations confirm that idiom-aware rewriting is the primary driver, while sentence typing and multimodal fusion enhance stability across languages.

## Method Summary
PolyFrame employs a two-stage strategy. First, an idiom-aware rewriting module substitutes idiomatic expressions with more literal synonyms, reducing linguistic ambiguity. Next, an LLM-based sentence-type predictor classifies the sentence as literal or figurative, informing later scoring. The system then processes text (original and/or rewritten) through a multilingual BGE M3 encoder and processes images via a CLIP-style encoder. The resulting embeddings are combined with logistic regression in a distractor-aware scoring module that ranks candidate items. Finally, Borda rank fusion aggregates multiple ranking lists to produce the final order. All large encoders remain frozen, ensuring efficiency and cross-lingual transferability.

## Key Results
- **Development (English):** PolyFrame raised Top‑1 accuracy from 26.7% (CLIP baseline) to 60.0%.
- **Zero-shot transfer:** Achieved 60.0% Top‑1 on English and Portuguese, with Portuguese also scoring 0.822 NDCG@5.
- **Multilingual blind test (15 languages):** Average Top‑1 / NDCG scores of 0.35 / 0.73 for image+text ranking (Subtask A) and 0.32 / 0.71 for text‑only ranking (Subtask B).
- **Ablation study:** Idiom-aware rewriting contributed the largest performance gain; sentence typing and multimodal fusion further improved robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The frozen CLIP and BGE M3 encoders supply high-quality, language‑agnostic representations that capture both visual and textual semantics without dataset‑specific fine‑tuning. The lightweight trainable modules focus on the core challenge: literalizing idioms (rewriting), adjusting for linguistic style (sentence typing), and combining heterogeneous similarity scores (distractor‑aware scoring and Borda fusion). This modular design allows the system to adapt to idiom disambiguation while inheriting the generalization strengths of large pre‑trained models. The Borda fusion particularly mitigates modality‑ and language‑specific weaknesses by aggregating complementary ranking cues.

- **Idiom‑aware rewriting:** Substitutes idiomatic phrases with synonyms to expose literal meaning; directly addresses ambiguity. *Quick check:* Verify that rewritten sentences retain the intended sense using human evaluation or a literalness classifier.
- **Sentence‑type prediction:** LLM‑based classification (literal/figurative/mixed); needed to weight modules appropriately. *Quick check:* Measure classification accuracy on a small labeled set.
- **Distractor‑aware scoring:** Adjusts ranking scores based on presence of plausible incorrect candidates; improves robustness in competitive settings. *Quick check:* Introduce synthetic distractors and observe score separation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (image, text) → Idiom Substitution → Sentence‑Type Prediction → Encoders (CLIP + BGE M3) → Distractor‑Aware Scoring (Logistic Regression) → Borda Rank Fusion → Final Ranking  

**Critical path:** The text undergoes rewriting and sentence typing; then both image and text are encoded; features are merged and scored; finally multiple ranking sources are fused to output the ordered list of candidates.  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Frozen encoders reduce adaptation capacity but minimize training cost and enable zero‑shot transfer.  
- External LLM for sentence typing adds latency but provides high‑quality type labels.  
- Borda fusion is parameter‑free and stable, yet suboptimal if rankings have varying reliability across languages.  
- Idiom substitution relies on a static list and may mishandle contextual nuances.  

**Failure signatures:**  
- Poor handling of culturally specific idioms not covered by the substitution dictionary.  
- Degraded performance when the image is abstract or does not clearly depict the literal meaning of the idiom.  
- Cascading errors from mispredicted sentence types affecting downstream scoring.  
- Language‑specific outliers, likely due to uneven encoder coverage or idiom density.  

**Exactly 3 first experiments:**  
1. Ablate the idiom substitution module to quantify its contribution to overall performance.  
2. Replace Borda fusion with a learned weighted average (e.g., via ridge regression) to assess potential gains.  
3. Evaluate the pipeline with a different frozen multimodal encoder (e.g., BLIP) to test encoder robustness.

## Limitations
- Generalizability unknown beyond the MWE‑2026 AdMIRe 2 benchmark.  
- Component contributions within the Borda fusion are not fully isolated.  
- Significant variance across the 15 test languages, with specific failure modes unanalyzed.  
- Performance may depend heavily on the choice of frozen encoders.  
- Dataset is very recent and niche; external validation is limited.

## Confidence
- **High:** Internal ablation study results (e.g., idiom‑aware rewriting dominant).  
- **Medium:** Specific numeric results on provided development and test sets (e.g., 60.0% Top‑1, 0.35/0.73 averages).  
- **Low:** Broader claims about general effectiveness and robustness without fine‑tuning.
