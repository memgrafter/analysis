# Causal Manifold Fairness: Enforcing Geometric Invariance in Representation Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03032
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03032
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** On the Warped Swiss Roll dataset, CMF reduces the proposed fairness metrics by orders of magnitude: Metric Error drops from 16.387 to 0.018 and Curvature Error from 4.317 to 0.046, while preserving high task utility (classification accuracy 0.995 vs. 1.000 for the baseline, with a modest increase in reconstruction MSE from 0.070 to 0.754).

## Executive Summary
Standard fairness methods often only shift distributions without addressing how sensitive attributes distort the underlying geometry of the data manifold. This paper introduces Causal Manifold Fairness (CMF), a framework that enforces geometric invariance by constraining the decoder’s Jacobian (metric tensor) and Hessian (curvature). This ensures that the local Riemannian structure remains stable under counterfactual interventions on sensitive attributes. On a synthetic “Warped Swiss Roll” dataset, CMF achieves dramatic reductions in the proposed geometric fairness metrics while maintaining high classification accuracy, demonstrating effective disentanglement of sensitive geometric warping.

## Method Summary
CMF builds on an autoencoder architecture with an encoder `E` mapping data `X` to a latent `Z` and a decoder `D` reconstructing `X`. The training objective combines three losses: (1) a task loss (reconstruction and classification), (2) an alignment loss `L_align` that minimizes the Euclidean distance between latent representations of factual and counterfactual samples (generated via an SCM intervention on the sensitive attribute `A`), and (3) a geometric loss `L_geo` that enforces invariance of the decoder’s Jacobian and Hessian. The Jacobian defines the pullback metric `G(z)=J_D(z)^T J_D(z)` (first-order geometry), while the Hessian captures second-order curvature. The decoder uses `C²`-continuous activations (ELU) to enable stable Hessian computation. The total loss `L = L_task + L_align + λ_geo L_geo` is minimized with gradient-based optimization.

## Key Results
- Metric Error (Frobenius norm of metric tensor difference) reduced from 16.387 (baseline) to 0.018.
- Curvature Error (sum of Hessian differences) reduced from 4.317 to 0.046.
- Task utility nearly preserved: classification accuracy 0.995 vs. 1.000 baseline, with reconstruction MSE increasing from 0.070 to 0.754.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** CMF eliminates first-order geometric distortion (stretching/compression) caused by sensitive attributes by enforcing invariance of the decoder's Jacobian (metric tensor).
- **Mechanism:** The Jacobian `J_D(z)` determines how infinitesimal distances in `Z` are scaled in `X`. CMF minimizes `||G(z) - G(z_cf)||_F` between factual (`z=E(x)`) and counterfactual (`z_cf=E(x_cf)`) points, ensuring the local scaling (metric) is identical across groups. This removes any influence of `A` on linear stretching.
- **Core assumption:** The sensitive attribute `A` causally influences the first-order geometry via the SCM. Enforcing `G` invariance aligns the geometric ruler across interventions.
- **Break condition:** Fails if bias operates solely through higher-order curvature or via non-causal paths not captured by `do(A)`.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** CMF eliminates second-order geometric distortion (bending/twisting) by enforcing invariance of the decoder's Hessian (curvature).
- **Mechanism:** The Hessian `∇²D_k(z)` describes local bending. CMF penalizes `||H_k(z) - H_k(z_cf)||_F` for each output dimension, aligning curvature across counterfactuals. This prevents `A` from encoding bias in the manifold’s second-order structure.
- **Core assumption:** Sensitive attributes can independently warp the manifold’s curvature beyond linear effects. Aligning `G` alone is insufficient.
- **Break condition:** Fails if Hessian is numerically unstable (especially in high-dimensional outputs) or if the bias is purely affine (captured by `G`).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The combined loss disentangles the intrinsic causal variable `U` from the geometric warp induced by `A`, learning a representation where the decoder’s induced Riemannian structure is causally invariant.
- **Mechanism:** Simultaneously minimizing latent alignment (`||z - z_cf||²`) and geometric invariance (`||G(z)-G(z_cf)||_F + β Σ||H_k(z)-H_k(z_cf)||_F`) forces the encoder to discard `A`-related information that manifests as geometric distortion. The decoder must then reconstruct data using a single manifold geometry that fits both factual and counterfactual distributions, removing the warp.
- **Core assumption:** The intrinsic factors `U` define a unique underlying manifold geometry, while `A` applies a deterministic, geometry-altering transformation; `U` and `A` are conditionally independent given `U`.
- **Break condition:** Fails if `A` influences `U` (`A → U`) or if the decoder lacks capacity to fit a unified manifold.

## Foundational Learning
- **Structural Causal Models (SCMs) & Counterfactuals**  
  *Why needed:* CMF relies on interventions `do(A)` to generate counterfactuals and define fairness as invariance under such interventions. Distinguishing `P(x|do(A))` from `P(x|A)` is fundamental.  
  *Quick check:* Given `X = f(U, A)`, what is the structural equation for `X_{A←a'}`? How does it differ from sampling `P(X|A=a')`?

- **Riemannian Metric & Pullback Metric**  
  *Why needed:* The pullback metric `G(z)=J_D(z)^T J_D(z)` defines the local geometry induced by the decoder. CMF’s fairness objective is expressed as invariance of this metric.  
  *Quick check:* If `D` is linear, what is `G(z)`? How does `G(z)` change for a nonlinear MLP with ELU activations?

- **Autoencoder Latent Space Geometry**  
  *Why needed:* In VAEs/autoencoders, the latent space `Z` is not inherently Euclidean; the decoder’s derivatives define the relevant metric. CMF directly regularizes the decoder’s induced geometry, not the latent Euclidean distances.  
  *Quick check:* Why does CMF focus on the decoder’s derivatives rather than the encoder’s output distribution?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input x (with attribute a) -> Encoder E -> latent z; Counterfactual generator (via SCM) produces x_cf -> Encoder E -> z_cf; Decoder D: z -> x_hat; Jacobian/Hessian module computes J_D(z) and ∇²D_k(z) for all k; Loss modules compute L_task (reconstruction + classification), L_align (||z - z_cf||²), L_geo (metric + curvature invariance); Optimizer updates E and D.

**Critical path:**  
1. For each sample (x, a, y), generate counterfactual x_cf by intervening on a in the SCM.  
2. Compute z = E(x), z_cf = E(x_cf).  
3. Decode x_hat = D(z).  
4. Compute J_D(z) and ∇²D_k(z) for k=1..d_x using autograd.  
5. Compute G(z)=J_D(z)^T J_D(z) and H_k(z)=∇²D_k(z).  
6. Compute L_task from x_hat and predicted y.  
7. Compute L_align = ||z - z_cf||².  
8. Compute L_geo = ||G(z)-G(z_cf)||_F + β Σ||H_k(z)-H_k(z_cf)||_F.  
9. Total loss L = L_task + L_align + λ_geo L_geo.  
10. Backpropagate L and update E, D.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Hessian cost:** Exact Hessian scales as O(d_z² * d_x); can be prohibitive for moderate d_z/d_x. May require small batches or approximations (e.g., Hessian-vector products).  
- **β weighting:** Balances metric vs. curvature constraints. Too high β can harm utility; too low leaves curvature unregularized. Requires tuning.  
- **Activation continuity:** Requires C² activations (ELU) for stable Hessian; smoother functions (tanh, softplus) may improve stability but affect representation quality.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. NaNs in Hessian: indicates non-differentiable activation or numerical instability; check activation choice and input range.  
2. High L_geo throughout training: counterfactual generator may be incorrect or weak; verify SCM implementation and that A is the sole parent of warp.  
3. Utility collapse (accuracy ~0.5) with low L_geo: λ_geo or β too high; model ignores task to satisfy geometry.  
4. High L_geo but low Metric Error, high Curvature Error: Hessian term ineffective; check β and scaling, possibly need per-output weighting.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Gradient sanity check:** On a single (x, x_cf) pair, compute L_geo and backpropagate. Verify all parameters in E and D receive finite, nonzero gradients, especially through Hessian.  
2. **Warped Swiss Roll replication:** Train CMF on the synthetic dataset. Expect Metric Error ≈ 0.02, Curvature Error ≈ 0.05, accuracy > 0.99, MSE ~0.75. If errors >1.0, check Hessian and counterfactual generation.  
3. **Ablation: Hessian term:** Set β=0 (only metric constraint). Measure Curvature Error; expect it to remain high (like baseline ~4.0) while Metric Error drops. Isolates Hessian contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Scalability to high-dimensional data:** How can CMF be applied to images without full Hessian computation? The paper suggests approximate Hessian-vector products but does not validate them.
- **Extension to non-Euclidean data:** Can the framework handle graphs or manifolds with complex topology? The current formulation assumes Euclidean latent space and smooth decoder.
- **Real-world fairness impact:** Do the geometric invariance metrics correlate with improved fairness outcomes in high-stakes decision-making? The evaluation is limited to synthetic data.
- **Theoretical properties:** What are the identifiability and sample complexity guarantees for learning representations under metric and curvature invariance with finite data? No theoretical analysis is provided.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter/architecture details missing:** Network sizes, latent dimension, λ_geo, β, and training schedule are unreported, hindering exact replication.
- **Task variable Y not fully specified:** The structural equation for Y is absent; assuming Y=f(U) is critical for interpreting accuracy results.
- **Hessian practicality unaddressed:** Computational cost and numerical stability of exact Hessian for decoder networks are not evaluated; scalability beyond very small latent/output spaces is questionable.

## Confidence
- **Jacobian/Metric Invariance (Mechanism 1):** Medium. Mathematical formulation is clear and supported by synthetic ablation, but lacks validation on real-world data and prior work on causal geometric fairness.
- **Hessian/Curvature Invariance (Mechanism 2):** Low. Concept introduced and measured on synthetic data where curvature warping is engineered; no independent evidence of necessity outside this setting.
- **Combined Disentanglement (Mechanism 3):** Medium. Loss design is logical and results on synthetic data are compelling, but confidence is limited by single-dataset evaluation and potential Hessian practical issues.

## Next Checks
- **Hyperparameter grid search:** Systematically vary λ_geo and β on the Warped Swiss Roll to replicate the reported trade-off (errors ~0.02/0.05 vs. MSE ~0.75). Identify the exact regime achieving the paper's numbers.
- **Clarify Y’s causal role:** Contact authors or infer from code the exact SCM for Y. Confirm Y depends only on U (intrinsic variable) and is independent of A after intervention.
- **Stress-test Hessian computation:** Measure GPU memory and runtime for Hessian as functions of latent dimension d_z and output dimension d_x. Determine practical limits and whether results generalize beyond d_z=2, d_x=3.
