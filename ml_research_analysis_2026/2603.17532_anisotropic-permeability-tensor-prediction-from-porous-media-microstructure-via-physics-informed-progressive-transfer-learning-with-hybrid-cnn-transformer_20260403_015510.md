# Anisotropic Permeability Tensor Prediction from Porous Media Microstructure via Physics-Informed Progressive Transfer Learning with Hybrid CNN-Transformer

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17532
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17532
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Achieves variance-weighted R² = 0.9960 with 120 ms inference per sample and enforced tensor symmetry/positive-definiteness, enabling 10³–10⁴× speedups over lattice-Boltzmann simulation.

## Executive Summary
Predicting full permeability tensors from porous media images via direct numerical simulation (e.g., lattice-Boltzmann method) is computationally prohibitive, scaling to hours per sample and blocking large-scale uncertainty quantification. This paper introduces a physics-informed deep learning framework that combines a hybrid MaxViT (CNN-Transformer) backbone with progressive transfer learning and differentiable physical constraints. Trained on 24,000 synthetic 128×128 binary images, the model predicts 2×2 permeability tensors with near-machine precision for symmetry (ε_sym = 3.95×10⁻⁷) and positive-definiteness (100%), while reducing unexplained variance by 33% over a supervised baseline. Inference completes in ~120 ms per sample on a single GPU, unlocking real-time core analysis and Monte Carlo workflows.

## Method Summary
The framework uses a three-phase progressive curriculum on a publicly available dataset of 24,000 synthetic 2D binary pore scans with lattice-Boltzmann permeability labels. A pretrained MaxViT-Base ( adapted for single-channel 128×128 inputs) serves as the encoder, paired with a 4-output regression head for tensor components. Phase 2 establishes a supervised baseline with D4-equivariant augmentation applied jointly to images and tensors, progressive layer unfreezing, and differentiable penalties for symmetry (L_sym) and positive-definiteness (L_pos). Phase 3 introduces advanced augmentations (morphological ops, elastic warps, cutout) and prioritizes off-diagonal loss weighting. Phase 4 freezes the backbone, integrates porosity conditioning via FiLM (γ,β modulation at Stages 2–4), and applies stochastic weight averaging (SWA) and exponential moving averaging (EMA). Test-time D4 augmentation with inverse-transform ensembling yields final predictions.

## Key Results
- **Predictive accuracy**: Variance-weighted R² = 0.9960 (component R²: K_xx=0.9967, K_xy=0.9758) on 4,000 held-out samples.
- **Physical constraints**: Symmetry error ε_sym = 3.95×10⁻⁷; 100% of predictions are positive-definite.
- **Speed**: 120 ms inference per sample (10³–10⁴× faster than lattice-Boltzmann simulation).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hybrid MaxViT architecture captures both local pore-scale geometry (via convolutional stem) and long-range spatial dependencies (via transformer blocks), essential for modeling anisotropic flow fields. Progressive transfer learning leverages ImageNet-pretrained weights to stabilize training on limited scientific data, while the three-phase curriculum gradually increases task complexity and data diversity. Differentiable penalties enforce Onsager reciprocity and positive-definiteness to machine precision, projecting predictions onto the physically admissible manifold without sacrificing data fidelity. The FiLM conditioning allows seamless porosity modulation without retraining, enhancing generalization across porosity ranges.

- **D4 equivariance**: Why needed—ensures rotational consistency of permeability predictions under 90° rotations; quick check—validate that rotating input yields rotated tensor output.
- **Progressive curriculum**: Why needed—stabilizes training by scaffolding from simple to complex objectives; quick check—track validation R² after each phase.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input Image -> MaxViT Encoder (Stages 1–4) -> FiLM Modulation (at Stages 2–4) -> Global Pool -> Regression MLP (768→256→128→4 with GELU/Dropout) -> Permeability Tensor (K_xx, K_xy, K_yx, K_yy).
- **Critical path**: Phase 4 execution: backbone frozen; FiLM parameters trained alongside head; SWA from epoch 400 (η=5e-6); EMA (λ=0.9999) maintained; test-time D4 ensemble (8 passes) averaged with inverse tensor transforms.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Hybrid CNN-Transformer balances local feature extraction and global context; penalty weights (λ_sym, λ_pos) trade constraint strictness vs. data fit; frozen backbone preserves pretrained features but limits adaptation; FiLM adds conditioning capacity with minimal overhead.
- **Failure signatures**: 
  - Low off-diagonal R² (<0.90) and high RRMSE (>25%) on near-isotropic samples (anisotropy ratio ≈1.1).
  - Symmetry error ε_sym > 1e-5 or any negative diagonal values (K_xx ≤ 0).
  - Validation loss divergence during Phase 3 when augmentations are too aggressive.
- **First experiments**: 
  1. Reproduce Phase 2 supervised baseline without D4 augmentation to isolate augmentation impact.
  2. Ablate FiLM conditioning by removing γ,β and concatenating porosity instead.
  3. Sweep λ_sym and λ_pos (e.g., {0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5}) to map constraint-penalty tradeoff surface.

## Limitations
- Dataset is synthetic 2D binary images; real 3D multi-phase porous media may degrade performance.
- Progressive curriculum and hybrid architecture lack full ablation; individual component contributions are not quantitatively isolated.
- Physical constraint enforcement via penalties may not generalize outside training distribution (e.g., extreme morphologies).
- Speedup claim (120 ms/sample) is hardware-specific (NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada); other GPUs may vary.
- Implementation details for elastic deformation (Equation 21) and failed self-supervised pretraining are underspecified, adding replication uncertainty.

## Confidence
- **Predictive accuracy (R²=0.9960)**: High (reproducible on given dataset with specified protocol).
- **Constraint satisfaction (ε_sym=3.95e-7, 100% PD)**: High (enforced via differentiable penalties to machine precision).
- **Speedup (10³–10⁴× over LBM)**: Medium (hardware-dependent; LBM baseline configuration not detailed).
- **Generalizable principles**: Medium (evaluated on a single synthetic 2D dataset; real-world transfer untested).
