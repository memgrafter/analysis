# MRI-to-CT synthesis using drifting models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28498  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28498  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** One-step, millisecond MRI-to-CT synthesis with state-of-the-art accuracy on pelvic datasets (e.g., SSIM 0.948, PSNR 30.82 dB, RMSE 0.030 on SynthRAD2023).  

## Executive Summary
The need for fast, accurate MRI-to-CT synthesis drives the development of MR-only radiotherapy workflows. This paper introduces a conditional drifting model that learns a continuous distribution drift during training. The core mechanism is an attraction-repulsion field in raw image space, which pushes generated samples toward the real CT distribution. This internalization allows one-step inference at test time, taking only milliseconds per slice.

On two pelvic datasets (Gold Atlas, SynthRAD2023), the model achieves state-of-the-art or competitive quantitative results. On SynthRAD2023 it reports SSIM 0.948, PSNR 30.82 dB, RMSE 0.030, outperforming UNet, VAE, WGAN-GP, PPFM, and diffusion variants (DDIM, FastDDPM), and matching DDPM quality with dramatically faster inference. Visual results show sharper cortical bone edges and fewer artifacts at tissue boundaries. The model also exhibits lower pixel-wise uncertainty compared to diffusion and PPFM baselines. These findings highlight the method's potential for clinical settings where both accuracy and speed are critical.

## Method Summary
The method uses a conditional generator `fθ`, implemented as a UNet, that takes an MRI image `m` and a noise vector `ϵ` (drawn from a simple distribution) as input and outputs a synthetic CT image `ĉ`. The training objective is a drifting loss that encourages `ĉ` to be at equilibrium under a data-dependent vector field `V_{p,q}`. This field is computed by sampling real CT patches `c+` (positive) and other generated patches `c−` (negative) from the same MRI, then applying a kernel `k(a,b)=exp(-||a-b||²/τ)` to weight attractions and repulsions. Specifically, `V_{p,q}(ĉ) = V⁺_p(ĉ) + V⁻_q(ĉ)`, where `V⁺` aggregates real patches and `V⁻` repels generated patches. The loss `L_drift = ||fθ(m,ϵ) - sg(fθ(m,ϵ) + V_{p,q})||²` (with stop-gradient) pushes the generator to produce samples that are unmoved by the drift. An optional L1 term `λ1 ||ĉ - c||₁` aids pixel-wise accuracy. Training proceeds by iteratively sampling patches, computing the drift, and updating `fθ` via backpropagation. At inference, the generator produces a CT volume in one forward pass, enabling real-time performance.

## Key Results
- On SynthRAD2023, the model attains SSIM 0.948, PSNR 30.82 dB, RMSE 0.030, surpassing UNet, VAE, WGAN-GP, PPFM, and diffusion variants (DDIM, FastDDPM), and matching DDPM quality with far faster inference.
- One-step inference takes milliseconds per slice, orders of magnitude faster than iterative diffusion models.
- Produces sharper cortical bone edges and fewer artifacts at bone-soft-tissue boundaries compared to baselines; pixel-wise uncertainty maps show lower variability than diffusion and PPFM.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The model achieves one-step, millisecond inference by internalizing the generative transport dynamics into the training process, avoiding iterative sampling.
- **Mechanism:** During training, the model learns a continuous "drifting" vector field (Eq. 4–9) that describes how a generated sample should move toward the real data distribution. This field is defined via an attraction-repulsion mechanism in raw image space using a kernel (Eq. 8). The training loss (Eq. 12) forces the generator's output to match the equilibrium point of this field. At test time, the learned generator directly maps MRI input (plus noise) to a CT sample in one step; the transport process is already encoded in the weights.
- **Core assumption:** The equilibrium fixed-point condition (Eq. 10) can be effectively learned via the drifting loss (Eq. 12) with a simple UNet-like backbone and that this learned mapping sufficiently approximates the conditional data distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "drifting models—which learn a continuous distribution drift during training—for conditional synthesis, enabling one-step inference at test time (milliseconds)..."
  - [section 3.3]: Details the drifting field formulation ( Eqs. 4–9) and the training update rule (Eq. 11) converted to the drifting loss (Eq. 12).
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers (e.g., `59038` on "Comparative Analysis of GAN and Diffusion") highlight the clinical need for fast inference, contextualizing the efficiency contribution.
- **Break condition:** If the drifting field fails to converge to a stable fixed point during training (e.g., training loss plateaus at a high value), the one-step generator may not approximate the target distribution, leading to poor image quality.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The attraction-repulsion mechanism in raw image space improves preservation of high-frequency anatomical details (e.g., cortical bone edges) compared to methods that operate in feature space or use adversarial/cycle losses.
- **Mechanism:** The kernel-based interactions (Eq. 5–6) explicitly encourage a generated pixel `ˆc` to be attracted to nearby real pixels `c+` and repelled from other generated pixels `c−`. This direct, non-linear adjustment in intensity space may better preserve sharp transitions and reduce blurring/artifacts at tissue interfaces compared to L1/L2 losses (prone to smoothing) or adversarial training (prone to hallucinations).
- **Core assumption:** Performing the drift computation directly on image intensities (rather than in a latent space) provides a more faithful and controllable signal for preserving fine-grained spatial structures critical for bone segmentation and dose calculation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Qualitatively, it produces sharper cortical bone edges and reduced artifacts at bone-soft-tissue boundaries..."
  - [section 4.1 & Figure 1]: Visual comparisons show drifting producing sharper bone (e.g., cortical bone, sacral curvature) than UNet/VAE (blurry) and WGAN-GP (artifacts). Quantitatively, it often exceeds these baselines in SSIM/PSNR.
  - [corpus]: Corpus signal indicates neighboring work focuses on GAN artifacts (`59038`) and diffusion over-smoothing (`255777`), aligning with the problems drifting appears to mitigate.
- **Break condition:** If the kernel bandwidth `τ` is poorly chosen (too large/small), the attraction/repulsion may either fail to correct local details or over-correct, introducing new artifacts. Evidence of failure would be persistent blurring (τ too large) or speckled noise/over-sharpening (τ too small) in high-contrast regions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The model exhibits lower inference uncertainty (pixel-wise variability across random seeds) than diffusion and PPFM baselines, suggesting more stable and deterministic outputs.
- **Mechanism:** Because synthesis is a deterministic one-step function `fθ(m, ϵ)` where `ϵ` is only a minor noise input, the output space is less sensitive to `ϵ` than in iterative diffusion models where small noise perturbations can accumulate. The drifting field's training objective (Eq. 12) uses stop-gradient and sample-based interactions that may encourage a smoother, more robust generator mapping.
- **Core assumption:** The variance across repeated inferences with different `ϵ` is a valid proxy for epistemic/model uncertainty relevant to clinical reliability. A lower variance implies the model's output is less dependent on arbitrary random initialization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.4 & Figure 4]: Presents pixel-wise standard deviation maps from 20 samples. The paper states: "the proposed drifting model shows a distinct uncertainty pattern... highlighting fewer or more localized regions, suggesting improved control of sampling variability."
  - [abstract]: Mentions "lower inference uncertainty evidenced by pixel-wise standard deviation maps."
  - [corpus]: Corpus is weak here; neighbor papers do not specifically discuss inference uncertainty metrics for sCT. Evidence is primarily from the paper's own analysis.
- **Break condition:** If the generator's mapping is highly non-linear or poorly conditioned, even a one-step model could show high sensitivity to `ϵ`. Evidence would be standard deviation maps from drifting showing widespread high variance similar to PPFM/DDPM, indicating the attraction-repulsion field hasn't produced a stable fixed-point mapping.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Conditional Generative Modeling**
  - *Why needed here:* The task is conditional image-to-image translation (MRI → CT). The engineer must understand how to condition a generative process (here, via a drifting field) on a source image, distinct from unconditional generation.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the conditioning variable `m` (MRI) modulate the drifting field `Vp,q(ˆc)` in Eq. 4? (Answer: It defines the conditional distributions `p(c+|m)` and `q(c−|m)` from which positive/negative samples are drawn, making the drift field MRI-dependent.)

- **Concept: Kernel-Based Density Estimation/Dynamics**
  - *Why needed here:* The core attraction-repulsion mechanism uses a kernel density estimate (Eq. 5–6) to define interactions between samples. Understanding kernel choice (Gaussian, bandwidth `τ`) is critical for interpreting the "neighborhood" in image space that influences the drift.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the effect of decreasing the kernel bandwidth `τ` in Eq. 8 on the drifting field's locality? (Answer: A smaller `τ` makes `k(a,b)` decay faster with distance, making attraction/repulsion more local and sensitive to fine details but potentially noisier.)

- **Concept: Distribution Shift via Optimal Transport Analogy**
  - *Why needed here:* Drifting models are framed as learning a dynamics to shift a simple source distribution (generator outputs conditioned on MRI+noise) to the target CT distribution. This connects to concepts of manifold matching and vector-field based transport.
  - *Quick check question:* In the fixed-point equation (Eq. 10), what does it mean for `fθ(m, ϵ)` to equal `fθ(m, ϵ) + Vp,q(...)`? (Answer: It means the generator's output is already at equilibrium—applying the drift field induces no net change, indicating the generated sample's distribution matches the target conditional distribution.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Conditional Generator (`fθ`)**: A UNet-like backbone taking concatenated MRI and noise `ϵ` channels as input, outputting a CT image `ˆc`.
  2. **Drifting Field Module**: Computes `V+_p(ˆc)` and `V−_q(ˆc)` (Eqs. 5–6) by sampling real (`c+`) and generated (`c−`) patches, applying kernel `k(·,·)` (Eq. 8), and computing weighted averages. This is detached from generator gradients via `sg(·)` in the loss.
  3. **Loss Computation**: `Ldrift` (Eq. 12) compares the generator's output before and after adding the computed drift. Optional `L1` term (Eq. 13) for pixel-wise fidelity.
  4. **Training Loop**: For each batch, generate `ˆc`, sample real/generated patches, compute drift, calculate `Ltotal`, backpropagate only through the generator.
  5. **Inference Engine**: A single forward pass of `fθ(m, ϵ)`.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize `fθ` (UNet).
  2. For each training iteration:
     a. Forward pass `ˆc = fθ(m, ϵ)`.
     b. Sample 16 real patches `c+~p(·|m)` and 16 generated patches `c−~q(·|m)` (from `ˆc`).
     c. Compute `V+_p(ˆc)` and `V−_q(ˆc)` using kernel `k` on these patches.
     d. Compute `Ldrift = ||fθ(m,ϵ) - sg(fθ(m,ϵ) + Vp,q)||²`.
     e. Optionally add `λ1 * ||ˆc - c||₁`.
     f. Backpropagate `Ltotal` and update `θ`.
  3. After convergence, save `fθ` for one-step inference.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Kernel bandwidth `τ`**: Small `τ` → finer detail preservation but risk of noise/instability; large `τ` → smoother, more global adjustments but potential blurring. Must be tuned.
  - **Loss weighting (`λ_drift`, `λ1`)**: High `λ1` improves pixel accuracy but may suppress generative diversity; high `λ_drift` emphasizes distribution matching at potential cost of L1 fidelity. Tradeoff between realism and exact HU matching.
  - **Patch sampling strategy**: Number/size of patches for drift computation affects computational cost and locality. Smaller patches capture local details but increase variance; larger patches are more stable but less sensitive to fine edges.
  - **Backbone capacity**: A more powerful backbone (e.g., transformer) might better represent complex drifts but increases memory/compute. The paper uses a standard UNet.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Blurry bone edges**: Likely `λ1` too high or `τ` too large, forcing output toward smooth L1 optimum.
  - **Checkerboard/noise artifacts**: Could indicate `λ_drift` too high or patch sampling too sparse, making the repulsion term unstable.
  - **Poor cross-dataset generalization**: Model overfits to training scanner/sequence characteristics. Check performance drop on the second dataset (SynthRAD vs. Gold Atlas).
  - **High inference uncertainty (per Fig. 4)**: Drift field failed to produce a stable fixed point; might need more training, different `τ`, or architectural changes.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce loss curves**: Train on a small subset (5 patients) and verify `Ldrift` and `Ltotal` decrease steadily. A plateau suggests optimization failure.
  2. **Visualize kernel interactions**: For a fixed `ˆc` patch, manually compute `k(ˆc, c+)` for several `c+` patches. Confirm that nearest-neighbor real patches (in intensity) have highest kernel weights, validating the attraction mechanism.
  3. **One-step sanity check**: After minimal training (1 epoch), run inference on a validation MRI. The output should already be a plausible CT-shaped image (correct intensities, anatomy roughly present). If output is pure noise or MRI copy, the generator conditioning or initialization is broken.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### 1. Clinical acceptability for radiotherapy planning and PET/MR attenuation correction
The authors explicitly state that future studies should integrate radiotherapy planning and PET/MR attenuation experiments to quantify dosimetric and functional impact. However, the current evaluation is limited to image fidelity metrics (SSIM, PSNR, RMSE) and qualitative anatomy; downstream tasks like dose calculation and attenuation correction are not assessed. Resolving this requires task-specific evaluations, including DVH metrics, gamma analysis for dose distributions, and SUV accuracy in PET/MR attenuation correction across a diverse patient cohort.

### 2. Extension to unpaired or semi-supervised training
The paper focuses on paired supervised training and does not explore unpaired or semi-supervised regimes. Extending drifting models to unpaired translation—potentially by combining with cycle or structural consistency constraints—could broaden applicability. Needed evidence would be the development and evaluation of an unpaired version on standard benchmarks, compared against paired supervised performance using image similarity and downstream task metrics.

### 3. Impact of advanced architectures and adaptive kernels
The discussion suggests that more expressive architectures (e.g., transformers) or adaptive, spatially varying kernels could further enhance performance, but no systematic investigation was performed. Resolution would involve ablation studies comparing different backbone networks (CNN, transformer, hybrid) and kernel designs (fixed vs. spatially adaptive) on the same datasets, using quantitative metrics and qualitative assessment.

### 4. Generalization to other anatomies and multi-center scenarios
The model was evaluated only on male pelvic data; extending beyond the pelvis to other anatomies (brain, head-and-neck, thorax) and assessing performance under domain adaptation is essential to understand generalization. Required evidence includes training and testing on multi-center datasets covering diverse anatomies and acquisition protocols, with evaluation of both image fidelity and potential task performance to assess robustness.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (kernel bandwidth τ, noise distribution, exact UNet architecture) are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction and potentially affecting performance.
- Evaluation limited to two pelvic datasets; unknown generalizability to other anatomies, scanners, or pathologies.
- Uncertainty reduction claim lacks independent validation or statistical testing.

## Next Checks
- **Reproduce loss convergence and fixed-point condition:** Train the model on a small subset (e.g., 5 patients) and verify that both `Ldrift` and `Ltotal` decrease steadily and that the drift vector `V_{p,q}` becomes small after training, indicating convergence to a fixed point.
- **Ablate kernel bandwidth τ:** Train models with varying τ (e.g., 0.05, 0.1, 0.2) and evaluate bone edge sharpness (using a boundary detection metric) and overall SSIM/PSNR to determine sensitivity to this hyperparameter.
- **Cross-dataset generalization test:** Train on Gold Atlas and test on SynthRAD2023 (and vice versa), measuring degradation in quantitative metrics and qualitative bone detail to assess robustness to domain shift across datasets.
