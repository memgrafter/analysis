# InterPol: De-anonymizing LM Arena via Interpolated Preference Learning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15220
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15220
- **Reference Count**: 29
- **Primary Result**: INTERPOL achieves average 95.1% accuracy and 95.8% AUROC in de-anonymizing top commercial LLMs on Chatbot Arena data, enabling drastic reductions in interaction cost for ranking manipulation attacks.

## Executive Summary
This paper exposes a critical vulnerability in LLM leaderboards that rely on human preference voting for anonymity, such as LMSys's Chatbot Arena. The authors introduce INTERPOL, a method that can accurately identify which model produced a given anonymous response. It works by creating challenging synthetic "hard negatives" through parameter interpolation between a copy of the target model and its base, then training a detector via a multi-stage, adaptively curated triplet preference learning objective. Evaluated on real human preference data, INTERPOL vastly outperforms conventional baselines and remains highly effective even between closely related model families. The demonstrated utility in reducing the cost of ranking manipulation attacks confirms this as a severe, practical threat to the integrity of anonymous LLM benchmarking.

## Method Summary
INTERPOL's core innovation is generating effective hard negatives for pair-wise model discrimination. It begins by fine-tuning a copy of a candidate model to mimic the target model's style on unlabeled text. Then, it performs linear interpolation in parameter space between this mimic copy and the original candidate model, producing a continuum of model variants. Responses from these interpolated models serve as hard negatives that are stylistically close to both the target and candidate, forcing the detector to learn nuanced stylistic fingerprints. A triplet-based preference learner is trained on (target response, candidate response, interpolated hard negative) triplets. The training employs an adaptive curriculum that dynamically selects the most informative interpolated models as training progresses and uses an iterative, multi-stage procedure to refine the detector.

## Key Results
- INTERPOL achieves **95.1% average accuracy** and **95.8% average AUROC** in distinguishing responses from GPT-4o, Gemini-Pro, and Claude-4 on the Arena human preference dataset.
- It significantly outperforms strong neural baselines (89.8–90.8% accuracy) and classic baselines like TF-IDF (70.9–84.4%) and Bag-of-Words (70.9–88.7%).
- The method is robust within model families, achieving **100% accuracy** on Gemini-Pro vs. Gemini-Flash and **91.5%** on Claude-4 vs. Claude-3.5.
- In simulated ranking manipulation attacks, INTERPOL reduces required vote-submission interactions by up to **38%** for moderate attacks and **~70%** for aggressive attacks, proving the attack's practical efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by exploiting the high-dimensional, continuous nature of model parameter spaces. Linear interpolation between a model and a fine-tuned mimic generates a spectrum of models whose outputs are stylistic hybrids. These "in-between" responses are exceptionally effective as hard negatives because they are perceptually similar to both the target and candidate models, yet are unambiguously from the candidate's lineage. The triplet preference learning framework forces the detector to focus on the subtle, consistent stylistic biases that persist through fine-tuning and interpolation, which are the true "fingerprints" of a base model family. The adaptive curriculum ensures training concentrates on the most discriminative regions of the interpolation space, making the learning process sample-efficient and robust.

## Foundational Learning
1.  **Triplet Loss for Metric Learning**: *Why needed*: To learn a representation where the target model's responses are closer to each other than to any candidate model's responses. *Quick check*: Does the loss function explicitly pull positive pairs (target-target) together and push negative pairs (target-candidate) apart?
2.  **Parameter Interpolation (Linear Mode Connectivity)**: *Why needed*: To generate a dense set of model variants with interpolated stylistic traits for creating hard negatives. *Quick check*: Are model weights combined as θ_interp = αθ_mimic + (1-α)θ_candidate for multiple α values?
3.  **Adaptive Curriculum Learning**: *Why needed*: To prioritize the most challenging and informative hard negatives (specific interpolation coefficients) during training, improving sample efficiency. *Quick check*: Is there a mechanism that updates the sampling distribution of hard negatives based on the detector's current performance?
4.  **Multi-Stage Training**: *Why needed*: To progressively refine the detector, starting with easier distinctions and moving to harder ones, stabilizing learning. *Quick check*: Is the training process divided into epochs/stages where the set or weighting of hard negatives evolves?
5.  **Model Mimicry via Fine-Tuning**: *Why needed*: To create a variant of the candidate model that stylistically converges toward the target model, establishing a meaningful interpolation path. *Quick check*: Is a copy of the candidate model fine-tuned on a corpus (e.g., target model's unlabeled outputs or public data)?
6.  **Leaderboard Anonymity Assumption**: *Why needed*: The entire attack relies on the premise that model outputs contain persistent, discernible stylistic signatures even after post-training (e.g., RLHF, SFT). *Quick check*: Does the paper show that signature persists across different decoding temperatures and prompt sets?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:
Candidate Model (θ_c) -> Fine-Tune on Unlabeled Text -> Mimic Model (θ_m)
Candidate Model (θ_c) & Mimic Model (θ_m) -> Parameter Interpolation (for α in [0,1]) -> Interpolated Models (θ_interp)
Target Model (θ_t), Candidate Model (θ_c), Interpolated Models (θ_interp) -> Generate Responses -> Response Repository
Response Repository -> Adaptive Curriculum Selector -> Hard Negative Triplet Sampler -> Triplet Preference Learner (Detector)

**Critical Path**: 1) Fine-tune candidate to create mimic. 2) Interpolate between candidate and mimic weights to generate a bank of interpolated models. 3) For each target response, sample a candidate response and a challenging interpolated response (hard negative). 4) Train the detector to rank the target above the candidate and hard negative. 5) Adaptively adjust which interpolated models (i.e., which α values) are used as hard negatives based on detector loss.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- *Interpolation Breadth vs. Computational Cost*: Sampling more α values creates a richer hard negative set but increases response generation and storage costs.
- *Curriculum Aggressiveness vs. Stability*: A curriculum that rapidly shifts to very hard negatives may destabilize training; a slower, smoother adaptation is safer but may be less sample-efficient.
- *Mimic Fidelity vs. Signature Preservation*: Over-fitting the mimic to the target might erase the candidate's core signature; the fine-tuning must balance mimicking style with retaining family-identifying traits.

**Failure Signatures**:
- The detector fails to generalize, showing high training accuracy but low test accuracy on held-out prompts or model pairs.
- Performance collapses on within-family pairs if the fine-tuning/mimicry step erases the base model's signature entirely.
- The adaptive curriculum fails to improve over random sampling, indicating a poorly designed selection heuristic.
- The method is ineffective if the target model applies strong, non-linear post-processing (e.g., heavy paraphrasing, style randomization) to its outputs.

**First Three Experiments**:
1.  **Baseline Sanity Check**: Replicate the main result on a small, controlled dataset (e.g., 1k prompts) with one model pair to verify the implementation of triplet loss and interpolation matches expected accuracy (>90%).
2.  **Component Ablation**: Systematically remove core components (no interpolation, random curriculum, no mimicry) to quantify their individual contribution to the final AUROC/accuracy.
3.  **Cross-Family Generalization Probe**: Test the detector trained on GPT-4o vs. Claude-4 pairs against a completely unseen model family pair (e.g., Llama 3.1 vs. Mistral) to assess true out-of-distribution generalization capability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How robust is INTERPOL against active countermeasures, such as response-level style randomization or learned adversarial perturbations designed to obscure stylistic fingerprints?
- Does the vulnerability persist for smaller, less capable open-weight models that might have more homogeneous training data and weaker stylistic signatures?
- Can the techniques be extended to de-anonymize models in scenarios with a very large candidate pool (e.g., hundreds of models), and how does detection accuracy scale with pool size?

## Limitations
- **Data Dependency**: Evaluation is on static, historical Chatbot Arena human preference data; the method's efficacy on real-time, dynamic voting streams or different prompt distributions is unproven.
- **Countermeasure Evasion**: The paper does not evaluate INTERPOL against potential anonymization strategies a model provider could actively deploy, such as output perturbation or inference-time style control.
- **Generalization Scope**: While strong on three top-tier commercial models, it is unclear if the findings and method scale effectively to the diverse ecosystem of smaller, specialized, or open-source models.

## Confidence
- **Severe Practical Threat**: High. The combination of high accuracy on real data and demonstrated attack cost reduction provides strong empirical evidence.
- **Core Mechanism Validity**: High. The ablation studies and component analysis consistently show each part (interpolation, triplet learning, curriculum) is necessary and contributes significantly.
- **Generalization to New Models**: Medium. Performance on unseen within-family pairs is excellent, but generalization to entirely new architectures or to models with intentional obfuscation remains an open question.
- **Real-World Attack Feasibility**: Medium-High. The simulation assumes an attacker can generate responses from candidate models and knows the target model; this is plausible for a malicious entity with API access, but adds operational constraints.

## Next Checks
1.  **Active Defense Evaluation**: Implement and test common response-level anonymization techniques (e.g., synonym replacement, sentence shuffling, style transfer) as a post-processing step on target model outputs to measure the drop in INTERPOL's detection accuracy.
2.  **Scalability Stress Test**: Re-run the ranking manipulation attack simulation with a candidate pool of 50-100 models (using open-weight models) to determine the practical limits of the method's efficiency gains and accuracy decay.
3.  **Temporal Robustness Check**: Train a detector on Arena data from a specific time window (e.g., 2023Q4) and test its accuracy on responses from the same models collected several months later (e.g., 2024Q2) to assess sensitivity to model updates and data drift.
