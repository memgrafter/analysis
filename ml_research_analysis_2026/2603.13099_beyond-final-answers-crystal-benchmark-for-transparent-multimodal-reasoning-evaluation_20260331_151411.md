# Beyond Final Answers: CRYSTAL Benchmark for Transparent Multimodal Reasoning Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13099
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13099
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Causal Process Reward (CPR) with curriculum (CPR-Curriculum) improves Match F1 by 32% (+0.153) on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, while additive reward strategies fail.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal benchmarks that judge only final answers encourage shortcuts and obscure reasoning quality. CRYSTAL addresses this by providing a diagnostic benchmark with 6,372 instances, each with verifiable intermediate reasoning steps generated via a Delphi-inspired multi-agent pipeline. It introduces step-level metrics (Match F1, Ordered Match F1) that expose systematic failures—such as universal cherry-picking and disjointed reasoning—in 20 state-of-the-art MLLMs, including frontier commercial systems. For training, the multiplicative Causal Process Reward (CPR) coupled with a curriculum (CPR-Curriculum) successfully improves reasoning fidelity without manual step annotation, yielding a 32% Match F1 gain via GRPO.

## Method Summary
CRYSTAL consists of a dataset and an evaluation/training framework. The dataset provides (image, question, answer, reference_steps) tuples. References are built by having four diverse MLLMs generate candidate reasoning steps, which are clustered via semantic similarity (all-distilroberta-v1, τ=0.35), medoid-selected, ordered by dependency, validated by a fifth MLLM, and human-checked. Evaluation requires models to output JSON with `reasoning_steps` and `answer`. Match F1 computes precision/recall via greedy 1:1 semantic matching of steps; Ordered Match F1 multiplies by the longest-increasing-subsequence ratio (α=0.3). For training, GRPO optimizes a Causal Process Reward: if the final answer is correct, reward = `a_w + s_w * F1_step`; if incorrect, reward = `s_w * F1_step * λ` (λ=0.3). CPR-Curriculum first trains with answer-only reward (Phase 1) then introduces full CPR while progressively increasing example difficulty (by reference step count) in Phase 2.

## Key Results
- Universal cherry-picking: all models exhibit high precision but low recall, indicating they generate only a safe subset of correct steps.
- Non-monotonic scaling trade-offs: larger models do not consistently improve step coverage or ordering.
- Disjointed reasoning: no model preserves more than 60% of matched steps in correct order.
- CPR-Curriculum with GRPO yields a 32% Match F1 improvement (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 0.480→0.633); additive reward combinations fail with training collapse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Step-level semantic matching exposes hidden failures.** By decomposing reasoning into atomic steps and matching each predicted step to a reference via sentence embeddings, Match F1 computes precision (fraction of predicted steps that align) and recall (fraction of reference steps covered). A high precision but low recall reveals cherry-picking, where models avoid generating necessary intermediate steps. The core assumption is that semantic similarity approximates logical equivalence. Evidence: [abstract] notes these failures are invisible to answer accuracy; Section 3.2 defines the greedy matching; Section 4.2/Table 2 show near-universal P/R asymmetry across 20 models. Break condition: the encoder may conflate distinct steps in the borderline similarity range (0.35–0.70), though human agreement studies (Section S5) show 100% agreement below threshold and 84% overall.

**Mechanism 2: Delphi-inspired multi-agent pipeline generates robust references.** Four independent MLLMs generate candidate steps; these are embedded and clustered (similarity ≥ τ=0.45) to group paraphrastic steps; a medoid is selected per cluster; a fifth MLLM validates logic, coherence, and grounding; a human gate ensures final correctness. This aggregates Diverse outputs into a consensus chain, reducing single-model bias. Core assumption: clustering converges on a minimal, correct reasoning path. Evidence: [abstract] describes the pipeline; Section 3.1 details phases; Section 4.4 shows encoder/threshold stability; use of architecturally diverse generators (Qwen, InternVL, Gemma, Llama) mitigates correlated bias. Break condition: if all generators share a blind spot (e.g., omit a reasoning pattern), the references will perpetuate it.

**Mechanism 3: Multiplicative CPR with curriculum aligns accuracy and step fidelity.** CPR defines per-token reward as `aw + sw * F1_step` if correct, else `sw * F1_step * λ`. The multiplicative link means a model cannot maximize reward by being correct with poor steps (only gets `aw`) or by good steps with a wrong answer (heavily discounted). CPR-Curriculum first trains with answer-only reward to establish format/accuracy, then introduces full CPR at a lower learning rate while progressively increasing example complexity (by step count). PCGrad resolves gradient conflicts. Core assumption: the reward structure incentivizes both correct answers and faithful step coverage; the curriculum prevents early collapse. Evidence: [abstract] reports +32% Match F1; Section 3.3 defines reward and curriculum; Section 4.5/Table 3 shows CPR-Curriculum gains (+7.67pp Acc, +0.153 F1) while additive Composite fails; Figure 5 shows stable vs. collapsing training; Section S6.4 confirms cross-model transfer (InternVL3.5-4B: +8.15pp Acc, +0.401 F1). Break condition: word-overlap-based F1_step cannot verify numeric correctness, leading to calculation regressions (Figures S11, S13).

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
CRYSTAL Reference Pipeline: (Q, I, A) -> Four MLLMs (Qwen, InternVL, Gemma, Llama) -> Candidate steps -> Sentence encoder (all-distilroberta-v1) -> Similarity graph -> Connected components (τ=0.45) -> Medoid selection -> Dependency ordering -> Fifth MLLM (Molmo) validation -> Human gate -> Reference steps.  
Metrics Engine: Reference steps + Predicted steps -> Sentence encoder -> Greedy 1:1 matching (cosine ≥0.35) -> Match F1 / Ordered Match F1.  
Training Module: Dataset with references -> GRPO sampling (K=5) -> Parse JSON -> Compute F1_step & Accuracy -> CPR reward -> PCGrad update -> Curriculum scheduler (sort by step count) -> Model update.

Critical path:  
1. Data assembly: Gather (image, question, answer) tuples.  
2. Reference generation: Run multi-agent pipeline to produce step chains.  
3. Evaluation: Generate model predictions; compute metrics via encoder matching.  
4. Training: Initialize base model (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B); run GRPO with CPR; follow curriculum schedule; monitor validation Match F1.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Multi-agent generation overhead vs. reference diversity/quality: Using 4 large models is expensive but reduces single-model bias.  
- Fixed encoder/threshold vs. adaptability: Simplicity and stability (Section 4.4) but may not capture domain-specific semantics.  
- Multiplicative CPR vs. additive: Stronger incentive alignment but requires careful weight balancing to avoid gradient instability.  
- Curriculum by step count vs. true semantic difficulty: Step count is a proxy; some short chains may be conceptually harder than long ones.

Failure signatures:  
- Cherry-picking: High Precision, low Recall (e.g., LLaVA-v1.6 P=0.961, R=0.370).  
- Disordered reasoning: High Match F1 but low LIS ratio (<0.6) among matched steps.  
- Training collapse: Additive Composite reward leads to accuracy collapse and NaN gradients.  
- Numerical regression: CPR improves step coverage but may degrade precise calculation on math problems.

First 3 experiments:  
1. Metric validation: Conduct human agreement study on 100 step pairs across adversarial similarity bands to confirm encoder threshold τ=0.35 yields zero false matches below threshold.  
2. Baseline evaluation: Score 20 MLLMs on CRYSTAL test set to reproduce Findings 1–4 (P/R asymmetry, non-monotonic scaling, order failure, cherry-picking).  
3. GRPO replication: Train Qwen2.5-VL-3B with CPR-Curriculum; expect +30% Match F1 gain over additive baselines, with stable training (no NaN) and improved recall without catastrophic accuracy drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can reference reasoning steps be generated to encompass all valid multimodal reasoning paths, avoiding penalization of alternative correct approaches?** The multi-agent pipeline prioritizes consensus, which may discard minority but valid strategies, biasing evaluation against diverse reasoning. Evidence: Conclusion, Limitations states "References may not capture all valid paths." Resolution would require multiple gold-standard chains per instance and measuring stability across reference sets.
2. **Do domain-specific sentence encoders or adaptive similarity thresholds improve Match F1’s discriminative power across specialized reasoning domains?** The fixed encoder and threshold were selected on a mixed benchmark; they may not generalize to domains like mathematical proofs vs. spatial narratives. Evidence: Conclusion, Limitations notes this. Resolution would involve evaluating various encoders and thresholds on domain-specific benchmarks and correlating with human judgments.
3. **Does the Causal Process Reward (CPR) generalize to a broad spectrum of MLLM architectures, and what architectural factors mediate its effectiveness?** Only two architectures (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3.5) have been tested; architectural differences may affect CPR's efficacy. Evidence: Supplementary Section S6.4 poses this question. Resolution would require applying CPR-Curriculum to diverse MLLMs and analyzing correlation with architectural characteristics.
4. **How can Ordered Match F1 be extended to model causal step dependencies, and would this better predict human reasoning quality and downstream performance?** Current LIS-based metric checks order but not logical prerequisite relationships; steps can be ordered yet lack causal links. Evidence: Conclusion, Limitations. Resolution would incorporate explicit dependency constraints (e.g., graph alignment) and validate against human coherence ratings and task performance.

## Limitations
- Encoder may conflate semantically distinct reasoning steps in borderline similarity (0.35–0.70), inflating Match F1.
- Delphi references may inherit systematic omissions from generator biases despite clustering and human validation.
- CPR's word-overlap reward cannot verify numeric correctness, leading to calculation regressions in math-heavy tasks.
- Reproduction is hindered by unspecified dataset URL/license, incomplete evaluation prompts, and lack of access to commercial model APIs.

## Confidence
- **Match F1 exposes hidden failures:** High (strong multi-model evidence; consistent P/R asymmetry across 20 models).
- **Delphi pipeline yields reliable references:** Medium (human gate helps, but risk of shared generator biases remains).
- **CPR-Curriculum improves reasoning:** Medium (gains demonstrated, but additive collapse and numerical trade-offs indicate limitations).

## Next Checks
1. **Encoder validation:** Conduct human evaluation on 200 borderline step pairs (similarity 0.35–0.70) to quantify false positive/negative rates for the chosen threshold.
2. **Bias audit:** Manually annotate 500 CRYSTAL instances to identify systematic omissions in reference steps compared to alternative valid reasoning paths.
3. **Numerical integrity:** Test CPR-trained models on the math subset of CRYSTAL using exact numeric matching alongside Match F1 to quantify calculation accuracy trade-offs.
