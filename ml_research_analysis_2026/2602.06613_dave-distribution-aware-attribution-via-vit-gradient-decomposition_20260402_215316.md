# DAVE: Distribution-aware Attribution via ViT Gradient Decomposition

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06613  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06613  
- **Reference count:** 29  
- **Primary result:** DAVE achieves state-of-the-art localization on ImageNet-1k, with GridPG improvements up to +11.92 pp on DeiT-III-B and EnergyPG scores up to 82.23% on DeiT-B, while producing high‑resolution, faithful attribution maps for Vision Transformers.

## Executive Summary
DAVE introduces a novel gradient decomposition approach to generate stable, high‑resolution pixel‑level attribution maps for Vision Transformers (ViTs). It addresses the common patch‑grid artifacts and coarse explanations by decomposing the input gradient into an effective transformation (stable, locally equivariant) and an operator variation term (unstable). The effective transformation is averaged over small spatial transformations and Gaussian perturbations, suppressing architecture‑induced artifacts. Evaluated on ImageNet-1k, DAVE sets new state‑of‑the‑art localization scores and yields sharper, object‑aligned maps for inherently interpretable B‑cos ViTs.

## Method Summary
The method first computes the gradient of the target class score with respect to the input image. This gradient is decomposed into two components: the effective transformation, which is stable under local transformations, and the operator variation, which captures unstable, artifact‑prone variations. Only the effective transformation is retained. To further enhance stability, this component is averaged across multiple perturbed versions of the input: small spatial transformations (e.g., translations, rotations) and Gaussian noise perturbations. The resulting averaged gradient serves as the pixel‑level attribution map, leveraging distribution‑aware averaging to reduce grid artifacts and improve faithfulness.

## Key Results
- DAVE yields GridPG improvements of up to +11.92 percentage points on DeiT‑III‑B and achieves top EnergyPG scores (e.g., 82.23% on DeiT‑B) on ImageNet‑1k.
- Pixel deletion curves exhibit flatter slopes, indicating more faithful and consistent attributions compared to existing methods.
- For B‑cos ViTs, DAVE produces sharper, object‑aligned attribution maps with noticeably reduced background noise versus gradient‑ and attention‑based alternatives.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The decomposition isolates the gradient component that is locally equivariant—i.e., stable under small input transformations—which corresponds to meaningful, semantically coherent features. In contrast, the discarded operator variation term captures high‑frequency fluctuations and artifacts introduced by the ViT’s patch processing. Averaging the effective transformation over spatial transformations and Gaussian perturbations further smooths residual noise and enforces consistency across similar inputs, leading to high‑resolution, stable attribution maps that better align with object boundaries.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Input image → ViT forward pass → Compute input gradient → Decompose into effective transformation & operator variation → Retain effective transformation → Average over spatial transformations & Gaussian perturbations → Output attribution map

**Critical path**  
1. Forward pass through ViT to obtain target logit.  
2. Backpropagation to get input gradient.  
3. Gradient decomposition (matrix factorization or analytical split).  
4. Loop over perturbations: apply transform/noise, recompute effective transformation.  
5. Average all effective transformations and produce final map.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Quality vs. cost:* Multiple perturbations improve robustness but increase memory and compute (many forward/backward passes).  
- *Perturbation scale:* Larger spatial offsets and noise amplitudes may oversmooth details; careful calibration needed.  
- *Decomposition precision:* Approximations might discard useful information; the exact formulation must balance fidelity and stability.

**Failure signatures**  
- Oversmoothing: overly large perturbation radii lead to blurry attributions that lose fine details.  
- Mode collapse: if the decomposition is too aggressive, important gradients (e.g., subtle edges) may be eliminated.  
- Domain mismatch: performance degrades on datasets with very different texture or layout from ImageNet.
