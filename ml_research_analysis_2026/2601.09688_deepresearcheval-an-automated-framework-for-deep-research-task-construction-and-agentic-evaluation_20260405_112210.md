# DeepResearchEval: An Automated Framework for Deep Research Task Construction and Agentic Evaluation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.09688
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09688
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Automated evaluation of 9 systems on 100 tasks; top quality: Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research (8.51/10); top factual correctness: Manus (82.3% Right statements ratio).

## Executive Summary
Deep research tasks—complex, multi-source information synthesis—pose unique evaluation challenges due to annotation-intensive creation, static metrics, and unreliable fact-checking for uncited claims. This paper introduces DeepResearchEval, an automated framework that addresses these gaps through (1) persona-driven task construction with two objective filters (Task Qualification, Search Necessity) to ensure multi-source retrievability, and (2) agentic evaluation combining Adaptive Point-wise Quality Evaluation (task-specific dimensions/weights) and Active Fact-Checking (verifies all statements via web search, even uncited ones). The authors evaluate nine state-of-the-art systems on 100 tasks each, finding Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research leads in overall quality (8.51/10) while Manus achieves the highest factual correctness (82.3% Right statements). Notably, all systems score lower on task-specific criteria than on general scores, exposing a performance gap in meeting specialized research demands.

## Method Summary
The framework consists of two core modules. The Task Constructor automatically generates research queries by instantiating diverse personas (e.g., “medical researcher”, “policy analyst”) and applying two filters: Task Qualification checks complexity and answerability; Search Necessity ensures the task requires information from multiple sources. This yields a corpus of 100 challenging, multi-source tasks. The Evaluator then scores system outputs through agentic processes: Adaptive Point-wise Quality Evaluation breaks down the report into granular aspects (e.g., depth, coherence, citation use) tailored to the persona and task, assigning weighted scores; Active Fact-Checking independently verifies every factual claim—cited or not—by issuing web searches and cross-referencing results. The final assessment combines quality and factual correctness metrics, enabling consistent, scalable evaluation of deep research agents without human annotation.

## Key Results
- Evaluated 9 systems on 100 tasks each; Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research achieved the highest quality score (8.51/10); Manus achieved the highest factual correctness (82.3% Right statements ratio).
- All systems exhibited lower scores on task-specific criteria compared to general scores, indicating a systematic shortfall in specialized requirement fulfillment.
- The automated task construction produced tasks with low average citation uptake (0.0) and moderate relatedness (FMR 0.463), suggesting limited reliance on provided sources.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DeepResearchEval works because its two-stage design directly tackles the core evaluation bottlenecks. Persona-driven task generation diversifies query intent and, via the two filters, guarantees that tasks are both complex and genuinely multi-source—forcing systems to perform retrieval and synthesis rather than recall. The agentic evaluator mimics human judgment: Adaptive Point-wise Quality Evaluation aligns scoring with the nuanced priorities of each research persona (e.g., a policy analyst values legislative implications over technical details), while Active Fact-Checking eliminates reliance on cited sources by proactively verifying every claim against the live web. This combination yields a holistic, reliable assessment that is both automated and sensitive to task-specific success criteria.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Persona-driven task generation**  
   *Why needed*: To simulate realistic research intents that vary across domains and expertise levels, ensuring evaluation covers a broad spectrum of deep research scenarios.  
   *Quick check*: Does each task specify a clear persona and context? Does it require synthesis beyond simple fact lookup?  

2. **Task Qualification & Search Necessity filters**  
   *Why needed*: To objectively enforce multi-source retrieval and exclude tasks solvable by a single document or parametric knowledge, maintaining evaluation integrity.  
   *Quick check*: Are tasks automatically rejected if they fail either filter? Do qualified tasks have at least two distinct information needs?  

3. **Adaptive Point-wise Quality Evaluation**  
   *Why needed*: To move beyond generic rubrics and assess how well outputs meet the specific goals and conventions of the given persona.  
   *Quick check*: Are evaluation dimensions derived from the persona description? Is the weighting configurable per task?  

4. **Active Fact-Checking**  
   *Why needed*: To guarantee factual reliability by treating every claim as if it were uncited, thereby exposing hallucinations and outdated information.  
   *Quick check*: Does the evaluator perform a live web search for each atomic claim? Are results aggregated to determine “Right”, “Wrong”, or “Not Supported”?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Persona Generator → Task Qualification Filter → Search Necessity Filter → Task Repository  
System Output → Adaptive Quality Scorer → Quality Score  
System Output → Active Fact Checker → Factual Score  
Quality Score + Factual Score → Aggregated Evaluation  

**Critical path**  
1. Persona/task creation passes filters → 2. Task stored → 3. System produces report → 4. Adaptive quality scoring applies persona-specific rubric → 5. Active fact-checking verifies every claim via web search → 6. Scores combined → 7. Final metrics.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Automation vs. nuance*: Filters simplify task selection but may discard borderline tasks that are still valuable.  
- *Adaptivity vs. stability*: Task-specific weights allow tailored evaluation but reduce comparability across tasks.  
- *Active fact-checking cost*: Verifying every claim is thorough but computationally heavy and may retrieve noisy results.

**Failure signatures**  
- Low average citation uptake (0.0) indicates tasks may not incentivize citation use.  
- Moderate FMR (0.463) suggests evaluated outputs often drift from expected topic relevance.  
- High variance in fact-checking outcomes points to ambiguous claim decomposition or retrieval mismatches.

**First experiments**  
1. Baseline pipeline validation: Run the full framework end-to-end on a trivial system (e.g., random snippet retrieval) to confirm filters and evaluators are operational.  
2. Filter ablation: Disable Search Necessity and re-evaluate a random subset of systems to quantify impact on task difficulty and score distribution.  
3. Weight sensitivity test: For a fixed set of tasks, vary the weights of quality dimensions (e.g., depth vs. clarity) and observe ranking stability of top systems.

## Limitations
- Framework validation scope: Low citation uptake and moderate relatedness raise questions about task representativeness and external verification.
- Fact-checking generalizability: Active Fact-Checking remains untested on nuanced, contextual, or “unreasonable” claims.
- Statistical robustness: Reported performance gaps lack significance tests or confidence intervals, risking overfitting to the specific task set.

## Confidence
- Claim: “Automated persona-driven task construction ensures multi-source retrieval.” → **Medium**  
- Claim: “Agentic evaluation improves factual correctness via universal verification.” → **Medium-Low**  
- Claim: “All systems underperform on specialized task-specific criteria.” → **Medium**  

## Next Checks
1. Independent task replication: Generate 100 research queries via crowd-sourcing (not persona templates) and re-evaluate top 3 systems to test framework generalizability and filter robustness.
2. Fact-checking audit: Manually verify 100 randomly sampled uncited claims from system outputs using domain experts to calculate precision/recall of Active Fact-Checking against human judgment.
3. Error stratification analysis: Categorize failure cases where task-specific scores drop (e.g., query ambiguity, source scarcity) across all 9 systems to identify bottleneck dimensions and weight calibration issues.
