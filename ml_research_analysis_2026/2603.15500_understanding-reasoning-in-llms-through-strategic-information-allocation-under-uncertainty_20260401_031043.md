# Understanding Reasoning in LLMs through Strategic Information Allocation under Uncertainty

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15500
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15500
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Reasoning performance correlates with uncertainty externalization (epistemic verbalization), not specific tokens like "Wait", and its removal during distillation causes severe performance collapse.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the unclear mechanisms behind LLM "Aha moments" and self-correction by proposing an information-theoretic framework that decomposes reasoning into two components: procedural information (step-by-step execution) and epistemic verbalization (externalization of uncertainty). Theoretically, it proves that purely procedural reasoning can stagnate after an incorrect trajectory divergence, while sporadic epistemic verbalization guarantees continued information acquisition toward sufficiency. Empirically, strong reasoning performance correlates with the externalization of uncertainty rather than any particular surface token. Distillation experiments show that removing epistemic verbalization from training data drastically degrades performance, and successful distillation depends on alignment between the teacher's epistemic style and the student model's capabilities.

## Method Summary
The authors model reasoning as a closed-world self-Bayesian process where the model iteratively updates its belief about a latent reasoning state. Procedural reasoning generates step-by-step deductions but can become informationally stagnant if an early error leads to a divergent, self-reinforcing trajectory with no new information. Epistemic verbalization—expressions of doubt, reconsideration, or uncertainty—is treated as an intervention that forces the model to recontextualize and draw from a broader information space, escaping stagnation. Theoretical analysis formalizes these dynamics and proves convergence guarantees under conditions of sporadic epistemic verbalization. Empirically, they correlate reasoning accuracy with the frequency of uncertainty-related tokens across benchmarks and conduct distillation experiments by training smaller models on traces with/without epistemic content.

## Key Results
- Purely procedural reasoning trajectories can become informationally stagnant after an initial error; theoretical proof shows no guarantee of convergence without external information.
- Sporadic epistemic verbalization guarantees convergence to a correct belief state under the self-Bayesian model, as it induces information gain.
- Empirical performance across reasoning benchmarks strongly correlates with the degree of uncertainty externalization, not with specific tokens like "Wait".
- Distilling only procedural traces (removing epistemic verbalization) causes severe performance degradation (e.g., -25% on DeepSeek-32B), while preserving epistemic traces aligns student success with teacher epistemic style.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework explains reasoning breakdowns and recoveries as an information dynamics problem. An incorrect procedural step can trap the model in a locally consistent but globally wrong belief state (informational stagnation), as subsequent steps only reinforce the erroneous path. Epistemic verbalization acts as a strategic "information injection" that disrupts this reinforcement by externalizing uncertainty, prompting the model to access and integrate alternative evidence or perspectives. This reopens the information space, allowing the reasoning process to continue toward sufficiency. The guarantee holds because verbalizing uncertainty requires the model to acknowledge missing or conflicting information, which is a necessary condition for updating beliefs away from a stagnant, incorrect state.

## Foundational Learning
- **Information-Theoretic Reasoning**: Treats reasoning as a process of reducing uncertainty about the solution state. *Why needed*: Quantifies the "information gain" from each reasoning step. *Quick check*: Does the model's trajectory show decreasing entropy in its belief distribution over time without epistemic interventions?
- **Self-Bayesian Updating**: The model maintains a posterior over possible reasoning states, updated with each new deduction. *Why needed*: Formalizes how beliefs evolve and stagnate. *Quick check*: Can we model the model's confidence in intermediate conclusions as a probability distribution that collapses prematurely?
- **Epistemic vs. Procedural Content**: Procedural content executes logical steps; epistemic content comments on the reasoning process itself (e.g., "but wait", "this seems off"). *Why needed*: Distinguishes the "what" from the "how do I know" in reasoning traces. *Quick check*: Can we separate tokens/segments that state facts from those that express doubt or re-evaluation?
- **Informational Stagnation**: A state where further procedural steps yield no new information (mutual information with the true solution is zero). *Why needed*: Explains why some errors are fatal while others are recoverable. *Quick check*: Do erroneous chains often enter loops or produce increasingly similar, unvarying conclusions?
- **Strategic Information Allocation**: The idea that reasoning is goal-directed information gathering, not just step generation. *Why needed*: Shifts focus from token prediction to information economics. *Quick check*: Do high-performing models more frequently pause to "think about thinking" when uncertain?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Input Query -> Procedural Reasoner (generates steps) -> Belief State Tracker -> [Stagnation Detected?] -> Epistemic Verbalizer (triggers recontextualization) -> Information Gain -> Continued Procedural Reasoner -> Converged Belief -> Output Answer

**Critical Path**  
The core loop is: generate a procedural step -> update belief -> check for stagnation (no new info, high confidence in error). If stagnation, an epistemic verbalization must occur to inject new information and reset the information gain. Convergence is only guaranteed if epistemic verbalization happens sporadically after stagnation.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- **Verbosity vs. Efficiency**: More frequent epistemic verbalization ensures against stagnation but increases token count and latency. The theoretical guarantee only requires *sporadic* interventions, not constant ones.
- **Generality vs. Guarantees**: The closed-world self-Bayesian assumption enables formal proofs but limits direct application to open-world settings with external tools.
- **Surface Form vs. Mechanism**: Using tokens like "Wait" as a proxy for epistemic states is practical but may not capture all forms of internal uncertainty signaling.

**Failure Signatures**  
- **Procedural Monotonicity**: A long chain of steps with no epistemic interjections that ends in an incorrect answer, indicating stagnation.
- **Epistemic Collapse in Distillation**: Student model performance drops when trained on teacher traces with epistemic content removed, even if procedural content is preserved.
- **Style Misalignment**: Distillation from a teacher with high epistemic verbosity to a student incapable of producing similar patterns leads to degraded or incoherent outputs.

**First Experiments**  
1. **Stagnation Induction & Rescue**: Manually trigger a wrong early step in a reasoning chain and measure if adding a forced epistemic phrase ("Actually, let's reconsider") restores accuracy compared to a control chain without it.
2. **Cross-Model Token Correlation**: For a fixed set of reasoning problems, compute the correlation between answer accuracy and frequency of a diverse set of epistemic tokens (e.g., "maybe", "uncertain", "rethink") across models of different sizes and families, not just "Wait".
3. **Representational Probe**: Train a classifier on hidden states to predict whether the model is in a "stagnant" vs. "information-gaining" phase, and test if the classifier's predictions align with the presence of surface epistemic tokens.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the theoretical framework of reasoning as strategic information allocation be extended to open-world settings (with external observations and tool use) while maintaining formal convergence guarantees?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper explicitly acknowledges that its theoretical development assumes closed-world self-Bayesian reasoning and states: "While we discuss a conceptual extension to world-Bayesian reasoning in Appendix E, we do not provide formal theoretical guarantees or systematic empirical validation in open-world or tool-augmented scenarios. Extending the framework to such settings is an important direction for future work."
- **Why unresolved:** Without formal guarantees in open-world settings, we cannot theoretically predict when and how epistemic verbalization will aid reasoning with external tools or environment interaction.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Proofs establishing conditions under which epistemic verbalization ensures information sufficiency when external observations are available; systematic empirical evaluation comparing reasoning trajectories with and without external tools, measuring the role of verbalized uncertainty in information gain.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can epistemic verbalization be operationalized directly from model representations (e.g., attention patterns, hidden states) rather than relying on surface linguistic tokens as proxies?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper admits: "Because such internal epistemic states are not directly observable, we operationalize epistemic verbalization using surface-level linguistic behaviors in the present work. This approach provides a practical and model-agnostic starting point for analyzing the informational structure of reasoning processes, while leaving room for extension toward richer, representation-level characterizations."
- **Why unresolved:** Surface tokens are an imperfect proxy; they may not capture all forms of internal uncertainty assessment and may vary superficial patterns across models and languages. Direct representation-level measurement would provide a more robust and generalizable indicator.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Methods to identify neural correlates of epistemic states (e.g., via probing, activation analysis) that consistently correlate with performance and can be manipulated causally (e.g., through steering) to improve or degrade reasoning.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Under what conditions does epistemic verbalization yield diminishing returns or net negative impacts on reasoning efficiency, and can we quantitatively predict this trade-off?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper states: "Although epistemic verbalization enables continued information acquisition, we do not establish quantitative criteria for distinguishing when uncertainty externalization is beneficial versus when it leads to unproductive verbosity or increased computational cost. Characterizing this trade-off remains an open problem."
- **Why unresolved:** Current work treats epistemic verbalization as universally beneficial, but excessive or poorly timed expressions of uncertainty may increase response length without accuracy improvement and could even be distractive.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Controlled experiments that systematically vary the frequency, timing, and type of epistemic expressions, measuring both accuracy and efficiency (tokens used, inference time). A theoretical model relating the information-theoretic value of each verbalization to its cost.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What specific pre-training characteristics (architecture, data, objectives) make a base model amenable to successfully distilling epistemic verbalization from a teacher model?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper finds large variation in distillation outcomes: some models (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) improve greatly with LIMO data, while others (e.g., DeepSeek-Math-7B-Instruct) collapse. They attribute this to "alignment between the teacher’s and the student’s epistemic verbalization and control capabilities" but do not identify the precise factors.
- **Why unresolved:** Without knowing which base model properties align with epistemic-rich traces, we cannot predict or ensure successful distillation, limiting the practical application of small, high-reasoning datasets.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Comparative studies across diverse model families, analyzing features such as base model entropy distributions, epistemic token likelihoods, or training data composition; controlled experiments where these factors are manipulated to test causal effects on distillation success.

## Limitations
- The framework's generalizability beyond the evaluated models (DeepSeek-32B, unspecified others) is uncertain; performance on encoder-decoder or smaller architectures is unknown.
- The causal role of epistemic verbalization in distillation is inferred from observational data; confounding factors like data quality or training hyperparameters may influence results.
- Theoretical guarantees assume idealized information access and may not hold under real-world tokenization constraints or stochastic decoding.

## Confidence
- *Theoretical guarantees for convergence via sporadic epistemic verbalization*: **High** (formal proof under defined assumptions).
- *Empirical correlation between uncertainty externalization and reasoning performance*: **Medium** (consistent across benchmarks but correlational).
- *Distillation dependency on teacher epistemic style alignment*: **Low** (observational, limited model scope; no controlled ablation of style variables).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-architecture replication**: Apply the framework to encoder-decoder (e.g., T5) and significantly smaller models (<7B parameters) to test boundary conditions of the information stagnation theory.
2. **Causal distillation ablation**: Conduct controlled experiments where only epistemic verbalization patterns are manipulated in teacher outputs, isolating style from content, and measure student convergence.
3. **Mechanistic probing**: Use internal state analysis (e.g., layer-wise entropy, attention patterns) to verify whether tokens like "Wait" correspond to measurable shifts in uncertainty representation versus superficial mimicry.
