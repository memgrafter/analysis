# A multimodal slice discovery framework for systematic failure detection and explanation in medical image classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.24183
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24183
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Multimodal slice discovery improved Precision@10 over unimodal and baseline across three failure scenarios on MIMIC-CXR-JPG.

## Executive Summary
Medical image classifiers often exhibit systematic failures that are hard to detect with existing auditing tools. This paper introduces an automated framework that extends slice discovery methods (SDMs) to multimodal data—combining images, clinical report text, and metadata. The method fuses embeddings from BioMedCLIP, applies a label‑conditioned Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to identify error‑prone subgroups, and generates clinically interpretable explanations via TF‑IDF token analysis validated by CLIP‑style similarity. Evaluated on MIMIC-CXR-JPG, multimodal embeddings consistently outperformed unimodal approaches in key failure modes (spurious correlation, rare‑slice undertraining, noisy labels). The framework thus enables comprehensive black‑box auditing and transparent failure explanation in medical imaging.

## Method Summary
The pipeline operates in four stages: (1) **Embedding extraction** uses BioMedCLIP to encode images, report text, and metadata (converted to text) into vectors; (2) **Fusion & reduction** concatenates all vectors and applies PCA to obtain a joint representation; (3) **Slice discovery** fits a GMM whose likelihood is conditioned on ground‑truth labels and model predictions, biasing clusters toward high‑error samples; a probability threshold assigns samples to error slices; (4) **Explanation** compares TF‑IDF token frequencies between the error slice and a reference slice of correctly predicted samples, with CLIP‑score validation ensuring visual‑textual alignment. The approach requires only model predictions on an audit set and is evaluated by Precision@10, measuring how many of the top‑10 discovered samples share the known error attribute.

## Key Results
- **Spurious correlation (pneumothorax/device):** Image+Metadata yielded Precision@10 of 0.638, outperforming image-only (0.567). Explanation tokens captured device‑related cues like "tube".
- **Rare‑slice undertraining (cardiomegaly/lateral view):** Metadata‑only achieved Precision@10 of 0.909, successfully identifying lateral‑view errors; key token "lateral" emerged.
- **Noisy‑label injection (finding classifier):** Report+Metadata reached Precision@10 of 0.744, with the distinctive token "portable" reflecting the injected bias.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Multimodal embedding fusion (image + report text + metadata) enables the discovery of error slices that unimodal embeddings miss by capturing complementary failure signals from different data representations.
- **Mechanism:** The framework constructs a joint embedding vector `u_i` by concatenating representations from image, clinical report text, and structured metadata (converted to text). This unified space allows samples sharing semantically meaningful attributes (e.g., medical devices, view positions) to cluster together, even if the visual cues are weak or ambiguous in the image alone. The GMM then identifies clusters with high error rates in this richer representational space.
- **Core assumption:** The relevant attributes for systematic failure are encoded across at least one available modality, and equal‑weight concatenation before PCA sufficiently preserves their joint structure for clustering without modality‑specific weighting.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - abstract: "multimodal information generally allows more comprehensive and effective auditing of classifiers" and Precision@10 improvements.
  - section 2.2: describes constructing `u_i` via concatenation of all modality embeddings followed by PCA.
  - corpus (e.g., *Slicing Through Bias*) validates that image‑only SDMs miss errors tied to non‑visual factors, supporting the need for multimodal extension.
- **Break condition:** If the failure mode is not encoded in any available modality, or if concatenation destroys discriminative signal (e.g., scale mismatches), multimodal fusion provides no benefit (e.g., `Image+Text+Metadata` scored 0.513 in noisy‑label case).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Clustering via Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) in the joint embedding space, conditioned on ground‑truth labels and model predictions, isolates coherent error‑prone subgroups (slices) by balancing cluster coherence with error rate.
- **Mechanism:** The likelihood function in Eq. (2) biases GMM components toward samples that are both semantically similar (`P(u_i|S(j)=1)`) and have high error (`P(y_i|S(j)=1)P(\hat{y}_i|S(j)=1)`). Hyperparameter `γ` controls this trade‑off. Slices are formed by thresholding membership probabilities (`P(x_i|S_j) > β`).
- **Core assumption:** Systematic failures manifest as dense, separable clusters in the multimodal embedding space when conditioned on labels and predictions; the GMM density estimation accurately approximates these clusters.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - section 2.2 formulates the GMM objective `L(ϕ)` with label and prediction conditionality.
  - section 3.2 reports valid slice discovery across bootstrap iterations, with Precision@10 > baseline in most settings.
  - builds on DOMINO [2], which validated GMM‑based slice discovery for image‑only tasks.
- **Break condition:** If systematic failures are not clustered (e.g., uniformly diffuse or involve rare outliers), GMM will miss them or produce incoherent slices; noisy‑label experiment shows instability when the underperforming group is small (20%) and error is widespread.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Token‑level TF‑IDF analysis, validated by CLIP‑style cross‑modal similarity, generates clinically interpretable explanations for discovered error slices without requiring expert annotation.
- **Mechanism:** For each error slice `S_err`, a reference slice `S_ref` of correctly classified samples from the same class is constructed. Tokens from report/metadata are compared via their mean TF‑IDF scores between slices (Eq. 3); high Distinctiveness Score (DS) tokens indicate over‑represented attributes. Validity is checked by measuring how much more these tokens align with error‑slice images than reference‑slice images using a multimodal similarity score (Eq. 4) from the same CLIP model.
- **Core assumption:** Error slices share textual tokens that are both statistically distinctive and semantically related to the visual failure pattern; the CLIP encoder captures this alignment without fine‑tuning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - abstract: "Token analysis extracted clinically relevant attributes like 'tube' and 'portable'".
  - section 2.3 defines DS(t) and `r_attr` validation, reporting consistent emergence of tokens like "lateral" and "portable".
  - section 4: baseline global TF‑IDF yields generic tokens ("normal"), while slice‑aware analysis reveals specific failure tokens.
- **Break condition:** If the error slice lacks distinctive textual tokens (e.g., failure due to subtle unreported image artifacts), TF‑IDF fails to yield meaningful explanations; biased or under‑trained CLIP text encoder may invalidate `r_attr`.

## Foundational Learning
- **Slice Discovery Methods (SDMs):** Methods that automatically identify subgroups (slices) where a model underperforms. Needed because the entire framework extends SDMs to multimodal data, building on DOMINO.  
  Quick check: How does conditioning GMM on both labels `y_i` and predictions `\hat{y}_i` help focus on error‑prone clusters rather than just semantic clusters?

- **Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM):** Probabilistic density estimation that fits a mixture of Gaussians. Used as the core clustering engine to assign samples to slices based on the multimodal embedding distribution, with a modified likelihood that incorporates error information.  
  Quick check: What is the effect of hyperparameter `γ` on the balance between finding large coherent clusters vs. high‑error clusters?

- **Multimodal Representation Learning (e.g., BioMedCLIP):** Pre‑trained models that embed different modalities (image, text) into a shared semantic space. Needed to align medical images with clinical text and metadata; quality of `u_i` depends on this alignment.  
  Quick check: Why convert tabular metadata to short textual descriptions instead of using a separate tabular encoder? What are the trade‑offs?

- **Precision@K for Slice Discovery Evaluation:** Metric measuring the fraction of the top‑K most confident slice members that truly belong to the targeted error attribute. It reflects the purity and utility of the discovered slice for an auditor.  
  Quick check: Why not use recall or F1‑score for the entire slice? What does Precision@K specifically measure about the method’s utility for an auditor?

- **Black‑box Model Auditing:** The framework assumes no access to the classifier’s internals, only its predictions on the audit set. This constrains the method to post‑hoc analysis using outputs and auxiliary data.  
  Quick check: What are the limitations of an auditing approach that only uses model predictions and multimodal inputs, compared to one with full training data access?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Input Layer (image, clinical report, DICOM metadata) -> Embedding Extractor (BioMedCLIP image & text encoders; metadata converted to text and encoded) -> Fusion & Reduction (concatenation of all modality vectors, then PCA) -> Slice Discovery (GMM clustering with label and prediction conditioning) -> Explanation Module (TF‑IDF token distinctiveness between error slice and reference slice) -> Validation Module (CLIP‑score similarity `r_attr`) -> Output (error slices with top distinctive tokens and validation scores)

Critical path: Embedded multimodal data → Fusion/PCA → GMM clustering → Slice assignment → Token extraction → Reference slice construction → Distinctiveness Score calculation → CLIP validation → Final ranked attributes.

Design tradeoffs:
- Concatenation vs. learned fusion: Concatenation is simple and preserves all information but assumes equal modality importance; learned fusion could weight modalities but adds complexity.
- GMM cluster count: Not specified; too few merges distinct errors, too many yields incoherent slices. Requires tuning.
- Text dependency: Explanations rely on textual reports/metadata; if such text is missing, token analysis fails.
- Black‑box constraint: No gradient access limits attribution; must cluster in data space.

Failure signatures:
- Noisy‑label instability: GMM tends to form large clusters; when the underperforming group is small (20%) and error is widespread, metadata‑only fails often (>50% runs). Slice discovery becomes unstable.
- Modality mismatch: In spurious correlation (device presence), Image+Metadata outperforms image‑only, indicating image alone misses subtle devices. In rare‑slice (lateral view), Metadata‑only wins because the attribute is purely in metadata.
- Explanation validity: In noisy‑label runs, token "portable" emerges even though the induced attribute is "frontal view", showing the method finds correlated attributes that may not be causal.

First 3 experiments (replication plan):
1. **Spurious correlation (Pneumothorax):** Train a pneumothorax classifier with high correlation between label and presence of medical devices. Expect Image+Metadata to achieve highest Precision@10 and extract tokens like "tube", "line".
2. **Rare‑slice undertraining (Cardiomegaly):** Train a cardiomegaly classifier with only 2% positive lateral views. Expect Metadata‑only to achieve highest Precision@10 and token "lateral".
3. **Noisy‑label injection (Finding classifier):** Train a binary finding classifier with 30% label noise on frontal positive samples. Expect Report+Metadata to achieve best Precision@10 among multimodal combos, and token "portable" to emerge; observe instability in metadata‑only runs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can the multimodal slice discovery framework be adapted to handle noisy‑label injection scenarios where metadata‑based embeddings exhibit instability?**  
  Basis: explicit (Section 4 and conclusion). The authors note metadata‑only performed worst in noisy‑label settings (>50% bootstrap runs produced no valid slices) and suggest addressing data sparsity.  
  Why unresolved: Current GMM favors larger, high‑error groups; noisy labels increase error across both underperforming and other samples, making clusters unstable for small target slices.  
  Evidence needed: Systematic experiments comparing clustering only misclassified samples vs. original pipeline under varying noise rates and underperforming proportions.

- **What multimodal fusion strategies beyond equal‑weight concatenation can improve the quality of joint embeddings for slice discovery without prior modality importance?**  
  Basis: explicit (conclusion calls for exploration of more advanced fusion).  
  Why unresolved: Simple concatenation followed by PCA may discard discriminative features or amplify noise from less relevant modalities.  
  Evidence needed: Comparative studies of attention‑based fusion, tensor product networks, etc., on MIMIC‑CXR‑JPG, evaluated by Precision@10 and explanation relevance across failure scenarios.

- **Does restricting Gaussian Mixture Model clustering to misclassified samples within each class improve the detection of low‑prevalence error slices compared to the label‑aware DOMINO approach?**  
  Basis: explicit (preliminary experiment showed >100% Precision@5 gain for 5% underperforming groups).  
  Why unresolved: Initial results are limited to one iteration and a specific noisy‑label setting; generalizability to other failure types and explainability is unclear.  
  Evidence needed: Extensive bootstrap analysis across all three failure scenarios with varying underperforming proportions, comparing slice metrics and token consistency.

- **To what extent do biases in the pretrained BioMedCLIP model affect the validity and clinical interpretability of discovered error slices?**  
  Basis: inferred (framework relies entirely on BioMedCLIP without evaluating its potential biases).  
  Why unresolved: If BioMedCLIP encodes spurious correlations, embeddings and CLIP‑score may reinforce or obscure true error attributes, leading to misleading audits.  
  Evidence needed: Analysis of BioMedCLIP embeddings on diverse medical subgroups; perturbation studies with injected biases to test detection consistency.

## Limitations
- Missing critical hyperparameters (PCA dimensionality, GMM component count, membership threshold) hinders exact replication.
- The method assumes error slices form dense clusters; less dense failures (e.g., noisy‑label with small underperforming groups) lead to instability.
- Explanations depend on rich textual reports/metadata; lack thereof degrades performance.
- The framework relies on the pretrained BioMedCLIP model without assessing its potential biases, which may affect slice discovery and explanation validity.
- Evaluation is limited to a single dataset (MIMIC‑CXR‑JPG) and a single model architecture (ResNet‑18), restricting generalizability.
