# Benchmarking Self-Supervised Models for Cardiac Ultrasound View Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15339  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15339  
- **Reference count:** 20  
- **Primary result:** USF-MAE (ultrasound-specific masked autoencoder) outperforms MoCo v3 (ImageNet-pretrained) on cardiac ultrasound view classification: mean testing accuracy 99.33% vs 98.99%, macro ROC-AUC 99.99% vs 99.97%, F1-score 99.33% vs 98.99% (p=0.0048).

## Executive Summary
This study addresses reliable automated classification of cardiac ultrasound views using self-supervised learning. It compares ultrasound-specific masked autoencoder (USF-MAE) pretrained on OpenUS-46 against MoCo v3 pretrained on ImageNet, both fine-tuned on the CACTUS dataset under identical protocols. Across 5-fold cross-validation, USF-MAE achieves mean testing accuracy of 99.33% vs 98.99% for MoCo v3, macro ROC-AUC of 99.99% vs 99.97%, and F1-score of 99.33% vs 98.99%. The paired t-test on F1-scores gives p=0.0048, confirming statistical significance. The relative error reduction is 33.7%. These results highlight the benefit of domain-specific pretraining and the generative MAE objective for cardiac ultrasound.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates two self-supervised learning models—USF-MAE (masked autoencoder pretrained on OpenUS-46 ultrasound images) and MoCo v3 (contrastive pretraining on ImageNet-1K)—for classifying cardiac ultrasound views into six classes using the CACTUS dataset (37,736 images). Both models share a ViT-B/16 backbone and undergo identical fine-tuning: end-to-end with AdamW (learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 0.01), cosine learning rate scheduler, batch size 32, for 15 epochs per fold, using weighted cross-entropy loss (inverse frequency per training fold). Preprocessing normalizes inputs via sector masking/cropping (pie-slice region from bottom center, angles 210°–330°, radius 90% of image height), HSV-based annotation removal (yellow/blue/red thresholds) with 5×5 dilation and Navier-Stokes inpainting, and resizing to 224×224. Evaluation employs stratified 5-fold cross-validation, reporting accuracy, macro ROC-AUC, and F1-score, with model selection based on lowest training loss.

## Key Results
- Mean testing accuracy: USF-MAE 99.33% vs MoCo v3 98.99%
- Macro ROC-AUC: USF-MAE 99.99% vs MoCo v3 99.97%
- F1-score: USF-MAE 99.33% vs MoCo v3 98.99%
- Paired t-test on F1-scores: p=0.0048 (statistically significant)
- Relative error reduction: 33.7% (from 1.01% to 0.67%)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Domain-Aligned Pretraining Yields Discriminative Features
- **Claim:** Ultrasound-specific self-supervised pretraining (USF-MAE) yields more discriminative features for cardiac view classification than natural-image pretraining (MoCo v3) due to domain alignment.
- **Mechanism:** USF-MAE's pretraining on the OpenUS-46 ultrasound dataset exposes its MAE to ultrasound-specific visual patterns (speckle noise, sector fields, annotations). The masked patch reconstruction objective forces learning of coherent anatomical structures and context from these domain-specific inputs, creating representations attuned to cardiac ultrasound. In contrast, ImageNet-pretrained MoCo v3 lacks exposure to such artifacts and has different texture/color distributions.
- **Core assumption:** The feature distribution of cardiac ultrasound is sufficiently distinct from natural images; pretraining on the latter provides suboptimal initialization.
- **Evidence anchors:** The study directly compares USF-MAE (OpenUS-46 pretraining) vs. MoCo v3 (ImageNet-1K) with identical architectures and fine-tuning; USF-MAE consistently outperforms. The paper cites USF-MAE's origin (ultrasound-specific foundation model) and notes controlled differences only in pretraining data and objective.
- **Break condition:** If USF-MAE's advantage diminishes on a different ultrasound domain with artifact distribution diverging from OpenUS-46, or if MoCo v3 trained on a larger, diverse in-domain ultrasound corpus matches USF-MAE.

### Mechanism 2: Generative MAE Objective Captures Spatial Layout Better Than Contrastive Learning
- **Claim:** The MAE pretext task (masked patch reconstruction) is more effective than contrastive learning (MoCo v3) for learning representations that discriminate subtle anatomical view differences in cardiac ultrasound.
- **Mechanism:** Cardiac view classification requires sensitivity to fine-grained spatial arrangements of cardiac structures. MAE's reconstruction requires understanding global context and generating plausible local details, encouraging a holistic internal model of cardiac anatomy. MoCo v3's instance discrimination focuses on invariance between augmented views of the same image, possibly over-emphasizing texture and artifact invariances at the expense of precise spatial layout needed to distinguish similar views.
- **Core assumption:** The reconstruction objective induces a richer, more structurally coherent latent space for classification when the downstream task depends on precise spatial organization.
- **Evidence anchors:** USF-MAE's superior performance across multiple metrics, with minimal confusion even for similar views in the confusion matrix. The controlled experiment isolates the SSL objective as a contributing factor.
- **Break condition:** If ablation shows that replacing USF-MAE's MAE objective with contrastive on the same OpenUS-46 data eliminates its advantage, or if MoCo v3 with domain-specific augmentation that preserves spatial cues matches USF-MAE.

### Mechanism 3: Ultrasound-Specific Preprocessing Removes Confounding Artifacts
- **Claim:** The ultrasound-specific preprocessing pipeline (sector masking, annotation inpainting) contributes to performance by standardizing inputs and removing non-anatomical distractors, allowing SSL models to focus on relevant cardiac structures.
- **Mechanism:** Cardiac ultrasound images contain non-diagnostic information: sector borders, measurement markers, text overlays. Without removal, these consistent but irrelevant features could be exploited as shortcuts, leading to brittle representations. The pipeline isolates the anatomically relevant region and inpaints away colored annotations, forcing reliance on tissue texture, chamber shapes, and structural relationships.
- **Core assumption:** Manual annotations and scanner artifacts are domain-wide confounders that degrade representation quality if not removed, and this effect is significant for downstream classification.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper's methodology section details the preprocessing; the best USF-MAE model achieves per-class sensitivities >97.5%, suggesting preprocessing preserved critical information.
- **Break condition:** If applying the same preprocessing to MoCo v3 yields a disproportionate performance drop compared to USF-MAE, indicating USF-MAE's representations are less reliant on the cleaned visual field; or if retaining annotations improves performance on a dataset where annotations correlate with view identity.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (Ultrasound Frame) → Preprocessing Module (Sector Crop + Inpainting) → ViT-B/16 Backbone (SSL-Pretrained) → Linear Classification Head (6 classes)

**Critical path:** Validated preprocessing → Correct SSL weight loading → Identical fine-tuning hyperparameters → Stratified 5-fold cross-validation execution → Metric computation (macro AUC, accuracy, F1)

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *MAE Masking Ratio:* USF-MAE uses 25% masking; higher ratios force harder learning but might discard critical anatomical patches.  
- *Preprocessing Aggressiveness:* Inpainting removes all colored annotations; over-aggressive inpainting could erase subtle diagnostic textures (e.g., valve cusp details).  
- *Fine-Tuning Scope:* Full fine-tuning risks catastrophic forgetting of SSL representations; a linear probe would test representation quality but was not used.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High variance across 5 folds: indicates unstable training or data split issues (e.g., non-stratified "Random" class).  
- Random class F1 significantly lower than others: suggests overfitting to patterns of standard views, failure on out-of-distribution non-diagnostic images.  
- MoCo v3 performance drops more than USF-MAE with added real-world noise: would indicate USF-MAE's domain-specific pretraining confers robustness.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replication Baseline:** Run the exact pipeline (preprocessing, MoCo v3/USF-MAE fine-tuning) on one fold to confirm achieving ~99% accuracy and matching Table 3 metrics.  
2. **Preprocessing Ablation:** Re-run fine-tuning with the original annotated images (without inpainting) to quantify the performance delta attributable to annotation removal.  
3. **Objective Isolation:** Train a ViT-B/16 from scratch on CACTUS (no SSL) with identical fine-tuning to establish the pure supervised baseline lift provided by SSL pretraining.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extending to grading and diagnostic performance:** The authors state future work will extend beyond view classification to grading and diagnosis using CACTUS. Unresolved because only view classification was evaluated; grading/diagnosis may have different difficulty and performance patterns. Resolution would require fine-tuning both models on CACTUS-derived grading/diagnosis labels and comparing metrics.
- **Generalization to real fetal echocardiography datasets:** The authors plan to validate on real fetal echocardiography datasets. Unresolved because the current study used simulator images; real-world data includes varied image quality, artifacts, and anatomical variability. Resolution would involve direct evaluation on a large, diverse real fetal echocardiography dataset.
- **Impact on congenital heart defect (CHD) detection and multi-class diagnostic classification:** The authors aim to examine the impact on CHD detection and multi-class diagnosis. Unresolved because these complex tasks were not assessed. Resolution would involve applying USF-MAE and MoCo v3 on labeled CHD datasets, measuring sensitivity, specificity, and AUC.

## Limitations
- Preprocessing specifics (exact HSV thresholds, crop coordinates) are unreported, hindering exact replication and potentially affecting feature extraction consistency.
- Statistical assumptions: The paired t-test on 5-fold F1-scores assumes independence; possible inter-fold correlation (e.g., patient overlap) could inflate the reported significance.
- External validity: Performance may not generalize to other ultrasound devices, acquisition protocols, or cardiac pathologies not represented in CACTUS/OpenUS-46; the "Random" class definition may not reflect real-world non-diagnostic scenarios.
