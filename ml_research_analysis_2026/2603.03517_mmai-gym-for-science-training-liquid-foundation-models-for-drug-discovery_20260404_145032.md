# MMAI Gym for Science: Training Liquid Foundation Models for Drug Discovery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03517
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03517
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** The 2.6B parameter LFM2-2.6B-MMAI model, trained with MMAI Gym's domain-specific SFT+RFT recipes, achieves near-specialist performance on key drug discovery benchmarks, surpassing substantially larger generalist models while remaining far more efficient.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents MMAI Gym, a curated suite of molecular data formats and training recipes designed to teach foundation models the "language of molecules." By combining supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on over 200 reasoning tasks with reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) using task-specific rewards, the approach transforms a general-purpose LLM into a capable drug discovery agent. The resulting LFM2-2.6B-MMAI model attains state-of-the-art or competitive results on benchmarks such as MuMO molecular optimization, FGBench functional group reasoning, and TDC ADMET prediction, outperforming models orders of magnitude larger. The work demonstrates that focused domain supervision and efficient architecture can yield specialist performance without the cost of massive scale.

## Method Summary
Training proceeds in two stages using a hybrid LFM2 architecture (2.6B parameters). First, SFT is applied to a multi-task corpus spanning ADMET, optimization, retrosynthesis, and other categories, with a multi-stage balancing strategy to ensure coverage. Inputs undergo format augmentation (random SMILES↔SELFIES↔IUPAC conversions, non-canonical traversal) and are wrapped in special tokens to separate chemical syntax. SFT uses AdamW (lr=1e-5, warmup 5%, cosine decay) for 100k steps with sequence packing. Second, RFT employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with per-task rewards for validity, property improvement, and sequence length (encouraging "thinking"), plus format adherence. RFT runs for 1000+ steps (group size 8, batch 128, lr=1e-6, beta=0.4, temperature=1.4). Evaluation uses a multi-repetition protocol to aggregate paraphrased and augmented prompts.

## Key Results
- LFM2-2.6B-MMAI achieves state-of-the-art or competitive success rates on MuMO molecular optimization, surpassing larger generalists.
- It obtains top accuracy on FGBench functional group reasoning tasks.
- It matches or exceeds the 27B TxGemma model on multiple TDC ADMET prediction tasks, demonstrating regression and classification proficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Domain-specific supervision via multi-task SFT+RFT teaches coherent molecular reasoning chains, overcoming brittle in-context learning.**  
The MMAI Gym curates 200+ tasks across drug discovery modalities. SFT instills task-format adherence and domain patterns; RFT refines outputs toward chemically and biologically plausible solutions using rewards for validity, property improvement, and reasoning length. This shifts the model from generic next-token prediction to goal-directed reasoning. Core assumption: a diverse curriculum of reasoning traces can shape an efficient model’s internal representations to capture domain causal structure without massive scale. Evidence: [abstract] "supervised plus reinforcement training... to teach foundation models the 'language of molecules'"; [section 3.1] "200+ tasks spanning dozens of categories... multi-stage balancing strategy". Break condition: if task distribution or reward design misaligns with real-world validation (e.g., optimizing benchmark metrics that don’t correlate with experimental success), performance degrades on held-out OOD tests.

**Hybrid LFM2 architecture (gated short convolutions + sparse GQA) provides a compute-memory Pareto frontier for long-context molecular data.**  
ShortConv layers (kernel size 3) perform linear-time local feature extraction, while a few GQA layers inject global mixing. This design maintains expressivity for long molecular sequences and experimental records while drastically reducing quadratic attention costs. Pre-RMSNorm and SwiGLU further streamline computation. Core assumption: most molecular reasoning relies more on local chemical motifs than ultra-long-range dependencies; global context can be handled by sparse attention. Evidence: [section 2] architecture details and Figure 1 showing prefill/decode throughput superiority; [abstract] "efficient 2.6B parameter LFM2... far more efficient". Break condition: tasks requiring precise modeling of extremely long-range interactions (e.g., full protein-ligand dynamics over thousands of timesteps) may exceed the global mixing capacity of limited GQA layers.

**Chemical format augmentation and specialized tokenization force abstraction over molecular syntax, enabling format-agnostic concept learning.**  
Random SMILES↔SELFIES↔IUPAC conversions and non-canonical traversal prevent overfitting to a single string representation. Special tokens segregate chemical syntax from natural language, and molecules are wrapped in consistent tags. This teaches the model to map variations to the same underlying chemical entity. Core assumption: chemical conceptual knowledge is representation-independent; exposing the model to multiple valid serializations improves invariance to input formatting. Evidence: [section 3.2] "format augmentation helps the model to learn modality-independent molecular concepts"; [abstract] "teach foundation models the 'language of molecules'". Break condition: excessive augmentation (e.g., invalid SMILES) could introduce noise; encountering a completely novel molecular notation not covered in training may cause performance drops.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Molecular string representations (SMILES, SELFIES, FASTA)**  
  Why needed: All inputs/outputs are textual encodings of molecules/proteins; understanding syntax (branches, rings, brackets) is prerequisite to any prediction.  
  Quick check: How would you represent acetic acid in SMILES vs. SELFIES, and what does the difference imply for tokenization?
- **Concept: Task-specific reward shaping (format, thinking, correctness)**  
  Why needed: RFT optimizes for domain-specific objectives (validity, property improvement, reasoning length) that cannot be captured by simple cross-entropy loss.  
  Quick check: In a regression task, what two components make up the reward and why does the length penalty exist?
- **Concept: Hybrid sequence operators (ShortConv vs. GQA)**  
  Why needed: Understanding why the architecture mixes local convolutions and sparse attention explains efficiency claims and informs adaptation to new long-context tasks.  
  Quick check: If a new task required capturing interactions between atoms separated by >100 positions, which operator(s) would be critical and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input → format-aware tokenizer (special tokens for SMILES/FASTA) → embedding → N LFM2 blocks [pre-RMSNorm → (ShortConv | GQA) → pre-RMSNorm → SwiGLU FFN] → output head.
- **Critical path:** 1) Tokenization and augmentation (determines input distribution). 2) ShortConv layers (extract local chemical patterns). 3) GQA layers (integrate global context). 4) Reward-compatible output generation (format tags).
- **Design tradeoffs:** More GQA layers → better long-range reasoning but higher latency; fewer → faster but may miss distal dependencies. Deep ShortConv stack favors local motif learning. RFT temperature (1.4) trades off diversity vs. stability.
- **Failure signatures:** High invalid molecule rate in generation tasks (failure at chemical validity); poor OOD generalization (e.g., on URSA-expert-2026); low success rate on multi-objective optimization despite high similarity (overfitting to single properties).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Tokenization sanity check: Feed SMILES, SELFIES, and IUPAC strings of the same molecule; verify token counts and embedding similarity.  
  2. Efficiency benchmark: Measure tokens/sec for prefill/decode at 4k/8k context lengths vs. Llama3.1-8B; confirm ~2× speedup.  
  3. Task-specific RFT ablation: Train with/without thinking reward on FGBench; measure RMSE change to validate reasoning encouragement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Why does multi-task training fail to consistently outperform single-task training on TDC ADMET regression tasks?**  
  Basis: Table 4 shows single-task RFT variants occasionally achieve higher Spearman correlations than multi-task counterparts (e.g., Half Life: ST/think 0.576 vs. MT/think 0.458). Unresolved because the paper does not analyze potential negative transfer or data imbalance between regression and classification tasks. Evidence needed: ablation studies controlling for task grouping by similarity, measuring gradient conflicts, and comparing data quantities per task type while isolating reward function impacts on regression.
- **Does the thinking reward genuinely enhance chemical reasoning quality, or merely encourage verbose outputs?**  
  Basis: Section 3.3 defines a thinking reward targeting sequences up to 5,000 characters, and Tables 2–4 compare /think and /no think models with mixed gains, yet reasoning trace content is never evaluated. Unresolved because no assessment of whether longer traces contain chemically plausible steps or correlate with accuracy. Evidence needed: human expert scoring of reasoning traces for validity and relevance, correlation analysis between token count and accuracy, and reward ablation to identify optimal weighting.
- **How well does the model generalize to entirely novel chemical scaffolds or biological targets?**  
  Basis: The paper uses predefined OOD test sets (e.g., URSA-expert-2026) and mentions challenges with "new chemo-types," but these may not capture diversity of real-world scenarios with unprecedented molecular spaces. Unresolved because without stress-testing on truly unseen distributions, claims about robustness are limited. Evidence needed: evaluation on recently released or proprietary datasets with significantly different chemotypes or targets, adversarial generation of outlying molecules, and systematic studies of performance decay with increasing Tanimoto distance from training data.

## Limitations
- Exact composition of the 200+ tasks, dataset splits, and weighting—including undisclosed proprietary Insilico data—remains unknown, obscuring what the model actually learned.
- Detailed reward shaping (e.g., regression normalization, thinking penalty coefficients) and augmentation probabilities that drive RFT behavior are not specified.
- Risk of reward misalignment: optimizing for benchmark metrics may not translate to real-world experimental success.
- Generalization to out-of-distribution molecular spaces (e.g., URSA-expert-2026) is untested beyond provided benchmarks.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (SFT+RFT supervision):** Medium. Benchmark gains are compelling, but reward specifications and potential overfitting to benchmark metrics remain opaque.
- **Mechanism 2 (Hybrid LFM2 architecture):** High. Architecture details, efficiency measurements, and corroborating work strongly support the claim.
- **Mechanism 3 (Chemical format augmentation):** Low. The claimed cross-representation invariance lacks direct ablation evidence; corpus support is weak.

## Next Checks
1. **Public-data replication:** Retrain LFM2-MMAI using only open datasets (TDC, USPTO-50K, GEOM) and compare benchmark results to the paper’s numbers to isolate the impact of proprietary data.
2. **Format augmentation ablation:** Disable SMILES↔SELFIES conversion and non-canonical traversal, then evaluate cross-representation robustness (e.g., FGBench accuracy on SELFIES inputs) to quantify the benefit of multi-format training.
3. **Long-range dependency probe:** Use gradient-based attribution or attention visualization on molecules with >100 atom separations to verify that GQA layers indeed capture distal interactions as claimed.
