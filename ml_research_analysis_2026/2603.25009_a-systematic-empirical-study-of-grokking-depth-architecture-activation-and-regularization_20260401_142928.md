# A Systematic Empirical Study of Grokking: Depth, Architecture, Activation, and Regularization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25009
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25009
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** Grokking is driven by a narrow “Goldilocks” zone of weight decay that pushes weight norms below a universal threshold (~0.022), with architecture and activation modulating only the speed of reaching that threshold.

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically isolates the factors behind grokking—the phenomenon where neural networks generalize long after perfect training accuracy—using controlled experiments on modular addition (mod 97). By matching training regimes across architectures, the authors demonstrate that weight decay is the dominant control parameter, operating within a narrow optimal band that enables the transition from memorization to generalization. They find that depth effects are non‑monotonic (flat depth‑4 MLPs fail, depth‑8 residual MLPs recover), the performance gap between Transformers and MLPs shrinks dramatically under matched hyperparameters, and activation functions like GELU accelerate convergence only under lighter regularization. Across configurations, grokking consistently occurs when the RMS weight norm falls near 0.022, with all models converging to sparse Fourier representations post‑grokking, though Transformers achieve higher sparsity.

## Method Summary
The study uses the algorithmic task of modular addition (mod 97) with a fixed 20%/80% train/test split. Models include MLPs of varying depth, width, and activation (ReLU, GELU, Tanh) and a 1‑layer Transformer with AdamW. Training uses full‑batch updates, gradient clipping, and extensive sweeps over weight decay and learning rates. The primary metric is grokking delay ΔT (steps between 99% training accuracy and 99% test accuracy). Additional diagnostics track RMS weight norms and post‑training Fourier spectra of embeddings.

## Key Results
- Depth has a non‑monotonic effect: depth‑4 flat MLPs fail to grok, while depth‑8 residual MLPs recover generalization.
- The Transformer vs. MLP generalization gap shrinks to only 1.11× when their respective optimal weight decay values are used.
- GELU outperforms ReLU by up to 4.3× in speed, but only under lighter regularization regimes; under stronger decay, both fail.
- Weight decay exhibits a narrow “Goldilocks” zone (e.g., λ ≈ 10⁻³ for SGD‑trained MLPs) necessary for grokking; too little or too much prevents generalization.
- Grokking consistently occurs when RMS weight norm drops below a threshold near 0.022, regardless of architecture or activation.
- All grokked models converge to sparse Fourier representations, with Transformers achieving 98.5% sparsity in the top‑5 frequency energies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Weight decay strength is the dominant control parameter that determines whether grokking occurs, operating within a narrow “Goldilocks” regime.
- **Mechanism:** Weight decay continuously shrinks weights. Too little leaves the model in a high‑norm memorizing basin; too much prevents fitting the training data. Only within an optimal band does decay push weights into a lower‑norm region containing a generalizing solution, enabling the delayed transition.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states weight decay is dominant with a narrow Goldilocks regime. Section 5.5 shows λ=10⁻³ yields grokking for MLP, while λ=2×10⁻³ causes memorization failure; Transformer optimal λ=5.0 vs. failure at λ=0.01.
- **Break condition:** Below lower bound: model memorizes but never generalizes. Above upper bound: model cannot even memorize (training accuracy stalls <99%).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Grokking is triggered when RMS weight norm falls below a universal threshold near 0.022 (for width‑512 models).
- **Unknown:** The universality of this threshold across different model widths, depths, and architectures is not fully established (confidence: Low).
- **Mechanism:** As weight decay reduces parameters, the model eventually reaches a critical norm below which a sparse Fourier‑based solution becomes viable, causing a sharp rise in validation accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes consistent threshold ~0.022. Section 5.6 logs norms at grokking for six configurations (mean 0.0219±0.0032).
- **Break condition:** If weight norm never falls below threshold (due to weak decay or instability), grokking does not occur within training budget.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Architectural stability (residuals, LayerNorm) and activation smoothness modulate the speed of weight norm decay, affecting grokking delay but not the threshold or fact of grokking.
- **Mechanism:** Deep flat networks (depth‑4 MLP) suffer optimization pathology, causing norm plateaus. Residuals/LayerNorm stabilize gradients, enabling continued decay. Smooth activations (GELU) yield more consistent gradients, accelerating decay; ReLU can cause dead neurons under strong regularization, slowing descent.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes non‑monotonic depth effects and regime‑dependent activation speedups. Section 5.2 shows depth‑4 fails, depth‑8 residual succeeds. Section 5.4 shows GELU 4.32× faster than ReLU only at λ=5×10⁻⁴, not at λ=2×10⁻³. Section 5.6 confirms similar final norms despite huge delay differences.
- **Break condition:** Without stabilization or with activation causing dead units, weight norm may stall above threshold, preventing grokking.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Data pipeline (modular addition pairs) -> Model architectures (MLP/Transformer with configurable depth/width/activation) -> Training loop (SGD/AdamW, weight decay, full‑batch, gradient clipping) -> Evaluation suite (train/test accuracy, grokking delay, RMS weight norm, Fourier spectra).

**Critical path:** (a) Reproduce canonical grokking curve (Exp 1) to establish baseline. (b) Conduct weight decay sweep for MLP and Transformer (Exp 5) to locate Goldilocks zone and confirm weight decay’s dominance. (c) Compare activation functions at optimal λ (Exp 4) to observe speed differences while threshold remains constant.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Optimizer: MLP uses SGD; Transformer uses AdamW with decoupled weight decay requiring ~5000× larger λ.
- Depth vs. stability: Flat depth ≥4 leads to optimization failure; residual connections + LayerNorm enable stable deep training but increase compute.
- Activation: GELU is smoother and faster under light regularization but can be over‑regularized; ReLU is more robust to strong decay but slower.
- Weight decay precision: Optimal range is narrow (e.g., MLP λ between 5×10⁻⁴ and 10⁻³). Outside this band causes failure modes.
- Batch size: Full‑batch reduces gradient noise; mini‑batch would require retuning optimal λ.
- Training horizon: Delays can exceed 100k steps; budgets <400k risk false DNF labels.

**Failure signatures:**
- No memorization: training accuracy never hits 99% → λ too high or depth too large without residual.
- No grokking: test accuracy stays near chance after memorization → λ too low, missing residual, or activation causing dead neurons.
- High variance: large spread in ΔT across seeds (especially Transformer) indicates sensitivity to initialization and slingshot effects.
- Weight norm plateau: RMS norm stalls >0.025 without crossing threshold.
- Depth‑4 flat MLP: consistent failure signals need for stabilization.
- GELU at high λ: immediate memorization failure.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Canonical Grokking:** Train 2‑layer MLP (width 256, GELU) with SGD (lr=1e‑2, momentum=0.9, wd=2e‑3) on 20% mod‑97 addition. Expect memorize at ~1k, grok at ~30k.
2. **Weight Decay Sweep:** For depth‑4 GELU MLP (width 512, SGD, lr=3e‑2), test λ in {1e⁻⁵,5e⁻⁵,1e⁻⁴,5e⁻⁴,1e⁻³,2e⁻³,5e⁻³}. Identify Goldilocks zone (λ≈1e⁻³) where all seeds grok, and regimes where memorization collapses (λ≥2e⁻³).
3. **Activation Comparison at Optimal λ:** Using λ=1e⁻³ from Exp 2, train three MLPs identical except activation (GELU, ReLU, Tanh). Expect GELU to grok in all seeds with mean ΔT ~45k, ReLU much slower (~200k), Tanh mixed or slower.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Do grokking dynamics generalize beyond modular addition?** Experiments only on mod 97 addition; generalization to other algorithmic tasks and real data is unknown.
- **How does depth interact with weight decay in grokking?** Depth‑4 MLPs failed while depth‑8 residuals succeeded at fixed λ; joint depth‑λ effects unexplored.
- **What internal computational circuits drive grokking, and how do they differ by architecture?** Fourier sparsity is known, but evolving circuit structure, attention vs. MLP roles, and sparsity differences lack mechanistic explanation.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter opacity: Missing details (embedding dimension, bias inclusion in norm, data split) hinder replication and may shift thresholds.
- Generalization scope: Claims based on single task (mod 97 addition); unknown if weight‑norm threshold holds for other arithmetic or larger models.
- Optimizer–decay confounding: MLP (SGD) vs. Transformer (AdamW) use different scales; weight decay dominance may partly reflect optimizer effects.
- **Full-batch training:** The experiments use full-batch gradient updates, which may not generalize to mini-batch settings commonly used in larger-scale or online learning scenarios. This could affect the optimal weight decay values and the norm threshold.

## Confidence
- **Weight decay as primary driver:** Medium (Goldilocks zone robust but limited to one task and entangled with optimizer).
- **Universal norm threshold (~0.022):** Low (CV ≈ 15% indicates variability; no cross‑scale validation).
- **Architecture modulates speed, not threshold:** Medium (supported by depth/activation experiments but only few architectures).
- **Sparse Fourier solution:** High (directly observed spectra confirm high sparsity).

## Next Checks
1. **High‑seed reproducibility:** Repeat weight‑decay sweep with ≥20 seeds; confirm Goldilocks zone and norm threshold stability.
2. **Cross‑scale threshold test:** Vary width (256, 512, 1024) and use AdamW for MLP; check if ~0.022 threshold persists.
3. **Norm‑constraint experiment:** Replace decay with explicit RMS‑norm clipping; grokking should appear only when clip target ≈ 0.022.
