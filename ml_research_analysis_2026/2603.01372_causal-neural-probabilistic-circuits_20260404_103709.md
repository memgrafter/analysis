# Causal Neural Probabilistic Circuits

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.01372
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01372
- **Reference Count**: 23
- **Primary Result**: CNPC combines a neural predictor with a causal probabilistic circuit via a Product of Experts to enable exact tractable causal inference, achieving substantial out-of-distribution robustness (up to +24% accuracy) under interventions compared to standard concept bottleneck models.

## Executive Summary
Causal Neural Probabilistic Circuits (CNPC) addresses a key limitation of Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs): ignoring causal dependencies among concepts during interventions. CNPC fuses a neural attribute predictor with a causal probabilistic circuit (compiled from a causal graph) using a Product of Experts (PoE) to approximate the intractable interventional attribute distribution. This allows exact, tractable computation of interventional class predictions. Theoretically, CNPC's interventional error is compositionally bounded and can be lower than standard NPC when the circuit's interventional marginal is more accurate. Empirically, across five datasets, CNPC outperforms five baselines in task accuracy as more attributes are intervened on, with particularly large gains (up to 24%) in out-of-distribution settings (unseen rotations, adversarial perturbations, spurious correlations), demonstrating superior intervention efficiency.

## Method Summary
CNPC consists of two trained modules: a neural multi-head attribute predictor and a causal probabilistic circuit (PC) compiled from a given causal graph over attributes and the class label. For an intervention on attributes \(A_j\), the neural predictor provides an observational distribution \(P_\theta(A_{1:K}|X)\) with \(A_j\) clamped. The causal PC computes the exact interventional marginal \(P_w^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K})\) by mutilating the graph. These two distributions are fused via a Product of Experts: \(\tilde{P}^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K}|X) \propto [P_\theta^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K}|X)]^{1-\alpha} [P_w^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K})]^{\alpha}\), where \(\alpha \in [0,1]\) balances reliance on the neural predictor versus the causal structure. The final interventional class distribution uses the PC's conditional \(P_w(Y|A_{1:K})\) with the fused attribute distribution. Theoretically, the expected interventional KL error decomposes into weighted neural and PC errors plus a common PC conditional error; if the PC's interventional marginal is more accurate than the neural predictor's under intervention, CNPC's bound is tighter than NPC's.

## Key Results
- CNPC achieves higher task accuracy than five baselines as more attributes are intervened on across five datasets.
- Out-of-distribution improvements are substantial: up to 22% higher accuracy on MNISTAdd with one intervention and 24% on CelebA with two interventions.
- The balancing weight \(\alpha\) critically affects performance; optimal values are higher under OOD shifts (e.g., rotations, adversarial attacks) where the neural predictor is less reliable.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: CNPC approximates the intractable interventional attribute distribution under do-operations by fusing two complementary experts via a Product of Experts (PoE).
- **Mechanism**: For intervention \(\text{do}(j)\), CNPC computes: (i) the neural predictor's distribution with \(A_j\) clamped to its ground-truth, \(P_\theta^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K}|X) = P^*(A_j|X) \cdot \prod_{k \neq j} P_\theta(A_k|X)\), and (ii) the exact interventional marginal over all attributes from the causal PC, \(P_w^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K})\). These are combined as \(\tilde{P}^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K}|X) \propto [P_\theta^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K}|X)]^{1-\alpha} [P_w^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K})]^{\alpha}\), then used with the PC's conditional \(P_w(Y|A_{1:K})\).
- **Core assumption**: The neural predictor's evidence (input-dependent) and the causal PC's interventional marginal (causal structure-dependent) provide complementary information about the post-intervention attribute distribution. \(\alpha\) controls their relative influence.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states the PoE fusion; Section 4.2 derives it; neighboring work on NPCs and causal PCs supports feasibility.
- **Break condition**: If either expert is severely misaligned with the true interventional distribution (neural predictor arbitrary under OOD, causal PC from incorrect graph), PoE fusion may degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The causal PC enables exact, tractable computation of interventional marginals \(P^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K})\) and conditionals \(P_w(Y|A_{1:K})\) by compiling the causal graph into an arithmetic circuit.
- **Mechanism**: The causal graph (over attributes and class) is compiled into an arithmetic circuit via variable elimination scheduling. This circuit encodes the network polynomial, allowing interventional queries to be answered in a single forward pass after modifying CPD entries of intervened variables to 1 and setting evidence indicators.
- **Core assumption**: The causal graph is correct and acyclic with no unobserved confounders among the variables in the graph.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 3.3 describes compilation and interventional computation; Section 4.1 states the need for exact marginals; neighboring work on tractable causal inference validates the approach.
- **Break condition**: An incorrect causal graph (missing/extra edges) yields inconsistent interventional marginals, harming predictions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: CNPC's interventional error is compositionally bounded and can be lower than NPC's when the causal PC's interventional marginal is more accurate than the neural predictor's under intervention.
- **Mechanism**: Theoretical analysis (Corollary 5) decomposes expected interventional KL error into three terms: (i) neural predictor's interventional error (weighted \(1-\alpha\)), (ii) causal PC's interventional marginal error (weighted \(\alpha\)), (iii) PC's conditional error (unweighted). Since term (iii) is common to both NPC and CNPC, if \(\alpha>0\) and term (ii) < term (i), CNPC's total bound is tighter.
- **Core assumption**: Decomposition holds under Assumptions 1 (sufficient attributes) and 2 (structural assumption of causal graph); perfect alignment not required for comparative advantage.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 5 (Theorem 3, Corollaries 4,5) provides formal bounds; Section 6.2.4 shows CNPC surpasses NPC under OOD, where neural predictor error is large and PC marginal error is smaller; neighboring work on error decompositions in CBMs supports relevance.
- **Break condition**: If neural predictor error is very low (benign settings) or PC interventional marginal error is high (poor circuit fitting/wrong graph), increasing \(\alpha\) may harm performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Probabilistic Circuits (PCs)**: Tractable representation of joint distributions using sum/product nodes, enabling exact marginal and conditional inference in linear time. *Why needed*: CNPC's causal PC provides exact interventional marginals and conditionals without exponential complexity. *Quick check*: How do sum nodes (marginalization) and product nodes (factorization) in a PC correspond to operations in the underlying distribution?
- **Causal Graphs and do-Calculus**: Directed acyclic graphs encoding causal relationships; interventions (do-operations) modify the graph by cutting incoming edges to intervened variables. *Why needed*: The causal PC is compiled from such a graph, and interventions change the distribution according to d-separation. *Quick check*: Given graph \(A \rightarrow B \leftarrow C\), what is \(P(B|\text{do}(A=a))\) versus \(P(B|A=a)\)?
- **Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs)**: Two-stage models predicting interpretable concepts first, then the final task. Interventions overwrite predicted concepts with ground-truth. *Why needed*: CNPC is a CBM variant; standard CBMs ignore causal dependencies among concepts during intervention, which CNPC addresses. *Quick check*: In a standard CBM, if you intervene on concept \(A_1\) that causally influences \(A_2\), should the predicted distribution for \(A_2\) change? How does standard CBM handle this?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Neural Attribute Predictor -> Causal Probabilistic Circuit -> PoE Fusion Module -> Prediction Module
- **Critical path**:
  1. **Data & Graph Prep**: Obtain dataset \((X, A_{1:K}, Y)\) and define/learn causal graph \(\mathcal{C}\) over \(A_{1:K} \cup \{Y\}\). Validate acyclicity and no hidden confounders.
  2. **Train Neural Predictor**: Fit \(\theta\) on \(X \rightarrow A_{1:K}\) using cross-entropy. Evaluate attribute accuracy.
  3. **Compile & Fit Causal PC**: Use \(\mathcal{C}\) to compile an arithmetic circuit. Learn parameters \(w\) from joint distribution of \((A_{1:K}, Y)\) via maximum likelihood (e.g., EM). Validate \(P_w(Y|A_{1:K})\) accuracy.
  4. **Deployment with Interventions**: For test \(X\): no intervention uses Eq. (1); intervention set \(J\) clamps \(P_\theta(A_j|X)\) to ground-truth, computes \(P_\theta^{\text{do}(J)}(A_{1:K}|X)\) and \(P_w^{\text{do}(J)}(A_{1:K})\) via PC, fuses via PoE with \(\alpha\), computes final \(\tilde{P}(Y|X)\).
  5. **Hyperparameter Tuning**: Choose \(\alpha\) based on expected neural predictor reliability (e.g., low for in-distribution, high for OOD). Use validation with simulated interventions.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - \(\alpha\) selection: Low \(\alpha\) trusts neural predictor; high \(\alpha\) trusts causal structure. Optimal \(\alpha\) depends on OOD severity; requires validation or OOD detector.
  - Graph correctness: Advantage hinges on correct causal graph; errors invalidate interventional marginals. Graph learning/validation is a prerequisite cost.
  - Circuit scalability: Circuit size grows with graph complexity (treewidth); compilation/inference cost must be acceptable for \(K\) attributes.
  - Attribute predictor design: Only needs observational \(P(A_k|X)\) accuracy; no need to capture attribute causal dependencies, simplifying training.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Severe OOD + low \(\alpha\): Neural predictor fails, CNPC behaves like NPC and accuracy plummets.
  - Incorrect causal graph: Even with perfect \(\alpha\), interventional marginals are wrong, harming accuracy under intervention.
  - \(\alpha\) mis-specification in benign setting: High \(\alpha\) may slightly hurt by overcorrecting based on PC.
  - Circuit fitting failure: Poor likelihood on validation means \(P_w(Y|A_{1:K})\) is poor, harming all settings.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Validate PoE Fusion Against Oracle: On synthetic data with known ground-truth interventional distributions, compute true \(P^{\text{do}(j)}(Y|X)\). Compare CNPC (varying \(\alpha\)) against NPC and an oracle using true \(P^{\text{do}(j)}(A_{1:K}|X)\). Verify optimal \(\alpha \in (0,1)\) and error matches Corollary 5 decomposition.
  2. Test \(\alpha\) Sensitivity Under Controlled OOD: On MNISTAdd, degrade neural predictor accuracy (label noise/corrupted subset). Run interventions with \(\alpha \in \{0.0, 0.1, \dots, 1.0\}\). Expect optimal \(\alpha\) to increase as predictor reliability decreases. Plot task accuracy vs. \(\alpha\) for 1–3 interventions.
  3. Compare Against Causal Baselines Under Intervention: On CelebA with manual causal graph, run full experiment suite (benign, rotation, PGD, spurious). Ablate with randomly permuted causal graph. Expect valid-graph CNPC to outperform both NPC and wrong-graph CNPC, especially under OOD.

## Limitations
- Requires a correct, acyclic causal graph; errors invalidate interventional marginals.
- Balancing weight \(\alpha\) is sensitive; mis-tuning degrades OOD performance.
- Probabilistic circuit size may become intractable for many concepts (high graph treewidth).

## Confidence
- Theoretical error decomposition: **High**
- Exact interventional marginals via circuit: **Medium** (depends on graph and fitting)
- PoE fusion (empirical): **Medium** (\(\alpha\) selection heuristic)
- OOD robustness improvements: **Medium** (limited datasets)
- Overall claim of superiority: **Medium** (needs independent verification)

## Next Checks
1. **Synthetic oracle test**: On a dataset with known interventional ground truth, compare CNPC (varying \(\alpha\)) against NPC and an oracle. Confirm optimal \(\alpha\) lies in (0,1) and error matches Corollary 5.
2. **\(\alpha\) sensitivity under degradation**: Systematically degrade neural predictor accuracy (label noise, corruption) on MNISTAdd and CelebA. Plot task accuracy vs \(\alpha\) for 1–3 interventions. Expect optimal \(\alpha\) to increase as predictor worsens.
3. **Graph correctness ablation**: Replace true causal graph with a random permutation in CNPC. Compare performance against NPC and valid-graph CNPC under benign and OOD. A significant drop confirms reliance on graph validity.
