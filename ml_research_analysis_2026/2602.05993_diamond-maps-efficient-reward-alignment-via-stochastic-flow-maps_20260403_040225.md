# Diamond Maps: Efficient Reward Alignment via Stochastic Flow Maps

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05993
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05993
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Weighted Diamond Maps on SANA-Sprint 0.6B achieve GenEval score 0.83 in 10 seconds, outperforming Flux-dev (0.68, 23s) and SD3-Medium (0.70, 4.4s) with more efficient scaling than Best-of-N.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the reward alignment problem in flow and diffusion models, where existing methods are either computationally expensive (fine-tuning) or statistically inaccurate (guidance). It proposes Diamond Maps, a class of stochastic flow maps that enable efficient, inference-time alignment to arbitrary rewards. Two complementary designs are introduced: (1) Posterior Diamond Maps, which distill a pre-trained GLASS Flow into a one-step sampler for the exact posterior distribution over clean images given a noisy observation; and (2) Weighted Diamond Maps, which endow any deterministic flow map with stochasticity and a "recovery reward" to form an unbiased, consistent estimator of the reward-tilted value function. Experiments demonstrate that both designs achieve stronger reward alignment and better scaling behavior compared to prior test-time alignment techniques.

## Method Summary
Posterior Diamond Maps train a neural network to directly sample from the exact posterior distribution \( p_{1|t}(\cdot|x_t) \) in a single step. This is achieved by distilling the multi-step dynamics of a pre-trained GLASS Flow (itself derived from a denoiser) via a loss that matches the velocity field of an inner "sufficient statistic" process. Weighted Diamond Maps take a different approach: starting from any deterministic flow map, they introduce stochasticity by first re-noising the current state to an earlier time step, applying the flow map, and then reweighting the resulting samples using a combination of the target reward and a "recovery reward" term derived from the Gaussian conditional log-density. This reweighting, along with a score-based correction, yields an unbiased Monte Carlo estimator of the value function \( V_t^r(x_t) \), enabling accurate gradient-based guidance.

## Key Results
- Weighted Diamond Maps on SANA-Sprint 0.6B achieve state-of-the-art GenEval (0.83) in just 10 seconds, scaling more efficiently than Best-of-N.
- Posterior Diamond Maps exhibit superior robustness at high guidance scales, avoiding the quality degradation seen in deterministic guidance.
- Posterior Diamond Maps enable optimal Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) proposals via "Diamond Early Stop" sampling, which exactly recovers DDPM transition kernels in one step.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Posterior Distillation
**Claim:** Posterior Diamond Maps distill a GLASS Flow into a one-step stochastic sampler that directly draws from the exact posterior distribution \( p_{1|t}(\cdot|x_t) \).
**Mechanism:** The model \( X^\theta_{s,r}(\bar{x}_s | x_t, t) \) uses an internal "inner time" \( s \) to evolve a Gaussian noise sample \( \bar{x}_0 \) towards a clean sample. The target dynamics are defined by the GLASS velocity field \( \bar{u}_s(\bar{x}_s | x_t, t) \), which is derived from a pre-trained denoiser \( D_t \) via a linear reparameterization and a time remapping \( t^*(s,t) \). Distillation via a Lagrangian/Eulerian loss trains the map to match this multi-step ODE in a single step.
**Core Assumption:** The pre-trained denoiser accurately captures the marginal vector field \( u_t(x) \), and the derived GLASS ODE is a valid posterior sampler.
**Break Condition:** If the pre-trained model is biased or the one-step distillation fails to capture complex multi-step dependencies, the posterior samples will be inaccurate.

### Mechanism 2: Stochastic Reweighting for Unbiased Estimation
**Claim:** Weighted Diamond Maps turn any deterministic flow map into an unbiased value function estimator by combining renoising, a recovery reward, and score corrections.
**Mechanism:** Given \( x_t \), the method first re-noises to an earlier \( x_{t'} \) using the forward diffusion kernel. The deterministic flow map produces a clean sample \( z \). The estimator averages rewards \( r(z_i) \) weighted by \( v_i \propto \exp(r(z_i) - \frac{\|x_t - \alpha_t z_i\|_2^2}{2\sigma_t^2} + \delta_i^{\text{score}}) \), where the quadratic term is the "recovery reward" approximating \( \log p_t(x_t|z) \), and \( \delta_i^{\text{score}} \) corrects for the non-posterior distribution induced by renoising.
**Core Assumption:** The conditional distribution \( p_t(x_t|z) \) is accurately modeled as Gaussian with variance \( \sigma_t^2 \); the score correction and numerical quadrature for \( \gamma_i \) are precise for small \( t' - t \).
**Break Condition:** If the probability path is non-Gaussian or the recovery reward is mis-scaled, the estimator becomes biased; effective sample size (ESS) can collapse for flat or multimodal reward landscapes.

### Mechanism 3: Exact DDPM Transition Proposals
**Claim:** Posterior Diamond Maps implicitly contain the exact DDPM transition kernel \( p^{\text{DDPM}}_{t'|t} \) as a valid one-step transition, enabling optimal SMC proposals.
**Mechanism:** By stopping the inner flow at an inner time \( s = r^* \) chosen such that \( t^*(r^*, t) = t' \), the inner state \( \bar{x}_{r^*} \) can be mapped back to an outer state \( x_{t'} \) via the sufficient statistic \( S_{r^*,t} \). This yields \( x_{t'} \sim p^{\text{DDPM}}_{t'|t}(\cdot|x_t) \) in one step, avoiding explicit denoise-then-re-noise cycles.
**Core Assumption:** The GLASS process correctly models the DDPM forward/backward kernels, and the mapping \( x_{t'} = \alpha_{t'} S_{r^*,t}(\bar{x}_{r^*}, x_t) \) is bijective and numerically stable.
**Break Condition:** Mismatches between GLASS and the true DDPM kernel, or instability in the \( g^{-1} \) function near \( t=0,1 \), break the equivalence.

## Architecture Onboarding
Pre-trained Flow Model -> GLASS Velocity Field -> Diamond Flow Map Model (with Renoiser for Weighted) -> Guidance/SMC Orchestrator

**Critical Path:**
1. **Training (Posterior):** Sample clean \( z \), draw noisy \( x_t \sim p_t(\cdot|z) \), sample inner noise \( \bar{x}_s \sim p_s(\cdot|z) \). Compute target GLASS velocity \( \bar{u}_s \) from pre-trained \( D_t \). Train \( X^\theta_{s,r} \) to minimize \( \|\partial_r X^\theta_{s,r} - \bar{u}_r(X^\theta_{s,r})\|^2 \). At inference, sample \( \bar{x}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I) \), map to \( z = X^\theta_{0,1}(\bar{x}_0|x_t,t) \) for posterior sampling.
2. **Inference (Guidance):** At each outer step \( t \), for Weighted: renoise to \( x_{t'}(\epsilon) \), map to \( z_i = X_{t',1}(x_{t'}(\epsilon_i)) \), compute weights \( v_i \) with recovery reward and score correction, estimate \( \nabla_x V_t \), update \( x_t \).

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Posterior vs. Weighted: Posterior requires GLASS distillation (higher training cost, needs model internals) but gives true posterior samples with \( O(N) \) sample complexity. Weighted works with any distilled map (lower training cost) but has ESS-dependent sample complexity \( O(N/\text{ESS}) \), often needing more particles \( K \).
- Stochasticity vs. Determinism: Stochastic maps trade per-iteration compute (multiple forward passes) for better Monte Carlo efficiency in value estimation.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Posterior collapse: Posterior Diamond Map samples show near-zero variance → under-distilled or high flow map error.
- Reward hacking: Weighted Diamond Map generates high-reward but low-quality samples → mis-scaled recovery reward \( 1/\sigma_t^2 \) or critically low ESS (check softmax entropy).
- Numerical instability: \( r^* \) becomes NaN near \( t=0 \) or \( 1 \) → requires time clamping (e.g., to \([0.01, 0.99]\)).
- Gradient explosion: Very large reward scales cause unstable updates; Posterior Diamond Maps are more robust due to inherent stochasticity.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. **Posterior Sampling Quality (CIFAR-10/CelebA):** Compute FID vs. NFEs for Posterior Diamond Maps against the GLASS Flow baseline. Success: Diamond Map FID approaches GLASS Flow with 1-8 steps; failure if significantly worse.
2. **Guidance Scaling vs. Best-of-N (GenEval):** Plot GenEval score vs. NFE for Weighted Diamond Maps (\( K=4 \)) and Best-of-N baselines. Success: Diamond scaling curve dominates Best-of-N; failure if slope is shallower or plateau occurs earlier.
3. **SMC Robustness at High Reward Scales:** For linear inverse problems (e.g., super-resolution), sweep guidance scale \( b_t \) and measure PSNR/FID. Success: Posterior Diamond maintains quality at high \( b_t \) where deterministic guidance degenerates; failure if performance matches the degraded deterministic baseline.

## Limitations
- Posterior Diamond Maps depend on accurate pre-trained denoisers; one-step distillation may not fully capture complex multi-step dynamics, and errors propagate.
- Weighted Diamond Maps rely on the Gaussian conditional assumption for the recovery reward; this can break for non-VP probability paths, leading to bias.
- Numerical instability near \( t=0,1 \) requires heuristic time clamping.
- Evaluation is primarily on SANA-Sprint 0.6B; broader validation across architectures and domains is needed.

## Confidence
- **Scaling efficiency (Weighted):** Medium — strong empirical result on one model, but generalizability unproven.
- **Posterior robustness:** Medium — theoretical justification is solid, but practical performance is model-dependent.
- **Weighted consistency (unbiasedness):** Medium — holds under Gaussian assumption; assumes correction terms and quadrature are accurate for small inner steps.
