# Can Large Language Models Simulate Human Cognition Beyond Behavioral Imitation?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27694  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27694  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** LLMs achieve behavioral imitation (e.g., conditioning on author history improves semantic scores by ~0.1–0.19) but show limited cognitive internalization, with a consistent gap of ~0.33 between using in-domain recent work and out-of-domain history across five cognitive dimensions.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) can simulate human cognition beyond surface-level behavioral imitation. Using a novel benchmark built from the publication trajectories of 217 researchers, the study evaluates cross-domain, temporal-shift generalization to test whether models can transfer stable cognitive patterns across distinct research topics. Results indicate that while LLMs can imitate an author’s stylistic and topical preferences when conditioned on their past work, they fail to internalize deeper, cross-domain cognitive invariants (e.g., foundational premises, strategic reasoning). Human evaluation confirms that over 89% of outputs are classified as imitation, with more than 95% failing at cognitive internalization. The authors conclude that current LLMs, trained on aggregated human text, smooth out individual cognitive signatures and rely on lazy shortcuts that mimic concrete methods rather than abstract reasoning principles.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a benchmark from arXiv papers of 217 AI researchers. Each paper’s introduction is segmented into cognitive elements (Background, Motivation, Approach, Solution) via LLM-based extraction (validated on a small human-annotated subset). Papers are clustered by topic using embedding-based k-means; the most recent cluster serves as the test set, while older clusters form the training history. For each test paper, the model is prompted to generate an Approach/Solution for a new problem, conditioned on retrieved papers from the author’s history. Evaluation occurs at three levels: lexical (ROUGE, BERTScore), semantic (LLM-as-a-judge), and cognitive consistency across five dimensions (Foundational Premise, Strategic Preference, Logic Mapping, Keyword Style, Taboo Avoidance). Models are tested under conditions including BASEMODEL, SELF (author’s out-of-domain history), SELFAKIN (author’s in-domain recent history), LEAKY (oracle cognitive trajectory), and with prompting or fine-tuning enhancements.

## Key Results
- Conditioning on an author’s history (SELF) yields behavioral imitation gains (~0.1–0.19 over random) in lexical and semantic metrics.
- Cognitive internalization is limited: performance gains from in-domain recent work (SELFAKIN) exceed those from out-of-domain history (SELF) by an average of 0.33 across five cognitive dimensions.
- Human evaluation classifies >89% of outputs as behavioral imitation (B/C), with >95% failing internalization (C/D).
- Oracle access (LEAKY) produces much larger gains, indicating that abstraction—not execution—is the primary bottleneck.
- Prompting techniques (PERSONALITYTRAIT, COGNITIVEGUIDE) offer modest cognitive improvements but remain bounded by base capacity; unified fine-tuning (TRAINUNIFIED) improves lexical metrics but degrades cognitive consistency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLMs achieve behavioral imitation by capturing surface-level statistical patterns from author-specific histories but fail to internalize cross-domain cognitive invariants because their training objective optimizes for next-token prediction over aggregated human text, which smooths out individual cognitive signatures.
- **Mechanism:** The self-supervised learning objective minimizes prediction error on token sequences, encouraging the model to learn high-frequency lexical and topical correlations within an author’s work. When conditioned on that history (SELF), it reproduces domain-specific terminology and stylistic patterns. However, because the objective does not explicitly reward abstracting stable, cross-domain reasoning strategies, the model defaults to mimicking concrete methods or phrases from the retrieved context rather than transferring the author’s underlying cognitive framework to a novel domain.
- **Core assumption:** Human cognition exhibits stable, individual-level patterns across research topics; LLMs can only access externalized artifacts (text) of that cognition.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Results show LLMs achieve behavioral imitation... but cognitive internalization remains limited: performance gains when using the author’s recent work (SELFAKIN) exceed those from out-of-domain history (SELF) by an average of 0.33..." [section 3.2] "The discrepancy between providing the latest in-domain research (SELFAKIN) and the out-of-domain research histories (SELF) is consistently large... suggesting that LLMs struggle to identify and internalize higher-level cognitive invariants..." [section A.1] "LLMs are trained to predict the next token by learning from an aggregation of human-generated text... Human cognition is characterized not by statistical regularities but by individual differences... precisely the irregularities that are smoothed out on large-scale data."
- **Break condition:** If a model were explicitly trained with an objective that rewards extracting and applying cross-domain cognitive patterns (e.g., a meta-learning objective that penalizes reliance on superficial lexical cues), the SELF–SELFAKIN gap might narrow.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LLMs exhibit a "lazy" shortcut behavior when attempting cross-domain cognitive transfer: they preferentially appropriate concrete technical approaches or experimental setups directly from the retrieved context rather than abstracting the author’s problem-framing or strategic principles, because such direct copying requires less syntactic and semantic reconstruction than genuine abstraction.
- **Mechanism:** In the retrieval-augmented setting, the model is provided with concrete paper excerpts. The path of least resistance is to extract specific method names, dataset choices, or evaluation metrics and insert them into the new task’s solution, rather than inferring deeper reasoning heuristics. This is evidenced by higher rates of "simple imitation" (B) ratings for SELF compared to SELFAKIN, and by lexical gains (ROUGE) that outpace cognitive consistency improvements.
- **Core assumption:** Direct copying of technical details is a lower-entropy, more probable transformation under the language modeling objective than abstracting and reapplying a cognitive principle.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] "LLMs exhibit a lazy tendency that bypasses the effortful extraction of meta-level human cognition and instead takes shortcuts by simply mimicking specific technical approaches or experimental setups from the references... evidenced by SELF having substantially higher A or B ratings (31% on average) than SELFAKIN." [section 3.2] "Human evaluation in Table 3 shows that LLM outputs... are predominantly characterized by annotators as behavior-level imitation, with at least 89% of cases rated as B or C."
- **Break condition:** If the retrieval context were stripped of all concrete method names and only contained meta-level descriptions of reasoning, the model’s ability to imitate behavior would be forced to rely on abstraction, potentially reducing the lexical-cognitive gap.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Current prompting techniques guided by cognitive theories provide only marginal cognitive improvements because they lack mechanisms to override the model’s pre-trained, population-averaged behavioral priors; they merely steer the expression of reasoning rather than instantiating a new, author-specific cognitive module.
- **Mechanism:** Techniques like PERSONALITYTRAIT and COGNITIVEGUIDE add scaffolding to the prompt. While this may prime the model to adopt certain stylistic or argumentation patterns, the underlying computation still occurs within the same frozen weights that encode broad statistical patterns from internet-scale text. The model cannot fundamentally reconfigure its internal reasoning pathways to match an individual’s cognitive fingerprint; it can only select from its existing repertoire of reasoning expressions that best match the prompt’s constraints. This explains why gains are modest compared to oracle (LEAKY) access and why unified training (TRAINUNIFIED) often degrades cognitive consistency—the model averages many cognitive styles during fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption:** Cognitive internalization requires learning a structured, persistent representation of an individual’s reasoning heuristics that can be flexibly applied; prompting cannot create such representations in a model with fixed parameters.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] "Prompting techniques guided by cognitive theories can provide some gains, but their effectiveness is highly constrained by the base cognitive capacities... prompting appears to reshape the expression of reasoning, rather than reliably inducing deeper cognitive abstraction." [section 3.3] "For TRAINUNIFIED, LLMs achieve substantial improvements in lexical metrics... but suffer a systematic collapse in cognitive-level performance. This suggests that averaging individual cognitive traits in massive training will erase their specific patterns."
- **Break condition:** If prompting were combined with a mechanism that dynamically constructs and consults an author-specific, structured cognitive state (e.g., a neural memory that updates based on the author’s history and is queried during generation), cognitive fidelity might improve.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Behavioral Imitation vs. Cognitive Internalization  
  - **Why needed here:** The paper’s core thesis distinguishes between reproducing surface actions (e.g., using a specific algorithm) and adopting the underlying reasoning principles (e.g., a preference for theoretical rigor). Understanding this distinction is critical to interpreting results: lexical/semantic score improvements under SELF do not equate to cognitive alignment.  
  - **Quick check question:** If a model generates a solution with the same technical method as the author but different problem-framing motivations, is this behavioral imitation or cognitive internalization?

- **Concept:** Cross-Domain, Temporal-Shift Generalization  
  - **Why needed here:** The benchmark’s design uses disjoint train/test topics and a temporal split to force the model to transfer cognitive patterns, not just topical knowledge. This setting is what makes the evaluation a test of cognition rather than topic-specific recall.  
  - **Quick check question:** Why would a model performing well on in-domain recent work (SELFAKIN) not necessarily demonstrate cognitive internalization?

- **Concept:** Multi-Dimensional Cognitive Consistency  
  - **Why needed here:** Cognition is not monolithic. The paper evaluates five dimensions (Premise, Strategy, Logic, Keywords, Taboos) based on psychological theories. Success requires consistency across all, not just one or two.  
  - **Quick check question:** Why is a high score in "Keyword Style" insufficient evidence of cognitive internalization?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Ingestion -> Cognitive Extraction -> Clustering -> Conditioning & Generation -> Evaluation Hierarchy  
- **Critical path:** Author paper collection → Cognitive extraction → Topic clustering → Temporal segmentation → Retrieval for test case → Generation → Multi-level scoring. A failure in clustering (e.g., incorrect topics) breaks the cross-domain assumption.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Expert vs. Automatic Annotation:** Expert annotation yields richer cognitive elements but is not scalable. Automatic annotation is scalable but introduces summarization bias and potential noise.
  - **Oracle (LEAKY) vs. Realistic (SELF):** LEAKY provides the author’s true Motivation/Approach, isolating execution capability. SELF tests actual cognitive transfer. The large LEAKY–SELF gap shows most failure is in abstraction, not execution.
  - **Unified vs. Individual Training:** TRAINUNIFIED is parameter-efficient but causes cognitive averaging; TRAININDIVIDUAL preserves individuality but doesn’t scale. This tradeoff mirrors the paper’s core claim about current paradigms averaging out cognitive signatures.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Small SELF–OTHER gap:** If conditioning on a random author’s history (OTHER) yields similar scores to conditioning on the target author’s history (SELF), the model is not using author-specific signals—likely ignoring the context or using generic academic patterns.
  2. **Large SELFAKIN–SELF gap:** A large gap indicates the model relies on in-domain topical/lexical cues rather than cross-domain cognitive invariants.
  3. **DECREASING cognitive scores with PERSONALITYTRAIT/COGNITIVEGUIDE:** If prompting harms cognitive consistency, it suggests the model cannot reconcile added cognitive constraints with its internal representations, leading to superficial compliance.
  4. **TRAINUNIFIED lexical gain + cognitive drop:** This pattern confirms that multi-author fine-tuning improves surface imitation (memorizing phrases) but destroys individual cognitive fidelity.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline behavioral imitation test:** Run SELF vs. OTHER for a model (e.g., QWEN-3). Expect: SELF > OTHER on all metrics, confirming sensitivity to author history.
  2. **Cognitive internalization test:** Compare SELF vs. SELFAKIN scores on cognitive dimensions. Expect: SELFAKIN >> SELF (average gap ~0.33), demonstrating limited cross-domain transfer.
  3. **Oracle ceiling test:** Run LEAKY and compare to SELFAKIN. Expect: LEAKY > SELFAKIN significantly, indicating that even with perfect cognitive knowledge, the model’s execution is not the limiting factor—abstraction is.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Can training objectives be redesigned to incentivize cognitive internalization rather than behavioral alignment, overcoming the limitations of next-token prediction on aggregated data?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors identify that current LLMs rely on passive emergence from large-scale training, which optimizes for statistical pattern matching and smooths out individual cognitive signatures (Appendix A.1, A.2). Their results show that even with oracle access (LEAKY), gains reflect execution capability rather than true cognitive transfer.
- **Why unresolved**: Existing enhancement techniques (prompting, supervised learning) yield only marginal cognitive improvements and fail to close the gap between SELF and SELFAKIN (average 0.33 deficit). The training objective itself may prevent models from acquiring stable, abstract cognitive patterns that generalize across domains.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Development and evaluation of alternative objectives (e.g., cognitive meta-learning, process-aware rewards) that explicitly penalize superficial imitation and reward cross-domain consistency on the proposed benchmark, ideally achieving performance where SELF approaches SELFAKIN without oracle leakage.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can we construct training data and scenarios that preserve and transfer individual-specific cognitive patterns, including their temporal evolution and social dynamics?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors note that human cognition is characterized by individual differences and long-term stability, but current data either aggregates across individuals (population-level) or is synthetic, lacking authentic longitudinal traces (Section 1, Appendix A.2). Their benchmark uses publication trajectories but focuses on cross-domain rather than intra-individual evolution.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper’s segmentation protocol enforces domain shifts, but does not explicitly model cognitive change within the same individual over time (e.g., how a researcher’s problem-framing evolves). They also mention gaps in understanding assimilation/divergence in group settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Datasets that track the same individuals across multiple decades, with fine-grained cognitive annotations (beyond publication texts), and models that demonstrate accurate prediction of cognitive shifts or consistent pattern transfer in intra-author, cross-temporal settings.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What architectural inductive biases (e.g., hybrid neural-symbolic components, explicit world models) are necessary for LLMs to maintain coherent, counterfactual-ready cognitive representations?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The discussion suggests current probabilistic modeling paradigms may be insufficient for structured, hierarchical cognition (Appendix A.2). The authors propose incorporating symbolic state representations or hybrid systems to support counterfactual and long-horizon reasoning.
- **Why unresolved**: The evaluated models are standard transformer-based LLMs; architectural modifications were not explored. The observed "lazy tendency" and reliance on shortcuts indicate a lack of persistent internal models that can be flexibly applied.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implementation of specific inductive biases (e.g., differentiable memory, causal graphs) and rigorous testing on tasks requiring mental simulation (e.g., theory of mind, planning) with improved cognitive consistency scores, particularly on logic mapping and taboo avoidance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How can evaluation of cognitive internalization move beyond behavioral proxies to directly measure underlying mental representations and reasoning processes?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors propose a multi-dimensional cognitive alignment metric but acknowledge it relies on LLM-as-judge and may not capture deeper structures (Section 2.3, Appendix D.3). They also note that human evaluation classified >95% of outputs as failed internalization.
- **Why unresolved**: Current metrics (lexical, semantic, cognitive consistency) are still output-based and can be gamed by stylistic mimicry. There is no direct access to the model’s internal cognitive state, making it hard to distinguish true internalization from sophisticated imitation.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Development of process-tracing evaluations (e.g., eye-tracking, neural activation alignment with humans during reasoning, or generative models of thought streams) that correlate with human cognitive data and predict generalization beyond behavioral outcomes.

## Limitations
- Benchmark not public: reconstruction introduces noise in paper collection and cognitive extraction.
- Judge model (GPT-5-nano) proprietary; scoring may not generalize; alternatives could differ.
- Automatic cognitive extraction likely misses nuances; validation only on small human subset.
- Cross-domain split may still conflate topical knowledge with cognitive invariants.
- Findings limited to AI researchers; unknown if generalizes to other domains or newer models.

## Confidence
- **Behavioral imitation vs cognitive internalization:** High (consistent across models, human eval).
- **Mechanistic explanations (objective smoothing, lazy shortcuts, prompting limits):** Medium (inferred, not causally tested).
- **Quantitative gaps (e.g., Δ=0.33):** High for tested models; Medium for broader generalization.
- **Overall conclusion:** Medium-High (strong evidence but tempered by limitations).
