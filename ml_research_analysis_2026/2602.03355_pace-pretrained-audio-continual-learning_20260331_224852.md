# PACE: Pretrained Audio Continual Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.03355
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03355
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: PACE achieves state-of-the-art results across six audio continual learning benchmarks, with gains of at least +5.3% on TIMIT-2, +4.1% on TIMIT-3, +6.3% on VocalSet and narrows the gap to joint training to 0.8%–7.6%.

## Executive Summary
Audio continual learning (CL) with pretrained models (PTMs) is challenged by severe upstream-downstream mismatch, leading to representation shifts and catastrophic forgetting. This paper introduces PACE, a framework that combines improved first-session adaptation (FSA) with a regularized analytic classifier and multi-session adaptation (MSA) using adaptive subspace-orthogonal parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and spectrogram-based boundary-aware perturbations. PACE is evaluated on six audio CL benchmarks (ESC-50, US8K, SC2, TIMIT-2, TIMIT-3, VocalSet), consistently outperforming vision CL methods and standard PEFT baselines. The approach achieves significant gains on fine-grained tasks and reduces the gap to joint training, demonstrating robustness and scalability.

## Method Summary
PACE operates in three stages adapted to audio PTMs. First, in the initial session, it applies asymmetric training: a low learning rate for the classification head and a higher rate for deeper backbone layers, while freezing shallow layers based on a CKA similarity threshold. The backbone is tuned via LoRA, and after a warm-up phase, the trainable head is replaced with an analytic classifier that recursively updates decision boundaries using second-order statistics. For subsequent sessions, PACE adds session-specific LoRA modules and projects their gradients into the null space of historical feature representations (computed via SVD on the unlearned model’s outputs) to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Additionally, it employs boundary-aware perturbations: for each current-session sample, time-frequency masks generate challenging boundary samples; these are used in a regularization loss that enhances intra-class compactness and inter-class separation. After a certain number of samples (N_stop), the backbone is frozen and only the analytic classifier updates. This design balances plasticity and stability, addressing both coarse-grained (representation saturation) and fine-grained (representation drift) audio CL challenges.

## Key Results
- PACE achieves at least +5.3% average accuracy on TIMIT-2, +4.1% on TIMIT-3, and +6.3% on VocalSet compared to the strongest baselines.
- The gap to joint training is reduced to 0.8% on ESC-50, 1.6% on US8K, 2.5% on SC2, 6.6% on TIMIT-2, 5.0% on TIMIT-3, and 7.6% on VocalSet.
- Vision CL methods and standard PEFT approaches fail on audio CL benchmarks, while PACE’s analytic classifier and subspace-orthogonal adaptation provide strong performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Improved First-Session Adaptation (FSA)
- **Claim**: FSA mitigates catastrophic forgetting in coarse-grained audio CL by selectively adapting deeper backbone layers and using a regularized analytic classifier to align representations without disrupting pretrained low-level features.
- **Mechanism**: (1) Asymmetric training: low learning rate for head, high for backbone; (2) freeze shallow layers via a CKA-based threshold ρ_layer; tune only later layers with LoRA; (3) replace the trainable head with an analytic classifier that recursively updates decision boundaries without bias accumulation.
- **Core assumption**: Pretrained audio backbones encode sufficient coarse-grained semantics; further tuning must avoid erasing low-level spectral patterns (shallow layers) while refining high-level semantics (deep layers).
- **Evidence anchors**: Figure 3(c) shows freezing shallow layers improves first-session performance; Figure 5 shows shallow layers remain stable (high CKA) during CL.
- **Break condition**: If ρ_layer is too low (freezing too many layers), deep semantic adaptation is insufficient; if too high, shallow features distort, causing saturation or forgetting.

### Mechanism 2: Adaptive Multi-Session Subspace-Orthogonal PEFT (MSA)
- **Claim**: MSA enables fine-grained audio CL by progressively aligning the backbone to misaligned downstream semantics while constraining feature drift via gradient projection into a null subspace of historical representations.
- **Mechanism**: For sessions t > 1, (1) compute an unlearned model W_unlearn^t = W_0 − Σ_{τ=0}^{t−1} A_τ B_τ; (2) find the null space U_t via SVD of the covariance of f_unlearn^t(X_t); (3) project gradients: g_update = P_{U_t} g_original. Adaptation stops after N_stop samples to prevent cumulative drift.
- **Core assumption**: Naive multi-session tuning causes catastrophic forgetting; adaptation must be confined to directions orthogonal to historical feature subspaces.
- **Evidence anchors**: Figure 7 shows MSA with gradient projection stabilizes accuracy across sessions; Table 4 shows that without gradient projection, forgetting is severe (e.g., TIMIT-2 accuracy drops from 90.95 → 85.63).
- **Break condition**: If U_t poorly estimates the historical subspace (e.g., due to small N_t), projection may be inaccurate, limiting plasticity or failing to prevent drift.

### Mechanism 3: Boundary-Aware Perturbation Regularization
- **Claim**: This mechanism reduces representation overlap between old and new classes during MSA by synthesizing boundary-prone samples via time-frequency masking and enforcing intra-class compactness / inter-class margin.
- **Mechanism**: (1) Generate perturbed samples using SpecAugment-style masking (time/frequency bands). (2) Identify boundary set B_t if perturbed samples are misclassified by a temporary model with a frozen backbone. (3) Loss L_reg pulls features toward class centroid and pushes from the nearest boundary point.
- **Core assumption**: Class boundaries are defined by local spectral perturbations; enhancing separation at these boundaries prevents future task confusion.
- **Evidence anchors**: Figure 8 shows time-frequency masking preserves manifolds better than Gaussian noise; Table 4 shows ablating L_reg hurts fine-grained benchmarks (e.g., VocalSet –3.33%).
- **Break condition**: If perturbation is too strong (high mask ratios), it distorts semantics; if too weak, B_t is not representative, offering minimal regularization.

## Foundational Learning
### Continual Learning (CL) Formulation
- **Why needed**: PACE assumes a sequence of sessions with disjoint label spaces and must retain performance on all previous tasks, avoiding catastrophic forgetting.
- **Quick check**: What distinguishes task-incremental from class-incremental CL in this paper’s setup?

### Pretrained Model (PTM) Mismatch
- **Why needed**: Upstream pretraining objectives (e.g., spectrogram reconstruction) differ from downstream semantics (e.g., speaker ID), causing representation shifts that must be addressed.
- **Quick check**: Why do vision PTMs suffer less mismatch than audio PTMs?

### Analytic Classifier
- **Why needed**: Provides closed-form weight updates that avoid bias accumulation and align with frozen/stabilized backbone representations.
- **Quick check**: How does an analytic classifier differ from a standard linear probe in CL?

### Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)
- **Why needed**: Allows controlled backbone adaptation without full fine-tuning; in PACE, LoRA is used in FSA and MSA to balance plasticity and stability.
- **Quick check**: Why is LoRA preferred over full fine-tuning for audio CL?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Pretrained Backbone (EAT) -> LoRA Modules (FSA/MSA) -> Analytic Classifier; plus Subspace Estimator (U_t) and Gradient Projector (P_{U_t}) and Perturbation Generator (time-frequency masks) integrated into MSA.

### Critical path
1. **Stage 1 (FSA)**: Detect L_tune via CKA -> warm up head -> backbone LoRA on deep layers -> replace head with analytic classifier.
2. **Stage 2 (MSA)**: For sessions t>1, add new LoRA, compute U_t, project gradients, apply boundary regularization L_reg.
3. **Stage 3**: After total seen samples ≥ N_stop, freeze backbone; only analytic classifier updates.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Plasticity vs. Stability**: Controlled by ρ_layer and N_stop; more LoRA layers increase plasticity but risk forgetting.
- **Computational cost**: FSA adds tuning cost; MSA adds SVD cost; both are cheaper than prompt-based methods.
- **Representation saturation vs. drift**: Over-restricting (high ρ_layer) causes saturation; under-restricting (low ρ_layer) causes drift.

### Failure signatures
- **Coarse-grained**: Accuracy plateaus after session 1; gap to joint training remains ~3% on ESC-50 w/o FSA.
- **Fine-grained**: Severe forgetting after second session; w/o gradient projection, session-1 accuracy drops from 100% → 7.9% on TIMIT-2.
- **Boundary collapse**: New classes cluster near old class boundaries; ablating L_reg causes performance drop.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Replicate FSA on ESC-50**: Run with/without layer-freezing; measure first-session accuracy and forgetting after session 2. Expect: freezing later layers improves session-1 accuracy and reduces forgetting.
2. **Test Boundary-Aware Perturbation on VocalSet**: Ablate L_reg and compare t-SNE of class centroids vs. boundary points. Expect: w/o L_reg, old/new class clusters overlap; w/ L_reg, separation increases.
3. **Validate Stopping Criterion N_stop**: On TIMIT-2, vary N_stop around chosen value (220) and plot average accuracy vs. adaptation sessions. Expect: performance peaks near chosen value and degrades if adaptation continues.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: What are the underlying causes of the remaining performance gap (up to 7.6%) between PACE and joint training on fine-grained audio CL benchmarks (e.g., VocalSet), and what modifications to the multi-session adaptation or boundary-aware regularization can effectively close this gap?
- **Basis**: Explicitly reported in the abstract and Table 2.
- **Why unresolved**: Persistent gap suggests current components are insufficient for highly fine-grained mismatch; possibly due to limited adaptation data or suboptimal alignment.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How does PACE generalize to audio models with architectures beyond ViT (e.g., CNNs, Conformers) and pretraining objectives other than masked spectrogram prediction (e.g., contrastive CPC, HuBERT)?
- **Basis**: Inferred; evaluation only uses ViT-based self-supervised models (EAT, SSLAM) with similar objectives.
- **Why unresolved**: Design choices (CKA-based layer freezing, LoRA on later layers, analytic classifier) may rely on transformer-specific hierarchical features; other architectures may require adjustments.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can the hyperparameter sensitivity of PACE (e.g., ρ_layer, ρ_svd, N_stop) be reduced through automated or adaptive mechanisms?
- **Basis**: Inferred; thresholds selected via grid search on validation sets.
- **Why unresolved**: Manual tuning requires representative validation data and expertise; real-world CL scenarios with non-stationary streams may lack such conditions.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Does PACE scale to lifelong audio CL scenarios with hundreds of sessions and thousands of classes in terms of memory and computational efficiency?
- **Basis**: Inferred; evaluation covers up to 315 sessions and 630 classes, but parameter expansion and memory growth are concerns.
- **Why unresolved**: Linear growth of LoRA parameters and analytic classifier memory may become prohibitive; long-term resource requirements not analyzed.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter ambiguity**: Key parameters (e.g., LoRA rank, analytic classifier regularization γ, optimizer settings, masking details) are not fully specified, risking reproduction errors.
- **Implementation details unclear**: Ambiguities in subspace projection (U_t computation) and boundary detection could lead to incorrect implementation.
- **Model dependence**: Results are tied to the EAT pretrained backbone; generalization to other audio PTMs remains unverified.
