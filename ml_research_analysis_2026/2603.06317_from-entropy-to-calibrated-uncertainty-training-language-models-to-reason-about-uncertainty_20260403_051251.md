# From Entropy to Calibrated Uncertainty: Training Language Models to Reason About Uncertainty

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.06317
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06317
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art calibration with ECE 7.28% on in-domain QA and 3.15% on out-of-domain GSM8k without test-time sampling.

## Executive Summary
Large language models often exhibit overconfidence and poor calibration, especially in high-stakes applications. This paper introduces a three-stage pipeline to train models to reason about uncertainty: (1) compute fine-grained entropy-based uncertainty scores using von Neumann entropy over embedding distributions from sampled outputs, (2) calibrate these scores via Platt scaling to produce interpretable probability targets, and (3) post-train the LLM using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a reward that aligns verbalized uncertainty to the calibrated targets. On in-domain QA (TriviaQA+NQ), the method reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) to 7.28% and achieves AUROC 81.53%. On out-of-domain GSM8k, it achieves ECE 3.15% and AUROC 66.73%. Crucially, inference remains computationally efficient as uncertainty is generated directly without sampling.

## Method Summary
The method begins by sampling multiple outputs from the language model for a given input and encoding them into embeddings. A distribution over these embeddings is constructed, and von Neumann entropy is computed to capture the spread of the outputs as a fine-grained uncertainty score. On a held-out set, Platt scaling—a sigmoid-based calibration—is applied to map these entropy scores to well-calibrated probability targets. Finally, the model is post-trained using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where the reward encourages the model's verbalized uncertainty (e.g., confidence statements) to match the Platt-scaled targets. This decouples uncertainty estimation from test-time sampling, enabling efficient inference while improving calibration.

## Key Results
- **In-domain QA (TriviaQA+NQ):** ECE reduced to 7.28% (vs. 41.99% Base), AUROC 81.53%, Spearman correlation 0.67.
- **Out-of-domain GSM8k:** ECE reduced to 3.15% (vs. 32.22% Base), AUROC 66.73%.
- **Efficiency:** Inference generates uncertainty directly without sampling, avoiding computational overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism leverages von Neumann entropy over embedding distributions to capture nuanced uncertainty in the model's output space. By calibrating these entropy scores via Platt scaling, the method produces statistically sound probability targets. GRPO then aligns the model's internal uncertainty representations (verbalized) with these targets, effectively teaching the model to express uncertainty that is both fine-grained and well-calibrated. The decoupling of uncertainty score computation from inference allows for efficient deployment while maintaining calibration benefits.

## Foundational Learning
- **von Neumann entropy:** Measures uncertainty of a mixed quantum state; here applied to embedding distributions to capture semantic spread. *Why needed:* Provides a continuous, differentiable uncertainty score sensitive to output diversity. *Quick check:* Does entropy decrease when embeddings cluster?
- **Platt scaling:** Fits a sigmoid to map model scores to probabilities. *Why needed:* Transforms raw entropy into well-calibrated probabilities via held-out data. *Quick check:* Does calibration improve on a validation set?
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** A reinforcement learning algorithm for language models that optimizes relative rewards within a group. *Why needed:* Enables fine-grained alignment of verbalized uncertainty with targets without catastrophic forgetting. *Quick check:* Does training improve calibration on held-out prompts?
- **Expected Calibration Error (ECE):** Bins predictions by confidence and measures weighted difference between accuracy and confidence. *Why needed:* Standard metric for calibration quality. *Quick check:* Is ECE close to zero for a perfectly calibrated model?
- **AUROC:** Area under the ROC curve for uncertainty discrimination. *Why needed:* Evaluates separation between correct and incorrect predictions. *Quick check:* Does AUROC exceed 0.5 for random?
- **Spearman correlation:** Non-parametric rank correlation between confidence and accuracy. *Why needed:* Measures monotonic relationship without assuming linearity. *Quick check:* Is correlation positive and significant?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Output Sampler -> Embedding Encoder -> Distribution Builder -> Von Neumann Entropy Calculator -> Platt Scaling Module -> Target Generator -> GRPO Trainer -> Language Model.

**Critical path:** The pipeline's correctness hinges on: (1) embedding diversity reflecting output uncertainty, (2) Platt scaling generalizing to new data, (3) GRPO reward shaping preserving model capabilities while aligning uncertainty.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Entropy source: von Neumann entropy captures semantic uncertainty but depends on embedding quality; alternatives like predictive entropy may be simpler but less nuanced.
- Two-stage calibration vs. end-to-end: Separating entropy computation and Platt scaling allows modular tuning but risks error propagation; joint training might be more coherent but unstable.
- GRPO vs. SFT: GRPO optimizes relative rewards, avoiding distribution shift, but requires careful reward design to prevent reward hacking.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low embedding entropy despite diverse outputs: indicates embedding collapse or poor diversity in sampling.
- Platt scaling overfits: calibration degrades on out-of-distribution data.
- GRPO reduces QA accuracy: reward over-optimization leads to capability loss.

**First experiments:**
1. Compute von Neumann entropy on a fixed set of outputs; verify it correlates with human uncertainty ratings on a small sample.
2. Apply Platt scaling to entropy values from a held-out set; measure ECE improvement versus uncalibrated entropy.
3. Run a brief GRPO training run; check that verbalized uncertainty distributions shift toward Platt targets without drops in exact-match QA accuracy.

None.

## Limitations
- Out-of-domain generalization: Performance drops on reasoning (GSM8k) vs. factual QA; confidence medium that entropy remains a valid proxy.
- Platt scaling risks: Potential overfitting to the held-out set used for fitting, limiting robustness.
- Entropy-probability alignment: Mapping entropy to true probability via Platt scaling lacks theoretical guarantee, especially with biased sampling.

## Confidence
- In-domain calibration improvement (TriviaQA/NQ): High.
- Computational efficiency at inference: High.
- General calibration principle: Low to Medium.

## Next Checks
1. Ablate the entropy source: Replace von Neumann entropy with alternative estimators (e.g., semantic entropy, NMR) and compare calibration performance.
2. Test calibration stability: Evaluate the calibrated model on multiple held-out distributions (e.g., different QA datasets) without retraining Platt scaling to measure robustness.
3. Compare to direct training baseline: Implement a GRPO baseline where the model is trained to match verbalized uncertainty to targets derived from test-time sampling (bypassing Platt scaling) to isolate the two-stage calibration's contribution.
