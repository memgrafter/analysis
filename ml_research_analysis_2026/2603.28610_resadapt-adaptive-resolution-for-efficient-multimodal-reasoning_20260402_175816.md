# ResAdapt: Adaptive Resolution for Efficient Multimodal Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28610
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28610
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Matches or exceeds uncompressed baselines while discarding over 90% of visual tokens, enabling up to 16× more frames under equivalent compute.

## Executive Summary
ResAdapt tackles the prohibitive visual token growth in multimodal LLMs by adapting input resolution before encoding, rather than compressing tokens afterward. It introduces a lightweight Allocator that predicts per-frame resolution for a video sequence, guided by Cost-Aware Policy Optimization (CAPO) and temporal-similarity regularization. This input-side approach preserves the backbone's native encoder interface, avoids iterative latency, and drastically reduces FLOPs (by a factor proportional to ρ², where ρ is the token retention ratio). Evaluated on video QA and grounding benchmarks, ResAdapt achieves accuracy comparable to or better than uncompressed high-resolution baselines while discarding most tokens, and demonstrates significant gains in long-video reasoning and wall-clock speed.

## Method Summary
ResAdapt inserts a lightweight Allocator module before the fixed visual encoder. For a given video sequence, the Allocator predicts a resolution for each frame, determining how many visual tokens will be generated. The frames are then encoded at their allocated resolutions using the standard backbone (e.g., CLIP-ViT), and the resulting tokens are fed to the LLM decoder. The Allocator is trained with CAPO, a reinforcement learning-style objective that jointly optimizes task accuracy and a cost penalty proportional to the number of tokens produced. A temporal-similarity regularization term encourages the Allocator to assign similar resolutions to temporally correlated frames, preventing erratic allocation that could harm video understanding. This design shifts the computational burden to a cheap, pre-encoding decision, avoiding the full encoder cost of post-encoding compression methods.

## Key Results
- Discards >90% of visual tokens while matching or exceeding uncompressed baseline accuracy on VideoMMMU and temporal grounding benchmarks.
- Under equivalent compute, processes up to 16× more frames, yielding >15% relative gains on long-video reasoning tasks.
- At ~10% token retention, outperforms model-side compression methods (e.g., pruning) by >6 points on VideoMMMU; temporal grounding mIoU doubles that of pruning baselines at similar budgets.
- Backbone FLOPs scale down by ρ² (up to 83× reduction at 11% token retention); wall-clock speedups reach 59.5% for 128-frame sequences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ResAdapt works because it moves the adaptive decision to the input side, before the expensive visual encoder runs. Predicting resolution per frame is a low-dimensional, lightweight task suitable for a small Allocator. By training this Allocator with CAPO, it learns a cost-aware policy that balances task accuracy against token budget, avoiding the common failure mode of "policy collapse" where the model might otherwise minimize cost at the expense of accuracy. The temporal-similarity regularization leverages the inherent coherence of video—nearby frames often have similar informational content—so allocating similar resolutions preserves temporal consistency without needing extra tokens. Crucially, this approach does not alter the backbone encoder's operation; it simply varies its input size, thus retaining the encoder's pretrained feature extraction capabilities while drastically reducing the number of tokens fed forward.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal LLMs**: Models that process both visual and textual inputs within a unified language modeling framework. *Why needed:* Integrating vision into LLMs introduces a token explosion problem due to high-resolution image/video inputs. *Quick check:* Measure how token count grows when scaling spatial resolution (e.g., 224→448) or frame count in a video.
- **Visual token explosion**: The quadratic or near-quadratic increase in token count when encoding high-resolution or long-duration visual inputs with patch-based encoders (e.g., ViT). *Why needed:* This growth quickly exhausts context window limits and dominates compute/memory costs. *Quick check:* Compute tokens per second for a 10-second video at 1 fps vs. 10 fps at fixed resolution.
- **Policy optimization for resource allocation**: Framing resolution selection as a sequential decision problem optimized via a reward that combines task performance and token cost. *Why needed:* Disentangles the cost-sensitive control from the backbone's intrinsic capabilities. *Quick check:* Ablate CAPO’s cost term; observe if Allocator collapses to uniform high resolution.
- **Temporal reasoning in video**: Understanding dependencies across time, not just per-frame content. *Why needed:* Videos contain motion and event progression; inconsistent resolution across frames can break temporal coherence. *Quick check:* Evaluate on a video task requiring cross-frame comparison (e.g., action localization) with/without temporal regularization.
- **Input-side vs. model-side adaptation**: Distinguishing whether adaptation occurs before the backbone encoder (input-side) or by modifying/compressing the encoder’s output (model-side). *Why needed:* Input-side avoids distorting the backbone’s learned representations and skips encoder FLOPs entirely. *Quick check:* Compare FLOPs of a model-side pruning method (encoder still runs at full resolution) vs. ResAdapt’s input-side approach.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Video Frames -> Allocator (predicts per-frame resolution) -> Visual Backbone Encoder (processes frames at allocated resolutions) -> LLM Decoder (generates text output)

**Critical path:** The Allocator runs once on the entire video (or chunk) to produce a resolution plan before any frame is encoded. This plan determines the exact number of patches each frame will be split into, which in turn fixes the token count entering the encoder and decoder.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Allocator simplicity (few layers, small hidden dim) vs. prediction accuracy for complex scenes: a more powerful Allocator could better allocate but adds overhead and risk of overfitting.
- CAPO reward weighting (accuracy vs. cost) vs. training stability: aggressive cost penalties may lead to under-allocation and catastrophic accuracy drops.
- Temporal regularization strength vs. flexibility: too strong forces all frames to the same resolution, losing adaptive benefits for scenes with varying motion/content.

**Failure signatures:**
- Policy collapse: Allocator converges to a single low resolution for all frames, ignoring content.
- Accuracy drop on high-motion or detail-rich scenes: Allocator under-allocates resolution where it is most needed.
- Temporal inconsistency: Allocator assigns wildly different resolutions to adjacent frames, causing jitter in visual features and harming video understanding.
- Training instability: CAPO reward hacking, where Allocator learns to game the cost metric without improving task accuracy.

**First experiments:**
1. Train ResAdapt’s Allocator with CAPO on a small video QA dataset; plot per-step token allocation entropy and validation accuracy to detect early signs of policy collapse or reward hacking.
2. Generate token-accuracy curves by sweeping the cost penalty weight in CAPO; compare against a uniform high-resolution baseline and a post-encoding pruning baseline (e.g., token merging) to verify the claimed >6-point advantage at ~10% retention.
3. Apply a pretrained Allocator (trained only on video QA) directly to an out-of-distribution task like NExT-QA or TVQA without fine-tuning; measure the relative accuracy drop to assess generalization robustness.

## Limitations
- Generalization beyond video QA/grounding (e.g., to detailed image captioning or audio-visual tasks) is unverified.
- CAPO’s robustness to reward function design and its susceptibility to policy collapse during long-horizon training are not deeply explored.
- Wall-clock speedups are reported without full system-level integration details (memory bandwidth, decoder-side overheads), limiting practical deployment insights.

## Confidence
- **Token Reduction & Compute Efficiency** (discarding >90% tokens, ρ² FLOPs reduction, 16× more frames): **High** — Directly measured in controlled experiments.
- **Accuracy Matching/Exceeding Baselines** on video benchmarks: **Medium** — Benchmark-dependent; lacks comparison with all contemporaneous model-side methods on identical splits.
- **CAPO Preventing Policy Collapse & Temporal Regularization Efficacy**: **Medium-Low** — Method described but ablation isolating CAPO vs. regularization term not provided in summary.
- **Generalization to Long-Video Reasoning Gains** (>15% relative): **Medium** — Validated on specific long-video tasks; unclear if gains hold for fine-grained spatial analysis across many frames.

## Next Checks
