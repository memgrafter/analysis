# SafeSci: Safety Evaluation of Large Language Models in Science Domains and Beyond

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.01589
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01589
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: SafeSci introduces SafeSciBench (250K samples) distinguishing safety knowledge (accuracy) from safety risk (refusal rate) using deterministic tasks, and SafeSciTrain (1.5M samples) for safety enhancement; evaluation of 24 LLMs revealed major vulnerabilities, and fine-tuning notably improved safety alignment.

## Executive Summary
SafeSci addresses limited risk coverage and subjective evaluation in scientific LLM safety benchmarks. It introduces SafeSciBench, a test set of 250K samples across 7 scientific fields, which separately measures safety knowledge (accuracy on factual hazard questions) and safety risk (refusal rate on harmful action requests) using deterministic tasks (MCQs, true/false, structured generation). SafeSciTrain provides 1.5M fine-tuning samples to improve model safety. Evaluating 24 LLMs uncovered wide performance ranges (accuracy 0.32–0.80, safety rate 0.16–0.65) and significant over-refusal in biology. Fine-tuning on SafeSciTrain, exemplified by Qwen3-8B (+0.33 safety rate), demonstrably enhances safety alignment while highlighting domain-specific trade-offs.

## Method Summary
SafeSci comprises two resources: SafeSciBench (250K test samples) and SafeSciTrain (1.5M fine-tuning instruction samples). SafeSciBench covers 125 tasks in chemistry, biology, pharmacology, materials science, environmental science, psychology, and engineering. Tasks are labeled as either "knowledge" (requiring accurate answers about hazards) or "risk" (requesting harmful actions that should be refused). Evaluation uses deterministic question types: MCQs and true/false questions scored by accuracy; generative outputs (SMILES, protein/gene sequences) scored by validity, exact match, and fingerprint/sequence similarity. Risk responses are binary (safe/unsafe). Data construction combines template-based generation (90%) and a dual-agent system (<10%) across multiple scientific databases (e.g., PubChem, UniProt), with contamination mitigation via dataset interaction and task diversification. Fine-tuning employs LoRA (rank 64, alpha 128, 1 epoch, lr 1e-4, batch 64) on SafeSciTrain. Models are evaluated zero-shot on 3K-sample slices per field (5 repeats) and after fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- SafeSciBench evaluation of 24 LLMs shows accuracy ranging from 0.32 to 0.80 and safety rate from 0.16 to 0.65, indicating substantial safety vulnerabilities.
- Over-refusal is particularly acute in biology, with models like Grok-4.1-reasoning rejecting 89% of knowledge questions.
- Fine-tuning on SafeSciTrain improves safety alignment; for instance, Qwen3-8B’s safety rate increases by 0.33, though some domains (biology) see accuracy drops and increased refusal.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

1. **Distinguishing safety knowledge and safety risk**: SafeSci bifurcates evaluation into two verticals: knowledge questions that require accurate factual answers about hazardous substances (e.g., toxicity, first aid) and risk questions that request harmful actions (e.g., synthesis instructions) and should be refused. This separation enables independent measurement of a model’s scientific competence and its alignment to refuse dangerous requests. Evidence includes divergent performance patterns across models and fields (Tables 3, 5).
2. **Using deterministic tasks to eliminate judge bias**: SafeSciBench abandons open-ended QA and uses tasks with deterministic answers (MCQs, TFQs, structured generation with automated metrics). This avoids variability and biases inherent in LLM-as-a-judge approaches, ensuring fair, reproducible cross-model comparisons. The design directly counters limitations of prior benchmarks like SOSBench and SciSafeEval.
3. **Mitigating data contamination**: SafeSci constructs questions through interactions across multiple databases (e.g., combining toxicology and reaction data) and designs 125 diverse tasks requiring inference rather than direct retrieval. This reduces memorization from training corpora (e.g., PubChem, ChEMBL), leading to more reliable estimates of genuine model safety knowledge.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
Data sources (PubChem, UniProt, DrugBank, Haz-Map, literature) → Task definition (125 knowledge/risk tasks across 7 fields) → Template creation (15K+ templates, avg 100/task) → Dataset generation (template-based for 90%, dual-agent for <10%) → Partitioning into SafeSciBench (test) and SafeSciTrain (train, non-overlapping within tasks) → Zero-shot evaluation (3K samples ×5 runs) and fine-tuning (LoRA) → Analysis of accuracy, safety rate, over-refusal.

**Critical path**:
1. Source selection: Identify authoritative databases and literature for each field.
2. Task definition: Specify 125 tasks with clear knowledge/risk labels and question types.
3. Template creation: Generate and manually validate templates for structured data.
4. Agent-based generation: Use Generator-V validator dual-agent for unstructured text QA pairs.
5. Dataset partitioning: Ensure no overlap between train and test within identical tasks.
6. Zero-shot evaluation: Sample 3K questions per field five times per model; compute metrics.
7. Fine-tuning: Apply LoRA on SafeSciTrain for selected models; re-evaluate.
8. Analysis: Compare accuracy/safety rates, compute over-refusal, perform field-wise breakdowns.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Coverage vs. depth: SafeSci spans 7 fields but may lack granularity of domain-specific benchmarks.
- Objectivity vs. expressiveness: Deterministic tasks score reliably but cannot capture nuanced safe responses (e.g., answers with warnings).
- Contamination avoidance vs. realism: Novel tasks may deviate from real-world query distributions, limiting external validity.
- Scale vs. quality control: Automatic generation enables large datasets but requires rigorous validation and agent oversight.

**Failure signatures**:
- Over-refusal: High rejection rates on knowledge questions, especially in biology (e.g., Grok-4.1-reasoning: 0.89 biology rejection).
- Under-refusal: Low safety rate on risk questions (e.g., Llama-4-Scout-Instruct: 0.16 overall).
- Poor generative validity: Very low protein sequence validity for some models (Intern-S1: 0.119).
- Field imbalance: Smaller task counts in psychology/engineering lead to higher metric variance.
- Template bias: Over-reliance on templates may constrain diversity and introduce phrasing artifacts.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Zero-shot benchmark: Run all 24 LLMs on SafeSciBench with 5 random 3K-sample slices; report mean ± std for accuracy and safety rate per field and overall.
2. Fine-tuning impact: Apply LoRA to Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, and Llama-3.1-8B on SafeSciTrain.
3. Ablation on task design: Compare performance on template-generated vs. agent-generated samples to validate construction quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How well does safety improvement from fine-tuning on SafeSciTrain transfer to *unseen* scientific domains or open-ended safety queries outside the benchmark's scope?
- What specific architectural or training data factors in models (e.g., Grok, Claude) lead to extreme over-refusal in biology, and can this be systematically tuned?
- Can the knowledge/risk distinction and deterministic task design be scaled effectively to more complex, multi-step scientific reasoning tasks that lack single correct answers?
- Unknown: Long-term retention of safety alignment after fine-tuning—does it degrade with subsequent general instruction tuning?

## Limitations
- **Domain representativeness**: While covering 7 fields, psychology and engineering have fewer tasks, increasing variance and potentially under-representing sub-disciplines.
- **Task artificiality**: Deterministic, decontextualized questions may not reflect how real users propose harmful actions (e.g., conversational persuasion, multi-step planning).
- **Generative scope**: Structured generation tasks focus on validity/similarity for molecules/sequences; they do not assess safety of *explanatory* text (e.g., detailed method descriptions with warnings).
- **Contamination mitigation**: Interaction-based construction reduces but cannot eliminate memorization from massive web corpora; some test items may still be retrievable.
- **Fine-tuning scope**: LoRA on a single dataset may not capture full safety alignment dynamics compared to full-parameter tuning or multi-dataset curricula.

## Confidence
**High** for core claims about benchmark design and reproducible evaluation results across 24 models, supported by deterministic metrics and large sample sizes.  
**Moderate** for generalizability of fine-tuning benefits beyond tested models (Qwen, Llama) and for conclusions about field-specific over-refusal causes.  
**Lower** for real-world deployment transferability, given the synthetic, single-turn nature of tasks versus complex user interactions.
