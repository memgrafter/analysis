# Tunable Soft Equivariance with Guarantees

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.26657
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26657
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A projection-based framework converts any pre-trained vision network into a soft equivariant model with theoretical error bounds, improving task metrics while reducing equivariance error across multiple benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Tunable Soft Equivariance, a method that transforms any pre-trained neural network into a soft equivariant model by projecting its weights onto the space of equivariant linear maps. The approach operates at the weight level, targeting all linear and convolutional layers, and uses Lie algebra representations to define equivariance constraints. A cutoff parameter controls the softness of the projection, trading off equivariance error for task performance. The method is evaluated on a diverse set of backbones (ViT, DINOv2, ResNet) and tasks (classification, segmentation, trajectory prediction), showing consistent improvements in primary metrics (e.g., top-1 accuracy, mIoU) while reducing measured equivariance errors. It outperforms prior approaches like canonicalization and residual pathway priors, and efficiency is achieved via a Schur decomposition for SO(2) rotations.

## Method Summary
The core idea is to replace the weight matrix W of any linear layer (including flattened convolutions) with a projected version derived from the Lie algebra of the transformation group (e.g., SO(2) for rotations). The projection matrix B_eq is constructed from the nullspace of a constraint matrix L = A^T ⊗ I_d' - I_d ⊗ A, where A is the Lie algebra generator. The projected weights are given by vec(W_proj) = B_eq θ, where θ are learnable parameters initialized from the original vec(W). Softness is controlled by a cutoff b: only singular vectors of L with singular values below b are retained. For efficiency with normal matrices (as in SO(2)), a Schur decomposition is used to construct B_eq without full SVD. During training, the model is fine-tuned with augmentation (e.g., ±30° rotations), and the projected weights are updated via gradient descent on θ. No extra parameters are introduced beyond θ.

## Key Results
- On ImageNet with a ViT-B/16 backbone, top-1 accuracy increases from 81.67% to 82.28% while invariance error (iErr) drops from 0.0036 to 0.0015.
- On CIFAR-100 using a DINOv2 backbone, combined accuracy rises by 0.68% to 88.90% with iErr reduced by 0.0785.
- The method consistently outperforms canonicalization and residual pathway priors across classification, segmentation (PASCAL VOC), and trajectory prediction tasks, while providing theoretical bounds on equivariance error.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The projection ensures that the adopted linear maps commute with the group action up to a controlled error determined by the cutoff b. By projecting only the weights—not the activations—the model retains the original architecture and feature hierarchies while enforcing equivariance at the operator level. The learnable parameters θ allow the model to adapt within the equivariant subspace, preserving task-relevant information that might be discarded by a hard equivariance constraint. The theoretical bounds link the cutoff directly to the worst-case equivariance violation, providing a tunable knob: a larger b retains more of the original weight structure (higher task performance potential but higher equivariance error), while a smaller b enforces stricter equivariance at the risk of over-projection. The use of Schur decomposition for SO(2) exploits the normal structure of the constraint, making the construction scalable to large layers.

## Foundational Learning
- **Lie Groups and Algebras**: Continuous symmetry groups (e.g., SO(2)) and their infinitesimal generators (A) define how transformations act on vector spaces. *Why needed*: The generator A is used to build the equivariance constraint matrix L. *Quick check*: Verify that exp(θA) recovers the rotation matrix for a given θ.
- **Weight Projection**: Projecting a weight matrix onto the space of equivariant maps via a basis B_eq for the nullspace of L. *Why needed*: Enforces that the linear operator commutes with the group action. *Quick check*: For a simple 2×2 layer, compute B_eq and confirm that B_eq^T L = 0.
- **Soft Equivariance**: Allowing deviations from exact equivariance by truncating the projection basis with a cutoff b. *Why needed*: Exact equivariance can hurt task performance; softness provides a tunable trade-off. *Quick check*: Compute the singular values of L and observe that increasing b includes more singular vectors, reducing the projection’s restrictiveness.
- **Invariance/Equivariance Error (iErr/eErr)**: Metrics measuring the divergence (e.g., KL, ℓ2) between original and transformed outputs under group actions. *Why needed*: Quantifies the degree of equivariance violation. *Quick check*: On a fixed input, apply a rotation, compute outputs, and measure mean squared difference.
- **Schur Decomposition**: For normal matrices (like A from SO(2)), an orthogonal Schur form yields a block-diagonal structure that simplifies construction of B_eq. *Why needed*: Avoids expensive SVD on large constraint matrices; leverages structure for efficiency. *Quick check*: For A = [[0,-1],[1,0]], compute torch.linalg.eig(A) and verify complex conjugate eigenvalues and block structure.
- **Rotation Augmentation**: Applying rotations (±30°) during fine-tuning. *Why needed*: Encourages the model to learn rotation-invariant features and aligns with the equivariance constraint. *Quick check*: Confirm that the training dataloader includes random rotations within the specified range.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Pre-trained Backbone -> Layer Identifier -> Projection Constructor (Schur/SVD) -> SoftEquivariantLinear Module -> Fine-tuned Equivariant Backbone -> Task Head -> Evaluation Metrics

**Critical Path**:  
1. Load pre-trained backbone and identify all target layers (Linear, Conv2d, patch embedding FC).  
2. For each layer, compute the equivariance constraint matrix L from the Lie algebra generator.  
3. Construct projection basis B_eq via Schur (for SO(2)) or SVD, applying cutoff b to mask singular vectors.  
4. Replace each target layer with a custom module that projects weights using B_eq and applies them in forward pass.  
5. Fine-tune the entire model with rotation augmentation, optimizing θ (the projected weight coefficients).  
6. Evaluate on validation set for task metrics (accuracy, mIoU) and equivariance metrics (iErr, aAcc).

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- **Hard vs Soft Cutoff**: A hard cutoff (binary mask) is simpler but may cause abrupt changes; the paper’s smooth exponential cutoff (Eq. 56) improves results but introduces an extra hyperparameter s.  
- **SVD vs Schur**: SVD is general but costly for large layers; Schur exploits the normal structure of SO(2) generators, reducing compute but requiring careful implementation of real Schur for complex eigenvalues.  
- **Parameter Efficiency**: θ has same dimension as original vec(W), so no extra storage, but projection adds overhead during training (matrix multiplications with B_eq).  
- **Generality**: Method applies to any linear layer, but extending to non-abelian groups (e.g., SO(3)) requires handling multiple generators and their combined constraint, which is less straightforward.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- **Accuracy collapse**: Validation accuracy drops significantly, often when b is too small (over-projection). Check the fraction of retained singular vectors; if very low (<30% for deep layers), increase b.  
- **Minimal iErr reduction**: iErr remains high despite training. Verify rotation augmentation is active; ensure projected weights are actually used (no shape mismatches in B_eq application).  
- **Excessive memory/time**: Training slows dramatically, especially on ViT patch embedding. Confirm Schur path is taken for SO(2); for large layers, SVD may be prohibitive.  
- **Boundary artifacts**: Augmented metrics (aAcc) and iErr are corrupted by padding effects, especially in segmentation. Ensure consistent padding (e.g., zero-pad to original size) and cropping during evaluation; mask out-of-bounds pixels.

**First Experiments**:  
1. **Cutoff Sweep**: Train ViT-B/16 on ImageNet with b ∈ {1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2} to map the trade-off between task accuracy and iErr.  
2. **Smooth Cutoff Ablation**: Fix b (e.g., 1e-3) and vary the smoothness parameter s ∈ {0.01b, 0.1b, 0.5b} to confirm benefit of smooth mask over hard.  
3. **Padding Protocol Test**: During iErr/aAcc computation, compare zero-padding, reflection padding, and valid cropping with masking to identify the protocol matching reported numbers.

## Limitations
- The optimal cutoff value b per backbone/dataset is not disclosed; reproduction requires validation-based tuning.
- The smooth cutoff hyperparameter s (Eq. 56) is unspecified, introducing ambiguity in exact implementation.
- For non-abelian groups (e.g., SO(3)), the exact composition of multiple Schur operators and the approximation’s tightness remain unclear.
- Evaluation under rotation may suffer from boundary artifacts; padding/cropping strategies are not detailed, potentially affecting reported iErr/aAcc.
