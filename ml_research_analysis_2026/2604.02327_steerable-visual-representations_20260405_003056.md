# Steerable Visual Representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.02327  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02327  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** SteerViT adds 21M parameters to a frozen ViT and achieves 96.0% top-1 accuracy on the CORE text-guided retrieval benchmark while preserving representation quality.

## Executive Summary
Pretrained vision models cannot flexibly focus on non-salient objects via text prompts without degrading their visual representation quality. SteerViT solves this by equipping a frozen Vision Transformer with steerable features through lightweight cross-attention layers that fuse text prompts directly into the visual encoder (early fusion). Trained with a referential segmentation objective, SteerViT uses only 21M additional trainable parameters yet achieves 96.0% top-1 accuracy on the novel CORE benchmark for text-guided retrieval—far surpassing query-agnostic encoders like DINOv2 (43.7%) and MLLMs. The method also preserves the underlying visual quality, as shown by linear probing and segmentation benchmarks, and demonstrates strong zero-shot transfer to anomaly detection (82.1 PRO on MVTec AD) and personalized object discrimination.

## Method Summary
SteerViT freezes a pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT) and inserts gated cross-attention (CA) layers into every other transformer block. A frozen RoBERTa encodes the text prompt; its embeddings are projected to the visual space via a trainable two‑layer MLP. In each CA block, ViT patch tokens serve as queries while the projected text serves as keys and values. The CA output is added to the residual stream through a tanh‑gated connection with a layer‑specific scalar α initialized to zero, ensuring the model starts as the identity function. Only the CA parameters, the MLP, and the gating scalars are trained, using a patch‑level soft cross‑entropy loss from a referential segmentation pretext task. This supervision encourages spatial grounding of the text within the image, yielding globally and locally steered features without forgetting the original visual semantics.

## Key Results
- SteerViT achieves 96.0% top‑1 accuracy on CORE, far outperforming DINOv2 (43.7%), CLIP, SigLIP, and MLLMs.
- Only 21M additional trainable parameters are needed; representation quality (linear probing, segmentation) is preserved relative to the frozen backbone.
- Zero‑shot transfer yields 82.1 PRO on MVTec AD for anomaly segmentation and state‑of‑the‑art results on personalized object discrimination (PODS).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Injecting text prompts into the *early layers* of a frozen Vision Transformer (ViT) via cross‑attention enables the visual representation to be steered by language, whereas late fusion (post‑encoding) fails to do so.
- **Mechanism:** Lightweight cross‑attention (CA) layers are interleaved within the ViT's transformer blocks. Vision patch tokens serve as *Queries*, while text embeddings (projected into visual space) serve as *Keys/Values*. A tanh‑gated residual connection (initialized to zero) allows the model to start as the identity function and gradually incorporate text conditioning during training. This modifies the internal feature trajectories *during* visual encoding.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic alignment between vision and language can be injected into an already‑trained visual encoder without catastrophic forgetting, provided the intervention is gated and lightweight.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: "inject text directly into the layers of the visual encoder (early fusion) via lightweight cross‑attention."  
  - [section 3.1]: Describes the gated CA layer insertion and zero‑initialization: "We insert CA layers into every other Transformer encoder block... integrated through a tanh gate with a layer‑specific learnable scalar αℓ, initialized to zero."  
  - [section 4.1]: Late fusion (CLIP, SigLIP) shows no steerability improvement over vision‑only features, while early fusion (SteerViT) achieves 96% on CORE.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Training with a *referential segmentation* pretext task forces the cross‑attention layers to learn to route relevant textual information to the correct spatial locations in the visual token grid, producing both globally steered semantics and locally grounded patch features.
- **Mechanism:** The model must predict a binary mask over image patches indicating which ones correspond to the object described in the text prompt. The soft cross‑entropy loss provides a dense, spatial learning signal that encourages the CA layers to modulate patch token representations based on prompt relevance.
- **Core assumption:** Solving referential segmentation requires integrating language and vision at the level of intermediate feature maps; this skill transfers to general steerability.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 3.2]: "In order to encourage the vision encoder to leverage and incorporate language clues, we design a pretext task requiring consideration of the prompt to be solved. We select referential segmentation... A linear classifier head maps each patch representation... through softmax."  
  - [section 4.3]: SteerViT preserves representation quality (linear probing, segmentation) while being steerable, indicating the segmentation objective did not degrade core visual features.  
  - [section 4.4]: SteerViT's performance on personalized object discrimination (PODS) improves with more descriptive prompts, showing the segmentation training taught fine‑grained text‑vision alignment.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The *tanh‑gated* cross‑attention design, zero‑initialized at training start, preserves the original ViT's representation quality while allowing gradual, stable incorporation of text signals, creating a controllable trade‑off between vanilla ViT behavior and fully steered features.
- **Mechanism:** The gate `Z^(ℓ+1) = Z^(ℓ) + tanh(αℓ)·Â` with `αℓ` initialized to 0 means the residual branch is initially disabled (`tanh(0)=0`). The gradient `∂Z/∂α = sech²(α)·Â` is non‑zero at init, so `αℓ` can learn to open the gate. At inference, scaling `αℓ` by a factor ω∈[0,1] smoothly interpolates between unsteered (ω=0) and fully steered (ω=1) behavior.
- **Core assumption:** The identity initialization prevents destabilizing the pretrained visual features at the start of training, allowing the new text‑conditioning pathway to be learned without overwriting robust low‑level representations.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 3.1]: "Since tanh(0)=0, the model is identical to the frozen ViT at initialization... the gate still receives a learning signal since ∂Z^(ℓ+1)/∂αℓ = sech²(αℓ)·Â, and sech²(0)=1."  
  - [section 4.3] & Fig. 7: Shows the continuous control knob; scaling factor ω=0.6 gives optimal Pareto trade‑off for DINOv2/SigLIP, and for MAE, quality monotonically improves with ω.  
  - [section 4.7, Table 4]: Removing the tanh gate (ungated CA) degrades performance on representation quality (FG‑CLS) and steerability (CORE, PODS), confirming its role.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Vision Transformer (ViT) patch embedding and self‑attention**  
  - **Why needed here:** SteerViT modifies a standard ViT. An engineer must understand how image patches are tokenized, how self‑attention aggregates spatial information, and the residual stream structure to grasp where and how to inject cross‑attention.  
  - **Quick check question:** Given an image split into N patches, what are the shapes of the Query, Key, and Value matrices in a standard ViT self‑attention block, and how do they interact?

- **Concept: Cross‑attention in multimodal models (e.g., Flamingo)**  
  - **Why needed here:** SteerViT's core operation is inverted cross‑attention (vision queries to text keys/values). Understanding the canonical form (text queries to vision keys/values) and gating mechanisms (like in Flamingo) is prerequisite to designing the `CA(Z_v, H_t)` block.  
  - **Quick check question:** In Flamingo's gated cross‑attention, what are the queries, keys, and values, and what is the purpose of the learned gating scalar?

- **Concept: Referential/grounding segmentation as a training objective**  
  - **Why needed here:** The entire training signal comes from this proxy task. An engineer must understand how a patch‑level segmentation loss (`L = -Σ y_i log p_i`) creates a spatial, content‑match supervision that forces text‑vision alignment in intermediate features.  
  - **Quick check question:** Why is a soft cross‑entropy loss over patch masks preferred over a point‑based (center) loss for teaching a model "what an object looks like" rather than just "where roughly it is"?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Frozen Vision Encoder (ViT) → [Gated Cross‑Attention Layers interleaved every other block] → Final Steered Patch Tokens  
Frozen Text Encoder (RoBERTa) → MLP Projector → (Keys/Values to all CA layers)  
Segmentation Head (linear classifier on patch tokens) – used during training only

**Critical path:**  
Image patches → ViT Block 1 → [Gated CA (with text)] → ViT Block 2 → [Gated CA] → … → Final patch tokens (steered features)  
Text path runs in parallel: prompt → RoBERTa → MLP → H_t (shared across CA layers).  
**Key:** Text influences visuals *within* the ViT's processing pipeline, not after.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Early vs. Late Fusion: Early (CA within ViT) is necessary for true steerability of *features*; late fusion (adding text vector to final image vector) only steers the final aggregated output, not the spatial token representations.
- Gating vs. Ungated CA: The tanh gate with α=0 initialization is crucial for preserving base ViT quality. Ungated CA disrupts pretrained features.
- MLP vs. Linear Projector: A two‑layer MLP better aligns RoBERTa's semantic space with the ViT's than a single linear layer.
- CA Insertion Frequency: Paper inserts in every other block (e.g., 6 for ViT‑B/12). Less frequent insertion may under‑utilize text; more may destabilize training.
- Including FFN after CA (like Flamingo) increases parameters (+67%) and consistently hurts steerability/transfer for MAE backbones.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Poor CORE retrieval (<50%):** CA layers failed to learn meaningful text‑vision routing. Check: training loss convergence, αℓ values (should be non‑zero), text adapter output distribution.
- **Degraded linear probing/segmentation (FG‑CLS, ADE20k):** Catastrophic forgetting or disruption of base ViT features. Check: gating parameters saturating (`|αℓ|` >> 1), learning rate too high for CA layers.
- **No sensitivity to random prompts:** Means no cross‑modal interaction in the visual encoder. Verify: CA layers are actually being called and have non‑zero gradients.
- **Attention maps unchanged from DINOv2:** CA layers might be dead (zero output). Diagnose by visualizing `Â` norms per layer.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Sanity Check – CORE with Correct vs. Random Prompt:** Immediately evaluate on CORE with the correct object prompt and a random/invalid prompt (e.g., "toaster" for a "fruit bowl"). SteerViT should show a *large drop* (~50 points) for random prompts, confirming active text dependence. CLIP/SigLIP show no drop.
2. **Visualize MOSAIC Attention (Qualitative):** Create a 2×2 mosaic from 4 PASCAL‑VOC images. Condition on a class present in only one quadrant (e.g., "person"). Visualize final [CLS]‑to‑patch attention. It should *highlight* the correct quadrant/object, not the overall most salient region.
3. **Linear Probe for Representation Quality:** Train a linear classifier on frozen SteerViT features (conditioned on a superclass like "dog") for a fine‑grained dataset (ImageWoof). Accuracy should be *comparable* to the frozen base ViT (e.g., ~90% for DINOv2), confirming quality preservation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What mechanistic explanation accounts for the backbone‑dependent layer‑wise divergence patterns observed when conditioning ViTs with text via early fusion (e.g., MAE diverges mid‑layers then reconverges, while DINOv2 diverges late)?
2. Does SteerViT maintain steerability and representation quality when conditioned on linguistically complex prompts (e.g., negations, attribute conjunctions) that are out‑of‑distribution relative to the referential segmentation training data?
3. Why does including a feed‑forward network (FFN) in the cross‑attention block degrade performance for MAE‑based SteerViT while having negligible or positive effects for DINOv2‑based models, per Table 11?
4. Can the SteerViT framework be extended to sequential data modalities (e.g., video or audio) while preserving both steerability and representation quality, and what architectural modifications are necessary to handle temporal dynamics?

## Limitations
- Prompt tokenization details (max length, truncation) unspecified; may affect text encoding capacity.
- SAM2 mask generation process for VG/LVIS unclear; potential noise in referential supervision.
- CORE benchmark is novel; risk of dataset bias/overfitting without external validation.
- Evaluation limited to ViT backbones; generalizability to CNN or newer architectures unknown.
- Prompt phrasing sensitivity not studied; performance may vary with linguistic rewrites.

## Confidence
- **Early fusion via cross‑attention gives strong steerability:** High (large CORE gains >50 points consistent across DINOv2/SigLIP/MAE).
- **Referential segmentation training preserves representation quality:** Medium (linear probing shows minor drops for DINOv2/SigLIP and gains for MAE; evaluation covers only a few downstream tasks).
- **Gated cross‑attention maintains frozen ViT behavior:** High (ablations removing tanh gate cause severe drops in both quality and steerability).
- **Zero‑shot transfer to anomaly detection/PODS:** Medium (results shown on single datasets; broader task generalization not yet demonstrated).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑encoder ablation:** Replace RoBERTa with CLIP’s text encoder and re‑evaluate CORE and linear probing to test encoder agnosticism.
2. **Mask noise robustness:** Train with ground‑truth segmentation masks (e.g., RefCOCO) versus SAM2 masks on a held‑out subset; quantify impact on CORE accuracy.
3. **External steerability benchmark:** Test the best SteerViT model on FocalLens’s conditional retrieval task; compare against published baselines to verify generalization.
