# Tucano 2 Cool: Better Open Source LLMs for Portuguese

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03543
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03543
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Tucano2-qwen-3.7B-Base achieves aggregate NPM 59.21, surpassing larger multilingual baselines (Qwen2.5-7B: 57.97, Qwen3-4B: 57.86) with less than 0.3% additional compute for continual pretraining.

## Executive Summary
Tucano 2 introduces the first fully open-source stack for Portuguese large language models, addressing the scarcity of high-performing monolingual models. The release includes GigaVerbo-v2 (∼320B tokens with educational/toxicity annotations), a synthetic dataset (9.3B tokens), dedicated SFT and preference datasets, and a refined evaluation suite. Using a custom 49K SentencePiece tokenizer and multi-stage training (both from-scratch and continual pretraining), the 3.7B parameter model outperforms larger multilingual baselines on the Normalized Preferred Metric (NPM) while requiring minimal extra compute. The instruct and think variants demonstrate strong reasoning and chain-of-thought capabilities in Portuguese, enabling fully reproducible development.

## Method Summary
The authors employ a two-pronged training strategy. First, from-scratch pretraining of 0.5B, 1.5B, and 3.7B models on curated mixtures of real (GigaVerbo-v2) and synthetic (GigaVerbo-v2 Synth) Portuguese data using a three-stage Warmup-Stable-Decay curriculum. Second, continual pretraining of Qwen3 via tokenizer transplantation: they replace Qwen3’s 151K vocabulary with a Portuguese-optimized 49K SentencePiece tokenizer using OMP (training-free), then continue training on pure Portuguese mixtures for 50–100B tokens. Post-training includes supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) on instruction or reasoning data followed by Anchored Preference Optimization (APO). Evaluation aggregates 15+ Portuguese benchmarks into NPM. The entire pipeline—including tokenizer, data, code, and models—is open‑sourced.

## Key Results
- The flagship Tucano2-qwen-3.7B-Base reaches an aggregate NPM of 59.21, exceeding Qwen2.5‑7B (57.97) and Qwen3‑4B (57.86).
- The instruct variant (Tucano2-qwen-3.7B‑Instruct) leads its class in knowledge & reasoning with NPM 56.22.
- The think variant produces coherent Portuguese chain‑of‑thought outputs; the stack is fully open and reproducible.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Educational and synthetic data mixtures improve downstream performance
- **Claim**: High‑quality educational text and synthetic data with explicit chain‑of‑thought traces enhance reasoning and knowledge acquisition.
- **Mechanism**: Educational content provides structured, coherent material for learning reasoning patterns; synthetic data fills domain gaps and includes CoT traces that improve problem‑solving.
- **Core assumption**: Data quality directly shapes model capabilities; synthetic data can approximate missing real content.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] GigaVerbo‑v2 (∼320B tokens with educational/toxicity annotations), synthetic dataset (9.3B tokens); [section 3.4] Data Ablation Study showing Edu+Synth model outperforms Non‑Edu and pure Synth; [corpus] 119B educational tokens (37% of GigaVerbo‑v2 default subset).
- **Break condition**: Mislabeling of educational content or synthetic data distribution shift could diminish gains.

### Mechanism 2: Custom Portuguese‑optimized tokenizer improves computational efficiency
- **Claim**: A smaller, domain‑tailored tokenizer reduces embedding parameters and FLOPs while improving compression.
- **Mechanism**: Training SentencePiece on Portuguese, English, and code with 49,152 vocabulary yields lower subword fertility and higher characters‑per‑token, making training more efficient.
- **Core assumption**: Tokenization efficiency is language‑dependent; a specialized tokenizer can outperform generic multilingual ones.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] custom efficient tokenizer (49K vocab); [section 4.2] lowest subword fertility (1.51) and highest compression (2.88 char/token); [corpus] evaluation on 600k Portuguese words confirms superiority.
- **Break condition**: May underperform on mixed‑language text; benefits narrow if base models already have good Portuguese coverage.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: GigaVerbo‑v2, GigaVerbo‑v2 Synth, GigaVerbo‑v2 SFT, GigaVerbo‑v2 Preferences -> Tokenizer (SentencePiece 49K) -> Base Models -> Instruct/Think (via SFT & APO) -> Evaluation Suite (lm‑evaluation‑harness on Easy/Hard, IFEval‑PT, GSM8K‑PT, RULER‑PT, HumanEval).

- **Critical path**:  
  1. Build tokenizer on representative data.  
  2. Preprocess corpora (filtering, deduplication, annotation).  
  3. Conduct ablation studies to select data mixtures.  
  4. Train base models (from‑scratch pretraining).  
  5. Optionally perform continual pretraining on Qwen3 with transplantation.  
  6. Perform SFT and Anchored Preference Optimization for Instruct/Think variants.  
  7. Evaluate on comprehensive suite.  
  8. Release artifacts.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Synthetic data cost vs. coverage: High energy consumption (∼73% total) for limited domain diversity.  
  - Model size vs. compute: Smaller models train faster but sacrifice performance; continual pretraining leverages existing compute investments.  
  - Tokenizer size: Smaller vocab reduces parameters but may hurt multilingual coverage.  
  - Post‑training data volume: SFT dataset ∼2B tokens but reasoning samples <2%; limited detail may restrict advanced reasoning.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Data: High toxicity scores or poor educational annotation accuracy could degrade downstream tasks.  
  - Training: Gradient explosion, loss plateau, no improvement on Easy Set within first 200B tokens.  
  - Tokenizer: High fertility (>2) indicating poor compression; OMP transplantation causing semantic degradation (see Table J.35 drop on Easy Set).  
  - Evaluation: Discrepancy between Easy and Hard Set performance; poor long‑context results (RULER‑PT scores drop sharply with length).  
  - Alignment: Instruct models lagging baselines on instruction‑following (IFEval‑PT) and coding (HumanEval), indicating insufficient task diversity in SFT.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Data Mixture Ablation: Train four 1.5B models on Edu, Synth, Edu+Synth, Non‑Edu to quantify contribution of each component.  
  2. Tokenizer Benchmarking: Evaluate fertility, compression, and downstream NPM across candidate tokenizers to select the most efficient.  
  3. Continual Pretraining Scaling: Train Qwen3‑based models with varying token budgets (50B, 100B) to find compute‑performance tradeoff.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What architectural or data mixture modifications can mitigate the performance gap between Instruct and Think variants for coding and instruction adherence?**  
   - Basis: Instruct variants lag behind on Instruction Following (IFEval‑PT) and Coding (HumanEval); SFT dataset contains only ∼4% coding samples and lacks samples designed to benchmark IFEval‑style prompts.  
   - Why unresolved: The exact imbalance in task‑specific data and potential negative interference from the multi‑task mixture are not systematically analyzed; the optimal ratio or selection strategy remains unknown.  
   - Resolution: Ablation experiments varying the proportion and quality of coding and instruction‑following data within a fixed compute budget, coupled with an analysis of task interference during multi‑task training.

2. **Can synthetic data generation costs be reduced by 1–2 orders of magnitude while maintaining the quality gains observed from GigaVerbo‑v2 Synth, and what alternative strategies achieve this?**  
   - Basis: Synthetic data generation accounted for roughly 73% of the total tracked energy; authors state future work should prioritize low‑cost generation strategies.  
   - Why unresolved: No exploration of cost‑reduction techniques (e.g., using smaller models, active learning, iterative refinement) is attempted; the marginal utility of additional synthetic tokens relative to generation cost is unquantified.  
   - Resolution: Comparative study using different model scales (e.g., 7B vs. 32B) for generation, filtered by learned quality classifiers, with a cost‑benefit analysis of downstream benchmark performance per kWh.

3. **Is the observed advantage of the "educational" quality filter in GigaVerbo‑v2 a proxy for a broader set of stylistic or structural features rather than content alone, and can these features be distilled into a more general data scoring function?**  
   - Basis: Ablation shows Edu+Synth mixtures vastly outperform Non‑Edu data. The Educational Quality annotation prompt was designed for school‑level use, while best‑performing models excel on reasoning exams (ENEM, OAB), suggesting the filter may capture more than narrow educational content.  
   - Why unresolved: The paper does not analyze correlation between the specific criteria in the LLM judge prompt and downstream performance on different benchmarks; synthetic data lacks explicit educational annotations yet is beneficial.  
   - Resolution: Feature ablation study decomposing the "educational" classifier’s decision into linguistic features (lexical diversity, syntactic complexity, factual density) and correlating them with performance across reasoning, coding, and general knowledge benchmarks.

4. **To what extent is the success of continual pretraining with OMP‑based tokenizer transplantation generalizable to other language pairs and base model families beyond Portuguese and Qwen3?**  
   - Basis: The method is presented as a key contribution, but prior literature shows mixed results (e.g., Curio’s success vs. other failures). The impact of vocabulary reduction depth (151K → 49K) is not isolated from the choice of Qwen3.  
   - Why unresolved: No experiments varying the base architecture (e.g., Llama vs. Gemma) or target language morphological properties; generalizability remains untested.  
   - Resolution: Controlled study applying the same OMP transplantation to a different multilingual base model (e.g., Llama‑3.2 or Mistral) for a different low‑resource language, measuring performance delta against a from‑scratch baseline with matched tokenizer.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: Exact OMP transplantation parameters (sparsity level, iteration count) are unspecified, risking embedding reconstruction quality.
- Infrastructure dependencies: Precise GPU count, micro‑batch sizes, and kernel optimizations (FlashAttention2, Liger) are not detailed, affecting throughput and final model quality.
- Synthetic data fidelity: The degree to which synthetic CoT traces match genuine reasoning distribution remains unvalidated; over‑reliance may limit generalization.

## Confidence
- Performance claims (SOTA NPM, efficiency vs. larger models): **Medium**
- Tokenizer efficiency advantages (lowest fertility, highest compression): **High**
- Continual pretraining compute savings (<0.3% additional): **Medium**
- Data mixture ablations (Edu+Synth benefit): **Medium‑High**
