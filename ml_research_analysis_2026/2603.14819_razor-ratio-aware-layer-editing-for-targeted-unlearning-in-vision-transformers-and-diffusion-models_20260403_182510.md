# RAZOR: Ratio-Aware Layer Editing for Targeted Unlearning in Vision Transformers and Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14819
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14819
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RAZOR achieves state-of-the-art unlearning with near-perfect forgetting and retention on CLIP, superior generative fidelity on Stable Diffusion, and effective multimodal reasoning preservation in VLMs, while being robust to 4-bit quantization.

## Executive Summary
RAZOR introduces a framework for precise, efficient unlearning in transformer-based vision and diffusion models without retraining. It identifies a minimal set of critical layers and attention heads using a ratio-aware gradient saliency score, which balances forgetting pressure against retention preservation. Model parameters are then updated via a multi-objective loss that enforces forgetting, maintains utility, and stabilizes representations. An iterative refinement process expands the edited set until unlearning targets are met. Evaluations on CLIP, Stable Diffusion, and LLaVA demonstrate superior performance over prior methods, with strong robustness under 4-bit quantization.

## Method Summary
The RAZOR pipeline begins by computing one-shot gradients for each candidate layer/head on both the forget set ($g_f^l = \nabla_{\theta_l} L_{\text{forget}}$) and retain set ($g_r^l = \nabla_{\theta_l} L_{\text{retain}}$). Layers are scored by $\phi(l) = \frac{\|g_f^l\|^2}{\|\theta_l\|^2 + \varepsilon} \cdot (1 - \cos(g_f^l, g_r^l))^\alpha$, favoring high forgetting influence and low retention alignment. An initial set $K$ is selected by thresholding $\phi(l)$. For each layer in $K$, a composite gradient $g_{\text{RAZOR}} = -\lambda_f \rho g_f^l + g_r^l + \lambda_m g_{\text{mismatch}}$ is computed, where $g_{\text{mismatch}}$ penalizes drift from a frozen baseline. A per-layer step size $\lambda_l$ is found via binary search on validation metrics, and parameters are updated. If unlearning targets are unmet, $\phi(l)$ is recomputed on the updated model, and the next highest-salience layer is added to $K$, repeating for up to $T_{\text{max}}$ iterations.

## Key Results
- **CLIP:** Near-perfect forgetting (M1 ↓ to 40.0% from 42–97%, M2 ↓ to 22.0), perfect retention stability (M5 ↑ to 100.0%), high utility (M4 ↑ to 94.0%), and robustness to 4-bit quantization.
- **Stable Diffusion:** Near-perfect generative fidelity (UA/IRA/CRA ↑ to 98.80–100.00) with superior efficiency (trade-off score 66.86 vs. 59.42 for SLUG).
- **LLaVA:** Forget accuracy reduced to 2.2% while preserving multimodal reasoning scores (MME, GQA, MMBench).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Ratio-Aware Gradient Scoring
- **Claim:** Ratio-aware gradient scoring identifies layers where altering parameters will efficiently induce forgetting while minimally disrupting retained knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** For each layer $l$, compute $g_f^l = \nabla_{\theta_l} L_{\text{forget}}$ and $g_r^l = \nabla_{\theta_l} L_{\text{retain}}$. Combine via $\phi(l) = \frac{\|g_f^l\|^2}{\|\theta_l\|^2 + \varepsilon} \cdot (1 - \cos(g_f^l, g_r^l))^\alpha$. The first term measures forgetting pressure (gradient magnitude relative to weight norm). The second term penalizes layers where forgetting and retention gradients are aligned (high cosine similarity), favoring edits orthogonal to retention directions.
- **Core assumption:** The geometric relationship (cosine similarity) between forgetting and retention gradient directions predicts a layer's contribution to collateral damage when edited. High $\phi(l)$ indicates we can push the model away from forget-set knowledge with minimal interference to retain-set utility.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states the method identifies layers contributing to forgetting while preserving knowledge; [section 3.2] defines $\phi(l)$ and explains its interpretation. The orthogonality assumption is novel and primarily validated by the paper's experiments.
- **Break condition:** If forget and retain gradient distributions are inherently aligned (high cosine similarity across many layers), $\phi(l)$ will be low for most layers, making it difficult to find high-saliency candidates and potentially stalling the iterative process.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-Objective Regularized Loss
- **Claim:** A constrained, multi-objective loss function ($L_{\text{RAZOR}}$) simultaneously enforces forgetting, preserves utility, and stabilizes latent representations, preventing destructive interference.
- **Mechanism:** The composite loss is $L_{\text{RAZOR}}(\theta) = L_{\text{retain}} + \lambda_f \rho L_{\text{forget}} + \lambda_m L_{\text{mismatch}}$. $L_{\text{retain}}$ (e.g., symmetric InfoNCE for CLIP) maintains alignment on retain data. $L_{\text{forget}}$ (e.g., cosine push-away) reduces alignment on forget data, implemented as gradient ascent. $L_{\text{mismatch}}$ (e.g., similarity drift regularizer) penalizes changes to forget-set representations relative to a frozen baseline. $\rho \in (0,1]$ directly trades off forgetting strength versus retention preservation.
- **Core assumption:** Separating objectives and using $\rho$ allows a single update step to resolve the conflict between $L_{\text{forget}}$ and $L_{\text{retain}}$. $L_{\text{mismatch}}$ prevents over-correction and erasure beyond the target.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions "carefully regularized rule"; [section 3.3] defines the three losses; [section 5.4] ablation shows removing any component degrades performance.
- **Break condition:** Incorrect $\rho$ (too high/low) leads to under- or over-editing. $\lambda_m$ must be balanced; too high anchors the model too closely to the original, preventing effective forgetting.

### Mechanism 3: Iterative Layer Expansion
- **Claim:** Iterative, greedy expansion of the edited layer set ($K$) ensures sufficient forgetting without a priori knowing how many layers are needed, while avoiding unnecessary edits.
- **Mechanism:** Start with $K_0 = \{l : \phi(l) > \tau\}$. Apply blended gradient update to all layers in $K_0$. Evaluate unlearning metrics. If targets not met, recompute $\phi(l)$ on the updated model, find next most salient unused layer $l^*$, append to $K$, and update that layer. Repeat up to $T_{\text{max}}$ (e.g., 6).
- **Core assumption:** Knowledge of a target concept is distributed across multiple layers/heads, not perfectly localized. An initial high-saliency selection is a good seed, but additional layers must be added progressively. Recomputing saliency after each edit accounts for shifts in internal representations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes gradual growth; [section 3.5] details the iteration steps; [section 5.4] shows "All Losses, No Iterations" performs worse than full iterative method.
- **Break condition:** If the concept is extremely distributed or has deep residuals, even 6 iterations may be insufficient ($t > T_{\text{max}}$). Conversely, if $K_0$ already cascades effectively, further iterations may add negligible compute overhead.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Gradient-based saliency for parameter importance**  
  *Why needed:* RAZOR's selection mechanism relies on per-layer gradient norms ($\|g_f^l\|^2$, $\|g_r^l\|^2$) to measure sensitivity to forget and retain losses.  
  *Quick check:* If a layer's gradient w.r.t. the forget loss is large, what does that imply about that layer's role in representing the forget-set concept?

- **Concept: Multi-objective optimization with a trade-off parameter**  
  *Why needed:* The $\rho$ parameter in $L_{\text{RAZOR}}$ is a Lagrange multiplier-style knob balancing $L_{\text{forget}}$ and $L_{\text{retain}}$. Understanding how a single scalar controls the Pareto frontier between two competing goals is key to using RAZOR.  
  *Quick check:* If you increase $\rho$ in $\lambda_f \rho L_{\text{forget}}$, what is the expected effect on M1 (Forget Accuracy) and M4 (Retain Accuracy)?

- **Concept: Transformer architecture granularity (layers, heads, MLPs)**  
  *Why needed:* RAZOR operates at the level of individual transformer layers and, implicitly, attention heads (as components within a layer $l$). Knowing the structure of ViT/CLIP (MSA, MLP blocks) and diffusion text encoders is necessary to map $l$ to actual parameter groups.  
  *Quick check:* In a ViT block, which sub-components (MSA, MLP) are typically considered separate "layers" $l$ for the purpose of gradient computation and editing in RAZOR?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Pretrained Model $f_\theta$ -> Gradient Compute Module -> Saliency Scorer -> Loss Composer -> Update Engine -> Iteration Controller -> Metrics Evaluation (with Frozen Baseline $\theta^0$ used for $L_{\text{mismatch}}$)

- **Critical path:**  
  Gradient Computation -> Saliency Scoring -> Initial Layer Selection -> Loss Definition -> Per-Layer Update (with Binary Search) -> Metric Evaluation -> (If needed) Saliency Re-computation -> Next Layer Selection -> Repeat.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Precision vs. Efficiency:** Editing more layers ($|K|$ larger) increases forgetting completeness but risks utility loss and higher compute. $\tau_{\text{init}}$ and $T_{\text{max}}$ control this.  
  - **Forgetting Strength vs. Stability:** $\rho$ and $\lambda_m$ are coupled. High $\rho$ (strong forget) needs sufficient $\lambda_m$ (drift control) to prevent catastrophic forgetting.  
  - **Generality vs. Tuning:** The framework is model-agnostic, but the instantiation of $L_{\text{retain/forget/mismatch}}$ is family-specific (CLIP vs. Diffusion vs. VLM). Each requires correct loss formulation.  
  - **One-shot vs. Iterative:** Iteration (Sec 3.5) is necessary; a one-shot edit (like SLUG) is brittle for distributed concepts.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Over-forgetting:** $L_{\text{retain}}$ or M4/IRA drops significantly. Likely cause: $\rho$ too high, $\lambda_m$ too low, or $K$ too large.  
  - **Under-forgetting:** M1/FA or UA remains high. Likely cause: $\tau_{\text{init}}$ too high (too few layers), $\rho$ too low, or $T_{\text{max}}$ reached without convergence.  
  - **Quantization Catastrophe:** Some methods (SSD, full fine-tuning) fail under 4-bit. RAZOR's layer-sparse edits are more robust, but failure would show a sharp metric drop post-quantization.  
  - **Iteration Stall:** $\phi(l)$ becomes non-positive for all remaining $l$ before metrics meet targets. Suggests either $\tau_{\text{init}}$ was too permissive initially, or the forgetting is impossible without full-model changes.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **CLIP Identity Unlearning (CIFAR-10/LAION):** Forget a specific celebrity (e.g., "Sydney Sweeney"). Primary metrics: M1 (Forget Accuracy), M2 (Cosine Similarity), M4/M5 (Retention/Stability). Goal: Show RAZOR achieves lower M1/M2 than SLUG/SSD while keeping M4/M5 near 100%.  
  2. **Stable Diffusion Style/Object Unlearning (UnlearnCanvas):** Forget a style (e.g., "pixel art") or object. Primary metrics: UA (Unlearned Accuracy), IRA (In-Retain Alignment), CRA (Content Alignment). Goal: Show RAZOR achieves near-perfect UA/IRA/CRA with better efficiency (Trade-off score) than ESD/FMN.  
  3. **LLaVA Identity Unlearning (CelebA):** Forget a celebrity in a VLM. Primary metrics: FA (Forget Accuracy), MME/GQA/MMBench (retained multimodal reasoning). Goal: Show drastic FA drop (to ~2%) with minimal drop on reasoning benchmarks, proving concept erasure without general capability loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How robust is RAZOR to adversarial attacks aiming to recover forgotten information?**  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The conclusion states "Future work should explore robustness under adversarial unlearning."  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper evaluates unlearning on standard benchmarks but does not include adversarial scenarios where attackers probe for forgotten data.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Conduct membership inference or model inversion attacks on RAZOR-edited models and compare recovery rates to baselines, measuring privacy leakage under adversarial settings.

- **Can RAZOR's multi-layer editing strategy generalize across diverse unlearning tasks without task-specific tuning?**  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] Future work mentions "cross-task generalization."  
  *Why unresolved:* RAZOR is evaluated separately on identity, style, and object removal; no experiments test applying a model unlearned on one task directly to another task.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Train RAZOR on one unlearning task (e.g., identity erasure) and apply the same edited model to a different task (e.g., concept removal) without re-tuning, then assess forgetting and retention metrics for significant degradation.

- **Is RAZOR effective for unlearning in audio and video generation models?**  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The conclusion calls for exploring "broader application domains such as audio and video generation."  
  *Why unresolved:* RAZOR is demonstrated only on vision transformers and text-to-image diffusion models, not on models for other modalities.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Adapt RAZOR to audio (e.g., AudioLDM) or video (e.g., Stable Video Diffusion) models, define unlearning objectives (e.g., removing specific sounds or motions), and benchmark with modality-specific metrics for fidelity and retention.

- **How does model scale affect RAZOR's layer selection and unlearning performance?**  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] RAZOR is tested on CLIP ViT-L/14 and SD-v3, but not on much larger models; efficiency claims lack stress tests at billion-parameter scales.  
  *Why unresolved:* Larger models may distribute knowledge differently, potentially breaking the assumption that a small set of layers is sufficient or that the ratio-aware saliency remains predictive.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Apply RAZOR to increasingly large models (e.g., 2B+ parameter diffusion models), measure the number of layers edited, computation time, memory use, and unlearning metrics (M1–M5 or UA/IRA/CRA), analyzing trends with scale.

## Limitations
- The ratio-aware saliency's orthogonality assumption (low cosine similarity predicting minimal collateral damage) is a novel but untested geometric prior across diverse architectures.
- Hyperparameter tuning ($\rho$, $\lambda_m$, $\tau_{\text{init}}$) is required per model family, with no published guidelines beyond ad-hoc validation searches.
- Iterative expansion is capped at $T_{\text{max}} \leq 6$; if concept distribution is severely deep or residual, convergence may stall without clear diagnostic signals.

## Confidence
- **Ratio-aware saliency ($\phi(l)$):** Medium (novel combination of terms lacks direct evidence; orthogonality assumption is plausible but unverified outside the paper's experiments).
- **Multi-objective loss ($L_{\text{RAZOR}}$):** Medium-High (ablations confirm necessity of all three terms, but hyperparameter interdependence ($\rho$, $\lambda_m$) is model-specific and not fully characterized).
- **Iterative layer expansion:** High (empirical gains over one-shot baselines (SLUG) are demonstrated, though scalability beyond six iterations is unexplored).

## Next Checks
1. **Orthogonality stress test:** Compute per-layer forget/retain gradient cosine similarities across CLIP, SD, and LLaVA on varied forget sets (e.g., fine-grained vs. abstract concepts). Correlate with measured post-edit utility loss to validate the $\phi(l)$ assumption.
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Systematically vary $\rho \in [0.1, 1.0]$ and $\lambda_m$ across orders of magnitude on a held-out validation set. Map the Pareto frontier of M1/M2 vs. M4/M5 to define safe operating ranges.
3. **Iteration ceiling ablation:** Run RAZOR with $T_{\text{max}} \in \{3,6,9,12\}$ on a subset of tasks. Track when metrics plateau and whether additional edits ever degrade performance, testing the sufficiency of the 6-iteration heuristic.
