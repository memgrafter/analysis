# Beyond the "Truth": Investigating Election Rumors on Truth Social During the 2024 Election

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.04631
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04631
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A multi-stage rumor detection agent achieved 94.6% accuracy and empirically confirmed that each additional rumor exposure increases sharing probability on Truth Social.

## Executive Summary
This study developed a scalable multi-stage Rumor Detection Agent to identify and analyze election rumors on Truth Social during the 2024 election. The agent combined a fine-tuned RoBERTa classifier, keyword filtering, and a two-pass GPT-4o mini verification, reducing LLM calls by over 93% while maintaining high precision. Analysis of nearly 15 million posts revealed a dose-response relationship: a user’s probability of sharing a rumor increased with each prior exposure, providing large-scale evidence for the illusory truth effect in an ideologically homogeneous network. Network simulations showed rapid contagion, with nearly one-quarter of users “infected” within four propagation iterations. Donald Trump emerged as the central node, with his posts driving 40–60% surges in platform-wide rumor volume.

## Method Summary
Researchers collected ~15 million Truth Social posts (September–December 2024, with historical data from May 2023) and defined rumors based on CISA’s list of election-related falsehoods (e.g., “Ballot Mail-In Fraud”). They built a Rumor Detection Agent with three stages: (1) a RoBERTa classifier fine-tuned on 10,000 synthetic examples (5k rumor, 5k non-rumor) generated via GPT-4o mini few-shot prompting; (2) a keyword filter aligned with CISA categories; (3) a two-pass GPT-4o mini verification that first classifies a post as rumor or not, then assesses certainty. Only ~6.8% of posts reached the LLM stage, drastically cutting costs. To study psychological dynamics, they constructed a directed repost network, counted cumulative user exposures to rumors, and computed sharing probability as a function of exposure count (k). They simulated propagation with a weighted threshold cascade model and measured user influence via a metric combining follower counts of authors and reposters.

## Key Results
- The Rumor Detection Agent achieved 94.6% accuracy and ~89.2% positive predictive value on a validation sample.
- Sharing probability rose monotonically with each additional rumor exposure, confirming the illusory truth effect in an ideologically homogeneous network.
- Donald Trump was the top influencer; his rumor posts correlated with 40–60% surges in overall platform rumor volume.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Efficient High-Precision Rumor Detection**
- **Claim**: The multi-stage pipeline achieves high labeling precision with minimal LLM usage.
- **Mechanism**: A fine-tuned RoBERTa classifier and keyword filters screen posts, passing only ~6.8% to a two-pass GPT-4o mini verification, reducing API calls by >93%.
- **Core assumption**: Non-rumor posts have linguistically distinctive patterns catchable by supervised models and keywords; borderline cases require LLM reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states the pipeline reduces LLM calls by over 93%; Section 3.2 details stages; corpus shows other detection methods but not this specific design.
- **Break condition**: If Truth Social language evolves (new slang), RoBERTa may degrade, increasing LLM-pass rate and eroding cost savings.

**Mechanism 2: Dose-Response Illusory Truth Effect**
- **Claim**: Each additional rumor exposure increases sharing probability, confirming the illusory truth effect in an ideologically homogeneous network.
- **Mechanism**: Repeated exposures enhance perceived accuracy through familiarity; without dissenting views in echo chambers, this compounds monotonically.
- **Core assumption**: Sharing behavior is driven by cumulative exposure counts, not temporal decay or source diversity.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract notes sharing probability rises steadily with exposure; Section 4.2 presents Figure 4 and Eq. 3 showing monotonic rise; corpus lacks psychological exposure dynamics.
- **Break condition**: If the network becomes heterogeneous, counter-exposures could flatten or invert the curve.

**Mechanism 3: Elite Amplification via Central Node**
- **Claim**: Donald Trump functions as a central node whose rumor posts drive 40–60% surges in platform-wide rumor volume.
- **Mechanism**: High-follower accounts seed rumors; their posts trigger reposts and inspire new homologous posts by other influencers, amplifying cascades.
- **Core assumption**: Influence is measured by potential audience reach (follower counts of authors and reposters), and Trump’s posts uniquely catalyze secondary diffusion.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states Trump’s posts drive surges; Section 4.5 quantifies his influence score and temporal diffusion; corpus has general influencer studies but not this Truth Social finding.
- **Break condition**: If Trump’s account is deplatformed or posting decreases, contagion may slow, but other elite nodes could partially compensate.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Data ingestion -> RoBERTa classifier -> Keyword filter -> LLM Pass 1 (classification) -> LLM Pass 2 (certainty) -> Post-label output  
**Critical path**: Raw post → RoBERTa scoring (if rumor-prob > threshold) → keyword check (if match) → LLM prompt 1 → LLM prompt 2 → final label; non-rumor posts exit early.  
**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Cost vs. precision: Filtering reduces LLM calls but risks missing nuanced rumors.  
- Synthetic data boosts RoBERTa training but may not capture all real language.  
- Strict CISA-aligned definition minimizes false positives but excludes gray-area posts.  
**Failure signatures**:  
- Sudden increase in LLM-pass rate (>10%) indicating filter drift.  
- High false positive rate on ambiguous claims (e.g., speculative vulnerabilities).  
- Plateau in sharing probability curve suggesting saturation not captured.  
- Influence analysis showing multiple central nodes post-Trump spike.  
**First 3 experiments**:  
1. Validate the agent on a fresh stratified sample of 500 Truth Social posts, measuring accuracy, PPV, and false positive reasons against human labels.  
2. Simulate propagation with varied threshold ϕ (1–10) and compare infection rates to empirical exposure data.  
3. Perform an interrupted time-series analysis around Trump’s rumor posts to quantify volume surges.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Do election rumors originating on Truth Social propagate to mainstream social media platforms, and what are the mechanisms and scale of such cross-platform diffusion? (Unresolved due to lack of cross-platform tracking.)
- Which intervention strategies (e.g., prebunking, flagging, algorithmic downranking) are most effective at reducing rumor spread on ideologically homogeneous platforms? (Unresolved; study focused on detection, not intervention.)
- How does accounting for overlapping follower sets alter user influence measurement, and does it change key spreader identification? (Unresolved; overlap data not fully observable, study uses upper-bound metric.)
- To what extent are the observed rumor dynamics generalizable to other alt-tech or ideologically homogeneous platforms? (Unresolved; study is platform-specific, no cross-platform comparison.)

## Limitations
- Synthetic data generation prompts and seed examples for RoBERTa training are unspecified, risking domain mismatch.
- Full CISA rumor list (beyond categories) is missing, hindering keyword filter completeness and LLM verification fidelity.
- “Impression” definition for exposure counting is ambiguous, potentially biasing the dose-response analysis.
- Influence metric (Eq. 2) details on follower overlap handling are vague, possibly inflating Trump’s score.
- Findings may not generalize beyond Truth Social’s ideologically homogeneous network.

## Confidence
- **Rumor Detection Agent Efficiency**: Medium (pipeline clear but synthetic data specifics and keyword completeness uncertain).
- **Illusory Truth Dose-Response**: Medium (empirical curve robust but exposure definition unverified; plateau may reflect sparsity).
- **Trump as Central Node**: Medium (spike correlation quantified but influence metric reproducibility weak; secondary diffusion inferential).
