# SAIL: Similarity-Aware Guidance and Inter-Caption Augmentation-based Learning for Weakly-Supervised Dense Video Captioning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv**: 2603.05437
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05437
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: On ActivityNet Captions, SAIL boosts weakly-supervised CIDEr from 33.42 to 35.38 and F1 from 56.20 to 57.00, exceeding prior SOTA and some fully-supervised methods in localization.

## Executive Summary
SAIL addresses the core challenges of weakly-supervised dense video captioning (WSDVC), where models must localize and describe all events in untrimmed videos using only sentence-level captions. Existing Gaussian masking methods produce uniform, semantically misaligned masks and suffer from sparse supervision. SAIL introduces two synergistic components: a similarity-aware training objective that leverages CLIP’s cross-modal alignment to steer masks toward caption-relevant visual content, and an LLM-based caption augmentation strategy that generates synthetic event descriptions for intermediate segments. These synthetic captions are integrated via an auxiliary inter-mask mechanism, which densifies supervision without interfering with the primary captioning objective. Experiments on ActivityNet Captions and YouCook2 show consistent gains, establishing new SOTA in the weakly-supervised setting and even surpassing several fully-supervised methods in localization. Ablations confirm the additive contribution of both components, with negligible extra computation.

## Method Summary
SAIL extends the ILCACM baseline, which learns Gaussian masks (center, width) via a transformer decoder conditioned on event queries. Complementary captioning produces a positive caption from the masked region and a negative caption from the remainder, trained with a caption decoder (Distilled GPT-2). SAIL adds (1) a similarity-aware loss L_sim that maximizes cosine similarity between pooled masked video features (CLIP) and the corresponding caption embedding, using a margin ranking loss to distinguish mismatched pairs; and (2) an LLM augmentation pipeline that, for each consecutive pair of ground-truth captions, prompts Qwen3-8B to infer a plausible intermediate caption. These synthetic captions guide an auxiliary inter-mask mechanism: inter-masks are placed between predicted event centers (fixed width w_inter=0.6), and their features are aligned with synthetic captions via L_aug. The total loss combines L_pos, L_neg, L_sim, and α_aug L_aug (α_aug=0.25). During inference, the model first generates a full caption, predicts event masks, then refines captions with a masked prompt. CLIP encoders are frozen; temporal features are aggregated from uniformly sampled frames (32 for ActivityNet, 100 for YouCook2).

## Key Results
- CIDEr improves from 33.42 to 35.38 and localization F1 from 56.20 to 57.00 on ActivityNet Captions, beating prior weakly-supervised SOTA.
- SAIL outperforms several fully-supervised methods in localization despite no boundary supervision.
- Gains are consistent on YouCook2; ablations show both proposed components contribute additively, with synthetic captions beneficial even at 25% density.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Similarity-Aware Mask Guidance
The model is trained to align masked video features with their corresponding captions in the CLIP embedding space via a margin ranking loss. This encourages masks to highlight semantically relevant regions rather than uniformly partitioning the video. Core assumption: CLIP’s shared space preserves cross-modal similarity; visual regions pertinent to a caption yield high cosine similarity. Evidence: The loss directly optimizes this similarity (Sec. 3.2). Break condition: If CLIP embeddings are misaligned (e.g., domain shift), the similarity signal may become noisy, leading to irrelevant masks.

### LLM-Based Caption Augmentation
To densify sparse supervision, synthetic event captions are generated for segments between consecutive ground-truth captions using an LLM (Qwen3-8B). The narrative flow between known events implies plausible intermediates, which the LLM infers via structured prompts. These captions provide additional alignment signals. Evidence: The augmentation is described in Sec. 3.3 and Algorithm 1. Break condition: Noisy or temporally incoherent synthetic captions could harm performance if not properly regularized.

### Auxiliary Inter-Mask Guidance
Synthetic captions are not forced to match exact regions; instead, inter-masks (centered between predicted event masks with fixed width) are created, and their features are aligned with synthetic captions via L_aug. This acts as a soft regularizer that refines mask boundaries without degrading the main objective. Evidence: Table 6 shows using L_aug outperforms treating synthetic captions as hard negatives. Break condition: Poor choices of inter-mask width w_inter may cause overlap or miss transitions, reducing effectiveness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Weakly-Supervised Dense Video Captioning (WSDVC)**: Task of localizing and describing all events in untrimmed videos using only sentence-level captions (no timestamps). *Why needed*: SAIL targets WSDVC to avoid expensive boundary annotations. *Quick check*: What supervision is available? Only a set of captions per video, not their temporal extents.
- **Gaussian Masking and Complementary Captioning (ILCACM Baseline)**: Learns differentiable Gaussian masks over time to select event-specific segments. Uses complementary captioning: a positive caption from the masked region and a negative caption from the remainder to ensure non-overlapping coverage. *Why needed*: SAIL builds on this baseline and addresses its limitation (uniform masks). *Quick check*: How does the baseline enforce that masks cover different temporal regions? Through non-overlap and complementary captioning losses.
- **Cross-Modal Similarity with CLIP**: CLIP embeds images and text into a shared space where cosine similarity reflects semantic relatedness, pre-trained on large-scale image-text pairs. *Why needed*: SAIL leverages CLIP to compute similarity between masked video features and captions, providing a training signal without extra annotation. *Quick check*: What does a high CLIP similarity score indicate? That the video region is semantically aligned with the caption.
- **LLM-Based Data Augmentation**: Using a large language model to generate additional training data, here synthetic event captions, guided by prompts. *Why needed*: Sparse GT captions limit supervision; synthetic intermediates densify the signal. *Quick check*: How does SAIL generate synthetic captions? By prompting Qwen3-8B with consecutive GT captions and asking for a transitional description.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Video encoder (CLIP ViT-L/14 spatial features) → Temporal encoder → Mask decoder (Transformer) → Gaussian mask generation (predict center c_i, width w_i) → Masked feature extractor (element-wise multiply + average pooling) → Caption decoder (Distilled GPT-2). Additionally: CLIP text encoder for caption embeddings; LLM (Qwen3-8B, offline) for synthetic captions; inter-mask generator for auxiliary loss.

**Critical path (training)**:
1. Input video → spatial features → temporal feature sequence.
2. For each event query, predict Gaussian mask parameters (c_i, w_i) and build mask M_i.
3. Positive masked features V · M_i → caption decoder → positive caption → L_pos.
4. Negative masked features V · (1-M_i) → caption decoder → negative caption → L_neg.
5. Pool positive masked features → compute similarity with corresponding caption (using CLIP) → apply margin ranking loss L_sim.
6. After all event centers are predicted, generate inter-masks between consecutive centers (fixed width w_inter) → pool their features → compute similarity with synthetic transition caption → L_aug.
7. Total loss L = L_pos + L_neg + L_sim + α_aug L_aug (+ optional mask diversity term).
8. Backpropagate; update mask decoder, caption decoder, temporal encoder (CLIP encoders typically frozen).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- CLIP encoders are frozen to reduce cost and leverage strong zero-shot alignment, but may not adapt to video dynamics; fine-tuning could help but risks overfitting.
- Synthetic captions are integrated via an auxiliary loss; using them as hard negatives degrades performance (Table 6), but requires tuning α_aug and w_inter.
- Gaussian masks are smooth and differentiable but may be less precise; binary masks would be exact but non-differentiable; Cauchy masks were empirically worse.
- Inter-mask width w_inter is fixed; too large → overlap with main masks, too small → miss transitions.

**Failure signatures**:
- Masks collapse to uniform widths across videos: check width standard deviation; may need to increase L_sim weighting or ensure caption features are normalized.
- L_sim or L_aug not decreasing: indicates poor alignment; inspect masked feature quality and CLIP embeddings.
- Synthetic caption quality low: evaluate via human inspection; if noisy, reduce α_aug or improve LLM prompt.
- Degraded metrics after adding synthetic augmentation: suggests noise outweighs benefit; adjust synthetic density or prompt.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline replication**: Train ILCACM on ActivityNet and verify reported numbers (CIDEr ~33.42, F1 ~56.20).
2. **Ablation of L_sim**: Train SAIL without the similarity-aware loss; expect drop in both captioning and localization (Table 4 row 1 vs 2).
3. **Ablation of synthetic L_aug**: Train SAIL without synthetic captions and L_aug; expect performance drop, especially under sparse annotation (Table 4 row 1 vs 3).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can SAIL be generalized to other multimodal tasks (e.g., video QA, temporal action localization) to address data scarcity?
- How does SAIL perform on videos with a single annotated event, where the LLM augmentation (which requires consecutive captions) cannot be applied?
- To what extent do errors or biases in LLM-generated synthetic captions affect the model, and how does the auxiliary inter‑mask mechanism robustly mitigate potential noise compared to using them as hard negatives?
- Is the fixed inter‑mask width hyperparameter (w_inter) optimal across all videos and event types, or should it be adaptively determined based on content or predicted characteristics?

## Limitations
- Ambiguities in inference details (e.g., mask thresholding, refinement) and training specifics (event query initialization, handling variable event counts) hinder faithful reproduction.
- Dependence on CLIP embeddings may suffer from domain shift, and the method is sensitive to hyperparameters like w_inter and α_aug.
- LLM-generated synthetic captions could introduce noise if temporally incoherent, though the auxiliary loss mitigates this to some extent.

## Confidence
