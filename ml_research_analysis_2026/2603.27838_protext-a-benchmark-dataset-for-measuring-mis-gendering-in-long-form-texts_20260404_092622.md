# ProText: A benchmark dataset for measuring (mis)gendering in long-form texts
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.27838
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27838
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LLMs rarely misgender when explicit gendered pronouns are present (<5%), but frequently introduce gendered pronouns (often stereotypical) when inputs use neutral/they/no pronouns; also exhibit masculine default and heteronormative assumptions.

## Executive Summary
ProText is a new benchmark dataset designed to measure (mis)gendering in generative LLM outputs for long-form English texts. It comprises 640 texts crossing three dimensions: Theme (names, occupations, titles, kinship terms), Theme Category (stereotypically male, female, neutral), and Pronoun Category (he, she, they, none). A mini case study with GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash on two rewrite tasks reveals that models maintain gender correctly when inputs contain explicit gendered pronouns but often inject gendered pronouns otherwise, reinforcing stereotypes and defaulting to masculine for ambiguous nouns. The dataset enables systematic evaluation beyond the binary, uncovering biases such as singular they pluralization and heteronormative assumptions.

## Method Summary
The evaluation employs a 240-text subset drawn from the 640-item ProText benchmark. Each text is characterized by a Theme (names, occupations, titles, kinship), Theme Category (stereotypically male, female, neutral), and Pronoun Category (he, she, they, none). Texts average ~342 characters and exhibit stylistic variation. Participants are two commercial LLMs, GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash (late April 2025), each prompted with two rewrite tasks: (P1) “Transform this passage into a journalistic writing style” and (P2) “Rewrite the text to make it more humorous”. For each input, a single output is recorded (first variant if multiple).

Outputs are automatically scored by a DSPy-based autograder using Claude Sonnet 3.5 to classify the gender of the primary Theme individual. Misgendering is defined as changing the input’s gender; gendering is the introduction of a gendered pronoun where the input had neutral/they/none. The automated labels are supplemented by manual review, especially for the “none” category and ambiguous cases.

## Key Results
- Misgendering rates are very low (<5%) when input texts explicitly contain gendered pronouns.
- Gendering occurs frequently, especially for titles and kinship terms, and aligns with stereotypes (e.g., adding “she” for nurses).
- Models default to masculine pronouns for ambiguous nouns, show marginally higher misgendering for stereotypically feminine nouns, enforce heteronormative assumptions, and sometimes pluralize singular “they”.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ProText isolates gender bias by systematically varying theme and pronoun categories across realistic long-form texts. Its design ensures that any change in gendered references can be directly attributed to the model's transformation rather than input ambiguity. The combination of an automated classifier (validated with manual checks) allows scalable measurement while capturing nuanced errors like introducing gender where none existed or reinforcing stereotypes. The benchmark's focus on non-binary neutral pronouns extends evaluation beyond the traditional male/female binary, revealing biases that simpler datasets miss.

## Foundational Learning
• **Concept 1: Gendered Pronouns (he/she/they)**
  - *Why needed*: These are the primary signals the benchmark tracks; understanding their roles is essential to grasp what constitutes misgendering or gendering.
  - *Quick check*: Identify the gendered pronoun in: "The teacher explained they would grade the papers." Answer: they.

• **Concept 2: Stereotypical Gender Associations**
  - *Why needed*: Theme Category (male/female/neutral) relies on societal stereotypes; the benchmark tests whether models reinforce these.
  - *Quick check*: List two occupations stereotypically associated with women (e.g., nurse, librarian) and two with men (e.g., engineer, plumber).

• **Concept 3: Misgendering vs. Gendering**
  - *Why needed*: Distinct error types: misgendering changes existing gender, gendering adds gender where none existed.
  - *Quick check*: Provide an example of misgendering (e.g., changing "she" to "he") and gendering (e.g., changing "they" to "she").

• **Concept 4: Long-form Text Transformation**
  - *Why needed*: The benchmark evaluates models on rewriting real-world communicative texts, not isolated sentences, affecting anaphora resolution and consistency.
  - *Quick check*: What makes a text "long-form" in ProText? (Approximately 65 words, includes context beyond a single mention.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Input Text + Prompt -> LLM -> Output Text -> Autograder -> Gender Classification -> Metric Computation

**Critical Path:** 1) Define themes and categories. 2) Generate/collect texts per (Theme, Theme Category, Pronoun Category). 3) Apply transformation prompts via target LLMs. 4) Run autograder on all outputs. 5) Manually verify ambiguous cases (especially "none" category). 6) Aggregate mis/gendering rates.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
- Autograder vs. manual labeling: autograder enables scale but has limited F1 (0.54 for neutral), requiring manual audits.
- Prompt selection: two rewrite prompts cover distinct styles but may not generalize to other tasks.
- Text length/style: short communicative texts allow controlled experiments but may not capture complexities of longer narratives.
- Coverage: themes limited to four types; kin/occupations may not represent all domains.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Autograder misidentifies the primary Theme individual, particularly when input has no explicit pronoun (F1=0.54).
- Models gender secondary individuals (e.g., author) escaping detection if autograder only checks primary.
- Singular "they" outputs pluralized (e.g., "they are" -> "these journalists are"), indicating over-correction.
- Prompt-induced artifacts (e.g., journalistic style prompting indirect speech, increasing author gendering).

**Three First Experiments:**
1. Autograder validation: Manually label ~100 outputs, compute agreement, adjust autograder prompts if needed.
2. Prompt consistency test: Run both prompts on the same subset of inputs to ensure they induce expected style changes without confounding gender effects.
3. Stereotype amplification check: Compare gendering rates for stereotypically male vs. female occupations to verify differential bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Neopronoun Handling**: How do contemporary LLMs handle neopronouns (e.g., fae/faer, ze/hir)? ProText only includes they/them due to author unfamiliarity; anecdotal testing suggests struggles even with they/them. Resolution requires modifying ProText texts with neopronouns and measuring (mis)gendering.
2. **Generalization to Longer/Diverse Genres**: Do patterns hold for longer texts (e.g., essays, narratives)? ProText texts average ~65 words and are communicative; longer texts may introduce anaphoric complexities. Resolution: create extended ProText variants with longer, genre-diverse texts.
3. **Multiple Individuals**: How do LLMs handle texts mentioning multiple people with conflicting gender cues? ProText focuses on one primary individual and the author; multiple individuals are excluded. Resolution: augment dataset with secondary characters of different genders and test pronoun consistency.
4. **Cross-lingual Generalizability**: Are patterns consistent across languages with grammatical gender (e.g., German, French) or without (e.g., Chinese)? ProText is English-only. Resolution: translate ProText to selected languages, preserve categories, and compare (mis)gendering frequencies.
5. **Prompt/Model Specificity**: Are observed behaviors specific to the two prompts and models used? Authors note possible prompt-induced effects (e.g., gendering authors in P1). Resolution: replicate with diverse transformation prompts (e.g., summarization) and multiple LLM families/versions.

## Limitations
- Exact 240-text subset IDs and full prompt wording are unreported, hindering precise replication.
- Autograder reliability is limited, especially for neutral pronoun cases (F1=0.54), potentially missing errors.
- Model API parameters (temperature, etc.) are unspecified, introducing variability in reproductions.

## Confidence
- Dataset existence & design: High
- Case study results: Medium
- Autograder accuracy: Low
