# VideoAtlas: Navigating Long-Form Video in Logarithmic Compute

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17948
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17948
- **Reference count**: 32
- **Primary result**: Video-RLM achieves logarithmic compute scaling (O(log T)), up to 9.7× fewer tokens than linear baselines on 10-hour videos, with minimal accuracy degradation (<1%).

## Executive Summary
VideoAtlas addresses long-form video understanding by representing video as a lossless, navigable hierarchical grid. Unlike methods relying on captions or preprocessing, any video can be encoded as a recursive K×K grid where each cell contains a representative frame. The Video-RLM agent employs a parallel Master-Worker architecture to explore this grid recursively: a Master coordinates global exploration while Workers concurrently drill into promising subtrees. Evidence is collected into a multimodal scratchpad preserving raw frames, enabling final decisions based on actual pixels. This design yields compute that scales logarithmically with video duration, up to 9.7× fewer tokens compared to uniform sampling at 10 hours, and incurs only a tiny accuracy drop (e.g., 0.7% on Video-MME) where baselines collapse. A maximum exploration depth provides a principled compute-accuracy trade-off, and compute allocation emerges adaptively from query granularity without supervision.

## Method Summary
The core of VideoAtlas is a hierarchical grid environment. For a video of duration T, the root grid (depth 0) divides the timeline into K² contiguous intervals and displays a representative frame (typically at the midpoint) in each cell. An EXPAND action descends into a child grid for a selected cell, increasing temporal resolution by a factor of K² per depth. At depth d, the time span per cell is Δt_d = T / K^{2(d+1)}. The agent navigates this grid using actions such as EXPAND, BACKTRACK, ZOOM, INVESTIGATE, and ADD_TO_SCRATCHPAD, all defined within a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where the state includes the current position, depth, and memory structures (M⁺ for evidence, M⁻ for dead zones, and a navigation stack). Video-RLM instantiates this MDP with a parallel Master-Worker scheme: the Master examines the root grid and current evidence, assigns the most promising cells to multiple Workers, and after each round performs uncertainty analysis (sufficiency check, temporal interpolation, noise erasure) to guide further exploration. Workers operate independently within their assigned subtrees, performing depth-first or breadth-first exploration and committing evidence to M⁺. The final answer is produced by querying the vision-language model with a rendered grid of all collected evidence, avoiding compression artifacts. The system can be built with any VLM backbone (e.g., Qwen, Gemini) and leverages multimodal prefix caching (via vLLM) to reuse image tokens across calls, reducing effective compute.

## Key Results
- Compute scales logarithmically with video duration (O(log T) vs. O(T)), achieving up to 9.7× fewer tokens than linear baselines at 10 hours.
- On 10-hour long-video benchmarks, Video-RLM exhibits minimal accuracy degradation (e.g., 0.7% drop on Video-MME) while uniform sampling and caption-based methods collapse (13.2% drop).
- Maximum exploration depth provides a principled compute-accuracy hyperparameter; accuracy rises with depth until a plateau.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Hierarchical Grid Representation Enables Logarithmic Access
- **Claim**: Representing video as a lossless, recursively zoomable K×K grid reduces the temporal search space from linear (O(T)) to logarithmic (O(log T)) with video duration.
- **Mechanism**: The root grid (S0) provides a full-video overview. Each EXPAND action descends into a child grid, increasing temporal resolution by a factor of K². Accessing any specific frame requires at most ⌈log_{K²}(T·fps)⌉ steps, as depth grows logarithmically with duration.
- **Core assumption**: Temporal locality can be exploited via coarse-to-fine navigation; relevant moments are identifiable at lower resolutions before committing to fine-grained inspection.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] and [section 3.1] state the logarithmic depth property and per-frame access bound.
- **Break condition**: If relevant events are visually indistinct even at maximum depth (sub‑millisecond spans) or the VLM cannot discriminate them, finer resolution yields no additional information.

### Mechanism 2: Parallel Master‑Worker Architecture Manages Exploration
- **Claim**: Coordinating global exploration (Master) with concurrent local search (Workers) efficiently accumulates lossless evidence without redundant computation.
- **Mechanism**: The Master assigns promising root‑grid cells to Workers each round. Workers operate independently in their assigned subtrees, performing DFS/BFS navigation and ADD_TO_SCRATCHPAD commits. The Master updates M⁺/M⁻ and performs uncertainty analysis to guide next‑round assignments, avoiding re‑exploration of dead zones.
- **Core assumption**: Video subregions are conditionally independent; evidence collection in one subtree does not depend on real‑time findings in another, allowing parallelization.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] and [section 3.2] describe the parallel architecture and independent worker exploration.
- **Break condition**: If the query requires cross‑subtree temporal reasoning (e.g., ordering events across non‑adjacent regions), parallel Workers may gather fragmented evidence requiring expensive late‑stage synthesis by the Master.

### Mechanism 3: Environment Budgeting via Depth Control & Emergent Adaptive Compute
- **Claim**: Bounding maximum exploration depth (d) provides a principled compute‑accuracy trade‑off, while compute allocation emerges adaptively from query granularity without supervision.
- **Mechanism**: d directly caps temporal precision (Δt_d = T / K^{2(d+1)}). Higher d yields finer resolution but more steps. The Master’s sufficiency check and uncertainty analysis dynamically allocate more exploration rounds for queries with scattered evidence (observed: 40% more tokens for 3+ answer positions vs. localized).
- **Core assumption**: Query difficulty correlates with spatial dispersion of relevant moments; more dispersed evidence requires more exploration rounds to gather sufficient context.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] and [section 4.4] show accuracy rising with depth and token variance across query types.
- **Break condition**: If query granularity is misestimated (e.g., specific detail but Master defaults to BFS), compute may be wasted on broad scans rather than targeted deep dives.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Hierarchical Grid as Markov Decision Process (MDP)**
  - *Why needed here*: The entire VideoAtlas framework is built on a formally defined MDP (state: current position/depth/memory; actions: navigation/perception/commit; observations: grid images). This formalism enables principled budgeting and future RL training.
  - *Quick check question*: How does defining video navigation as an MDP differ from treating it as a pure perception task? What component of the MDP directly controls compute?

- **Concept: Lossless Multimodal Scratchpad (M⁺)**
  - *Why needed here*: Unlike caption‑based methods, evidence is stored as raw visual frames with burned‑in metadata, preserving details discarded by text abstraction. This enables final decisions based on actual pixels, not summaries.
  - *Quick check question*: Why might storing evidence as a grid image (vs. separate image list) be critical for the Master’s final decision? What visual property does it enforce?

- **Concept: Recursive Exploration (from RLMs)**
  - *Why needed here*: Video‑RLM extends Recursive Language Models (RLMs) to video by using the grid as the recursive environment. Workers act as subagents exploring subtrees, accumulating evidence in M⁺ without compressing it into the main context.
  - *Quick check question*: How does VideoAtlas provide the “structured environment” that RLMs require? Why wouldn’t RLMs work directly on a raw video stream?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: VideoAtlas Environment (grid renderer, state tracker (p, d, M⁺, M⁻, σ), action executor (EXPAND, ZOOM, INVESTIGATE, ADD_TO_SCRATCHPAD, BACKTRACK, FINISHED)) → Video‑RLM Agents (Master: frontier queue, uncertainty analysis, final decision; Workers: autonomous subtree exploration with DFS/BFS) → Scratchpad Renderer (converts M⁺ tuples into single labeled grid image) → Cache Layer (vLLM for multimodal prefix caching).
- **Critical path**: Query → Search task extraction → Master initial probing (S0 scan) → Round loop: Master assigns cells → Workers parallel explore → Update M⁺/M⁻ → Master uncertainty analysis → (if insufficient) repeat → Final decision from M⁺ grid.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Depth vs. Resolution*: Higher d yields finer temporal precision but more steps. Setting d to first sub‑second layer auto‑adapts to duration.
  - *DFS vs. BFS*: DFS for localized details, BFS for distributed events; auto‑selection via query analysis.
  - *Parallelism vs. Coordination*: More Workers reduce wall‑clock time but increase memory for concurrent grid states; Master remains lightweight.
  - *Backbone‑dependent vs. Environment‑limited*: Performance ceiling is VLM perceptual quality; environment prevents collapse but cannot overcome fundamental VLM errors.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - *Perception error*: Correct region visited, but VLM misreads/contradicts visual content (e.g., color, text).
  - *Surface‑text latching*: Agent anchors on subtitle phrase matching a candidate without contextual verification.
  - *Early anchoring*: Master declares sufficiency after first supporting evidence, ignoring later contradictory or missing evidence.
  - *No‑anchor overhead*: Root grid shows no obvious query‑relevant visuals; requires extra rounds to find initial anchor.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Logarithmic scaling validation**: Measure tokens vs. duration (1 min → 10 hrs). Expect sub‑linear growth; 9.7× reduction vs. uniform at 10 hrs.
  2. **Environment budgeting**: Vary max depth d on fixed 10‑hr subset. Expect accuracy plateaus after first sub‑second layer; tokens increase with d.
  3. **Adaptive compute emergence**: Group queries by ground‑truth evidence dispersion (1 vs. 3+ positions). Expect scattered queries consume significantly more tokens without explicit supervision.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can we develop methods to surface semantically relevant information into upper depth layers to reduce exploration overhead when the root grid lacks obvious anchors?
- **Basis in paper**: Limitation 2 (Section 5): "No‑anchor exploration overhead: when the root grid S0 contains no visually obvious anchor for the query, the agent may require additional exploration rounds... Developing methods to surface semantically relevant information into upper depth layers could substantially improve efficiency and is a promising direction for future work."
- **Why unresolved**: The current system relies solely on visual salience in the root grid. When no clear anchor exists, the Master must probe many cells blindly, increasing rounds and tokens. No mechanism exists to enrich the root representation with semantic cues that could guide initial cell selection.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Propose and integrate semantic enrichment techniques (e.g., pre‑computed object/text embeddings, learned saliency maps, or multimodal tagging of root cells) into VideoAtlas. Evaluate on a set of anchorless queries (e.g., queries about specific events not visually prominent in overview) measuring reduction in exploration rounds, tokens, and time to first relevant evidence compared to baseline.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can the VideoAtlas MDP formulation be effectively applied to other video understanding tasks beyond multiple‑choice QA, such as temporal grounding, summarization, and anomaly detection? If so, how should the reward function be defined for each task?
- **Basis in paper**: Limitation 3 (Section 5): "Evaluation scope: we validate on multiple‑choice QA. The MDP formulation supports temporal grounding, summarization, and anomaly detection (only the reward signal changes. The environment does not), but these remain to be demonstrated empirically."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only demonstrates QA. Tasks like temporal grounding require precise time localization; summarization requires producing a text summary; anomaly detection requires identifying unusual events. The environment’s action space and memory may need adjustments, and crucially, the reward signal must be defined to train or evaluate agents for these tasks. Without empirical validation, it’s unclear if VideoAtlas transfers readily.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Adapt VideoAtlas for each task: for grounding, define reward based on temporal IoU with ground‑truth intervals; for summarization, use ROUGE/LVLMEval against reference summaries; for anomaly detection, use frame‑level anomaly labels. Train or evaluate agents (zero‑shot or RL) on standard datasets for these tasks and compare against task‑specific baselines.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can reinforcement learning (e.g., PPO, DQN) be used to learn exploration policies within VideoAtlas that outperform zero‑shot VLM reasoning, and what are the key challenges in reward design, training stability, and generalization?
- **Basis in paper**: Limitation 4 (Section 5): "Zero‑shot only: we solve the MDP entirely via zero‑shot VLM reasoning... The discrete, finite action space makes VideoAtlas directly compatible with RL training (PPO, DQN), which would likely improve exploration efficiency, but we leave this to future work."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper uses a zero‑shot VLM agent, which is simple but suboptimal. RL could learn policies that better balance exploration vs. exploitation, adapt to query types, and potentially use less compute. However, challenges include: defining a dense reward that guides the agent without full ground truth (since exploration is about finding evidence), handling large state/action spaces (the grid can be large), ensuring policies generalize across videos of different durations and genres, and training stability with function approximators.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implement an RL agent (e.g., PPO) that interacts with the VideoAtlas environment. Use a sparse reward at the end (e.g., +1 for correct answer, 0 otherwise) or densify with intermediate rewards (e.g., for finding evidence relevant to the query). Train on a dataset of video QA pairs. Compare sample efficiency (number of environment steps) and final accuracy against the zero‑shot VLM agent and perhaps against a supervised imitation learning baseline. Also analyze learned policies to understand what strategies emerge.

## Limitations
- Grid rendering details (frame interpolation, variable FPS handling) are underspecified, which could affect temporal precision and scaling guarantees.
- The Master’s cell‑scoring function and sufficiency thresholds are not fully described, hindering independent verification of adaptive compute claims.
- The construction of 10‑hour benchmark videos (concatenation method, subtitle merging) lacks scripts/splits, compromising reproducibility and generalizability of accuracy results.

## Confidence
- **Logarithmic Compute Scaling**: Medium (strong internal evidence depends on unverified grid fidelity and sufficiency logic).
- **Structural Reuse & Accuracy Retention**: Medium (cache hit rates rely on vLLM behavior; benchmark reproducibility is uncertain).
- **Adaptive Compute Emergence**: Medium (requires correct Master uncertainty analysis, not validated externally).
