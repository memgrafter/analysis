# PerturbDiff: Functional Diffusion for Single-Cell Perturbation Modeling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19685
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19685
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PerturbDiff achieves state-of-the-art results across 14 metrics, with per-perturbation win rates exceeding 96% on PBMC and Tahoe100M benchmarks, and pretraining significantly improves performance under data scarcity.

## Executive Summary
PerturbDiff addresses single-cell perturbation prediction by modeling the distribution of cellular responses under unobserved latent factors, rather than predicting a single deterministic outcome. It represents cell populations via kernel mean embeddings in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space and defines a diffusion process directly over these embeddings, aligning entire distributions using a Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) loss. A pretraining stage leverages large-scale atlases (e.g., CellxGene) to learn marginal cell distributions, enhancing adaptation when perturbation data is limited. On three major benchmarks—PBMC (signaling), Tahoe100M (drug), and Replogle (genetic)—PerturbDiff outperforms baselines like STATE on 14 evaluation metrics, with particularly large gains in differential expression metrics. Ablations confirm the necessity of the MMD objective, and scaling experiments show optimal performance with moderate model sizes (~114M parameters).

## Method Summary
The task is to predict perturbed single-cell gene expression distributions given control cells and perturbation metadata. Inputs are normalized (library-size → log1p ×10) and feature-selected (top 2,000 HVGs). The model uses a Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) with two parallel token streams (control and perturbed) that interact via joint attention. Conditions (diffusion timestep, cell type, perturbation identity, dose, batch) are injected via AdaLN-Zero. Training minimizes a combined loss: MMD² (using energy-distance kernel) + λ·MSE (λ=1). Sampling uses DDIM with classifier-free guidance. A pretraining phase first learns marginal cell distributions from large unlabeled atlases (e.g., CellxGene, 61M cells) and then fine-tunes on downstream perturbation data.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art on 14 Cell-Eval metrics across PBMC, Tahoe100M, and Replogle datasets, with per-perturbation win rates >96% on PBMC and Tahoe100M.
- Pretraining closes the performance gap on the low-data Replogle benchmark and demonstrates superior sample efficiency in downsampling experiments.
- Ablation studies confirm the MMD objective is critical; without it, performance collapses to baseline levels.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PerturbDiff captures response variability from unobserved latent factors (e.g., cell cycle, microenvironment) by modeling entire distributions rather than point estimates. The kernel mean embedding representation allows direct application of MMD to measure distributional discrepancy between predicted and true perturbed populations. The diffusion process operates in this embedding space, enabling the model to learn a mapping from control to perturbed distributions. This functional diffusion approach, combined with pretraining on large marginal distributions, provides a robust prior that adapts efficiently even with sparse perturbation data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Kernel Mean Embeddings:** Represent population distributions as elements in an RKHS, enabling differentiable pairwise similarity via MMD. *Why needed:* Allows distribution alignment without density estimation. *Quick check:* Monitor MMD loss during training; it should decrease steadily.
- **Diffusion Models:** Learn to reverse a Markov process that gradually adds noise. *Why needed:* Provides a probabilistic framework for generating diverse samples. *Quick check:* Verify noise schedule (linear β from 0.0001 to 0.02) and sampling quality (e.g., DDIM with 100 steps).
- **Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD):** Non-parametric statistical test using kernel embeddings. *Why needed:* Directly optimizes distributional similarity, capturing variability. *Quick check:* Ensure MMD term contributes meaningfully to total loss; ablating it should degrade performance.
- **Self-Conditioning & Classifier-Free Guidance:** Techniques to improve sample fidelity. *Why needed:* Self-conditioning reduces error accumulation; CFG enables controllable generation. *Quick check:* Ablate dropout probabilities (CFG) and self-conditioning rate to observe impact on DE metrics.
- **AdaLN-Zero:** Adaptive layer norm with zero initialization. *Why needed:* Stable conditioning injection for diffusion timesteps and covariates. *Quick check:* Verify condition vectors modulate scale/shift parameters without causing training instability.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Control tokens -> Linear projection -> Transformer blocks (AdaLN-Zero) -> Joint attention (concatenated with perturbed stream) -> Independent MLPs -> Output projection (ReLU)  
Perturbed tokens -> Linear projection -> Transformer blocks (AdaLN-Zero) ->  
Condition embeddings (timestep, covariates) -> MLP -> s  

**Critical path:** Input batches (control/perturbed) → Encode conditions → Process through MM-DiT blocks → Denoise perturbed stream → Reconstruct gene expressions.  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- MM-DiT vs. separate models: Joint attention enables cross-stream interaction but increases compute.  
- MMD vs. MSE weighting: MMD aligns distributions but may vanish; λ balances global vs. local fidelity.  
- Pretraining scope: Learning marginal distributions helps low-data regimes but may introduce bias if cell type distributions shift.

**Failure signatures:**  
- MMD term collapses early → check kernel choice and batch size (O(m²) memory).  
- Overfitting on large datasets → monitor validation DEOver; use EMA and early stopping.  
- Gene vocabulary mismatch between pretraining and downstream → ensure consistent HVG selection and mapping.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Verify loss decomposition: Confirm MMD² and MSE terms both decrease during initial training steps; adjust λ if MMD dominates.  
2. Test guidance weight: Sample with w=0, 1.0, 2.5, 5.0 to find optimal tradeoff between distribution alignment and sample quality.  
3. Ablate pretraining: Compare from-scratch vs. pretrained initialization on a small subset of Replogle to confirm benefit before full runs.
