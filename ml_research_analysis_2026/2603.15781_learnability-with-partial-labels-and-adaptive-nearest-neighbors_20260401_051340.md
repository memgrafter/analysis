# Learnability with Partial Labels and Adaptive Nearest Neighbors
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15781
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15781
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PL A-kNN is the most general Bayes-consistent algorithm for partial-label learning under the minimal label-aligned condition and significantly outperforms existing methods across diverse datasets and noise ratios.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes the foundational theory for Partial Label Learning (PLL), where each training instance has a set of candidate labels. It proves that learnability is equivalent to the linear independence of per-instance bag-generation vectors. The authors propose PL A-kNN, an adaptive nearest-neighbor algorithm that incrementally eliminates improbable labels based on local bag-frequency margins. Theoretically, PL A-kNN is shown to be Bayes consistent under the label-aligned condition—a minimal and necessary assumption for any algorithm to succeed. Empirically, PL A-kNN matches the performance of an oracle kNN and substantially exceeds prior state-of-the-art methods on standard vision and real-world datasets under various noise conditions.

## Method Summary
PL A-kNN is an adaptive instance-based algorithm for PLL. It begins with extensive feature preprocessing: centering, ℓ₂-normalization, Gaussian-weighted KNN smoothing, and a local density transform to ensure Euclidean distances reflect semantic similarity. For a query point, the algorithm retrieves neighbors for increasing neighborhood sizes `k = 1, 2, ..., T`. At each step, it computes the empirical bag-frequency `τ_y` for each candidate label `y` within the current `k` neighbors. A data-dependent threshold `Δ = c₁√(log n + log(|Y|/δ)/√k` is used to eliminate any label whose margin below the current maximum frequency exceeds `Δ`. The process stops when a single label remains or `k = T`. If multiple candidates persist, a disambiguation heuristic selects the label with the smallest margin-to-threshold gap across all `k`. The method is tuning-free aside from the fixed constants `c₁=0.5`, `δ=0.1`, and dataset-specific `T`.

## Key Results
- The necessary and sufficient condition for PLL learnability is that, for every instance `x`, the bag-generation vectors `{p_{y,x}}_y` are linearly independent.
- PL A-kNN is Bayes consistent under the label-aligned condition and is provably the most general consistent algorithm possible.
- Empirically, PL A-kNN matches the performance of an ideal oracle kNN and significantly outperforms CLPL, APL, LWS, and PRODEN across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, Mirflickr, and MSRCv2 under a wide range of noise ratios.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** PLL is learnable if and only if the per-instance bag-generation vectors are linearly independent.
**Mechanism:** For a fixed `x`, the matrix `M(x)` has columns `p_{y,x}` (probability of a bag given true label `y`). Full column rank (linear independence) ensures that if two label distributions `Q1, Q2` induce the same bag distribution, then `M(x)(Q1-Q2)=0` implies `Q1=Q2`. Thus, the Bayes optimal label is uniquely determined by the observable bag distribution.
**Core assumption:** The bag-generation process `p_{y,x}` can vary with `x`.
**Evidence anchors:** Theorem 2.2 (Section 2.2) formalizes `M(x)` and proves the equivalence; Corollary 2.4 shows label-alignment implies linear independence.
**Break condition:** Linear dependence means distinct `Q1, Q2` yield identical bag distributions but different Bayes rules, making recovery impossible.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** PL A-kNN achieves Bayes consistency under the minimal assumption of label-alignment.
**Mechanism:** The algorithm grows `k` and eliminates label `i` if its empirical frequency `τ_i` is more than `Δ` below the maximum `m`. Under label-alignment (`argmax_y P(y|x) = argmax_y P(S_y|x)`), for large `n` and `k`, the true label's frequency will dominate while suboptimal labels' frequencies will be suppressed by more than `Δ`, causing their elimination. The adaptive `k` stops when only the true label (or a set containing it) remains.
**Core assumption:** The distribution satisfies the label-aligned condition (3): `argmax_y P(y|x) = argmax_y P(S_y|x)`.
**Evidence anchors:** Theorem 3.1 proves consistency and optimality; the algorithm's design directly enforces the condition via margin thresholds.
**Break condition:** On a set `G` where label-alignment fails, a misclassification risk bounded by `θ·P(G)` persists (Theorem 3.5).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Instance-specific convergence rates are governed by an "advantage" metric.
**Mechanism:** The *advantage* `adv_A(x) = p·γ²` captures local label separation. `p` is the minimum probability mass of a ball `B(x,r)` for `r ≤ r_A(x)`, and `γ` is the minimum margin by which the top bag-frequency exceeds others in that ball. Higher `adv_A(x)` means fewer samples `n ~ 1/adv_A(x)` suffice for correct classification (Theorem 3.2). Global risk rates depend on the tail distribution `P(adv_A(x) ≤ a_n)` (Theorem 3.4).
**Core assumption:** Input space is a finite-dimensional normed space with a smooth underlying distribution (e.g., `(α, L)`-smooth, β-margin condition).
**Evidence anchors:** Definition 3.1 and Theorems 3.2, 3.4; Appendix B.1 provides finite-sample rates under smoothness.
**Break condition:** Instances with `adv_A(x) = 0` (e.g., arbitrarily close ties) cannot be guaranteed correct classification even asymptotically.

## Foundational Learning
- **k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN):** The base nonparametric method that estimates local class proportions from nearest neighbors. PL A-kNN adapts this to sets of candidate labels by using bag-frequency margins instead of single-label counts.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input `x` -> Preprocessing (apply training stats: center, ℓ₂-normalize, smooth, density transform) -> For `k=1..T`: retrieve `k`-th neighbor `l_k` -> update `τ_y = count(y∈s_l)/k` for all `y` in candidate set `Ŝ` -> compute threshold `Δ = c₁√(log n + log(|Y|/δ)/√k` -> eliminate `y` where `m - τ_y ≥ Δ` (`m` = max `τ` in `Ŝ`) -> if `|Ŝ|=1` break -> if `k=T` and `|Ŝ|>1`, apply disambiguation heuristic (select `y` minimizing `√k(Δ - (τ_y - second_max))` over `k=1..T`) -> output final label.
- **Critical path:** The core loop incrementally grows `k`, updates empirical bag-frequencies, and prunes the candidate label set using a confidence-bound threshold. The path terminates early when a single candidate remains.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Threshold sensitivity:* `c₁` and `δ` control elimination aggressiveness; poor choices cause premature elimination or excessive neighbor usage.
  - *`T` vs. performance:* Larger `T` allows more refinement but risks incorporating irrelevant distant points.
  - *Preprocessing dependency:* Euclidean distances must preserve label-relevant neighborhoods; hubness or poor scaling breaks consistency.
  - *Disambiguation heuristic:* The `min M(k,y)` rule is ad-hoc; it only guarantees `h(x) ∈ Ŝ` for misaligned cases, not optimal recovery.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Degenerate bags:** Region where all bags are identical yields `adv_A(x)=0`; algorithm cannot distinguish true labels.
  2. **Strong non-alignment:** If a suboptimal label's bag-frequency is very close to the maximum but its true probability is much smaller, elimination may fail before `T`.
  3. **Poor metric structure:** If preprocessing fails to make nearest neighbors share true labels, empirical frequencies `P_n(S_y|B_k(x))` will not converge to `P(S_y|B(x,r))`.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Vary noise ratio:** Train on datasets with controlled bag-generation noise and measure error vs. noise level. Verify PL A-kNN degrades gracefully while baselines collapse at specific thresholds.
  2. **Label-alignment vs. non-alignment:** Construct datasets where alignment holds only on a subset `G`. Plot risk vs. `P(G)` to confirm the bound `≤ R* + θ·P(G)`.
  3. **Feature ablation:** Compare performance with/without the full preprocessing pipeline (smoothing, density scaling). Expect severe drop without it, confirming dependency on a well-behaved metric.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on strong distributional assumptions (e.g., label-alignment) whose prevalence in real data is unclear.
- Preprocessing steps are described but not fully specified; small implementation differences may significantly affect nearest-neighbor geometry and performance.
- Empirical evaluation uses only five datasets; broader validation across domains and data modalities is needed.
- The linear independence condition is not practically testable without knowledge of the true bag-generation process.
- The disambiguation heuristic for remaining candidate sets lacks optimality guarantees.

## Confidence
- **Learnability condition (linear independence):** High – proofs are rigorous and follow from basic linear algebra.
- **PL A-kNN Bayes consistency under label-alignment:** High – the proof is solid and demonstrates algorithmic optimality within the assumption.
- **Instance-specific advantage rates:** Medium – depends on smoothness and margin assumptions that may not hold universally; empirical validation limited.
- **Empirical superiority over baselines:** Medium – results are strong but based on a limited set of datasets and specific implementations of baselines.

## Next Checks
1. **Expand benchmark coverage:** Evaluate PL A-kNN on at least ten diverse PLL datasets, including tabular, audio, and text domains, to test generalizability beyond vision.
2. **Preprocessing ablation study:** Systematically remove each preprocessing step (centering, ℓ₂-normalization, smoothing, density transform) and measure the impact on error rates using statistical tests.
3. **Empirical label-alignment assessment:** On held-out data, estimate local bag-frequencies with large `k` and compute `P(S_y|x)`; quantify the fraction of points where `argmax_y P(y|x) = argmax_y P(S_y|x)` to assess real-world alignment prevalence.
