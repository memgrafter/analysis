# Artificial intelligence-enabled single-lead ECG for non-invasive hyperkalemia detection: development, multicenter validation, and proof-of-concept deployment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14177
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14177
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Multicenter validation across 34,439 patients achieved AUROC up to 0.936 (internal) and 0.808 (external); negative predictive value exceeded 99.3% for moderate-to-severe hyperkalemia.

## Executive Summary
This study presents Pocket-K, an artificial intelligence system that uses single-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) data to non-invasively screen for hyperkalemia. The system fine-tunes a pre-trained ECG foundation model (ECGFounder) on Lead I ECG segments. Validated across multiple centers in China with over thirty-four thousand patients, Pocket-K demonstrated strong detection performance, particularly for moderate-to-severe hyperkalemia, with an external negative predictive value above 99.3%. A handheld prototype enabled near-real-time inference, showcasing technical feasibility for out-of-hospital use.

## Method Summary
The task was binary classification of hyperkalemia (serum potassium > 5.5 mmol/L) and moderate-to-severe hyperkalemia (≥ 6.0 mmol/L) from single-lead ECG. Lead I was extracted from 12-lead ECGs, band-pass filtered (0.5–40 Hz), split into non-overlapping 10-second clips, resampled to 500 Hz, and z-score normalized per clip. Labels were venous potassium measurements within ±1 hour of the ECG. The ECGFounder foundation model (a Net1D-style network pretrained on >10 million ECGs) was fine-tuned using binary cross-entropy loss and the Adam optimizer with a learning rate schedule and early stopping. Data partitioning respected patient-level separation to prevent leakage. Primary evaluation metric was the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), with uncertainty estimated via clustered bootstrap at the patient level.

## Key Results
- Internal test AUROC: 0.936; temporal validation AUROC: 0.858; independent external validation AUROC: 0.808.
- For moderate-to-severe hyperkalemia (≥6.0 mmol/L), temporal validation AUROC improved to 0.940 and external validation AUROC to 0.861.
- External negative predictive value exceeded 99.3% for potassium ≥6.0 mmol/L.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The ECGFounder model, pretrained on extensive unlabeled ECG data, learns robust general representations of cardiac electrophysiology. Fine-tuning on Lead I data specializing for hyperkalemia allows the model to detect subtle T-wave and QRS complex alterations associated with elevated serum potassium. Using a single lead simplifies hardware requirements for portable deployment while preserving diagnostic information from the frontal plane. The 10-second clip duration provides sufficient temporal context to capture relevant waveform changes without prohibitive latency.

  Why needed: Learns invariant cardiac features without manual labels, capturing baseline morphology and variability.  
  Quick check: Confirm ECGFounder used a pretraining objective (e.g., masked prediction) on >10M ECGs.

- **Transfer learning to a downstream screening task**  
  Why needed: Adapts general ECG representations to the specific signature of hyperkalemia with relatively few labeled examples.  
  Quick check: Verify fine-tuning used a low initial learning rate (1e-4) and discriminative layer updates.

- **Single-lead ECG as a viable diagnostic modality**  
  Why needed: Enables wearable or handheld devices; Lead I retains key electrical axis information relevant to potassium disturbances.  
  Quick check: Ensure preprocessing consistently extracts Lead I and applies band-pass filtering (0.5–40 Hz).

- **Temporal convolutional networks (Net1D) for sequential data**  
  Why needed: Efficiently models local and long-range dependencies in continuous ECG signals.  
  Quick check: Identify 1D convolution layers with residual connections in the architecture.

- **Clinically oriented threshold selection for screening**  
  Why needed: Balances sensitivity and specificity for a rule-out test where high NPV is crucial.  
  Quick check: Determine whether reported operating points used Youden’s index or a prespecified potassium threshold.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input -> Lead I extraction -> Band-pass filter (0.5–40 Hz) -> 10-s clip segmentation -> Resample (500 Hz) -> Z-score normalization per clip -> Fine-tuned ECGFounder backbone (1D CNN) -> Classification head -> Hyperkalemia probability

Critical path: Raw 12-lead ECG → Lead I isolation → preprocessing → model inference. Performance hinges on accurate Lead I extraction and noise-robust preprocessing to preserve T-wave morphology.

Design tradeoffs:
- Model capacity vs. interpretability: Deep network captures subtle changes but lacks explainability.
- Clip duration vs. latency: 10-second clips balance temporal context and near-real-time feasibility.
- Generalization vs. overfitting: Diverse pretraining helps, but external AUROC drop suggests residual dataset bias.

Failure signatures:
- Motion artifact or poor electrode contact distorts baseline and T-wave amplitude.
- Arrhythmias (e.g., atrial fibrillation) introduce irregular RR intervals that may confound potassium-related features.
- Incorrect Lead I extraction from non-standard 12-lead placements shifts electrical axis.

First experiments:
1. Internal replication: Fine-tune ECGFounder on 80% development set; expect internal test AUROC ≥0.936.
2. External portability check: Apply trained model to external validation set; quantify AUROC drop (>0.10 indicates significant shift).
3. Threshold robustness: Vary decision threshold across validation sets; assess sensitivity/specificity tradeoff at potassium ≥6.0 mmol/L.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does Pocket-K perform on native single-lead ECG recordings from handheld devices or smartwatches compared to Lead I extracted from clinical 12-lead systems?
- Does AI-guided hyperkalemia screening using Pocket-K improve clinical outcomes or healthcare workflow efficiency compared to standard care?
- How well does Pocket-K generalize to populations outside China and across different ethnicities, comorbidities, and ECG platforms?
- What are the specific failure modes of Pocket-K in real-world conditions, such as during arrhythmias, motion artifacts, or low-signal quality?

## Limitations
- Exact ECGFounder architecture and full pretraining details are not disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Threshold selection for clinical metrics is ambiguous; prespecified vs. optimal (Youden) thresholds differ.
- External validation AUROC (0.808) is meaningfully lower than internal (0.936), indicating limited transportability.

## Confidence
- **Performance metrics (AUROC/NPV):** Medium. Multicenter validation supports reported numbers, but incomplete model specification and threshold ambiguity impede replication fidelity.
- **Handheld prototype feasibility:** High. Proof-of-concept inference demonstrates technical viability with low latency.
- **Generalizability to new settings:** Medium. External AUROC 0.808 suggests substantial distribution shift; real-world single-lead device performance unknown.

## Next Checks
1. **Model reconstruction:** Obtain official ECGFounder weights and codebase; replicate fine-tuning protocol and confirm internal test AUROC ≥0.936.
2. **Threshold verification:** Determine whether sensitivity/specificity used an optimal threshold; recompute at a clinically prespecified potassium cutoff (e.g., ≥6.0 mmol/L) and report variance.
3. **Real-world deployment test:** Evaluate Pocket-K on an independent cohort using actual wearable single-lead ECG devices; measure AUROC and NPV under varied signal quality and demographic conditions.
