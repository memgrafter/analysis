# LLMs for Explainable Business Decision-Making: A Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning Approach

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04208
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04208
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: LEXMA fine-tunes LLMs via RL to generate audience-appropriate explanations for business decisions, achieving high predictive performance (F1 0.897–0.893) and superior explanation quality per expert and consumer evaluations.

## Executive Summary
LEXMA is a reinforcement learning fine-tuning framework designed to help large language models produce faithful, audience-tailored explanations for high-stakes business decisions like mortgage approvals. It combines reflection-augmented supervised fine-tuning with two stages of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and uses a modular adapter architecture to separate decision logic from communication style. The framework employs a three-phase generation schema—reasoning, then explanation, then prediction—to ground explanations in case-specific factors. Empirical evaluation on mortgage data shows LEXMA matches strong baselines in predictive accuracy while generating explanations that experts view as more risk-focused and consumers judge clearer, more actionable, and more polite. The approach addresses key challenges of explanation faithfulness, multi-audience support, and label efficiency by using outcome-based rewards instead of human-annotated explanations.

## Method Summary
LEXMA begins with reflection-augmented supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to instill a structured output format: a reasoning trace, an explanation, and a final prediction. It then applies two sequential GRPO stages using parameter-efficient LoRA adapters. The first stage fine-tunes a “correctness” adapter (ACC) with rewards based on ground-truth decision labels, ensuring accurate predictions. The second stage fine-tunes a separate “tone” adapter (TONE) with rule-based readability rewards and a shared ACC, optimizing explanations for consumer audiences without altering decision boundaries. The ACC adapter is shared across expert and consumer prompts, while TONE activates only for consumer-facing outputs, enabling style specialization. GRPO samples multiple outputs per input, computes group-relative advantages from the reward signals, and updates policies without requiring learned reward models.

## Key Results
- Predictive performance: F1 scores of 0.897 (expert prompt) and 0.893 (consumer prompt) on mortgage approval, competitive with XGBoost, neural networks, and gradient boosting baselines.
- Expert evaluation: Explanations from LEXMA-rated as more risk-focused and evidence-grounded compared to baseline LLM outputs.
- Consumer evaluation: Explanations perceived as clearer, more actionable, and more polite than those from ablated or baseline models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Causal grounding via three-phase generation
- **Claim**: The Reasoning→Explanation→Prediction sequence creates a causal link between explanation and decision, ensuring explanations are grounded in factors that drive correct predictions.
- **Mechanism**: By conditioning the final prediction on both the reasoning trace and the generated explanation, the model learns that explanations must support accurate outcomes, tightly coupling narrative and decision.
- **Core assumption**: The reasoning trace captures relevant case-specific factors, and the explanation distillation preserves salient evidence.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states the framework uses “reward signals that do not rely on human-annotated explanations” and that accuracy rewards flow back through explanation tokens that set up correct outcomes.
- **Break condition**: If explanations become generic or fail to reference case-specific evidence, the model may decouple narrative from decision logic.

### Mechanism 2: Modular adapter separation
- **Claim**: The two-adapter architecture (ACC for correctness, TONE for style) enables audience-specific explanations while preserving consistent decision boundaries.
- **Mechanism**: The ACC adapter is shared across both expert and consumer prompts, embedding the decision logic and risk-focused content. The TONE adapter is only active for consumer explanations, adjusting communication style without updating ACC.
- **Core assumption**: Decision boundaries can be learned independently of communication style; fine-tuning tone without updating ACC preserves decision consistency.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract notes fine-tuning “two separate parameter sets to improve decision correctness and satisfy stylistic requirements.” Section explicitly describes ACC capturing decision boundary and TONE adjusting consumer style.
- **Break condition**: If ACC is inadvertently updated during TONE fine-tuning, explanations may drift while decisions change.

### Mechanism 3: Label-efficient GRPO optimization
- **Claim**: Group Relative Policy Optimization enables effective fine-tuning using only outcome-based rewards, avoiding dependence on human-annotated explanations.
- **Mechanism**: For each input, GRPO samples multiple trajectories, computes group-relative advantages based on ground-truth decisions and simple rule-based tone metrics, and updates policies via comparison-based learning.
- **Core assumption**: Ground-truth decision labels provide a sufficiently grounded signal for optimizing both decision correctness and explanation quality through relative rewards.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract emphasizes reward signals “do not rely on human-annotated explanations.” Section states GRPO optimizes using rewards dependent “only on ground-truth decisions and simple rule-based tone metrics.”
- **Break condition**: If group size is too small or reward signal too noisy, the policy may converge to suboptimal explanations or decisions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Reinforcement learning for language models
  - Why needed: Enables optimization of explanation quality without requiring human-annotated explanation pairs.
  - Quick check: How does GRPO differ from standard RL (e.g., PPO) in reward computation and policy update stability?
- **Concept**: Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) with LoRA
  - Why needed: Allows fine-tuning of specialized adapters while freezing the base model, reducing computational cost and avoiding catastrophic forgetting.
  - Quick check: What are the trade-offs between LoRA adapters and full fine-tuning for controlling separate aspects (e.g., correctness vs. style)?
- **Concept**: Chain-of-thought reasoning in LLMs
  - Why needed: Provides internal reasoning traces that can be distilled into concise, faithful explanations while preserving decision-relevant information.
  - Quick check: How does the model ensure the distilled explanation captures the most salient factors from the full reasoning trace?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Qwen3-4B base model (frozen) → Reflection-augmented SFT stage → ACC adapter (fine-tuned for correctness) → GRPO-Step1 (decision optimization) → TONE adapter (fine-tuned for consumer style) → GRPO-Step2 (tone optimization) → Prompt templates (expert vs. consumer).
- **Critical path**: SFT → GRPO-Step1 (ACC) → GRPO-Step2 (TONE). Each stage builds on the previous; skipping or reordering breaks separation of concerns.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Two-adapter vs single-adapter (cleaner separation but added complexity); GRPO vs PPO (group comparisons may be more stable but require more samples); rule-based vs learned rewards (label-efficient but may miss nuance).
- **Failure signatures**: Decision inconsistency (ACC and TONE not properly separated); Poor readability (tone tuning ineffective or readability reward too lenient); Low accuracy (insufficient GRPO or poor reward signal).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run SFT on a small subset to verify format consistency and baseline performance.
  2. Test GRPO-Step1 with different group sizes (G=8 vs G=16) to find the sweet spot between performance and training time.
  3. Validate that GRPO-Step2 maintains decision accuracy while improving readability metrics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How does LEXMA's predictive performance compare to specialized tabular models (e.g., TabPFN, TabNet) on the same HMDA dataset? The paper benchmarks against general ML models but omits these architectures.
- **Open Question 2**: What is the optimal group size (G) for GRPO, and how sensitive is performance to this hyperparameter across different datasets? Only G=8 and G=16 were tested, with marginal gains at higher cost.
- **Open Question 3**: How does LEXMA perform in real-time, streaming scenarios for mortgage applications? Evaluation used a static test set; latency and throughput in dynamic deployment are unmeasured.
- **Open Question 4**: How does LEXMA generalize when using different backbone models (e.g., Llama, Mistral) instead of Qwen3-4B? Results may be model-specific; transferability is unknown.

## Limitations
- Domain specificity: Evaluation only on mortgage approvals; generalizability to other high-stakes decisions or less-structured outcomes is unclear.
- Explanation evaluation subjectivity: Expert and consumer ratings rely on small-scale human judgments that may not capture all real-world utility dimensions.
- Audience definition simplicity: Only “expert” vs. “consumer” tested; real-world stakeholder diversity (e.g., regulators, loan officers) and evolving needs are unaddressed.

## Confidence
- Three-phase schema creates causal grounding: **Medium** (architectural intent supported, but generic explanation failure mode not fully stress-tested).
- Modular adapter separation preserves decisions: **High** (clean separation with shared ACC; empirical consistency across audiences directly supports claim).
- GRPO enables label-efficient tuning: **Medium** (outcome-based rewards used and show promise, but sensitivity to hyperparameters and rule-based reward ceiling not fully mapped).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain replication**: Run LEXMA on a structurally different high-stakes task (e.g., medical triage, fraud detection) to test if the three-phase schema and adapter separation maintain both predictive performance and explanation quality.
2. **Adapter separation ablation**: Intentionally update ACC during TONE-stage fine-tuning and measure drift in decision boundary (via held-out accuracy) versus style metrics; this tests the break condition for architectural integrity.
3. **GRPO hyperparameter sensitivity sweep**: Systematically vary group size (G=4,8,16,32) and reward scaling on the mortgage dataset to chart stability regions for F1 and readability; identify minimal computational overhead for target performance.
