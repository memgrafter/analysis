# RealHD: A High-Quality Dataset for Robust Detection of State-of-the-Art AI-Generated Images

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10546
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10546
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Models trained on the RealHD dataset achieve superior cross-dataset generalization (e.g., 66.25% accuracy on Chameleon) and the proposed Non-Local Means noise entropy method boosts accuracy by up to 15.90% relative while improving robustness to JPEG compression.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the poor real-world generalization of existing AI-generated image detectors, which stems from low-quality, low-diversity training data. It introduces RealHD, a large-scale, high-quality dataset containing over 730,000 images (real and generated) guided by 10,000+ detailed prompts and covering multiple generation tasks (text-to-image, inpainting, refinement, face-swapping). As a detection method, it proposes a lightweight approach that classifies images based on the entropy of Non-Local Means (NLM) noise residuals. Experiments show that models trained on RealHD generalize significantly better to challenging, human-vetted benchmarks like Chameleon. The noise entropy feature further enhances performance and robustness, demonstrating that both data quality and a focus on high-frequency statistical patterns are crucial for robust detection.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is the construction of the RealHD dataset, which emphasizes high image quality, aesthetic appeal, and prompt diversity across four generative tasks. Real images are sourced from museums, news, and portraits, filtered for resolution and quality. Generated images are created using state-of-the-art models (Stable Diffusion variants, FLUX) guided by thousands of LLM-generated, richly detailed prompts. For detection, the paper proposes extracting a noise entropy (NE) tensor: an image is denoised with Non-Local Means, the residual noise η is resized, and block-wise Shannon entropy is computed and stacked into a 3×n×n tensor. This tensor, rather than raw RGB pixels, is fed to standard classifiers (Xception, ResNet, EfficientFormer). Training uses standard augmentation and Adam optimization.

## Key Results
- Models trained on RealHD achieve state-of-the-art cross-dataset generalization, e.g., Xception reaches 66.25% accuracy and 0.7202 AUC on the human-vetted Chameleon benchmark, significantly outperforming models trained on prior datasets.
- The proposed noise entropy (NE) feature boosts detection accuracy on RealHD by up to 15.90% relative (e.g., Xception accuracy from 85.21% to 98.76%) and improves robustness under heavy JPEG compression (Quality=50).
- RealHD-trained models show strong transfer to other datasets, while models trained on prior datasets (GenImage, DMimageDetection) fail to generalize to RealHD, highlighting the dataset's quality and diversity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dataset Diversity Mitigates Artifact Overfitting
- **Claim:** A high-quality, diverse dataset covering multiple generation tasks and realistic prompts reduces model dependence on dataset-specific artifacts, improving real-world generalization.
- **Mechanism:** By using state-of-the-art generators with thousands of detailed prompts across categories and tasks, the dataset forces detectors to learn features correlated with the underlying generative process (e.g., diffusion priors, inpainting inconsistencies) rather than superficial, spurious cues tied to a single dataset's compression, resolution, or prompt style. This is evidenced by lower in-dataset performance but superior cross-dataset and Chameleon results.
- **Core assumption:** Generalization is limited by training data diversity and quality; a dataset mirroring real-world complexity produces more robust detectors.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statements on existing dataset limitations; cross-dataset and Chameleon evaluation tables showing consistent gains.
- **Break condition:** Substantially reducing diversity in generation methods, prompts, or real sources would diminish the generalization advantage.

### Mechanism 2: Noise Entropy Captures a Robust, Artifact-Agnostic Signal
- **Claim:** Extracting and classifying on Non-Local Means (NLM) noise entropy provides a lightweight signal more robust than learning from raw RGB pixels.
- **Mechanism:** Real camera images contain stochastic sensor noise (shot, read noise). AI-generated images lack this physical noise and instead exhibit structured, model-specific artifacts. NLM denoising approximates a clean signal; the residual captures high-frequency noise. Shannon entropy on this residual aggregates the uncertainty/randomness of these patterns. Real images, with stochastic sensor noise, are expected to have higher entropy than generated images with more regular artifacts. This high-frequency statistical feature is less salient to CNNs/Transformers trained on ImageNet, hence the boost when explicitly provided.
- **Core assumption:** The statistical distribution (entropy) of high-frequency noise residuals is a discriminative invariant between real (sensor-noise dominated) and AI-generated (process-artifact dominated) images.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4 defining the NE extraction; Figure 3 showing distributional separation.
- **Break condition:** Future generative models explicitly synthesizing realistic sensor noise would close the entropy distribution gap.

### Mechanism 3: Chameleon Benchmark as a Strict Validation of Perceptual Realism
- **Claim:** Evaluating on a challenging, human-vetted external benchmark (Chameleon) is a more valid test of real-world detector utility than in-dataset or standard cross-dataset tests.
- **Mechanism:** Chameleon consists of images from AI-painting communities that passed a human "Turing Test," meaning they are perceptually realistic. Performance here measures a detector's ability to find subtle, non-obvious technical artifacts that humans miss—a stricter requirement for content moderation. RealHD's design aligns with creating such challenging examples, hence the performance leap.
- **Core assumption:** Human perceptual realism is the operational definition of "difficult" synthetic images; detectors failing here are less useful.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 5.1 focusing on Chameleon results; consistent gains over other training sets.
- **Break condition:** If Chameleon's collection methodology becomes unrepresentative of mainstream generative threats, its value as a generalization probe decays.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Frequency Domain Analysis & Sensor Noise**
  - **Why needed here:** The detection method operates on high-frequency noise residuals, not RGB pixels. Understanding why cameras produce stochastic noise while generative models produce structured artifacts is key to interpreting the entropy feature's power.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might JPEG compression at Q=50 not completely erase the utility of NLM noise entropy for detection, as seen in Table 10?

- **Concept: Dataset Construction Bias & Spurious Correlation**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's thesis is that poor dataset design leads to detectors exploiting spurious cues. Understanding this failure mode is crucial to appreciate RealHD's design choices.
  - **Quick check question:** If a dataset uses only ImageNet-style prompts ("a photo of a [class]"), what spurious correlation might a detector learn that would fail on a news photo of a specific politician?

- **Concept: Cross-Domain Generalization Evaluation**
  - **Why needed here:** The primary evidence for RealHD's quality is its cross-dataset performance. This requires understanding that "domain" is defined by generator family, prompt style, image quality, and task.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is training on GenImage (only T2I) and testing on RealHD (T2I + inpainting + etc.) a harder generalization test than training on GenImage and testing on DiffusionForensics (also mostly T2I)?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Data Pipeline (Real/Genuine Image Collection → Prompt Generation → Image Synthesis across 4 tasks → Fine-grained Annotation) → Feature Extractor (NLM Denoising → Residual Calculation → Entropy Tensor `H`) → Classifier (ResNet/Xception/EfficientFormer on `H`)

### Critical Path
1.  Obtain RealHD dataset and verify splits/annotations.
2.  Implement NLM noise extraction (Eq. 2-4) and entropy tensor computation (Eq. 5-6). Validate outputs against Figure 3 distributions.
3.  Train baseline classifiers on RealHD RGB images. Record in-dataset performance.
4.  Train same classifiers on NE tensors (`H`). Compare performance boost.
5.  Conduct cross-dataset evaluation: train on other datasets, test on RealHD; train on RealHD, test on Chameleon.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **NLM Parameters:** Filter strength `h` and patch size control noise estimate. Unspecified in paper; incorrect values capture image texture instead of noise.
- **Entropy Block Size (`n`):** Larger `n` loses spatial resolution; smaller `n` increases dimensionality. Tensor shape description in paper is inconsistent.
- **Computational Cost:** NLM is O(N²) naively. Pre-extracting and storing `H` tensors for 730k+ images is storage-intensive.
- **Dataset Scale:** Large size demands significant compute and storage for both RGB and NE tensor training.

### Failure Signatures
1.  **Poor cross-dataset generalization despite NE:** RealHD+NE model scoring <60% ACC on Chameleon indicates insufficient dataset diversity or NE feature weakness.
2.  **NE feature indistinguishability:** Overlapping entropy distributions for a new generator (no K-S test significance) predict NE failure for that model family.
3.  **JPEG robustness paradox:** NE model showing *lower* AUC than RGB at Q=90/75 (as in Table 10) suggests overfitting to RealHD's high-quality noise patterns; robustness advantage only appears at Q=50.
4.  **Inpainting mask ignorance:** NE ignores provided masks; may fail if inpainted region's noise is locally dominant but not isolated.

### First 3 Experiments
1.  **Reproduce Baseline Generalization Gap:** Train Xception on RealHD (RGB) vs. GenImage (RGB). Confirm in-dataset (GenImage ~99%, RealHD ~85%) and cross-dataset gaps (GenImage→RealHD ACC ~58%).
2.  **Validate NE Feature Separation:** For 1k real and 1k generated (SDv1.5) images, compute NE tensors. Plot per-channel entropy distributions and perform Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Success: p-value < 0.01, non-overlapping distributions.
3.  **Measure NE Boost & Robustness Curve:** Train Xception on RealHD+NE. Target: ~98.76% ACC, 0.9991 AUC on RealHD test. Apply JPEG compression (Q=100→30) to test set and plot accuracy/AUC. Success: NE degrades slower than RGB at Q≤50.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Should the RealHD dataset be extended to include generative methods beyond the four covered (text-to-image, inpainting, refinement, face-swapping), such as GAN-based generation or video synthesis, to further improve detector generalization?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that existing datasets lack coverage of diverse tasks and RealHD includes these four. They do not justify why not others (e.g., GANs, video) or if more would yield diminishing returns.
- **Why unresolved:** No evaluation of incorporating other paradigms (e.g., StyleGAN) on benchmarks like Chameleon, where accuracy remains moderate.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation study adding images from excluded methods (GANs, video forgeries) to RealHD, measuring performance on diverse benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent do the fine-grained annotations (inpainting masks, refinement sources) improve detection performance and explainability versus image-level labels?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Rich metadata (masks, source images) is collected but detection experiments use only image-level labels.
- **Why unresolved:** Utility of masks/source annotations for training or explanation is claimed but not empirically validated. No comparison with multi-task models using this supervision.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments comparing models that consume masks/source pairs vs. standard image-level classification on RealHD.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does the proposed noise entropy method show reduced robustness under severe JPEG compression (e.g., Q=50), and can the extraction be made more invariant?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Table 10 shows NE sometimes performs worse than baseline at Q=90/75 and both collapse at Q=50. Paper attributes general robustness to sensor noise persistence but doesn't analyze the high-compression failure.
- **Why unresolved:** No explanation for the sharp degradation at Q=50 or proposed modifications (e.g., adaptive binning) to mitigate it.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis of entropy distribution shifts under increasing JPEG, followed by a modified NE pipeline evaluated across compression levels.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Are the detailed prompts and high aesthetic quality of RealHD truly responsible for improved generalization, or is the diversity of generative models the dominant factor?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Paper emphasizes 10k+ prompts and quality. However, comparisons (Tables 6,7) are against other multi-model datasets (GenImage). Prompt quality vs. model diversity contribution is disentangled.
- **Why unresolved:** No controlled experiment where RealHD images are regenerated with simple prompts or other datasets augmented with detailed prompts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Generate a RealHD subset with both original detailed prompts and equivalent simple prompts; measure detection performance differences to isolate prompt complexity effect.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Does the noise entropy feature generalize to detect images from future, unseen generative models without retraining?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] NE relies on a discrepancy between camera sensor noise and synthetic noise. All evaluations use known diffusion architectures (SD, FLUX). No test on completely novel paradigms.
- **Why unresolved:** Future models might simulate realistic sensor noise or use new architectures (e.g., hybrid diffusion-autoregressive), potentially erasing the discrepancy. No evidence of out-of-distribution generalization to held-out model families.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate NE-trained detectors on images from a held-out generative model (e.g., a new model released post-dataset creation) vs. models trained on more diverse synthetic sets.

## Limitations
- **Unreported NLM parameters:** Critical filtering strength `h` and patch size for NLM are unspecified, hindering faithful replication of the noise entropy feature.
- **Unclear data splits & balance:** Official train/test split indices and stratification details for RealHD are not provided, risking inconsistent evaluation.
- **Chameleon benchmark validity:** Its long-term value as a "hard" benchmark is unproven; it may become unrepresentative of mainstream generative threats.
- **NE feature stability:** The artifact-agnostic claim relies on current generators; future models synthesizing realistic sensor noise could collapse the separation.
- **Corpus support gap:** Neighboring work on robustness (RAID) or human detection (Comprehensive Dataset) does not directly validate RealHD's specific construction principles.

## Confidence

- **Claim 1 (Dataset diversity enables generalization):** Medium. Strong empirical cross-dataset evidence, but weak direct validation that the *specific* construction (10k+ prompts, 4 tasks) is *necessary* versus simply having more data or model variety.
- **Claim 2 (NLM noise entropy provides robust signal):** Medium. Theoretically sound and results show boosts, but reliance on unstated parameters and paradoxical performance under mild JPEG compression (Table 10) warrant caution.
- **Claim 3 (Chameleon as gold-standard benchmark):** Low. Compelling operational definition, but its validity as a persistent "hard" benchmark is unproven and depends on the continued realism of community-submitted images.
