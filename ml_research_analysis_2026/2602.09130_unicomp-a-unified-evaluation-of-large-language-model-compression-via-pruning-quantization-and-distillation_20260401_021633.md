# UniComp: A Unified Evaluation of Large Language Model Compression via Pruning, Quantization and Distillation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09130
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09130
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Compression methods exhibit a consistent "knowledge bias"—knowledge-intensive tasks remain stable while reasoning, multilingual, and instruction-following capabilities degrade substantially; quantization offers the best performance-efficiency trade-off, distillation yields the highest runtime gains at great computational expense, and performance and reliability preservation are decoupled.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces UniComp, a unified framework for evaluating large language model (LLM) compression across three critical dimensions: performance (knowledge, multilingual/cultural, reasoning, instruction following), reliability (truthfulness, safety, fairness, robustness, privacy, ethics), and efficiency (runtime acceleration, inference efficiency, compute cost). By applying six compression techniques to modern models and evaluating on over 40 datasets, the study reveals a "knowledge bias" where factual recall is preserved but higher-order skills deteriorate. The work underscores that no single compression method excels across all dimensions, highlighting the need for multi-faceted evaluation and task-specific calibration (e.g., reasoning-aware data) to mitigate degradation.

## Method Summary
The UniComp framework systematically compares three compression families—pruning (SparseGPT, Wanda), quantization (GPTQ, AWQ), and knowledge distillation (Minitron, Low-Rank Clone)—using LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B as primary models. Evaluation spans 40+ benchmarks, including MMLU, GSM8K, MATH-500, Global-MMLU-Lite, and the TrustLLM suite. Performance and reliability are measured via normalized retained scores relative to the uncompressed base model, while efficiency scores are normalized to the best performer per metric. Key procedures include: one-shot pruning without retraining, post-training weight-only quantization (W4A16), and distillation pipelines that generate students directly from teachers using large-scale token budgets. The study also explores calibration data composition, finding that reasoning-centric calibration sets (derived from MATH, GSM8K, ARC-c) improve pruned models' reasoning accuracy by up to 50% relative.

## Key Results
- **Knowledge bias:** Across all methods, knowledge-intensive tasks (e.g., MMLU) are preserved, while reasoning (GSM8K, MATH), multilingual (Global-MMLU), and instruction-following (IFBench) capabilities drop sharply.
- **Trade-off leaders:** Quantization provides the best overall balance of performance and efficiency; distillation achieves the strongest runtime acceleration but requires massive compute (194B tokens); pruning suffers severe reasoning degradation unless calibrated with task-matched data.
- **Performance-reliability decoupling:** No compression method simultaneously optimizes task performance and reliability metrics (truthfulness, safety, etc.), indicating these dimensions are governed by different model properties.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper documents robust empirical patterns but does not identify the underlying mechanistic causes. It speculates that reasoning and multilingual capabilities rely on more fragile, distributed representations that accumulate errors under compression, whereas factual knowledge may be redundantly encoded. The benefit of reasoning-aware calibration for pruned models suggests that preserving data distributions relevant to multi-step inference helps maintain critical representations. However, the exact pathways—such as layer-wise sensitivity, neuron importance, or activation shifts—remain unanalyzed, leaving the "knowledge bias" and performance-reliability decoupling as descriptive findings rather than explained phenomena.

## Foundational Learning
- **Pruning:** Removing redundant weights (structured or unstructured) to reduce model size. *Why needed:* Directly lowers parameter count and memory footprint. *Quick check:* Does pruning affect early vs. late layers differently for reasoning vs. knowledge tasks?
- **Quantization:** Reducing numerical precision (e.g., 4-bit) of weights/activations. *Why needed:* Cuts memory and accelerates inference via narrower arithmetic. *Quick check:* How does quantization noise propagate through long reasoning chains?
- **Knowledge Distillation:** Training a small student to mimic a large teacher's outputs. *Why needed:* Recovers performance lost by other compression methods. *Quick check:* Why does distillation incur such high computational cost (token budgets)?
- **Knowledge bias:** The observed phenomenon where factual recall resists compression while reasoning and multilingual skills degrade. *Why needed:* Central finding that challenges naive compression assumptions. *Quick check:* Is the bias tied to task type (single-hop vs. multi-hop) or data distribution?
- **Reliability metrics:** Evaluations of truthfulness, safety, fairness, robustness, privacy, ethics. *Why needed:* Ensures compressed models remain safe and unbiased. *Quick check:* How are these diverse metrics aggregated into a single score without obscuring trade-offs?
- **Multi-dimensional evaluation:** Assessing compression across performance, reliability, efficiency. *Why needed:* Prevents over-optimizing one dimension at the expense of others. *Quick check:* Why do performance and reliability preservation become orthogonal under compression?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Compression Methods (Pruning/Quantization/Distillation) -> UniComp Evaluation Pipeline -> Performance/Reliability/Efficiency Scores

**Critical path:** Apply compression to base model -> Evaluate on 40+ benchmarks across three dimensions -> Normalize scores relative to base/best -> Compare methods and analyze trade-offs (e.g., knowledge bias, decoupling).

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- Pruning offers size reduction with minimal retraining but damages complex capabilities.
- Quantization balances performance and efficiency but may harm knowledge tasks when heavily quantized.
- Distillation recovers performance at extreme computational cost.
- Calibration data choice (general vs. task-specific) can mitigate pruning's reasoning loss but risks knowledge task degradation.

**Failure signatures:**
- Evaluation backend mismatch (LightEval vs. lm-eval-harness) causing benchmark score drift.
- `vLLM` failing to recognize compressed weight formats, nullifying efficiency gains.
- Normalization errors in reliability aggregation (e.g., incorrect inversion of "lower is better" metrics).
- Calibration data leakage into test sets inflating reasoning improvements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What underlying mechanisms cause the "knowledge bias" in LLM compression, where factual recall is preserved but reasoning and multilingual abilities degrade?
- **Open Question 2:** Why is there a decoupling between performance retention and reliability preservation under compression?
- **Open Question 3:** Why does reasoning-aware calibration improve the reasoning ability of pruned models but not quantized models?
- **Open Question 4:** How do compression techniques generalize to other model families (e.g., Mixture-of-Experts, larger scales) and tasks (e.g., code generation, multi-agent collaboration) beyond the evaluated models and benchmarks?

## Limitations
- **Evaluation specificity:** Exact construction of the reasoning-aware calibration set and precise few-shot prompts for reasoning benchmarks are undisclosed, risking variance in core findings.
- **Efficiency measurement rigor:** Hardware and `vLLM` configuration details for throughput/latency are unspecified, hindering validation of efficiency rankings.
- **Reliability aggregation:** The inversion formula for "lower is better" TrustLLM metrics and per-dataset weighting within sub-scores is not demonstrated, clouding reliability preservation claims.
- **Distillation protocol opacity:** Teacher model identity and data mixture for Minitron's 194B-token distillation are unreported, blocking exact replication.

## Confidence
- **High:** Existence of "knowledge bias" (stable knowledge tasks vs. degraded reasoning/multilingual) is robust across methods and models.
- **Medium:** Quantitative rankings (e.g., quantization best trade-off) and magnitude of calibration gains (+50% relative) depend on correct execution of unspecified details.
- **Low:** Specific efficiency numbers and computational cost-benefit analysis for distillation, due to missing hardware/system specifications.
