# Aggregation Alignment for Federated Learning with Mixture-of-Experts under Data Heterogeneity

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.21276
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21276
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: FedAlign-MoE significantly outperforms baselines like FedAvg and FedMoE, achieving up to 4-7% higher accuracy under severe non-IID settings (α=0.1) and 1.3-1.7× faster convergence.

## Executive Summary
Federated fine-tuning of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) large language models is challenged by data heterogeneity, which leads to misaligned global routing networks and blurred expert specialization across clients. To address these aggregation issues, the paper introduces FedAlign-MoE, a method that jointly aligns gating behaviors and expert semantics. It aligns routing distributions via a consistency-weighted global reference and local distribution regularization, while quantifying semantic consistency among experts using input-space assignments and weight update directions. Selective aggregation via a region-conditioned gated mechanism with an adaptive threshold preserves specialization and accelerates convergence. Experiments on multiple text classification and reasoning datasets demonstrate substantial improvements over state-of-the-art baselines, especially in severe non-IID scenarios.

## Method Summary
FedAlign-MoE operates in a standard federated learning loop with a central server and multiple clients. Each client locally fine-tunes an MoE model, computing during training its empirical routing distribution, the overlap and decision margin of expert selections, and the mean hidden states routed to each expert. On the server, two key aggregation steps are performed. First, a global routing reference distribution is built by weighting each client’s routing distribution with a consistency score that combines expert overlap and decision margin. Clients then regularize their local gating outputs toward this reference using an adaptive KL divergence term. Second, for each expert index, the server measures semantic consistency across clients by comparing their mean routed hidden states (input-space similarity) and the cosine similarity of their weight updates (direction consensus). A pairwise aggregation weight is computed as a gated function that activates only when input-space similarity exceeds an adaptive threshold derived from the global dispersion of similarities. Global expert parameters are updated by a weighted average of the aligned local updates. The adaptive threshold automatically relaxes alignment when semantic dispersion is high, making the mechanism robust to varying degrees of heterogeneity. The method preserves discriminative client-specific routing preferences while promoting cross-client stability and expert specialization, leading to faster convergence and higher final accuracy under non-IID data.

## Key Results
- Up to 4-7% higher test accuracy than FedAvg and FedMoE on AGNews, PIQA, HellaSwag, and MMLU under severe non-IID (α=0.1).
- 1.3-1.7× faster convergence (fewer communication rounds to reach target accuracy).
- Robust performance as the number of clients increases or heterogeneity intensifies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Aligning routing distributions via consistency-weighted global reference and local distribution regularization preserves cross-client stability in expert selection while maintaining discriminative local preferences.
- **Mechanism:** The server aggregates client-specific empirical routing distributions using per-expert weights derived from expert overlap (external consistency) and routing decision margin (internal confidence), forming a global reference distribution. Clients then regularize their local gating outputs toward this reference via KL divergence with adaptive expert-level weights, allowing soft functional alignment without parameter-level consensus.
- **Core assumption:** Routing output distributions directly encode client-specific expert selection preferences and can be aligned in function space to achieve behavioral consensus, even under heterogeneous data.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract], [section III-C], empirical gains in non-IID settings (Table I, Fig. 8).
- **Break condition:** If client heterogeneity is extreme, the global reference may become overly smoothed; if λ is too high, it may override unique local routing boundaries.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Semantic-aware expert aggregation that selectively combines updates from semantically aligned clients stabilizes expert specialization and accelerates global convergence under data heterogeneity.
- **Mechanism:** For each expert, the server quantifies semantic consistency across clients using two indicators: input-space assignment (mean hidden states routed to the expert) and local weight update direction (cosine similarity). A region-conditioned gated aggregation weight is computed per client pair, combining input-space similarity (above adaptive threshold) and direction consensus; global expert updates are weighted averages of these aligned local updates.
- **Core assumption:** Expert semantics—both the input regions they serve and their learned feature-label mappings—are captured by the centroids of routed hidden states and optimization directions; similarity in these indicators implies functional compatibility.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract], [section III-D1], novel semantic quantification not mirrored in related papers.
- **Break condition:** If semantic metrics are noisy or clients have orthogonal update directions despite similar input regions, selective aggregation may incorrectly suppress beneficial diversity or include conflicting updates.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** An adaptive threshold for region-conditioned aggregation dynamically calibrates alignment sensitivity per expert based on consensus dispersion, enabling robust selective aggregation across varying specialization states.
- **Mechanism:** For each expert, the server computes the mean M(e) and standard deviation Σ(e) of pairwise input-space similarities across all clients. The adaptive threshold τ_t^e is set as M(e) - β·Σ(e), tightening when semantics are aligned (low Σ) and relaxing when diverse (high Σ), modulating the gated aggregation weight.
- **Core assumption:** The statistical dispersion of expert semantics across clients reflects the stability of their functional roles; low dispersion indicates a mature, shared specialization, while high dispersion indicates ongoing divergence or client-specific adaptation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section III-D2], distinctive contribution not mirrored in related works.
- **Break condition:** If similarity statistics are estimated from insufficient samples or dispersion is high due to inherent task heterogeneity, the threshold may become unstable.

- **Concept: Federated Learning with Non-IID Data**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire problem stems from data heterogeneity across clients in FL, which drives gating misalignment and expert semantic divergence.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the primary effects of non-IID data on local model updates in vanilla FedAvg, and how do they manifest in MoE architectures?

- **Concept: Functional Alignment vs. Parameter Aggregation**
  - *Why needed here:* FedAlign-MoE shifts from parameter-level averaging to aligning functional behaviors (routing distributions, expert semantics); this distinction is core to its design.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might directly averaging gating network parameters across clients fail under heterogeneity, and what does aligning routing distributions instead achieve?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  Client-side: local MoE model (experts E, gating G) -> optimization of L_total -> computation of empirical routing distribution ¯p_i(e), expert overlap o_i(e), decision margin m_i(e), input-space assignment µ_i(e), weight update ∆θ_e^i -> upload to server.
  Server-side: receives client data -> computes global reference p_g(e) with consistency weighting ω_i(e) -> computes semantic statistics M(e), Σ(e), adaptive threshold τ_t^e -> computes region-conditioned aggregation weights γ_i,j(e) -> aggregates expert updates θ_e(t+1) -> sends global reference and updated experts to clients.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Round start: Server distributes current global experts θ_e(t) and global routing reference p_g^t(e).
  2. Client fine-tuning: Each client computes adaptive consistency weight α_i(e), optimizes L_total, records routing metrics and semantic indicators.
  3. Upload: Clients send ¯p_i(e), ∆θ_e^i, µ_i(e), o_i(e), m_i(e) to server.
  4. Server aggregation: Update p_g^{t+1}(e) with ω_i(e); compute M(e), Σ(e), τ_t^e; for all client pairs compute γ_i,j(e); aggregate θ_e(t+1) via weighted sum.
  5. Repeat until convergence.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Alignment strength vs. local adaptation: Higher λ enforces stricter gating alignment but risks suppressing client-specific routing preferences.
  - Selectivity vs. knowledge integration: Lower adaptive thresholds include more client updates but may admit semantically conflicting experts; higher thresholds preserve specialization but might exclude useful diversity.
  - Communication overhead: Uploading routing distributions and semantic indicators adds modest overhead compared to full model parameters.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Gating divergence: Post-aggregation local accuracies vary widely; routing distributions remain disjoint.
  - Semantic blurring: Same-indexed experts show high variance in output embeddings on shared test data; per-expert performance on diverse inputs deteriorates.
  - Convergence stagnation: Global test accuracy plateaus early or oscillates; check consistency weights ω_i(e) uniformity or adaptive threshold calibration.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Benchmark comparison:** Evaluate test accuracy on IID/non-IID splits to establish superiority over FedAvg, FedProx, PFL-MoE, FedMoE.
  2. **Convergence analysis:** Measure epochs to target accuracy to demonstrate faster convergence from semantic alignment.
  3. **Ablation on gating alignment:** Test variants without consistency weighting or adaptive α_i(e) to isolate contributions of distribution alignment components.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** How does FedAlign-MoE's semantic alignment mechanism generalize to MoE architectures with different routing strategies (e.g., Top-2 vs. Switch) or to non-text modalities like vision or multimodal models?
- **Open Question 2:** Under what conditions does the adaptive threshold τ^t_e fail to preserve expert specialization, particularly when client semantics are extremely heterogeneous or expert roles are highly dynamic?
- **Open Question 3:** Can the consistency-based gating alignment be extended to scenarios where clients have local gating networks with different architectures or handle incomplete participation (client dropouts)?
- **Open Question 4:** How sensitive is FedAlign-MoE to hyperparameter choices (λ, η, β) across diverse datasets, and can these be auto-tuned without full validation on each client's local data?
- **Open Question 5:** Does the semantic-aware aggregation inadvertently reinforce majority semantics and suppress emerging minority or client-specific expert roles that could be globally beneficial?

## Limitations
- Ambiguity in the exact computation of µ_i(e) (whether it uses input or output hidden states of the MoE layer).
- Insufficient details on tracking activated experts and pooling hidden states for ¯p_i(e) and µ_i(e), including memory/compute strategies.
- Baseline implementations (PFL-MoE, FedMoE) are not fully elaborated, risking inaccurate comparisons.
- Training regime nuances (e.g., number of local epochs, learning rate schedule) are not thoroughly explored.
