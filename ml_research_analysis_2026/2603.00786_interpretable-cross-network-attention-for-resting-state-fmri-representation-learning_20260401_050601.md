# Interpretable Cross-Network Attention for Resting-State fMRI Representation Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00786
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00786
- Reference count: 30
- Primary result: 77.53% balanced accuracy and 0.850 AUC on three-way Alzheimer's classification (CN/MCI/AD).

## Executive Summary
BrainInterNet is a self-supervised framework for resting-state fMRI representation learning that enhances interpretability by leveraging predefined functional networks. It masks entire networks during pretraining and uses a cross-attention decoder to reconstruct them from the remaining context, explicitly quantifying inter-network dependencies. Trained on 3,087 multi-cohort rs-fMRI recordings (ABCD, HCP Development, HCP Young Adults, HCP Aging) and evaluated on 2,366 ADNI scans (total 5,582), it achieved strong classification performance on Alzheimer’s disease staging (77.53% balanced accuracy, 0.850 AUC). The model uncovered systematic alterations in default mode, limbic, and attention networks, and encoder embedding norms correlated with disease severity longitudinally. Cross-attention weights provided interpretable profiles of network coupling changes, addressing the interpretability gap in self-supervised rs-fMRI models.

## Method Summary
The framework starts by parcellating rs-fMRI data into a set of predefined functional networks (e.g., based on a standard atlas). During self-supervised pretraining, it randomly selects a network to mask (i.e., removes its time series) and tasks the model with reconstructing that network’s signal from the remaining networks. An encoder processes the unmasked networks into contextual embeddings. A cross-attention decoder then attends to these embeddings to generate a reconstruction of the masked network. The training objective minimizes the reconstruction error (e.g., mean squared error). The pretrained encoder yields latent representations that can be used for downstream tasks. For Alzheimer’s classification, the authors extracted the norm of the encoder embeddings for each subject and used it as a severity marker, achieving competitive accuracy. The cross-attention weights themselves serve as interpretable measures of inter-network coupling, as they reflect how much the model attends to each context network when reconstructing a target network.

## Key Results
- Achieved 77.53% balanced accuracy and 0.850 AUC on three-way Alzheimer’s classification (CN/MCI/AD) on ADNI.
- Revealed systematic alterations in default mode, limbic, and attention networks via cross-attention weight patterns.
- Encoder embedding norms tracked disease severity longitudinally, correlating with clinical progression.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BrainInterNet’s design directly enforces learning of inter-network relationships by masking entire functional networks, forcing the model to rely on context from other networks for reconstruction. The cross-attention decoder provides a natural way to weight contributions from different context networks, yielding an interpretable coupling map. Because the masking and reconstruction are based on biologically meaningful networks rather than arbitrary patches, the learned representations capture system-level organization relevant to disease. The self-supervised pretraining on diverse cohorts exposes the model to a broad range of healthy and pathological variations, leading to robust embeddings that generalize to AD classification. The simplicity of using embedding norms as a disease severity proxy emerges from the fact that disease-related network disruptions affect the overall magnitude of the representation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self-supervised learning**: Needed to learn rich representations from unlabeled rs-fMRI data. *Quick check*: Pretext task performance (reconstruction loss) improves with training and generalizes to downstream tasks.
- **Masked reconstruction**: Forces the model to infer missing information from context, capturing dependencies. *Quick check*: Masked inputs can be reconstructed with low error; ablations show performance drop when random masking is used.
- **Cross-attention**: Enables the model to dynamically weight context networks, yielding interpretable coupling. *Quick check*: Attention weights are non-uniform and vary across target networks; ablating cross-attention reduces performance.
- **Functional networks**: Provide semantically meaningful units that align with brain organization. *Quick check*: Using atlas-defined networks vs. arbitrary regions yields better downstream performance and interpretability.
- **Embedding norm**: Serves as a compact summary of overall activation magnitude, sensitive to disease-related changes. *Quick check*: Norm correlates with clinical severity or cognitive scores across subjects.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input rs-fMRI → Parcellation into functional networks → Encoder (E) → Cross-Attention Decoder (CA) → Reconstruction Head (linear projection). For downstream tasks: Encoder → Embedding pooling → Classifier (or norm extraction).  

**Critical path**: During training, the system parcellates data, selects a network to mask, encodes the remaining networks, applies cross-attention to generate the masked representation, computes reconstruction loss, and updates parameters. For classification, all networks are encoded, embeddings are aggregated (e.g., norm), and a linear classifier maps to diagnostic labels.

**Design tradeoffs**: Masking entire networks improves interpretability but discards within-network temporal dynamics; cross-attention provides interpretable weights at the cost of additional parameters; using predefined networks anchors the model to biology but may limit discovery of novel subnetworks; multi-cohort training enhances generalizability but introduces scanner and demographic heterogeneity.

**Failure signatures**: (1) Reconstruction loss plateaus at a high value, indicating the model cannot learn inter-network dependencies. (2) Cross-attention weights become uniform or random, suggesting the decoder ignores context. (3) Downstream classification accuracy is near chance, meaning embeddings failed to capture discriminative features. (4) Embedding norms show no correlation with disease severity.

**Three first experiments**:
1. **Reconstruction validation**: Compute masked network reconstruction error (e.g., MSE) on a held-out cohort; expect low error consistent across different masked networks.
2. **Downstream classification**: Freeze the encoder, extract embedding norms, and train a logistic regression for CN vs. AD on ADNI; report balanced accuracy and AUC to confirm representational utility.
3. **Attention weight analysis**: Visualize average cross-attention weight matrices for CN and AD groups; test for statistically significant differences (e.g., t-tests) in weights linking specific networks, especially in default mode and attention systems.

## Limitations
- **Limited external validation**: Model was trained on ABCD/HCP and tested only on ADNI, raising concerns about domain shift (age, scanner) and generalizability to other cohorts.
- **Interpretability lacks biological grounding**: Cross-attention weights are proposed as network coupling changes but were not correlated with established biomarkers (amyloid PET, tau, CSF) or cognitive scores.
- **Masking strategy oversimplifies**: Masking entire predefined networks ignores intra-network dynamics and may bias results toward larger networks; partial masking could provide finer-grained insights.

## Confidence
- Classification performance: Medium (strong but single test set, no comparison to other self-supervised fMRI models)
- Network coupling alterations: Low (based on attention patterns without external biomarker validation)
- Longitudinal embedding norms tracking: Medium (cross-sectional correlation provided; prospective prediction untested)

## Next Checks
- **Independent replication**: Apply the pretrained BrainInterNet model to an external dataset (e.g., OASIS-3 or UK Biobank) and report balanced accuracy and AUC for Alzheimer’s classification to assess generalizability.
- **Biomarker correlation**: In ADNI participants with PET/CSF data, correlate attention-derived coupling metrics (e.g., average attention weight from DMN to attention networks) with amyloid and tau burden, adjusting for age and sex.
- **Prospective prediction**: Use baseline embedding norms to predict conversion from MCI to AD over 24 months in a holdout ADNI longitudinal sample; compare discrimination (AUC) and calibration against clinical baseline models (e.g., CDR-SB, MMSE, APOE genotype).
