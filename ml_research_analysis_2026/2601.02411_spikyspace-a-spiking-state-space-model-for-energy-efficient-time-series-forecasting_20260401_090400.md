# SpikySpace: A Spiking State Space Model for Energy-Efficient Time Series Forecasting
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.02411
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02411
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** A fully spiking state space model that reduces estimated energy consumption by over 96% compared to leading ANN and SNN baselines while achieving state-of-the-art forecasting accuracy among efficient models (e.g., 0.994 R² on Electricity).

## Executive Summary
SpikySpace introduces the first fully spiking implementation of a selective state space model (SSM) for time series forecasting, targeting extreme energy efficiency on edge devices. It replaces the dense matrix multiplications and nonlinear activations of conventional SSMs with event-driven computations. Core innovations include a "spiking selective scan" that updates the hidden state only on binary spike events using bit-shift operations, and hardware-friendly PTSoftplus/PTSiLU activation approximations that avoid exponentials and divisions. Through an ANN-to-SNN conversion pipeline with quantization-aware training, the model achieves up to a 98.73% reduction in estimated energy relative to iTransformer while maintaining competitive or superior forecasting accuracy across four benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The method follows an ANN-to-SNN conversion paradigm. First, a continuous-valued "teacher" ANN is trained, incorporating the paper's proposed PTSoftplus and PTSiLU activation functions and a spiking SSM backbone. This ANN is trained with quantization-aware training (using LSQ) to regularize weights and activations. Second, this trained quantized ANN is converted into a spiking neural network (SNN). The conversion maps the quantized activation magnitudes to spike rates via an Average Integrate-and-Fire neuron model and applies "temporal rescaling" (scaling weights and thresholds by the timestep T) to drastically reduce the final spike rate. The SNN's core is a spiking selective scan: the SSM state update `h_t = A h_{t-1} + B x_t` is made sparse by gating `A` with a binary spike `Δ_s` derived from the continuous `Δ` parameter. When `Δ_s=1`, `A` becomes a power-of-two matrix, enabling a bit-shift operation instead of a multiplication; the input `x_t` is also a sparse spike train. The final output is computed via a sparse gating mechanism using PTSiLU.

## Key Results
*   **Energy Efficiency:** Reduces estimated energy consumption by 98.73% vs. iTransformer and 96.24% vs. iSpikformer, with a total cost of 0.17 mJ on the Electricity dataset.
*   **Accuracy:** Achieves an average R² of 0.994 on Electricity (vs. 0.978 for iTransformer) and surpasses the best SNN baseline by 3.0% R² on Metr-la.
*   **Efficiency-Accuracy Trade-off:** Maintains competitive accuracy with only 0.868M parameters, demonstrating that neuromorphic (spiking) efficiency can be combined with modern sequence modeling capacity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Replacing dense multiplications in state space models with event-driven bit-shift operations drastically reduces energy consumption while preserving temporal modeling capacity.
*   **Mechanism:** The core SSM recurrence `h_t = A h_{t-1} + B x_t` is transformed. The decay matrix `A` is constrained to powers-of-two. State updates are gated by a binary spike `Δ_s`. If `Δ_s,t == 1`, the state undergoes a bit-shift (`2^K * h_{t-1}`); otherwise, it is preserved. The input term `B_t x_t` becomes a sparse accumulation because `x_t` is a spike train.
*   **Core assumption:** The sparsity induced by `Δ_s` and input spike trains `s` is high enough that the number of actual addition operations is far fewer than dense operations. The piecewise power-of-two approximation of `exp(ΔA)` is a sufficient proxy.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...eliminating dense multiplications... spiking selective scan... Energy reduction by 98.73%..."
    *   [section 4.2] "At = (2^K if Δ_s,t=1, I if Δ_s,t=0). ... This transforms the core state transitions into efficient bit-shift operations and integer accumulations, free of multiplication."
    *   [corpus] Weak direct evidence; the mechanism's claimed efficiency gain is primarily anchored in the paper's own analysis and energy model.
*   **Break condition:** If the spike generation mechanism produces a firing rate approaching 100%, the computational cost regresses to dense operations. The paper mitigates this with temporal rescaling. A break would occur if the average spike rate `s` approached 1.0 for a given dataset/configuration.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** PTSoftplus and PTSiLU eliminate the exponentials and divisions in standard activations with bounded error, enabling hardware-friendly inference without destabilizing training.
*   **Mechanism:** These are piecewise linear/affine functions built from `2^x` (bit-shift) and constants, with analytically derived junction points to ensure C1 continuity. Lemmas 4.2 & 4.4 prove bounded error in function value and derivative (~0.9/0.37 for PTSoftplus, ~0.3/0.26 for PTSiLU).
*   **Core assumption:** The bounded maximum error is small enough that the optimizer can compensate via downstream parameters without crippling expressive power.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...proposes PTsoftplus/PTSiLU approximations that replace exponentials and divisions with power-of-two arithmetic."
    *   [section 4.3, 4.4, appendix C] Full mathematical definitions, derivations, and proofs of continuity and bounded error.
    *   [corpus] Weak; the concept exists in quantized ANNs, but the specific power-of-two, analytically-bounded design for SNNs/SSMs is novel per the paper.
*   **Break condition:** If during training on a specific dataset, gradients through PTSoftplus/PTSiLU cause instability (nan/inf) or a significant performance drop (e.g., >5% R²) versus exact activations in a matched ANN setup, the bounded error guarantee would be insufficient.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** ANN-to-SNN conversion with quantization-aware training (QAT) effectively bridges the gap between high-precision continuous training and low-precision, spike-based deployment, preserving accuracy.
*   **Mechanism:** A continuous-valued "teacher" ANN (with PT-approximated activations) is first trained with MSE loss. Weights/activations are quantized during training using LSQ with STE. After convergence, the ANN's weights and quantized activation ranges are converted into an SNN. Spike thresholds and membrane dynamics are set based on learned quantization parameters (`α`, `β`) to match average firing rates over timestep window `T`.
*   **Core assumption:** The dynamics of an IF neuron with properly scaled threshold/input can emulate the average activation value of a quantized ReLU-based ANN over `T`. The quantization ranges learned in the ANN phase are directly transferable.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 4.5] Describes the conversion pipeline using LSQ and references standard SNN conversion works.
    *   [section 5.1] Results show SpikySpace (SNN) outperforms some dense ANNs and SNN baselines, supporting conversion fidelity.
    *   [corpus] The conversion pipeline is standard in efficient SNN literature; evidence is strong for the general paradigm but specific to this SSM+PT-activation design, evidence is mainly the reported results.
*   **Break condition:** A large accuracy gap (>10% R²) between the pre-conversion quantized ANN and the final converted SNN would indicate the spike-based representation cannot approximate the learned continuous function for this SSM architecture, possibly due to highly non-uniform activation distributions.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input Encoder (Conv1D + SN) -> Spike Train `s_in` -> Selective Parameter Gen (Linear proj on `s` -> `B`, `C`, `Δ_raw` -> SN -> Q -> PTSoftplus -> SN -> binary `Δ_s`) -> **Spiking Selective Scan** (Core loop: gated bit-shift `A_t`, masked `B_t`, update `h_t = SN(A_t h_{t-1} + B_t s_t)`, output `y_t = SN(C_t h_t + D s_t)`) -> Output Gating (Residual branch -> Q -> PTSiLU -> `y` acts as sparse mask `y ⊙ PTSiLU(...)` -> Linear `W_o`).
*   **Critical path:** The **Spiking Selective Scan**. It defines the O(L) recurrent update replacing attention/dense SSM scans. Every timestep `t` depends on previous binary state `h_{t-1}` and gating spike `Δ_s,t`. Performance/energy hinges on sparsity of `Δ_s` and `s`, and bit-shift implementation.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Sparsity vs. Representational Capacity:* Higher spike rate preserves more analog info but increases energy. Temporal rescaling trades temporal resolution for lower spike rate.
    *   *Approximation Rigor vs. Hardware Simplicity:* PT-approximations use simple piecewise functions but introduce bounded error. More complex approximations could reduce error but increase gate count.
    *   *Timestep `T` vs. Latency/Accuracy:* Larger `T` reduces effective firing rate (better energy) but increases simulation latency and may degrade temporal precision.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Saturation failure:** Average spike rate (`s`) exceeds ~50-60% despite temporal rescaling, indicating poor sparsity and negating energy gains.
    *   **Error accumulation:** Performance degrades significantly on long horizons (e.g., H=24) vs. short (H=3), as linear recurrence may not propagate info as effectively as global attention.
    *   **Conversion collapse:** Large validation R² drop (>5%) between converged quantized ANN and final converted SNN, indicating spike encoding cannot represent learned continuous function.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Accuracy Benchmark:** Train/evaluate SpikySpace (T=3) against iTransformer, iSpikformer, SpikeSTAG on all four datasets for horizons {3,6,12,24}. Report R² and RRSE to confirm competitive/best performance among SNNs and competitiveness with dense ANNs.
    2.  **Energy Estimation Protocol:** Implement the energy model (Eq. 14) on a 22nm process. Measure/estimate `E_acc`, `E_move`, `E_weight` for one SpikySpace layer (using reported spike rates) and a comparable iTransformer dense layer. Compute total `E_total` for Electricity (H=3) to validate the ~98% reduction claim with a grounded hardware model including memory access.
    3.  **Activation Approximation Ablation:** Train the *full-precision ANN* variant (no spikes, just PT/standard activations) on Solar/Metr-la. Compare: (a) SSM with exact Softplus+SiLU, (b) SSM with PTSoftplus+SiLU, (c) SSM with Softplus+PTSiLU, (d) SSM with both PT versions. Isolate the impact of approximations *without* spike conversion noise.
