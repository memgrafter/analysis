# Call2Instruct: Automated Pipeline for Generating Q&A Datasets from Call Center Recordings for LLM Fine-Tuning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.14263
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14263
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The pipeline converts noisy call center audio into instruction-formatted Q&A pairs; fine-tuning Llama 2 7B on the resulting dataset yields a model with domain-adapted responses (qualitative validation).

## Executive Summary
Call2Instruct tackles the challenge of creating high-quality instructional Q&A datasets from real, noisy call center recordings. The proposed end-to-end automated pipeline integrates audio preprocessing (denoising, IVR removal, ASR), textual cleaning and anonymization, semantic extraction of customer demands and agent responses using vector embeddings, and ChatGPT-based rewriting and synthesis to form instruction-formatted pairs. The generated dataset is used to fine-tune a Llama 2 7B model, which demonstrates functional domain adaptation in qualitative assessments. While promising, the work currently lacks rigorous quantitative evaluation and detailed reproducibility information, outlining future directions for benchmarking, generalization, multi-turn context, and error mitigation.

## Method Summary
The pipeline processes raw call audio (sourced from AWS) through sequential stages: denoising and IVR filtering, transcription with a Whisper variant, and speaker diarization if needed. The raw transcripts are then cleaned (punctuation restoration, disfluency removal) and anonymized to mask personally identifiable information. Next, ChatGPT rewrites customer utterances into objective, direct questions and condenses agent responses. Both rewritten demands and responses are embedded with `text-embedding-ada-002` and indexed in Elasticsearch. For each demand, a semantic search retrieves the top-N most similar agent responses from other calls; these candidates are individually refined with ChatGPT and subsequently consolidated into a single coherent answer. The final Q&A pairs are formatted for Instruct fine-tuning (instruction, input, output). Practical validation consists of fine-tuning Llama 2 7B via the Lamini platform and conducting a qualitative review of the adapted model's outputs.

## Key Results
- Generated an instructional Q&A dataset from real telecommunication call recordings (Portuguese) and used it to fine-tune a Llama 2 7B model.
- The fine-tuned model demonstrated domain adaptation, understanding context and staying on-topic, though some responses were overly generic.
- The ChatGPT-based rewriting step required prompt engineering; an early version failed on 19 out of 40 demands, later resolved with an improved prompt.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Semantic Search over Vector Embeddings**  
- **Claim:** Matching customer demands to agent responses via semantic search across calls overcomes noise and non-literal phrasing.  
- **Mechanism:** Utterances are rewritten for clarity, converted to dense vectors, and a similarity search retrieves the most relevant agent response vectors for each demand vector.  
- **Core assumption:** The embedding space preserves core intent despite ASR noise and disfluencies.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.3 describe semantic extraction and cross-call matching; internal results show discovery of relevant information across interactions.  
- **Break condition:** Domain jargon or ASR errors could push semantically equivalent intents apart, leading to irrelevant retrievals.

**Mechanism 2: LLM-based Rewriting for Standardization**  
- **Claim:** Rewriting raw oral transcripts into objective, concise forms improves semantic representations and final answer quality.  
- **Mechanism:** ChatGPT, guided by refined prompts, converts customer speech into direct questions and refines candidate agent responses for clarity and conciseness.  
- **Core assumption:** The LLM accurately infers the instructional intent and produces a better training example than the raw transcript.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3: improved prompt reduced rewriting failures from 19/40 to 0; Section 4.4 details response refinement and synthesis.  
- **Break condition:** Hallucinations or meaning distortion during rewriting create a distribution shift, harming model performance.

**Mechanism 3: Functional Validation via Fine-Tuning**  
- **Claim:** Successful Instruct fine-tuning of an LLM on the generated dataset validates the pipeline’s viability for domain adaptation.  
- **Mechanism:** The dataset is formatted for standard Instruct fine-tuning; training a base model (Llama 2 7B) yields a model adapted to the call center domain.  
- **Core assumption:** If the model learns from the data, the data contains a usable signal for the target task.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.5 report successful fine-tuning; qualitative validation showed the model learned the call center context.  
- **Break condition:** Without controlled evaluation, the model may superficially adapt (e.g., memorization) without gaining generalizable Q&A capability.

## Foundational Learning
- **ASR Robustness & Word Error Rate (WER) Propagation:** ASR errors in noisy audio introduce semantic noise that can survive cleaning and distort embeddings. A high WER on a technical query can corrupt the derived demand and its matching. Quick check: How might a 20% WER on “modem not working” affect the rewritten demand and subsequent semantic search?
- **Dense Vector Semantics for Information Retrieval:** The core pairing relies on the assumption that similar meanings yield similar embeddings (e.g., `text-embedding-ada-002`). Domain jargon or phrasing variations can weaken this assumption. Quick check: Are embeddings for “I can’t connect to the internet” and “The network is down” sufficiently close? What factors might make them distant?
- **PII Detection/Anonymization in Noisy Text:** ASR transcripts are noisy and contain sensitive data; standard NER tools may fail. Robust anonymization is essential before dataset release. Quick check: How would you identify and redact an account number spoken as “one two three four five” in a transcript?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Raw Audio (AWS buckets) -> [1. Audio Preproc: Diarization (if needed), Denoising, IVR filtering] -> [2. ASR (Whisper variants)] -> Raw Transcripts -> [3. Text Cleaning: Punctuation, Disfluency removal] -> [4. Anonymization: PII detection/masking] -> Clean Corpus -> [5. Rewriting: ChatGPT prompt for demands/responses] -> Rewritten Corpus -> [6. Embedding: text-embedding-ada-002] -> Vector DB (Elasticsearch) -> [7. Semantic Search & Candidate Retrieval] -> [8. Response Refinement: ChatGPT rewrite] -> [9. Answer Synthesis: ChatGPT consolidate N candidates] -> Final Instruction Dataset (JSON) -> [10. Validation: Fine-tune Llama 2 7B (Lamini platform), qualitative review].

**Critical path:** Audio Preproc → ASR → Text Clean/Anonymize → Rewriting → Embedding → Search/Synthesis. The rewriting step (5) is a pivotal quality gate; failure here degrades all subsequent semantic operations.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Automation vs. Control:** Using Lamini simplifies fine-tuning but reduces hyperparameter/experiment control.  
- **Accuracy vs. Cost/Latency:** ChatGPT for multiple rewriting/synthesis steps improves quality but incurs API cost, latency, and non-determinism.  
- **Cross-Context Matching vs. Fidelity:** Pairing a demand with a response from a different call aggregates knowledge but risks contextual mismatch.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **ASR Bleed-through:** Persistent nonsensical words or phrases survive cleaning, causing meaningless embeddings.  
- **Anonymization Gap:** Residual PII (names, numbers) appears in final dataset samples.  
- **Semantic Mismatch in Pairs:** The generated answer does not address the question (e.g., billing answer for an internet speed query).  
- **Generic Fine-Tuned Output:** Post-fine-tuning model outputs vague, non-specific responses despite training on the dataset.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Module Isolation Test:** Run a small set (n=50) of calls through stages 1–4. Manually verify PII removal and basic transcript readability.  
2. **Embedding Space Sanity Check:** Take 20 rewritten demands, compute cosine similarity to their original in-call agent responses (expect >0.7), and inspect the top‑3 cross‑call retrieved responses for thematic relevance.  
3. **End-to-End Mini Dataset & Sanity Fine-Tune:** Generate a tiny dataset (e.g., 500 Q&A pairs) and fine‑tune a small model (Llama 2 7B, 1 epoch). Perform qualitative prompting with a test demand; check alignment between the model’s response and the intended answer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper explicitly identifies the following open questions for future work:

1. **Quantitative Evaluation Framework:** How can rigorous, domain‑specific benchmarks and human assessment be implemented to measure Q&A dataset quality and fine‑tuned LLM performance? (Current validation is qualitative and functional.)
2. **Generalization Across Domains and Languages:** To what extent does the pipeline generalize to other call center domains (e.g., banking, healthcare) and multiple languages? (It has been tested only on telecommunications, likely Portuguese.)
3. **Multi‑Turn Context Incorporation:** How can multi‑turn conversational context be effectively used to capture complex, multi‑utterance requests and solutions? (Current approach isolates individual utterance segments.)
4. **Impact of Rewriting Step and Alternatives:** What is the comparative impact of the ChatGPT‑based rewriting on final dataset quality, and can alternative LLM summarization or keypoint extraction methods improve specificity and relevance? (No ablation of this step has been done.)
5. **Mitigating ASR Error Propagation:** How can errors from the ASR component be mitigated to improve overall dataset quality? (No analysis of WER or correlation with downstream failures has been provided.)

## Limitations
- **Reproduction gaps:** Exact prompts, semantic search parameters, and anonymization implementation details are omitted, hindering faithful replication.
- **Evaluation insufficiency:** Validation relies on qualitative fine‑tuning outcomes without quantitative metrics (e.g., BLEU, ROUGE, human scores), limiting objective assessment of dataset quality.
- **Risk of propagation:** ASR errors, embedding model limitations, rewriting hallucinations, and cross‑call contextual mismatches may degrade data quality; PII leakage remains a concern due to unspecified anonymization robustness.

## Confidence
We assess confidence in the paper’s key claim clusters as follows:

| Claim Cluster                     | Confidence |
|-----------------------------------|------------|
| Semantic extraction & pairing     | Medium     |
| LLM-based rewriting               | Medium     |
| Fine‑tuning as validation         | Low        |

- **Semantic extraction & pairing:** Conceptually sound and internally supported, but no ablation isolates its contribution or measures failure rates.
- **LLM‑based rewriting:** Prompt iteration reduced failures, yet extrinsic impact on dataset quality remains unquantified.
- **Fine‑tuning as validation:** Successful fine‑tuning is necessary but not sufficient; without controlled evaluation, it does not guarantee high‑quality dataset generation.

## Next Checks
1. **Embedding sanity test:** For a set of 50 rewritten demands, compute cosine similarity to their original in‑call agent responses (expect high) and to the top‑3 cross‑call retrievals (inspect thematic relevance). Report mean scores and manual relevance judgments.
2. **Dataset audit:** Randomly sample 200 Q&A pairs; have domain experts annotate for answer relevance (yes/no) and specificity (1–5). Compute inter‑annotator agreement and aggregate scores.
3. **Controlled fine‑tuning comparison:** Fine‑tune Llama 2 7B on (a) the generated Call2Instruct dataset and (b) a baseline of raw transcript pairs (same calls, no rewriting/semantic search). Evaluate on a held‑out test set using BLEU, BERTScore, and human preference (which response is more correct/helpful?).
