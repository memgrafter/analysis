# NorwAI's Large Language Models: Technical Report

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03034
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03034
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: NorwAI developed Norwegian-specific LLMs (369M-24B params) that outperform generic models on Norwegian tasks

## Executive Summary
This report introduces NorwAI's suite of large language models specifically tailored to Norwegian and Scandinavian languages. The team developed multiple models ranging from 369M to 24B parameters using architectures like GPT, Llama, Mistral, Mixtral, and Magistral, trained on curated Norwegian corpora (25B–88.45B tokens). A key contribution is the creation of NLEBench, a comprehensive benchmark dataset for evaluating Norwegian generative language modeling across diverse tasks including summarization, conversation, NLU, and instruction following. The models demonstrate strong performance on Norwegian language understanding and generation tasks, with the Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct model achieving the highest average score in human evaluations of news summarization. The work addresses the underrepresentation of Norwegian in NLP by providing openly available, culturally adapted models and evaluation tools.

## Method Summary
NorwAI developed Norwegian-specific LLMs through a multi-stage process: curating Norwegian corpora (25B-88.45B tokens) covering news, transcription data, and multilingual texts; pretraining or continually pretraining models using Transformer architectures (GPT, Mistral, Llama, Mixtral, Magistral); applying LoRA fine-tuning on Norwegian instruction datasets and RLHF for preference optimization; and evaluating using NLEBench and NorEval benchmarks. The approach combines large-scale Norwegian data with advanced post-training strategies to create models adapted to Norwegian linguistic and cultural contexts.

## Key Results
- Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct achieved highest average score in human evaluations for news summarization
- Models demonstrate strong performance on Norwegian NLU, generation, and instruction-following tasks
- NLEBench benchmark provides comprehensive evaluation framework for Norwegian generative language modeling
- Models address Norwegian underrepresentation in NLP with culturally adapted, openly available models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The NorwAI model family achieves strong Norwegian language performance through targeted data curation and architecture selection.
- Mechanism: Models are trained from scratch or continually pretrained on Norwegian-specific corpora, using architectures (GPT, Llama, Mistral, Mixtral, Magistral) that have proven effective for language modeling.
- Core assumption: Norwegian linguistic properties are sufficiently captured by large-scale training on Norwegian text.
- Evidence anchors: Models are specifically tailored to Norwegian and Scandinavian languages, trained on curated Norwegian corpora (25B–88.45B tokens); The models are adapted to Norwegian data and trained using advanced post-training strategies.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Instruction tuning with Norwegian cultural data improves model alignment and usability for local applications.
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning on high-quality annotated instruction datasets with Norwegian-specific expressions and cultural knowledge, using parameter-efficient methods like LoRA.
- Core assumption: Norwegian users have distinct communication patterns and cultural references that standard English-trained models miss.
- Evidence anchors: Instruction-tuned variants showcase strong assistant-style capabilities for practical deployment; Fine-tuning on curated instruction datasets containing Norwegian culture, daily life, history, and geography.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The mixture-of-experts (MoE) architecture enables scaling to larger models without proportional compute increases.
- Mechanism: Mixtral-8x7B uses 8 experts (7B parameters each) with only 2 activated per token, providing high capacity while maintaining efficiency.
- Core assumption: Norwegian language tasks benefit from larger model capacity without requiring full activation of all parameters.
- Evidence anchors: Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct model achieved highest average score in human evaluations; Sparse activation in MoE models enables scaling parameter counts without proportionally increasing computation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of NorwAI's Norwegian language models compare to multilingual models like GPT-4 or DeepSeek on Norwegian-specific tasks such as summarization, NLU, and conversation, and what factors contribute to any observed differences?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that GPT-3.5-Turbo did not exhibit advantages in understanding Norwegian conversations and that GPT-3.5-Turbo and NB-GPT-J-6B outperformed NorGPTs on BLEU and ROUGE metrics in news summarization.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides some comparative results but does not offer a comprehensive analysis of how NorwAI's models perform against state-of-the-art multilingual models across all Norwegian-specific tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic evaluation of NorwAI's models against multilingual models on a standardized set of Norwegian tasks, including detailed analysis of performance gaps and contributing factors.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the long-term impact of using copyrighted material in the training of Norwegian language models, and how does the reduction of such material in newer corpora affect model performance and cultural representation?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the reduction of copyrighted material in successive corpus versions and its implications for model training.
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions the trend, it does not explore the potential impact on model performance, cultural representation, or legal considerations in depth.
- What evidence would resolve it: An empirical study comparing models trained on different proportions of copyrighted vs. non-copyrighted material, assessing performance and cultural bias, and an analysis of legal and ethical implications.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the performance and usability of NorwAI's language models vary across different Norwegian dialects and minority languages such as Nynorsk and Sámi, and what steps can be taken to improve support for these languages?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that NorwAI-Magistral-24B was trained on corpus V3, which includes substantial Nynorsk and Sámi data, and that NorwAI-Mistral-7B and NorwAI-Mixtral-8x7B were trained on corpus V2 with limited coverage for these languages.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide detailed performance metrics or usability assessments for different dialects and minority languages.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive evaluation of model performance across Norwegian dialects and minority languages, including user studies and recommendations for improving support.

## Limitations

- Evaluation framework relies on limited human evaluation (10 journalists rating 2,500 samples) that may not represent broader Norwegian language use
- Corpus composition details remain vague regarding dialect representation and minority language coverage
- Performance comparisons to English-language models are absent, making it difficult to contextualize Norwegian-specific improvements
- Report doesn't address potential biases in training data or model outputs, nor robustness testing across different Norwegian sociolects

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Technical implementation of model training and architecture choices is well-documented and follows established practices; corpus sizes (25B-88.45B tokens) and model parameter counts (369M-24B) are verifiable specifications.

**Medium Confidence**: Performance claims on Norwegian tasks are supported by benchmark results, but lack of comparison to baseline English models and limited human evaluation scope reduce confidence in practical impact assessment.

**Low Confidence**: Claims about cultural adaptation and instruction-following improvements lack concrete evidence beyond stating that Norwegian-specific data was used; report doesn't demonstrate how these models outperform generic multilingual approaches for Norwegian users.

## Next Checks

1. **Benchmark Comparison Test**: Evaluate NorwAI models against multilingual models (e.g., GPT-4, Claude) on identical Norwegian tasks to establish true performance gains from Norwegian-specific training.

2. **Cultural Coverage Audit**: Analyze the training corpus distribution across Norwegian dialects, Nynorsk/Bokmål balance, and minority language representation to verify claims of comprehensive Norwegian coverage.

3. **Bias and Robustness Analysis**: Test model outputs across different Norwegian sociolects, registers, and potentially sensitive topics to assess bias patterns and robustness that the report doesn't address.
