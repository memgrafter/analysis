# When Does Margin Clamping Affect Training Variance? Dataset-Dependent Effects in Contrastive Forward-Forward Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00951
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00951
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** Margin clamping inflates test accuracy variance on CIFAR-10 (5.90×) by saturating gradients, but reverses on datasets with low positive-pair density or extreme accuracy; effect depends on clamp activation rate and task difficulty.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines seed sensitivity in Contrastive Forward-Forward (CFF) learning, comparing two margin implementations in supervised contrastive loss: saturating similarity clamping (`min(s+m,1)`) versus gradient-neutral post-log-probability subtraction. On CIFAR-10 with standard margins, clamping yields identical mean test accuracy but 5.90× higher variance across random seeds (p=0.003). Diagnostics reveal that layer-0 clamp activation reaches 60.7% and gradient norms are 4.0× lower under clamping, indicating saturation-driven gradient truncation. The effect is dataset-dependent: on CIFAR-100 (low positive-pair density), SVHN (high accuracy), and Fashion-MNIST, variance ratios are inverted (0.39×, 0.25×, 0.08×). An SVHN difficulty sweep shows that task difficulty interacts with positive-pair density; the variance ratio transitions from 0.25× at 97% accuracy to 16.73× at 25% accuracy. The variance inflation occurs only when both layer-0 clamp activation is high and task difficulty is intermediate. These findings highlight that seemingly equivalent margin implementations can dramatically affect training stability in layer-wise contrastive learning.

## Method Summary
The study uses a two-stage Contrastive Forward-Forward (CFF) pipeline with a Vision Transformer (ViT; depth L=8, hidden dimension d=128, 4 attention heads, 4×4 patches → 64 tokens). In Stage 1, each layer ℓ is trained independently for 600 epochs using a supervised contrastive loss (mean-over-positives reduction) with a layer-specific linear margin schedule (m₀=0.4 → m₇=0.1) and temperature τ=0.15. Gradients flow only through layer ℓ due to stop-gradient operations between layers. Two margin variants are compared: (i) clamping the similarity `s_uv,ℓ` to `min(s+m_ℓ, 1)`; (ii) subtracting the margin from the log-probability (`log p_uv,ℓ - m_ℓ`) which is gradient-neutral. After Stage 1, all layers are frozen and a linear probe is trained for 50 epochs on the representations (mean-pooled tokens). Test accuracy is reported from the best validation checkpoint. The primary comparison metric is the variance ratio (VR) of test accuracy between clamped and subtracted conditions across random seeds.

## Key Results
- On CIFAR-10, clamping produces **5.90× higher test accuracy variance** than subtraction (p=0.003) while achieving the same mean accuracy.
- Clamping leads to **high saturation**: layer‑0 clamp activation rate (CAR) reaches **60.7%** and layer‑0 gradient norms are **4.0× lower**, confirming gradient truncation.
- The variance effect is **dataset‑dependent**: on CIFAR‑100 (low positive‑pair density) VR=0.39×; on SVHN VR=0.25×; on Fashion‑MNIST VR=0.08×.  
- An SVHN difficulty sweep reveals a **transition**: VR moves from 0.25× at ~97% accuracy to 16.73× at ~25% accuracy, indicating that intermediate task difficulty is necessary for variance inflation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Three interrelated mechanisms explain the observations.

### Gradient Truncation via Saturation
Saturating similarity clamping (`min(s+m,1)`) makes the loss constant for positive pairs where `s+m > 1`. The direct gradient `∂L/∂s` becomes zero for those pairs. Although gradients can still flow indirectly through the denominator of the softmax, the truncation is data‑dependent: which positive pairs saturate varies across minibatches and random seeds (initialization, augmentation). This creates seed‑specific patterns of gradient zeroing, especially at early layers (layer 0). Because Forward‑Forward training isolates layers via stop‑gradients, these early‑layer truncations cannot be compensated by deeper layers, causing optimization trajectories to diverge more than under the non‑truncating subtraction baseline. The effect accumulates over training, inflating final test accuracy variance. High clamp activation rates (e.g., 60.7% on CIFAR‑10) directly indicate frequent saturation. The mechanism is supported by a 4.0× reduction in layer‑0 gradient norms under clamping at the final epoch.

### Positive‑Pair Density as a Mediator
The frequency of saturation depends on the number of same‑class pairs per minibatch, i.e., positive‑pair density. Datasets with many classes (e.g., CIFAR‑100) have far fewer positive pairs (~2,580 per batch of 512) than CIFAR‑10 (~25,700). Consequently, cosine similarities are less likely to exceed `1‑m`, leading to a lower clamp activation rate (e.g., 29.0% for CIFAR‑100) and weaker gradient truncation. In such low‑density regimes the variance inflation observed on CIFAR‑10 does not appear; instead the variance ratio is inverted (<1). Thus, positive‑pair density determines whether the truncation pathway activates and whether variance is inflated or reduced.

### Task Difficulty Moderates the Effect
Task difficulty, approximated by final accuracy, further modulates the relationship. At very high accuracy (e.g., SVHN ~97%), all seeds converge to similar optima regardless of gradient perturbations, compressing variance. At very low accuracy (e.g., SVHN with aggressive augmentations ~25%), optimization is so unstable that variance is dominated by failure modes rather than subtle truncation differences. Only in an intermediate difficulty window (roughly 70–85% accuracy, as on CIFAR‑10 ~78%) can seed‑to‑seed separation driven by gradient truncation manifest as increased variance. This is evidenced by the SVHN difficulty sweep, where VR shifts from 0.25× (high accuracy) to 16.73× (low accuracy) as accuracy decreases, with the transition occurring around the intermediate regime.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
ViT (L=8, d=128, H=4, 4×4 patches → 64 tokens) → For each layer ℓ: representation extraction z̄_ℓ → Supervised contrastive loss L_ℓ with margin m_ℓ (clamp or subtract) → Gradients update Θ_ℓ only (stop‑gradient) → After 600 epochs, freeze all layers → Linear probe on frozen representations (50 epochs) → Test accuracy

### Critical Path
1. Forward pass through all layers to obtain normalized mean‑pooled token representations `z̄_i,ℓ`.  
2. For each layer ℓ, compute pairwise cosine similarities `s_uv,ℓ` between positive and negative examples.  
3. Apply margin variant: clamp `min(s+m_ℓ, 1)` or subtract `log p - m_ℓ`.  
4. Compute per‑anchor log‑probabilities with temperature τ=0.15 and a numerical stability shift.  
5. Backpropagate `L_ℓ` only through layer ℓ’s parameters (stop‑gradients block inter‑layer flow).  
6. After Stage 1, freeze all layers and train a linear probe; select best validation checkpoint for test evaluation.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Margin implementation:** Clamp risks gradient truncation; subtraction is gradient‑neutral but may be less effective in non‑saturated regimes. Mean accuracy was identical on CIFAR‑10, suggesting subtraction is a safe alternative.  
- **Temperature‑margin coupling:** Since logits are `(s+m)/τ`, the effective shift is `m/τ`. Higher margins or lower temperatures increase saturation risk.  
- **Augmentation aggressiveness:** More aggressive cropping/jitter increases task difficulty and alters pairwise similarity distributions, affecting CAR and consequently variance.

### Failure Signatures
- **High variance + high CAR:** Indicates saturation‑driven truncation (CIFAR‑10 pattern). Confirm L0 CAR >50% and intermediate accuracy (70–85%).  
- **Inverted variance (clamp lower variance):** Likely due to low positive‑pair density (many classes) or very high accuracy (SVHN, Fashion‑MNIST). Verify with CAR measurement and accuracy level.  
- **Bimodal seed performance under hard augmentation:** Suggests task difficulty pushed into a regime where some seeds fail catastrophically (observed in SVHN hard sweep).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** What are the exact thresholds of positive‑pair density and task difficulty that determine whether margin clamping inflates training variance in Contrastive Forward‑Forward learning?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred from cross‑dataset comparisons and an SVHN difficulty sweep; no quantitative model or precise boundaries provided.  
**Why unresolved:** Experimental coverage limited to four datasets with uncontrolled variations; interaction and transition points remain speculative.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments varying number of classes (to control positive‑pair density) and augmentation intensity across a wide range, measuring layer‑0 CAR and variance ratios to map a phase diagram.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How sensitive is the variance inflation effect to variations in Vision Transformer architecture (e.g., depth, width, number of heads)?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred; all experiments use a fixed architecture (d=128, H=4, L=8), which the paper notes as a limitation.  
**Why unresolved:** Model scale could amplify or attenuate the effect, but the single‑architecture design prevents generalization.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the CIFAR‑10 factorial with multiple depths (4, 12, 16), widths (64, 256), and head counts, measuring variance ratios and clamp activation rates.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** To what extent does the total seed‑to‑seed variance originate from the representation learning stage (Stage 1) versus the linear probe stage (Stage 2)?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred; the reported test accuracy combines both stages, and mechanistic analysis focused on Stage 1 diagnostics, but individual contributions are not separated.  
**Why unresolved:** The two‑stage pipeline conflates potential sources; understanding the dominant stage is crucial for targeted mitigation.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate variance of linear probe accuracy when using frozen representations trained with clamp vs. subtract, and separately compute variance of linear probe training itself (using identical representations) to disentangle the components.

## Limitations
- **Implementation details missing:** Critical aspects of the referenced Aghagolzadeh and Ezoji [2025] codebase (stop‑gradient placement, representation extraction `z̄`, layer‑wise margin schedule integration) are not fully specified, hampering faithful reproduction.  
- **Diagnostic protocols underspecified:** The handling of the numerical stability shift (`max(·)`) and precise methods for computing CAR and gradient norms are insufficiently detailed.  
- **Lack of directly related prior work:** Corpus analysis finds no previous studies on clamping‑induced variance in contrastive Forward‑Forward learning, limiting external validation of the mechanisms.
