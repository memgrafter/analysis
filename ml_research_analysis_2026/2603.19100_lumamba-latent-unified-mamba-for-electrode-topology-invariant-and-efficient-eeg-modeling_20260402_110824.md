# LuMamba: Latent Unified Mamba for Electrode Topology-Invariant and Efficient EEG Modeling

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.19100
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19100
*   **Reference Count:** 19
*   **Primary Result:** A 4.6M-parameter EEG foundation model achieving state-of-the-art Alzheimer’s detection (0.97 AUPR) and 80.99% balanced accuracy on TUAB, with 377× fewer FLOPs than Transformer baselines and robust cross-montage generalization.

## Executive Summary
LuMamba addresses two fundamental challenges in building foundation models for EEG: varying electrode montages that harm cross-dataset performance and the prohibitive quadratic complexity of Transformers for long sequences. It architecturally fuses LUNA’s channel-unifying cross-attention to achieve montage-robust spatial encoding with FEMBA’s bidirectional Mamba blocks for linear-complexity temporal modeling. Furthermore, it introduces the first adaptation of the LeJEPA self-supervised learning framework to biosignals. Pre-trained on over 21,000 hours of diverse EEG, its mixed reconstruction-LeJEPA objective yields representations that are both structured and isotropic, enabling state-of-the-art performance on multiple downstream benchmarks while scaling efficiently to very long sequences.

## Method Summary
The architecture processes variable-channel EEG inputs via a three-stage pipeline. First, a LUNA-style embedder creates per-channel token embeddings from patched signal windows, incorporating temporal, spectral, and 3D electrode positional information. Second, a channel unifier applies cross-attention with a fixed set of learned queries over the variable channel dimension, projecting any input montage into a consistent latent space. Third, a bidirectional Mamba (bi-Mamba) encoder models temporal dependencies in this unified representation with linear complexity. Pre-training uses a mixed objective: standard masked reconstruction (60% patches) combined with LeJEPA’s predictive loss and SIGReg (Epps–Pulley test) regularization on latent embeddings to encourage isotropy. For fine-tuning, the decoder is replaced with a lightweight classifier.

## Key Results
*   **Task Performance:** Achieves 0.97 AUPR on Alzheimer’s detection (APA VA) and 80.99% balanced accuracy on TUAB.
*   **Cross-Montage Robustness:** The mixed LeJEPA-reconstruction pre-training objective improves APA VA AD detection by over 20% AUPR compared to a reconstruction-only model.
*   **Efficiency:** Requires 377× fewer FLOPs than the LaBraM Transformer and can process sequences 12× longer before hitting memory limits, while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Topology-Invariant Encoding via Cross-Attention
*   **Claim:** The model maintains consistent performance when transferring between datasets with different electrode counts (16–26 channels) without retraining the channel unifier.
*   **Mechanism:** LUNA’s channel-unifying cross-attention uses a fixed number of learned query vectors to attend over the variable channel dimension (C). This projects any electrode configuration into a fixed-size latent representation (Q×E), decoupling the model's internal dimensionality from the input montage. 3D positional encodings based on electrode coordinates preserve essential spatial relationships.
*   **Core Assumption:** A fixed, learned query set can abstract away specific electrode positions while retaining sufficient spatial-semantic information for downstream clinical tasks.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   Abstract: "fuses LUNA's channel-unifying cross-attention... enabling topology-invariant EEG modeling across heterogeneous montages"
    *   Section III.C: Explicit description of the cross-attention module where `Q` learnable queries attend to the channel dimension `C` to produce `X_lat`.
    *   Corpus: Strong support from the original LUNA paper, which validated this mechanism for montage invariance.

### Mechanism 2: Linear-Complexity Temporal Modeling with Bidirectional Mamba
*   **Claim:** The model efficiently models long EEG sequences (linear FLOPs, 12× longer than Transformer baselines) without degradation in temporal representation quality.
*   **Mechanism:** The unified channel representation is processed by FEMBA’s bidirectional Mamba (bi-Mamba) blocks. State-space models (SSMs) maintain a constant-size hidden state per channel, allowing information propagation across the entire sequence with O(sequence_length) complexity, unlike the O(sequence_length²) of self-attention.
*   **Core Assumption:** Bidirectional SSMs can effectively capture EEG's non-stationary, long-range dependencies in both forward and backward temporal directions, matching the contextual capacity of bidirectional attention at a fraction of the computational cost.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   Abstract: "FEMBA's bidirectional Mamba blocks for efficient temporal modeling... requiring 377× fewer FLOPS"
    *   Section II: Contextualizes Mamba's selective state updates and explains why bidirectional variants are crucial for EEG.
    *   Corpus: Strong corroboration from other EEG Mamba works (FEMBA, SAMBA) demonstrating efficiency and performance parity with Transformers on temporal tasks.

### Mechanism 3: Regularized Joint Embeddings via Mixed LeJEPA-Reconstruction
*   **Claim:** Combining masked reconstruction with LeJEPA’s distributional regularization (SIGReg) yields latent representations that are both semantically structured (for in-distribution tasks) and isotropic (for cross-montage generalization).
*   **Mechanism:** The mixed objective sums a reconstruction loss (forcing detailed patch recovery) with LeJEPA’s losses: a predictive loss aligning local and global temporal views and SIGReg (Epps–Pulley test) that penalizes deviation from a multivariate Gaussian in projected latent space. Reconstruction provides fine-grained signal fidelity, while SIGReg smooths the latent manifold, reducing overfitting to spurious correlations with specific channel layouts.
*   **Core Assumption:** An isotropic latent space, encouraged by SIGReg, decouples the learned representations from montage-specific geometry, enhancing generalization to unseen electrode configurations.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   Abstract: Direct claim that the mixed objective "yields robust cross-montage generalization, improving APA VA AD detection by over 20% AUPR versus reconstruction-only."
    *   Section IV.A: Ablation showing reconstruction alone yields more clustered (but less generalizable) t-SNE plots, while the mixed objective yields more diffuse embeddings.
    *   Corpus: Weak; LeJEPA’s application to biosignals is novel, so evidence for this specific mechanism relies primarily on the paper’s own experiments.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Self-Supervised Learning for Time Series**
    *   *Why needed:* The model pre-trains on 21k+ hours of unlabeled EEG. Understanding how objectives like masked reconstruction and JEPA (predicting one view from another) extract temporal features without labels is fundamental to grasping its representation learning.
    *   *Quick check:* How does randomly masking patches in reconstruction differ from creating separate local/global views in JEPA for time series, and what distinct inductive biases does each objective impose on the learned features?
*   **State-Space Models (Mamba)**
    *   *Why needed:* The core temporal backbone is bi-Mamba. Grasping how continuous SSMs are discretized, how the input-dependent selection mechanism works, and why this yields linear complexity is key to understanding the efficiency claims.
    *   *Quick check:* Why does Mamba achieve O(L) complexity with respect to sequence length L, and how does its input-dependent selection mechanism differ from the fixed transition dynamics of a standard RNN?
*   **Cross-Attention for Variable-Channel Inputs**
    *   *Why needed:* LUNA’s channel unifier is the primary mechanism for topology invariance. Understanding how learned queries decouple the model's latent dimension from the input channel count (C) is central to handling montage heterogeneity.
    *   *Quick check:* If the input has C channels but the cross-attention uses Q learned queries (where Q << C), how does the model preserve necessary spatial information, and what specific role do the 3D positional encodings play in this process?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Raw EEG (B,C,T) -> Patching -> LUNA Embedder (patches+conv+FFT+3D coords) -> Channel Unifier (Cross-attention with Q queries) -> Flattened (B, Q*E, T_patch) -> FEMBA Encoder (2 bi-Mamba blocks) -> Pre-training: (a) Decoder (Cross-attention) -> Reconstruction Loss; (b) LeJEPA Heads (View sampling + Projections) -> Predictive Loss + SIGReg. Fine-tuning: Decoder replaced by Classifier.
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  **Pre-training:** Input signals are segmented into non-overlapping windows. The LUNA embedder creates a token per original channel per time patch. The channel unifier compresses the variable C dimension to a fixed Q using cross-attention. The resulting sequence of Q latent vectors per patch is fed through the bi-Mamba encoder. The pre-training loss is a weighted sum of the masked reconstruction loss (from the decoder) and the LeJEPA loss (predictive + SIGReg).
    2.  **Fine-tuning:** The decoder is removed. A lightweight classifier (often a single linear layer or a shallow Mamba-enhanced MLP) is attached to the final encoder output for the specific downstream task. The entire network is fine-tuned.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Efficiency vs. In-distribution Performance:* Reconstruction-only pre-training tends to yield better performance on datasets with montages identical to pre-training (e.g., TUH/TUAB) but poorer generalization to unseen montages (e.g., APA VA). The mixed LeJEPA-reconstruction objective trades a small potential gain on in-distribution tasks (~1-2%) for a large improvement in cross-montage robustness (+20% AUPR).
    *   *Parameter Budget:* The total parameter count is fixed at 4.6M. Key knobs are the number of channel queries (Q) in the unifier and the hidden state dimension of the Mamba blocks, which trade capacity against compute.
    *   *Pre-training Objective Mix:* The weighting (λ) between reconstruction and LeJEPA losses and the number of SIGReg projection slices (e.g., 60 vs. 300) are critical. More slices increase regularization strength, improving TUAB performance but potentially risking over-regularization on other tasks.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   *Cross-montage failure:* A sharp drop in performance on datasets with electrode layouts not represented in pre-training (e.g., using APA VA's 16 channels when pre-trained only on 20–22 channel TUEG) indicates the channel unifier or LeJEPA regularization failed to achieve true topology invariance.
    *   *Memory scalability breach:* Despite Mamba's theoretical O(L) memory, out-of-memory errors on long sequences (>5000 samples at 256Hz) suggest a bottleneck in the channel unifier's cross-attention over Q queries or in the bidirectional processing's activation accumulation.
    *   *Representation collapse:* t-SNE or PCA visualizations of pre-trained embeddings showing no discernible cluster structure (a homogeneous blob) suggests excessive SIGReg regularization or an imbalance in the mixed loss, collapsing all discriminative information.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Objective Ablation:** Train three model seeds with (a) reconstruction-only, (b) LeJEPA-only, and (c) mixed LeJEPA-reconstruction objectives on TUEG. Visualize final-layer embeddings (t-SNE) for TUAB (in-distribution) and APA VA (cross-montage) validation sets. Quantify cluster separation (e.g., silhouette score) and downstream AUPR/accuracy. Hypothesis: reconstruction will show tighter clusters but poorer APA VA; mixed will show more diffuse embeddings with superior APA VA.
    2.  **TUAB Benchmark Replication:** Fine-tune the mixed-objective pre-trained model on the official TUAB train split for 30 epochs using a learning rate of ~1e-4 (AdamW). Report balanced accuracy, AUROC, and AUPR on the test split, averaging over 3 seeds. Expect to replicate ~80.99% ±0.5% balanced accuracy.
    3.  **Long-Sequence Scalability Profiling:** For sequence lengths from 2^10 to 2^18 samples at 256Hz, measure peak GPU memory consumption and compute FLOPs (using thop or similar) for LuMamba (batch size 1). Plot against a Transformer baseline (e.g., LaBraM) and a prior Mamba variant (FEMBA). Confirm sub-quadratic memory growth and identify the sequence length where LuMamba OOMs, testing the "12× longer" claim relative to the Transformer baseline's limit.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** What is the optimal configuration of LeJEPA hyperparameters (e.g., SIGReg projection slices, local/global view sizes) for different downstream EEG tasks, given that the paper only presents a limited grid search on TUAB?
*   **Open Question 2:** What is the mechanistic reason behind the observed trade-off where reconstruction-only pre-training yields more clustered latent spaces but poorer cross-montage generalization, while LeJEPA produces isotropic embeddings with better generalization?
*   **Open Question 3:** Can the mixed LeJEPA-reconstruction objective be adaptively tuned (e.g., via loss weighting or scheduling) to overcome its current underperformance on highly class-imbalanced datasets like TUSL?
*   **Open Question 4:** Does LuMamba’s claimed topology-invariant encoding hold for extreme electrode configurations beyond the 16–26 channel range tested (e.g., single-channel or very high-density >128 channel EEG)?

## Limitations
*   **LeJEPA Adaptation Unproven:** The novel application of LeJEPA to biosignals lacks external validation; its inductive biases and regularization effects in the EEG domain are not fully understood.
*   **Hyperparameter Gaps:** Critical training details (exact learning rates, LeJEPA loss weight λ, view durations) are not specified, requiring reliance on defaults from related work and introducing reproducibility risk.
*   **Untested Scalability Boundary:** The claim of processing 12× longer sequences is based on theoretical FLOPs; actual memory usage patterns for extreme lengths, especially with bidirectional Mamba and cross-attention, remain empirically unverified.

## Confidence
*   **Topology-Invariant Encoding (High):** Relies on the LUNA mechanism, which is a published, peer-reviewed component with demonstrated montage robustness.
*   **Linear-Complexity Temporal Modeling (High):** Based on the well-established Mamba architecture and its proven O(L) complexity, further supported by other EEG Mamba implementations.
*   **Mixed Pre-Training Objective Efficacy (Medium):** Evidence for the synergy between reconstruction and LeJEPA (SIGReg) for cross-montage generalization is primarily internal to this paper. The claim would be strengthened by independent replication or theoretical analysis.
