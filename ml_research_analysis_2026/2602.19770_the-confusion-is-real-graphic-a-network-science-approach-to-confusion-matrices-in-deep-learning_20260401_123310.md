# The Confusion is Real: GRAPHIC - A Network Science Approach to Confusion Matrices in Deep Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.19770
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19770
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GRAPHIC reveals increasing semantic modularity (~0.25→>0.7), detects dataset biases (maple/oak color bias, boy/girl label ambiguity), and uncovers divergent linear separability trends (ResNet=monotonic, EffViT=non-monotonic) during training.

## Executive Summary
Understanding how deep neural networks confuse classes during training remains a challenge, with most analyses limited to aggregate accuracy or post-hoc confusion matrices. GRAPHIC addresses this by training linear classifiers on frozen intermediate features to produce per-layer confusion matrices, which are then interpreted as directed graphs using network science tools. Applied to ResNet-50 and EffViT on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet, the method shows that semantic communities (e.g., animals, trees) emerge with modularity rising from ~0.25 to >0.7, uncovers dataset biases such as seasonal tree color confusion and human class labeling ambiguity, and reveals architectural differences: ResNet’s linear separability improves monotonically with depth, while EffViT’s early decoders show a rise-and-fall pattern.

These findings demonstrate that a systematic, graph-based approach can move beyond scalar metrics to diagnose model behavior, audit datasets, and compare learning trajectories across architectures—all with minimal overhead relative to standard linear probes.

## Method Summary
GRAPHIC operates on a trained or in-training deep network. At selected epochs and layers, feature activations are extracted for a held-out subset of data. A linear classifier (LC) is trained on these features using a hybrid loss that mixes ground-truth labels and the network’s own predictions via a coefficient λ. λ=0 captures the network’s current internal confusion state; λ=1 measures the maximum achievable linear separability. The trained LC produces per-sample probability distributions over classes, which are aggregated into a normalized confusion matrix (CM). Off-diagonal entries become directed edges from true to predicted classes, weighted by confusion probability, forming a directed graph. Community detection (Directed Louvain) partitions the graph into confusion communities, and metrics such as modularity and assortativity quantify the strength of semantic grouping. Repeating this process across layers and epochs yields detailed trajectories of confusion structure, enabling comparisons of learning dynamics.

## Key Results
- Semantic groupings (e.g., animals, trees) emerge during training, with modularity increasing from ~0.25 to >0.7.
- Dataset biases are uncovered: a seasonal color bias in tree classification (maple/oak confusion) and labeling ambiguity in human classes (boy/girl/baby), validated by a human study (83% repeat labeling accuracy).
- Divergent linear separability trends: ResNet-50 shows monotonic improvement (layer accuracy rising from ~25% to ~70%), while EffViT’s early decoders initially increase then decline, indicating architectural differences in learning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Linear classifiers (LCs) trained on intermediate feature representations can systematically generate class-level confusion matrices (CMs) that reflect both feature space linear separability and the network's current prediction behavior.
- **Mechanism:** For a given intermediate layer’s activations, an LC is trained with a hybrid loss combining ground-truth labels (λ=1) for separability analysis and model-predicted labels (λ=0) for capturing the network's internal decision state. The trained LC outputs probability distributions over classes for held-out feature vectors; aggregating these predictions into a normalized CM reveals per-class error patterns. This transforms continuous, high-dimensional feature vectors into a discrete, class-level error structure.
- **Core assumption:** The feature representations at a given layer contain sufficient linear signal about class membership for an LC to achieve meaningful accuracy, and the model’s predictions (when λ=0) faithfully represent its internal class confusions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "It leverages confusion matrices derived from intermediate layers using linear classifiers."
  - [Section 4.2] Details the LC training loss function (Eq. 4) and the two boundary cases (λ=0, λ=1) for different analytical goals.
  - [corpus] The neighborhood search retrieved papers on XAI and confusion analysis, but none specifically use linear probes on intermediate layers to generate directed confusion graphs for network science analysis, indicating novelty.
- **Break condition:** If the LC fails to converge to accuracy significantly above random chance (e.g., <10% on a 100-class problem) for a given layer, the feature space may be too entangled or the LC underparameterized, invalidating the CM as a meaningful representation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Interpreting normalized CMs as adjacency matrices of directed weighted graphs enables the application of network science tools (e.g., modularity maximization, assortativity) to quantify semantic class relationships and their evolution during training.
- **Mechanism:** Off-diagonal entries in the CM (errors) become directed, weighted edges (from true class to predicted class). Community detection (e.g., via directed modularity optimization) then partitions the graph into confusion communities (CCs). Metrics like modularity (Q) quantify the strength of these groupings, while assortativity (r) tests alignment with external group labels (e.g., natural vs. man-made).
- **Core assumption:** The directed graph structure derived from class confusions meaningfully captures the semantic or perceptual similarities learned by the network, and community detection algorithms can recover these groupings from the topology.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We interpret these as adjacency matrices of directed graphs, allowing tools from network science to visualize and quantify learning dynamics."
  - [Section 4.1] Defines the directed graph formalism and formally introduces modularity (Eq. 3) and assortativity (Eq. 2) as analysis metrics.
  - [Section 5.2] Shows empirical results where modularity increases with training and CCs align with semantic groups (e.g., animals, trees).
- **Break condition:** If the graph is extremely dense (high connectivity) or extremely sparse (few edges), community detection may yield unstable or trivial partitions (e.g., all nodes in one community or isolated nodes), making modularity uninterpretable.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Tracking network science metrics (modularity, assortativity) and graph topology across training epochs and network layers reveals architectural learning differences (e.g., CNN vs. ViT) and latent dataset biases.
- **Mechanism:** By repeating the LC probing and graph construction at multiple checkpoints (epochs) and layers, temporal and depth-wise trends in CC structure emerge. For example, increasing modularity indicates refining class groupings; diverging linear separability trends (LC accuracy) across architectures indicate different learning trajectories.
- **Core assumption:** Changes in graph metrics over time/space directly correspond to meaningful changes in the network’s internal representation learning, not just noise from LC training or data sampling.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 5.2] "As training progresses, semantic groupings like animals or trees begin to form, with clearer, more refined communities and reduced intergroup confusion at convergence."
  - [Section 5.4] Shows ResNet-50’s monotonic separability increase vs. EffVit’s early-decoder non-monotonic trend, suggesting architectural differences.
  - [Section 5.3] Demonstrates how persistent strong edges (e.g., maple↔oak, flatfish↔man) reveal dataset biases validated by human study and color manipulation.
- **Break condition:** If LC training is unstable (high variance across seeds) or metrics do not change systematically with known factors (e.g., accuracy improvement), the graph signals may be dominated by methodological noise rather than learning dynamics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Linear Classification Probes**
  - *Why needed here:* GRAPHIC relies on training simple LCs on frozen intermediate features to extract class-discriminative structure without altering the main network. Understanding probe training, loss functions, and accuracy interpretation is essential to generate valid CMs.
  - *Quick check question:* If an LC trained on a middle layer reaches 90% accuracy on held-out data, does that mean the network is 90% accurate? (Answer: No—it means the features are *linearly separable* to that degree; the network's final accuracy may differ due to its nonlinear classifier.)
- **Concept: Graph-Based Community Detection**
  - *Why needed here:* Converting CMs to graphs and applying modularity optimization is the core analysis step. One must understand directed graphs, modularity maximization, and how edge weights influence community boundaries.
  - *Quick check question:* Would an undirected graph constructed from a CM (averaging reciprocal confusion) hide asymmetric relationships like A→B strong but B→A weak? (Answer: Yes—directionality is lost, potentially obscuring important biases.)
- **Concept: Confusion Matrix Semantics**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire method interprets class-level error patterns. Recognizing that row sums represent true class frequencies and column sums represent predicted frequencies, and that off-diagonal strength indicates confusion, is foundational.
  - *Quick check question:* If a CM has high values only on the diagonal, what does that imply about the graph? (Answer: Very sparse graph with few edges, indicating high class separability and weak/no CCs.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Main model:** Pretrained or in-training CNN/Transformer (e.g., ResNet-50, EffVit).
  2. **Feature extractor:** Hooks/snapshots at desired intermediate layers/blocks.
  3. **Linear probe module:** Single linear layer (+softmax) trained on extracted features.
  4. **CM generator:** Aggregates probe predictions on held-out data into normalized confusion matrices.
  5. **Graph constructor:** Converts CM (minus identity) to directed weighted adjacency matrix.
  6. **Network analyzer:** Computes modularity (via directed Louvain), assortativity (vs. external groups), and visualizes graphs.
  7. **Temporal manager:** Coordinates probing at specified epochs/layers.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Train/freeze main model to desired epoch.
  2. For each target layer: extract features for train/validation splits.
  3. Train LC on 80% of training features (using λ=0 or 1) until convergence (monitor validation loss).
  4. Generate CM on remaining 20% training + full validation set.
  5. Build directed graph from CM, run community detection, compute metrics.
  6. Repeat across layers/epochs; aggregate results (e.g., modularity heatmap, CC evolution).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **λ selection:** λ=0 captures model's *current* confusion state; λ=1 measures maximum linear separability. λ∈(0,1) blends both but may be less interpretable.
  - **Layer selection:** More layers = finer-grained insight but higher compute. Early layers show coarse/semantic groupings; later layers show refined, task-specific confusions.
  - **Epoch frequency:** Dense probing in early rapid-learning phases vs. sparse in late convergence. Use main model accuracy as proxy: probe more frequently when accuracy changes quickly.
  - **Graph sparsity:** For very large class counts (>200), visualizations become cluttered; consider plotting only top-k edges per node or analyzing individual CCs.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - LC accuracy near random chance (~1% for 100 classes) → features not linearly separable; skip layer or reduce LC capacity?
  - Modularity <0.2 consistently → no meaningful community structure; check if dataset has inherent semantic groupings or if LC training failed.
  - Extreme sparsity in graphs (many zero edges) with high main model accuracy → model is highly confident; CCs may not be informative.
  - High variance in LC metrics across random seeds → LC training unstable; adjust learning rate, increase LC capacity, or add regularization.
  - Assortativity negative or near zero for meaningful external groups → network does not align with that semantic distinction.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline separation test:** On a converged model, train LCs (λ=1) on final layer. Expected: LC accuracy ≈ model accuracy. If much lower, features may be non-linearly separable.
  2. **Depth-wise separability trend:** Train LCs (λ=1) on all layers at final epoch. Expected: Accuracy increases with depth for CNNs (ResNet). If ViT shows drop in early decoders, confirms paper's observation.
  3. **Dataset bias detection:** At final epoch, visualize graphs for layers 2–4. Look for strong asymmetric edges (e.g., A→B >> B→A) and dense CCs containing semantically ambiguous classes (e.g., boy/girl/baby). If found, inspect raw images for labeling or context artifacts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the computational overhead of GRAPHIC be reduced?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Sec. 6 identifies overhead as a limitation and proposes integrating graph construction into training.
- **Why unresolved:** The proposal (e.g., reusing LCs) lacks implementation and evaluation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Build an integrated version sharing LC parameters across epochs, compare runtime and graph quality to baseline.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can confusion graph interpretation be automated to detect dataset biases?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Sec. 6 says manual interpretation is needed to understand confusions.
- **Why unresolved:** No automated criteria; current detection is manual ad‑hoc.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Define metrics for anomalous edges/communities, validate on known biases (maple/oak, human classes).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why do early decoders of EffViT lose linear separability, unlike CNNs?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Sec. 5.4 shows rise‑and‑fall pattern in EffViT's early decoders.
- **Why unresolved:** The speculated link to attention patterns lacks evidence; generality unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Track attention locality/globality and feature norms during training; test across transformer variants.

## Limitations
- Reproducibility gaps: exact layer selection and linear probe hyperparameters are not fully specified, risking implementation drift.
- Generalizability limited to two architectures (ResNet-50, EffViT) and two datasets (CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet); unclear if trends hold for other models or domains.
- Methodological assumptions lack direct validation: λ=0 LC predictions may not perfectly match the network's empirical confusion matrix; break condition not systematically tested.

## Confidence
- LCs generate meaningful CMs: Medium.
- Graph interpretation reveals semantics: Medium-High.
- Architectural learning differences: Low-Medium.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** For a fixed model/dataset (e.g., ResNet-50 on CIFAR-100), vary LC learning rates (e.g., 1e-4 to 1e-2) and layer sampling density. Check if modularity trends and the ResNet/EffVit divergence pattern persist. Failure mode: trends vanish with small LR changes.
2. **Cross-architecture replication:** Apply GRAPHIC to at least two additional architectures (e.g., VGG-19, Swin Transformer) on CIFAR-100/Tiny ImageNet. Specifically test whether ViT-style models consistently show non-monotonic LC accuracy in early decoders. Failure mode: divergence only appears in EffVit due to its specific training setup.
3. **Direct confusion-state validation:** Compare λ=0 LC predictions against the main network's *actual* validation predictions (not just final accuracy). Compute agreement rates per class and epoch. If λ=0 CMs strongly correlate (ρ>0.8) with the network's empirical CM, the method's core assumption holds. Failure mode: low correlation, especially in early layers, indicating LC introduces artifacts.
