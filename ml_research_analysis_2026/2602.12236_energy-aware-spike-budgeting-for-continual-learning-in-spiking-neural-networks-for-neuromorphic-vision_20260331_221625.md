# Energy-Aware Spike Budgeting for Continual Learning in Spiking Neural Networks for Neuromorphic Vision

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12236
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12236
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Integrates experience replay, learnable LIF neuron parameters, and an adaptive spike scheduler; reduces spike rates by up to 47% on frame datasets while improving accuracy by up to 17.45 percentage points on event datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles catastrophic forgetting in spiking neural networks (SNNs) for neuromorphic vision by proposing an energy-aware spike budgeting framework. The method combines three core components: an experience replay buffer to retain past knowledge, learnable leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neuron parameters to adapt to diverse temporal dynamics, and an adaptive spike scheduler with proportional control to enforce dataset-specific energy constraints during training. Evaluated on five benchmarks—frame-based (MNIST, CIFAR-10) and event-based (DVS-Gesture, N-MNIST, CIFAR-10-DVS)—the framework consistently optimizes the accuracy-energy trade-off, cutting spike rates by up to 47% on dense inputs while boosting accuracy by up to 17.45 percentage points on sparse event streams.

## Method Summary
The framework integrates three mechanisms into a unified training pipeline. First, a fixed-size episodic memory buffer stores class-balanced samples from previous tasks; minibatches mix current-task data with random replay samples to mitigate forgetting by stabilizing the optimization objective. Second, the membrane decay (β) and firing threshold (V_thr) of LIF neurons are made learnable per layer, allowing the network to tune its temporal integration window to match input statistics without parameter overhead. Third, an adaptive spike scheduler treats energy as a first-class objective: a proportional controller updates a regularization coefficient λ_rate based on the error between measured mini-batch spike rate and a dataset-specific target r_target, applying a squared penalty in the loss. This bidirectional modulation enforces sparsity on dense frame data while relaxing constraints on sparse event data, achieving modality-adaptive energy control.

## Key Results
- On frame-based datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10), the method reduces spike rates by up to 47% while improving accuracy (MNIST: 93.44%→95.75%).
- On event-based datasets (DVS-Gesture, N-MNIST, CIFAR-10-DVS), controlled budget relaxation yields accuracy gains up to 17.45 percentage points (DVS-Gesture: 74.48%→91.93%) with minimal spike increase.
- Across all five benchmarks, the framework consistently enhances the accuracy-energy trade-off, advancing practical neuromorphic continual learning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Experience Replay
- **Claim:** Experience replay mitigates catastrophic forgetting by exposing the network to a mixed distribution of past and current task data during training.
- **Mechanism:** A fixed-size episodic memory buffer stores a class-balanced subset of samples from previously learned tasks. During training on a new task, minibatches are constructed by concatenating current-task samples with random samples from this buffer. This stabilizes the optimization objective, approximating the joint distribution of all seen tasks and reducing interference between new learning and old knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** Random replay samples are sufficiently representative of past task distributions to maintain performance without requiring generative models or complex consolidation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...integrates experience replay..." [section 3.4.2] "We maintain a fixed-size episodic memory buffer M that stores a class-balanced subset of samples from previous tasks..." [section 4.3] "Experience replay [11] is essential, eliminating catastrophic forgetting [6] and establishing a strong 93.44% baseline."
- **Break condition:** If the replay buffer size is too small relative to task complexity or number of tasks, representative coverage degrades, leading to increased forgetting. High intra-class variance (e.g., CIFAR-10) requires larger buffers than simpler datasets (MNIST).

### Mechanism 2: Learnable LIF Neuron Parameters
- **Claim:** Learnable LIF neuron parameters (membrane decay β and firing threshold V_thr) enhance the network's ability to adapt to diverse temporal dynamics across modalities without additional parameter overhead.
- **Mechanism:** Standard LIF neurons use fixed decay and threshold values. By making β and V_thr learnable scalars per layer (O(L) parameters), the network can tune its temporal integration window and sensitivity to match input statistics—slower dynamics for static frames, faster responses for event streams—improving feature discrimination.
- **Core assumption:** A single set of fixed LIF parameters is suboptimal for multi-modal continual learning (frame vs. event data); adaptive parameters can compensate for constraints imposed by the spike scheduler.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...learnable leaky integrate-and-fire neuron parameters..." [section 3.3] "To enhance the network’s ability to capture diverse temporal dynamics... we make the membrane decay parameter β and the firing threshold V_thr learnable." [section 4.3] "Learnable LIF parameters (C2) provide a modest accuracy gain (+0.81 percentage points) without energy cost..." [section 4.2] "Learnable neuron dynamics act as a neutral regularizer... adaptive temporal parameters are most valuable for highly dynamic inputs (DVS streams)."
- **Break condition:** If learning rates for β/V_thr are too high, unstable training may occur. On datasets with very low temporal complexity, learnable parameters may confer negligible benefit or slight overfitting without the scheduler's regularization.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Spike Scheduler
- **Claim:** The adaptive spike scheduler (proportional controller) modulates network activity via a regularization term, treating energy as a first-class optimization objective and enabling modality-adaptive behavior.
- **Mechanism:** A clipped proportional controller updates a regularization coefficient λ_rate based on the error between the measured mini-batch spike rate (r_spike) and a dataset-specific target (r_target): Δλ_rate = η·(r_spike - r_target). This λ_rate weights a squared penalty (r_spike - r_target)² in the total loss. If r_spike > r_target, λ_rate increases, strengthening sparsity pressure; if r_spike < r_target, λ_rate decreases, relaxing the constraint to allow increased activity for temporal feature extraction.
- **Core assumption:** Spike rate is a valid proxy for dynamic energy consumption in neuromorphic hardware (synaptic operations dominate power), and a simple proportional controller can stably regulate activity across diverse datasets without per-dataset retuning of control law structure.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...adaptive spike scheduler with proportional control to enforce dataset-specific energy constraints..." [section 3.2] "We formulate spike budgeting as a feedback control problem... ∆λ_rate = η·(r_spike - r_target)..." [section 4.2] "On frame-based datasets... spike budgeting acts as implicit regularization... On event-based datasets... controlled budget relaxation enables substantial accuracy gains..." [section A.4] "We use r_target = 5–12% for frame-based datasets, and r_target = 0.5–3% for DVS datasets..."
- **Break condition:** Poorly chosen r_target (too low) can silence useful neurons on event data, harming accuracy; too high erodes energy benefits. Controller gain η must be tuned per modality; aggressive η can cause oscillations. The scheduler relies on replay mixing old/new data to measure a representative r_spike.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Replay Buffer -> Mixed Minibatch Sampler -> Spiking Backbone (LIF layers with learnable β/V_thr) -> Spike Rate Monitor -> Scheduler (λ_rate) -> Loss Function (L_task + λ_rate*(r_spike - r_target)^2)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize replay buffer, model (with/without learnable LIF), scheduler (λ_rate=0), set r_target.
  2. For each task in sequence: train for N epochs.
  3. Per training step: Sample minibatch = [current_task_samples ∪ replay_buffer_samples]. Forward pass → compute task loss & r_spike. Compute L_total. Backward pass → update weights & learnable β/V_thr.
  4. Post-optimizer-step: Update scheduler: λ_rate ← clip(λ_rate + η*(r_spike - r_target), [λ_min, λ_max]). (Do not backprop through λ_rate).
  5. Post-task: Update replay buffer with new task samples (class-balanced subset).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Replay buffer size vs. Memory:** Larger buffers improve forgetting mitigation but increase memory footprint. Paper uses fixed per-dataset budgets.
  - **r_target tuning:** Must be set per modality (frame: 5-12%, DVS: 0.5-3%). A single value for all datasets fails due to inherent density differences.
  - **Controller hyperparameters (η, λ_max):** Tuned per modality, not per dataset. Too high η causes instability; too low λ_max weakens enforcement. Paper uses η=0.1-0.3, λ_max=5.
  - **Learnable LIF overhead:** Near-zero parameter cost (O(L)), but may require learning rate scheduling to stabilize.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Diverging λ_rate or oscillating spike rate:** Controller gain η too high, or r_target infeasible.
  2. **Severe forgetting despite replay:** Replay buffer too small, or replay sampling frequency insufficient.
  3. **Accuracy collapse on DVS datasets:** r_target set too high or λ_max too restrictive, preventing necessary activation for temporal features.
  4. **No energy improvement on frame data:** r_target set too high (above natural C1 spike rate), so penalty never activates.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline sanity check:** Train C0 (naive) and C1 (replay only) on MNIST (frame). Expect C0 catastrophic forgetting (~20% final ACC), C1 high ACC (~93%) with fixed spike rate (~15%). Validates replay buffer and basic training pipeline.
  2. **Modality verification:** Train C1 (replay only) on DVS-Gesture and MNIST. Measure baseline spike rates. Expect DVS-Gesture << 1%, MNIST ~15%. Confirms fundamental density difference.
  3. **Scheduler isolation test:** On MNIST, train C3 (replay + scheduler only). Expect ACC to drop slightly and spike rate to decrease significantly (e.g., 15% → 10%). Isolates scheduler's sparsity effect.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** To what extent does the spike-rate proxy accurately predict actual energy consumption on neuromorphic hardware like Loihi or TrueNorth? *Basis:* Authors state they plan to deploy on hardware to verify power savings, implying the proxy's validity is unverified. *Why unresolved:* Real hardware incurs additional costs (routing, leakage, analog effects) that may decouple spike rate from power. *Resolution evidence:* Empirically measure power on neuromorphic chips and correlate with spike rates across benchmarks.
- **Open Question 2:** How can the spike scheduler's hyperparameters (η, λ_min, λ_max) be automatically tuned for task-free or open-world continual learning streams without manual per-modality calibration? *Basis:* Discussion notes reliance on hand-tuned gains per modality and proposes automatic tuning for task-free streams. *Why unresolved:* Manual tuning limits deployment in dynamic environments with unknown task boundaries. *Resolution evidence:* Develop an online hyperparameter adaptation algorithm and evaluate on class-incremental and task-free protocols.
- **Open Question 3:** Can generative replay mechanisms (e.g., VAEs, GANs) replace the experience replay buffer to reduce memory overhead while preserving accuracy-energy trade-offs? *Basis:* Conclusion plans to pair scheduler with lightweight generative replay to reduce memory footprints. *Why unresolved:* Generative replay may alter spike statistics or forgetting behavior due to model imperfections. *Resolution evidence:* Replace episodic buffer with generative model, compare CL metrics and spike rates against original baseline, measuring memory savings and trade-off degradation.
- **Open Question 4:** Is the proportional feedback controller the optimal control strategy, or could more advanced controllers (e.g., PID, model-predictive, learned) further improve the trade-off? *Basis:* Method uses simple proportional controller without comparing alternatives; ablation focuses on component inclusion, not controller design. *Why unresolved:* Current controller may be suboptimal for balancing stability and responsiveness across varying temporal dynamics. *Resolution evidence:* Replace with PID/adaptive variants, test on all benchmarks, measure sensitivity, convergence stability, and final metrics vs. proportional baseline.

## Limitations
- **Energy-proxy validity:** Spike rate assumes dynamic power dominated by synaptic events, but real hardware includes routing, leakage, analog effects that may decouple spike rate from actual energy.
- **Controller robustness:** Proportional scheduler stability and modality adaptation rely on tuned hyperparameters per modality; generalization to unseen datasets or hardware with different energy-spike relationships is uncertain.
- **Replay buffer representativeness:** Class-balanced random sampling may fail for high-variance classes or significant distribution shifts with small buffers; does not address correlated forgetting.

## Confidence
- **Replay mitigates forgetting:** High (well-established, clear ablation)
- **Learnable LIF improves accuracy neutrally:** Medium (modest gains, context-dependent, not rigorously verified on spike statistics)
- **Adaptive scheduler optimizes accuracy-energy trade-off:** Medium (strong empirical results, but balance partly confounded by fixed r_target)
- **Overall method advances practical neuromorphic CL:** Medium (consistent benefits across benchmarks, but hardware deployment implications unvalidated)
