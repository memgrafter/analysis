# Detecting low left ventricular ejection fraction from ECG using an interpretable and scalable predictor-driven framework

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.28532
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28532
- **Reference count**: 12
- **Primary result**: An interpretable framework (ECGPD-LEF) using foundation model-derived diagnostic probabilities and XGBoost achieves AUROC 88.4% (internal) and 86.8% (external) for low LVEF detection, outperforming an end-to-end deep learning model.

## Executive Summary
This study introduces ECGPD-LEF, a scalable and interpretable system for detecting low left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF ≤45%) from 12-lead electrocardiograms (ECG). The framework leverages a pre-trained ECG foundation model (ST-MEM) to extract 71 structured diagnostic probability predictors from raw ECG waveforms. These predictors serve as features for a lightweight XGBoost classifier, bypassing the need for commercial ECG measurements and avoiding black-box deep learning. Trained on 72,475 ECG-echocardiogram pairs and validated on large internal (n=5,442) and external (n=16,017) cohorts, the optimal configuration (Transformer-PT-XGBoost) demonstrates strong performance (AUROC 88.4% internal, 86.8% external) and consistent superiority over an end-to-end CNN baseline across demographic and clinical subgroups. The approach offers inherent interpretability via SHAP explanations, highlighting clinically relevant predictors such as normal ECG and incomplete left bundle branch block. Zero-shot analyses confirm that LVEF information is intrinsically encoded in the structured predictor representations, enabling single-predictor detection with AUROC up to 81.0%.

## Method Summary
The proposed pipeline consists of three main stages. First, a transformer-based ECG foundation model (ST-MEM) is fine-tuned on the PTB-XL dataset to predict 71 standard ECG diagnoses, outputting continuous probability scores for each. Second, these 71 probabilities become features for a tabular classifier (XGBoost) trained on paired ECG and echocardiography data to predict binary low LVEF status. Third, SHAP values are computed to explain individual predictions in terms of the diagnostic predictors. The system is designed to be vendor-agnostic and interpretable, contrasting with end-to-end deep learning models that operate directly on raw signals. Ablation studies compare different foundation models (Transformer vs. CNN) and tabular heads (XGBoost vs. logistic regression), and single-predictor zero-shot evaluations assess the intrinsic LVEF signal in each diagnostic probability.

## Key Results
- Transformer-PT-XGBoost achieves AUROC of 88.4% on internal validation and 86.8% on external validation, with F1 scores of 64.5% and 53.6% respectively.
- The framework outperforms the end-to-end Columbia mini model across all demographic and clinical subgroups.
- Single-predictor zero-shot inference reaches AUROC up to 81.0% (NORM predictor), indicating LVEF information is encoded in structured predictors even without joint training.
- Top interpretable predictors include normal ECG (NORM), incomplete left bundle branch block (ILBBB), and subendocardial injury (INJAL).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Structured predictor probabilities as standardized features
A pre-trained ECG foundation model (ST-MEM) converts raw 12-lead ECG into 71 continuous probability scores corresponding to clinically defined ECG diagnoses. These probabilities act as vendor-agnostic features that encode both normal and abnormal patterns associated with LV dysfunction. A gradient-boosted tree model (XGBoost) then learns nonlinear combinations of these scores to predict low LVEF. The core assumption is that the foundation model's latent space captures subclinical ECG alterations indicative of LV dysfunction, reflected even in sub-threshold predictor values. Evidence: The predictor extractor achieves macro AUROC 94.5% on PTB-XL; single-predictor zero-shot AUROCs reach up to 81%, showing LVEF information is present in individual predictors. Failure condition: If the foundation model's pre-training lacks LV dysfunction patterns, predictor probabilities will not correlate with LVEF.

### Mechanism 2: Ensemble across distributed diagnostic dimensions
LVEF reduction manifests as a constellation of subtle, often sub-threshold ECG changes across multiple diagnostic categories. XGBoost integrates signals from all 71 predictors, assigning weights via SHAP. Global SHAP analysis shows performance improves with more predictors, and top predictors align with known cardiac remodeling patterns. This ensemble captures synergistic effects (e.g., mild ST changes plus conduction abnormalities) that individually are weak but collectively robust. Evidence: Transformer-PT-XGBoost outperforms all ablations with fewer predictors; logistic regression underperforms XGBoost, indicating nonlinear interactions are essential. Failure condition: If predictors are highly correlated or omit critical patterns, ensemble gains may saturate or fail.

### Mechanism 3: Clinically plausible SHAP explanations
For each prediction, Tree SHAP computes each predictor's contribution to the log-odds. Local explanations show that high-impact predictors (e.g., NORM, ILBBB) often have values near or above their diagnostic thresholds in positive cases, making the reasoning interpretable to cardiologists. Global SHAP dependence plots reveal that even predictor values far below diagnostic thresholds (e.g., ILBBB probability 0.0003) can shift predictions, indicating the model uses graded deviations. The core assumption is that cardiologists can understand model outputs when features correspond to known ECG diagnoses and SHAP signs align with clinical intuition (e.g., higher NORM reduces LEF risk). Evidence: Local explanations in positive cases show NORM and ILBBB values exceeding LEF-positive thresholds; global SHAP beeswarm aligns with top single-predictor F1 rankings. Failure condition: SHAP instability or non-physiological feature contributions (e.g., inverse signs) would collapse clinical plausibility.

## Foundational Learning
- **Transfer Learning with Foundation Models**: Pre-training on large unlabeled ECG corpora (e.g., Chapman, Ningbo, CODE-15%) provides a robust feature extractor, overcoming insufficient labeled ECG-echocardiogram pairs (72k). Quick check: If the foundation model were randomly initialized, single-predictor zero-shot AUROCs would not reach ~75-81%.
- **Tabular Ensemble Methods for Structured Data**: The 71 predictor probabilities form a low-dimensional, mixed-signal feature set ideal for gradient boosting, which handles non-linearities and sparsity better than linear models and offers interpretability via SHAP. Quick check: Why not use a shallow neural net? (Potentially less interpretable; not tested in paper.)
- **Game-Theoretic Feature Attribution (SHAP)**: SHAP provides consistent, additive explanations for tree models, bridging black-box performance and clinician trust by expressing predictions in terms of clinically meaningful predictors. Quick check: Could LIME be used instead? (Yes, but SHAP's consistency properties are preferred; not experimentally compared.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Raw ECG waveform -> ST-MEM (Predictor Extractor) -> 71 diagnostic probabilities -> XGBoost (Tabular Classifier) -> LEF prediction. SHAP (Explainer) uses XGBoost and the 71 probabilities to generate feature attributions. Single-Predictor Module thresholds individual probabilities independently.

**Critical path**:
1. Preprocess raw ECG to 10 s, 12‑lead, resampled to 500 Hz.
2. Run fine‑tuned ST‑MEM to obtain 71 probabilities.
3. Feed probabilities to trained XGBoost model to get LEF risk score.
4. (Optional) Compute SHAP values for explanation.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Accuracy vs. Interpretability: XGBoost+SHAP offers better interpretability than end‑to‑end CNN; surprisingly, it also outperforms the CNN baseline, challenging the typical trade‑off.
- Scalability vs. Granularity: 71 predictors are computationally efficient but may omit subtle patterns; expanding predictor set could improve performance but increase complexity.
- Generalization vs. Calibration: Foundation model outputs are probabilities; if poorly calibrated, downstream performance suffers. Paper does not report predictor calibration metrics.

**Failure signatures**:
- Predictor Extractor failure: PTB‑XL test AUROC macro <90% → unreliable features.
- Tabular model failure: XGBoost performance plateaus or degrades with >~30 predictors (Figure 2c‑e) or logistic regression matches XGBoost → nonlinear interactions may be unnecessary.
- Explanation failure: SHAP shows high contributions from rare/incoherent predictors or signs opposite to clinical expectation.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Validate predictor extractor on PTB‑XL test set; macro AUROC should be ≳94.5%.
2. Benchmark single‑predictor zero‑shot AUROC on hold‑out; top predictors should exceed ~75%.
3. Compare XGBoost vs. logistic regression on same 71 predictors; if AUROC gain <2%, linear model may suffice.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Inter‑observer variability in echocardiogram interpretation**: Labels derived from ECHO reports may be inconsistent, affecting training and evaluation. Further validation with consensus readings or quantitative methods is needed.
2. **International generalizability**: Validation cohorts are primarily US‑based; performance in diverse ethnic, demographic, and healthcare system settings remains unknown.
3. **Impact of scaling up pretraining**: The predictor extractor was pretrained on moderately sized datasets; larger high‑quality ECG corpora could enhance feature representation and LEF detection performance.
4. **Prospective clinical validation**: Retrospective results need confirmation in real‑world screening programs to assess impact on outcomes, workflow integration, and cost‑effectiveness.

## Limitations
- **Causal mechanism uncertainty**: Zero‑shot evidence that LVEF is encoded in predictors is indirect; confounders (e.g., age, comorbidities) may drive associations.
- **Generalizability concerns**: External validation used MIMIC‑LEF with LLM‑based EF extraction; real‑world deployment faces diverse ECG devices and variability.
- **Interpretability not clinically validated**: SHAP explanations are plausible but lack evaluation by clinicians; clinical utility remains unproven.

## Confidence
- **Performance metrics (AUROC, F1 vs. baselines)**: High (robust internal/external validation with large cohorts)
- **Framework superiority over end‑to‑end CNN**: High (consistent gains across subgroups)
- **Structured predictors as interpretable features**: Medium (SHAP aligns with known patterns but clinical utility unverified)
- **LVEF information encoded in predictor space**: Medium‑Low (zero‑shot evidence indirect; could reflect correlated pathologies)

## Next Checks
1. **Probe foundation model latent space**: Train a linear probe on ST‑MEM embeddings (pre‑predictor heads) to predict LVEF. Matching full‑pipeline performance would confirm encoding occurs prior to the 71 predictors.
2. **Clinician evaluation of SHAP explanations**: Present randomized model decisions with SHAP plots to cardiologists; measure agreement with their clinical reasoning and perceived usefulness.
3. **Benchmark alternative tabular heads**: Replace XGBoost with a shallow neural net or logistic regression on the same 71 predictors using identical splits; confirm nonlinear interactions are essential for performance gains.
