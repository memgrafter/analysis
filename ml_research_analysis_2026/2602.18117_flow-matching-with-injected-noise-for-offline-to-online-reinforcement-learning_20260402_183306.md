# Flow Matching with Injected Noise for Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18117
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18117
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** FINO consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across 45 tasks from OGBench and D4RL, achieving higher success rates and returns under limited online interaction budgets.

## Executive Summary
Offline-to-online reinforcement learning seeks to leverage static offline data and then refine policies with online interaction, but faces the critical challenge of effective exploration. FINO addresses this by enhancing flow matching policies. During offline pre-training, it injects time‑dependent Gaussian noise into the flow matching objective, expanding the policy’s action distribution beyond the offline dataset while preserving the mean. In the online fine‑tuning phase, FINO samples multiple candidate actions and selects them using an entropy‑adapted temperature parameter, which dynamically balances exploration and exploitation as new data arrives. Evaluated on 45 tasks from OGBench and D4RL, FINO repeatedly achieves superior success rates and returns, especially in complex navigation and manipulation environments, even under stringent online interaction budgets. These results demonstrate the method’s strength in improving sample efficiency and policy robustness.

## Method Summary
FINO consists of two main phases. In offline pre‑training, it learns a flow matching policy from the static dataset but deliberately perturbs the learning objective with time‑dependent Gaussian noise. This noise injection intentionally widens the support of the policy’s action distribution, enabling it to propose actions that go beyond the demonstrated behavior, while keeping the mean action aligned with the data to maintain performance. During online fine‑tuning, the policy interacts with the environment; at each step it generates several candidate actions from the (possibly noisy) flow matching distribution and then selects one using a temperature‑scaled softmax. The entropy‑adapted temperature parameter evolves based on the entropy of the action distribution, increasing when uncertainty is high to favor exploration and decreasing when uncertainty is low to favor exploitation. This combination allows the policy to efficiently explore the environment without forgetting the skills learned offline.

## Key Results
- FINO achieves significantly higher success rates and returns compared to state‑of‑the‑art baselines on 45 tasks from OGBench and D4RL.
- The improvements are especially notable in complex navigation and manipulation environments.
- FINO maintains its advantage even with limited online interaction, highlighting its sample efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FINO’s effectiveness stems from two complementary mechanisms. In the offline phase, injecting time‑dependent Gaussian noise into the flow matching objective expands the policy’s action distribution, allowing it to generate actions that are not present in the offline dataset. This promotes exploration capabilities while preserving the mean action, which helps retain the skills learned from the data. In the online phase, the entropy‑adapted temperature parameter dynamically adjusts the exploration‑exploitation balance: when the action distribution is highly entropic (uncertain), the temperature increases to encourage more exploration; when it is low‑entropic (confident), the temperature decreases to exploit promising actions. This adaptive scheme enables the policy to efficiently gather informative experience under limited interaction budgets, leading to improved performance on challenging tasks.

## Foundational Learning
This work builds on several foundational concepts:
- **Flow matching**: a generative modeling technique that learns a continuous normalizing flow to transform a simple base distribution (e.g., Gaussian) into a target data distribution. In reinforcement learning, flow matching can represent stochastic policies by modeling the trajectory of actions.
- **Offline reinforcement learning**: learning a policy from a pre‑collected dataset without interacting with the environment, which faces challenges of distribution shift and limited exploration.
- **Offline‑to‑online RL**: a paradigm that combines offline pre‑training with subsequent online fine‑tuning to leverage large static datasets while still allowing adaptation through interaction.
- **Entropy‑adapted temperature**: a method to control the randomness of action selection by scaling the softmax temperature; adapting it based on the entropy of the action distribution automates the exploration‑exploitation trade‑off.
- **Exploration in RL**: the problem of discovering rewarding states and actions, particularly critical when the initial policy is trained only on limited offline data.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** The FINO system comprises a flow matching policy network that outputs actions given states; a noise injection mechanism that adds time‑dependent Gaussian noise during offline pre‑training to expand the policy’s action distribution; an online action sampler that generates multiple candidate actions from the (potentially noisy) policy; an entropy calculator that computes the entropy of the action distribution; and an entropy‑adapted temperature module that adjusts the temperature parameter for softmax selection based on the computed entropy.
- **Critical path:** The overall training pipeline proceeds as follows: (1) Offline pre‑training: The flow matching policy is trained on the static dataset with injected noise, learning to match the data distribution while preserving the mean action. (2) Online fine‑tuning: For each environment step, the policy produces several candidate actions, the entropy of the distribution is estimated, the temperature is adapted accordingly, and one action is selected via temperature‑scaled softmax. The environment is stepped, the transition is stored (often in a replay buffer that mixes offline and online data), and the policy may be updated using the combined data. The temperature adaptation continues throughout online interaction, balancing exploration and exploitation as new data arrives.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Injecting Gaussian noise during offline training widens the policy’s support, enabling exploration of novel actions at the cost of potential degradation on demonstrated behaviors if the noise magnitude is too high. The entropy‑adapted temperature dynamically balances exploration and exploitation but introduces an additional hyper‑parameter (the adaptation schedule). Using multiple candidate actions per step improves action selection but increases computational overhead. Preserving the mean action during noise injection helps maintain offline performance while still encouraging diversity.
- **Failure signatures:** Excessive noise variance during offline pre‑training may cause the policy to forget demonstrated skills, leading to poor initial online performance. An improperly tuned temperature adaptation (e.g., too slow to increase entropy) can result in insufficient exploration, causing the agent to converge to suboptimal policies. If the flow matching model fails to accurately capture the offline data distribution, both the noise‑injected distribution and the subsequent online updates may be ineffective.
- **First experiments:** The authors likely first validate the flow matching policy on offline data to ensure it can reproduce the demonstrated behavior distribution. Next, they would test the effect of different noise schedules on the diversity of the learned policy, perhaps by measuring coverage of the action space. Then, they would evaluate the entropy‑adapted temperature mechanism in isolation, comparing against fixed‑temperature baselines. Finally, they combine both components and benchmark on the full suite of tasks against prior offline‑to‑online methods.

## Limitations
- **External Validation:** The corpus shows zero citations and a moderate average neighbor FMR (0.556), indicating limited independent evaluation and an evolving field; claim strength largely rests on the presented benchmarks.
- **Scope of Evaluation:** Performance is demonstrated only on 45 tasks from OGBench and D4RL; generalizability to other domains (e.g., real‑world robotics, discrete action spaces) is unknown.
- **Ablation Clarity:** The separate contributions of injected noise (offline) and entropy‑adapted temperature (online), and the claim that injected noise preserves the mean, require clearer isolation and verification.

## Confidence
- **High:** Methodological soundness of the FINO algorithm as a coherent technical extension.
- **Medium:** Empirical superiority on the presented OGBench and D4RL tasks under limited online interaction, given the standard nature of these benchmarks but lack of widespread validation.
- **Low:** The broad assertion that FINO effectively solves the exploration challenge in offline‑to‑online RL universally, due to narrow benchmark scope and unknown performance in other settings.

## Next Checks
1. Independent replication of FINO on at least two additional, distinct offline‑to‑online benchmark suites (e.g., Meta‑World, CARLA) to test generalizability.

3. Sensitivity analysis of key hyperparameters: noise schedule/variance and temperature adaptation schedule to understand robustness and identify critical tuning requirements.
