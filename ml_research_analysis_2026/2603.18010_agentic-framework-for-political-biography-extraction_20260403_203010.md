# Agentic Framework for Political Biography Extraction
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18010  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18010  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: A two-stage "Synthesis-Coding" agentic framework extracts structured elite biographies from web sources at high accuracy (F1 ~0.76–0.77) and ~3% of manual collection costs.

## Executive Summary
This work tackles the bottleneck in creating large-scale political datasets: extracting structured elite biographies from vast unstructured web sources, which traditionally relies on costly manual coding. The authors propose a two-stage "Synthesis-Coding" agentic framework. First, an upstream synthesis stage uses recursive LLM-agents to search, filter, and curate a biography from heterogeneous web sources. Second, a downstream coding stage maps this curated synthesis into a structured dataframe. Validations show that LLM coders match or exceed human experts when given curated contexts, the agentic system synthesizes more comprehensive information than Wikipedia (recovering a "long tail" of granular facts), and the synthesis stage mitigates performance degradation from noisy, multilingual corpora by condensing evidence into signal-dense representations. The framework achieves high F1 scores at a fraction of manual cost, offering a scalable, transparent method for building political databases.

## Method Summary
The framework decomposes extraction into synthesis and coding. In synthesis, a Supervisor agent maintains a research objective, codebook, running summary, and a gap list (todo). A Searcher agent iteratively executes web searches, retrieves documents, and archives content, updating the gap list. After sufficient evidence collection, the Supervisor compiles a synthetic report. In coding, a separate LLM maps this report into structured claims using the codebook. Evaluations use political elites from China (Baidu Baike), US (Wikipedia), and OECD (variable coverage). Ground truth is constructed by pooling multiple bios and validating with an automated judge. Key diagnostics compare coding on raw concatenated retrieval versus refined synthetic reports, and agent synthesis versus Wikipedia.

## Key Results
- LLM coders achieve higher recall (10–16 percentage points) and F1 (e.g., +0.109 for Grok-4.1-Fast) over human experts when provided with curated contexts.
- Agentic synthesis recovers more comprehensive information than Wikipedia, with recall gains of +31.4 percentage points at a modest precision trade-off (-5.2), indicating recovery of long-tail facts omitted by human curation.
- Direct coding from raw, noisy, multilingual corpora degrades performance; the synthesis stage mitigates this by condensing evidence into signal-dense representations, enabling F1 scores in the high eighties (~0.76–0.77) at approximately 3% of manual collection costs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Decomposing extraction into synthesis (evidence curation) and coding (structured output) mitigates performance degradation from long, noisy, multilingual corpora.** The synthesis stage proactively searches, filters, and compresses dispersed web evidence into a structured, signal-dense summary, preventing the coder from being overwhelmed by irrelevant content and reducing the "lost-in-the-middle" effect. Evidence: [abstract] and [section 7] show that a refined representation yields significantly higher F1/recall than raw concatenation, isolating the value of active compression. Break condition: If synthesis fails to retrieve critical evidence due to search errors or source unavailability, recall loss is guaranteed regardless of coding accuracy.

**Mechanism 2: Recursive, tool-using LLM agents enable adaptive, path-dependent information gathering that static retrieval cannot achieve, leading to more comprehensive evidence collection.** The agentic system (Supervisor-Searcher) interleaves reasoning and action: it plans searches based on knowledge gaps, executes, inspects results, updates its summary, and iteratively refines understanding. This allows query refinement based on intermediate discoveries. Evidence: [section 4] contrasts static RAG with the recursive loop; [section 6, Experiment 2] shows agentic synthesis achieves higher recall than Wikipedia baseline. Break condition: The agent's reasoning may be biased by initial results, leading to premature convergence on certain source types; URL diversity analysis (Table 3) implies sensitivity to query formulation.

**Mechanism 3: Agentic synthesis produces a "long-tail" expansion of biographical coverage by recovering granular, low-salience facts that human curators omit due to editorial heuristics favoring prominence.** The agent's searches are driven by codebook-defined gaps (e.g., all positions between ages 18–65), including less prominent roles, whereas human curation compresses or omits lower-visibility information. Evidence: [section 6, Experiment 2] shows largest gains in recall (+31.4 pp) with modest precision drop (-5.2), supporting recovery of missing facts. [section 8] states synthesis bridges gaps left by human curation. Break condition: For figures with minimal digital footprint (e.g., low-level officials in data-poor regions), exhaustive search may still return nothing; value diminishes as digital trace approaches zero.

## Foundational Learning
- **Extraction vs. Classification:** Extraction requires open-domain discovery, multi-field mapping, and temporal sequencing from dispersed sources, unlike classification which maps fixed text to a finite label set. Misapplying classification methods to extraction leads to failure. Quick check: Can you identify whether "code every appointment a minister ever held from their entire web presence" is extraction or classification? Why?
- **Agentic Workflows (ReAct Paradigm):** The synthesis stage is a tool-using, iterative reasoning-action loop (thought → tool call → observation → repeat), enabling adaptive search strategies. Quick check: If the Supervisor did not maintain a todo_list of remaining gaps, how would that affect retrieval?
- **Signal-to-Noise Ratio in Long Contexts:** Coding fails when relevant facts are diluted in noisy, unstructured text. Experiment 3 proves that a curated, condensed representation outperforms raw concatenation, emphasizing information density over mere context length. Quick check: If you increased the coder's context window to 1M tokens but kept the same raw concatenation, would performance necessarily improve? Why?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Task Input → Supervisor → Searcher ↔ Archive (Searcher reads/writes; Supervisor reads) → loop until finish → Supervisor (synthetic report) → Coder → Structured Output.

Critical path: Supervisor parses codebook and initial gap list; Searcher executes search-retrieve-archive cycles; Supervisor updates summary and todo; repeat until termination condition; Supervisor generates final synthetic report; Coder maps report to structured claims.

Design tradeoffs:
- Cost vs. Coverage: More search iterations and URLs increase recall but raise token costs (Searcher consumes ~89% of tokens); paper uses budgets (~15–25 searches) to balance.
- Breadth vs. Precision: Broad queries cast wide net but return noise; too narrow queries may miss pages; agent must dynamically adjust.
- Synthesis Depth vs. Coding Capacity: Very long synthetic reports may exceed coder context limits; ~10k tokens is manageable; beyond this, recall suffers.
- Non-Wiki vs. Wiki Access: Blocking Wikipedia forces agent to rebuild evidentiary base from scratch, more costly but reduces dependency on potentially incomplete human curation.

Failure signatures:
- Low Recall, High Precision: Synthesis failed to retrieve key evidence. Check query formulation, search result filtering, gap list management; may indicate premature termination or query stagnation.
- Low Precision, Variable Recall: Coding stage hallucinating. Check coder prompt grounding (require evidence snippets), codebook clarity, contradictory/ambiguous synthetic report.
- Stagnant Gap List: Todo items persist with high counters but unresolved. Indicates search strategy cannot find evidence (source unavailable or malformed queries).
- Language Mismatch: High non-English token share in coding input correlates with lower recall. Improve retrieval language targeting or add translation.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Coding Benchmark (Wiki-only):** For a subset with high-quality curated sources (e.g., Baidu Baike/Wikipedia), run only the coder on the curated text. Benchmark against human-coded data to establish baseline coding capability. Verifies: Can the coder accurately map a clean, short corpus?
2. **Agent Synthesis vs. Wikipedia:** For a set without comprehensive wiki page (or to test robustness), run full agentic synthesis pipeline. Compare agent's output biography to the one coded from Wikipedia for same figure. Verifies: Does synthesis discover information that Wikipedia lacks?
3. **Raw vs. Refined Representation:** Using fixed agent retrieval trajectory, create two coding inputs: (a) raw concatenation of all retrieved pages, (b) agent's synthetic report. Compare coding outputs. Verifies: Is the synthesis/refinement step necessary, or would a longer context suffice?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Does the synthesis-then-coding architecture generalize to political constructs usually cast as classification problems (e.g., regime type, populism)?** Basis: Conclusion identifies testing for such labels as "promising frontier." Why unresolved: Paper only validates extraction of biographical facts. What evidence: Replicate existing classification benchmarks by converting labels to extractable claim sets and compare accuracy/auditability against direct classification.
- **How should the framework be extended to interpretive attributes where ground truth is contested (e.g., rhetorical frames, policy positions)?** Basis: Conclusion raises harder questions about ground truth and human-machine judgment combination. Why unresolved: Current evaluation uses verifiable facts; contested concepts require new CGT definitions and evaluation. What evidence: Build multi-annotator datasets on interpretive attributes, model disagreement, develop hybrid protocols; then test framework variants.
- **To what extent do performance gains depend on specific agent design and LLM choices, particularly in multilingual or low-resource contexts?** Basis: Paper uses single agent architecture and limited models; multilingual degradation and long-context issues hint at design sensitivity, but no systematic analysis. Why unresolved: Without ablations, unknown which components matter or how to optimize for diverse settings. What evidence: Conduct factorized experiment varying retrieval steps, condensation methods, and model families across languages and source types, measuring cost/accuracy trade-offs.

## Limitations
- Termination logic for the synthesis agent (step budgets, sufficiency thresholds) is unspecified, blocking cost/recall replication.
- The evidence-conditional judge for ground truth (prompt, model version) is undisclosed, risking noisy evaluation metrics.
- Performance on figures with minimal digital footprints (e.g., low-level officials in data-poor regions) is unstated; claims about "long-tail" recovery may not generalize to obscurity extremes.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Synthesis-Coding decomposition): Medium-High. Strong internal diagnostics isolate the effect, but assumes synthesis always retrieves sufficient evidence—an untested break condition.
- Mechanism 2 (Recursive agent vs. static retrieval): Medium. Supported by recall gains over Wikipedia and analogies in neighbor papers, but search stagnation and bias risks are noted.
- Mechanism 3 (Long-tail recovery): Medium. Recall gains clear, but "long-tail" defined relative to Wikipedia’s omissions; unknown if gaps filled are politically material or trivial.
- Foundational concepts (extraction vs. classification, signal density): High. Well-established in IR and LLM literature.
- Corpus relevance: Medium. Neighbor papers confirm architectural trends but do not directly validate this political biography task.

## Next Checks
1. **Termination protocol reverse-engineering:** Run provided prompts with increasing step budgets (e.g., 10, 20, 30) on a held-out sample; observe marginal recall/cost curves to infer effective thresholds.
2. **Ground truth audit:** Replicate CGT construction using disclosed pooling method but with an independently prompted judge (e.g., GPT-4o with citation grounding). Compare agreement rates with the paper’s GPT-5-mini judge on a 100-claim subset.
3. **Low-footprint stress test:** Select 50 officials from countries with <20 Wikipedia pageviews and minimal web presence (e.g., small Pacific island ministers). Run full synthesis; measure recall vs. high-footprint controls. If recall collapses, the "long-tail" claim is constrained to digitally visible elites.
