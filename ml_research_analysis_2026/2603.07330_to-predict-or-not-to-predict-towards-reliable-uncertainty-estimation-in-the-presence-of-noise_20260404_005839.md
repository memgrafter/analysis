# To Predict or Not to Predict? Towards reliable uncertainty estimation in the presence of noise

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07330
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07330
- Reference count: 0

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates nine uncertainty estimation (UE) methods for multilingual sentence complexity classification under noisy and domain-shifted conditions. Using a fine-tuned mBERT model across seven languages and three datasets, the authors find that Monte Carlo dropout techniques consistently outperform softmax-based and feature-based outlier detectors in low-resource and out-of-domain scenarios. Softmax methods, while competitive in high-resource in-domain settings, become overconfident under distribution shift. Feature-based outlier detectors show high variance across languages and domains, limiting their reliability. The work further reveals that standard UE metrics like ROC-AUC do not always correlate with practical gains from selective prediction (abstaining on uncertain examples), underscoring the need for context-aware UE selection.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune mBERT for binary sentence complexity classification using the Readme++ dataset via 5-fold cross-validation. For each trained model, they compute uncertainty scores using nine methods: softmax-based (SR, ENT), Monte Carlo dropout (SMP, ENT-MC, PV, BALD), and feature-based outlier detection (MD, HUQ-MD, LOF, ISOF). Evaluation occurs on in-domain held-out folds and two out-of-domain datasets (Vikidia, Simplext). Metrics include discrimination (ROC-AUC, AU-PRC), calibration (C-Slope, CITL, ECE), and selective prediction (RC-AUC, N.RC-AUC, E-AUoptRC, TI). The impact of abstention is measured by rejecting the top k% most uncertain predictions (1–15%) and computing the change in macro F1. Results are aggregated across folds and languages using z-scores for comparison.

## Key Results
- Monte Carlo dropout methods (SMP, ENT-MC) demonstrate robust calibration and discriminative power across all languages and under domain shift, while softmax-based methods (SR) degrade significantly.
- Abstaining on the 10% most uncertain predictions yields a macro F1 improvement from 0.81 to 0.85 in the Readme task, with MC-dropout methods providing the largest gains.
- Feature-based outlier detectors (ISOF, LOF) exhibit high variance across languages and domains, limiting their reliability for consistent uncertainty estimation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Epistemic uncertainty via MC dropout
Monte Carlo dropout methods (SMP, ENT-MC) maintain robust uncertainty estimation under domain and language shifts by capturing epistemic uncertainty. Multiple stochastic forward passes with dropout approximate a Bayesian posterior; the variance across predictions indicates model uncertainty, which increases on out-of-distribution inputs. This provides a reliable signal for abstention. Core assumption: epistemic uncertainty is a consistent indicator of prediction reliability across linguistic and domain contexts. Evidence: the abstract states MC-dropout approaches offer "more robust calibration, stable decision thresholds, and greater discriminative power even under adverse conditions"; Section 4.2 shows they remain strong under OOD while softmax degrades. Break condition: if the architecture cannot represent uncertainty or the shift is extreme, MC dropout may fail.

### Mechanism 2: Softmax overconfidence under shift
Softmax-based uncertainty (SR) degrades under distribution shift because it primarily reflects aleatoric uncertainty and becomes overconfident on out-of-distribution inputs. The maximum softmax probability is a poor proxy for confidence when the data distribution changes; the model may assign high probabilities to incorrect classes due to mis-calibration. Core assumption: softmax confidence captures data ambiguity, not model ignorance, and does not increase for unfamiliar inputs. Evidence: the abstract notes softmax reliability declines in low-resource or domain-shift scenarios; Section 4.2 shows SR performs well in-domain but MC dropout is more reliable under OOD. Break condition: if the test distribution closely matches training (in-domain, high-resource), SR can remain effective.

### Mechanism 3: Embedding space instability
Feature-based outlier detectors (ISOF, LOF) exhibit high variance because their uncertainty scores depend on the stability of the embedding space, which varies with language and domain. These methods compute distances or densities in the latent feature space; multilingual models produce language-specific embeddings, and domain shift alters manifold geometry, causing outlier score instability. Core assumption: embedding spaces are homogenous enough across languages and domains for consistent OOD detection. Evidence: the abstract states these detectors "exhibit high variance, limiting reliability"; Section 4.2 notes their "aggregate stability is weaker" and highlights dataset-dependent behaviour. Break condition: if embeddings are normalized (e.g., via contrastive learning) or the shift is small, variance may decrease.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: mBERT classifier -> [CLS] embeddings -> UE methods (softmax, MC-dropout, outlier detectors) -> uncertainty scores -> evaluation metrics & abstention -> macro F1 change.
- **Critical path**:
  1. Train mBERT via 5-fold CV on Readme++ (AdamW, lr=5e-5, dropout=0.3, early stopping).
  2. For each fold and test set (in-domain/OOD), compute uncertainty scores for all nine methods.
  3. Compute metrics per fold/language; aggregate via z-scores for heatmaps.
  4. For selective prediction, vary rejection threshold (1–15%) and calculate ∆F1 relative to no abstention.
  5. Compute correlations (Kendall's τ) between metrics.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Computational: MC dropout ~20× slower than softmax; outlier detectors require training embeddings.
  - Robustness vs. cost: SR/ENT cheap but fail OOD; MC dropout robust but expensive.
  - Metric choice: ROC-AUC correlates with selective prediction only in some cases; context-aware selection is needed.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High variance across folds for LOF/ISOF indicates instability.
  - Negative ∆F1 at low rejection rates means uncertainty is anti-correlated with errors.
  - Calibration metrics (CITL, ECE) show severe overconfidence on OOD (e.g., CITL << 0).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Meta-uncertainty framework**: Can a framework adaptively select or combine UE metrics based on data context? The paper explicitly proposes this as future work but notes no single method excels universally. Resolution would require building a meta-model (e.g., using classifier confidence, embedding statistics) to choose or blend metrics, demonstrating consistent improvements across domains.
- **Generalization to multi-class tasks**: How do UE robustness patterns generalize beyond binary classification? The paper states more research is needed for multi-class predictions across text tasks. Resolution would involve replicating the design with multi-class datasets (e.g., topic categorization) in multiple languages.
- **Noise-uncertainty relationship**: What is the quantitative link between noise types (label errors, irrelevant content) and uncertainty? The paper manually categorized uncertain predictions but did not systematically correlate noise characteristics with UE scores. Resolution would require controlled noise injection experiments and integration of noise detectors to filter/reweight uncertainty estimates.
- **Metric misalignment**: Why do standard UE discrimination metrics (e.g., ROC-AUC) often fail to predict selective prediction gains? The paper highlights this discrepancy but does not explain it. Resolution would involve large-scale analysis linking metric values to actual ∆F1 gains and proposing new metrics optimized for downstream robustness.
- **Stabilizing outlier detectors**: How can feature-based outlier detectors (e.g., ISOF, LOF) be stabilized to reduce high variance? The paper notes their instability but does not diagnose causes or propose fixes. Resolution would involve analyzing embedding geometries across languages/domains and testing modifications like domain-adapted embeddings or ensemble aggregations.

## Limitations
- Cross-validation splits and random seeds are not disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Hyperparameters for outlier detectors (LOF, ISOF, Mahalanobis) are missing, potentially affecting reported variance.
- Evaluation is restricted to mBERT on sentence complexity only; generalizability to other models and tasks is unclear.
