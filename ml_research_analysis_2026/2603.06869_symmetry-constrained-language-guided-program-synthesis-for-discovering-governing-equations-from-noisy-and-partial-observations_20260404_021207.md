# Symmetry-Constrained Language-Guided Program Synthesis for Discovering Governing Equations from Noisy and Partial Observations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06869
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06869
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves 83.7% exact structural recovery at 10% noise (+22.4 pp over next best), reduces OOD extrapolation error by 61%, and correctly flags 91.3% of non-identifiable cases as ambiguous.

## Executive Summary
SymLang addresses the challenge of recovering compact governing equations from noisy, partially observed time-series data while quantifying structural uncertainty. It integrates three key components: (i) a typed symmetry-constrained grammar that enforces dimensional, parity, and rotational invariance as hard rules, pruning 71.3% of candidate expressions; (ii) a fine-tuned 7B-parameter language model conditioned on interpretable data descriptors to efficiently propose high-probability candidates; (iii) a minimum description length (MDL)-regularized Bayesian model selection augmented with block-bootstrap stability analysis to assess uncertainty and identify degeneracies. Evaluated on 133 dynamical systems, SymLang attains 83.7% exact recovery at 10% noise, outperforming the next best method by 22.4 percentage points, and reduces out-of-distribution extrapolation error by 61%. It also nearly eliminates conservation-law violations and reliably flags non-identifiable cases (91.3% accuracy) even under 50% state occlusion.

## Method Summary
SymLang follows a five-stage pipeline. First, raw time-series data are preprocessed: derivatives are estimated via smoothing splines or total-variation regularization, and all variables are nondimensionalized to order unity. Second, a typed context-free grammar is constructed from user-specified symmetry constraints (e.g., parity, rotational invariance); this grammar encodes dimensional homogeneity and invariance properties as production rules that reject invalid subtrees before evaluation. Third, a data summarizer extracts spectral features, symmetry scores, conserved-quantity candidates, and correlation structure, which condition a fine-tuned 7B-parameter language model. The model autoregressively generates S-expression candidates that respect the grammar’s type system. Fourth, constants in each candidate are fitted to the observed derivatives using L-BFGS-B with ℓ2 and optional physical penalties. Fifth, candidates are scored by MDL: negative log-likelihood of residuals plus λ times the description length of the expression tree. The top-K candidates are re-evaluated via block-bootstrap (200 resamples) to compute stability metrics (rank preservation σ, coefficient of variation of constants). Final weights combine MDL score and stability, producing a ranked list with uncertainty estimates.

## Key Results
- 83.7% exact structural recovery at 10% noise (+22.4 pp over next best).
- Out-of-distribution extrapolation error reduced by 61% relative to baselines.
- Conservation-law violations near-eliminated (3.1e-3 vs. 187.3e-3).
- Under 50% state occlusion: 61.2% exact recovery; 91.3% ambiguous-case flagging accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Typed symmetry-constrained grammars drastically prune the candidate expression space by embedding dimensional analysis, parity, and group invariance as hard production rules.
- **Mechanism:** The grammar’s nonterminals carry type annotations (dimension vectors, parity tags, invariance classes). Each production rule enforces type consistency via bottom-up propagation. For example, addition requires identical dimensions; trigonometric functions require dimensionless arguments; rotational invariance restricts dependencies to rotational scalars (e.g., ‖x‖²). Invalid subtrees are rejected without evaluation.
- **Core assumption:** The true governing equation respects the specified physical symmetries (dimensional homogeneity, parity, temporal/spatial invariances). Mis-specification of these constraints will eliminate the correct equation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States the grammar enforces “dimensional, parity, and rotational invariance as hard rules, pruning 71.3% of candidates before any fitting.”
  - [section] Section “Stage 2: Nondimensionalization and unit constraints” and “Stage 3: Symmetry-constrained grammar construction” detail the type system and constraint families (parity, rotational invariance, time-translation invariance). Table 4 shows removing the grammar reduces exact recovery by 17.5 pp.
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., “Physics-constrained robust learning of open-form PDEs” [2309.07672], “Invariance constrained deep learning network for PDE discovery” [2402.03747]) also pursue symmetry constraints, supporting their general utility, but do not directly validate SymLang’s specific grammar implementation.
- **Break condition:** If the system exhibits an unknown or user-missed symmetry (e.g., a hidden conservation law not encoded), the grammar may exclude the correct expression class. The framework’s performance degrades gracefully (Table 5 shows domain variations), but complete recovery fails for systems outside the grammar’s expressive scope.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A fine-tuned language model (LM) conditioned on interpretable data descriptors efficiently proposes high-probability candidate expressions within the constrained grammar space.
- **Mechanism:** The LM (7B-parameter decoder-only transformer) is fine-tuned on (data-summary, expression) pairs from simulated systems. At inference, it autoregressively generates S-expressions consistent with the grammar’s type system, guided by a prefix summarizing spectral features, symmetry scores, conserved-quantity candidates, and correlation structure. Nucleus sampling encourages diversity.
- **Core assumption:** The LM’s training distribution covers a sufficiently broad manifold of physically plausible expressions such that its conditional proposals are relevant to the target system. Out-of-distribution systems may receive low-probability, irrelevant proposals.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Notes the LM “efficiently navigates the constrained search space.” Ablation (Table 4) shows replacing LM with uniform grammar sampling reduces recovery by 11.3 pp and increases runtime 3.7×.
  - [section] Section “Stage 4: Language-guided program synthesis” describes training data (820k pairs from Feynman benchmark, BioModels, synthetic systems) and the conditioning context. The proposer wall-clock time is <5% of total pipeline.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like “Transformer-based planning for symbolic regression” (NeurIPS 2023) and “End-to-end symbolic regression with transformers” (NeurIPS 2022) demonstrate LMs can generate expressions, but SymLang’s integration with a typed grammar is a novel combination not directly evidenced elsewhere.
- **Break condition:** For systems with highly exotic functional forms absent from the LM’s training corpus (e.g., novel special functions, non-standard compositions), the LM may fail to propose the correct skeleton within a reasonable budget. The grammar’s pruning is still helpful, but proposal quality limits recovery.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** MDL-regularized Bayesian model selection combined with block-bootstrap stability analysis quantifies structural uncertainty and identifies degeneracies, preventing overconfident incorrect single-equation outputs.
- **Mechanism:** Each candidate expression fitted to constants is scored by S(e) = −log p(D|e, θ̂) + λ·len(e), balancing data fit (Gaussian residuals) with description length of tree structure and constants. Top-K candidates are re-evaluated via block-bootstrap (200 resamples of contiguous windows). Stability σ(e) (fraction of bootstrap runs preserving MDL rank) and coefficient of variation of fitted constants flag unstable or degenerate structures. Model weights w(e) ∝ exp(−S(e)/T) are reported.
- **Core assumption:** The MDL criterion appropriately penalizes complexity relative to data support, and bootstrap resampling captures structural variability due to finite-sample noise. Non-stationarities or strong autocorrelations beyond block length could invalidate bootstrap estimates.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Reports the framework “correctly flags 91.3% of non-identifiable cases as ambiguous” and “near-eliminates conservation-law violations.” Figure 2(b) shows weight distributions for identifiable, binary-degenerate, and continuously degenerate regimes.
  - [section] Section “Model Selection and Uncertainty Quantification” defines MDL scoring, bootstrap procedure (block length 10Δt), and stability thresholds (σ < 0.7, CV > 0.5). Ablation (Table 4) shows removing bootstrap reduces recovery by only 3.4 pp but eliminates uncertainty information.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like “A Bayesian Framework for learning governing PDE from Data” [arXiv:219834] use Bayesian model averaging but lack SymLang’s explicit bootstrap stability for structure; SymLang’s integration of MDL with bootstrap for symbolic expressions is novel.
- **Break condition:** If the system is structurally non-identifiable (multiple equations fit equally well), the framework correctly flags ambiguity, but if bootstrap blocks are too short/long relative to correlation times, stability estimates may be inaccurate, causing mis-flagging or missed degeneracy.

This analysis stays conditional on the paper’s reported results and methodology, anchoring claims in explicit sections while noting where corpus evidence is supportive but indirect.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Dimensional Analysis & Buckingham Π Theorem**
  - *Why needed here:* The typed grammar’s unit constraints (Section “Stage 2”) rely on propagating dimension vectors through operators. Nondimensionalization reduces all variables to order unity and makes dimensionless combinations explicit, enabling hard pruning of dimensionally inconsistent subtrees (e.g., adding meters to seconds). This encodes fundamental physical consistency as a deductive filter.
  - *Quick check question:* Why must the argument of sin(A) have dimension [A] = 0, and how does this rule eliminate entire branches of the expression tree before evaluation?

- **Concept: Minimum Description Length (MDL) Principle**
  - *Why needed here:* MDL scoring (Equation 10) balances goodness-of-fit against expression complexity (tree node counts and constant encoding). This prevents overfitting to noise—a critical failure mode for sparse regression or likelihood maximization (Table 4 shows −14.6 pp drop without MDL). The description length metric is tailored to symbolic trees, not just parameter count.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the description length len(e) in Equation 9 differ from AIC/BIC penalties, and why is it more suitable for symbolic expressions?

- **Concept: Transformer-based Conditional Generation**
  - *Why needed here:* The LM proposer (Section “Stage 4”) must generate syntactically valid S-expressions consistent with a dynamic, user-specified grammar. Fine-tuning on (data-summary, expression) pairs teaches the model to map interpretable data features (spectra, symmetry scores) to plausible governing equation forms. This is a program synthesis task where the grammar is a type system.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the data-summary prefix (≤200 tokens) condition the LM, and why is it preferred over conditioning directly on raw time series?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Preprocessing** (Derivative estimation: smoothing spline or TV-regularized differentiation).
  2. **Nondimensionalization** (Infer characteristic scales, produce dimensionless state/derivative arrays).
  3. **Grammar Builder** (From symmetry spec S, construct typed CFG enforcing unit/parity/rotational/time constraints).
  4. **Data Summarizer** (Extract spectral, symmetry, conserved-quantity, correlation features).
  5. **LM Proposer** (Conditioned on grammar + summaries, generate N candidate S-expressions).
  6. **Constant Fitter** (L-BFGS-B with ℓ2/phys penalty to match ˙y).
  7. **MDL Scorer** (Compute S(e) using held-out noise estimate).
  8. **Bootstrap Stability** (Block-bootstrap B=200, compute rank stability σ(e) and constant CV).
  9. **Ranker** (Apply stability down-weighting, output top-K {(e_i, w_i, σ_i)}).

- **Critical path:** Preprocessing → Nondimensionalization → Grammar + Summaries → LM Proposals → Constant Fitting → MDL Scoring → Bootstrap → Weighted Ranking. The grammar must be built before LM proposals (to enforce hard constraints during generation), and summaries must be computed before LM conditioning.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Grammar expressivity vs. pruning:* More constraints (e.g., full SO(3)) prune more aggressively (higher recovery in CM/ED) but risk excluding correct equations if symmetry is approximate or misspecified. Ablation shows −17.5 pp without grammar.
  - *LM proposal diversity vs. quality:* High nucleus sampling (p=0.9) increases coverage but may waste budget on grammatically valid but physically implausible forms. Uniform sampling (ablation) is worse.
  - *Bootstrap block length:* Must balance preserving temporal correlations vs. enough resamples. Paper uses 10Δt; Ext. Tab. 2 shows stability over one order of magnitude in block length.
  - *MDL λ:* Higher λ favors simpler models; too high underfits, too low overfits. Selected by leave-one-trajectory CV.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Low exact recovery (<60% at 10% noise)* likely indicates mis-specified symmetry constraints or poor derivative estimation (check TV vs. spline choice).
  - *High OOD physical drift* (>1e−1) suggests grammar constraints were bypassed (e.g., dimensionally inconsistent constants fitted) or conservation penalty η too low.
  - *Bootstrap σ(e) < 0.7 for top candidate* indicates structural instability—multiple forms explain data equally; user should examine weight distribution (Figure 2b).
  - *All candidates flagged non-identifiable* (Fisher info λ_min < 1e−4) implies insufficient data or unobserved states; consider latent augmentation k>0.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. *Grammar pruning verification:* For a single system (e.g., harmonic oscillator), count candidate expressions at depth ≤6 with/without unit/parity constraints. Expect ~70% reduction matching paper’s average.
  2. *LM proposal relevance:* Generate 200 proposals with the trained LM vs. uniform grammar sampling. Compute fraction containing known “building blocks” (e.g., x², sin(x), x·˙x). Expect LM to show higher concentration on physically plausible forms.
  3. *Bootstrap reproducibility:* On a system with known degeneracy (e.g., A·sin(ωt+φ) vs. B·sin(ωt)+C·cos(ωt)), compute weight distribution over 10 bootstrap seeds. Expect near-equal weights (≈50/50) and low σ(e) for both forms.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Can applicable symmetry groups (e.g., parity, rotational invariance) be automatically detected from trajectory data, removing the need for user specification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The "Limitations" section states that symmetry specification requires user input and that "future work could automate symmetry detection from data" using correlation-based tests extended to continuous groups (Page 9).
- Why unresolved: Current framework assumes the practitioner knows the system's symmetries, which may not hold for novel physical regimes, limiting applicability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Develop an algorithm that infers Lie group generators or discrete invariances from observed trajectories, then constructs the corresponding grammar constraints automatically; validate on systems with unknown or mixed symmetries.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the language model's training distribution bias discovery toward familiar functional forms, and how can this be mitigated to enable genuinely novel equation discovery?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The "Limitations" section notes that the LM "may assign low probability to genuinely novel functional forms that have no precedent in its training distribution" (Page 9).
- Why unresolved: The LM proposer, while efficient, risks missing equations outside its training distribution, potentially overlooking novel physical laws in unfamiliar domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train or fine-tune the LM on a broader, more diverse corpus of synthetic equations; or interleave LM-guided proposals with uniform grammar sampling to ensure coverage; test recovery on adversarially designed systems with unfamiliar functional forms.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can grammar-constrained search be scaled to high-dimensional state spaces (d ≳ 20) without prohibitive combinatorial explosion in candidate expressions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The "Limitations" section identifies that for high-dimensional spaces, "the number of admissible functional forms ... grows superexponentially, and even a constrained grammar may contain too many candidates" (Pages 9–10).
- Why unresolved: Current method evaluates thousands of candidates; scaling to high dimensions could make exhaustive search intractable, limiting use in complex multi-particle or field-theoretic systems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement adaptive search strategies (e.g., beam search with learned heuristics, hierarchical grammar decompositions) and measure exact recovery rate vs. dimension, comparing to uniform sampling baselines.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How can the typed grammar formalism be extended to support integral, stochastic differential, and partial differential operators to capture a broader class of governing equations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The "Future directions" section lists "extensions include: integral operators (for delay-differential and Volterra integral equations), Itô stochastic differential operators, partial differential operators" (Page 10).
- Why unresolved: The current grammar only handles ODEs with algebraic and transcendental operators, precluding application to systems with memory, noise, or spatial extent.
- What evidence would resolve it: Define type propagation rules for integral and derivative operators, incorporate stochastic calculus constraints, and test on benchmark PDE and SDE systems, measuring recovery against unconstrained baselines.

## Limitations
- The codebase and 133-system benchmark dataset are not yet public, hindering direct replication.
- Performance depends on correct user-specified symmetry constraints; misspecification can exclude the true equation.
- The LM's proposal quality may degrade for systems with exotic functional forms outside its training distribution.

## Confidence
- **High**: Core mechanism claims (grammar pruning, MDL/bootstrap stability) are strongly supported by paper's ablations and internal validation (e.g., Table 4, Figure 2b).
- **Medium**: Generalization to unseen physical domains; performance relies on accurate symmetry specification and derivative estimation, which vary in practice.
- **Low**: Faithful independent reproduction without the released code/dataset; external validation on user-provided noisy/occluded data.
