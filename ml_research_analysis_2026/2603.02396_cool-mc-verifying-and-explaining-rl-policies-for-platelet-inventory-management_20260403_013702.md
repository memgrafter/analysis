# COOL-MC: Verifying and Explaining RL Policies for Platelet Inventory Management

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02396
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02396
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Trained PPO policy achieves 2.9% stockout and 1.1% inventory-full probabilities over 200 steps on platelet inventory MDP, with >99.6% state space reduction via policy-induced DTMC.

## Executive Summary
COOL-MC is a framework combining reinforcement learning with probabilistic model checking and explainable RL to verify and explain policies for safety-critical inventory management. Applied to a platelet inventory MDP (Haijema et al.), it trains a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent, builds the policy-induced discrete-time Markov chain (DTMC) by exploring only reachable states, and verifies probabilistic safety properties via PCTL. The approach reduces the state space by over 99.6% and provides feature-level and counterfactual explanations, showing the policy relies primarily on age-structured inventory features rather than day-of-week or pending orders. This enables formal trust in RL-driven healthcare supply chains.

## Method Summary
COOL-MC proceeds in three stages: (1) encode the platelet inventory MDP in PRISM with age-structured variables, stochastic Poisson demand, and discrete ordering actions; (2) train a deterministic PPO policy by interacting with the model; (3) construct the induced DTMC by depth-first traversal applying the policy to every reachable state, then verify PCTL properties (e.g., probability of stockout within 200 steps) using Storm. Explanations are generated by modifying the policy (feature pruning, permutation importance, counterfactual action replacement), rebuilding the DTMC, and assessing changes in verification results.

## Key Results
- Baseline safety: `P(◊≤200 empty) = 0.029`, `P(◊≤200 full) = 0.011`.
- State space reduction: The induced DTMC contains only ~0.4% of the full MDP’s 1.68M states, making verification tractable.
- Feature importance: Pruning/permutation reveal the policy relies on age distribution (freshest and oldest stock, `x5` and `x1`) while day-of-week and pending orders are negligible.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Policy-induced DTMC reduces state space
**Claim:** The policy-induced DTMC construction reduces the verification state space by >99.6% by exploring only states reachable under the trained policy.  
**Mechanism:** Starting from the initial state, a depth-first traversal applies the deterministic policy π at each state, recursively adding only successors with non-zero transition probability (Algorithm 1). This on-the-fly construction avoids enumerating the full MDP's 1.68M states.  
**Core assumption:** The reachable state fragment under π sufficiently captures all behaviors relevant to the PCTL properties of interest (e.g., stockout within 200 steps).  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "constructs the policy-induced discrete-time Markov chain (which includes only the reachable states under the trained policy to reduce memory usage)... The induced DTMC reduces the state space by over 99.6%". Section 4.3 explains the incremental approach that constructs only the reachable fragment.  
**Break condition:** If the policy's behavior depends critically on states outside the reachable fragment (e.g., due to non-Markovian effects or poorly trained policy), verification results may be incomplete or misleading.

### Mechanism 2: Feature pruning and permutation reveal policy focus
**Claim:** Feature pruning and permutation importance reveal that the policy primarily relies on age-structured inventory features (`x4`, `x5`) for stockout avoidance and oldest stock (`x1`) for overstock prevention, while ignoring pending orders.  
**Mechanism:** Pruning zeroes a feature’s input weights, breaking its influence; permutation randomly shuffles a feature’s values across states. Both are evaluated via re-verifying the modified policy’s DTMC. Large changes in safety probabilities indicate feature importance.  
**Core assumption:** Changes in the reachable DTMC’s probabilistic properties after feature modification reflect the feature’s true influence on decision-making for the specified horizon.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract says "primarily attends to the age distribution of inventory rather than other features such as day of week or pending orders... Feature pruning and permutation importance show...". Section 5.2.2 quantifies: pruning `x5` increases stockout by +1048%, `x1` by +37% for full inventory; permutation shows `x1` critical early, `x3` later.  
**Break condition:** Feature interactions (e.g., nonlinear dependencies) may not be captured by single-feature ablation; the policy might rely on conjunctions of features that appear unimportant individually.

### Mechanism 3: Counterfactual action replacement identifies robust actions
**Claim:** Counterfactual replacement of medium-large orders (`pr14`) with smaller ones (`pr6`) leaves safety probabilities nearly unchanged, indicating those orders occur in well-buffered states.  
**Mechanism:** COOL-MC modifies the policy by substituting specific action labels (`pr14→pr6`) across all reachable states where they occur, reconstructs the induced DTMC, and re-verifies PCTL properties. The near-zero delta (0.029→0.0287 for stockout) indicates the altered actions are not critical.  
**Core assumption:** The counterfactual modification isolates the causal effect of the action change without altering other policy behaviors or state transitions.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "Counterfactual replacement ... leaves safety probabilities nearly unchanged, indicating those orders occur in well-buffered states." Section 5.2.2 reports the shift from 0.029 to 0.0287.  
**Break condition:** If `pr14` and `pr6` states differ systematically in unobserved state features or transition dynamics, the comparison may conflate action effects with state distribution shifts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Markov Decision Processes (MDPs)**  
  *Why needed:* The platelet inventory problem is formalized as an MDP (states, actions, stochastic transitions, rewards). Understanding MDP semantics is prerequisite to grasping how COOL-MC transforms it into a DTMC via a policy.  
  *Quick check:* Given an MDP state (day, age-structured inventory, pending order), what are the enabled actions and how does demand stochasticity affect transitions?

- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**  
  *Why needed:* PPO is the RL algorithm used to train the neural policy π. Knowing its output (a deterministic mapping from observations to actions) is essential to understand what "induced DTMC" means.  
  *Quick check:* Why might PPO produce a deterministic policy even though the underlying MDP has stochastic transitions?

- **Probabilistic Computational Tree Logic (PCTL)**  
  *Why needed:* PCTL properties (e.g., `P≤0.05(◊≤200 empty)`) formalize the safety questions being verified. Understanding path operators (◊, U) and probability bounds is key to interpreting results.  
  *Quick check:* What does `P=?(◊≤200 "pr6")` compute, and how would you interpret a result of 0.15?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  PRISM MDP Encoder -> RL Trainer (PPO) -> Induced DTMC Builder -> PCTL Interpreter (Storm) -> Explainability Modules (Feature pruner, Permutation ranker, Action labeler, Counterfactual replacer)

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Encode platelet inventory MDP in PRISM (state variables, transitions, labels).  
  2. Train PPO policy by interacting with PRISM simulator.  
  3. Build policy-induced DTMC from initial state via depth-first traversal applying policy π.  
  4. Run PCTL verification queries (e.g., `P=?(◊≤200 empty)`).  
  5. For explanation: modify policy (prune, permute, replace actions), go back to step 3, repeat verification.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *State space reduction vs. coverage*: Reachability-based construction is tractable but may miss states relevant to unbounded properties or under-trained policies.  
  - *Feature ablation vs. model-agnostic*: Pruning alters policy internals; permutation assesses sensitivity without structural change but may create unrealistic input distributions.  
  - *Global vs. local*: Global changes (pruning, counterfactuals) affect entire DTMC; local per-state labels require aggregating over reachable fragment.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Induced DTMC size remains large (>10% of full MDP) → policy visits too many states, possibly due to randomness or poor convergence.  
  - Pruning a feature causes large increase in stockout probability → feature is safety-critical; removal leads to unsafe policy.  
  - Counterfactual replacement of an action increases stockout probability → that action is deployed in low-buffer states; replacing it harms safety.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline verification: Train PPO, construct DTMC, verify `P=?(◊≤200 empty)` and `P=?(◊≤200 full)`. Confirm ~99.6% state reduction and record safety probabilities.  
  2. Feature pruning sensitivity: For each state feature (x1..x5, day, pend), prune and re-verify stockout probability. Identify most critical features.  
  3. Action reachability: Compute `T=?(F"pr*")` for each order quantity pr0..pr30. Determine which actions are reachable and their expected hitting times.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to other healthcare inventory MDPs:** How can COOL-MC be effectively adapted to verify and explain RL policies for different perishable medical products with varying shelf lives, demand patterns, or cost structures? The current demonstration is limited to a specific platelet inventory MDP; generalizability remains untested.  
2. **Integration of domain-expert knowledge:** What practical methodologies can integrate blood bank managers' expertise into COOL-MC's automated analysis through interactive property specification or validation of feature importance, to enhance trust and adoption? The current pipeline operates without human-in-the-loop input.  
3. **Combining with policy-agnostic test suites:** How can methods like MPTCS be systematically integrated with COOL-MC to identify critical initial states for bounded reachability verification, and does this significantly reduce computational overhead for large-scale inventory MDPs? MPTCS is proposed but not implemented or evaluated.

## Limitations
- **Reachable fragment assumption:** Verification on the policy-induced DTMC assumes all safety-critical behaviors occur within the reachable states; unreachable states could contain failure modes for unbounded or off-policy scenarios.  
- **Feature interaction missing:** Single-feature ablation may overlook critical conjunctive dependencies; the policy might rely on interactions not captured by individual pruning/permutation.  
- **Counterfactual confounding:** Global action replacement may conflate action effects with systematic state differences (e.g., `pr14` states might have inherently different demand patterns).

## Confidence
- **High:** State space reduction claim (>99.6%) and baseline verification results (2.9% stockout, 1.1% full). The on-the-fly DTMC construction is algorithmically clear and corroborated by similar case studies.  
- **Medium:** Feature importance rankings. The ablation methodology is sound, but absence of multivariate or interaction tests weakens causal claims. Permutation results are model-agnostic but may create unrealistic feature distributions.  
- **Low:** Counterfactual causal interpretation. The global action replacement is powerful but lacks formal identification; confounding by state is a credible threat.

## Next Checks
1. **MDP implementation audit:** Reconstruct the PRISM model from Haijema et al.’s description and validate transition semantics (demand Poisson rates, aging, FIFO, delivery lag) against the paper’s aggregated units and cost parameters.  
2. **Policy convergence verification:** Train PPO for 25k episodes and confirm the sliding-window average reward stabilizes near the reported ~-47.13 before DTMC construction; if not, extend training and measure DTMC size sensitivity.  
3. **Feature interaction test:** Perform a multivariate ablation study: remove pairs of features (e.g., `x1`+`pend`, `x4`+`x5`) and re-verify safety probabilities. A significant delta would indicate underestimated interaction effects.
