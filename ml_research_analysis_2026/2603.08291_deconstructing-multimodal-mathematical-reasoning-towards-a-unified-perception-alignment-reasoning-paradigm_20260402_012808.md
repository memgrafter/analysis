# Deconstructing Multimodal Mathematical Reasoning: Towards a Unified Perception-Alignment-Reasoning Paradigm

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.08291
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08291
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces the Perception-Alignment-Reasoning (PAR) paradigm and Answer-Process-Executable (APE) evaluation hierarchy to unify and diagnose multimodal mathematical reasoning research, revealing widespread lack of explicit alignment and fine-grained evaluation.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal mathematical reasoning (MMR) research suffers from fragmentation, with methods and benchmarks varying widely in scope and evaluation criteria. This paper proposes a unified framework—the Perception-Alignment-Reasoning (PAR) pipeline—to decompose MMR into three interdependent stages: extracting structured mathematical evidence (perception), mapping evidence to symbolic/executable representations (alignment), and performing verifiable inference (reasoning). Complementing this, the Answer-Process-Executable (APE) evaluation hierarchy escalates from answer-only to process and executable verification. Through systematic categorization of over 40 recent MMR methods and 30+ benchmarks, the authors find that most methods omit explicit alignment and rely on answer-centric benchmarks, while only a minority provide process or executable supervision. The analysis identifies critical challenges: improving structured perception, unifying alignment interfaces, and scaling executable evaluation. The PAR-APE framework offers a common language for comparing MMR systems and guiding future development toward more rigorous, interpretable reasoning.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a meta-analysis rather than a new model. It conducts a systematic literature review of multimodal mathematical reasoning methods and benchmarks, classifying them via the proposed PAR and APE taxonomies. For methods, each is assigned to one of the PAR stages based on its primary contribution (perception, alignment, or reasoning). For benchmarks, they are labeled with an APE level (Answer, Process, or Executable) depending on the supervision granularity provided. The authors aggregate these classifications to reveal trends and gaps in the field. No empirical experiments are performed; the study relies on qualitative synthesis of existing works.

## Key Results
- Proposed the PAR framework, decomposing MMR into perception (fact extraction), alignment (executable intermediate construction), and reasoning (verifiable inference), alongside the APE evaluation hierarchy (Answer, Process, Executable).
- Analysis of 40+ methods and 30+ benchmarks shows that most methods do not explicitly address alignment and that answer-only evaluation dominates, with only a minority offering process or executable supervision.
- Identifies key challenges: developing robust structured perception, standardizing alignment interfaces, and creating scalable executable evaluation protocols.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decomposition isolates failure modes
**Claim:** Decomposing multimodal mathematical reasoning into the Perception–Alignment–Reasoning (PAR) pipeline isolates failure modes and enables targeted improvements.
**Mechanism:** The PAR framework forces explicit separation: perception extracts structured mathematical facts; alignment maps these facts to symbolic/executable intermediates; reasoning performs verifiable inference over aligned representations. This prevents ungrounded "black-box" reasoning where visual evidence is lost.
**Core assumption:** Errors primarily stem from one of three deficiencies: inaccurate perception, misalignment, or flawed inference over misaligned data.
**Evidence anchors:** The paper defines the three stages and notes that existing works implicitly validate subsets (e.g., Math-PUMA, Pi-GPS implement alignment and executability). No direct contradiction found in the corpus.
**Break condition:** If end-to-end MLLMs achieve high accuracy without explicit alignment or executable intermediates on diverse tasks, the necessity of full PAR for verifiable reasoning would be weakened. Current answer-centric benchmarks allow such shortcuts.

### Mechanism 2: Executable intermediates enforce verifiability
**Claim:** Using executable intermediates (e.g., programs, formal proofs) during alignment enforces verifiability and grounds reasoning in computable symbolism.
**Mechanism:** Requiring models to produce an executable intermediate representation makes every step falsifiable via a symbolic engine (solver/interpreter). This moves evaluation beyond answer-matching to process validation (APE's "Executable" level).
**Core assumption:** Executable intermediates bridge neural perception and symbolic reasoning, reducing hallucination and enabling automatic correctness checking.
**Evidence anchors:** The paper argues that executability anchors alignment and makes reasoning verifiable; benchmarks like GeoQA+ use executable programs, showing empirical viability. No evidence suggests executability harms performance when properly integrated.
**Break condition:** If executable intermediates consistently degrade performance on diverse MMR tasks due to brittleness or coverage gaps, their utility would be limited to narrow domains. The paper notes this fragility as an open challenge.

### Mechanism 3: APE hierarchy diagnoses reasoning fidelity
**Claim:** Shifting evaluation from answer-only (A) to process (P) and executable (E) levels (APE hierarchy) better diagnose reasoning fidelity and distinguish luck from genuine understanding.
**Mechanism:** APE provides graduated supervision: answer-level is cheap but ambiguous; process-level checks step validity; executable-level offers machine-verifiable pass/fail. This incentivizes models to produce faithful, auditable chains.
**Core assumption:** Final answer accuracy is an insufficient proxy for reasoning quality in complex multimodal settings where perception and alignment errors can be masked by lucky guesses.
**Evidence anchors:** The paper notes that only a minority of benchmarks provide fine-grained supervision; examples like MM-MATH (process) and GeoQA+ (executable) exist but are rare. This supports the claim that evaluation is lagging.
**Break condition:** If process/executable evaluation proves too costly or inconsistent to scale across diverse MMR tasks, the field may default to answer-only metrics, undermining PAR's diagnostic power.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) architecture**  
  Why needed: Understanding PAR requires knowing how visual encoders, language models, and projection layers interact; misalignment often occurs at the vision-language interface.

- **Concept: Symbolic execution & domain-specific languages (DSLs)**  
  Why needed: Alignment's executability relies on converting perception outputs into a formal language that a solver can run; knowledge of DSLs (e.g., geometry description language, SQL) is prerequisite.  
  Quick check: Why is a geometry "constraint-based DSL" more suitable for executable reasoning than a free-form natural language program trace?

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Process Feedback (RLHF/RLAIF variant)**  
  Why needed: Reasoning paradigms often use RL to optimize long-horizon chains; process reward models (PRMs) are key for training MMR systems with intermediate supervision.  
  Quick check: What is the difference between an outcome reward (final answer correct) and a process reward (step is valid and grounded), and why is the latter crucial for MMR?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (multimodal) -> Perception (Vision Encoder + Parser) outputs F (structured facts) -> Alignment (Translator) outputs I (executable intermediate) -> Reasoning (LLM/Solver) outputs C (chain) and a (answer) -> APE Evaluator (scores A/P/E).

**Critical path:** Information flows sequentially: perception must extract accurate F; alignment must produce executable I; reasoning must generate faithful C; evaluation must assess all levels. Failures at any stage cascade downstream.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- *Structured vs. Neural Perception:* Hand-crafted parsers (precise, brittle) vs. MLLM-based extraction (flexible, noisy).
- *Executable vs. Flexible Alignment:* DSLs enable verification but restrict expressiveness; free-form code is expressive but harder to verify.
- *Deep vs. Adaptive Reasoning:* Fixed-depth CoT (cheap, may drift) vs. RL/MCTS (stable, computationally heavy).

**Failure signatures:**
- Perception error: missed geometric primitive or misread chart label → corrupt F.
- Alignment drift: I semantically diverges from F (e.g., referencing non-existent point) → silent until answer fails.
- Reasoning hallucination: invalid inference step unsupported by F or I → detected only by process/executable evaluation.
- Evaluation mismatch: high answer score but low process/executable score → indicates lucky guesses or data contamination.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Perception sanity check:** Isolate F extraction on synthetic geometry/charts with gold F; measure precision/recall. Target: >95% on simple, >85% on complex.
2. **Alignment executability test:** Use gold F from Exp 1, run alignment to I, feed I to a symbolic solver; measure execution success rate. Target: >90%. If low, alignment is bottleneck.
3. **End-to-end APE correlation:** Run full PAR pipeline on a small benchmark with APE annotations; compare answer, process, executable scores. Expect: Answer score ≥ Process ≥ Executable; large gaps indicate shortcuts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several key challenges that represent open questions for future research:
- Developing robust structured perception.
- Standardizing alignment interfaces.
- Creating scalable executable evaluation protocols.

## Limitations
- Manual categorization of 40+ methods and 30+ benchmarks introduces subjectivity; inter-annotator agreement is unreported.
- No empirical ablation of PAR components; claims about necessity rely on indirect evidence from existing works.
- APE evaluation depends on sparse fine-grained annotations; scaling may be costly or inconsistent across modalities.
- Analysis may exclude very recent MLLMs that could implicitly achieve alignment, potentially undermining the explicit separation premise.
- Low citation counts and moderate neighbor field relevance indicate the framework has not yet seen wide adoption.

## Confidence
- PAR decomposition isolates failure modes: Medium
- Executable intermediates enforce verifiability: Medium
- APE hierarchy diagnoses reasoning fidelity: Medium-Low
- Unified framework offers a common language for MMR: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Replication of taxonomy:** Systematically sample a new set of MMR papers and benchmarks, annotate for PAR stages and APE levels, compute inter-annotator agreement to quantify reliability.
2. **Controlled PAR ablation:** On two representative MMR tasks, compare an end-to-end MLLM against a system with explicit perception→alignment→reasoning modules; measure answer, process, and executable scores to test whether PAR components improve verifiable performance.
3. **Error-mode audit:** Collect failure cases from existing MMR models (with gold intermediates), manually label each as perception, alignment, or reasoning error, and analyze distribution to validate alignment as a dominant bottleneck.
