# Make Geometry Matter for Spatial Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- ArXiv ID: 2603.26639
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26639
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GeoSR achieves 51.9% average accuracy on VSI-Bench (static) and 66.1% on DSR-Bench (dynamic), outperforming prior spatial reasoning models.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language models (VLMs) often rely on 2D appearance shortcuts—such as textures and object co-occurrence—when answering spatial questions, bypassing genuine 3D geometric reasoning. GeoSR addresses this by introducing two complementary mechanisms: Geometry-Unleashing Masking, which forces the model to consult geometry tokens by strategically masking vision tokens during training, and Geometry-Guided Fusion, a token‑ and channel‑wise gated router that adaptively integrates geometric evidence where it is most needed. On the VSI-Bench (static) and DSR-Bench (dynamic) benchmarks, GeoSR sets new state‑of‑the‑art results of 51.9% and 66.1% average accuracy, respectively. Ablations confirm that both components are essential, with masking being particularly critical for dynamic scenes.

## Method Summary
GeoSR processes a video and associated question using frozen encoders. The VLM’s vision encoder produces 2D vision tokens *F_V*. A pretrained geometry model (VGGT for static scenes, π3 for dynamic ones) generates geometry tokens *F_G*. To make the model depend on geometry, GeoSR applies **Geometry‑Unleashing Masking**: for static data, a random subset (γ=80%) of *F_V* is zeroed with probability β=0.5; for dynamic data, a QFormer computes relevance scores between question‑guided bottleneck tokens and *F_G*, then masks the top‑K most relevant vision tokens, removing easy 2D cues. The geometry tokens are prepared—static uses an MLP and reshape to match *F_V* resolution, while dynamic uses a cross‑attention to redistribute a global summary *Z_G* (from the QFormer) to token‑level geometry, creating ˜*F_G*. **Geometry‑Guided Fusion** then computes a gate α per token and channel from normalized ˜*F_V* and ˜*F_G*: fused tokens are α ⊙ ˜*F_V* + (1‑α) ⊙ ˜*F_G*. For dynamic inputs, *Z_G* is also appended. The fused sequence (plus text tokens) is fed to the frozen VLM backbone (Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B), which is fine‑tuned along with the fusion gate (and the QFormer for dynamic scenarios). Masking is disabled during inference.

## Key Results
- **Static benchmark (VSI‑Bench):** GeoSR reaches 51.9% average accuracy, setting a new state‑of‑the‑art.
- **Dynamic benchmark (DSR‑Bench):** GeoSR achieves 66.1%, substantially surpassing the previous best (GSM at 58.9%) and proprietary models.
- **Ablation study:** Removing either masking or gated fusion degrades performance; masking removal causes a larger drop on DSR‑Bench (from 66.1% to 58.1%), underscoring its particular importance for dynamic scenes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Geometry‑Unleashing Masking
- **Claim:** Random or geometry‑guided masking of 2D vision tokens during training forces the VLM to rely on geometric evidence from the injected geometry tokens for spatial reasoning, counteracting its default reliance on appearance shortcuts.
- **Mechanism:** A subset of vision tokens is zeroed. Static masking is random; dynamic masking targets regions corresponding to high‑relevance geometry tokens identified via QFormer attention. This removes easy 2D cues, making geometry tokens necessary.
- **Core assumption:** The VLM backbone, when unimpeded, prefers 2D appearance features over less‑salient geometry tokens for spatial questions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract explicitly mentions masking to weaken non‑geometric shortcuts; Section 3.2 provides equations for random and TopK masking; neighboring works do not employ such masking for geometry enforcement.
- **Break condition:** If the masking ratio is too high, essential visual context is destroyed; if too low, shortcuts remain available.

### Mechanism 2: Geometry‑Guided Fusion
- **Claim:** A token‑ and channel‑wise gated fusion mechanism adaptively amplifies the contribution of geometry tokens only where and when they provide critical evidence, preventing dilution or dismissal of geometric information.
- **Mechanism:** After masking, the masked vision tokens ˜*F_V* and redistributed geometry ˜*F_G* are combined via a learned gate α computed per token and channel from normalized features; the fused token is α·V + (1‑α)·G.
- **Core assumption:** The utility of geometry tokens is spatially and semantically heterogeneous; some regions/times require strong geometric grounding while others do not.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes gated routing; Section 3.3 defines the gated fusion equations; adjacent works do not use a learned fine‑grained gating router triggered by masking.
- **Break condition:** If the gating module fails to learn meaningful alignment (α becomes uniform), fusion degenerates to naive addition or concatenation.

### Mechanism 3: Synergy of Unleashing and Guidance
- **Claim:** The combination of “unleashing” (forcing geometry use via masking) and “guidance” (adaptive fusion) is synergistic and essential; neither alone achieves the reported gains, especially on dynamic benchmarks.
- **Mechanism:** Masking creates a training signal that makes geometry consultation necessary. Gated fusion then provides a controlled pathway to integrate that evidence without overwhelming non‑geometric context where irrelevant, making geometry both actionable and controllable.
- **Core assumption:** Simple injection (naive fusion) is insufficient because the model can and will ignore geometry; effective use requires both coercion and intelligent routing.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions both designs; Section 4.3 ablation tables show clear performance drops when either component is removed, with masking being particularly critical for dynamic scenes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: 2D Appearance Shortcuts in VLMs**  
  - **Why needed here:** The entire premise is that VLMs default to 2D visual signals (textures, object co‑occurrence) for spatial questions when available, bypassing 3D geometric reasoning. Understanding this failure mode is the problem’s root.  
  - **Quick check:** If a VLM sees a photo of a large car next to a small person, what shortcut might it take to answer “how far away is the car from the person?” instead of inferring 3D distances?
- **Concept: Token‑Level Modality Fusion**  
  - **Why needed here:** GeoSR operates at the token level, merging vision and geometry embeddings before the LLM. Knowing the baseline (simple addition/concatenation) is necessary to contrast with the gated approach.  
  - **Quick check:** What are two common but naive ways to combine two sets of visual tokens (e.g., RGB and depth) before feeding them to a transformer?
- **Concept: Attention‑Based Relevance Scoring**  
  - **Why needed here:** For dynamic masking, the paper uses QFormer attention weights between text‑guided bottleneck tokens and geometry tokens to identify which geometry tokens are question‑relevant. This is a specific technique for soft region selection.  
  - **Quick check:** In a cross‑attention module where queries are text‑derived and keys are geometry tokens, what does a high attention weight for a specific geometry token imply about that token’s role?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. **Input:** Sequence of video frames + text question.  
  2. **Frozen Vision Tokenizer:** (e.g., from Qwen2.5‑VL) encodes frames into *F_V*.  
  3. **Frozen Geometry Tokenizer:** (e.g., VGGT for static, π3 for dynamic) encodes frames into *F_G*.  
  4. **Geometry‑Unleashing Masking:** Generates binary mask *m* applied to *F_V* to produce ˜*F_V*. Implementation differs for static (random) vs. dynamic (TopK from QFormer attention).  
  5. **QFormer (Dynamic‑only):** Takes *F_P* (text) to get *F_B*, then cross‑attends to *F_G* to get *Z_G* and relevance scores *s* for masking and later fusion.  
  6. **Geometry Feature Prep:** For static: ˜*F_G* = MLP(Reshape(*F_G*)). For dynamic: ˜*F_G* = CrossAttn3(Interp(ˆ*F_G*′), *Z_G*) redistributes *Z_G* to token‑level geometry.  
  7. **Geometry‑Guided Fusion Gate:** Computes α = σ(*W*<sub>g</sub>[V∥G] + *b*<sub>g</sub>) where V=LN(˜*F_V*), G=LN(˜*F_G*).  
  8. **Fused Tokens:** *F* = α * V + (1‑α) * G. For dynamic, *Z_G* is also appended: *F*<sub>final</sub> = [*F*, *Z_G*].  
  9. **Frozen VLM Backbone:** (e.g., Qwen2.5‑VL) processes *F*<sub>final</sub> + text tokens to generate answer.  
  10. **Training:** Only Fusion Gate, (optionally) VLM backbone, and (for dynamic) QFormer are trained. Tokenizers and 3D models are frozen.

- **Critical path:** ˜*F_V* ← Masking(*F_V*); ˜*F_G* ← Prep(*F_G*); *F* ← Gate(˜*F_V*, ˜*F_G*); → VLM. For dynamic, additionally *Z_G* ← QFormer(text, *F_G*) (used in masking and fusion/appending).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Masking ratio (γ):** Higher forces geometry use but risks losing vital 2D context.  
  - **Dynamic vs. static masking:** TopK adds QFormer compute but targets relevant regions; random masking is simpler for static scenes.  
  - **Fusion gate complexity:** Adds minimal parameters but requires training; naive fusion is cheaper but less effective.  
  - **Frozen 3D model choice:** VGGT (static) vs. π3 (dynamic)—using the wrong prior harms performance.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Masking‑related: Performance plummets if β=1.0 or γ too low;  
  - Gate‑related: α statistics near 0 or 1 globally, indicating no routing;  
  - Architecture mismatch: Applying QFormer‑style fusion for static tasks yields negligible gains;  
  - Geometry source mismatch: Feeding a dynamic‑optimized model to static scenes (or vice versa) underperforms.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Ablation on Masking:** Train with/without Geo. Mask. (keep Geo. Fus.). Expect significant drop, especially on DSR‑Bench.  
  2. **Ablation on Fusion:** Train with Geo. Mask. but replace Geo. Fus. with naive addition/concatenation. Expect drop on both benchmarks.  
  3. **Geometry Branch Ablation:** Train full GeoSR then zero out *F_G* at inference. Expect major drop, proving reliance on the geometry stream.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How to build spatial reasoning benchmarks with unambiguous geometry and consistent annotations?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** The authors state: “An important future direction is therefore to improve dataset quality, especially in terms of geometry‑aware question construction and annotation consistency.”  
   - **Why unresolved:** Current benchmarks contain ambiguous questions and inconsistent labels (Figures 6,7), undermining reliable evaluation.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** New benchmarks created with expert annotators, geometric constraint validation, and inter‑annotator agreement metrics; test models on these datasets to isolate capability from data issues.

2. **Why is Geometry‑Unleashing Masking more critical for dynamic than static spatial reasoning?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** The abstract notes: “Ablations confirm both components are essential, with masking being particularly critical for dynamic scenes.”  
   - **Why unresolved:** The underlying cause—whether due to greater appearance shortcuts, motion complexity, or geometry informativeness—remains unexplained.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze reliance on 2D cues, study masking patterns, and evaluate on dynamic vs. static subsets with varying difficulty.

3. **Is the geometry‑guided TopK masking necessary for dynamic spatial reasoning, or could random masking suffice?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** GeoSR uses TopK masking for dynamic scenes based on QFormer relevance scores, but only random masking for static; no direct comparison for dynamic.  
   - **Why unresolved:** TopK may be crucial for handling spatiotemporal dynamics or may be an over‑engineered solution.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare random vs. TopK masking on dynamic benchmarks, measuring accuracy and examining mask relevance to geometric ground truth.

4. **How well do GeoSR’s hyperparameters (masking ratio γ, enable probability β) generalize across different VLM backbones and geometry extractors?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** Hyperparameters were tuned on Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B with VGGT/π3; no study of sensitivity to model variations.  
   - **Why unresolved:** Different architectures have varying token resolutions, attention mechanisms, and geometry representations, potentially affecting optimal settings.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate GeoSR with diverse VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, InternVL) and geometry models (e.g., DUSt3R, TripoSR), performing hyperparameter sweeps and analyzing performance trends.

## Limitations
- Dependence on specific pretrained geometry extractors (VGGT/π3) and the Qwen2.5‑VL backbone may limit generalizability to other model families.
- The evaluation relies on the paper’s proprietary benchmarks (VSI‑Bench, DSR‑Bench); without external validation, results may not reflect broader spatial reasoning capabilities.
- Hyperparameters (masking ratio γ, enable probability β, learning rates) were tuned for a single setup; their sensitivity to architecture changes is unknown, potentially affecting reproducibility and transfer.
