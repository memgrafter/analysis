# LK Losses: Direct Acceptance Rate Optimization for Speculative Decoding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23881
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23881
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: LK losses (likelihood-based and hybrid adaptive) directly optimize the acceptance rate, achieving up to 8–10% higher average acceptance length τ compared to standard KL divergence training, with no added computational cost.

## Executive Summary
Speculative decoding accelerates large language model inference by using a small draft model to predict multiple tokens ahead, which are then verified in parallel by the larger target model. The training objective for the draft model is typically KL divergence minimization, which serves as a proxy for maximizing the acceptance rate. However, for capacity-limited drafts, KL minimization leads to suboptimal acceptance because it encourages mode-covering behavior. This paper introduces LK losses—two novel objectives that directly optimize the acceptance rate: a likelihood-based loss (−log α) and a hybrid loss blending KL and total variation (TV) divergence with an adaptive scheduler. Evaluated across four draft architectures and six target models (8B to 685B parameters), both LK variants consistently improve average acceptance length τ, with the hybrid adaptive loss achieving gains up to 8–10%, particularly for smaller drafts. LK losses require no architectural changes and serve as drop-in replacements for existing training objectives.

## Method Summary
The authors propose two training objectives that directly maximize the acceptance rate α = Σ min(p(x), q(x)), where p is the target distribution and q the draft distribution. The first, Lα_LK = −log α, is a likelihood-based loss that naturally scales gradients. The second, Lλ_LK, is a hybrid: λ·KL(p∥q) + (1−λ)·TV(p,q) with λ = exp(−η·sg[α]), adaptively scheduling between KL (early training, stable gradients) and TV (late training, direct optimization). The training pipeline uses a corpus of 660K prompts; for each prompt the target model generates responses to obtain token-level target distributions. The draft model (e.g., EAGLE-3, MEDUSA) is trained from scratch (except DeepSeek-MTP fine-tuned) with aggregated loss across K speculative heads, using exponential decay weighting (γ=0.8). Evaluation is performed with a patched vLLM ensuring proper rejection sampling at temperature T=1. The method introduces no overhead and replaces the standard KL loss.

## Key Results
- Both LK losses (likelihood and hybrid) consistently outperform KL training in average acceptance length τ across all tested draft architectures and target models.
- The hybrid adaptive loss yields the strongest improvements, achieving up to +8.2% τ gain over KL, with especially large gains (8–10%) for smaller draft models (e.g., MEDUSA, MLP speculator).
- Gains are robust across target model sizes (8B to 685B) and evaluation datasets (MT-bench, HumanEval, GSM8K), and are observed at both greedy (T=0) and sampled (T=1) inference.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: KL divergence proxy fails for capacity-limited drafts**  
Standard KL(p∥q) minimization does not guarantee maximum acceptance when the draft cannot perfectly match the target. KL penalizes any discrepancy but assigns finite penalty to all target support, encouraging the draft to spread probability mass broadly (mode-covering) to avoid high penalties on low-probability tokens. This reduces overlap with the target's high-probability tokens, lowering acceptance. The issue arises because both objectives share a global optimum (p=q) but diverge at suboptimal solutions; KL's gradient direction does not align with acceptance maximization when capacity is insufficient. Evidence: The paper's Gaussian mixture example shows KL yields α=50.2% while TV achieves α=60.2%. If the draft capacity is high enough to match the target, the problem vanishes.

**Mechanism 2: Pure TV minimization is impractical from random initialization**  
While TV distance directly optimizes acceptance, its gradient has problematic scaling when training starts from random weights. In typical settings, the target p is concentrated on k tokens while the draft q is nearly uniform over vocabulary size V. The gradient magnitude of TV scales as O(√k/V), which is vanishingly small for large V (e.g., 100k+). Moreover, the gradient depends only on the sign of (qi−pi) and is non-differentiable along the manifold q=p. These properties cause slow or failed training. Evidence: Section 4.1 derives ∇TV = ½ q ⊙ (s−E_q[s]) and shows its norm is O(√k/V). Section 4.2 states these issues make pure TV impractical from scratch. Pretrained drafts might avoid this, but training from scratch does not.

**Mechanism 3: Adaptive hybrid LK loss combines stable early gradients with direct late optimization**  
The hybrid Lλ_LK = λ·KL + (1−λ)·TV, with λ = exp(−η·sg[α]), adaptively transitions from KL-dominated early training to TV-dominated later training. Early on, low acceptance α → λ≈1, providing the smooth, well-scaled gradients of KL that keep q within a trust region. As α improves, λ decays, allowing TV to refine the overlap directly. This mimics a trust-region approach: KL acts as a constraint preventing q from drifting into regions where TV gradients become unstable. Evidence: Section 6.1 shows the hybrid (η=3) beats pure KL, pure TV, and fixed λ=0.5. Section 6.2 demonstrates consistent gains (up to +8.2%) across diverse targets and drafts, especially for small drafts.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Speculative Decoding Mechanics**  
Why needed here: To understand the acceptance rate α (expected tokens accepted per speculation) and why maximizing α directly improves inference speed.  
Quick check: Given token distributions p = [0.7, 0.2, 0.1] and q = [0.5, 0.3, 0.2], compute α = Σ min(p,q) = min(0.7,0.5)+min(0.2,0.3)+min(0.1,0.2)=0.5+0.2+0.1=0.8. If q changes to [0.6,0.3,0.1], α = min(0.7,0.6)+min(0.2,0.3)+min(0.1,0.1)=0.6+0.2+0.1=0.9 – the second q is better.

**Concept: Probability Divergences (KL vs. TV)**  
Why needed here: To grasp why KL is only a proxy and TV directly corresponds to acceptance, and their differing optimization incentives (mode-covering vs. mode-seeking).  
Quick check: For a target peaked on one token (e.g., p(x*)=0.9, others 0.1/(V−1)), minimizing KL(p∥q) encourages q to allocate mass to all tokens to avoid infinite penalty, while minimizing TV(p,q) encourages q to put all mass on x* to minimize max|p−q|. The latter yields higher expected min(p,q) (acceptance).

**Concept: Gradient Dynamics in Neural Training**  
Why needed here: To understand why gradient magnitude and smoothness matter when training from random initialization, and how the hybrid schedule leverages different gradient behaviors at different stages.  
Quick check: In the regime of diffuse q (uniform) and concentrated p (on k tokens), the gradient norm of TV scales with √k/V (e.g., V=100k, k=5 → √5/100k ≈ 0.00007), while KL's gradient scales with something like 1 (independent of V). The TV gradient vanishes, making learning impossible.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Training Corpus -> Target Model (p) -> Target Distributions -> Draft Model (q) -> Loss Module (KL/TV/LK) -> Optimizer -> Trained Draft Model.  
Inference: Input -> Draft Model (samples draft tokens) -> Target Model (verifies) -> Accepted/Rejected -> Output.

**Critical path**:  
1. Data Generation: Run each target model on 660K prompts to collect (context, target distribution) pairs for each speculative position.  
2. Model Initialization: Build draft architecture (EAGLE-3, MEDUSA, MLP, or load DeepSeek-MTP). For EAGLE-3 with truncation, define vocabulary mask.  
3. Training Loop:  
   a. Forward pass: draft outputs q_probs.  
   b. Compute loss L(p,q) per position, using appropriate masking for truncated vocab.  
   c. Calculate acceptance α per position for loss weighting and λ scheduling.  
   d. Aggregate loss across K heads with exponential decay γ^(n-1).  
   e. Backward, optimizer step (AdamW, lr=4e-4, clip=0.5).  
4. Evaluation: Load trained draft into patched vLLM; measure τ on MT-bench, HumanEval, GSM8K at T=0 and T=1.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Likelihood-based (Lα_LK) vs. Hybrid (Lλ_LK): Lα_LK simpler, automatically scaled; hybrid may achieve higher final τ by switching to TV but requires tuning η (e.g., η=3 for most, η=10 for MEDUSA).  
- Vocabulary Truncation: Reduces draft LM head latency but breaks unmodified KL; LK losses naturally handle truncation, while KL needs masking (approximation).  
- Architectural Capacity: Gains from LK are larger for lower-capacity drafts (MEDUSA, MLP) that cannot match target; for high-capacity drafts (EAGLE-3), KL already performs well, so gains are smaller but still present.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Pure TV training collapses: τ near random (e.g., 2.6-3.4 vs KL ~3.4-4.8). Check gradient norms early; expect vanishing ∇TV.  
- Hybrid with bad λ schedule: Fixed λ=0.5 underperforms; adaptive λ must decay with rising α. Monitor α and λ; if α does not increase, λ stays high and behaves like KL.  
- Training-evaluation mismatch: Using greedy draft sampling during evaluation (unpatched vLLM) underreports α, especially at T=1. Ensure stochastic drafting.  
- Truncation handling: Unmasked KL yields NaN/Inf for OOV tokens; must mask before softmax.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. Baseline replication: Train EAGLE-3 for Llama-3.1-8B with KL loss; verify τ on MT-bench (T=1) matches paper (~3.39).  
2. Gradient magnitude check: In first 100 steps, log ∥∇KL∥, ∥∇TV∥, ∥∇Lα∥ on fixed batch; confirm ∇TV is orders smaller and scales with V, while ∇KL and ∇Lα remain stable.  
3. Ablation of λ scheduling: Train with hybrid but fixed λ=0.5 vs. adaptive (η=3); compare τ on HumanEval. Also test η=1, η=10 to see sensitivity (MEDUSA needs η=10).  

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1: Direct optimization of system efficiency**  
Question: How can we directly optimize system efficiency (ratio of accepted tokens to drafted tokens) instead of acceptance length τ, and does this yield further speedup?  
Basis: Conclusion's Future Work explicitly mentions this extension.  
Why unresolved: τ correlates with efficiency but is not identical; a different trade-off might exist.  
Evidence needed: Derive a loss that maximizes efficiency, train and compare both τ and wall-clock speed.

**Open Question 2: Learnable per-head loss aggregation**  
Question: Can we replace the fixed exponential decay weighting over draft heads with a learnable, dynamic scheme that adapts to cumulative acceptance rates, and would this boost τ?  
Basis: Future Work notes that static weighting may be suboptimal.  
Why unresolved: The current fixed schedule does not account for varying per-head acceptance across architectures/targets.  
Evidence needed: Design a parameterized weighting module trained alongside the draft; compare τ against fixed exponential.

**Open Question 3: Incorporating deployment-level sampling (top-k, top-p)**  
Question: How can we include top-k and top-p parameters in the training objective, and does this increase acceptance under those regimes?  
Basis: Future Work states training should account for typical truncations.  
Why unresolved: Current LK losses assume full temperature-scaled sampling; mismatch with truncated deployments may lower acceptance.  
Evidence needed: Extend losses to condition on (k,p) pairs; evaluate τ under various top-k/p settings with and without conditioning.

## Limitations
- The exact computation of `sg[α]` (stop-gradient acceptance aggregation across batch/heads) is unspecified, risking scheduler mismatch.
- The required vLLM patch for correct rejection sampling is not detailed; evaluation may suffer from greedy draft sampling artifacts.
- Training data generation details (target model decoding strategy, response truncation) are missing, potentially affecting draft-target alignment.

## Confidence
- KL divergence fails to maximize acceptance for limited-capacity drafts: High
- Pure TV optimization is impractical from random initialization: High
- Hybrid adaptive LK loss yields the strongest acceptance gains: High

## Next Checks
1. **Gradient norm scaling**: With random draft initialization, compute ∥∇KL∥, ∥∇TV∥, ∥∇Lα∥ on a fixed batch. Verify ∇TV is orders of magnitude smaller and scales with V, while ∇KL and ∇Lα remain stable.
2. **Adaptive scheduler dynamics**: Train a draft with Lλ_LK for η = 1, 3, 10. Record α and λ per step. Expect λ to decay as α rises; confirm η=3 (or η=10 for MEDUSA) gives highest τ and that fixed λ=0.5 underperforms.
3. **Independent evaluation verification**: Reimplement correct rejection sampling (chain-based, stochastic drafting) outside vLLM. Run inference with a trained hybrid-loss draft on 100 test prompts; compare τ against paper's vLLM-based numbers to rule out patch-specific artifacts.
