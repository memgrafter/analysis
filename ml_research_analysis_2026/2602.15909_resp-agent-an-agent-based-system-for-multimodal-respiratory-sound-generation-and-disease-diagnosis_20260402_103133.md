# Resp-Agent: An Agent-Based System for Multimodal Respiratory Sound Generation and Disease Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15909
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15909
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves 72.7% on ICBHI benchmark (Sp=79.3, Se=66.1), exceeding prior SOTA by over 5 absolute points; under cross-domain evaluation, Thinker-A2CA-guided synthesis raises long-tail Macro-F1 to 0.598.

## Executive Summary
Resp-Agent is a closed-loop agent system that tackles two key challenges in respiratory sound diagnosis: information loss from traditional spectrogram features and severe class imbalance limiting rare disease data. It orchestrates a generator and a diagnoser via a Thinker-A2CA planner. The generator synthesizes controllable audio by disentangling pathology (from LLM-generated clinical text) and acoustic style (from BEATs tokens). The diagnoser employs modality weaving to fuse text and strategic audio anchors, capturing transient pathological events. On the ICBHI benchmark, Resp-Agent sets a new state-of-the-art. Under a strict cross-domain evaluation on the large-scale Resp-229k dataset, its targeted synthesis strategy significantly boosts performance on rare classes compared to random rebalancing or non-generative methods.

## Method Summary
The system is a closed loop where a Thinker-A2CA (based on DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp) schedules audio synthesis to address the diagnoser's weaknesses. The **Generator** (Resp-MLLM) takes a disease label (converted to text by an LLM) and style tokens from pooled BEATs features. It predicts acoustic units autoregressively with a leak-free masking strategy, then uses Conditional Flow Matching with a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to decode a mel-spectrogram, finally synthesized to waveform by a neural vocoder. The **Diagnoser** is a Longformer that performs *modality weaving*: at the embedding layer, a trainable projection injects BEATs frame features in place of audio placeholder spans. It uses *strategic global attention* focused on the `[CLS]` token, `[DESCRIPTION]` text sentinel, and audio anchors placed every 4 frames (~80ms). Both components are trained with standard optimizers (AdamW) and regularization like token/frame dropout.

## Key Results
-   **ICBHI Benchmark:** 72.7% score (Sp=79.3, Se=66.1), a >5 point absolute improvement over previous state-of-the-art.
-   **Cross-Domain Long-Tail Performance:** On the strict Resp-229k test set (KAUH/COUGHVID), Thinker-A2CA-guided synthesis elevates Macro-F1 on the 8 rarest classes to 0.598, substantially outperforming random rebalancing (0.512) and non-generative remedies (0.267).
-   **Component Contribution:** Ablations confirm the benefit of modality weaving and strategic audio anchoring in the diagnoser.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core innovation is the **closed-loop agent** that actively identifies and fills data gaps. Instead of blind oversampling, Thinker-A2CA uses error profiles and calibrated confidence to schedule *targeted* synthesis for weak classes/demographics. This focuses computational resources where they most improve the diagnoser. The generator's **disentangled representation** (LLM text for pathology, BEATs for style) enables controllable synthesis of specific diseases while preserving natural acoustic diversity. The diagnoser's **modality weaving with strategic anchors** ensures early and frequent fusion of text and audio, which is critical for capturing brief, transient respiratory events (like crackles) that might be lost in global pooling or late fusion.

## Foundational Learning
*   **BEATs Acoustic Tokens:** Pre-trained model providing dense, semantically rich audio representations. *Why needed:* Serves as a discrete, compressible "style" code, disentangled from disease label text. *Quick check:* Extract features for a sample clip; verify dimensionality and that nearby tokens have similar acoustic properties.
*   **Flow Matching (CFM):** A simulation-free diffusion method that learns a deterministic ODE path between noise and data. *Why needed:* Enables high-quality, fast generation from the predicted acoustic unit sequence. *Quick check:* Train a small CFM on unit embeddings; confirm reconstruction loss decreases and generated samples are coherent.
*   **Modality Weaving:** Early injection of one modality's features (BEATs) into the embedding space of another (text) via placeholder replacement and projection. *Why needed:* Prevents late-fusion information loss and allows the transformer to learn fine-grained cross-modal alignments for transient sounds. *Quick check:* Visualize attention maps; verify audio anchors attend to relevant text tokens describing wheezes/crackles.
*   **Uncertainty-Aware Synthesis Scheduling:** Using model confidence (e.g., MC dropout) to decide which classes need more synthetic data. *Why needed:* Addresses class imbalance efficiently by focusing on hard/rare examples. *Quick check:* Compute per-class confidence on validation set; confirm a negative correlation with error rate.
*   **Long-Tail Learning via Synthetic Augmentation:** Using a generative model to create data for underrepresented classes. *Why needed:* Mitigates the real-world scarcity of rare respiratory condition recordings. *Quick check:* After adding synthetic data for a rare class, check that its per-class F1 increases without degrading performance on common classes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Thinker-A2CA (Planner) -> Generator (Qwen3-0.6B + CFM DiT + Vocoder) -> Synthetic Audio + Labels -> Diagnoser (Longformer with Modality Weaving) -> Error Profile -> Thinker-A2CA

**Critical Path:**  
1. Diagnoser identifies weak classes/demographics via uncertainty/error analysis on validation set.  
2. Thinker-A2CA schedules generation requests (disease label, style sampled from source pool).  
3. Generator produces controllable audio.  
4. New synthetic samples are added to the training set.  
5. Diagnoser is retrained. Loop continues until convergence.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
-   **Generative Fidelity vs. Diversity:** High-fidelity style transfer risks mode collapse; the system must preserve pathological content while varying acoustic style.
-   **Computational Cost:** Closed-loop training requires multiple diagnoser retrainings and massive audio synthesis. Thinker aims to minimize total compute by targeting.
-   **Anchoring Density:** More audio anchors (smaller stride `s`) capture more transient events but increase sequence length and computational cost for the Longformer.

**Failure Signatures:**  
-   **Data Leakage:** Unrealistically high test performance that collapses on a truly held-out domain. Diagnose by auditing filenames across splits.
-   **Semantic Drift in Synthesis:** Generated audio lacks the specified pathology (e.g., synthesizing a "wheeze" label but outputting normal breathing). Diagnose via listening checks and per-class FAD/style similarity.
-   **No Long-Tail Gain:** Adding synthetic data does not improve Macro-F1 on rare classes. Diagnose by comparing per-class scores before/after augmentation; likely cause is poor synthetic quality or modal misalignment.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1.  **Baseline Diagnoser:** Train the Longformer with modality weaving on the *original*, imbalanced ICBHI/SPRSound/UK COVID-19 train set. Record overall Accuracy/Macro-F1 and per-class performance on the strict cross-domain test set (KAUH/COUGHVID). Expect poor long-tail scores.
2.  **Random Synthetic Augmentation:** Generate a fixed number (e.g., 50k) of samples *randomly* across all classes (using the Generator conditioned on random disease labels). Augment the baseline train set and retrain the diagnoser. Compare long-tail Macro-F1 to baseline. Expect moderate improvement, but less than targeted method.
3.  **Simple Uncertainty-Based Planner:** Replace the proprietary Thinker-A2CA with a simple heuristic: compute per-class validation error (or MC dropout uncertainty) after Experiment 1. Generate additional samples *only* for the 8 worst-performing (rarest) classes until they are balanced. Retrain diagnoser. This tests if the core benefit comes from *targeted* synthesis rather than the specific LLM-based planner.

## Limitations
-   The closed-loop Thinker-A2CA algorithm's exact decision logic (e.g., how it weights label rarity, domain hardness, diagnostic error) is not fully specified.
-   Exact training hyperparameters (learning rates, batch sizes, CFM noise schedule, vocoder config) for the Generator and Diagnoser are missing.
-   The precise file lists and preprocessing scripts that enforce the strict cross-domain split without any leakage are not provided.

## Confidence
*   **Closed-loop agent benefit:** Low – The claimed advantage depends critically on the underspecified Thinker-A2CA scheduling heuristic.
*   **Benchmark performance numbers (ICBHI 72.7%, cross-domain long-tail gains):** Medium – Results are large and specific, but rely on the integrity of the unreleased cross-domain split and full training procedure.
*   **Component architectural descriptions (Generator disentanglement, Diagnoser modality weaving):** Medium – The designs are clearly explained, but missing quantitative ablations and full hyperparameter details limit reproduction confidence.

## Next Checks
1.  **Leakage audit:** Rigorously verify that no audio file (by exact hash/filename) from the test domains (KAUH, COUGHVID) appears in the training or validation sets from ICBHI, SPRSound, and UK COVID-19.
2.  **Synthetic quality validation:** Generate samples using the released (or replicated) Generator. Compute Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD) and style cosine similarity against a held-out set of *real* samples for each disease class. Publish per-class metrics.
3.  **Thinker ablation:** Conduct a controlled ablation where the closed-loop synthesis is replaced by a naive random sampling strategy (same final dataset size). The gap in long-tail Macro-F1 between targeted and random augmentation should be significant to validate the planner's role.
