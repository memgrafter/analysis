# myMNIST: Benchmark of PETNN, KAN, and Classical Deep Learning Models for Burmese Handwritten Digit Recognition

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18597
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18597
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** CNN achieves highest performance (F1-score: 0.9959, Accuracy: 0.9970) on the myMNIST benchmark.

## Executive Summary
This study establishes the first systematic benchmark, named myMNIST, for Burmese handwritten digit recognition using the BHDD dataset. Eleven architectures—including classical models (CNN, LSTM, Transformer), KAN variants, an energy-based model (JEM), and physics-inspired PETNNs—were evaluated under a unified training protocol. The CNN achieved the highest performance (F1-score: 0.9959, Accuracy: 0.9970). PETNN with GELU activation was a close second (F1: 0.9955, Accuracy: 0.9966), outperforming LSTM, GRU, Transformer, and KANs. The results provide reproducible baselines and highlight PETNN's strong competitiveness for this task. Error analysis reveals persistent confusions, notably between visually similar digits ၀ and ၁.

## Method Summary
The BHDD dataset was standardized into 28×28 grayscale images, split into 60,000 training and 27,561 test samples. All models were trained using a unified protocol: AdamW optimizer (learning rate 3e-4, weight decay 1e-4), OneCycleLR scheduler, cross-entropy loss, batch size 128, dropout (0.1–0.3), layer normalization, gradient clipping (max norm 1.0), and early stopping. Eleven architectures were implemented with consistent weight initialization (Xavier for MLP, Kaiming for CNN, orthogonal for RNNs) and evaluated using precision, recall, F1-score, and accuracy.

## Key Results
- CNN achieves state-of-the-art with F1-score 0.9959 and accuracy 0.9970.
- PETNN with GELU activation follows closely with F1-score 0.9955 and accuracy 0.9966.
- PETNN outperforms LSTM, GRU, Transformer, and all KAN variants, demonstrating the effectiveness of physics-inspired gated dynamics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: CNN Spatial Inductive Biases
**Claim:** The Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) achieves top performance primarily due to its built-in spatial inductive biases that align with the structure of handwritten images.  
**Mechanism:** The convolutional operation applies learned filters across local spatial neighborhoods, enforcing translation equivariance. Hierarchical pooling reduces spatial resolution while increasing feature abstraction, building complex shape detectors from simple edges. This encodes the prior that nearby pixels are more correlated than distant ones, suiting digit strokes and loops.  
**Core assumption:** Optimal discriminative features are spatially local and hierarchical; hard-coding this prior is more beneficial than learning it from raw pixels.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states CNN remains a strong baseline with best scores; Section III.B defines convolution and discusses spatially-localized feature extraction and translation equivariance; neighboring Burmese text classification work also uses convolutional priors.  
**Break condition:** Performance degrades on tasks where spatial locality is invalid (e.g., tabular data with pixel-order-independent features) or digit morphology relies on long-range dependencies.

### Mechanism 2: PETNN Gated State Transition
**Claim:** The Physics-inspired Energy Transition Neural Network (PETNN) achieves competitive performance through a gated state transition mechanism that models discrete, non-linear changes in an internal "potential energy" state, capturing stroke formation dynamics.  
**Mechanism:** A transition gate (Tt) is computed; if it crosses a threshold (≤0), a transition mask (mt) activates, updating a separate memory cell (Ct). The final hidden state (ht) combines a recurrent projection of the previous state, current input, and a residual connection. This allows the network to "commit" to a state change when evidence accumulates, mimicking quantization.  
**Core assumption:** Recognizing handwritten digits benefits from modeling the *process* of stroke execution as energy state changes, not just the static image.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes PETNN (GELU) closely follows CNN; Section II.H details equations where Tt acts as potential energy and mt triggers memory updates; Section V.B shows performance sensitivity to activation (GELU/SiLU > Sigmoid).  
**Break condition:** Fails if digit morphology is better described by continuous state evolution (where standard RNN/GRU suffices) or if optimal features are purely spatial (where CNN excels).

### Mechanism 3: JEM Energy-Based Unification
**Claim:** The Joint Energy Model (JEM) performs well by unifying discriminative classification with a generative energy-based model, encouraging features robust to perturbations and better calibrated.  
**Mechanism:** JEM defines a joint energy function Eθ(x,y) where lower energy indicates higher compatibility between image x and label y. Training combines cross-entropy loss with a contrastive divergence term via Langevin dynamics (MCMC sampling in x-space). This forces the classifier to assign high energy not only to wrong labels for a given x but also to *plausible* synthetic images, pushing decision boundaries away from the data manifold.  
**Core assumption:** Learning a discriminative classifier in isolation can yield overconfident, brittle boundaries; injecting a generative, manifold-aware objective improves robustness and uncertainty estimation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states JEM performs competitively; Section III.F notes joint training enforces semantically meaningful features and better calibrated confidence; Section V ranks JEM in the top tier.  
**Break condition:** Benefit diminishes if Langevin sampling is too noisy/expensive for data complexity, or if the dataset is already large and clean enough (unlikely with handwritten variation).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)**  
  **Why needed here:** The CNN is the top-performing model. Understanding its spatial priors (local connectivity, weight sharing, pooling) is fundamental to interpreting *why* it beats MLP and many sequence models on image data.  
  **Quick check question:** Explain how weight sharing in a convolutional layer provides translational equivariance, and why this is useful for digit recognition.

- **Concept: Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM/GRU) and Gating**  
  **Why needed here:** PETNN and the classical RNN baselines rely on gated state transitions. Understanding LSTM/GRU gates (input, forget, output) is prerequisite to appreciating PETNN's novel "transition gate" and memory cell design.  
  **Quick check question:** What is the purpose of the forget gate in an LSTM, and how does PETNN's transition mask (`mt`) differ in its triggering condition?

- **Concept: Energy-Based Models (EBMs) and Langevin Dynamics**  
  **Why needed here:** JEM's mechanism is directly inherited from EBMs. Grasping the unnormalized density `exp(-E(x,y))` and MCMC (Langevin) for sample generation is crucial to understanding its training objective and calibration benefits.  
  **Quick check question:** In JEM, what does the Langevin dynamics step try to achieve, and why does adding this step to training improve calibration?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Input (28×28 grayscale image) -> Core Processor (one of 11 architectures: CNN, LSTM, GRU, Transformer, KAN variants, PETNN, JEM, MLP) -> Classification Head (Linear layer to 10 classes)

### Critical path
1. Start with the **CNN baseline** (Section III.B). It requires the least tuning and establishes a high, reproducible SOTA.
2. Implement **PETNN-GELU** (Section II.H). Verify gated dynamics and activation sensitivity, aiming to match CNN within 0.0004 F1.
3. Replicate **JEM** (Section III.F). Test energy-based training with Langevin dynamics, focusing on calibration improvement.
4. Compare all models using unified metrics (precision, recall, F1, accuracy) on the fixed test set.

### Design tradeoffs
- **CNN vs. PETNN:** Spatial priors (CNN) offer efficiency and strong performance; PETNN's sequential stroke modeling may provide interpretability for handwriting dynamics but is computationally heavier.
- **JEM vs. discriminative models:** Generative objective improves calibration but increases training complexity and requires careful tuning of Langevin hyperparameters.
- **KANs vs. MLPs:** Spline activations enable feature visualization and smooth interpolation but introduce spline hyperparameters (grid size, order) and slower training.

### Failure signatures
- Persistent ၀↔၁ confusion across all models indicates inherent script ambiguity or insufficient modeling of stroke loops/gaps.
- PETNN-Sigmoid accuracy below 0.994 suggests gating failure due to saturating activations hindering transition dynamics.
- JEM not improving calibration may result from improper Langevin step size (η) or noise level, or insufficient sampling steps.

### First experiments
1. Train CNN with specified protocol; verify test accuracy >0.9965 to confirm implementation parity.
2. Train PETNN with GELU activation; confirm F1-score within 0.0004 of CNN and outperforming PETNN-Sigmoid by >0.001.
3. Train JEM; measure expected calibration error (ECE) and compare to pure CNN to assess generative benefit.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can targeted data augmentation techniques, such as stroke-level transformations or synthetic sample generation, reduce the persistent ၀↔၁ misclassification confusion that affects all evaluated models?
- **Open Question 2:** What is the mechanistic relationship between the activation function in PETNN's gated dynamics (GELU/SiLU vs. Sigmoid) and its ability to model the stroke transitions and topological features of Burmese digits?
- **Open Question 3:** Does the observed performance hierarchy (CNN > PETNN > JEM > KAN/RNNs) generalize beyond BHDD to other complex, non-Latin handwritten scripts (e.g., Thai, Devanagari, Arabic), where morphological and stroke characteristics differ?
- **Open Question 4:** Can the interpretability afforded by KAN's spline-parametrized univariate functions be leveraged to diagnose and potentially correct its specific weaknesses, such as the directional confusion between ၃ and ၄, thereby improving its accuracy on Burmese digits?
