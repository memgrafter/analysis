# Exploring Information Seeking Agent Consolidation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.00585
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00585
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Data-level consolidation via joint training on mixed agent trajectories is a strong, stable baseline for unifying web, document, and RAG agents, while parameter-level merging is efficient but suffers from interference unless using fine-grained, data-aware methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the consolidation of specialized information-seeking agents—namely web search, document understanding, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)—into a single unified model. It systematically compares two consolidation strategies: **data-level consolidation** (jointly fine-tuning on a mixture of domain-specific interaction trajectories) and **parameter-level consolidation** (merging independently trained expert models by combining their weight updates). Experiments on the Qwen3-30B-A3B-Think model show that data mixing consistently preserves capabilities across all domains, serving as a robust baseline. In contrast, parameter merging methods vary widely in effectiveness; while some (e.g., RegMean++) approach data-mixing performance, others (e.g., TIES, SCE) exhibit severe interference, particularly on heterogeneous tasks like RAG. The study identifies key factors for successful merging—including matrix-level granularity, normalized updates, and data-driven consensus—and highlights fundamental challenges when merging LoRA-trained models.

## Method Summary
The paper’s core methodology involves training three specialized "expert" agents on distinct datasets: `Dweb` (web search via tools like Search/Visit), `Ddoc` (document understanding with Search/Read), and `Drag` (RAG with dense retrieval). For **data-level consolidation**, a single model is fine-tuned on the combined dataset `Dall = Dweb ∪ Ddoc ∪ Drag`, requiring it to learn a unified policy that conditions its behavior on the input context and toolset. For **parameter-level consolidation**, the three experts are first trained separately. Their **task vectors** (the difference `Δ = θ_expert - θ_base` from a shared pretrained base model) are then merged using one of 20 algorithms (e.g., averaging, TIES, RegMean++, SCE). The merged parameters `θ_merge = θ_base + merge(Δ_web, Δ_doc, Δ_rag)` are evaluated on a suite of nine benchmarks spanning the three domains. The paper also ablates factors like merging granularity (model-level vs. per-layer), normalization of update magnitudes, and the use of proxy data for data-aware methods.

## Key Results
- Data-mixing achieves strong, balanced performance: 64.08% accuracy on GAIA (web) and 38.00% exact match on HotPotQA (RAG), while top parameter methods like TIES reach 62.14% on GAIA but underperform on RAG (18.60% EM).
- RegMean++, a data-aware merging method, closes the gap with data-mixing on RAG (45.50% EM vs. 38.00%) and is robust to LoRA training, while subspace-based methods (TSV, WUDI) collapse when merging LoRA experts.
- Critical success factors for merging include fine-grained (matrix-level) operations, normalizing task vector magnitudes, and using activation-based alignment (as in RegMean++) rather than heuristic sign consensus.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Joint training on a mixture of domain-specific agent trajectories (`Dall = Dweb ∪ Ddoc ∪ Drag`) produces a single model that retains capabilities across web, document, and knowledge-base environments by learning a unified policy that can condition its behavior on the implicit structure of the input and tool calls.
- **Mechanism:** The model encounters diverse `(query, tool-call, observation)` sequences from all domains within the same training run. This forces the model's internal representations to develop disentangled pathways or context-sensitive activations that route processing appropriately, rather than learning isolated, domain-specific weight configurations.
- **Core assumption:** The base model's pre-trained knowledge provides a sufficiently broad prior that allows it to absorb multiple, distinct interaction patterns without catastrophic interference, provided the optimization trajectory is carefully managed (e.g., via learning rate, data ordering).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...data-level consolidation via data mixture is conceptually simple... data-level consolidation remains a strong and stable baseline."
  - [section 3.1] Describes the formation of `Dall` and notes the distinct tool usage patterns (Figure 2) that the single model must learn to harness.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers focus on multimodal and long-horizon seeking, suggesting the core challenge is integrating diverse interaction *patterns*, not just facts.
- **Break condition:** High task heterogeneity (e.g., merging RAG's dense retrieval with web's dynamic search) or sequential training with poor shuffling can lead to severe forgetting, as indicated by the 74.38% performance drop on GAIA when data sequence is Web→Doc→RAG (section 5.1).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Parameter-level consolidation (merging) works by identifying and combining the *task vectors* (the delta `Δ = θ_expert - θ_base`) from independently trained expert models, under the hypothesis that the beneficial weights for a task form a coherent directional subspace in the full parameter space.
- **Mechanism:** Methods like TIES, RegMean++, or SCE operate on these `Δ` vectors. They attempt to filter out noise and conflicting directions (e.g., via sign voting, subspace projection, or activation-weighted averaging) to construct a merged `θ_merge = θ_base + merge(Δ_web, Δ_doc, Δ_rag)` that captures shared and unique components.
- **Core assumption:** The individual task vectors have a non-trivial overlap in the parameter space that represents common, beneficial capacities (e.g., basic reasoning, tool-calling syntax), while task-specific elements can be isolated and recombined without destructive interference.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...parameter-level consolidation... offers a promising, efficient alternative but suffers from interference... including fine-grained merging granularity, awareness of task heterogeneity, and principled consensus strategy."
  - [section 4] Defines `merge(·)` and lists 20 methods, all of which take task vectors as input and apply a consensus strategy.
  - [section 5.2] Shows that matrix-level methods and data-aware methods (RegMean++) reduce interference, implying the existence of a "consensus subspace."
- **Break condition:** When task vectors are largely orthogonal or reside in disjoint subspaces (e.g., with LoRA adapters due to random low-rank initialization), consensus strategies fail catastrophically, as seen with TSV and WUDI collapse in Table 4.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Effective parameter-level consolidation depends critically on (a) operation at a fine granularity (per-matrix), (b) normalization of task vector magnitudes, and (c) consensus strategies that use informative, data-derived signals (like activation alignment) rather than heuristics like sign consistency alone.
- **Mechanism:** Layer-wise or matrix-wise operations respect the functional specialization within the network (e.g., earlier layers may handle tool syntax, later layers handle domain-specific reasoning). Normalization prevents tasks with larger update norms (often from more complex environments like Web) from dominating. Data-driven consensus (RegMean++'s `W_merge = (∑ Ĝ_i)^-1 ∑ Ĝ_i W^i`) uses cached forward passes to estimate input feature covariance (`Ĝ_i`), aligning weights based on actual data flow, which is more robust to interference.
- **Core assumption:** The "effective capability" of a parameter matrix is not just its magnitude but its alignment with the feature space encountered during inference. Heuristic methods (sign, magnitude) are poor proxies for this.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 6] "Granularity Matters... finer granularity...", "Normalize Parameter Update...", "Bridge the Gap with Cost-Controlled Data Calibration...".
  - [section 5.2] "❷ Heuristic consensus strategy like sign consistency... is fragile... Data-dependent methods... achieve improved stability."
  - [section 5.1] "RegMean++ consistently attains performance comparable to that of data mixing" in LoRA setting, while subspace methods fail.
- **Break condition:** Applying model-level averaging (ignoring granularity), omitting normalization, or relying solely on sign-based consensus (like TIES on highly heterogeneous tasks) leads to performance collapses (e.g., Consensus TA on RAG, ISO-CTS completely).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: ReAct Paradigm for Agents**
  - *Why needed here:* All three agent types (Web, Doc, RAG) are built on the ReAct (Reasoning + Acting) loop. Understanding this `(thought, action, observation)` cycle is essential to see what each expert model has actually learned and what needs to be consolidated.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the `Action` space (Search/Visit vs. Dense Retrieval) and the `Observation` format (snippets vs. full page vs. doc chunks) differ across the three agent types in Table 1 and Appendix D?

- **Concept: Task Vectors and Model Merging**
  - *Why needed here:* Parameter-level consolidation is defined entirely on the manipulation of task vectors (`θ_expert - θ_base`). Key methods (TIES, RegMean++, etc.) are fundamentally different ways to combine these vectors.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the core difference between a *heuristic* consensus strategy (e.g., TIES's sign voting) and a *data-aware* one (e.g., RegMean++'s activation matching)?

- **Concept: LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper shows LoRA-trained experts behave fundamentally differently during merging (subspace methods fail). This is because LoRA's low-rank updates are constrained to random, likely orthogonal subspaces.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might the singular value spectra of full fine-tuning updates differ from those of LoRA updates, and how does this affect subspace-based merging methods?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Base Model (θ_base) -> Expert Models (θ_web, θ_doc, θ_rag) -> Task Vectors (Δ_i) -> Merging Operator (merge(·)) -> Consolidated Model (θ_merge) -> Evaluation Suite (GAIA, MMLongBenchDoc, HotPotQA, etc.)

- **Critical path:**
  1.  Train three expert models on their respective datasets.
  2.  Compute task vectors (`Δ`).
  3.  Apply chosen merging method to `Δ`s.
  4.  Add result to `θ_base` to get `θ_merge`.
  5.  Evaluate `θ_merge` on all nine benchmarks (3 domains × 3 benchmarks each).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Data vs. Parameter Level:** Data mixing (high cost, stable) vs. Merging (low cost, fragile).
  - **Granularity:** Model-level (simple, high interference) vs. Matrix-level (complex, preserves functionality).
  - **Data-Dependence:** Data-free (heuristic, fragile on heterogeneity) vs. Data-required (RegMean++, robust but needs proxy data).
  - **Training Paradigm:** Full fine-tuning (overlapping subspaces) vs. LoRA (orthogonal subspaces) requires different merging logic.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Catastrophic collapse:** Performance near 0% on an entire domain (e.g., Consensus TA on RAG, ISO-CTS everywhere).
  - **See-saw trade-off:** High gain on one domain (often the most homogeneous pair, Web/Doc) paired with severe loss on another (usually RAG).
  - **LoRA-specific failure:** Subspace-based methods (TSV, WUDI) yield performance worse than the base model when experts are LoRA-trained.
  - **Instability:** Performance highly sensitive to random seed or data ordering (common in heuristic methods).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline Replication:** Replicate Data Mixing (`Dall`) and simple Average merging on the 30B model. Confirm the ~2% gap on GAIA (64.08% vs. 60.19%) and larger gap on RAG (38.00% EM vs. 18.60%).
  2.  **Granularity Ablation:** Compare Model-level Average vs. a hypothetical Matrix-level Average (average each weight matrix independently). Expect improved balance, especially on RAG.
  3.  **LoRA vs. Full-FT Merging:** Train experts with LoRA (r=8). Apply top-performing full-FT methods (TIES, RegMean++) and subspace methods (TSV). Expect TIES to degrade less than TSV/WUDI, and RegMean++ to remain strong. This isolates the effect of the *training paradigm* on merging compatibility.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we design parameter-level consolidation methods that are robust to task heterogeneity without requiring additional data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Tables 3 and 4 show that data-free methods like Consensus TA and DELLA fail catastrophically on certain domains (e.g., Consensus TA achieves 0.16 EM on HotPotQA, DELLA 24.27% on GAIA), while data-dependent methods like RegMean++ achieve much better results (45.50 EM on HotPotQA) but need unlabeled data. This trade-off is highlighted in Section 6, principle 4.
- Why unresolved: Data-free methods rely on heuristics (e.g., sign consistency) that break under heterogeneity, while data-dependent methods sacrifice the efficiency and privacy advantages of parameter merging. A solution that maintains data-free operation while matching data-dependent robustness remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: A novel data-free merging algorithm that consistently achieves performance within 5% of RegMean++ across all domains (Web, Doc, RAG) without using any unlabeled data, validated on the paper's benchmark suite.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How should parameter-level consolidation be adapted for LoRA-trained models to avoid catastrophic performance degradation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] In Section 5.1 and Table 4, subspace-based merging methods (TSV, WUDI) completely collapse when merging LoRA models (e.g., WUDI achieves only 0.10 EM on HotPotQA). The authors attribute this to orthogonal low-rank updates from LoRA's random initialization, which cause these methods to discard task-specific information (Section 6, principle 5).
- Why unresolved: Existing merging methods assume task vectors share overlapping subspaces, but LoRA updates reside in mutually orthogonal subspaces. No effective LoRA-specific merging strategy is proposed, limiting applicability of parameter merging in parameter-efficient fine-tuning scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: A LoRA-aware merging technique that preserves orthogonality while achieving performance comparable to full fine-tuning merging (e.g., >40 EM on HotPotQA) across different LoRA ranks and initializations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What theoretical conditions govern interference in model merging, and can they be used to design provably robust algorithms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] In Section 5.2, Research Question I explicitly asks: "Why does model merging exhibit a trade-off (see-saw) effect across tasks or capabilities?" The authors provide empirical insights (e.g., task homogeneity, granularity, consensus indicators) but no formal theoretical explanation.
- Why unresolved: Without a theoretical foundation, merging methods remain heuristic and may fail unpredictably. A formal model would enable predicting when interference occurs and deriving merging objectives that provably minimize it.
- What evidence would resolve it: A mathematical framework (e.g., based on loss landscape geometry, task vector alignment, or Fisher information) that explains the trade-off and yields a merging algorithm with theoretical guarantees, demonstrating reduced interference on diverse task pairs in experiments.

## Limitations
- **Unreleased data and hyperparameters:** The training trajectories (4,500 per domain) and full training details (learning rate, batch size, prompt templates) are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- **Limited model and benchmark scope:** All experiments use a single model architecture (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Think), and the benchmarks are static simulations that may not capture real-world tool failures or domain diversity.
- **Theoretical gap:** Parameter merging remains heuristic, with no formal theory to predict or guarantee performance under heterogeneous task consolidation.

## Confidence
- **Data-level consolidation effectiveness:** High – stable baseline across all domains (Table 3).
- **Parameter-level consolidation (interference trade‑off):** High – consistent patterns across 20 merging methods, with clear failure modes.
- **Critical factors for effective merging** (fine‑grained granularity, normalized updates, data‑aware consensus): Medium – supported by ablations but domain‑specific; broader validity unproven.
