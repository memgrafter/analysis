# Auto Researching, not hyperparameter tuning: Convergence Analysis of 10,000 Experiments
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15916
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15916
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Architectural choices (backbone × encoder) explain 94% of performance variance; LLM-guided search converges as a power law (c=0.11) and outperforms random search, discovering a novel high-performing configuration.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether LLM agents autonomously designing machine learning experiments perform true architecture search or merely hyperparameter tuning. In an extensive study of 10,469 experiments on dashcam collision detection, the authors find that architectural decisions (selection of backbone and temporal encoder) account for 94% of the performance variance, while hyperparameters within fixed architectures contribute only 6%. The LLM-driven search follows a power-law convergence pattern (exponent c=0.11) and reaches an average precision of 0.985 by the 50th experiment, surpassing a random search baseline. Notably, the agents propose a configuration combining V-JEPA2 video features with a Zipformer temporal encoder—a design not previously explored by humans—with AP=0.9245. Cross-task validation on a second collision dataset confirms that architecture remains the dominant factor, albeit with a different optimal backbone, suggesting genuine architecture discovery across tasks.

## Method Summary
The study uses the Nexar dashcam collision dataset (1,500 videos, 80/10/10 split). Frame-level features are extracted from six pre-trained backbones (VJEPA2, DINOv3-B/L, DINOv2-B, SigLIP2, ConvNeXt). Each experiment trains a temporal encoder (Zipformer, RetNet, BiMamba, Hybrid) on frozen backbone features. Two LLM agents (Claude Opus, Gemini 2.5 Pro) observe a shared leaderboard and generate structured YAML configurations proposing base ideas. A sweep module expands each base idea into a set of hyperparameter variants. An orchestrator deduplicates configurations, schedules them on 16 H100 GPUs, and triggers training (AdamW optimizer, 5–30 epochs). A self-healing component uses an LLM to analyze runtime failures and patch configurations automatically. Completed experiments report validation average precision (AP) to the leaderboard. The authors analyze the search dynamics using ANOVA decomposition, power-law modeling of cumulative best performance, and information-theoretic metrics (entropy, Jensen–Shannon divergence).

## Key Results
- Architectural choices (backbone × encoder) explain 94% of the performance variance (η²=0.94); hyperparameter variation within fixed architectures accounts for only 6%.
- LLM-guided search achieves power-law convergence with exponent c=0.11 (post-bugfix, R²=0.93) and reaches AP=0.985 at N=50, outperforming from-scratch random search (AP=0.9648).
- The agents discover a novel configuration (V-JEPA2 + Zipformer) achieving 0.9245 AP, not previously proposed by humans; cross-task validation shows architecture dominance (75% variance) with a different winning backbone.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The LLM agents succeed by concentrating on high-level architectural decisions, which the results show dominate performance. Their ability to rapidly explore a vast combinatorial space of backbone–encoder pairings leads to early discovery of near-optimal architectures, reflected in the power-law convergence. The sweep module then efficiently fine-tunes hyperparameters within promising architectural regions, boosting final scores without overshadowing the architectural signal. The self-healing mechanism maintains experiment throughput, preventing minor failures from stalling the search. Together, these components create a robust pipeline where architectural innovation is the primary driver of improvement, explaining why the agents outperform random search.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
LLM Agents (Claude Opus, Gemini 2.5 Pro) → YAML proposals → Sweep Module (hyperparameter expansion) → Orchestrator (deduplication, scheduling) → Execution Workers (16× H100 GPUs) → Leaderboard (AP scores) → feedback to agents. Parallel: Failure detector → LLM patch generator → fixed configuration → orchestrator.

**Critical path**  
1. Agents generate base configuration.  
2. Sweep module produces hyperparameter variants.  
3. Orchestrator deduplicates and schedules on GPUs.  
4. Training runs (AdamW, 5–30 epochs).  
5. Validation AP computed and uploaded to leaderboard.  
6. Agents observe updated leaderboard and propose new configurations.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Frozen backbones* reduce compute but limit adaptation; may miss backbone fine-tuning benefits.  
- *LLM-driven proposals* inject prior knowledge but risk bias away from unexplored regions.  
- *Hyperparameter sweeps* refine promising ideas but inflate experiment count, potentially conflating architecture vs tuning effects.  
- *Self-healing via LLM* increases robustness but may inadvertently modify configurations in unintended ways.

**Failure signatures**  
- Parsing errors for legacy configuration formats.  
- Instability in power-law fitting when the post-bugfix subset is small or unevenly spaced.  
- Missing or corrupted dataset files causing training crashes.

**First three experiments**  
1. Parse the released experiment logs into a clean tabular dataset, filtering to runs completed after the bug fix and with valid backbone/encoder assignments.  
2. Perform ANOVA on the cleaned data to compute eta-squared for backbone × encoder and within-group variation.  
3. Order the post-bugfix experiments chronologically, compute cumulative best AP, and fit the power-law AP*(N)=a−b·N^{-c} with nonlinear least squares; compare exponent to a random baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How transferable are the discovered architectures to other video understanding tasks and datasets beyond collision detection?
- What is the impact of LLM agent choice (e.g., Claude vs. Gemini) and prompting strategies on search efficiency and final performance?
- Can the observed power-law convergence in cumulative best performance be theoretically derived or does it depend empirically on the search space structure?
- Does freezing pre-trained backbones, while computationally efficient, limit the potential for discovering novel architectures compared to end-to-end search where all parameters are optimized?

## Limitations
- Dataset schema and access URL are unspecified, hindering replication efforts.
- The exact definition of "after the final bug fix" is ambiguous, potentially altering the post-bugfix subset.
- Architecture space is limited to predefined backbones and encoders, constraining the scope of genuine architecture discovery.
- The automatic hyperparameter expansion blurs the boundary between architecture search and tuning.
