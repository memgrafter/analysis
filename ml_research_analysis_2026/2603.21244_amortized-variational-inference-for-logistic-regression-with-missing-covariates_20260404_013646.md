# Amortized Variational Inference for Logistic Regression with Missing Covariates

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21244  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21244  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** AV-LR matches or exceeds EM-like accuracy (e.g., SAEM) while reducing inference cost by orders of magnitude; its MNAR extension improves calibration and discrimination.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Amortized Variational Inference for Logistic Regression (AV-LR), a method for binary logistic regression with missing covariates. AV-LR jointly learns regression parameters and imputes missing values by maximizing an evidence lower bound (ELBO) using a single amortized inference network. This avoids the computational burden of iterative EM-like procedures while offering fast inference via a forward pass. For missing-not-at-random (MNAR) data, AV-LR explicitly models the missingness mechanism, further enhancing performance. Evaluations on synthetic and real datasets (including NHANES) demonstrate that AV-LR attains accuracy comparable to or better than state-of-the-art baselines, with significant speed gains and robustness across missingness scenarios.

## Method Summary
AV-LR defines a generative model: \(p_\theta(y|x)\) (logistic regression) and \(p_\theta(x)\) (Gaussian over covariates). An amortized inference network \(q_\phi(x_{\text{mis}}|x_{\text{obs}},y)\) approximates the posterior over missing values. Training maximizes the importance-weighted ELBO, comprising the logistic log-likelihood \(\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log p_\theta(y|x)]\), the Gaussian prior term \(\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log p_\theta(x)]\), and the negative entropy \(-\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log q_\phi]\). For MNAR, the model is extended with \(p_\psi(r|x,y)\), adding \(\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log p_\psi(r|x,y)]\) to the ELBO. Gradients flow simultaneously to update \(\theta\), \(\phi\), and (if needed) \(\psi\). At inference, a single forward pass through the encoder yields variational parameters; missing values are sampled via reparameterization, and predictions are made using importance sampling to compute posterior predictive probabilities. This joint, amortized approach eliminates per-dataset iterative imputation, enabling orders-of-magnitude speedups while aligning imputation with the predictive task.

## Key Results
- AV-LR achieves parameter and imputation RMSE comparable to or better than SAEM on MCAR/MAR data, with dramatically faster test-time inference.
- Under MNAR, the extension that models \(p_\psi(r|x,y)\) yields higher AUC and lower Brier score than the ignorable variant.
- Empirical results across synthetic and real datasets (NHANES) show consistent robustness to varying missingness rates and mechanisms.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Joint ELBO Optimization
AV-LR directly optimizes a joint objective over regression parameters \(\theta\) and imputation network \(\phi\) by maximizing the ELBO. This couples imputation and regression: gradients from the logistic likelihood term update \(\theta\) using imputed covariates, while gradients from the covariant prior and entropy terms refine the encoder \(q_\phi\) to produce better imputations. The ELBO ensures both components are trained to be consistent with each other, avoiding the separate-step suboptimality of traditional EM. Core assumption: the Gaussian prior on \(x\) and the variational family are sufficiently flexible to capture the true data structure. Evidence: The paper’s derivation (Eq. 3) and experiments show SAEM-level accuracy. Break condition: severe mis-specification (e.g., heavy-tailed covariate distributions) could lead to poor local optima.

### Mechanism 2: Amortization for Fast Inference
The encoder network maps observed features \((x_{\text{obs}}, y)\) directly to parameters of \(q_\phi\) in a single forward pass, avoiding iterative sampling (e.g., SAEM’s S-step) at test time. Training amortizes across all data points, so the network learns to approximate the conditional posterior for any given pattern of missingness. This yields orders-of-magnitude speedup during inference. Core assumption: the mapping from observed data to posterior parameters is smooth and shareable across observations; network capacity is sufficient for the diversity of missingness patterns. Evidence: The abstract and Section 3.2 emphasize amortization; neighbor work on amortized inference supports the efficiency claim. Break condition: highly heterogeneous missingness or complex MNAR dependencies on unobserved latent factors may cause the encoder to fail for some observations.

### Mechanism 3: Explicit MNAR Modeling
For non-ignorable missingness, AV-LR augments the generative model with a parametric missingness mechanism \(p_\psi(r|x,y)\). The ELBO then includes an extra term encouraging the model to explain the observed mask \(r\) given the full covariates \(x\) and outcome \(y\). This allows the imputation distribution to correct bias that would otherwise arise from ignoring the informative missingness. Core assumption: the chosen form for \(p_\psi\) (e.g., logistic neural net) correctly captures the dependence of missingness on \((x,y)\). Evidence: Section 4 introduces the extension; Figure 3 shows improved AUC/Brier under MNAR. Break condition: if the true missingness mechanism lies outside the model family, bias may persist or worsen.

## Foundational Learning
- **Variational Inference & ELBO**  
  *Why needed:* AV-LR uses amortized VI to approximate the intractable posterior \(p(x_{\text{mis}}|x_{\text{obs}},y)\). Maximizing the ELBO jointly learns a generative model and an inference network.  
  *Quick check:* Why does maximizing the ELBO with respect to both \(\theta\) and \(\phi\) yield imputations useful for estimating \(\theta\)?
- **Amortization**  
  *Why needed:* Amortization replaces per-dataset iterative inference with a learned mapping, drastically reducing computational cost at test time.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key difference between amortized VI and traditional VI in terms of optimization cost per new data point?

  *Why needed:* The choice of whether to model missingness explicitly depends on whether data are ignorable (MCAR/MAR) or not (MNAR).  
  *Quick check:* How would you test whether missingness in a dataset is likely MNAR rather than MAR using observed data patterns?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input \((x_{\text{obs}}, y, r)\) → Encoder MLP \(q_\phi\) → Outputs mean \(\mu_q\) and Cholesky factor \(L_q\) for \(x_{\text{mis}}\) → Reparameterized sample \(x_{\text{mis}} = \mu_q + L_q \epsilon\) → Reconstruct full \(x = x_{\text{obs}} \cdot \text{mask} + x_{\text{mis}} \cdot (1-\text{mask})\) → Compute log \(p(y|x)\), log \(p(x)\), (log \(p_\psi(r|x,y)\) for MNAR) → Form importance-weighted ELBO loss → Backpropagate to update \(\theta, \phi\) (and \(\psi\) if MNAR).
- **Critical path:** During training, the forward pass produces \(q_\phi\), samples imputations, evaluates generative probabilities, and aggregates the IWELBO; the backward pass updates all trainable parameters. At inference, the encoder alone produces \(q_\phi\); sampling and integration yield predictions without further training.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Simplicity vs. flexibility:* Using a single Gaussian \(q_\phi\) and linear \(\beta\) reduces complexity but may underfit non-Gaussian posteriors.  
  - *Imputation vs. prediction coupling:* Joint training aligns imputation with classification loss, but may overfit to predictive performance at the expense of imputation accuracy.  
  - *MNAR modeling cost:* Adding \(p_\psi\) improves MNAR handling but increases parameters and risks mis-specification or identifiability issues.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Training: ELBO plateaus early, imputation RMSE stays high, or \(\beta\) diverges → check encoder capacity, learning rate, Gaussian assumption.  
  - MNAR extension: performance degrades under MNAR compared to ignorable version → \(p_\psi\) may be mis-specified or untrainable.  
  - Inference: slow per-sample prediction despite amortization → encoder too deep; consider simplification.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Basic sanity check on synthetic MCAR data (30% missing, \(d=5\)): verify parameter recovery (RMSE) and speed vs. SAEM.  
  2. Sensitivity to missingness rate: increase missingness (50%→80%) and monitor degradation in AUC/RMSE; compare against MICE/SAEM.  
  3. MNAR validation: simulate MNAR (e.g., missingness depends on \(x_{\text{mis}}\)); compare AV-LR (MNAR) vs. AV-LR (ignorable) in AUC/Brier; inspect learned \(p_\psi\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Flexible variational families:** Could normalizing flows improve AV-LR’s ability to capture complex posteriors?  
- **Extension to GLMs and high dimensions:** Is the framework applicable to other generalized linear models or high-dimensional covariates?  
- **Real-world MNAR handling:** How well does AV-LR perform on naturally occurring MNAR data, and can its missingness model be tailored to domain specifics?

## Limitations
- The Gaussian covariate prior may be mis‑specified for non‑Gaussian data, biasing imputation and regression estimates.  
- The amortized encoder might underfit heterogeneous or intricate missingness patterns, leading to inaccurate posteriors.  
- The MNAR extension relies on correct specification of \(p_\psi(r|x,y)\); identifiability issues or mis‑specification can limit gains.  
- Reproduction is hindered by missing details on network architecture, hyperparameters (e.g., \(K\), batch size), and baseline tuning.
