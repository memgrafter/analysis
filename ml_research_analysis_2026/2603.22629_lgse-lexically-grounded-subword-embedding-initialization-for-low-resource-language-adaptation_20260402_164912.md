# LGSE: Lexically Grounded Subword Embedding Initialization for Low-Resource Language Adaptation

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.22629
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22629
*   **Reference count:** 0
*   **Primary result:** LGSE + LAPT achieves state-of-the-art average F1/accuracy on Amharic and Tigrinya QA (78.25), NER (79.2), and text classification (76.5), outperforming FOCUS + LAPT baselines by ~1.5–2 points.

## Executive Summary
LGSE is a method for adapting pretrained language models (XLM-R) to low-resource, morphologically rich languages like Amharic and Tigrinya. It addresses the problem that standard subword tokenization (BPE) fragments words into semantically meaningless pieces. The solution has two parts: first, a lexically grounded embedding initialization for novel tokens, which decomposes words into morphemes and averages their pretrained representations (from FastText or overlapping subwords). Second, a regularization term during Language-Adaptive Pretraining (LAPT) stabilizes these new embeddings, preventing them from drifting away from their linguistically meaningful starting point. Evaluated on question answering, named entity recognition, and text classification, LGSE consistently outperforms baselines, reduces token fertility, and lowers inference latency.

## Method Summary
LGSE operates in two phases following the acquisition of a morphology-aware tokenizer (e.g., MoVoC). First, it expands the model's embedding matrix for the target language. For each novel token (a subword not in the original XLM-R vocabulary), it initializes its embedding by decomposing the token into constituent morphemes, retrieving or composing pretrained morpheme vectors (via FastText character n-grams or overlapping XLM-R subwords), averaging them, and applying a learned linear projection to align the resulting vector with XLM-R's embedding space. Tokens without morpheme decompositions are initialized by sampling from a Gaussian estimated from the original embeddings. In the second phase, Language-Adaptive Pretraining (LAPT) is performed on monolingual data using Masked Language Modeling. Crucially, an auxiliary regularization loss penalizes large deviations of the newly initialized embeddings from their starting values, preserving their semantic grounding while allowing the rest of the model (the frozen encoder layers) to adapt.

## Key Results
*   LGSE + LAPT yields average test F1 scores of 78.25 (QA) and 79.2 (NER), and 76.5 accuracy (text classification) on Amharic and Tigrinya, exceeding the FOCUS + LAPT baseline (76.65 QA F1, 77.8 NER F1, 74.45 accuracy).
*   The morphology-aware tokenization reduces token fertility (tokens per word) significantly compared to BPE (e.g., 6 vs. 21 tokens for a sample Tigrinya sentence).
*   The approach also leads to reduced inference latency relative to standard BPE-tokenized models due to more compact sequences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Morpheme-Grounded Embedding Initialization
*   **Claim:** Initializing embeddings for novel tokens by averaging pretrained morpheme representations creates semantically coherent starting points for morphologically complex words.
*   **Mechanism:** For a novel token, LGSE segments it into linguistically valid morphemes (using a morphology-aware tokenizer). It then obtains a vector for each morpheme, either directly from a pretrained FastText model (as an average of its character n-gram vectors) or by averaging overlapping subword embeddings from the original XLM-R. These morpheme vectors are averaged to form an initial embedding for the novel token, which is finally projected into XLM-R's space via a learned linear matrix `W`.
*   **Core assumption:** Lexical meaning in morphologically rich languages is primarily carried by morphemes; the pretrained embedding space is compositional, so averaging constituent morpheme vectors yields a reasonable approximation of the full word's vector.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The formal averaging equations and projection step (section 4.1); the precursor work *MoVoC* (FMR 0.515) validates morphology-aware segmentation for the Ge'ez script family.
*   **Break condition:** Failure of the morphological segmenter to produce correct morpheme boundaries leads to noisy averaging and poor initialization.

### Mechanism 2: Regularization-Stabilized Language Adaptation
*   **Claim:** A regularization term during LAPT prevents the newly initialized embeddings from deviating significantly from their grounded initial values, preserving cross-lingual alignment while allowing necessary adaptation.
*   **Mechanism:** During LAPT, the standard MLM loss is combined with `Lreg = λ ||enew − µ||²`, where `enew` is the current embedding of a novel token and `µ` is its initial LGSE-computed value. This penalty discourages large updates to these embeddings.
*   **Core assumption:** The LGSE-initialized embedding is already a good approximation within the pretrained model's distributional space; large gradient-driven updates would corrupt this semantic anchor.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The explicit definition of `Lreg` (section 4.2, abstract). Direct evidence is weak; the mechanism is primarily an author-proposed solution to a potential pitfall.
*   **Break condition:** An inappropriate λ (too high) freezes embeddings, preventing adaptation; too low renders the mechanism ineffective.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid Morphology-Aware Tokenization
*   **Claim:** A tokenizer that respects morpheme boundaries (by applying BPE only within morphemes) produces longer, more semantically coherent subwords, reducing token fertility and enabling meaningful LGSE decomposition.
*   **Mechanism:** The tokenizer first segments a word into morphemes using a supervised analyzer. Then, a small BPE model is applied separately to each morpheme. The final vocabulary (`V = VBPEsmall ∪ Vmorph`) contains many whole morphemes and short substrings confined within morphemes.
*   **Core assumption:** Morpheme boundaries are the natural units for semantic composition in these languages. Fragmentation across these boundaries destroys lexical integrity.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Arguments against BPE fragmentation (abstract); formal tokenizer definition (section 6.1); empirical fertility reduction (section 8.5); strong validation in the MoVoC precursor.
*   **Break condition:** Requires an accurate, language-specific morphological analyzer. Poor segmentation undermines the entire pipeline.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Subword Tokenization (BPE/SentencePiece)
    *   **Why needed here:** LGSE is a response to the failure modes of standard frequency-based BPE on morphologically rich languages, which produces semantically arbitrary fragments.
    *   **Quick check:** Why does pure BPE tend to split Amharic words like "እንቁላል" (egg) into non-morphemic pieces, and how does that hurt the embedding of the whole word?
*   **Concept:** Distributed Semantic Representations & Compositionality
    *   **Why needed here:** LGSE's core operation (averaging morpheme vectors) assumes meaning is compositional in the pretrained embedding space.
    *   **Quick check:** What is the key assumption about the XLM-R embedding space that justifies combining vectors for "እን", "ቁ", "ላል" to approximate "እንቁላል"?
*   **Concept:** Language-Adaptive Pretraining (LAPT) / Continued Pretraining
    *   **Why needed here:** LGSE provides a good initialization but does not fully adapt the model to the target language's corpus statistics. LAPT is the subsequent tuning phase.
    *   **Quick check:** In the LGSE pipeline, what is the intended functional difference between the *role* of the embedding layer during initialization versus during LAPT?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Pre-trained XLM-R Base Model -> Frozen Encoder Layers
    MoVoC Tokenizer -> Hybrid Vocabulary (V = VBPEsmall ∪ Vmorph)
    Target Language Monolingual Corpus -> Novel Token Identification -> LGSE Embedding Initializer (Morpheme Decomposition -> FastText/Averaging -> Projection W) -> Expanded Embedding Matrix (E_original ⊕ E_new)
    LAPT Engine (MLM Loss + Lreg) -> Adapted Model (Embeddings updated, Encoders frozen)
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  **Prepare Tokenizer:** Obtain/apply a morphology-aware tokenizer (MoVoC) to the target corpus.
    2.  **Expand Vocabulary:** Identify all novel tokens from the tokenizer's output not in XLM-R's vocabulary.
    3.  **Initialize Embeddings:** For each novel token, compute its initial embedding via LGSE's morpheme averaging and projection.
    4.  **Inject & Adapt:** Replace/expand XLM-R's embedding layer with the new matrix. Freeze original embeddings. Train with LAPT using MLM + `Lreg`.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Hybrid Vocabulary Ratio (r):** Higher `r` (more morpheme tokens) improves morphological fidelity but may sacrifice modeling of common sub-morphemic patterns.
    *   **Projection Complexity:** The learned projection `W` adds parameters and a pre-training step (requires aligned data). Omitting it risks space misalignment.
    *   **Regularization Strength (λ):** Balances preserving initialization vs. allowing corpus-driven adaptation. Requires tuning.
    *   **Resource Dependency:** Performance is contingent on a high-quality morphological analyzer and language-specific FastText embeddings.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Poor Tokenization:** High token fertility (near BPE levels) indicates the morphology-aware tokenizer failed. Manually inspect word splits.
    *   **Misaligned Spaces:** Initialized new embeddings form a distinct cluster from original XLM-R embeddings in a t-SNE plot. Indicates projection `W` issues.
    *   **Over/Under-Regularization:** Dev performance degrades vs. random init baseline. Monitor `||enew - µ||²` during LAPT; if it never decreases, λ is too high. If it drops to near zero, adaptation may be insufficient.
    *   **Vocabulary Mismatch:** High OOV rate in evaluation data. Ensures tokenizer and vocabulary are identical between LAPT and fine-tuning.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Tokenization & Fertility Baseline:** Run MoVoC and standard BPE on the same corpus. Report average tokens per word and show explicit splits for 10 complex words (e.g., `እንቁላል`).
    2.  **Initialization Ablation (No LAPT):** For a fixed set of novel tokens, compare the *initial* embedding quality of LGSE (with projection) vs. Random Init vs. FOCUS. Use a lexical similarity task (nearest neighbor search in XLM-R space for known words).
    3.  **Regularization Sweep:** For LGSE + LAPT on NER, vary λ in {0.0, 0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0}. Plot dev F1 and average `||enew - µ||` vs. λ to find the sweet spot.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Generalization to Decoder Architectures:** How can LGSE be adapted for decoder-based (LLaMA) or encoder-decoder (T5) models?
*   **Vocabulary Strategy Trade-offs:** What are the trade-offs between vocabulary expansion (current method) and full vocabulary replacement in extremely low-resource settings?
*   **Cross-Linguistic Generalizability:** Does the effectiveness of morpheme-aware tokenization generalize beyond the Ge'ez script family (e.g., to Turkish, Finnish, Arabic)?
*   **Resource-Limited Variants:** How can LGSE be applied without supervised morphological analyzers or language-specific FastText models (e.g., using unsupervised segmentation or multilingual projections)?

## Limitations
*   **Unspecified Projection Training:** The method for learning the alignment matrix `W` is not described, creating a major reproducibility gap.
*   **Hyperparameter Omissions:** Exact values for regularization strength `λ`, FastText training details (dimensionality, n-grams), and hybrid vocabulary ratio `r` are missing.
*   **Critical Tooling Dependency:** The entire pipeline's success hinges on the availability and accuracy of a language-specific morphological analyzer (MoVoC).

## Confidence
*   **Main Outcome Claims (LGSE+LAPT SOTA):** **High.** Specific, multi-task results with clear baselines are reported.
*   **Mechanism 1 (Morpheme-Grounded Init):** **Medium-High.** Supported by tokenization evidence and a precursor study, but contingent on segmentation quality.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Regularization Stabilization):** **Low.** Primarily an internal claim with weak external evidence; the necessity and optimality of `Lreg` are not firmly established.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Hybrid Tokenizer Efficacy):** **Medium.** Validated for Ge'ez scripts via MoVoC, but its direct interaction with LGSE and generalizability are assumed.
