# Unifying On- and Off-Policy Variance Reduction Methods

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08370
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08370
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** Unifies online A/B testing and off-policy evaluation, proving mathematical equivalence between Difference-in-Means and an optimal IPS estimator with control variates, and identifies a critical degrees-of-freedom correction for variance estimation.

## Executive Summary
The paper "Unifying On- and Off-Policy Variance Reduction Methods" bridges the long-standing methodological divide between online A/B testing and offline off-policy evaluation (OPE). It proves that the standard online Difference-in-Means (DiM) estimator is mathematically identical to an off-policy IPS estimator augmented with an optimal variance-reducing control variate β★. Moreover, common regression-adjusted online techniques (CUPED, CUPAC, ML-RATE) are shown to be special cases of Doubly Robust estimation when the reward model is action-agnostic. The analysis also uncovers a subtle but crucial degrees-of-freedom correction: when the control variate is estimated from data, the variance estimator must divide by |D|-2 rather than the usual |D|-1.

This unification reveals that the on/off-policy distinction is largely artificial, opening the door for direct cross-pollination of variance reduction methods. The findings have immediate practical implications for variance estimation in A/B tests and OPE, and they provide a theoretical foundation for developing hybrid estimators that leverage the strengths of both paradigms.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a unified theoretical framework that treats both online and off-policy estimators through a common lens of control variates. Starting from the general formulation of the average treatment effect (ATE) as a weighted sum of outcomes, they derive the optimal additive baseline β★ that minimizes variance under known propensities. Substituting this baseline yields an estimator equivalent to DiM. They then extend the framework to include regression adjustment, showing that when the adjustment function f(x) does not depend on the action, the resulting estimator matches the standard RADiM used in online experiments. The equivalence holds under standard causal assumptions (SUTVA, ignorability) and known logging policies. A key technical contribution is the identification of the correct degrees-of-freedom for variance estimation when control variates are estimated from the same data, which impacts confidence interval coverage.

## Key Results
- Proves that the standard online Difference-in-Means (DiM) estimator equals an off-policy IPS estimator with optimal control variate β★.
- Shows that regression-adjusted online methods (CUPED, CUPAC, ML-RATE) are equivalent to Doubly Robust estimation under an action-agnostic reward model.
- Identifies a critical degrees-of-freedom correction for variance estimation when the control variate is estimated from data (|D|-2 instead of |D|-1).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1**
- **Claim:** The standard online Difference-in-Means (DiM) estimator is mathematically identical to an off-policy Inverse Propensity Scoring (IPS) estimator augmented with an optimal variance-minimising additive control variate (β★).
- **Mechanism:** In an A/B test, the logging policy assigns treatments with probability p. The off-policy Δ- estimand uses importance weights w(a) = 𝕀(a=π)/p - 𝕀(a=π')/(1-p). The optimal baseline β★ is derived as the weighted average of group means: β★ = (1-p)μ̂_π + pμ̂_π'. Substituting this into the Δβ-IPS estimator yields exactly the DiM estimator μ̂_π - μ̂_π'. Their variance formulas also match analytically when accounting for the estimation of β★.
- **Core assumption:** The treatment assignment is independent (SUTVA holds) and the logging policy is known. The equivalence relies on the specific parameterisation where the "action" in OPE corresponds to policy choice, not item-level actions.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states the equivalence; [section 3] provides full derivation; [corpus] includes related work on optimal baselines.

**Mechanism 2**
- **Claim:** Common regression-adjusted online methods (CUPED, CUPAC, ML-RATE) are structurally equivalent to Doubly Robust (DR) estimation when the reward model is constrained to be action-agnostic (i.e., f(x,a) ≡ f(x)).
- **Mechanism:** The online Regression-Adjusted DiM (RADiM) uses μ̂_f(Y,π) = (1/|D_π|)∑(y_i - f(x_i)). The off-policy DR estimator for ATE, when f is action-agnostic, simplifies because ∑_a (π(a|x)-π'(a|x))f(x) = 0. The remaining IPS-weighted residual w(y - f(x)) has expectation and variance matching RADiM when f(x) is centred at β★.
- **Core assumption:** The regression adjustment model f(x) must be action-agnostic and correctly centred at the optimal baseline β★ to achieve variance equivalence.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] claims the equivalence; [section 4] derives Δ-DR, imposes action-agnostic constraint, and matches variance to RADiM; [corpus] supports variance reduction relevance.

**Mechanism 3**
- **Claim:** Correct variance estimation for the unified estimators requires a specific degrees-of-freedom correction: when the control variate (β★ or f̂(x)) is estimated from the same data, the variance should be divided by |D|-2 (or |D_π|-1 + |D_π'|-1 for two groups) rather than |D|-1.
- **Mechanism:** The optimal baseline β★ depends on two sample means (from treatment and control), consuming two degrees of freedom. The naive variance estimator for the transformed variable Z = w(Y - β★) would use denominator |D|-1, but the correct unbiased estimator uses |D|-2. This matches the sum of the two independent group variances (each with n-1) used in DiM.
- **Core assumption:** The control variate is estimated from the same sample used for final estimation. The correction accounts for the fact that the estimator of β★ is not fixed but random, inducing dependence.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions the correction; [section 3] discusses the discrepancy; [corpus] shows related techniques but not this specific correction.

  - **Quick check:** How does the variance of DiM change with unequal allocation ratios?
- **Concept:** Inverse Propensity Scoring (IPS)
  - **Why needed:** The foundational off-policy estimator; the paper shows DiM is a special case of a variance-reduced IPS.
  - **Quick check:** What is the bias-variance trade-off in IPS when propensities are extreme?
- **Concept:** Control Variates / Regression Adjustment
  - **Why needed:** The key technique linking both worlds; optimal choice (β★) enables the equivalence.
  - **Quick check:** Why does subtracting a function of covariates reduce variance without introducing bias when that function is independent of treatment?
- **Concept:** Doubly Robust (DR) Estimation
  - **Why needed:** The off-policy analogue of regression-adjusted online methods under the action-agnostic constraint.
  - **Quick check:** What happens to a DR estimator if either the outcome model or the propensity model is correct?
- **Concept:** Degrees-of-Freedom Correction
  - **Why needed:** Critical for accurate variance inference after estimating a control variate from data.
  - **Quick check:** Why does estimating β★ from data cost two degrees of freedom?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Data Ingestion Module → Policy Definition Module → Control Variate Module → Estimator Core → Variance Calculator

**Critical path:** Data → Policy/Weight Computation → Control Variate Estimation (β★ or f̂(x)) → Point Estimate → Variance (with dof correction)

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Bias-Variance:** Using an action-dependent f(x,a) in DR (off-policy only) can reduce variance further but breaks equivalence with online RADiM and requires more complex modelling.
- **Model Dependency:** The equivalence for RADiM/DR assumes f̂(x) is action-agnostic. Using a complex ML model for f (CUPAC/ML-RATE) is fine online but may not be the variance-minimising choice off-policy without action dependence.
- **Compute Cost:** Estimating β★ is cheap (group means). Training f̂(x) is more expensive but can yield higher variance reduction if predictive.

**Failure signatures:**
- Variance estimates that drastically differ between DiM and β★IPS implementations.
- Confidence intervals that are too narrow when using a single |D|-1 denominator for β★IPS.
- RADiM and Δ-DR point estimates matching but variances differing due to dof mishandling in the DR implementation.
- Significantly higher variance in off-policy estimates when logging policy has low overlap, even with control variates (expected, but indicates need for clipping or other techniques beyond this unification).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Numerical Equivalence Check:** Run a synthetic A/B test with known parameters. Implement DiM, β★IPS, RADiM, and Δ-DR (action-agnostic). Verify point estimates are identical (up to floating point) and variances match after applying the correct dof (DiM's sum of group variances vs. β★IPS with |D|-2 denominator).
2. **Dof Correction Impact:** On a semi-synthetic dataset (e.g., Criteo), compute 95% CI width for β★IPS using denominator |D|-1 vs. |D|-2. Quantify the under-coverage rate of the naive CI.
3. **Action-Agnostic Constraint Test:** On a filtering/ranking dataset with known item-level rewards, implement a DR estimator with action-dependent f(x,a) and compare its variance to action-agnostic DR/RADiM. Measure the variance reduction gap to validate the paper's hypothesis that online methods are leaving variance on the table.

## Limitations
- Equivalence relies on strong assumptions (SUTVA, known logging policy, action-agnostic reward models).
- Degrees-of-freedom correction for Δ-DR with a learned f(x) lacks justification regarding model complexity; the impact on inference is uncertain.
- The potential benefits of action-dependent models in online settings remain unexplored; action-dependent DR may offer lower variance but breaks the established equivalence.

## Confidence
- **DiM ≡ Δβ★‑IPS:** High
- **RADiM ≡ Δ‑DR (action‑agnostic f):** Medium
