# MedProbCLIP: Probabilistic Adaptation of Vision-Language Foundation Model for Reliable Radiograph-Report Retrieval
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.16019  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16019  
- **Reference count**: 31  
- **Primary result**: MedProbCLIP achieves RSUM 438.62 in retrieval (vs. CXR-CLIP 406.75) and mean zero-shot accuracy 0.7101 (vs. 0.6619), with better calibration, selective retrieval, and robustness to clinically relevant corruptions on MIMIC-CXR.

## Executive Summary
MedProbCLIP addresses the unreliability of deterministic vision-language models in medical retrieval by introducing probabilistic contrastive learning with Gaussian embeddings. It models uncertainty and many-to-many radiograph-report correspondences, using multi-view radiograph and multi-section report encoding during training to achieve fine-grained alignment, regularized by a variational information bottleneck. On MIMIC-CXR, it significantly improves retrieval performance, zero-shot classification, calibration, selective retrieval, and robustness compared to prior deterministic approaches.

## Method Summary
MedProbCLIP extends CLIP-style training by encoding images and texts as diagonal Gaussian distributions (mean and variance) instead of deterministic points. During training, each study provides two radiograph views (e.g., PA and lateral) and two report sections (Findings, Impression); missing inputs are handled via augmentation. The probabilistic contrastive loss (CSD) measures distribution discrepancy across all inter- and intra-modal pairs. A variational information bottleneck (VIB) regularizes each Gaussian toward a unit-variance prior via KL divergence, with weight parameters β_I and β_T. At inference, a single image and full report are used. The framework employs ViT-Base for images and BioMedBERT for text, with shallow MLP heads producing Gaussian parameters. Training uses AdamW, mixed precision, and gradient clipping, optimizing a weighted sum of probabilistic NLL, intra-modal symmetry losses, and KL terms.

## Key Results
- Retrieval RSUM improves to 438.62 on MIMIC-CXR test set, surpassing CXR-CLIP (406.75).
- Zero-shot classification accuracy on 14 pathology labels reaches 0.7101 mean, exceeding CXR-CLIP's 0.6619.
- Better calibration: lower area under the risk-coverage curve (AURC) for selective retrieval.
- Increased robustness to synthetic corruptions (blur, noise, brightness/contrast, rotation) with smoother performance degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Probabilistic Gaussian embeddings capture inherent ambiguity and uncertainty in radiograph-report correspondences.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of deterministic point embeddings, each input is encoded as a diagonal Gaussian (mean µ, variance σ²). The contrastive stochastic distance (CSD) combines normalized mean difference and the log of summed variances. Ambiguous inputs (e.g., subtle findings, multi-view studies) express higher variance; confident matches remain compact.
- **Core assumption**: Medical image-text relationships are many-to-many; deterministic one-to-one matching introduces false negatives and overconfident errors.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section 3.2 define CSD and probabilistic NLL loss. Neighboring work on probabilistic embeddings for frozen VLMs supports the trend toward uncertainty-aware modeling.
- **Break condition**: If KL regularization (L_KL) is too weak, variances may collapse to near-zero (overconfident) or explode (uninformative), nullifying uncertainty benefits.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: A variational information bottleneck (VIB) mitigates overconfident predictions by regularizing latent Gaussian distributions toward a unit-variance prior.
- **Mechanism**: For each modality’s Gaussian embedding, a KL divergence term penalizes deviation from N(0, I). This compresses only the most relevant information into the mean while controlling variance magnitude.
- **Core assumption**: Deterministic contrastive learning induces embeddings that overfit to dataset-specific biases and ignore ambiguity. VIB encourages disentangled, uncertainty-aware representations.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 3.4 integrates KL terms into total loss. Section 4.2.3 shows superior selective retrieval (lower AURC), indirectly supporting VIB’s role.
- **Break condition**: Mis-tuned β weights (too high/low) lead to over-regularization (loss of discriminative power) or under-regularization (uninformative variance).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Multi-view radiograph and multi-section report encoding during training provides fine-grained, clinically aligned supervision that improves representation learning, while single-input inference maintains practicality.
- **Mechanism**: During training, each batch contains two images and two text segments. Contrastive losses are applied across all image-text pairs and within-modality pairs. This forces alignment of complementary clinical cues and reconciles many-to-many relationships at a sub-study level. At inference, only one image and one full report are used.
- **Core assumption**: Radiology datasets contain natural multi-view (multiple images per study) and multi-section (structured report parts) structures exploitable for richer supervision without extra annotations.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract notes multi-view training and single-input inference. Section 3.3 details the four-probabilistic-embedding design and augmentation for missing inputs. Section 4.2.1 shows multi-view CXR-CLIP outperforms single-view CLIP, and MedProbCLIP further improves.
- **Break condition**: Poor augmentation for missing views/sections introduces noise; inconsistent dataset structure yields inconsistent training signal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Contrastive Learning (Deterministic)**: Needed as the base paradigm for vision-language alignment but limited by point-embedding, one-to-one matching that conflicts with many-to-many medical correspondence. Quick check: "Can a single radiology report accurately describe more than one chest X-ray view?"
- **Probabilistic Embeddings (Gaussian)**: Needed to quantify ambiguity and express predictive uncertainty for calibrated similarity scores and selective retrieval. Quick check: "Does the model output a variance estimate along with each embedding dimension, and are higher variances correlated with ambiguous inputs?"
- **Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB)**: Needed to prevent the probabilistic encoder from ignoring uncertainty and to regularize the latent space to retain only task-relevant information. Quick check: "Is the KL divergence between the learned Gaussian and a unit prior included in the training loss, and are its weight parameters tuned?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input (two images, two texts) → Encoders (ViT-Base, BioMedBERT) → Deterministic embeddings → MLP distribution heads (Gaussian µ, σ²) → CSD computation → Loss (probabilistic NLL + intra-modal + KL) → Backpropagation.
- **Critical path**:
  1. Encode two images and two texts to deterministic vectors.
  2. Transform each to Gaussian parameters (µ, σ²).
  3. Compute CSD for all paired distributions.
  4. Apply probabilistic NLL to all pairs (inter + intra).
  5. Add weighted KL divergences to unit Gaussian.
  6. Backpropagate total loss.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Multi-view training vs. inference simplicity: Training uses four inputs for richer supervision but requires augmentation for missing views/sections; inference uses one input each. Augmentation quality directly affects supervision quality.
  - Variance parameterization: Diagonal Gaussian offers efficiency but cannot capture covariance; richer distributions (e.g., full covariance) increase capacity and compute.
  - Loss weighting (λ, β): Balances inter-modal alignment, intra-modal consistency, and regularization; requires validation tuning.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Overfitted variances: Test-set variances collapse to near-zero → β too low or model ignoring uncertainty.
  - Poor selective retrieval: AURC close to or worse than deterministic baselines → not producing well-calibrated uncertainties.
  - Instability under corruption: Performance degrades erratically → unreliable variance estimates or training instability.
  - Multi-view degradation: Single-view CXR-CLIP performs similarly to MedProbCLIP → multi-view design not providing signal (e.g., poor augmentations).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Retrieval benchmark: Evaluate R@K and RSUM on MIMIC-CXR test set against CLIP, CXR-CLIP, PCME++; expect RSUM improvement >30 over CXR-CLIP.
  2. Calibration check: Plot risk-coverage curves for R@1, R@5, R@10; compute AURC. Expect MedProbCLIP to have lower AURC than baselines, especially at low coverage.
  3. Robustness sanity check: Apply Gaussian blur (severity 1-3) and measure relative R@10 drop. Expect MedProbCLIP’s degradation curve to be smoother than deterministic baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does reliance on multi-view radiograph and multi-section report encoding during training, with only single inputs at inference, degrade uncertainty calibration and selective retrieval?
2. What is the optimal trade-off between VIB regularization strength (β parameters) and retrieval accuracy, and how does it affect uncertainty calibration across pathology categories?
3. How does robustness to synthetic image corruptions translate to real-world clinical variations (e.g., different scanners, protocols, demographics)?
4. Would replacing diagonal Gaussian embeddings with more expressive uncertainty models (e.g., mixture of Gaussians, normalizing flows) further improve retrieval, calibration, and robustness?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter values (λ_I, λ_T, β_I, β_T) not provided; crucial for replication and may affect outcomes.
- Augmentation strategy for missing views/sections unspecified; could introduce bias.
- Gaussian diagonal assumption may be insufficient to capture full uncertainty; covariance ignored.
- Evaluation limited to MIMIC-CXR; external validation lacking.

## Confidence
- **Probabilistic embeddings improve retrieval**: High – consistent gains across metrics and supported by calibration/robustness results.
- **VIB regularization yields better calibration**: Medium – AURC improvement suggests benefit but weight sensitivity not fully explored.
- **Multi-view training aligns fine-grained semantics**: Medium – ablation shows improvement, but augmentation details unknown.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sweep**: Train with a range of λ/β values to assess stability of retrieval performance and calibration, identify optimal regime.
2. **Variance analysis**: Measure average per-dimension variance on clean vs. corrupted inputs; verify variance increases with ambiguity/perturbations, confirming uncertainty modeling.
3. **External generalization**: Test on a different chest X-ray dataset (e.g., CheXpert) to evaluate transferability and robustness beyond MIMIC-CXR.
