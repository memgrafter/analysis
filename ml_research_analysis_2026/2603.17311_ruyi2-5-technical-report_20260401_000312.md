# Ruyi2.5 Technical Report
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17311
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17311
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** Ruyi2.5-Camera-8B achieves 93.33% accuracy, 95.50% precision, 91.87% recall, and 93.67 F1-score on privacy-constrained surveillance tasks, substantially outperforming Qwen3-VL-8B (81.67%, 82.93%, 84.10%, 82.77%).

## Executive Summary
Ruyi2.5 addresses the inherent conflict between privacy protection and accurate behavior recognition in surveillance video analysis. Its core innovation is the Ruyi2.5-Camera system, which employs a shared-backbone architecture to co-train edge and cloud models of varying scales in a single training run, ensuring semantic consistency across deployment tiers. The edge model applies an information-bottleneck-guided irreversible transformation to de-identify raw video frames at the source, preventing leakage of personal identifiable information. The cloud model then performs deep multimodal reasoning on these anonymized features. Additionally, Binary Prefix Policy Optimization (BPPO) accelerates the reinforcement learning fine-tuning stage by 2–3× compared to GRPO, improving training efficiency.

Experimental results show that Ruyi2.5 matches the performance of state-of-the-art Qwen3-VL on general multimodal benchmarks, confirming that broad capability is maintained despite the privacy constraints. More critically, on privacy-sensitive surveillance tasks, Ruyi2.5-Camera-8B dramatically surpasses Qwen3-VL-8B, achieving an F1-score of 93.67% versus 82.77%. These findings establish a viable path for deploying intelligent video analysis systems that respect user privacy while delivering high accuracy.

## Method Summary
Ruyi2.5 builds upon the Qwen3-VL architecture with three key modifications. First, it introduces a shared-backbone familial design: a unified vision encoder and language model backbone is trained once, with sub-models branching via parameter masking (e.g., selecting 1 or 36 decoder blocks). This yields deployable edge and cloud models with consistent semantic representations. Second, the edge model incorporates an irreversible de-identification module that applies learned nonlinear mappings and stochastic noise to visual features, optimized under an information bottleneck objective to compress private information while preserving behavior-relevant signals. Third, the reinforcement learning fine-tuning stage uses Binary Prefix Policy Optimization (BPPO), which reduces gradient redundancy by selecting only one positive and one negative response per group and restricts backpropagation to response prefixes, cutting training time by 2–3×. The overall training pipeline begins with supervised fine-tuning on a large-scale multimodal dataset (covering VQA, captioning, OCR, STEM, and document understanding), followed by BPPO-based RL on geometry-focused data to enhance reasoning.

## Key Results
- Ruyi2.5 attains parity with Qwen3-VL on standard multimodal benchmarks, confirming that general capability is maintained.
- On privacy-constrained surveillance recognition, Ruyi2.5-Camera-8B dramatically surpasses Qwen3-VL-8B: Accuracy 93.33% vs 81.67%, Precision 95.50% vs 82.93%, Recall 91.87% vs 84.10%, F1-score 93.67% vs 82.77%.
- BPPO provides a 2–3× speedup in RL fine-tuning relative to GRPO.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Shared-Backbone Co-Training for Semantic Consistency
- **Claim:** A single training run yields multiple deployable models (e.g., edge and cloud) that remain semantically aligned, avoiding redundant training while ensuring consistent outputs across deployment tiers.
- **Mechanism:** A unified backbone (vision encoder + LLM) is trained once, with sub-models branching via parameter masking or pruning (e.g., using 1 vs. 36 decoder blocks). Gradients flow through the shared trunk, aligning representations across scales.
- **Core assumption:** The semantic space learned by the full model is a superset of that needed by smaller sub-models, so inheritance preserves capability without relearning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "shared-backbone architecture that co-trains models of varying scales... ensuring semantic consistency across all deployment tiers."
  - [section 2] "a single training run yields multiple deployable sub-models... through parameter inheritance and flexible branching."
  - [corpus] Ruyi2 Technical Report (fmr 0.58) describes similar "Train Once, Deploy Many" for text models, suggesting lineage but weak direct support for multimodal extension.
- **Break condition:** Semantic drift occurs between sub-models (e.g., edge misinterprets concepts the cloud handles), indicating alignment failure.

### Mechanism 2: Irreversible De-identification via Information Bottleneck
- **Claim:** Edge-side transformation mathematically prevents reconstruction of raw video while preserving behavior-discriminative features for cloud analysis.
- **Mechanism:** The edge model applies a learned nonlinear mapping + stochastic noise to frame features, optimized under the information bottleneck objective: minimize mutual information between output and input (I(X;Z)) while maximizing relevant information for behavior recognition (I(Z;Y)). This yields abstract embeddings that cannot be inverted to reconstruct pixels.
- **Core assumption:** The behavior-relevant signal survives the bottleneck transformation, and the noise/mapping is sufficiently nonlinear to thwart inverse modeling.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "edge model applies information-bottleneck-guided irreversible feature mapping to de-identify raw frames at the source."
  - [section 2] "applying nonlinear mapping and stochastic noise injection... guided by information bottleneck theory... resulting abstract feature vectors mathematically preclude reconstruction."
  - [corpus] Ye et al. (2024) on privacy via IB is cited, but no direct evidence that this implementation achieves irreversibility.
- **Break condition:** Successful model inversion attack reconstructs identifiable imagery, violating the claim.

### Mechanism 3: Binary Prefix Policy Optimization (BPPO) for RL Efficiency
- **Claim:** BPPO accelerates reinforcement learning fine-tuning 2–3× over GRPO by reducing gradient redundancy while preserving learning signal.
- **Mechanism:** Two orthogonal constraints: (1) **Binary response selection:** From G group samples, keep only one positive and one negative response based on reward, eliminating redundant gradient computation from correlated samples. (2) **Prefix-only backpropagation:** Restrict gradients to early tokens (prefix) of the response, based on the assumption that early steps determine trajectory, reducing memory/time for long chains.
- **Core assumption:** (a) Positive/negative response gradients are highly similar within each group, so one each suffices; (b) Late tokens contribute little new optimization signal compared to prefix.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "reduces sample redundancy via binary response selection and focuses gradient updates on response prefixes, achieving a 2–3× training speedup over GRPO."
  - [section 3.2.3] "gradients induced by positive responses within the same group are highly similar... BPPO therefore retains only one representative positive... gradient propagation is restricted to the response prefix."
  - [corpus] Neighbors include DeepSeek-R1 (GRPO origin) and DAPO (RL scaling), but no direct replication of BPPO design; speedup claim rests solely on internal analysis.
- **Break condition:** Performance degrades on tasks where late reasoning tokens are critical (e.g., multi-step corrections), or when positive/negative gradients are not redundant.

## Foundational Learning

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. Vision Encoder (Ev): Shared ViT (27 blocks, 1152 dim) processes input image I.
  2. Projection Layer (Pv→l): Aligns visual features to LLM space.
  3. LLM Backbone: Text decoder with variable depth (1-block for edge, 36-block for cloud); unified vocabulary.
  4. Edge/Cloud Branching: Controlled by which decoder blocks are active; edge uses only first block(s).
  5. BPPO Module: During RL, selects binary responses per query and masks gradients to prefix tokens.
  6. De-identification Head (Edge): Additional nonlinear layer + noise injection post-Ev, trained with IB loss.

- **Critical path:**
  - Inference: Input frame → Ev → edge de-id head → anonymized features → cloud LLM (full blocks) → behavior output.
  - Training: Unified SFT on multimodal data → RL with BPPO (updates entire backbone, but edge/cloud paths are fixed per deployment target).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Privacy vs. utility: Stronger IB (more compression) risks losing behavior cues; paper claims 93% F1 suggests utility preserved.
  - Compute vs. latency: Edge model depth directly trades accuracy for speed; 1.9B vs. 8B cloud model shows edge-only underperforms cloud-assisted.
  - Training cost vs. deployment flexibility: Single training run saves compute but may not optimize each scale perfectly.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Reconstruction attack success: high SSIM between original and reconstructed frames from cloud features.
  - Semantic drift: edge model outputs features that cloud model misinterprets (e.g., inconsistent action labels).
  - BPPO degradation: plateau or collapse on complex reasoning if prefix truncation removes crucial late tokens.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reversibility test:** Attempt to reconstruct raw frames from edge-output features using a decoder network. Success would break the irreversible claim.
  2. **Cross-model consistency probe:** Feed same video to edge-only and cloud-assisted pipelines; measure agreement on behavior labels. Low agreement indicates failed semantic alignment.
  3. **BPPO vs. GRPO sample efficiency:** Train with equal wall-clock time; compare final reward on geometry RL dataset. If BPPO doesn't reach GRPO's reward, prefix assumption may be invalid.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extending modality support:** How can the shared-backbone architecture incorporate additional modalities (audio, temporal video) while preserving semantic consistency? (Future work: deeper shared foundations for richer modality mixtures; current system limited to image-text; integration risks disrupting unified semantic space. Evidence needed: extend with modality encoders, train on multimodal datasets like AudioSet/Kinetic, evaluate cross-modal tasks.)
- **Finer-grained stratification:** What mechanisms enable finer capability stratification across tiers (device, edge, cloud) to optimize resource allocation without losing "Train Once, Deploy Many" efficiency? (Future work: finer stratification; current system uses only two fixed scales (1.9B edge, 8B cloud); intermediate tiers unexplored. Evidence: train familial models with 3–5 intermediate scales, implement dynamic tier router, measure accuracy-latency-cost curves.)
- **Adaptive routing under network dynamics:** Can adaptive edge-cloud routing maintain real-time performance and privacy guarantee under fluctuating bandwidth? (Future work: adaptive routing; current static routing may force raw video fallback or stall if cloud unreachable. Evidence: design routing policy using network metrics and task sensitivity, test in emulated environments, evaluate latency, accuracy, privacy.)
- **Robustness of de-identification:** Does the IB-guided transformation provide robust privacy against advanced reconstruction attacks (e.g., diffusion-based inversion with auxiliary data)? (Claim "mathematically preclude reconstruction" lacks adversarial evaluation; advanced attacks could recover details. Evidence: apply recent inversion attacks (e.g., face GANs) to edge outputs, quantify reconstruction (LPIPS, SSIM), compare to baselines.)
- **BPPO applicability beyond geometry:** For which multimodal tasks does restricting gradients to response prefixes fail to capture critical signals? (Validated only on geometry; iterative refinement tasks may suffer. Evidence: test BPPO with varying prefix lengths on diverse RLVR tasks like visual dialog, code generation; measure vs. full GRPO; analyze gradient norms per token.)

## Limitations
- Dataset details and licensing are undisclosed, preventing exact replication.
- The edge de-identification module's transformation specifics and the irreversibility claim lack empirical validation (e.g., reconstruction attack tests).
- BPPO's design (binary selection, prefix-only) lacks ablations; its robustness across diverse RLVR tasks is uncertain.

## Confidence
- Shared-backbone co-training: Medium
- Irreversible de-identification: Low
- BPPO acceleration: Medium
- Surveillance task superiority: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Reconstruction attack:** Train an adversarial decoder to reconstruct images from edge features; low SSIM supports irreversibility, high SSIM would refute the claim.
2. **Cross-tier agreement:** Compute label consistency between edge-only (1.9B) and cloud-assisted (8B) outputs on a balanced surveillance test set; >90% agreement is required for semantic alignment.
3. **Sample-efficiency comparison:** Fine-tune base models with BPPO and GRPO under identical wall-clock limits on a geometry RL benchmark; BPPO should achieve ≥2× speedup with comparable final reward.
