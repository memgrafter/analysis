# Impact of Connectivity on Laplacian Representations in Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.08558
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08558
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The paper provides an end-to-end error decomposition for Laplacian-based state representations in RL, linking approximation error to algebraic connectivity λ₂ and spectral gaps, and validates empirically in gridworlds.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the use of graph Laplacian eigenvectors as state representations for reinforcement learning with linear value function approximation. It introduces a Φ-self-adjoint Laplacian formulation to correctly handle non-symmetric state-transition graphs and derives a rigorous error decomposition. The total error splits into truncation error (from limiting the number of eigenvectors to k) and estimation error (from learning eigenvectors via the Graph Drawing Objective). Truncation error scales inversely with algebraic connectivity λ₂ and the (k+1)-th eigenvalue λ_{k+1}, while estimation error depends on the optimality gap ε of the GDO and the spectral gap λ_{k+1}−λ_k. Theoretical analysis highlights connectivity as a key factor: poorer connectivity (smaller λ₂) leads to larger truncation error. Empirical tests in gridworld environments confirm that decreasing connectivity increases approximation error, aligning with the theory.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a state-transition graph from an MDP, computes its Laplacian matrix using the Φ-self-adjoint transformation to ensure symmetry, and extracts the first k eigenvectors as features. Value functions are approximated as linear combinations of these features. The error analysis considers two sources: (1) truncation error from ignoring higher-order eigenvectors, bounded by terms involving λ₂ and λ_{k+1}; (2) estimation error from approximating eigenvectors by minimizing the Graph Drawing Objective (a stress-based method), bounded by the GDO optimality gap ε and the spectral gap λ_{k+1}−λ_k. The results hold for arbitrary policies without requiring symmetry or other structural assumptions on the transition matrix.

## Key Results
- End-to-end error decomposition: total error = truncation error (O(1/λ₂ + 1/λ_{k+1})) + estimation error (O(ε/(λ_{k+1}−λ_k))).
- Algebraic connectivity λ₂ is a critical determinant: smaller λ₂ implies larger truncation error, emphasizing the role of graph connectivity.
- Empirical validation on gridworlds shows a clear correlation between λ₂ and approximation error, supporting the theoretical predictions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Laplacian eigenvectors form an orthonormal basis of functions that are smooth over the state graph; low-frequency eigenvectors (with small eigenvalues) capture global structure, while high-frequency ones capture local variations. Truncation error arises from discarding components beyond the first k eigenvectors; its magnitude is controlled by the eigenvalues of the omitted components. Since λ₂ is the smallest non-zero eigenvalue, it governs how quickly eigenvalues grow—if the graph is poorly connected, many eigenvalues are near zero, so even the first k eigenvectors may not span enough of the function space. Estimation via the Graph Drawing Objective minimizes a stress function that approximates the eigenvectors; the approximation quality (ε) translates to error in the learned features, which is magnified when the spectral gap between λ_k and λ_{k+1} is small, making the eigenvectors nearly indistinguishable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Laplacian Matrix**: Encodes connectivity and edge weights in a graph; needed to define smoothness of functions over states. Quick check: For a simple undirected chain of three nodes with unit weights, L = [[1,-1,0],[-1,2,-1],[0,-1,1]].
- **Algebraic Connectivity (λ₂)**: The second smallest eigenvalue of L; measures how well-connected the graph is. Why needed: Appears directly in the truncation error bound. Quick check: For a disconnected graph, λ₂ = 0.
- **Spectral Gap (λ_{k+1} − λ_k)**: Difference between consecutive eigenvalues; indicates separation between the k-th and (k+1)-th eigenvector. Why needed: Appears in estimation error bound; small gap increases sensitivity to ε. Quick check: For equally spaced eigenvalues, gap is constant.
- **Φ-self-adjoint Laplacian**: Modified Laplacian L_Φ = D^−1/2 Φ (symmetrized) ensuring symmetry for non-symmetric transition matrices. Why needed: Guarantees real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors, enabling spectral analysis. Quick check: Verify L_Φ = L_Φ^T for a simple directed cycle.
- **Graph Drawing Objective (GDO)**: Optimizes node embeddings to preserve graph distances: minimize ∑_{ij} w_{ij}||φ_i−φ_j||^2. Why needed: Allows learning Laplacian eigenvectors from samples without full matrix knowledge. Quick check: The minimizer of GDO yields the eigenvectors corresponding to the smallest non-zero eigenvalues.
- **Linear Value Approximation**: Value function approximated as V(s) ≈ ∑_{i=1}^k α_i ψ_i(s), where ψ_i are eigenvectors. Why needed: Reduces RL to a linear regression problem with low-dimensional features. Quick check: Given exact eigenvectors, solve least-squares for α.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Data (transitions) → Graph construction (edges, weights) → Laplacian computation (Φ-self-adjoint) → Eigen decomposition (exact or via GDO) → Feature extraction (first k eigenvectors) → Linear value approximation → Policy evaluation/improvement.

**Critical path**: Graph construction → Laplacian computation → Eigen decomposition → Feature extraction → Linear value approximation. The quality of the graph and eigen decomposition most directly impacts representation accuracy.

**Design tradeoffs**:
1. Number of eigenvectors k: larger k reduces truncation error but may increase estimation error if the spectral gap is small, and increases feature dimensionality.
2. Exact eigen decomposition vs. approximate GDO: exact gives no estimation error but requires full matrix; GDO scales to large graphs but introduces ε-dependent error and requires optimization.
3. Choice of φ in Φ-self-adjoint (e.g., state visitation frequencies) affects symmetry; poor choice may distort spectral properties.

**Failure signatures**:
- Very small λ₂ (near-zero connectivity) leads to huge truncation error regardless of k.
- Small spectral gap (λ_{k+1} ≈ λ_k) makes estimation error blow up for any non-zero ε.
- Inaccurate graph construction (e.g., insufficient samples, noisy edges) distorts both eigenvalues and eigenvectors, increasing both error components.
- GDO optimization fails to converge (ε remains large), causing large estimation error.

**First experiments**:
1. Vary graph connectivity: In a gridworld, modify transition probabilities to make the graph more or less connected; compute λ₂ and approximation error for a fixed k; expect negative correlation.
2. Sweep over k: Using exact eigenvectors, plot total error, truncation error (by ignoring higher eigenvectors), and estimation error (by injecting noise proportional to ε); verify tradeoff and scaling with λ_{k+1} and spectral gap.
3. Compare GDO vs. exact: For a fixed graph, run GDO with varying optimization budgets (affecting ε) and measure error; check that error scales linearly with ε and inversely with spectral gap.

## Limitations
- Theoretical bounds rely on well-defined spectral properties of the state-transition graph; generalizability to environments lacking a clear graph construction (e.g., visual inputs, continuous control) is uncertain.
- Empirical validation is limited to small gridworlds with uniform connectivity; behavior in sparse, heterogeneous, or dynamically changing graphs remains untested.
- The analysis assumes linear value approximation and exact knowledge of the graph Laplacian; robustness under neural function approximation or noisy graph estimation is not established.

## Confidence
- **End-to-end error decomposition**: High (rigorous linear algebra derivation, no symmetry assumptions).
- **Dominance of connectivity (λ₂) in truncation error**: Medium (theoretically sound but validated only on small, regular grids; sensitivity to graph irregularities untested).
- **Estimation error bound via Graph Drawing Objective gap ε**: Medium (depends on optimization landscape of GDO, which may be non-convex in practice).
- **Φ-self-adjoint Laplacian correctness**: High (resolves known mathematical issues in non-symmetric settings).
- **Empirical confirmation of theory**: Low (limited to toy domains; no comparison to complex MDPs or real-world data).
