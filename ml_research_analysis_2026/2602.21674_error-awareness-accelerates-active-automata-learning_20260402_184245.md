# Error-awareness Accelerates Active Automata Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.21674
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21674
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Error-aware learning variants reduce interactions by up to 3 orders of magnitude when given sound and complete reference knowledge about non-error inputs.

## Executive Summary
Active automata learning often struggles with large input alphabets due to the high cost of conformance testing. This paper introduces the notion of error-persistence and leverages domain knowledge in the form of a reference language L comprising words known to be non-error. By adapting the classic L# algorithm to use L, the authors present four variants—L#e, AL#e, L#e,S, and L#e,SC—covering different levels of available reference knowledge (none, arbitrary, sound, sound and complete). The key technique is to truncate conformance tests as soon as an error is detected and to employ refined apartness relations derived from L. Empirical evaluation on benchmarks from TLS, ASML, and monitor synthesis demonstrates dramatic reductions in the number of symbols exchanged; with sound and complete L, the L#e,SC variant improves by up to three orders of magnitude and eliminates equality queries entirely. Conversely, using an incorrect reference leads to erroneous models, emphasizing the necessity of accurate domain knowledge.

## Method Summary
Reproduction notes for this work were not provided. The method builds upon the L# active learning framework by incorporating a reference language L of non-error words. The learner uses L to define a refined apartness relation between words and to truncate conformance tests when an error state is encountered. Four algorithm variants are proposed, each tailored to a different assumption about L: no reference (L#e), an arbitrary reference (AL#e), a sound reference (L#e,S), and a sound and complete reference (L#e,SC). The variants differ in how they safely apply truncation and whether equality queries (full conformance tests) are necessary. Theoretical adaptations ensure correctness under the respective assumptions, and empirical results validate the claimed performance gains.

## Key Results
- With a sound and complete reference, L#e,SC reduces interactions by up to three orders of magnitude and eliminates equality queries entirely (e.g., 8,368 vs. 1,000,000 symbols on ASML).
- With a sound reference, speedups of one order of magnitude are observed.
- Using an incorrect reference yields wrong models, demonstrating the critical dependence on accurate domain knowledge.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism hinges on two properties: error-persistence and the availability of a reference language L of non-error words. Error-persistence means that once a system reaches an error state, subsequent inputs keep it in that state. This allows a conformance test to be terminated as soon as an error is observed because the remainder of the test word cannot change the outcome—the hypothesis and system are already proven different. The reference language L is used to define an apartness relation: two words are apart if one is in L and the other is not, or more refined notions depending on L's properties. When testing a hypothesis, the conformance tester generates words that are apart according to L; if the system's response differs from the hypothesis's prediction on such a word, the hypothesis is rejected immediately, often before processing the full word. Truncation and apartness together shrink the number of symbols exchanged during conformance tests. Moreover, if L is sound (every word in L is indeed non-error) the learned model is guaranteed correct; if L is also complete (covers all non-error words), the learner never needs a full equality query, as any hypothesis that agrees on all words in L must be correct. Thus, the approach accelerates learning by leveraging domain knowledge to avoid unnecessary work, while maintaining correctness under appropriate assumptions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Active Automata Learning**: A learning paradigm where a learner constructs an automaton model through interactions with a teacher, using membership and conformance queries. *Why needed*: Provides the overall framework. *Quick check*: Does the learner always converge when the teacher answers correctly?
- **L# Algorithm**: A specific active learning algorithm that uses an observation table and counterexamples to iteratively refine hypotheses. *Why needed*: Baseline algorithm extended in this paper. *Quick check*: Does L# guarantee termination with a correct model under a minimally adequate teacher?
- **Error-persistence**: Property that once an input leads to an error state, further inputs remain in an error state. *Why needed*: Enables safe truncation of conformance tests. *Quick check*: If error-persistence holds, can we stop a test upon first error without losing correctness?
- **Reference Language (L)**: A set of words known a priori to be non-error (i.e., accepted by the target automaton). *Why needed*: Supplies domain knowledge to refine apartness and truncation decisions. *Quick check*: Is L sound if all its words are indeed accepted? Is it complete if every accepted word is in L?
- **Apartness Relation**: A binary relation indicating that two words are guaranteed to be distinguished by the target automaton based on L. *Why needed*: Allows early hypothesis rejection during testing. *Quick check*: If two words are apart, are they always mapped to different states or outputs?
- **Conformance Test Truncation**: The technique of stopping a test midway when an error is detected, rather than processing the full word. *Why needed*: Reduces the number of symbols transmitted during tests. *Quick check*: Does truncation preserve the ability to find counterexamples under error-persistence?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Learner → MembershipOracle ; Learner → ConformanceTester ; ConformanceTester → MembershipOracle ; ConformanceTester → ReferenceLanguage.

**Critical path**: 1. Learner issues membership queries to Oracle to fill observation table. 2. Learner constructs a hypothesis automaton from the table. 3. ConformanceTester tests the hypothesis using a test suite derived from L, truncating tests upon errors. 4. If ConformanceTester finds a counterexample, Learner refines the table; otherwise learning terminates.

**Design tradeoffs**: Leveraging L yields massive interaction savings but imposes soundness requirements on L. Variants balance this: L#e,SC assumes L is sound and complete, enabling elimination of equality queries; L#e uses only sound L and retains some equality checks; L#e (no L) reverts to standard L#. More permissive assumptions (arbitrary L) risk unsoundness. The tradeoff is between performance gain and the difficulty of obtaining a reliable L.

**Failure signatures**: (i) Unsounded L (containing an error word) can cause the apartness relation to be invalid, leading to acceptance of an incorrect hypothesis. (ii) Lack of error-persistence in the target system makes truncation unsafe; full counterexamples might be missed, potentially causing non-termination or wrong models. (iii) Incomplete L reduces the effectiveness of early truncation, leading to performance degradation but still correct if L is sound.

## Limitations
- Obtaining a sound and especially a sound and complete reference language L may be challenging in practice, limiting the method's applicability.
- The experimental evaluation is confined to three benchmark families (TLS, ASML, monitors), raising questions about generalizability to other domains.
- The approach relies on error-persistence; if this property does not hold, correctness or performance gains could be compromised.

## Confidence
- Performance acceleration (orders-of-magnitude reduction, elimination of equality queries): High
- Correctness with sound references: High
- Failure with incorrect references: High
- Scalability to large alphabets: Medium
