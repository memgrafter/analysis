# Deep EM with Hierarchical Latent Label Modelling for Multi-Site Prostate Lesion Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14418  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14418  
- **Reference count:** 17  
- **Primary result:** HierEM yields significant improvements in multi-site prostate lesion segmentation, with Dice gains of 27.91–32.67% and better HD95 compared to baselines.

## Executive Summary
Multi-site prostate lesion segmentation suffers from poor cross-site generalisation due to site-specific annotation variability. This paper introduces a hierarchical expectation‑maximisation (HierEM) framework that treats each site’s annotation as a noisy observation of an underlying latent “clean” lesion mask. The method alternates between inferring a voxel‑wise posterior over this latent mask (E‑step) and training a CNN using the posterior as a soft target (M‑step), while hierarchically estimating site‑specific sensitivity and specificity to reduce local bias. In leave‑one‑site‑out evaluations across three cohorts, HierEM significantly outperforms baselines, achieving Dice coefficient improvements of 27.91–32.67% (p<0.039) and lower Hausdorff distances. The approach also provides well‑calibrated uncertainty estimates and interpretable per‑site label quality metrics, offering a robust solution for multi‑site medical segmentation.

## Method Summary
HierEM models each site’s binary lesion annotation as a noisy version of an ideal latent mask. The EM algorithm iterates: in the E‑step, given the current CNN predictions and site‑specific noise parameters (sensitivity/specificity), it computes a voxel‑wise posterior distribution over the latent mask using Bayesian updating. This posterior serves as a soft target for training. In the M‑step, the CNN is updated to minimise the divergence between its predictions and the soft targets, while a hierarchical Bayesian update refines the site‑specific noise parameters by sharing statistical strength across all sites. This decoupling allows the model to learn from uncertain annotations without overfitting to site‑specific artefacts, and the hierarchical sharing improves noise estimation especially for sites with limited data.

## Key Results
- Dice improvements of 27.91–32.67% over baselines (p<0.039) in leave‑one‑site‑out evaluation.  
- Lower HD95 distances, indicating more accurate lesion boundaries.  
- Well‑calibrated uncertainty and per‑site label quality estimates: sensitivity ranges 31.5–47.3% at specificity ≈0.99.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method succeeds by explicitly acknowledging and modelling annotation noise as a site‑specific observation process. The EM framework separates the learning of the segmentation model from the estimation of noise parameters, preventing the CNN from fitting spurious site‑dependent patterns. Hierarchical Bayesian pooling shares information across sites, yielding robust noise estimates even for small sites. The posterior soft targets encode uncertainty and act as a regulariser, while the iterative refinement gradually aligns the CNN predictions with the underlying true anatomy. This results in a model that generalises better to unseen sites and provides reliable uncertainty.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Site Annotation -> Site‑specific Noise Params (sensitivity, specificity) -> E‑step (Latent mask posterior inference) -> Soft Target -> M‑step (CNN weight update) -> Hierarchical Noise Params Update -> (repeat)

**Critical path**  
1. Initialise CNN and site noise parameters.  
2. E‑step: For each site, compute posterior over the latent mask using current CNN outputs and site noise.  
3. M‑step: Update CNN by minimising the KL divergence to the posterior (soft targets).  
4. Hierarchical update: Refine site noise parameters using population‑level priors.  
5. Repeat steps 2‑4 until convergence.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Computational cost*: EM iterations add overhead vs. standard CNN training.  
- *Hierarchical strength*: Too strong priors may bias site‑specific estimates; too weak reduces benefits of sharing.  
- *Soft target quality*: Uncertain posteriors provide regularisation but may slow convergence.

**Failure signatures**  
- EM fails to converge (posterior collapses or oscillates).  
- Site sensitivity/specificity become extreme (near 0 or 1), indicating poor initialization or data issues.  
- CNN overfits to soft targets if posterior variances are underestimated.

**First experiments**  
1. Baselines: Compare single‑site CNN, multi‑site naive pooling, and HierEM.  
2. Ablation: Remove hierarchical sharing (train site noise parameters independently) to measure its impact.  
3. Calibration check: Compute expected calibration error (ECE) and reliability diagrams for HierEM vs. MC‑dropout.
