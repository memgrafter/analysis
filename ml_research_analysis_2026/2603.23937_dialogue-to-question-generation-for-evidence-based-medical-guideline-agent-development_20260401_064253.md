# Dialogue to Question Generation for Evidence-based Medical Guideline Agent Development

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.23937
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23937
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A multi-stage LLM pipeline (summarization → question generation → evaluation) reduces guideline misalignment errors (9.17% vs. 17.22%) compared to zero-shot prompting, producing clinically useful questions (average 5.6/7) but relies on human oversight due to unreliable LLM-as-judge evaluation.

## Executive Summary
Physicians often struggle to apply evidence-based medicine during brief consultations due to the inaccessibility of clinical guidelines. This paper presents an ambient LLM-based assistant (using Gemini 2.5) that generates guideline-relevant questions from real-time patient dialogues. The system compares a zero-shot prompting approach against a multi-stage reasoning pipeline involving summarization, question generation, and evaluation. Six physicians evaluated 80 cases over 90+ hours; both methods yielded clinically meaningful questions, but the multi-stage design reduced guideline misalignment and maintained quality across varied dialogue lengths. However, automated LLM-as-judge evaluation showed inflated scores and missed safety issues, underscoring the necessity of human oversight for clinical deployment.

## Method Summary
The proposed system processes a patient health record questionnaire and a dialogue transcript through three sequential agents. First, the Summarizer Agent condenses the raw dialogue into a structured clinical note (e.g., SOAP format). Second, the Question Generator Agent produces ten candidate questions by grounding the summary in specific guidelines (e.g., American Academy of Sleep Medicine) using few-shot prompting with expert-verified examples. Third, the Question Evaluator Agent employs chain-of-thought reasoning to score each candidate against seven criteria (e.g., relevance, guideline navigation) and selects the top three. This multi-stage pipeline is contrasted with a zero-shot baseline that directly generates questions from the dialogue without intermediate steps. All agents use Gemini models (Flash for summarization, Pro for generation/evaluation).

## Key Results
- Multi-stage and zero-shot methods both generate clinically useful questions, with average human usefulness ratings of ~5.6/7 and no significant difference in overall quality.
- Multi-stage reduces guideline misalignment errors (9.17%) compared to zero-shot (17.22%), a relative reduction of ~47%.
- Question quality remains stable across truncated dialogue contexts (30%, 70%, 100%), with no significant difference in human ratings (ANOVA p > 0.05).
- LLM-as-judge evaluation shows directional agreement with humans but systematically inflates scores and fails to detect certain guideline misalignment and safety errors, indicating unsuitability for standalone clinical assessment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Converting unstructured dialogue into a structured clinical summary improves relevance and completeness of guideline-based questions.  
**Mechanism:** The Summarizer Agent uses a prompt based on clinical schemas (SOAP, FHIR) to extract key slots (e.g., chief complaint, history of present illness), reducing conversational noise and creating an information-dense representation for downstream agents.  
**Evidence:** Pipeline design ([section 4.3]); summary schema grounded in clinical documentation practices ([section 4.3]); reduced misalignment in multi-stage results ([results 6]).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Few-shot prompting with expert-verified examples guides the LLM to mirror clinical reasoning patterns for guideline consultation.  
**Mechanism:** The Question Generator is provided two high-quality examples in its prompt, each embedding patient context and a specific guideline, steering the model to generate specific, evidence-oriented, and categorized questions.  
**Evidence:** Prompt includes expert-verified examples ([section 4.3]); pipeline specification ([abstract]); neighboring work on structured query elements (PICO) supports clinical grounding but not exact curation ([corpus]).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Chain-of-thought evaluation yields more discriminative selection of high-quality questions than single-step scoring.  
**Mechanism:** For each candidate, the evaluator first reasons about pros/cons against seven criteria, then scores each (1.0–5.0) and computes a mean to rank and select top three, preventing uniform high scores and improving comparison.  
**Evidence:** CoT procedure described ([section 4.3]); misalignment reduction in multi-stage ([results 6]); neighboring CoT applications in medical AI but not this specific context ([corpus]).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** `x_phr` (patient health record) + `x_dlg` (dialogue transcript) → Summarizer Agent → `s` (structured summary) → Question Generator Agent → 10 candidate questions → Question Evaluator Agent → `Q = {q1, q2, q3}` (selected questions).
- **Critical path:** Raw dialogue → Summarizer → structured summary → Generator → 10 candidates → Evaluator → top-3 selection → clinician evaluation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Multi-stage vs. Zero-shot:* Multi-stage reduces misalignment (9.17% vs. 17.22%) but adds ~60s latency and token cost; zero-shot is faster/cheaper but less reliable.  
  *Human vs. LLM evaluation:* Human evaluation is gold-standard for safety but costly (~$10k for 90 hours); LLM-as-judge scales cheaply but inflates scores and misses errors.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  Summarizer omits key details (e.g., negated symptoms) → generator questions lack context.  
  Evaluator is lenient or fails to detect guideline mismatches → safety risks.  
  Human rater variability (wide score ranges) → subjective interpretation, single-rater bias.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Summarizer validation:** Compare agent summaries to clinician-authored SOAP notes on a held-out set; measure slot-level F1 for clinical details.  
  2. **Context length stability:** Generate questions at 30%, 70%, 100% truncation; compute variance in human ratings across truncation levels (per ANOVA, should be non-significant).  
  3. **LLM-as-judge correlation:** Compute Spearman correlation between LLM and human scores per metric (expected weak ~0, confirming LLM-as-judge only directional).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Proactive Surfacing Timing:** How can the assistant learn optimal timing, content type, and necessity of surfacing questions to minimize cognitive burden without disrupting the visit flow?  
   *Basis:* Discussion states future work on proactivity.  
   *Why unresolved:* Current system generates a fixed set at every truncation without considering visit phase or physician focus.  
   *Evidence needed:* Longitudinal studies logging presentation/feedback; interactive learning from acceptance/rejection rates.
2. **Grounding Answers in Guidelines:** How can the system reliably ground question answers in specific, up-to-date clinical guidelines with clear provenance, ensuring accuracy and accountability?  
   *Basis:* Discussion notes need for question answering with trusted sources.  
   *Why unresolved:* Only question generation is implemented; answer retrieval/synthesis absent.  
   *Evidence needed:* Implement RAG using authoritative guideline databases; human eval on answer accuracy, citation correctness, utility.
3. **Latency Optimization:** Can the multi-stage pipeline be optimized to reduce computational latency (~60 seconds) while maintaining quality and safety for real-time deployment?  
   *Basis:* Inferred from limitations on processing delays.  
   *Why unresolved:* Sequential multi-agent design causes delays; no speed-quality trade-off explored.  
   *Evidence needed:* Benchmark alternatives (e.g., single-stage with CoT, model distillation) on quality and latency.
4. **Cross-Specialty Generalization:** To what extent does the approach generalize beyond primary care, especially to specialties reliant on visual data (e.g., dermatology) or complex procedural reasoning?  
   *Basis:* Inferred from limitations on evaluation limited to primary care.  
   *Why unresolved:* Evaluation only on text-based primary care; other specialties may require different question types, multimodal inputs.  
   *Evidence needed:* Curate specialty-specific datasets (e.g., with images), adapt pipeline for visual encoders, cross-specialty human evaluations.
5. **Automated Evaluation Reliability:** How can automated evaluation (LLM-as-judge) be improved to align with clinician safety judgments, particularly in detecting guideline misalignment and hallucinations?  
   *Basis:* Inferred from results showing LLM-as-judge misses safety errors and has weak correlation with humans.  
   *Why unresolved:* LLM evaluators unsuitable as standalone assessors for clinical safety.  
   *Evidence needed:* Develop frameworks using physician-annotated error datasets; calibrate LLM judges with expert preferences; combine metrics with uncertainty quantification.

## Limitations
- Generalizability limited to primary care text-based dialogues; specialties relying on visual data or procedural reasoning may require adaptation.
- Clinician rater variability (wide score ranges) may affect reproducibility of human evaluation.
- Single-model dependency (only Gemini 2.5 tested); performance may not generalize across LLM families.

## Confidence
- **High:** Multi-stage pipeline reduces guideline misalignment (9.17% vs. 17.22%) and maintains quality across truncations; backed by 90+ hours of physician evaluation.
- **Medium:** Summarization into structured notes improves question relevance; evidence indirect (pipeline result) but break condition identified if summaries omit details.
- **Low:** LLM-as-judge as reliable substitute for human evaluation; paper shows inflated scores, missed safety issues, weak human-LLM correlation (~0).

## Next Checks
1. **Summarizer Ablation:** Remove Summarizer Agent; feed raw dialogues directly to Question Generator. Measure change in guideline misalignment and human usefulness ratings to isolate summarization's contribution.
2. **Few-Shot Diversity Stress Test:** Curate few-shot examples with minimal condition/type diversity; evaluate if generated questions lose breadth or fail on underrepresented cases (e.g., chronic vs acute).
3. **Cross-LLM & Safety Audit:** Replicate pipeline with another LLM (e.g., GPT-4). Systematically assess safety-critical errors (e.g., harmful medication suggestions) missed by LLM-as-judge but caught by clinicians.
