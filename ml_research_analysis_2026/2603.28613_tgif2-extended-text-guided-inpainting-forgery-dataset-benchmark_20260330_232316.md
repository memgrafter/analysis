# TGIF2: Extended Text-Guided Inpainting Forgery Dataset & Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.28613
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: TGIF2 extends the TGIF dataset with FLUX.1-generated inpainted images and random non-semantic masks to address evolving forensic challenges.

## Executive Summary
TGIF2 introduces an expanded dataset and benchmark for evaluating image forgery detection methods under increasingly sophisticated attack scenarios. The work systematically evaluates state-of-the-art image forgery localization (IFL) and synthetic image detection (SID) methods across various generative model outputs and post-processing attacks. Results reveal significant performance gaps between traditional spliced edits and fully regenerated images, highlighting the evolving challenges in digital forensics. The study demonstrates that while current methods perform well on conventional manipulations, they struggle with advanced generative content and post-processing operations like super-resolution.

## Method Summary
The study extends the TGIF dataset by incorporating FLUX.1-generated inpainted images with random non-semantic masks, creating a more comprehensive benchmark for forgery detection. Researchers evaluated multiple IFL methods (CAT-Net, TruFor) and SID approaches (B-Free, StainedGlassForensics) on this expanded dataset. The evaluation included testing performance across different generative model subsets (FLUX.1, SD2, SDXL), analyzing fine-tuning effects on specialized data, and assessing robustness against generative super-resolution attacks using Real-ESRGAN. The benchmark provides quantitative metrics including F1 scores for IFL and AUC scores for SID across various experimental conditions.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art IFL methods achieve F1 >0.8 on spliced edits but drop to F1 <0.1 on fully regenerated images without fine-tuning
- Fine-tuning improves IFL localization (TruFor F1 up to 0.87 on FLUX.1 FR-Sem) but performance degrades on random masks (F1 down to 0.52)
- Generative super-resolution attacks severely impact forensic performance, reducing IFL F1 by up to 0.80 and SID AUC by up to 0.37

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of current forensic methods relies on detecting specific artifacts and inconsistencies in manipulated regions. For spliced edits, these methods successfully identify boundary artifacts, lighting inconsistencies, and semantic mismatches. However, fully regenerated content lacks these traditional forensic traces, making detection significantly more challenging. The performance degradation on random masks after fine-tuning reveals that methods may be learning object-specific features rather than robust forensic signatures. Super-resolution attacks effectively smooth out the high-frequency artifacts that forensic methods typically exploit, explaining the severe performance drops observed across both IFL and SID tasks.

## Limitations
- IFL methods show severe performance degradation on fully regenerated images compared to spliced edits
- SID methods demonstrate limited generalization across different generative model architectures
- Current forensic traces are highly vulnerable to generative super-resolution attacks
- Object bias in IFL methods suggests learning of spurious correlations rather than robust features

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: IFL performance degradation on fully regenerated vs spliced images is consistently demonstrated across multiple methods.

**Medium Confidence**: SID method performance on different generative model subsets shows consistent patterns but may be influenced by dataset characteristics.

**Medium Confidence**: Impact of generative super-resolution attacks on forensic performance is demonstrated but may vary with different implementation parameters.
