# Concept-based explanations of Segmentation and Detection models in Natural Disaster Management

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23020
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23020
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** Introduces an explainability framework for flood segmentation (PIDNet) and car detection (YOLO) in natural disaster management, extending LRP to handle sigmoid-gated fusion layers and applying PCX for concept-level explanations, showing reliable identification of prediction strategies and outliers with near real-time performance suitable for resource-constrained platforms.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the lack of transparency in deep learning models used for natural disaster management, specifically flood segmentation and car detection, which hinders operator trust. It proposes a novel explainability framework that extends Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) to handle the sigmoid-gated fusion layers in PIDNet and, for the first time, applies Prototypical Concept-based Explanations (PCX) to segmentation and detection tasks. Evaluated on a UAV flood dataset, the framework delivers reliable, concept-level explanations, successfully uncovers model prediction strategies and flags outliers (like a roof window misdetected as a car), and operates near real-time for deployment on constrained platforms like UAVs. Quantitative perturbation evaluations confirm that LRP-based concept relevances are more faithful than gradient or activation baselines.

## Method Summary
The method comprises three core components: first, an extension of LRP to PIDNet’s element-wise sigmoid-gated fusion layers via a “signal-take-all” redistribution rule, ensuring end-to-end attribution coverage. Second, PCX is applied: LRP relevances are summed per feature map to form “concept relevance vectors,” which are clustered (via GMM) over the training set to derive prototypical prediction strategies. At inference, a test sample’s concept vector is matched to the nearest prototype; large dissimilarity flags atypical predictions. Third, explanation quality is quantified through perturbation-based metrics (AOC for deletion, AUC for insertion), measuring how perturbing feature maps ranked by relevance affects output logits.

## Key Results
- The framework provides reliable, concept-level explanations for both flood segmentation and car detection models.
- It successfully identifies model reasoning strategies and flags outlier predictions, exemplified by detecting a roof window misclassified as a car.
- Inference is near real-time, making it suitable for resource-constrained platforms like UAVs.
- LRP-based concept relevances consistently outperform gradient and activation methods in perturbation-based fidelity evaluations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Extending LRP to PIDNet's sigmoid-gated fusion layers enables end-to-end attribution explanations covering the entire network.
- **Mechanism:** Introduces a "signal-take-all" redistribution rule (Eq. 2) for element-wise multiplication layers `y = x ⊗ σ(g)`. Relevance from output `y` is assigned entirely to the signal branch `x`, while the gating branch `g` receives zero relevance, interpreting `σ(g)` as a pure modulator.
- **Core assumption:** The gating function’s effect is fully captured by the signal’s relevance flow; the gate itself does not contribute independent evidence.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 3.2] Explicit proposal of the redistribution strategy for `y = x ⊗ σ(g)` and relation to LSTM prior work.
  - [abstract] Highlights the novel redistribution strategy extending LRP to sigmoid-gated fusion layers.
  - [corpus] Focus on NDM architectures lacks LRP extensions for such fusion modules, indicating novelty.
- **Break condition:** Assumes forward-pass multiplication is the only interaction. If the fusion modifies gradients or uses additive/non-modulatory pathways, relevance misassignment occurs, breaking conservation `∑𝑅(𝑙)𝑖 = ∑𝑅(𝑙+1)𝑗`.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Applying PCX to segmentation and detection reveals dataset-wide prediction strategies and flags atypical/outlier predictions.
- **Mechanism:** For each prediction, latent LRP relevances are summed per feature map into a “concept relevance vector.” Training set vectors are clustered (GMM); centroids are “prototypes” representing strategies. Test samples match nearest prototype; large dissimilarity indicates an outlier.
- **Core assumption:** (1) Individual feature maps act as detectors for human-interpretable concepts. (2) Clustering groups samples by similar internal reasoning. (3) Dissimilarity to any prototype indicates an unreliable prediction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] Details pipeline: LRP relevances → per-feature-map sum → GMM clustering → prototype matching for outlier detection.
  - [section 4.4] Outlier detection example: a roof window misdetected as a car had a concept profile (“white vehicle side windows”) over-used relative to its prototype, correctly flagging the error.
  - [section 4.3] Shows semantically meaningful prototypes for car detection (white cars, dark cars, ambulances, blurry) and flood segmentation (linear floods, wide inundation).
  - [corpus] PCX is a recent method (2024 workshop); neighboring NDM papers do not apply concept-based clustering to detection/segmentation.
- **Break condition:** If feature maps are not semantically aligned or clustering fails to separate meaningful strategies (high intra-cluster variance), explanations become noise. Outlier detection assumes training set covers true data distribution; novel disaster scenarios not represented would all be flagged.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Perturbation-based evaluation (AOC/AUC of concept deletion/insertion) quantitatively validates that LRP-based concept relevances are more faithful than activations or random.
- **Mechanism:** For a batch of samples, feature maps are sorted by their summed LRP relevance (concept importance). The prediction logit is measured while progressively *deleting* (setting to zero) the most relevant feature maps (AOC) or *inserting* (activating) them from a baseline (AUC). Higher area indicates that impactful concepts, as ranked by the method, more strongly affect the output.
- **Core assumption:** A faithful explanation method should rank feature maps whose perturbation causes larger logit changes as more relevant. The perturbation metric (logit change) is a valid proxy for alignment with the model’s internal decision logic.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] Reports AOC/AUC scores across layers for PIDNet and YOLO, showing LRP variants (`ε`, `z+`, `γ`) and Grad-CAM/Gradient outperform Activation and Random.
  - [Figure 1] Visualizes these scores, with LRP rules consistently highest.
  - [section 2.2] Context: States that perturbation-based evaluations in controlled settings have demonstrated superior quality of decomposition-based methods like LRP.
  - [corpus] This evaluation paradigm (feature flipping) is standard in XAI; neighboring papers do not report such quantitative fidelity scores for their models.
- **Break condition:** Assumes *linear additive* effects of feature maps on the logit. Highly non-linear or redundant feature interactions could cause compensation, understating true importance. Evaluation is layer-specific and aggregated; a method could score well on average but fail critically on specific layers.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) backward pass rules**
  - *Why needed:* The entire explainability pipeline relies on LRP to generate pixel-wise and latent feature attributions. Understanding conservation (`∑𝑅(𝑙)𝑖 = ∑𝑅(𝑙+1)𝑗`) and rule design (e.g., `ε`, `z+`) is key to interpreting heatmaps and concept vectors.
  - *Quick check:* Why is the `ε`-rule used for linear layers, and what does the `ε` parameter control?
- **Concept: Feature maps as concept detectors**
  - *Why needed:* PCX treats convolutional feature maps as semantic concept detectors. The engineer must grasp that summing LRP relevances over a feature map's spatial dimensions yields a scalar "concept importance" for that channel.
  - *Quick check:* If a feature map's spatial pattern looks like "curved white edges," what might its summed LRP relevance indicate about a model's reasoning for a "flood" prediction?
- **Concept: Clustering for prototype discovery (GMM)**
  - *Why needed:* PCX uses Gaussian Mixture Model clustering on concept relevance vectors to find prototypical prediction strategies. The engineer must understand cluster assignment, centroids as prototypes, and metrics like cosine similarity to the global mean.
  - *Quick check:* If a cluster centroid has a large negative cosine similarity to the dataset's mean concept vector, what does that suggest about the samples in that cluster?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Trained Model (PIDNet/YOLO) -> LRP Engine (modified for custom layers) -> Concept Vector Extractor (sums relevances per feature map) -> Prototype Database (pre-computed GMM clusters from training set) -> PCX Explainer (matches test sample to prototype, retrieves maximally-activating references) -> Evaluation Harness (perturbation for AOC/AUC)
- **Critical path:** Forward pass (image→prediction) → LRP backward pass (prediction→input & all layers) → sum relevances per feature map → compute cosine similarity to all prototype centroids → if dissimilarity > threshold, flag as outlier → retrieve & visualize.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *LRP rule choice:* `z+`-rule is often preferred for vision for fidelity, but `ε` performed best in perturbation tests. Tradeoff: empirical performance vs. theoretical properties.
  - *Clustering granularity:* More clusters (`K`) yields finer-grained strategies but risks overfitting. Paper used 4 (YOLO) and 10 (PIDNet) based on manual inspection.
  - *Concept interpretability:* Relies on feature maps being semantically meaningful. If not, retrieved reference images become uninterpretable, reducing utility.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - LRP fails to produce smooth, object-focused heatmaps (shattered gradients) → check if propagation rules are correctly implemented for all layer types (especially custom fusion).
  - All prototypes have high intra-cluster variance and low inter-cluster separation → GMM `K` is wrong or concept vectors are not discriminative.
  - Perturbation AOC/AUC scores near random → concept relevances are not aligned with actual feature importance; likely LRP rule or concept extraction issue.
  - PCX flags nearly all test samples as outliers → training set does not cover operational data distribution; or similarity threshold is too strict.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Unit test LRP extension:** Take a minimal PIDNet fusion module, feed a dummy input, manually compute forward pass (`y = x ⊗ σ(g)`), apply Eq. (2) backward, verify `𝑅𝑥 + 𝑅𝑔 = 𝑅𝑦` (conservation).
  2. **Prototype sanity check:** On a small subset (100 training images), run PCX pipeline with `K=2`. Manually inspect the top 5 retrieved images per prototype's top 3 concepts. They should show coherent visual patterns (e.g., all "white car sides" for one prototype).
  3. **Perturbation baseline comparison:** For a single convolutional layer, compute concept relevances via LRP, Gradient, and Activation. Perform deletion (AOC) on 10 random samples. Expect LRP to show steeper initial drop in logit.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does the LRP extension for sigmoid-gated fusion layers generalize to other architectures with different gating mechanisms, such as attention-based models or alternative segmentation networks in NDM?
  - Basis in paper: The method is specifically designed for PIDNet's fusion layers and not evaluated on other models.
  - Why unresolved: Other architectures may have structural differences affecting LRP propagation, limiting claims of broad applicability.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Apply the LRP extension to various NDM architectures, compare explanation quality via perturbation metrics or human evaluation, and assess computational overhead.
- **Open Question 2:** What systematic approach can determine the optimal number of clusters in PCX for segmentation and detection tasks to balance prototype interpretability and coverage of prediction strategies?
  - Basis in paper: Fixed cluster counts (4 for YOLO, 10 for PIDNet) are used without rationale or sensitivity analysis.
  - Why unresolved: Cluster count directly impacts granularity; arbitrary choices may lead to over-splitting or lumping distinct behaviors.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments varying cluster counts across datasets, evaluate using metrics like cluster cohesion, silhouette score, and downstream task performance.
- **Open Question 3:** Do concept-based explanations from PCX effectively enhance operator trust and situational awareness in real-world disaster response scenarios, as measured by human expert feedback?
  - Basis in paper: The framework aims to build trust for emergency response, but no user study with domain experts is conducted.
  - Why unresolved: Technical feasibility is demonstrated, but empirical validation with end-users is missing, leaving practical impact unclear.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Implement the framework in a simulated disaster management environment, conduct controlled user studies with emergency responders, and measure changes in trust, comprehension, and action selection.
- **Open Question 4:** How robust are PCX prototypes and outlier detections to data heterogeneity, such as varying image quality, sensor types (e.g., satellite vs. UAV), or disaster categories beyond floods?
  - Basis in paper: Evaluation is limited to a single UAV flood dataset; outlier examples hint at data quality issues (e.g., blurry cars).
  - Why unresolved: Real-world NDM involves diverse conditions; untested robustness risks unreliable explanations in deployment.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Test the framework on multiple NDM datasets (wildfire, earthquake), including synthetic or degraded data, and quantify explanation stability, prototype shift, and outlier detection consistency.

## Limitations
- Reconstruction requires missing training details (augmentation, optimizer, learning rate, preprocessing) and PCX hyperparameters (GMM covariance type, number of clusters, channel selection criteria, concept retrieval method).
- Correctness of the LRP signal-take-all rule depends on exact implementation of PIDNet’s fusion modules; scaling or additive components would break relevance conservation.
- Semantic interpretability of feature maps is assumed; some prototypes show negative similarity to the dataset mean, indicating potential misalignment.
- Perturbation fidelity (AOC/AUC) assumes additive feature effects; non-linear interactions or redundancy may invalidate the scores.
- Evaluation uses a single UAV flood dataset; generalizability to other disaster scenarios, sensors, or geographic regions is unknown.
- Training data distribution may not cover all operational conditions, causing PCX outlier detection to flag many valid predictions.
- Ad-hoc choice of cluster counts (4/10) and lack of quantitative validation for PCX prototype quality undermine robustness.
- Real-time inference claim lacks hardware specifications and benchmark details, preventing assessment of resource constraints.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (LRP extension for sigmoid-gated fusion):** Medium – rule is plausible and supported by overall LRP success in perturbation tests, but lacks direct, independent validation (e.g., unit tests or ablation).
- **Mechanism 2 (PCX for strategy/outlier detection):** Medium – qualitative prototypes and outlier example are promising, yet feature map semantics are unverified and cluster selection is ad‑hoc.
- **Mechanism 3 (Perturbation evaluation):** High – consistent quantitative superiority across layers and strong baseline comparisons back the claim, though it relies on the linearity assumption.
