# CLewR: Curriculum Learning with Restarts for Machine Translation Preference Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05858  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05858  
- **Reference count:** 17  
- **Primary result:** CLewR systematically reuses an easy-to-hard data ordering at every training epoch to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, yielding consistent improvements in BLEU and COMET across multiple LLMs and preference methods.

## Executive Summary
CLewR introduces a simple yet effective wrapper for preference optimization in machine translation. It addresses the overlooked problem of catastrophic forgetting of easy examples when using a standard easy-to-hard curriculum. By repeatedly restarting the same sorted order every epoch, the model reinforces learning on all samples. The method uses a multi-metric similarity score to rank sample difficulty and can be combined with various preference techniques (DPO, CPO, ARPO). Enhanced variants of ARPO incorporate external MT metric signals into the distance function. Extensive experiments across Gemma2, Qwen2.5, and Llama3.1 show statistically significant gains in translation quality.

## Method Summary
CLewR first computes a difficulty score `s` for each preference triplet `(x, yw, yl)` as the average of normalized BLEU, COMET-22, and METEOR between the chosen and rejected translations. Triplets are sorted by increasing `s` (easy to hard). During training, for each epoch, mini-batches are formed by sequentially slicing this fixed list—no shuffling—so the same order is reused every epoch. This restart mechanism combats catastrophic forgetting. The approach acts as a wrapper around any preference optimizer (e.g., DPO, CPO, ARPO). For ARPO, the authors propose CLewR-z (using `-zθ`) and further variants where the distance function `z'` fuses model-intrinsic distance with metric-derived distances (BLEU/COMET), controlled by scalar weights `η`.

## Key Results
- On Llama3.1 with ARPO, CLewR boosts COMET from 87.48 to 87.94 (en→xx) and BLEU from 35.92 to 37.99 (xx→en).
- Consistent and statistically significant improvements in BLEU and COMET across Gemma2, Qwen2.5, and Llama3.1 models.
- CLewR outperforms both non-curriculum baselines and the prior curriculum method CurriDPO.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Restarting the Curriculum to Mitigate Catastrophic Forgetting**  
Repeatedly cycling through a fixed easy-to-hard order across training epochs forces the model to repeatedly encounter and refine its predictions on easy samples. This spaced repetition counteracts the tendency of later, harder samples to overwrite previously learned stable patterns, reducing catastrophic forgetting.

**Mechanism 2: Multi-Metric Similarity Scoring for Sample Easiness**  
The composite score `s` (average of normalized BLEU, COMET-22, METEOR differences between chosen and rejected) creates a robust ranking of sample difficulty. Using multiple complementary metrics captures nuances a single metric might miss, leading to a more reliable easy-to-hard ordering.

**Mechanism 3: Metric-Derived Distance Integration in ARPO**  
For ARPO, the distance function `z'` is augmented with external metric signals (BLEU/COMET distances). This makes the adaptive penalty term sensitive to both model uncertainty (`zθ`) and external quality assessments, enabling finer-grained preference margins and further performance gains.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Raw Preference Data -> Scoring (compute `s`) -> Sorted Index List `I` -> Training (Epoch 1: `I` slices; Epoch E: repeat `I` slices) -> Final Model.

**Critical path:**  
The non-standard step is the deterministic reuse of `I` across epochs, which interleaves the same easy-to-hard order into every pass.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Scoring function complexity (multi‑metric) vs. robustness and computational cost.  
- Fixed restart frequency (every epoch) vs. adaptive schemes (e.g., restart every N steps).  
- Metric selection (BLEU/COMET/METEOR) vs. task‑specific or model‑based metrics that may align better with the policy.

**Failure signatures:**  
- No improvement or degradation versus non‑curriculum baseline, especially on DPO.  
- High variance across random seeds due to fixed ordering.  
- Metric mismatch: if chosen metrics poorly discriminate quality, the easy/hard split becomes arbitrary.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Scoring function sanity check: On a held‑out set of 100 triplets, manually verify that low `s` corresponds to obviously different translations and high `s` to subtle ones.  
2. Restart vs. single pass: Train two models with the same fixed order—one with CLewR (restarts each epoch) and another performing a single pass without restarts—to isolate the restart effect.  
3. Metric ablation: For a fixed model/PO (e.g., Llama3.1+ARPO), train CLewR variants where `s` is derived from only BLEU, only COMET, only METEOR, and random ordering to identify which metric(s) drive gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does CLewR generalize to other language families and LLM families?  
2. Why does CLewR harm DPO but help CPO/ARPO?  
3. Would a dynamic curriculum that updates ordering during training improve over static CLewR?

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to Romance languages; broader language family testing is needed.  
- Exact normalization method for score `s` and evaluation details (Flores‑200 split) are unspecified.  
- Critical hyperparameters (β for DPO/CPO, η for ARPO‑z′, LoRA configuration, learning rate, seeds) are missing, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- CLewR yields consistent, statistically significant improvements across models and methods: **High**  
- Restarting the curriculum mitigates catastrophic forgetting: **Medium**  
- Multi‑metric similarity scoring produces a reliable easy‑to‑hard ordering: **Medium**  
- Metric‑derived distance integration (ARPO‑z′) improves performance: **Medium**  
- Full reproducibility from the provided information: **Low**
