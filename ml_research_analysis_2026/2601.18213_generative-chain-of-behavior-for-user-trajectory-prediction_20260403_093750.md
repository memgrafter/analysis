# Generative Chain of Behavior for User Trajectory Prediction
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.18213
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18213
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** GCB outperforms state-of-the-art sequential recommenders (e.g., SASRec, DCRec) on multi-step trajectory prediction across two Amazon datasets.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the limitation of existing sequential recommenders that focus solely on single-step next-item prediction, neglecting long-term user behavior trajectories and evolving intents. It proposes the Generative Chain of Behavior (GCB) framework, which first encodes items into a structured discrete latent space using Residual Quantized VAE (RQ-VAE) with k-means codebook initialization, and then uses a transformer-based autoregressive generator to produce coherent multi-step future behavior chains. Experiments on Amazon Beauty and Cell Phones datasets show consistent and significant improvements over strong baselines across multiple prediction horizons, demonstrating superior multi-step accuracy and trajectory coherence.

## Method Summary
GCB consists of two core modules. First, an RQ-VAE with k-means–initialized codebooks quantizes continuous item embeddings into hierarchical discrete semantic IDs, creating a compact vocabulary where semantically similar items share proximate codes. Second, a transformer decoder, trained with teacher forcing, autoregressively generates sequences of these semantic IDs representing future user behavior, conditioned on the encoded history. At inference, beam search generates candidate token sequences, which are partitioned into fixed-length blocks and mapped back to actual items via a precomputed dictionary. This design shifts the generation challenge from a vast continuous item space to a structured discrete latent space, mitigating error accumulation in long-horizon prediction.

## Key Results
- On Amazon Beauty Seq-1 (predicting next item), GCB achieves MHR@5 of 0.0454 vs. SASRec’s 0.0327 and DCRec’s 0.0393.
- Performance gains persist for longer horizons (Seq-2, Seq-3) and transfer to the Cell Phones dataset.
- GCB demonstrates superior trajectory-level consistency (SeqHR/SeqNDCG) compared to baselines that optimize per-step accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Structured Discrete Latent Space Stabilizes Generation
- **Claim:** Mapping items to a structured discrete semantic ID space stabilizes multi-step generative prediction.
- **Mechanism:** RQ-VAE with k-means codebook initialization quantizes item embeddings into hierarchical discrete codes via residual quantization. This creates a compact vocabulary where semantically similar items share nearby codes, reducing entropy and combinatorial explosion when generating long sequences.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic proximity in the original item embedding space is preserved through residual quantization and k-means clustering, enabling coherent transitions between generated codes.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.1 describe the RQ-VAE quantization and k-means refinement. Figure 2 shows L1 clusters correspond to high-level categories and L2 sub-clusters to specific types.
- **Break condition:** If k-means fails to capture global item structure or RQ-VAE reconstruction loss is high, the semantic ID space becomes noisy, leading to incoherent trajectories.

### Mechanism 2: Autoregressive Transformer Models Intent Evolution
- **Claim:** The autoregressive transformer generator can model long-horizon intent evolution by predicting chains of semantic IDs.
- **Mechanism:** The transformer decoder predicts future semantic ID tokens sequentially, conditioning on the encoded user history and previously generated tokens. This explicit chain generation forces the model to capture dependencies across multiple future steps, not just the immediate next item.
- **Core assumption:** User behavior trajectories exhibit temporal patterns that can be modeled as a conditional distribution over sequences of semantic behaviors, and transformer self-attention is sufficient to capture these dependencies.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "transformer-based autoregressive generator predicts multi-step future behaviors as a coherent chain." Section 3.2 formalizes the conditional distribution \( p_\theta(Y_u|X_u) \) and training via teacher forcing.
- **Break condition:** If historical context \( X_u \) is insufficiently expressive or model capacity is too low, the generator may produce semantically inconsistent or repetitive token sequences, especially for longer horizons (Seq-3).

### Mechanism 3: Hierarchical Semantic IDs Constrain Generation Space
- **Claim:** Hierarchical semantic IDs (via multi-level RQ-VAE) encode coarse-to-fine item semantics that improve trajectory coherence.
- **Mechanism:** The first RQ-VAE level (L1) groups items into broad categories, while subsequent levels (L2) refine into specific subtypes. This hierarchy constrains the generation space: early tokens in a semantic ID block set a coarse category, and later tokens select precise items within it, reducing invalid transitions.
- **Core assumption:** Item semantics are naturally hierarchical (e.g., "chargers" → "wireless chargers"), and this structure is discoverable via residual quantization on pre-trained item embeddings.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4, Figure 2 shows category distribution in L1 and L2 clusters. Section 3.1 describes multi-level quantization \( c_i = (c_{i,1}, ..., c_{i,L_c}) \).
- **Break condition:** If pre-trained item embeddings lack clear hierarchical structure or higher-level k-means produces fragmented clusters, semantic IDs may not reflect meaningful item relationships, harming generation quality.

## Foundational Learning
### Residual Quantized VAE (RQ-VAE)
- **Why needed:** Provides the core mechanism for converting continuous item embeddings into discrete codes with a compressed, semantically structured vocabulary.
- **Quick check:** How does residual quantization at multiple levels differ from a single-level k-means clustering in terms of reconstruction error and codebook utilization?

### K-means codebook initialization
- **Why needed:** Prevents codebook collapse in RQ-VAE by seeding clusters with globally distributed centroids, ensuring a diverse initial discrete space.
- **Quick check:** Why is k-means applied to the *residuals* at each level rather than to the original embeddings?

### Autoregressive sequence generation with transformers
- **Why needed:** The transformer decoder models the joint distribution of future semantic ID tokens, capturing dependencies across steps in the trajectory.
- **Quick check:** In the training objective (Eq. 18), why is teacher forcing used, and what mismatch does it introduce at inference time?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Item Feature Encoder → RQ-VAE Module (with K-means Initializer) → Semantic-ID Tokenizer → History Tokenizer → Transformer Generator (Encoder-Decoder) → Beam Search Decoder

**Critical path:**
1. Raw item is converted to a feature vector by the Item Feature Encoder.
2. The RQ-VAE Module (with K-means Initializer) encodes the feature vector into a sequence of discrete semantic ID tokens (length L_c=4).
3. The Semantic-ID Tokenizer maps the item to its token sequence.
4. The History Tokenizer concatenates a user's historical semantic ID sequences (with EOS and padding) to form the input sequence X_u.
5. The Transformer Generator's encoder processes X_u to produce contextual representations.
6. The decoder autoregressively generates the target token sequence Y_u (future tokens) conditioned on the encoder output and previously generated tokens.
7. The Beam Search Decoder explores multiple candidate token sequences (beam size 100).
8. Each candidate token sequence is partitioned into blocks of length L_c (4 tokens) and each block is mapped to an item via the `code_to_item` dictionary (built during training).
9. Invalid beams (with blocks not in the dictionary) are discarded, and the remaining trajectories are ranked.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Discrete vs. Continuous IDs:* Discrete IDs enable stable long-horizon generation but lose fine-grained similarity; continuous embeddings retain nuance but suffer from compounding errors in multi-step prediction.
- *Hierarchical RQ-VAE:* More levels capture finer semantics but increase token length and risk of codebook collapse; paper uses fixed length 4 with one level for collision avoidance.
- *Beam search size:* Larger beam (100) improves trajectory quality but increases compute; smaller beams may yield less coherent chains.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Codebook collapse:** Many codes unused, semantic diversity lost. Monitor code usage frequency during RQ-VAE training.
- **Invalid beams:** Generated token sequences cannot be mapped to any item (missing `code_to_item` entry). Ensure dictionary covers all possible code combinations from training.
- **Degeneracy at long horizons (Seq-3):** Sharp metric drop across all models indicates inherent difficulty; if GCB's drop is disproportionate, check if transformer depth is sufficient for long-range dependencies.
- **Incoherent trajectories:** Generated items within a trajectory are semantically unrelated (e.g., "phone case" → "shampoo"). Indicates semantic ID space not preserving proximity or transformer not modeling cross-step constraints.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ablation on RQ-VAE code levels:** Train GCB with \( L_c \in \{2,3,4,5\} \) and compare multi-step metrics. Expect performance peak at \( L_c=4 \) as in paper; lower levels lose semantics, higher levels introduce noise/length issues.
2. **K-means vs. random initialization:** Compare GCB with randomly initialized codebooks (no k-means). Expect significant drop in multi-step accuracy, confirming k-means stabilizes the discrete space.
3. **Beam search tuning:** Vary beam size \( B \in \{10, 50, 100\} \) and measure MHR@5 for Seq-1/2/3. Larger beams should improve longer-horizon metrics due to better sequence-level optimization, but with diminishing returns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not include a dedicated open questions or future work section. However, the identified limitations implicitly raise several important questions for subsequent research: (1) How well does GCB generalize to non-e-commerce domains (e.g., video, social platforms)? (2) Will trajectory coherence translate to improved user satisfaction in online deployments? (3) Can the approach scale efficiently to million-item catalogs? Addressing these questions would strengthen the practical impact of GCB.

## Limitations
- **Dataset Scope:** Evaluation limited to two Amazon categories; generalizability to other domains (e.g., video, social) is unknown.
- **Real-World Efficacy:** Results are offline metrics; no online A/B test or user study confirming trajectory coherence in practice.
- **Scalability:** Beam search (size 100) and RQ-VAE training may not scale to million-item catalogs; compute costs unreported.

## Confidence
- **Multistep accuracy gains (MHR/SeqHR):** High
- **Semantic ID mechanism validity (RQ-VAE + k-means preserving proximity):** Medium
- **Long-horizon coherence:** Medium
- **Generalization vs. new generative paradigms (diffusion/flow):** Low

## Next Checks
1. Cross-domain replication: evaluate GCB on a non-e-commerce dataset (e.g., MovieLens sequences) to test transferability of semantic ID structure.
2. Efficiency-stress test: measure training/inference time and memory consumption as item catalog size scales (10k → 100k items), comparing to SASRec.
