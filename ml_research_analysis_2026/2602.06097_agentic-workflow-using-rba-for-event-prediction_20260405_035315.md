# Agentic Workflow Using RBA$_θ$ for Event Prediction

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.06097
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06097
*   **Reference count:** 30
*   **Primary result:** Proposes an event-first, frequency-aware paradigm using enhanced RBAθ, achieving event prediction precision up to ~0.84 at 24h and high-fidelity trajectory reconstruction (R² ~0.90).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel agentic forecasting system for wind power ramps, shifting from predicting continuous power to forecasting discrete, operationally relevant ramp events. The core innovation integrates an enhanced Ramp-by-Angle (RBAθ) method for event extraction with wavelet-based frequency decomposition to isolate ramp dynamics into mid-frequency bands. A contextual multi-armed bandit agent dynamically selects between specialized forecasting workflows (e.g., LSTM, Transformer) based on dataset characteristics. The system demonstrates strong performance on event prediction and trajectory reconstruction, with evidence of robust zero-shot transfer to unseen wind farms, suggesting a pathway toward adaptable, decision-oriented renewable forecasting.

## Method Summary
The methodology centers on a modular pipeline orchestrated by an agentic selector. Input wind power series are decomposed via Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) into frequency bands. Enhanced RBAθ is applied per band to extract event features (onset, duration, magnitude, type). These features, combined with weather covariates and temporal encodings, are refined by a stratified multi-armed bandit (MAB) for horizon-sensitive feature selection. Sequence models (LSTM or Transformer) then predict event attributes and band-specific magnitude/duration. A reconstruction module synthesizes a continuous power trajectory from the predicted events and the DWT approximation band. The agentic layer uses a contextual bandit to choose the best workflow (e.g., SARIMAX, Random Forest, LSTM, Transformer) for a given dataset context, updating its policy from execution rewards. Key assumptions include the operational relevance of event-based targets and the concentration of ramp energy in mid-frequency bands. Reproduction requires specifying RBAθ parameters (threshold multiplier, minimum duration) and LSTM architecture details, which are partially underspecified.

## Key Results
*   Event prediction precision reaches approximately 0.84 at a 24-hour forecast horizon.
*   Trajectory reconstruction achieves high fidelity with R² ~0.90 and RMSE ~51.1 MW.
*   The agentic workflow selection demonstrates improved cumulative reward and enables zero-shot transfer to unseen wind farms, yielding precision ~0.60.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Using enhanced RBAθ to extract ramp events provides a compressed, operationally relevant representation that aligns forecasting with grid decisions.
*   **Mechanism:** RBAθ applies slope-angle analysis with adaptive thresholds to identify ramp intervals and derive features (onset, duration, magnitude, type). These event-level descriptors become the primary prediction targets rather than dense power values.
*   **Core assumption:** Grid operations are triggered by discrete ramp events, not small pointwise deviations; therefore, focusing learning on these rare but impactful occurrences improves decision utility.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...proposes an event-first, frequency-aware forecasting paradigm that directly predicts ramp events and reconstructs the power trajectory thereafter."
    *   [section II-A] Details of RBAθ event extraction, including adaptive thresholds and feature computation.
    *   [corpus] Neighboring papers on wind ramp classification also move toward event-based formulations, indicating community recognition of this shift.
*   **Break condition:** If event definitions become inconsistent across datasets or adaptive thresholds fail to capture regime shifts, the representation loses alignment with operational reality and degrades downstream performance.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Wavelet-based frequency decomposition isolates ramp dynamics into specific mid-frequency bands, enabling targeted modeling and noise suppression.
*   **Mechanism:** Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) splits the power series into one approximation band and multiple detail bands (D1–D4). Empirical analysis shows that ramp energy concentrates in the mid-frequency bands (D3, D4), while low-frequency captures trends and high-frequency contains turbulence. The model then predicts event attributes per band, effectively focusing on informative scales.
*   **Core assumption:** Wind ramps are multi-scale phenomena with physically distinct drivers; separating these scales prevents a single model from conflating trend, ramp, and noise dynamics.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...frequency-aware forecasting paradigm...integrate wavelet-based frequency decomposition."
    *   [section II-C2] "Multi-resolution targets and inputs...level-4 DWT decomposes P(t) into...D3 (approx 2–8 h) exhibits the largest variance...most ramp-related energy."
    *   [section IV-B] Per-band R² heatmaps confirm that magnitude and duration predictions are strongest in Details_3 and Details_4.
*   **Break condition:** If ramp energy is not reliably concentrated in mid-frequencies (e.g., due to extreme turbulence or very slow ramps), the band partition fails to isolate predictive content, and reconstruction may suffer from missing high- or low-frequency contributions.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** An agentic workflow selection layer, using contextual multi-armed bandits, dynamically chooses the most suitable forecasting pipeline for a given dataset and operational context, enhancing robustness and transferability.
*   **Mechanism:** The agent maintains an experience base of past executions (workflow used, dataset context, reward). For a new dataset, it computes a context vector (volatility, entropy, etc.), retrieves similar past executions, estimates expected utility for each workflow via a weighted average, and selects using an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) rule with adaptive exploration. Contradiction detection decays synthetic bootstrap influence when it diverges from real evidence.
*   **Core assumption:** No single forecasting architecture dominates across all wind farm regimes; context-aware selection yields higher cumulative reward (forecast fidelity + event quality + efficiency) than a fixed model.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...agentic forecasting layer...workflows are selected dynamically based on operational context."
    *   [section III] Full specification of the CMAB agent, including context representation, reward definition, and exploration strategy.
    *   [section IV-E] Agentic system shows cumulative reward improvement over fixed rules, demonstrating adaptive selection.
*   **Break condition:** If the context features do not capture regime differences, or if the reward function misweights event precision versus other metrics, the agent may consistently pick suboptimal workflows, especially under rapid environmental changes.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Enhanced RBAθ Event Extraction**
    *   *Why needed:* It defines the event semantics (onset, duration, magnitude, type) that become the prediction targets. Without understanding this representation, the event-first paradigm cannot be implemented.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does RBAθ’s adaptive thresholding improve robustness compared to static thresholds for ramp detection?
*   **Concept: Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) for Multi-Resolution Analysis**
    *   *Why needed:* DWT provides the frequency bands that isolate ramp dynamics from trends and noise. This decomposition is central to the frequency-aware design and reconstruction.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why are mid-frequency bands (e.g., D3) typically more informative for ramp magnitude than the approximation band?
*   **Concept: Contextual Multi-Armed Bandit (CMAB) for Adaptive Model Selection**
    *   *Why needed:* It enables the system to autonomously pick the best forecasting workflow (e.g., LSTM vs. Transformer) based on dataset characteristics, which is critical for robustness across heterogeneous wind farms.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does the agent balance exploitation of known good workflows with exploration of alternatives, and why is this balance necessary?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Raw Power & Weather -> DWT (A4,D4,D3,D2,D1) -> RBAθ per Band -> Feature Engineering (RBAθ, DWT stats, weather, temporal, Hawkes) -> Stratified MAB Selection -> Sequence Encoder (LSTM/Transformer) -> Prediction Heads (occurrence, type, time, band mag/dur) -> Reconstruction (inverse DWT with A4 anchor) -> Agentic Selector (Context computation, experience retrieval, UCB/ε-greedy selection, reward logging)
*   **Critical path:** For a new dataset D: 1) Agent computes context C(D) & retrieves similar executions. 2) Agent selects workflow w_i based on estimated utility + exploration. 3) Execute w_i pipeline: DWT -> RBAθ -> feature engineering -> MAB selection -> encode -> predict -> reconstruct. 4) Compute composite reward R(w_i,D) & store record. 5) Repeat, refining agent policy.
*   **Design tradeoffs:*
    *   *Speed vs. richness:* SARIMAX-RBAθ (Approach 1) is fast but lacks event prediction; deep workflows (3/4) provide rich outputs but require hours of training.
    *   *Precision vs. recall:* Approach 3 (LSTM) tends toward higher recall; Approach 4 (Transformer) yields smoother, higher-precision event sequences.
    *   *Complexity vs. transferability:* Deep models risk overfitting, but frequency-aware design and MAB selection mitigate this, enabling zero-shot transfer.
*   **Failure signatures:*
    *   Event extraction yields irregular/fragmented events -> check RBAθ adaptive thresholds and minimum duration parameters.
    *   MAB selection converges to non-predictive feature subset -> monitor feature importance distributions; increase exploration if a category's quota is unmet.
    *   Agent repeatedly selects same workflow despite changing context -> examine contradiction detection; raise exploration if synthetic and real best workflows diverge.
    *   Short-horizon (1–3 h) precision remains below ~0.5 -> consider incorporating NWP inputs or increasing short-term context window.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline evaluation:** Train and test all four approaches on the primary dataset (Baltic Eagle) to obtain reference metrics (event F1, reconstruction R², training time).
    2.  **Ablation study (Approach 3):** Systematically remove RBAθ features and/or MAB selection; quantify degradation in precision and F1 at multiple horizons to validate component necessity.
    3.  **Zero-shot transfer & agentic selection:** Apply the best workflow(s) to unseen farms (Baie de Saint‑Brieuc, London Array) without retraining. Compare agent‑chosen workflow against a fixed‑best baseline to demonstrate adaptive generalisation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** Can a modular, agentic forecasting system dynamically select and combine specialized workflows in real-time based on evolving signal characteristics (e.g., volatility, horizon, regime) without prohibitive computational overhead?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] RQ1 in Table I directly poses the design of a modular, agentic system for workflow selection based on signal characteristics.
*   **Why unresolved:** The proposed agentic layer uses a contextual multi-armed bandit with only four workflows and handcrafted context features. Computational costs, context representation limits, and scalability to many workflows or high-dimensional contexts remain unevaluated.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic benchmarks with 10+ workflows, learned context embeddings (e.g., via representation learning), and latency measurements on streaming data from diverse wind farms, showing consistent reward improvement with bounded decision time.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** Does joint optimization of ramp detection and forecasting require explicit event semantics (e.g., RBAθ), or can end-to-end models learning from raw power series achieve comparable interpretability, transferability, and operational alignment?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] RQ2 asks for a unified framework jointly optimizing ramp detection and forecasting.
*   **Why unresolved:** Ablation studies show catastrophic performance collapse without RBAθ features, especially at short horizons. It is unclear if alternative event representations (e.g., unsupervised segmentation) or raw-signal models can match the event-aware architecture’s robustness and transferability.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative experiments replacing RBAθ with other event extraction methods (e.g., change-point detection) within the same multi-resolution framework, evaluating cross-site transferability and semantic interpretability of predictions.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** What computationally efficient methods exist for providing well-calibrated, event-level uncertainty estimates (e.g., confidence in timing, magnitude, duration) in multi-resolution deep forecasting models?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] RQ3 calls for event-level uncertainty modeling and quantification.
*   **Why unresolved:** The authors explicitly state that Bayesian techniques (e.g., MC dropout, ensembles) were incompatible with computational constraints, leaving only implicit uncertainty via RBAθ and Hawkes processes. No probabilistic calibration metrics for events are reported.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Integration of efficient approximate Bayesian inference (e.g., stochastic variational inference) or quantile regression into the multi-band LSTM/Transformer architecture, validated using event-specific proper scoring rules (e.g., CRPS for event intervals) and reliability diagrams.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Do event-first, frequency-aware forecasts yield measurable operational benefits (e.g., reduced reserve activation, lower curtailment, improved revenue) compared to trajectory-first methods in actual power system decision-making?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] RQ4 seeks benefits of the event-first, frequency-aware approach regarding accuracy, robustness, reconstruction, and computational efficiency.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates statistical improvements (precision, R²) on reanalysis data but does not quantify impacts on operational outcomes like unit commitment costs, balancing market performance, or turbine fatigue. Computational trade-offs are noted but not optimized for operational constraints (e.g., forecast latency requirements).
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Simulation or field trials integrating event-based forecasts into grid optimization tools (e.g., security-constrained unit commitment), comparing key performance indicators (costs, emissions, reliability) against trajectory-based forecasts under realistic scenarios.

### Open Question 5
*   **Question:** How robust is the agentic, event-centric framework when applied to wind farms with highly heterogeneous data regimes (e.g., severe imbalance, short records, missing data) and across different temporal resolutions (e.g., 10-min, daily), and can it generalize to other domains (e.g., solar ramp events)?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] RQ5 addresses robustness across sites, temporal resolutions, data regimes, and generalization beyond wind power.
*   **Why unresolved:** Zero-shot transfer succeeded on two offshore farms but failed on another dataset due to feature mismatch and data imbalance. The framework assumes long, balanced time series and single-site modeling; its performance on sub-hourly data, sparse records, or multi-farm settings is untested. Generalization to other renewables is speculative.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Comprehensive evaluation on a benchmark suite of wind farms with varying data quality, event frequencies, and resolutions; development of domain adaptation techniques for event-aware models; and adaptation to solar ramp events with modified event definitions (e.g., cloud-induced ramps).

## Limitations
*   **Event Extraction Robustness:** Exact RBAθ parameters (e.g., threshold multiplier `k`, minimum duration `d_min` per band) are underspecified, risking inconsistent event definitions on diverse wind regimes.
*   **Agentic Selection Context:** The precise formulation of the context vector and composite reward function are opaque, limiting understanding of the agent's generalization boundaries.
*   **Transferability Assumptions:** Zero-shot claims rely on latent similarity between training and target farms; the source and degree of this similarity are not validated, and failure on one farm indicates fragility.

## Confidence
*   **High:** Event-First Paradigm & RBAθ Utility. Strongly supported by methodology and outcome metrics (precision ~0.84).
*   **High:** Frequency-Aware Decomposition (Wavelet). Empirically validated via per-band R² heatmaps and energy analysis.
*   **Medium:** Agentic Workflow Selection. Validated by cumulative reward improvement, but core context/reward details are underspecified.
*   **Low:** Generalization / Zero-Shot Transfer. Based on limited experiments; failure on one farm suggests unexamined covariate shift issues.
