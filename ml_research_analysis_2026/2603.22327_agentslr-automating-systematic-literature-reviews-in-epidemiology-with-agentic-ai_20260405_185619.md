# AgentSLR: Automating Systematic Literature Reviews in Epidemiology with Agentic AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.22327
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22327
- Reference count: 40
- AgentSLR achieves 58x speed-up in SLR production while maintaining comparable quality to human reviewers

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces AgentSLR, an open-source, end-to-end AI pipeline for automating systematic literature reviews in epidemiology. The system performs article retrieval, screening, structured data extraction, and report synthesis using large language models with tool-calling. Evaluated against expert-curated ground truth on nine WHO priority pathogens, AgentSLR achieves performance comparable to human researchers while reducing review time from approximately 7 weeks to 20 hours (a 58x speed-up). Human expert validation found 79.8% average extraction accuracy, with reviewers rating the system as moderately to highly competent. Model ablations across five frontier LLMs showed performance depends more on model-specific capabilities than size or cost, with no single model dominating all stages. The results demonstrate that agentic AI can substantially accelerate scientific evidence synthesis in specialized domains while maintaining quality through human-in-the-loop validation.

## Method Summary
AgentSLR is an autonomous, multi-stage pipeline that automates systematic literature reviews for epidemiological research. The system performs article retrieval through bibliographic database queries, screening via language reasoning models, PDF-to-Markdown conversion for full-text processing, structured data extraction using schema-constrained tool calling, and report generation with iterative refinement. The pipeline processes articles through two screening stages (title/abstract then full-text) before extracting epidemiological parameters, models, and outbreak data. Evaluation compared performance across five frontier LLMs and against human expert ground truth for four WHO priority pathogens, measuring recall, precision, and F1 scores across screening and extraction stages.

## Key Results
- AgentSLR achieves 58x speed-up (20 hours vs 7 weeks) while maintaining performance comparable to human researchers
- Direct full-text screening improves recall to 0.89 but reduces precision to 0.68 compared to two-stage screening (recall 0.81, precision 0.83)
- Open-weight models achieve competitive performance (F1=0.70) at over 96× lower cost than top closed models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: AgentSLR achieves orders-of-magnitude efficiency gains while maintaining coverage through strategic design choices in screening and full-text processing.
- Mechanism: The pipeline reduces active review time by 19.3x through parallel execution, automated PDF-to-Markdown conversion, and direct full-text screening that avoids the bottleneck of human abstract triage. At screening stages, conditioning full-text screening on ground-truth (human) abstract screening decisions improves recall to 0.92 and precision to 0.83, while direct full-text screening improves recall to 0.89 at the cost of precision (0.68). This trade-off is acceptable because false inclusions are correctable downstream while false exclusions are not.
- Core assumption: Human abstract screening is the primary bottleneck in systematic review production, and abstracts under-report relevance compared to full texts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - AgentSLR completes each report with an average wall clock time of 20 hours, processing 9,132 articles at title/abstract screening, 1,102 at full-text screening and 395 at data extraction. Figure 2 shows the time comparison to human expert estimates, where AgentSLR's runtime (≤ 1 day) represents a 19.3-times efficiency gain over the corresponding human processes (385 hours).
  - With each paper taking minutes to process (Wallace et al., 2010), direct full-text screening is operationally infeasible for human teams. Our autonomous two-stage screening achieves a recall of 0.81, and skipping abstract screening to process full-texts directly improves recall to 0.89 at a 2.3× runtime increase.
- Break condition: If abstracts become comprehensive enough to capture all relevant details, or if full-text processing becomes prohibitively expensive, the advantage of direct full-text screening diminishes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Current open-weight models offer a viable foundation for scientific SLR deployment, achieving performance comparable to closed-source frontier models at substantially lower cost.
- Mechanism: Within the evaluation, open-weight models achieve performance comparable to closed-source frontier models while operating at substantially lower cost: gpt-oss-120b achieves similar performance (F1 = 0.70) at over 96× lower cost than GPT-5.2 (F1 = 0.69), while Kimi-K2.5 achieves the best overall performance (F1 = 0.74) at a mid-range cost. The convergence in performance across models during extraction stages suggests that tool use during extraction may offset differences in base model capabilities.
- Core assumption: Open-weight models with sufficient context windows and tool-calling capabilities can match the performance of larger, more expensive closed models for structured scientific tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Our comparison of five frontier models reveals that performance on SLR is driven less by model size or inference cost than by each model's distinctive capabilities.
  - gpt-oss-120b achieves competitive average performance (F1 = 0.70) at the lowest total cost ($13.9), over 96 times cheaper than GPT-5.2 (F1 = 0.69), while Kimi-K2.5 achieves the best overall performance (F1 = 0.74) at a mid-range cost ($277).
- Break condition: If model capabilities diverge significantly for complex scientific reasoning tasks, or if open-weight models face restrictions on scientific content, the cost-performance advantage may diminish.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Human-in-the-loop deployment is the appropriate mode for automated SLR tools like AgentSLR, delivering substantial efficiency gains within human-led processes.
- Mechanism: While AgentSLR lacks the contextual understanding to fully automate an epidemiological SLR, it delivers substantial efficiency gains within human-led processes. Manual review limits SLR scalability, and full-text processing requires substantially more resources than abstract-only triage. Given strong classification performance, AgentSLR is well-suited to expedite full-text screening after human abstract filtering. For data extraction, high recall ensures that relevant evidence persists for human validation, and experts report improved efficiency when provided with AgentSLR's outputs.
- Core assumption: Human expertise remains essential for contextual understanding, quality assurance, and final decision-making in systematic reviews.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Through human-in-the-loop validation, we identify key failure modes. Our results demonstrate that agentic AI can substantially accelerate scientific evidence synthesis in specialised domains.
  - Human-in-the-loop deployment is the appropriate mode for automated SLR tools like AgentSLR. While AgentSLR lacks the contextual understanding to fully automate an epidemiological SLR, it delivers substantial efficiency gains within human-led processes.
- Break condition: If model capabilities advance to match or exceed human contextual understanding for scientific reasoning, or if human validation becomes prohibitively expensive relative to model performance, full automation may become feasible.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Article Search and Retrieval -> Title and Abstract Screening -> PDF-to-Markdown Conversion -> Full-text Screening -> Data Extraction -> Report Generation

- Critical path: Article Search and Retrieval → Title and Abstract Screening → Full-text Screening → Data Extraction → Report Generation
  - Each stage depends on successful completion of the previous stage, with data extraction being the most computationally intensive

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Open-weight vs closed-source models: Cost vs performance trade-off, with open-weight models offering comparable performance at lower cost
  - Direct full-text vs two-stage screening: Recall vs precision trade-off, with direct full-text screening improving recall but reducing precision
  - Tool-calling vs fine-tuning: Flexibility vs specialization trade-off, with tool-calling enabling adaptation without model retraining

- Failure signatures:
  - Low recall in screening: May indicate overly strict exclusion criteria or model limitations in understanding complex inclusion criteria
  - Low precision in extraction: May indicate model struggles with ambiguous or implicit information in scientific texts
  - High variance across models: May indicate task difficulty rather than model-specific weaknesses, particularly for complex extraction tasks

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run AgentSLR on a small test corpus (e.g., 100 articles) and compare screening performance against manual screening to validate recall/precision metrics
  2. Test different model configurations (e.g., different open-weight models) on the same test corpus to identify performance-cost trade-offs
  3. Validate extracted data against ground truth for a subset of articles to assess field-level accuracy and identify common error patterns

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the specific error patterns in AgentSLR's parameter extraction when handling population context fields?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Human expert validation showed AgentSLR struggles with population fields due to many options with specific epidemiological interpretations
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a weakness but doesn't provide detailed error analysis or propose solutions for handling population context
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic analysis of incorrect population field extractions with categorization of error types and suggested improvements to the tool-calling schema

### Open Question 2
- Question: How would heterogeneous multi-agent configurations perform compared to single-model approaches across all AgentSLR pipeline stages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "future work could explore generalization to additional scientific fields" and "different models excel at different stages"
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows performance differences across models for individual stages, it doesn't test routing sub-tasks to complementary models
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments routing different pipeline stages to models with complementary capabilities and measuring overall performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the operational impact of systematic versus random error patterns in AgentSLR's screening stages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The discussion notes that "error structure to matter just as much as error rate" and distinguishes between random and systematic failures
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as important but doesn't characterize the systematic error modes or their downstream consequences
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of error distribution across study designs, publication periods, and parameter reporting styles to identify systematic biases

## Limitations

- Performance variability across SLR stages, with extraction showing more variable results (F1 0.47-0.82) compared to screening (F1 0.73-0.92)
- Limited evaluation scope covering only four of nine WHO priority pathogens and one human expert reviewer
- Struggles with population context fields in parameter extraction due to complex epidemiological interpretations

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- AgentSLR achieves substantial efficiency gains compared to human reviewers (19.3x improvement)
- Open-weight models can achieve performance comparable to closed-source models at significantly lower cost
- Human-in-the-loop validation remains essential, with expert reviewers rating the system as moderately to highly competent

**Medium Confidence:**
- Direct full-text screening improves recall at the cost of precision, making it suitable for human-led processes
- Model-specific capabilities drive performance more than model size or cost across the five evaluated LLMs
- The system achieves 79.8% average extraction accuracy through human expert validation

**Low Confidence:**
- The system's performance generalizes to all nine WHO priority pathogens (only four have been evaluated)
- The optimal model choice for different SLR stages (no single model dominates all stages)
- The long-term viability of open-weight models for scientific SLR applications given potential content restrictions

## Next Checks
