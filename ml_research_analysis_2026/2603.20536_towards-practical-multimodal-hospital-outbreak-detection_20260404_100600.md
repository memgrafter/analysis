# Towards Practical Multimodal Hospital Outbreak Detection
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20536
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20536
- **Reference count**: 23
- **Primary result**: Trimodal integration of MALDI-TOF, antimicrobial resistance, and EHR data achieved highest clustering fidelity (NMI: 0.831) and positive predictive value (0.295), enabling a tiered surveillance paradigm that reduces WGS referrals while maintaining detection accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the high cost and slow turnaround of whole genome sequencing (WGS) for hospital outbreak detection by developing a machine learning framework that fuses three lower-cost, faster data modalities: MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry, antimicrobial resistance (AR) patterns, and electronic health records (EHR). The integrated model approximates genetic relationships derived from WGS, allowing for accurate clustering of likely transmission events. A tiered surveillance strategy uses this model to triage isolates, referring only predicted singletons for WGS confirmation, thereby reducing sequencing burden. Counterfactual analysis of EHR data pinpoints high-risk clinical procedures, offering actionable targets for infection control interventions.

## Method Summary
The framework was trained on 4,921 bacterial isolates across 17 pathogen species, with outbreak clusters defined by WGS single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) distances ≤15 SNPs. Each modality is encoded separately: MALDI-TOF spectra via a 1D convolutional neural network (128-D), AR phenotypes via a deterministic mapping followed by PCA (128-D), and EHR procedure codes via a TF-IDF → truncated SVD → MLP pipeline (256-D). Embeddings are standardized, PCA-aligned to a common dimension, and fused using fixed weighted averages (e.g., trimodal: MALDI 0.2, AR 0.6, EHR 0.2). Agglomerative Ward clustering on fused vectors identifies outbreak clusters, with distance thresholds selected per species to maximize F1-score on training data. The tiered paradigm refers isolates outside any cluster for WGS. Counterfactual risk analysis removes groups of procedure codes from EHR inputs, re-encodes isolates, and measures Wasserstein distance shifts in predicted intra-cluster SNP distributions to flag high-risk exposures.

## Key Results
- Trimodal integration (MALDI+AR+EHR) outperformed all unimodal and bimodal baselines, achieving a Normalized Mutual Information of 0.831 and positive predictive value of 0.295 across 17 species.
- The tiered surveillance paradigm reduced the percentage of isolates requiring WGS referral while maintaining combined sensitivity and positive predictive value above a null random-referral baseline.
- Counterfactual EHR ablation identified specific high-risk clinical procedures, such as those involving ureter guidewires and esophagoscopy, as potential contamination routes driving outbreak clusters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Integrating complementary signals from MALDI-TOF, AR patterns, and EHR improves outbreak detection fidelity beyond any single modality.
- **Mechanism**: Each modality encodes distinct transmission risk aspects (microbial fingerprint, resistance phenotype, shared clinical exposure). Feature embeddings are fused via PCA and weighted averaging, then clustered. Fusion weights are optimized to maximize downstream clustering F1-score, enabling complementary evidence combination.
- **Core assumption**: WGS SNP distances (≤15 SNPs) define true transmission events, and the learned multimodal distances meaningfully approximate these genetic relationships.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Trimodal model achieves highest NMI (0.831) and PPV (0.295) versus unimodal baselines (Table 2).
  - Species-specific PPV gains from EHR integration, especially for complex pathogens like *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Proteus mirabilis* (Figure 2).
- **Break condition**: If pathogen transmission is driven by genetic factors poorly captured by MALDI/AR/EHR (e.g., rapid microevolution without resistance or procedure changes), multimodal integration may not help or could degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: A tiered surveillance paradigm using multimodal models as front-line triage reduces WGS referral volume while maintaining combined detection accuracy above a null baseline.
- **Mechanism**: The model assigns isolates to predicted clusters. Unclustered isolates (predicted singletons) are referred for WGS confirmation. By varying the clustering distance threshold, one trades off referral percentage (p) against model sensitivity/PPV. Combined metrics are weighted averages: `Combined Sensitivity = Sensitivity_model × (1−p) + 1 × p` (WGS sensitivity = 1).
- **Core assumption**: The model’s false negatives are acceptably low, and WGS confirmation of unclustered isolates captures them, preserving overall sensitivity. The model’s PPV is sufficiently high to avoid excessive false-positive investigations.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Tiered paradigm reduces WGS need (abstract).
  - Figure 3 plots combined sensitivity/PPV vs. % WGS referral for key species, showing curves above the diagonal null baseline for all multimodal models.
- **Break condition**: If model sensitivity is too low, combined sensitivity drops significantly even with WGS fallback. If model PPV is too low, combined PPV suffers due to many false-positive clusters.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Counterfactual feature ablation of EHR procedure codes identifies high-risk clinical procedures associated with outbreak clusters.
- **Mechanism**: For each isolate in a cluster, remove all procedure codes in a target group (e.g., "ESOPHAGOSCOPY") from its EHR history, re-encode through the frozen pipeline (TF-IDF → SVD → MLP), and compute the Wasserstein distance between distributions of predicted intra-cluster SNP distances before vs. after removal. A large shift indicates the procedure group strongly contributed to the model grouping that isolate with others.
- **Core assumption**: The EHR encoder’s representations causally reflect shared exposure risk from procedures, and removing a truly causal procedure significantly alters the isolate’s embedding distance to its cluster mates.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Counterfactual analysis identified ureter guidewires and esophagoscopy as high-risk (abstract).
  - Table 3 lists top risk groups with high mean/max risk scores and ΔSNPpred.
  - Methods detail the ablation pipeline and risk metric.
- **Break condition**: If the EHR encoder learned spurious correlations, ablation may flag low-risk procedures. If cluster formation is dominated by MALDI/AR signals, EHR ablation may show low risk even for truly risky procedures.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Distance-mapping loss function
  - **Why needed**: The goal is to cluster isolates into outbreak groups matching WGS SNP distances. A classification loss alone does not guarantee that embedding Euclidean distances correlate with genetic distances. The dual-objective loss explicitly penalizes divergence between pairwise embedding distances and true SNP distances, shaping the latent space for clustering.
  - **Quick check**: If the model accurately predicts species but its embeddings group genetically distant strains together, would it be suitable for outbreak detection? Why? 
- **Concept**: TF-IDF with frequency filtering and Truncated SVD
  - **Why needed**: Raw procedure codes are high-cardinality categorical text. TF-IDF weights discriminative procedures. Filtering removes extremely rare procedures (overfitting noise) and ubiquitous ones (non-informative baseline). SVD reduces dimensionality to a dense, denoised representation capturing latent clinical pathways.
  - **Quick check**: Why might a procedure appearing in >90% of patient records be excluded from the TF-IDF matrix?
- **Concept**: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for modality alignment
  - **Why needed**: Unimodal encoders produce features of different dimensionalities (MALDI: 128-D, AR: 142-D, EHR: 256-D). PCA projects AR and EHR vectors onto 128-D subspaces that maximally retain variance, ensuring fusion isn’t biased by original dimensionality differences.
  - **Quick check**: If we directly concatenated the raw standardized vectors without PCA alignment, what potential problem could arise in the weighted sum fusion?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Isolate -> [MALDI enc] -> [AR enc] -> [EHR pipe] -> Standardize & PCA-align -> Weighted sum fusion -> Agglomerative clustering -> Cluster assignment; Risk analysis: Isolate -> Frozen EHR pipe -> Counterfactual ablation -> Wasserstein distance.
- **Critical path**: Isolate -> unimodal encoders -> fusion -> clustering -> assignment. Risk analysis runs offline on clustered data.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Precision (PPV) vs. Sensitivity: Raising clustering threshold increases PPV but reduces sensitivity. Tiered paradigm shifts this tradeoff by using WGS to recover sensitivity.
  - Modality weight optimization: Heavier AR weight (0.6 in trimodal) may improve PPV for complex species but could overfit if AR patterns are noisy.
  - EHR window size: Fixed 30-day window; shorter may miss exposures, longer introduces noise.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High singleton rate (>80%) with low combined sensitivity: Model fails to identify clusters; threshold too strict or modalities uninformative.
  - Low PPV (<0.1) despite high sensitivity: Clusters are largely false positives; fusion weights or modality representations misleading.
  - Counterfactual risk scores near zero for all procedures: EHR encoder failed to learn procedure-exposure associations, or outbreaks not driven by tracked procedures.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Unimodal baseline establishment**: Train MALDI, AR, and EHR encoders separately. Evaluate clustering performance (NMI, ARI, Sens, PPV) on held-out folds. Confirm MALDI is strong on PPV, AR on sensitivity, EHR on NMI.
  2. **Trimodal integration validation**: Fuse all three modalities with paper-specified weights. Compare performance against unimodal and bimodal baselines. Expect highest NMI and PPV.
  3. **Tiered surveillance curve sweep**: For a target species (e.g., *E. coli*), run clustering at 20 evenly spaced distance thresholds. For each, compute % isolates needing WGS, combined sensitivity, and combined PPV. Plot curves to verify they lie above the diagonal null baseline. Identify a practical operating point (e.g., 40% WGS referral with >90% combined sensitivity).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Can transformer-based architectures that integrate spatial metadata (e.g., patient room locations, equipment usage logs) improve outbreak detection sensitivity and positive predictive value for pathogens with complex cluster structures compared to the current MLP-based EHR encoding?
- **Basis**: The Discussion states they plan to explore advanced architectures like transformers and incorporate spatial metadata.
- **Why unresolved**: The current framework uses an MLP on TF-IDF encoded procedure codes, which may not effectively model long-range dependencies or spatial relationships critical for transmission inference.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implement a transformer variant that processes sequences of patient procedures augmented with spatial coordinates and equipment identifiers. Evaluate its performance against the existing model, particularly for high-complexity species.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What specific population genetic and antimicrobial resistance biology factors explain the observed species-specific variation in multimodal integration benefits, such as why EHR integration markedly improves PPV for some pathogens but not for others?
- **Basis**: The Discussion notes plans to investigate underlying population genetics and resistance biology.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper demonstrates heterogeneous gains across species but does not investigate genomic correlates (e.g., recombination rates, clonality, resistance gene mobility) that might drive these differences.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extract genomic features from WGS data for each species. Statistically model the relationship between these features and the delta in performance metrics from unimodal to trimodal across the 17 species.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: To what extent do the arbitrary EHR processing choices—specifically the fixed 30-day procedure window and frequency-based filtering (excluding procedures with <10 or >90% prevalence)—constrain the framework's ability to detect outbreaks linked to rare procedures or those with longer latency periods?
- **Basis**: Methods describe the 30-day window and filtering thresholds without empirical justification or sensitivity analysis.
- **Why unresolved**: These thresholds could discard epidemiologically relevant signals: a 30-day window might miss procedures with long incubation, and filtering rare procedures could eliminate low-frequency but high-risk equipment uses.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct a systematic ablation study varying the time window (e.g., 7, 30, 90, 180 days) and frequency thresholds (e.g., min 5, 20 isolates; max 80%, 95% prevalence). Measure changes in overall clustering performance and assess whether high-risk procedures are retained or lost.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Does the proposed tiered surveillance paradigm maintain comparable outbreak detection accuracy and cost-effectiveness when deployed in hospitals with heterogeneous EHR systems, clinical coding practices, and baseline pathogen epidemiology?
- **Basis**: The study validates on a single-hospital dataset; Discussion notes future economic analyses are needed.
- **Why unresolved**: EHR data structures, procedure code taxonomies, and patient populations vary widely across institutions. The model’s feature extraction may not generalize, and the trade-off between WGS reduction and detection accuracy could differ based on local factors.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Perform a multi-center validation study using data from 3–5 diverse hospitals. Apply transfer learning or domain adaptation. For each site, compute combined sensitivity/PPV curves and WGS reduction percentages, and simulate cost-benefit analyses using site-specific cost parameters.

## Limitations
- **U1**: Exact architectures and hyperparameters of the MALDI-CNN and EHR-MLP (layers, filter sizes, learning rates, etc.) are unspecified, risking replication fidelity.
- **U2**: The mapping from raw EHR procedure codes to the 121 categorized groups is not provided, preventing risk analysis reproduction.
- **U3**: Fusion weight optimization method is unclear; published weights may be dataset-specific and not generalize.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Multimodal Fusion)**: High – Strongly supported by Table 2/Figure 2 metrics across 17 species.
- **Mechanism 2 (Tiered Surveillance)**: Medium – Figure 3 curves show utility, but depend on untuned weights/thresholds and lack real-world cost analysis.
- **Mechanism 3 (Counterfactual Risk)**: Medium-Low – Table 3 identifies procedures, but ablation logic assumes causal EHR embeddings without external validation.
