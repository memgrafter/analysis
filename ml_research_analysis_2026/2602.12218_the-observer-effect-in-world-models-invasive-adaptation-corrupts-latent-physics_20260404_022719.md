# The Observer Effect in World Models: Invasive Adaptation Corrupts Latent Physics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12218
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12218
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Frozen linear probing (PhyIP) reveals latent physics in SSL world models, while invasive adaptation (fine-tuning) corrupts these structures and produces misleading evaluation outcomes.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a critical "observer effect" in evaluating neural world models: standard practices that fine-tune models on downstream physics tasks can corrupt their internal representations of physical laws, leading to inaccurate assessments of pre-trained knowledge. The authors introduce PhyIP, a non-invasive protocol that uses frozen linear probes and symbolic regression to test whether physical quantities are linearly decodable from self-supervised learning (SSL) representations. Across fluid dynamics (TheWell) and orbital mechanics, PhyIP recovers laws like internal energy (ρ>0.90) and gravity (ρ≈0.91) under out-of-distribution shifts when SSL error is low. Invasive methods (full fine-tuning, nonlinear probes) instead collapse decodability (ρ≈0.05) and mask SSL failures—for instance, in supernova simulation, invasive probing reports 18.3% MAPE while PhyIP reveals 140% error. Mechanistically, fine-tuning induces representational drift (CKA<0.2) and selectively erases dynamic variables (speed, radius) while preserving static ones (mass). Thus, low-capacity linear probes on frozen models offer a more reliable assessment of latent physics, demonstrating that adaptation itself can be destructive.

## Method Summary
PhyIP is a non-invasive probing framework for evaluating physical knowledge in pre-trained world models. First, a backbone model (Transformer/U-Net/FNO) is trained via self-supervised next-state prediction. The model is then frozen, and activations from a deep layer (e.g., block 9) are extracted. A linear probe (ridge regression) is trained to predict a physical quantity (e.g., energy) from these frozen representations. Performance is assessed on out-of-distribution test sets using Pearson correlation (ρ) and MAPE. Symbolic regression (PySR) distills the linear probe’s weights into an interpretable physical formula. The method is contrasted with invasive baselines: full fine-tuning (IBP) and nonlinear MLP probes, which update the backbone or use high-capacity probes. Evaluation also includes metrics like SSL error (ϵ) and Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) to quantify representational drift. The protocol is validated on fluid dynamics (TheWell) and orbital mechanics datasets.

## Key Results
- PhyIP recovers internal energy (E≈1.5P, ρ>0.90) and inverse-square gravity (ρ≈0.91) under OOD shifts when SSL error is low.
- Invasive methods (full fine-tuning, nonlinear probes) collapse decodability (ρ≈0.05) and mask SSL failures; e.g., in supernova simulation, IBP reports 18.3% MAPE while PhyIP shows 140% error.
- Fine-tuning induces representational drift (CKA<0.2 in deep blocks) and selectively erases dynamic variables (speed, radius) while preserving static ones (mass).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Invasive Adaptation Causes Representational Collapse.**  
Full fine-tuning overwrites the latent world model by inducing representational drift that selectively erases dynamic state variables (speed, radius) while preserving static ones (mass). The optimizer minimizes task loss by discarding complex time-varying features (e.g., Hamiltonian dynamics), shifting reliance to constant identifiers that suffice for narrow fine-tuning distributions. Evidence includes concentrated parameter modifications (δ>10%) in deep blocks, drastic CKA drops (CKA<0.2), and targeted erosion in decodability of dynamic variables (∆ρ≈−0.15).

**Mechanism 2: Linear Decodability as a Valid Probe of Learned Physics.**  
Physical quantities are linearly decodable from frozen SSL representations when SSL error (ϵ) is low. The SSL training objective aligns the latent space with local physical gradients. For a smooth functional Φ, linear probe error is bounded by ϵ and the functional’s curvature K_Φ. When ϵ is low, the probe achieves high ρ>0.90 on OOD tests, and symbolic regression can reliably extract the underlying law.

**Mechanism 3: Invasive Probes Produce False Positives by Learning Ex Nihilo.**  
High-capacity invasive probes (full fine-tuning, nonlinear MLPs) can achieve low downstream error even when the SSL backbone failed to learn correct dynamics (high ϵ), because they overwrite the backbone and fit the task independently. This yields an illusion of competence (e.g., invasive MAPE=18.3% on supernova) while frozen probing reveals catastrophic failure (PhyIP MAPE=140.4%).

## Foundational Learning
- **Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH)**  
  *Why needed:* PhyIP assumes physical quantities align with linear subspaces in activation space; without this, linear probes would not meaningfully extract latent physics.  
  *Quick check:* What does the Linear Representation Hypothesis claim, and why would it hold for physical quantities but not necessarily for arbitrary high-level concepts?

- **Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)**  
  *Why needed:* CKA quantifies representational drift between SSL and fine-tuned models; a drop in deep blocks signals corruption of latent geometry.  
  *Quick check:* How does CKA differ from simple correlation when comparing layer activations, and why is it preferred for measuring similarity of neural representations?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
SSL Backbone (pre-trained) → extract frozen activations from deep layer → Linear Probe (ridge regression) → evaluate on OOD sets → Symbolic Regression (PySR) → validated physical law.

**Critical path:**  
1. Pre-train SSL backbone on next-state prediction until low validation ϵ.  
2. Extract frozen activations h_t from a mid-to-deep layer (e.g., block 9).  
3. Train linear probe PW on (h_t, Φ(x_{t+1})) pairs from in-distribution data.  
4. Evaluate PW on OOD test sets → obtain ρ, MAPE.  
5. Run SR with inputs x_t and PW predictions on OOD → obtain symbolic formula ˆΦ_SR.  
6. Validate ˆΦ_SR as a standalone physical model on OOD.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Linear vs. Nonlinear probes:* Linear tests disentangled representation (LRH); nonlinear risks learning the task directly.  
- *Frozen vs. Fine-tuning:* Frozen preserves SSL invariance; fine-tuning alters the representation (observer effect).  
- *Layer choice:* Early layers may lack abstraction; deep layers may have drifted; paper finds optimal linearity at block 9 in transformers.  
- *SR constraints:* Dimensional consistency prevents invalid formulas; simplicity bias avoids overfitting but may miss corrections.

**Failure signatures:**  
- PhyIP low ρ + low ϵ: law not linearly decodable or suboptimal layer.  
- PhyIP high ρ + high ϵ: spurious correlation; check raw input baseline.  
- Invasive succeeds while PhyIP fails: invasive method overwrote backbone; check CKA drop.  
- SR finds unphysical formula: probe learned bad mapping; revisit layer or SSL hyperparameters.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Harmonic Oscillator:** Train MLP on 1D spring data (x,v→next state); probe for energy E. Expect ρ≈1.0; SR recovers E≈v²+x² (with m=k=1).  
2. **Controlled SSL Error:** Train two oscillators: one with ample data (low ϵ), one with very little (high ϵ). Apply PhyIP. Expect high ρ only for low-ϵ model.  
3. **Invasive vs. Non-invasive comparison:** On low-ϵ oscillator, run full fine-tuning to predict E. Compare OOD generalization of PhyIP vs. fine-tuned model’s linear readout. Expect PhyIP to generalize while fine-tuned model fails due to representational collapse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Why do deeper transformer layers spontaneously linearize representations of physical quantities while earlier layers require non-linear probes, and what architectural inductive biases govern this phenomenon?  
*Basis in paper:* Figure 4 shows that while physical information is initially entangled (requiring non-linear MLP probes), the representation linearizes with depth, peaking at block 9 (ρ≈0.91). The mechanism remains unexplained.

**Open Question 2:** What precise properties of certain physical simulations (e.g., 3D Supernova) cause SSL error to remain high and linear decodability to fail, even when models achieve reasonable next-step prediction accuracy within training distribution?  
*Basis in paper:* For 3D Supernova, all architectures had high ϵ≈0.30–0.37 and no model achieved ρ>0.2 with PhyIP. The inherent difficulty is not analyzed.

**Open Question 3:** Why does fine-tuning selectively erase dynamic state variables (speed, radius) while preserving static ones (mass), and can this erasure be mathematically linked to specific optimization dynamics or gradient flow properties?  
*Basis in paper:* Section 4 shows targeted erasure: decoding of Speed and Radius drops (∆ρ≈−0.15) while Mass remains stable. The paper suggests a simplicity bias on narrow distributions but provides no theoretical account.

**Open Question 4:** How does the diversity of fine-tuning data quantitatively relate to the preservation of latent physics, and what minimum diversity thresholds are needed to avoid representational collapse?  
*Basis in paper:* The fine-tuning dataset’s lack of diversity drives erasure, but quantitative relationships (e.g., parameter coverage width vs. CKA) are not established.

**Open Question 5:** Can weight-preserving or subspace-constrained adaptation protocols be designed that allow models to acquire new capabilities while strictly protecting linearly decodable physical invariants, and would such methods improve out-of-distribution generalization in scientific domains?  
*Basis in paper:* The limitations note this as future work; no concrete algorithms are proposed.

## Limitations
- Exact architecture hyperparameters (e.g., Fourier modes, layer counts) for FNO/TFNO and Transformer backbones are unspecified, limiting exact replication.
- OOD parameter specifications (e.g., cooling rates, stellar masses) are not fully detailed, hindering external generalisation tests.
- The linear representation hypothesis assumption may not hold universally; evidence is confined to fluid dynamics and orbital mechanics.

## Confidence
- **Invasive adaptation causes representational collapse:** Medium
- **Linear decodability of physics as a valid probe:** High
- **Invasive probes mask SSL failures:** High

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain generalisation test:** Apply PhyIP to at least two additional physical systems (e.g., electromagnetic wave propagation, thermodynamics) to assess whether linear decodability holds beyond the studied domains.
2. **Fine-tuning diversity ablation:** Systematically vary the diversity of fine-tuning data (e.g., uniform vs. narrow parameter ranges) and measure its impact on representational drift (CKA) and selective erasure of dynamic vs. static variables to test the simplicity bias hypothesis.
3. **Alternative drift metrics:** Compute representation similarity using SVCCA, RSA, and other metrics beyond CKA to confirm that the observed collapse under invasive adaptation is robust to the choice of similarity measure.
