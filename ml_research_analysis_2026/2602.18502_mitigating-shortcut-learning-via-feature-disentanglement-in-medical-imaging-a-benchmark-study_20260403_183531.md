# Mitigating Shortcut Learning via Feature Disentanglement in Medical Imaging: A Benchmark Study

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18502
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18502
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Feature disentanglement, especially via dCor+Rebal and MINE, significantly improves model robustness to distribution shift caused by spurious correlations, with AUROC gains up to 24% on inverted test distributions.

## Executive Summary
This study systematically benchmarks feature disentanglement methods to mitigate shortcut learning in medical image classification, where models exploit spurious correlations (confounders). Using three datasets with natural or synthetic confounders under strong training correlations (~95% co-occurrence), the authors compare adversarial learning (AdvCl) and latent space splitting techniques that penalize statistical dependence between subspaces (via distance correlation, mutual information estimation, or maximum mean discrepancy), with and without data rebalancing. The core finding is that combining rebalancing with distance correlation (dCor+Rebal) and using standalone mutual information estimation (MINE) consistently deliver the highest and most robust performance improvements on test distributions where the spurious correlation inverts. While MINE achieves the strongest disentanglement, its computational cost is substantial; dCor+Rebal provides a favorable efficiency-performance trade-off. The work underscores that latent space analyses reveal representation flaws not captured by primary task metrics alone.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates a class of methods that enforce a 4-dimensional latent representation split into two 2-dimensional subspaces: one dedicated to the primary task (\(z_1\)) and one expected to capture confounder information (\(z_2\)). During training, a penalty term minimizing statistical dependence between \(z_1\) and \(z_2\) is added to the primary classification loss. The dependence measures compared are distance correlation (dCor), mutual information estimated via MINE, and maximum mean discrepancy (MMD). Adversarial learning (AdvCl) is also tested, which uses a gradient reversal layer on a shared latent space to make the representation confounder-invariant. Several methods are combined with a simple data rebalancing (oversampling) technique that reduces the empirical co-occurrence of task and confounder labels in the training set. All models use standard CNN or ResNet encoders, with hyperparameters (e.g., dependence loss weight \(\lambda\)) selected via 5-fold cross-validation. Performance is assessed on three test splits: the original distribution, a balanced distribution with no correlation, and an "inverted" distribution where the spurious correlation is reversed.

## Key Results
- dCor+Rebal and MINE (with or without rebalancing) achieved the highest and most robust AUROC improvements on the challenging inverted test distributions across all datasets.
- Improvements were substantial, reaching up to 10% absolute AUROC gain in Morpho-MNIST and 24% in OCT under 95% training correlation.
- MINE provided the strongest disentanglement as measured by latent space kNN analyses (off-diagonal accuracies near 50% chance level), but required approximately 50x more training time than dCor+Rebal.
- MMD-based methods consistently failed to improve robustness, showing poor disentanglement and AUROC gains.
- Latent space analyses (confusion matrices, visualizations) revealed nuances in representation quality not reflected in AUROC alone, highlighting the value of disentanglement-specific evaluation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Combining data rebalancing with distance correlation (dCor)-based latent splitting robustly mitigates shortcuts by simultaneously weakening spurious correlations in the training distribution and explicitly minimizing statistical dependence between task-relevant and confounder-related subspaces.
- **Mechanism:** Rebalancing reduces the empirical co-occurrence strength between the primary task label \(y_1\) and confounder \(y_2\). The encoder learns a 4D latent \(z\) split into \(z_1\) and \(z_2\). A dCor penalty \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{dCor}}\) is applied between \(z_1\) and \(z_2\), encouraging independence. The combined loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{dCor}}\) forces \(z_1\) to primarily encode \(y_1\)-predictive signal while \(z_2\) captures \(y_2\)-related information, with rebalancing providing a cleaner separation signal.
- **Core assumption:** Spurious correlations can be weakened by (a) balancing the joint distribution of \((y_1, y_2)\) and (b) enforcing independence between subspaces via a consistent, zero-if-independent dependence measure.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "dCor+Rebal ... achieved the highest and most robust AUROC improvements." [section 4.1]: "dCor+Rebal ... achieved the best performance" on inverted tests. [section 4.2]: Confusion matrix off-diagonals near 50% for \(z_1 \rightarrow y_2\).
- **Break condition:** If training correlation is moderate (e.g., conditional prevalence < 90%), the added value of dCor over simple rebalancing diminishes [section 4.3]. Poor \(\lambda\) tuning can render the dependence penalty ineffective.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Mutual Information Neural Estimation (MINE) provides the strongest disentanglement by directly minimizing a tight lower bound on the mutual information between subspaces, aggressively suppressing confounder information from the task subspace.
- **Mechanism:** MINE trains a neural network \(T_\theta\) to estimate \(\text{MI}(z_1, z_2)\) via the Donsker-Varadhan representation. The encoder is updated to minimize \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{MI}} = -\sup_\theta (\mathbb{E}[T_\theta] - \log \mathbb{E}[e^{T_\theta}])\). This direct optimization of an MI lower bound encourages near-independence more effectively than distance-based metrics for complex distributions.
- **Core assumption:** MI is a valid and measurable measure of dependence for latent vectors; the neural estimator can be optimized stably alongside classification.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "MINE provided the strongest disentanglement." [section 4.2]: MINE shows off-diagonal kNN accuracies closest to 50%. [section 4.4]: MINE achieves disentanglement scores near 1.0 (maximum).
- **Break condition:** Computational burden is extreme (~50x more time than dCor+Rebal) [Table 4]. Optimization can be unstable; MINE may fail to converge or require careful tuning. If subspaces are already independent, the MI gradient may become uninformative.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Adversarial learning (AdvCl) mitigates shortcuts by enforcing confounder invariance in a *shared* latent space via a gradient reversal layer, making \(y_2\) unpredictable from the representation while preserving \(y_1\) predictive power.
- **Mechanism:** A single encoder outputs \(z\). Two linear heads predict \(y_1\) and \(y_2\). Loss: \(\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(y_1) - \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(y_2)\). The gradient reversal layer inverts the gradient from the \(y_2\) predictor. Hence, the encoder learns to increase \(y_2\) prediction loss (maximize confusion) while decreasing \(y_1\) loss, yielding a representation invariant to \(y_2\).
- **Core assumption:** A single representation can be highly predictive for \(y_1\) and unpredictable for \(y_2\); the adversarial game converges to a meaningful equilibrium.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "AdvCl ... improved primary task AUROC ... with gains up to ... 19% in OCT." [section 3.3]: Describes minimax objective and GRL. [section 4.2]: For AdvCl, \(z \rightarrow y_2\) accuracy remains high, indicating incomplete confounder removal.
- **Break condition:** If \(y_1\) and \(y_2\) share underlying features, the adversarial objective may force the encoder to discard \(y_1\)-predictive signal to achieve \(y_2\) invariance, hurting primary performance. This is evidenced by AdvCl's generally lower AUROC gains vs. subspace methods [Table 2]. Sensitive to \(\lambda\) and \(y_2\) predictor capacity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Causal Graphs & Confounders**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper frames shortcut learning as a model capturing spurious correlations induced by a confounder \(C\) that affects both the true causal feature \(X\) and the shortcut feature \(S\) (here, \(y_2\)). Understanding the graph \(C \rightarrow (X, S)\) and \(X \rightarrow y_1, S \rightarrow y_1\) is essential to grasp why disentanglement is causal mitigation.
  - *Quick check question:* If a confounder affects both the disease feature and the imaging artifact, why would learning an *invariant* representation to the confounder help generalization?
- **Concept: Feature Disentanglement via Latent Splitting**
  - *Why needed here:* The core technical approach is to split the latent space into subspaces (\(z_1, z_2\)) and minimize their dependence. This contrasts with other invariance methods (like AdvCl) and is key to understanding the benchmark.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the difference between minimizing dependence *between* subspaces (dCor, MINE) and making a *single* subspace invariant to a confounder (AdvCl)?
- **Concept: Statistical Dependence Measures (dCor, MI, MMD)**
  - *Why needed here:* The methods differ primarily in *how* they penalize dependence. dCor captures linear and nonlinear distance-based dependence; MINE estimates mutual information; MMD compares distributions via kernel means. Their properties (computational cost, sensitivity) drive the performance trade-offs.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might MMD, a distribution matching metric, perform poorly for disentanglement compared to dependence measures like dCor or MI?
- **Concept: Shortcut Learning & Distribution Shift**
  - *Why needed here:* The evaluation hinges on the "inverted" test distribution where \(p(y_1|y_2)\) reverses. This is the operational definition of a shortcut: a model that relied on \(y_2\) will fail catastrophically.
  - *Quick check question:* If a model achieves 99% AUROC on the original test set but 46% on the inverted set, what does that imply about its learned features?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Encoder -> Latent Splitter -> Task Heads (Primary, Confounder) & Dependence Regularizer. For AdvCl: Encoder -> Shared Latent -> Task Heads (Primary, Confounder) with Gradient Reversal Layer on path to Confounder Head.
- **Critical path:** Input image \(x\) -> Encoder \(f_\theta(x)\) -> 4D latent \(z\). \(z\) is sliced into 2D subspaces \(z_1, z_2\). \(z_1 \rightarrow C_{\psi_1} \rightarrow \hat{y}_1\). Loss: \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}(\hat{y}_1, y_1) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{dep}}(z_1, z_2)\). For AdvCl: \(z \rightarrow (C_{\psi_1}, C_{\psi_2})\); Loss: \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}(\hat{y}_1, y_1) - \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}(\hat{y}_2, y_2)\) with gradient reversal on \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}(\hat{y}_2, y_2)\) path.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Latent dimensionality:** Fixed at 4D total (2D per subspace). Higher dimensions may capture more task features but risk increased dependence; lower dimensions risk underfitting and collapse.
  - **λ scheduling:** Fixed \(\lambda\) used. Too low → no disentanglement; too high → poor classification. No adaptive scheduling explored.
  - **Rebalancing integration:** Applied as pre-processing (oversampling) to alter data distribution before training. Combined additively with disentanglement loss during model training.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Latent collapse:** Scatter plot of \(z_1\) shows points aligned on/near a line, indicating one dimension dominates and may discard information.
  2. **Confounder leakage:** kNN confusion matrix shows \(z_1 \rightarrow y_2\) accuracy >> 50%, meaning \(z_1\) still encodes confounder info.
  3. **Inverted test collapse:** AUROC on inverted distribution drops near 50% (chance), indicating complete shortcut reliance.
  4. **Training instability:** Particularly for MINE – loss oscillation, NaN MI estimates, or failure to converge.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity check disentanglement:** Train each method on rebalanced training data. Evaluate kNN confusion matrix on a *balanced* test set. Expect off-diagonal (\(z_1 \rightarrow y_2\)) near 50%. If not, \(\lambda\) is likely too low or method failing.
  2. **Shortcut reliance test:** Evaluate AUROC on the *inverted* test distribution. Compare to baseline. A successful mitigation should show >10% absolute AUROC gain over baseline at 95% training correlation. If gain is <5%, the method is not effectively removing the shortcut.
  3. **Efficiency benchmark:** Record training time to convergence. If MINE takes >100 epochs equivalent while dCor+Rebal converges in <30, consider the computational budget. Validate that gain in disentanglement score justifies the time cost.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do feature disentanglement methods scale to large medical imaging datasets (e.g., NAKO, UK Biobank), and do foundation models inherently mitigate shortcuts?
- How effective are disentanglement methods when multiple confounders coexist and interact, as in real clinical data?
- Does increasing latent subspace dimensionality improve disentanglement quality and classification robustness?
- Can hybrid disentanglement approaches (e.g., combining adversarial loss and distance correlation) outperform individual methods?
- Do disentangled representations enhance interpretability for clinicians and remain robust in longitudinal or continual learning scenarios?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (\(\lambda\), MINE's NB) are dataset-specific and require code for exact reproduction.
- MINE's computational cost (~50x slower than dCor+Rebal) limits scalability to large datasets.
- MMD consistently failed to disentangle and improve AUROC; theoretical explanation is lacking.
- Fixed 4D latent (2D subspaces) may cause collapse or underfitting for some tasks.
- Only extreme (95%) confounding was evaluated; performance under moderate shifts is unclear.
- AUROC and kNN are proxies that may not directly translate to clinical utility.

## Confidence
- Disentanglement methods improve inverted-test AUROC: **High**
- dCor+Rebal and MINE deliver top AUROC gains and robustness: **High**
- MINE achieves strongest disentanglement but is computationally expensive: **High**
- dCor+Rebal offers best efficiency-performance trade-off: **High**
- MMD is ineffective for this task: **Medium**
- Latent-space analyses reveal nuances missed by AUROC: **Medium**
- Methods are sensitive to correlation strength and \(\lambda\): **Low**

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sweep:** Systematically vary \(\lambda\) (e.g., 0.01–10) and MINE's NB (1–10) across all datasets to quantify robustness of each method's performance and disentanglement quality.
2. **Evaluate on additional medical datasets:** Test top methods (dCor+Rebal, MINE) on 2–3 other medical imaging datasets (e.g., brain MRI, histopathology slides) with diverse, naturally occurring confounders to assess generalizability.
3. **Efficient MI estimator replacement:** Replace MINE with more efficient mutual information estimators (e.g., InfoNCE, CLUB) and compare their disentanglement quality and training time against MINE and dCor.
