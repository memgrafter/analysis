# Campaign-2-PT-RAG: LLM-Guided Semantic Product Type Attribution for Scalable Campaign Ranking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10577  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10577  
- **Reference count:** 20  
- **Primary result:** The method achieves 78–90% precision and over 99% recall in mapping campaigns to product types, enabling scalable training labels for campaign ranking.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of generating training labels for e-commerce campaign ranking, where creative campaign language often does not explicitly mention purchasable products. The authors propose Campaign-2-PT-RAG, a retrieval-augmented generation framework that leverages large language models (LLMs) to interpret campaign intent, retrieve candidate product types via semantic search, and classify their relevance. The resulting product coverage set labels user purchases after campaign exposure. Evaluated on real and synthetic Walmart campaigns, the approach attains 78–90% precision and >99% recall, far surpassing lexical and embedding-only baselines. The work demonstrates that LLM-guided semantic alignment can produce high-quality, scalable supervision signals for campaign ranking systems.

## Method Summary
Campaign-2-PT-RAG is a four-stage, zero-shot pipeline. First, an LLM generates a concise semantic summary of the campaign, distilling implicit product associations from creative text. Second, this summary and structured product-type (PT) nodes (represented as `Category|Family|Type`) are embedded; all PTs with cosine similarity above a low threshold τ are retrieved to favor recall. Third, an optional cross-encoder reranks the candidates. Fourth, an LLM classifies each PT as `strong`, `weak`, or `irrelevant` based on the summary and PT hierarchy; the final coverage set includes `strong` and `weak` PTs. This set is later intersected with user purchase PTs to create training labels for downstream ranking models. No model fine-tuning is performed; all components rely on pre-trained models and LLM APIs.

## Key Results
- Achieves 78–90% precision and over 99% recall on Walmart campaign data against human and LLM-as-judge annotations.  
- Substantially outperforms lexical matching and embedding-only baselines, highlighting the value of LLM-guided reasoning.  
- Produces thematically coherent PT sets, confirming that the summaries capture consistent campaign intent.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: LLM-based campaign interpretation**  
- **Claim:** Transforms creative campaign language into a semantically enriched summary that captures implicit product intent.  
- **Mechanism:** An LLM is prompted to generate a natural-language summary `𝑠𝑐` from raw campaign content, distilling latent themes, lifestyle associations, and implied product categories.  
- **Core assumption:** LLMs possess sufficient world knowledge and contextual reasoning to infer implicit product associations from marketing language.  
- **Evidence:** [abstract] "captures implicit intent"; [section 3.4] "richer and more context-aware retrieval query".  
- **Break condition:** The summary becomes overly generic, misses key associations, or introduces hallucinations, leading to irrelevant retrievals.

**Mechanism 2: Low-threshold dense semantic retrieval**  
- **Claim:** Retrieves high-recall candidate PTs by embedding the summary and PT descriptions, capturing both obvious and subtle thematic alignments.  
- **Mechanism:** Cosine similarity between the summary embedding and each PT embedding is computed; all PTs above a low threshold τ are kept.  
- **Core assumption:** A shared embedding space aligns campaign summaries with canonical PT descriptions even for abstract concepts.  
- **Evidence:** [section 3.4] "choose 𝜏 to favor high recall"; [section 4.4] shows spurious matches when τ is too low.  
- **Break condition:** The embedding model poorly separates themes, or τ is set too low (excessive noise) or too high (misses relevant PTs).

**Mechanism 3: Structured LLM relevance classification**  
- **Claim:** Filters retrieved PTs by explicitly reasoning over hierarchical taxonomy relationships and campaign intent, distinguishing transactional relevance from loose thematic association.  
- **Mechanism:** For each candidate PT, an LLM judges relevance (`strong`/`weak`/`irrelevant`) given the summary and the PT's structured `Category|Family|Type` string.  
- **Core assumption:** An LLM can perform fine-grained, structured semantic judgment more effectively than similarity scores alone.  
- **Evidence:** [section 4.6] Ablation shows adding the LLM classifier yields largest gains in precision and F1.  
- **Break condition:** The classifier is biased, misinterprets hierarchy, or is inconsistent due to prompt sensitivity.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for classification**  
- **Why needed:** The framework uses RAG not for generation but for structured retrieval and reasoning: a campaign summary retrieves candidate PTs, and an LLM generates a relevance judgment conditioned on that context. Understanding this pattern is key to adaptation.  
- **Quick check:** How does using retrieved candidates as context for an LLM judge differ from zero-shot LLM classification over the full taxonomy, and why does it mitigate context window and positional bias issues?

**Concept: Structured representation of hierarchical taxonomies**  
- **Why needed:** PTs are not flat strings but hierarchical nodes (`Category|Family|Type`). The LLM’s reasoning and embedding similarity depend on this structure; misrepresentation leads to poor alignment.  
- **Quick check:** Why is concatenating `Category|Family|Type` into a single text field a reasonable design choice for both embedding and LLM prompting, and what are its limitations?

**Concept: LLM-as-judge evaluation protocol**  
- **Why needed:** Intrinsic PT mapping quality is evaluated via LLM-as-judge when human labels are incomplete. This is a core validation method in the paper.  
- **Quick check:** What are the risks of using an LLM-as-judge to evaluate another LLM’s outputs, and how does the paper attempt to validate this approach (e.g., agreement with human annotators)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Signal Layer: Kafka streams for impressions/clicks → Spark (clicks) & Orion (orchestration)  
Inference Layer: Neuron/Hydra services trigger the Campaign-2-PT-RAG pipeline  
RAG Pipeline: (a) Campaign Interpreter (LLM), (b) FAISS Retriever (bi-encoder embeddings), (c) Optional Reranker (cross-encoder), (d) Classifier (LLM for 3-way judgment)  
Knowledge Base: PT Taxonomy (7,147 nodes) stored as `Category|Family|Type` strings  
Decisioning & Serving: Neuron combines signals + PT coverage → Hydra ranks → cached for personalization layer

**Critical path:**  
Campaign event → Trigger RAG pipeline → Generate PT coverage set `PT(𝑐)` → User purchase event → Match purchased PTs to `PT(𝑐)` → Label `𝑦𝑢,𝑐` → Feed to downstream ranking model training

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Precision-Recall Threshold: Low τ increases recall but requires more LLM classification calls (cost/latency). Higher τ reduces cost but risks missing relevant PTs.  
- Reranker inclusion: Cross-encoder reranking improves semantic ordering modestly but adds significant latency; optional based on SLA.  
- LLM judge vs. human labels: Using LLM-as-judge scales evaluation but introduces potential bias; paper mitigates by measuring human-LLM agreement.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High false positives in PT sets: Retrieval threshold too low, LLM classifier too permissive on `weak` relevance, or campaign interpretation summary too broad.  
- Low recall/coverage: Retrieval threshold too high, LLM classifier overly strict, or campaign interpretation missing key themes.  
- System latency spikes: LLM interpretation and classification calls are sequential; volume spikes or model slowdowns directly impact throughput.  
- Semantic incoherence: Predicted PT set lacks thematic consistency (measured by `Coherence(𝑐)`), indicating failure in interpretation or classification.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replicate Human Annotation Comparison:** Process a held-out set of expert-labeled Walmart campaigns. Compute precision/recall/F1 against human `PTtrue(𝑐)`. Verify ~89% precision and ~98% recall.  
2. **Retrieval Threshold Sensitivity Test:** Run the pipeline with only retrieval (no LLM classifier) across thresholds (e.g., 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) on synthetic campaigns. Measure LLM-judge precision/recall. Confirm high recall but low precision without the classifier, and identify a threshold that balances candidate set size.  
3. **Ablation - LLM Interpretation:** Disable the campaign interpretation step; use raw campaign text directly for retrieval and classification. Compare LLM-judge metrics to the full pipeline. Expect significant drop in precision and coherence, confirming the value of the interpretation step.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can we develop lightweight reasoning models that maintain the accuracy of LLM-based campaign-PT mapping while reducing latency and computational cost for real-time deployment? (Basis: Section 5 identifies latency of optional reranking and LLM inference as a limitation; future work proposes exploring lighter-weight models.)
- **Open Question 2:** How can Campaign-2-PT-RAG be integrated more tightly with downstream ranking objectives to optimize campaign performance beyond label quality? (Basis: Section 5 notes the need for tighter integration with ranking objectives; current framework is decoupled from ranking optimization.)
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent does subjectivity in relevance judgments—from both human annotators and LLMs—affect the reliability of campaign-PT mappings, and how can we standardize these judgments? (Basis: Section 5 acknowledges inherent subjectivity; evaluation relies on LLM-as-judge with reported agreement but no mitigation strategies.)
- **Open Question 4:** How valid is the assumption that user purchases matching inferred PTs post-exposure are causally influenced by the campaign, and what methods can validate or refine this causal link? (Basis: Section 3.1 defines labels as PT overlap but performs no causal validation; evaluations focus on PT mapping accuracy, not behavioral impact.)

## Limitations
- Undisclosed implementation details (LLM prompts, taxonomy structure, hyperparameters) block exact replication.  
- LLM-as-judge evaluation protocol lacks full specification, limiting reproducibility of reliability metrics.  
- Latency from LLM calls may hinder real-time deployment in high-throughput settings.

## Confidence
- **Overall RAG efficacy** (78–90% precision, >99% recall): **High** (metrics are reported but unreproducible without undisclosed details).  
- **Value of LLM interpretation step**: **Medium** (plausible per ablation, but prompt sensitivity unknown).  
- **Low-threshold retrieval + LLM filtering**: **Medium** (logic is sound, but τ and embedding model choices lack full specification).  
- **LLM-as-judge reliability**: **Medium-Low** (agreement with humans is cited but protocol details missing).

## Next Checks
1. **Retrieval threshold sensitivity sweep:** Run pipeline with τ ∈ [0.3, 0.5, 0.7] on a validation set; measure precision/recall trade-off to identify a robust operating point.  
2. **Interpretation step ablation:** Replace LLM summary with raw campaign text; re-evaluate precision/recall and semantic coherence to quantify the summary’s marginal contribution.  
3. **LLM-as-judge vs. human agreement:** Sample 50 campaign-PT pairs judged by the LLM-as-judge; have domain experts re-label; compute Cohen’s κ to validate the evaluation protocol.
