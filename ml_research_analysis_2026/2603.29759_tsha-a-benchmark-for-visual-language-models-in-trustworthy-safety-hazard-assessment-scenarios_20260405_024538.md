# TSHA: A Benchmark for Visual Language Models in Trustworthy Safety Hazard Assessment Scenarios

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29759
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29759
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Training VLMs on the diverse, real-world TSHA benchmark via GRPO yields large gains on safety hazard assessment (+18.3 points) and modest improvements on general VL benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
TSHA addresses critical shortcomings in existing VLM safety benchmarks by introducing a large-scale, real-world dataset for indoor domestic hazard assessment. It comprises 81,809 training samples aggregated from four diverse sources (existing datasets, internet content, AIGC, and newly captured scenes) and a challenging test set of 1,707 samples featuring videos and panoramas with multiple hazards. A rigorous four-stage GPT-4o annotation pipeline with human verification ensures high-quality labels. Evaluation of 23 VLMs reveals poor average performance (~65 points). Fine-tuning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on TSHA substantially improves model accuracy on the test set (up to +18.3 points) and also yields small but consistent gains on general vision-language benchmarks like Blink and MMStar, demonstrating both task-specific and transferable benefits.

## Method Summary
The work constructs the TSHA benchmark to overcome the synthetic data and oversimplification limitations of prior safety benchmarks. Data is sourced from four complementary channels: existing indoor hazard datasets, internet videos/images, AIGC-generated images, and newly captured real-world scenes. All sources undergo manual filtering for physical plausibility and realism. A four-stage GPT-4o pipeline (Scene Description → Preliminary Hazards → Final Hazards with evidence citation → Human Verification) annotates samples for two task formats: open-ended QA and multiple-choice. Models are fine-tuned using GRPO, a reinforcement learning algorithm, on the training set and evaluated on the test set via an LLM-as-a-judge metric (blending accuracy, conciseness, coherence) for QA and standard accuracy for multiple-choice. Generalization is assessed on established VL benchmarks (Blink, AI2D, MMStar, etc.).

## Key Results
- VLMs perform poorly on TSHA's realistic test set, averaging ~65 points, with a large gap between multiple-choice (~80%+) and open-ended QA (~60%) accuracy.
- GRPO fine-tuning on TSHA yields significant improvements: up to +18.3 points on the TSHA test set for Qwen2.5-VL-3B.
- Training on TSHA also improves performance on general VL benchmarks (e.g., +1.6 on Blink for 3B models), indicating transferable reasoning gains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Reducing Synthetic-to-Real Domain Gap
**Claim:** Training on TSHA improves hazard assessment by mitigating the reality gap inherent in prior simulator-only benchmarks.
**Mechanism:** Aggregating diverse, high-fidelity data (internet, newly captured) alongside AIGC exposes models to authentic visual distributions of domestic hazards, moving beyond synthetic oversimplification.
**Evidence:** [abstract] "mitigating the reality gap inherent in prior simulators"; [section 3.1] details four data sources and manual filtering for physical plausibility.
**Break condition:** Systematic biases in newly captured/internet data (e.g., limited cultural contexts) could partially limit gap reduction.

### Mechanism 2: High-Quality, Structured Annotation
**Claim:** The four-stage GPT-4o pipeline with human verification produces more reliable hazard labels than prior simplistic methods.
**Mechanism:** Chain-of-thought reasoning, evidence citation, confidence differentiation, and human spot-checking promote accuracy and reduce hallucination in a nuanced task.
**Evidence:** [section 3.3] defines pipeline emphasizing "systematic and reliable hazard analysis"; [section 3.1] notes 10% human verification with "high consistency."
**Break condition:** GPT-4o's own biases in safety perception could propagate, especially for ambiguous or culturally specific hazards.

### Mechanism 3: Transferable Reasoning via Challenging Task
**Claim:** GRPO fine-tuning on TSHA enhances general visual reasoning, as shown by gains on Blink/MMStar.
**Mechanism:** The complex, realistic hazard assessment task forces finer-grained visual discrimination and structured reasoning (e.g., spatial relationships, evidence enumeration) that transfers to other VL tasks.
**Evidence:** [abstract] "improves scores on general benchmarks"; [section 4.3] Table 3 shows consistent average improvements; [section 4.4] ablation confirms all data sources contribute synergistically.
**Break condition:** Small gains could be stochastic; if improvements were only on TSHA and not others, transfer would be invalid.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Representation Learning:** Understanding how VLMs fuse visual patches with text tokens is key to interpreting how TSHA training changes internal representations.  
  *Quick check:* How might a VLM's visual encoder represent "cluttered floor" vs. "clear floor" for hazard classification?
- **Reinforcement Learning from Environmental Feedback (RLAIF/RFL):** GRPO fine-tuning requires grasping how reward signals (derived from hazard label quality) shape the VLM's output distribution beyond supervised learning.  
  *Quick check:* What serves as the reward signal in GRPO for this task? Is it based on hazard description accuracy or a metric combination?
- **Dataset Curation for Safety-Critical AI:** Creating hazard benchmarks involves challenges: covering rare critical events, avoiding unrealistic scare-mongering, and balancing synthetic scale with real-world fidelity.  
  *Quick check:* What risks do AIGC-generated hazard images pose, and how did "physical plausibility"/"realism of danger" filtering mitigate them?
- **Chain-of-Thought Annotation:** Using LLMs for multi-stage, reasoning-based annotation scales dataset creation for complex tasks like hazard identification.  
  *Quick check:* Why might requiring evidence citation in the Final Hazards stage reduce annotation hallucination?
- **Generalization in Vision-Language Models:** Assessing whether task-specific training (safety hazards) can improve broad VL capabilities requires understanding transfer learning mechanisms in multimodal models.  
  *Quick check:* What types of reasoning skills (e.g., spatial, causal) are likely shared between hazard assessment and benchmarks like Blink?
- **Evaluation via LLM-as-a-Judge:** Using a powerful LLM to score open-ended responses introduces efficiency but also potential bias and variability.  
  *Quick check:* How might the choice of judge model (GPT-4o vs. Claude) and prompt affect the reported QA scores?

## Architecture Onboarding
*Component map:* Source Collection → Filtering → GPT-4o 4-Stage Annotation → Human Verification → QA/Choice Formatting → GRPO Fine-Tuning (ms-swift) → Evaluation (LLM-judge + VLMEvalKit) → Benchmark Scores (TSHA, Blink, etc.)

*Critical path:* Data Pipeline (Source → Filtering → Annotation → Verification) is the most quality-sensitive; failures here corrupt the training set. GRPO Training Stability is a secondary critical path, as RL fine-tuning can collapse with poor reward shaping or KL penalties.

*Design tradeoffs:*
- Data Diversity vs. Annotation Cost: Newly captured/internet data are high-fidelity but expensive; AIGC is scalable but risks unrealistic hazards.
- Evaluation Granularity vs. Reliability: LLM-as-a-judge provides nuanced scores but introduces evaluator bias; human evaluation is gold-standard but not scalable.
- Task Complexity: Open-ended QA is more realistic and challenging than multiple-choice but harder to score automatically.

*Failure signatures:*
- **Data-level:** High false-positive hazard rate in AIGC samples; low inter-annotator agreement in human verification.
- **Model-level:** After GRPO, overfitting to TSHA (high QA score) with degradation on general VL tasks, or no gain on TSHA indicating ineffective reward.
- **Evaluation-level:** Discrepancy >2-3 points between LLM-judge and human scores suggests misaligned evaluation.

*First 3 experiments:*
1. **Baseline Establishment:** Evaluate a base VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B) on full TSHA test set. Record overall QA/Choice scores and per-category accuracy to identify weak hazard types.
2. **Ablation on Training Data Sources:** Train four models, each on one TSHA training source (Existing, Internet, AIGC, New). Evaluate all on TSHA test set and Blink to isolate individual and synergistic contributions.
3. **GRPO Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Grid search key GRPO parameters (KL penalty, learning rate, rollouts) for a single model size (e.g., 3B). Monitor TSHA gain vs. Blink score to avoid over-optimization (e.g., QA up but coherence down).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Why do VLMs exhibit a significant performance gap between choice questions and open-ended QA in safety hazard assessment, and what architectural or training modifications can bridge this gap? The paper identifies the gap but does not investigate root causes (e.g., insufficient generative fine-tuning, difficulty in long-form reasoning).

### Open Question 2
Which of the four data sources contributes most to model robustness against novel hazards, and how can their optimal combination be determined? The paper shows all four sources improve performance synergistically but does not quantify individual marginal contributions or interactions.

### Open Question 3
To what extent do improvements on TSHA generalize to entirely new domains (e.g., outdoor, industrial) or hazard types not covered, and does training induce overfitting to indoor domestic scenarios? The paper tests generalization on general benchmarks (Blink, MMStar) but not on new safety-specific contexts.

## Limitations
- GRPO hyperparameters and exact LLM judge evaluation details (model version, temperature, prompts) are unreported, risking replication variance.
- Potential residual biases in the "newly captured" and internet data (geographic/cultural coverage) are not quantified.
- Generalizability gains on Blink/MMStar are small (+1.2–1.6) and lack statistical significance testing; could be noise.

## Confidence
- **Benchmark real-world diversity:** High – four explicit data sources with human filtering are well-documented.
- **Annotation pipeline quality:** Medium – four-stage GPT-4o process with 10% human spot-check is described, but full inter-annotator agreement metrics are absent.
- **Generalizable improvement via GRPO:** Low – cross-benchmark gains are modest and lack significance testing; risk of overfitting to TSHA’s style.

## Next Checks
1. **Data source audit:** Sample 100 instances from each of the four training sources to quantify "physical plausibility" and hazard diversity, checking for systematic omissions (e.g., non-Western homes).
2. **GRPO hyperparameter sweep:** Replicate training with the reported baseline (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B) across KL penalties (0.001–0.1) and learning rates (1e-6–1e-4) to isolate settings yielding the claimed +18.3 gain without harming Blink scores.
3. **Evaluation protocol fidelity:** Run the LLM judge (same model version, temperature=0) on a fixed subset of TSHA test QA pairs and compare to human annotations; measure score delta to establish a correction factor for future comparisons.
