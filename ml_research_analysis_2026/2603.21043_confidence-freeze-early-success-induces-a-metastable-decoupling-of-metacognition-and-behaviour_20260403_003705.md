# Confidence Freeze: Early Success Induces a Metastable Decoupling of Metacognition and Behaviour

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21043  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21043  
- **Reference count:** 37  
- **Primary result:** High early success (90%) induces a "confidence-freeze" state where participants persist through long loss streaks while confidence simultaneously drops, decoupling metacognition from behavior.

## Executive Summary
In a study with 332 participants using a multi-reversal two-armed bandit task, induced high early success (90% reward rate) versus normal early success (60%) led to maladaptive persistence. After task reversals, the high-success group endured significantly longer loss streaks (mean 6.2 vs. 1.8) despite a sharp decline in confidence ratings (from 5 to 2 on a 7-point scale). This decoupling, quantified by a freeze index, was markedly elevated (34% vs. 12%). Two brief interventions—making outcome trajectories explicit and using metacognitive prompts—both substantially reduced persistence and the freeze index, demonstrating the reversibility of this state.

## Method Summary
Participants performed a two-armed bandit task with multiple reversals of reward contingencies. The experimental manipulation assigned participants to experience either a high (90%) or normal (60%) reward rate during an early "training" phase. After reversals, behavioral persistence (number of consecutive losses before switching) and trial-by-trial confidence ratings were measured. A freeze index quantified the discrepancy between low confidence and continued choice of the same option. Mixed-effects modeling analyzed loss sensitivity and switching odds. Two interventions were tested: one provided explicit graphical outcome trajectories, and the other inserted metacognitive prompts before choices.

## Key Results
- Participants with high early success persisted through longer loss streaks after reversal (mean 6.2 vs. 1.8 losses) while confidence dropped from 5 to 2.
- Freeze index was substantially higher in the high-success group (34% vs. 12%).
- Early success suppressed loss sensitivity (interaction β = −0.07, p = .048) and reduced switching odds by 23%.
- Both interventions (explicit trajectories and metacognitive prompts) reduced persistence to ~2 trials and freeze index to 14–18%, with Cohen's d ~0.7.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that early success creates a metastable state where behavioral policy becomes rigidly anchored to an initially successful choice, even as subjective confidence declines due to accumulating losses. Suppressed loss sensitivity (β = −0.07) suggests attenuated integration of negative feedback. The precise cognitive or neural mechanism for the decoupling—such as impaired evidence accumulation, over-reliance on cached values, or disrupted conflict monitoring—remains unidentified, representing a key gap.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Two-armed bandit task**  
   *Why needed:* A canonical reinforcement learning paradigm for studying choice under uncertainty.  
   *Quick check:* Repeatedly choose between two options with differing reward probabilities.

2. **Reversal learning**  
   *Why needed:* Tests adaptability when reward contingencies switch.  
   *Quick check:* Switch choices after the higher-reward option changes.

3. **Confidence rating**  
   *Why needed:* Measures metacognitive awareness of choice certainty.  
   *Quick check:* Rate confidence on a 1–7 scale after each decision.

4. **Freeze index**  
   *Why needed:* Quantifies the decoupling of low confidence from persistent behavior.  
   *Quick check:* Proportion of trials where confidence is low but the same option is chosen.

5. **Loss sensitivity**  
   *Why needed:* Captures how outcomes influence subsequent choices.  
   *Quick check:* Decline in choosing an option after receiving losses from it.

6. **Metacognitive prompt**  
   *Why needed:* Forces explicit consideration of current strategy.  
   *Quick check:* Present a question like "Are you sure?" before each choice.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Early Success Manipulation (90% vs. 60%) → Behavioral Persistence (loss streaks) & Confidence Rating (drop) → Freeze Index → Interventions (Explicit Trajectories, Metacognitive Prompts) → Reduced Persistence & Freeze

**Critical path:** High early success → suppressed loss sensitivity → reduced switching odds → confidence declines while behavior persists → decoupling.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Simplified lab task (two-armed bandit) offers control but may not capture complexity of real-world decisions.  
- Brief interventions are scalable but their long-term efficacy without support is unknown.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Consecutive losses >5 with simultaneous confidence rating <3.  
- High freeze index (>30%) indicating persistent choice despite low confidence.

**First experiments:**  
1. Replicate the manipulation and measure freeze index in a non-student, cross-cultural sample.  
2. Implement similar early-success conditions in an ecologically valid task (e.g., simulated financial trading or medical diagnosis).  
3. Use fMRI/EEG during the bandit task to identify neural correlates (e.g., prefrontal conflict monitoring, striatal prediction error signals) that differentiate freeze vs. adaptive states.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
None reported.

## Limitations
- Sample consists likely of WEIRD undergraduates, limiting generalizability to diverse populations and real-world contexts.  
- The two-armed bandit paradigm is overly simplistic; freeze state in complex, volatile environments remains untested.  
- Underlying cognitive/neural mechanisms for the decoupling are not identified.

## Confidence
- **Core decoupling effect:** High (robust within-task findings, significant interactions, successful interventions).  
- **Quantitative parameters (e.g., freeze index, β coefficient):** Medium (rely on model assumptions; reanalysis could alter precision).  
- **Generalizability and theoretical integration:** Low (extrapolation beyond specific task/sample is speculative; mechanism unknown).

## Next Checks
1. Test the early-success manipulation and freeze index in non-student populations (e.g., older adults, professional decision-makers) and across cultures.  
2. Transfer the paradigm to ecologically valid tasks (e.g., simulated trading, clinical decision-making) to assess persistence and confidence decoupling.  
3. Use neuroimaging (fMRI/EEG) during the task to identify whether freeze correlates with specific neural signatures separable from behavioral outputs.
