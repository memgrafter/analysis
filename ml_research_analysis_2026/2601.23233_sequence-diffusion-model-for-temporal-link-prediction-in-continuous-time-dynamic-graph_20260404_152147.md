# Sequence Diffusion Model for Temporal Link Prediction in Continuous-Time Dynamic Graph
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.23233
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23233
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SDG achieves state-of-the-art performance on ten benchmarks, with relative improvements up to 14.48% in MRR and 8.72% in HR@10 over the best baselines, particularly on large-scale datasets.

## Executive Summary
Temporal link prediction in dynamic graphs is hindered by existing discriminative models that produce point estimates without capturing uncertainty or sequential structure. This paper introduces SDG, a sequence-level diffusion framework that injects noise into the entire historical interaction sequence and reconstructs all destination embeddings via a conditional denoising process with a cross-attention decoder. SDG is trained end-to-end with a cosine-based diffusion loss and a ranking objective. On ten benchmarks, SDG consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance, with relative improvements up to 14.48% in MRR and 8.72% in HR@10 over the best baselines, particularly on large-scale datasets. Ablation studies confirm the contribution of sequence denoising and the cosine loss. SDG also demonstrates superior robustness to noisy edges and more efficient scaling compared to transformer-based competitors.

## Method Summary
SDG processes each source node u at time t by extracting its L most recent historical interactions S_u,t, which are encoded with a causal Transformer to produce context Z_ctx. The target sequence of destinations T_u,t (including the positive destination) is mapped to embeddings X^0 via a learnable node embedding table. Forward diffusion adds Gaussian noise to X^0 over K steps: X^k = √ᾱ_k X^0 + √(1-ᾱ_k) ε. The denoising network f_θ takes the noisy sequence X^k, timestep k (via sinusoidal embedding), and context Z_ctx. It first adds the time embedding to X^k, then encodes Z_ctx with a causal Transformer to obtain Z_ctx, and finally applies a cross-attention Transformer where Z_ctx serves as queries and X^k as keys/values, producing the predicted clean embeddings ˆX^0. The last position of ˆX^0 is used to score candidate nodes: an MLP ingests the dot product between this embedding and candidate node embeddings, along with elapsed time encoding γ(Δt), and outputs a relevance score. Training minimizes a combined loss: a ranking loss (BCE or BPR) applied to sequence positions, weighted by λ_inter, plus a cosine-based diffusion reconstruction loss L_diff = (1/L) Σ_i (1 - cos(ˆX^0_i, X^0_i))^2 with weight λ_diff. During inference, the reverse diffusion starts from X^K ∼ N(0,I) and iteratively denoises for K steps to obtain ˆX^0, then ranks candidates by the final scores.

## Key Results
- SDG outperforms 7 strong baselines across 10 diverse datasets, achieving up to 14.48% relative gain in MRR and 8.72% in HR@10, with especially strong results on large-scale TGB-Seq benchmarks.
- Ablation studies verify the necessity of sequence-level diffusion, cross-attention decoder, and cosine-based loss; removing any causes significant performance degradation.
- SDG shows better robustness to noisy edges and more favorable scaling behavior compared to transformer-based competitors like DyGFormer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
We discuss three key mechanisms.

### Mechanism 1: Sequence-level diffusion captures joint distribution of destination sequences
**Claim:** SDG injects noise into the entire historical interaction sequence and jointly reconstructs all destination embeddings, thereby capturing uncertainty and sequential dependencies.
**Mechanism:** The forward process adds Gaussian noise to the clean target sequence X^0 over K steps: X^k = √ᾱ_k X^0 + √(1-ᾱ_k) ε. The reverse process is a conditional Markov chain that iteratively denoises, each step conditioned on the encoded history Z. This forces the model to learn the full conditional distribution p(X^0|Z) rather than a point estimate.
**Core assumption:** Future interactions’ distribution can be effectively modeled by a diffusion process on embedding sequences, with historical context Z sufficient.
**Evidence:** Abstract explicitly states “injects noise into the entire historical interaction sequence and jointly reconstructs”; Section 4.2 mathematically defines forward and reverse processes. Ablation “w/o Seq” (Table 3) shows that removing sequence diffusion drastically reduces performance, confirming its importance.
**Break conditions:** Too few diffusion steps (small K) fail to capture distribution complexity; too many add compute without benefit (Figure 2a). Poor noise schedule (e.g., sqrt) causes instability (Figure 5). The diffusion steps must be appropriately tuned.

### Mechanism 2: Cross-attention decoder aligns denoising with historical context
**Claim:** The cross-attention denoising decoder uses the encoded historical interaction sequence as queries to guide the reconstruction of noisy destination embeddings, effectively conditioning the diffusion on temporal context.
**Mechanism:** The historical sequence Z_1:L(S_u,t) is processed by a causal Transformer to produce Z_ctx. In each denoising step, Z_ctx serves as queries while the time-conditioned noisy sequence ˆX^k (with timestep embedding) serves as keys/values in a cross-attention layer. This allows the history to influence each position’s denoising.
**Core assumption:** Temporal dependencies between historical interactions and future destinations can be captured by attention that respects order (causal for history, cross for reconstruction).
**Evidence:** Abstract mentions “cross-attention denoising decoder to guide the reconstruction”; Section 4.3 formalizes Z_ctx = Transformer(Z_1:L(S_u,t), M) and ˆX^0 = CrossTransformer(Z_ctx, ˆX^k_1:L). Ablation replacing CrossTransformer with an MLP (Table 3, [MLP]) shows performance drop, especially on large datasets, indicating the decoder’s importance.
**Break conditions:** If historical context is weak (short sequences or noisy edges), cross-attention may fail to guide denoising effectively. Incorrect causal masking could violate temporal order. Model must have sufficient capacity.

### Mechanism 3: Cosine-based diffusion loss aligns with ranking objectives
**Claim:** Replacing the standard mean-squared error (MSE) diffusion loss with a cosine-based reconstruction loss yields better alignment with the ranking objective and robustness to embedding scale.
**Mechanism:** The diffusion loss L_diff = (1/L) Σ_i (1 - cos(ˆX^0_i, X^0_i))^2 measures directional similarity between predicted and true embeddings, invariant to norm variations. This orientation is more correlated with ranking metrics like MRR than Euclidean distance.
**Core assumption:** The quality of node embeddings for ranking is determined more by cosine similarity (orientation) than by absolute distance; optimizing cosine similarity thus improves ranking performance.
**Evidence:** Section 4.4 justifies replacing MSE with cosine loss; Equation (15) defines L_diff. Ablation (Table 3) compares [MSE] vs. [Cosine]; on GoogleLocal, replacing cosine with MSE drops MRR from 62.60 to 54.89, confirming the benefit.
**Break conditions:** If λ_diff is too high, diffusion loss may dominate ranking loss, harming performance (Figure 2b). The theoretical equivalence to MSE relies on unit-norm embeddings (Appendix C); improper normalization may affect behavior.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text flow):**  
S_u,t (historical neighbor sequence) → causal Transformer → Z_ctx;  
T_u,t (target destination sequence) → embedding lookup → X^0;  
X^0 → forward diffusion (noise addition) → X^k;  
X^k plus timestep embedding + Z_ctx → cross-attention decoder → ˆX^0;  
ˆX^0 (last position) → dot product with candidate node embeddings + time encoding γ(Δt) → MLP → scores.

**Critical path (inference):**  
(a) Encode source history S_u,t → Z_ctx.  
(b) Initialize X^K ∼ N(0,I).  
(c) Iteratively denoise for K steps: X^{k-1} = denoise(Z_ctx, X^k, k).  
(d) Take last position of ˆX^0 as destination embedding.  
(e) Compute dot-product scores with all candidate node embeddings and rank.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Sequence-level diffusion vs. point-level diffusion: Joint modeling captures sequential dependencies but increases memory/computation.
- Number of diffusion steps K: More steps improve modeling but increase inference latency; paper finds K=32 often optimal.
- Cosine vs. MSE loss: Cosine aligns with ranking and is scale-invariant but may be less stable early; MSE is simpler but misaligned.
- Cross-attention vs. MLP decoder: Cross-attention leverages context effectively but adds parameters; MLP is simpler but underperforms on large datasets.

**Failure signatures:**
- Memory pressure: Memory usage grows with neighbor length L and batch size; can become prohibitive on very large graphs.
- Slow inference: Iterative denoising adds overhead; SDG slower than TGN but faster than DyGFormer.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: Incorrect λ_diff, λ_inter, K, or noise schedule can severely degrade performance.
- Dataset mismatch: Slight underperformance on highly repetitive datasets (Last.fm, UCI) suggests diffusion may overcomplement for low-uncertainty regimes.
- Ablation failures: Removing sequence diffusion, cross-attention, or cosine loss each causes clear drops, indicating criticality.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Main results** (Tables 1 & 2): SDG outperforms 7 strong baselines on 10 datasets, achieving relative improvements up to 14.48% MRR and 8.72% HR@10, especially on large-scale TGB-Seq datasets.
2. **Ablation study** (Table 3): Four variants—remove sequence diffusion (w/o Seq), remove diffusion entirely (w/o Diff), replace cosine with MSE, replace CrossTransformer with MLP—each degrade performance, confirming the contribution of each design choice.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity** (Figures 2, 5, 6): Systematically varying K, λ_diff, λ_inter, noise schedule, and neighbor length L reveals optimal ranges and tradeoffs (e.g., K=32, cosine loss beneficial, intermediate supervision helpful).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How to adapt SDG for inductive temporal link prediction?
- Basis in paper: Future work mentions “addressing inductive/cold-start … by incorporating node attributes” (Conclusion, Appendix E).
- Why unresolved: SDG relies on historical neighbor sequences, which are absent for cold-start nodes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluate on TGB-Seq inductive splits, with and without node features, measuring MRR/HR gains.

**Open Question 2:** Can alternative diffusion frameworks improve SDG?
- Basis in paper: Future work states “exploring other diffusion frameworks … Discrete-based Diffusion or Diffusion Bridge” (Conclusion, Appendix E).
- Why unresolved: DDPM may be suboptimal for discrete sequences; alternatives could be more efficient or expressive.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement discrete diffusion/bridge variants; compare MRR, HR, and inference time against the DDPM baseline.

**Open Question 3:** How to reduce SDG’s computational overhead?
- Basis in paper: Future work suggests “reducing diffusion steps, using distillation, one-step generation” (Appendix E).
- Why unresolved: Multi-step denoising increases training and inference time, hindering scalability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Distill to a single-step model; measure speed-accuracy trade-off; test reducing diffusion steps.

**Open Question 4:** Can SDG be applied to other dynamic graph tasks?
- Basis in paper: Limitation: “SDG does not directly support other tasks such as dynamic node classification” (Appendix E).
- Why unresolved: SDG generates destination embeddings, not evolving node representations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Modify SDG to output node embeddings over time; evaluate on dynamic node classification benchmarks (e.g., TGB-Node).

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps**: Exact Transformer configuration (heads, FFN size, dropout), scoring MLP depth, negative sampling count, and padding/masking for variable sequence length L are unspecified, risking inconsistent reproductions.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Performance depends strongly on λ_diff, λ_inter, K, and noise schedule; optimal values per dataset are not provided, making out-of-the-box deployment difficult.
- **Computational overhead**: Iterative denoising increases latency versus discriminative models, and memory grows with L and K, though scaling is claimed better than some transformer baselines.

## Confidence
- Sequence-level diffusion captures joint distribution: **High** (theoretical clarity, strong ablation evidence).
- Cross-attention decoder aligns denoising with history: **Medium** (ablation supports, but potential confound with capacity; architecture details missing).
- Cosine-based loss improves ranking: **High** (consistent gains over MSE in ablation).
- Overall SOTA claim: **Medium** (results strong but rely on proper baseline reimplementation and hyperparameter choices; no independent replication).
- Robustness and scaling claims: **Medium** (limited diversity in stress tests; claims based on paper’s own experiments).
