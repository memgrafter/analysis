# ATP-Bench: Towards Agentic Tool Planning for MLLM Interleaved Generation
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.29902
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29902
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces Agentic Tool Planning and ATP-Bench; Gemini 3 Pro achieves 79.88 final score with 81.77% tool success rate.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of unified interleaved text-image generation in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), where current methods treat image retrieval and generation as separate, failing to combine factual grounding with creative synthesis. The core proposal is "Agentic Tool Planning," where the model autonomously orchestrates multiple tools (Reference, Diffusion, Search, Code, Edit) to produce coherent interleaved outputs. To evaluate this, the authors introduce ATP-Bench, a benchmark of 7,702 human-verified QA/VQA pairs across 8 categories and 25 intents, and the Multi-Agent MLLM-as-a-Judge (MAM) system. MAM assesses tool-call precision, missed opportunities, and response quality without ground truth or execution. Evaluation of 10 SOTA MLLMs shows significant struggles with coherent planning, particularly in Travel and Renovation. Gemini 3 Pro leads with a 79.88 final score and 81.77% tool success rate, while models exhibit distinct tool-use strategies and open-source lags substantially. The work establishes a framework for evaluating and advancing agentic multimodal systems.

## Method Summary
The study proposes a two-part framework: (1) ATP-Bench, a dataset of 7,702 human-verified visual-critical queries spanning 8 categories and 25 intents, each accompanied by gold-standard interleaved tool plans; (2) the Multi-Agent MLLM-as-a-Judge (MAM) evaluation system, which employs three specialized agents to score model outputs on precision, recall (missed opportunities), and holistic quality without executing tools. Models are prompted—zero-shot or few-shot—to generate responses containing `<tool>` tags that specify tool calls. This design isolates planning ability from execution reliability, enabling systematic assessment of agentic tool orchestration in MLLMs.

## Key Results
- Gemini 3 Pro achieves the highest final score of 79.88 and tool success rate of 81.77%.
- Models exhibit distinct tool-use strategies; open-source models lag significantly behind proprietary ones.
- Travel and Renovation categories are particularly challenging, with high missed image counts and low success rates across all models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Agentic Tool Planning improves interleaved text-image generation by dynamically combining reference, generation, search, code, and edit tools under a unified controller, surpassing paradigms that treat retrieval and generation as mutually exclusive.
- **Mechanism:** The MLLM acts as a central planner that analyzes a visual-critical query and a source document set, then produces an interleaved response with inserted `<tool>` tags. Each tag specifies a tool (Reference, Diffusion, Search, Code, Edit) with parameters (e.g., `img_index`, `prompt`), allowing the system to both cite provided images and synthesize new visuals where needed.
- **Core assumption:** A single MLLM can learn to orchestrate multiple tools autonomously if trained or prompted to recognize "visual gaps" and select appropriate tools based on capability boundaries.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We argue that the next milestone in this field is Agentic Tool Planning, where the model serves as a central controller that autonomously determines when, where, and which tools to invoke."
  - [section 3] Formalizes tool-calling space `S_tool` with five modules and defines visual-critical queries as needing "visual information augmentation, cognitive acceleration, or structural illustration."
  - [corpus] Strong alignment with emerging agentic MLLM research (e.g., SpecEyes’ iterative tool invocation, BEAR’s atomic capabilities benchmarking), indicating community momentum toward orchestrated multimodal agents. *Corpus evidence is conceptual alignment, not direct validation.*
- **Break condition:** If tool boundaries are ambiguous (e.g., search vs. diffusion for real-world entities), models exhibit inconsistent adoption (Section 6 ablation), revealing fragility in the planning assumption without robust capability framing.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Multi-Agent MLLM-as-a-Judge (MAM) system enables reliable, execution-agnostic evaluation of tool-planning quality by decomposing assessment into precision (per-tool validity), recall (missed opportunities), and holistic pacing.
- **Mechanism:** Three specialized agents evaluate a model’s interleaved plan: (1) Precision Inspector scores each tool call on necessity, placement, parameter accuracy, and format; (2) Recall Inspector flags omitted visuals where text alone is insufficient; (3) Chief Judge synthesizes reports into a final score (0–100) across five tiers.
- **Core assumption:** MLLM-based judges, when specialized and constrained to specific evaluation dimensions, can agree with human annotators at high rates (~85%) and provide stable cross-model rankings without executing tools or requiring ground-truth images.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] Details the three-agent framework with explicit rubrics (e.g., 1a–2c metrics for precision, omission conditions for recall).
  - [section 5.3] Shows high inter-judge Spearman correlations (ρ > 0.89 for FS, ρ > 0.92 for MI) across Claude, Gemini, GPT judges, and human agreement of 84–88%.
  - [corpus] Related work (MLLM-as-a-judge for interleaved generation) typically evaluates end-to-end output; MAM’s *planning-focused* decomposition is novel. *Corpus shows prior art but not MAM’s specific design.*
- **Break condition:** Human agreement drops for weaker models (e.g., LLaMA-3.2-11B), suggesting MAM reliability degrades when tool plans are severely malformed or hallucinated, as judges may fail to detect certain omission patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Benchmark reliability hinges on human-verified visual-critical queries and multi-stage ground-truth construction with fine-grained tool-tag annotation, which together ensure that tool-planning evaluation reflects real-world utility rather than artifact.
- **Mechanism:** (1) Query collection uses expert annotators to verify 95% of LLM-generated queries for visual-criticality and clarity. (2) Ground-truth creation involves text generation, image-insertion with strict tool-capability rules, and 15 annotators refining tool calls for necessity, correctness, and placement. This yields a dataset (7,702 QA pairs) with gold-standard interleaved tool plans.
- **Core assumption:** Human-annotated tool plans provide a consistent gold standard for measuring precision/recall, and the visual-critical intent taxonomy (25 intents) reliably triggers tool-use opportunities across categories.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4] Describes three-stage GT collection with annotator filters: "A tool call is retained only if its expected image materially improves understanding... and is placed immediately after the relevant text."
  - [section 5.3] Reports that categories like Travel and Renovation are hardest (lowest FS, highest MI), suggesting the taxonomy successfully differentiates planning difficulty.
  - [corpus] Prior benchmarks (RAG-IGBench, MRAMG-Bench) lack hybrid image sourcing and expert tool-tag annotation; ATP-Bench’s additions address these gaps. *Corpus confirms novelty but not superiority.*
- **Break condition:** If tool capabilities evolve (e.g., new image-editing features), the fixed GT tool calls may become misaligned, requiring periodic re-annotation as noted in limitations (Section 7).

## Foundational Learning
### Visual-Critical Query Design
- **Why needed here:** The entire benchmark rests on identifying queries where interleaved visuals meaningfully enhance understanding versus text-only responses. Misclassifying such queries invalidates the task’s premise.
- **Quick check:** For a given query (e.g., "How do I pair these earbuds?"), list three reasons why an inserted diagram or annotated photo would reduce cognitive load compared to pure text.

### Tool-Augmented MLLM Architecture
- **Why needed here:** Understanding how MLLMs parse and emit structured tool calls (JSON within `<tool>` tags) is prerequisite to modeling agentic planning. This includes recognizing tool boundaries (e.g., Reference vs. Diffusion).
- **Quick check:** Given a tool call `<tool>{"tool_name": "edit", "params": {"img_index": "IMG#2-1", "prompt": "highlight the buttons"}}</tool>`, explain why this might be inappropriate if the referenced image is a product photo without interactive elements.

### Interleaved Generation Evaluation
- **Why needed here:** Unlike single-image tasks, interleaved generation requires assessing pacing, coherence, and necessity of multiple visual insertions. Traditional metrics (BLEU, CLIPScore) are insufficient.
- **Quick check:** Why is measuring "missed opportunities" (Recall Inspector) as important as tool-call precision for evaluating agentic planning?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Toolkit -> Planner -> MAM Judges -> Dataset Construction Pipeline -> Execution Engine

### Critical Path
The critical path: Input processing → Query understanding → Tool selection (when/where/which) → Structured plan generation → MAM evaluation (without execution). Key loop: Model’s plan → MAM feedback (if used for training) → updated planning.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Planning vs. Execution separation:** MAM evaluates plans without tool execution, isolating reasoning ability from API reliability but possibly missing execution-feedback loops.
- **Tool granularity:** Five tools cover common needs but exclude modalities (audio/video) and atomic actions (e.g., OCR), limiting scope.
- **Judge specialization:** Three agents reduce bias vs. single-judge but increase complexity; ablation shows judge model choice affects score calibration but not rankings.

### Failure Signatures
- **Formatting errors:** Malformed JSON or missing `</tool>` tags (InternVL3.5-14B’s 27.95% format failure rate).
- **Redundant visuals:** Low necessity score (LLaMA-3.2-11B’s 44.99% on 1a) indicates decorative or empty images.
- **Parameter mismatch:** Incorrect `img_index` or vague diffusion prompts (Qwen2.5-VL-72B’s 59.27% on 2b).
- **Category-specific collapse:** Travel queries show high MI (missed images) across models; Renovation shows low SR (success rate) despite moderate MI, indicating misplaced tool calls vs. omission.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Tool boundary sensitivity:** Test model tool adoption under Original/Search Enhanced/Diffusion Enhanced prompts (Section 6, Figure 6). Expect Claude/Gemini to shift with framing; GPT-5 conservative.
2. **Judge model robustness:** Run MAM with Claude/Gemini/GPT as judges (Section 6, Table 8). Expect stable Spearman ρ > 0.89 but score calibration differences.
3. **Few-shot prompting:** Add 3-shot demonstrations to models (Section 6, Table 7). Expect GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL to improve SR significantly; LLaMA-3.2 marginal gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can Agentic Tool Planning be extended to handle interleaved generation involving modalities beyond text and image, such as audio or video?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors list as a limitation that their "interleaved generation setting focuses on text–image outputs and does not cover richer modalities such as audio or video."
- **Why unresolved:** Current tools and evaluation are designed solely for image sourcing and generation. Extending to other media would require new tool definitions (e.g., audio synthesis, video retrieval), multimodal fusion strategies, and likely new evaluation metrics to assess cross-modal coherence.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of an extended toolkit including audio/video tools, creation of a multimodal interleaved benchmark with audio/video queries, and demonstration of an MLLM capable of orchestrating such diverse tools with validated performance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the scalability limits of the current five-tool kit, and how does expanding the number and diversity of available tools impact MLLMs' planning coherence and execution reliability?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note their toolkit is "restricted to five tools and therefore does not capture broader agentic capabilities."
- **Why unresolved:** The benchmark and experiments fix the tool set. Real-world agentic systems may need dozens of specialized tools. It is unclear whether models can maintain precision and avoid confusion with larger, more heterogeneous tool inventories, or if new planning architectures are needed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments where the toolset is incrementally expanded (e.g., adding variants of Edit, new retrieval APIs, calculation tools) and measuring changes in tool-call precision, missed opportunities, and overall score. Analysis of failure modes as tool count grows.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How robust and generalizable is the proposed Multi-Agent MLLM-as-a-Judge (MAM) evaluation framework, particularly when compared to alternative evaluation paradigms like LLM-as-a-judge with image captions or human-centric studies?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state they "focus on direct evaluation using MLLM-as-a-judge, and do not study alternative pipelines such as captioning images for LLM-as-a-judge."
- **Why unresolved:** While a human agreement study is provided, the MAM system's judgments may still exhibit specific biases tied to the chosen judge model (Gemini 2.5 Pro by default) or the agent design. Alternative evaluation approaches could yield different rankings or highlight different aspects of quality.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Running the same model evaluations using alternative judge setups (e.g., different base MLLMs for agents, caption-then-LLM pipeline) and comparing correlations with human end-to-end scores and with each other. Discrepancy analysis would reveal which aspects are sensitive to evaluation design.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are the underlying architectural or training factors that cause different MLLMs to adopt distinct tool-use strategies (e.g., conservative vs. aggressive, generation-focused vs. retrieval-focused)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper observes "models exhibit distinct behavioral tendencies in terms of tool-call frequency and preference" and shows varied adoption rates for Diffusion, Search, Code, and Edit, but does not investigate why.
- **Why unresolved:** The work is primarily empirical and does not drill into model internals. Differences could stem from pre-training data mixtures, fine-tuning objectives, tool representation in the prompt, or inherent reasoning biases. Understanding these causes is key to intentional design of tool-augmented MLLMs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled ablation studies: (1) testing the same model with different system prompts that emphasize certain tools; (2) probing tool selection decisions via step-by-step reasoning traces; (3) correlating tool strategies with model size, architecture (e.g., presence of explicit tool heads), or training regimen details.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Why are Travel and Renovation categories disproportionately challenging for current MLLMs, and what specific planning deficits do they reveal compared to easier categories like Academic or Encyclopedia?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Results show Travel and Renovation have the lowest final scores and highest missed image counts across models. The paper speculates Travel is "MI-dominated" (missed opportunities) while Renovation is "SR-dominated" (execution failures), but does not analyze the root causes of these category-specific difficulties.
- **Why unresolved:** The taxonomy defines intents, but the cognitive or sequential planning demands of hard categories are not decomposed. Is it due to need for longer-range planning, more complex tool chaining, or domain-specific knowledge? Without this analysis, improvements remain generic.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-grained error analysis on Travel and Renovation samples: (1) count the average number of sequential tool calls required vs. other categories; (2) classify failure types (e.g., inability to break down a multi-step renovation into discrete visual steps); (3) evaluate whether models fail on spatial reasoning, real-world entity recognition, or creative synthesis within these domains.

## Limitations
- **Execution gap:** MAM evaluates tool plans without execution, isolating reasoning but missing real API failures.
- **Tool boundary fragility:** Models show inconsistent adoption when tool capabilities blur (e.g., Search vs. Diffusion for real-world entities), exposing planning instability.
- **Static ground truth:** Fixed tool annotations may misalign as tool capabilities evolve, risking benchmark staleness.
