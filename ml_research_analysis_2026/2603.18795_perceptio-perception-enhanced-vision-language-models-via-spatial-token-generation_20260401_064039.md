# Perceptio: Perception Enhanced Vision Language Models via Spatial Token Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv**: 2603.18795
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18795
- **References**: 40
- **Primary result**: Perceptio improves spatial grounding in LVLMs via explicit segmentation and depth token generation, achieving SOTA on RefCOCO (+1.4 cIoU avg), HardBLINK (+10.3%), and MMBench (+1.0%).

## Executive Summary
Perceptio addresses a key limitation in large vision-language models (LVLMs): poor fine-grained spatial grounding. Instead of relying solely on implicit visual features, it explicitly generates 2D semantic segmentation and 3D depth tokens within the autoregressive sequence, enabling a spatial chain-of-thought. The method integrates SAM2-based segmentation tokens and VQ-VAE depth tokens (distilled from Depth Anything V2) into an InternVL2.5-based LLM, with composite depth-token losses and a soft-merging technique for differentiable training. Perceptio achieves state-of-the-art results, improving referring expression segmentation by +0.8 to +1.4 cIoU across RefCOCO/+/g, boosting HardBLINK spatial understanding accuracy by 10.3%, and raising MMBench by 1.0%. Ablations confirm that both segmentation and depth tokens are crucial: removing depth tokens collapses HardBLINK accuracy by 25.8 points, while removing segmentation tokens degrades general VQA by up to 2.3 points.

## Method Summary
Perceptio uses InternVL2.5-8B as the base LVLM. For training, it constructs a 56K dataset from RefCOCO/+/g, where each sample includes an image, a text query, a segmentation mask, and a depth map (from Depth Anything V2). Depth maps are tokenized via a VQ-VAE (codebook size 128) into a sequence of 100 depth tokens bounded by [dstart] and [dend] markers. The text answer is augmented with a special [seg] token inserted before the depth tokens. The [seg] token is consumed by a fine-tuned SAM2 decoder (with frozen image encoder) to predict the segmentation mask. The LLM is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 256) using a multi-task loss: LLM NLL loss, segmentation reconstruction (cross-entropy + DICE), composite depth-token loss (marker, token, count), and soft depth reconstruction (MSE). The total loss weights are λm=0.3, λt=0.5, λc=0.2, λd=1.0, λr=1.0. Additionally, 1.1M auxiliary image-text pairs from LLaVA-1.5, grounding conversations, and ADE20k (with perception tokens) are mixed in. Training uses AdamW (4e-5), batch size 512 (grad accum 8), sequence length 8192, for 24 hours on 64 A100s. At inference, the LVLM autoregressively outputs the structured token sequence; if needed, SAM2 and VQ-VAE decode the segmentation and depth from the special tokens.

## Key Results
- RefCOCO/+/g: Absolute cIoU gains of +0.8, +1.4, and +1.1 respectively over previous SOTA.
- HardBLINK spatial reasoning accuracy: +10.3% absolute improvement.
- MMBench general VQA accuracy: +1.0% absolute improvement.
- Ablations: Removing depth tokens reduces HardBLINK by 25.8 points; removing segmentation tokens reduces MMBench by up to 2.3 points.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Perceptio's explicit spatial token generation enforces a structured chain-of-thought that grounds language in fine-grained spatial layouts. By forcing the LLM to produce a segmentation token and a depth token sequence before the textual answer, the model must internalize spatial relationships and object boundaries, rather than relying on fuzzy attention patterns. The segmentation token, decoded by SAM2, provides precise 2D object masks, while the depth tokens (via VQ-VAE) capture 3D scene geometry. The composite depth losses (marker, token, count) ensure the generated token sequence faithfully reconstructs the depth map, and the soft-merging technique allows end-to-end gradient flow. This multi-task setup aligns the LLM's representations with perception tasks, leading to improved spatial reasoning without sacrificing general capabilities when balanced properly.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input image -> Vision encoder (InternVL2.5) -> Visual tokens -> LLM (InternVL2.5 LLM with LoRA) -> Output tokens: [seg] token, depth token sequence (bounded by [dstart]/[dend]), text answer. Parallel: The same image features from vision encoder feed SAM2 decoder (along with [seg] token) to produce segmentation mask. Depth token sequence -> VQ-VAE decoder -> depth map.

**Critical path**:  
The end-to-end inference flow: an image and text prompt are processed by the vision encoder and fed to the LLM. The LLM autoregressively generates the structured sequence. If spatial outputs are required, the [seg] token is extracted and passed to the SAM2 decoder (using cached image features) to yield the mask; the depth token sequence is decoded by the VQ-VAE to yield the depth map. The critical path involves the LLM's ability to emit the correct token sequence format and the proper functioning of the SAM2 and VQ-VAE decoders.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- **Frozen teachers vs. end-to-end**: Using frozen Depth Anything V2 and SAM2 encoders avoids expensive joint training but ties performance to teacher quality and introduces a discrepancy between training (teacher-generated tokens) and inference (student-generated tokens).
- **Fixed depth sequence length**: Depth tokens are generated as a fixed-length (100) sequence, which may be inefficient for scenes with varying complexity and forces the model to predict exactly 100 tokens regardless of actual detail needed.
- **Multi-task loss balancing**: The weighting parameters (λ) must be carefully tuned; ablations show that depth token generation can harm general VQA if overemphasized, indicating a trade-off between spatial specialization and broad language capabilities.

**Failure signatures**:  
- **Malformed depth token sequence**: Missing [dstart]/[dend] markers, incorrect length, or interleaved non-depth tokens. This suggests the LLM is not respecting the structured output format, possibly due to insufficient prompting or loss weighting issues.
- **No segmentation masks**: The [seg] token is not generated or SAM2 decoder fails to produce a valid mask (e.g., all-zero). Could indicate that the LLM did not learn to emit [seg] or that SAM2 decoder fine-tuning was inadequate.
- **Degraded general VQA**: A significant drop in MMBench/MME scores compared to the base LVLM suggests that multi-task training is interfering with language modeling, perhaps due to excessive focus on perception losses or poor data mixing.
- **Training instability**: Diverging depth-token losses (marker, token, count) or huge gradients in soft-merging indicate implementation errors in span parsing or masking.

**First experiments**:  
1. **Token sequence format validation**: Generate outputs for a held-out set of RefCOCO samples. Parse each sequence to detect [seg] token and a depth span between [dstart] and [dend] of exactly 100 tokens. Compute the success rate and analyze failure modes (missing markers, wrong length). This verifies that the LLM learns the required structure.
2. **SAM2 decoder sanity check**: Using ground-truth [seg] tokens (instead of LLM-generated), run the SAM2 decoder on a few image-mask pairs from RefCOCO. Compute mask reconstruction loss (CE+DICE) and visualize masks. This isolates SAM2's capability and ensures the decoder is properly fine-tuned and fused.
3. **Loss component monitoring**: During early training steps, track individual loss terms (L_NLL, L_seg, L_marker, L_token, L_count, L_depth_recon). Verify that all are decreasing and that none dominate. Also check gradient norms for LoRA parameters. This detects optimization issues and unbalanced losses.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can models learn generalizable spatial concepts that transfer across tasks and domains?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future Work section states: "we are motivated by the question of how models can learn generalizable spatial concepts that transfer across tasks and domains, for instance, extending perception tokens to encode surface normals or optical flow, moving toward a unified spatial intelligence within a single autoregressive framework."
- **Why unresolved:** Perceptio only transfers to similar segmentation and depth benchmarks; it is unclear whether the learned perception tokens generalize to novel spatial tasks (e.g., normals, flow) or unseen domains.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation on a held-out suite of spatial tasks (surface normal estimation, optical flow, 3D reconstruction) across diverse datasets without task-specific fine-tuning; strong performance would confirm transferability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can Perceptio be extended to video to achieve temporally consistent depth tokens and object tracking?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Limitations state: "our training and evaluation are limited to static images; extending to video, where temporally consistent depth tokens and object tracking introduce new optimization challenges, remains open."
- **Why unresolved:** The current architecture processes each frame independently via independent token generation, lacking explicit temporal modeling or cross-frame token linking necessary for consistent video understanding.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a video extension with mechanisms such as recurrent token propagation or cross-frame attention; validate on video benchmarks by measuring temporal consistency of depth/segmentation tokens and tracking accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can the optimization tension between depth token generation and text-only tasks be mitigated?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Ablations show removing depth tokens slightly improves general VQA metrics; Limitations note that "depth token generation introduces a mild optimization tension with text-only tasks" and suggest "task-adaptive curriculum learning" as a direction.
- **Why unresolved:** Multi-task training with depth objectives may interfere with LLM language modeling; the root cause and optimal balancing of losses remain unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement task-adaptive curriculum or dynamic loss weighting; if both depth token accuracy and VQA scores improve simultaneously compared to fixed weighting, the tension is mitigated.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How can we improve Perceptio's robustness to errors in the frozen teacher models (Depth Anything V2, SAM2)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Limitations mention: "Perceptio relies on frozen teacher models... whose errors propagate to the student; improving robustness to teacher noise is important for deployment."
- **Why unresolved:** Teacher mistakes during training become supervision noise, which can degrade the student's performance in real-world settings where perfect teacher predictions are not guaranteed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduce noise-aware training techniques (e.g., confidence-based loss weighting, label smoothing, ensemble teachers); evaluate on data with intentionally corrupted teacher outputs or out-of-distribution scenes; robustness would be indicated by smaller performance gaps under noise.

## Limitations
- Key implementation details omitted (prompt templates, SAM2 decoder fusion, data mixing strategy, LoRA module targeting) impede faithful reproduction and full assessment.
- Dependence on frozen teacher models (Depth Anything V2, SAM2) introduces bias propagation and may limit robustness to out-of-distribution scenes.
- Trade-off between spatial performance gains and general VQA scores observed in ablations indicates fragility in multi-task balancing; the optimal loss weighting and data mixing remain unclear.
