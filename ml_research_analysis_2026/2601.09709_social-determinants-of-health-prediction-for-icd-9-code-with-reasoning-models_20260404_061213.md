# Social Determinants of Health Prediction for ICD-9 Code with Reasoning Models
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2601.09709
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09709
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: Predicting fine-grained SDoH ICD-9 V-codes from clinical notes using few-shot prompting of reasoning LLMs achieved 72.2% exact match and 89.1% micro-F1 on a curated evaluation set with amended codes.

## Executive Summary
Under-capture of Social Determinants of Health (SDoH) in structured medical data limits research and care interventions. This study addresses the gap by directly predicting fine-grained ICD-9 V-codes from unstructured clinical notes. The core method employs few-shot prompting of reasoning-capable large language models with a highly detailed prompt that encodes hierarchical coding rules, precision-first criteria, and negative examples for multi-label classification at the hospital admission level. The best-performing model, GPT-5-mini, achieved 72.2% exact match and 89.1% micro-average F1 on a curated evaluation set where codes were amended to include all clinically evident SDoH. Evaluation against the original physician-assigned codes showed lower performance (48.9% exact match, 67.0% micro-F1) and identified 139 admissions with documented but uncoded SDoH factors. Competitive results from open-source models (e.g., Qwen3-32B, 65.5% exact match) indicate feasibility without proprietary systems.

## Method Summary
The study used the MIMIC-III database, filtering for adult medical admissions with relevant notes (discharge summaries, social work notes). Original physician-assigned ICD-9 codes were extracted. To create a more complete gold standard, a single annotator reviewed notes and added any SDoH-related V-codes that were documented but missing, resulting in an "amended" label set. The authors designed a system prompt that casts the LLM as a certified medical coder, providing explicit hierarchical rules (e.g., exclusions), a precision-first criterion (better to miss a code than falsely predict), and carefully selected positive and negative few-shot examples. No model fine-tuning was performed; instead, various reasoning-capable LLMs were prompted with concatenated note text for each admission. Predicted code lists were compared to the amended gold standard using exact match, micro-F1, and macro-F1. The paper also includes analyses of label-specific performance and comparisons with original codes.

## Key Results
- GPT-5-mini achieved 72.2% exact match and 89.1% micro-F1 on the amended-code evaluation set.
- Against original physician codes, performance dropped to 48.9% exact match and 67.0% micro-F1, revealing 139 admissions with documented but uncoded SDoH.
- Open-source Qwen3-32B reached 65.5% exact match, demonstrating competitive performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Structured Prompt Encodes Complex Decision Logic
- **Claim**: A highly structured, rule-encoded prompt enables precise multi-label classification by constraining model outputs.
- **Mechanism**: The prompt acts as a procedural knowledge base, embedding the ICD-9 coding manual's hierarchy (e.g., V60.0 excludes V60.2), precision-first criteria, and curated negative examples. This externalizes complex decision logic, reducing reliance on the model's internal, potentially inconsistent, knowledge of billing codes.
- **Core assumption**: The model's few-shot learning capability can reliably follow complex, conditional instructions when they are explicitly and unambiguously stated.
- **Evidence**: The abstract describes "few-shot prompting of reasoning-capable large language models with a highly detailed prompt encoding hierarchical rules, precision-first criteria, and negative examples." Section 4 details prompt components (role, labeling criteria, examples). Section 5 notes that "the conservative prompting strategy for codes with low support may have limited the model’s ability to capture more nuanced cases."
- **Break condition**: If the model predicts mutually exclusive codes (e.g., V60.0 and V60.2), the prompt's constraint mechanism fails, indicating instruction-following limitations or prompt ambiguity.

### Mechanism 2: Reasoning for Long-Distance Dependencies
- **Claim**: Reasoning-capable LLMs outperform traditional models by handling long-distance dependencies and complex conjunctions in lengthy admission notes.
- **Mechanism**: Chain-of-thought reasoning allows the model to iteratively synthesize evidence scattered across many notes for a single admission, applying nuanced inclusion/exclusion logic (e.g., distinguishing "unemployed" from "retired").
- **Core assumption**: The reasoning process inherent in models like GPT-5-mini can be directed by the prompt's logical structure to perform evidence aggregation analogous to a human coder reviewing a full chart.
- **Evidence**: The abstract states that "prediction in large admissions or longitudinal notes is challenging given long distance dependencies." Section 5 reports: "The performance difference between traditional and reasoning-capable models indicates that multi-label SDoH classification presents challenges that benefit from advanced reasoning capabilities, particularly when processing lengthy clinical narratives with distributed evidence." It also says: "Reasoning models leverage the chain-of-thought... These models perform better given the length of the prompt, and complexity of conjunctions."
- **Break condition**: If the performance gain disappears on shorter, single-note inputs or on codes with explicit, local keywords, the advantage is specific to long-context, distributed-evidence tasks.

### Mechanism 3: Amended Codes Reveal Under-Capture
- **Claim**: Evaluating against amended codes (adding clinically evident but missed V-codes) reveals a gap between documentation and coding, which the model can help bridge.
- **Mechanism**: The model's high performance on amended codes, combined with its identification of 139 admissions with documented but uncoded SDoH, suggests it can surface latent information in notes that human coders missed, functioning as a supplemental review tool.
- **Core assumption**: The amended gold standard, created by a single annotator based on explicit note evidence, is a more complete representation of the true SDoH status than the original physician-assigned codes.
- **Evidence**: The abstract reports 89% micro-F1 on amended codes and identification of 139 uncoded SDoH admissions. Section 4 describes the amendment process. Section 5 contrasts: 48.9% exact match and 67.0% micro-F1 on non-amended codes vs. higher on amended, reflecting additional evidence.
- **Break condition**: Low inter-rater reliability on creating amended codes would undermine the benchmark and the apparent "gap."

## Foundational Learning
### Clinical Coding Hierarchy & Mutual Exclusivity
- **Why needed**: The V-code taxonomy includes strict inclusion/exclusion rules (e.g., homelessness V60.0 supersedes inadequate resources V60.2; physical abuse V15.41 excludes emotional abuse V15.42). Encoding these in the prompt ensures accurate, non-contradictory predictions.
- **Quick check**: Given a note stating "Patient is homeless and cannot afford medications," which single V-code should be predicted, and why?

### Multi-Label Classification at the Admission Level
- **Why needed**: The task requires aggregating evidence across all notes within an admission to predict a *set* of applicable codes, rather than single-label or sentence-level classification.
- **Quick check**: How does predicting "V60.0, V62.0" for one admission differ from predicting a single code in terms of evidence requirements?

### Few-Shot Learning with Negative Examples
- **Why needed**: The prompt uses "enhanced negative examples" to teach the model precise boundaries and common failure modes, which is more effective for complex, rule-based tasks than positive examples alone.
- **Quick check**: What is the pedagogical purpose of the example "History of physical and sexual abuse -> V1541 ONLY" in the prompt?

### Design tradeoffs
- **Precision vs. Recall**: The prompt prioritizes precision (better to miss a code than falsely predict), trading off recall for nuanced codes.
- **Prompt Complexity vs. Interpretability**: A rule-heavy prompt improves control but makes debugging difficult and increases token cost.
- **Model Choice**: Frontier models (e.g., GPT-5-mini) offer higher accuracy but are costly/closed; open-source models (e.g., Qwen3-32B) are accessible but may require finer tuning.

### Failure signatures
- **Hierarchical Rule Violation**: Model predicts mutually exclusive codes (e.g., V60.0 and V60.2), indicating failure to follow prompt constraints.
- **Label-Specific Recall Collapse**: Very low recall on codes requiring nuanced inference (e.g., emotional abuse V15.42) despite high precision, suggesting insufficient prompt examples or knowledge gaps.
- **Amended vs. Non-Amended Gap**: An unexpectedly small gap might indicate prompt overfitting to the amended annotation style or unusually thorough original coding.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Prompt Ablation**: Remove hierarchical rules and negative examples from the prompt, rerun on a subset. Expect significant drop in precision and exact match, confirming the importance of rule constraints.
2. **Input Context Window Test**: Truncate admission notes to increasing token limits (e.g., 2k, 4k, 8k). Monitor performance, especially for codes with long-distance dependencies. Degradation with shorter context would validate the need for long-context reasoning.
3. **Label-specific Failure Analysis**: Manually review all false negatives for the lowest-performing code (e.g., V15.42 emotional abuse). Categorize errors into truly absent, differently phrased, or inferential evidence to guide prompt refinement.

## Limitations
- **Amended Gold Standard Validity**: Amended codes were added by a single annotator without inter-rater reliability assessment, creating a potentially unstable benchmark.
- **Prompt Overfitting & Brittleness**: The highly specific, rule-heavy prompt may overfit to the curated evaluation set and underperform on diverse real-world note styles or other institutions.
- **Generalizability of Model Choice**: Results are tied to specific LLM versions; performance may drop with other models or future updates.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Rule-encoded prompt): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (Reasoning for long context): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (Bridging coding gap): Low

## Next Checks
1. Measure inter-rater reliability on amended labels: have multiple clinicians independently apply the amendment protocol to a stratified sample and report Cohen's kappa.
2. Conduct a controlled prompt ablation study: systematically remove hierarchical rules, negative examples, and precision criteria to quantify their individual contributions.
3. Test generalization on an external EHR dataset: replicate the pipeline on a different hospital's notes to assess performance consistency.
