# AMPS: Adaptive Modality Preference Steering via Functional Entropy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12533
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12533
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** AMPS improves visual-preference accuracy from 48.1% to 73.0% and text-preference accuracy from 63.6% to 76.2% on Qwen2.5VL-7B while reducing generation errors.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal LLMs often exhibit a bias toward one modality (e.g., text over vision), making it hard to steer them reliably toward another. Static steering either disrupts sensitive samples or fails to shift robust ones because a single intensity cannot fit all cases. AMPS introduces instance‑aware adaptive steering: it measures per‑sample modality contribution via functional entropy bounds (MCR), predicts a scaling factor from the input hidden state, and applies an intensity‑scaled steering vector. On the MC2 benchmark, AMPS significantly outperforms both static steering and the prior adaptive method L2S across Qwen2.5‑VL and LLaVA models, achieving large gains in preference accuracy while lowering generation error rates.

## Method Summary
AMPS consists of three steps. (1) **MCR estimation**: For each sample, perturb the KV cache entries corresponding to visual or text tokens with Gaussian noise (σ tuned per model), compute the cross‑entropy loss for each perturbation, and take the gradient norm of the loss with respect to the perturbed KV segment. The resulting Modality Contribution Scores (C_V, C_T) are normalized to obtain MCR. (2) **Steering target construction**: Using two contrastive prompts (“based on visual context” vs. “based on text context”), extract the difference of hidden states at layer 14. Scale this difference by (1+γ), where γ = 1 + β*(r_v - ęrv)/ęrv; r_v is the sample’s visual MCR and ęrv is the average visual MCR over the sample’s MC2 task. (3) **MLP training**: Train a lightweight two‑layer MLP g_Θ to map the unsteered input hidden state (at layer 14) to the scaled steering target. At inference, the predicted vector is added to the model’s activations at the same layer with a fixed coefficient α (typically 1.0), achieving adaptive steering.

## Key Results
- **Qwen2.5VL‑7B**: Visual‑preference accuracy rises to 73.0% (baseline 48.1%), text‑preference accuracy to 76.2% (baseline 63.6%).
- **Lower error rates**: AMPS reduces generation errors compared to static steering and L2S.
- **Cross‑model validity**: Similar improvements are observed on LLaVA‑7B and LLaVA‑13B.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core insight is that a sample’s response to steering is not uniform but depends on how much each modality contributes to the original prediction. MCR quantifies this contribution in an instance‑specific, differentiable manner via perturbations of the KV cache; a higher visual MCR indicates the sample relies more on visual information and thus is more amenable to visual‑boosting steering. The MLP then learns to translate the unsteered hidden representation into an appropriate scaling of the steering direction, effectively predicting the needed intensity for each sample. This adaptive mechanism ensures that samples that are already visually biased are not over‑steered (avoiding degradation), while those that are text‑biased receive stronger visual push. The strong empirical gains confirm that MCR is predictive and that the learned adaptive scaling generalizes, yielding a better trade‑off between preference shift and output stability than any uniform approach.

## Foundational Learning
- **Modality Contribution Ratio (MCR)**: Normalized scores (∈[0,1], sum to 1) indicating each modality’s influence on the output. *Why needed*: Enables sample‑specific control of steering intensity. *Quick check*: On a batch, sum of visual and text MCR equals 1 for every sample.
- **Functional entropy bounds**: Estimates a modality’s information contribution by measuring loss changes caused by adding noise to its KV representation. *Why needed*: Provides a model‑agnostic, differentiable metric without retraining. *Quick check*: Perturbing one modality should increase loss more if that modality is more influential.
- **KV cache perturbation**: Injecting Gaussian noise into the key/value tensors of a modality to simulate reduced information. *Why needed*: Simulates ablation in a way that preserves gradient flow for MCR estimation. *Quick check*: Visual perturbation should degrade performance on vision‑heavy samples more than on text‑heavy ones.
- **Steering vector**: Difference between hidden states under visual‑prompt and text‑prompt conditions. *Why needed*: Defines a direction in activation space that biases the model toward the desired modality. *Quick check*: Adding this vector to hidden states should shift the output toward the visual modality in controlled tests.
- **Adaptive scaling factor γ**: Normalized deviation of a sample’s visual MCR from its task‑level average, controlling the magnitude of the steering vector. *Why needed*: Adjusts steering strength per sample to avoid overshooting or undershooting. *Quick check*: γ should be near zero for samples with average MCR and scale with the absolute deviation.
- **Two‑layer MLP predictor**: A small neural network (input: unsteered hidden state; output: predicted steering vector). *Why needed*: Learns to produce the correct scaling in an end‑to‑end fashion from data. *Quick check*: Training loss should decrease; predictions should correlate with the target scaling on a held‑out set.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input → Tokenization → KV cache → Compute MCR (perturb → loss → gradients) → Compute γ (from MCR, task average) → Extract h_unsteered, h⁺, h⁻ → Compute v_target = (1+γ)*(h⁺-h⁻) → Train MLP g_Θ(h_unsteered) ≈ v_target → Inference: ˜h^l = h^l + α·g_Θ(h^l) (at layer 14).

**Critical path**: Training pipeline requires per‑sample MCR computation first, then γ, then target construction, then MLP training. Inference uses only the trained MLP and the steering addition.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Perturbation vs. analytical influence: Perturbation is simple and model‑agnostic but noisy; analytical methods may be more precise but model‑specific.
- MLP capacity: A 2‑layer MLP balances expressivity and overfitting; deeper nets risk overfitting to training MCR estimates.
- Steering layer: Layer 14 is chosen for Qwen2.5‑VL; other architectures may require different layers (e.g., earlier for shallow models).
- Noise magnitude σ: Too small yields weak MCR signals; too large may destroy useful information. The paper sets σ=0.1 for Qwen, 1.0 for LLaVA.

**Failure signatures**:
- High variance in MCR values across perturbation trials → unreliable metric; increase N or adjust σ.
- MLP training loss stagnates → check target vector scaling or hidden state extraction indices.
- Steering causes drastic quality drop (e.g., repetition, nonsense) even for α=1 → γ may be mis‑computed or layer mis‑specified.
- No correlation between visual MCR and steering success on validation → MCR formula may need revisiting.

**First experiments**:
1. **MCR sanity check**: Compute MCR for 10 diverse MC2 samples using N=3 perturbations. Verify that visually grounded samples (e.g., object identification) have higher visual MCR than text‑heavy ones (e.g., sentiment).
2. **Target vector validation**: For a sample with high visual MCR, compute v_target and manually inject it (replace the MLP) at layer 14. Generate outputs and confirm a shift toward visual correctness (e.g., describing the image correctly) compared to unsteered generation.
3. **MLP fit test**: Train the MLP on a small subset (e.g., 200 samples) with a fixed random seed. Ensure training loss decreases steadily and predictions on validation samples approximate the target scaling factors.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter opacity**: Exact MLP architecture (hidden dimension), training schedule (epochs, learning rate), and β scaling factor are not specified, requiring guesswork for replication.
- **Implementation ambiguities**: Prompt templates for P⁺/P⁻ and modality token ranges in the KV cache are underspecified, making model‑specific adaptation tricky.
- **Generalization of steering layer**: The selected layer 14 may only be optimal for Qwen2.5‑VL; other MLLMs could need different layers, limiting transferability.

## Confidence
- MCR reliability: **High**
- Adaptive scaling effectiveness: **Medium**
- Overall superiority over static/L2S: **Low**
