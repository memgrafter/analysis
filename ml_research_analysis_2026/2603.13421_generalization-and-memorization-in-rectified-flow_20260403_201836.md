# Generalization and Memorization in Rectified Flow

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13421
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13421
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Rectified Flow models memorize most at t=0.5; a U-shaped timestep distribution reduces memorization without harming FID, while complexity-calibrated MIA (Tmc_cal) improves detection by up to 15% AUC and 45% TPR@1%FPR.

## Executive Summary
Rectified Flow (RF) models, trained via Flow Matching, exhibit memorization that can be quantified using membership inference attacks (MIA). Existing MIA metrics are confounded by image complexity; the paper introduces Tmc_cal, which normalizes reconstruction error by compressed bitrate to decouple these effects. Theoretical analysis reveals that memorization peaks at the integration midpoint (t=0.5) because the intermediate input becomes orthogonal to the target velocity, forcing the network to rely on non-linear features. To mitigate this, the timestep distribution is changed from uniform to a symmetric exponential (U‑shaped) distribution, reducing exposure to the vulnerable midpoint. Experiments on CIFAR-10, SVHN, and TinyImageNet confirm that Tmc_cal substantially boosts MIA performance (up to 15% higher AUC and 45% higher TPR@1%FPR) and that U‑shaped sampling suppresses the memorization peak while preserving generative quality (FID).

## Method Summary
The authors train RF models using the Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) objective with a UNet architecture. During training, a data sample \(x_1\) is paired with noise \(x_0\), and a timestep \(t\) is sampled (default: uniform). The forward process constructs \(x_t = t x_1 + (1-t)x_0\); the velocity network \(v_\theta\) predicts the vector field, and the loss is the mean squared error between the prediction and the true conditional velocity \((x_1-x_0)/t\). For MIA, three test statistics are defined: **Tnaive** (per‑sample training loss), **Tmc** (Monte Carlo average over multiple noise samples at a fixed \(t\)), and **Tmc_cal** (Tmc divided by the compressed bitrate of the image). To measure memorization, these scores are computed on training and validation sets at a fixed \(t\) (usually 0.5) and separation is quantified via AUC and TPR@1%FPR. A temporal sweep across \(t\) reveals the memorization profile. For mitigation, uniform \(t\) sampling is replaced by a symmetric exponential distribution \(p(t;\alpha)\) that concentrates probability near the boundaries (0 and 1), thereby reducing the number of steps at the vulnerable midpoint. Generative quality is monitored using FID.

## Key Results
- The complexity‑calibrated metric **Tmc_cal** improves MIA detection: up to **15% higher AUC** and up to **45% higher TPR@1%FPR** compared to uncalibrated baselines across CIFAR‑10, SVHN, and TinyImageNet.
- Memorization in RF **peaks at \(t=0.5\)**, which the authors attribute to orthogonality between the intermediate state \(x_t\) and the target velocity \(v\); this is supported by both theoretical derivation and empirical measurements of the linearity gap \(\Delta v\).
- Using a **symmetric exponential (U‑shaped) timestep distribution** reduces the memorization peak (lower AUC) while preserving FID, demonstrating that temporal regularisation can mitigate privacy risks without sacrificing generation quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Complexity‑Calibrated MIA
Raw reconstruction error (e.g., Tmc) is biased by image spatial complexity: simpler images yield lower errors regardless of membership. This confounds the memorization signal. Tmc_cal divides Tmc by a complexity proxy—compressed bitrate—to remove this bias. The core assumption is that compression bitrate correlates with Kolmogorov complexity and thus with modeling difficulty. Empirically, strong positive correlations (>0.5) between Tmc and bitrate are observed for both training and validation sets. If this correlation were absent, calibration would fail to disentangle memorization from complexity.

### Mechanism 2: Orthogonality at the Midpoint
Under normalized data (zero mean, identity covariance) and independent noise \(x_0\), the cross‑covariance between the target velocity \(v = (x_1-x_0)/t\) and the intermediate state \(x_t\) is \(\Sigma_{v,x_t} = (2t-1)I\). At \(t=0.5\) this covariance is zero, meaning \(x_t\) is statistically orthogonal to \(v\). Consequently, the optimal linear estimator of \(v\) given \(x_t\) collapses to the data mean (zero). Any residual error after linear estimation must stem from non‑linear, sample‑specific features—i.e., memorization. The deviation \(\Delta v = v_\theta - v_{\text{linear}}\) is therefore maximal for training data at \(t=0.5\), as measured by the linearity gap in Figure 4. This orthogonality condition relies on proper data normalization; violations could shift the peak away from 0.5.

### Mechanism 3: U‑Shaped Timestep Sampling as Regularization
The memorization peak at \(t=0.5\) suggests that training steps involving this midpoint contribute disproportionately to privacy leakage. Sampling timesteps from a symmetric exponential distribution \(p(t;\alpha)\) (U‑shaped) reduces the probability mass near 0.5 and increases it near the boundaries. This directly limits the number of steps where non‑linear memorization is required, slowing the accumulation of privacy loss. The key assumption is that memorization risk at the boundaries is inherently lower and does not become a dominant new threat. Experiments show that increasing \(\alpha\) lowers the MIA AUC peak while keeping FID stable (Figure 2). However, if boundary memorization becomes significant, the net benefit may diminish.

## Foundational Learning

### Flow Matching & Rectified Flow
- **Why needed:** The analysis builds on the Flow Matching objective, which defines the forward process \(x_t = t x_1 + (1-t)x_0\) and the vector field regression task. These equations are essential for deriving test statistics and studying temporal behavior.
- **Quick check:** Why does the Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) objective have the same optima as the original FM objective?  
  *Answer: Both aim to match the same conditional velocity field; the CFM loss is a conditional version that averages to the FM marginal loss.*

### Membership Inference Attack (MIA)
- **Why needed:** MIA quantifies memorization. The paper tailors MIA to flow models by leveraging the training loss, leading to statistics like Tnaive and Tmc.
- **Quick check:** How can a per‑sample reconstruction error (Tmc) indicate whether that sample was in the training set?  
  *Answer: Memorized samples yield lower error on unseen \(t\) due to overfitting; the error distribution differs between members and non‑members.*

### Linear Minimum Mean Square Error (LMMSE) Estimator
- **Why needed:** The LMMSE provides a theoretical baseline for linear, non‑memorizing behavior. The deviation from this baseline (the linearity gap \(\Delta v\)) isolates non‑linear memorization.
- **Quick check:** For RF, how do you derive the closed‑form \(v_{\text{linear}}(x_t)\) using the global data mean and covariance?  
  *Answer: Under assumptions, \(v_{\text{linear}}(x_t) = \Sigma_{v,x_t} \Sigma_{x_t}^{-1} (x_t - \mu)\); with normalized data this simplifies to \(((2t-1)I) x_t\), which collapses to zero at \(t=0.5\).*

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (training):** Timestep Sampler -> \(t\); \((x_1, x_0, t)\) -> Forward Process Module -> \(x_t\) -> Velocity Network (\(v_\theta\)) -> Loss Calculator (with \(x_1, x_0\)) -> CFM loss.  
**Component map (MIA):** \(x_1\) -> MIA Module (fixed \(t\), samples multiple \(x_0\), queries \(v_\theta\)) -> Tmc -> \(C(x_1)\) -> Tmc_cal.

**Critical path:**  
1. Train an RF model on the training set using either uniform or U‑shaped timestep sampling.  
2. For evaluation, select a timestep \(t\) (e.g., 0.5) and compute MIA scores (Tnaive, Tmc with \(N_{mc}=5\), Tmc_cal) for all images in both training and validation sets.  
3. Compute separation between score distributions via AUC and TPR@1%FPR.  
4. Optionally, sweep \(t\) across \([0,1]\) to analyze the memorization profile.  
5. For mitigation, train models with U‑shaped sampling (vary \(\alpha\)) and compare peak memorization and FID to the uniform baseline.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Monte Carlo samples (\(N_{mc}\))**: Larger \(N_{mc}\) stabilizes Tmc but increases inference cost; the paper uses \(N_{mc}=5\).  
- **Complexity calibration**: Computing compressed bitrate adds preprocessing but yields large MIA gains (TPR improvements up to 44.7%).  
- **U‑shaped sampling concentration (\(\alpha\))**: Higher \(\alpha\) more aggressively reduces midpoint sampling, further lowering memorization, but may harm generation if over‑done; \(\alpha\) must be tuned to maintain FID.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **No \(t=0.5\) peak**: If the MIA AUC vs. \(t\) curve does not peak near 0.5, the orthogonality assumption may be violated (e.g., data not normalized).  
- **Calibration backfires**: If Tmc_cal shows worse separation than Tmc, compression bitrate is not a valid complexity proxy for the dataset.  
- **FID collapse**: When using U‑shaped sampling, a sharp increase in FID indicates excessive distortion of the training distribution.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline MIA**: Train RF with uniform \(t\)‑sampling. At \(t=0.5\), compute Tnaive, Tmc (vary \(N_{mc}\)), and Tmc_cal on train/validation splits. Report AUC and TPR@1%FPR. Expect ordering: Tmc_cal > Tmc > Tnaive.  
2. **Temporal sweep**: For a trained uniform model, compute Tmc_cal across a grid of \(t\) values (e.g., 0.0 to 1.0 in 0.05 increments). Plot AUC vs. \(t\). Expect a clear peak near 0.5.  
3. **U‑shaped intervention**: Retrain models with symmetric exponential sampling (\(\alpha=2\) and \(\alpha=4\)). Repeat the temporal sweep and compare the peak AUC. Also track FID over training steps. Expect peak AUC to decrease with higher \(\alpha\) while FID remains comparable to the uniform baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** For non‑standardized data distributions, what timestep maximizes memorization in Rectified Flow, and how does it depend on data statistics?
- **Basis in paper:** Theoretical analysis assumes \(\mu=0, \Sigma=I\), yielding peak at \(t=0.5\) due to orthogonality. Experiments on TinyImageNet (Appendix H) show the peak shifts, attributed to violated assumptions.
- **Why unresolved:** The orthogonality condition \(\Sigma_{v,x_t}=0\) is not generalized to arbitrary \(\mu\) and \(\Sigma\), so the peak location is unpredictable for real‑world data.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derive \(\Sigma_{v,x_t}\) for general distributions; identify \(t^*\) that maximizes \(\|\Delta v\|\) or MIA score; validate on datasets with controlled shifts in mean/covariance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does U‑shaped timestep sampling effectively reduce memorization in large‑scale Rectified Flow models (e.g., those behind Stable Diffusion 3) without compromising high‑fidelity generation?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors propose Symmetric Exponential sampling and test on CIFAR‑10/SVHN/TinyImageNet with small UNets (Table 4). They note RF powers foundation models but do not evaluate at that scale.
- **Why unresolved:** The method’s impact on memorization vs. generation quality in billion‑parameter models remains unknown; scaling may introduce new trade‑offs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train large RF models (e.g., >100M parameters) on high‑resolution data with U‑shaped sampling, assess memorization via MIA and generation via FID, precision/recall, and human studies.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Are there image complexity metrics superior to compression bitrate for calibrating MIA in Rectified Flow, potentially improving detection of subtle memorization?
- **Basis in paper:** They calibrate MIA scores using compressed bitrate, acknowledging it approximates Kolmogorov complexity but may not capture semantic structure (Section 3.2).
- **Why unresolved:** Bitrate reflects low‑level redundancy; memorization might involve high‑level features that are uncorrelated with bitrate, limiting calibration effectiveness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare MIA performance when calibrating with alternative complexity measures (e.g., neural feature entropy, perceptual metrics) across diverse datasets; identify which yields strongest separation between train/val.

## Limitations
- Incomplete specification of data preprocessing, compression algorithm, and hyperparameters hinders reproducibility.
- Compression bitrate may be an inadequate complexity proxy; alternative measures remain unexplored.
- Theoretical assumptions (zero‑mean, identity covariance, independent noise) may not hold in practice, potentially shifting the memorization peak; boundary effects of U‑shaped sampling are insufficiently evaluated.

## Confidence
- **Complexity‑calibrated MIA (Tmc_cal) efficacy:** High (supported by multi‑dataset gains up to 15% AUC and >0.5 correlations).
- **Peak memorization at t=0.5:** Medium (theory aligns with experiments but relies on strong assumptions).
- **U‑shaped sampling reduces memorization without harming FID:** Medium (empirical reduction in AUC with stable FID, but boundary effects need scrutiny).

## Next Checks
1. **Reproduce calibration correlation:** Compute raw Tmc vs. compression bitrate across multiple settings (PNG, JPEG at various qualities) and test alternative complexity proxies (e.g., entropy, fractal dimension) to check robustness.
2. **Test orthogonality on raw data:** Train RF models without mean‑centering or whitening, then measure the AUC‑vs‑t curve. A significant shift of the peak from 0.5 would challenge the orthogonality claim.
3. **Boundary memorization analysis:** For models using U‑shaped sampling, evaluate Tmc_cal at extreme timesteps (t=0.01, 0.99) and compare to uniform sampling. Substantial increases would indicate a trade‑off; otherwise the mitigation is likely safe.
