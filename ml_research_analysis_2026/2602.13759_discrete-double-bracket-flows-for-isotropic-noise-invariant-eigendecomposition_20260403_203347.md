# Discrete Double-Bracket Flows for Isotropic-Noise Invariant Eigendecomposition

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13759  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13759  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Exact σ²-immunity—the trajectory and Lyapunov value of a discrete double-bracket flow depend only on the trace-free covariance, enabling globally convergent matrix-free eigendecomposition under arbitrarily high isotropic noise.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a discrete double-bracket flow on the orthogonal group to solve the matrix-free eigendecomposition problem when observations are corrupted by large isotropic noise (σ²I) and anisotropic perturbations. The generator’s commutator structure ([A, D]) makes the update algebraically independent of any isotropic shift, yielding exact invariance to σ². Under distinct signal eigenvalues and a Haar-random initialization, the method converges globally with an accelerated saddle-escape rate. The algorithm uses a Cayley retraction implementable via a Neumann series, requiring only matrix-vector products and O(n²) arithmetic per iteration. Experiments show robust convergence across σ² ∈ [0, 1000], while baseline methods fail or degrade.

## Method Summary
The algorithm optimizes M ∈ SO(n) to minimize f(M)=½∥off(MᵀCₑM)∥_F², where Cₑ is the trace-free part of the observed covariance C_k. Each iteration computes A_k = M_kᵀC_kM_k, D_k = diag(A_k), and the generator Ω_k = [A_k, D_k]. The update is M_{k+1} = M_k · Cay(η Ω_k), with Cay implemented as M_k · S · (I + ηΩ_k/2) where S = Σ_{ℓ=0}^{L} (ηΩ_k/2)^ℓ (L=3 or 4). The step size η is constant (e.g., 0.1/∥Cₑ∥₂²) or decaying. Because [αI, ·]=0, the isotropic component σ²I does not affect Ω_k, so the trajectory is σ²-invariant. The method is matrix-free except for n matrix-vector products per iteration.

## Key Results
- Exact σ²-immunity: trajectory and f(M) are independent of isotropic noise level; maximal stable step size η_max ∝ 1/∥Cₑ∥₂².
- Global convergence under distinct eigenvalues: almost-sure convergence to eigenbasis with accelerated saddle-escape O(log(1/ζ)).
- Input-to-state stability: steady-state noise ball radius r_f ∝ ∥Cₑ∥₂²·∥Eₑ∥/δ²; sample complexity O(∥Cₑ∥₂²/(δ² ε))–σ² does not appear.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Algebraic Filtering via Commutator
- **Claim:** The discrete trajectory and Lyapunov values are pointwise invariant to isotropic noise σ²I.
- **Mechanism:** The generator Ω(M) = [A, D] with A = MᵀCₑM satisfies Ω_{C+αI}(M) = Ω_C(M) because [I, X]=0. Hence the update depends only on the trace-free part Cₑ.
- **Core assumption:** Observations are C_k = C_sig + σ²_k I + E_k; the isotropic shift commutes with the generator.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Theorem 3.1(i)–(ii): algebraic identity, trajectory invariance, and stability η_max ∝ 1/∥Cₑ∥₂².  
  - Abstract: "Exact σ²‑immunity—trajectory and Lyapunov value f(M)=½∥off(MᵀCₑM)∥_F² are independent of σ²_k."
- **Break condition:** Invariance holds only for isotropic shifts; anisotropic perturbations (non‑trace‑free) affect the trajectory via the input bound.

### Mechanism 2: Input-to-State Stability in Spectrally Separated Domain
- **Claim:** Under bounded trace‑free noise Eₑ, the algorithm reaches a steady‑state noise ball with radius r_f ∝ ∥Cₑ∥₂²·∥Eₑ∥/δ², independent of σ²_k.
- **Mechanism:** In N_δ = {M ∈ SO(n) : δ(M) ≥ δ}, f satisfies a discrete ISS recursion: √f_{k+1} ≤ (1−c₁δ²η)√f_k + c₂∥Cₑ∥₂ η ∥U_k∥_F. Dissipation comes from the local PL inequality ∥Ω(M)∥_F² ≥ 2δ² f(M); the gain ∥Ω(M)∥_F ≤ 2√2∥Cₑ∥₂ √f limits input sensitivity.
- **Core assumption:** Trajectory stays inside N_δ (non‑escape condition, Theorem 4.5).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Theorem 4.3: derives the discrete ISS recursion and ultimate bound.  
  - Abstract: "ISS‑Based Robustness; steady‑state noise ball radius r_f ∝ ∥Cₑ∥₂²·∥Eₑ∥/δ²."
- **Break condition:** If initial condition plus ISS steady‑state ball exceeds the domain radius R_δ, the trajectory can escape N_δ and the local analysis fails.

### Mechanism 3: Global Convergence via Strict Saddle Geometry
- **Claim:** With distinct eigenvalues of Cₑ and Haar‑random initialization, iterates converge almost surely to the global minimum; saddle escape occurs deterministically in O(log(1/ζ)) steps.
- **Mechanism:** Critical points of f on SO(n) are either global minima or strict saddles with explicit Givens directions. At a degenerate block (A_ii = A_jj, A_ij ≠ 0), the generator Ξ = E_ij − E_ji yields f(M exp(tΞ)) = f(M) − b² sin²(2t), giving deterministic escape (t = π/4 reduces f by b²). Morse‑Bott structure and Łojasiewicz inequality ensure global convergence; Haar initialization avoids stable saddles.
- **Core assumption:** Cₑ has distinct eigenvalues.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Proposition 5.3: Givens escape formula and deterministic escape.  
  - Theorem 5.7: almost‑sure global convergence.
- **Break condition:** Repeated eigenvalues may create non‑saddle critical points (Morse‑Bott manifolds), so strict saddle property fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Optimization on matrix manifolds (SO(n))**  
  *Why needed:* The variable M lies on SO(n); Riemannian operations (gradient, retractions) preserve orthogonality.  
  *Quick check:* Relationship between Euclidean gradient ∇f and Riemannian gradient? Ans: gradf = -M[A, diag(A)]; the Euclidean gradient’s tangent component is -MΩ.
- **Lyapunov functions and discrete ISS**  
  *Why needed:* f(M) proves convergence and robust error bounds under stochastic perturbations.  
  *Quick check:* State the spectral sandwiching inequality in N_δ and how ∥Ω(M)∥_F relates to f(M). Ans: 2δ² f(M) ≤ ∥Ω(M)∥_F² ≤ 8∥Cₑ∥₂² f(M).
- **Saddle escape via explicit curvature**  
  *Why needed:* To ensure global convergence from generic initialization, trajectories must avoid non‑optimal critical points; Givens rotations provide explicit escape with negative curvature.  
  *Quick check:* At a saddle with A_ii = A_jj and b = A_ij ≠ 0, what is ∂²/∂t² f(M exp(t(E_ij − E_ji))) at t=0? Ans: -8b² (strictly negative).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input oracle → compute Y = C_k M_k → A = M_kᵀ Y → D = diag(A) → Ω = AD - DA → Cayley retraction (Neumann series) → M_{k+1} → (optional periodic QR)
- **Critical path:** The dominant cost is n matrix‑vector products (oracle calls) and O(n²) matrix arithmetic (forming A, D, Ω, and matrix products in Cayley). Step size η must satisfy η < 1/L_C with L_C = c_n ∥Cₑ∥₂², c_n = 2√n+8.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Neumann truncation L:* L=3 yields ≈10⁻⁶ accuracy; L=4 improves to 10⁻⁸. Higher L increases O(n³) cost with diminishing returns.  
  - *Step size:* Use constant η = 0.1/∥Cₑ∥₂² for stationary noise; for non‑stationary E, decay η_k = c/(k + k₀).  
  - *Re‑orthogonalization:* QR every T=50–100 steps prevents orthogonality drift; does not break σ²‑immunity.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *f(M_k) increases:* η too large (exceeds 1/L_C); reduce η by factor 2.  
  - *δ(M_k) drops below threshold:* trajectory exiting N_δ; likely due to large observation noise or poor step size.  
  - *Stagnation at high f:* possible saddle; check curvature via directional probing (compute ⟨Ω, Ξ⟩ for candidate Givens directions).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Verify pathwise σ²‑immunity: run with σ²=0 and σ²=1000 (same M₀, C_sig); compute max ∥M_k - M'_k∥_F. Expect ~10⁻¹².  
  2. Monotone descent threshold sweep: test η ∈ [0.001, 0.05] scaled by 1/∥Cₑ∥₂² for n=20; record non‑monotone events. Expect none for η < 0.03/∥Cₑ∥₂².  
  3. ISS scaling: add trace‑free noise E with ∥E∥_F=ε_E; measure lim sup √f. Expect linear scaling with ε_E and constant across σ².

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Structured noise extension:** Can the commutator generator be extended to handle structured (e.g., low‑rank, colored) noise while maintaining σ²‑invariance? The core identity [αI,·]=0 only eliminates scalar shifts; no generalization is provided.
2. **Degenerate spectra:** What is the convergence behavior when Cₑ has repeated eigenvalues? The analysis assumes distinct eigenvalues to guarantee strict saddles; degenerate cases may lead to Morse‑Bott manifolds with unclear convergence.
3. **Complexity reduction:** Can the O(n³) per‑iteration cost be reduced (e.g., to O(n²)) for large n while preserving exact σ²‑invariance? The current full matrix operations limit scalability.
4. **Adaptive step sizes:** Can step sizes be chosen without prior knowledge of ∥Cₑ∥₂ while maintaining stability and σ²‑invariance? Fixed steps require ∥Cₑ∥₂; adaptive schemes might avoid exit from N_δ or improve convergence.

## Limitations
- Exact σ²‑immunity holds only for isotropic shifts; anisotropic noise enters via an ISS bound and can affect accuracy.
- Global convergence guarantees require distinct signal eigenvalues and that the trajectory stays inside the spectral separation region N_δ.
- Practical implementation relies on Neumann series truncation; too low L causes orthogonality drift, and extreme σ² may induce floating‑point instability despite theoretical invariance.
