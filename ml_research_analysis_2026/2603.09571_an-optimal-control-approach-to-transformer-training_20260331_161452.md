# An Optimal Control Approach To Transformer Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09571
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09571
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** A triply quantized optimal control approach for transformer training ensuring global optimality guarantees (without convexity/smoothness) and achieving up to ~70% test error reduction on a toy self-attention task.

## Executive Summary
This paper reformulates Transformer training as an optimal control problem for a discrete-time McKean‑Vlasov particle system with shared actions. The self‑attention mechanism induces non‑Markovian particle dynamics because each particle’s evolution depends on the full empirical measure. By lifting the particle state to a probability measure, the authors obtain a Markov decision process (MDP) on measure flows, with positional encodings embedded to preserve sequence order. Under compactness assumptions, dynamic programming yields a globally optimal deterministic Markov policy. This closed‑loop policy is equivalent to an initial‑distribution‑dependent open‑loop policy, which fixes network weights after training and ensures realized‑input independence. For tractability, a triply quantized training algorithm discretizes state, measure, and action spaces, with provable near‑optimality guarantees. Theoretically, the value function is continuous with respect to initial empirical measure perturbations, implying empirical consistency and robustness to distributional shifts as data size grows. Numerically, on a toy self‑attention approximation task, the method reduces test error by up to ~70%, though runtime grows quadratically with the number of quantized actions.

## Method Summary
The method models a Transformer as a discrete‑time controlled particle system where particles correspond to sequence elements and controls are the weight tensors at each layer. Self‑attention introduces a dependence on the empirical measure of all particles, making the dynamics a McKean‑Vlasov system. To obtain Markovianity, the authors lift the state from individual particles to the empirical measure μ_t, producing a measure‑valued MDP with state space ℙ(𝒳)^K (K batches). Positional encodings are incorporated into the particle state space 𝒳 = PEN × 𝒮 before lifting to preserve sequence order. Under compactness of 𝒮 and the action space 𝒰, the MDP satisfies conditions for existence of a globally optimal deterministic Markov policy via dynamic programming. To make dynamic programming computationally feasible, they introduce three quantizations: (1) state space discretized to a finite 1/n‑net 𝒮^n, (2) action space reduced to a finite set 𝒰^m, and (3) measure space ℙ(𝒮^n) further compressed to a finite grid ℙ^(ℓ)(𝒮^n) via Reznik’s algorithm. The resulting finite MDP admits exact backward DP, and its optimal policy is provably ε‑near‑optimal for the original continuous problem, with ε → 0 as the quantization refinements increase under a prescribed growth condition on ℓ(n). Finally, the closed‑loop policy is unrolled to an open‑loop sequence of controls (weights) that depends only on the initial empirical measure, yielding a fixed‑weight Transformer at inference.

## Key Results
- Global optimality guarantee for Transformer training via DP on a lifted measure‑valued MDP, with no convexity or smoothness assumptions.
- Triply quantized training algorithm with a rigorous near‑optimality bound (Theorem 14).
- Empirical test error reduction up to ~70% on a toy self‑attention approximation task, with observed quadratic runtime scaling in the number of quantized actions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Transformer training dynamics, originally non‑Markovian due to self‑attention's dependence on the empirical measure, can be reformulated as a Markov decision process (MDP) over probability measures.
- **Mechanism:** The particle‑level states (X_{i,t}) are lifted to their time‑evolving empirical measure μ_t = (1/N)∑δ_{X_{i,t}}. This transforms the non‑Markovian McKean‑Vlasov dynamics X_{t+1} = f(X_t, U_t, μ_t) into a Markovian measure flow μ_{t+1} = Φ(μ_t, U_t) where Φ is the pushforward map. Positional encodings are embedded in the particle state space X = PEN × S before lifting to preserve sequence order.
- **Core assumption:** State space S and action space U are compact (Assumption 2).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...lift it to probability measures produces a fully-observed Markov decision process (MDP)... Positional encodings are incorporated into the state space to preserve the sequence order under lifting."
  - [section 3.2] "We lift the particle‑level dynamics to the space of probability measures... we endow P(X) with the weak*‑topology... Φ(μ,U) := μ∘T^{-1}_{μ,U}"
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on Koopman operators and optimal transport, which share lifting‑to‑linear‑space motifs but lack the measure‑valued MDP formulation. Corpus evidence for this specific McKean‑Vlasov → MDP lifting in Transformers is weak; no direct citations found.
- **Break condition:** The measure lifting destroys permutation invariance; without positional encodings, the MDP cannot model sequence‑to‑sequence functions (Section 2.1). If S or U are non‑compact, the weak Feller property and existence of optimal policies may fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Triply quantizing the lifted MDP (state, measure, action spaces) yields a finite‑state MDP whose optimal policies are provably near‑optimal for the original continuous problem.
- **Mechanism:** (1) State space X quantized via 1/n‑nets to finite X^n. (2) Measure space ℙ(X^n) quantized to finite grid ℙ^(ℓ)(X^n) via reconstruction points. (3) Action space U quantized to U^m. Dynamics become Φ^{ℓ,n,m}(μ, U^m) = R_ℓ(Φ^n(μ, U^m)). Dynamic programming on this finite MDP computes optimal closed‑loop policies; these are converted to initial‑distribution‑dependent open‑loop policies for weight fixation.
- **Core assumption:** State quantization radius 1/n and measure quantization index ℓ grow as O(n^{d+1}) (Assumption 11) to ensure stability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "For tractability, a triply quantized training algorithm (quantizing state, measure, and action spaces) is proposed. Optimal policies for the quantized MDP are provably near‑optimal for the original problem."
  - [section 4] "We define the map Φ^{(n)}... and the flow map... We define the quantizer R_ℓ... Then we define a new MDP, denoted MDP^{(ℓ,n,m)}... Theorem 14... any optimal policy for MDP^{(ℓ,n,m)} is near‑optimal for the original problem."
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related work on Koopman‑based MPC and optimal transport quantization, but not the triple quantization scheme. Evidence is primarily from the paper itself.
- **Break condition:** Poor choice of ℓ(n) can cause measure quantization error to dominate. If action quantization U^m is too coarse, near‑optimality gap ε(ℓ,n,m) grows. Runtime is quadratic in |U^m| (Section 6).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Closed‑loop policies for the lifted MDP are equivalent to initial‑distribution‑dependent open‑loop policies, which are realized‑input‑independent and thus compatible with standard Transformer weight fixation.
- **Mechanism:** For deterministic lifted dynamics μ_{t+1} = Φ(μ_t, U_t), a closed‑loop optimal policy γ_t(μ_t) can be recursively unrolled: U_t^* = γ_t(μ_t) = γ_t(Φ(...Φ(μ_0, γ_0(μ_0)), ...)). This defines functions g_t^*: ℙ(𝒳)^K → 𝒰 that depend only on initial measures μ_0. Fixing weights (U_t^*) before inference ensures input independence.
- **Core assumption:** Dynamics Φ are deterministic (no noise); ensemble size N fixed and known.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Closed‑loop policies in the lifted is equivalent to an initial‑distribution‑dependent open‑loop policy, which fix network weights after training, ensuring realized‑input independence."
  - [section 3.4] "By recursively expanding... we express U_t^* as a function g_t^* of μ_1^0,...,μ_K^0... This collection of maps (g_t^*)_t is classified as an initial condition‑dependent open‑loop policy."
  - [corpus] Corpus links to "Dual Filter" paper discuss transformer‑like architectures as hidden Markov models, but not this specific equivalence. Evidence is from the paper's derivation.
- **Break condition:** If dynamics include noise (stochastic self‑attention), the unrolling fails. If positional encodings are not fixed pre‑lifting, the initial‑distribution dependency may not preserve sequence order.

## Foundational Learning
- **McKean‑Vlasov dynamics**  
  *Why needed here:* Self‑attention makes particle evolution depend on the empirical measure of all particles. This is exactly the measure‑dependent drift in McKean‑Vlasov systems.  
  *Quick check:* Given dX_t = b(X_t, μ_t)dt with μ_t = Law(X_t), how does a small change in μ_t affect the flow of X_t? **Answer:** Through the b(·, μ_t) term.

- **Weak*‑topology on probability measures**  
  *Why needed here:* Lifting to ℙ(𝒳) requires a topology where convergence is pairwise convergence of expectations of continuous bounded functions. Weak*‑topology (narrow topology) makes ℙ(𝒳) compact under Assumption 2.  
  *Quick check:* If μ_n → μ weakly, does ∫ f dμ_n → ∫ f dμ for all f ∈ C_b(𝒳)? **Answer:** Yes, by definition.

- **Dynamic programming for MDPs with Borel state spaces**  
  *Why needed here:* The lifted MDP has uncountable state space ℙ(𝒳)^K. Existence of optimal deterministic Markov policies relies on the state space being compact metric with weak* topology and transition kernel being weak Feller.  
  *Quick check:* For an MDP with compact state space 𝒳 and transition kernel T(·|x,u) continuous in (x,u), does a measurable deterministic optimal policy exist? **Answer:** Yes, under mild conditions, see Hernández‑Lerma & Lasserre.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input data → particle states (with positional encodings) → empirical measure μ_t → lifted MDP (state=μ_t^K, action=U_t) → triply quantized MDP → backward DP → optimal closed‑loop policy γ_t → unrolled open‑loop weights (U_t^*) → fixed‑weight Transformer at inference.

### Critical Path
Steps 3–5 in the Method Summary: lifting creates Markovian MDP; quantization makes DP tractable. The DP recursion must compute C_t for all states in ℙ^(ℓ)(𝒮^n)^K, which is combinatorially large but finite for small ℓ, n. The measurement of action quanta m directly drives runtime.

### Design Tradeoffs
Finer state/action quantization (higher n, m) improves near‑optimality (smaller ε) but increases |𝒮^n| and |𝒰^m|, causing DP runtime ~ O(T·|ℙ^(ℓ)(𝒮^n)^K|·|𝒰^m|). Measure quantization ℓ controls grid coarseness; too coarse ℓ loses distributional detail. The λ parameter in W_{2,λ} must be > N/(2 diam(𝒮)^2) to enforce positional matching (Theorem 6).

### Failure Signatures
1. DP values explode or become NaN: Likely due to violating Assumption 2 (unbounded states/actions) or numerical overflow in measure quantization.
2. Test error does not decrease with increased m: Action quantization may be too coarse; try increasing m.
3. Positional information lost: Check that λ is sufficiently large; verify permutation invariance of cost (W_{2,λ}) requires identity coupling.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Verify lifting equivalence:** For small N, T, compute particle trajectory and measure trajectory Φ manually; check μ_{t+1} = (1/N)∑δ_{X_{t+1}}.
2. **Quantization stability test:** With fixed data and 𝒰^m, vary n and ℓ. Plot training error vs. 1/n and ℓ to see convergence.
3. **Runtime scaling:** Measure DP time for increasing m (keep n, ℓ fixed). Expect quadratic growth ~ 0.044·m² seconds (Section 6).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Mean‑field limit (N→∞):** What is the mean‑field limit of the optimal control formulation when the number of particles \(N \to \infty\), and can this connect to mean‑field game theory? The paper states this is possible but leaves it for future work.
- **Scalability of DP:** How can dynamic programming be made computationally tractable for high‑dimensional state and action spaces typical of large transformers? The current triple quantization still suffers exponential growth.
- **Runtime complexity:** What is the scalability limit of the triply quantized training algorithm in terms of sequence length, depth, and width of transformers? Can runtime be improved beyond \(O(M^2)\)?
- **Multi‑head extension:** Does the optimal control framework extend to multi‑headed self‑attention mechanisms as used in standard transformers? The paper suggests generalization is possible but provides no details.
- **Empirical performance on benchmarks:** How does optimal control‑based training perform on standard transformer benchmarks compared to gradient‑based methods in terms of accuracy, robustness, and generalization? Only a toy task was tested.

## Limitations
- **Idealizing assumptions:** The theory requires compact state/action spaces, fixed particle count N, deterministic dynamics, and exact positional encoding, which are unrealistic for training large modern Transformers.
- **Quadratic runtime scaling:** Dynamic programming over the quantized MDP becomes infeasible for moderate action set sizes (e.g., O(m²) for T=2), hindering scalability.
- **Limited empirical validation:** Experiments are restricted to a toy self‑attention task (N=4, T=2) with no comparison to gradient‑based methods on standard benchmarks.
- **Underspecified measure quantization:** The exact implementation of R_ℓ (Reznik’s algorithm) and its impact on the near‑optimality gap are not detailed, hindering exact replication.
