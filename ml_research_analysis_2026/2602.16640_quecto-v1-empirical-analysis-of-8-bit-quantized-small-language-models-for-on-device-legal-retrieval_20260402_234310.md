# Quecto-V1: Empirical Analysis of 8-bit Quantized Small Language Models for On-Device Legal Retrieval

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.16640
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16640
*   **Reference count:** 0
*   **Primary result:** A 124M-parameter model trained only on Indian statutes achieves 88.2% exact-match retrieval accuracy after 8-bit quantization, outperforming a 1.1B generalist model and fitting in a sub-150MB on-device footprint with a 2.5× CPU speedup.

## Executive Summary
Quecto-V1 is a domain-specific Small Language Model (124M parameters) designed to address the legal AI "resource divide" by enabling accurate, offline statutory retrieval on consumer hardware. The model is trained from random initialization solely on the Indian Penal Code, Code of Criminal Procedure, and Constitution. By applying 8-bit GGUF quantization, its size is reduced by 74% (to 132MB) with minimal accuracy loss (2.5% drop in exact match), yielding a 2.5× CPU inference speedup. This demonstrates that extreme specialization and quantization can make high-accuracy legal AI feasible on resource-constrained devices, challenging the notion that larger, generalist models are necessary for complex tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors trained a GPT-2 Small architecture (124M parameters) from scratch on a curated corpus of Indian statutes (IPC, CrPC, Constitution). Training used AdamW with linear warmup and cosine decay, in mixed precision (FP16). The primary evaluation metric was Exact Match (EM) accuracy on a held-out test set of statutory definition queries. The trained model was then converted to the GGUF format and quantized to 8-bit (Q8_0) using `llama.cpp`. Performance was benchmarked on CPU for inference speed, model size, and retrieval accuracy compared to the full-precision version and larger generalist baselines like TinyLlama-1.1B.

## Key Results
*   Quecto-V1 achieves **88.2% exact-match accuracy** on statutory definition retrieval, significantly outperforming the 1.1B-parameter TinyLlama (45%).
*   **8-bit GGUF quantization (Q8_0)** reduces model size by 74% (498MB → 132MB) with only a 2.5% drop in exact-match accuracy (91.0% → 88.5%) and yields a **2.5× CPU inference speedup** (45 ms/token → 18 ms/token).
*   The final quantized model has a **sub-150MB footprint**, enabling deployment on typical consumer hardware without internet connectivity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Domain-specific pre-training on Indian legal statutes enables a 124M-parameter model to achieve ≥88% exact-match retrieval accuracy, significantly outperforming larger generalist SLMs.
*   **Mechanism:** The model's limited capacity is entirely devoted to learning the constrained vocabulary, syntactic patterns, and semantic relationships within the legal corpus, achieving high "lexical density" in its representations. This specialization compensates for parameter count.
*   **Core assumption:** The retrieval task (mapping queries to exact statutory text) is primarily a pattern-matching problem over a closed, formal domain where general world knowledge is unnecessary or harmful.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "maximizes 'lexical density' within the legal domain" and "outperforming general-purpose SLMs in domain-specific exact match tasks"; [section 3] "we initialized the model with random weights... to strictly isolate the effects of domain-specific pre-training".
*   **Break condition:** If the task expands to include case law reasoning or requires integration with external knowledge, the narrow training will likely fail, as the model lacks world knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** 8-bit GGUF quantization (Q8_0) reduces model size by 74% with <3% accuracy loss for statutory retrieval, enabling on-device deployment.
*   **Mechanism:** Legal text's low entropy (repetitive formal phrases, limited vocabulary) means weight distributions have small variance, making them well-suited for uniform 8-bit quantization. The block-wise scaling preserves relative magnitudes critical for exact matching.
*   **Core assumption:** The retrieval task's performance correlates more with capturing exact token sequences than with subtle semantic nuances that quantization might blur.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "8-bit quantization yields a 74% reduction in model size with less than 3.5% degradation in retrieval accuracy"; [section 4] Exact match 91.0% → 88.5% (-2.5%), perplexity 18.42 → 18.95 (+0.53).
*   **Break condition:** For generative tasks requiring creativity or for domains with high linguistic variability (e.g., social media text), the same quantization may cause >10% accuracy drop.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** GGUF quantization yields a 2.5× CPU inference speedup while maintaining memory footprint <150MB, making real-time offline retrieval feasible on consumer hardware.
*   **Mechanism:** Integer-only arithmetic in GGUF reduces compute intensity and memory bandwidth; smaller model fits in CPU cache, minimizing RAM access stalls. The 124M parameter scale is within L2/L3 cache sizes of modern CPUs.
*   **Core assumption:** Target hardware (consumer laptops/phones) has at least 4GB RAM and a mid-range CPU with adequate cache hierarchy.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "yields a 2.5x CPU inference speedup" and "under 150 MB footprint"; [section 4] "Inference Speed (CPU) 45 ms/token to 18 ms/token".
*   **Break condition:** If context length exceeds 512 tokens or batch size >1, memory may exceed cache, causing speedup to diminish. Older CPUs without SIMD for integer ops may see less benefit.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Post-training quantization (GGUF Q8_0)**
    *   **Why needed here:** To compress the model for on-device deployment without retraining, preserving accuracy while reducing size 74%.
    *   **Quick check question:** What is the trade-off between bit-width and accuracy in quantization, and why might 8-bit be "Pareto optimal" for legal retrieval?

*   **Concept: Domain-specific pre-training from random initialization**
    *   **Why needed here:** To avoid contamination from general knowledge and maximize learning on the narrow legal corpus, ensuring all parameters are tuned for statutory patterns.
    *   **Quick check question:** How does training from scratch differ from fine-tuning a pre-trained model in terms of data requirements and final capability distribution?

*   **Concept: Exact-match evaluation for legal retrieval**
    *   **Why needed here:** Legal definitions require verbatim accuracy; semantic similarity is insufficient. The model must output the precise statutory wording.
    *   **Quick check question:** Why might perplexity be a poor proxy for retrieval accuracy in this domain?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Corpus -> Tokenizer (GPT-2 BPE) -> Model (GPT-2 Small, 124M) -> Training (AdamW, FP16) -> Quantization (GGUF Q8_0 via llama.cpp) -> Inference (llama.cpp CPU runtime)

**Critical path:**
1.  Collect/clean legal statutes → split into train/val/test
2.  Initialize random weights → train until convergence (monitor exact-match on val)
3.  Convert PyTorch checkpoint to GGUF via llama.cpp
4.  Apply Q8_0 quantization
5.  Benchmark: size, CPU latency, accuracy on test set
6.  Package for target platform (e.g., mobile app, desktop)

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Domain breadth vs. depth:** Including more legal domains may improve general utility but dilute lexical density and increase model size. Current scope (3 statutes) maximizes retrieval for those texts.
*   **Quantization method:** Q8_0 chosen for balance; Q4_0 would reduce size further but likely cause >5% accuracy drop in this small model.
*   **Training from scratch vs. fine-tuning:** Scratch ensures pure domain knowledge but requires more data/compute; fine-tuning a 1.1B model might achieve similar accuracy with less training time but larger final size.
*   **Sequence length:** 512 tokens chosen to balance memory and typical section length; longer contexts increase memory linearly.

**Failure signatures:**
*   High verbatim repetition: Model memorizes training text but fails on paraphrased queries → indicates overfitting or insufficient lexical diversity in training.
*   Post-quantization accuracy drop >5% → domain not quantization-tolerant; consider lower-bit methods only with additional calibration.
*   CPU inference >50 ms/token → model may be too large for cache; consider reducing hidden dimension or layers.
*   Out-of-domain queries produce plausible but incorrect statutes → model lacks disambiguation capability; need RAG or constraint decoding.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Baseline replication:** Train GPT-2 Small on the provided legal corpus, evaluate exact-match on a held-out set of section definitions. Target: ≥85% accuracy.
2.  **Quantization ablation:** Compare FP32, Q8_0, and Q4_0 on size, speed, and accuracy. Document the accuracy drop per bit-width; confirm Q8_0 drop <3%.
3.  **Cross-model comparison:** Evaluate against TinyLlama-1.1B (generalist) and LegalBERT (discriminative) on the same retrieval task to validate domain-specific advantage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How does integrating a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline with Quecto-V1 affect factual accuracy and hallucination rates on complex legal queries that require citing case law or cross-referencing statutes?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state future work will "integrate a RAG pipeline using a vector database... to fetch real-time citations from an external corpus of case laws before generating an answer."
*   **Why unresolved:** The current model relies solely on parametric memory from statute-only training, which leads to hallucinations. The impact of external retrieval on accuracy and latency for legal tasks remains unevaluated.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative benchmark results on a held-out set of complex legal queries, measuring exact match, citation accuracy, and hallucination rate with and without RAG integration, using a vector database of Indian case law.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** Can knowledge distillation from a large teacher model (e.g., Llama-3-8B) effectively transfer superior reasoning and logic capabilities to Quecto-V1 without increasing its inference cost or degrading its specialized statutory retrieval performance?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors plan to "explore Knowledge Distillation, using a larger 'Teacher' model (e.g., Llama-3-8B) to train a smaller 'Student' model (Quecto-V2)."
*   **Why unresolved:** It is unknown if the 124M parameter architecture can absorb distilled knowledge from a 8B+ model for legal reasoning tasks without overfitting or losing its domain-specific efficiency advantages.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study comparing Quecto-V2 (distilled) against the original Quecto-V1 and the teacher model on a suite of legal reasoning tasks (e.g., hypothetical scenario application, provision linking), while measuring identical CPU inference speed and memory footprint.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** Can multilingual fine-tuning on translated Indian legal corpora (e.g., Hindi, Tamil, Marathi) maintain high retrieval accuracy for statutory definitions while significantly expanding accessibility across India's linguistic demographics?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work involves "training on translated legal corpora (Hindi, Tamil, Marathi) to create a Multilingual Legal SLM."
*   **Why unresolved:** Translating legal text introduces semantic ambiguity and terminology mismatches. It is untested whether a model trained on multilingual legal data will suffer catastrophic interference or accuracy drops in its primary English retrieval task.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluation of a multilingual variant on parallel test sets (same query in multiple languages) for exact match accuracy, alongside assessment of cross-lingual transfer effects on the original English performance.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** How does expanding Quecto-V1's training corpus to include Indian Supreme Court judgments (case law) affect its performance on precedent-based retrieval and reasoning, compared to its current statute-only proficiency?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors intend to "expand the training corpus to include Supreme Court of India judgments, enabling the model to understand legal precedents and case citations."
*   **Why unresolved:** Case law involves narrative reasoning, citation networks, and rhetorical structure, which differ fundamentally from the declarative, definitional format of statutes. It is unclear if the same 124M architecture can learn both without degradation to its core statutory retrieval strength.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A comparative analysis of a "Case Law+Statute" model versus the original statute-only Quecto-V1 on two distinct test sets: one for statutory definitions (to check for catastrophic forgetting) and one for precedent-based queries (e.g., "Which judgments interpret Section 302?").

## Limitations
*   Corpus preprocessing details are omitted (section splitting, metadata handling, final token count), hindering exact replication.
*   Key training hyperparameters are incomplete (batch size, total steps, weight decay), requiring guesswork for reproduction.
*   Evaluation is limited to exact-match retrieval on statute definitions; performance on paraphrased queries, generative tasks, or cross-domain statutes is unknown.

## Confidence
*   **Domain-specific training benefit:** Medium. Evidence from a single experiment on three statutes; no cross-validation or comparison with fine-tuning.
*   **Quantization accuracy preservation:** Medium. Reported 2.5% EM drop for Q8_0, but calibration details and lower bit-width trade-offs unexplored.
*   **Speedup and memory footprint:** Medium. Size reduction factual (498MB→132MB); speedup hardware-dependent and unverified across devices.
