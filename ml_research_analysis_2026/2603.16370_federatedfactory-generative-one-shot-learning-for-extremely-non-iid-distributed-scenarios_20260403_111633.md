# FederatedFactory: Generative One-Shot Learning for Extremely Non-IID Distributed Scenarios

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16370
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16370
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary outcome:** Recovers centralized upper-bound performance in pathological non-IID FL (single-class silos) by exchanging generative priors in one round, boosting CIFAR-10 accuracy from 11.36% to 90.57% and ISIC2019 AUROC from 47.31% to 90.57%, while reducing communication by 99.4%.

## Executive Summary
Federated learning fails catastrophically under extreme non-IID data distributions—specifically when each client holds data from a single, mutually exclusive class—because gradients from local discriminative models point in opposite directions, making averaged updates harmful. FederatedFactory resolves this by replacing parameter transmission with a single-round exchange of generative diffusion models (Factories). Each client trains a local diffusion model on its single-class data. The aggregator (or peers) then synthesizes a class-balanced dataset ex nihilo by sampling from the shared latent space through all generators. A global classifier trained on this synthetic data achieves performance near the centralized upper bound, eliminating gradient conflict and dependency on external priors. The approach also supports precise modular unlearning by deleting specific generative modules.

## Method Summary
FederatedFactory is a one-shot federated framework for single-class silo scenarios. Clients locally train a diffusion model (EDM2) on their exclusive class data. In the centralized protocol, the server receives all generators, samples balanced synthetic data from each (using a standard normal latent space), and trains a global ResNet-50. In the decentralized peer-to-peer protocol, clients broadcast generators; each client synthesizes missing classes from peers' generators, augments its local data into a mixed set, and trains a local expert. Inference in P2P uses Product of Experts aggregation. The framework extends to multi-class clients by training per-class generators and synthesizing with quotas proportional to local sample counts. Unlearning is achieved by deleting specific generator modules and retraining the classifier on the remaining synthetic data.

## Key Results
- On CIFAR-10 with single-class silos, accuracy jumps from a collapsed 11.36% (FedAvg) to 90.57%, recovering 95.8% of the centralized upper bound (94.69%).
- On ISIC2019, AUROC improves from 47.31% to 90.57%.
- Communication overhead is reduced by 99.4% (~1,934 MB on CIFAR-10) compared to iterative methods, at the cost of increased local computational load.
- The framework enables exact modular unlearning of specific classes or clients via deterministic deletion of generative modules.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Generative priors avoid gradient conflict
- **Claim:** Exchanging generative priors instead of discriminative parameters avoids gradient conflict under single-class silos.
- **Mechanism:** Under disjoint label supports, local discriminative models learn opposing decision boundaries whose gradients interfere when averaged. Generative models (diffusion) learn mappings from a shared latent space Z ~ N(0,I) to local data manifolds; these pathways are complementary, not conflicting, because they occupy orthogonal subspaces in data space.
- **Core assumption:** Local generative models can be trained effectively on single-class data and their latent spaces remain aligned via the standard normal prior.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "eliminates gradient conflict"; [section 4.1] "transmission of generative model parameters"; neighboring work on FL for diffusion models does not address single-class conflict specifically.
- **Break condition:** If local diffusion training fails to capture the full data manifold (high ϵ<sub>k</sub>), synthetic samples contain OOD components that degrade classification.

### Mechanism 2: Ex nihilo synthesis approximates pooled distribution
- **Claim:** Aggregating generative models enables synthesis of a universal class-balanced dataset that approximates the true pooled data without raw data exchange.
- **Mechanism:** The server (or peers) samples z ~ N(0,I) and processes each client's Factory G<sub>θk</sub> to generate samples for class y<sub>k</sub>. Weighted by local proportions π<sub>k</sub>, this yields ˆp<sub>syn</sub>(x,y) = Σ π<sub>k</sub> p<sub>θk</sub>(x)I(y=y<sub>k</sub>). Diversity is bounded by the entropy of individual generative priors.
- **Core assumption:** Each G<sub>θk</sub> accurately models its local distribution p<sub>k</sub>(x) and the shared latent space enables meaningful interpolation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.1] "ex nihilo synthesis of a fully synthetic, class-balanced global dataset"; [section 3.1] Eq. (3) defines ˆp<sub>syn</sub>; [section 5] Figure 6 shows t-SNE alignment between real and synthetic distributions.
- **Break condition:** If any G<sub>θk</sub> suffers mode collapse or memorization, ˆp<sub>syn</sub> has missing modes or privacy leakage.

### Mechanism 3: Theoretical bound free of external prior mismatch
- **Claim:** Global risk is bounded solely by local generative errors, not by external prior mismatch.
- **Mechanism:** Theorem 1 proves |L<sub>true</sub>(w<sub>A</sub>) - L<sub>true</sub>(w<sup>*</sup>)| ≤ 2M¯ϵ where ¯ϵ = √(½Σ π<sub>k</sub>ϵ<sub>k</sub>). Unlike FM-dependent methods with an infinite λ term for OOD projection, FederatedFactory's bound depends only on KL(p<sub>k</sub> ∥ p<sub>θk</sub>) from Assumption 1. Training generative models directly on local data avoids projection errors.
- **Core assumption:** Assumption 1 (local diffusion ELBO minimization bounded by ϵ<sub>k</sub>) and Assumption 2 (bounded loss).
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.1] Full proof of Theorem 1 and Lemma 1 showing KL(p<sub>union</sub> ∥ ˆp<sub>syn</sub>) ≤ Σ π<sub>k</sub>ϵ<sub>k</sub>; [abstract] "zero-dependency approach eliminates ... external prior bias"; [section 2] critiques of FM-reliant methods.
- **Break condition:** If Assumption 1 fails (local diffusion doesn't converge to small ϵ<sub>k</sub>), bound becomes vacuous and performance regresses.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept 1: Non-IID Federated Learning and Gradient Conflict
- **Why needed here:** Standard FL (FedAvg) fails under single-class silos because each client's gradients point in opposite directions, making averaged updates harmful. Understanding this motivates moving away from parameter aggregation.
- **Quick check:** "Why do local gradients conflict when each client has only one class, and why can't proximal terms (FedProx) resolve this?"

### Concept 2: Diffusion Models as Generative Priors
- **Why needed here:** The core mechanism uses diffusion models (EDM2) as Factories. They map from a shared latent space, lack a deterministic encoder, and are trained via ELBO minimization. This makes their parameters safe to aggregate and ensures latent space alignment.
- **Quick check:** "How does a diffusion model generate an image from random noise, and why does its training objective minimize a KL divergence?"

### Concept 3: One-Shot vs. Iterative Federated Learning
- **Why needed here:** FederatedFactory is one-shot (C<sub>rounds</sub>=1), swapping communication for compute. This trade-off eliminates iterative gradient conflict but requires high-quality local generative training.
- **Quick check:** "What risks of one-shot FL does iterative FL mitigate, and why are those acceptable in cross-silo medical settings?"

## Architecture Onboarding
Client-side Factory -> Server Aggregator (centralized) OR P2P Broadcast -> Global/PoE Classifier

### Critical Path
1. Local Training: Each client optimizes G<sub>θk</sub> on D<sub>k</sub> via diffusion ELBO.
2. Transmission: Clients upload θ<sub>k</sub> once (centralized) or broadcast (P2P).
3. Synthesis: Server samples z to create ˆD<sub>syn</sub> (centralized) OR clients generate missing classes to form D<sub>mix</sub><sub>k</sub> (P2P).
4. Discriminative Training: Train global ResNet-50 on ˆD<sub>syn</sub> (centralized) OR local experts on D<sub>mix</sub><sub>k</sub> (P2P).
5. Inference: Predict with global model (centralized) or PoE aggregation of local experts (P2P).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Centralized vs Decentralized:** Centralized is simpler but requires trust in aggregator; P2P avoids single point of trust but needs all clients online at inference and has higher local compute.
- **Generator capacity vs compute:** Larger diffusion models (more steps, bigger architecture) improve synthetic quality (lower FID/KID) but increase local FLOPs substantially (e.g., 2.52×10<sup>20</sup> FLOPs on ISIC2019).
- **Synthetic dataset size:** Larger N<sub>target</sub> reduces variance but increases server compute and classifier training time; quotas must preserve global prior.

### Failure Signatures
- **High local generative error:** Per-class FID/KID >100 (e.g., PathMNIST class 3: FID=103.85) indicates poor manifold coverage.
- **Mode collapse:** Visual inspection shows repeated patterns; ECDF diversity has low spread.
- **PoE degeneracy:** Any local expert assigns near-zero probability to correct class causes product to zero; mitigated by log-space floor.
- **Unlearning failure:** After Γ<sub>c,k</sub> ← ∅, synthetic samples for (c,k) persist or classifier accuracy unexpectedly improves.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Local generative sanity check:** Train one Factory on one CIFAR-10 class (e.g., "airplane"). Generate 100 samples; human evaluator confirms >80% are recognizable. Compute FID against real class data; target <30.
2. **Synthetic dataset alignment:** After aggregating all generators, construct full ˆD<sub>syn</sub>. Compute t-SNE (DINOv2 features) comparing ˆD<sub>syn</sub> vs. D<sub>union</sub>; verify cluster overlap. Compute per-class ECDF fidelity; median L2 distance should be comparable across classes.
3. **Centralized upper bound gap:** Train ResNet-50 on real pooled D<sub>union</sub> (centralized upper bound). Compare accuracy to FederatedFactory (centralized mode) under single-class silos. Target gap <2% on CIFAR-10; if >5%, investigate local ϵ<sub>k</sub> (retrain Factories with more epochs/larger architecture).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Computational overhead reduction:** How can the high FLOPs (roughly an order of magnitude increase) be reduced while maintaining performance? Evidence needed on alternative generative architectures, prior compression, and trade-off analysis.
- **Privacy guarantees:** How to integrate formal differential privacy into FederatedFactory to prevent data extraction from transmitted priors? Evidence needed: DP mechanisms during local diffusion training, empirical attack evaluations, and privacy budget analysis.
- **Memorization vs generalization:** How to ensure local diffusion models generalize without memorizing training data, thereby supporting the theoretical convergence assumption (bounded ϵ<sub>k</sub>)? Evidence needed: memorization detection techniques, experiments varying dataset size/model capacity, correlation with synthetic quality metrics.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Exact EDM2 training settings (lr, batch size, sampling steps during training, total epochs) are unspecified; may affect local ϵ<sub>k</sub> and synthetic quality.
- **Synthetic quota definition:** Proportional quotas for class balance lack concrete total synthetic size formula; could bias classifier if mis-specified.
- **Decentralized PoE details:** Missing numerical value for probability floor and convergence criteria; may cause numerical instability or over-confidence.

## Confidence
- **Gradient conflict elimination (Mechanism 1):** Medium (intuitive gradient orthogonality, but no direct ablation; depends on diffusion quality).
- **Synthetic distribution approximation (Mechanism 2):** High (backed by Theorem 1 and empirical t-SNE/FID alignment).
- **Risk bound without external prior (Mechanism 3):** High (formal proof under Assumptions 1–2, though assumptions may be optimistic).
- **Recovery of centralized upper bound:** Medium (large improvements shown, but residual gap suggests room for better generators).
- **Exact modular unlearning:** Low (conceptually sound but minimal experimental evidence).
