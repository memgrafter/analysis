# A tree interpretation of arc standard dependency derivation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.27459
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27459
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: Arc-standard transitions can be interpreted as constructing a deterministic ordered tree that uniquely encodes projective dependency trees; a neural implementation achieves 95.94 UAS on English PTB-WSJ and 92.20 UAS on Chinese CTB 5.1.

## Executive Summary
The paper bridges dependency and constituency parsing by showing that arc-standard transition sequences directly build an ordered tree representation whose structure uniquely determines the original projective dependency tree. Each transition (shift, leftarc, rightarc) corresponds to a deterministic update that preserves surface-contiguous leaf spans and stable lexical anchoring. The representation exists if and only if the dependency tree is projective (single-headed). A neural parser built on Stanza’s constituency framework demonstrates feasibility, obtaining high unlabeled and labeled attachment scores on English and Chinese, with pseudo-projective lifting extending the approach to non-projective inputs.

## Method Summary
The authors define a mapping from arc-standard transitions to ordered tree updates: shift creates a leaf node; leftarc and rightarc attach the top stack tree as the leftmost or rightmost dependent of the next stack tree’s anchor, respectively. This construction yields a contiguous ordered tree `T(D)` that encodes the projective dependency tree `D`. For non-projective trees, pseudo-projective lifting transforms them into projective surrogates before parsing, and the inverse transformation restores original arcs after recovery. A neural transition parser is implemented by adapting Stanza’s constituency parser to use the three actions, training it to predict the oracle transition sequences derived from gold trees. The recovered unlabeled arcs from `T(D)` are then labeled using Stanza’s dependency-graph classifier.

## Key Results
- The ordered tree representation uniquely determines the original projective dependency arcs.
- Projectivity is characterized: a single-headed tree admits such a representation iff it is projective.
- Neural parser achieves 95.94 UAS and 94.90 LAS on English PTB-WSJ; 92.20 UAS and 91.13 LAS on Chinese CTB 5.1, confirming the feasibility of the derivational approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### 1. Deterministic tree updates with contiguity preservation
Each arc-standard transition updates a partial ordered tree in a way that maintains surface-contiguous yields and stable lexical anchoring. Shift adds a singleton leaf; leftarc/rightarc attach a subtree at the left or right boundary of the anchor, which is valid because projectivity ensures the attached subtree’s span lies entirely on one side of the anchor and is contiguous. The mapping is defined for projective trees (or their pseudo-projectivized versions). Evidence: formal definitions in Section 3.2; high UAS indicates the derived trees respect contiguity. Break condition: if pseudo-projective lifting fails to produce a projective surrogate, the mapping cannot be applied.

### 2. Characterization of projectivity
For a single-headed dependency tree, the existence of a contiguous ordered representation (with leaves in surface order and internal node yields contiguous) is equivalent to projectivity. A projective tree yields such a representation via the arc-standard derivation; conversely, a non-projective tree would force interleaving of subtree yields, violating contiguity. Evidence: Theorem 1 (Section 3.1); empirical success on largely projective treebanks supports the forward direction. Limitation: only single-headed trees are considered; multi-headed structures are outside the theorem’s scope.

### 3. Unique recoverability of dependency arcs
The ordered tree `T(D)` uniquely determines the original dependency arcs because each combination step corresponds to creating a new head-dependent relation; traversing the tree reveals the full set. For non-projective inputs, inverse pseudo-projective decoding restores the original crossing arcs after label prediction. Evidence: Theorem 3 (Section 3.2); high LAS after separate label prediction confirms accurate recovery. Break condition: if the predicted transition sequence deviates from a valid arc-standard derivation, the recovered arcs may be incorrect.

## Foundational Learning
- **Arc-standard transition system** (Nivre, 2003): A stack-based transition system with actions shift, leftarc, rightarc that incrementally builds projective dependency trees. *Why needed*: The entire interpretation is defined over these actions; understanding the stack/buffer dynamics reveals how tree fragments combine. *Quick check*: Given a projective dependency tree, what is the arc-standard transition sequence that constructs it? What invariant does the system impose on the stack content?
- **Projective dependency tree**: A dependency tree where every head’s dependents form a contiguous span in the surface word order. *Why needed*: Projectivity is necessary and sufficient for the ordered representation to exist; it guarantees that attachments made at boundaries align with contiguity. *Quick check*: How do you test if a dependency tree is projective? What does a crossing arc imply about surface spans?
- **Pseudo-projective lifting** (Kahane et al., 1998; Nivre & Nilsson, 1998): A reversible transformation that converts non-projective trees into projective ones by lifting arcs over intervening nodes. *Why needed*: It extends the derivational mapping to non-projective inputs, allowing the same arc-standard derivation to be applied. *Quick check*: When an arc (h, d) is lifted to (h, d') with d' a synthetic node, what information is encoded, and how is the original arc restored after parsing?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Neural parser core (Stanza constituency) -> Transition action mapper -> Ordered tree builder -> Label predictor (Stanza dependency-graph) -> (optional) Pseudo-projective inverter.

**Critical path**: Input sentence → (apply ϕ lifting if needed) → neural parser predicts transition sequence → transition actions construct ordered tree T(ϕ(D)) → extract unlabeled arcs → label predictor assigns labels → (apply ϕ⁻¹ if needed) → final labeled dependency tree.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Pros: The derivational approach yields a hierarchical, surface-ordered structure directly from the transition process; it preserves contiguity and lexical anchoring, and the mapping is deterministic.  
- Cons: The method is tied to arc-standard transitions; other systems (e.g., arc-eager) require separate analysis. Using a constituency parser backbone for dependency parsing may be less efficient than a native dependency parser.

**Failure signatures**:  
- *Structural*: A leftarc/rightarc tries to attach a subtree whose yield is not contiguous with the expected side; a lexical anchor appears in more than one internal node.  
- *Recovery*: The arcs extracted from T(D) do not form a valid tree (e.g., missing root, multiple heads), indicating an invalid transition sequence.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Projective sanity check**: For a small set of known projective sentences, run the oracle to generate transition sequences and simulate the updates; verify that the final ordered tree matches the expected T(D).  
2. **Non-projective pipeline test**: Take a non-projective sentence, apply ϕ lifting, parse with the mapped parser, then apply ϕ⁻¹; check that the recovered unlabeled arcs equal the original non-projective tree.  
3. **Label prediction integration**: Feed arcs from Experiment 1 into Stanza’s dependency-graph label predictor and compare LAS against gold to assess labeling performance in isolation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Other transition systems**: Do arc-eager or arc-hybrid systems admit ordered tree representations analogous to arc-standard? What hierarchical objects do they implicitly define?
2. **Practical benefits**: Does the derivational interpretation improve accuracy, efficiency, or interpretability compared to convertive approaches or standard dependency parsers?
3. **Non-projective without lifting**: Can the ordered tree representation be extended directly to non-projective trees (without pseudo-projective lifting) while retaining unique recoverability?
4. **Joint labeling**: Can dependency labels be integrated into the transition actions to enable end-to-end learning of structure and labels within the derivational framework?
5. **Cross-framework alignment**: How well do the subtree boundaries induced by the ordered tree align with gold-standard constituency annotations, and can this representation serve as a bridge for syntactic evaluation or downstream tasks?

## Limitations
- Reproduction requires unspecified neural hyperparameters and oracle generation details, hindering exact replication.
- Pseudo-projective lifting may fail or degrade accuracy for complex non-projective trees; failure rates are not reported.
- Evaluation is limited to two treebanks (English PTB-WSJ, Chinese CTB 5.1), restricting claims about generalizability.
