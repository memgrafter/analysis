# Creating a digital poet

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16578
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16578
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** In a blinded test, AI-generated poems were classified as human at 52% (95% CI: 0.437–0.602), not significantly above chance; human poems at 54% (95% CI: 0.457–0.622).

## Executive Summary
This study explored whether a machine can create poetry indistinguishable from human work. Over seven months, researchers used a workshop-style prompting protocol to iteratively guide GPT-4—without any model retraining—toward a consistent poetic persona named "Naomi Efron." Expert feedback and revision prompts accumulated in a persistent context, shaping a corpus of poems. A blinded evaluation with 50 humanities students and graduates showed that participants classified human poems as human at 54% and AI poems as human at 52%, with both confidence intervals including the 50% chance level. The resulting collection was commercially published under the model's self-chosen pen name, demonstrating the potential of in-context learning for creative shaping.

## Method Summary
The researchers implemented a 14-session workshop protocol over seven months. Each session introduced a specific poetic principle (e.g., avoid clichés, use intertextuality). The model generated draft poems, received structured expert critique, revised its output, and then distilled the feedback into a general rule. All session interactions—including principles, drafts, critiques, revisions, and distilled rules—were logged and used to initialize the context for subsequent sessions, creating a growing memory bank. After the workshop, the final accumulated context primed the model to generate additional poems, which were curated into a collection. The model also invented its own pen name ("Naomi Efron") and author image. The poetry was evaluated in a forced-choice blinded test where participants judged whether poems were human- or AI-authored.

## Key Results
- **Indistinguishability:** AI-generated poems were labeled human at 52% (95% CI: 0.437–0.602); human poems at 54% (95% CI: 0.457–0.622). Both intervals include chance (50%), indicating no significant discrimination.
- **Commercial publication:** The AI-generated corpus was published as a poetry collection under the model's self-chosen pen name, "Naomi Efron."
- **Corpus consistency:** Quantitative analysis revealed a stable semantic and stylistic fingerprint across the generated poems, supporting the emergence of a coherent persona.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Sustained, multi-session in-context learning can accumulate a stable poetic persona by treating prior interactions as a persistent, retrievable memory bank.
- **Mechanism:** Each workshop session initializes the LLM's context with logs from previous sessions (drafts, critiques, distilled principles). The model's generation is conditioned on this cumulative history, allowing stylistic and thematic constraints to compound over time without parameter updates. The "persona" emerges as a consistent pattern reinforced across the extended context window.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM's attention mechanism can effectively prioritize and integrate relevant historical constraints from a long, growing context across discrete interaction turns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...shaped a consistent poetic persona and corpus... workshop-style prompting can support long-horizon creative shaping."
  - [section] "Phase I: Context Initialization... Prior session knowledge initializes the context for new poem generation."
  - [corpus] The quantitative analysis of the final corpus (Table 2) reveals a consistent semantic and stylistic fingerprint, suggesting a coherent voice was maintained.
- **Break condition:** Context window overflow or degradation of relevant historical signal as the session log grows, leading to persona drift or repetition.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Structured, principle-driven feedback loops (introduce principle → generate → critique → revise → summarize) function as a form of "soft expert tuning" that distills abstract poetic goals into reusable heuristics.
- **Mechanism:** The critique step provides a dense, qualitative error signal. The subsequent revision and summarization steps force the model to map this feedback onto its internal representations and articulate a rule. This rule is then added to the persistent context, creating a growing library of implicit "style guidelines" the model consults in future generations.
- **Core assumption:** The model can perform meaningful self-critique and rule extraction from human feedback, and that these distilled rules are syntactically and semantically stable enough to be recalled and applied correctly in later, topically diverse sessions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...iteratively guided GPT-4 via expert feedback and revision prompts... shaping a consistent poetic persona."
  - [section] Supplementary Materials: "Each session followed a fixed feedback-loop format... the model was asked to revise its output, demonstrate internalisation of the comments, and formulate the principle it had just practised in its own words."
  - [corpus] Weak direct anchor; corpus consistency is the *outcome*, but the protocol in Supplementary Materials describes the mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Feedback becomes too complex, contradictory, or domain-specific for the model to generalize into a stable, reusable heuristic, causing noisy or inconsistent application.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Instructor-driven persona anchoring (assigning a fixed name, biography, and self-image) creates a powerful narrative and meta-textual constraint that the model can use to self-cohere and generate meta-poetic content.
- **Mechanism:** By treating the model as an entity ("a poet in the making") with a fixed social identity (name, gender, biography), the instructors provide a high-level, unifying narrative constraint. The model leverages this identity to generate self-referential poems (e.g., "My Name in a Voice Not Mine" - Table 1) and to make coherent choices about a book's structure and manifestos, which in turn reinforces the persona in subsequent context.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM can sustain a coherent self-narrative and generate plausible meta-textual content (about its own "work" and "identity") when consistently prompted from a fixed first-person perspective.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...it produced a pen name and author image."
  - [section] "The 'Naomi Efron' persona... the model was prompted to invent a persona for itself... it constructed a brief biography, proposed a profile picture... Some of the poems exhibit emotionally involved texts, describing intricate interactions..."
  - [corpus] Table 1 explicitly demonstrates self-reference ("My Name in a Voice Not Mine", "the language asked me to consider..."), showing the persona was integrated into the poetic output.
- **Break condition:** The model's generated self-narrative diverges significantly from instructor expectations or becomes nonsensical, or the persona is forgotten in favor of topical prompts without explicit re-anchoring.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: In-Context Learning (ICL) & Context Window Management**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire protocol relies on the model's ability to absorb, retain, and selectively attend to information across many thousands of tokens of accumulated conversation history. Understanding context window limits, potential for recency bias, and strategies for effective context summarization is critical.
  - **Quick check question:** If a model's context window is 128K tokens and each 2-hour workshop session adds ~2K tokens of logs, how many sessions can you run before you must start truncating or summarizing old context, and what is a safe truncation strategy?

- **Concept: Prompt Engineering for Iterative Refinement**
  - **Why needed here:** The shift from a single static prompt to a dynamic, multi-turn protocol with structured output requirements (draft, then revision, then summary) requires designing prompt templates that reliably elicit the desired intermediate steps (critique, rule extraction) and maintain state.
  - **Quick check question:** What specific prompt elements would you include to reliably elicit a *generalizable principle* from a model after it revises a poem based on your critique, rather than just a poem-specific apology or fix?

- **Concept: Domain-Specific Evaluation Design (Humanities/Literature)**
  - **Why needed here:** The claim of "indistinguishability" rests on a specific experimental design (blinded test, humanities-educated raters, matched stimuli). Replicating or assessing this requires understanding the limitations of such tests (small N, item effects, rater expertise) and what "chance" performance actually signifies about perceived literary quality.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might a 54% "human" label rate for human poems in a forced-choice binary test still be consistent with the AI poems having *lower* literary quality, and what supplementary analyses (beyond accuracy) would you need to probe quality?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
The system comprises the following components:
1. **Base LLM:** A large, general-purpose model with strong in-context learning and zero-shot reasoning (e.g., GPT-4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet). Must have a sufficiently large context window (100K+ tokens).
2. **Orchestrator/Session Manager:** A script or application that loads prior session logs, formats the context initialization prompt, manages the turn-by-turn dialogue with the LLM API, and saves the complete session transcript.
3. **Prompt Template Library:** Templates for: a) Context priming (loading persona + history), b) Principle introduction & draft generation, c) Structured critique delivery, d) Revision request, e) Principle distillation/summarization, f) Meta-tasks (name/image generation, book curation).
4. **Context Buffer & Summarizer:** A storage system for session logs. A component (could be manual or automated) to periodically generate summaries of past sessions to inject into new contexts when the raw log exceeds the window.
5. **Human Feedback Interface:** A simple UI (even a well-structured document) for the expert to deliver critique in a consistent, structured format that the model can parse.
6. **Evaluation Module (Offline):** Scripts to prepare balanced stimulus sets, run the blinded survey (via a platform like Qualtrics), and perform the statistical analysis (mixed-effects models, within-subject tests) as described in the SM.

### Critical path
    1. Initialize session: Load persona definition + summary of prior principles + select seeds from past poems.
    2. Introduce new poetic principle via prompt.
    3. Model generates draft poem(s) under constraint.
    4. Human expert provides structured critique (e.g., "Metaphor is cliché; strengthen verb choice in stanza 2").
    5. Model revises draft based on critique.
    6. Model is prompted to "summarize the key lesson from this session in one rule."
    7. Append full turn transcript (principle, draft, critique, revision, distilled rule) to permanent session log.
    8. Repeat from step 1 for next session. Periodically regenerate context summary from full log.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Context Depth vs. Breadth:** Using full raw logs preserves nuance but consumes context window. Using only summaries saves space but loses specific examples. A hybrid approach (summary + recent raw logs) is likely necessary.
- **Feedback Specificity:** Highly specific line-edit critiques may lead to overfitting to narrow issues. Broader principle-based critiques encourage generalization but risk being ignored. Need a balanced critique format.
- **Persona Stability:** The more open-ended the generation prompts, the more the persona must be anchored in *every* context initialization. Over-reliance on a single "You are Naomi Efron" sentence may be insufficient as context grows.

### Failure signatures
- **Persona Drift:** Model-generated poems start to ignore the accumulated principles and style, reverting to generic LLM output. (Detect via stylistic analysis of new batches vs. established corpus).
- **Principle Amnesia:** Model fails to apply a principle introduced in an early session in a later, thematically different session, even when the summarized rule is in context.
- **Degenerate "Safe" Output:** Model generates overly simplistic, cliché-avoidant but artistically vacuous poems, having over-optimized for a shallow reading of "avoid clichés."
- **Formal Constraint Failure:** Inability to sustain meter/rhyme (as noted in paper) despite repeated prompting, indicating a fundamental representation gap.

### First 3 experiments
    1.  **Persona Fidelity Probe:** After 5 sessions, prompt the model to write a poem on a novel topic *without* loading any session history (blank slate). Compare its output to a poem from the full-context session. Is the persona (themes, lexicon, meta-poetic focus) still present? This tests if the persona is truly internalized or purely context-dependent.
    2.  **Feedback Loop Integrity Test:** Introduce a deliberately subtle, multi-faceted critique in one session (e.g., "The emotional arc is flat; use more concrete imagery in the third stanza; vary line lengths more"). In the next session, without re-presenting the old poem, ask the model to "list the principles you learned from your last feedback." Check if all critique points are captured in the distilled rule.
    3.  **Evaluation Replication & Extension:** Re-run the blinded discrimination test (50 participants, 3+3 poems) with a new set of poems from the *same* digital poet but on different topics, and include a third condition: poems from a different, commercially available LLM (e.g., default ChatGPT) to test if the "Naomi Efron" persona is uniquely indistinguishable or if all high-quality LLM poetry is equally hard to discriminate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** To what extent do the findings generalize across different poetic forms (e.g., formal/metric poetry), languages, and reader demographics beyond Hebrew-speaking humanities students?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state the study "does not establish that the poems are equivalent in literary value, nor that results generalize across languages, genres, or broader populations." They also note their specific failure to teach consistent classical rhyme/meter via prompting alone.
- **Why unresolved:** The experiment used a single language (Hebrew), a narrow poetic style (contemporary free verse), and a specific participant pool (humanities students/graduates). The model's inability to handle strict formal constraints suggests the workshop protocol may not extend to all poetic traditions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replication studies with multiple languages, diverse poetic forms (e.g., sonnets, ghazals), and broader, culturally varied participant pools. Systematic variation of the workshop curriculum to target formal constraints would test boundary conditions.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How do the cultural backgrounds, aesthetic preferences, and number of human trainers shape the resulting "digital poet" persona and corpus?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors identify as a key limitation that "workshop-based shaping may embed the trainers’ preferences and cultural priors; future work should test trainer diversity, alternative workshop curricula."
- **Why unresolved:** The workshop was conducted by only two researchers (with specific literary backgrounds). The model's persona ("Naomi Efron") and stylistic output reflect this singular pedagogical perspective. It is unknown whether a more diverse trainer set would yield a different, more broadly representative, or more robust poetic voice.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative workshops with trainers of varied genders, ethnicities, literary traditions, and poetic philosophies, measuring divergence in the model's output (stylistic analysis) and the resulting indistinguishability in blinded tests.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what degree does the chance-level discrimination result depend on the specific, relatively small set of stimuli (3 human, 3 AI poems per participant) versus a fundamental human inability to identify AI authorship in this context?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note a limitation: "each participant evaluated only six poems (3+3), which increases uncertainty at the individual level." Their primary analysis accounts for repeated measures, but stimulus sampling is acknowledged as a constraint.
- **Why unresolved:** With only 3 poems per category judged per participant, results are sensitive to the particular poems chosen. A different subset of poems (e.g., more challenging or easier examples) could yield different discrimination rates. The confidence intervals are wide, reflecting this uncertainty.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A larger-scale study with more poems per participant and a fully crossed design where a larger, pre-rated corpus of human and AI poems is sampled more extensively for each participant, reducing item-level noise.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What modifications to the interaction protocol or model architecture are necessary to reliably elicit sustained formal poetic constraints (e.g., strict meter, end-rhyme) through prompt-based in-context learning?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper explicitly states their "attempts to teach consistent classical end rhyme and meter... were unsuccessful" and speculates this stems from a gap between the model's next-word prediction and the "advanced planning" required for rhyme. They cite prior work that uses specialized training objectives for rhyme, implying prompting alone may be insufficient.
- **Why unresolved:** The workshop protocol successfully shaped style and voice for free verse but failed for formal constraints. This suggests a fundamental limitation of pure in-context learning for tasks requiring global structural planning. It is unclear if a redesigned prompt protocol (e.g., more granular scaffolding, external rhyme dictionaries) or a hybrid approach (e.g., constrained decoding, fine-tuning) is needed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation studies comparing different prompting strategies (e.g., stepwise rhyme planning, explicit meter templates) and/or integrating light-weight decoding-time constraints for formal poetry, measuring success in generating consistent metrical and rhyming verse.

## Limitations
- **Implementation opacity:** The exact prompt templates, critique phrasing, and context management strategy (e.g., summarization frequency) are not detailed, making exact replication dependent on reverse-engineering.
- **Statistical fragility:** The primary indistinguishability claim rests on a single, relatively small-sample (N=50) test where both human and AI classification CIs inclusively contain 0.5 (chance). The result is suggestive but not definitive proof of perceptual equivalence.
- **Generalizability:** Success is demonstrated for one persona ("Naomi Efron") and a narrow curriculum focused on semantic/stylistic constraints, not formal structure (rhyme/meter failed). It is unclear if this process reliably scales to other poetic forms, voices, or LLM architectures.

## Confidence
- **Primary claim (indistinguishability):** Medium (statistically weak, CI includes chance).
- **Mechanism 1 (persistent context persona):** Medium-Low (plausible but lacks direct analysis of context weighting).
- **Mechanism 2 (feedback loop distillation):** Low (process described but no evidence principles were functionally applied).
- **Mechanism 3 (persona anchoring):** Medium (self-referential poems provide direct evidence).

## Next Checks
1.  **Internalization Test:** After the full workshop, generate a set of poems on a novel topic with *no* accumulated context (only the base persona prompt). Analyze these against the full-context poems for loss of thematic/stylistic consistency. A sharp drop supports context-dependency over internalized learning.
2.  **Principle Application Audit:** For a subset of distilled principles (e.g., "avoid cliché"), run a follow-up session where the model is given only the rule (no historical context) and asked to generate. Blindly judge the outputs against the original workshop revisions for adherence. Failure indicates principles weren't truly learned.
3.  **Cross-Model Replication & Baseline:** Replicate the entire protocol (same curriculum/feedback script) with a different, contemporary LLM (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet). Then, run the same blinded discrimination test comparing *this* new digital poet, the original "Naomi Efron" corpus, a standard "no-workshop" baseline from the same model, and human poems. This isolates the protocol's effect from the base model's inherent poetic capability.
