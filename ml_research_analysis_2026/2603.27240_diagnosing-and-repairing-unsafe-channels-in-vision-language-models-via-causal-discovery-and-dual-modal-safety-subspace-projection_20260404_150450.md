# Diagnosing and Repairing Unsafe Channels in Vision-Language Models via Causal Discovery and Dual-Modal Safety Subspace Projection

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.27240
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27240
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CARE reduces attack success rate (ASR) from 25%–40% to below 10% on Qwen2.5-VL and LLaVA-OneVision while preserving general multimodal performance with only 2%–8% drop on standard benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language models (LVLMs) remain vulnerable to multimodal jailbreaks, where adversaries bypass safety protocols using crafted images or text. Existing defenses lack precision and often degrade model utility. This paper introduces CARE, a training-free framework that first diagnoses safety-critical mid-layer neurons via causal mediation analysis, then learns separate visual and textual safety subspaces through generalized eigen-decomposition. At inference, CARE projects activations onto these subspaces and fuses them adaptively, suppressing harmful features while preserving semantic content. Evaluations show strong reduction in ASR across multiple benchmarks with minimal impact on general performance.

## Method Summary
CARE operates in two phases: diagnosing and repairing. Diagnosis uses causal mediation analysis—blocking entire layers or specific components (FFN/MHSA)—to identify mid-layers where disabling them increases attack success rate, indicating causal responsibility for safety violations. Repair involves: (1) attributing importance to visual and textual tokens via RBF kernels to select influential tokens; (2) collecting FFN activations from these tokens in safety-critical layers for benign and malicious samples; (3) computing covariance matrices for both sets and solving a generalized eigenvalue problem to extract the top-256 eigenvectors that span a malicious subspace; (4) projecting activations onto the orthogonal safe subspace using a regularization term that anchors to benign activations; and (5) fusing separately projected visual and textual streams with an adaptive weight that balances corrections based on relative deviation magnitudes. The method requires no retraining and intervenes only at inference.

## Key Results
- On JailbreakV and MMSafety benchmarks, CARE reduces ASR from 25%–40% to below 10% for Qwen2.5-VL and LLaVA-OneVision.
- Under PGD attacks, ASR drops from 50%–70% to 5%–15%.
- General multimodal performance on MMBench, MM-Vet, and SQA degrades by only 2%–8%.
- Defense transfers effectively to unseen attacks within similar families.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Mid-layer FFN neurons are causally responsible for unsafe behaviors in LVLMs.
- **Mechanism:** Causal mediation analysis via surgical ablation (blocking) of entire layers or specific components (FFN/MHSA). A significant increase in Attack Success Rate (ASR) after blocking a layer indicates that layer's causal role in mediating safety.
- **Core assumption:** Unsafe behaviors can be intercepted by disabling computational pathways in early-to-mid layers before harmful representations fully form and propagate to the output.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "employs causal mediation analysis to identify neurons and layers that are causally responsible for unsafe behaviors."
  - [section 2.1] Figure 2 shows ASR increases when blocking layers 12-14 (Qwen) or 16-18 (LLaVA), pinpointing critical layers.
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence for causal analysis in LVLM safety is limited; neighboring papers focus on detection or alignment, not mechanistic neuron localization. This is a novel contribution of the target paper.
- **Break condition:** If layer ablation does not produce a statistically significant change in ASR compared to a baseline, the hypothesised causal pathway for that layer is invalid.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Harmful visual and textual activations occupy a distinct, low-dimensional subspace that can be separated from benign activations via generalized eigen-decomposition.
- **Mechanism:** Compute covariance matrices `C_b` (benign) and `C_m` (malicious) from FFN activations in identified safety-critical layers. Solve `C_m u = λ C_b u`. Top eigenvectors (`U_k`) with largest `λ` span the "malicious subspace." Projecting activations onto its orthogonal complement (`P_safe = I - U_k U_k^T`) suppresses harmful components.
- **Core assumption:** The ratio of malicious-to-benign variance (`λ`) meaningfully defines directions that are specifically associated with unsafe outputs, and these directions are consistent enough across samples to form a stable subspace.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "learns separate visual and textual safety subspaces via generalized eigen-decomposition between benign and malicious activations."
  - [section 2.2, 3.2.1] Clustering metrics (Silhouette, Mahalanobis) peak at mid-layers, indicating separability. FFN activations show lower inter-sample correlation than MHSA, making them suitable for subspace projection.
  - [corpus] While subspace methods are used in other domains (e.g., model editing), their application to *dual-modal* safety projection in LVLMs is not evidenced in the provided corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the top eigenvalues `λ` are not significantly larger than 1, or if projection does not reduce ASR in a validation set, the malicious subspace is not well-defined.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** An adaptive, data-driven fusion of separately projected visual and textual activations preserves multimodal semantics more effectively than fixed or single-modality steering.
- **Mechanism:** Compute separate safety projections for visual (`h'_vis`) and textual (`h'_txt`) streams. Fuse them using weight `w_vis = ||h'_vis - h_txt|| / (||h'_vis - h|| + ||h'_txt - h||)`, which increases the weight of the modality whose projection deviates more from the *other* modality's original activation. This balances corrections based on relative "intervention strength."
- **Core assumption:** The relative magnitude of the correction in one modality (relative to the other modality's unmodified state) is a good proxy for which modality's safety correction is more critical for the current input.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "hybrid fusion mechanism that adaptively balances visual and textual corrections."
  - [section 3.2.3] Equation 18 defines `w_vis`. Ablation in Table 2 shows removing either modality harms performance on corresponding attack types, validating the need for dual projection.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus; adaptive fusion for multimodal safety is a novel design choice in this work.
- **Break condition:** If the adaptive weight `w_vis` does not correlate with attack type (e.g., high for image-based attacks, low for text-only) in a controlled validation set, the fusion heuristic is mis-calibrated.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Causal Mediation Analysis (CMA)
  - **Why needed here:** To *diagnose* which model components (layers, neurons) are *causally* involved in generating unsafe outputs, not just correlated. This enables precise, surgical intervention.
  - **Quick check question:** If blocking layer N increases ASR, what does that imply about the causal role of layer N, and what is the key counterfactual being compared?
- **Concept:** Generalised Eigenvalue Decomposition (GEVD)
  - **Why needed here:** To find projection directions (`U_k`) that maximise the variance of malicious activations *relative to* benign variance, rather than just on malicious data alone. This shapes a subspace that is specifically *harmful*.
  - **Quick check question:** Why not use PCA on malicious activations alone? What does the `C_b` matrix in `C_m u = λ C_b u` provide?
- **Concept:** Radial Basis Function (RBF) Kernel for Token Attribution
  - **Why needed here:** To compute nonlinear, cross-modal (image-text) and self-modal (text-text) relevance scores for individual tokens, identifying which parts of the input are most responsible for triggering unsafe pathways.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is kernel centering (`H`) necessary before computing token importance scores from the RBF kernel matrix?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  Input Processor: Receives image and text query.
  2.  Token Attribution Module: Extracts mid-layer activations for all visual/text tokens. Computes RBF-based importance scores (`MI_v`, `MI_t`) to select top-k most influential tokens per modality.
  3.  Subspace Learning (Offline): Using selected tokens from attack and benign datasets, computes `C_b` and `C_m`, solves GEVD to get `U_k` (malicious basis) and `P_safe`.
  4.  Inference-Time Projection: For a new input, extracts FFN activations at the critical layer for selected tokens. Applies `h' = P_safe h + β (I - P_safe) h_benign_anchor`.
  5.  Dual-Modal Fusion: Computes `w_vis` from deviation norms and fuses `h'_vis` and `h'_txt`.
  6.  Residual Stream Integration: Injects fused `h'` back into the model's residual stream for subsequent layers.
- **Critical path:** Token Attribution (Step 2) → Subspace Projection (Step 4) → Fusion (Step 5). The offline learning (Step 3) is a prerequisite.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Separation vs. Utility:** Larger `k` (subspace dimension) removes more harmful directions but risks removing useful semantic features. Projection strength `β` balances safety and utility.
  - **Modality Balance:** Adaptive fusion (`w_vis`) avoids manual tuning but assumes intervention magnitude is a valid signal. A poor `h_benign_anchor` can degrade general performance.
  - **Computational Cost:** Attribution and projection add overhead at inference, but it's training-free and only targets specific layers.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High ASR on specific attack types:** Indicates the corresponding modality's subspace was insufficiently learned or projection failed (check `C_m` separability).
  - **Significant drop on MMBench/MM-Vet:** Suggests over-projection (`k` too large, `β` too high) or poor `h_benign_anchor` diversity.
  - **Inconsistent layer performance:** If optimal intervention layers differ from those found in the initial causal tracing (Section 2.1), the localisation may be dataset-specific.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  Replicate Layer Localization: On a new model (e.g., InternVL), perform the layered blocking experiment from Section 2.1. Identify the "critical layer range" where ASR spikes. Validate that it is mid-layers.
  2.  Component Ablation: For the critical layers, ablate FFN and MHSA separately (as in Figure 4). Confirm FFN ablation causes a larger ASR increase, justifying the focus on FFN activations.
  3.  Modality-Specific Ablation: Run CARE with *only* visual or *only* textual projection (Table 2). Verify that image-based attacks (PGD) suffer more when visual projection is removed, and text-based jailbreaks suffer more when textual projection is removed. This confirms the need for dual-modal fusion.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can CARE be extended to enable adaptive multi-modal safety control and dynamic safety reasoning during inference?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion explicitly states: "In future, we plan to extend it toward adaptive multi-modal safety control and dynamic safety reasoning."
- **Why unresolved:** The current CARE framework employs a static safety subspace projection and a fixed hybrid fusion mechanism, which may not sufficiently adapt to varying input contexts or dynamically evolving jailbreak strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Developing a version of CARE that incorporates context-sensitive modulation of subspace projections or online updates based on input characteristics, followed by rigorous evaluation on benchmarks featuring adaptive and context-aware attacks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Under what conditions do visual versus textual safety subspaces contribute more critically to defense against different modalities of jailbreak attacks?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Ablation studies (Table 2) indicate that removing the textual subspace significantly degrades performance on text-driven jailbreaks (e.g., JailBreakV), while removing the visual subspace causes larger drops on image-led attacks (e.g., PGD). However, the quantitative interplay between modalities across attack types remains uncharacterized.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper confirms both modalities are necessary but does not dissect the relative contribution of each subspace under varying attack conditions or quantify cross-modal dependencies that might influence intervention efficacy.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic controlled experiments that isolate attack modalities (purely textual, purely visual, combined) and measure the marginal impact of each subspace's projection on attack success rates, possibly using interaction metrics or modality-specific attack simulations.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How sensitive is CARE's safety-utility trade-off to the choice of RBF kernel parameters (σ, ϵ) and the dimensionality of the safety subspace (k)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method sets σ as the scaled median of pairwise distances and ϵ as a small constant, and selects the top-256 eigenvectors without ablation. No sensitivity analysis on these hyperparameters is reported.
- **Why unresolved:** These parameters directly govern token attribution (RBF kernel) and the fidelity of the malicious subspace (eigen decomposition). Their stability across datasets, model scales, or attack types is unknown, potentially affecting robustness and general performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting a comprehensive hyperparameter sweep varying k (e.g., 64, 128, 256, 512) and σ (e.g., median, mean, quantile-based) across multiple models and benchmarks, measuring corresponding changes in ASR and utility scores (e.g., MMBench, MM-Vet).

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does CARE maintain defense efficacy against fundamentally novel jailbreak attack paradigms beyond perturbation-based and typographic jailbreaks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The abstract and Section 4.1 claim good transferability to "unseen attacks," but experiments are confined to JailbreakV, MMSafety, and PGD variants.
- **Why unresolved:** The term "unseen" likely refers to minor variations within similar attack families. The resilience of CARE to semantically distinct attacks (e.g., backdoor triggers, multilingual manipulations, or semantic misalignment attacks) remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluating CARE on a broader panel of recent jailbreak benchmarks (e.g., FigStep variants, cross-model transfer attacks, or culturally specific manipulation tactics) and reporting transfer success rates across diverse model architectures.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Is the adaptive fusion mechanism (weighted by intervention magnitude) optimal for integrating dual-modal corrections, or could alternative fusion strategies improve the safety-performance trade-off?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The fusion weight (Equation 18) is derived from the relative norm of activation changes and justified intuitively, but no comparative analysis with other fusion designs (e.g., learned, attention-based, or fixed ratios) is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** The assumption that larger intervention magnitude implies more reliable correction may not hold universally—e.g., one modality might be over-perturbed. Without ablation, the necessity and optimality of this specific fusion rule are unproven.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Designing alternative fusion modules (e.g., linear weighting with learnable parameters, gating networks, or confidence-based selection) and benchmarking them against the current approach on safety (ASR) and utility (MMBench, etc.) metrics to determine if superior trade-offs exist.

## Limitations
- **Layer/token selection rules** are model-specific and lack universal criteria; the 1/8 token ratio and layer ranges (e.g., layers 12–14 for Qwen2.5-VL) are chosen empirically without justification, limiting transferability.
- **Benign anchor definition** for projection regularization is ambiguous (per-sample vs. population mean) and its impact on utility preservation is not thoroughly analyzed.
- **Adaptive fusion heuristic** uses a novel weight formula without validation across diverse attack types; its calibration may be brittle.

## Confidence
- **High:** Mid-layer FFN neurons are causally implicated. Supported by the paper’s layer-blocking and FFN/MHSA ablation (ASR spikes when blocking mid-layers/FFNs).
- **Medium:** Malicious vs. benign activations occupy separable subspaces via GEVD. Supported by clustering metrics peaking at mid-layers, but external validation of subspace stability is absent.
- **Low:** Adaptive dual-modal fusion reliably balances safety and semantics. Limited to paper’s ablation; no analysis of weight distribution across attack types or correlation with ground-truth attack modality.

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate causal localization on a new LVLM:** Perform layer-blocking and FFN/MHSA ablation on a model like InternVL-Chat-V1.5 to validate that mid-layers (10–20) are critical and FFN ablation yields larger ASR increase than MHSA.
2. **Validate subspace separability via eigenvalue spectra:** Compute generalized eigenvalues `λ` from `C_m u = λ C_b u` on both paper’s datasets and a held-out set; ensure top-256 eigenvalues are significantly >1 (e.g., >5× median) to confirm a well-defined malicious subspace.
3. **Test adaptive fusion calibration under controlled attack types:** Using a dataset with known attack modality labels (image-based PGD, text-only prompts, multimodal implicit jailbreaks), record `w_vis` distribution per type; verify `w_vis` is statistically higher for image-based attacks and lower for text-only attacks.
