# WorldArena: A Unified Benchmark for Evaluating Perception and Functional Utility of Embodied World Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08971  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08971  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** WorldArena introduces a unified benchmark for embodied world models, revealing a significant perception–functionality gap: high video quality does not guarantee strong downstream task performance. The proposed EWMScore correlates strongly with human judgment (Pearson *r*=0.825) but only moderately/weakly with functional metrics.

## Executive Summary
WorldArena addresses the fragmented evaluation of embodied world models (EWMs) by proposing a unified benchmark that measures both perceptual video quality and functional utility. It evaluates models across three dimensions: (1) video perception quality using 16 metrics spanning six sub-dimensions (visual, motion, content consistency, physics adherence, 3D accuracy, controllability); (2) embodied task functionality through three roles—data engine, policy evaluator, and action planner; and (3) human subjective evaluation. An integrated metric, EWMScore, averages all 16 normalized metrics into a single index. Benchmarking 14 diverse models uncovers a notable perception–functionality gap: models with superior perceptual scores often underperform in functional tasks. EWMScore aligns well with human evaluation (*r*=0.825) but shows only moderate correlation with data engine performance (*r*=0.600) and weak correlation with action planning (*r*=0.360). This highlights the need for evaluation that goes beyond visual fidelity to assess real-world utility.

## Method Summary
The WorldArena benchmark uses the RoboTwin 2.0 dataset, which contains 2500 videos (2000 training, 500 test) covering 50 bimanual manipulation tasks. For each model, the evaluation proceeds in two main phases. First, video quality is measured automatically via 16 metrics, grouped into six sub-dimensions: visual quality (Image Quality, Aesthetic Quality, JEPA Similarity), motion quality (Dynamic Degree, Flow Score, Motion Smoothness), content consistency (Subject/Background/Photometric Consistency), physics adherence (Interaction Quality, Trajectory Accuracy), 3D accuracy (Depth Accuracy, Perspectivity), and controllability (Instruction Following, Semantic Alignment, Action Following). All metrics are normalized using the 1st and 99th percentiles across the test set, then averaged to produce the EWMScore (scaled to 0–100). Second, functional utility is assessed through three closed-loop interaction protocols: (1) Data Engine: synthetic videos are generated, pseudo-actions extracted via an inverse dynamics model (IDM), and a downstream policy (π0.5) is trained and evaluated on real RoboTwin; (2) Policy Evaluator: pre-trained policies with varying performance interact with the world model, and success rates are compared against real-simulator results; (3) Action Planner: the world model (paired with IDM) directly outputs action sequences that are executed in RoboTwin, assessing end-to-end success. Human evaluation with 70 annotators provides reference scores for overall quality, instruction following, and physical adherence.

## Key Results
- **Perception–functionality gap:** High EWMScore does not guarantee strong functional performance; e.g., Veo 3.1 achieves top visual quality but lags in data engine and action planning.
- **EWMScore–human correlation:** The unified metric shows a strong Pearson correlation of 0.825 with human judgments, validating its ability to capture perceived quality.
- **Role-specific variation:** Models exhibit different strengths across functional roles (e.g., CtrlWorld excels as policy evaluator, RoboMaster as data engine), underscoring the need for multi-faceted functional evaluation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
WorldArena’s effectiveness stems from three key design choices:

- **Comprehensive video quality assessment:** By measuring 16 metrics across six sub-dimensions (visual, motion, content consistency, physics adherence, 3D accuracy, controllability), the benchmark captures perceptual fidelity more holistically than single-metric evaluations. This prevents models from excelling in one aspect (e.g., sharpness) while failing others (e.g., physics). *Evidence:* Metric definitions are explicit (Section 3.1) and include differentiable measures like Dynamic Degree and Trajectory Accuracy; neighboring benchmarks (EWMBench, WorldScore) use fewer dimensions, highlighting novelty.
- **Functional utility evaluation through three roles:** Testing world models as data engines, policy evaluators, and action planners directly measures their usefulness for embodied decision-making. Each role probes a distinct closed-loop interaction pattern, ensuring functional capability is not conflated with open-loop video generation. *Evidence:* Experimental setups (Section 3.2) show varied performance across roles (e.g., CtrlWorld strong as policy evaluator, RoboMaster strong as data engine).
- **EWMScore as an integrated, human-correlated metric:** The arithmetic mean of all 16 normalized metrics yields a single index that strongly correlates with human judgment (*r*=0.825), enabling scalable automated benchmarking while capturing perceived quality. *Evidence:* Correlation analysis (Section 4.3) validates against 70 human annotators.

Together, these mechanisms ensure that benchmark results reflect both perceptual quality and functional relevance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Embodied World Model (EWM)**  
  *Why needed:* Distinguishes models that must capture action-conditioned physical dynamics for robotic tasks from general video generators. Core to understanding why standard video benchmarks (e.g., VBench) are insufficient.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key architectural difference between a text-to-video model (e.g., Wan 2.6) and an embodied world model (e.g., CtrlWorld) in terms of input conditions and evaluation goals?

- **Concept: Perception–Functionality Gap**  
  *Why needed:* The paper’s central finding. High scores on visual metrics do not guarantee success on downstream embodied tasks. This gap is the primary motivation for the benchmark.  
  *Quick check:* If Model A has a higher EWMScore than Model B, can you guarantee Model A’s policies will learn faster from its synthetic data? Why or why not?

- **Concept: Closed-Loop vs. Open-Loop Evaluation**  
  *Why needed:* WorldArena’s functional tasks require closed-loop interaction (actions affect future states), whereas traditional video generation is open-loop (single prompt → video). This distinction is critical for the “embodied” requirement.  
  *Quick check:* In the “Action Planner” task, why is executing the predicted action sequence in the RoboTwin simulator a closed-loop test, while generating a video from an instruction is open-loop?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
RoboTwin 2.0 dataset -> Model inference -> Video generation -> Video Quality Module (16 metric calculators) -> EWMScore Integrator -> Functional Task Module (Data Engine, Policy Evaluator, Action Planner) -> Success rates -> Correlation Analyzer -> Leaderboard

**Critical path:**  
Model inference on RoboTwin test set generates open-loop videos; these are scored by the 16 perceptual metrics and aggregated into EWMScore. Separately, the videos are fed into three functional pipelines that close the loop: (1) synthetic data generation and downstream policy training; (2) policy rollouts in the world model; (3) action planning with an IDM and execution in RoboTwin. Functional success rates are correlated with EWMScore and human scores.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Metric comprehensiveness vs. computational cost:* 16 metrics require multiple heavy models (VLM, depth estimator, optical flow), trading speed for diagnostic granularity.  
- *Automation vs. human judgment:* Human evaluation (70 annotators) is expensive but captures nuanced qualities (e.g., physical plausibility); EWMScore aims to replace it but correlates at *r*=0.825.  
- *Task diversity vs. depth:* 50 bimanual tasks cover diversity but may not probe all robotic domains (e.g., locomotion), limiting generalizability.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High EWMScore + low functional task success → classic perception–functionality gap (e.g., Veo 3.1).  
- Low `Trajectory Accuracy` + high `Motion Smoothness` → model makes smooth but incorrect motions.  
- High `Instruction Following` + low `Interaction Quality` → model follows text but generates physically impossible interactions.  
- Human correlation strong but action planner correlation weak (*r*=0.360) → perceptual metrics may optimize for “human-looking” videos, not “robot-useful” ones.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Reproduce Video Metrics Pipeline:** Run a single model (e.g., CogvideoX) on one RoboTwin video through all 16 metric calculators. Verify outputs are in [0,1] and sanity-check with examples from Appendix C.  
2. **Validate Functional Task 1 (Data Engine):** Fine-tune one world model (WoW) on RoboTwin training set, generate 25 synthetic trajectories for a task, extract pseudo-actions with IDM, train π0.5 policy, and evaluate success rate on real RoboTwin. Compare to baseline (25% real data).

If not, check normalization steps (Appendix A.17).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** What underlying factors cause the perception–functionality gap in embodied world models, and how can architectures or training be modified to close it?  
  *Basis:* The paper empirically demonstrates a significant gap (Abstract, Sec. 4.3) but does not investigate root causes (e.g., missing physical priors, misaligned objectives).  
  *Unresolved:* Without understanding these, progress toward functionally reliable world models remains hindered.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Ablations linking each of the 16 metrics to downstream performance; architectural interventions (e.g., explicit dynamics modeling); joint optimization of perceptual and functional losses.

- **Question 2:** Why does EWMScore strongly align with human judgment (*r*=0.825) yet poorly predict action planning success (*r*=0.360), and how can the metric be refined?  
  *Basis:* The weak correlation with action planning suggests EWMScore overlooks control-relevant features (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 5).  
  *Unresolved:* The linear aggregation of perceptual scores may not reflect what matters for planning.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Identify which EWMScore components correlate with planning; redesign EWMScore with learned weights or functional proxies; validate across more models/tasks.

- **Question 3:** How can WorldArena be expanded to assess a wider variety of world models and embodied scenarios beyond bimanual manipulation, to ensure generalizable insights?  
  *Basis:* The current domain (RoboTwin 2.0) covers only manipulation; most benchmarks evaluate fewer than ten models (Introduction).  
  *Unresolved:* Findings may not generalize to other robotics domains (e.g., locomotion, navigation).  
  *Resolution evidence:* Incorporate additional simulators (Habitat, MuJoCo); curate datasets for varied robots (humanoids, drones); evaluate >50 models across a broader task taxonomy; analyze cross-domain performance patterns.

## Limitations
- **Simulator and model access:** RoboTwin 2.0 simulator and exact metric model weights (e.g., V-JEPA, SAM3) are not publicly released, blocking full replication.  
- **Normalization sensitivity:** EWMScore relies on 1st/99th percentiles computed on the authors’ private 14-model video set; different corpora shift scores.  
- **Underspecified functional pipelines:** Details of the inverse dynamics model and π0.5 policy training (hyperparameters, data splits) are lacking, hindering independent verification of functional results.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (comprehensive video quality assessment): **Medium** – Metric definitions are explicit, but validity depends on correct implementation of external models and normalization.  
- Mechanism 2 (functional role evaluations): **Low** – Reproducibility obstructed by unavailable simulator/IDM, making independent verification of the perception–functionality gap infeasible.  
- Mechanism 3 (EWMScore–human correlation): **Medium** – Reported *r*=0.825 is strong but based on *n*=14 models; small sample and normalization sensitivity reduce confidence.
