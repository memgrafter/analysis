# Toward Trustworthy Evaluation of Sustainability Rating Methodologies: A Human-AI Collaborative Framework for Benchmark Dataset Construction
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.17106
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17106
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposes a human-AI framework (STRIDE trust scoring + SR-Delta discrepancy analysis) to construct trustworthy ESG benchmark datasets and evaluate rating methodologies, demonstrated with a case study identifying specific score divergences.

## Executive Summary
Widespread divergence in ESG/sustainability ratings for the same company undermines their comparability and decision-making utility. This paper introduces a human-AI collaborative framework to address this problem. STRIDE is a trust-scoring system that guides the construction of firm-level benchmark datasets using LLMs, grounded in principles of credibility, reliability, intimacy, and self-purpose. SR-Delta is a discrepancy analysis procedure that applies STRIDE-guided data to evaluate existing rating methodologies. A case study on Luxshare Precision yielded a moderate STRIDE trust score (τ=0.56) and revealed two concrete discrepancies: an MSCI executive pay penalty not supported by STRIDE's disclosure interpretation, and an overestimated chemical safety score relative to evidence-based assessment.

## Method Summary
The framework combines dataset construction with evaluation. First, a STRIDE-guided benchmark dataset is built for a target company by ingesting sustainability reports and standards, generating a consolidated metric list (global to firm-specific), and using LLMs for extraction with domain-expert validation and human oversight logging. Second, the STRIDE trust score τ is computed from component scores (Credibility, Reliability, Intimacy, minus Self-Served Purpose) using a sigmoid-weighted formula. Third, SR-Delta applies the same rating logic (e.g., MSCI's methodology) to both the STRIDE dataset and the agency's proprietary data, comparing outputs to identify and quantify discrepancies. The process is iterative, with human feedback incorporated via defined intimacy metrics.

## Key Results
- STRIDE trust score for Luxshare Precision benchmark dataset: τ=0.56 (moderate trust).
- SR-Delta identified an executive pay score discrepancy: MSCI imposed a -1.2 penalty, while STRIDE found no penalty based on disclosure interpretation.
- SR-Delta identified a chemical safety scoring discrepancy: MSCI scored 6.1, whereas STRIDE evidence suggested a more appropriate range of 5–5.5.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Quantifiable Trust Score**
STRIDE computes a composite trust score τ(x) as a sigmoid-weighted sum of four principles (Credibility, Reliability, Intimacy, minus Self-Served Purpose), each composed of sub-metrics measuring data quality, model rigor, human-AI collaboration depth, and transparency. This quantifies benchmark dataset credibility, enabling standardized evaluation of ESG rating methodologies. The core assumption is that structured human-AI collaboration yields more trustworthy datasets in ambiguous domains like sustainability. Evidence includes the formal mathematical formulation (Eq. 6-28) and the case study application.

**Mechanism 2: Discrepancy Analysis via Controlled Comparison**
SR-Delta isolates data construction as the variable by applying a fixed rating methodology to two data pipelines: the agency's proprietary data and the STRIDE-constructed benchmark. Output differences are attributed to data inputs and interpretation, not algorithmic changes. This surfaces specific biases or flaws, as shown in the Luxshare case with executive pay and chemical safety. The mechanism assumes rating divergence primarily stems from inconsistent data rather than fundamentally different philosophies.

**Mechanism 3: Intimacy Metrics for Context-Sensitive Oversight**
Intimacy (I) is operationalized through metrics: Human-Governed (HG), Domain-Expert-in-the-loop (DE), and Iterative Feedback (IF). These quantify the extent and structure of human oversight, ensuring context-sensitive interpretation while retaining LLM scalability. The assumption is that sustainability data interpretation is normative and context-dependent, requiring human judgment. Evidence includes definitions (Section 3.3) and case study values (e.g., HG≈0.106, DE≈0.53).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Data ingestion -> Metric consolidation engine -> Extraction & scoring agents -> Human oversight layer -> STRIDE scoring engine -> SR-Delta comparator -> Provenance tracker

**Critical path**: 1. Report acquisition 2. Metric list generation (global→firm-specific) 3. Sample selection 4. Ground-truth annotation 5. LLM-based value extraction 6. Human intervention/validation 7. Compute STRIDE component scores 8. Calculate τ 9. Input both datasets into target rating methodology 10. SR-Delta discrepancy analysis 11. Generate adjustment insights

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Automation vs. human oversight: More human involvement boosts intimacy/credibility but reduces scalability (captured in HG/DE metrics).
- Generalizability vs. specificity: STRIDE aims for universality, but metric selection must balance standard layers with firm-specificity (IM).
- Static vs. dynamic: Benchmarks decay; agility (AG) rewards improvement but may compromise comparability.

**Failure signatures**:
- τ score <0.4 (low trust indicator).
- Extremely high human intervention rate (HG→1) suggests unreliable automation.
- Large variance in SR-Delta outputs across similar firms indicates inconsistent STRIDE implementation.
- Missing disclosed assumptions (T(x)=0) or lack of role separation (RS(x)=0) undermine trust.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Replication**: Apply the full pipeline to a second company in a different sector (e.g., an oil major) to test generalizability.
2. **Sensitivity analysis**: Vary STRIDE weight parameters (α, w) and observe impact on τ and SR-Delta outputs.
3. **Agency comparison**: Run SR-Delta against multiple rating agencies (e.g., MSCI, Sustainalytics, S&P) for the same STRIDE dataset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can the STRIDE–SR-Delta framework be empirically validated at scale across diverse companies, industries, rating agencies, and regulatory environments to assess its generalizability and robustness?
- How should the STRIDE framework’s discrete (binary) parameterizations—such as for inclusivity of external datasets or the applicability of audit trails—be replaced with continuous, graded measures to more accurately capture nuanced variations in data quality and process rigor?
- What principled methodology can be established for assigning the relative weights (e.g., \(w_C^{IM}\), \(w_R^{GT}\)) within the STRIDE trust equation to reflect the domain-specific importance of different credibility, reliability, intimacy, and self-purpose components?
- What principled thresholding rules should be defined for the STRIDE trust score \(\tau(x)\) to differentiate levels of dataset readiness (e.g., “fit for purpose,” “requires augmentation,” “unfit”) and to guide the SR-Delta discrepancy analysis?

## Limitations
- Parameter calibration: STRIDE’s weighting coefficients (α, w) are unspecified; without empirical stakeholder consensus, τ may not reliably measure dataset fitness.
- Sub-metric ambiguity: Definitions like Auditable/Traceable (AT) and Transparency (T) are binary or vaguely applied, risking inconsistent scoring.
- Weak corpus support: Neighboring papers focus on ESG extraction, not trust scoring or discrepancy analysis; low citation count suggests lack of external validation.

## Confidence
- **STRIDE trust score mechanism**: Medium (precise formulation but untested weights and weak literature basis limit generalizability).
- **SR-Delta discrepancy detection**: Medium (conditional; case study shows concrete divergences but hinges on moderate τ=0.56).
- **Intimacy (human-AI) metrics**: Low (operationalization is novel but unvalidated for ESG contexts; risk of over/under-quantifying human input).

## Next Checks
