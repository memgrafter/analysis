# HomeAdam: Adam and AdamW Algorithms Sometimes Go Home to Obtain Better Provable Generalization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.02649
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02649
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: HomeAdam(W) provably achieves O(1/N) generalization error (matching SGD) and empirically outperforms Adam/AdamW on vision and language tasks.

## Executive Summary
Adam and AdamW are known to exhibit worse provable generalization (O(1/√N)) than SGD (O(1/N)), a gap linked to overly large adaptive steps when the second-order momentum is small. This paper introduces HomeAdam and HomeAdamW, which address this by conditionally reverting to momentum‑SGD updates whenever the per‑coordinate adaptive step would exceed a threshold τ. Theoretically, HomeAdam(W) attains an O(1/N) generalization bound and a convergence rate of O(1/T^{1/4}), improving upon prior Adam‑type analyses. Experiments across CIFAR‑10, Tiny‑ImageNet, and WikiText‑2/103 confirm consistent gains in test accuracy and perplexity over Adam, AdamW, SWATS, AdaBelief, and MIAdam.

## Method Summary
HomeAdam(W) modifies the Adam(W)-srf algorithm. It maintains the usual first (m_t) and second (v_t) moment estimates and applies bias correction to obtain ˆv_t = v_t/(1-β2^t). The key innovation is a threshold check on the minimum coordinate of ˆv_t: if min_j(ˆv_t)_j ≥ τ, the standard adaptive update (using m_t and v_t, with optional weight decay) is performed; otherwise, the optimizer switches to a momentum‑SGD update that uses m_t with a fixed step size and weight decay. This simple “going home” mechanism prevents excessively large steps when v_t is small, which is the root cause of Adam’s poorer generalization guarantees. The full algorithm is given as Algorithm 2 in the paper.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical**: HomeAdam(W) achieves generalization error O(1/N) (matching SGD) while Adam/AdamW only achieve O(1/√N). Its convergence rate is O(1/T^{1/4}), improving over Adam(W)-srf’s O(1/(ρ+ε)^{2T} N) bound.
- **Empirical**: On CIFAR‑10, Tiny‑ImageNet, and WikiText‑2/103, HomeAdam(W) yields higher test accuracy (vision) and lower perplexity (language) compared to Adam, AdamW, SWATS, AdaBelief, and MIAdam.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Adam’s per‑coordinate adaptation can produce huge steps when the estimated second moment v_t becomes very small for some coordinates, because the effective learning rate scales as η/(√(v_t)+ε) (or η/(v_t+ε) in the sqrt‑free variant). Such large steps destabilize training and harm generalization. HomeAdam(W) monitors the minimum of the bias‑corrected second moment ˆv_t across all coordinates. Whenever any coordinate’s ˆv_t falls below τ, the algorithm temporarily abandons adaptation and uses a uniform step based on momentum (SGDM). This “going home” to an SGD‑like update prevents the dangerous overshooting that underlies Adam’s worse O(1/√N) bound. The threshold τ (≥1 in theory) controls how readily the switch occurs; by ensuring adaptation only when all second moments are sufficiently large, the algorithm retains the stability of SGD while preserving adaptivity when safe. This mechanism is shown theoretically to limit the influence of small v_t, yielding the improved O(1/N) guarantee.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Gradient g_t → m_t = β1 m_{t-1} + (1-β1) g_t, v_t = β2 v_{t-1} + (1-β2) g_t^2 → bias correction: ˆv_t = v_t/(1-β2^t) → threshold check: if min_j(ˆv_t)_j ≥ τ then adaptive update: x ← x - η (m_t/(v_t+ε) + λ x) else SGDM update: x ← x - η (m_t + λ x).

**Critical Path**:  
The algorithm’s main loop consists of: (1) computing the gradient, (2) updating the biased moments m_t and v_t, (3) computing bias‑corrected ˆv_t, (4) evaluating the switching condition on min_j(ˆv_t)_j, and (5) applying either the adaptive or SGDM update. The threshold check is the pivotal decision point that governs the “going home” behavior.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- **Threshold τ**: Larger τ forces more frequent switches to SGDM, potentially improving generalization but losing adaptivity; smaller τ retains more Adam‑like behavior. Theory requires τ ≥ 1, but empirical choice is not fully resolved.
- **ε value**: Adds stability to division by v_t+ε in the adaptive branch. Too small risks numerical explosions; too large may overly dampen steps.
- **Weight decay handling**: Must be included in both branches; forgetting λ in the SGDM branch is a common pitfall.
- **Computational overhead**: The min reduction over coordinates is O(d) and negligible compared to gradient computation, but still adds a small cost.

**Failure Signatures**:
- **No switching**: Always taking the adaptive branch suggests the condition uses the wrong statistic (e.g., max instead of min) or τ is set too low relative to typical ˆv_t scales.
- **Numerical spikes**: Sudden loss explosions indicate division by near‑zero in the adaptive branch; verify ε is applied and v_t is not underflowing.
- **Performance worse than Adam**: Could be due to τ being too high (excessive SGDM) or too low (insufficient switching); perform a τ sweep.
- **Baseline mismatch**: Ensure Adam/AdamW baselines use identical hyperparameters (β1, β2, ε, weight decay) except for the switching logic.

**First Experiments**:
1. **Adam baseline**: Train ResNet34 on CIFAR‑10 with Adam (η=0.001, β1=0.9, β2=0.99, ε=1e-8, weight decay=0) for 200 epochs; record test accuracy.
2. **HomeAdam (τ=1)**: Same as (1) but with HomeAdam (τ=1, other hyperparameters identical). Expect comparable or slightly better test accuracy.
3. **HomeAdamW (τ=1)**: Same as (1) but with HomeAdamW (τ=1, weight decay λ=0.05). Compare against an AdamW baseline (same hyperparameters with λ=0.05). Expect HomeAdamW to match or exceed AdamW.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions or future work. However, its limitations suggest several avenues for further research:

- Determining the optimal threshold τ for different model architectures and datasets.
- Clarifying which variant of HomeAdam(W) (full‑vector or element‑wise) was used in the experiments and ensuring consistent hyperparameter reporting.
- Extending the theoretical analysis to more realistic settings where assumptions like bounded gradients and smoothness may not hold.
- Investigating the sensitivity of HomeAdam(W) to the choice of ε and other hyperparameters across a wider range of tasks.
- Exploring the computational overhead of the min reduction in very high‑dimensional settings and potential optimization strategies.

## Limitations
- The exact threshold τ values used in the paper’s experiments are not disclosed; only τ ≥ 1 is stated theoretically.
- The element‑wise variant HomeAdam(W)-ew mentioned in Appendix F is not clearly distinguished in the main experiments, leaving ambiguity about which version was used.
- Hyperparameters for HomeAdam(W) (especially ε) are inconsistently documented across tasks.
- Training duration (epochs/iterations) for the reported results is not explicitly stated, hindering precise replication.
- Theoretical guarantees rely on assumptions (bounded gradients, smoothness, β2 close to 1) that may not fully hold in deep neural network training, limiting the strict applicability of the O(1/N) bound.
