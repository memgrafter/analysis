# Eye image segmentation using visual and concept prompts with Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM3)

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17715
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17715
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Primary result**: SAM2 outperformed SAM3 in accuracy, noise robustness, and inference speed for zero‑shot eye segmentation; SAM3's concept prompting provided no benefit.

## Executive Summary
This study compares the Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM2) and 3 (SAM3) for zero‑shot segmentation of eye anatomical features using visual prompts (spatial points/boxes) and, for SAM3, concept prompts (text). Experiments were conducted on high‑frame‑rate laboratory videos (FLEX dataset) and the in‑the‑wild TEyeD dataset. SAM2 with visual prompts produced cleaner signals (lower RMS‑S2S precision) and less data loss than SAM3 with visual prompts on the lab data. On TEyeD, SAM2 achieved higher mean Intersection‑over‑Union (mIoU) for the pupil, iris, and whole eye opening, while SAM3’s visual prompts yielded higher sclera mIoU but with a consistently higher false alarm rate. SAM3’s concept prompts failed for “iris” and “sclera” and performed worst overall for “pupil”. SAM2 also demonstrated superior inference speed. The authors conclude that SAM2 remains the most effective and efficient model for eye image segmentation, and that SAM3’s new concept‑prompting capability does not provide an advantage in this domain.

## Method Summary
The authors perform a zero‑shot evaluation of SAM2 and SAM3 for segmenting the pupil, iris, sclera, and corneal reflection in eye images. No model fine‑tuning is applied. Visual prompting—providing spatial points or boxes—is used with both models. For SAM3, concept prompting via textual labels (“pupil”, “iris”, “sclera”) is additionally tested. Laboratory experiments use high‑resolution (1000 Hz) FLEX videos; visual prompts are manually refined on a per‑participant basis to achieve accurate segmentations. The in‑the‑wild TEyeD dataset employs an automated script that places prompts based on ground‑truth ellipse annotations to ensure consistent prompting. On the lab data, segmentation quality is assessed by deriving positional signals (e.g., pupil center) and computing RMS‑S2S precision and data loss. On TEyeD, standard region‑based metrics (mIoU, false alarm rate, miss rate, Youden’s J) are computed against the provided ground truth. The study also measures inference speed on various hardware setups.

## Key Results
- On FLEX lab videos, SAM2 with visual prompts achieved lower RMS‑S2S precision (less noisy signals) and lower data loss than SAM3 with visual prompts.
- On TEyeD, SAM2 attained higher mIoU for pupil, iris, and whole eye opening, while SAM3 visual prompts had higher sclera mIoU but a consistently higher false alarm rate for all features.
- SAM3 with concept prompts performed worst overall, and it completely failed to provide segmentations for “iris” and “sclera”. SAM2 also demonstrated superior inference speed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: SAM2's specialized architecture yields superior segmentation precision for eye features compared to SAM3.
- **Mechanism**: SAM2 uses HieraDet encoder trained specifically for spatial prompt segmentation, while SAM3's Perception Encoder is multitask‑trained for concept understanding, diluting its boundary precision for eye anatomies.
- **Core assumption**: Vision‑only fine‑grained segmentation benefits more from specialization than multimodal generality.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract] "SAM2 achieved lower RMS‑S2S precision... SAM3 with visual prompts had higher sclera mIoU but a consistently higher false alarm rate"
  - [section 3] "SAM 2 outperformed SAM 3 in terms of mIoU for the pupil, iris and entire eye opening... SAM 3 outperforms SAM 2 in terms of miss rate, [but] coupled with a very high false alarm rate"
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers discuss SAM3's broader capabilities but not eye‑specific performance.
- **Break condition**: If SAM3 is fine‑tuned on eye segmentation data, the multitask advantage may overcome specialization.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Concept prompting fails for eye segmentation because SAM3's textual vocabulary lacks anatomical grounding.
- **Mechanism**: SAM3's concept vocabulary was trained on general image corpora (e.g., COCO) and does not include fine‑grained anatomical terms like "pupil" or "iris" as distinct, segmentable concepts; it may map them to broader categories.
- **Core assumption**: Zero‑shot concept transfer requires the concept to be represented in the model's pretraining distribution.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [section 2.2.2] "no segmentation was returned for the iris and sclera... only results for 'pupil' are reported"
  - [section 4] "SAM 3's inferior zero‑shot performance, and complete inability to provide a segmentation given the concept prompts 'iris' and 'sclera'"
  - [corpus] Neighbor "The SAM2‑to‑SAM3 Gap..." explicitly discusses prompt‑based expertise not transferring to concept‑driven paradigm.
- **Break condition**: If eye‑specific concepts are injected via fine‑tuning or prompt engineering.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: SAM3's computational inefficiency limits practical deployment despite architectural advances.
- **Mechanism**: SAM3's unified multimodal architecture (shared backbone, DETR detector, dense‑memory tracker) introduces overhead; Perception Encoder may have higher memory bandwidth requirements, causing throughput drops during long video sequences.
- **Core assumption**: Architectural generality trades off against runtime efficiency.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [section 2.2] "SAM 3... ran at 13 fps on system (A)... slowed down to 0.5 fps for longer (e.g. 70000 frames) videos"
  - [section 4] "SAM 2 not only performed better but was also faster"
  - [corpus] Neighbor "EfficientSAM3... remains prohibitive for on‑device use" confirms efficiency challenges.
- **Break condition**: If distillation (as in EfficientSAM3) reduces computational footprint without accuracy loss.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Image Encoder (SAM2: HieraDet; SAM3: Perception Encoder) -> Prompt Encoder (visual or textual) -> Mask Decoder -> Memory Module (for video) -> Post‑processing

### Critical path
1. Preprocess frame, select prompt coordinates/text
2. Encode image with backbone, extract multi‑scale features
3. Encode prompts into embedding space
4. Decode mask via iterative refinement using attention
5. For video: store object memories, use in next frame
6. Post‑process masks: largest contour (pupil/CR), ellipse fitting (iris), morphological cleanup

### Design tradeoffs
- **SAM2**: Optimized for spatial prompts → faster, cleaner boundaries, but manual prompting required.
- **SAM3**: Adds concept prompting → potential for automation, but loses precision, fails on fine anatomy terms, slower.
- **Prompt strategy**: Refined (many prompts, negatives) vs minimal (fully scripted) trades off accuracy vs automation.

### Failure signatures
- Concept prompt returns zero masks → concept not in vocabulary (likely "iris", "sclera").
- Multiple masks for "pupil" → ambiguous concept; requires tracking logic to select correct blob.
- High false alarm rate on sclera → model includes iris in sclera mask despite negative prompts.
- Throughput collapse on long videos → memory mechanism inefficient in SAM3.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Lab dataset validation**: Use refined visual prompts on high‑quality 1000 Hz videos, compute RMS‑S2S precision and data loss for CR/pupil/iris/sclera signals to quantify noise.
2. **In‑the‑wild generalization**: Apply minimal visual prompting script to TEyeD datasets, compute mIoU, false/miss rates, Youden's J against ground truth ellipses/polygons.
3. **Concept prompt stress test**: Test "pupil", "iris", "sclera" on subset; note failures; implement blob selection/tracking to salvage pupil results; compare against visual prompts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Fine‑tuning for anatomical concepts** – SAM3’s zero‑shot failure to segment “iris” and “sclera” might be resolved by fine‑tuning on eye images with ground‑truth masks. No fine‑tuning experiments were conducted. Resolution would require fine‑tuning SAM3 on a dataset like TEyeD and re‑evaluating accuracy.
2. **Distillation for efficiency** – SAM3’s computational overhead, especially on long videos, is a bottleneck. Ongoing distillation efforts (e.g., EfficientSAM3) may alleviate this but were not tested. Evidence would come from applying distillation to SAM3 and measuring speed/accuracy trade‑offs on eye data.
3. **Complex prompting for specificity** – SAM3 currently only accepts simple noun phrases; it cannot handle multi‑attribute instructions such as “left eye pupil”. Adaptations that enable more complex prompts could reduce manual effort. Testing would involve extending the prompt encoder and evaluating segmentation quality with richer instructions.

## Limitations
- FLEX lab dataset not publicly available, hindering reproducibility of the high‑frame‑rate experiments.
- Manual visual‑prompt refinement procedure for the lab data is undocumented, making exact replication difficult.
- SAM3’s concept prompts fail for “iris” and “sclera” in zero‑shot settings; the exact cause (vocabulary vs. architecture) is unclear, and its long‑video performance degradation lacks a mechanistic explanation.

## Confidence
- **SAM2 precision & efficiency superiority**: High
- **Concept prompt failure**: High
- **Multitask efficiency trade‑off**: Medium
