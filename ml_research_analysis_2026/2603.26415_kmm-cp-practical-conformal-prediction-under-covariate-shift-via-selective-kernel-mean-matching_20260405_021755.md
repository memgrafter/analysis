# KMM-CP: Practical Conformal Prediction under Covariate Shift via Selective Kernel Mean Matching

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26415
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26415
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** KMM-CP reduces coverage gap by over 50% and lowers mean absolute deviation from nominal coverage by 55% on average, achieving best mean rank across datasets.

## Executive Summary
KMM-CP introduces a novel approach to conformal prediction under covariate shift by leveraging Kernel Mean Matching to compute bounded importance weights that align the calibration distribution with the test distribution. This avoids the instability of density-ratio estimation. A selective extension (SKMM) further restricts correction to test points with reliable support overlap via learned selection variables, boosting effective sample size. Theoretical analysis links coverage error to a bias-variance decomposition controlled by maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) and effective sample size (ESS), providing asymptotic guarantees under mild conditions. Empirical evaluation on five molecular property benchmarks with realistic structural shifts demonstrates that KMM-CP reduces the coverage gap by over 50% relative to classifier- and KDE-based baselines, lowers mean absolute deviation from nominal coverage by 55% on average, and attains the best mean rank across both global and class-conditional calibration settings.

## Method Summary
The method extends split conformal prediction to covariate shift by reweighting calibration samples using bounded importance weights derived via Kernel Mean Matching. Given labeled data split into training and calibration, and an unlabeled test set, a base predictor (AttentiveFP graph neural network) is trained on the training split. Embedding vectors from the penultimate layer serve as features. Standard KMM solves a convex optimization to match the kernel mean of the weighted calibration distribution to that of the test distribution, subject to weight bounds (B=30) and mass conservation. The selective variant (SKMM) introduces additional target selection variables α_j, solved via a double-weighted KMM objective with a yield constraint (τ=0.5) and thresholding (θ=0.2) to filter test points with low support overlap. After weight normalization, a weighted (1-α) quantile of calibration non-conformity scores is computed to construct prediction sets, with optional class-conditional (Mondrian) quantiles.

## Key Results
- KMM-CP reduces coverage gap by over 50% relative to classifier- and KDE-based baselines.
- It lowers mean absolute deviation (MAD) from nominal coverage by 55% on average across five molecular property benchmarks.
- It achieves the best mean rank across both global and class-conditional (Mondrian) calibration settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Bounded importance weights from KMM increase effective sample size (ESS), reducing variance in weighted quantile estimation and stabilizing coverage.
- **Mechanism:** KMM solves a constrained optimization (Eq. 5–6) that matches kernel means while enforcing \(0 \leq w_i \leq B\) and mass conservation. This prevents a few samples from dominating the weight distribution, unlike classifier-based ratios which can produce heavy tails under limited overlap.
- **Core assumption:** The RKHS kernel is characteristic, and the calibration sample size is large enough for the empirical MMD to converge.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "bounded importance weights, avoiding unstable density-ratio estimation"
  - [Section 3.2, Theorem 3]: Coverage error bound includes \(\sqrt{1/\text{ESS}}\); ESS is explicitly controlled by weight constraints.
  - [corpus]: Weak—neighbors discuss covariate shift but not KMM’s bounded weights (e.g., *Conformal Drug Property Prediction...* uses KDE).
- **Break condition:** If the weight bound \(B\) is set too high, extreme weights may still occur; if too low, bias increases due to under-matching.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Direct MMD minimization in an RKHS controls the bias term (distributional mismatch) in the coverage error without requiring explicit density-ratio estimation.
- **Mechanism:** KMM aligns the weighted calibration covariate distribution \(\hat{P}^w_S\) with the test distribution \(P_T\) by minimizing \(\text{MMD}(\hat{P}^w_S, P_T)\) in a characteristic RKHS. Theorem 2 shows this bounds the supremum difference between the population score CDFs \(F_T\) and \(F_w\).
- **Core assumption:** The conformity score function’s conditional CDF \(g_t(x)\) is sufficiently smooth (bounded RKHS norm and uniform approximation error).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Kernel Mean Matching to align calibration and test distributions"
  - [Section 3.1]: KMM objective (Eq. 5) directly minimizes MMD.
  - [Section 3.2, Theorem 2]: \(\sup_t |F_T(t) - F_w(t)| \leq B_H \text{MMD} + 2\epsilon_\infty\).
- **Break condition:** If the kernel is not characteristic or bandwidth is poorly chosen, MMD may not reflect true distributional discrepancy; also, high dimensionality can degrade MMD estimation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Selective KMM (SKMM) improves coverage in low-overlap regimes by restricting correction to test points with reliable support via learned selection variables \(\alpha_j\), thereby boosting ESS and reducing MMD.
- **Mechanism:** Joint optimization over source weights \(w_i\) and target selection \(\alpha_j\) (Eq. 7–8) encourages sparsity in \(\alpha_j\) (boundary values 0 or 1) via KKT conditions (Appendix B). This filters unsupported test points, concentrating moment matching on overlapping regions.
- **Core assumption:** The test points with \(\alpha_j \approx 1\) have adequate support in the calibration distribution; the yield constraint \(\tau\) is chosen so that enough test points remain.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "selective extension that identifies regions of reliable support overlap"
  - [Section 3.3]: Double-weighted KMM objective and yield constraint.
  - [Section 6.3, Figure 4]: Empirical \(\alpha\) distributions concentrate near 0 and 1.
- **Break condition:** If the overlap is extremely low (e.g., disjoint supports), even selective filtering may not salvage ESS; also, incorrect \(\tau\) can discard too many test points.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Data splitting:** Labeled data → training + calibration; unlabeled test set.
  2. **Model training:** Train base predictor on training split.
  3. **Conformity scoring:** Define non-conformity score \(s(x,y)\) (e.g., 1 – softmax probability).
  4. **Selective filter:** (Optional) Solve double-weighted KMM to get \(\alpha_j\); threshold to select test subset \(T_{\text{sel}}\).
  5. **KMM solver:** Solve standard KMM between calibration and \(T_{\text{sel}}\) to get weights \(w_i\).
  6. **Weight normalization:** Normalize \(w_i\) to sum to 1.
  7. **Weighted quantile:** Compute \(\hat{q}_{1-\alpha}\) as weighted \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of calibration scores.
  8. **Prediction set:** For each \(z \in T_{\text{sel}}\), \(\hat{C}(z) = \{ y : s(z,y) \leq \hat{q}_{1-\alpha} \}\).

- **Critical path:** Steps 1–3 must precede any weighting. Step 4 is conditional; if selective, it must precede KMM. Steps 5–7 are sequential; step 8 depends on step 7.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Kernel bandwidth:* Affects MMD; median-distance heuristic is robust but may need cross-validation in some domains.
  - *Weight bound \(B\):* Larger \(B\) reduces bias but risks low ESS; smaller \(B\) stabilizes weights but may under-correct. Paper sets \(B=30\).
  - *Selection fraction \(\tau\):* Larger \(\tau\) retains more test points but may include unsupported ones; smaller \(\tau\) increases conditional coverage reliability but reduces evaluated set size. Paper uses \(\tau=0.5\).
  - *Threshold \(\theta\) for \(\alpha_j\):* Paper uses threshold 0.2; this can be tuned if labeled validation is available.

- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Extremely low ESS** (e.g., < 10): Indicates weight collapse; coverage will be highly variable. Check support overlap and reduce \(B\) or use selective filtering.
  2. **High MMD** after reweighting: Indicates persistent distributional mismatch; consider kernel choice or representation refinement.
  3. **Selective filter removes nearly all test points:** Overly aggressive \(\tau\) or \(\theta\); adjust upward or improve feature space.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate global calibration on one dataset:** Run KMM-CP (non-selective) vs. uniform CP on Tox21; verify MAD reduction and calibration curve (Figure 2).
  2. **Ablate selective component:** Compare KMM vs. SKMM on a dataset with low overlap (BBB-Martins); check ESS and MMD terms (Figure 3).
  3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Vary \(B\) (e.g., {10, 30, 100}) and \(\tau\) (e.g., {0.3, 0.5, 0.7}) on AMES; measure coverage gap and filtration ratio (Table 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we automatically determine optimal hyperparameters (weight bound \(B\), selection fraction \(\tau\), threshold \(\theta\)) for KMM-CP across different datasets and shift intensities?
- **Basis:** [explicit] The paper uses fixed hyperparameters (\(B=30\), \(\tau=0.5\), \(\theta=0.2\)) and explicitly states that performance depends on them.
- **Why unresolved:** Without adaptive selection, the method may underperform or become unstable on data with varying covariate shift magnitude or dimensionality.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments using cross-validation or a held-out calibration set to tune hyperparameters on diverse molecular and non-molecular benchmarks, comparing coverage gaps and effective sample size against fixed values.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the KMM-CP framework be extended to handle more complex distribution shifts beyond covariate shift (e.g., concept shift or label shift)?
- **Basis:** [explicit] The paper acknowledges that real-world deployment may involve more complex distribution shifts, and covariate shift is only one special case.
- **Why unresolved:** The theory and method rely critically on the assumption that \(P(Y|X)\) remains invariant, which may not hold in many applications.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop new theoretical guarantees for weighted conformal prediction under other shift models, implement modifications (e.g., reweighting both \(X\) and \(Y\) or using doubly robust methods), and validate on synthetic and real datasets with known conditional shifts.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What coverage guarantees (if any) hold for test points discarded by the selective filtering stage of SKMM, which defers predictions in low-support regions?
- **Basis:** [explicit] The paper states that coverage applies conditionally only to the selected test instances, implying no formal guarantee for discarded points.
- **Why unresolved:** Deferring predictions on a subset may be undesirable in high-stakes settings where coverage is required for all instances; understanding the behavior of discarded points is necessary for risk management.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis of the selection mechanism’s impact on marginal coverage; empirical evaluation of what fraction of discarded points would be covered if included, and how to incorporate rejection options with statistical guarantees.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does KMM-CP generalize to non-molecular domains (e.g., vision, NLP) where data representations and shift patterns differ significantly?
- **Basis:** [explicit] The paper notes that generalization to other domains and data modalities remains to be validated, limiting the claimed scope.
- **Why unresolved:** The method’s reliance on kernel embeddings and learned representations might behave differently with high-dimensional, structured data (images, text) or under fundamentally different shift mechanisms.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the study on standard covariate shift benchmarks from computer vision (e.g., image corruption, style transfer) and natural language processing (e.g., topic shift), measuring coverage calibration against baselines and reporting computational efficiency.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter generality: Fixed hyperparameters (B=30, τ=0.5, θ=0.2) may not generalize across datasets and shift intensities, potentially underperforming or becoming unstable.
- Kernel MMD reliability: Theoretical assumptions (characteristic kernel, smooth CDF) may not hold for high-dimensional molecular embeddings, affecting MMD's ability to capture true distributional discrepancy.
- Selective filter justification: The ad-hoc threshold θ=0.2 for α_j balancing filtration vs. coverage is not systematically validated across varying shift severities.

## Confidence
- Main empirical claim (KMM-CP reduces MAD by ~55% vs. baselines): **High**
- Theoretical coverage bound (controlled by MMD and ESS): **Medium**
- Selective extension benefit (boosts ESS in low-overlap): **Medium**
