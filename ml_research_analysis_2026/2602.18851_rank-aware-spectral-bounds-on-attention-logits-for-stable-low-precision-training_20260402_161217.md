# Rank-Aware Spectral Bounds on Attention Logits for Stable Low-Precision Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.18851
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18851
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Predictive weight-based scaling eliminates FP8 overflow during training transients with negligible overhead and no accuracy loss.

## Executive Summary
Training transformers in FP8 precision risks numerical overflow due to large attention logits, especially during transient phases like checkpoint loading where history-based scaling fails. This paper derives a rank-aware concentration inequality that exploits the low-rank structure (rank \(d_h\)) of the query-key interaction matrix \(M = W^Q W^{K⊤}\), tightening the tail probability bound by a factor of \(d/(\gamma d_h)\). This enables a predictive, weight-only scale factor computation via implicit power iteration to estimate \(\|M\|_2\). Across models up to Llama-2-70B, the proposed geometry-aware scaling completely eliminates overflows in transient scenarios while matching the accuracy of delayed scaling and incurring only 1–4% overhead.

## Method Summary
The method replaces delayed scaling with per-layer scale factors computed from the spectral norm of \(W^Q W^{K⊤}\). The spectral norm \(\sigma\) is estimated by implicit power iteration on the factors, avoiding an explicit \(d \times d\) product. For grouped query attention (where number of query heads exceeds key/value heads), a special formulation uses REPEATBLOCKS/SUMGROUPS on intermediate vectors to avoid expanding \(W^K\). The scale factor is \(s = \alpha \cdot \sigma \cdot d / (\sqrt{d_h} \cdot \eta_{\text{fp8}} \cdot 448)\), where \(\alpha\) is a safety margin (or auto-calibrated during a burn-in phase) and \(\eta_{\text{fp8}} = 0.8\). This scale is applied to pre-softmax logits before FP8 quantization, ensuring no overflow while maximizing FP8 dynamic range.

## Key Results
- Eliminates all overflows in transient scenarios (e.g., checkpoint loading) where delayed scaling fails.
- On Llama-2-13B, auto-α calibration achieves MMLU accuracy 33.6% vs delayed scaling 33.0%, with higher FP8 utilization.
- Computational overhead is negligible (\(\approx\)1–4% runtime increase).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Rank-Aware Concentration Improves Tail Bounds
The interaction matrix \(M = W^Q W^{K⊤}\) has rank at most \(d_h\) (head dimension). This low-rank structure forces query-key products to lie in a low-dimensional subspace, making extreme values much less likely. The derived concentration inequality shows that the probability of a large maximum logit decays as \(\exp(-d^2\alpha^2/(\gamma d_h))\) instead of the standard \(\exp(-d\alpha^2)\), tightening the bound by a factor of \(d/(\gamma d_h)\) (8–28× tighter). This relies on uniform spherical distribution of token vectors after LayerNorm and the rank property. The result (Proposition 3.4) is supported by random matrix theory on such products. The bound fails if the rank assumption is violated (e.g., tied projections) or token directions are anisotropic.

### Mechanism 2: Worst-Case Bound Provides Predictive Scale
Under pre-LN, LayerNorm constrains \(\|x_i\| \approx \sqrt{d}\). By the variational definition of spectral norm, \(|x_i^\top M x_j| \leq \|M\|_2 \cdot \|x_i\| \cdot \|x_j\|\), yielding a worst-case bound \(B_{\text{max}} = \|M\|_2 \cdot d / \sqrt{d_h}\). Setting a calibration \(\alpha < 1\) and a margin \(\eta_{\text{fp8}}\) produces a scale \(s = \alpha \cdot \|M\|_2 \cdot d / (\sqrt{d_h} \cdot \eta_{\text{fp8}} \cdot 448)\) that depends only on current weights, eliminating history staleness. This bound is tight relative to naive alternatives (Corollary 3.3). It breaks if token norms are unbounded (e.g., missing LayerNorm) or singular vectors align worst-case (measure-zero but possible with poor initialization).

### Mechanism 3: Implicit Power Iteration Enables Efficiency
Estimating \(\|M\|_2\) without forming \(M\) is achieved by power iteration that never materializes the \(d \times d\) product: \(Mv = W^Q(W^{K\top}v)\) and \(M^\top u = W^K(W^{Q\top}u)\). For grouped query attention, the iteration avoids key expansion by replicating/summing small intermediates (size \(n_{kv} \cdot d_h\)). Persistent vectors \(u, v\) across training steps track slow weight drift; one iteration per forward pass suffices except after large jumps (then 5 warm-start iterations). This keeps overhead negligible (\(O(n_{\text{heads}} \cdot d_h \cdot d)\)). The approach is kernel-fusion compatible. The equivalence for GQA is proven (Proposition 4.1). Failure during extreme weight shifts is mitigated by warm-start.

## Foundational Learning
- **Low-rank matrix concentration**: The rank-aware bound hinges on \(\text{rank}(M)=d_h \ll d\); without this low-rank structure, the bound would be orders of magnitude looser, making auto-α impractical.  
  *Quick check*: Why does a low-rank interaction matrix lead to much tighter probability bounds on the maximum bilinear form?
- **Spectral norm and power iteration**: The bound depends on \(\|M\|_2\); an efficient estimator that does not form the \(d \times d\) product is needed.  
  *Quick check*: How does power iteration estimate the spectral norm of a product matrix using only the factors, and why is it efficient?
- **FP8 overflow and dynamic range**: Choosing the scale factor \(s\) correctly is critical: if \(\max|S| > s \cdot 448\), overflow yields NaNs; if \(s\) is too large, FP8 precision is wasted.  
  *Quick check*: What is the direct relationship between the maximum attention logit and the FP8 scale factor to avoid overflow?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Weight matrices (W^Q, W^K) → Implicit power iteration (persistent u, v) → σ_est → Scale computation (α, d, d_h, η_fp8, 448) → Divide pre-softmax logits → FP8 quantization → Softmax. Optional: Auto-α module records max logits during burn-in → adjusts α.
- **Critical path**: Weights → σ estimation → scale factor s → scaled logits → FP8 quantization → softmax → output.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Conservative α guarantees safety but underutilizes FP8; auto-α improves utilization at cost of worst-case guarantee. Per-layer scaling handles spectral heterogeneity but adds overhead; implicit power iteration keeps overhead negligible. GQA handling avoids key expansion via tiled summations.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Overflow: NaNs in loss indicates s too small (σ underestimated or α insufficient). Check power iteration warm-start.
  - Underutilization: FP8 histogram shows narrow range; α likely too large.
  - Stale power iteration: After checkpoint load, initial underestimation may cause overflow; solution: run 5 iterations before forward.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Load a pretrained checkpoint: run one forward pass; verify zero overflows (all scaled logits < 448).
  2. Simulated weight spike: multiply all W^Q, W^K by 4; delayed scaling should overflow, geometry-aware should not.
  3. LR spike: increase learning rate 100×; monitor first 10 steps for overflows; geometry-aware should have none.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Positional encoding generality**: The method's reliance on RoPE orthogonality may not hold for absolute embeddings or ALiBi; theoretical and empirical validation is needed.
2. **Anisotropy sensitivity**: The rank-aware concentration assumes uniform token directions; real models may be anisotropic, weakening the bound.
3. **Diagonal term conservatism**: Including self-attention terms (i=j) in the union bound may be overly conservative; a tighter bound excluding diagonal could reduce α.
4. **Auto-α safety**: The calibration assumes burn-in distribution is representative; distribution shifts may cause overflow after calibration.
5. **Power iteration tracking**: One iteration per step may not track rapid weight changes (e.g., extreme LR spikes) sufficiently; worst-case drift not quantified.

## Limitations
- Power iteration with one update per step may lag during extreme weight transients; warm-start heuristic lacks rigorous iteration budget.
- Auto-α calibration requires materializing max logits, which conflicts with fused kernels; practical implementation is unspecified.
- The rank \(d_h\) assumption can be violated by architectural modifications (e.g., tied Q/K projections), and token isotropy is not guaranteed on non-i.i.d. data.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical bound improvement**: High (rigorous proof, corroborated by random matrix theory literature).
- **Predictive scale factor effectiveness**: Medium (strong empirical results on standard models, but unproven under pathological initializations/extreme LR spikes).
- **Implicit power iteration overhead**: High (\(O(n_{\text{heads}} \cdot d_h \cdot d)\) complexity, validated on 70B model).
- **Auto-α accuracy matching**: Medium (reported numbers plausible, but burn-in methodology and kernel incompatibility unresolved).
