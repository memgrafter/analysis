# Automatic In-Domain Exemplar Construction and LLM-Based Refinement of Multi-LLM Expansions for Query Expansion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08917
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08917
- **Reference count:** 33
- **Primary result:** An automated framework using in-domain pseudo-relevant exemplars, clustering-based diversity, and a two-LLM refinement ensemble yields consistent, statistically significant gains in NDCG@10, P@10, and Recall@100 over BM25, Rocchio, zero-shot, and fixed few-shot baselines on TREC DL20, DBPedia, and SciFact.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the scalability and domain sensitivity of LLM-based query expansion by proposing an end-to-end automated framework. It constructs in-domain exemplar pools via BM25 reranked by MonoT5, selects diverse demonstrations through training-free clustering, and employs a two-LLM ensemble where a refinement LLM consolidates independent expansions. Evaluated on three diverse datasets, the method demonstrates robust and statistically significant improvements across standard retrieval metrics, offering a label-free and reproducible solution for effective query expansion.

## Method Summary
The pipeline operates in three stages. First, an offline pool is built: for seed queries, BM25 retrieves top-100 passages, MonoT5 reranks them, and the top-1 passage becomes a pseudo-relevant demonstration, forming (query, passage) pairs. Second, for each test query, Contriever embeddings of all pool items are clustered via k-means (k=4), and the medoid of each cluster is selected as a few-shot exemplar. Third, two heterogeneous LLMs (e.g., Qwen, Llama) independently generate expansions using the same prompt; a third LLM then refines both expansions into a single coherent version. The final query concatenates five copies of the original query with the refined expansion, which is used by a BM25 retriever.

## Key Results
- The proposed **Cluster-ICL QE with Two-LLM Refinement** consistently outperforms BM25, Rocchio, zero-shot, and fixed few-shot baselines in NDCG@10, P@10, and Recall@100 across all datasets.
- Improvements are statistically significant, demonstrating the robustness of the approach.
- Ablations confirm that in-domain exemplar construction and the refinement step are primary contributors to performance gains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: In-Domain Pseudo-Exemplars** – Automatically harvesting pseudo-relevant passages via BM25→MonoT5 creates a large pool of domain-specific demonstrations that capture relevant vocabulary and context, avoiding the pitfalls of fixed out-of-domain examples. Break condition: If initial retrieval fails in highly specialized domains, the pool becomes noisy and degrades expansion quality.

**Mechanism 2: Diversity via Clustering** – Selecting medoids from k-means clusters ensures the few-shot examples span multiple semantic facets of the pool, preventing the LLM from overfitting to a single sub-topic and encouraging comprehensive expansions. Break condition: If the embedding space poorly separates topics, clusters may lack semantic coherence.

**Mechanism 3: Two-LLM Ensemble with Refinement** – Heterogeneous LLMs produce complementary expansions (e.g., lexical vs. semantic); a third LLM consolidates them, merging useful entities while reducing redundancy and noise. Break condition: If LLMs are too similar or the refiner is poorly instructed, the consolidation may fail to improve upon single-model outputs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Pseudo-relevance feedback (PRF)** – Needed because the exemplar construction relies on BM25+MonoT5 to generate pseudo-labels, which are inherently noisy but scalable. Quick check: What is the key difference between pseudo-relevance feedback and actual relevance feedback in terms of label quality?
- **In-context learning (ICL)** – Needed because expansion is driven by few-shot prompting; ICL is highly sensitive to example selection and ordering. Quick check: Why does the order and content of few-shot examples significantly impact an LLM's in-context performance?
- **Clustering for exemplar diversity** – Needed to ensure broad coverage of semantic aspects from a large candidate pool, avoiding bias from repetitive examples. Quick check: How does k-means clustering help achieve diversity when selecting few-shot examples from a large candidate pool?

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Offline pool construction (BM25 + MonoT5) -> Demonstration selector (Contriever + k-means) -> Expansion generators (two LLMs) -> Refinement module (third LLM) -> Final query formatter -> Retriever (BM25)
- Critical path: 1. Build in-domain exemplar pool from seed queries. 2. For test query: encode pool, cluster, select cluster medoids. 3. Two LLMs generate expansions independently from same prompt. 4. Refinement LLM merges expansions into one. 5. Form final query (5× original + refined expansion). 6. Retrieve documents.
- Design tradeoffs: Pool size vs. noise; number of clusters (k) vs. medoid relevance; LLM heterogeneity vs. inference cost; refinement token limit vs. expansion richness.
- Failure signatures: Degraded metrics; off-topic or hallucinated expansions; semantically homogeneous clusters; refinement output identical to one input; unstable clustering across seeds.
- First experiments: 1. Replicate main results on TREC DL20 comparing all baselines. 2. Ablate exemplar selection: Cluster-ICL vs. Fixed Few-Shot under same LLM. 3. Ablate multi-LLM ensemble: single-LLM vs. two-LLM concat vs. two-LLM refine.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Optimal number of exemplars (k)** – The paper fixes k=4 without analysis; varying k might balance diversity and relevance differently across domains.
2. **Refinement LLM choice** – The refiner is the same model as one generator; using a truly independent LLM could further improve consolidation.
3. **LLM pair generalizability** – Only one heterogeneous pair (Qwen, Llama) is evaluated; the synergy for other model combinations is unknown.
4. **Improved pseudo-relevance pipeline** – BM25+MonoT5 may not be optimal; more advanced rerankers or PRF methods could yield higher-quality exemplar pools.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (exact k, full prompt templates, decoding settings) are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Pseudo-relevance noise poses a risk: if BM25 fails to retrieve relevant passages in low-resource domains, the exemplar pool degrades.
- The refinement step’s benefit is sensitive to LLM heterogeneity and instruction quality; similar LLMs may not yield gains.
- Evaluation is limited to three datasets; performance on other domains or corpora remains unverified.

## Confidence
- Primary claim (consistent gains over baselines): **High**
- Mechanism 1 (in-domain exemplars): **Medium**
- Mechanism 2 (clustering diversity): **Medium**
- Mechanism 3 (two-LLM refinement): **Medium-Low**
- Reproducibility of exact setup: **Medium-Low**

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt reverse-engineering and sensitivity:** Reconstruct exact few-shot and refinement prompts from paper descriptions; vary phrasing to measure impact on expansion quality and retrieval metrics.
2. **Clustering parameter ablation:** Systematically vary k (e.g., 2, 4, 8) and algorithm (k-means vs. hierarchical) on a held-out set to assess robustness of Cluster-ICL QE gains.
3. **PRF pipeline stress test:** Degrade the pool construction by using weaker retrieval/reranking (e.g., BM25 top-10, no reranker) to identify the breakpoint where exemplar quality falls below fixed few-shot performance.
