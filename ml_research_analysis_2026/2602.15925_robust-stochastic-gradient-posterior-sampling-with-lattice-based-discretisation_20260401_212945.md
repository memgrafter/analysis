# Robust Stochastic Gradient Posterior Sampling with Lattice Based Discretisation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15925
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15925
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SGLRW achieves robustness to minibatch size and heavy-tailed gradient noise without sacrificing asymptotic correctness, outperforming SGLD in posterior approximation quality.

## Executive Summary
Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) and similar SG-MCMC methods suffer from sensitivity to minibatch size and heavy-tailed gradient noise, limiting scalability and stability. This paper introduces Stochastic Gradient Lattice Random Walk (SGLRW), a discretisation of Langevin dynamics that replaces Gaussian increments with bounded binary/ternary lattice updates. In SGLRW, the minibatch noise influences only the off-diagonal elements of the update covariance, while diagonal variances remain fixed. This design yields theoretical and empirical robustness: the mean-squared error covariance bound is never larger than SGLD’s and strictly smaller when minibatch noise varies; SGLRW remains stable under heavy-tailed noise where SGLD diverges; and it consistently improves posterior approximation metrics across Bayesian linear and logistic regression, as well as large-scale sentiment classification.

## Method Summary
SGLRW modifies the SGLD update by replacing the Gaussian step with a coordinate-wise lattice random walk. For each parameter coordinate \(i\), the step magnitude is fixed at \(\sqrt{2\delta_t}\), but the direction is sampled with probability \(p_i^+ = 0.5 - 0.5 \cdot \text{clip}(\sqrt{\delta_t/2} \cdot |d\partial_i U(\theta;B)|, -1, 1) \cdot \text{sign}(d\partial_i U)\), and \(p_i^- = 1 - p_i^+\). The clipping ensures valid probabilities when the scaled drift exceeds 1. The minibatch gradient \(d\nabla U(\theta;B)\) is computed as usual. The step size follows a decaying schedule \(\delta_t = \delta_0 (1+t)^{-0.55}\). This yields a drop-in replacement for SGLD that decouples diagonal noise from minibatch stochasticity. Compared to clipped SGLD (which clips the entire gradient step), SGLRW’s lattice structure provides a principled, per-coordinate treatment that preserves asymptotic correctness while maintaining bounded updates.

## Key Results
- Theoretical: Covariance error of SGLRW ≤ SGLD, strictly smaller when minibatch gradient noise has non-zero variance (Lemma 4.3, Theorem 4.2).
- Empirical: On Bayesian linear and logistic regression, SGLRW achieves lower KL divergence and posterior covariance error across batch sizes and learning rates.
- Robustness: SGLRW remains stable under synthetic heavy-tailed (\(\alpha=1.5\)) gradient noise where SGLD diverges; on IMDB sentiment classification with fixed embeddings, SGLRW consistently outperforms clipped SGLD in negative log-likelihood and calibration error, especially for small-to-moderate minibatch sizes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**1. Bounded lattice updates decouple minibatch noise from diagonal step magnitude.** In SGLRW, each coordinate takes a fixed-magnitude step (\(\pm\sqrt{2\delta_t}\)). The probability of direction is determined by the scaled drift \(\sqrt{\delta_t/2}|d\partial_iU(\theta;B)|\), which is clipped to 1. The minibatch noise \(\zeta\) only appears in off-diagonal covariance terms via products like \(\partial_iU \cdot \zeta_j\); diagonal variances remain fixed at \(2\delta_t\) regardless of batch size or noise tail. This prevents destabilizing large parameter jumps. Evidence: abstract, Section 4.2 (Eq. 13). Break condition: without clipping, if \(\sqrt{\delta_t/2}|d\partial_iU| > 1\) probabilities become invalid; clipping introduces bias if drift consistently exceeds the bound.

**2. Modified second-order error \(M_n\) yields tighter MSE bounds.** The mean-squared error decomposition includes a covariance error term proportional to \(\mathbb{E}[||M_n||^2]\). Lemma 4.3 shows \(M_{n,\text{SGLRW}} = \text{offdiag}(M_{n,\text{SGLD}})\). Since \(M_{n,\text{SGLD}}\) has non-zero diagonal contributions (\(\zeta_i^2\) terms), its Frobenius norm is larger whenever \(\text{Var}(\zeta_i^2) > 0\). Thus \(\mathbb{E}_{\text{SGLRW}}^{\text{cov}} \leq \mathbb{E}_{\text{SGLD}}^{\text{cov}}\), strictly smaller when minibatch noise varies. Assumptions: finite second moments of \(\zeta\); strict inequality requires \(\text{Var}(\zeta_i^2) > 0\). Evidence: Theorem 4.2, Lemma 4.3, Figure 3. Break condition: if \(\zeta\) has zero variance (exact gradient), both errors vanish and become equal.

**3. Fixed-magnitude steps confer robustness to heavy-tailed noise.** In SGLD, a large \(\zeta\) directly scales the Gaussian step \(\sqrt{2\delta_t}\xi - \delta_t\zeta\), causing occasional explosive jumps that destabilise the chain. In SGLRW, the step norm is deterministic (\(||\Delta\theta||^2 = 2d\delta_t\)); \(\zeta\) only biases the direction via the lattice probability. This bounds the step size, preventing divergence from outlier gradients. Evidence: abstract, Figure 2 (synthetic \(\alpha=1.5\) noise). Break condition: extreme drift magnitudes require clipping; if drift consistently exceeds the clipping threshold, the update direction becomes biased toward saturation.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Minibatch Gradient Estimator → Lattice Probability Calculator (with clipping) → Discrete Step Sampler → Parameter Update

**Critical path:** The sequence: compute gradient, for each coordinate compute scaled drift \(\sqrt{\delta_t/2} \cdot d\partial_iU\) and clip to \([-1,1]\), compute direction probabilities \(p_i^+, p_i^-\), sample step direction (independently), update parameters. The clipping step must occur before probability calculation.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Clipping vs. Theoretical Validity:* Clipping ensures valid probabilities but introduces a bias if the drift consistently exceeds the clipping threshold. Tradeoff: stability for small batches vs. exact asymptotic correctness.
- *Fixed Diagonal vs. Adaptive Preconditioning:* SGLRW’s diagonal update variance is fixed (\(2\delta_t\)). This sacrifices potential adaptation benefits of preconditioners (like in pSGLD) for robustness.
- *Computational Cost:* Lattice sampling requires one random number per parameter (like Gaussian sampling) and cheap coordinate-wise probability calculations; no matrix operations, unlike some preconditioned SG-MCMC.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **KL divergence explosion:** In linear regression (Section 5.2), very high KL (e.g., >10 for B=32, \(\delta_0=10^{-3}\)) indicates divergence.
2. **Calibration error spike:** On classification tasks (Section 5.4), suddenly high ECE (>0.5) after stable training suggests chain instability.
3. **Clipping saturation:** If >5-10% of coordinates consistently hit the \(s_i=1\) clip bound across many iterations, the drift magnitude may be too large for the chosen \(\delta_t\).

**First 3 experiments (implementation order):**
1. **Bayesian linear regression:** Implement on synthetic data (N=1000, d=20). Verify that for moderate \(\delta_0\) (e.g., 1e-3), SGLRW’s empirical covariance matches the analytic posterior closely, while SGLD diverges for small batches (e.g., B=8). Use the KL metric from Table 1.

Compare SGLRW and SGLD trajectories. SGLRW should remain near modes; SGLD should produce occasional explosive jumps.

Verify SGLRW’s KL is lower than SGLD’s for small B and comparable to Clipped-SGLD. Check that no NaN parameters occur.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Clipping bias:** How does heuristic clipping of \(\sqrt{\delta_t/2}|d\partial_iU(\theta;B)|\) to unity affect the asymptotic bias and convergence of SGLRW relative to the theoretical unbounded lattice scheme? The analysis assumes the validity condition holds; clipping may break the off-diagonal covariance property.
2. **Deep learning applicability:** Does SGLRW preserve its robustness advantages when applied to fully Bayesian deep learning with trainable embeddings, rather than fixed embeddings as in the IMDB experiment? The fixed-embedding regime isolates the algorithm’s effect on a low-dimensional head; joint training may alter noise structure.
3. **Hardware efficiency:** Can the lattice-based update mechanism be implemented on stochastic or low-precision hardware to achieve tangible energy and latency savings, and does this offset any potential loss in sampling efficiency? Claims are based on bounded steps being compatible with analog/stochastic circuits but lack prototype validation.
4. **Underdamped extension:** Can the lattice discretization idea be extended to underdamped Langevin dynamics (e.g., Stochastic Gradient Hamiltonian Monte Carlo) to produce a robust SG-MCMC method that combines momentum with minibatch-noise resilience? The paper only addresses overdamped dynamics.

## Limitations
- Unreported experimental details (exact synthetic data distribution, chain initialization, KL divergence direction, NUTS reference sample size) hinder exact replication and may affect result interpretation.
- Clipping of scaled gradients ensures stability but introduces an unquantified bias; the theoretical analysis (Lemma 4.3, Theorem 4.2) assumes the validity condition without clipping.
- Theoretical MSE bound relies on finite second moments of minibatch noise; the heavy-tailed infinite-variance case lacks proof, and the advantage may vanish for deterministic gradients.

## Confidence
- **Decoupling of diagonal noise via bounded steps:** High – derivation is clear, though clipping may affect asymptotics.
- **MSE bound smaller than SGLD:** Medium – relies on finite second moments and non-zero diagonal noise variance; heavy-tailed case unproven.
- **Robustness to heavy-tailed noise:** Medium – synthetic \(\alpha\)-stable results are encouraging but not theoretically guaranteed.
- **Empirical superiority:** Medium – depends on unreported details (data scaling, seeds) and may not generalize to deeper models.
