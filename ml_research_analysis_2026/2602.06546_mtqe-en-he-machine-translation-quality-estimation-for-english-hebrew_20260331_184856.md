# MTQE.en-he: Machine Translation Quality Estimation for English-Hebrew
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06546
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06546
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Released first English-Hebrew MTQE dataset (959 pairs); ensembling ChatGPT, TransQuest, CometKiwi improves Pearson by 6.4 pp over best single model.

## Executive Summary
This work introduces MTQE.en-he, the first public dataset for English-Hebrew Machine Translation Quality Estimation, comprising 959 translation pairs with human Direct Assessment scores. The study benchmarks ChatGPT prompting, TransQuest, and CometKiwi, and evaluates ensembling and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods. Key findings show that ensembling the three models yields substantial gains (Pearson +6.4 pp), while PEFT on a small training set (300 samples) delivers stable improvements of 2–3 pp. Full-model fine-tuning fails due to overfitting, and all models exhibit a bias against predicting high-quality scores.

## Method Summary
The task is to predict Direct Assessment scores (0–100) for Hebrew translations of English source segments. The dataset is split into 300 training, 100 validation, and 559 test samples via stratified sampling. Baselines include ChatGPT (prompted with freestyle/guideline instructions), TransQuest (XLM-RoBERTa-large, "en-any" variant), and CometKiwi (InfoXLM-large with layerwise attention). Fine-tuning experiments compare full fine-tuning (FullFT), LoRA (rank-8 on attention), BitFit (biases + head), and FTHead (head only) on the training set for 50 steps with fixed hyperparameters (LR=3e-5, batch=32). Performance is evaluated using Pearson and Spearman correlation against mean human scores.

## Key Results
- Ensembling ChatGPT, TransQuest, and CometKiwi improves Pearson by 6.4 pp and Spearman by 5.6 pp over the best single model (CometKiwi).
- Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, BitFit, FTHead) on 300 samples yields 2–3 pp improvements in both metrics; full fine-tuning degrades performance.
- TransQuest and CometKiwi never predict scores above 0.91, despite 31% of human scores being in the "excellent or perfect" range (91–100).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Ensembling mitigates model-specific biases by averaging predictions from models with independent error patterns. ChatGPT exhibits score banding, while TransQuest and CometKiwi under-predict high scores; their moderate inter-correlations (e.g., Pearson ~0.644) allow error cancellation, yielding a 6.4 pp Pearson gain. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning avoids overfitting on the small dataset by updating only a tiny fraction of parameters (e.g., LoRA’s low-rank increments), preserving pretrained generalization while adapting to the task. Full fine-tuning overfits quickly, causing distribution collapse. High-score under-prediction stems from the dataset’s skew (73% scores ≥70), leading models to learn conservative estimates that rarely reach the top of the scale.

- **Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT):** Methods like LoRA (low-rank updates) and BitFit (bias-only) adapt models with minimal parameter changes to prevent overfitting on small datasets. *Why needed:* FullFT fails on 300 samples; PEFT balances adaptation and stability. *Quick check:* What representational difference makes LoRA (attention updates) potentially more expressive than FTHead (head-only) for this task?
- **Ensemble diversity for regression:** Combining models with uncorrelated errors reduces prediction variance and improves correlation metrics. *Why needed:* Ensembling gains 6+ pp; diversity comes from architectural differences (prompted LLM vs. fine-tuned transformers). *Quick check:* If two models have Pearson 0.65 with each other and 0.5 with ground truth, what variance reduction is expected from averaging their predictions?

**Design tradeoffs:** Small training set (300) limits FullFT but enables rapid iteration; uniform hyperparameters (LR=3e-5) may not optimize all PEFT methods; Pearson/Spearman measure linear/monotonic association, not absolute error, and DA subjectivity caps ceiling.

**Failure signatures:** FullFT shows validation degradation after one epoch and score distribution collapse; models never predict >0.91 despite 31% high-score samples; ensemble gain requires moderate model correlation (~0.64).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Dataset expansion:** How can MTQE.en-he be augmented to include more low-quality translations without altering the natural distribution? The current skew (73% ≥70) limits training on low-score samples. Resolved by: larger balanced dataset or successful synthetic data generation.
2. **FullFT vs PEFT cause:** Why does full fine-tuning overfit while parameter-efficient methods remain stable? No analysis of dataset size, model capacity, or optimization dynamics is provided. Resolved by: controlled experiments varying data size, model size, and regularization.
3. **Ensemble complementarity:** What specific quality aspects do ChatGPT, TransQuest, and CometKiwi capture differently? The paper notes score banding and under-prediction but lacks per-sample error analysis. Resolved by: error analysis by domain, error type, or score range.
4. **Synthetic data utility:** Can back-translation or controlled error injection effectively augment MTQE.en-he, especially for underrepresented low-quality translations? Proposed but untested. Resolved by: experiments generating synthetic translation errors and evaluating impact on low-score performance.

## Limitations
- ChatGPT model version, temperature, and exact prompts are unspecified, hindering exact replication and possibly causing score banding.
- Fine-tuning implementation details (e.g., LoRA module selection, custom layers for CometKiwi) are not released; hyperparameter choices may not be optimal.
- Small dataset size (959 pairs, 300 train) limits generalizability and increases result variance.
- Score distribution is heavily skewed toward high quality (73% ≥70), which may bias model predictions and reduce applicability to lower-quality translations.

## Confidence
- Ensembling gain (≥6.4 pp Pearson): High (measured consistently).
- PEFT improvements (2–3 pp): Medium (stable but hyperparameter-sensitive).
- FullFT failure: High (clear overfitting pattern).
- High-score under-prediction: Medium (observed, likely calibration-related).
- Model error independence: Medium (moderate correlations ~0.64 support diversity).

## Next Checks
1. **Error correlation analysis:** Compute pairwise prediction correlations across all seeds; simulate random ensembles to quantify how much of the gain is due to model diversity versus individual strength.
2. **Scaling study:** Train models on 600 and 900 samples (via data augmentation or larger splits) to determine if FullFT improves with more data or if PEFT gains saturate.
3. **Calibration diagnostics:** Apply temperature scaling or isotonic regression to model outputs; reassess high-score bias and overall Pearson/Spearman to see if calibration closes the gap in top-score predictions.
