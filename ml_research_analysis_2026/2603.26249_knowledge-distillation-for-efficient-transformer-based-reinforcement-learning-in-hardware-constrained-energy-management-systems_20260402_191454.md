# Knowledge Distillation for Efficient Transformer-Based Reinforcement Learning in Hardware-Constrained Energy Management Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26249
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26249
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Knowledge distillation compresses Decision Transformers by up to 96% in parameters and 63% in inference time while maintaining or slightly improving electricity cost performance on residential battery dispatch.

## Executive Summary
Transformer-based Decision Transformers (DTs) are effective for residential battery dispatch but are computationally demanding, hindering deployment on hardware-constrained energy management systems. This paper applies response-based knowledge distillation, training compact student models to mimic the continuous action logits of larger teacher models using Smooth L1 loss. On the Ausgrid dataset across 20 buildings, distilled models preserve or slightly improve control performance, with the best configuration (medium teacher to small student) reducing mean electricity cost to 200.28€ from the teacher's 201.30€. Crucially, distillation achieves massive compression—parameter count reduced by up to 96%, inference memory by up to 90%, and inference time by up to 63%—while still outperforming benchmarks in 55–60% of buildings under extreme compression.

## Method Summary
The method trains large teacher Decision Transformer models on an offline dataset of trajectories generated by a DDPG agent interacting with a residential battery environment. Student models with smaller architectures are then trained to mimic the teacher's continuous action logits using a Smooth L1 loss, with the teacher held fixed. This response-based distillation transfers the teacher's policy to the student. The models are evaluated on a fixed 4-week test period across 20 buildings from the Ausgrid dataset, comparing mean electricity cost, parameter count, memory footprint, and inference time.

## Key Results
- Massive compression: up to 96% fewer parameters, 90% less inference memory, 63% faster inference.
- Best configuration (Medium teacher → Small student) reduces mean electricity cost to 200.28€ vs. teacher's 201.30€.
- Under extreme compression (Medium→Tiny), distilled models outperform benchmarks in 55–60% of buildings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1:** Knowledge distillation transfers the teacher's continuous action policy to a smaller student model by minimizing the discrepancy between their output logits. The student is trained with Smooth L1 loss to match the scalar action logits produced by a frozen teacher for states in the offline dataset. Core assumption: the teacher's logits represent a superior policy that the student can approximate with fewer parameters. Evidence: distillation objective in Equation 10; abstract states matching teachers' actions with Smooth L1 loss. Break condition: if the student's capacity is too low to capture the mapping, fidelity loss occurs.

**Mechanism 2:** Offline self-distillation (distilling a model into an identical architecture) acts as a regularizer that improves generalization and control performance. By training a model to replicate its own previously trained outputs, the process smooths the policy and suppresses idiosyncratic noise or overfitting to specific trajectories. Core assumption: the teacher's outputs provide a cleaner signal than original regression targets. Evidence: abstract mentions self-distillation as an effective regulariser; section 2.2 describes smoother internal representations. Break condition: if the teacher's policy is poorly fitted or highly stochastic, self-distillation could lock in suboptimal behavior.

**Mechanism 3:** The causal transformer architecture with self-attention and a causal mask enables stable offline RL by avoiding value-function bootstrapping errors and allowing direct modeling of state-action-return sequences. By framing control as conditional sequence generation, the Decision Transformer sidesteps the "deadly triad" of bootstrapping, function approximation, and off-policy data. The causal mask enforces autoregressive dependency consistent with an MDP. Core assumption: the offline dataset contains diverse, high-quality trajectories. Evidence: abstract; section 2.1 details DT architecture and conditioning on Cost-to-Go. Break condition: if the offline dataset lacks coverage of important state regions, performance may degrade due to distributional shift.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Offline Dataset (from DDPG) → Teacher DT Training → Student DT Distillation (via Smooth L1 loss) → Evaluation (Battery Environment + Metrics)

**Critical path:**
1. Generate offline trajectories using DDPG agent in battery environment.
2. Train teacher DT models (various sizes) on the offline dataset.
3. For each teacher, train smaller student models to mimic teacher's action logits.
4. Evaluate all models on fixed test period per building, compute mean cost, compression metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Teacher capacity: Medium optimal; Large overfits to building-specific patterns.
- Student size: Smaller gives more compression but risks fidelity loss; Medium→Tiny still performs well in >50% of buildings.
- Distillation objective: Smooth L1 robust for continuous actions; matching hidden states not explored.

**Failure signatures:**
- Student cost much higher than teacher: student too small or poor distillation.
- High variance across seeds: unstable training or poor early stopping.
- Inference time not reduced as expected: bottleneck in data loading or environment step.
- Performance collapse on specific buildings: teacher's data lacks coverage for those states.

**First experiments:**
1. Replicate DT baseline: train all 5 DT sizes (Tiny, Mini, Small, Medium, Large); verify Medium best and Large underperforms.
2. Run core KD pipeline: distill Small from Medium; verify ~1€ cost improvement and compression ratios (params, memory, time) as in Table 2.
3. Stress-test extreme compression: distill Tiny from Medium; confirm >50% building wins and ~96% parameter reduction; validate inference time reduction (~63%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Question 1:** What alternative distillation objectives, beyond matching continuous action logits with Smooth L1 loss, can further improve the performance and efficiency of distilled Decision Transformers for battery dispatch?  
*Basis:* Conclusion states future work will investigate alternative distillation objectives.  
*Why unresolved:* Only response-based KD with Smooth L1 was used; other objectives (feature-based, different losses) unexplored.  
*Resolving evidence:* Comparative experiments on Ausgrid using various objectives (MSE, Huber, attention matching) with metrics: cost, parameters, latency.

**Question 2:** How does the quality of the offline trajectories used to train the teacher Decision Transformer affect the effectiveness of knowledge distillation in terms of student performance and robustness?  
*Basis:* Conclusion proposes assessing offline trajectory quality.  
*Why unresolved:* Single dataset from DDPG agent; no variation in trajectory optimality/diversity.  
*Resolving evidence:* Evaluate KD using datasets of varying quality (MILP-optimal, noisy expert, heterogeneous policies) measuring student cost, compression, generalization.

**Question 3:** Can the proposed knowledge distillation framework for Decision Transformers be extended to handle more complex residential energy management scenarios involving multiple flexible devices, such as batteries, electric vehicles, and HVAC systems?  
*Basis:* Conclusion suggests extension to multiple flexible devices.  
*Why unresolved:* Current work addresses single-battery dispatch; multi-device coordination requires architectural modifications.  
*Resolving evidence:* Implement KD in multi-device environment (battery, EV, HVAC); compare distilled student vs. teacher in cost savings, scalability, hardware efficiency.

**Question 4:** Why does the performance of Decision Transformers in residential battery dispatch exhibit a non-monotonic relationship with model capacity, where medium-sized models outperform larger ones?  
*Basis:* Section 4 Discussion notes non-monotonicity; speculation about overfitting.  
*Why unresolved:* No empirical analysis isolating causes; hypotheses untested.  
*Resolving evidence:* Ablation studies varying dataset size, building diversity, regularization (dropout, weight decay) across DT sizes, monitoring validation loss, overfitting metrics, attention patterns.

## Limitations
- Exact DDPG hyperparameters and offline dataset construction (trajectory sampling, splits) are unspecified, affecting teacher policy quality.
- Precise DT architecture dimensions (hidden size, number of heads) for each model variant are ambiguous, hindering exact replication of compression ratios.
- Evaluation period (specific 4 weeks) and consistency across all 20 buildings are unclear, potentially introducing seasonal bias.
- Reported performance improvements (e.g., ~1€ cost reduction) may be within statistical variance; multiple seeds needed for robustness.

- **Performance preservation/improvement via KD:** Medium
- **Massive compression with retained performance:** Medium
- **Self-distillation as regularizer:** Low
