# FlexGuard: Continuous Risk Scoring for Strictness-Adaptive LLM Content Moderation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23636  
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23636  
- **References:** 40  
- **Primary result:** FlexGuard achieves +4.5% average F1 over binary baselines on FlexBench, with robust performance across strictness regimes.

## Executive Summary
Existing LLM content moderators treat safety as a binary fixed‑threshold task, making them brittle when deployments require varying strictness. FlexBench (4K instances, three regimes) shows current moderators drop up to 19.2% F1 when strictness shifts. FlexGuard addresses this by predicting a calibrated continuous risk score (0–100) and risk categories; a per‑deployment threshold then yields binary decisions. On FlexBench, FlexGuard attains the highest average and worst‑regime F1 for both prompt and response moderation, and gains transfer to public benchmarks.

## Method Summary
FlexGuard trains Qwen3‑8B via a three‑phase pipeline. (1) Rubric‑guided distillation: Doubao‑1.6‑Pro annotates Aegis2.0 and WildGuardMix with categories, scores, and step‑by‑step rationales using expert rubrics. (2) Label‑consistent calibration: raw scores are rescaled to safe [0,40] or unsafe [30,100] to align with binary labels. (3) Two‑stage alignment: first, SFT (LoRA, 1 epoch, lr=5e‑4) teaches output format and reasoning; second, GRPO (VERL, 2 epochs, lr=1e‑6, KL=0.001, rollout=8) fine‑tunes with dense reward R = s_category (+1/-1) + s_score (linear penalty on |error|/Emax). Deployment thresholds are either fixed (20/40/60) or optimized on a validation set.

## Key Results
- **Prompt moderation:** avg F1 81.78% (+4.56% vs best baseline 77.26%).  
- **Response moderation:** avg F1 80.29% (+2.86% vs 77.43%).  
- **Worst‑regime robustness:** up to 19.2% relative F1 gain; improvements also hold on WildGuardTest.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Continuous scoring**: Enables strictness adaptation by simple thresholding; evidence: stable F1 across regimes (Section 4.1).  
- **Rubric distillation + calibration**: LLM judge produces fine‑grained labels; calibration rescales scores to intervals consistent with binary sources, reducing bias.  
- **Two‑stage alignment**: SFT establishes format; GRPO uses combined reward (category accuracy + score regression) to align model’s internal risk scale, improving cross‑regime robustness; ablations confirm each component’s contribution.

## Foundational Learning
- **Continuous risk scoring**: Ordinal output per deployment rather than fixed classes. *Why needed*: Allows tuning without retraining. *Quick check*: For an extreme‑risk focus, set a high threshold after inspecting score distribution on a sample.
- **Rubric‑based annotation**: Structured, reproducible LLM labeling. *Why needed*: Scales severity annotation. *Quick check*: Overlap in calibration ([0,40] safe vs [30,100] unsafe) preserves original label ambiguity; narrower intervals reduce noise but increase discard.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: RL with group‑normalized advantages for stable fine‑tuning. *Why needed*: Aligns scores with dense reward more robustly than standard PPO. *Quick check*: Combining category and score rewards ensures both correct risk type and accurate severity; GRPO’s group‑relative term reduces reward hacking.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data → Doubao‑1.6‑Pro (judge) → Label‑consistent calibration → SFT (LoRA) → GRPO → FlexGuard → Threshold selector → Binary decision.  
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Deduplicate training queries against FlexBench (exact match).  
  2. Run Doubao‑1.6‑Pro with rubric prompts.  
  3. Apply calibration (safe→[0,40], unsafe→[30,100]).  
  4. SFT warm‑up (1 epoch, lr 5e‑4, LoRA).  
  5. GRPO alignment (2 epochs, lr 1e‑6, KL 0.001, rollout 8).  
  6. Select regime thresholds (rubric defaults 20/40/60 or validation‑sweep).  
  7. Evaluate on FlexBench test splits.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  * Judge strength vs. annotation cost/time.  
  * Calibration interval overlap: wider increases ambiguity; narrower discards valid scores.  
  * Fixed reward weighting may not be optimal; tuning requires extra validation.  
  * Rubric thresholds are simple but not data‑optimal; calibration needs representative validation set.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  * LLM–human agreement <60% → judge quality insufficient.  
  * Score histogram peaks in overlap → model cannot discriminate severity.  
  * GRPO curve: category accuracy drops while score error decreases (or vice versa) → reward imbalance.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate baseline brittleness: evaluate binary moderators on FlexBench to confirm >19% F1 swing.  
  2. Ablate calibration and GRPO to isolate their impact on average and worst‑regime F1.  
  3. Compare fixed vs. calibrated thresholds on FlexBench and WildGuardTest.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multilingual performance:** Evaluation only on English; transfer to other languages or code‑mix unknown.
- **Data distribution impact:** Pipeline relies on Aegis2.0/WildGuardMix; adding other sources may affect calibration and robustness.
- **Advanced post‑training algorithms:** Alternatives to GRPO (e.g., DAPO) could further improve score consistency or reduce pseudo‑label noise sensitivity.
- **Rubric completeness:** Fixed five‑tier system may not capture all real‑world policy variations across platforms and cultures.
- **Computational trade‑offs:** Continuous scoring adds reasoning steps and latency; cost‑benefit in large‑scale deployments unanalyzed.

## Limitations
- LLM judge bias and arbitrary severity scaling; overlapping calibration intervals introduce ambiguity.  
- GRPO hyperparameters (KL coefficient, lr) are sensitive; risk of reward hacking.  
- Experiments restricted to English data; generalizability to other domains and cultures untested.
