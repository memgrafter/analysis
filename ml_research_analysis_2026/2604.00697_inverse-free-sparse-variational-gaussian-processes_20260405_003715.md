# Inverse-Free Sparse Variational Gaussian Processes
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00697
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00697
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: Introduces an inverse-free sparse variational GP that matches the predictive performance of Cholesky-based methods while training up to 2.5× faster on modern parallel hardware.

## Executive Summary
Sparse variational Gaussian processes (SVGPs) are powerful for scalable Bayesian inference but rely on Cholesky decompositions, which are inefficient on modern parallel hardware. This work proposes R‑SVGP, an inverse‑free alternative that optimizes a variational bound using only matrix multiplications (matmuls). The approach introduces an auxiliary matrix \(T\) to approximate the inverse of the jittered inducing covariance, a natural‑gradient (NG) update for \(T\) that ensures stable convergence, and a preconditioner for the inducing mean that matches the dynamics of the whitened bound. Across regression and classification benchmarks, R‑SVGP achieves predictive performance (NLPD, error) on par with standard Cholesky‑based SVGPs (W‑SVGP, L‑SVGP). With carefully tuned heuristics (step schedules, stopping criteria) it trains up to 2.5× faster. R‑SVGP can serve as a drop‑in replacement in complex SVGP‑based models such as deep Gaussian processes and convolutional GPs, removing the need for matrix decompositions entirely.

## Method Summary
R‑SVGP rewrites the standard SVGP ELBO so that the predictive mean \(\mu_n = k_{nu} P \tilde{m}\) and variance \(\sigma_n^2 = k_{nn} - k_{nu} P k_{nu}\) depend on an auxiliary matrix \(T = LL^\top\) through the preconditioner \(P = 2T - T\tilde{K}T\), where \(\tilde{K} = K_{uu} + \text{diag}(\tilde{S})\). The KL divergence is bounded by \(\frac12[-\operatorname{tr}(P K_{uu}) + \operatorname{tr}(\tilde{K}T) - M + \tilde{m}^\top P K_{uu} P \tilde{m} - \log|T| - \log|\tilde{S}|\), with the trace terms estimated via Hutchinson’s method—all using only matmuls. Optimization alternates between a natural‑gradient update for the Cholesky factor \(L\) (which directly minimizes the KL) and Adam steps for the remaining parameters (kernel hyperparameters \(\theta\), inducing locations \(Z\), whitened mean \(\tilde{m}\), and diagonal \(\tilde{S}\)). Key heuristics include a residual‑based stopping criterion for the NG loop, Z‑regularization (freeze then low learning rate), and a fixed probe count for trace estimation. This combination yields a fully inverse‑free SVGP that integrates seamlessly with existing architectures.

## Key Results
- Matches predictive performance (NLPD and error) of Cholesky‑based baselines (W‑SVGP, L‑SVGP) across regression and classification benchmarks.
- Trains up to 2.5× faster than baselines when using tuned heuristics (step schedules, stopping criteria).
- Enables drop‑in replacement for complex SVGP‑based models (e.g., deep Gaussian processes, convolutional GPs) without requiring any matrix decompositions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
R‑SVGP relies on three core mechanisms that together enable inverse‑free, stable, and high‑performance training.

#### Mechanism 1: Inverse‑free bound via auxiliary matrix \(T\)
**Claim:** The bound avoids matrix inverses by introducing an auxiliary matrix \(T\) that approximates \(\tilde{K}^{-1}\).  
**Mechanism:** The L‑SVGP predictive variance \(\sigma_n^2 = k_{nn} - k_{nu} \tilde{K}^{-1} k_{un}\) is upper bounded by \(k_{nn} - k_{nu} (2T - T\tilde{K}T) k_{un}\). The KL divergence is rewritten (Eq. 4) to depend on \(T\) and \(\tilde{S}\) via traces and determinants only, estimable Hutchinson‑style. At optimum \(T = \tilde{K}^{-1}\) the bound matches L‑SVGP.  
**Core assumption:** \(T\) can be optimized sufficiently close to \(\tilde{K}^{-1}\) without computing the inverse; the bound remains tight in that regime.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “introduces an inverse‑free SVGP (R‑SVGP) by optimizing a variational bound using only matrix multiplications (matmuls).”  
- [section 2] “the resulting ELBO … is free of matrix decompositions, … recovers the L‑SVGP solution when \(T = \tilde{K}^{-1}\).”  
- [corpus] Distinguishes from prior sparse GP methods that still require Cholesky.  
**Break condition:** If \(T\) drifts far from \(\tilde{K}^{-1}\) (e.g., due to poor optimization), the bound becomes loose, increasing NLPD and error; monitor residual \(\|L^\top \tilde{K}L - I\|_F/\sqrt{M}\).

#### Mechanism 2: Natural‑gradient update for \(T\)  
**Claim:** Using a natural‑gradient (NG) update for the auxiliary matrix \(T\) (via its Cholesky factor \(L\)) markedly improves stability and convergence compared to Adam.  
**Mechanism:** The auxiliary objective \(\ell_A(L) = \mathrm{KL}(\mathcal{N}(0, LL^\top) \| \mathcal{N}(0, \tilde{K}^{-1}))\) is minimized by NG steps: \(\tilde{\nabla}\ell = L\big[\mathrm{tril}(L^\top \tilde{K} L) - \frac12(I + \mathrm{diag}(L^\top \tilde{K} L))\big]\) (Proposition 1). This is equivalent to a Newton‑Schulz iteration for matrix inversion, offering local quadratic convergence. NG updates for \(L\) are interleaved with Adam steps for other parameters.  
**Core assumption:** The Fisher information for the \(L\)-parameterization is accurately captured by the closed form; the Newton‑type step benefits from \(L\) being near \(L_{\tilde{K}^{-1}}\).  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 3.1] Derivation of Proposition 1; Fig. 1 shows R‑SVGP(N) and R‑SVGP(NP) converge stably while Adam‑only variants do not.  
- [remark] Link to Newton‑Schulz iteration provides theoretical justification.  
- [corpus] Natural gradients are established in variational GP inference but here targeted at the auxiliary inversion objective.  
**Break condition:** If \(\tilde{K}\) is ill‑conditioned or \(L\) initialized poorly, the NG step may require many iterations; the residual‑based stopping criterion mitigates excessive cost.

#### Mechanism 3: Preconditioning the inducing mean  
**Claim:** Preconditioning the inducing mean \(m = K_{uu} P(R) \tilde{m}\) with \(P(R)=2T - T\tilde{K}T\) (or \(P(L)=\tilde{K}^{-1}\) for L‑SVGP) is necessary to match W‑SVGP performance.  
**Mechanism:** The preconditioner rescales \(m\) to account for the covariance structure. For L‑SVGP, \(P(L)=\tilde{K}^{-1}\) yields gradients identical to a “whitened” bound akin to W‑SVGP. For R‑SVGP, \(P(R)\) (a single Newton‑Schulz step) matches those gradients when \(T\) tracks \(\tilde{K}^{-1}\) (Proposition 2). This improves optimization dynamics.  
**Core assumption:** \(P(R)\) accurately approximates \(\tilde{K}^{-1}\) during training; Proposition 2 holds when \(T\) is fixed at \(\tilde{K}^{-1}\) during the step (stopgrad).  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 3.2] Proposition 2 proves gradient equivalence under \(T = \mathrm{stopgrad}(\tilde{K}^{-1})\); Fig. 1 shows only R‑SVGP(NP) matches W‑SVGP/L‑SVGP(P).  
- [section 4.1] Results on toy datasets confirm preconditioning + NG is essential.  
- [corpus] Whitening is common in SVGP; this work provides an inverse‑free analogue.  
**Break condition:** If \(T\) is not sufficiently accurate (e.g., too few NG steps), \(P(R)\) deviates from \(\tilde{K}^{-1}\) and the preconditioning benefit disappears.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
1. Kernel: computes \(K_{uu}\), \(k_{nu}\), \(k_{nn}\).  
2. Variational parameters: inducing mean \(\tilde{m}\) (init 0), diagonal \(\tilde{S}\) (init small), auxiliary \(T=LL^\top\) (init \(\beta I\)).  
3. Preconditioner \(P(R)=2T - T\tilde{K}T\) (or \(P(L)=\tilde{K}^{-1}\) for L‑SVGP).  
4. ELBO evaluation: likelihood \(\sum \mathbb{E}[\log p(y|f)]\) using \(\mu_n = k_{nu} P \tilde{m}\), \(\sigma_n^2 = k_{nn} - k_{nu} P k_{nu}\); KL bound (Eq. 7) with Hutchinson traces for \(\operatorname{tr}(P K_{uu})\), \(\log|T|\).  
5. Optimizer: outer Adam for \(\{\theta, Z, \tilde{m}, \tilde{S}\}\); inner NG loop for \(L\) (T) with step \(\gamma\).

**Critical path per minibatch:**  
1. Compute \(K_{uu}\), add \(\tilde{S}\) to form \(\tilde{K}\).  
2. Run \(t^*\) NG updates for \(L\): compute residual \(L^\top \tilde{K} L - I\), NG step (Eq. 6), update \(L\).  
3. Compute \(P\) from current \(T\).  
4. Compute \(\mu\), \(\sigma^2\) for batch.  
5. Estimate ELBO: log‑likelihood term; KL term via Hutchinson (K probes for traces).  
6. Take Adam step on all parameters except \(L\) (which is updated via NG).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Probe count \(K\) for Hutchinson: ↑K ↓ noise but ↑ cost; ~256 often balances.  
- NG steps \(t^*\): ↑accuracy but cubic cost; use residual stopping (\(\epsilon=10^{-3}\)–\(5\times10^{-3}\)) or fixed small (1–3) with Z‑regularization.  
- Must combine NG with preconditioning; \(P(R)\) alone is insufficient.  
- Precision: matmul‑only enables low‑precision (FP16) acceleration; Cholesky needs higher precision.

**Failure signatures:**  
- ELBO plateaus at high value (poor bound) if residual norm remains large (T inaccurate).  
- Divergence if NG step size too large or T initialized singular (L off‑diagonal zero).  
- Underperformance on small M (e.g., M=1000 on kin40k) if Z is over‑regularized (frozen too long or tiny LR).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Toy convergence: run R‑SVGP on `snelson` (M=10, fixed Z). Compare loss traces with Adam‑only vs NG (Fig. 1). Verify R‑SVGP(NP) matches L‑SVGP(P).  
2. Speed benchmark: train on `elevators` (N≈16k) with M=2000, B=100. Measure wall‑clock time to target NLPD; compare W‑SVGP, L‑SVGP, R‑SVGP with K=256 trace estimation.  
3. Generality test: build a 2‑layer deep GP on `kin40k` (M=500 per layer) using R‑SVGP layers (share \(\tilde{S}\) per layer). Compare NLPD to L‑SVGP and W‑SVGP (Table 1). Ensure no Cholesky calls.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Z regularization in deep GPs** – How can Z‑regularization heuristics (freezing, learning rate, β1) from shallow SVGP be adapted to deep Gaussian processes for stable and fast R‑SVGP training? The paper notes that finding suitable initialization for deeper models is more challenging and leaves a systematic study to future work.  
2. **Natural gradient for W‑SVGP analogue** – Can a natural‑gradient update for the auxiliary parameter \(R\) in the W‑SVGP‑like inverse‑free bound be derived to use only matmuls, and does it yield competitive performance? The paper sketches an alternative bound but still contains an inverse in an inner KL term; deriving NG for \(R\) is open.  
3. **Low‑precision arithmetic** – Does lower‑precision arithmetic (FP16/BF16) during training significantly speed up R‑SVGP compared to Cholesky‑based methods without sacrificing accuracy? The authors state this merits investigation.  
4. **Stopping criterion and step schedule** – What stopping criterion (residual vs. ELBO slack) and step‑size schedule for NG updates of \(T\) minimize total training time while maintaining predictive performance across different SVGP configurations? The paper uses heuristics (ϵ=5e‑3, log‑linear schedule) but admits the choice is ad hoc.

## Limitations
- No theoretical guarantees for the convergence of the alternating natural‑gradient and Adam updates, particularly on ill‑conditioned kernels or with aggressive Z optimization.  
- Bound tightness depends on the accuracy of \(T\); the residual stopping criterion (ϵ=5e−3) is heuristic and may not generalize.  
- Speedup results are GPU‑specific and limited to modest M (≤2000); performance on CPUs, larger M, or low‑precision regimes remains unexplored.
