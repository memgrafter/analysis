# Benchmarking the Energy Cost of Assurance in Neuromorphic Edge Robotics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13880
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13880
- **Reference Count:** 27
- **Primary Result:** A fully defended neuromorphic system reduces adversarial success rates by >55 percentage points while lowering energy per inference to 45.1 µJ—a 6.4% reduction from the undefended baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a benchmark of adversarial robustness energy costs in neuromorphic edge robotics. It evaluates the Hierarchical Temporal Defense (HTD) framework on the BrainChip Akida AKD1000 processor for cislunar rover anomaly detection. The study finds that adding three defensive layers—Bayesian Spike Pattern Superposition encoding, homeostatic adaptive thresholds, and volatility-gated metaplasticity—not only improves robustness against gradient-based and temporal jitter attacks but also unexpectedly reduces energy consumption. This counter-intuitive outcome is attributed to the metaplasticity mechanism inducing higher network sparsity during attacks, which decreases dynamic power on the event-driven hardware. The work challenges the conventional assumption that robustness necessarily incurs an energy penalty in edge AI systems.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct a cumulative ablation study on the Cislunar Anomaly and Risk Dataset (CARD) using a spiking neural network implemented on a BrainChip Akida AKD1000 neuromorphic processor. The baseline employs standard rate coding and LIF neurons with STDP. The HTD framework adds three sequential assurance layers: (1) Input Assurance via Bayesian Spike Pattern Superposition (BSPS) for probabilistic input encoding, (2) Neuronal Assurance using homeostatic adaptive thresholds to filter burst patterns, and (3) Synaptic Assurance applying volatility-gated metaplasticity to suppress learning on unstable synapses during attacks. For each configuration, they measure adversarial success rate (ASR) against PGD (ε=0.1) and temporal jitter (max shift 3ms) attacks, energy per inference via on-board rail sampling at 1 kHz, latency, and normalized network activity. Results are averaged over 10 runs.

## Key Results
- Adversarial success for PGD attacks drops from 82.1% (undefended) to 18.7% (fully defended).
- Temporal jitter attack success reduces from 75.8% to 25.1%.
- Energy per inference decreases to 45.1 µJ, 6.4% below the undefended baseline of 48.2 µJ.
- Normalized network activity decreases to 0.94x with full defense, correlating strongly (r > 0.95) with energy reduction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Bayesian Spike Pattern Superposition (BSPS) encoding increases energy consumption due to higher computational complexity and increased spike activity.
- **Mechanism:** BSPS replaces deterministic rate coding with a probabilistic belief state over multiple spike pattern hypotheses. This requires additional arithmetic for belief updates and often activates more spikes simultaneously, increasing synaptic events processed by the neuromorphic hardware.
- **Core assumption:** Probabilistic superposition maintains multiple active hypotheses, leading to a higher event rate compared to a single deterministic pattern.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "The addition of the Input Assurance layer (BSPS)... introduces a measurable energy overhead, increasing consumption from 48.2 to 51.4 microjoules." [section V.A] Table I shows normalized activity increases to 1.08x with +Input Assurance.
- **Break condition:** If the belief update mechanism aggressively prunes unlikely hypotheses early, reducing net activity; paper shows a net increase, so this condition is not met in current implementation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Homeostatic Adaptive Thresholds improve robustness with negligible net energy change by filtering high-frequency burst patterns associated with attacks.
- **Mechanism:** Dynamic thresholds that rise after a spike and decay exponentially suppress consecutive burst firings while passing nominal signals. This marginally reduces effective spike rates without major computational overhead.
- **Core assumption:** Adversarial temporal perturbations (e.g., jitter) manifest as unnatural high-frequency spike bursts that can be distinguished from nominal dynamics.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section V.A] "+Neuronal Assurance" configuration shows a small energy decrease (51.4 → 49.8 µJ) and normalization activity ~1.02x. [section III.B.3] Describes the mechanism as "acts as a temporal filter, suppressing high-frequency burst patterns."
- **Break condition:** If adaptive thresholds are set too sensitively, they could suppress legitimate rapid signal changes, harming detection performance; paper shows stable F1, suggesting parameters are balanced.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Volatility-Gated Metaplasticity reduces energy consumption by inducing sparsity—it selectively suppresses synaptic updates for weights exhibiting high volatility, a signature of adversarial attacks.
- **Mechanism:** The mechanism monitors variance of recent weight updates per synapse. During adversarial attacks, weight updates become more volatile; gating reduces the learning rate or disables plasticity for these synapses, decreasing the number of synaptic events and thus dynamic power on the event-driven Akida chip.
- **Core assumption:** Gradient-based and temporal jitter attacks cause abnormally high volatility in synaptic weight trajectories compared to nominal data, providing a detectable signal for gating.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "counter-intuitive reduction in dynamic power consumption... attributed to volatility-gated plasticity mechanisms that induce higher network sparsity." [section V.D] Full Defense has normalized activity 0.94x and lowest energy (45.1 µJ); Figure 4 shows strong linear correlation (r > 0.95) between activity and energy. [section III.B.4] "Synapses exhibiting high volatility... have their plasticity suppressed."
- **Break condition:** If attacks are crafted to minimize weight volatility (e.g., very small, consistent perturbations), gating may not engage, preventing sparsity gains. Also, if legitimate learning requires volatile synapses, long-term performance could degrade.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Event-driven neuromorphic processing**
  - **Why needed:** The entire energy-robustness synergy hinges on the Akida's dynamic power being proportional to synaptic events, not clock cycles. Without this foundation, the observed "negative energy cost" would not be possible.
  - **Quick check:** Why does suppressing synaptic events on the Akida AKD1000 directly lower dynamic power consumption?
- **Concept: Adversarial attacks on spiking neural networks**
  - **Why needed:** The HTD framework is evaluated against specific attacks (PGD, temporal jitter). Understanding their mechanics—gradient-based input perturbation vs. spike-timing manipulation—is essential to interpret the robustness results.
  - **Quick check:** How does temporal jitter exploit the temporal precision of SNNs differently from a gradient-based PGD attack?
- **Concept: Energy-robustness trade-off in edge AI**
  - **Why needed:** The paper challenges the conventional wisdom that robustness always costs energy. Recognizing the baseline assumption (trade-off is positive) clarifies the significance of the counter-intuitive result.
  - **Quick check:** In traditional deep learning on von Neumann hardware, why do defense mechanisms like adversarial training typically increase energy consumption?

## Architecture Onboarding
Sensor window -> BSPS belief state -> spike generation -> Akida NPU mesh (LIF propagation with adaptive thresholds) -> STDP updates (volatility-gated) -> readout anomaly score. Host PC handles BSPS encoding; communication via PCIe. Akida AKD1000 NSoC contains 80 Neuro Processor Units (NPUs) with 4-bit weights.
The sequence from sensor data ingestion to final classification event. Host computes BSPS (potential bottleneck), then spikes are streamed to Akida; on-chip propagation and plasticity occur; result read back. Energy sampling overlaps the on-chip processing window.
- **BSPS on host vs. on-chip:** Host-side encoding adds PCIe latency and host energy overhead but leverages flexible compute; moving it on-chip would reduce overhead but may exceed Akida's native instruction set.
- **Gating aggressiveness:** High metaplasticity threshold maximizes sparsity/energy savings but risks under-training on legitimate patterns; low threshold preserves learning but gains less energy.
- **Temporal window W:** Larger W in BSPS improves jitter robustness but increases belief update computations and spikes.
- **Energy >50 µJ:** Indicates sparsity not achieved; likely metaplasticity disabled or Input layer dominant without compensating sparsity.
- **ASR >40% (PGD):** Defense ineffective; suggests metaplasticity not engaging or thresholds too conservative.
- **Latency >2 ms (device):** Excessive spike rate or burst activity overwhelming NPUs; check BSPS output rate.
- **F1-score drop >0.05 vs. baseline:** Over-suppression of spikes; adaptive thresholds or gating too aggressive.
1. **Baseline validation:** Run undefended model on clean CARD data and PGD (ε=0.1) attacks. Record energy, latency, ASR. Verify energy ~48 µJ, ASR ~82%. Establish activity-energy linearity.
2. **Ablation - Input layer only:** Enable BSPS only. Confirm energy increase (~51-52 µJ) and jitter ASR drop. Check if activity increases as expected.
3. **Ablation - Full HTD:** Enable all three layers. Confirm energy drops to ~45 µJ and ASR (PGD) <20%. Log per-synapse volatility histograms during attacks vs. clean to validate gating engagement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalizability to other neuromorphic hardware:** Does the observed synergy between robustness and energy efficiency generalize to other neuromorphic hardware platforms beyond BrainChip Akida? (Basis: evaluation restricted to a single platform; Sec. VI.D)
- **Effectiveness against physical attacks:** How effective is the HTD framework against real-world physical adversarial attacks (e.g., optical dazzling, fault injection) and what is the associated energy cost? (Basis: digitally injected perturbations do not capture physical complexity; Sec. VI.D)
- **Applicability to other robotic workloads:** Is the robustness-efficiency synergy maintained for other robotic workloads such as closed-loop control or SLAM? (Basis: remains to be verified for workloads with different temporal dynamics; Sec. VI.D)
- **Causal mechanism of sparsity:** What is the specific causal mechanism by which volatility-gated metaplasticity induces network-wide sparsity during adversarial attacks? (Basis: paper shows correlation but not detailed spatiotemporal sparsity patterns; inferred)

## Limitations
- Exact network architecture (layers, widths, connectivity) and weight initialization are not specified.
- Hyperparameters for BSPS (η, K, window W), adaptive thresholds (baseline, decay τ, ΔV), and metaplasticity (volatility window, threshold) are missing.
- Energy measurement methodology lacks full detail on calibration and idle-power subtraction.
- Evidence for metaplasticity-induced energy reduction is correlational (r>0.95) and not demonstrably causal.
- Evaluation limited to two attack types; generalizability to other adversarial perturbations is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Energy reduction claim (6.4% reduction to 45.1 µJ):** Medium – based on a single hardware setup; hyperparameter gaps prevent full confidence.
- **Robustness improvement (ASR drop):** Medium – ablation results consistent, but attack implementations and dataset specifics could affect generality.
- **Mechanism 1 (BSPS energy increase):** Medium – supported by Table I but without parameter sensitivity analysis.
- **Mechanism 2 (Adaptive thresholds negligible effect):** Medium – small change observed; evidence limited.
- **Mechanism 3 (Volatility-gated metaplasticity drives sparsity/energy reduction):** High – strong correlation (r>0.95) and consistent activity drop; break condition noted but not observed.

## Next Checks
1. **Disclose full hyperparameters** for BSPS, adaptive thresholds, and metaplasticity gating to enable exact replication.
2. **Measure and report per-synapse plasticity gating rates** during clean, PGD-attacked, and jitter-attacked inferences; confirm that gating engagement is significantly higher under attack and that synaptic event reduction quantitatively explains the energy savings.
3. **Conduct a sensitivity analysis** of the metaplasticity threshold across a range (e.g., lower/higher volatility thresholds) to ensure the energy-robustness benefit is robust to tuning and does not collapse for attacks designed to minimize weight volatility.
