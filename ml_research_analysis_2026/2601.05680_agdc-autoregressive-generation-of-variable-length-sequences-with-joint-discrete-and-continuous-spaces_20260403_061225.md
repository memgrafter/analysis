# AGDC: Autoregressive Generation of Variable-Length Sequences with Joint Discrete and Continuous Spaces

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.05680
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05680
- **Reference count**: 27
- **Primary result**: AGDC achieves superior functional correctness on semiconductor layouts (e.g., CLC 0.088 vs. 0.397) and graphic layouts (FID 4.58 vs. 186.61), and enables infinite-precision generation.

## Executive Summary
AGDC addresses precision loss in autoregressive models for hybrid discrete-continuous sequences such as semiconductor layouts and SVGs. It jointly predicts discrete tokens via a categorical distribution and continuous attributes via a diffusion process, thus avoiding quantization errors. The method incorporates an EOS logit adjustment and a differentiable length regularization to control variable sequence length. On ContLayNet (334K semiconductor layouts), AGDC significantly lowers Design Rule Check violations—e.g., Circuit Linkage Constraint drops to 0.088 from 0.397 (LT) and 0.227 (DLT)—and dramatically improves graphic layout quality (FID 4.58 vs. 186.61). For text‑to‑SVG, it produces infinite‑precision vectors, demonstrating the advantage of continuous modeling.

## Method Summary
AGDC uses a transformer decoder that processes embedded atomic units, each comprising a discrete identifier and a continuous vector. The decoder’s hidden state *z* feeds two parallel heads: a discrete branch (MLP + softmax) for categorical predictions and a continuous branch (denoising MLP) that models the continuous attributes via diffusion. An EOS logit adjustment adds an MLP‑computed offset to the end‑of‑sequence token’s logit, and a length regularization term penalizes deviation from a target expected length. The total loss combines cross‑entropy for discrete prediction, diffusion loss (with noise sampling), and the length term weighted by λ₁ = 100 and λ₂ = 0.1. During generation, discrete tokens are sampled from the adjusted logits; continuous values are obtained by reversing the diffusion (e.g., 100 DDPM steps) conditioned on *z*.

## Key Results
- On ContLayNet, AGDC reduces key DRC violations: Circuit Linkage Constraint (CLC) 0.088 vs. 0.397 (LT) and 0.227 (DLT); similar improvements in PDC, HSC, VSC.
- Graphic layout generation: FID 4.58, far surpassing prior best (e.g., 186.61).
- Text‑to‑SVG: infinite‑precision vectors with FID 48.73 (vs. 37.35 for 8‑bit IconShop).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
AGDC preserves continuous precision by modeling continuous attributes with diffusion rather than discretizing them, eliminating quantization loss. The discrete branch handles categorical primitives, while the continuous branch produces high‑fidelity numeric values. The EOS logit adjustment and length regularization provide a differentiable mechanism to steer the termination decision and overall sequence length, leading to outputs that respect domain‑specific constraints. This joint training objective yields coherent hybrid sequences that achieve superior functional correctness, as reflected in DRC metrics and FID scores.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Atomic Unit → Embedding (discrete embedding + linear projection) → Transformer Decoder → hidden state *z* → (MLP_EOS modifies EOS logit) + (MLP_DISC predicts discrete logits) + (MLP_CONT denoises continuous vector). The continuous branch also includes noise sampling and reverse diffusion steps.
- **Critical path**  
  Training: Forward pass through Transformer yields *z*. Compute discrete CE loss from MLP_DISC. For each continuous element: sample diffusion timestep *t* and noise, compute MSE using MLP_CONT. Apply EOS adjustment. Compute length loss λ₂·(E[length] − L_target)². Sum: L_total = L_d + λ₁L_c + λ₂L_ℓ.  
  Generation: Autoregressive loop: compute *z* from past tokens, adjust EOS logit, sample next discrete. If not EOS, run reverse diffusion (conditioned on *z*) to sample continuous attributes.
- **Design tradeoffs**  
  - Loss weighting (λ₁, λ₂): Balances discrete, continuous, and length objectives; imbalance degrades one aspect.  
  - Diffusion steps: More steps improve continuous precision but increase inference time; paper uses 100.  
  - EOS adjustment magnitude α: Larger α strengthens length control but may destabilize EOS prediction if over‑scaled.
- **Failure signatures**  
  - Length drift: Generated sequences too long/short, indicating weak length regularization or α.  
  - High DRC violations: Continuous values imprecise or incoherent with discrete tokens; check diffusion loss.  
  - Frequent discrete errors: Weak discrete head or loss imbalance; monitor CE loss.
- **First experiments**  
  1. Train a small‑scale AGDC (reduced hidden size) on a ContLayNet subset with default hyperparameters to verify loss convergence and basic sample quality.
  2. Ablate EOS adjustment (α=0) and length regularization (λ₂=0) separately; compare length distributions and DRC scores to isolate each component’s effect.
  3. Vary diffusion steps (e.g., 20, 50, 100), measuring inference speed vs. FID/DRC to understand the efficiency‑quality trade‑off.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How to mitigate error propagation in AGDC for long hybrid sequences?  
  *Basis*: Conclusion states all methods, including ours, struggle with longer sequences due to error propagation.  
  *Why unresolved*: Error accumulation causes functional failures; current components (EOS adjustment, length regularization) do not directly target it.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Evaluate error versus sequence length; propose and test architectural changes (e.g., recurrent feedback, latent alignment, non‑autoregressive refinement) on DRC metrics for long sequences.
- **Open Question 2**: Can AGDC be extended to handle variable‑length continuous vectors within atomic units?  
  *Basis*: Section 4.1 assumes a fixed‑size continuous vector *c_i*; variable dimensions per unit are not addressed.  
  *Why unresolved*: Many domains (e.g., CAD, 3D shapes) have continuous attributes that differ per discrete token, breaking the fixed projection matrix *W*.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Design type‑specific heads to predict a variable number of continuous values; test on synthetic or real datasets with variable continuous attribute counts, measuring precision and functional correctness.
- **Open Question 3**: Can inference speed be improved for AGDC without sacrificing quality, especially for high‑precision continuous components?  
  *Basis*: Section A.2 shows reducing diffusion steps accelerates inference but degrades DRC metrics, indicating a speed‑quality trade‑off.  
  *Why unresolved*: Diffusion steps are computationally expensive; many steps hinder practical deployment.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Develop efficient samplers (e.g., knowledge distillation, adaptive step schedules) for hybrid sequences; benchmark speed‑quality trade‑offs on ContLayNet and other benchmarks to maintain quality with fewer steps.
- **Open Question 4**: To what extent does AGDC generalize to other high‑precision hybrid sequence domains beyond the three evaluated?  
  *Basis*: The paper claims domain‑agnosticity but only validates on three domains; broader generalization remains untested.  
  *Why unresolved*: Domain‑specific constraints (e.g., 3D geometry, relational data) may require tailored adaptations; current empirical evidence insufficient.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Apply AGDC to additional domains (e.g., 3D model generation, molecular structures, ECG segmentation) with appropriate atomic unit definitions; analyze performance, identify failure modes, and assess whether the same hyperparameters and architecture transfer unchanged.

## Limitations
- Incomplete diffusion specification: exact MLP_CONT architecture (layer types, normalization, time conditioning) and noise schedule (β_t) are not detailed, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Dataset accessibility: ContLayNet’s public availability is unclear; without it, replication on the primary domain is impossible.
- Missing ablations: the paper does not isolate contributions of EOS logit adjustment (α) and length regularization (λ₂), making it hard to verify their claimed roles.

## Confidence
- Functional correctness (DRC metrics): Medium (sound method, but reproduction blocked by missing specs and data).
- Graphic layout quality (FID): Medium (dramatic improvement depends on identical evaluation setup).
- Text‑to‑SVG (FID): Low (comparison ambiguous due to bit‑depth mismatch and unclear baseline).
- Infinite‑precision generation: Medium in principle, but quantitative support hinges on the above results.
