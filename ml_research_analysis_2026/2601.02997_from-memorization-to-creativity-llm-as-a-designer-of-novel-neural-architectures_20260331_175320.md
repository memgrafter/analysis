# From Memorization to Creativity: LLM as a Designer of Novel Neural-Architectures
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.02997
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02997
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: Through 22 iterations of a closed-loop self-training cycle, the LLM significantly improves its ability to generate valid, high-performing, and structurally novel neural network architectures for CIFAR-10, producing 455 unique designs absent from its initial training data.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether an LLM can autonomously enhance its capability to design deep neural network architectures via iterative self-training. The authors implement a closed-loop synthesis cycle: the model (DeepSeek-Coder-7B-Instruct) generates PyTorch architectures, which are filtered for syntactic validity, evaluated on a low-fidelity signal (single-epoch CIFAR-10 accuracy), and checked for novelty with a MinHash–Jaccard filter. Accepted architectures are used to fine-tune the model via LoRA. Over 22 cycles, the LLM exhibits clear progress: valid generation rate stabilizes at 50.6% (peak 74.5%), mean first-epoch accuracy rises from 28.06% to 50.99%, and the fraction of candidates exceeding 40% accuracy grows from 2.04% to 96.81%. Notably, the model synthesizes 455 performant architectures not present in its initial corpus, indicating a shift from memorization toward creative, performance-guided design.

## Method Summary
The reproduction plan specifies the following pipeline. Starting with DeepSeek-Coder-7B-Instruct-v1.5, the initial training set is built by deduplicating the LEMUR Neural Network Dataset (109,913 records) to 849 chat-format prompt–code pairs covering MNIST and CIFAR-10 tasks. Each of 22 cycles proceeds: (1) generate a large pool of candidate architectures using a fixed system prompt and user instruction specifying CIFAR-10 constraints (3×32×32 input, 10 classes, ≤500k parameters), with temperature=0.2, top-k=50, nucleus-p=0.9; (2) validate each candidate by parsing, instantiating, and running a dummy forward pass; (3) train valid models for one epoch on CIFAR-10 with a fixed augmentation pipeline and record top-1 validation accuracy; (4) apply a two-stage filter: require ≥40% first-epoch accuracy, and ensure token-level novelty via MinHash–Jaccard similarity <0.90 against the current training corpus and same-cycle accepted models; (5) convert accepted models into chat-format pairs and append to the training set; (6) fine-tune the LLM with LoRA (rank=32, alpha=32, dropout=0.05) for 5 epochs on the expanded corpus (learning rate 1e-5, batch size 1, gradient accumulation 4, cosine schedule, bfloat16). Key unknowns include the exact LEMUR preprocessing script, the precise wording of prompts, and the hardware configuration used; these may affect reproducibility.

## Key Results
- Valid generation rate stabilizes at 50.6% (peaking at 74.5%).
- Mean first-epoch CIFAR-10 accuracy increases from 28.06% to 50.99% over cycles.
- The proportion of valid candidates achieving ≥40% accuracy jumps from 2.04% to 96.81%.
- The LLM eventually produces 455 high-accuracy, structurally novel architectures not found in the initial corpus.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
We present three core mechanisms that drive improvement.

### Mechanism 1: Validity Reinforcement Loop
**Claim**: Iterative fine-tuning on syntactically valid samples increases the LLM's rate of generating executable PyTorch architectures.
**Mechanism**: Each cycle, the LLM generates many candidates; only those that parse, instantiate, and pass a dummy forward are retained. These valid examples are turned into prompt–code pairs and used to fine-tune the LLM via LoRA. Repeated exposure to patterns associated with compilable code (correct API usage, tensor shape compatibility) internalizes structural priors.
**Core assumption**: Code-level patterns correlated with validity can be learned via supervised fine-tuning on valid examples and generalize to new generations.
**Evidence**: The abstract and Section 4 report the valid generation rate stabilizing at 50.6% after peaking at 74.5%; Figure 2 shows the trend.
**Break condition**: A sustained decline in the valid rate would indicate overfitting to a narrow subset of valid patterns or exhaustion of novel valid patterns within the prompt constraints.

### Mechanism 2: Performance Distribution Shift
**Claim**: Filtering training additions by a first-epoch accuracy threshold (≥40%) shifts the LLM's output distribution toward architectures that exhibit fast early learning on CIFAR-10.
**Mechanism**: After validation and single-epoch training, candidates are ranked by accuracy. Only those exceeding the threshold are added to the fine-tuning corpus. The LLM, trained via next-token prediction on these high-accuracy examples, gradually aligns its generation distribution with the implicit performance priors embedded in the selected code (e.g., useful layer sequencing, normalization placement, connectivity patterns). Performance information enters solely via data selection, not the loss function.
**Core assumption**: First-epoch validation accuracy is a meaningful low-fidelity proxy for an architecture's ultimate utility, and code snippets achieving it share discoverable textual/structural features.
**Evidence**: The abstract and Section 4 (Table 2, Figure 3) show the mean accuracy rising from 28.06% to 50.99% and the fraction ≥40% growing from 2.04% to 96.81%.
**Break condition**: The observed plateau after cycle 18 suggests saturation; further gains might require a more nuanced signal or risk overfitting to early-epoch optima that do not generalize to full training.

### Mechanism 3: Novelty Preservation via MinHash–Jaccard Filtering
**Claim**: A token-level near-duplicate filter prevents the training corpus from collapsing onto redundant code variants, sustaining structural diversity across cycles.
**Mechanism**: During candidate sampling, after passing validity and accuracy checks, the code is compared against the current training corpus and earlier samples in the same cycle via MinHash signatures over 10-gram token shingles. If the maximum Jaccard similarity exceeds τ≈0.90, the candidate is rejected as a near-duplicate. This ensures only architecturally distinct implementations are added, forcing exploration of new design regions rather than reinforcing minor variants.
**Core assumption**: Token-shingle Jaccard similarity is a computationally cheap yet effective proxy for architectural novelty at the source-code level, and avoiding near-duplicates correlates with functional diversity.
**Evidence**: The abstract notes the synthesis of 455 novel models; Section 3 details the filter; Section 4 reports cumulative novel models and ablations showing diversity collapse without it.
**Break condition**: If τ is too strict, it may exclude valid novel architectures with superficial textual similarity; if too lenient, near-duplicates accumulate, reducing effective diversity.

## Foundational Learning
The paper relies on several established concepts:

- **Low-fidelity performance proxies in NAS**: Single-epoch CIFAR-10 accuracy serves as a cheap approximation of an architecture's learning potential, making iterative search feasible. *Why needed*: Full training is computationally prohibitive. *Quick check*: What are the risks of using a single-epoch threshold, and how might they manifest in the observed plateauing?

- **Code-level novelty metrics (MinHash–Jaccard)**: Token-shingle Jaccard similarity is used as a lightweight filter to avoid redundancy in the fine-tuning corpus. *Why needed*: Prevents the self-training loop from collapsing onto repetitive patterns, maintaining diversity. *Quick check*: Why might token-level similarity fail to capture truly functionally distinct architectures, and what are the implications for the "novel" architectures reported?

- **Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA)**: Low-rank adaptation allows updating only a small subset of parameters, enabling iterative refinement of a 7B model with limited resources. *Why needed*: Full fine-tuning across many cycles would be prohibitively expensive. *Quick check*: How might fixed LoRA hyperparameters (rank=32, applied layers) limit the model's capacity to internalize new patterns over time?

## Architecture Onboarding
The system comprises the following components:

LLM Generator (DeepSeek-Coder-7B-Instruct-v1.5) -> Validity Checker (parse/instantiate/forward) -> Performance Evaluator (1-epoch CIFAR-10 training) -> Novelty Filter (MinHash archive, Jaccard <0.9) -> Corpus Builder (chat-format pairs) -> Fine-Tuning Module (LoRA) -> back to Generator (for next cycle)

Critical path: Generate N candidates -> filter for validity -> train each 1 epoch -> filter by accuracy (≥40%) -> filter by novelty -> accept -> convert to training pairs -> fine-tune LLM -> repeat.

Design tradeoffs: 
- *Novelty vs. Performance*: Strict novelty threshold may admit many low-accuracy models; loose threshold risks redundancy. The paper uses high τ=0.90 after a 40% accuracy threshold to balance.
- *Simplicity vs. Adaptivity*: Fixed LoRA hyperparameters and constant accuracy threshold simplify analysis but may not be optimal; dynamic thresholds could improve long-term gains.
- *Proxy vs. Ground Truth*: Single-epoch accuracy is cheap but imperfect; architectures that learn quickly early may plateau.

Failure signatures:
- Validity rate declines monotonically after a peak.
- Accuracy distribution plateaus early (e.g., by cycle 18) with narrow confidence intervals.
- Novelty admissions per cycle drop to near zero.
- Training corpus homogeneity increases (repetitive motifs).

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline establishment: Run the full 22-cycle loop as described; record validity, accuracy, and novelty trends.
2. Ablation - No novelty filter: Disable MinHash–Jaccard; observe if corpus diversity (unique models added) drops while accuracy trends remain similar.
3. Ablation - No accuracy threshold: Keep novelty filter but admit all valid, non-duplicate models; check if accuracy distribution shift weakens, confirming performance-guided selection is necessary.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can the self-trained LLM distribution serve as an effective architectural prior for existing NAS frameworks, improving their search efficiency or final accuracy? (Future work proposes integration with frameworks like LLMatic or SEKI.)
2. Does multi-task prompting across multiple datasets and tasks yield transferable architectural regularities, or does the learned prior remain specialized to CIFAR-10? (Interleaving tasks is suggested.)
3. Would replacing the binary 40% accuracy threshold with continuous performance signals or reinforcement learning fine-tuning accelerate convergence and improve the quality of discovered architectures? (Recommendation: performance-weighted sampling or RL.)
4. Does text-level novelty (MinHash-Jaccard over code tokens) correlate with functional diversity in terms of learned representations or performance trajectories? (Limitations note potential mismatch between textual and functional novelty.)

## Limitations
- Exact LEMUR dataset version and preprocessing details are not disclosed, hampering exact replication.
- The single-epoch CIFAR-10 accuracy proxy risks optimizing for early-learning optima that may not generalize; token-level MinHash novelty may admit functionally redundant architectures.
- Fixed hyperparameters (LoRA rank, accuracy threshold) and lack of hardware specifications limit reproducibility and may affect long-term dynamics.

## Confidence
- Validity reinforcement loop: Medium
- Performance distribution shift: Medium
- Novelty preservation: Medium
- Overall trajectory (improved validity, accuracy, novel synthesis): High
