# HybridFL: A Federated Learning Approach for Financial Crime Detection

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.19207
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19207
*   **Reference Count:** 14
*   **Primary Result:** HybridFL substantially outperforms a transaction-only local model (AUPRC: 0.53→0.80 on AMLSim, 0.65→0.78 on SWIFT) and approaches centralized performance.

## Executive Summary
HybridFL introduces a federated learning framework for financial crime detection that operates under a hybrid data partition: horizontally disjoint banks and vertically split transactions between banks and a central transaction processor. The method enables collaboration without sharing raw data by having banks maintain local account feature encoders. For each transaction, the central party fuses its transaction embedding with bank-provided sender and receiver embeddings to make a prediction and propagate gradients back to the banks. Periodic federated averaging of bank encoder parameters ensures representation compatibility. Evaluations on AMLSim and SWIFT datasets show significant improvements over isolated models and near-parity with a centralized benchmark.

## Method Summary
HybridFL treats banks as horizontally partitioned participants (non-overlapping customers) and each transaction as a vertically partitioned sample linking a sender bank, receiver bank, and transaction processor. The transaction party (active) coordinates training. It samples minibatches of transaction data, requests sender/receiver embeddings from the respective banks' local encoders, concatenates these with its own transaction embedding, and passes the result to a top fusion model for prediction. After computing loss and gradients, the transaction party sends the embedding gradients back to the banks for local backpropagation. Crucially, the sender and receiver encoder parameters across all banks are periodically synchronized via federated averaging to maintain a shared representation space.

## Key Results
*   On AMLSim, HybridFL achieves an AUPRC of 0.80, compared to 0.53 for the transaction-only baseline.
*   On the highly imbalanced SWIFT dataset, HybridFL achieves an AUPRC of 0.78 versus 0.65 for the transaction-only model.
*   Performance consistently approaches that of a centralized model that has access to all raw features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** HybridFL effectively learns from data horizontally split across disjoint banks and vertically split between banks and a transaction processor.
*   **Mechanism:** The system defines a consistent relational structure: each transaction links exactly one sender and one receiver from distinct banks. Banks act as horizontal participants. The transaction between a sender, receiver, and transaction data forms a vertical partition event. The active transaction party fuses embeddings from itself and the two involved passive banks.
*   **Core Assumption:** The hybrid partition has a stable, bipartite relational structure (one sender bank, one receiver bank per transaction).
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract]: "integrates horizontal model aggregation across banks with vertical feature fusion via embedding exchange." [section III]: "Horizontally, banks hold non-overlapping customer records, while vertically, transactions connect complementary information from multiple organisations."
*   **Break Condition:** If the relational structure is violated (e.g., multi-party transactions, overlapping customer sets between banks), the defined vertical fusion pattern fails.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Gradient flow through shared embedding spaces enables end-to-end learning without exposing raw bank features.
*   **Mechanism:** The transaction party computes loss and gradients w.r.t. the sender, receiver, and transaction embeddings (`eS`, `eR`, `eT`). It transmits only the gradients for `eS` and `eR` to the respective banks. Each bank locally backpropagates through its encoder (`h_S^b`, `h_R^b`) using these received gradients to update its parameters.
*   **Core Assumption:** The fixed-length embedding vectors are sufficient intermediaries to convey meaningful gradient signals for the bank encoders without revealing raw features (`x^b`).
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [section IV]: "The transaction party computes the loss and its gradients with respect to the three embeddings... The gradients corresponding to sender and receiver embeddings are transmitted to the respective banks." [abstract]: "vertical feature fusion via embedding exchange."
*   **Break Condition:** If the encoder transformations cause excessive information loss (e.g., insufficient embedding dimension `P`), the gradients become inadequate for effective local updates, stalling learning.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Periodic federated averaging of bank encoder parameters prevents catastrophic drift and maintains representation compatibility.
*   **Mechanism:** After local updates from embedding gradients, the sender encoder parameters (`θ_S^b`) from all banks are averaged (synchronized). The same occurs for receiver encoders (`θ_R^b`). This aligns the output distributions of different banks' encoders, ensuring the transaction party's fusion head receives embeddings from a coherent feature space.
*   **Core Assumption:** Independent local updates on non-IID bank data would cause encoder outputs for similar account behaviors to diverge across banks, rendering a single fusion head ineffective. Averaging mitigates this representation misalignment.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [section IV]: "Because all banks must learn compatible representations, their sender and receiver encoders are periodically synchronised using federated averaging." [figure 2]: Shows "Federated average of {θ^b_S, θ^b_R}" as a distinct step.
*   **Break Condition:** If banks' data distributions are extremely heterogeneous (e.g., one bank exclusively corporate, another retail), simple parameter averaging may produce a compromised global encoder that performs poorly on individual bank data distributions.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Horizontal Federated Learning (HFL)**
    *   **Why needed here:** Banks are disjoint horizontal participants. Understanding HFL's `FedAvg` principle is prerequisite to grasp how bank encoder parameters are synchronized.
    *   **Quick Check:** In standard HFL, what is aggregated on the server, and why does it assume a common feature space?
*   **Concept: Vertical Federated Learning (VFL)**
    *   **Why needed here:** The transaction-bank interaction for a single transaction is a vertical split (complementary features on overlapping samples). The core training loop (embedding exchange, gradient return) is standard VFL.
    *   **Quick Check:** In VFL, which party typically holds the labels and computes the loss? How are gradients communicated for passive party updates?
*   **Concept: Embedding as a Privacy-Preserving Intermediate Representation**
    *   **Why needed here:** The entire mechanism hinges on using fixed-length embeddings (`eT`, `eS`, `eR`) as the shared artifact instead of raw features. This is the key interface enabling collaboration.
    *   **Quick Check:** What are the two main privacy benefits of sharing embeddings instead of raw features or gradients of raw features?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    Transaction Party (Active) -> (requests embeddings, sends gradients) -> Banks (Passive)
    Transaction Party holds `D_T` (transaction features + label), runs `h_T` (transaction encoder) and `f_F` (fusion head). Each Bank `b` holds `D_b` (account features), runs two local models: `h_S^b` (sender encoder) and `h_R^b` (receiver encoder). The transaction party knows the sender bank `b_S` and receiver bank `b_R` for each transaction sample.
*   **Critical Path:**
    1.  Transaction party samples minibatch `B` from `D_T`.
    2.  For each transaction `i` in `B`, it requests `eS_i` from `b_S(i)` and `eR_i` from `b_R(i)`.
    3.  It computes `eT_i`, concatenates `[eT_i, eS_i, eR_i]`, gets prediction `ŷ_i` from `f_F`, computes loss `L`.
    4.  Computes gradients `∇_{eT}`, `∇_{eS}`, `∇_{eR}` and sends `∇_{eS}` to `b_S(i)`, `∇_{eR}` to `b_R(i)`.
    5.  Banks use received gradients to update `θ_S^b` / `θ_R^b` via local backpropagation.
    6.  Transaction party updates `θ_T` and `θ_F` using `∇_{eT}` and other gradients.
    7.  After `C` communication rounds/minibatches, **trigger federated averaging** of all `{θ_S^b}` and separately all `{θ_R^b}` across banks.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Encoder Capacity vs. Communication:** Larger embedding dimension `P` carries more information but increases communication cost (3P per transaction). Choices: `P=16` (AMLSim) vs `P=64` (SWIFT).
    *   **Separate Sender/Receiver Encoders vs. Shared:** Separate encoders model role-specific behavior but double the number of bank models to train/sync. A shared encoder simplifies but risks conflating signals.
    *   **Fusion Head Complexity:** `f_F` must learn interactions between three embedding sources. Its capacity must match the complexity of the combined feature space (input size `E=3P`).
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   **Gradient Vanishing/Explosion at Banks:** Monitor norm of received gradients (`‖∇e_S‖`, `‖∇e_R‖`). Near-zero norms indicate insufficient learning signal; exploding norms indicate instability.
    *   **Encoder Misalignment:** Without sufficient averaging, `h_S^b1` and `h_S^b2` produce embeddings in different scales/distributions, harming `f_F`. Track embedding statistics (mean, variance) across banks for semantically similar accounts.
    *   **Data Heterogeneity Collapse:** If one bank's data is vastly different (e.g., corporate vs. retail), its averaged encoder may become a poor fit for its local data, reducing local utility. Monitor per-bank validation loss.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Sanity Check - Single Bank:** Run HybridFL with only one bank (all transactions involve it as sender/receiver). Performance should match a "centralized" model where transaction and bank features are locally combined. Failure indicates broken embedding/gradient flow.
    2.  **Baseline Comparison:** Replicate Table III/IV. Verify `HybridFL AUPRC > Transaction-Party-Only AUPRC` and `HybridFL AUPRC < Centralised AUPRC`. The gap should be significant on SWIFT's imbalanced data.
    3.  **Ablation - Encoder Synchronization:** Disable the federated averaging step (skip step 7). Performance should degrade, especially for banks with fewer samples, confirming the need for alignment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions or future work. However, the identified limitations and discussion points imply several research directions:
- Scalability to a larger number of banks and extreme data heterogeneity.
- Formal privacy analysis of the embedding exchange mechanism.
- Sensitivity to hyperparameter choices (e.g., communication frequency, embedding dimension) and data preprocessing details.
- Extension to more complex transaction structures (e.g., multi-party transactions, overlapping customer sets).

## Limitations
*   **Data preprocessing specifics:** Exact definitions of derived features (e.g., transaction velocity windows, normalization methods) are unspecified, hindering faithful replication.
*   **Training hyperparameters:** Critical details like communication round frequency, local update steps per round, and federated averaging weighting (sample-size vs. uniform) are omitted.
*   **Generalizability & Scalability:** Evaluation on only 2–10 banks; scalability to extreme heterogeneity (e.g., one bank serving exclusively corporate clients) or many more participants remains untested.

## Confidence
*   **Main efficacy claim** (HybridFL outperforms transaction-only and nears centralized): **Medium** – Results are compelling but lack independent replication or theoretical guarantees.
*   **Mechanism 1** (hybrid partition structure works under defined relational constraints): **Medium** – Logical design matches dataset schemas, but break conditions are untested.
*   **Mechanism 2** (gradient flow via embeddings preserves privacy and enables learning): **Medium** – VFL literature supports plausibility, but no analysis of embedding information leakage.
*   **Mechanism 3** (federated averaging aligns encoders): **Low–Medium** – Only paper’s own evidence; neighbors discuss embedding aggregation, not parameter averaging for hybrid roles.
*   **Privacy guarantees:** **Low** – No formal privacy proof (e.g., differential privacy); embedding exposure risk unquantified.

## Next Checks
1.  **Feature pipeline replication:** Implement the exact feature extraction logic (including derived features and normalization) on a small sample of AMLSim/SWIFT. Compare feature distributions against paper’s Table II descriptions.
2.  **Federated averaging ablation:** Run HybridFL with averaging disabled and with uniform vs. sample-size weighting. Measure (a) performance degradation, and (b) encoder misalignment via cosine similarity of `h_S` outputs across banks for similar account patterns.
3.  **Gradient norm diagnostics:** Log per-bank gradient norms (`‖∇θ_S^b‖`, `‖∇θ_R^b‖`) and embedding gradients (`‖∇e_S‖`, `‖∇e_R‖`) during training on SWIFT. Identify vanishing/exploding trends, especially for minority-class transactions.
