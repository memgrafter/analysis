# Training-free Composition of Pre-trained GFlowNets for Multi-Objective Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21565  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21565  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A training‑free inference‑time composition of pre‑trained GFlowNets that exactly recovers the target distribution for linear scalarization and accurately approximates it for nonlinear logical operators, yielding up to 70× speedups while preserving sample validity.

## Executive Summary
Existing multi‑objective GFlowNets require retraining from scratch for every new combination of objectives, which is computationally expensive. This paper introduces a training‑free framework that composes multiple pre‑trained single‑objective GFlowNets at inference time. The method mixes the forward policies of the base models, weighting each by its state reaching probability derived from the learned state flow. For linear scalarization, the induced distribution provably matches the desired target; for nonlinear logical operators (e.g., harmonic mean, contrast) it yields an accurate approximation whose error concentrates in low‑density regions. Experiments on synthetic 2D grids show lower L1 error than preference‑trained baselines and comparable performance to classifier guidance. On real‑world molecule generation tasks, the approach matches or exceeds the sample quality (average reward, diversity) of retraining‑based methods while being 40–70× faster for logical operators and preserving validity where classifier guidance fails.

## Method Summary
The method takes *k* pre‑trained GFlowNets, each trained on a single reward function \(R_i(x)\). Every base model provides a forward policy \(p_{i,F}(s'|s)\), a state flow estimator \(F_i(s)\), and a partition function \(Z_i\). At inference, for a current state \(s\), the composed forward policy is defined as  
\[
p_{M,F}(s'|s) \propto G\bigl(u_1(s)p_{1,F}(s'|s),\dots,u_k(s)p_{k,F}(s'|s)\bigr),
\]  
where \(u_i(s) = F_i(s)/Z_i\) is the state reaching probability and \(G\) is a user‑chosen composition operator (e.g., weighted sum for linear scalarization, harmonic mean or contrast for logical operations). The normalizing constant \(N_M(s)\) sums the values over all possible successors. For linear scalarization with exponent \(\beta=1\), the distortion factor becomes constant across all terminating states, guaranteeing exact recovery of the target distribution. For nonlinear \(G\), the distribution approximates the target with error that empirically concentrates in low‑density regions. No additional training is required; the base GFlowNets can be trained with any flow‑matching objective (Sub‑TB, FM, DB). The generator follows the mixed policy to sample trajectories until termination, producing final samples.

## Key Results
- Eliminates the need for retraining when changing objective combinations, enabling flexible multi‑objective generation at inference time.
- Theoretically, exact recovery of the target distribution for linear scalarization; for nonlinear logical operators, accurate approximation with error concentrated in low‑density regions.
- On molecule generation (fragment‑based and atom‑based), matches or exceeds the sample quality (average reward, diversity) of retraining‑based methods, while achieving 40–70× speedups for logical operators and preserving validity where classifier guidance fails.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The mixing policy weights each pre‑trained GFlowNet’s forward transitions by its state reaching probability \(u_i(s)\), which prioritizes models that route more probability mass through the current state.
- **Mechanism:** For a state \(s\), the composed forward policy computes \(u_i(s) \cdot p_{i,F}(s'|s)\) for each model \(i\), then applies the composition function \(G\) (e.g., weighted sum, harmonic mean) and normalizes. The reaching probability \(u_i(s)\) is derived from the learned state flow \(F_i(s)\) via \(u_i(s) = F_i(s) / Z_i\).
- **Core assumption:** The state flow \(F_i(s)\) learned by each GFlowNet accurately estimates the true flow from \(s\) to the sink, and the partition function \(Z_i\) is well‑estimated (e.g., via flow‑matching or detailed‑balance objectives).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "mixing their forward policies, weighted by each model’s state reaching probability derived from learned flows."
  - [section 4.1]: Definition of mixing policy \(p_{M,F}\) in Eq. (4) and reaching probability estimation via \(F_i(s)/Z_i\).
  - [corpus]: Weak direct corpus support; neighbor papers like *Order‑Preserving GFlowNets* and *Embarrassingly Parallel GFlowNets* address GFlowNet training nuances but not composition.
- **Break condition:** If the base GFlowNets are poorly trained such that \(F_i(s)\) does not reflect true reaching probabilities (e.g., due to mode collapse or inadequate exploration), the weighting becomes misleading, degrading the composed distribution.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** For linear scalarization (\(G(p_1,\dots,p_k) = \sum_i \omega_i Z_i p_i\)), the distortion factor \(\delta(x) = u_M(x) / N_M(x)\) is constant across all \(x\), ensuring exact recovery of the target distribution.
- **Mechanism:** The linearity of \(G\) causes the normalization constant \(N_M(s)\) to simplify to \(u_M(s)\) (sum of \(v_i u_i(s)\) with \(v_i = \omega_i Z_i / \sum_j \omega_j Z_j\)). This forces \(\delta(x) = 1/Z_M\) for all terminating states \(x\), as shown in Proposition 4.1.
- **Core assumption:** The base GFlowNets are trained to satisfy flow‑matching conditions exactly; the mixing policy’s recursion correctly propagates \(u_M(s)\).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "provably recovers the exact target distribution; for nonlinear logical operators… error concentrates in low‑density regions."
  - [section 4.2 & A.1]: Proof that \(\delta(x)\) is constant for linear scalarization with \(\beta=1\).
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; neighboring work on multi‑objective GFlowNets (e.g., MOGFN) uses retraining.
- **Break condition:** For nonlinear \(G\) (e.g., harmonic mean, contrast, or scalarization with \(\beta\neq1\)), \(\delta(x)\) varies, causing approximation error. The error is concentrated in low‑density regions where \(G(p_i)\) is small, potentially missing rare but high‑reward states.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The approximation error for nonlinear operators is bounded in high‑density regions because \(\delta(x)\) remains close to \(1/Z_M\) where \(G(p_1,\dots,p_k)\) is large.
- **Mechanism:** The L1 error decomposes as \(\sum_x |\delta(x) - 1/Z_M| \cdot G(\dots)\). Empirically (Section 5.3), \(\delta(x)\) deviates primarily when \(G(\dots)\) is small (low‑density/outlier states), so the product \(|\delta - 1/Z_M| \cdot G(\dots)\) remains small overall.
- **Core assumption:** The target distribution’s mass is concentrated in high‑density regions; low‑density regions contribute negligibly to the overall L1 error.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.3]: Fig. 3 shows \(\delta(x)\) vs. \(G(p_1,p_2)\) for logical operators; outliers (low \(G\)) have high \(\delta\) variance but low impact on L1.
  - [section 5.2]: Table 2 shows low L1 errors for harmonic mean/contrast, comparable to classifier guidance despite approximation.
  - [corpus]: Corpus does not directly address distortion analysis; related work on compositional sculpting uses classifiers, not flow mixing.
- **Break condition:** If the target distribution has significant mass in low‑density regions (e.g., multi‑modal with isolated high‑reward islands), the approximation error may become substantial because \(\delta(x)\) is uncontrolled there.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** GFlowNet reaching probability and state flow.  
  *Why needed here:* The mixing policy’s weighting \(u_i(s)\) is defined via reaching probability, which is derived from state flow \(F_i(s)\) (Appendix A.2). Understanding how \(u_i(s)\) relates to the learned flow is essential to grasp why the weighting makes sense.  
  *Quick check question:* How does the training objective (e.g., SubTB) ensure that \(F_i(s)\) approximates the true flow from \(s\) to sink?

- **Concept:** Multi‑objective composition operators (scalarization vs. logical operators).  
  *Why needed here:* The method handles both linear scalarization (weighted sum) and nonlinear logical operators (harmonic mean, contrast). Recognizing their algebraic forms and intended semantics (e.g., conjunction vs. subtraction) is crucial for applying the correct \(G\).  
  *Quick check question:* What is the intuitive meaning of the harmonic mean operator \(p_1 \otimes p_2\) in terms of high‑reward regions?

- **Concept:** Distortion factor \(\delta(x) = u_M(x)/N_M(x)\).  
  *Why needed here:* It quantifies the gap between the induced distribution \(p_M(x)\) and the target \(p^*_M(x)\). Exact recovery for linear scalarization occurs when \(\delta\) is constant.  
  *Quick check question:* Why does linear scalarization guarantee constant \(\delta(x)\) while nonlinear operators do not?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
1. Base GFlowNets: *k* pre‑trained models, each with forward policy \(p_{i,F}\), state flow estimator \(F_i(\cdot)\), and partition function \(Z_i\). Trained separately on rewards \(R_i\).  
2. Mixing module: At inference, for each state \(s\), query all *k* models to get \(u_i(s)=F_i(s)/Z_i\) and \(p_{i,F}(s'|s)\). Compute weighted sum/product \(G(u_i(s)p_{i,F}(\cdot))\), normalize via \(N_M(s)\) to obtain \(p_{M,F}(s'|s)\).  
3. Composition function \(G\): User‑specified operator (e.g., \(\sum_i \omega_i Z_i p_i\) for scalarization, harmonic mean for conjunction). Encodes the multi‑objective goal.

**Critical path:** The generator must (a) reach a state \(s\), (b) compute \(u_i(s)\) for all \(i\) using stored \(F_i\) and \(Z_i\), (c) evaluate \(p_{i,F}(s'|s)\) for all successors \(s'\), (d) apply \(G\), (e) normalize, (f) sample next state. This is performed stepwise during trajectory generation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Memory vs. speed:** Storing \(F_i(s)\) for visited states speeds repeated visits but increases memory; otherwise compute on‑the‑fly via network forward pass.  
- **Parallelism:** *k* forward passes per step are independent; can be parallelized across models but adds overhead.  
- **Operator choice:** Linear scalarization is exact but limited to convex combinations; logical operators enable subtraction/conjunction but introduce approximation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Invalid molecules:** If base models are valid but composed samples become invalid, likely due to operator distortion steering into invalid regions (observed with classifier guidance).  
- **Blurry modes:** In scalarization, if \(\delta(x)\) varies widely, the induced distribution may underrepresent certain Pareto‑optimal regions. Check by comparing \(\delta(x)\) across high‑reward states.  
- **Slow inference:** For logical operators, if \(N_M(s)\) computation requires evaluating all successors for each model (like classifier guidance), it becomes expensive. The mixing policy only evaluates \(u_i(s)\) at current \(s\); ensure implementation avoids unnecessary successor evaluations.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Synthetic 2D grid validation:** Pre‑train single‑objective GFlowNets on known reward landscapes. Apply mixing for scalarization (varying \(\omega\)) and logical operators. Compute L1 error against analytically known target distributions (Section 5.1). Verify exactness for linear case and approximation quality for nonlinear.  
2. **Molecular generation sanity check:** For fragment‑based task, use pre‑trained SEH, SA, QED GFlowNets. Compose via scalarization (e.g., \(0.5R_\text{SEH} + 0.5R_\text{QED}\)). Check that top‑10 samples show balanced improvements on both objectives (Table 3) and that diversity is maintained.  
3. **Logical operator contrast test:** Compose \(p_\text{SA} \cdot p_\text{SEH}\). Verify that generated samples have high SA but low SEH (target bin in Table A3). Compare with classifier guidance to confirm that mixing does not degrade validity (Table 6).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the proposed mixing policy be extended to GFlowNets trained with the Trajectory Balance objective, which does not explicitly learn state flows \(F_i(s)\)?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Appendix A.2 explains that the method requires \(F_i(s)\) and \(Z_i\), which are directly available from flow‑matching, detailed balance, and sub‑trajectory balance objectives, but not from Trajectory Balance (TB). This limitation is not addressed.  
- **Why unresolved:** Trajectory Balance is a widely used GFlowNet training objective; extending the mixing framework to TB‑incompatible models would broaden its applicability without retraining.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A practical estimator for \(F_i(s)\) derived from TB parameters (e.g., via backward policies or auxiliary networks) and experimental validation showing that the resulting mixing policy approximates the target distribution comparably to flow‑based objectives.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What theoretical bounds can be derived on the total variation distance between the distribution induced by the mixing policy and the target distribution for non‑linear composition operators?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 4.2 states that for non‑linear operators the distortion factor \(\delta(x)\) is not constant and that the authors “empirically show that our mixing rule provides an accurate approximation in high‑density regions,” but no formal bounds are provided.  
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical guarantees are needed to understand when and how well the approximation holds for operators such as \(\beta\neq1\) or logical operations, especially in low‑density regions where errors concentrate.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A mathematical analysis linking \(\delta(x)\) to properties of component distributions (e.g., support overlap, smoothness) and a derived bound on the total variation distance; empirical verification across diverse reward functions showing the bound is informative and often tight.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does the performance of the mixing policy relative to classifier guidance vary across different compositions (e.g., sometimes better, sometimes worse), and what factors determine this variation?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The text (Section 5.2, Table 2) reports that for logical operators the method achieves “comparable performance to the classifier‑guided approach, with results varying across different composition types: our method outperforms on some compositions while the classifier‑guided baseline performs better on others.”  
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding the factors that favor one method over the other would guide practitioners in selecting the appropriate approach for a given multi‑objective problem without costly trial‑and‑error.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments systematically varying reward distributions (e.g., correlation, modality), operator types (harmonic mean vs. contrast), and dimensionality; analysis correlating performance gaps with characteristics such as the overlap of component distributions or the curvature of the composition function.

## Limitations
- Relies on accurate state flow estimates from pre‑trained GFlowNets; poorly trained base models (e.g., mode collapse, inadequate exploration) degrade composition.
- Nonlinear operators (harmonic mean, contrast) assume error concentrates in low‑density regions; this may fail for multi‑modal targets with significant mass in rare states.
- Architectural and hyperparameter details for the molecule‑generation Graph Transformer are under‑specified, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- Training‑free composition via reaching‑probability weighted mixing: **Medium**  
- Exact recovery for linear scalarization: **High**  
- Accurate approximation for nonlinear logical operators: **Medium**
