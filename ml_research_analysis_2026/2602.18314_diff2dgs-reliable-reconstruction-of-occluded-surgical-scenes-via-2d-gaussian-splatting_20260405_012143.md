# Diff2DGS: Reliable Reconstruction of Occluded Surgical Scenes via 2D Gaussian Splatting
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18314
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18314
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art image quality (38.02 dB PSNR on EndoNeRF) and significantly lower depth error in occluded regions (8.21 mm RMSE on SCARED) by explicitly optimizing geometry via adaptive depth loss.

## Executive Summary
Surgical scene reconstruction is challenged by tissue deformation and persistent instrument occlusion, which corrupt geometry even if rendered images appear plausible. Diff2DGS introduces a two-stage pipeline to address this: first, a temporally consistent diffusion model inpaints tissue regions obscured by instruments; second, a deformable 2D Gaussian Splatting framework with a Learnable Deformation Model (LDM) and an adaptive depth loss models tissue dynamics and forces accurate 3D geometry. Evaluations on EndoNeRF, StereoMIS, and the depth-ground-truth SCARED dataset show that optimizing for depth—particularly in occluded zones—is essential for reliable reconstruction, not just high image quality metrics like PSNR.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two stages. Stage 1 employs a temporal diffusion inpainting model (based on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with temporal attention) to generate plausible, temporally coherent tissue appearances behind instruments. This model is fine-tuned on surgical data using a mask-weighted L2 loss in latent space, with inference accelerated by a Phased Consistency Model to 2 steps. Stage 2 initializes a 2D Gaussian Splatting representation from inpainted frames using stereo depth (e.g., from RAFT). The core innovation is a Learnable Deformation Model (LDM) that learns per-Gaussian deformation parameters via a weighted sum of Gaussian basis functions, applied to position, rotation, and scale. Crucially, an adaptive depth loss modulates its weight over time based on a function of the rendered alpha (`α=0.5`), compelling the optimization to prioritize geometric accuracy in occluded regions where image-only losses are unreliable.

## Key Results
- **Image quality:** State-of-the-art PSNR on EndoNeRF (38.02 dB) and StereoMIS (34.40 dB).
- **Geometry in occlusion:** Significantly lower depth RMSE in occluded regions on SCARED (8.21 mm) compared to prior work.
- **Core insight:** High image quality alone does not guarantee accurate 3D geometry; explicit depth optimization (via adaptive loss) is necessary for reliable reconstruction under occlusion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The two-stage separation allows the diffusion model to handle complex appearance hallucination with temporal priors, while the 2DGS+ LDM focuses on efficient, deformable geometry modeling. The adaptive depth loss is the key mechanism: it increases the influence of depth supervision precisely in regions where image reconstruction loss is ambiguous (i.e., instrument-occluded tissue), thereby preventing the model from fitting plausible but geometrically incorrect surfaces. By marrying a representation (2DGS) that excels at rendering quality with a deformation model that maintains explicit geometric constraints, the system achieves both visual fidelity and metric depth accuracy where prior methods failed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Temporal Diffusion Inpainting:** Why needed: To generate plausible, temporally coherent tissue appearances behind moving instruments where no signal exists. Quick check: Measure Temporal Consistency Score (TCS) on inpainted sequences; higher indicates better temporal coherence.
- **2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS):** Why needed: Provides an efficient, differentiable rendering pipeline for high-quality novel view synthesis with explicit per-pixel geometry. Quick check: Render a held-out frame and compute PSNR/SSIM against ground truth; should match reported baselines.
- **Learnable Deformation Model (LDM):** Why needed: To model non-rigid tissue motion without explicit biomechanical priors, using a compact set of basis functions. Quick check: Visualize deformation fields over time; they should be smooth and correspond to expected tissue motion.
- **Adaptive Depth Loss:** Why needed: To dynamically weight geometry supervision, focusing on ambiguous (occluded) regions where image loss is insufficient. Quick check: Monitor per-iteration λ_depth(t) values; they should be higher in early training and for pixels with low alpha (`α`).
- **Stereo Depth Estimation:** Why needed: To provide initial 3D geometry for Gaussian initialization from video frames. Quick check: Compare estimated depth (e.g., from RAFT) against sparse ground truth (if available) to ensure reasonable initialization.
- **Deformable Radiance Fields:** Why needed: To represent time-varying scenes within a unified optimizable framework. Quick check: Render the same viewpoint at different times and compute difference; should show plausible deformation, not static artifacts.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Surgical Videos -> Inpainting Diffusion Model (w/ Temporal Attention) -> Inpainted Frames + Stereo Depth -> 2DGS Initialization -> Deformable 2DGS (w/ LDM & Adaptive Depth Loss) -> Optimized Gaussian Parameters -> Rendered Views & Depth

**Critical path:** Inpainting quality directly affects Gaussian initialization; adaptive depth loss schedule must be tuned to avoid over-flattening or noisy geometry. Failure often originates from one stage propagating errors to the next.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- Inpainting speed vs. quality: PCM acceleration reduces steps but may degrade temporal consistency.
- LDM complexity vs. generalization: More basis functions capture finer deformation but risk overfitting to training sequences.
- Depth loss weight: Too high leads to flat, blurry renderings; too high λ at wrong times causes instability.

**Failure signatures:**
- Blurry or temporally flickering inpainted regions: Check masked PSNR and TCS on inpainted frames.
- Noisy or inaccurate depth in occluded zones: Visualize rendered depth vs. GT; likely λ_depth is too low or initialization poor.
- Training divergence or NaNs: Common if Gaussian count not frozen early (first 600 iters) or λ_depth initial weight too high.
- Over-smoothed renderings with decent PSNR but high depth RMSE: Indicates over-reliance on image loss; reduce depth loss weight or adjust α.

**First three experiments:**
1. Validate the inpainting module alone: Fine-tune on a subset of SCARED, compute TCS and masked PSNR on validation sequences to confirm temporal coherence before proceeding to 3D.
2. Compare depth initialization quality: For a few frames, render depth from initialized Gaussians using ground-truth SCARED depth vs. RAFT-predicted depth; quantify RMSE to assess sensitivity to stereo depth accuracy.
3. Ablate the adaptive depth loss: Train with fixed λ_depth (no adaptation) and with varying w_init/w_final; measure occluded depth RMSE on SCARED validation to confirm the adaptation mechanism's contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can camera motion modeling be integrated into Diff2DGS to handle scenarios with significant endoscope movement?
- **Open Question 2:** What are the limitations of diffusion-based inpainting in reconstructing severely occluded or highly deformable tissue regions, and how can inpainting fidelity be improved?
- **Open Question 3:** How accurately does the Learnable Deformation Model (LDM) capture the non-linear and anisotropic properties of tissue deformation, and does it generalize to unseen deformation patterns?
- **Open Question 4:** Are current evaluation metrics (e.g., PSNR, SSIM, depth RMSE) sufficient for assessing surgical scene reconstruction for clinical applications, or are new geometry-centric metrics needed?

## Limitations
- Unspecified LDM architecture details (number of basis Gaussians, network structure, parameter initialization) hinder exact replication.
- Missing exact hyperparameters for adaptive depth loss (w_init, w_final, β in g(r)=1+β·tanh(r−1)) require empirical tuning.
- Dependence on high-quality stereo depth for initialization; performance may degrade if RAFT or similar stereo matching fails on challenging tissue textures.
- Instrument segmentation masks for SCARED are not explicitly addressed; their availability or need for a separate detector is unclear.
- General reliability claim is medium-low due to lack of robustness validation across diverse occlusion patterns and camera motions.

## Confidence

- **Claim:** Occluded-region depth RMSE on SCARED (8.21 mm) is significantly better than prior work. **Confidence:** Medium (core adaptive depth loss mechanism described but critical hyperparameters missing).
- **Claim:** The framework yields reliable reconstruction for deformable, instrument-occluded scenes. **Confidence:** Medium-Low (depends on unspecified factors and lacks failure mode analysis).
