# Do Images Clarify? A Study on the Effect of Images on Clarifying Questions in Conversational Search
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08700
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08700
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Multimodal clarifying questions increase user preference and query reformulation precision but lead to lower retrieval performance for answering tasks compared to text-only; images also help maintain engagement across expertise levels.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the effect of adding images to clarifying questions in conversational search. A controlled within-subjects study with 73 participants compared text-only and multimodal (text + image) clarifying questions across two tasks: answering the clarifying question and reformulating the search query. Results reveal a task-dependent dichotomy: while users strongly prefer multimodal questions for answering, text-only answers yield higher retrieval performance (NDCG@1: 0.185 vs. 0.079). In contrast, for query reformulation, images improve precision and retrieval performance (NDCG@1: 0.204 vs. 0.091). Additionally, images help sustain user engagement across different expertise levels and are most beneficial for queries about physical objects and processes. The findings underscore the need for adaptive multimodal clarification strategies that consider task type and user characteristics.

## Method Summary
The study employed a within-subjects experimental design with 73 participants recruited via Prolific. The experiment used 30 search topics adapted from the ClariQ dataset and curated to be visually relevant. Each topic was associated with a clarifying question presented either in a text-only or multimodal (text plus one or two images retrieved from Google) condition. Participants completed 6 topics per condition, with order counterbalanced, performing either (i) an answering task or (ii) a query reformulation task (separate participant pools for each task). Data collection included post-clarification ratings (clarity, usefulness, satisfaction on 5-point scales), response text, completion time, and an exit questionnaire. Retrieval performance was measured using NDCG@1,5,10 via BM25 on participant-generated answers or reformulations. Statistical analysis involved mixed-effects models (participant as random effect), independent t‑tests with Bonferroni correction, ANOVA with Tukey HSD for expertise effects, and thematic analysis of open-ended feedback.

## Key Results
- **Answering task:** Text-only answers achieved significantly higher retrieval performance (NDCG@1: 0.185) than multimodal answers (NDCG@1: 0.079), despite users’ strong preference for multimodal questions.
- **Reformulation task:** Multimodal clarifying questions produced more precise query reformulations and higher retrieval performance (NDCG@1: 0.204 vs. 0.091 for text‑only).
- **Expertise and query type:** Images helped maintain engagement across novice, familiar, and expert users, and were reported most useful for queries concerning physical objects (≈50%) and processes (14–19%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Visual Anchoring for Precision**  
Images provide concrete visual reference points that help users generate more specific and precise responses or reformulations, particularly for tangible queries (e.g., physical objects, processes). By mapping visual features directly to textual descriptors, users can narrow search intent more effectively. Evidence: The reformulation condition showed higher precision and NDCG@1 when images were present; thematic analysis highlighted “contextual enhancement” and “cognitive efficiency” as frequent user rationales. Break condition: For abstract or highly conceptual queries, images may introduce cognitive overload and decrease precision.

**Mechanism 2: Task‑Dependent Retrieval Effects**  
The impact of images differs fundamentally between answering and reformulation tasks. In reformulation, images offer immediate contextual grounding, leading to tighter query expressions and better retrieval. In answering, the absence of images forces users to produce more comprehensive textual descriptions that, coincidentally, align better with a text‑only retrieval model (BM25) used in the study. Evidence: Contrasting NDCG patterns between tasks—without‑image answers were longer yet ranked higher. Break condition: If the retrieval backend incorporated visual embeddings, the compensatory advantage of text‑only answers could diminish.

**Mechanism 3: Expertise Gap Mitigation**  
Images act as knowledge mediators, offering layered interpretation that benefits both novices and experts. They supply foundational context for novices while allowing experts to extract nuanced details, thereby reducing performance gaps observed under text-only conditions. Evidence: In the without‑image condition, higher background knowledge correlated with lower clarity/satisfaction ratings; with images, this negative correlation was less pronounced. Break condition: Highly technical images without explanatory anchors may primarily benefit experts, potentially widening the gap.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Query Analyzer -> Image Retriever/Selector -> Clarification Generator -> Response/Reformulation Processor -> Retrieval Engine -> Feedback Loop

**Critical path:**  
1. User issues an initial query.  
2. Query Analyzer classifies query type and estimates user expertise.  
3. If ambiguity is detected, Clarification Generator formulates a clarifying question, optionally including an image from Image Retriever.  
4. User responds with either an answer or a reformulated query.  
5. Response/Reformulation Processor extracts key terms.  
6. Retrieval Engine (text‑based, e.g., BM25) retrieves and ranks documents.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Image relevance vs. cognitive load:* More images or complex diagrams may aid precision but risk overload, especially for abstract queries.  
- *Static vs. adaptive multimodal strategy:* Fixed rules (e.g., always add images for physical objects) are simple but may ignore individual expertise; dynamic per‑user modeling is complex but could maximize utility.  
- *Retrieval model alignment:* A text‑only backend requires that images indirectly improve textual quality; a multimodal backend could directly leverage image content.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low user preference for multimodal in abstract/conceptual tasks.  
- Retrieval performance drop when images are present in question‑answering (NDCG decrease).  
- High variance in reformulation quality when images are irrelevant.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Replication with varied image selection:* Test sensitivity to image relevance/quality using different retrieval models (e.g., CLIP‑based vs. keyword‑based).  
2. *Dynamic image gating:* Implement a classifier to predict whether a query type benefits from images (e.g., physical objects/processes) and compare against always‑on multimodal.  
3. *Expertise‑adaptive presentation:* For known expert users, replace generic images with technical diagrams/schematics; measure engagement and retrieval gains relative to uniform image presentation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How should multimodal clarifying questions be adaptively designed based on task type (answering vs. reformulation) to maximize both user satisfaction and retrieval performance?  
*Basis:* Explicitly stated in the discussion; calls for adaptive visual enhancement strategies.  
*Why unresolved:* The study identifies differences but does not provide concrete design principles for varying image presentation per task.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Controlled experiments manipulating image attributes (e.g., detail, quantity) separately for answering and reformulation tasks, measuring satisfaction, engagement, and retrieval metrics.

**Open Question 2:** What specific visual characteristics of images (e.g., level of detail, contextual relevance, abstraction) most effectively support user comprehension and query reformulation for different query types?  
*Basis:* Inferred from the limitation noting the study did not explore how different image characteristics affect interaction.  
*Why unresolved:* Images were curated but not systematically varied; the driving properties remain unknown.  
*What evidence would resolve:* A factorial design study with image characteristics (schematic vs. realistic, high vs. low detail) across query types, analyzing user responses and reformulations.

**Open Question 3:** How do multimodal clarifying questions influence long‑term user engagement, learning, and search efficiency in conversational search systems compared to text‑only approaches?  
*Basis:* Explicit future work call.  
*Why unresolved:* The study was single‑session; no data on repeated exposure effects.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Longitudinal field studies or multi‑session lab experiments over weeks, tracking changes in query complexity, clarification acceptance, task success, and self‑reported learning.

**Open Question 4:** To what extent does the retrieval model’s modality (text‑only vs. multimodal embedding) moderate the effectiveness of image‑enhanced clarifying questions on retrieval performance?  
*Basis:* Inferred from the note that BM25 may explain the preference‑performance mismatch.  
*Why unresolved:* The mismatch may stem from using a text‑only retriever; true utility of image‑guided responses unclear with a multimodal backend.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Re‑evaluate the collected user responses using a multimodal retriever (e.g., dual‑encoder) and compare performance gains across conditions.

**Open Question 5:** What cognitive mechanisms explain the observed mismatch between user preference for multimodal clarifying questions and the superior retrieval performance of text‑only answers in question‑answering tasks?  
*Basis:* Inferred from the contrast; paper speculates but does not probe cognitive causes.  
*Why unresolved:* Outcomes were measured but underlying processes (e.g., cognitive load, depth of processing) were not.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Mixed‑methods studies with eye‑tracking, think‑aloud protocols, and cognitive load measures during answering, correlated with response quality and retrieval metrics.

## Limitations
- Exact details of topics, clarifying questions, images, corpus, and relevance judgments are unavailable, hindering faithful replication.
- The study used a text‑only retrieval model (BM25); findings may not generalize to systems with multimodal retrieval capabilities.
- Sample size (N=73) may be underpowered to detect subtle interaction effects, such as expertise × modality.

## Confidence
- **Images increase precision/help reformulation:** High
- **Task‑dependent effects; text‑only Q&A better:** High
- **Images mitigate expertise effects:** Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Image Relevance Sensitivity Test:** Re‑run reformulation tasks using three image retrieval sources (Google, CLIP‑based, human‑curated) to establish a relevance threshold where benefits plateau or reverse.
2. **Query‑Type Gating Validation:** Implement a pre‑classifier to predict whether a query is of a “physical object/process” type; compare fixed multimodal against classifier‑gated multimodal on NDCG and user satisfaction.
3. **Expertise‑Adaptive Presentation:** For known expert users, replace generic images with technical diagrams/schematics; measure whether this maintains the mitigated expertise gap or exacerbates it.
