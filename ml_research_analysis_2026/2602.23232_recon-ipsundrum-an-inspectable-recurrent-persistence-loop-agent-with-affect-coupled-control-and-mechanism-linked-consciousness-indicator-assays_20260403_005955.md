# ReCoN-Ipsundrum: An Inspectable Recurrent Persistence Loop Agent with Affect-Coupled Control and Mechanism-Linked Consciousness Indicator Assays

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23232
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23232
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** A recurrent persistence loop causally supports post-stimulus sensory persistence, while an affect-coupled control proxy enables stable scenic preference, structured local investigation, and prolonged planned caution—dissociable mechanisms linked to hypothetical consciousness markers.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ReCoN-Ipsundrum, an inspectable agent that extends a ReCoN state machine with a recurrent "ipsundrum" loop and an optional affect proxy. The work addresses a key challenge in machine consciousness assessment: moving beyond behavioral markers that could be gamed toward mechanism-linked indicators. Through fixed-parameter ablations and targeted within-episode lesions across a suite of controlled assays, the study demonstrates two core dissociations. First, recurrence in the ipsundrum is necessary for sustained internal salience signals after a stimulus ends. Second, affect coupling into the control loop is necessary for stable preference under novelty competition, structured scanning during reward-free exploration, and lingering caution after a hazard. The evidence supports the implementability of Humphrey's ipsundrum hypothesis and links affect-modulated control to specific, non-recurrence-dependent marker-like behaviors.

## Method Summary
The core method is an inspectable, hand-tuned agent built on a ReCoN (Recursive Constructivist) state machine. The agent's architecture includes a sensory state \(N_s\), an internal salience signal \(N^s\), an optional recurrent ipsundrum loop that updates \(N^s\) via a gated integration of sensory drive and an efference copy, and an optional affect proxy that computes valence \(N_v\), arousal \(N_a\), and a body budget \(bb\). Action selection uses a forward model to simulate outcomes and score them with a weighted sum that incorporates these internal signals. Evaluation uses three primary assays: a familiarity-controlled corridor test for "qualiaphilia" (stable scenic preference), a reward-free exploratory play grid for structured local investigation, and a "pain-tail" hazard probe for prolonged planned caution. Causal attribution is achieved via within-episode lesions that selectively disable the recurrent feedback integration or the affect terms in the scoring function, allowing isolated measurement of each mechanism's contribution to specific behavioral and internal signal markers.

## Key Results
- Recurrent persistence (ipsundrum) is causally necessary for post-stimulus sensory persistence: lesioning the feedback+integration reduces \(N^s\) AUC by 27.62 (27.9%) in ipsundrum variants, while ReCoN remains unaffected.
- Affect coupling enables stable scenic preference under novelty competition (Δscenic-entry = 0.01 vs. 0.07 for non-affect variants) and persists even when the scenic route is less novel.
- Affect coupling drives structured exploratory play (scan events 31.4 vs. 0.9; cycle score 7.6) and prolonged planned caution after hazard exposure (tail duration ~90 steps vs. 5), dissociating these from recurrence alone.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The recurrent persistence loop (ipsundrum) causally supports post-stimulus sensory persistence in the internal salience signal \(N^s\).
- **Mechanism:** A gated recurrent update integrates current sensory drive \(I_t\) with a low-pass-filtered efference copy \(E_{t-1}\) and precision \(\pi_t\) (Eq. 1–6). This creates a decaying yet self-sustaining "thick moment" where \(N^s\) remains elevated after a transient stimulus ends.
- **Core assumption:** Humphrey's ipsundrum hypothesis—that privatized, recurrent sensorimotor loops generate sustained sensation—is implementable as a state update function and that persistence is measurable as area under the curve (AUC) of \(N^s\) above baseline after stimulus removal.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Lesioning feedback+integration selectively reduces post-stimulus persistence in ipsundrum variants (AUC drop 27.62, 27.9%) while leaving ReCoN unchanged.
  - Figure 4 shows no AUC drop for ReCoN but significant drops for Ipsundrum (~19.12) and Ipsundrum+affect (~27.62) when feedback+integration is lesioned.
  - Corpus neighbors focus on unrelated "Recon" systems; no direct evidence for recurrence-driven persistence.
- **Break condition:** If lesioning recurrent feedback fails to reduce \(N^s\) persistence (AUC drop ≈ 0), the implemented recurrence mechanism is not the source of observed persistence, or the loop gain is too low.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Affect coupling (a constructionist interoception proxy modulating control) enables stable preference for scenic routes under novelty competition, decoupling value from novelty-seeking.
- **Mechanism:** Affect variables (valence \(N_v\), arousal \(N_a\), budget \(bb\)) are updated by signed sensory drive \(I_t\) and enter the action scoring function (Eq. 8). When scenic routes provide negative \(I_t\) (beneficial cues), they increase valence and decrease arousal relative to dull routes, stabilizing preference even when scenic is less novel.
- **Core assumption:** "Qualiaphilia" (preference for sensory experience for its own sake) can be operationalized as a familiarity-controlled choice where scenic preference persists despite novelty manipulation, and that this stability requires affect-modulated scoring rather than pure curiosity or scripted behavior.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Affected variant shows stable scenic preference (Δscenic-entry = 0.01) while non-affect variants increase scenic-seeking when scenic is novel (Δscenic-entry ≈ 0.07), under familiarity control (median Δnovelty ≈ −0.43).
  - Figure 1 shows the dissociation; split-point probe predicts higher valence/lower arousal for scenic.
  - Corpus neighbors do not address affect-coupled decision-making or scenic preference.
- **Break condition:** If the affect variant shows novelty-sensitive scenic-entry (Δscenic-entry > 0.05) under familiarity control, the affect proxy is not sufficiently modulating control to decouple value from novelty, or the scoring weights are misconfigured.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Affect coupling supports structured local investigation during reward-free exploratory play and prolonged planned caution after hazard exposure.
- **Mechanism:** Affect variables (especially arousal \(N_a\) and budget error) modulate action scoring, promoting scanning (rapid orienting turns) when arousal is moderate and sustaining turn-rate suppression after a hazard (high predicted arousal) via a caution penalty.
- **Core assumption:** "Exploratory play" and "lingering caution" are marker-like behaviors that should dissociate from recurrence alone; they require affect to enter control loops. Structured scanning is not random dithering but repeated cycles at a location; lingering caution is a sustained reduction in planned turn rate after a single hazard contact.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - In reward-free exploratory play, the affect variant shows structured local investigation (scan events 31.4 vs. 0.9; cycle score 7.6) with low action entropy (1.29 vs. random 1.99).
  - In a pain-tail probe, only the affect variant sustains prolonged planned caution (tail duration 90 vs. 5). Persistence in \(N^s\) (AUC) is present in both ipsundrum variants but does not produce caution without affect.
  - Corpus shows no related work on affect-modulated exploration or hazard-response prolongation.
- **Break condition:** If the affect variant does not increase scan events (>10 vs. <2 for non-affect) or show prolonged caution (tail duration <20 steps), the affect proxy is not effectively coupled into the planning forward model or action scoring, or the arousal-gated caution penalty is ineffective.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Recurrent ipsundrum dynamics
  - **Why needed here:** To engineer a persistent internal signal that outlives transient stimuli, testing the hypothesis that recurrence underlies "sensory persistence" as a marker-like signature.
  - **Quick check:** If you set recurrence decay \(d = 1\) (no decay) and \(g_{eff} = 0\) (no feedback gain), what happens to \(N^s\) persistence after a stimulus? (It becomes a moving average of recent \(N_s\), with no self-amplification.)
- **Concept:** Affect proxy as interoceptive coupling
  - **Why needed here:** To test whether modulating control via an internal "body budget" and valence/arousal readouts yields dissociable behaviors (stable preference, structured play, lingering caution) that recurrence alone does not.
  - **Quick check:** If you disable the affect terms in Eq. 8 (set \(w_v = w_a = w_{bb} = 0\)) but keep affect variables updating internally, will scenic preference stability be preserved? (No; stability requires affect to enter scoring.)
- **Concept:** Mechanism-linked assay with within-episode lesion
  - **Why needed here:** To move beyond behavioral markers and establish causal attribution; a lesion that removes recurrence should selectively reduce \(N^s\) persistence without affecting other competencies.
  - **Quick check:** Why not just compare different architectures across episodes instead of within-episode lesion? (Within-episode lesion controls for policy and environment; it isolates the effect of removing recurrence at a specific time.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Sensor input \(I_t\) → Ipsundrum state updates (\(X_t, M_t, N^s_t, E_t\)) → Affect updates (\(bb_t, N_{v,t}, N_{a,t}\)) → Policy driver (forward simulation with variant-aligned dynamics) → Action scoring via Eq. 8 → Action execution → repeat.

**Critical path:**
1. Sensor input \(I_t\) enters.
2. Ipsundrum state updates \(X_t, M_t, N^s_t, E_t\) if recurrence enabled.
3. Affect updates \(bb_t, N_{v,t}, N_{a,t}\) if affect enabled; may modulate gain.
4. Forward simulation for each candidate action using variant-aligned dynamics (ipsundrum step vs. predict_one_step_recon).
5. Score actions via Eq. 8 (includes \(N_s, N_v, N_a, bb\) if present).
6. Choose highest-scoring action; execute; repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Simplicity vs. expressivity: Fixed parameters and scalar \(N^s\) keep mechanism transparent but limit representational richness.
- Mechanism fidelity vs. generalization: Explicit lesion flags ensure causal attribution but the system is not learned; behaviors are parameter-tuned.
- Affect abstraction: A signed exteroceptive scalar \(I_t\) drives the "body budget"; this is a minimal proxy, not physiological interoception.

**Failure signatures:**
- Lesion does not reduce \(N^s\) persistence → recurrence not actually implemented or gain too low.
- Non-affect variant shows stable scenic preference → scoring inadvertently includes scenic value or familiarity control failed.
- High action entropy in exploratory play (>1.8) → lack of structured scanning; affect coupling insufficient or curiosity weight too high.
- Pain-tail tail duration ~0 for all variants → affect not coupled into planned action scoring or caution penalty ineffective.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Run goal-directed navigation (CorridorWorld/GridWorld) with progress term on; verify all variants reach goals and Ipsundrum+affect reduces hazard contacts (Table 4). Confirms basic competence.
2. Run familiarity-controlled corridor assay; check Δscenic-entry for non-affect ≈ 0.07 and for affect ≈ 0.01 (Fig 1). Validates qualiaphilia operationalization and affect's role.
3. Run pain-tail assay; verify ipsundrum variants show non-zero \(N^s\) AUC post-stimulus, but only affect variant has tail duration >50 steps (Fig 3). Confirms dissociation.

## Limitations
- Fixed, hand‑tuned parameters: no learning, limiting adaptability and ecological validity.
- Affect proxy is a minimal scalar derived from exteroceptive drive, lacking physiological interoceptive realism.
- Assays are confined to narrow grid‑world scenarios; generalization to complex, high‑dimensional environments is untested.

## Confidence
- **Recurrence → sensory persistence:** High. Causal lesion selectively reduces \(N^s\) AUC (~27.6% drop) while leaving ReCoN intact.
- **Affect → stable scenic preference:** High. Large dissociation (Δscenic‑entry 0.01 vs. 0.07) under novelty control, supported by split‑point probe predictions.
- **Affect → structured play & prolonged caution:** High. Robust effects: scan events 31.4 vs. 0.9, cycle score 7.6, tail duration 90 vs. 5; persistence alone fails these assays.

## Next Checks
1. **Parametric recurrence test:** Systematically vary recurrence decay \(d\) and feedback gain \(g_{eff}\); measure post‑stimulus \(N^s\) AUC to confirm monotonic scaling predicted by equations.
2. **Within‑episode affect lesion:** During assay episodes, set \(w_v, w_a, w_{bb} = 0\) in scoring while keeping recurrence active; expect immediate loss of preference stability, scanning, and lingering caution.
3. **Cross‑environment generalization:** Deploy the agent in 3D navigation and object‑rich scenes; replicate corridor, exploratory play, and pain‑tail assays to verify persistence of effects beyond grid‑worlds.
