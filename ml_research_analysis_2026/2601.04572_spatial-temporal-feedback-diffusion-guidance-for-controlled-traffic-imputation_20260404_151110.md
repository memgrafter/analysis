# Spatial-Temporal Feedback Diffusion Guidance for Controlled Traffic Imputation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04572
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04572
- Reference count: 21
- One-line primary result: FENCE achieves state-of-the-art imputation accuracy, reducing MAPE by an average of 6.26% compared to the second-best baseline, with consistent improvements in MAE and RMSE.

## Executive Summary
FENCE addresses a critical limitation in diffusion-based traffic imputation: fixed guidance scales often cause poor performance for nodes with high missing rates. It introduces a dynamic feedback mechanism that adapts guidance strength based on posterior likelihood approximations, increasing scale when generated values diverge from observations and decreasing it to avoid overcorrection. Additionally, it employs cluster-level guidance by grouping nodes via spatial-temporal attention scores to tailor scaling across correlated groups. Evaluated on PEMS04, PEMS07, and PEMS08 datasets under SR-TC and SC-TC missing patterns, FENCE delivers state-of-the-art results, significantly lowering MAPE, MAE, and RMSE.

## Method Summary
FENCE leverages a two-stage training procedure. First, an unconditional diffusion model learns the prior distribution of traffic data. Second, the model is fine-tuned to generate conditional samples given observed values. At inference, a feedback controller dynamically adjusts the guidance scale λ per diffusion step and per cluster of nodes. λ is derived from an estimated posterior likelihood comparing the conditional and unconditional score predictions. Nodes are clustered each step using k-means on spatial attention scores from the conditional network, enabling shared posterior estimation and stable λ per cluster. The guided denoising step then produces the next sample.

## Key Results
- MAPE reduced by an average of 6.26% over the second-best baseline.
- Consistent improvements in MAE and RMSE across all three PEMS datasets.
- Outperforms baselines under both SR-TC (spatially random) and SC-TC (spatially clustered) missing patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** FENCE dynamically adjusts the guidance scale during diffusion to prevent drift toward the unconditional prior for nodes with sparse observations.
- **Mechanism:** At each denoising step *k*, it computes a guidance scale λ(*x*ₖ, *k*) based on an estimated posterior likelihood *p*ₜ,ₖ(*c*|*x*ₖ). When the posterior is low (generated values diverge from observations), λ increases to strengthen conditional guidance. When the posterior is high (good alignment), λ decreases toward 1 to avoid overcorrection. The posterior is updated across steps using a Gaussian transition model comparing conditional and unconditional means.
- **Core assumption:** The learned conditional distribution *p*ₜ,ₖ(*x*ₖ|*c*) is a linear mixture of the true conditional and unconditional distributions, parameterized by prior confidence π.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "introduces a dynamic feedback mechanism that adaptively adjusts guidance strength based on posterior likelihood approximations—increasing scale when generated values diverge from observations and reducing it to prevent overcorrection."  
  [section "Posterior-Driven Dynamic Guidance Scaling"] Equation (9) defines λ as a function of posterior; equation (13) gives the update rule.  
- **Break condition:** If the posterior estimation becomes unstable (e.g., due to extremely sparse observations making *p*ₜ,ₖ(*c*|*x*ₖ) intractable), the feedback loop may produce erratic λ values, degrading imputation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Cluster-aware guidance scales improve stability and accuracy by leveraging spatial-temporal correlations instead of applying a global scale per step.
- **Mechanism:** Nodes are grouped into clusters at each step using *k*-means on spatial attention scores from the conditioning network. A cluster-level posterior is averaged over nodes (equation 14). This shared posterior computes a cluster-specific λ (equation 15), which is then applied to all nodes in that cluster.
- **Core assumption:** Nodes with similar attention patterns have correlated missingness patterns and thus similar alignment needs; aggregating posteriors reduces variance from individual node noise.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "uses cluster-level guidance by grouping nodes via attention scores to leverage spatial-temporal correlations for tailored scaling."  
  [section "Cluster-Aware Feedback Guidance"] Describes clustering via attention scores and cluster-level posterior aggregation.  
- **Break condition:** If attention scores fail to capture meaningful groupings (e.g., in datasets with weak spatial dependencies or highly heterogeneous traffic patterns), cluster-level scaling may misapply guidance, harming performance relative to node-specific scaling.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Two-stage training (unconditional prior first, then conditional fine-tuning) stabilizes learning of both prior and conditional distributions, enabling accurate score estimation for feedback.
- **Mechanism:** First train an unconditional diffusion model on masked data to learn *p*ₜ(*x*). Then initialize the conditional model from these weights and train on observed values to learn *p*ₜ(*x*|*c*). During inference, both models’ predicted means (µₜ(*x*ₖ) and µₜ(*x*ₖ|*c*)) are used to compute posterior updates.
- **Core assumption:** Pre-training on the unconditional prior provides a better initialization for the conditional model than random training, preventing collapse of the conditional distribution to the prior when observations are sparse.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section "Training and Inference"] "we adopt a two-stage training procedure. First, we train an unconditional generative model... After convergence, the weights... are saved. Next, we fine-tune this pre-trained model..."  
  [ablation study] wo-U variant (no unconditional modeling) underperforms, showing importance of two-stage training.  
- **Break condition:** If the unconditional prior is poorly fit (e.g., due to excessive missingness in training), the conditional model’s initialization may be biased, leading to inaccurate posterior estimates and feedback.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Diffusion Models (Score-Based Generative Modeling)**  
  *Why needed here:* FENCE builds on diffusion models that learn to reverse a noising process; understanding the forward/backward process, noise schedule, and score function ∇log*p*(*x*) is essential to grasp how guidance is applied.  
  *Quick check question:* How does the denoising network ϵₜ(*x*ₖ, *k*, *c*) relate to the conditional score function?
- **Concept: Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)**  
  *Why needed here:* FENCE extends CFG, which combines conditional and unconditional scores with a fixed scale λ. FENCE’s innovation is making λ dynamic and cluster-aware.  
  *Quick check question:* In CFG, what does a larger λ imply about adherence to conditional observations?
- **Concept: Spatial-Temporal Graph Modeling**  
  *Why needed here:* Traffic data is a graph with nodes (sensors) and edges (spatial relations). FENCE uses attention to model spatial-temporal dependencies and cluster nodes.  
  *Quick check question:* Why might spatial attention scores be more suitable for clustering than the static adjacency matrix?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Conditioning Network (F_cond) → Conditional Denoising Network (ϵ_cond) + Unconditional Denoising Network (ϵ_uncond) → Feedback Controller → Sampler.  
  The conditioning network processes observed data to produce context *c*; the denoising networks predict means µ(*x*ₖ|c) and µ(*x*ₖ); the feedback controller computes cluster-level λ; the sampler updates the latent sample.
- **Critical path:** The feedback loop (posterior estimation → λ computation → sampling → posterior update) is critical; any error propagates through the reverse diffusion process.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Per-node vs. cluster scaling:* Node-specific λ would be ideal but unstable under sparsity; cluster-level trades granularity for stability.  
  - *Two-stage training cost:* Longer training but avoids conditional collapse; could use joint training with regularization.  
  - *Attention-based clustering:* Dynamic, captures evolving correlations, but adds *k*-means overhead per step; could precompute clusters.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Posterior update yields NaNs if variance σ²ₖ is too small or if µ(*x*ₖ|*c*) and µ(*x*ₖ) are nearly identical (no guidance signal).  
  - Clustering fails if attention matrix is degenerate (e.g., all nodes in one cluster), reducing to global scaling.  
  - Guidance scale λ diverges if posterior estimate approaches (1–π) from below; clipping may be needed.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Validate feedback loop:** Run inference on a synthetic dataset with a node having zero observations. Compare λ trajectory and imputation error vs. fixed-λ CFG. Expect λ to increase over steps when posterior is low.  
  2. **Test cluster sensitivity:** Ablate clustering by using global λ (wo-C variant). Measure performance drop, especially on SC-TC (spatially clustered missing) pattern.  
  3. **Check two-stage training necessity:** Compare wo-U variant (no unconditional pre-training) against full FENCE. Expect significant MAPE increase, confirming prior modeling helps posterior estimation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Accuracy of posterior estimation:** How accurate is the posterior likelihood estimation used in the feedback mechanism, especially for nodes with very high missing rates where conditional scores may be unreliable? The paper does not validate fidelity against true posteriors.
- **Stability and semantics of attention-based clusters:** Do the attention-based clusters correspond to stable, semantically meaningful spatial-temporal communities, or do they introduce instability in guidance scaling? No analysis on cluster interpretability or consistency is provided.
- **Generalization to other domains:** Can the FENCE framework generalize effectively to non-traffic spatial-temporal domains (e.g., climate or social networks) with different data characteristics and missing patterns? Experiments are limited to traffic flow datasets.

## Limitations
- Posterior estimation is sensitive to hyperparameters (τ, δ, π) and can become unstable under extreme missingness, leading to erratic guidance scales.
- Attention-based clustering may produce unstable or noisy clusters if attention scores are uninformative, degrading performance.
- Two-stage training increases computational cost and requires careful convergence of the unconditional prior; a poorly fit prior biases the conditional model.

## Confidence
- Dynamic feedback guidance (Mechanism 1): Medium
- Cluster-aware guidance scaling (Mechanism 2): Medium
- Two-stage training stabilization (Mechanism 3): High
