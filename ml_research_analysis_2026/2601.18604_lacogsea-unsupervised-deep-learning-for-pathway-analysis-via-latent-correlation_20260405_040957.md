# LaCoGSEA: Unsupervised deep learning for pathway analysis via latent correlation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.18604
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18604
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** LaCoGSEA enables unsupervised pathway enrichment by using deep autoencoder latent correlations to generate dense gene rankings, achieving superior clustering (ARI 0.372 vs. 0.240) and known pathway recovery (mean rank 17.5) over PCA and XAI methods.

## Executive Summary
LaCoGSEA introduces an unsupervised framework for pathway analysis that eliminates the need for phenotypic labels, a core limitation of standard GSEA. It trains a deep autoencoder to capture non-linear transcriptomic structure, then ranks genes by their Pearson correlation with each latent dimension. This generates biologically dense, co-regulated gene lists that reflect underlying transcriptional programs. Standard GSEA is applied to these lists, aggregating results across latent dimensions to identify significant pathways. Validated across multiple cancers and neurodegenerative datasets, the method outperforms linear PCA and gradient-based explainable AI techniques, particularly for clustering known molecular subtypes and prioritizing established disease pathways. It remains robust even with small sample sizes (N=30).

## Method Summary
LaCoGSEA processes a gene expression matrix (samples × genes) via log2(x+1) transformation and trains a deep autoencoder with reconstruction loss plus Elastic Net (L1+L2) regularization. After training, it computes a correlation matrix *P* where entry *P<sub>j,k</sub>* is the Pearson correlation between gene *j*'s expression vector and latent dimension *k*'s activation vector across samples. For each latent dimension *k*, genes are sorted by absolute correlation to produce a ranked list *L<sub>k</sub>*. Standard GSEA (e.g., `fgsea`) is run on each *L<sub>k</sub>* against pathway collections (e.g., KEGG). The model-level pathway rank is the best (lowest) rank among dimensions where FDR < 0.05. To derive sample-level pathway activities, a weight matrix *W* is built from normalized enrichment scores (NES) across dimensions, and activity for sample *i* and pathway *p* is *A<sub>i,p</sub>* = Σ<sub>k</sub> z<sub>i,k</sub> · *W<sub>k,p</sub>*, where *z* is the latent representation.

## Key Results
- LaCoGSEA achieves an Adjusted Rand Index of 0.372 for clustering breast cancer PAM50 subtypes, vastly outperforming the best baseline (0.240).
- It recovers known disease-associated pathways with a mean rank of 17.5 across five independent datasets, while PCA and SHAP/DeepLIFT obtain mean ranks of 44–77.
- The method maintains high performance robustness even with limited sample sizes (N=30), demonstrating practical utility for small cohorts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Global Pearson correlation between gene expression and latent dimensions generates dense, biologically robust gene rankings that outperform sparse, gradient-based XAI methods (SHAP/DeepLIFT) for pathway enrichment.
- **Mechanism:** For each latent dimension *k*, compute the Pearson correlation ρ between the *N*-dimensional expression vector of each gene *j* and the *N*-dimensional activation vector of latent dimension *k* across all samples. Sort genes by |ρ| to create a ranked list *L<sub>k</sub>*. This captures the full spectrum of co-regulated genes within a latent biological program, preserving pathway-level redundancy.
- **Core assumption:** Latent dimensions from a trained autoencoder represent disentangled biological signals such that linear correlation in the latent space is a valid proxy for gene-level association with that signal.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states it "generates dense gene rankings without prior labels... outperforms... gradient-based XAI methods (mean ranks 44–77)". Methods section defines the Pearson correlation ρ<sub>j,k</sub>.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A deep autoencoder captures non-linear, overlapping transcriptional programs more effectively than linear PCA, leading to higher pathway discovery and better downstream clustering.
- **Mechanism:** The autoencoder's non-linear encoder/decoder and lack of orthogonality constraints allow it to model complex, non-additive gene interactions and overlapping pathway structures. This results in latent dimensions that retain stronger, less fragmented biological signals compared to PCA components.
- **Core assumption:** Biological pathways exhibit non-linear and overlapping dependencies poorly modeled by linear, orthogonal decompositions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes "capture non-linear transcriptomic structure... superior unsupervised clustering... substantially outperforming linear PCA." Results (saturation analysis, Fig. 2) show AE identifies more KEGG pathways than PCA.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The "unsupervised pre-ranking" framework enables application of the statistically rigorous GSEA algorithm in the absence of phenotypic labels by using latent-dimension correlation scores as a surrogate for differential expression.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of a supervised DE statistic, LaCoGSEA generates *D* pre-ranked gene lists from the unsupervised correlation matrix *P*. Standard GSEA is run on each list; the model-level pathway rank is the best rank across all *D* lists where FDR<0.05.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant sources of variation captured by the autoencoder's latent space are biologically meaningful and correlate with known pathway activity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract introduces "unsupervised pre-ranking framework". Results show consistent pathway recovery across diverse unsupervised datasets (Alzheimer’s, Trauma).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: GSEA's supervised requirement and ranking statistic.**
  - *Why needed here:* LaCoGSEA's core innovation is creating a valid pre-ranked list without labels. Understanding standard GSEA's dependence on a supervised DE metric clarifies the problem being solved.
  - *Quick check question:* Why does classic GSEA fail in an unsupervised exploratory analysis of a single cohort with no known groups?

- **Concept: Autoencoder architecture and training dynamics.**
  - *Why needed here:* The non-linear representation learning is the engine. An engineer must grasp reconstruction loss, latent space *z = f(x)*, and the effect of regularization (L1/L2) on learning interpretable, non-sparse features.
  - *Quick check question:* How does an L1 penalty on the encoder weights influence the sparsity of the latent dimensions versus the gene-latent correlation pattern?

- **Concept: Pathway biology and redundancy.**
  - *Why needed here:* The method's rationale hinges on pathways being dense, co-regulated networks. Understanding why a few genes (sparse) are insufficient for pathway detection explains the failure of SHAP/DeepLIFT.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a method that selects only 5-10 "important" genes for a latent dimension perform poorly in GSEA compared to a method that ranks 100+ correlated genes?

## Architecture Onboarding
Input Processor -> Autoencoder Core -> Correlation Engine -> Ranking & GSEA Module -> Pathway Activity Calculator

**Critical path:** Data preprocessing → Train AE to convergence → Compute gene–latent correlation matrix *P* → Generate ranked lists *{L<sub>k</sub>}* → Run GSEA → (Optional) Compute sample pathway activities *A*.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Latent dim *D*:** Larger *D* increases pathway discovery capacity but risks fragmentation and overfitting. Paper uses *D*=64 for large datasets, suggests lower for small *N*.
- **AE depth/width vs. dataset size:** Overly complex AE on small *N* (~30) may overfit. Simpler architectures may suffice.
- **Correlation vs. other metrics:** Pearson is simple and dense. Mutual information could capture non-linear associations but is noisier with finite samples.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **Poor reconstruction loss:** High training MSE indicates lossy latent space; pathway ranks will be poor.
2. **Saturation curve never rises:** In a Fig. 2-like analysis, if number of significant pathways doesn't increase with *D*, the AE isn't learning biological signal.
3. **Negative control failure:** Running on permuted or Gaussian noise data should yield ~0 significant pathways. Any consistent signal indicates a bug or overfitting.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reproduce Fig 2 (saturation):** On SCAN-B, plot number of significant KEGG pathways vs. *D* (1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128) for AE and PCA (both "Corr" and "Weights"). Verify AE plateaus higher and PCA drops at high *D*.
2. **Reproduce Fig 3e (ARI clustering):** Using *D*=64, compute sample pathway activity matrix *A* = *Z* · *W*ᵀ. Run K-means (*k*=5) and compute ARI against PAM50. Expect ~0.37 for LaCoGSEA, <0.24 for baselines.
3. **Target pathway rank (Fig 5 style):** On a new dataset (e.g., GSE48350 Alzheimer's), compute model rank of the pre-specified target pathway. Compare rank to PCA Corr, AE SHAP, AE DeepLIFT. Expect LaCoGSEA rank < 20, others > 40.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Optimal latent dimensionality selection:** How to systematically determine *D* given varying dataset sizes, signal complexity, and biological resolution goals? Current guidance is qualitative.
- **Correlation vs. XAI performance drivers:** Is the superiority of global Pearson correlation over SHAP/DeepLIFT solely due to pathway density vs. XAI sparsity, or do autoencoder non-linearity and latent structure also critically contribute?
- **Breakdown conditions for correlation proxy:** When does the assumption that Pearson correlation between genes and latent dimensions provides a valid unsupervised differential expression proxy fail, particularly for latent dimensions encoding mixed or non-linearly combined signals?
- **Clinical utility of unsupervised discoveries:** Can LaCoGSEA's pathway activities be consistently linked to specific clinical outcomes (e.g., survival, drug response), or do they primarily capture broad molecular subtypes without direct prognostic utility?

## Limitations
- Key implementation details are unspecified: exact autoencoder architecture (layer sizes, activations), regularization coefficients (λ₁, λ₂), training hyperparameters (learning rate, epochs, early stopping), gene filtering criteria, and GSEA permutation settings.
- The identity of the target disease pathway for each GEO dataset in the ranking benchmark is missing, hindering precise replication of cross-dataset results.
- The aggregation method for SHAP/DeepLIFT (e.g., mean absolute vs. max) is not described, making baseline comparisons difficult to reproduce exactly.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1 (Global Pearson correlation yields dense, robust rankings outperforming XAI):** Medium confidence. Supported by comparative results and negative-control experiments, but theoretical reliance on latent disentanglement lacks direct validation.
- **Claim 2 (Deep autoencoder captures non-linear, overlapping programs better than PCA):** Medium confidence. Saturation analysis and ARI improvements are compelling, but ablations on autoencoder capacity and comparisons to other non-linear models are absent.
- **Claim 3 (Unsupervised pre-ranking framework enables label-free GSEA):** High confidence. The method is clearly specified and validated across diverse unsupervised datasets, with noise controls confirming no spurious enrichment.
