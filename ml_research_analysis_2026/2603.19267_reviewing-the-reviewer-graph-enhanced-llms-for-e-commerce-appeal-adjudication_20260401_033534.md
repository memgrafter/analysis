# Reviewing the Reviewer: Graph-Enhanced LLMs for E-commerce Appeal Adjudication

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.19267
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19267
- **Reference count**: 32
- **Primary result**: The EAFD framework achieves 95.8% offline accuracy (vs. 70.8% LLM-only) and 96.3% alignment with 100% approval precision in online deployment over 136 cases.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles information asymmetry in e-commerce appeal review, where second-tier Checkers use verification actions unavailable to first-tier Makers, leading to LLM hallucinations. The authors propose the Evidence-Action-Factor-Decision (EAFD) graph schema and a conflict-aware reasoning framework. By extracting structured graphs from historical disagreements, aggregating them into a retrievable knowledge base, and performing top-down deductive reasoning for new cases, the system significantly outperforms baselines. A Request More Information (RMI) output acts as a safety boundary when evidence is insufficient. Offline results show 95.8% accuracy, and online deployment confirms high alignment with zero wrongful seller reinstatements.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two phases. Offline, historical Maker-Checker cases are parsed into EAFD graphs (Evidence, Action, Factor, Decision nodes with directed edges) using an LLM; graphs are validated for structural invariants, action types are merged for generality, and case summaries are embedded for retrieval. Online, a new case’s Maker graph is constructed, similar historical graphs are retrieved and refined, and Maker factors are aligned to historical ones. For each aligned factor, the system projects a historical Checker resolution path—instantiating a Checker factor, adapting associated actions to the current entities, and grounding each action in available evidence. If any critical action lacks grounding, the system outputs RMI with specific requests; otherwise it aggregates factor outcomes to Approve/Reject.

## Key Results
- Offline accuracy: 95.8% vs. 70.8% for LLM-only baseline.
- Online alignment: 96.3% over 136 cases, with 100% approval precision.
- RMI successfully prevented wrongful seller reinstatements by recognizing system boundaries.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The EAFD schema mitigates hallucination by grounding reasoning in explicit verification actions that are traceable to evidence items.
- **Mechanism**: The schema enforces a directed chain (Evidence→Action→Factor→Decision). Each Action must reference specific Evidence, and each Factor must be supported by at least one Action. This inferential constraint prevents unsupported claims. Offline, graphs are extracted, validated (type compatibility, relational completeness, cardinality), and indexed. Online, the Maker graph is built, and Checker reasoning is projected by instantiating actions and evidence for aligned historical factors.
- **Core assumption**: Historical logs consistently document verification steps; these steps can be abstracted into recurring canonical actions; and the schema constraints block invalid reasoning chains.
- **Break condition**: If logs lack explicit action descriptions, actions are too case-specific, or structural invariants are violated (e.g., an action linked to multiple factors), grounding weakens and hallucination may occur.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Conflict‑aware retrieval and top‑down FAE deduction effectively transfer correction knowledge from past Maker‑Checker disagreements to new cases.
- **Mechanism**: Overturned cases (with Maker factors and conflicting Checker factors) are aggregated into a knowledge base. For a query, the system retrieves candidate precedents, aligns the current Maker factors to historical ones, and projects the Checker reasoning path via conflict edges: hypothesize Checker factor, adapt historical actions, and ground them in current evidence. This top‑down Factor→Action→Evidence chain reuses validated patterns while staying anchored to available evidence.
- **Core assumption**: Similar cases share common adjudication logic; conflict edges correctly represent how Maker factors were overturned; and factor alignment plus action adaptation accurately map patterns to the new context.
- **Break condition**: No relevant precedents retrieved, factor alignment fails due to semantic mismatch, or adapted actions become inapplicable (e.g., wrong entity substitution).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The Request More Information (RMI) outcome acts as a conservative safety boundary, preventing wrongful decisions when critical evidence is absent.
- **Mechanism**: During FAE deduction, each action’s grounding status is evaluated (Verified if evidence exists, Missing otherwise). Actions are marked critical if their absence blocks any approval (based on historical patterns). If any critical action is Missing, the system outputs RMI with a recommendation listing missing actions (e.g., “provide original invoice”). RMI is thus triggered by a structural evidence gap, not model uncertainty.
- **Core assumption**: Critical actions for each factor can be reliably identified from historical Checker reasoning; evidence search accurately determines grounding; and the natural‑language translation yields actionable requests.
- **Break condition**: Misclassifying action criticality inflates RMI rate; failing to detect a missing critical action may lead to an incorrect decision; incomplete historical coverage of critical actions creates gaps.

## Architecture Onboarding
Offline Knowledge Base Construction: Hierarchical Graph Extraction -> Action Refinement & Merging -> Structural Invariant Validation -> Knowledge Indexing  
Online Reasoning Engine: Maker Graph Construction -> Retrieval Module (coarse embedding + LLM refinement) -> Factor Alignment -> FAE Deduction -> Adjudication & RMI  
Supporting Layers: VLM preprocessing, LLM backbone (Claude Sonnet 4.0), embedding model (Amazon Titan), production monitoring.

Critical path:
1. Preprocess raw case materials (multimodal evidence) into structured text with source traces.
2. Construct Maker EAFD graph from rejection record and evidence.
3. Encode case summary → query knowledge base (embedding similarity).
4. Retrieve top‑20 candidates, refine to top‑5 analogous cases via LLM.
5. Align each Maker factor to a historical Maker factor.
6. For each aligned factor: hypothesize Checker factor (via conflict edge), adapt actions, ground each in current evidence.
7. Compute action status (Verified/Missing) and mark critical actions.
8. If any critical action Missing → output RMI + recommendations; else aggregate factor outcomes → Approve/Reject.
9. Log decision for audit (no online KB updates).

Design tradeoffs:
- Structured extraction vs. scalability: EAFD extraction is heavy but yields traceable graphs; throughput may be limited.
- Action merging reduces sparsity but risks over‑generalizing distinct verification intents.
- RMI conservatism maximizes safety but increases human workload; tuning critical‑action thresholds is needed.
- Retrieval scope: larger K improves recall but adds noise and latency; LLM refinement improves precision but adds cost.
- LLM dependency: extraction or alignment errors propagate without updates.

Failure signatures:
- High RMI rate (>30% of cases) → evidence quality or KB coverage issues.
- Low action hit rate (<70%) → misalignment between system‑recommended and Checker‑performed actions.
- Frequent graph validation errors during offline build → extraction pipeline failures (missing factors, invalid edges).
- Misalignment cases in production → analyze root: factor alignment error, absent conflict edge, or truly novel case.
- Elevated retrieval latency or irrelevant top‑K candidates → embedding index or query summary problems.
- Poor VLM preprocessing (inaccurate text extraction) → evidence‑action grounding fails.

First 3 experiments:
1. Offline main evaluation: Run full EAFD pipeline against baselines (LLM‑only, LLM+RMI, CBR, LLM+CBR, LLM+CBR+RMI) on held‑out data, measuring accuracy and Macro‑F1.
2. Ablation study: Remove (a) action grounding (LLM+RMI vs. LLM+CBR+RMI) and (b) structured representation (LLM+CBR vs. EAFD) to quantify contributions.
3. Online deployment monitoring: Track cumulative alignment rate, per‑class precision/recall, and RMI occurrence over initial batch (e.g., 136 cases) to verify safety and identify bottlenecks.
