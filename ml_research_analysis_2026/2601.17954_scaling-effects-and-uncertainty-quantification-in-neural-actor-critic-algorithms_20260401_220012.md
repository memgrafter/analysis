# Scaling Effects and Uncertainty Quantification in Neural Actor Critic Algorithms

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17954
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17954
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The paper derives an asymptotic expansion for neural Actor‑Critic outputs, showing that variance scales as \(N^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta}\) (decreasing with larger \(\beta\)) and bias is minimized at \(\beta = 3/4\), and provides rigorous hyperparameter guidelines for stable learning.

## Executive Summary
This work studies the neural Actor‑Critic algorithm with a scaling parameter \(\beta \in (1/2,1]\) that governs the initialization scale of network weights. Moving beyond convergence speed, it provides a full statistical characterization of the algorithm’s outputs. An asymptotic expansion reveals the leading random term (variance) scales as \(N^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta}\) and decays as \(\beta \to 1\), while the deterministic bias scales as \(N^{\max\{\beta-1,\frac{1}{2}-\beta\}}\) with optimal decay at \(\beta = 3/4\). Theoretical conditions on learning rates and exploration decay ensure the expansion holds, and experiments on a forest MDP confirm that \(\beta\) close to 1 yields faster convergence and lower variance. The results yield concrete, implementable guidelines for setting hyperparameters as functions of network width \(N\) and \(\beta\).

## Method Summary
The studied algorithm uses two shallow networks for a finite‑state, finite‑action MDP. Both actor and critic have one hidden layer of width \(N\). Weights are initialized i.i.d. Uniform\([-0.5,0.5]\) and scaled by \(N^{-\beta}\). The critic approximates \(Q_\omega(x,a)=N^{-\beta}\sum_{i=1}^N C_i\sigma(W_i\cdot(x,a))\) and is trained by SGD with step size \(\alpha_N = \alpha \cdot N^{\beta-2}\). The actor policy is \(f_\theta(x,a)=\text{softmax}(P_\theta(x,a))\) with \(P_\theta(x,a)=N^{-\beta}\sum_{i=1}^N B_i\sigma(U_i\cdot(x,a))\) and uses a step size \(\zeta_N^k = N^{\beta-2}/(1+k/N)\). To maintain sufficient exploration, the behavior policy is \(g_k(x,a)=\eta_k/|A|+(1-\eta_k)f_k(x,a)\) with \(\eta_k = 1/(1+\log_2(1+k/N))\). Training alternates between updates on two coupled Markov chains over \(\lfloor N T\rfloor\) steps. Performance is measured by the mean‑squared error of the stochastic policy relative to the exact optimal deterministic policy (computed via value iteration) and by the average return (computed by solving the Bellman equation for the current policy). The plan is complete apart from the initial state‑action distribution (taken uniform) and the positive constants \(\alpha,\zeta_0\) (both set to 1).

## Key Results
- The asymptotic expansion shows that the leading random term (variance) scales as \(N^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta}\), decreasing as \(\beta \to 1\).
- The leading‑order bias scales as \(N^{\max\{\beta-1,\frac{1}{2}-\beta\}}\) and is minimized (fastest decay) at \(\beta = 3/4\).
- Experiments confirm faster convergence and lower variance for \(\beta\) near 1, and the prescribed learning‑rate and exploration schedules ensure favorable statistical behavior.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The variance of the neural actor‑critic outputs decays as \(N^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta}\), improving statistical robustness as \(\beta\) approaches 1.
- **Mechanism:** In the asymptotic expansion (Theorem 3.1), the leading random term—capturing initialization uncertainty—is multiplied by \(N^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta}\). For \(\beta=1/2\) this factor is 1 (no decay), while for \(\beta\to1\) it becomes \(N^{-1/2}\).
- **Core assumption:** Shallow networks, smooth activation, initial weights scaled as \(1/N^\beta\) with \(\beta\in(1/2,1)\), and the Markov chain sampling is ergodic and uniformly mixing.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]
  - [section]
  - [corpus] – The neighboring study (Sirignano & Spiliopoulos, 2020) found analogous variance scaling in neural network regression, indicating broader applicability.
- **Break condition:** If \(\beta\le 1/2\) the expansion breaks down and variance may not vanish; deeper architectures could alter the exponent.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The leading‑order bias scales as \(N^{\max\{\beta-1,\frac{1}{2}-\beta\}}\) and is minimized (fastest decay) at \(\beta = 3/4\).
- **Mechanism:** The asymptotic expansion separates deterministic (bias) and stochastic (variance) terms. The dominant bias exponent is the larger of \(\beta-1\) and \(\frac{1}{2}-\beta\). These two linear functions intersect at \(\beta=3/4\), where the exponent is \(-\frac{1}{4}\); any deviation increases the exponent (less negative) and thus the bias.
- **Core assumption:** The expansion structure holds for \(\beta\in(1/2,1)\) and deterministic terms dominate until the stochastic term; the bias terms are captured by powers \(N^{k(\beta-1)}\) with \(k<n\).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]
  - [section]
  - [corpus] – Weak direct evidence; related papers discuss bias‑variance trade‑offs in actor‑critic but not this exact \(\beta\)-optimality.
- **Break condition:** The optimum relies on the bias being dominated by two competing exponents. Additional bias sources (e.g., optimization error) or different network depths could shift the optimum.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The learning rates (\(\alpha_N,\zeta_N\)) must scale as \(\Theta(N^{2\beta-2})\) and the exploration rate \(\eta_N\) must decay slowly enough (e.g., \(\eta_k = 1/(1+\log_2(1+k/N))\)) to guarantee favorable statistical behavior.
- **Mechanism:** The continuum limit analysis yields conditions (2.0.20) that ensure the rescaled process converges to the correct ODE and that the martingale terms remain controlled. Specifically, \(\zeta_t\) must satisfy \(\int_0^\infty \zeta_t dt = \infty\) and \(\int_0^\infty \zeta_t \eta_t dt < \infty\), forcing \(\eta_t\) to be \(o(1/\zeta_t)\).
- **Core assumption:** The online SGD updates follow the assumed forms; the Markov chain induced by the exploration policy is uniformly ergodic and its stationary distribution depends Lipschitz‑continuously on the policy (Assumption 2.2).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]
  - [section] (see equation 2.0.20 and subsequent concrete schedule 2.0.21)
  - [corpus] – The paper “Closing the Gap…” also addresses hyperparameter scaling for neural actor‑critic, supporting the importance of proper schedules.
- **Break condition:** Violating the scaling (e.g., using constant learning rates) leads to either non‑vanishing bias or exploding variance; exploration decaying too fast (\(\eta_k = O(1/k)\) with exponent > 0) can break the uniform ergodicity required for the law of large numbers.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**
- Environment (MDP): Provides transition probabilities \(p(x'|x,a)\) and reward \(r(x,a)\).
- Actor network: \(P_\theta(x,a) = \frac{1}{N^\beta}\sum_{i=1}^N B_i \sigma(U_i\cdot(x,a))\); policy \(f_\theta = \text{Softmax}(P_\theta)\). Parameters \(\theta = (B_i, U_i)\).
- Critic network: \(Q_\omega(x,a) = \frac{1}{N^\beta}\sum_{i=1}^N C_i \sigma(W_i\cdot(x,a))\). Parameters \(\omega = (C_i, W_i)\).
- Exploration policy: \(g_k(x,a) = \frac{\eta_k}{|A|} + (1-\eta_k)f_k(x,a)\) to ensure all actions are visited.
- Training loop: Alternates between sampling \((x_k,a_k)\) (critic) and \((\tilde{x}_k,\tilde{a}_k)\) (actor), then performs SGD updates with step sizes \(\alpha_N\) and \(\zeta_N\). Maintains empirical measures \(\mu_k, v_k\) (averages over parameters) and updates exploration rate \(\eta_k\).

**Critical path**
1. Initialize parameters with variance \(\sim 1/N^\beta\).
2. For \(k = 0,\dots, \lfloor N T\rfloor-1\):
   - Draw \((x_k,a_k)\) from \(g_k\); observe next state \(x_{k+1}\).
   - Compute TD error: \(\delta_k = r(x_k,a_k) + \gamma Q_\omega(x_{k+1},a_{k+1}) - Q_\omega(x_k,a_k)\).
   - Update critic: \(C_i \gets C_i + \frac{\alpha_N}{N^\beta} \delta_k \sigma(W_i\cdot (x_k,a_k))\), \(W_i \gets W_i + \frac{\alpha_N}{N^\beta} \delta_k C_i \sigma'(W_i\cdot(x_k,a_k)) (x_k,a_k)\).
   - Obtain separate sample \((\tilde{x}_k,\tilde{a}_k)\) from the auxiliary chain driven by \(g_k\).
   - Update actor: \(B_i \gets B_i + \frac{\zeta_N}{N^\beta} Q_\omega(\tilde{x}_k,\tilde{a}_k)\Big(\sigma(U_i\cdot\tilde{\xi}_k) - \sum_{a'} f_k(\tilde{x}_k,a')\sigma(U_i\cdot(\tilde{x}_k,a'))\Big)\), similarly for \(U_i\).
   - Decay \(\eta_k\) (e.g., \(\eta_k = 1/(1+\log_2(1+k/N))\)).
3. After \(k \approx N T\) steps, the networks converge in distribution to \(P^{(0)}, Q^{(0)}\) plus higher‑order corrections.

**Design tradeoffs**
- *β* vs bias/variance: Larger \(\beta\) reduces variance but may increase bias if \(\beta>3/4\); optimal bias decay at \(\beta=3/4\).
- *Learning‑rate scaling:* Must be \(\Theta(N^{2\beta-2})\). Too large → instability; too small → slow learning.
- *Exploration decay:* Must satisfy \(\eta_k = \Omega(1/k^a)\) for all \(a>0\) but also \(\zeta_k/\eta_k\to0\). Fast decay harms mixing; slow decay slows convergence.
- *Width \(N\):* Larger \(N\) improves statistical properties (lower variance, better bias) but increases computation.

**Failure signatures**
- Diverging Q‑values or probabilities: likely learning‑rate too large for the chosen \(\beta\).
- No reward improvement: learning rates too small, insufficient exploration (\(\eta_k\) dropped too fast), or \(\beta\) in a poor range.
- High run‑to‑run variance: \(\beta\) too close to \(1/2\) (variance scaling \(N^{\frac12-\beta}\) large).
- Policy fails to cover states: exploration rate decay not slow enough; Markov chain not mixing.

**First 3 experiments**
1. *β sweep on Forest MDP* – Train networks with \(\beta\) values (e.g., 0.6, 0.75, 0.85, 0.95, 1.0) for fixed \(N=10,000\) and compare average reward and actor MSE over time.
2. *Variance scaling validation* – For several \(N\) and \(\beta\), run many seeds, compute standard deviation of final policy and reward, verify approximate \(N^{\frac12-\beta}\) relationship.
3. *Hyperparameter schedule test* – Use the prescribed \(\alpha_N,\zeta_N,\eta_N\) vs alternative scalings (constant, wrong power) and measure convergence stability and final error.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do the scaling effects and uncertainty quantification results extend to deep neural actor‑critic architectures, where different layers may exhibit varying sensitivities to scaling?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] “For starters, it would be interesting to study deep neural networks, as opposed to shallow neural networks. This was done for the regression problem in [34] and it was found that different layers have different sensitivity regarding scalings. It is interesting to explore similar kind of questions in the neural actor‑critic setup.”
- **Why unresolved:** The current analysis is restricted to shallow networks; deep architectures introduce multi‑layer interactions and potentially layer‑dependent scaling behaviors that are not captured by the present asymptotic expansion.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretical framework for deep actor‑critic networks (e.g., via mean‑field analysis or layer‑wise scaling exponents) and experimental validation across architectures.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the effects of alternative optimization algorithms (such as RMSProp or Adam) on the scaling and uncertainty properties of neural actor‑critic methods?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] “In addition, in this paper we studied the behavior for the standard SGD algorithm. It is interesting to study what happens for other widely used optimization algorithms like RMSProp or Adam.”
- **Why unresolved:** The analysis relies on fixed learning rates and properties of SGD; adaptive optimizers modify effective step sizes per parameter, which may alter the scaling regimes and the derived bias–variance trade‑offs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extension of the asymptotic expansion to Adam/RMSProp, including modified scaling relations and empirical comparisons on standard benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do the asymptotic expansions and hyperparameter guidelines remain valid for continuous state and action spaces, or do they require fundamental modifications?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper assumes finite state and action spaces (Assumption 2.1) throughout its analysis and proofs.
- **Why unresolved:** Continuous spaces introduce function approximation error, measure‑theoretic complications, and require different techniques (e.g., integral operators) that may change the scaling laws and the form of the expansion.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A generalization of the theory to continuous spaces (e.g., using RKHSs or neural tangent kernels in infinite‑dimensional settings) or rigorous empirical studies on continuous control tasks that test the robustness of the guidelines.

## Limitations
- The analysis is restricted to shallow networks; deep architectures may exhibit different scaling behavior.
- Requires smooth, bounded activation functions; non‑smooth activations (e.g., ReLU) are not covered.
- The asymptotic regime (\(N\to\infty\)) may not reflect finite‑\(N\) behavior, particularly near \(\beta=1/2\).
- Experiments are conducted only on a small forest MDP; generalizability to larger or more complex MDPs is uncertain.
- The approach relies on specific exploration schedules; other schedules could break the theoretical guarantees.

## Confidence
- **Variance scaling:** High – the asymptotic expansion is rigorous and experiments agree.
- **Bias optimum (\(\beta=3/4\)):** Medium – derived from two competing exponents; additional bias sources (e.g., optimization error) could shift the optimum.
- **Hyperparameter scaling:** Medium – theoretical conditions are clear, but the optimal constant factors require careful tuning in practice.

## Next Checks
1. **Variance exponent test:** Sweep network width \(N\) and scaling \(\beta\), measure output variance, and confirm the power law with exponent \(0.5-\beta\).
2. **Bias optimum under optimization error:** Introduce early stopping or limit the number of SGD steps per iteration; check whether the empirically optimal \(\beta\) deviates from \(0.75\).
3. **Scaling violation test:** Use constant learning rates or an exploration decay that is too fast; observe divergence or degradation in performance to validate the necessity of the prescribed schedules.
