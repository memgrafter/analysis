# Real-Time LiDAR Point Cloud Densification for Low-Latency Spatial Data Transmission

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.01210
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01210
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** Achieves Full HD dense depth maps at 30 fps with 23 ms processing per frame, 15× faster than BP‑Net, while producing accurate multi‑view geometry without ghosting artifacts.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a real‑time system for densifying sparse LiDAR point clouds to enable low‑latency spatial data transmission. The approach merges data from three 10 fps LiDAR sensors per viewpoint to achieve a pseudo‑30 fps depth stream, then uses a GPU‑optimized joint bilateral filter guided by high‑resolution RGB images to produce Full HD dense depth maps at 30 fps. Preprocessing steps remove motion‑induced ghosts and occluded points, ensuring accurate multi‑view geometry. The method runs in 23 ms per frame on an RTX 4090, over 15× faster than the learning‑based BP‑Net, while maintaining high quality without ghosting artifacts.

## Method Summary
The task is real‑time depth completion of sparse LiDAR point clouds projected into camera views. For each viewpoint, the pipeline acquires an RGB frame and three LiDAR scans (operating at 10 fps each) that are interleaved to approximate a 30 fps depth stream. The scans are merged into a single depth image. Preprocessing removes motion ghosts (by detecting moving regions via frame differencing on the RGB stream) and discards occluded points (by depth consistency checks). The sparse depth is then densified using a two‑stage joint bilateral filter: first downsample to ⅓ resolution, filter with radius 4; then upsample and filter again with radius 2. The filter is implemented via convolution‑like tensor operations (convolution, element‑wise multiplication, transpose convolution) to exploit GPU parallelism. Finally, a contour removal step thresholding the depth gradient suppresses noisy points along edges. No training is required; all parameters are empirically set.

## Key Results
- **Real‑time performance:** 23 ms per frame at Full HD (1080p), achieving 30 fps.
- **Speedup:** Over 15× faster than BP‑Net (420 ms per frame).
- **Quality:** Accurate multi‑view geometry without ghosting or double‑surface artifacts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Combining three LiDAR sensors per viewpoint, staggered in time, increases effective point density and frame rate, providing a denser sparse depth input for subsequent processing.
- **Mechanism:** Each LiDAR operates at 10 fps; interleaving their captures yields a pseudo‑30 fps stream. The three scans cover slightly different scene regions due to temporal changes and possible spatial offsets, increasing overall point coverage when merged and projected into the camera view.
- **Core assumption:** The relative poses and timing of the LiDAR sensors are precisely calibrated, and the scene is relatively static between frames (or motion is handled by preprocessing).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2]: “by interleaving three LiDAR scans, we obtain a pseudo‑30 fps colored depth stream for that viewpoint.”
  - [section 4.1]: “This multi‑frame fusion increases point density because each LiDAR captures slightly different parts of the scene.”
  - [corpus]: Nearby works (e.g., CTE‑MLO, GS‑LIVO) also use multi‑LiDAR fusion, but the specific integration and benefit described here are from the paper.
- **Break condition:** If motion between LiDAR frames is too fast for preprocessing to remove ghosts, or if sensors are poorly calibrated, the merged point cloud may contain misalignments and occlusions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Joint bilateral filtering with RGB guidance propagates sparse depth values while preserving edges aligned with color cues, yielding a dense depth map that respects object boundaries.
- **Mechanism:** For each pixel lacking depth, the filter computes a weighted average of nearby depth observations, where weights decay with color difference (from the RGB image) and spatial distance. This biases interpolation toward pixels of similar color, maintaining discontinuities at color edges.
- **Core assumption:** Color edges in the RGB image are well‑correlated with geometric depth edges (i.e., objects with different depths have distinct colors). Also, the sparse depth points are accurately measured and correctly projected.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “applies a joint bilateral filtering strategy”
  - [section 4.2]: Equations and Algorithm 1 detail the weighting and aggregation steps.
  - [corpus]: Many guided depth completion methods rely on this assumption; the paper’s specific implementation is self‑contained.
- **Break condition:** In scenes where color and depth are uncorrelated (e.g., uniformly colored objects with depth variations), the filter may blur depth details. Noisy or poorly exposed RGB imagery can also degrade guidance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reformulating bilateral filtering as a sequence of convolution‑like tensor operations enables highly parallel GPU execution, achieving real‑time performance at Full HD resolution.
- **Mechanism:** The algorithm decomposes the filter into: (1) compute color differences (like a convolution over a neighborhood), (2) apply color Gaussian (element‑wise), (3) multiply by spatial Gaussian (element‑wise), (4) distribute depth values via a transpose convolution (scatter), and (5) normalize. These map to optimized GPU kernels in frameworks like PyTorch.
- **Core assumption:** The GPU hardware and deep‑learning library can efficiently execute these tensor operations, and filter radii are small enough to keep memory/compute bounded.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2]: “We implement this filtering process using operations that can be mapped to efficient GPU kernels.”
  - [section 5]: “Our method took only about 23 ms” on an RTX 4090 for 1080p.
  - [corpus]: Other real‑time methods also leverage GPU parallelism, but this speed claim is directly from the paper.
- **Break condition:** Very large filter radii or ultra‑high resolutions (e.g., 4K) may exceed GPU memory or compute, reducing frame rate. Older or less‑optimized GPUs will also perform worse.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Each viewpoint runs a pipeline:  
  1. **LiDAR merger** projects and merges three LiDAR frames into a depth image.  
  2. **Preprocessor** removes afterimages (motion‑based) and occluded points (depth‑based).  
  3. **Depth densifier** applies a two‑stage joint bilateral filter (downsample → filter → upsample → filter).  
  4. **Contour remover** thresholds depth gradient to suppress boundary noise.  
  All viewpoints then overlay their dense colored point clouds into a common 3D coordinate system.
- **Critical path:**  
  For one viewpoint:  
  Acquire RGB + three LiDAR frames → Merge LiDAR → Preprocessing (motion detection, occlusion removal) → **Two‑stage JBF (computational bottleneck)** → Contour removal → Output point cloud.  
  Parallel processing across viewpoints and the 23 ms per‑view densification enable 30 fps end‑to‑end.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Speed vs. accuracy:** A simple filter‑based method was chosen over learning‑based (e.g., BP‑Net) for 15× speedup, potentially sacrificing modeling of complex depth patterns.
  - **No training:** Avoids need for dense ground truth and domain adaptation, but parameters (σ_c, σ_p, thresholds) are fixed or manually tuned, limiting adaptability.
  - **Two‑stage filtering:** Downsampling reduces compute but may lose fine details; the second stage with a small radius tries to recover them.
  - **Heuristic preprocessing:** Simple thresholds (τ_m, depth offset) are fast but may fail under low motion contrast or with transparent/reflective surfaces.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Ghosting artifacts** around moving objects: preprocessing missed afterimages.
  - **Double surfaces** in multi‑view fusion: depth bias from per‑view processing (JBF or preprocessing).
  - **Excessive contour noise:** points scattered along edges, before/after contour removal.
  - **Frame‑rate drop:** below 30 fps, indicating computational overload (e.g., too large radius or resolution).
  - **Color misalignment:** points colored incorrectly due to occlusion removal errors.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Static scene accuracy:** Capture a planar target at known distance; compare densified depth against high‑resolution ground truth to validate geometric fidelity.
  2. **Motion ghosting test:** Have a person move at various speeds; inspect the point cloud for residual ghost trails behind the person.
  3. **Multi‑view consistency:** Use two or more viewpoints on a depth‑varying object; merge point clouds and check for double surfaces or misalignment. Also record per‑view latency to confirm real‑time performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to unseen environments** – The method is evaluated only on a single indoor scene; it is unclear how it performs outdoors or on diverse datasets without retraining or parameter adjustment.
2. **Parameter sensitivity and adaptation** – The bilateral filter parameters (σ_c, σ_p, radii) and thresholds are set empirically; no study explores their impact across different scene contents or how to adapt them automatically.
3. **Contour removal effectiveness** – The post‑processing step of depth gradient thresholding is not quantified; its impact on fine detail preservation and multi‑view consistency remains unknown.
4. **Scalability to higher resolutions and frame rates** – The system is benchmarked only at 1080p/30 fps on a high‑end GPU; it is unclear if real‑time performance can be maintained at 4K or 60 fps.

## Limitations
- **Parameter ambiguities:** Exact values for critical parameters (e.g., bilateral filter sigmas, motion threshold τ_m, occlusion removal thresholds) are not provided, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Edge‑preservation assumption:** The joint bilateral filter relies on a strong correlation between color edges and depth discontinuities; this assumption may fail in textureless or uniformly colored regions, causing edge blurring.
- **Hardware‑specific performance:** Real‑time results are demonstrated only on an RTX 4090; performance on lower‑end GPUs or at higher spatial/temporal resolutions is not investigated.

## Confidence
- **Multi‑LiDAR fusion:** High
- **Edge‑preserving densification:** Medium
- **Real‑time performance:** High
