## Executive Summary
GraphDancer introduces a reinforcement learning framework that trains moderate-sized LLMs to interact with heterogeneous graph environments through interleaved reasoning and function execution. The core novelty lies in a graph-aware curriculum that gradually exposes the model to structurally more complex questions, stabilizing training and enabling robust multi-hop exploration. Experiments on the GRBench benchmark and four unseen domains show that a 3B backbone outperforms larger models (14B) and GPT-4o-mini, with substantial improvements in format validity, loop/timeout rates, and accuracy on medium/hard samples.

## Method Summary
The method formulates graph QA as a Markov decision process where the agent emits alternating `<think>` and `<graph>` blocks; the environment executes graph functions (e.g., neighbor search, feature retrieval) and returns `<information>` updates. A rule-based reward combines exact match with shaping terms for format validity and answer presence. Training uses Proximal Policy Optimization with KL regularization to keep the policy close to the initial instruct model. The curriculum sampler biases sampling toward easier questions early on by mixing a time-varying Gaussian distribution over difficulty levels with a uniform prior, where difficulty is defined by number of reasoning rounds and neighborhood expansions (E‑rounds). This controlled exposure helps the model acquire basic graph interaction skills before facing harder instances.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Curriculum Sampler → (x, G, y⋆) → Policy LLM (generates <think>, <graph>) → Graph Environment (executes, returns <information>) → Interaction Buffer (stores trajectories) → RL Trainer (actor‑critic update with KL penalty) ↔ Reward Calculator.

### Critical Path
1. Curriculum sampler selects a training instance with difficulty *k*.
2. Rollout for up to *T* rounds: LLM emits `<think>` and `<graph>`; environment executes, appends `<information>`.
3. Transcript τ is formed.
4. Reward r(x, G, τ) is computed.
5. Advantages are estimated on agent tokens.
6. Policy π<sub>θ</sub> is updated via PPO with KL regularization.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Curriculum mixture η(t): Linear 0.2→0.8 balances progress and stability; too aggressive causes instability, too slow wastes compute.
- KL coefficient β=0.001: Prevents collapse but may slow learning.
- Interaction budget T=10: Trade‑off between exploration depth and loop risk.
- Format reward λ<sub>struct</sub>, λ<sub>final</sub>: Must be tuned to avoid overfitting format or neglecting accuracy.

### Failure Signatures
- Invalid format: missing/malformed blocks, extra text in `<graph>`.
- Loop/Timeout: exceeds *T* rounds, often due to aimless exploration.
- Premature stop: early incorrect answer, especially on Hard.
- Low evidence hit: gold answer never appears in `<information>`.
- Schema violation: arguments mismatch node/feature types.
