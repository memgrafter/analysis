# Hidden Clones: Exposing and Fixing Family Bias in Vision-Language Model Ensembles

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17111
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17111
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** Family-aware ensemble methods recover catastrophic failures on the Misleading tier (+18–26 pp); LCS achieves state-of-the-art 87.83% on VQAv2 test-standard with consistent gains across benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper uncovers a subtle but significant source of error in ensembles of vision-language models (VLMs): family bias, where models sharing the same architectural backbone exhibit correlated errors. This correlation reduces the effective number of independent voters in majority voting to just 2.5–3.6, and creates a “Misleading tier” of questions (1.5–6.5% of the dataset) where the majority vote is wrong even though the best individual model is correct. The phenomenon is demonstrated across three VQA benchmarks using a pool of 17 state-of-the-art VLMs from eight families.

To mitigate family bias, the authors propose three novel aggregation strategies. Hierarchical Family Voting (HFV) performs a two-stage aggregation, first within each family and then across families, which decorrelates the voting pool and recovers 18–26 percentage points on the Misleading tier. Quality-weighted Redundancy-Corrected Calibrated Voting (QualRCCV) is a training-free method that combines calibration, inverse family size weighting, and family-level quality scaling to consistently surpass standard calibrated voting. Finally, Learned Candidate Scoring (LCS) re-ranks answer candidates using a diverse set of per-answer features (support breadth, family diversity, supporter quality) and a cross-validated classifier, achieving the largest gains (+0.68% VQAv2, +0.61% TextVQA, +2.45% GQA) and never degrading performance; on VQAv2 test-standard, LCS reaches 87.83%.

## Method Summary
The paper combines empirical analysis and algorithmic contributions to address family bias in VLM ensembles. Using predictions from 17 VLMs (8 families) on VQAv2, TextVQA, and GQA, it quantifies within-family error correlation (mean r=0.67) versus cross-family (r=0.53), and shows the effective voting dimensionality is below 4. The proposed methods are: (1) HFV, which aggregates within families using calibrated voting (weights = log-odds of family Stage1 accuracy) and then across families using family log-odds; (2) QualRCCV, which scales each model’s calibrated weight by (family_best_accuracy)^γ / (family_size)^ρ; (3) LCS, which takes the top-5 QualRCCV candidates per question, extracts features like number of supporting models/families, total weight, margin, average/maximum supporter accuracy, and whether the globally best model supports, then trains a LightGBM classifier to pick the correct candidate. Reproduction requires obtaining model outputs, computing accuracy-based weights, implementing the weighting schemes, extracting features, and performing 5-fold cross-validation with careful recomputation of statistics to avoid leakage.

## Key Results
- HFV recovers 18–26 percentage points on the Misleading tier.
- QualRCCV provides consistent training-free gains (+0.17% VQAv2, +0.21% TextVQA, +0.31% GQA).
- LCS yields the largest improvements (+0.68% VQAv2, +0.61% TextVQA, +2.45% GQA), never degrades any benchmark, and reaches 87.83% on VQAv2 test-standard.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Hierarchical Family Voting (HFV) recovers accuracy on the “Misleading tier” by decorrelating the voting pool through two-stage aggregation.
- **Mechanism:** HFV first aggregates model votes *within* each architectural family using calibrated voting, producing one family-level answer per family. It then aggregates these family-level answers *across* families using weights based on each family’s aggregated accuracy. This prevents a large family with highly correlated errors from dominating the final vote.
- **Core assumption:** Within-family error correlation (ρw) is significantly higher than between-family correlation (ρb), and all families have accuracy >50% on the target tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “Family-correlated errors reduce effective ensemble dimensionality to 2.5–3.6 independent voters and create a Misleading tier… HFV aggregates within families first, recovering +18–26 pp on the Misleading tier.”
  - [section 4.3] “within-family r= 0.67±0.12, cross-family r= 0.53±0.07… effective dimensionality (participation ratio) is only 2.86.”
  - [corpus] Evidence is weak; neighboring papers address bias and spatial reasoning, not ensemble family structure.
- **Break condition:** HFV degrades performance when a family’s accuracy is near or below random chance (e.g., InternVL3 at 49.3% on TextVQA), as equalizing family influence then amplifies poor predictions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Quality-weighted Redundancy-Corrected Calibrated Voting (QualRCCV) provides consistent, training-free gains by simultaneously down-weighting redundant votes from large families and up-weighting families with at least one strong performer.
- **Mechanism:** QualRCCV modifies standard calibrated voting by scaling each model’s weight by (family_best_accuracy)^γ / (family_size)^ρ. This reduces the cumulative influence of large families (correcting redundancy) while preserving the contribution of high-quality families regardless of size.
- **Core assumption:** The highest individual accuracy within a family (qf) is a meaningful proxy for the family’s overall utility, and the redundancy correction factor (1/|F|^ρ) accurately reflects the marginal information added by additional models from the same family.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “QualRCCV… weighting models by calibration, family quality, and inverse family size… beats calibrated voting on all three benchmarks (+0.17% VQAv2…)”
  - [section 5.4] “QualRCCV is entirely training-free and universally improves over calibrated voting on all three benchmarks.”
  - [corpus] Not directly supported; corpus focuses on bias and robustness, not ensemble weighting schemes.
- **Break condition:** The hyperparameters (ρ, γ) may need re-tuning for significantly different model pools or tasks; the method assumes family quality is stable and representative.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Learned Candidate Scoring (LCS) achieves the largest, most robust gains by re-ranking individual answer candidates using features that capture per-answer support breadth, family diversity, and supporter quality.
- **Mechanism:** For each question, LCS generates a shortlist of candidate answers (e.g., top-5 from QualRCCV voting). It extracts features for each candidate (e.g., number of supporting models/families, total voting margin, accuracy of supporters, whether the best overall model supports it). A cross-validated classifier (GBM) scores each candidate; the highest-scoring candidate is selected. This allows the system to trust a minority answer if it is backed by high-quality, diverse supporters.
- **Core assumption:** The correct answer possesses a distinctive feature profile (strong consensus, diverse family support, backed by accurate models) that a learned classifier can identify more reliably than any fixed aggregation rule.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “Learned Candidate Scoring (LCS)… re-ranks candidate answers using support breadth, family diversity, and model quality… achieving the largest gains: +0.68% VQAv2… is the only learned method that never degrades any benchmark.”
  - [section 6.1] “LCS achieves 87.83% on VQAv2 test-standard… LCS is the only learned method that never degrades any benchmark.”
  - [section 6.1] “On GQA, where standard voting falls below the single best model, LCS recovers to 66.47%—more than 2.2 pp above the best individual model.”
  - [corpus] Weak; neighboring papers use learning for routing or bias mitigation, but not candidate scoring with these specific features.
- **Break condition:** Requires a labeled validation set for training the scorer; performance depends on the quality and diversity of the initial candidate pool (K). If the correct answer is not in the top-K candidates, LCS cannot recover it.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Ensemble Diversity & Correlation**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper’s core diagnosis is that models from the same architectural family have correlated errors, violating the independence assumption in Condorcet’s jury theorem and reducing the effective number of independent voters. Understanding this correlation structure is prerequisite to designing family-aware methods.
  - **Quick check question:** If two models always err on the same questions, what happens to the marginal benefit of adding the second model to a majority vote?

- **Concept: Calibration & Weighted Voting**
  - **Why needed here:** The baselines (calibrated voting) and proposed methods (QualRCCV, HFV weights) rely on transforming model accuracies into log-odds weights. Understanding why raw accuracies are suboptimal and how log-odds calibration works is essential to follow the weight modifications.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might a model with 80% accuracy not be assigned a weight proportional to 0.8 in a calibrated voting scheme?

- **Concept: Answer-Level vs. Method-Level Routing**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper contrasts prior learned methods (e.g., FAAR-learn) that route *between* fixed aggregation strategies per question with LCS, which scores *individual answer candidates* per question. This granularity shift is key to LCS’s robustness.
  - **Quick check question:** What is a scenario where method-level routing fails but answer-level scoring could succeed?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Model Pool:** Heterogeneous VLMs from known architectural families (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL, Molmo).
  2. **Family Detector:** Static mapping from model name/ID to family label (no runtime clustering needed if families are known).
  3. **Aggregation Engine:** Implements multiple voting strategies:
     - Baselines: Majority, Calibrated (per-model log-odds weights).
     - Training-free: RCCV, QualRCCV (soft family redundancy correction), HFV/HFV-sharp (two-stage hierarchical voting).
     - Learned: LCS (candidate generation, feature extraction, cross-validated classifier).
  4. **Evaluation Harness:** Runs all methods on benchmark splits (VQAv2, TextVQA, GQA), computes per-tier metrics (Misleading recovery), and performs statistical testing (bootstrap CIs, paired tests).
  5. **Analysis Toolkit:** Computes error correlation matrices, effective dimensionality (participation ratio), and spectral clustering for family verification.

- **Critical path:**
  1. **Validate family structure:** Compute pairwise model error correlations on a held-out validation set. Confirm within-family correlation significantly exceeds cross-family correlation (expected Δr ~0.13–0.15).
  2. **Compute baselines:** Establish single-model, majority, and calibrated voting accuracies. Identify the size of the Misleading tier (questions where best model is correct but majority vote is wrong).
  3. **Apply family-aware methods:** Implement HFV and QualRCCV. Benchmark them against calibrated voting on all three benchmarks. Expect HFV to show large gains on Misleading tier but potential degradation on benchmarks with a very weak family (TextVQA).
  4. **Train LCS:** For each benchmark, perform 5-fold cross-validation. For each fold: recompute calibration weights on train fold, generate candidate lists on validation fold, train GBM on train-fold features, evaluate on validation fold. Ensure no data leakage.
  5. **Test generalization:** Train LCS on full validation set and submit to held-out test set (EvalAI for VQAv2).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Training-free vs. Learned:** HFV/QualRCCV are zero-cost, interpretable, and safe for immediate deployment but may not capture complex per-question patterns. LCS requires labeled data and cross-validation but yields larger, more consistent gains and is the only method that never degrades any benchmark.
  - **Hard vs. Soft Family Correction:** HFV uses a hard hierarchical collapse (one vote per family), maximizing decorrelation but risking quality dilution if families are imbalanced. QualRCCV uses soft weighting, preserving some influence of large families while still correcting redundancy.
  - **Candidate List Size (K):** Larger K gives LCS more opportunity to find a correct minority answer but increases classifier noise and computational cost. Ablation shows K=5 is near-optimal; gains plateau after K=3.
  - **Feature Engineering:** LCS features include consensus metrics (margin), diversity metrics (family count), and quality metrics (supporter accuracies). The margin is the dominant feature, but quality features are crucial for GQA gains.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **HFV degrades on a benchmark:** Check family accuracy distribution. If one family’s accuracy is far below others (e.g., InternVL3 ~49% on TextVQA), HFV’s equalization is harmful. Switch to HFV-sharp (α>1) or QualRCCV.
  - **QualRCCV underperforms:** Verify hyperparameters (ρ=0.4, γ=1.0) are appropriate for the current family size distribution. If families are more balanced, the redundancy correction may be too aggressive.
  - **LCS degrades a benchmark:** Inspect feature ablation. If dropping quality features causes large drop, the training set may not have enough examples where minority high-quality supporters indicate correctness. Ensure cross-validation is strict (no leakage of model/test accuracies into training folds).
  - **No Misleading tier recovery:** If the Misleading tier is small (<1.5%) or family correlation gap (ρw–ρb) is small, family-aware methods will have minimal impact. Confirm the tier exists by counting questions where `best_model_correct & majority_wrong`.
  - **Metric mismatch:** VQAv2 and TextVQA require soft accuracy (match any of 10 ground-truth answers); GQA requires exact match; ensure same normalization.
  - **Ties in weighted sums:** implement deterministic tie-breaking (e.g., alphabetical order) or fix random seed.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Correlation & Dimensionality Analysis:** On a held-out validation set (e.g., VQAv2 minival), compute the full 17x17 error correlation matrix. Calculate within-family vs. cross-family mean correlation, conduct Mann-Whitney test. Compute participation ratio (effective voters). Confirm eigenvalue decay (λ1 >50%, top-5 >75%). This validates the paper’s core premise.
  2. **Misleading Tier Identification & HFV Test:** For each benchmark, classify questions into the 5-tier taxonomy (Trivial, Easy, Misleading, Hard, Impossible). Specifically extract the Misleading tier. Run HFV and calibrated voting *only* on this tier. Expect HFV to achieve ~20-26 pp recovery while calibrated remains near 0%. This is the cleanest mechanistic validation.
  3. **Ablation of LCS Components:** Train LCS with sequential feature ablation (remove quality, remove consensus, remove answer properties) and varying candidate list sizes (K=1,3,5,10). Plot accuracy vs. K and feature set. Confirm margin is dominant but other features add value, especially on GQA. Also run a “per-model family” ablation for HFV (Table 9) to confirm that finer-grained families (sub-families) can improve over coarser ones but “per-model” (no within-family aggregation) hurts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does the degree of family size imbalance (e.g., one family having many more models) affect the performance gains of family-aware methods like HFV?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Limitation 1 notes the ensemble is dominated by Qwen2.5-VL (5/17 models) and suggests more balanced pools may show smaller effects; Proposition 1 condition (iii) requires imbalance.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper does not systematically vary family size distributions, so the separate contributions of correlation strength and imbalance remain unclear.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Create synthetic ensembles with fixed within-family error correlation but varying family size ratios, then measure HFV/QualRCCV performance across benchmarks.

- **Open Question 2:** Do family-correlated errors and the benefits of family-aware methods generalize to generative VLM tasks (e.g., image captioning, free-form VQA)?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Limitation 2 states evaluation was limited to short-answer VQA, not open-ended generation.
  - **Why unresolved:** Generative tasks have different output spaces and evaluation metrics; family correlation may manifest differently, and methods like LCS may need redesign.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the study on generative benchmarks, quantify family correlation structure, and compare gains of HFV/QualRCCV/LCS to short-answer VQA results.

- **Open Question 3:** Can LCS’s gains be achieved without training on validation data, i.e., via a training-free heuristic using the same features?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Limitation 3 notes LCS hyperparameters were tuned on development data, unlike the training-free QualRCCV.
  - **Why unresolved:** It is unknown whether LCS’s learned scoring is essential or if a simple rule-based approach using per-answer features could suffice.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop training-free scoring functions (e.g., weighted sums of LCS features) and evaluate them against LCS on multiple benchmarks with varying training set sizes.

- **Open Question 4:** Why are LCS improvements concentrated on specific question types (e.g., numerical questions in VQAv2, query/logical in GQA) and not others?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Per-type analysis (Section 6.6) shows LCS gains vary widely: +2.01% on number vs. negligible on yes/no in VQAv2; large gains on query/logical in GQA—unexplained.
  - **Why unresolved:** Understanding this concentration could guide when to deploy LCS and inspire better feature design.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Correlate per-type LCS gains with question characteristics (e.g., answer diversity, model disagreement entropy, reasoning depth) and conduct qualitative error analysis on type-specific cases.

## Limitations
- The evaluation uses a fixed pool of 17 VLMs from 8 families, which may not represent broader model diversity.
- LCS implementation details (e.g., feature engineering, LightGBM hyperparameters) are incompletely specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Test-standard results use only 12 of the 17 models, possibly biasing estimates.
- The Misleading tier definition depends on the specific ensemble composition.

## Confidence
- **Family bias reduces effective voters to ~3:** High (supported by correlation analysis and participation ratio).
- **HFV recovers Misleading tier:** Medium (gains are large but HFV degrades on benchmarks with a weak family, e.g., InternVL3 on TextVQA).
- **QualRCCV beats calibrated voting:** Medium (consistent small gains; sensitivity to hyperparameters ρ,γ unknown).
- **LCS provides robust large gains:** High (never degrades, +0.68% VQAv2, +2.45% GQA), but requires validation data and top-K candidates.
