# Beyond the Unit Hypersphere: Embedding Magnitude in Contrastive Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.09229
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09229
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Magnitude-aware contrastive learning (via dot product or unilateral normalization) significantly boosts out-of-domain retrieval and RAG accuracy but degrades symmetric tasks; normalizing exactly one side consistently outperforms full normalization or none, with the Fisher Information Matrix condition number guiding the choice.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates the role of embedding magnitude in contrastive learning, challenging the common practice of using cosine similarity which discards magnitude. By independently controlling magnitude on the query and document sides, the authors find that learning magnitude benefits asymmetric tasks like retrieval and RAG, but harms symmetric tasks like semantic textual similarity and CLIP. Document magnitude scales inference scores, while query magnitude modulates gradient magnitudes via an effective temperature. Normalizing exactly one side (query-only or document-only) yields the best performance across tasks. The condition number of the Fisher Information Matrix predicts which side should be normalized, providing a practical guideline. Magnitude-aware methods achieve substantial out-of-domain gains (up to +72%) and RAG improvements (up to +24%), but require retrieval-focused pretraining or sufficient data.

## Method Summary
The authors modify standard contrastive learning pipelines by replacing cosine similarity with dot product or partial normalization schemes. In unilateral normalization, either the query embeddings or the document embeddings are normalized to unit length, while the other side retains its learned magnitude. This allows independent control of magnitude on each side. The training objective remains the InfoNCE loss, but the similarity function changes. The magnitude of the document embedding directly scales the similarity score at inference, while query magnitude affects the gradient scale, effectively acting as a temperature parameter. The authors also analyze the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) of the embeddings and find its condition number correlates with whether query-side or document-side normalization is preferable.

## Key Results
- Magnitude learning (dot product or unilateral norm) improves retrieval and RAG (asymmetric tasks) but catastrophically degrades STS and CLIP (symmetric tasks).
- Normalizing exactly one side (query-only or document-only) consistently outperforms normalizing both sides (cosine) or neither.
- The FIM condition number predicts which side to normalize: high condition number suggests normalizing queries; low suggests normalizing documents.
- Unilateral normalization yields up to +72% out-of-domain retrieval gains vs +7% in-domain, and up to +24% RAG accuracy improvement.
- Benefits require retrieval-specialized pretraining or large data; they vanish with standard MLM pretraining or small datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
In asymmetric tasks (e.g., retrieval), the query and document play different roles: the query is a short, information-seeking input, while the document is a longer, content-rich target. Document magnitude encodes the amount of content or relevance information; larger magnitude indicates a more informative document, scaling the similarity score. Query magnitude, on the other hand, modulates the effective temperature of the softmax loss: larger query magnitudes sharpen the distribution, increasing gradient strength. Normalizing the wrong side disrupts these role-specific effects, leading to catastrophic failures on symmetric tasks where query and document are interchangeable (e.g., STS). The FIM condition number captures the curvature of the loss landscape; a high condition number indicates ill-conditioned gradients where query normalization stabilizes training, while a low condition number suggests document normalization better preserves information.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Query Encoder -> Query Embedding -> (Optional Normalization) -> Similarity Computation with Document Embedding (from Document Encoder) -> InfoNCE Loss. Document Encoder may also have optional normalization. The normalization step can be applied to query only, document only, both, or neither.
- **Critical Path**: During training, a batch of query-document pairs is encoded. The similarity matrix is computed using the chosen similarity function (dot product or normalized variants). The InfoNCE loss is backpropagated. At inference, the document embeddings are indexed; for a query, similarity scores are computed against all documents, and the top-k are returned. Document magnitude directly scales the scores; query magnitude influences the ranking indirectly via training.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Normalizing both sides (cosine) discards magnitude, harming asymmetric tasks. Normalizing neither can lead to gradient instability. Unilateral normalization preserves magnitude on the side where it is beneficial (document for scaling scores, query for temperature) while normalizing the other to stabilize training. The choice of which side to normalize depends on data distribution and pretraining, as indicated by FIM condition number.
- **Failure Signatures**: On symmetric tasks (STS, CLIP), any magnitude learning (dot product or unilateral norm) causes large performance drops because magnitude introduces unwanted bias when roles are symmetric. Also, if the wrong side is normalized for asymmetric tasks, gains diminish.
- **First Three Experiments**:
  1. Compare dot product vs cosine vs unilateral normalization (query-only, document-only) on a retrieval benchmark (e.g., MS MARCO) and an STS benchmark to observe the asymmetric/symmetric divergence.
  2. Ablate the effect of document magnitude by scaling document embeddings at inference and measuring retrieval score changes.
  3. Compute the FIM condition number for different encoders (e.g., BERT-base, RoBERTa) and correlate with the optimal normalization side (query vs document) on a suite of retrieval tasks.

## Limitations
- Findings are limited to text embeddings and CLIP; generalization to other modalities or pretraining objectives is unverified.
- The FIM condition number predictor is empirical; its theoretical justification and stability across architectures and datasets need validation.
- Benefits require retrieval-specialized pretraining or large data; applicability to low-resource or standard MLM-pretrained models is unclear.
