# Speak or Stay Silent: Context-Aware Turn-Taking in Multi-Party Dialogue

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11409
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11409
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Supervised fine-tuning with LoRA and distilled reasoning traces improves balanced accuracy by up to 23 percentage points, achieving or exceeding human-level performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles disruptive turn-taking in multi-party conversations, where AI assistants incorrectly speak at every pause. The authors construct a benchmark of over 120K labeled decision points across three corpora, categorizing contexts into four types that distinguish when a participant should speak or stay silent. Zero-shot evaluations of eight large language models reveal consistent failure, particularly on nuanced cases. The proposed solution—supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with LoRA adapters and distilled reasoning traces—substantially improves performance, with the best models matching human accuracy. The work demonstrates that context-aware turn-taking is not an emergent capability and must be explicitly trained.

## Method Summary
The method involves supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of pretrained LLMs using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). The input context is formatted with special tags to include the full conversational history, the current utterance, and the target speaker ID. Models are trained to output either `<speak>` or `<silent>`. Two training modes are compared: a decision-only mode and a reasoning-with-decision mode that first generates a one-sentence justification (distilled from a teacher model, Gemini 2.5 Flash) before the decision. A balanced batch sampler is used to mitigate category imbalance. LoRA is applied to attention and MLP layers with rank 32, and the loss is computed only on the decision tokens (decision-only) or both reasoning and decision tokens (reasoning mode).

## Key Results
- A benchmark of 120K+ labeled turn-taking decision points across three multi-party corpora (Friends, Switchboard, AMI) with four contextual categories.
- Zero-shot evaluation of eight LLMs shows uniformly poor performance, with a strong bias toward speaking, especially on the "Referenced but not addressed" (S2) category.
- SFT with LoRA improves balanced accuracy by up to 23 percentage points. The best model (Llama-3.1-8B) achieves 63.75% average balanced accuracy, matching human performance.
- Distilling reasoning traces provides an additional gain of ~7 balanced accuracy points. Ablation studies confirm the optimal LoRA rank of 32.
- Cross-dataset training yields strong generalization, with an average balanced accuracy of 71.73%, matching in-domain results.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Zero-shot LLMs fail because the required pragmatic distinctions (e.g., "referenced but not addressed") are not an emergent capability of instruction tuning on general corpora.
- **Mechanism:** Instruction-tuned LLMs lack explicit training on nuanced multi-party conversational dynamics that differentiate silent bystandership from implicit invitation. Their pre-training focuses on dyadic response generation, not per-pause participation decisions grounded in full context.
- **Evidence anchors:** Uniformly poor zero-shot performance across eight models (Table 2); strong SPEAK bias on S2 category; corpus schema explicitly isolates challenging S2 cases.
- **Break condition:** If zero-shot S2 performance approached human levels (~27.67% BalAcc, Table 4) without training, the mechanism would be invalidated. Observed zero-shot S2 BalAcc is ~20–30%.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** SFT with LoRA improves performance by adapting internal representations to the supervised decision boundary without catastrophic forgetting.
- **Mechanism:** LoRA injects low-rank trainable matrices into attention and MLP layers, allowing the model to learn task-specific features (e.g., "ACTIVE PARTICIPANT" vs. "BYSTANDER" cues) while preserving base knowledge. Balanced batch sampling prevents category dominance.
- **Evidence anchors:** Consistent BalAcc gains (up to +23 pp) across models (Table 3); rank-32 ablation optimal; strong cross-dataset generalization (Table 5).
- **Break condition:** SFT would fail if it caused performance collapse on any category or failed to generalize. Instead, I1 remains high and cross-dataset BalAcc is strong.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Distilling reasoning traces boosts performance by making the decision process interpretable and aligned with human-like categorization (ACTIVE/BYSTANDER).
- **Mechanism:** Generating a one-sentence justification before the decision forces the model to articulate pragmatic cues, acting as a regularizer that prevents learning spurious correlations (e.g., keyword-based SPEAK bias).
- **Evidence anchors:** Reasoning mode improves Friends BalAcc by +7.2 points over decision-only (Table 5); generated justifications directly map to category definitions (Appendix).
- **Break condition:** If reasoning traces were ambiguous, they would not improve performance. Consistent gains support the mechanism.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** **Turn-Taking Theory (Adjacency Pairs & TRPs)**  
  *Why needed here:* Multi-party turn-taking extends dyadic Transition Relevance Places (TRPs) to ambiguous pauses with multiple listeners. The model must recognize when an utterance projects a next turn *for a specific participant*.  
  *Quick check:* After "Ross: Uhh, yes I did but there isn’t.", why does Phoebe (bystander) stay silent while Ross (addressed) might speak? (Answer: The utterance completes Ross's answer to Rachel; no invitation to others.)
- **Concept:** **Addressee Recognition**  
  *Why needed here:* The core decision hinges on whether the *target speaker* is the addressee, even if not explicitly named (I2) or mentioned third-person (S2). This requires tracking speaker intent beyond surface form.  
  *Quick check:* Why doesn't Chandler's "Get there faster Joey!" elicit a SPEAK decision for Joey? (Answer: Joey already reacted non-verbally; the utterance is a催促, not an invitation to speak.)
- **Concept:** **Class Imbalance & Pragmatic Bias**  
  *Why needed here:* The dataset has more SILENT (S1+S2) than SPEAK (I1+I2) points (Table 1). Models exhibit a SPEAK bias zero-shot; SFT must overcome this to learn the cost of false interruption.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a model default to SPEAK on S2 examples without training? (Answer: Surface heuristics like "name mention = response expected" fail because S2 involves mention without address.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Tokenized conversation context + target speaker ID -> Input encoder (with `<|context|>`, `<|current|>`, `<|instruction|>` tags) -> LoRA adapters (rank=32, α=64) on attention/MLP layers -> (optional: autoregressive reasoning token generation) -> Decision head (linear layer) -> {SPEAK, SILENT} decision token.
- **Critical path:** Context encoding -> LoRA adaptation -> (reasoning token generation, if enabled) -> decision token prediction. Loss computed on decision tokens only (decision-only) or on both reasoning and decision tokens (reasoning mode).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *LoRA rank:* Rank-32 optimal; rank-16 underperforms (insufficient capacity), rank-64 yields no gain (diminishing returns).  
  - *Context length:* Truncated to 2048 tokens; losing early turns may harm S2 where distant reference matters.  
  - *Reasoning cost:* Adds autoregressive step and latency; deploy only if accuracy gain justifies delay.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Zero-shot: SPEAK bias on S1/S2 (e.g., >80% SPEAK on S1).  
  - SFT: Poor cross-dataset generalization if trained on single corpus only; rank ≤16 causing underfitting on I2/S2.  
  - Reasoning mode: Repetitive/irrelevant justifications if teacher distillation prompt is misaligned.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Zero-shot baseline:** Run all 8 models on test splits with single system prompt; expect low BalAcc (~50–65%) and high SPEAK bias. Confirm failure.  
  2. **SFT decision-only:** Fine-tune with LoRA (r=32) and balanced batches; expect +10–20 BalAcc points. Verify I1 remains high, S1/S2 improve.  
  3. **Ablation - reasoning vs. decision-only:** Train same model in both modes on Friends; expect reasoning mode to win by ~5–7 BalAcc points (Table 5). Check reasoning quality qualitatively.

## Limitations
- Corpus limited to three English multi-party datasets; unclear if findings generalize to other languages, cultures, or dialogue genres (e.g., meetings, customer service).
- Reasoning traces distilled from a single Gemini model; teacher bias and stability not examined; no comparison to human-annotated justifications.
- No evaluation of inference latency; reasoning mode adds autoregressive steps which may impede real-time deployment.
- Human performance reported as average without inter-annotator agreement; weakens model-human parity claim.
- Class imbalance mitigation (balanced batch sampler) may not reflect natural distribution, risking deployment mismatch.

## Confidence
- **Zero-shot LLMs lack emergent context-aware turn-taking:** High (uniform failure across eight models, especially on S2, with accuracy far below human levels).
- **SFT with LoRA (rank 32) yields large improvements and cross-dataset generalization:** High (up to +23 pp BalAcc, rank-32 validated, cross-dataset ≈71.73% matches in-domain).
- **Distilled reasoning traces boost performance by aligning with human categorization:** Medium (~7 pp gain observed, but only one teacher used; reasoning quality and teacher dependence not deeply analyzed).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain generalization:** Test fine-tuned models on held-out multi-party corpora from different genres (e.g., work meetings, multilingual dialogues) to assess robustness beyond the three training corpora.
2. **Teacher diversity study:** Replace Gemini with multiple teachers (different sizes, architectures, and human annotators) to determine whether gains are contingent on the specific teacher or generalize to any structured reasoning trace.
3. **Latency and resource profiling:** Measure end-to-end inference time and memory for decision-only vs. reasoning modes in a simulated real-time environment to evaluate deployment feasibility.
