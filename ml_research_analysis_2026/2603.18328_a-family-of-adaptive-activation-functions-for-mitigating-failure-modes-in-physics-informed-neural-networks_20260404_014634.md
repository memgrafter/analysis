# A Family of Adaptive Activation Functions for Mitigating Failure Modes in Physics-Informed Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18328  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18328  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Adaptive wavelet-based activation functions dramatically improve PINN accuracy and stability on complex PDEs, reducing errors by orders of magnitude compared to standard tanh.

## Executive Summary
Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) often fail to accurately solve complex partial differential equations (PDEs), producing overly smooth or incorrect solutions. This work introduces a family of adaptive wavelet-based activation functions (e.g., SoftMexTanh, SoftGaborTanh) that combine trainable wavelet components with fixed or trainable hyperbolic tangent and softplus nonlinearities. By integrating these activations into a standard 4-layer, 512-neuron MLP and training with L-BFGS, the authors achieve substantial improvements across four benchmark PDEs. The proposed functions significantly lower training loss and relative error, particularly on high-frequency and nonlinear problems, and outperform recent PINN variants like PINNsFormer. The gains stem from enhanced representational capacity and adaptive tuning of activation parameters during training.

## Method Summary
The method replaces the standard tanh activation in PINNs with a custom function that blends a wavelet-shaped kernel (e.g., Mexican hat, Gabor) with a tanh term. Key wavelet parameters (e.g., frequency ω, Gaussian width α) are made trainable via softplus to maintain positivity, and optionally the tanh scaling β is also learnable. The neural network architecture remains a multilayer perceptron with four hidden layers of 512 units each. The PINN loss combines mean squared errors of the PDE residual and initial/boundary conditions, minimized using L-BFGS for 1000 iterations. The wavelet component introduces localized oscillatory behavior, enabling the network to capture high-frequency solution features that standard smooth activations miss. Variants with fixed β (denoted with “W”) are also explored to reduce parameters and sometimes improve accuracy on convection-dominated problems.

## Key Results
- On 1D convection (β'=50), SoftGaborTanhW achieves relative RMSE (rRMSE) of 0.0068, while standard tanh yields 0.796.  
- On 2D Navier–Stokes, SoftGaborTanhW reduces rRMSE from 12.356 (tanh) to 0.590.  
- The proposed activations consistently outperformed PINNsFormer and other recent PINN variants in both accuracy and computational efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Wavelet-based activations increase the neural network's representational capacity, enabling approximation of high-frequency or multi-scale PDE solutions that standard smooth activations cannot capture efficiently.  
**Mechanism:** Wavelet functions (e.g., Mexican hat, Gabor) are localized in both time/space and frequency. When combined with tanh and made trainable via softplus-parameterized coefficients, the activation dynamically adjusts its oscillatory behavior, frequency, and envelope during training. This allows the network to represent complex, high-frequency solution features without requiring prohibitively deep architectures.  
**Core assumption:** Standard PINNs fail on complex PDEs partly because bounded, low-frequency activations like tanh do not cover a broad enough frequency spectrum in the network’s hypothesis space.  
**Evidence anchors:** The paper defines activations like `SoftGaborTanh(x) = tanh(βx) + c·exp(-αx²)cos(ωx)` with trainable α, ω, c; experiments show dramatic error reductions on high-frequency problems.  
**Break condition:** If the PDE solution is very smooth (e.g., simple diffusion), the added wavelet complexity may overfit or converge slower than tanh. Successful adaptation also depends on stable gradient updates; poor initialization could leave wavelet parameters ineffective.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Making wavelet parameters trainable (via softplus) allows the activation function to adapt its shape to the specific PDE’s solution characteristics during training, leading to better loss landscapes and convergence.  
**Mechanism:** Parameters such as α (Gaussian width), ω (frequency), and β (tanh scaling) are updated alongside network weights via backpropagation. This low-dimensional, problem-specific tuning lets the activation, for example, increase oscillation frequency for convection-dominated problems or adjust localization for sharp gradients.  
**Core assumption:** A fixed one-size-fits-all activation is suboptimal across diverse PDEs; adaptivity at the activation level can regularize optimization and guide the network toward more accurate minima.  
**Evidence anchors:** The paper shows that for the 1D convection equation (β'=50), the W-variants (fixed β, trainable wavelet params) perform best, highlighting the importance of wavelet adaptivity. Ablation tables compare full vs. W variants.  
**Break condition:** Adaptive parameters might get stuck in poor local optima early due to initialization or loss weighting, causing incorrect specialization. The softplus positivity constraint helps but is not sufficient for guaranteed stability.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The combination of a smooth, bounded nonlinearity (tanh) with a wavelet kernel mitigates vanishing/exploding gradient issues common in deep PINNs, improving training stability and final accuracy.  
**Mechanism:** The tanh component provides bounded output and a derivative that does not explode. The wavelet envelope (e.g., `exp(-αx²)`) modulates the input’s effective scale, potentially keeping pre-activation values in a region with more stable gradients. Softplus on parameters ensures positivity, avoiding degenerate configurations like zero frequency.  
**Core assumption:** Standard tanh alone can still saturate in deep networks; the wavelet envelope can rescale inputs to maintain activations in tanh’s linear region more often.  
**Evidence anchors:** The paper reports consistently lower loss values (often orders of magnitude) for wavelet activations versus tanh across all tested PDEs. The use of softplus for parameterization is explicitly motivated by stability.  
**Break condition:** If the wavelet component induces high-frequency oscillations in the activation itself, it could create new gradient cliffs. The balance between tanh smoothing and wavelet modulation is empirical; poor hyperparameter choices might worsen stability.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input coordinates -> Affine transformation -> Custom activation (e.g., SoftGaborTanh) -> (repeat ×4 hidden layers) -> Linear output (u)

**Critical path:**  
1. Forward pass: Input -> [Affine + Activation] ×4 -> output prediction û.  
2. Compute PDE residuals: Use autograd to differentiate û w.r.t. inputs to obtain required derivatives (e.g., u_t, u_xx).  
3. Evaluate loss: Sum of MSEs for PDE residual, initial conditions, and boundary conditions (weights λ=1).  
4. Backward pass: Compute gradients for all network weights and activation parameters (if trainable); update via L-BFGS.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- W vs. non-W variants: Fixed β (W) reduces parameters and often improves convection results; non-W with trainable β can be better for smoother PDEs (e.g., reaction, wave).  
- Computational cost: Minimal overhead from extra activation parameters; main cost is L-BFGS on a 4×512 MLP.  
- Initialization: Wavelet parameters start near 1 (via softplus(1)); Gabor ω₀ should be 3 or 5. Poor initialization may cause gradient starvation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Training loss plateaus at high value (>1e-2) while tanh converges: activation parameters may be stuck.  
- Solution overly smooth despite low loss: wavelet frequency ω too small; check learned values.  
- High error on convection (β'=50) with SoftMexTanh/SoftMorTanh: switch to SoftGaborTanhW.  
- Autograd numerical instability: activation parameters explode; add regularization or adjust softplus.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. 1D reaction equation (smooth): compare tanh vs. SoftGaborTanh; expect faster loss drop and rRMSE ~0.002 vs 0.973.  
2. 1D convection (β'=50, high-frequency): tanh fails (rRMSE ~0.796) while SoftGaborTanhW succeeds (~0.0068).  
3. 2D Navier-Stokes (complex): tanh rRMSE ~12.35; SoftGaborTanhW should achieve <1.5; visualize pressure fields.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does hybrid Adam–L-BFGS optimization improve convergence for wavelet-activation PINNs?  
- Why does disabling trainable β (W-variants) sometimes reduce relative error while increasing overall loss?  
- For which classes of PDEs do full trainable-β variants underperform W-variants, and what PDE characteristics predict this?  
- How do the proposed activations scale with network depth and width beyond the tested 4-layer, 512-unit architecture?  
- How well do they generalize to high-dimensional, fractional, or stochastic PDEs?

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to only four PDEs; generalizability to broader classes (e.g., high-dimensional, stochastic) is uncertain.  
- Hyperparameter details are incomplete: fixed β value for W-variants not explicitly stated; Gabor ω₀ initialization choices (3 or 5) lack per-PDE guidance.  
- No analysis of computational overhead (runtime, memory) compared to standard activations or other PINN variants.  
- Theoretical justification for wavelet expressivity improving frequency coverage is intuitive but not formalized.

## Confidence
- **Performance on complex PDEs**: High – consistent order-of-magnitude error reductions reported.  
- **Mechanism (wavelet expressivity + adaptivity)**: Medium – supported by architecture and ablations but lacks direct empirical measurement of frequency spectrum or expressivity bounds.  
- **Superiority over recent PINN variants**: Medium – comparisons provided but may suffer from inconsistent baselines and lack of controlled experiments.

## Next Checks
- Extended benchmark suite: test the proposed activations on a diverse set of PDEs (including high-dimensional, time-dependent, and nonlinear systems) to assess robustness and generalizability.  
- Ablation on trainable parameters: systematically vary the learnable state of each activation component (β, α, ω) across different PDE classes to isolate contributions.  
- Sensitivity and resource analysis: perform initialization sensitivity tests (ω₀, α₀) and measure training time/memory overhead to ensure practical stability and quantify hidden costs.
