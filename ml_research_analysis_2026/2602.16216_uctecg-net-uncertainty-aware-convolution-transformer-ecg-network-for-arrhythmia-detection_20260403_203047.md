# UCTECG-Net: Uncertainty-aware Convolution Transformer ECG Network for Arrhythmia Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16216
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16216
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** UCTECG-Net achieves state-of-the-art arrhythmia detection accuracy (up to 98.58% on MIT-BIH) while providing superior uncertainty quantification for trustworthy clinical decision support.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical reliability gap in deep learning for ECG arrhythmia detection, where models typically lack explicit uncertainty estimates needed for safety-critical healthcare. It introduces UCTECG-Net, a hybrid neural network that processes raw ECG signals and their spectrograms in parallel—using convolutional layers for time-domain morphology and a Transformer for time-frequency spectral patterns. The framework integrates multiple uncertainty quantification methods (Monte Carlo Dropout, Deep Ensembles, Ensemble MCD) to produce well-calibrated confidence scores. Evaluated on standard benchmarks (MIT-BIH, PTB), UCTECG-Net not only outperforms LSTM, CNN, and Transformer baselines in classification accuracy but also demonstrates significantly better uncertainty awareness, as measured by Uncertainty Accuracy (UAcc) and related metrics. The results indicate a pathway toward more trustworthy AI-assisted cardiac diagnosis.

## Method Summary
UCTECG-Net employs a dual-branch architecture. The convolutional branch processes raw 1D ECG waveforms through four Conv1D-ReLU blocks and adaptive pooling to extract local temporal features like QRS complexes. The Transformer branch operates on spectrogram representations (generated via STFT) of the same signals, using a linear embedding followed by a two-layer Transformer encoder to model long-range spectral dependencies. Features from both branches are concatenated and classified through fully connected layers. For uncertainty quantification, the model is wrapped with three inference strategies: Monte Carlo Dropout (stochastic passes with dropout enabled), Deep Ensembles (averaging predictions from independently trained models), and Ensemble MCD (combining both). Predictive entropy derived from the aggregated output distribution serves as the uncertainty metric, enabling risk-aware classification.

## Key Results
- UCTECG-Net achieves classification accuracy of **98.58% on MIT-BIH** and **99.14% on PTB**, surpassing LSTM, CNN1D, and pure Transformer baselines.
- With Deep Ensembles or Ensemble MCD, the model attains the highest **Uncertainty Accuracy (UAcc)**—98.51% (MIT-BIH) and 99.14% (PTB)—indicating that its confidence estimates align closely with actual correctness.
- Uncertainty-aware evaluation shows **better calibration** than single-model or MCD-only counterparts, with superior separation between "certain-correct" and "uncertain-incorrect" predictions (higher USen/USpe).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Joint Raw and Spectrogram Processing
- **Claim:** Jointly processing raw ECG signals and their spectrograms in parallel branches captures complementary time-domain morphology and time-frequency spectral patterns, leading to more robust feature representations.
- **Mechanism:** The convolutional branch extracts localized temporal features (e.g., QRS complex shape, P-wave morphology) via 1D convolutions, while the Transformer branch on spectrograms learns long-range spectral dependencies and global context via self-attention. The fused features combine these complementary views, improving discrimination of subtle arrhythmias and noise robustness.
- **Core assumption:** ECG abnormalities manifest in both precise waveform morphology (best seen in raw signal) and distributed energy shifts across frequency bands (best seen in spectrograms); neither alone is sufficient for maximal accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "combines one-dimensional convolutions and Transformer encoders to process raw ECG signals and their spectrograms jointly."
  - [section 3.3] "The convolutional branch processes the raw ECG waveform... The transformer branch embeds the input... to extract long-range dependencies and global contextual relationships."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; neighbor papers (e.g., ECGformer, EKGNet) use single-modality or different hybrids, but none explicitly pair raw+spec with CNN+Transformer for uncertainty-aware ECG.
- **Break condition:** If spectrogram computation introduces misleading artifacts (e.g., from windowing parameters) that degrade Transformer learning, or if convolutional branch fails to extract meaningful morphology due to sampling rate or normalization issues.

### Mechanism 2: Ensemble-Based Uncertainty Quantification
- **Claim:** Integrating ensemble-based uncertainty quantification (Deep Ensembles, EMCD) within the UCTECG-Net framework yields better-calibrated confidence estimates aligned with actual correctness, enabling risk-aware flagging.
- **Mechanism:** Deep Ensembles train multiple model instances with varied initializations, capturing epistemic uncertainty through prediction diversity. EMCD adds stochastic dropout sampling per ensemble member, combining model uncertainty and approximate Bayesian posterior sampling. Predictive entropy from the aggregated distribution separates confident-correct from uncertain-incorrect predictions more cleanly than single-model MCD.
- **Core assumption:** Predictive uncertainty in ECG classification is dominated by model epistemic uncertainty (architecture/data limitations) rather than pure aleatoric noise; ensemble diversity exposes this effectively.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.2-2.3] Describes Deep Ensembles and EMCD predictive entropy as uncertainty metrics.
  - [section 4.2] UCTECG-Net with Ensemble/EMCD achieves higher UAcc (98.51% MIT-BIH, 99.14% PTB) vs. standalone Transformer or single-model MCD.
  - [corpus] Related studies [34,35] apply UQ to ECG but not within this specific hybrid architecture; evidence is indirect.
- **Break condition:** If ensemble members become overly similar (e.g., due to small architecture differences or data augmentation) reducing diversity, or if computational budget prevents sufficient ensemble size/dropout samples, leading to underestimated uncertainty.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid Architecture Stabilization
- **Claim:** The hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture inherently stabilizes latent feature learning, reducing prediction variance across runs and improving the separation between high-confidence and low-confidence prediction regions.
- **Mechanism:** Conv layers provide strong, consistent local feature detectors (translation-equivariant), while Transformer layers integrate context across the sequence. This multi-granularity yields less noisy gradients and more peaked predictive distributions for correct samples, increasing certainty separation (seen in density plots like Figure 1). The model's internal consistency makes ensemble variations more meaningful.
- **Core assumption:** ECG patterns require both invariant local detectors and flexible global integration; a single architecture type (pure LSTM or pure Transformer) is more prone to feature collapse or noisy attention, harming uncertainty calibration.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1] UCTECG-Net shows lower standard deviation in accuracy metrics vs. LSTM/CNN1D/Transformer baselines.
  - [section 4.2] UCTECG-Net-EMCD has higher USen/USpe than Transformer-EMCD, indicating better alignment of uncertainty with correctness.
  - [corpus] No direct neighbor evidence; this is a novel claim specific to this hybrid design.
- **Break condition:** If the fusion layer (concatenation + FC) becomes a bottleneck, or if the Transformer branch fails to attend meaningfully due to short sequence length (187 samples), negating the benefit of hybrid design.

## Foundational Learning
- **Monte Carlo Dropout (MCD) as Bayesian approximation**
  - Why needed here: Enables uncertainty estimation without altering training; by enabling dropout at inference and sampling multiple forward passes, it approximates posterior over weights.
  - Quick check: In MCD for ECG classification, what does high predictive entropy across T=20 stochastic passes indicate about a heartbeat prediction?
- **Time-frequency representation via Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)**
  - Why needed here: Spectrogram generation is a preprocessing step to feed the Transformer branch; understanding window size, overlap, and dB scaling is crucial to avoid distorting frequency patterns.
  - Quick check: If the STFT uses a window length too short for a 125 Hz resampled ECG, what artifact might dominate the spectrogram and why?
- **Self-attention in Transformers for sequence modeling**
  - Why needed here: The Transformer branch processes the embedded spectrogram patches; attention allows it to relate non-local spectral events (e.g., distributed high-frequency noise) that a CNN might miss.
  - Quick check: How does multi-head attention in a 2-layer Transformer encoder with 2 heads enable it to learn different spectral relationships across the 187-time-step sequence?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (1x187 raw + spectrogram) -> Parallel Branches: Conv Branch (4x Conv1D+ReLU -> AdaptiveAvgPool -> 64-dim) / Transformer Branch (Linear Embed -> 2-Layer Transformer Encoder (2 heads) -> Time-AvgPool -> 128-dim) -> Concatenation (192-dim) -> FC(192->64) + ReLU + Dropout(0.2) -> FC(64->C) + Softmax -> Output (C classes)
- **Critical path:** Raw ECG and STFT spectrogram generation -> parallel forward through Conv and Transformer branches -> feature fusion -> classification -> (for UQ) aggregate predictions from multiple inference passes/ensemble members -> compute predictive entropy.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Accuracy vs. compute: Transformer + spectrogram increases parameters/FLOPS vs. CNN-only; Ensembles/EMCD multiply cost by N.
  - Modality balance: Incorrect STFT parameters can make spectrogram branch a liability; requires tuning (window size, n_fft) per dataset sampling rate.
  - UQ method choice: Deep Ensembles gives best UAcc but highest runtime; MCD is cheapest but may underestimate uncertainty; EMCD balances but still costly.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High Incorrect & Certain (IC) count in uncertainty confusion matrix → poor UQ calibration.
  - Class-wise UAcc drop on rare classes (e.g., F, Q in MIT-BIH) → model overconfident on minority patterns.
  - Large variance in UAcc across 5 runs (Table 4) → unstable training; check learning rate, initialization.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Baseline verification: Train and evaluate LSTM, CNN1D, and Transformer baselines on MIT-BIH/PTB without UQ to replicate Table 4 performance.
  2. Single-modality ablation: Train UCTECG-Net with only raw ECG branch; compare accuracy and UAcc to full model to isolate spectrogram contribution.
  3. UQ stress test: Apply MCD with T=10,20,50 to UCTECG-Net on PTB; plot UAcc vs. T to confirm convergence of uncertainty estimates.

## Limitations
- Spectrogram generation parameters (window size, overlap, n_fft, scaling) are unspecified, directly impacting the Transformer branch input and comparability.
- Critical training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, epochs, dropout rate, optimizer) are missing, hindering faithful replication.
- Ensemble size (N) for Deep Ensembles and EMCD is not stated, and the thresholding strategy for converting predictive entropy to binary decisions is unclear.
