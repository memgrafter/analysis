# Heterogeneous Time Constants Improve Stability in Equilibrium Propagation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03402
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03402
- **Reference count:** 2
- **Primary result:** Heterogeneous time steps (HTS) in equilibrium propagation improve training stability while maintaining competitive test accuracy on MNIST, KMNIST, and Fashion-MNIST.

## Executive Summary
Equilibrium propagation (EP) is a biologically plausible learning framework that typically uses a uniform scalar time step for discrete-time integration. This paper investigates the effect of introducing neuron-specific time steps (heterogeneous time steps, HTS) inspired by the heterogeneity of membrane time constants in biological neurons. The authors replace the shared step size with individual values sampled from normal, log-normal, or gamma distributions, bounded for stability. Experiments on three image classification benchmarks (MNIST, KMNIST, Fashion-MNIST) demonstrate that HTS consistently enhances training stability without sacrificing test accuracy. For example, on KMNIST, a gamma-distributed HTS model achieves 91.28±0.11% accuracy compared to 91.11±0.07% for the scalar baseline, indicating increased robustness. The results suggest that temporal heterogeneity acts as a mild regularizer and disrupts phase-locked instabilities, offering a simple yet effective modification to EP.

## Method Summary
The paper builds on equilibrium propagation (EP), a method where networks evolve toward an energy-defined equilibrium under input and task nudging. The core dynamics use Euler integration: each neuron's state updates as s ← s - dt * ∇E(s). The authors modify this by assigning a fixed time step dt_i to each hidden neuron, sampled once from a specified distribution (normal, log-normal, or gamma with mean μ=0.3, standard deviation σ=0.1) and clamped to [10^{-3}, 0.5]. The output layer retains a scalar dt, which is varied across experiments. The network architecture is a single hidden layer (1024 leaky ReLU units) followed by a sigmoid output layer. Training follows the predictive EP rule: a free phase (125 steps, nudging from the network’s own predictions) followed by a clamped phase (12 steps, stronger nudging from true labels). Weight updates are scaled by the difference between the two convergent states, with separate learning rates for input-to-hidden (α1=0.5) and hidden-to-output (α2=0.1) connections. Experiments use MNIST, KMNIST, and Fashion-MNIST with 50 epochs, batch size 256, and 10 random seeds; test accuracy and training stability are reported.

## Key Results
- HTS models achieve comparable or slightly higher test accuracy than scalar baselines on all three datasets, e.g., KMNIST: 91.28±0.11% (gamma) vs 91.11±0.07% (scalar).
- Training stability is consistently improved: loss curves exhibit lower variance across random seeds and fewer divergent runs.
- The benefit is robust across different distributions (normal, log-normal, gamma) and persists even when the output layer’s dt varies, underscoring HTS as a generally applicable modification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Heterogeneous time steps (HTS) disrupt phase-locked instability that can occur with uniform scalar time steps in equilibrium propagation.
**Mechanism:** With a shared dt, all neurons update synchronously, making the collective dynamical system susceptible to coherent oscillations or divergent trajectories near equilibrium. Introducing neuron-specific dt_i desynchronizes update magnitudes, breaking positive feedback loops that amplify small perturbations.
**Core assumption:** Instability in EP's gradient flow is partly due to correlated, synchronous state updates across neurons.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "HTS improves training stability while maintaining competitive task performance."
- [section: Methods - Heterogeneous Time Steps]: "To ensure numerical stability... all sampled time steps were restricted to a bounded interval" and "Bounding dt_i prevents excessively large integration steps that could destabilize gradient-flow dynamics."
- [corpus]: Weak direct evidence. Neighbor papers like "Unconditional stability of a recurrent neural circuit implementing divisive normalization" suggest heterogeneous time constants are studied for stability in RNNs generally, but do not directly cite this EP mechanism.
**Break condition:** If the distribution of dt_i is extremely narrow (e.g., all values within 0.001 of the mean), the desynchronization effect vanishes, and instability approximates the scalar baseline.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** HTS introduces a form of implicit temporal regularization that smooths the effective loss landscape during the free phase.
**Mechanism:** Neurons with different integration timescales traverse the energy landscape at different speeds. This creates a "temporal jitter" where some neurons adjust quickly to local gradients while others lag, preventing the system from rapidly descending into sharp, narrow minima that may not generalize.
**Core assumption:** Uniform time steps allow the collective network state to too-closely follow the instantaneous gradient of E, potentially overfitting to noise in the nudging signal.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "suggesting a mild regularizing effect similar to activation-based regularization in EP."
- [section: Results and Conclusion]: "On KMNIST and Fashion-MNIST, EP models with heterogeneous time steps (HTS) show consistent but modest improvements over the scalar baseline."
- [corpus]: The neighbor paper "Neural heterogeneity promotes robust learning" (Perez-Nieves et al., 2021) is cited in the introduction as motivation, providing indirect evidence that heterogeneity aids robustness in other learning systems.
**Break condition:** If the dt_i distribution is excessively heavy-tailed without proper clamping (e.g., many dt_i near dt_max or dt_min), the regularization becomes either too weak (large dt -> slow, few updates) or too disruptive (small dt -> frozen neurons), harming performance.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** HTS better approximates the dynamical constraints of biological neural circuits, where membrane time constants (τ_m) are inherently diverse, leading to more robust convergence properties that are evolutionarily validated.
**Mechanism:** Biological neurons operate with a distribution of τ_m due to differences in ion channel composition and morphology. This heterogeneity is not noise but a designed feature that allows population-level processing across timescales. HTS mirrors this, possibly leveraging principles of timescale segregation that improve signal separation and gradient estimation.
**Core assumption:** Biologically realistic temporal heterogeneity confers computational advantages that abstract uniform models miss.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [introduction]: "biological neurons exhibit heterogeneous membrane time constants" and "prior studies in spiking neural networks have shown that heterogeneous time constants can improve performance."
- [section: Methods - Heterogeneous Time Steps]: Distributions (normal, log-normal, gamma) are chosen as "biologically motivated."
- [corpus]: Weak. The neighbor "Hierarchical temporal receptive windows..." discusses heterogeneity in a different context (scale-invariant networks), not directly on EP stability.
**Break condition:** If the chosen distributions are not representative of biological τ_m distributions (e.g., uniform instead of log-normal/gamma), the hypothesized evolutionary advantage may not translate.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Equilibrium Propagation Dynamics**
  - **Why needed here:** Understanding the free phase (network relaxes to E minimum under nudging) and clamped phase (targets are clamped) is essential to see where dt is applied and how HTS perturbs this convergence.
  - **Quick check question:** How would you derive the discrete-time update rule s_{t+1} = s_t - dt * ∂E/∂s_t from the continuous-time equation τ ds/dt = -∂E/∂s?

- **Concept: Discrete-Time Numerical Integration & Stability**
  - **Why needed here:** EP uses Euler integration. The choice of dt (or dt_i) directly impacts the numerical stability of the gradient flow. Conditions for stable Euler updates (e.g., dt < 2/|λ_max| for linear systems) are altered by heterogeneity.
  - **Quick check question:** For a simple linear system dx/dt = -Ax, what happens to the stability region if dt becomes a vector [dt_1, dt_2, ...]?

- **Concept: Biological Neuron Membrane Time Constants**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's core premise is that dt models τ_m. Knowing that τ_m = C_m / g_m (membrane capacitance over conductance) and varies due to biophysical factors grounds the motivation in biology.
  - **Quick check question:** If a neuron has a larger membrane capacitance or smaller leak conductance, what happens to its τ_m and how would that affect its dt_i in this model?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Input Layer -> Hidden Layer (1024 Leaky ReLU neurons with neuron-specific dt_i) -> Output Layer (sigmoid neurons with scalar dt)
Energy function E defines the equilibrium; gradients ∂E/∂s drive updates.
Phase Controller manages free and clamped phases with fixed step counts.

**Critical path:**
1. Initialize dt_i for all hidden neurons from chosen distribution (clamped to [1e-3, 0.5]).
2. For each mini-batch:
   a. Free phase: Run recurrent updates s_{t+1}^{hid} = s_t^{hid} - dt_i * ∂E/∂s_t^{hid} for 125 steps.
   b. Clamped phase: Run for 12 steps with stronger nudging (β=1.0) from true labels.
   c. Weight update: ΔW ∝ (s^{clamped} - s^{free}) * (∂E/∂W terms) using learning rates α1=0.5, α2=0.1.
3. Repeat for epochs.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Stability vs. Speed: Larger dt_max risks divergence but may speed convergence; smaller dt_min ensures stability but slows dynamics. Clamping [1e-3, 0.5] balances this.
- Distribution choice: Log-normal/gamma produce heavy tails, potentially increasing stability benefits but also risk of extreme dt_i values. Normal is more controlled.
- Layer selectivity: Applying HTS only to the hidden layer (not output) recognizes the output’s distinct role. Extending to output could destabilize classification readout.

**Failure signatures:**
1. NaNs or Infs in activations/gradients: Typically from dt_i too large (>0.5 not clamped enough) causing explosive Euler steps.
2. Loss divergence or oscillation: May indicate dt_i distribution variance too high, or output dt mismatched with hidden HTS dynamics.
3. No improvement over scalar baseline: Could mean dt_i distribution is too narrow (σ=0.1 insufficient) or task already stable.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline replication: Train scalar dt model on KMNIST to verify baseline accuracy (~91.11%) and observe training stability.
2. Distribution sweep: Train three HTS models (Normal, Log-normal, Gamma) with μ=0.3, σ=0.1 on KMNIST; compare final test accuracy and training loss variance across seeds.
3. Output layer dt interaction: Fix hidden layer to best HTS distribution (e.g., Gamma). Train models with output dt in {0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.35}; assess robustness of HTS stability gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** What is the mechanistic explanation for why heterogeneous time steps enhance training stability in equilibrium propagation?
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper reports improved stability with HTS but does not investigate the underlying reasons, such as effects on energy function gradients or convergence properties.
**Why unresolved:** Without a theoretical or empirical analysis of dynamics, the cause of stability improvement remains speculative.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze the variance of weight updates or energy changes during training with scalar vs. HTS models; simulate simplified systems to isolate heterogeneity effects.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** Are the selected distributions (normal, log-normal, gamma) and their fixed parameters (mean 0.3, std 0.1) optimal for HTS, or could other configurations yield better stability-accuracy trade-offs?
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] Only three distributions with specific parameters were tested, and clamping bounds were set for stability without optimization.
**Why unresolved:** No systematic comparison to alternative distributions or parameter sweeps; optimal settings for different tasks are unknown.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a grid search over distribution types, means, variances, and clamping thresholds; measure stability through metrics like training loss variance across seeds.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Does the advantage of HTS extend to deeper EP architectures and more complex datasets, such as those used in standard deep learning benchmarks?
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] Experiments used a single hidden layer on MNIST variants; no tests on convolutional networks or larger datasets.
**Why unresolved:** EP's application to deep networks is nascent, and HTS's scalability is unproven for challenging tasks.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Implement HTS in state-of-the-art EP models for deep networks (e.g., convolutional EP) and evaluate on datasets like CIFAR-10 or ImageNet, comparing stability and accuracy to scalar baselines.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** How do the time constant distributions used in HTS correspond to actual measurements of membrane time constants in biological neurons?
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper cites biological motivation but does not reference empirical data on neuronal time constant heterogeneity; distributions are chosen without biological validation.
**Why unresolved:** The claim of increased biological plausibility lacks support from neuroscience literature; biological realism is asserted but not demonstrated.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Survey experimental studies on neuronal time constants, derive empirical distributions, and replace current HTS parameters with biologically measured ones; assess if performance and stability are maintained or improved.

## Limitations
- **Missing implementation details:** The exact energy function E and the predictive learning rule from Luczak et al. (2022) are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- **Limited evaluation scope:** Experiments are confined to three small MNIST-variant datasets; generalization to deeper networks and more complex tasks remains untested.
- **Speculative mechanisms:** The proposed reasons for HTS stability (phase-locking disruption, temporal regularization) lack direct empirical validation.
