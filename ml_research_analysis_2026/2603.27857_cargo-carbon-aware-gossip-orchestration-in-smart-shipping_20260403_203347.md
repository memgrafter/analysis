# CARGO: Carbon-Aware Gossip Orchestration in Smart Shipping

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27857
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27857
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CARGO achieves high predictive accuracy (R² up to 0.941, RMSE down to 0.0108) while significantly reducing carbon footprint and communication volume compared to decentralized baselines under maritime stress.

## Executive Summary
CARGO addresses the unsuitability of server-coordinated federated learning for maritime AI due to intermittent connectivity and commercial sensitivity. It introduces a separation of control and data planes to enable carbon-aware gossip orchestration. The control plane dynamically selects participating vessels, activates communication edges, sets compression levels, and triggers recovery based on utility, carbon cost, and fairness telemetry. Evaluated on operational bulk-carrier engine data under trace-driven maritime stress, CARGO attains strong predictive performance while lowering emissions and communication overhead compared to accuracy-competitive decentralized baselines like D-PSGD and CHOCO. In matched-budget comparisons, it delivers the best mean predictive performance at fixed carbon and top-tier results at fixed communication budgets.

## Method Summary
CARGO employs a dual-plane architecture. The data plane executes standard decentralized gossip learning with compressed communication (e.g., error-feedback) and local SGD. The control plane orchestrates per-round decisions: it scores each available vessel based on a utility term (inverse loss and disagreement), a carbon proxy (estimated compute and communication emissions), and a fairness penalty (inactivity streak and participation rate). Using these scores, it selects the active set of vessels, determines which neighbor edges to activate (ranking edges by informativeness-to-carbon ratio), assigns compression modes (None, INT8, Top-K) according to local carbon intensity, and occasionally triggers resynchronization to recover from packet loss. The control plane updates dual variables via subgradient steps to satisfy long-term carbon and fairness constraints. The system operates without a central server, leveraging only local telemetry and neighbor information.

## Key Results
- High accuracy: R² up to 0.941, RMSE as low as 0.0108 on bulk-carrier engine prediction.
- Significant sustainability gains: CARGO reduces carbon footprint and communication volume compared to D-PSGD and CHOCO under identical accuracy constraints.
- Matched-budget dominance: At fixed carbon budgets, CARGO achieves the strongest mean predictive performance; at fixed delivered-communication budgets, it is among the top performers.
- Robustness: Graceful degradation under packet loss (p up to 0.2) and fragmented connectivity, maintaining competitive RMSE with lower energy use.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Separating control (orchestration) and data (learning update) planes allows dynamic adaptation to maritime conditions without altering the core decentralized optimizer.
- **Mechanism:** The control plane observes telemetry (loss, disagreement, inactivity, carbon intensity) and computes scores per vessel to decide participation, edge activation, and compression. The data plane executes standard compressed gossip updates (e.g., error-feedback) using these decisions, preserving convergence properties of the underlying decentralized algorithm.
- **Core assumption:** The underlying gossip-based data plane (e.g., CHOCO-style mixing) remains stable and convergent under the time-varying graph and compression induced by the control plane’s decisions.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  Abstract: “CARGO separates learning into a control and a data plane. The data plane performs local optimization with compressed gossip exchange, while the control plane decides… which vessels should participate, which communication edges should be activated, how aggressively updates should be compressed, and when recovery actions should be triggered.”  
  Section IV.A: “The data plane retains standard local optimization with compressed gossip exchange, while the control plane decides at each round which vessels should participate… These decisions are guided by a compact telemetry interface…”  
  Corpus: Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers discuss gossip learning and orchestration separately but not their integrated carbon-aware control for maritime settings.
- **Break condition:** If the control plane’s aggressive scheduling (e.g., too few participants, excessive compression) degrades the mixing matrix spectral properties beyond the convergence guarantee bounds of the chosen decentralized optimizer (e.g., CHOCO). The paper assumes the data plane method’s robustness covers the induced variability but does not prove it for all decision sequences.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Carbon-aware scheduling (via a carbon cost proxy) reduces overall emissions by aligning communication and computation with temporal/spatial carbon intensity signals, while maintaining predictive accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** Each vessel’s telemetry includes its current carbon intensity (gCO₂/kWh). The controller estimates a per-vessel carbon burden combining compute energy (based on FLOPs, device throughput, active power) and communication energy (based on payload size, compression ratio, fanout). This proxy penalizes activation of high-carbon nodes and edges in the scoring function.
- **Core assumption:** The proxy model accurately maps local compute/communication actions to incremental carbon emissions, and that minimizing this proxy over decisions leads to lower actual carbon footprint under the given intensity traces.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  Abstract: “guided by compact telemetry that combines predictive utility, carbon- and energy-related signals, and participation-history signals.” and “reducing carbon footprint and communication overheads.”  
  Section IV.B: Details Eq. (12)-(13) computing compute and communication carbon proxies using device parameters and intensity χᵢ(t).  
  Section V.E: Describes carbon accounting: “total carbon emissions are Cg(t) = (Etot(t)/3.6e6) * CI(t)” where CI(t) is carbon intensity.  
  Corpus: Weak; neighboring papers like “Carbon-Aware Orchestration of Integrated Satellite Aerial Terrestrial Networks” consider carbon in orchestration but not in gossip learning for maritime.
- **Break condition:** If carbon intensity signals are highly volatile or mispredicted, the controller may make suboptimal trade-offs (e.g., postpone critical updates to low-carbon periods, harming convergence). The paper uses trace-driven intensity, so real-world unpredictability could break the assumed alignment.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** CARGO’s joint control over participation, topology, and compression, combined with recovery mechanisms (resynchronization), maintains robust convergence under maritime stress (dropout, packet loss, fragmented connectivity).
- **Mechanism:** The controller adapts:  
  - Participation: Scores balance utility (low loss, high disagreement) against carbon and fairness (inactivity streak, participation rate).  
  - Topology: Edges ranked by informativeness-to-carbon ratio (Δ model / carbon proxy), with degree caps.  
  - Compression: Threshold-based on local carbon intensity (INT8 for medium, Top-K for high).  
  - Recovery: Periodic resynchronization (flag rₜ) resets memory to mitigate drift from packet loss.
- **Core assumption:** These levers together can compensate for unreliable communication better than static baselines, because they dynamically reduce load during stressed conditions and strategically reinstate information.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  Abstract: “under trace-driven maritime stress (dropout, packet loss) shows CARGO achieves high predictive accuracy… while significantly reducing carbon footprint and communication volume compared to… D-PSGD and CHOCO.”  
  Section IV.C-E: Details topology scheduler (Eq. 19), carbon-aware compressor (Eq. 20), and resynchronization (Eq. 29).  
  Section VI.B: Packet-loss sensitivity results across fragmented/mid/well-connected regimes show CARGO maintains competitive RMSE with lower carbon/energy.
- **Break condition:** Under extreme, sustained partitioning (e.g., fleet split into isolated clusters for many rounds) or very high packet loss (p→1), even adaptive scheduling may fail to propagate updates, causing divergence. The paper’s traces cover up to p=0.2 and moderate fragmentation; beyond that, the mechanisms may not suffice.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Control Plane: Orchestrator (computes gᵢ), Participation Scheduler (selects Aₜ), Topology Scheduler (selects Eₜ), Carbon‑Aware Compressor (sets Cₜ), Gossip Mixer (builds Wₜ).  
  Data Plane: Local SGD (produces exᵢ), Error‑Feedback (memory hᵢ), Compression (Qᵢ), Mixing (fWₜ), Update (xᵢ ← exᵢ + γ(¯h‑hᵢ)).  
  Inputs: topology Gₜ, telemetry zᵢ(t), previous states (xᵢ, hᵢ), duals (λ_C, λ_F).  
  Outputs: decision bundle Dₜ = (Aₜ, Eₜ, Wₜ, Cₜ, rₜ), updated models xᵢ, memories hᵢ, updated duals.

- **Critical path (one round):**  
  1. Orchestrator computes gᵢ(t) for all available vessels (Eq. 11‑15).  
  2. Participation Scheduler thresholds gᵢ to form Aₜ, applying cardinality correction to meet target Kₜ (Eq. 16‑18).  
  3. For each i∈Aₜ: Topology Scheduler picks up to d_max neighbors using edge score ψᵢⱼ (Eq. 19); Compressor sets mode Cₜ(i) based on χᵢ(t) thresholds (Eq. 20).  
  4. Gossip Mixer constructs Wₜ from Eₜ via Metropolis rules (Eq. 21).  
  5. Data plane: each active i runs local SGD → exᵢ, computes residual eᵢ = exᵢ‑hᵢ, compresses to bₑᵢ = Qᵢ(eᵢ), updates memory hᵢ←hᵢ + bₑᵢ.  
  6. Packet‑loss mask mᵢⱼ applied; delivered matrix fWₜ derived (Eq. 25).  
  7. Each i mixes: ¯hᵢ ← Σⱼ fwᵢⱼ hⱼ, updates xᵢ ← exᵢ + γ(¯hᵢ‑hᵢ).  
  8. Inactive vessels keep xᵢ, hᵢ unchanged.  
  9. If rₜ=1 (periodic), perform resynchronization.  
  10. Dual variables λ_C, λ_F updated via subgradient (Eq. 33‑34).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Carbon vs. Accuracy:** λ_C multiplier trades off carbon cost against utility. Higher λ_C favors low‑carbon nodes but may exclude informative ones, potentially slowing convergence.  
  - **Compression vs. Reliability:** Higher compression (Top‑K) reduces bytes but increases quantization error; controller uses carbon intensity as throttle. In high‑loss regimes, preset uses lower d_max and sparser Top‑K to improve robustness.  
  - **Participation fairness vs. Efficiency:** Fairness penalty Φᵢ forces long‑excluded nodes in, preventing starvation but possibly lowering per‑round utility; λ_F balances this.  
  - **Degree budget (d_max):** Lower d_max reduces communication but may weaken mixing; paper uses d_max=3 (standard) vs. 2 (loss‑robust preset).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **High carbon despite low traffic:** Inefficient compression (e.g., None when χᵢ high) or activating distant, high‑carbon edges; check Cₜ(i) and edge selection logic.  
  - **Accuracy drop with low participation:** λ_C too high or fairness threshold S_max too low leads to too few active vessels; monitor |Aₜ| and ρᵢ(t).  
  - **Divergence under packet loss:** Resynchronization interval R too large; error accumulation from dropped messages may destabilize; check fWₜ sparsity and memory drift.  
  - **Starvation of specific vessels:** Nodes with high local loss or high χᵢ may have persistently low scores; monitor inactivity streak sᵢ and participation rate ρᵢ.

- **First experiments:**  
  1. **Fidelity and Convergence** (Fig 4): Run CARGO vs. baselines (D‑PSGD, CHOCO, SGP, Gossip) with no added stress. Plot R² vs. cumulative compute energy, loss trajectories, and example predictions to verify CARGO reaches high accuracy without instability.  
  2. **Packet‑Loss Sensitivity** (Fig 5, Table V): Over three topology regimes (well‑connected, mid, fragmented), vary packet loss p ∈ {0,0.05,0.1,0.2}. Plot RMSE, carbon, energy. Expect CARGO to degrade gracefully and maintain lower resource use at similar accuracy vs. baselines.  
  3. **Client Stress** (Table IV): Vary availability dropout p_d ∈ {0.2,0.5} and participation fraction f ∈ {0.25,0.5,1.0} under mid topology. Record RMSE, carbon, energy, communication. CARGO should achieve accuracy comparable to D‑PSGD/SGP/Gossip but with lower carbon/communication, especially under f<1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does CARGO maintain its efficiency advantages when scaled to larger fleets (N>>5) with heterogeneous vessel capabilities and communication patterns?
- **Basis:** Future work section: “broader fleet‑scale evaluation over larger and more heterogeneous maritime deployments”
- **Why unresolved:** Current evaluation uses only N=5 vessels and homogeneous device profiles; real fleets involve hundreds of vessels with diverse hardware, schedules, and connectivity, which may affect control decisions and overhead.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Large‑scale trace‑driven simulations with realistic fleet distributions and hardware heterogeneity, or operational field trials on commercial shipping fleets.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does CARGO perform under richer maritime communication models that include variable delay and bursty packet loss, beyond independent Bernoulli drops?
- **Basis:** Future work: “richer communication models that capture delay and bursty loss in addition to packet drops”
- **Why unresolved:** The current protocol uses a simple independent packet‑loss model; real maritime channels exhibit temporal correlation and burst errors due to fading and interference.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Simulations with trace‑driven or synthetic bursty‑loss patterns validated against real maritime communication datasets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can CARGO be integrated with secure or adversary‑robust decentralized learning mechanisms without sacrificing its carbon‑aware orchestration benefits?
- **Basis:** Future work: “integration with secure or adversary‑robust decentralized learning mechanisms”
- **Why unresolved:** Security, privacy, and robustness mechanisms were excluded to isolate orchestration effects; their combination may interfere with control decisions or increase overhead.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Empirical evaluation combining CARGO with state‑of‑the‑art secure aggregation or Byzantine‑robust protocols under maritime stress conditions.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are the theoretical convergence properties of the proposed primal‑dual control policy under time‑varying graphs and stochastic telemetry?
- **Basis:** Inferred: The controller solves the online problem via round‑wise primal‑dual updates but provides no convergence analysis; existing gossip theory may not apply due to control actions.
- **Why unresolved:** Without guarantees, it is unclear whether the heuristic policy satisfies long‑term objectives (e.g., carbon budget, fairness) under realistic dynamics.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Deriving convergence bounds for the specific dual update rules under assumptions on topology dynamics and telemetry statistics.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility:** Exact maritime traces (vessel positions, carbon intensity series) are not provided, and key components (disagreement signal δᵢ, edge ranking ψᵢⱼ, compression thresholds) are underspecified, hindering perfect replication.
- **Scalability:** Evaluation is restricted to 5 vessels and a homogeneous device profile; fleet‑scale heterogeneity and larger N remain untested.
- **Proxy accuracy:** The carbon cost proxy lacks empirical validation against real device measurements, raising questions about its fidelity under diverse hardware and operating conditions.

## Confidence
- **Control/data plane separation:** Medium. Architecture described and results positive, but no convergence guarantee for all control‑induced graph/compression sequences.
- **Carbon‑aware scheduling:** Medium. Emissions reduction demonstrated, but proxy accuracy and trace variability leave robustness uncertain.
- **Robustness under stress:** Medium. Graceful degradation shown for moderate packet loss (p≤0.2) and fragmentation; extreme conditions not covered.
- **Overall claims:** The paper shows promising empirical results, but theoretical foundations are incomplete and evaluation scope is limited.
