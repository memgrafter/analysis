# A Unified Theory of Random Projection for Influence Functions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10449
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10449
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Random projection provably approximates influence functions when sketch size scales with effective dimension under ridge regularization, or matches rank for unregularized case.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a unified theoretical framework for approximating influence functions—quantities of the form \(g^\top F^{-1}g'\) where \(F\) is a curvature matrix (e.g., empirical Fisher)—using random projection sketches. The core finding is that the required sketch size depends critically on whether ridge regularization is used: without regularization, the sketch must preserve the full rank of \(F\) (requiring \(m \geq \text{rank}(F)\)); with regularization, the burden reduces to the effective dimension \(d_\lambda(F) = \text{tr}(F(F+\lambda I)^{-1})\), which can be orders of magnitude smaller. The theory also covers Kronecker-factored curvature (e.g., K-FAC) and treats test gradients that lie outside the range of \(F\), revealing a leakage term that decays as \(O(m^{-1/2})\). Experiments on MNIST and CIFAR validate the theory, demonstrating approximation error decay proportional to \(m/d_\lambda(F)\) and a fidelity-utility trade-off in downstream tasks.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a sketched approximation of the influence function by first projecting gradients \(g, g'\) and curvature \(F\) via a random matrix \(P \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}\) (with i.i.d. sub-Gaussian entries), then computing \((Pg)^\top (PFP^\top + \lambda I_m)^{-1} (Pg')\) in the lower-dimensional space. For unregularized influence (\(\lambda=0\)), the sketch must be injective on \(\text{range}(F)\) to avoid singularities; thus \(m\) must be at least \(\text{rank}(F)\). For ridge-regularized influence (\(\lambda>0\)), the effective sensitivity is governed by \(d_\lambda(F)\), and with high probability the sketched quantity uniformly approximates the true one up to relative error \(\varepsilon\) when \(m \gtrsim (d_\lambda(F) + \log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^2\). When \(F\) has a Kronecker structure \(A \otimes E\), using a separable sketch \(P = P_A \otimes P_E\) preserves these guarantees, though the required sketch sizes for the factors multiply in the worst case. Theorems provide both upper and lower bounds, and experiments confirm the practical relevance of effective dimension.

## Key Results
- **Exactness barrier for unregularized projection:** Uniform multiplicative approximation holds iff the sketch \(P\) is injective on \(\text{range}(F)\), forcing \(m \geq \text{rank}(F)\); otherwise approximation fails catastrophically for some gradients.
- **Regularized scaling with effective dimension:** With ridge regularization (\(\lambda>0\)), the sketch size needed scales linearly with \(d_\lambda(F) = \text{tr}(F(F+\lambda I)^{-1})\) instead of \(\text{rank}(F)\), enabling significant dimensionality reduction when curvature spectra decay.
- **Kronecker decoupling:** For \(F = A \otimes E\), decoupled sketches \(P = P_A \otimes P_E\) maintain the same guarantees (exactness requires matching factor ranks; regularized case scales with factor-level effective dimensions), though the statistical cost multiplies (i.e., total \(m\) must satisfy a product condition).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Unregularized random projection preserves influence \(\tau_0(g,g') = g^\top F^{-1}g'\) for all \(g,g' \in \text{range}(F)\) **if and only if** the sketch \(P\) is injective on \(\text{range}(F)\), which forces \(m \geq \text{rank}(F)\).
- **Mechanism:** The influence involves an inverse-sensitive bilinear form over the range-space of \(F\). If \(P\) collapses any non-zero direction in \(\text{range}(F)\), the sketched curvature \(PFP^\top\) becomes singular on that direction, making \((PFP^\top)^\dagger\) undefined or arbitrarily large, breaking any uniform approximation.
- **Core assumption:** Training and test gradients lie in \(\text{range}(F)\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Theorem 1, corollary on multiplicative approximation failure when \(\text{rank}(PU) < \text{rank}(F)\); the injectivity requirement is novel relative to general sketching literature.
- **Break condition:** \(m < \text{rank}(F)\). Any sketch with fewer rows than the numerical rank of \(F\) has a non-trivial nullspace intersecting \(\text{range}(F)\), causing exact breach and catastrophic failure for some \(g,g'\).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** With ridge regularization (\(\lambda > 0\)), the required sketch size scales with the **effective dimension** \(d_\lambda(F) = \text{tr}(F(F+\lambda I)^{-1})\) rather than \(\text{rank}(F)\). Guarantees hold when \(m = \Omega((d_\lambda(F) + \log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^2)\).
- **Mechanism:** The damping term \(\lambda\) suppresses sensitivity to small eigenvalues of \(F\). The sketching analysis need only preserve the metric on the \(\lambda\)-whitened subspace \(F^{1/2}(F+\lambda I)^{-1/2}\), whose effective dimension is \(d_\lambda(F)\). Directions with \(\lambda_i(F) \ll \lambda\) contribute negligibly.
- **Core assumption:** Oblivious sketching matrix with i.i.d. sub-Gaussian rows.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Theorem 2 (upper bound via ridge-regularized sandwich inequality), Theorem 4 (worst-case lower bound matching \(d_\lambda(F)/\varepsilon^2\) scaling for Gaussian sketches); effective dimension is unique to this influence context.
- **Break condition:** If \(m = o(d_\lambda(F)/\varepsilon^2)\), there exists a curvature instance (even with Gaussian sketch) where the projected influence error is \(\Omega(\varepsilon) \tau_0(g,g')\) for some \(g,g'\).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** For Kronecker-factored curvature \(F = A \otimes E\) and decoupled sketch \(P = P_A \otimes P_E\), the same guarantees hold: for \(\lambda=0\) exactness requires \(\text{rank}(P_A) = \text{rank}(A)\) and \(\text{rank}(P_E) = \text{rank}(E)\); for \(\lambda>0\) the sketch size scales with \(m_A = \Omega((d_{\lambda/\|E\|_2}(A) + \log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^2)\) and \(m_E\) analogously.
- **Mechanism:** The Kronecker structure enables exact computation of \(PFP^\top\) without materializing the full matrix. The analysis uses a factorized covariance deviation bound; error terms split into factor-level primitives. Regularization stabilizes cross-coupling terms.
- **Core assumption:** \(F\) has exact Kronecker structure; \(P_A, P_E\) independent oblivious sketches.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Theorem 6 (regularized bound), Theorem 5 (exactness barrier for unregularized factorized projection); the trade-off (computational saving vs. statistical cost multiplication) is a novel contribution.
- **Break condition:** Unregularized: if either \(\text{rank}(P_A) < \text{rank}(A)\) or \(\text{rank}(P_E) < \text{rank}(E)\), exact preservation fails. Regularized: if \(m_A < c\cdot d_{\lambda/\|E\|_2}(A)\) or \(m_E\) analogously, the uniform bound can break.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Effective dimension**  
  *Why needed here:* The effective dimension \(d_\lambda(F)\) governs the difficulty of sketching the regularized influence. Understanding its spectrum-decay behavior is essential to predict whether \(m\) can be made sublinear in \(\text{rank}(F)\).  
  *Quick check:* If eigenvalues of \(F\) decay rapidly (e.g., exponentially), how does \(d_\lambda(F)\) compare to \(\text{rank}(F)\) for moderate \(\lambda\)?  
  *Answer:* \(d_\lambda(F) \ll \text{rank}(F)\) because many small eigenvalues are down-weighted.
- **Concept: Range and kernel decomposition**  
  *Why needed here:* The leakage analysis for out-of-range test gradients \(g'\) hinges on decomposing \(g'\) into components in \(\text{range}(F)\) and \(\ker(F)\). The kernel component is annihilated by the true inverse but induces a spurious term under sketching.  
  *Quick check:* Why is \(\tau_\lambda(g, g'_\perp)=0\) for \(g'_\perp \in \ker(F)\) despite \(F\) being singular?  
  *Answer:* \((F+\lambda I)^{-1}\) and \(g\) lie in \(\text{range}(F)\), which is orthogonal to \(\ker(F)\); hence the bilinear form vanishes.
- **Concept: Kronecker product algebra**  
  *Why needed here:* Kronecker products enable efficient distributed computation of projected influence. Verifying that \((P_A \otimes P_E)(A \otimes E)(P_A \otimes P_E)^\top = (P_A A P_A^\top) \otimes (P_E E P_E^\top)\) is a key algebraic step.  
  *Quick check:* Does \((P_A \otimes P_E) \text{vec}(G) = \text{vec}(P_E G P_A^\top)\) hold for a matrix \(G\)?  
  *Answer:* Yes, it is the mixed-product property; this avoids forming the full \(P\) matrix.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Curvature \(F\) (PSD matrix from per-example gradients) → Sketch \(P\) (random matrix \(m \times d\)) → Projected influence \(\hat{\tau}_\lambda(g,g') = (Pg)^\top (PFP^\top + \lambda I_m)^{-1} (Pg')\). For K-FAC: factorized \(F = A \otimes E\) and \(P = P_A \otimes P_E\), yielding factor-level projected curvatures.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Choose curvature approximation: full \(F\) vs K-FAC (reduces memory/compute).  
  2. Select sketch type: standard i.i.d. (Gaussian/Rademacher) or factorized (only for K-FAC).  
  3. Determine sketch size \(m\):  
     - Regularized: compute \(d_\lambda(F)\); set \(m \approx C \cdot d_\lambda(F)\) with \(C \in [10,100]\).  
     - Unregularized: require \(m \geq \text{rank}(F)\) (usually impractical).  
     - K-FAC: ensure \(m_A \geq c \cdot d_{\lambda/\|E\|_2}(A)\) and \(m_E \geq c \cdot d_{\lambda/\|A\|_2}(E)\); then total \(m = m_A m_E\).  
  4. Tune \(\lambda\) on validation set with sufficiently large \(m\) (to decouple approximation from bias), then reduce \(m\) to \(C \cdot d_{\lambda^*}(F)\) for chosen \(\lambda^*\).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Unregularized vs regularized: Regularization reduces \(m\) to \(d_\lambda(F)\) but introduces modeling bias relative to \(F^{-1}\).  
  - Factorized vs full sketch: Factorized saves memory/compute (two smaller matmuls) but multiplies statistical cost (requires \(m_A m_E \gtrsim (d_{\lambda_E}(A) d_{\lambda_A}(E))/\varepsilon^4\) vs \(\varepsilon^2\)).  
  - \(\lambda\) selection: Larger \(\lambda\) lowers \(d_\lambda(F)\) → smaller \(m\) needed, but may oversmooth; validation essential.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Normalized error \(\varepsilon_\lambda(g,g')\) does not decay with \(m/d_\lambda(F)\) (Fig. 2): sketch size too small relative to effective dimension.  
  - Error plateaus or worsens with larger \(m\) without regularization: unregularized barrier (\(m < \text{rank}(F)\)) manifests.  
  - Downstream utility (e.g., LDS) peaks at intermediate \(\lambda\) (Fig. 3): approximation-utility trade-off; using poor \(\lambda\) wastes resources.  
  - Leakage term persists when \(g'\) has kernel component: ensure \(m\) also scales with \(r + \log k\) from Theorem 8 if many test points may be out-of-range.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Spectrum & effective dimension sweep:** Compute eigenvalues of \(F\) (or factors \(A, E\)). Plot eigenvalue decay and compute \(d_\lambda(F)\) for candidate \(\lambda\). Choose \(\lambda\) where \(d_\lambda(F)\) is comfortably smaller than \(\text{rank}(F)\) but not so large that \(d_\lambda(F)\) becomes trivial.  
  2. **Normalized error vs. normalized sketch size:** For chosen \(\lambda\), compute \(\varepsilon_\lambda(g,g')\) across training/validation gradient pairs. Plot 95th percentile vs \(m/d_\lambda(F)\); verify decay matches theory (error \(\sim O(1/\sqrt{m})\) or better).  
  3. **Downstream metric (e.g., LDS) vs. \(m\) and \(\lambda\):** Sweep \(\lambda\) on a held-out set with large \(m\) to find \(\lambda^*\). Then fix \(\lambda = \lambda^*\) and sweep \(m\) to see where LDS plateaus. This determines minimal \(m\) for faithful attribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does the theory extend to curvature approximations beyond standard K-FAC, such as EK-FAC, which introduce additional eigenvalue correction steps that alter spectral structure?
- **Basis in paper:** "extending the theory to more sophisticated curvature approximations such as EK-FAC ... remains highly nontrivial. Unlike standard K-FAC, EK-FAC introduces an additional eigenvalue correction step, which fundamentally alters the spectral structure of the curvature operator and breaks many of the techniques used in our analysis. Developing a projection theory that accounts for this additional structure is an interesting and challenging open problem."
- **Why unresolved:** Existing proofs rely on the raw Kronecker structure; eigenvalue corrections change the decomposition and break the invariance conditions used, requiring a new framework.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretical analysis that handles curvature operators that are Kronecker approximations with post-processing, e.g., by bounding how corrections affect effective dimension or designing new concentration arguments.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the combination of random projection, ridge regularization, and curvature approximations affect the bias of influence functions as estimators of the true leave-one-out (LOO) effect, beyond the approximation error studied here?
- **Basis in paper:** "We do not study how projection, regularization, or curvature approximations affect the quality of influence functions as estimators of the underlying leave-one-out (LOO) quantity ... Understanding how projection and other techniques jointly affect both approximation error and modeling bias remains an important open direction."
- **Why unresolved:** The theory guarantees \(\hat{\tau}_\lambda\) approximates \(\tau_\lambda\), but \(\tau_\lambda\) itself is biased relative to LOO. The interaction between projection-induced error and modeling bias from regularization/K-FAC is not quantified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A finite-sample analysis that decomposes total error (e.g., MSE vs. LOO) into approximation and modeling bias terms, validated by experiments varying hyperparameters.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** For non-Gaussian oblivious sketches (e.g., sparse JL, Rademacher), is the \(\Omega(d_\lambda(F)/\varepsilon^2)\) sample complexity lower bound tight, or can some sketches beat the effective dimension barrier?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred: Theorem 4 shows a worst-case lower bound \(m = \Omega(d_\lambda(F)/\varepsilon^2)\) for Gaussian sketches, but upper bounds hold for all sub-Gaussian sketches. The lower bound’s reliance on Gaussian anti-concentration leaves open the tightness for other distributions.
- **Why unresolved:** The anti-concentration argument (Lemma 14) is specific to Gaussian sample covariance; other sketching distributions may have different tail behaviors that could require smaller \(m\) on some \(F\).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Either a universal lower bound for all (or a broad class of) oblivious sketches matching the upper bound up to constants, or a constructive non-Gaussian sketch achieving \(o(d_\lambda(F)/\varepsilon^2)\) sample complexity on some \(F\).

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can one automatically select the regularization \(\lambda\) and sketch size \(m\) without a validation set, with provable guarantees for downstream utility?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred: The experiments propose a two-stage validation-based procedure: pick \(\lambda\) that maximizes LDS on a held-out set, then choose \(m \geq C d_{\lambda^*}(F)\) with an empirically tuned constant \(C\). No theory justifies this decoupling or the constant.
- **Why unresolved:** The trade-off between approximation error (favors larger \(m\) and higher \(\lambda\)) and downstream utility (may peak at intermediate \(\lambda\)) is not characterized. The need for a validation set is unsatisfying for large-scale scenarios.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretical derivation of optimal \((\lambda, m)\) as a function of measurable data quantities (e.g., curvature spectrum, target error), and empirical validation showing that the derived choices approach oracle performance across tasks.

## Limitations
- **Sketch type robustness:** The theory assumes i.i.d. sub-Gaussian sketches (Gaussian, Rademacher). Performance of structured sketches (CountSketch, SRHT) may degrade; constants or rates are not fully analyzed.
- **Effective dimension estimation in practice:** Curvature \(F\) is estimated from minibatches, introducing spectral noise. Sensitivity of \(d_\lambda(F)\) to estimation error and its impact on choosing \(m\) are uncharacterized.
- **Kronecker approximation error:** K-FAC provides an approximate Kronecker structure; the analysis assumes exact factorization. Tolerance to deviations (e.g., off-diagonal covariances) is unquantified.

## Confidence
- **Unregularized injectivity barrier (\(m \geq \text{rank}(F)\)):** High. Theorem 1 is an if-and-only-if; failure when \(m < \text{rank}(F)\) is catastrophic and aligns with inverse sensitivity.
- **Regularized scaling with \(d_\lambda(F)\):** High. Matching upper (Theorem 2) and lower (Theorem 4) bounds for Gaussian sketches; experimental decay matches \(m \propto d_\lambda(F)\).
- **Kronecker decoupled guarantees:** High theoretically (Theorem 6), but practical statistical cost multiplication (requiring \(m_A m_E\) growth) may be Medium—empirical validation on real K-FAC approximations needed.
- **Leakage term bounds:** Medium. Proof exists (Theorem 8), but tightness and practical relevance for typical gradients unclear; no experiments isolate the effect.

## Next Checks
1. **Non-sub-Gaussian sketch test:** Run the full experimental pipeline with CountSketch and SRHT matrices. Compare normalized error decay against sub-Gaussian baselines; check if the \(m \propto d_\lambda(F)\) rate holds or if failure modes appear earlier.
2. **Noisy curvature estimation:** Estimate \(F\) from minibatches of increasing size; compute \(d_\lambda(F)\) from the noisy estimate; set \(m\) accordingly. Quantify the extra error due to \(d_\lambda(F)\) misestimation versus using the true \(d_\lambda(F)\).
3. **Leakage term quantification:** Construct test gradients with controlled components in \(\ker(F)\) (e.g., zero out top-\(k\) singular directions). Measure error scaling with \(m\) and verify the \(O(m^{-1/2})\) rate; also test with real validation gradients that may be partially out-of-range.
