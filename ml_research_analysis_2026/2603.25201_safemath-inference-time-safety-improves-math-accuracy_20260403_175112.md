# SafeMath: Inference-time Safety improves Math Accuracy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25201
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25201
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** SafeMath reduces harmful outputs while increasing math accuracy by 2–5% over base models via inference-time steering with two in-context vectors.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses harmful outputs from large language models (LLMs) when solving math problems containing toxic narratives. It introduces the **ToxicGSM** dataset (1,900 arithmetic problems with embedded harmful context) to audit model behavior. The proposed **SafeMath** method uses inference-time steering via two in-context vectors (ICVs): one for safety (**ICVSF**) and one for mathematical correctness (**ICVMA**), combined with scaled linear addition. On math fine-tuned models (LlamaMath, DeepSeekMath, Qwen2Math), SafeMath (θSFM) reduces harmful content while increasing accuracy by 2–5% over the base model (θM) and improving upon safety-only steering (θSF). Safety scores (via Beaver cost model) improve across harm categories, and gibberish responses decrease, demonstrating that safety alignment can enhance both safety and reasoning performance without retraining.

## Method Summary
SafeMath computes steering vectors from contrastive datasets: **D_ICLS** (unsafe vs. safe QA pairs from TechHazardQA) yields **ICVSF**, and **D_ICLM** (incorrect vs. correct math answers from GSM8K with operator swaps) yields **ICVMA**. For each contrastive pair, hidden states from the last token across all layers are extracted; principal component analysis (PCA) on the difference vectors (safe–unsafe, correct–incorrect) produces the first principal component as the ICV (layer-wise). During inference, these ICVs are added to the model’s hidden states at each layer with tunable scales α (for ICVMA) and β (for ICVSF), followed by L2 renormalization to preserve activation norms. Hyperparameters α and β are optimized via grid search on a validation split of ToxicGSM to balance mathematical accuracy (percentage of correct \boxed{} answers) and safety (Beaver cost model scores).

## Key Results
- Harmful content is reduced while accuracy increases by 2–5% over base models on math fine-tuned LLMs.
- Joint steering (θSFM) outperforms safety-only steering (θSF) in accuracy (e.g., LlamaMath: 89.84% vs. 88.02%).
- Beaver safety scores improve across all harm categories, and the rate of gibberish/NaN responses decreases significantly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Safety steering via ICVSF reduces harmful outputs by implicitly shifting model attention away from toxic narrative tokens toward numerically relevant tokens.
- **Mechanism:** ICVSF, derived from contrastive pairs (harmful question + harmful answer vs. harmful question + safe answer), encodes a latent direction that suppresses activations associated with harmful language generation. When added to the model’s hidden states during inference, it attenuates the influence of toxic context in the input, as evidenced by reduced attribution scores for harm-related tokens and increased focus on numerical tokens.
- **Core assumption:** The latent space contains separable subspaces for linguistic harm and mathematical reasoning; steering in the "safety" subspace does not catastrophically interfere with the "math" subspace.
- **Evidence anchors:** Attribution analysis (Integrated Gradients) shows θSF increases attribution to numerical tokens and decreases attribution to harmful tokens compared to θM.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Jointly applying ICVSF and ICVMA with scaled linear addition (θSFM) synergistically improves both safety and accuracy by capturing complementary steering signals.
- **Mechanism:** PCA over contrastive differences yields two principal directions. Adding both vectors (with scales α, β) moves the model’s activations toward a region of latent space that is simultaneously safer and more mathematically accurate. The improvement beyond safety-only (θSF) suggests the math steering vector (ICVMA) counteracts any slight over-correction toward safety that might blunt reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** The two directions (safety and correctness) are roughly orthogonal or at least not mutually antagonistic, allowing additive combination without destructive interference.
- **Evidence anchors:** Table 1 shows θSFM achieves higher accuracy than θSF across all models.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Inference-time steering avoids the performance degradation often seen in training-time safety alignment (e.g., over-alignment, catastrophic forgetting) by preserving the base model’s knowledge while modulating output generation.
- **Mechanism:** By intervening only at the latent representation level during forward passes—without updating weights—SafeMath retains the base model’s (θM) full pretrained and fine-tuned knowledge. The normalization step (Eq. 4) keeps activations within a distribution the model’s layers expect, preventing destabilization of downstream computation.
- **Core assumption:** The base model already possesses the capability to generate safe and correct responses; the intervention merely nudges it toward those modes rather than requiring relearning.
- **Evidence anchors:** θSFM maintains or improves accuracy while drastically improving safety scores; benchmark stability in Appendix E shows no meaningful drop in general capabilities.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: In-context Vectors (ICVs)**
- **Why needed:** ICVs are the core mechanism for test-time steering; they encode task directions (safety, correctness) in latent space without retraining.
- **Quick check question:** Given contrastive pairs (x⁻, x⁺), how does PCA on (h(x⁺) – h(x⁻)) yield a steering vector?

**Concept: Contrastive Data Curation**
- **Why needed:** Quality of ICVs depends on well-defined positive/negative pairs; wrong/unsafe answers must be valid counterexamples (e.g., operator-swap for math, toxic answers from unaligned models for safety).
- **Quick check question:** Why is swapping operators (add→subtract) a valid way to generate "wrong" math answers without making the problem ill-posed?

**Concept: Latent Space Steering vs. Prompting**
- **Why needed:** SafeMath uses latent manipulation instead of prompt engineering; understanding this distinction explains why it outperforms safe/few-shot prompting in Table 1.
- **Quick check question:** What is a key risk of relying solely on prompt-based safety instructions for math problems with embedded toxicity?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Base LLM (θM) -> ICV computation module -> Steering injection -> Evaluation

**Critical path:**
1. Prepare contrastive datasets D_ICLS (safety) and D_ICLM (math correctness).
2. Run base model on all pairs to collect hidden states (last token, all layers).
3. Compute differences (Hy - Hx) for each pair set; run PCA to get first principal direction → ICV (layer-wise segments).
4. For inference, load base model, hook into forward pass to add scaled ICVs per layer, normalize.
5. Grid search α, β on validation split of ToxicGSM to maximize (accuracy + safety).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Linear vs. non-linear ICV combination: Paper uses simple addition; non-linear methods (e.g., learned gates) might capture interactions but increase complexity and risk overfitting.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: α, β require tuning; improper scales cause safety/accuracy drops (Table 1 best values vary by model).
- Generalization: ICVs derived from GSM8K and TechHazardQA may not transfer to other harm categories or math problem types.

**Failure signatures:**
- Safety failure: Harmful tokens still appear in responses (check Beaver cost > threshold).
- Accuracy failure: \boxed{} answer incorrect or NaN tokens increase (Table 2 shows gibberish reduction as success indicator).
- Over-steering: Responses become evasive or off-topic (e.g., "I cannot answer that" for valid math problems).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **ICV extraction sanity check:** Compute ICVSF on 10 safety contrastive pairs; verify that adding it to a harmful query reduces Beaver cost score vs. base model.
2. **Single-vector ablation:** Test θSF (only ICVSF) on ToxicGSM; expect accuracy ≥ θM and safety improvement (Table 1).
3. **Dual-vector grid search:** Fix β=0.1, vary α in [0, 0.5] on validation set; plot accuracy vs. safety score to identify Pareto front for α, β selection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does SafeMath's inference-time steering remain effective when applied to larger language models (e.g., >20B parameters), or does its efficacy degrade or scale unexpectedly?
- Can non-linear fusion mechanisms (e.g., learned attention, neural networks) for combining ICVSF and ICVMA outperform the current linear addition, leading to better joint optimization of safety and mathematical correctness?
- What is the causal mechanism behind the observed accuracy improvement when applying safety-only steering (ICVSF), and can this effect be generalized to other reasoning tasks beyond arithmetic?
- How well do the safety and accuracy findings generalize to harm categories not included in ToxicGSM and to authentic educational materials where harmful narratives may be more subtle or culturally specific?
- Can the sensitivity to hyperparameters α and β be reduced, enabling SafeMath to generalize robustly across different models, problem types, and harm intensities without per-dataset tuning?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter details (α,β ranges, layer selection) omitted; exact replication difficult.
- Orthogonality of safety/math subspaces not proven; potential interference on harder problems.
- Generalization beyond ToxicGSM and TechHazardQA untested; ICV quality depends on contrastive pair design.

## Confidence
- Joint steering improves safety & accuracy: Medium
- ICVSF reduces harm-token attribution: Medium
- Synergy (θSFM > θSF): High
- Inference-time steering preserves knowledge: Medium
