# SLIM-Diff: Shared Latent Image-Mask Diffusion with Lp loss for Data-Scarce Epilepsy FLAIR MRI

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.03372
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03372
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: SLIM-Diff uses a shared-bottleneck U-Net and tunable Lp loss; x0-prediction significantly outperforms ε and v (p<0.001), with L1.5 optimal for image fidelity (KID 0.012±0.001) and L2.0 optimal for mask morphology (MMD-MF 0.95±0.13).

## Executive Summary
SLIM-Diff tackles joint synthesis of FLAIR MRI and lesion masks for focal cortical dysplasia (FCD) type II, a data-scarce epilepsy condition. By employing a single shared-bottleneck U-Net that processes a 2-channel input and allowing tunable Lp reconstruction loss, the method stabilizes training on only 78 cases. The study reveals a key trade-off: sub-quadratic penalties (L1.5) improve image realism, while quadratic loss (L2.0) better preserves lesion mask boundaries. Critically, predicting the clean sample (x0) rather than noise (ε) or velocity (v) yields superior performance across all Lp settings, demonstrating the importance of prediction parameterization in low-data regimes.

## Method Summary
The model takes a normalized 2-channel input comprising a FLAIR slice and its binary lesion mask. A compact U-Net (26.9M parameters) with a shared bottleneck processes both channels jointly, enforced by concatenating them at input. Conditioning incorporates axial position bin and pathology class via learned embeddings added to timestep embeddings and injected into each ResBlock. Training uses a diffusion forward process with identical noise applied to both channels, and the reverse model predicts either x0, ε, or v, trained with an Lp loss where p ∈ {1.5, 2.0, 2.5}. Sampling employs DDIM with 300 steps. The approach is validated on held-out data using KID and LPIPS for image fidelity, and MMD-MF with shape descriptors for mask morphology.

## Key Results
- **Prediction superiority**: x0-prediction significantly outperforms ε and v across all Lp settings (p<0.001, Cliff’s δ=1.0).
- **Image fidelity**: L1.5 loss yields best KID (0.012±0.001) and LPIPS (0.305±0.0001).
- **Mask morphology**: L2.0 loss yields best MMD-MF (0.95±0.13), indicating sharper lesion boundaries.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Shared-bottleneck coupling**  
A single 2-channel U-Net with a constrained bottleneck (256×20×20) forces image and mask representations through identical latent features, inducing spatial alignment without explicit multi-stream design. This acts as implicit regularization, preventing memorization in small datasets by requiring the latent to explain both intensity and geometry. Evidence: The architecture is explicitly designed for tight coupling (abstract, section 2.4). Break condition: With larger datasets or higher variability, the bottleneck may become an information bottleneck.

**Mechanism 2: Tunable Lp loss geometry**  
The per-pixel Lp penalty shapes gradient distribution: sub-quadratic p (e.g., 1.5) down-weights outlier residuals at fuzzy lesion boundaries, improving overall intensity distribution (lower KID/LPIPS). Quadratic p (2.0) treats all errors equally, enforcing strict boundary matching for binary masks (lower MMD-MF). Evidence: Empirical results show L1.5 best for image metrics, L2.0 best for mask morphology (Table 1). Break condition: Optimal p may shift with different MRI sequences or sharper lesion definitions.

**Mechanism 3: x0-prediction for stable gradients**  
Predicting the clean sample x0 ties the target to empirical data statistics, yielding lower-variance gradients compared to ε-prediction (unit-variance Gaussian noise) or v-prediction. This is crucial in low-data regimes where gradient stability is paramount. Evidence: Consistent outperformance of x0 across all Lp in matched ablation (p<0.001). Break condition: If pretraining on large external datasets is possible, ε-prediction might regain advantage.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input (2-channel [I, M]ᵀ) -> Conditioning (z_bin, p embeddings) -> Shared U-Net backbone (64→128→256→256, 2 ResBlocks/level, MHSA at deepest 2 levels) -> Bottleneck (256×20×20) -> Training loss (Lp on target: x0/ε/v) -> Sampling (DDIM, 300 steps, η=0.2)

**Critical path**: Preprocessing (registration, skull-strip, bias-correct, slice, normalize) -> Subject-level oversampling -> Train with fixed architecture sweeping (target, p) -> Select best via KID/LPIPS/MMD-MF -> Generate conditioned samples.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Shared bottleneck reduces overfitting but may limit maximum fidelity if image/mask representations conflict.  
- 2D slice training enables low-data learning but sacrifices volumetric continuity; requires post-hoc z-axis checks.  
- Lp tuning involves a trade-off: L1.5 for image realism, L2.0 for mask precision—no single p optimal for both.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Mode collapse/memorization: Validation loss decreases but KID/LPIPS do not improve; check EMA vs non-EMA samples.  
- Poor mask morphology (high MMD-MF): Likely p too low (<1.5) or wrong prediction target; inspect per-feature Wasserstein (circularity, eccentricity).  
- Z-axis inconsistency: Slices with same z_bin show anatomical discontinuities; visualize generated stacks.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. Baseline replication: Train with ε-prediction + L2 (DDPM-style); expect higher KID (>0.4) and MMD-MF (>15).  
2. Prediction parameterization sweep: Fix p=2.0, train ε/v/x0; validate x0 superiority via all metrics (expect p<0.001).  
3. Lp tuning for x0: Train x0-prediction with p∈{1.5,2.0,2.5}; expect L1.5 best for KID/LPIPS, L2.0 best for MMD-MF.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can volumetric anatomical consistency be enforced in 2D slice-trained diffusion models for full-brain generation without sacrificing data-scarce training stability? The authors propose future work on pseudo-3D mechanisms but did not implement them.
2. Under matched low-data epilepsy conditions, how does SLIM-Diff's shared-bottleneck architecture compare to dual-stream (e.g., MedSegFactory) or multi-stage (e.g., brainSPADE) frameworks? Direct cross-architecture benchmarking is pending due to domain mismatches and unavailable weights.
3. Is the empirical trade-off between sub-quadratic Lp loss (optimal for image fidelity) and quadratic L2 loss (optimal for mask morphology) generalizable beyond the specific FCDII dataset and architecture? This interaction remains untested across other pathologies, dataset scales, or model capacities.

## Limitations
- Very small dataset (78 FCDII cases, single site) limits generalizability and increases overfitting risk.
- Shared-bottleneck claim lacks direct architectural ablation; benefits may be confounded by other choices (EMA, conditioning).
- Evaluation relies solely on distribution metrics (KID, LPIPS, MMD-MF); no clinical plausibility ratings or downstream task validation (e.g., segmentation).
- Lp loss uses a single shared p; per-modality weighting not explored, potentially sub-optimal for the fidelity–morphology trade-off.
- 2D slice training may break 3D continuity; no metric for volumetric coherence.

## Confidence
- **Shared bottleneck effectiveness**: Medium (theoretically sound, empirically stable, but no comparative ablation).
- **Lp loss trade-off (image vs. mask)**: High (robust empirical support across metrics in Table 1).
- **x0-prediction superiority in low-data regimes**: High (consistent outperformance, statistically significant with large effect size).

## Next Checks
1. **Architectural ablation**: Train a dual-encoder U-Net (separate image/mask branches) with matched parameters on the 78-case dataset; compare to shared bottleneck to isolate its contribution.
2. **Per-modality Lp search**: Grid search over independent p_img and p_mask (e.g., (1.5,2.0), (2.0,2.0)); check if decoupled losses overcome the fidelity–morphology trade-off.
3. **External and 3D consistency test**: Apply the model to an external FCD dataset (if available) to verify generalizability; generate full volumes and compute inter-slice mask Dice to quantify z-axis consistency.
