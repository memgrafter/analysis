# De Jure: Iterative LLM Self-Refinement for Structured Extraction of Regulatory Rules

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.02276
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02276
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A fully automated pipeline extracts structured regulatory rules with >94% average quality and >84% RAG preference in compliance QA, using iterative LLM self-refinement without human annotation.

## Executive Summary
Regulatory documents impose legally binding obligations, but manually converting them into machine-readable rules is expensive and expertise-intensive. De Jure proposes a domain-agnostic, fully automated pipeline that transforms raw regulatory text into structured rule units without human annotation or domain-specific prompts. It employs four stages: normalization to Markdown, LLM-driven extraction, multi-criteria LLM-as-judge evaluation across 19 dimensions, and iterative repair guided by the judge with hierarchical ordering (metadata and definitions corrected before rules). Evaluated across finance, healthcare, and AI governance corpora using four models, De Jure achieves monotonic improvement, reaching peak quality within three iterations and sustaining >94% average scores. Downstream compliance QA via RAG shows De Jure-grounded responses are strongly preferred over prior work, demonstrating that extraction fidelity directly enhances utility.

## Method Summary
De Jure’s pipeline begins with document normalization into clean Markdown. An LLM then extracts candidate rule units (obligations, permissions, prohibitions) from the text. A separate LLM judge evaluates each extracted rule across 19 quality dimensions (e.g., completeness, unambiguity, traceability). Guided by these scores, an iterative repair phase refines the rules. Critically, repair follows a hierarchical order: metadata (e.g., jurisdiction, authority) and definitions are corrected first, providing a stable foundation for subsequent rule refinement. The process repeats until convergence or a maximum number of iterations, requiring no human input or domain-adapted prompts.

## Key Results
- Monotonic improvement observed within judge-guided iterations, with peak quality typically reached in three cycles.
- Sustained >94% average judge scores across finance, healthcare, and AI governance domains using four different LLMs.
- In downstream RAG-based compliance QA, De Jure-grounded responses are preferred over prior extraction methods in 73.8% of single-rule retrieval cases and 84.0% of ten-rule retrieval cases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system leverages iterative self-refinement: initial LLM extraction produces noisy rules; a specialized LLM-as-judge provides multi-dimensional feedback; and guided repair iteratively corrects errors. Hierarchical ordering ensures foundational elements (metadata, definitions) are stabilized first, preventing cascading corrections in dependent rules. This closed-loop, judge-driven process converges rapidly without human intervention, as each iteration targets specific quality deficits identified by the judge, leading to consistent gains across diverse regulatory domains.

## Foundational Learning
- **Iterative Self-Refinement**: Automatically corrects extraction errors through repeated LLM revision. *Why needed?* Manual correction is infeasible at scale. *Quick check*: Does quality improve monotonically over iterations?
- **LLM-as-Judge**: Uses a separate LLM to evaluate rules across 19 dimensions. *Why needed?* Enables scalable, granular assessment without human experts. *Quick check*: Are judge scores consistent across different LLM judges?
- **Hierarchical Repair**: Fixes metadata/definitions before rules. *Why needed?* Rules depend on definitions; correcting out-of-order causes instability. *Quick check*: Does randomizing repair order degrade convergence?
- **Domain-Agnostic Pipeline**: No domain-specific prompts or fine-tuning. *Why needed?* Regulatory texts vary widely; adaptation would limit applicability. *Quick check*: Does performance drop significantly on an unseen domain?
- **Structured Rule Extraction**: Converts unstructured text to standardized rule units. *Why needed?* Machines require structured obligations for automated reasoning. *Quick check*: Are extracted rules legally precise and actionable?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw Text -> Normalization (Markdown) -> LLM Extraction -> LLM-as-Judge Evaluation -> Iterative Repair (Hierarchical) -> Structured Rules

Critical path: Normalization creates a clean, consistent input format; extraction generates initial candidate rules; the judge assigns multi-dimensional scores; repair acts on low-scoring elements in hierarchical order until convergence.

Design tradeoffs: Eliminating human annotation boosts scalability but may miss subtle legal nuances; 19-dimensional evaluation improves granularity but increases computational cost; hierarchical repair adds ordering logic but stabilizes refinement and reduces iterations.

Failure signatures: Judge scores plateau or degrade after a few iterations; repair shows non-monotonic quality changes; high variance in scores across different regulatory domains or LLM models.

First experiments:
1. Execute the full pipeline on a single sample regulatory document, tracking judge scores and rule changes across iterations to confirm convergence.
2. Disable hierarchical repair (shuffle repair order) and compare iteration count and final quality against the ordered baseline.
3. Replace the LLM judge with a different model (e.g., from GPT-4 to Claude) to evaluate robustness of the 19-dimensional scoring.

## Limitations
- LLM-as-judge reliability is uncalibrated to human legal experts, risking undetected domain-specific errors.
- Domain-agnosticism claims are based on only three corpora; performance in other regulatory domains (e.g., environmental, international law) remains unknown.
- The >94% average scores lack a human-annotated gold standard, making absolute quality levels difficult to interpret.

## Confidence
- Iterative improvement: **High** (monotonic gains consistently observed in experiments).
- Domain-agnostic pipeline: **Medium** (validated on three domains, but untested elsewhere).
- Extraction fidelity → utility: **Medium-High** (strong RAG preference, though context-dependent).

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a human expert study scoring a stratified sample of extracted rules to measure correlation with the LLM judge’s 19-dimensional evaluations.
2. Apply De Jure to at least two entirely new regulatory domains (e.g., EU GDPR, maritime law) without prompt adaptation and measure performance degradation.
3. Perform an ablation study disabling hierarchical repair to isolate its contribution to convergence speed and final rule quality.
