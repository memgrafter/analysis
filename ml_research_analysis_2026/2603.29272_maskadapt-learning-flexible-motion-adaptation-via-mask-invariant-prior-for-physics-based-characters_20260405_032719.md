# MaskAdapt: Learning Flexible Motion Adaptation via Mask-Invariant Prior for Physics-Based Characters

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.29272
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29272
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MaskAdapt enables robust, flexible motion adaptation for physics-based humanoids through a mask-invariant base policy and a residual policy, achieving higher motion diversity and lower tracking errors than prior methods.

## Executive Summary
MaskAdapt addresses the challenge of adapting physics-based character motions to new tasks or partial user control. It introduces a two-stage framework: first, a base policy is trained with stochastic body-part masking and a mask-invariant (MI) loss to maintain consistent, diverse action distributions even when parts of the state are missing. Second, a residual policy adjusts only specified body parts while preserving the base behavior elsewhere. This approach supports motion composition (e.g., walking while waving) and text-driven partial tracking, significantly outperforming baselines like CML and MaskedMimic in diversity metrics and task success.

## Method Summary
The method consists of two training phases. **Phase 1 (Base Policy):** A policy π is trained within an adversarial motion prior (AMP) framework. Stochastic masking zeroes out state elements for randomly selected coarse body-part groups. An MI loss (KL divergence between π’s action distribution under full state and under masked state) regularizes the policy to be robust to partial observations without sacrificing diversity. **Phase 2 (Residual Policy):** The frozen base π generates actions. A residual network ψ adds adjustments only to masked body parts, guided by a mask-conditioned discriminator that ensures realism. For text-driven tracking, a pre-trained diffusion model provides kinematic goals for selected parts, which ψ tracks while π maintains locomotion.

## Key Results
- Base policy achieves normalized entropy 0.9025 under masking (vs. 0.7448 without MI loss), nearly matching the unmasked baseline of 0.9124.
- For motion composition, MaskAdapt yields higher entropy in both adapted and preserved regions (e.g., 0.8935 vs. 0.8788 for CML).
- In partial tracking, it reduces tracking error by 20% (0.055 m vs. 0.069 m) and pose error by 40% (0.047 m vs. 0.079 m) compared to CML, with a 100% success rate versus 95.5% for MaskedMimic.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Mask-Invariant Base Policy
Stochastic body-part masking combined with an MI loss regularizes the base policy to produce consistent action distributions under partial observations, preserving behavioral diversity. The MI loss, L<sub>MI</sub> = E<sub>m</sub>[D<sub>KL</sub>(π(·|s, m<sub>0</sub>) ∥ π(·|s̄, m))], aligns the masked policy output with the full-state distribution. This prevents collapse when inputs are missing. Evidence: Table 1 shows λ<sub>MI</sub>=1.0 yields H<sub>norm</sub>=0.9025 under masking, close to the unmasked baseline 0.9124 and far above the no-MI variant (0.7448). Break condition: λ<sub>MI</sub> too low loses robustness; too high may reduce adaptability; mask coverage must include later-adapted parts.

### Mechanism 2: Residual Policy for Localized Adaptation
A residual policy ψ, trained on the frozen base π, modifies actions only for masked body parts while preserving original behavior elsewhere. ψ takes state s, base action a = π(s̄, m), and mask m, outputting residual Δa; executed action is w<sub>gain</sub>·a + Δa. A mask-conditioned discriminator D<sub>ψ</sub> enforces realism of composed motion. Evidence: Table 2 shows higher entropy in both adapted and preserved regions versus CML across composition tasks. Break condition: Without a robust base (no MI), residual destabilizes unmasked parts; excessive residual magnitude overrides base; mismatched mask distributions cause failures.

### Mechanism 3: Text-Driven Tracking with Kinematic Goals
For semantic control, a pre-trained diffusion generator G produces future motion τ<sub>f</sub> from text c. Goals g<sub>t</sub> for designated parts are extracted and fed to ψ, which steers only those parts toward g<sub>t</sub> while π handles locomotion. Evidence: Table 4 shows 20% lower tracking error and 40% lower pose error than CML; Table 5 reports 100% success vs. 95.5% for MaskedMimic in arm tracking. Break condition: Infeasible targets from G (e.g., extreme rotations) cause drift; text irrelevance to target parts limits scope; G’s semantic errors degrade performance.

## Foundational Learning

- **Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP):** Uses a discriminator to provide an imitation reward, encouraging diverse motion coverage rather than direct L2 regression. *Why needed:* Enables the base policy to learn a broad motion prior from motion capture data without overfitting to specific trajectories. *Quick check:* How does a discriminator-based reward maintain diversity compared to an L2 imitation loss?

- **Residual Policy Learning:** A secondary policy adds adjustments to a frozen primary policy’s actions. *Why needed:* Allows precise adaptation of specific body parts without catastrophic forgetting of the base behavior. *Quick check:* Why is freezing the base policy during residual training critical instead of joint fine-tuning?

- **Stochastic Body-Part Masking:** Randomly zeroes out state elements for selected body-part groups during training. *Why needed:* Simulates partial-observation conditions that occur when the residual later modifies only some parts, driving the MI loss to enforce invariance. *Quick check:* How does exposing the base policy to masked states prepare it for the residual adaptation stage?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Base Policy (π) → Residual Policy (ψ) → Final Action  
Mask Sampler → Binary Mask (m<sub>t</sub>)  
MI Loss Module → KL Divergence Loss  
Discriminators: D<sub>π</sub> (base, unmasked), D<sub>ψ</sub> (residual, mask-conditioned)  
Trajectory Generator (G) → Goal Buffer (B<sub>τ</sub>) → Goals (g<sub>t</sub>)  
Physics Simulator (IsaacGym) → State (s<sub>t</sub>)  
Buffers: Replay buffer (base), B<sub>τ</sub> (tracking goals)

**Critical path:**  
1. Base training: Sample mask → compute masked state s̄ → π(a|s̄, m) → env step → store (s, m, a, s') → update D<sub>π</sub> on unmasked data → update π with PPO + L<sub>MI</sub>.  
2. Residual composition: Sample m → a = π(s̄, m) → ψ(Δa|s, a, m) → â = w<sub>gain</sub>·a + Δa → env → store → update D<sub>ψ</sub> conditioned on m → update ψ.  
3. Residual tracking: Periodically sample τ<sub>f</sub> from G into B<sub>τ</sub>; during rollout sample τ<sub>f</sub> → extract g<sub>t</sub> → feed to ψ with a → rest as above.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- λ<sub>MI</sub>: Higher improves robustness but may reduce adaptability; default 1.0 optimal.  
- Mask granularity: Coarse groups (5 parts) balance flexibility and data needs; finer masks increase adaptability but require more training coverage.  
- Conditional discriminator: A single D<sub>ψ</sub> conditioned on m scales to any mask combination vs. CML’s per-part ensemble.  
- Frozen base: Preserves prior and reduces compute but prevents adaptation to new dynamics from residual.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Base: H<sub>norm</sub> under masking drops significantly (<0.8), especially for legs.  
- Composition/Tracking: Entropy in preserved regions near zero; visual stiffness in non-adapted parts; residual altering non-masked regions.  
- Tracking: High joint deviation, frequent falls, broken walking gait; unstable root trajectories.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Base robustness evaluation:** Train with varying λ<sub>MI</sub> (including 0), measure H<sub>norm</sub> under single/double-part masks. Expect MI variants to approach unmasked baseline (~0.91), no-MI below 0.75.  
2. **Motion composition benchmark:** Train residual for compositions (e.g., Jump+Kick). Compute H<sub>norm</sub> separately for adapted and preserved regions; expect higher scores than CML in both.  
3. **Partial tracking test:** Train residual with text-conditioned diffusion G for arm tracking. Report tracking error (joint position) and pose error; expect lower errors than CML and near-perfect success.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can MaskAdapt handle conflicting motions on biomechanically coupled parts (e.g., kick one leg while jumping)? The residual policy does not model interactions between related parts.
2. Does reliance on a fixed pre-trained kinematic diffusion model limit the diversity and physical plausibility of text-driven motions? Inheriting the generator’s semantic and feasibility constraints.
3. Is the KL-divergence mask-invariant loss necessary and sufficient for preserving behavioral diversity, or could other regularizations (e.g., Jensen-Shannon, Wasserstein) yield better robustness?

## Limitations
- Exact masking protocol (joint grouping and sampling distribution) is unspecified, hindering reproducibility of base robustness.
- Conditional discriminator architecture (how mask m<sub>t</sub> conditions D<sub>ψ</sub>) is ambiguous, affecting scalability claims.
- Tracking goal integration details (blending scheme, whether g<sub>t</sub> includes rotations or end-effector positions) are unclear, critical for results.
- Generalizability to arbitrary fine-grained masks (beyond the 5 coarse groups used) is uncertain; confidence is low.

## Confidence
- **High:** Base policy entropy robustness under masking (directly supported by Table 1 and MI loss definition).
- **Medium:** Residual policy preserving non-targeted behaviors (supported by entropy comparisons but depends on unverified base stability).
- **Low:** Applicability to novel, fine-grained body-part masks (paper only evaluates coarse groups; no evidence for finer splits).

## Next Checks
1. **Mask coverage ablation:** Train base policies with finer mask groups (e.g., individual limbs) and evaluate H<sub>norm</sub> under held-out mask patterns to test generalizability of robustness.
2. **Discriminator conditioning test:** Implement three variants for D<sub>ψ</sub> (mask concatenated, mask embedded, no conditioning) and compare closed-loop stability and composition quality.
3. **Goal extraction sensitivity:** For tracking, vary g<sub>t</sub> definition (rotations only, full pose, end-effector positions); measure tracking error and fall rate to identify critical components.
