# PSQE: A Theoretical-Practical Approach to Pseudo Seed Quality Enhancement for Unsupervised Multimodal Entity Alignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22903  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22903  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PSQE is a plug-and-play module that improves state-of-the-art unsupervised MMEA methods by jointly optimizing pseudo-seed precision and graph coverage balance, yielding up to +3.8% H@1 gains on DBP15K and DWY15K datasets.

## Executive Summary
Unsupervised multimodal entity alignment (MMEA) relies on pseudo-alignment seeds to train contrastive models, but existing seed generation methods produce low-precision and Coverage-imbalanced sets. Low-precision seeds inject faulty gradients into the attraction term of contrastive loss, pushing correct pairs apart, while imbalanced coverage skews the repulsion term’s gradient distribution, under-optimizing sparse entities. PSQE addresses both issues via a three-stage pipeline: (1) multimodal fusion and clustering for initial high-precision, balanced seeds; (2) contrastive tuning and multimodal information correction (MIC) for precision refinement; (3) neighborhood expansion and re-checking for coverage balance. Theoretically, PSQE links seed quality to the intra-modal contrastive loss decomposition. Empirically, it consistently boosts four SOTA unsupervised MMEA models across two benchmark datasets, with ablations confirming the necessity of both precision and coverage optimization.

## Method Summary
PSQE is a modular seed enhancement framework that processes multimodal knowledge graphs (MMKGs) in three sequential stages. Stage I fuses multimodal features (visual, relational, attribute) and applies K-means clustering to sample high-similarity intra-cluster pairs, establishing a baseline of precise and coverage-balanced seeds. Stage II first fine-tunes entity embeddings using these seeds via contrastive loss, then performs all-pair similarity mining to discover cross-cluster candidates, which are filtered by MIC—a cross-modal consistency check that removes mismatches. Stage III expands the seed set by including one-hop neighbors of existing seeds (subject to a similarity threshold), followed by another MIC pass to prevent noise influx. The final seed set can be plugged into any unsupervised MMEA model’s training pipeline.

## Key Results
- Integrating PSQE with MEAformer yields absolute gains of +3.8% H@1 on ZH-EN and +1.4% on FR-EN (DBP15K), with similar improvements on DWY15K and other base models (EVA, MCLEA, PCMEA).
- Ablation studies show that removing MIC (precision component) or Stage III (coverage component) each degrades MRR by approximately 1%, confirming both are necessary.
- Multimodal ablation reveals visual features are dominant; removing them causes a 16% H@1 drop for MEAformer, more than removing relational or attribute features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Low-precision pseudo seeds inject faulty attraction gradients into contrastive learning, actively pushing correct entity representations apart.
- **Mechanism:** In the Intra-modal Contrastive Loss (ICL) lower bound (Theorem 1), the attraction term \( -\frac{1}{2}||h_i^1 - h_i^2|| \) is minimized. Mismatched seed pairs \( (h_i^1, h_i^2) \) force the model to compress incorrect pairs, which indirectly increases distance for correct pairs via shared gradient updates.
- **Core assumption:** The model cannot distinguish seed quality during training; all seeds are treated as positive samples.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states mismatched seeds introduce biased gradients; Section 5/Theorem 1/Remark 1 formalizes attraction term dependency on seed precision.
- **Break condition:** If seed precision falls below a dataset-specific threshold (~65% in Table 4), Hits@1 degrades faster than linear.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Imbalanced graph coverage from pseudo seeds skews the repulsion term’s gradient distribution, causing under-optimization of sparse entities.
- **Mechanism:** The repulsion term \( -\frac{1}{2D}\sum_{j \in N_i^{ng}} ||h_i^1 - h_j|| \) aggregates gradients across negative samples. When seeds cluster in dense graph regions, the corresponding negative sample sets \( N_i^{ng} \) are larger and more homogeneous, leading to disproportionally larger gradient magnitudes for dense-region entities.
- **Core assumption:** Negative sampling is in-batch and entity density correlates with number of nearby negatives within batch radius.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes imbalanced coverage causes prioritization of high-density regions; Section 5/Remark 2/Gradient Analysis (A.3) shows gradient magnitude per negative sample is consistent, so total repulsion gradient scales with count of negatives.
- **Break condition:** If graph coverage variance (e.g., Gini coefficient of seed node degrees) exceeds ~0.7, MRR on sparse-subgraph entities drops >5% relative.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** PSQE’s three-stage clustering-resampling jointly optimizes precision and coverage by decoupling their conflicting optimization signals.
- **Mechanism:**
  1. **Stage I:** Multimodal fusion filters noise (↑precision); K-means clustering enforces per-cluster seed quotas (↑coverage balance).
  2. **Stage II:** Contrastive tuning refines embeddings; global all-pair similarity mining captures cross-cluster seeds; MIC removes seeds violating cross-modal consistency (↑precision).
  3. **Stage III:** 1-hop neighbor expansion fills sparse regions; MIC reapply ensures expansion does not corrupt precision.
- **Core assumption:** Precision and coverage are orthogonal levers on the ICL bound; optimizing one does not inherently degrade the other if resampling is controlled.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4 details stage-by-stage design for both objectives; Section 6.4/Ablation shows removing MIC or Stage III each degrades MRR by ~1%.
- **Break condition:** If Stage II’s contrastive tuning overfits to Stage I seeds, MIC fails, causing precision collapse. If Stage III’s threshold \( \eta \) is too high (>0.9), expansion yields few seeds; if too low (<0.6), noise influx.

## Foundational Learning
- **Intra-modal Contrastive Loss (ICL) decomposition:** PSQE’s theory manipulates ICL’s attraction/repulsion terms via seed quality. *Why needed:* To understand how pseudo-seeds affect gradient updates. *Quick check:* How does a false positive seed pair affect the gradient update for a true positive pair’s anchor entity?
- **Graph coverage balance metric:** PSQE operationalizes “balance” via clustering and neighborhood expansion. *Why needed:* To quantify and address the skew in seed distribution across graph regions. *Quick check:* If 80% of seeds come from 20% of entities (Pareto), what repulsion term gradient bias occurs?
- **Multimodal feature fusion (visual/relational/attribute):** Precision improvement relies on cross-modal corroboration to cancel unimodal noise. *Why needed:* To generate robust embeddings that support accurate seed filtering. *Quick check:* Why might removing visual features (Table 3) cause a 16% H@1 drop, more than removing relation/attribute?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (MMKGs with pre-trained encoders) -> Multimodal Fusion (concatenate \( h_v, h_a, h_r \)) -> K-means Clustering -> Intra-cluster top-m sampling -> SeedSet \( S_1 \) -> Contrastive Tuning (train ICL on \( S_1 \)) -> All-pair Similarity Mining -> MIC (row-wise max-check) -> SeedSet \( S_2 \) -> 1-hop Neighbor Expansion (threshold \( \eta \)) -> MIC reapply -> SeedSet \( S_3 \) (final output).
- **Critical path:** 1) Generate initial multimodal embeddings. 2) Stage I cluster sampling (ensures baseline coverage). 3) Stage II contrastive tuning (refines embeddings) + MIC (filters errors). 4) Stage III expansion (fills coverage gaps) + MIC (re-filters). Skipping Stage II MIC or Stage III expansion breaks the precision-coverage tradeoff.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Precision vs. Coverage:** Stage II global sampling risks precision; MIC recovers it at cost of some coverage. Stage III expansion prioritizes coverage; MIC preserves precision.
  - **Computation vs. Quality:** Stage II’s all-pair similarity (\( O(n^2) \)) is heavy but necessary for global coverage. Cluster count \( K \) is low-sensitivity, allowing small \( K \) for speed.
  - **Modality weighting:** Fixed weights (0.8 visual, 0.1 rel, 0.1 attr) may need tuning for datasets with weak visual modality.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Precision drop:** If MIC removes >30% of Stage II candidates, Stage II’s contrastive tuning likely overfit to noisy seeds.
  - **Coverage stagnation:** If Stage III expansion adds <5% new seeds, threshold \( \eta \) too high or neighborhood graph sparse.
  - **Degraded downstream H@1:** Occurs if Stage I cluster count \( K \) << graph modality diversity, leaving seeds concentrated.
- **First experiments:**
  1. **Ablation (Section 6.4):** Remove MIC (precision) and Stage III (coverage) separately; expect MRR drop ~1% each.
  2. **Parameter sensitivity (Section 6.5):** Vary cluster count \( K \) from 2–5; expect <0.5% H@1 fluctuation if multimodal fusion effective.
  3. **Case study (Section 6.3.2/Fig. 5):** Compare UVP vs. PSQE seed sets; verify PSQE includes cross-cluster correct seeds missed by UVP’s dense-region bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What specific mechanisms cause multimodal precision improvements from PSQE to propagate into downstream contrastive learning, particularly regarding the interaction between corrected seeds and the repulsion term’s gradient dynamics?
- **Open Question 2:** How does the choice of clustering algorithm and the number of clusters \(K\) in Stage I interact with the modality weighting scheme to optimally balance coverage and precision across diverse knowledge graphs?
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent does the dominance of visual features in PSQE stem from inherent dataset biases versus the fundamental discriminative capacity of visual versus textual/relational signals?
- **Open Question 4:** How does PSQE’s three-stage pipeline compare against a unified trainable framework that jointly optimizes seed generation and alignment, and what are the scalability implications?
- **Open Question 5:** What is the theoretical limit of pseudo-seed quality improvement achievable by clustering-resampling strategies like PSQE, and is there a saturation point where additional stages yield diminishing returns?

## Limitations
- Theoretical derivations assume ideal contrastive loss behavior; real-world optimizers (e.g., Adam) may diverge from the ICL bound analysis.
- Fixed modality weights (0.8 visual) may not generalize to datasets with poor image quality or image-sparse KGs.
- No independent replication; results rely solely on the paper’s code/experiments, with low neighbor citations suggesting minimal external validation.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical mechanisms (attraction/repulsion gradient effects):** Medium (self-contained derivation, weak external anchors).
- **Empirical SOTA improvements (+3.8% H@1):** High (consistent across 4 models, 2 datasets, ablations).
- **Generalizability beyond DBP15K/DWY15K:** Low (no tests on newer/regional KG pairs).
