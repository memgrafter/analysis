# NAS-GS: Noise-Aware Sonar Gaussian Splatting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06285  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06285  
- **Reference count:** 16  
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis (PSNR >37 dB, SSIM ~0.98) and 3D reconstruction (Chamfer Distance <0.06) on simulated sonar data, and renders real offshore data at over 700 FPS.

## Executive Summary
NAS-GS tackles the difficult problem of 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis from underwater sonar imaging, which is corrupted by severe noise (side-lobes, speckle, multi-path) and lacks elevation data in single 2D pings. The framework introduces a Two-Ways Splatting technique that separately computes transmittance along range and intensity accumulation along elevation, enabling real-time rendering. A per-image Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) captures view-dependent noise patterns, preventing 3D Gaussians from overfitting to artifacts. On simulated datasets, it significantly outperforms prior NeRF and splatting methods in accuracy and speed, and demonstrates high-quality reconstructions and real-time rendering (700+ FPS) on large-scale real offshore sonar data.

## Method Summary
NAS-GS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with a sonar-specific geometry and rendering pipeline. A two-stage training procedure is used. First, 3D Gaussians are initialized from sonar pixels and optimized with a multi-view reconstruction loss using a custom Two-Ways Splatting renderer. This renderer projects Gaussians into two separate 2D frames: an Elevation-Azimuth (I_E) frame pre-computes a per-Gaussian transmittance value by sorting Gaussians by slant range and alpha-blending into an alpha buffer; a Polar (I_P) frame then accumulates intensity contributions using the pre-computed transmittance. Second, a learnable GMM noise model is introduced—two GMMs per image (one over azimuth per range bin, one over range per azimuth bin) generate additive noise maps that are added to the rendered I_P image. The GS and GMM parameters are then jointly optimized. Sonar geometry is handled by a rigorous sequence of coordinate transforms (world→sonar Cartesian→polar-elevation) and Jacobian-based covariance projection to correctly map 3D Gaussians into the I_P and I_E frames.

## Key Results
- On simulated scenes (Cabinet, Barrel, Panel), NAS-GS achieves PSNR >37 dB and SSIM ~0.98 for novel view synthesis, and Chamfer Distance <0.06 for 3D reconstruction.
- On large-scale real offshore sonar data (Tritech Gemini over a wind turbine foundation), it produces high-quality reconstructions and renders at over 700 FPS.
- Significantly outperforms prior NeRF-based (e.g., SonarNeRF) and splatting-based (e.g., SonarSplat) methods in both quality and speed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Two-Ways Splatting for Real-Time Rendering
- **Claim:** Two-Ways Splatting enables real-time rendering by decoupling intensity accumulation (along elevation) from transmittance calculation (along range).
- **Mechanism:** Rendering occurs in two separate image frames: (1) In the Elevation-Azimuth (I_E) frame, Gaussians are sorted by increasing slant range and alpha-blended into an alpha buffer; the transmittance value \( T_i \) for each Gaussian \( i \) is sampled as \( 1 - \alpha_{\text{buffer}} \) at its 2D mean in I_E. (2) In the Polar (I_P) frame, each Gaussian’s contribution \( I_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot T_i \) is splatted and accumulated additively without recomputing transmittance per pixel.
- **Core assumption:** Sonar imaging geometry separates the direction of intensity integration (elevation arcs) and attenuation (range), allowing a single transmittance value per Gaussian to approximate the integrated effect along the range dimension.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "Two-Ways Splatting technique that separately handles intensity accumulation along elevation and transmittance along range, enabling real-time rendering."
  - Section II-B.4: Full algorithmic description and equation (15).
  - Corpus: Prior splatting works (e.g., Z-Splat, SonarSplat) compute transmittance per pixel during blending, which is more costly.
- **Break condition:** If a Gaussian’s footprint spans a large region in I_E where transmittance varies significantly, the single per-mean sample \( T_i \) becomes a poor approximation, potentially causing visible over- or under-attenuation in dense scenes.

### Mechanism 2: GMM Noise Model to Prevent Overfitting to Sonar Artifacts
- **Claim:** The GMM-based noise model prevents 3D Gaussians from overfitting to view-dependent sonar artifacts (side-lobes, speckle, multi-path), improving reconstruction accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** For each training image, two learnable GMMs are defined: one over azimuth (per range bin) and one over range (per azimuth bin). These generate additive noise maps \( N_\theta \) and \( N_r \) that are added to the clean rendered polar image \( I_{\text{clean}} \). During training, the reconstruction loss encourages the 3D Gaussians to explain the clean scene geometry while the GMM parameters explain the residual stochastic noise, thereby disentangling geometry from noise.
- **Core assumption:** Sonar noise can be modeled as a spatially structured, view-specific additive process that is statistically separable from the true scene radiance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) based noise model captures complex sonar noise patterns... preventing 3D Gaussians from overfitting to noise."
  - Section II-C: Detailed GMM formulation (equations 16–20) and combined rendering equation (20).
  - Corpus: DenoiseSplat and similar works model noise for general photography, but none use a per-bin GMM tailored to sonar’s range-azimuth structure.
- **Break condition:** If noise statistics vary drastically within a single sonar image (e.g., due to extreme multi-path), a per-bin GMM with fixed component count may be insufficient, causing residual noise to be absorbed by the Gaussians or the GMM to diverge.

### Mechanism 3: Explicit Sonar Geometry Pipeline for Accurate Projection
- **Claim:** The explicit Sonar Geometry pipeline ensures accurate projection of 3D Gaussians into the unique polar-elevation sonar image space.
- **Mechanism:** A defined sequence of coordinate transforms maps world coordinates to sonar Cartesian (with known extrinsics), then to spherical polar-elevation (range, azimuth, elevation), and finally to two 2D image frames (Polar I_P: range-azimuth; Elevation-Azimuth I_E: elevation-azimuth) via similarity transforms (scale, rotation, translation). The 3D Gaussian covariance is projected to each 2D frame using the Jacobians of the nonlinear conversions and the linear parts of the similarity matrices, following equations (13)–(14).
- **Core assumption:** The sonar imaging model (arc-based elevation integration, range-based attenuation) is physically accurate and consistent across views.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section II-A: Complete mathematical derivation of coordinate frames and projection functions \( \pi_{\text{pe}}, \pi_p, \pi_e \).
  - Section II-B.3: Jacobian-based covariance projection for both image frames (equations 13–14).
  - Corpus: Prior sonar GS methods (e.g., Z-Splat) simplify geometry with a “Z-axis” model; this work’s pipeline is more rigorous.
- **Break condition:** Inaccurate sensor calibration (extrinsics, FOV, mounting angles) leads to systematic misalignment of projected Gaussians, causing blurring or ghosting regardless of noise modeling.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)**  
  *Why needed:* NAS-GS modifies core 3DGS operations (alpha blending, covariance projection, rasterization). Understanding the original tile-based rasterization, ordering by depth, and per-pixel transmittance computation is essential to grasp the Two-Ways changes.  
  *Quick check:* "In original 3DGS, how is the order of splats determined for alpha blending, and along which dimension is transmittance calculated?"

- **Concept: Underwater Acoustic Imaging Physics**  
  *Why needed:* The entire design is driven by sonar-specific artifacts (side-lobes, speckle, multi-path) and geometry (range-elevation-azimuth). Without this, the rationale for Two-Ways and the GMM is unclear.  
  *Quick check:* "Why do sonar images show intensity smeared along elevation arcs, and why does attenuation follow a range law?"

- **Concept: Differentiable Rendering & Gradient Flow**  
  *Why needed:* The system is trained end-to-end. Gradients must flow correctly through the Two-Ways rendering equation (especially through the sampled transmittance \( T_i \) and the GMM sampling) to update both GS and noise parameters.  
  *Quick check:* "How does the pre-computed transmittance in I_E affect gradient computation during the intensity accumulation step in I_P?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Sonar Geometry Module -> Two-Ways Renderer -> GMM Noise Generator -> Gaussian Optimizer

**Critical path:** For a new scene: Initialize Gaussians from sonar intensity thresholding -> Project to I_P/I_E using Geometry -> Render with Two-Ways + GMM noise -> Compute reconstruction loss (L1 + SSIM) -> Backpropagate through Rendering and GMM -> Update GS and GMM parameters alternately.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Speed vs. Noise Fidelity:* Two-Ways sacrifices per-pixel transmittance accuracy for ~2× speedup over naive dual-frame rendering. GMM component count \( K \) trades noise fit vs. parameter overhead.  
- *Geometry vs. Noise Separation:* Multi-view loss helps disentangle but requires sufficient viewpoints; single-view training risks overfitting.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Blurry/featureless reconstructions:* GMM over-regularizing or insufficient Gaussian density.  
- *Structured artifacts aligned with range/azimuth:* GMM components not converged or wrong \( K \).  
- *Ghosting/misalignment:* Incorrect sonar geometry calibration (TSW, scale/rotation params in transforms).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Geometry sanity check:** Render a single known 3D point (e.g., a sphere) from a fixed pose. Verify its projection follows the expected arc in I_P and line in I_E. Check Jacobian-based covariance shape matches expected elliptical footprints.  
2. **Two-Ways ablation:** Disable pre-computed transmittance (compute per-pixel) and measure FPS drop on a fixed Gaussian count. Expect ~2× slowdown confirming the mechanism’s efficacy.  
3. **GMM visualization:** After few epochs, visualize learned GMM means/variances for a single image. For a clean scene, GMM should capture high-frequency noise patterns, not low-frequency structure. Compare denoised vs. final rendering.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to real-time sonar SLAM and dynamic scenes:** The authors state future work will explore extending the framework to sonar SLAM and dynamic scenes with temporal coherence. The current framework assumes static scenes and known poses; integrating pose estimation and handling moving objects requires significant modifications. Evidence to resolve: Implement NAS-GS within a SLAM pipeline, evaluate on sequences with dynamic elements, and compare trajectory accuracy and reconstruction consistency over time against state-of-the-art sonar SLAM methods.
2. **Generalization of GMM noise model across sonar systems:** The GMM parameters are learned per image/scene, but the paper does not investigate whether these parameters transfer to other sonar hardware or environments. Scene-specific noise modeling may limit deployment flexibility. Evidence to resolve: Train the GMM on one sonar model/dataset (e.g., Blueview p900 in simulation) and test on a different model (e.g., Tritech Gemini) or real offshore data; measure rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM) and reconstruction accuracy (Chamfer Distance) with and without fine-tuning.
3. **Impact of transmittance approximation on large/anisotropic Gaussians:** The method approximates transmittance by computing it only at each Gaussian’s 2D mean position in the elevation-azimuth frame, rather than per-pixel. The impact of this approximation is not analyzed. Evidence to resolve: Compare the proposed rendering against a full per-pixel transmittance computation on synthetic scenes with controlled Gaussian sizes and orientations; quantify differences using image metrics (PSNR, SSIM) and visual inspection.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter opacity:** The GMM component count \( K \) and training hyperparameters (iterations, learning rates, loss weights) are not reported, hindering exact reproduction.
- **Real-data evaluation gap:** Quantitative results on real offshore data are limited to speed and qualitative visual assessment; lack of ground truth prevents objective reconstruction accuracy claims.
- **Noise model complexity & overfitting risk:** The per-image, per-bin GMM introduces many parameters; risk of overfitting is not analyzed, especially with limited multi-view coverage.

## Confidence
- **Two-Ways Splatting achieves real-time rendering:** Medium. Algorithmic description is solid and FPS numbers are impressive, but the claimed 2× speedup relies on specific scene-sparsity conditions; break condition warns of potential artifacts in dense regions.
- **GMM noise model prevents overfitting to sonar artifacts:** Medium. Strong theoretical formulation and improved metrics on simulation; however, the model’s capacity and stability across diverse noise patterns are not fully validated.
- **Explicit sonar geometry ensures accurate projection:** High. Mathematically rigorous derivation consistent with physics; closely matches prior simplifications but adds necessary Jacobians. Practical success hinges on calibration quality.
- **Overall SOTA performance on sonar tasks:** Medium. Results on simulated data are compelling, but real-world evaluation lacks quantitative ground truth, limiting definitive superiority claims.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation on GMM components:** Train NAS-GS with varying \( K \) (e.g., 1, 3, 5, 8) on a fixed split of simulated data; track PSNR, SSIM, and noise residual variance to identify the point of diminishing returns and any overfitting signs.
2. **Calibration robustness test:** Systematically perturb the sonar pose and intrinsic parameters (e.g., add Gaussian noise to mounting angles with \( \sigma \in \{2^\circ, 5^\circ, 10^\circ\} \)) and re-render; measure quality drop (PSNR) and geometric error (Chamfer Distance) to quantify sensitivity.
3. **Cross-dataset generalization:** Train on the provided simulated scenes, then directly evaluate on a held-out real sonar sequence (if accessible) or on a simulated scene with deliberately altered noise statistics (e.g., increased speckle variance). Compare performance against a model trained on real data to isolate the GMM’s adaptability.
