# Piecing Together Cross-Document Coreference Resolution Datasets: Systematic Dataset Analysis and Unification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00621
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00621
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** uCDCR unifies 12 English CDCR datasets into a consistent format, demonstrating that event and entity coreference are similarly challenging (≈54 vs. ≈56 F1 baseline) and that lexical diversity metrics predict dataset difficulty.

## Executive Summary
Fragmented datasets with heterogeneous formats and a predominant focus on event coreference have hindered progress in cross-document coreference resolution (CDCR). This paper introduces **uCDCR**, a unified benchmark that consolidates 12 publicly available English CDCR corpora into a standardized JSON format. The authors' pipeline re-parses documents with spaCy, corrects tokenization, enriches missing mention attributes (head lemma, POS, NER), and standardizes evaluation protocols. Analysis reveals that lexical diversity and ambiguity metrics (e.g., MTLD, PD, AL) correlate with performance on a simple same-head-lemma baseline, identifying the hardest datasets. Critically, event-only and entity-only subsets yield comparable baseline scores (~53.9 vs. ~56.4 F1), arguing against the field's historical event-centric bias. The unified resource enables fair cross-dataset evaluation and is shown to enhance model generalizability when datasets are combined.

## Method Summary
The authors construct uCDCR via a multi-stage pipeline: (1) ingest original datasets in varied formats (XML, CSV, CoNLL, JSON); (2) normalize and re-parse all document text with `en_core_web_sm` to enforce uniform tokenization, lemmatization, POS, and NER; (3) remap original mention spans to the new tokenization via string matching followed by token-level alignment; (4) extract and enrich mention attributes (head, head lemma, context window ±100 tokens); (5) construct a consistent topic-subtopic-document hierarchy per dataset-specific logic; (6) define or reuse train/dev/test splits at the subtopic level; and (7) output unified JSON files. This process corrects known inconsistencies (e.g., ECB+ tokenization errors) and adds missing linguistic features, enabling reproducible analysis and evaluation across all corpora.

## Key Results
- uCDCR consolidates 12 heterogeneous English CDCR datasets (both event and entity) into a single, consistently formatted benchmark with standardized splits and enriched annotations.
- Lexical diversity (MTLD, PD) and ambiguity (AL) metrics vary widely across datasets and strongly correlate with same-head-lemma baseline performance; hardest datasets include CD2CR, HyperCorefexp, ECB+METAm, and NewsWCL50r.
- Event and entity coreference yield nearly identical baseline F1 scores on the combined uCDCR test set (53.9 vs. 56.4), demonstrating that neither sub-task is lexically simpler and both require joint attention.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Unification Eliminates Fragmentation
- **Claim:** Converting 12 heterogeneous CDCR corpora into a single, consistently formatted benchmark (uCDCR) removes dataset-specific preprocessing hurdles and enables fair cross-dataset model evaluation, improving assessed generalizability.
- **Mechanism:** The pipeline standardizes format (JSON), corrects tokenization via spaCy re-parsing, extracts missing attributes (head lemma, POS, NER), and enforces a uniform topic-subtopic-document hierarchy. This allows direct performance comparison without bespoke adaptation per corpus.
- **Core assumption:** A unified format with consistent attribute extraction and evaluation protocols is a necessary precondition for meaningful cross-dataset benchmarking.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states uCDCR "consolidates diverse...corpora...corrects known inconsistencies, and enriches datasets with missing attributes." Section 3.2 details the standardization pipeline. Table 4 shows the test split is more balanced across datasets than the train split, supporting fairer evaluation.

### Mechanism 2: Lexical Metrics Diagnose Difficulty
- **Claim:** Lexical diversity (MTLD, PD, UL-a/o) and ambiguity (AL) metrics are predictive of dataset difficulty for a lemma-based baseline, serving as diagnostic tools for CDCR complexity beyond raw size.
- **Mechanism:** These metrics quantify variation in how the same entity/event is expressed (diversity) and how often a lexical form refers to different things (ambiguity). Higher values generally correlate with lower baseline scores, indicating greater linguistic challenge.
- **Core assumption:** Lexical properties are a primary, quantifiable source of difficulty for coreference resolution, exposed by a simple lemma-matching heuristic.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.2 defines metrics and their intended link to difficulty. Table 5 shows ECB+ has low lexical diversity (PD=1.50, MTLD=4.52) while HyperCorefexp and NewsWCL50r are high. Table 6 shows hardest baseline datasets (CD2CR, HyperCorefexp, ECB+METAm, NewsWCL50r) generally align with high lexical diversity from Table 5.

### Mechanism 3: Event-Entity Parity Undermines ECR-Only Focus
- **Claim:** Treating CDCR as exclusively event coreference resolution (ECR) is unjustified, as entity-only and mixed datasets show comparable baseline difficulty, suggesting both sub-tasks are similarly complex and should be addressed jointly.
- **Mechanism:** The same-head-lemma baseline is applied separately to event and entity mentions. Near-identical CoNLL F1 scores (events: 53.92, entities: 56.36 on uCDCR subtopic level) indicate neither type is inherently simpler at the lexical level.
- **Core assumption:** Lexical variation is a major component of difficulty for both event and entity coreference; a significant gap would appear if one were substantially easier.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "the almost identical performance...shows that resolving both types is a complex task and should not be steered toward ECR alone." Section 4.3, Table 7 provides split scores (e.g., ECB+ event=61.10, entity=59.77; uCDCR event=53.92, entity=56.36).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Cross-Document Coreference Resolution (CDCR)**  
  *Why needed:* The paper's core task—linking mentions of the same entity/event across multiple documents—requires understanding its distinction from within-document coreference and its larger candidate space.  
  *Quick check:* What key difference between within-document and cross-document coreference leads to larger candidate sets in CDCR?
- **Concept: Lexical Diversity & Ambiguity Metrics (MTLD, PD, UL-a/o, AL)**  
  *Why needed:* These metrics quantify the linguistic challenge of a dataset (variation in expression, polysemy) and are used to explain why some datasets are harder than others.  
  *Quick check:* How does Phrasing Diversity (PD) differ from Measure of Textual Lexical Diversity (MTLD), and what does a high value for each indicate about a coreference chain?
- **Concept: Topic-Subtopic-Document Hierarchy & Evaluation Levels**  
  *Why needed:* CDCR evaluation is defined at the subtopic (coherent event collection) or topic (cross-subtopic, more documents) level; this structure is fundamental to dataset organization and analysis in uCDCR.  
  *Quick check:* In uCDCR, what defines a subtopic versus a topic, and how does evaluation at the topic level increase difficulty?
- **Concept: Same-Head-Lemma Baseline**  
  *Why needed:* This simplest-possible heuristic establishes a lower bound and isolates the challenge of lexical variation across all datasets.  
  *Quick check:* What does the same-head-lemma baseline do, and why is it a particularly weak baseline for high lexical diversity/ambiguity datasets?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. Data Ingestion/Parse -> 2. Text Normalization & Re-parsing -> 3. Mention Remapping -> 4. Attribute Extraction & Enrichment -> 5. Hierarchy Construction -> 6. Split Management -> 7. Output Generation
- **Critical path:** Most failure-prone and computationally heavy steps are (2) Text Normalization & Re-parsing (especially for large datasets like HyperCorefexp) and (3) Mention Remapping; success here is prerequisite for accurate attribute extraction.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Uniformity vs. Fidelity:* Re-parsing ensures consistent tokenization but may deviate from original author segmentation (e.g., hyphenated words); tradeoff accepted for comparability.  
  *Completeness vs. Feasibility:* Extracting context windows (±100 tokens) enables fair-use analysis but may truncate long documents; fixed heuristic.  
  *Scalability vs. Annotation Style:* For massive datasets (e.g., HyperCorefexp, 29M tokens), training mentions are downsampled while full test sets are retained, balancing cost and evaluation integrity.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Low mention parsing rate (e.g., CERECexp ~98%) indicates remapping issues from original tokenization errors or formatting quirks.  
  - Attribute extraction failures, e.g., ECB+METAm where paraphrased sentences caused entity mentions to not map correctly.  
  - Hierarchy construction ambiguity for datasets without clear topic/subtopic (e.g., NewsWCL50r), requiring heuristics that may alter semantic grouping.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Re-parse & Remap Validation:** On ECB+ test, verify every gold mention span maps correctly to spaCy tokens and extracted head lemmas match original annotations; parsing rate should approach 100%.  
  2. **Lexical Metric Sanity Check:** Compute MTLD and PD on ECB+ vs. ECB+METAm splits; confirm ECB+METAm shows higher lexical diversity (expected: MTLD 4.52 vs. 8.78).  
  3. **Baseline Replication:** Implement same-head-lemma baseline; run on uCDCR test splits; verify CoNLL F1 scores approximate Table 6/7 (e.g., WEC-Eng ~83, ECB+ ~61). Large discrepancy indicates flaw in mention grouping or evaluation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
To what extent do state-of-the-art CDCR models generalize across the diverse datasets in uCDCR, and which dataset properties (e.g., lexical diversity, ambiguity, annotation guidelines) most significantly impact cross-dataset performance?  
*Basis:* The authors plan to "systematically evaluate how state-of-the-art CDCR models perform across the compiled datasets" (Section 4.4) and baseline results show stark performance variations correlated with lexical diversity.  
*Why unresolved:* No evaluation of modern neural CDCR models on the full unified benchmark; relationship between dataset characteristics and model failure modes unexplored.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Comprehensive empirical study training/evaluating leading CDCR architectures on all uCDCR splits; ablation experiments isolating properties (e.g., ECB+ vs. ECB+METAm for paraphrasing effects).

### Open Question 2
How can the field move beyond the predominant event-centric definition of CDCR to develop unified architectures that effectively resolve both event and entity coreference within a single framework, as encouraged by uCDCR?  
*Basis:* The paper critiques CDCR being "predominantly... defined as the event coreference resolution (ECR)" and shows comparable baseline difficulty for events vs. entities.  
*Why unresolved:* Existing CDCR models are designed and evaluated solely on event-focused datasets like ECB+; no architecture explicitly handles the full spectrum of mention types in uCDCR.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Development and rigorous evaluation of a single CDCR model ingesting both event and entity mentions, tested on combined uCDCR and on individual datasets with varying mention-type compositions.

### Open Question 3
To what extent do residual inconsistencies in annotation guidelines and mention granularity (e.g., maximum vs. minimum span, handling of near-identity) across the unified uCDCR datasets impact the validity of cross-dataset comparisons and model benchmarking?  
*Basis:* Inferred from the Limitations: "the quality and granularity of annotations vary across the included datasets... residual inconsistencies may affect cross-dataset comparability." Table 1 documents variations (span, mention type, identity relations) correlating with baseline score differences (e.g., NewsWCL50r's low F1 due to near-identity).  
*Why unresolved:* Harmonization cannot alter original annotation semantics; unclear if poor performance on datasets like NewsWCL50r is due to linguistic complexity or annotation philosophy mismatch.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Controlled annotation study where experts re-annotate a common subset from multiple uCDCR datasets following a single guideline; compare model performance and inter-annotator agreement on this subset vs. originals. Alternatively, develop evaluation metrics robust to annotation style variations.

### Open Question 4
Can the lexical diversity and ambiguity metrics (MTLD, PD, AL) reliably predict CDCR dataset difficulty for modern neural models beyond the simple same-head-lemma baseline, and can these metrics guide the creation of more challenging and realistic benchmarks?  
*Basis:* The paper introduces these metrics (Section 4.2) and finds correlations with baseline performance (Section 4.3); e.g., HyperCorefexp/NewsWCL50r high in diversity and hardest for baseline.  
*Why unresolved:* Correlation shown only for lexical heuristic; unknown if metrics predict performance gaps of neural models leveraging context/semantics. Metrics may not capture non-lexical challenges (discourse, world knowledge).  
*What evidence would resolve:* Correlational analysis across all uCDCR datasets between lexical metrics and performance drops of multiple neural CDCR models relative to the baseline. If strong, use metrics to procedurally generate new evaluation sets (e.g., high PD/AL) to stress-test specific capabilities.

### Open Question 5
How does the inclusion of less traditional domains (e.g., scientific text in CD2CR, emails in CERECexp) and the resulting increase in topic and lexical diversity in uCDCR affect the training and generalization of CDCR models compared to models trained solely on news-based corpora like ECB+?  
*Basis:* The authors emphasize uCDCR's domain diversity and show via t-SNE (Figure 3) that it covers a broader semantic space than ECB+ alone, stating multi-dataset training "will improve the generalizability of CDCR models."  
*Why unresolved:* No experimental verification that multi-domain training improves generalization; unknown if domain shifts introduce noise or beneficial regularization; optimal domain mixture unclear.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Controlled experiments training models on (a) ECB+ only, (b) balanced uCDCR news subset, (c) full uCDCR including non-news domains; evaluate on out-of-domain tests (e.g., CERECexp for emails) and held-in-domain data; analyze error patterns across domains.

## Limitations
- **Unification pipeline scope:** Assumes spaCy-compatible English text; likely fails for non-standard scripts, multimodal data, or fundamentally incompatible annotation schemes without major adaptation.
- **Metric generalizability:** Lexical diversity metrics' predictive power is validated only for the same-head-lemma baseline; their correlation with neural model performance is unproven and may miss non-lexical challenges (e.g., discourse structure, temporal reasoning).
- **Baseline simplicity:** The same-head-lemma baseline oversimplifies event vs. entity complexity by ignoring deeper semantic/pragmatic distinctions; event resolution may require temporal/causal reasoning not captured by lemma matching.
- **Reproduction fidelity:** Exact implementations of PD/MTLD and baseline details (lowercasing, borderline handling) are not fully documented, requiring reliance on provided code; minor deviations could affect reported metrics.

## Confidence
- **Unification enables cross-dataset benchmarking:** High confidence. Pipeline details are explicit and successfully unified 12 datasets; break conditions (non-English, multimodal) are acknowledged but do not undermine core utility for standard English text.
- **Lexical metrics predict difficulty (for baseline):** Medium confidence. Strong correlation observed in baseline results (Tables 5–6), but limited to a lexical heuristic; generalizability to neural models is an open question.
- **Event-entity parity (lexical complexity):** Medium confidence. Comparable baseline scores (53.9 vs. 56.4 F1) suggest similar lexical challenge, but the baseline ignores richer semantic differences that might differentiate the sub-tasks.
