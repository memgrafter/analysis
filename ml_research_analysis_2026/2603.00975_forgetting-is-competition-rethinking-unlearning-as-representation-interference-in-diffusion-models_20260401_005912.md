# Forgetting is Competition: Rethinking Unlearning as Representation Interference in Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00975
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00975
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SurgUn achieves high unlearning accuracy (94–98%) while preserving in-domain and cross-domain retention (86–96%) across multiple diffusion models, significantly outperforming prior methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of precise concept removal in text-to-image diffusion models. Existing unlearning methods often suffer from over- or under-erasure and unintended forgetting of unrelated capabilities. SurgUn introduces a two-part solution: a distractor‑conditioned loss that induces stable representation competition using a fixed set of semantically diverse distractors, and weight‑space localization that selects intervention sites based on pixel‑space behavior to minimize collateral damage. Evaluated across Stable Diffusion v1.5, SDXL, and SANA models on concept‑level unlearning, IP character removal, compositional settings, and robustness tests, SurgUn attains unlearning accuracy (UA) of 94–98% and retention (IRA/CRA) of 86–96%, outperforming state‑of‑the‑art baselines. It demonstrates robustness to paraphrased and adversarial prompts, prevents concept reassignment by dispersing outputs across multiple classes, and maintains performance in sequential unlearning. The approach is model‑agnostic and adds minimal overhead (< 5 % from checkpoint calibration).

## Method Summary
SurgUn comprises three stages. (1) **Weight‑space localization**: a lightweight diagnostic evaluates each candidate attention block by briefly training it with the distractor‑conditioned loss and measuring pixel‑space erase (UA) and retain (RA) metrics on a small prompt set. The block achieving the best trade‑off (ranked by MCDM/COMET) is selected. (2) **Distractor‑conditioned unlearning**: only the selected block’s LoRA weights (rank 64) are updated using a contrastive loss that pushes predictions away from the target concept toward a fixed set of ~100 semantically diverse distractor concepts; training runs for ~1000 steps with periodic checkpoint saving. (3) **Checkpoint calibration**: all saved checkpoints are ranked by UA and RA via MCDM; the top‑ranked checkpoint is refined via local search to capture the optimal point on the erasure‑retention curve. The pipeline is model‑agnostic and works for both U‑Net and DiT architectures.

## Key Results
- SurgUn attains unlearning accuracy (UA) of 94–98% and retention (IRA/CRA) of 86–96% across SD v1.5, SDXL, and SANA, significantly outperforming ESD, SalUn, and CA.
- It shows robustness to paraphrased/adversarial prompts, avoids concept reassignment (outputs disperse across multiple classes), and maintains performance in sequential unlearning of six styles.
- The method is model‑agnostic and incurs < 5 % additional compute from checkpoint calibration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Distractor‑conditioned representation competition
**Claim:** Injecting controlled representational competition via a fixed, semantically diverse distractor set destabilizes the target concept through retroactive interference while preserving unrelated knowledge.  
**Mechanism:** The distractor‑conditioned loss contrasts the model’s noise/flow predictions for the target prompt against those for multiple distractor prompts. This forces the denoising pathways to bifurcate: strengthening routes for distractors while weakening the exclusive pathway for the target. The fixed distractor set ensures stable, sustained competition during optimization, preventing drift toward adjacent concepts.  
**Core assumption:** Semantic concepts are distributed across parameters; weakening one concept’s activation can be achieved by reinforcing alternative, non‑target pathways without global retraining.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.2 define the losses. Figure 13 shows the full contrastive loss outperforms variants without distractors or without logarithmic contrast. Section 6.2 (Figure 4(c)) demonstrates fixed, diverse distractors balance suppression/retention better than resampled or similarity‑based sets. Related work on unstable unlearning underscores the need for stable interference.  
**Break condition:** If a distractor is highly entangled with the target (poor curation), interference may cause over‑erase of that distractor or fail to suppress the target. Assumes distractors span the semantic space without overly overlapping.

### Mechanism 2: Pixel‑space–guided weight‑space localization
**Claim:** Pixel‑space evaluation isolates a minimal sub‑circuit (attention block) where updates cause maximal target suppression with minimal collateral damage.  
**Mechanism:** A lightweight diagnostic phase updates each candidate block individually, generates sample images, and measures pixel‑space metrics (UA/RA). The block yielding the best erase‑retain trade‑off (via MCDM/COMET) is selected for all subsequent unlearning updates. This grounds localization in observable behavior rather than latent saliency.  
**Core assumption:** Semantic control in diffusion U‑Nets/DiTs is concentrated in specific intermediate attention blocks; intervening only in these blocks avoids disrupting global generative pathways used by unrelated concepts.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3 details Algorithm 1; Figures 11 & 12 show most blocks achieve high UA but RA varies drastically, confirming that only specific blocks balance both. Section 6.1 notes that suppression is ubiquitous while preservation is selective.  
**Break condition:** If the concept’s representation is not concentrated in any single block (highly distributed), any single‑block intervention will either under‑erase (poor UA) or over‑erase (poor RA). The method assumes a "sweet spot" block exists.

### Mechanism 3: Checkpoint calibration via multi‑criteria decision making
**Claim:** Training‑snapshot calibration via MCDM joint optimization of UA and RA selects a checkpoint that balances unlearning progress against catastrophic forgetting.  
**Mechanism:** During training, multiple checkpoints are saved. A two‑step MCDM (COMET) ranks them using UA and RA metrics. The top‑ranked checkpoint is refined via local search to capture sharp transitions in the erase‑retain trade‑off curve.  
**Core assumption:** Unlearning follows a non‑monotonic trajectory: target suppression improves over time, but retention eventually degrades due to over‑optimization. The optimal point is not the final checkpoint.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5.2.3 and Algorithm 2 describe calibration. Table 8 shows calibration improves RA (e.g., SD v1.5: 74% → 87.5%) while also increasing UA. Section 6.1 & Figure 14 illustrate the degradation curve (e.g., SDXL: retention drops sharply after checkpoint 400).  
**Break condition:** If unlearning is stable and monotonic (no over‑erase phase), calibration adds unnecessary overhead. Assumes an overfitting regime typical of fine‑tuning.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Retroactive Interference (Cognitive Psychology)
- **Why needed:** SurgUn’s core premise is that new learning (distractor concepts) can destabilize a stored memory (target concept) via competition. Understanding this psychological theory is essential to grasp why sustained distractor exposure causes forgetting without explicit deletion.
- **Quick check:** If you repeatedly practice a new piano piece that shares finger patterns with an old piece, why might the old piece become harder to play accurately?

### Concept: Diffusion Model Architecture (U‑Net & DiT)
- **Why needed:** SurgUn operates on specific blocks (attention layers) within the denoising network. Engineers must understand the block structure of SD v1.5/SDXL (U‑Net with intermediate transformer blocks) vs. SANA (DiT) to correctly apply weight‑space localization.
- **Quick check:** In a latent diffusion U‑Net, where does text conditioning primarily occur, and why are intermediate attention blocks likely sites for semantic concept encoding?

### Concept: Multi‑Criteria Decision Making (COMET)
- **Why needed:** SurgUn uses COMET to rank candidate blocks and checkpoints by jointly optimizing two competing metrics (UA vs. RA). Engineers need to understand how MCDM converts multiple scores into a stable ranking without manual weighting.
- **Quick check:** If you have two checkpoints—one with UA=98%, RA=70% and another with UA=95%, RA=90%—how might an MCDM method like COMET decide which is better for unlearning?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
SurgUn integrates with the diffusion training pipeline through three main components:
- **Distractor Set**: A fixed list of ~100 semantically diverse concepts used in every batch.
- **Loss Function**: Replaces the standard diffusion loss with `Lunlearn` (Eq. 3 for LDMs, Eq. 5 for DiTs), computed on both target and distractor prompts.
- **Localization & Calibration**: Includes a pre‑unlearning diagnostic (Algorithm 1) to select the intervention block; during unlearning, only that block’s LoRA weights are updated; post‑training, Algorithm 2 chooses the optimal checkpoint.

Represented as plain text: `Fixed Distractor Concepts -> Lunlearn Loss -> Diagnostic Phase (block selection) -> Targeted LoRA Update -> Checkpoint Calibration`.

### Critical Path
1. **Diagnostic phase**: For each candidate attention block, train briefly with `Lunlearn`, generate diagnostic images, compute UA and RA, and rank blocks via COMET.
2. **Full unlearning**: Freeze all blocks except the selected one; train with `Lunlearn` using target + fixed distractors for N steps (e.g., 1000), saving checkpoints periodically.
3. **Calibration**: Rank saved checkpoints via COMET on UA/RA; select the best checkpoint and optionally perform local search around it (±k steps).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Distractor set size vs. diversity**: Larger sets improve retention but increase diagnostic cost; the paper uses 100 fixed concepts.
- **Block selection granularity**: Operates at the attention‑block level (coarse). Finer granularity (e.g., layer‑wise) may improve accuracy but adds overhead.
- **Checkpoint calibration overhead**: Requires double evaluation (ranking + local search) but adds <5% total compute; strongly recommended for best performance.

### Failure Signatures
- **Over‑erase**: RA drops sharply after early checkpoints; indicates the intervention block is too central or the distractor set misaligned.
- **Under‑erase**: UA remains low across all candidate blocks; suggests the target concept is not concentrated in any single block or the loss is insufficient.
- **Concept reassignment**: Post‑unlearning outputs collapse to 1–2 proxy classes; implies distractors are not sufficiently diverse or block selection is poor.

### First 3 Experiments (Reproduction Checklist)
1. **Block localization sanity check**: Run diagnostic on a known easy target (e.g., "bird") across all blocks. Expect: most blocks achieve high UA, but only 1–2 blocks maintain RA > 85%.
2. **Distractor set ablation**: Compare SurgUn with 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of the full distractor set. Expect: UA stays high, while IRA/CRA increase with larger/more diverse sets.
3. **Checkpoint calibration validation**: Train without calibration, then apply Algorithm 2 post‑hoc. Expect: calibrated checkpoint improves RA by 5–15% without degrading UA.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1: Optimal distractor concept selection
- **Basis**: The paper states “identifying an optimal set remains an avenue for future work” (Section 6.2).
- **Why unresolved**: Although larger, more diverse sets improve retention, no principled method exists to determine the minimal sufficient set or the best semantic composition. Trade‑offs between set size, diversity, and computational cost remain unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve**: Systematic experiments varying distractor count (e.g., 25%, 50%, 75%, 100% coverage), semantic diversity (controlled via CLIP clustering), and category balance across many target concepts. Measuring UA/RA correlation curves and deriving a theoretical model of interference strength relative to set properties could yield practical heuristics.

### Open Question 2: Automatic block selection without pixel‑space diagnostics
- **Basis**: SurgUn localizes by evaluating pixel‑space behavior of each candidate block in a diagnostic phase (Algorithm 1). No alternative based solely on model internals is explored.
- **Why unresolved**: The diagnostic requires generating numerous images for every candidate block; scaling to thousands of unlearning requests or new architectures could become prohibitively expensive. Automating block selection would greatly enhance scalability.
- **What evidence would resolve**: Discovery of a proxy metric derived from model activations (e.g., attention entropy, cross‑attention alignment, gradient sensitivity) that strongly correlates with pixel‑space UA/RA scores. Validation across SD v1.5, SDXL, and SANA would show that selection via this metric yields performance comparable to full diagnostic while reducing image generation overhead by orders of magnitude.

### Open Question 3: Impact of long‑term sequential unlearning
- **Basis**: The paper evaluates sequential unlearning for six styles (Section D.5.2, Figure 5, Table 15) but does not address larger scales.
- **Why unresolved**: Each unlearning step updates weights within the same selected block. Over many steps, weight updates could accumulate in an unstable manner, destabilizing earlier unlearnings or causing overall generative quality to drop. The effectiveness of the fixed distractor set might also diminish as the model’s representational landscape shifts.
- **What evidence would resolve**: Extensive experiments sequentially unlearning 20–100 concepts (mixed objects and styles), tracking unlearning accuracy, retention, and quality metrics (e.g., FID, CLIP score) after each step. Analysis of weight drift and block saturation. If performance declines, testing periodic re‑localization or dynamic adjustment of distractor sets could provide remedies and define operational limits.

## Limitations
- Distractor set specifics (the exact list and composition) are undisclosed; replication requires approximating semantic diversity, and performance may be sensitive to target‑distractor overlap.
- The COMET‑based MCDM implementation details are vague; exact fuzzy‑rule parameterization could affect the reproducibility of block and checkpoint rankings.
- Primary evaluation metrics (UA/IRA/CRA) may overlook subtle regressions in image quality, diversity, or bias.
