# Devil is in Narrow Policy: Unleashing Exploration in Driving VLA Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06049
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06049
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves state-of-the-art Navsim scores: PDMS 90.3 (v1), EPDMS 85.4 (v2), and Best-of-N PDMS 94.8, with significant gains in trajectory diversity and quality.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the Narrow Policy problem in vision-language-action (VLA) models for autonomous driving, where imitation learning collapses exploration and limits reinforcement learning due to insufficient feedback diversity. Curious-VLA introduces a two-stage framework: during imitation learning, Feasible Trajectory Expansion generates multiple physically valid trajectories and step-wise normalization balances horizon-scale gradients; during reinforcement learning, Adaptive Diversity-Aware Sampling prioritizes high-diversity scenarios and Spanning Driving Reward applies focal-style weighting to amplify reward sensitivity. Evaluated on the Navsim benchmark, Curious-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance (PDMS 90.3, EPDMS 85.4, Best-of-N PDMS 94.8) and demonstrates substantially improved trajectory diversity and quality.

## Method Summary
Curious-VLA consists of two stages. In the imitation learning (SFT) stage, the pipeline first expands the training data by generating diverse trajectories for challenging scenes using a diffusion-based planner (ReCogDrive), filters them by safety (PDMS > 95), and annotates them with structured chain-of-thought reasoning. Step-wise normalization is then applied to balance gradients across the trajectory horizon. The model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) is fine-tuned in two phases: thinking alignment (freezing vision encoder) and end-to-end fine-tuning (unfreezing all). In the reinforcement learning stage, Adaptive Diversity-Aware Sampling (ADAS) selects scenarios that yield high-variance rollouts from offline trials, ensuring non-zero advantage estimates for GRPO updates. The Spanning Driving Reward (SDR) reformulates the PDMS/EPDMS metrics using focal-style exponents to magnify differences between suboptimal and optimal outcomes. The RL loop iterates over active scenarios with GRPO, producing the final policy.

## Key Results
- Navsim-v1 PDMS: 90.3 (state-of-the-art)
- Navsim-v2 EPDMS: 85.4
- Best-of-N PDMS: 94.8
- Behavioral Diagnostics show large improvements in trajectory diversity (mean-pFDE) and quality (min-FDE).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Curious-VLA systematically alleviates the exploit-explore trade-off by engineering exploration at both data initialization and online update stages. In imitation learning, Feasible Trajectory Expansion (FTE) combats the Narrow Policy by broadening the training distribution beyond a single ground-truth mode. It generates multiple physically valid trajectories via diffusion perturbation and applies step-wise normalization to equalize horizon-scale gradients, resulting in a high-entropy policy initialization. During reinforcement learning, Adaptive Diversity-Aware Sampling (ADAS) maintains diversity by filtering scenarios that produce high-variance rollouts, preventing the reward collapse that occurs when GRPO groups exhibit near-zero standard deviation. Complementarily, Spanning Driving Reward (SDR) reformulates the reward with focal-style exponents to amplify the span between suboptimal and optimal outcomes, sharpening the learning signal. The synergy of these components—diverse initialization, dynamic sampling, and amplified rewards—enables the VLA model to unleash its exploratory potential and achieve superior performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input (front camera image, ego state, intent) → Qwen2.5-VL-3B (vision encoder + LLM decoder) → Feasible Trajectory Expansion (ReCogDrive diffusion + step-wise normalization) → Two-stage SFT (Thinking Alignment → End-to-End FT) → Adaptive Diversity-Aware Sampling → GRPO trainer (group size G=8) → Spanning Driving Reward → Output (text waypoint tokens).

**Critical path**:  
1. *Data preparation*: Filter challenging Navsim scenes, generate and filter diverse trajectories with ReCogDrive, compute per-step statistics (µ_t, σ_t) for normalization, and add CoT annotations.  
2. *SFT*: Perform thinking alignment (freeze vision encoder) then end-to-end fine-tuning on the expanded dataset.  
3. *RL loop*: For each outer iteration (3 total), run M offline rollouts on the trainset, apply ADAS to select active scenarios, sample G online rollouts per scenario, compute SDR rewards, and update policy via GRPO (130 steps).

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *FTE diversity vs. safety*: More perturbation increases diversity but risks unsafe trajectories; filtering uses PDMS >95 to balance.  
- *ADAS strictness vs. data volume*: Tighter thresholds (ϵ_div, ϵ_conf) improve diversity guarantees but reduce active samples (e.g., 6k → 3k → 1k).  
- *SDR focal exponent*: Higher γ magnifies differences but may destabilize learning if reward distribution is sparse.

**Failure signatures**:  
- *IL failure*: Post-SFT Behavioral Diagnostics show low Diversity (mean-pFDE <0.3) despite decent Performance, indicating normalization failed to make diverse patterns separable.  
- *RL collapse*: Validation reward plateaus near zero; advantage estimates vanish; PDMS drops sharply.  
- *Safety regression*: Decreases in NC or DAC metrics, likely from over-aggressive exploration or unsafe FTE samples.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. *Baseline Behavioral Diagnostics*: Sample k=8 trajectories from the Qwen2.5-VL baseline on navtrain; compute Diversity, Quality, Performance. Expect low Diversity (<0.25) and stagnant Quality.  
2. *Ablate FTE components*: Start from CoT-only SFT, then add Diversity Expansion (w/o step normalization), then add step normalization. Evaluate diagnostics; DE-only may increase Diversity but hurt Quality, SN should improve both.  
3. *ADAS sensitivity test*: Train RL with ADAS using varying active sample sizes (1k, 3k, 6k). Monitor validation reward stability; too few samples cause high variance, too many may not filter enough, leading to collapse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Safety of FTE**: Does the increased trajectory diversity from Feasible Trajectory Expansion introduce safety risks not fully captured by the PDMS threshold?
- **Sim-to-real transfer**: Does Curious-VLA's exploration mechanism translate effectively from simulation to real-world closed-loop driving?
- **Normalization alternatives**: Is step-wise normalization the optimal approach for handling horizon-scale imbalance, or could other schemes improve separability and learning?
- **ADAS Bernoulli assumption**: Does the Bernoulli distribution assumption in ADAS accurately reflect driving reward diversity, and how does it impact sampling efficiency compared to continuous measures?

## Limitations
- **Exact Navsim evaluation**: The proprietary PDMS/EPDMS scorer details hinder precise replication and fair comparison.
- **Hyperparameter tuning**: ADAS thresholds and active sample counts are dataset-specific; their generalizability is unknown.
- **FTE dependency**: Reliance on ReCogDrive’s diffusion planner; alternative perturbations may not match its physical validity or diversity guarantees.

## Confidence

2. *ADAS threshold sweep*: Train RL with ϵ_div, ϵ_conf varied in {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}. Monitor group reward standard deviation and final PDMS to identify robust ranges and collapse points.
