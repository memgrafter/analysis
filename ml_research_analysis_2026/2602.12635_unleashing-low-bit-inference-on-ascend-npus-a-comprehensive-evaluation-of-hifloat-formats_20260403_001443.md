# Unleashing Low-Bit Inference on Ascend NPUs: A Comprehensive Evaluation of HiFloat Formats

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12635
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12635
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** HiF4 in W4A4 preserves over 96.5% BF16 accuracy on Qwen3-8B and avoids catastrophic degradation in KV cache quantization where INT4 and MXFP4 fail.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles efficient large language model inference on Ascend NPUs through low-bit quantization, evaluating novel HiFloat formats (HiF8, HiF4) against standard integer and floating-point approaches. Core findings reveal a bit-width-dependent performance split: at 8-bit, INT8 is optimal for weight quantization due to narrow ranges, while HiF8 with per-axis scaling matches MXFP8 for high-variance activations and KV cache. At 4-bit, hierarchical scaling in HiF4 prevents the accuracy collapse seen in INT4 and outperforms single-level MXFP4. HiF4 achieves strong accuracy preservation (e.g., >96.5% on Qwen3-8B) and shows synergy with post-training quantization techniques like SmoothQuant. The work underscores the importance of format-specific scaling strategies for low-bit deployment on specialized hardware.

## Method Summary
The study employs post-training quantization (PTQ) to compare HiFloat formats (HiF8, HiF4) against INT8/INT4 and MXFP8/MXFP4. Experiments are conducted on two decoder-only models: Qwen3-8B and openPangu-7B. Quantization is applied to weights, activations, and KV cache, with evaluation using perplexity and zero-shot accuracy. The paper also integrates PTQ frameworks such as SmoothQuant and SVDQuant to mitigate outlier effects. No custom training is performed; all results are derived from quantizing pre-trained BF16 models with round-to-nearest or stochastic rounding.

## Key Results
- **8-bit split:** INT8 excels for weight quantization due to narrow ranges, while HiF8 with per-axis scaling matches MXFP8 for high-variance activations and KV cache by leveraging dynamic range.
- **4-bit advantage:** HiF4’s hierarchical scaling (three-level blocks) prevents accuracy collapse, preserving over 96.5% of BF16 accuracy in W4A4 on Qwen3-8B with round-to-nearest.
- **KV cache robustness:** HiF4 significantly outperforms INT4 (catastrophic failure) and MXFP4 (severe drops, e.g., >60% on openPangu-7B) under KV cache quantization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HiF8 succeeds for activations/KV cache because its per-axis scaling adapts to varying channel-wise magnitudes, similar to MXFP8 but tailored for Ascend NPUs. HiF4’s hierarchical scaling allocates finer granularity to high-variance blocks (smaller block sizes at deeper levels), effectively representing wide value ranges without the collapse observed in single-level INT4 or MXFP4. Additionally, compatibility with SmoothQuant redistributes outlier magnitudes from activations to weights, stabilizing quantization and improving robustness across both formats. This combination of adaptive scaling and outlier mitigation enables low-bit fidelity on Ascend hardware.

- **Per-Axis (Channel-Wise) Scaling:** Applies separate scaling factors per output channel; needed when activation magnitudes vary across channels. Quick check: In which layer type (e.g., attention, MLP) is per-axis scaling most beneficial?
- **Hierarchical Scaling:** Uses multiple block levels with different granularities (e.g., 64, 8, 4); needed to capture both coarse and fine variance patterns in tensors. Quick check: How does hierarchical scaling reduce quantization error for outlier-heavy tensors?
- **Post-Training Quantization (PTQ):** Quantizes pre-trained models without retraining; needed for fast deployment. Quick check: What are two common PTQ calibration methods (e.g., max, percentile)?
- **SmoothQuant:** Migrates outlier magnitudes from activations to weights via affine transformation; needed to balance quantization ranges and avoid saturation. Quick check: Why are outliers particularly problematic in low-bit activation quantization?

## Architecture Onboarding
Calibration Data -> Scaling & Hierarchy Parameters -> HiFloat Quantizer (HiF4/HiF8) -> Ascend NPU Hardware -> Accuracy Metrics (Perplexity, Zero-shot)
**Critical path:** Collect calibration data from model forward passes → compute per-axis scaling and hierarchical block statistics → apply quantization with determined parameters → run inference on NPU → evaluate accuracy.
**Design tradeoffs:** Lower bit-width reduces memory/compute but increases collapse risk; hierarchical scaling adds implementation complexity but improves fidelity; hardware support for custom formats (like HiF4) may be NPU-specific.
**Failure signatures:** INT4 shows severe accuracy degradation (catastrophic collapse) on both models; MXFP4 incurs >60% drop on openPangu-7B; per-tensor scaling fails for high-variance activations.
**First experiments:**
1. Replicate HiF4 W4A4 on Qwen3-8B: quantize all linear layers with round-to-nearest, measure perplexity on WikiText2 vs. BF16 baseline.
2. KV cache stress test: Apply HiF4, INT4, MXFP4 to KV cache in openPangu-7B, generate text, and evaluate zero-shot accuracy on common benchmarks (e.g., HellaSwag, PIQA).
3. SmoothQuant integration: Pre-apply SmoothQuant to Qwen3-8B, then quantize with HiF8 (activations) and INT8 (weights), compare accuracy against vanilla PTQ.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How well do HiFloat formats generalize to transformer architectures beyond Qwen3-8B and openPangu-7B, such as encoder-decoder models or smaller parameter scales?
- Basis: [inferred] The study evaluates only two decoder-only LLMs, limiting architectural diversity.
- Why unresolved: Different architectures exhibit varying activation distributions and quantization sensitivity, which could alter HiFloat's relative advantages.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive benchmark across a diverse model suite (e.g., Llama, Gemma, Mistral, T5) with varying sizes, measuring accuracy (perplexity, zero-shot) and efficiency metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the hierarchical scaling parameters in HiF4 (block sizes, number of levels) be adaptively optimized per layer or per tensor to further improve quantization fidelity?
- Basis: [inferred] HiF4 employs a fixed three-level hierarchy with predetermined block sizes (64, 8, 4), as described in Section 4 and Appendix E.
- Why unresolved: Fixed hierarchies may not align with local variance patterns; adaptive schemes could better allocate representational capacity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Develop dynamic block-sizing algorithms based on data statistics (e.g., variance, outlier presence) and compare SQNR and end-to-end accuracy against the fixed design.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the synergistic effect of HiFloat with recent PTQ techniques like QuaRot, AffineQuant, or SpinQuant, which were not explored in the paper?
- Basis: [inferred] The experimental analysis covers only SmoothQuant and SVDQuant, omitting other state-of-the-art outlier mitigation methods.
- Why unresolved: Alternative PTQ methods use distinct strategies (e.g., rotation, affine transforms) that may interact differently with HiFloat's scaling mechanisms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Integrate HiFloat with these PTQ frameworks, evaluate on standard benchmarks (Wikitext, C4, downstream tasks), and report accuracy and quantization stability metrics.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What are the actual hardware efficiency gains (throughput, memory bandwidth) of HiFloat on Ascend NPUs compared to other low-bit formats, and how transferable are these gains to platforms like GPUs?
- Basis: [inferred] The paper focuses on accuracy outcomes without detailed hardware performance measurements, despite targeting Ascend NPUs.
- Why unresolved: Accuracy preservation does not directly imply speedups or memory savings; implementation overheads (e.g., hierarchical scaling) are hardware-dependent.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement HiFloat kernels on Ascend NPUs and GPUs, measure latency, throughput, and memory usage, and analyze conversion and dequantization overheads across formats.

## Limitations
- Results are tightly coupled to Ascend NPU microarchitecture (e.g., hierarchical scaling implementation), limiting generalizability to other NPUs/GPUs.
- Evaluation is restricted to two decoder-only LLMs (Qwen3-8B, openPangu-7B), lacking architectural diversity (e.g., encoder-decoder, MoE) or smaller scales.
- Hardware efficiency metrics (throughput, memory bandwidth) are not analyzed, despite the NPU inference focus.

## Confidence
- **High:** HiF4 hierarchical scaling prevents INT4-like collapse on evaluated models; per-axis HiF8 matches MXFP8 for high-variance activations and KV cache.
- **Medium:** Bit-width-dependent performance split (4-bit vs. 8-bit) and SmoothQuant compatibility improvements.
- **Low:** Extrapolation to broader model families, non-NLP tasks (vision/audio), and performance on non-Ascend hardware platforms.
