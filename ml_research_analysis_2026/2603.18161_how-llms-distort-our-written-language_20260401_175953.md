# How LLMs Distort Our Written Language

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18161
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18161
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LLM assistance causes significant semantic distortion: heavy use increases neutral stances by 68.9%, induces uniform embedding shifts even for grammar-only edits, and produces higher-scoring scientific reviews that underweight clarity and significance while emphasizing reproducibility.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how LLM-based writing assistants systematically distort the intended meaning of human-authored text. Through three complementary studies—a randomized controlled trial (RCT) with 100 essay writers, a counterfactual edit analysis using the ArgRewrite-v2 dataset, and an analysis of 9,000 ICLR 2026 peer reviews—the authors demonstrate that LLM use leads to (1) a 68.9% increase in essays adopting a neutral stance, (2) large, uniform semantic shifts in embedding space even under constrained prompts, and (3) AI-generated reviews that assign systematically higher scores and shift evaluation criteria toward reproducibility. The findings suggest that current LLMs, optimized for broad appeal and coherence, overwrite author voice and meaning, raising concerns about their widespread adoption in writing and scientific peer review.

## Method Summary
The authors combine three empirical streams. First, a user RCT with 100 Prolific participants (US native English speakers) writing an essay on “Does money lead to happiness?” compares a control group without LLM assistance to an AI-assisted group using embedded gpt-4o-mini. Participants were classified based on interaction transcripts into “LLM-Influenced” (<40% generated text) and “LLM” (heavy use). Second, a counterfactual analysis uses the pre-ChatGPT ArgRewrite-v2 dataset, which contains human essays and expert feedback. The authors prompted three LLMs (gpt-5-mini, gemini-2.5-flash, claude-haiku) to revise the essays under five edit types, then compared the resulting semantic shifts (via embeddings MiniLM-L6-v2 and Gemini-004) to human revisions. Third, they examined 9,000 ICLR 2026 peer reviews, where 21% were labeled AI-generated by the Pangram classifier, and compared scores and extracted strengths/weaknesses (via LLM-as-judge) between human and AI-only reviews. Across all studies, they measured semantic, lexical, and psychological features, including Jensen-Shannon divergence, POS distributions, NRC emotion words, LIWC analytic scores, and embedding distances.

## Key Results
- Heavy LLM use in the RCT increased the proportion of essays taking a neutral stance by 68.9% compared to the control, while users reported reduced creativity and sense of voice (t-tests, t-SNE clustering distinct).
- LLM edits—even with “grammar only” prompts—produced large, uniform semantic shifts in embedding space (PCA/t-SNE showed aligned arrows) that diverged significantly from the smaller, more diverse directions of human edits. Lexical and POS changes were also more homogeneous across models.
- AI-generated ICLR reviews assigned scores on average 10% higher than human reviews. Using an LLM-as-judge pipeline, they found LLM reviews were 32% less likely to mention clarity as a strength and 136% more likely to mention reproducibility as a strength (z-tests significant), indicating a systematic shift in evaluation criteria.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Heavy reliance on LLMs during writing increases text neutrality and diminishes perceived author voice/creativity.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs are optimized to produce safe, statistically average outputs that appeal to broad audiences, overwriting individual rhetorical style and expressive markers. Users who outsource content generation receive homogenized text that converges toward median viewpoints, leading to a sense of reduced ownership.
- **Core assumption:** LLM training objectives (e.g., RLHF) reward responses that are inoffensive and broadly acceptable, not those that preserve personal conviction or stylistic uniqueness.
- **Evidence anchors:** RCT showed 68.9% increase in neutral stances; t-tests indicated lower creativity and voice ratings; t-SNE revealed tight clustering of LLM-influenced essays distinct from human diversity.
- **Break condition:** If the neutral stance increase disappears when controlling for topic or user demographics, or if voice loss is not replicated with different models/prompts.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LLM editing—even when constrained to “grammar only”—induces large, uniform semantic shifts in embedding space, diverging from human edit patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Models internalize editing as global restructuring toward learned “improved” text distributions, where “improvement” aligns with training objectives (helpfulness, coherence) that reorient meaning toward model-preferred semantic regions, not minimal error correction.
- **Core assumption:** Alignment techniques like RLHF reward outputs that score highly on automated preference metrics, which correlate with stylistic/structural uniformity rather than fidelity to source intent.
- **Evidence anchors:** PCA/t-SNE of ArgRewrite-v2 edits showed human edits as small, diverse vectors, while all three LLMs produced large, aligned arrows pointing to a new region; lexical (JSD) and POS distributions were also more homogeneous across models.
- **Break condition:** If semantic shift magnitudes drop to human levels when explicitly prompting “preserve meaning” or using specialized fine-tuned copyediting models.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** LLM-generated scientific reviews shift evaluation criteria—down-weighting clarity/significance while emphasizing reproducibility/scalability—and assign systematically higher scores.
- **Mechanism:** Training on review corpora may cause models to associate certain linguistic patterns (e.g., statistical language, methodological detail) with “good” reviews, and RLHF may reward verbose, criteria-driven critiques that alter the weight distribution of evaluation dimensions.
- **Core assumption:** Preference models reward comprehensiveness and detail over nuanced critical judgment, leading to a bias toward formal, detail-oriented commentary.
- **Evidence anchors:** ICLR analysis: LLM reviews 32% less likely to mention clarity as a strength, 136% more likely to mention reproducibility as a strength; average review scores were 10% higher.
- **Break condition:** If criteria differences vanish after controlling for review length or conference norms, or if human-written reviews show the same pattern when authors are aware of AI use.

## Foundational Learning
- **Semantic embedding spaces and dimensionality reduction (PCA/t-SNE)**  
  Why needed: Central to Figures 1, 4b, 5 showing semantic drift; interpreting direction/magnitude of edits requires understanding that embedding proximity reflects meaning similarity.  
  Quick check: If two sentences have very different word choices but share the same core proposition, how close are their embeddings according to models like MiniLM-L6-v2?

- **Counterfactual analysis in NLP**  
  Why needed: ArgRewrite-v2 provides a “what humans would have done” counterfactual; comparing LLM edits to that baseline isolates model-specific distortions from general revision effects.  
  Quick check: What makes a dataset suitable for counterfactual editing analysis (pre-LLM collection, identical prompts/feedback)?

- **LLM-as-a-judge methodology and its biases**  
  Why needed: Used to classify essay stances, extract review strengths/weaknesses; must understand that GPT-4o-as-judge inherits its own alignment biases.  
  Quick check: When using an LLM to label text properties, how might the judge model's own training affect the labels it produces for human vs. AI text?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Three empirical streams (user RCT, counterfactual edit analysis, field observation of reviews) converge on shared metrics: semantic embeddings (MiniLM-L6-v2, Gemini-004), lexical (JSD), POS, emotional (NRC), psychological (LIWC), and LLM-as-judge categories.
- **Critical path:** Start with user study → identify behavioral patterns (neutral stance ↑, voice ↓) → replicate via controlled edit experiment (ArgRewrite-v2) → validate in wild with ICLR reviews. Each subsequent study tightens causal inference.
- **Design tradeoffs:** RCT provides causality but limited ecological validity; ArgRewrite-v2 offers clean counterfactual but uses artificial prompts; ICLR analysis is high-stakes ecological but observational with detection uncertainty (Pangram tool).
- **Failure signatures:** (1) User study effect disappears when stratifying by pre-existing writing skill. (2) Semantic shifts vanish when using fine-tuned edit models. (3) ICLR review differences attenuate after matching for paper quality or reviewer seniority.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate Figure 4b: embed user study essays, run t-SNE, verify cluster separation between Human, LLM-Influenced, LLM conditions.  
  2. Reproduce Figure 1: load ArgRewrite-v2, generate LLM D2 drafts with one model, compute PCA vectors, compare arrow alignment/magnitude vs human.  
  3. Validate Figure 11: train a simple logistic regression on review text to predict human vs LLM, check feature coefficients for criteria (clarity, reproducibility) align with LLM-as-judge findings.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) specifically alter the semantic content and intended meaning of LLM-generated text compared to models trained without RLHF?**  
   *Why unresolved:* The paper demonstrates semantic distortions using production RLHF models but does not isolate RLHF as causal.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Controlled experiments contrasting RLHF-tuned models with pre-RLHF checkpoints on standardized writing tasks, measuring semantic shifts against human edits.

2. **To what extent do LLM-induced writing distortions generalize to non-English languages and diverse cultural contexts?**  
   *Why unresolved:* All empirical studies involve English and US participants; cross-linguistic variability unexplored.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Replication of the three studies in multiple languages with culturally adapted prompts, comparing distortion magnitudes across groups.

3. **Will pervasive adoption of LLMs in scientific peer review systematically shift evaluation criteria and decision outcomes, and what safeguards can detect/correct such distortions?**  
   *Why unresolved:* Analysis is a single-conference retrospective correlation; long-term impact on publications and research unknown.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Longitudinal studies tracking review metrics and acceptance rates before/after AI adoption, with interventions like AI-disclosure policies.

4. **What technical modifications can enable writing assistance that preserves author meaning and voice without semantic distortion?**  
   *Why unresolved:* Paper diagnoses problem but does not evaluate mitigations.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Development and testing of novel fine-tuning (personalized RLHF), prompt templates, or model constraints that optimize for semantic similarity to pre-assist drafts.

## Limitations
- Original user-study data (transcripts, essays, surveys) are unreleased; new collections may differ in participant pool or LLM behavior.
- Model identifiers (e.g., gpt-5-mini) may be obsolete; substitutions could alter effect sizes.
- Pangram AI classifier is closed, making ICLR AI-labeling irreproducible without it; alternative detectors may not match.
- Exact hyperparameters for dimensionality reduction (t-SNE perplexity, seeds) and embedding models (Gemini-004) are unspecified, affecting visual replication.
- LIWC-22 requires a proprietary license; alternatives may not match original metric.
- Minor preprocessing choices (tokenization, filtering) are not fully documented and may affect JSD/POS results.

## Confidence
- Neutral stance increase (Mechanism 1): Medium
- Uniform semantic shifts (Mechanism 2): Medium
- Review score/criteria shifts (Mechanism 3): Low

## Next Checks
1. **User-study replication** with ≥200 participants, updated LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o), and pre-registered analysis to confirm neutral-stance effect while controlling for topic and baseline writing skill.
2. **Semantic-shift robustness test**: Run ArgRewrite-v2 experiment with a fine-tuned copyediting model (e.g., AWP-T5) and explicit “preserve propositional meaning” prompts; verify if shift magnitudes drop to human levels.
3. **Review-criteria triangulation**: Re-analyze ICLR reviews using two open-source AI detectors (e.g., OpenAI classifier, DetectGPT) and a matched sample of human-written reviews from a prior conference; check if clarity/significance underweighting persists.
