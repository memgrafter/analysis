# How to Train Your Long-Context Visual Document Model
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15257
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15257
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieved state-of-the-art on MMLongBenchDoc and MMLBD-C by systematically studying continued pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and preference optimization for up to 344K tokens, with improved transfer to text tasks.

## Executive Summary
Long-context vision-language models for document VQA lack reproducible recipes. This paper systematically investigates training methods for 24B and 32B parameter models up to 344K tokens, using synthetic data pipelines, ablations, and page indices. Key findings: matching training context to evaluation length yields +1.4–3.0 points on visual long-context average; page indices boost MMLBD-C by +2.8; self-improvement via recursive synthetic pipelines adds +3.2–3.8; and visual long-context training transfers to text (+11.5 on HELMET). The authors also release a corrected MMLBD-C benchmark.

## Method Summary
The authors build on base models (Mistral Small 3.1 Instruct, Qwen3 VL Instruct) and extend their context length via RoPE adjustments. Training proceeds in two stages: a short stage (up to ~104 pages or 128K tokens) and a long stage (up to ~336 pages or 336K tokens). Synthetic data is generated at scale: questions via Magpie-style single-page or multi-page pipelines, and answers via plain distillation from a strong teacher (Qwen3 VL 235B) or a recursive pipeline that first extracts per-page evidence then synthesizes. Training tasks include continued pretraining (CPT) with objectives like filler-in-the-middle (FIM), unshuffle, retrieval, and counting, followed by supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or preference optimization (LongPO). Model merging (scaling the delta before addition) mitigates catastrophic forgetting. The best results use page indices (“Page i:”) prepended to images in both training and evaluation.

## Key Results
- Matching training context to evaluation length (short stage only) improves visual long-context average by 1.4–3.0 points over training with longer contexts; page indices boost MMLBD-C by +2.8.
- Self-improvement via the recursive synthetic pipeline yields an additional +3.2–3.8 gain in visual long-context average.
- Visual long-context training transfers to text (+11.5 on HELMET) and achieves SOTA on MMLongBenchDoc/MMLBD-C, with a corrected benchmark release.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Matching training context to evaluation length
- **Claim:** Training on context lengths that match the evaluation benchmarks outperforms training on longer contexts.
- **Mechanism:** The model's attention and processing mechanisms adapt to the statistical distribution and difficulty of the training sequences. When evaluation contexts are significantly shorter than the maximum training context, the model's parameters become optimized for handling very long sequences, which may introduce inefficiencies (e.g., sparse attention patterns, positional encoding extrapolation errors) that degrade performance on the more common, shorter benchmark examples. Training on lengths similar to the evaluation distribution aligns the model's learned representations and attention sparsity patterns with the actual test-time demand.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant evaluation benchmarks (MMLongBenchDoc, MMLBD-C) contain examples with context lengths substantially shorter than the maximum context length used during training (e.g., 344K tokens). The training data distribution for "long" stages is genuinely long (median 156 images), but benchmarks peak around 128K tokens.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statement “matching training context to evaluation context length outperforms training on longer contexts”; Section 5.2.2 Tables 4 showing short-stage models outperforming combined-stage models; weak direct support from corpus neighbors.
- **Break condition:** If evaluation suite shifts to predominantly ultra-long examples (>250K tokens), the advantage may reverse.

### Mechanism 2: Explicit page indices
- **Claim:** Adding explicit page indices (e.g., “Page 1:”, “Page 2:”) during both training and evaluation provides a substantial, simple performance boost for long-document VQA.
- **Mechanism:** Page indices act as explicit, learnable positional anchors within the visual stream. They reduce the cognitive load of implicit page ordering and provide a clear reference signal for the model to locate, retrieve, and reason about information from specific pages. This is critical when questions refer to “the page with X” or “according to page Y”.
- **Core assumption:** Long-document VQA tasks frequently require referencing specific pages. The native VLM architecture lacks a strong prior for sequential page numbering beyond patch/position embeddings, which can become ambiguous across many pages.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “page indices boost MMLBD-C by +2.8 points”; Section 5.2.3 Table 5 shows benefit only when indices are present in both training and evaluation; no benefit when training without but evaluating with indices.
- **Break condition:** If all questions require holistic understanding without page-specific references, the benefit diminishes.

### Mechanism 3: Self-improvement via recursive synthetic pipelines
- **Claim:** Synthetic data pipelines enable self-improvement (weak-to-strong) for long-context capabilities without a stronger teacher model.
- **Mechanism:** The “recursive answer generation” pipeline simulates complex reasoning: first extract evidence from individual pages (a simpler short-context task), rank relevance, then synthesize a final answer using a teacher model. This distills an algorithm (search, filter, aggregate) into the student. When the student model after initial CPT/SFT is used as the teacher for the next iteration, it can generate higher-quality recursive traces, creating a positive feedback loop that progressively improves long-context reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** The core capability gap is not raw knowledge but the algorithmic process of navigating and synthesizing information across many pages; a recursive pipeline that explicitly demonstrates this process can be distilled effectively.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “self-improvement via synthetic pipelines yields +3.2–3.8 visual LC average gain”; Section 5.4 Table 7 shows self-improvement via CPT (+3.8 VA) and SFT (+3.2 VA) over base Mistral Instruct; Section 5.2.1 describes the recursive pipeline.
- **Break condition:** If the initial model is too weak to generate even minimally plausible per-page extractions or final answers, the pipeline produces low-quality data and degrades.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Base VLMs (Mistral Small 3.1 Instruct, Qwen3 VL Instruct) → Context Extension Layer (RoPE settings) → Training Stages (Stage 1 short up to 104 pages/128K tokens, Stage 2 long up to 336 pages/336K tokens) → Synthetic Data Pipelines (question generation via Magpie, answer generation via plain distillation or recursive) → Training Methods (CPT, SFT, LongPO) → Model Merging (delta scaling factor 0.25–0.5)

**Critical path:**  
1. Prepare a large corpus of long PDFs (250K+). Generate synthetic long-context QA examples using the pipelines.  
2. Optionally perform CPT on the base model with mixed tasks and a length/difficulty curriculum; save CPT vector.  
3. Merge CPT vector into Instruct checkpoint (scale 0.5 for Mistral, 0.25 for Qwen3 VL).  
4. Train (SFT or LongPO) on merged checkpoint using page indices in prompts.  
5. Merge the final delta into the instruct model with scaling factor 0.25.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- CPT vs. SFT-only: CPT improves text transfer (HELMET +7.5) and aids self-improvement, but SFT-only is competitive on MMLBD-C and saves compute.  
- SFT vs. LongPO: LongPO yields higher visual average (+2.1) but costs ~2× compute and requires a strong teacher. SFT with plain distillation reaches SOTA on MMLBD-C for Qwen3 VL 32B.  
- Context length: Training on shorter contexts (matching benchmarks) is better than full long-stage training, reducing compute and memory.  
- Recursive vs. Plain Distillation: Recursive enables self-improvement and better VA/LCA; plain is simpler and yields higher MMLBD-C when using a very strong teacher (235B).  

**Failure signatures:**  
- Catastrophic forgetting on instruct tasks after SFT/CPT without effective merging.  
- Poor MMLBD-C despite good visual average suggests failure on multi-page evidence-aggregation questions.  
- Large degradation from 32K to 128K in HELMET/MMLongBench indicates inadequate context extension or missing page indices.  
- Self-improvement loop fails (VA drops) likely due to overly complex recursive traces or insufficient hard negatives.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Page indices sanity check:** Train two SFT models on 10K examples, one with page indices, one without. Evaluate on MMLBD-C with/without indices. Expect +2.8 VA gain only when indices are used in both.  
2. **Context length matching:** Take Qwen3 VL 32B Instruct. Train two SFT checkpoints: short-stage (≤104 pages) vs. combined short+long-stage (≤336 pages). Compare visual average; expect short-stage to win by 1-3 points.  
3. **Recursive pipeline validation:** Generate 1K QA pairs using recursive pipeline (evidence extraction + scoring + full answer) vs. plain distillation from same teacher. Train a small SFT model on each set. Evaluate on MMLBD-C and HELMET subset. Expect recursive to show stronger transfer to long-context tasks and similar/better MMLBD-C.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How should CPT and SFT be combined optimally given their non-additive effects?**  
   The paper notes they do not compose additively across benchmarks, suggesting opportunities for mixed-stage training or replay mechanisms. Unresolved reasons (e.g., forgetting, distribution shifts) lack analysis. Resolution would require systematic variations of CPT/SFT order, mixing, and regularization.

2. **Why does training with context length matching evaluation yield higher gains than longer-context training?**  
   The phenomenon is observed but unexplained; it is unclear whether it stems from data distribution or optimization effects. Resolving it would require holding data distribution constant while varying max context and measuring loss curvature or attention utilization.

3. **What mechanism explains the effectiveness of page indices, and can it be improved?**  
   No analysis of why indices help; possibilities include positional signaling or ambiguity reduction. Further experiments (ablate index formats, visualize attention, test on different architectures) are needed.

4. **Do the training recipes scale effectively to extreme context lengths (>128K) as used in training (344K)?**  
   Evaluation suites under-represent extreme-length documents, so performance at the full trained context is unknown. Building a benchmark with 200+ pages and tasks requiring full-context reasoning would verify scaling.

## Limitations
- Exact teacher decoding settings (temperature, top_p, max_new_tokens) for answer distillation are unspecified, potentially affecting answer quality.
- Composition of external SFT datasets (Luth, Smoltalk2) lacks file-level granularity, preventing exact dataset reconstruction.
- Results may overfit to the synthetic data distribution; generalization to unseen real documents is unverified.

## Confidence
- Matching context to evaluation length: High (Table 4 shows consistent gains across model families).
- Page indices: High (Table 5 isolates effect with controlled train/eval pairing).
- Recursive self-improvement: Medium (Table 7 demonstrates gains but depends on teacher quality and pipeline hyperparameters; break condition noted).

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a teacher hyperparameter sweep (temperature ∈ {0.7, 1.0}, top_p ∈ {0.9, 1.0}) on answer generation and re-evaluate SFT performance on MMLBD-C to quantify sensitivity.
2. Train an identical SFT model using plain distillation (full-context answers only) with the same teacher and data volume; compare visual average and MMLBD-C to the recursive pipeline to attribute the improvement.
3. Test the best model on a new benchmark of 1K human-annotated multi-page document QA pairs (e.g., from DocVQA variants) to assess robustness beyond synthetic data.
