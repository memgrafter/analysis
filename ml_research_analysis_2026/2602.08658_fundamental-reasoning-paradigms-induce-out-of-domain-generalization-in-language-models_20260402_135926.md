# Fundamental Reasoning Paradigms Induce Out-of-Domain Generalization in Language Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08658 | Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08658 | References: 40
- Primary result: Knowledge‑free symbolic training yields up to 14.60 point OOD gains, with deduction most transferable and upcycling the strongest induction method.

## Executive Summary
Can inducing fundamental reasoning paradigms (deduction, induction, abortion) via symbolic trajectories improve OOD generalization? The authors generate ~17K symbolic problems and ~160K step‑by‑step traces from teacher LLMs (Qwen3-30B, Llama3.3-70B). Smaller students (Llama3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B) are trained using full fine‑tuning, LoRA, up‑scaling, and upcycling. Evaluations on OOD symbolic and realistic tasks (True Detective, αNLI, WinoWhy, FOLIO, RECV) show gains up to 14.60 points; deduction is most robust and transfers to other paradigms; upcycling delivers peak performance; students sometimes surpass teachers. Transfer is asymmetric: deduction aids others, while abduction transfers poorly.

## Method Summary
- **Data**: ~17K symbolic problems covering deduction (SAT formulas), induction (number patterns), abduction (logic puzzles). Splits: 100 dev, 100 test, remainder train.
- **Teachers**: Qwen3-30B‑Instruct & Llama3.3‑70B‑Instruct generate 5 stepwise solutions per question; responses <20 words filtered out.
- **Students**: Llama3.1‑8B‑Instruct (32 layers) and Qwen3‑8B (36 layers).
- **Induction methods**:
  * Full FT: all parameters updated.
  * LoRA: rank 1024 (Llama) / 512 (Qwen) on every linear layer.
  * Up‑scaling (LLaMAPro): 4 layer groups, +3 layers/group; new layers copy base weights, zero attention/MLP output projections.
  * Upcycling (Sparse‑Upcycling): odd‑numbered dense layers become MoE with 2 experts (original + copy); router randomly initialized.
- **Training**: cosine LR (max 5e‑6 → 5e‑7), 1× A100, FlashAttention‑2, bf16.
- **Evaluation**: Qwen3‑30B judge outputs TRUE/FALSE vs gold; symbolic tasks also judged for format robustness.

## Key Results
- Up to 14.60 absolute point OOD accuracy improvement.
- Deduction emerges as the most robust and transferable reasoning paradigm.
- Upcycling consistently achieves the highest performance; students even outperform teachers on realistic OOD tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Mechanism 1 (Pure logical structure)**: Symbolic, knowledge‑free trajectories isolate domain‑independent inference patterns, enabling transfer to natural language reasoning tasks.
- **Mechanism 2 (Capacity expansion)**: Up‑scaling (depth) and upcycling (MoE) add dedicated capacity, reducing interference from pre‑existing knowledge and allowing specialization.
- **Mechanism 3 (Asymmetric transfer)**: Deduction’s strict entailment creates a reusable scaffold that aids induction and abduction; abduction’s open‑ended plausibility lacks such scaffolding.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fundamental Reasoning Paradigms** (Deduction, Induction, Abduction): the three core reasoning types manipulated. *Quick check:* What is the key difference between deduction and abduction?
- **Parameter‑Efficient Fine‑Tuning (LoRA)**: low‑rank adaptation for efficient learning of new skills. *Quick check:* How does low‑rank decomposition reduce the number of trainable parameters while preserving expressiveness?
- **Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) & Upcycling**: capacity expansion via sparse experts; upcycling converts dense layers to MoE. *Quick check:* In upcycling, how are the new expert weights initialized?
- **Out‑of‑Domain Generalization**: evaluation of reasoning beyond the training distribution. *Quick check:* Why is OOD generalization a stricter test than in‑domain accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Symbolic tasks → Teacher trajectories → Student training (Full FT, LoRA, Up‑scaling, Upcycling) → Evaluation.
- **Critical path**: Data generation → Teacher tracing → Student training → Evaluation.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  * Full FT: full parameter update, strong adaptation but risks catastrophic forgetting.
  * LoRA: efficient low‑rank updates; may underfit if rank too low.
  * Up‑scaling: adds depth, increasing capacity but also latency.
  * Upcycling: MoE sparsity yields best gains, higher memory consumption.
- **Failure signatures**:
  * Noisy or error‑prone teacher trajectories harm student learning.
  * Mismatched teacher‑student model families reduce transfer to realistic tasks.
  * Mixing paradigms (Mix) dilutes the inductive bias of each.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train each student (Llama3.1‑8B, Qwen3‑8B) on deduction with all four induction methods; evaluate in‑domain, OOD symbolic, and realistic OOD.
  2. Repeat for induction and abduction; measure within‑paradigm accuracy and cross‑paradigm transfer effects.
  3. Swap teacher models (use Qwen vs. Llama) for trajectory generation and compare resulting student performance on realistic OOD.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Combining deduction, induction, abduction without interference?** Mixed training (Mix) underperformed single‑paradigm approaches; the cause of dilution and remedies (mixture ratios, curriculum, paradigm‑specific adapters) remain unknown.
- **Impact of symbolic task choice on OOD generalization?** Only one symbolic task per paradigm was tested; other tasks with the same abstract structure may yield different transfer patterns.
- **Generalization to non‑English languages?** All data and evaluations are in English; reasoning patterns may vary across languages, limiting cross‑lingual applicability.
- **Why does mixing all three paradigms degrade realistic OOD performance?** The hypothesis of “inductive bias dilution” is speculative; systematic ablations and gradient analyses are needed to pinpoint the mechanism.

## Limitations
- The study relies on a single internally generated symbolic dataset; no external validation of paradigm purity exists, and potential leakage of world knowledge cannot be ruled out.
- Critical hyperparameters (batch size, warm‑up steps, number of epochs) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication and risking under‑/over‑fitting.
- Cross‑paradigm transfer analysis is limited to one set of symbolic tasks; generalizability to other reasoning families is uncertain.
- Evaluation depends on a single judge model (Qwen3‑30B‑Instruct), whose biases may affect accuracy estimates.

## Confidence
- Knowledge‑free symbolic induction improves OOD generalization: **Medium**
- Upcycling yields highest gains: **High**
- Deduction is most robust and transferable: **Medium**
- Symbolic training imparts pure logical structure: **Low**

## Next Checks
1. **External benchmark replication:** Test top induced models on BBH, LogiQA, EntailmentBank and compare against same‑size baselines.
2. **Ablation on symbolic purity:** Inject world‑knowledge cues into symbolic data and measure resulting OOD drop.
3. **Judge independence:** Re‑evaluate with multiple judges (GPT‑4, Claude, NLI model) and report agreement rates.
