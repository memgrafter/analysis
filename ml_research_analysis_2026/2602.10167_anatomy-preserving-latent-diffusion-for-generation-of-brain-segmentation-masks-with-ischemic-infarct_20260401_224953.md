# Anatomy-Preserving Latent Diffusion for Generation of Brain Segmentation Masks with Ischemic Infarct
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10167
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10167
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: Unconditional, scalable generation of brain segmentation masks with ischemic infarct control via binary prompt, and release of 605 synthetic masks for data augmentation.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the scarcity of high-quality segmentation masks in non-contrast CT neuroimaging by proposing an anatomy-preserving latent diffusion framework. A variational autoencoder (VAE) trained exclusively on segmentation masks learns an anatomical latent representation, and a diffusion model operates in this latent space to generate new masks from noise, with optional binary prompting for infarct control. Qualitative results show generated masks preserve global anatomy, discrete tissue semantics, and realistic variability, avoiding common structural artifacts. Quantitatively, pixel-wise class distributions closely match real data, and the Fréchet Inception Distance achieves 61.88 at the optimal epoch, indicating high similarity. The method enables unconditional, scalable mask synthesis, and a dataset of 605 synthetic masks is publicly released for data augmentation.

## Method Summary
The framework uses a two-stage training pipeline. Stage I: A VAE (MaskVAE) is trained on one-hot encoded segmentation masks (7 classes, 256×256) using categorical cross-entropy reconstruction loss and KL divergence (β=0.01). The encoder comprises four strided convolutional blocks mapping to a 256-dimensional Gaussian latent; the decoder is an MLP that outputs categorical logits. Training employs weighted sampling (5× for slices containing infarct) to handle class imbalance. Stage II: The VAE decoder is frozen. A diffusion model (T=100 timesteps, linear β schedule) is trained in the VAE’s latent space. Its MLP denoiser takes input `[z_t; t/T; p(y)]`, where `p(y)` is a learnable embedding for the binary lesion prompt `y`. Latent codes `z_0` are precomputed via the VAE encoder for all training masks. At inference, noise is sampled from `N(0,I)`, iteratively denoised to `z_0`, decoded with the frozen VAE decoder, and converted to a mask via `argmax`.

## Key Results
- Generated masks preserve global anatomy, discrete tissue semantics, and realistic variability, avoiding structural artifacts common in pixel-space generative models.
- Pixel-wise class distributions closely match real data; Fréchet Inception Distance achieves 61.88 at the optimal epoch.
- Public release of 605 synthetic masks enables data augmentation for segmentation tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Training a VAE exclusively on segmentation masks creates an anatomical latent manifold that enforces global structure and tissue semantics during decoding.  
**Mechanism:** The VAE encoder maps one-hot masks to a diagonal Gaussian posterior `(µ, logσ²)`. The decoder reconstructs categorical logits from sampled latents `z`. Categorical cross-entropy loss (`L_rec`) and KL divergence (`L_KL`) force the decoder to learn a deterministic, anatomy-aware mapping from the latent space to valid discrete label maps. Any latent decoded through the frozen decoder must produce a mask with plausible topology and class relationships because the decoder was trained only on real masks.  
**Core assumption:** A compact latent space (D=256) trained with discrete classification loss can sufficiently capture the manifold of anatomically valid brain configurations.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "variational autoencoder trained exclusively on segmentation masks to learn an anatomical latent representation"  
- Section II.B: "CTseg... produces anatomically consistent maps" (ground truth masks used to train VAE are anatomically consistent)  
- Section III.B: Reconstruction treated as pixel-wise multi-class classification; categorical cross-entropy loss preserves discrete semantics  
- Corpus: Neighbors like "Diffusion Model in Latent Space for Medical Image Segmentation Task" (arxiv:2512.01292) use latent diffusion but do not assert anatomy preservation via mask-only VAE pretraining.  
**Break condition:** If the VAE decoder produces masks with broken topology (e.g., disconnected skull, inverted tissue adjacency) when fed latent samples from `N(0,I)`, the anatomical prior is ineffective.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Performing diffusion in the VAE’s latent space (not pixel space) avoids structural artifacts by constraining generation to the anatomical manifold.  
**Mechanism:** A diffusion model operates on latents `z_0 ~ q_ϕ(z|X)`. Forward diffusion adds Gaussian noise in latent space; reverse process denoises from `z_T ~ N(0,I)` back to `z_0`. Because `z_0` must be decodable by the frozen VAE decoder, all intermediate and final latents stay within the region of latent space corresponding to valid anatomy. This prevents pixel-space diffusion from directly synthesizing incoherent labels.  
**Core assumption:** The VAE latent space is sufficiently smooth and complete such that linear interpolation (via diffusion) between noise and a valid `z_0` remains within the valid anatomical manifold.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "diffusion is carried out in this latent space... avoiding the structural artifacts commonly observed in pixel-space generative models"  
- Section III.A: "By decoupling anatomical structure learning from stochastic generation... reduces the structural artifacts"  
- Section III.C.3: `ϵ_θ` is implemented as an MLP mapping from `R^D+1+dp` to `R^D` (operates on latent vectors)  
- Corpus: Neighbors like "LGESynthNet..." (arxiv:2603.18356) use latent diffusion but paper does not provide evidence that latent space diffusion inherently preserves anatomy; this is a claim based on VAE design.  
**Break condition:** If latent samples `z_T` denoised to `z_0` yield valid masks but intermediate `z_t` (t>0) decode to anatomically invalid masks, the diffusion trajectory leaves the valid manifold, indicating poor latent alignment.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A binary prompt (`y ∈ {0,1}`) embedded and concatenated to the latent diffusion input provides coarse, effective control over infarct presence without compromising anatomical coherence.  
**Mechanism:** A learnable embedding table maps the binary lesion flag `y` to a vector `p(y)`. During diffusion, `inp = [z_t; t/T; p(y)]` is fed to `ϵ_θ`. The model learns to steer the denoising trajectory toward latents that decode with or without infarct pixels based on `y`. Because the infarct class is part of the VAE’s learned manifold, conditioning biases sampling toward regions of latent space that produce infarct-annotated masks.  
**Core assumption:** Lesion presence/absence can be disentangled from other anatomical features in the latent space and is learnable from a simple binary signal.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "optional coarse control over lesion presence via a binary prompt"  
- Section III.C.2: "Conditioning is implemented by concatenation... which is sufficient for a binary control signal"  
- Section IV.B: Figure 5 shows synthetic distributions with `y=1` include infarct pixels matching real proportions; "natural variability in lesion extent" suggests the prompt sets a high-level mode.  
- Corpus: Neighbors like "Diffusion-Based Data Augmentation..." (arxiv:2508.17844) use text guidance; this paper uses binary prompt, but corpus does not assess effectiveness of binary vs. continuous conditioning for lesions.  
**Break condition:** If conditioning on `y=1` always produces identical infarct patterns (mode collapse) or fails to produce infarcts in any sample, the prompt lacks representational capacity or the VAE latent space confounds lesion anatomy with non-lesion features.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for discrete data**  
  *Why needed:* To learn a continuous latent representation from categorical segmentation masks while preserving sharp boundaries via cross-entropy reconstruction (not MSE). The VAE acts as a fixed anatomical decoder.  
  *Quick check:* If you sample `z ~ N(0,I)` and decode with `G_ϕ`, does `argmax(ˆL)` yield a mask with intact tissue topology (e.g., skull encircles brain, CSF surrounds parenchyma)?

- **Concept: Latent Diffusion Models**  
  *Why needed:* To generate new latent vectors `z_0` from noise while staying within the VAE’s valid latent region. Diffusion in latent space is computationally cheaper and leverages the VAE’s anatomical prior to avoid pixel-space incoherence.  
  *Quick check:* Does the denoiser `ϵ_θ` take inputs of dimension `D + 1 + d_p` (latent + timestep + prompt), and is trained to predict the noise added to `z_t`?

- **Concept: One-hot encoding for multi-class segmentation**  
  *Why needed:* Masks have `C=7` discrete tissue classes (including infarct). One-hot tensors `X ∈ {0,1}^{C×H×W}` enable categorical cross-entropy loss, which is critical for preserving discrete semantics and preventing label mixing during VAE training.  
  *Quick check:* During preprocessing, are label maps converted to one-hot before VAE training, and does the decoder output logits with `C` channels for `argmax` reconstruction?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**  
  MaskVAE: E_ϕ (encoder) -> latent z_0 <-> G_ϕ (decoder)  
  Diffusion: z_t -> ϵ_θ (MLP) -> predicted noise, with input concatenation [z_t; t/T; p(y)]  
  Inference: z_T ~ N(0,I) -> iterative denoising -> z_0 -> G_ϕ -> argmax mask

- **Critical path**  
  1. Train MaskVAE on all training masks (with lesion-weighted sampling) until reconstructions are anatomically valid (check qualitatively).  
  2. Freeze G_ϕ. Precompute latent codes z_0 for all training masks via E_ϕ.  
  3. Train diffusion model ϵ_θ on the precomputed latents z_0 with prompts y.  
  4. At inference, sample new z_0 from ϵ_θ (starting from z_T) and decode.

- **Design tradeoffs**  
  - 2D slices vs. 3D volumes: Paper uses 2D axial slices (section II.A). Tradeoff: simpler, more data; loses volumetric consistency.  
  - Binary prompt vs. spatial conditioning: Binary y gives coarse control; no spatial lesion map guidance. Tradeoff: simplicity vs. precision.  
  - MLP denoiser vs. U-Net: MLP is lightweight for low-D latents (D=256). Tradeoff: no spatial awareness (acceptable because VAE handles anatomy), but cannot model fine latent correlations if D were larger.  
  - β=0.01 in VAE: Balances latent regularization vs. reconstruction fidelity. Higher β may cause blurring; lower may cause latent holes.

- **Failure signatures**  
  1. Fragmented masks: VAE decoder trained with insufficient data or wrong loss (MSE instead of CE) produces mixed labels.  
  2. Lesion collapse: Diffusion with y=1 always outputs same infarct pattern (check by generating multiple samples; should vary in size/position per section IV.B).  
  3. Anatomical drift: Generated masks have misplaced structures (e.g., CSF inside brain parenchyma) → VAE latent space not constrained enough (increase β or VAE capacity).  
  4. Prompt insensitive: y=0 samples occasionally contain infarcts → prompt embedding not learned or dataset imbalance too severe.

- **First 3 experiments**  
  1. VAE reconstruction sanity check: Train MaskVAE for few epochs. Visualize argmax(G_ϕ(E_ϕ(X))) vs. X. Must show sharp boundaries and correct topology.  
  2. Latent sampling check: Sample 100 latents z ~ N(0,I) and decode. Should produce valid but random masks (no obvious duplicates or voids). Compute pixel-class distribution; should roughly match real prior.  
  3. Prompt conditioning validation: Generate 50 masks with y=0 and y=1. Manually inspect: y=0 should have near-zero infarct pixels; y=1 should have infarct in >95% of samples. Quantify infarct pixel ratio per class distribution plot (like Figure 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can the framework be extended to generate full 3D volumetric brain segmentation masks while preserving anatomical consistency across slices?
- Can lesion control be refined beyond binary presence to include controllable attributes like size, location, or shape?
- Do synthetic masks from this framework effectively augment training data for downstream segmentation models, improving their performance and robustness?
- How well does the learned anatomical latent space generalize to brain anatomies from stroke subtypes or imaging protocols outside the training distribution?

## Limitations
- Incomplete reporting of training hyperparameters (learning rates, batch sizes, diffusion β schedule) hinders exact replication.
- Evaluation relies on FID and pixel-wise class distributions, which do not directly measure anatomical validity (e.g., tissue topology, adjacency constraints).
- The framework processes 2D axial slices, ignoring volumetric consistency across slices.
- The binary prompt's ability to control lesion attributes (size, location) is not quantitatively assessed.

## Confidence
- VAE learns an anatomical latent manifold: Medium
- Latent diffusion avoids structural artifacts: Medium
- Binary prompt provides effective coarse lesion control: Low

## Next Checks
1. Topological validation: Compute connected components and Euler numbers for each tissue class on 100 generated masks and compare to real masks; significant deviations would indicate broken anatomy.
2. Ablation against pixel-space diffusion: Train a U-Net diffusion model directly on masks (same data) and compare artifact frequency (e.g., disconnected skull, label mixing) via automated detection or human evaluation.
3. Prompt conditioning quantification: Generate 500 masks for each prompt value (y=0, y=1). Measure (a) infarct pixel ratio per sample and its variance, and (b) spatial distribution (e.g., centroid spread). High variance and distinct means for y=1 would confirm effective conditioning.
