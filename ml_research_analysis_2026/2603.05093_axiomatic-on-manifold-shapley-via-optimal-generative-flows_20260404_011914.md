# Axiomatic On-Manifold Shapley via Optimal Generative Flows

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05093
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05093
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** On-manifold Aumann-Shapley attributions via Wasserstein-2 geodesic paths eliminate off-manifold artifacts, achieve near-zero Flow Consistency Error (1.78 vs. 2.8e5), and provide provable stability.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical issue of off-manifold artifacts in Shapley-based explainable AI (XAI), which arises when heuristic baselines generate unrealistic inputs outside the true data distribution. The authors propose a novel, axiomatically grounded method that computes feature attributions by integrating model gradients along the Wasserstein-2 geodesic—the kinetic-energy-minimizing optimal transport path—between a reference prior and the data manifold. This path is generated by a learned generative flow model. Theoretically, they prove that the gradient line integral along any smooth path is the *unique* attribution rule satisfying a natural extension of Shapley axioms (including efficiency and reparameterization invariance). Empirically, the method demonstrates strict adherence to the data manifold, drastically reducing geometric error metrics compared to prior methods, while offering superior semantic alignment and provable robustness to flow approximation errors.

## Method Summary
The core idea is to replace heuristic, often linear, interpolation paths (e.g., from Integrated Gradients) with a canonical, on-manifold trajectory derived from optimal transport. First, a generative flow model (e.g., Rectified Flow) is trained to approximate the velocity field of the Wasserstein-2 geodesic connecting a simple prior distribution \(p_0\) (e.g., Gaussian) to the data distribution \(p_1\). For a given input \(x\), the trajectory \(\gamma_x^*\) is obtained by solving the ODE \(\dot{x} = v_\theta(x, t)\) backward from \(t=1\) (at \(x\)) to \(t=0\). The feature attribution \(\Psi_i\) is then computed as the line integral of the model's gradient \(\nabla_x f\) along this path, discretized with a numerical integrator (e.g., trapezoidal rule over 50 steps). This construction is axiomatic: any path-based attribution satisfying key properties (efficiency, symmetry, locality, reparameterization invariance) must take this integral form. The method’s stability is formalized in a bound linking attribution error to the learned flow’s approximation error.

## Key Results
- **Geometric Fidelity:** Achieves Flow Consistency Error (FCE) of 1.78 on CelebA-HQ, compared to 2.8e5 for DDIM, confirming near-perfect manifold adherence.
- **Semantic Quality:** Attains Structure-Aware Total Variation (SATV) of 0.003 on CIFAR-10, versus 0.010 for Integrated Gradients, indicating sharper, more coherent attribution maps.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** Proves uniqueness of the gradient line integral under extended Shapley axioms and provides a stability bound where attribution error scales linearly with flow approximation error.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Off-manifold artifacts are eliminated by constraining attribution integration to paths lying on the data manifold, as generated by an optimal transport flow.
- **Mechanism:** The method selects the Wasserstein-2 geodesic—the minimizer of kinetic energy in the Benamou–Brenier dynamic formulation—as the canonical trajectory \(\gamma^*_x\). This geodesic’s time-marginals are absolutely continuous with respect to the data manifold, ensuring all interpolation points are valid samples. Attribution is computed as the gradient line integral along this trajectory, avoiding undefined or out-of-distribution regions.
- **Core assumption:** The data distribution \(p_1\) is absolutely continuous w.r.t. the reference prior \(p_0\), and the learned vector field sufficiently approximates the optimal velocity field.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.1 link the kinetic-energy-minimizing path to manifold support; Table 3 shows orders-of-magnitude FCE reduction.
- **Break condition:** A poor flow approximation (high error \(\varepsilon\)) causes trajectory drift off-manifold, increasing FCE (see Theorem 4.3).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The gradient line integral is the *unique* path-based attribution rule satisfying the extended Shapley axioms (efficiency, linearity, dummy, symmetry, locality, and reparameterization invariance).
- **Mechanism:** The representation theorem (Theorem 3.3) shows that any functional adhering to these axioms must equal \(\Phi_i(f,\gamma) = \int_0^1 \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}(\gamma(t)) \dot{\gamma}_i(t) dt\). This axiomatically justifies the path-integral form, elevating it from heuristic to necessity.
- **Core assumption:** The model \(f\) is \(C^1\) near the path image, and the axiom set is complete for continuous attributions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 and Appendix B define the axioms and state the uniqueness result.
- **Break condition:** Violating any axiom (e.g., using a non-reparameterization-invariant rule) invalidates the guarantee.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Attribution error scales predictably with flow approximation error, offering stability even with imperfect generative models.
- **Mechanism:** Theorem 4.3 bounds the discrepancy \(|\Psi_i - \hat{\Psi}_i| \leq C_1 L \varepsilon + C_2 L_\nabla \varepsilon^2\), where \(\varepsilon\) is the uniform flow error and \(L, L_\nabla\) are Lipschitz constants of \(f\) and \(\nabla f\). This implies that improving the flow (lower \(\varepsilon\)) provably improves explanations.
- **Core assumption:** \(f\) and \(\nabla f\) are Lipschitz on a compact set containing all trajectories; the flow error is uniformly bounded.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3 and Section 5.2.2 (Figure 3) show empirical correlation (Pearson \(r>0.95\)) between relative flow error and relative attribution error.
- **Break condition:** If \(f\) has high-frequency non-Lipschitz gradients (e.g., near decision boundaries) or \(\varepsilon\) is large, the constants blow up, weakening the bound.

## Foundational Learning
- **Optimal Transport (Wasserstein-2 geodesics):** Minizing \(\int \|v_t\|^2 \rho_t dx dt\) via the Benamou–Brenier formulation yields the "straightest" path in measure space, critical for defining a canonical on-manifold trajectory. *Quick check:* Why does minimizing kinetic energy produce a geodesic in the Wasserstein-2 metric, and how does this ensure straight paths on the manifold?
- **Aumann–Shapley values in continuous domains:** Extends discrete Shapley to continuous feature spaces via path integration. The efficiency axiom \(\sum_i \Phi_i = f(\gamma(1)) - f(\gamma(0))\) follows from the fundamental theorem of calculus. *Quick check:* How does the efficiency axiom naturally arise from the line integral formulation?
- **Flow Matching / Rectified Flows:** Training objective to match the conditional expectation of the optimal velocity field. Reflow iteratively rectifies paths to reduce the coupling gap toward the OT geodesic. *Quick check:* What is the Flow Matching loss, and why does iterative reflow monotonically decrease the transport cost?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Predictor model \(f\) (frozen classifier) -> Generative flow model \(v_\theta(x,t)\) (trained vector field) -> ODE solver (e.g., rk45) -> Attribution integrator (trapezoidal sum).

**Critical path:**
1. Train/load a Rectified Flow model to approximate the Wasserstein-2 geodesic.
2. For input \(x\), solve \(\dot{x} = v_\theta(x,t)\) backward from \(t=1\) to \(t=0\) to obtain path \(\gamma_x\).
3. Compute gradient \(\nabla_x f\) at each path state.
4. Integrate \(\nabla f \odot \Delta\gamma\) to get attributions \(\Psi_i\).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Integration steps \(K\):** Larger \(K\) improves accuracy (quadratic convergence) but increases compute linearly. Default \(K=50\) balances error (~5%) and cost.
- **Flow quality vs. speed:** 1-RF is fast but higher curvature; 2-RF+ reflow is closer to OT geodesic but needs more training.
- **Memory vs. fidelity:** Storing full trajectory allows analysis but uses \(O(Kd)\) memory; online accumulation uses \(O(d)\).

**Failure signatures:**
- **High FCE** (>1e3): trajectory drifts from learned velocity field → off-manifold integration.
- **High SATV:** attributions contain high-frequency noise → likely linear path on shattered manifold.
- **Completeness error >10%:** insufficient integration steps \(K\) or unstable ODE solver.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Sanity check on additive model \(f(x)=\sum \sin(x_i)\): verify \(\Psi_i\) matches analytic Shapley for straight path.
2. Axiom verification: sweep \(K\) to ensure efficiency (completeness) converges to <5% error.
3. FCE vs. baseline: compute Flow Consistency Error for your flow vs. IG/DDIM; expect orders-of-magnitude lower FCE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Flow error impact:** How do practical errors in approximating the optimal transport flow (beyond linear bound) quantitatively affect Shapley attributions in complex, high-dimensional data?
2. **Bias propagation:** How do biases in the generative model’s training data propagate into on-manifold attributions, and can the axiomatic framework mitigate or expose such biases?
3. **RF-OT gap impact:** For natural images, what is the practical impact of the Rectified Flow to Optimal Transport geometric gap on the semantic quality and stability of attributions?
4. **Flow architecture choice:** How does the choice of flow architecture (e.g., UNet vs. MLP) impact convergence speed, stability, and fidelity of the canonical on-manifold Shapley values?
5. **Extension to discrete domains:** Can the framework be extended to discrete/structured domains (text, graphs), and what constitutes a valid "generative flow" there?

## Limitations
- The approach depends critically on the quality of a pre-trained generative flow; suboptimal flows may reintroduce artifacts despite stability bounds.
- Hyperparameters and full training regimens for Rectified Flow models and classifiers are not exhaustively documented, hindering exact replication.
- Metric definitions (e.g., GPS) and oracle convergence criteria are underspecified, leaving room for implementation variance.

## Confidence
- **On-manifold elimination:** Medium–High (strong empirical FCE reduction, but flow quality varies).
- **Axiomatic uniqueness:** High (theoretical derivation is rigorous).
- **Stability guarantee:** Medium (bound holds under Lipschitz assumptions; empirical correlation strong).
- **Semantic superiority:** Medium (metric improvements reported; independent verification needed).
