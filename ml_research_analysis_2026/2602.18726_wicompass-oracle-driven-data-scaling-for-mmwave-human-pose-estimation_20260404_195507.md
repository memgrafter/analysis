# WiCompass: Oracle-driven Data Scaling for mmWave Human Pose Estimation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.18726
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18726
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: WiCompass consistently improves out-of-distribution generalization for mmWave human pose estimation under fixed acquisition budgets, achieving a steeper negative data scaling exponent (α_D = -0.0116 vs. -0.0008) and up to 30 mm lower MPJPE in simulation, plus 7.2 mm lower real-world test error versus conventional collection.

## Executive Summary
mmWave radar-based human pose estimation (HPE) suffers from poor out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization and inefficient data collection due to coverage gaps and redundancy in existing datasets. WiCompass addresses these issues by introducing a coverage-aware framework that distills large-scale motion-capture priors into a universal, modality-agnostic pose space oracle. This oracle quantifies dataset redundancy via k-NN analysis and identifies underrepresented motions. Guided by these analyses, a closed-loop acquisition policy prioritizes the most informative missing samples for collection. Experiments in simulation and real-world settings demonstrate superior scaling behavior—models trained with WiCompass improve more rapidly with added data—and achieve lower OOD errors at matched budgets.

## Method Summary
WiCompass centers on a frozen VQ-VAE tokenizer trained on AMASS mocap data, which maps any pose (mocap or mmWave skeleton) to a discrete latent code sequence. This shared latent space enables cross-dataset comparison. The framework computes directional k-NN coverage: for each reference AMASS pose, it checks whether the nearest neighbor in the current mmWave dataset lies within a locally adaptive radius (the k-NN interpolation zone). The set of uncovered reference poses forms a gap set. A Capped-Probability-Proportional-to-Size (Capped-PPS) sampler then selects target poses from this gap set, weighting each by its k-NN radius (larger radius indicates sparser region). Extreme outliers are capped to avoid implausible poses. Selected targets are decoded to clean skeletons and captured—either via a ray-tracing simulator (RF Genesis) or real human mimicry. The acquired data are added to the mmWave dataset, and the loop repeats until the gap set density falls below a threshold or the budget is exhausted. Finally, a Point-Transformer HPE model is trained on the augmented dataset.

## Key Results
- Achieves a more negative data scaling exponent α_D = -0.0116 (vs. -0.0008 for sequential collection) on a 40k simulated OOD benchmark, indicating faster improvement with added data.
- Reduces OOD MPJPE by 25–30 mm across 2k–40k simulated samples compared to baseline.
- In real-world validation (8k total collected), WiCompass yields a 7.2 mm lower test MPJPE (105.7 vs. 112.9 mm) with broader pose coverage and lower redundancy as measured by the Normalized Redundancy Index.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
A universal, modality-agnostic pose space oracle enables cross-dataset coverage analysis by distilling large-scale motion-capture priors into a discrete latent vocabulary. A VQ-VAE is trained exclusively on AMASS mocap data to learn a codebook of pose tokens. The encoder maps any pose (optical mocap or mmWave skeleton) to a token sequence in this shared space; the decoder reconstructs clean skeletons. Quantization makes distances tractable and semantically meaningful, assuming human motion can be represented by a finite set of compositional primitives where semantic similarity corresponds to latent distance. *Break condition*: If AMASS lacks diversity or discretization merges distinct motions, coverage estimates become biased.

### Mechanism 2
Directional k-NN coverage analysis in the latent space quantifies dataset redundancy and identifies undercovered motion regions. For each reference AMASS pose, its adaptive k-NN radius (defined by local density) operationalizes an "interpolation zone". A pose is covered by the target dataset if its nearest neighbor lies within that radius. Directional coverage is the fraction of covered reference poses; the uncovered subset defines the gap set. The Normalized Redundancy Index (NRI) uses the same radius to measure per-sample density scale-free. *Break condition*: If latent distances do not preserve functional/dynamic similarities, or the manifold is highly anisotropic, the k-NN proxy may fail.

### Mechanism 3
A closed-loop, coverage-driven acquisition policy (Capped-PPS sampling) prioritizes maximally informative missing poses under a fixed budget. From the gap set, each candidate is weighted by its k-NN radius (larger = sparser region); extreme values are capped to filter implausible outliers. Sampling without replacement via Efraimidis–Spirakis prioritizes sparsely covered regions. Reconstructed skeletons guide data acquisition (simulated or real). Iteration continues until a stopping criterion is met. *Break condition*: If the gap set contains many infeasible poses or the latent space does not align semantic novelty with generalization benefit, the policy may waste budget.

## Foundational Learning

### Manifold Hypothesis for Human Motion
**Why needed here**: The framework assumes human poses lie on a low-dimensional manifold within high-dimensional joint-coordinate space. This justifies using a compact latent space (VQ-VAE) and treating k-NN radii as density estimates on the manifold.  
**Quick check**: If the manifold hypothesis fails (poses fill ambient space densely), k-NN-based coverage analysis would overestimate sparsity and misidentify gaps.

### k-Nearest Neighbors for Density Estimation
**Why needed here**: k-NN provides a non-parametric way to estimate local sampling density in the latent space, driving coverage scores and redundancy metrics. The adaptive radius reflects how densely a region is sampled.  
**Quick check**: Why is the k-NN radius a better proxy for “interpolation zone” than a fixed-radius ball? Because it adapts to local density, avoiding over- or under-estimation in sparse vs. dense regions.

### Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)
**Why needed here**: VQ-VAE learns a discrete, compositional representation of poses that is modality-agnostic. It enables direct comparison between MoCap and mmWave data by mapping both to the same codebook, and its discrete nature makes coverage analysis tractable and interpretable.  
**Quick check**: What is the drawback of using a continuous latent space (e.g., standard VAE) instead? Distances are less interpretable and density estimation becomes more complex; quantization provides a natural notion of “token” neighborhoods.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Universal Pose Tokenizer -> Coverage Analyzer -> Target Selector -> Acquisition Interface -> Dataset Manager

### Critical path
1. Train VQ-VAE on AMASS (offline, one-time).
2. Encode all existing mmWave dataset poses to latent vectors.
3. Compute k-NN radii and directional coverage (AMASS → mmWave) to obtain gap set Φ_G.
4. For acquisition budget B, run Capped-PPS on Φ_G to select B targets.
5. Decode each target latent to a skeleton; capture corresponding mmWave data (real or simulated).
6. Add new samples to mmWave dataset; update latent vectors and recompute metrics.
7. Stop when Φ_G density falls below threshold or budget exhausted; evaluate final HPE model on OOD benchmark.

### Design tradeoffs
- **VQ-VAE**: Larger codebook K and longer sequence T improve reconstruction but reduce compression and increase analysis noise; defaults K=64, T=16 were chosen as a sweet spot.
- **k-NN scale**: Choice of k balances sensitivity (small k detects fine-grained gaps, large k reveals broader structural gaps); multi-scale evaluation is recommended.
- **Capping percentile TH**: Lower values filter more outliers but risk removing rare valid poses; TH=0.95 used empirically.
- **Sim vs. real**: Simulation scales cheaply but introduces domain gap; real capture is costly but authentic. The policy is modality-agnostic, so mixing is possible.

### Failure signatures
- Coverage plateau: Directional coverage stops increasing after several acquisition rounds.
- NRI stagnation: Intra-dataset NRI CDF remains narrow (low spread), indicating continued redundancy.
- OOD no-gain: Test MPJPE does not improve or worsens when adding WiCompass data.
- Gap set mismatch: Newly acquired samples do not shrink the gap set significantly.
- Decoder artifacts: Reconstructed skeletons from targeted latent codes are visibly distorted or implausible.

### First 3 experiments
1. **VQ-VAE reconstruction benchmark**: Sweep codebook size K ∈ {32,64,128} and sequence length T ∈ {8,16,24}. Compute L1 reconstruction error on AMASS test set and an unseen mmWave dataset. Confirm K=64, T=16 achieves <10 mm error.
2. **Coverage metric sanity check**: Randomly split AMASS into two subsets (80/20). Compute directional coverage from larger to smaller; expect ~80%. Then remove an entire pose category (e.g., sitting) from the mmWave dataset and verify coverage drops accordingly.
3. **Simulated acquisition comparison**: Under a fixed budget (e.g., 2k samples), run WiCompass vs. baseline (sequential action capture) in the RF Genesis simulator. Train identical HPE models on each collected dataset and evaluate on a held-out OOD benchmark (40k AMASS poses). Confirm WiCompass yields lower MPJPE and steeper negative scaling exponent.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **O1**: Does the data scaling exponent α_D remain constant at larger dataset scales typical of vision or language models?
- **O2**: Can hierarchical quantization in the VQ-VAE yield a more nuanced pose vocabulary that better captures fine-grained motion variations?
- **O3**: How can environmental factors be incorporated into the coverage framework to jointly target kinematic and environmental diversity?
- **O4**: Does the modality-agnostic pose oracle truly guarantee invariance to the sim-to-real gap, or are there hidden biases in the acquisition policy?

## Limitations
- The VQ-VAE architecture hyperparameters (MLP-Mixer layers, hidden size) and HPE training details (optimizer, LR schedule) are missing, blocking faithful replication.
- The RF Genesis simulator is not open-sourced; potential domain gap between simulated and real radar data may limit real-world applicability.
- Real-world acquisition assumes human subjects can accurately mimic targeted skeletons; feasibility and fidelity of mimicry are unquantified.
