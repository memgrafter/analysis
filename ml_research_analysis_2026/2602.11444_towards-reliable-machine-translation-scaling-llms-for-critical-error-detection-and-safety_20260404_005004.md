# Towards Reliable Machine Translation: Scaling LLMs for Critical Error Detection and Safety

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11444
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11444
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Fine-tuned LLMs achieve MCC up to 0.94 on SynCED-EnDe 2025 and 0.92 on WMT-22, outperforming encoder-only baselines and greatly improving minority-class error recall.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of detecting critical meaning errors (e.g., factual distortions, intent reversals) in machine translation, which standard metrics like BLEU overlook. It evaluates instruction-tuned large language models (LLMs) of various sizes using zero-shot, few-shot, and fine-tuning (LoRA) approaches on WMT and SynCED datasets. Results demonstrate that fine-tuning consistently yields superior performance, with GPT-4o-mini reaching a Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) of 0.94 on SynCED-EnDe 2025 and 0.92 on WMT-22. Fine-tuning markedly improves recall for the minority error class (ERR/BAD F1 up to 0.96). Committee voting (majority vote over three stochastic generations) further stabilizes predictions. The findings establish decoder-only LLMs as highly effective for reliable, safety-critical translation verification, with scaling and instruction alignment providing robust gains.

## Method Summary
The study addresses Critical Error Detection (CED), a binary classification task that labels translation pairs as ERR/BAD (meaning-critical failure) or NOT/OK (faithful). Data consists of English→German sentence pairs from WMT-21 (8k train), WMT-22 (155k train), and SynCED-EnDe 2025 (8k train), merged into a 171k training set. The authors compare encoder-only baselines (XLM-R, ModernBERT) against decoder-only LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, LLaMA-3.1-8B, LLaMA-3.3-70B, GPT-OSS-20B/120B) under zero-shot, few-shot, and LoRA fine-tuning regimes. For inference, they use prompt templates (P0–P4) tailored to model families; fine-tuning applies LoRA (rank, alpha unspecified) to the merged training set. Committee voting (three samples at temperature 0.2, majority vote) is used to reduce variance. Performance is measured primarily by Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), with per-class F1 scores as supplements.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned LLMs achieve state-of-the-art MCC: 0.94 on SynCED-EnDe 2025 and 0.92 on WMT-22.
- Fine-tuning significantly lifts minority-class (ERR/BAD) F1, reaching up to 0.96, thereby improving recall of critical errors.
- Decoder-only LLMs consistently surpass encoder-only baselines across datasets, demonstrating superior contextual reasoning for CED.
- Committee voting stabilizes predictions and reduces variance, particularly on noisier datasets like WMT-21.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Fine-tuning internalizes abstract decision boundaries
**Claim:** Fine-tuning instruction-tuned LLMs on CED data significantly improves detection of minority-class critical errors (ERR/BAD) by internalizing abstract decision boundaries that instruction prompts alone cannot reliably specify.  
**Mechanism:** Parameter-efficient LoRA adaptation updates model weights on ~170k labeled translation pairs, allowing the model to learn nuanced, task-specific patterns of meaning distortion beyond its pre-trained general instruction-following capabilities.  
**Core assumption:** The model’s pre-trained knowledge contains relevant semantic reasoning primitives, but requires supervised adjustment to apply them consistently to the CED decision boundary, especially for rare error types.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states fine-tuning improves ERR recall; Section 5.3 notes gains are most pronounced for the minority class.  
**Break condition:** If fine-tuning data lacks diversity in error types or contains systematic labeling noise, the model may overfit to spurious correlations, degrading ERR recall on out-of-distribution errors.

### Mechanism 2: Committee voting reduces stochastic variance
**Claim:** Committee voting (majority vote over 3 stochastic generations) stabilizes predictions and mitigates individual model flips, particularly on noisier datasets like WMT-21.  
**Mechanism:** By sampling multiple outputs (temperature t=0.2) per instance and taking a majority vote, the ensemble reduces variance from the model’s probabilistic decoding. Consistent errors across diverse samples are less likely, smoothing decision boundaries.  
**Core assumption:** Individual model errors on CED are somewhat independent and random given prompt stochasticity; thus, majority vote will correct a subset of singleton mistakes.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5.3 reports voting mitigates variance and corrects flips; Table 3 shows ensembles often yield higher MCC than single-model runs.  
**Break condition:** If all models in the committee share a systematic bias (e.g., prompt-induced blind spot for a specific error category), voting will amplify rather than mitigate the error.

### Mechanism 3: Decoder architecture enables contextual reasoning
**Claim:** Decoder-only LLMs outperform encoder-only baselines because their generative, auto-regressive architecture and instruction tuning enable contextual, discourse-level reasoning necessary to detect subtle meaning distortions.  
**Mechanism:** Decoder LLMs generate tokens conditioned on full left and (via attention) right context, and their instruction tuning aligns them to follow complex, multi-criteria definitions. This allows holistic synthesis of source and translation semantics, whereas encoder-only models produce fixed contextual embeddings that may not capture long-range cross-lingual dependencies or pragmatic shifts.  
**Core assumption:** Critical meaning errors often require non-local reasoning across the entire sentence (e.g., negation scope, numeric consistency), which decoder self-attention and next-token prediction objectives are better suited to model than encoder-based sentence-pair classification.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.2 highlight decoder advantage; Section 6 tables show top decoder models exceed best encoder (XLM-R-large) on MCC, especially on WMT-21.  
**Break condition:** For very short sentences with isolated lexical errors, encoder models might suffice; decoder advantage diminishes if the error is surface-level and does not require deep contextual synthesis.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Critical Error Detection (CED)**  
  Why needed: The entire study redefines MT quality assessment from aggregate scores to binary detection of meaning-critical failures. Understanding this precise task definition is essential to interpret all results—e.g., why NOT/OK includes minor grammatical errors.  
  Quick check: "Would a translation with correct named entities but flipped sentiment polarity be labeled ERR or NOT? Justify using the paper's error categories."

- **Concept: Instruction Tuning & Prompt Sensitivity**  
  Why needed: Model performance varies drastically across P0-P4 prompts. Success depends on the model's ability to parse task instructions and adhere to label semantics. Misalignment causes "instruction underfitting."  
  Quick check: "Why might a larger model like LLaMA-3.3-70B show a performance drop from P1 (few-shot) to P3 (tuned prompt), while a smaller model like LLaMA-3.1-8B improves?"

- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)**  
  Why needed: The paper's key result—fine-tuned LLMs achieving >0.9 MCC—relies on LoRA. Understanding that only a small subset of weights are adapted explains cost-effectiveness and avoids overfitting assumptions.  
  Quick check: "If LoRA updates only attention layer projections, what does this imply about what the model learns during CED fine-tuning—new factual knowledge or new decision patterns on existing knowledge?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (S, T) -> Prompt Engine (applies P0-P4 template) -> Model Backend (encoder or decoder) -> Adaptation Module (frozen or LoRA) -> Voting Committee (optional; 3 samples, majority vote) -> Scorer (MCC, F1).

**Critical path for replication:**  
1. Replicate encoder-only baselines (Table 1) to establish a performance floor.  
2. Run zero-shot LLM evaluation (P0) on a subset to observe raw instruction-following gap.  
3. Implement LoRA fine-tuning on a single model (e.g., GPT-OSS-20B) using the unified 170k corpus and validate on SynCED dev set.  
4. Add committee voting to assess variance reduction.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Cost vs. performance: GPT-4o-mini API fine-tuning is expensive but top results; LLaMA-3.1-8B LoRA cheaper but slightly lower MCC; committee voting triples inference cost.  
- Prompt specificity: Tuned prompts help smaller models but may overfit large ones; a generic "strict classifier" prompt (P2) is a safer default.  
- Dataset balance: SynCED is balanced; WMT-22 is extremely imbalanced (~94% NOT). Use class-weighted loss in fine-tuning to avoid majority-class bias.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low ERR Recall (<0.5 F1): Model is lenient; try better prompt (P2-P4) or fine-tuning.  
- High variance across committee: Stochastic decoding too diverse; reduce temperature to 0.1 or use deterministic decoding.  
- Prompt overfitting: Performance drops from few-shot to tuned prompt for large models; revert to simpler instruction.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate Table 1: Train linear probe on XLM-R-large with WMT-22 train/dev; verify MCC ~0.87.  
2. Zero-shot LLM probe: Run GPT-4o-mini with P0 on 100 SynCED dev samples; expect MCC ~0.92.  
3. Mini-LoRA run: Fine-tune LLaMA-3.1-8B on 1k balanced SynCED train samples for 1 epoch; ERR F1 should improve from ~0.78 to >0.85.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Document-level CED**: How can models be scaled to capture cross-sentence meaning errors? Current evaluation is sentence-level; document-level errors involve discourse coherence, coreference, and consistency.  
2. **Confidence calibration**: What methods can provide reliable per-instance confidence scores beyond majority voting? Committee voting lacks interpretability for selective deployment.  
3. **Cross-lingual generalization**: To what extent do EN→DE trained models generalize to other language pairs, especially low-resource or typologically diverse ones?  
4. **Prompt optimization across scales**: Why does prompt tuning degrade performance for larger instruction-tuned LLMs? The mechanism of instruction interference is unclear.  
5. **Robustness to natural errors**: How robust are models trained on synthetic/curated datasets (e.g., SynCED) when applied to naturally occurring translation errors with noisy labels and real-world distributions?

## Limitations
- Exact LoRA hyperparameters for local fine-tuning (rank, alpha, dropout, epochs) are unreported, hindering replication.  
- OpenAI API fine-tuning details for GPT-4o-mini (method, hyperparameters, cost) are opaque.  
- Committee voting implementation (self-ensemble vs. cross-model) is ambiguous, and its robustness to shared biases is untested.  
- Evaluation is confined to EN→DE; cross-lingual and domain generalization remain unknown.

## Confidence
- Fine-tuning & scaling yield robust performance gains: **High** (consistent improvements across models and datasets).  
- Committee voting stabilizes predictions: **Medium** (empirical support but implementation details lacking).  
- Decoder LLMs outperform encoder-only baselines: **High** (clear superiority in MCC and minority-class F1).
