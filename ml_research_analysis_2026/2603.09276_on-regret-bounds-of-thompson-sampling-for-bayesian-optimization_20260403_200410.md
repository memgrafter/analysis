# On Regret Bounds of Thompson Sampling for Bayesian Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.09276
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09276
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Improves high-probability and expected regret bounds for GP-TS, narrowing the theoretical gap with GP-UCB.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the regret guarantees of Gaussian Process Thompson Sampling (GP-TS) in Bayesian optimization. While GP-TS is widely used, its theoretical lags behind GP-UCB, notably in high-probability bounds that depend polynomially on the failure probability δ and the absence of lenient regret bounds. The authors provide a refined analysis under the Bayesian setting where the unknown objective is a sample from a GP prior. Key contributions include: a lower bound showing GP-TS can suffer Ω(1/δᶜ) regret with probability δ; an improved high‑probability bound O(√(T γ_T log T / δ)) by analyzing the second moment of cumulative regret; the first expected lenient regret bounds that are polylogarithmic in T; and an enhanced Õ(√T) high‑probability bound for squared exponential and Matérn (ν>2) kernels by relaxing smoothness constraints. These results significantly narrow the gap between GP‑TS and GP‑UCB.

## Method Summary
The paper conducts a theoretical analysis of GP‑TS, focusing on cumulative regret in the Bayesian setting. The methodology builds on standard GP posterior updates and Thompson sampling, but introduces refined analytical tools: a Cauchy‑Schwarz decomposition exploiting the symmetry between the posterior sample used for selection and the sample at the optimum; a potential‑type lemma for expected lenient regret that avoids Azuma‑Hoeffding; and an adaptive tolerance scheme that partitions rounds into tiers with decreasing regret thresholds to handle kernels with ν>2. Central to the analysis is the maximum information gain (MIG) and its concave interpolation, which controls the sum of posterior variances. The authors also derive a matching lower bound via a reduction to a two‑armed correlated Gaussian bandit.

## Key Results
- **Lower bound**: GP‑TS can suffer Ω(1/δᶜ) cumulative regret with probability δ, indicating inherent polynomial dependence on 1/δ.
- **High‑probability upper bound**: Cumulative regret is O(√(T γ_T log T / δ)) with probability at least 1−δ, improving the δ‑dependence from O(1/δ) to O(1/√δ).
- **Expected lenient and improved bounds**: First expected lenient regret bounds that are polylogarithmic in T; for ν>2 Matérn and squared exponential kernels, a high‑probability Õ(√T) cumulative regret bound is achieved by relaxing the smoothness condition.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Second Moment Concentration via Cauchy-Schwarz Decomposition
- **Claim**: The cumulative regret R_T of GP-TS concentrates as R_T = O(√(T γ_T log T / δ)) with probability 1−δ, tightening the best-known O(1/δ) dependence to O(1/√δ).
- **Mechanism**: The analysis decomposes E[R_T^2] by expanding (f(x^*) - f(x_t))^2 using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Given D_{t-1}, x^* and x_t are identically distributed and mutually independent. This symmetry separates the expectation into terms involving only f(x^*) and f(x_t), yielding E[R_T^2] ≤ O(T β_T γ_T) when bounding expectations of (f(x^*)-U_t(x^*))_+^2 and (μ_{t-1}(x_t)-f(x_t))^2 separately. Markov’s inequality then gives the high‑probability bound with 1/√δ dependence.
- **Core assumption**: Assumption 2.1 (GP prior) plus either finite |𝓧| or Assumption 2.2 (Lipschitz sample paths) to handle discretization.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract] “an upper bound on the second moment of cumulative regret yielding a high‑probability bound O(√(T γ_T log T / δ))”  
  - [section 3.2] “the proof for the upper bound of the second moment mostly follows the existing analysis [Russo and Van Roy, 2014, Takeno et al., 2024]”
- **Break condition**: Fails if (x^* | D_{t-1}) ⟂ (x_t | D_{t-1}) is broken, e.g., if GP‑TS is modified to break posterior sampling symmetry (greedy exploitation).

### Mechanism 2: Expected Lenient Regret via Potential Lemma
- **Claim**: The expected number of “bad” rounds |𝓣| = |{ t : f(x^*) - f(x_t) ≥ Δ }| satisfies E[|𝓣|] = Õ(β_T γ̃_{T_max} / Δ^2), yielding E[LRT] = Õ(√(β_T T_max γ̃_{T_max})).
- **Mechanism**: For 𝓣, use 1 ≤ (f(x^*)-f(x_t))/Δ, hence E[|𝓣|] ≤ (1/Δ^2) Σ_{t∈𝓣} E[(f(x^*)-f(x_t))^2]. Decompose f(x^*)-f(x_t) via g_t(x_t)-g_t(x^*) and credible intervals: |f(x^*)-f(x_t)| ≤ 2β_t^{1/2}σ_{t-1}(x^*) + 2β_t^{1/2}σ_{t-1}(x_t) under a high‑probability event. This reduces to bounding E[Σ_{t∈𝓣} σ_{t-1}^2(·)], controlled by the concave interpolated MIG γ̃ via Lemma E.7 and submodularity of MIG. The result avoids Azuma‑Hoeffding, directly giving expected bounds.
- **Core assumption**: Assumption 2.1 plus either finite |𝓧| or Assumption 2.2; β_t set as 2log(2|𝓧|T^2t(t+1)/δ) for high‑probability credible intervals.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [section 3.3] “we employ a proof inspired by that of the elliptical potential count lemma … we leverage the inequality |𝓣| ≤ Σ_{t∈𝓣} (f(x^*)-f(x_t))^2/Δ^2”  
  - [lemma E.7] E[Σ_{t∈𝓣} σ_{t-1}^2(x_t)] ≤ C_1 γ̃ E[|𝓣|]
- **Break condition**: Requires MIG submodularity; breaks for kernels with supermodular information gains or if β_t decay is too slow.

### Mechanism 3: Relaxed Smoothness via Adaptive Tolerance
- **Claim**: With ν > 2 for Matérn kernels, cumulative regret is Õ(√T) by repeatedly applying a lenient‑style analysis with adaptive regret tolerances Δ_i, whereas prior work required the stricter condition 2ν + d ≤ ν^2.
- **Mechanism**: Partition rounds by decreasing regret thresholds Δ_{i+1} < Δ_i, where Δ_1 = T^{-ν/(2(ν+d))}. For each tier i, the lenient analysis template forces x_t into a local ball B(q c_{quad}^{-1} Δ_i; x^*), whose MIG scales as Δ_i^{ν d/(2(ν+d))} |T_i|^{d/(2ν+d)} log^{4ν+d/(2ν+d)} |T_i|. Summing across tiers yields Õ(√T) if Δ_i decay sufficiently fast, which holds for ν > 2 via geometric series arguments. The key relaxation comes from avoiding the 2ν+d ≤ ν^2 condition through repeated Vietoris‑Rips type covering arguments over shrinking balls.
- **Core assumption**: Assumption 2.2 and Lemma 2.4 (unique maximizer, sup‑norm bound, local quadratic upper bound); ν > 2 ensures sufficiently strong MIG scaling.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract] “an improved high‑probability cumulative regret bound Õ(√T) for squared exponential and Matérn (ν>2) kernels by relaxing a smoothness condition”  
  - [section 3.4] “we repeatedly apply the arguments like the lenient regret upper bound and the property that the MIG over B(ρ; x^*) becomes small in proportion to ρ”  
  - [lemma 3.4] explicit bound transformations for SE and Matérn kernels
- **Break condition**: Fails if ν ≤ 2, as the series Σ_i Δ_i^{ν/(2ν)} does not converge fast enough to √T. Also fails if the sample path lacks the local quadratic property (condition 3 of Lemma 2.4).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Posterior variance and maximum information gain (MIG)  
  - Why needed here: Cumulative regret bounds rely on Σ_t σ_{t-1}^2(x_t) ≤ C_1 γ_T (Srinivas et al., 2010); second moments require bounding E[σ_{t-1}^2(x)]; lenient analysis needs γ̃ concavity to handle expected quantities.  
  - Quick check question: For a Matérn‑3/2 kernel on [0,1]^d, what is the scaling of γ_T with T?  
    Answer: γ_T = O(T^{1/2}) per Vakili et al., 2021a; the paper uses coarser bounds but retains sublinearity.

- **Concept**: Identical distribution of (x^* | D_{t-1}) and (x_t | D_{t-1}) in GP‑TS  
  - Why needed here: Mechanism 1’s decomposition relies on E[σ_{t-1}^2(x^*)] = E[σ_{t-1}^2(x_t)]; Mechanism 2 uses the symmetric coupling to map bad rounds under f to bad rounds under g_t.  
  - Quick check question: Is the equality of distributions valid if the GP prior mean is nonzero?  
    Answer: Yes, because Thompson sampling draws g_t ~ p(f|D_{t-1}) and maximizes it; the optimal x^* is fixed, but f(x^*) has the same marginal distribution as f(x_t) given D_{t-1} due to stationarity of the GP.

- **Concept**: Concave interpolation γ̃_t of MIG  
  - Why needed here: In expected lenient analysis (Lemma E.7), concavity allows Jensen: E[γ̃_|𝓣|] ≤ γ̃_{E[|𝓣|]}; this is subtle because 𝓣 is random but γ̃ is concave in its subscript.  
  - Quick check question: Why not use γ_t directly?  
    Answer: γ_t is only submodular and defined at integers; concave γ̃ enables smoothing for expected quantities.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  1. GP posterior (mean μ_{t-1}, variance σ_{t-1}^2) update per Rasmussen & Williams (2005)
  2. Thompson sampling: draw g_t ∼ GP(μ_{t-1}, σ_{t-1}^2), select x_t = argmax_x g_t(x)
  3. Theoretical analysis modules:
     a. MIG properties: submodularity → Σ_t σ_{t-1}^2(x_t) ≤ γ_T
     b. Credible interval bounds: Pr(|f(x) - μ_{t-1}(x)| > β_t^{1/2}σ_{t-1}(x)) ≤ δ_t via Borell‑TIS or union bound
     c. Discretization lemma: sup_{x∈𝓧} |f(x)-f([x]_τ)| ≤ 1/τ under Assumption 2.2 (Lipschitz sample paths)
     d. Potential count lemma: E[|𝓣|] ≤ O(β_T γ̃_{T_max}/Δ^2)

- **Critical path**:
  1. Establish posterior variance bounds: Σ_t σ_{t-1}^2(x_t) ≤ C_1 γ_T
  2. Build E[Σ_t (f(x^*) - U_t(x^*))^2] using (a)_+ moment lemma (Lemma E.5)
  3. For lenient analysis: Condition on credible intervals; decompose f(x^*)-f(x_t) via g_t bounds; apply Jensen on concave γ̃
  4. For Õ(√T) on Matérn: Set Δ_1, apply Lemma 2.4 to localize x_t, use Corollary 8 of Iwazaki (2025b) for MIG scaling in balls, sum geometric series

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - β_t choice: Logarithmic in t and |𝓧| (or d) gives high‑probability bounds; paper uses β_t = O(log t) but acknowledges that suboptimal δ‑dependence (Theorem 3.1) is inherent, not an artifact of β_t scaling.
  - Discretization vs. exact GP sampling: Analysis discretizes continuous 𝓧 to avoid measure‑theoretic issues; introduces O(1/t) discretization error (Lemma E.4). Alternative is using covering arguments directly on continuous space but requires more care with sets of measure zero.
  - Confidence parameter inflation: GP‑TS typically uses β_t ∼ log t like GP‑UCB, but the lower bound suggests that any polynomial‑in‑1/δ concentration cannot be improved to logarithmic without changing the algorithm (e.g., variance inflation as in Chowdhury & Gopalan 2017, which the paper notes as future work).

- **Failure signatures**:
  - When ν ≤ 2 for Matérn kernels, the local MIG scaling becomes too weak, and the tiered analysis fails to achieve Õ(√T); the bound degrades to polynomial in T.
  - If the global maximizer is not unique (violates Lemma 2.4(1)), the localization argument for small Δ_i fails because x_t may not converge to a single point.
  - If σ_t^2(x) is not uniformly bounded by 1 (Assumption 2.1 kernel bound), the variance sum bound γ_T may not hold; the constants C_1 in the regret bounds would change.
  - When observations are heteroscedastic or non‑Gaussian noise, the posterior variance decomposition and MIG definitions do not directly apply; new analysis needed.

- **First 3 experiments for a new engineer to validate implementation**:
  1. Reproduce the simple two‑armed lower bound (Theorem 3.1) by simulating GP‑TS on the correlated Gaussian arms, verify that for fixed δ the regret scales as Ω(1/δ^c) across δ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 0.2}.
  2. Test the second moment bound numerically: Run GP‑TS on SE kernel, compute empirical E[R_T^2] for T=100, check it scales as O(T log T) rather than O(T/δ^2).
  3. Validate the adaptive tolerance (Matérn ν>2) by simulating for ν=2.5, d=2, compare achieved regret to O(√T) and confirm it is tighter than prior O(√(T γ_T log(T/δ) / δ) from naive Markov.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What is the optimal dependence on the failure probability δ in high‑probability regret bounds for Thompson Sampling in Bayesian optimization? Specifically, can the O(√(Tγ_T log T / δ)) upper bound be improved to O(log(1/δ)) or is polynomial dependence inherent?
2. Does incorporating variance inflation into GP‑TS lead to improved high‑probability regret bounds with better dependence on δ, similar to known results in the frequentist setting? Can the analysis be extended to obtain polylogarithmic lenient regret bounds?
3. For Matérn kernels, is the condition ν > 2 necessary for achieving ~O(√T) cumulative regret bounds, or can the condition be relaxed further? Specifically, can results be obtained for ν ≤ 2 (e.g., ν = 1/2 or 3/2) with different regularity assumptions?
4. Can the refined analysis techniques (e.g., second moment bounds, lenient regret) be generalized to other randomized Bayesian optimization algorithms with sublinear regret guarantees, such as those based on predictive entropy search or knowledge gradient?
5. How can the regret analysis for GP‑TS be extended to more complex problem settings, such as multi‑fidelity, multi‑objective, constrained, or parallel Bayesian optimization, to provide theoretical guarantees in these practically relevant scenarios?

## Limitations
- High‑probability bounds still scale polynomially with 1/δ, whereas GP‑UCB achieves logarithmic dependence.
- The improved Õ(√T) bound requires kernel smoothness ν > 2, excluding many popular Matérn kernels (e.g., ν = 1.5).
- Theoretical analysis relies on idealized assumptions: unique global maximizer, noiseless or well‑behaved Gaussian noise, and exact GP sampling; practical approximations may violate these.

## Confidence

- Second moment bound (Theorem 3.2): Medium confidence.
- Expected lenient regret (Theorem 3.3): Medium confidence.
