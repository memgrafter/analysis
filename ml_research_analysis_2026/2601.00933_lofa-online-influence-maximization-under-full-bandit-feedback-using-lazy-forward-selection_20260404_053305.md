# LOFA: Online Influence Maximization under Full-Bandit Feedback using Lazy Forward Selection

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2601.00933
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00933
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** LOFA achieves lower cumulative regret and higher cumulative influence than ETCG and DART on a 534‑node Facebook network for budgets *k*=4,8,16 under full‑bandit feedback.

## Executive Summary

Influence maximization is the task of selecting a small set of seed nodes to maximize information spread in a network. In the online full‑bandit feedback setting, the learner repeatedly chooses seed sets and only observes the total influence of the chosen set, without knowledge of the network structure or edge activation probabilities. This paper introduces the Lazy Online Forward Algorithm (LOFA), which adapts the classic greedy algorithm for monotone submodular maximization to the bandit setting. LOFA exploits the diminishing returns property of submodularity to lazily recompute marginal gains only for the current top candidate in a priority queue, thereby drastically reducing the number of required diffusion simulations. The exploration schedule is theoretically grounded, choosing the number of plays per candidate to minimize regret. Experiments on a real Facebook network demonstrate that LOFA consistently outperforms state‑of‑the‑art baselines (ETCG and DART) in terms of cumulative regret and influence reward for various cardinality budgets, while also being computationally more efficient.

## Method Summary

LOFA constructs the seed set in a greedy, phased manner over the horizon *T*. In each phase *i* (1 ≤ *i* ≤ *k*), the algorithm maintains a max‑heap of all candidate nodes keyed by their estimated marginal gain Δ̂_u(S) with respect to the current partial seed set *S*. The top node is extracted. If this node was also the top node in the previous iteration, it is immediately added to *S*. Otherwise, due to submodularity, its marginal gain cannot have increased since the last evaluation; therefore LOFA recomputes its marginal gain (by simulating *m* independent cascades) to check whether it remains optimal. After updating its estimate, the node is reinserted into the heap; the process repeats until the true top node is identified. The parameter *m*, the number of plays per evaluation, is set to a closed‑form expression derived from minimizing the regret bound for stochastic submodular bandits. Once *k* nodes have been selected, LOFA exploits the final seed set for all remaining rounds. This lazy evaluation avoids unnecessary recomputation for all but the top candidate, leading to substantial savings in the number of diffusion simulations while preserving near‑optimality.

## Key Results

- LOFA achieves lower cumulative regret compared to ETCG and DART for cardinality budgets *k*=4, 8, and 16.
- LOFA attains higher cumulative influence reward over the same horizon.
- The lazy mechanism drastically reduces redundant marginal gain computations, resulting in improved computational efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LOFA reduces unnecessary marginal gain recomputations by exploiting submodularity, only re‑evaluating the current top candidate in the priority queue.
- **Mechanism:** The algorithm maintains a max‑heap keyed by estimated marginal gain. When selecting a node *u*, if *u* was not the best candidate in the previous iteration, its marginal gain can only have decreased (or stayed the same) due to submodularity's diminishing returns property. Thus, *u*’s prior estimate remains valid without recomputation. Only when *u* *was* the previous best (*u*.prev_best == last_seed) is its marginal gain recomputed to check if it remains optimal.
- **Core assumption:** The influence function under the IC model is monotone submodular.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.1] States the IC model and references Kempe et al. (2003) for submodularity proof.
  - [section 3] Describes the lazy evaluation logic: "If a node *u* was not the best candidate in the previous iteration, its marginal gain in the current iteration cannot exceed its previous marginal gain (due to submodularity)."
  - [corpus: weak] Corpus neighbors discuss submodular bandits but do not directly anchor LOFA's specific lazy mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If the reward function is not submodular, the "lazy" invalidation premise fails, and the heap ordering may become incorrect, leading to suboptimal selections.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LOFA uses a theoretically grounded exploration schedule (*m* plays per candidate) to balance learning and committing, minimizing regret.
- **Mechanism:** During each "phase" *i* (adding the *i*‑th node), each candidate node *u* is played *m* times to estimate its marginal gain Δ_u(S). The value *m* is set to ⌈( *T* / √(2 log(*T*) ( *n* + 2*n*k*√(2 log(*T*) ) ) )^(2/3)⌉, derived from minimizing the regret bound for stochastic submodular bandits (Nie et al., 2022). This ensures sufficient exploration per phase without excessive plays.
- **Core assumption:** Rewards are stochastic with bounded support ([0,1]) and independent across rounds; submodularity holds in expectation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3] Explicitly states: "We choose *m* to be this number as this is the number that minimizes the regret as shown by Nie et al. (2022)."
  - [section 2.2] Formalizes the stochastic full‑bandit feedback model and regret definition.
  - [corpus: indirect] Neighbor "Stochastic *k*-Submodular Bandits with Full Bandit Feedback" develops α‑regret bounds, supporting the theoretical grounding of exploration schedules.
- **Break condition:** If reward distributions are non‑stationary or heavy‑tailed (violating boundedness), the fixed *m* schedule may under‑explore or waste budget.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** LOFA employs a greedy, phased construction of the seed set (one node per phase) until the cardinality constraint *k* is met, after which it exploits the final set.
- **Mechanism:** Starting with *S*^(0)=∅, the algorithm runs *k* phases. In phase *i*, it identifies the node with highest estimated marginal gain to *S*^(*i*‑1) using the lazy mechanism, adds it to the seed set, and clears the queue for the next phase. After *k* selections, it plays the final seed set *S* for all remaining rounds. This mirrors the offline greedy algorithm but with bandit‑driven estimates.
- **Core assumption:** A (1‑1/e)-approximation greedy solution exists for the offline problem; the online process converges to a near‑optimal set after *k* phases.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Mentions "lazily recompute marginal gains only for the top candidate in a priority queue" and "adds one node to the selected set… greedily until the cardinality constraint is satisfied."
  - [section 3] Pseudocode shows the *for* phase *i* in {1,…,*k*} loop, adding one node per phase.
  - [section 1.1] References Nemhauser et al. (1978) for the greedy (1‑1/e) approximation guarantee for monotone submodular maximization.
- **Break condition:** If the optimal seed set changes over time (non‑stationary network or diffusion probabilities), the greedy construction of a single fixed set becomes suboptimal.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Submodularity (Diminishing Returns)**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire lazy evaluation hinges on the property that adding a node *v* to a smaller set yields at least as much marginal gain as adding it to a larger set (σ(A∪{v})−σ(A) ≥ σ(B∪{v})−σ(B) for A⊆B). This allows LOFA to safely skip recomputation for non‑top nodes.
  - *Quick check question:* If *f*(S)=|S| (linear), is it submodular? How would LOFA's lazy updates behave?

- **Concept: Full‑Bandit Feedback**
  - *Why needed here:* Defines the information constraint: only the total spread *f*(S_t) of the chosen seed set is observed, not individual edge activations or node‑level feedback. This forces learning via whole‑set plays.
  - *Quick check question:* In full‑bandit IM, can you directly observe the marginal gain of a single node? Why does this necessitate exploring whole sets?

- **Concept: Greedy Algorithm for Submodular Maximization**
  - *Why needed here:* LOFA's phased structure is an online, bandit adaptation of the offline greedy algorithm (add the node with highest marginal gain at each step). Understanding the offline baseline (1‑1/e‑approx) is key to defining regret.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the worst‑case approximation ratio of the greedy algorithm for monotone submodular maximization under a cardinality constraint?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Priority Queue (Max‑Heap) → Marginal Gain Estimators → Phase Manager → Playback Simulator
- **Critical path:** The core loop (Algorithm 1, lines after “for phase i…”) where the top node *u* is popped from the heap. The if‑else decides: (a) if *u* was top recently, add it; (b) else, recompute *u*.mg1 (and *u*.mg2 if needed), update heap, and reinsert. This lazy decision is the performance‑critical path.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Lazy vs. Full Recompute:* LOFA trades off potential suboptimality in marginal gain ordering (by not updating all nodes every phase) for massive savings in number of diffusion simulations. This is beneficial when *n* is large and *k* is small.
  - *Fixed *m* per phase:* Simplicity and theoretical grounding vs. adaptivity. A fixed *m* may over‑explore easy nodes or under‑explore uncertain ones compared to a fully adaptive schedule.
  - *Single fixed seed set:* Simplicity vs. non‑stationarity. Committing to one *S* for the tail of the horizon is optimal only if the environment is stationary.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *High regret with increasing *n* but fixed *T*:* If lazy updates cause persistent misordering, the selected *S* may be far from greedy‑optimal.
  - *Step‑like reward curves (as in Figures 1‑3):* Indicates distinct exploration→exploitation transitions. If steps are too large/frequent, exploration is inefficient.
  - *DART outperforms at large *k* (Figure 6):* As noted, DART’s exploration scales with chosen set size; LOFA’s fixed *m* per phase may under‑explore when *k* is large.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Comparison:** Run LOFA, ETCG, DART on the Facebook network (534 nodes) for *k*=4,8,16 and *T*=10^5. Measure cumulative regret and instantaneous reward curves. Expect LOFA to show steeper reward increases and lower regret than ETCG for small *k*.
  2. **Ablation on Lazy Mechanism:** Implement a “non‑lazy” variant that recomputes all marginal gains every phase. Compare number of diffusion simulations and final reward. Hypothesis: Non‑lazy uses ~O(*nk*) more plays, with diminishing returns on reward improvement.
  3. **Sensitivity to *m*:* Vary *m* around the theoretically derived value. Plot final cumulative reward vs. *m*. Expected: A U‑shaped curve; too small *m* leads to poor estimates, too large *m* wastes exploration budget.

## Limitations

- The exact formula for the exploration parameter *m* is obscured by typographical errors, requiring verification against the original source.
