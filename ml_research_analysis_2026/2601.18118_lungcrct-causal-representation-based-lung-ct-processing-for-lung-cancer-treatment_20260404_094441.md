# LungCRCT: Causal Representation based Lung CT Processing for Lung Cancer Treatment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.18118
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18118
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Achieves structural Hamming distance of 2.0 for causal graph recovery and 93.91% AUC, 91.67% accuracy, 90.84% macro F1-score for malignancy classification using a lightweight downstream model.

## Executive Summary
Lung cancer treatment analysis from CT scans often relies on deep learning models that lack interpretability and causal reasoning. LungCRCT introduces a causal representation learning framework that disentangles visual features into causal and non-causal subspaces, discovers a structural causal model among latent factors (tumor, angiogenesis, lymph node enlargement), and refines the decoder for sensitive interventions. The framework combines a Convolutional VAE, a graph autoencoder with DAG constraints, and an entropy-based refinement block. On lung CT data, it recovers a causal graph with structural Hamming distance 2.0 and achieves 93.91% AUC for malignant/non-malignant classification using only the extracted causal features with a lightweight model. This demonstrates the feasibility of interpretable, causal-driven lung cancer analysis from imaging.

## Method Summary
LungCRCT processes a 180x180 cropped lung CT scan and its cancer label. A Convolutional VAE encodes the image into an 8-dimensional latent vector Z, which is split into a 5-dimensional non-causal subspace Z_ncs and a 3-dimensional causal subspace Z_cs. The framework optimizes four losses: (1) Standard VAE reconstruction loss ℒ1; (2) Graph autoencoder loss ℒ2 that fits an adjacency matrix A under a log-determinant DAG constraint, using the concatenated vector Q_cs = [Z_cs, Y]; (3) Disentanglement loss ℒ3 based on distance correlation (dCor) that maximizes dependence between Z_cs and Y while minimizing dependence between Z_ncs and Y; (4) Entropy loss ℒ4 that ensures structured image changes when individual causal factors are perturbed. The total loss trains the CVAE and GAE jointly. After training, the causal features Z_cs are fixed and used to train a separate lightweight classifier for malignancy prediction. The design yields interpretable causal representations and interventions while maintaining high classification accuracy.

## Key Results
- Structural Hamming distance (SHD) of 2.0 between learned and ground truth causal graphs.
- Downstream malignant/non-malignant classification: AUC 93.91%, accuracy 91.67%, macro F1-score 90.84%.
- Extremely lightweight downstream model using only causal latent features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Distance correlation (dCor)-based disentanglement separates lung cancer-relevant causal visual features from irrelevant ones (e.g., scan angle, patient posture) in the latent space.
- **Mechanism:** After CVAE encoding produces latent space **Z**, it is split into causal subspace **Z_cs** and non-causal subspace **Z_ncs**. The loss **ℒ3** minimizes dCor between **Z_ncs** and label **Y** while maximizing dCor between **Z_cs** and **Y**, using a weighting parameter **τ**. This encourages **Z_cs** to contain features predictive of cancer status and **Z_ncs** to contain those that are not.
- **Core assumption:** dCor effectively captures non-linear dependencies between latent features and the cancer label, allowing for feature-wise separation based on informativeness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "distance Correlation(dCor) measures were applied within CVAE encoder training steps" for "lung cancer related/non-related visual information" disentanglement.
  - [section III.B.2]: Detailed definition of **ℒ3** using dCor, the goal to "enhance the possibility of splitting causal information into **Z_cs** and non-causal information... into **Z_ncs**".
  - [corpus]: Neighboring papers focus on classification accuracy (e.g., LECNN's 94.98% accuracy) without representation disentanglement, so direct comparative evidence is absent. The mechanism's novelty is its focus on causal separation, not just predictive performance.
- **Break condition:** If the mutual information (MI) between **Z_ncs** and **Y** remains high after training, indicating failure to remove cancer-related signals from the non-causal subspace. The paper reports mean MI(**Z_ncs**,Y)=0.008 vs. mean MI(**Z_cs**,Y)=1.1018 as success.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** An advanced graph autoencoder (GAE) with a log-determinant DAG constraint learns the non-linear structural causal model (SCM) linking latent causal factors (**Z_cs**) corresponding to tumor, angiogenesis, and lymph node enlargement.
- **Mechanism:** The GAE takes the concatenated input **Q_cs = <Z_cs, Y>**. Its encoder-decoder structure (**f1**, **f2**) and trainable adjacency matrix **A** are optimized under loss **ℒ2**. This loss combines reconstruction error of **Q_cs**, an L1 penalty on **A**, and an acyclicity penalty **h(A)** based on log-determinant (DAGMA). The weak supervision from **Y** guides discovery toward the physical causal graph.
- **Core assumption:** The true physical causal mechanism (Fig 1: mutations → tumor → angiogenesis → lymph nodes) can be faithfully represented by a DAG over a low-dimensional latent space (**Z_cs**) extracted from CT images.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "graph autoencoder based causal discovery algorithms" are used to retrieve "valid latent causal representations of physical causal factors."
  - [section III.B.1]: Equations (4)-(9) define the GAE loss **ℒ2** and the DAG constraint **h(A)**. Explicitly states the use of DAGMA's log-det constraint for better cycle detection.
  - [section IV.A]: Reports a Structural Hamming Distance (SHD) of 2.0 between the binarized learned adjacency matrix and the ground truth DAG from Fig 1, indicating high topological similarity.
- **Break condition:** If the learned adjacency matrix **A** does not converge to an acyclic structure (high persistent **h(A)**) or if SHD against the known ground truth DAG remains large (e.g., >5), indicating the GAE failed to recover the correct causal links.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** An entropy-based latent variation block refines the CVAE decoder to ensure it generates interpretable, non-random image changes when individual causal latent factors are varied, enabling qualitative causal intervention analysis.
- **Mechanism:** For each causal feature **Z_cs,i**, a small 1-sigma variation **ς_i** is applied, creating **Z_cs,i'**. The decoder **G** generates two images: **G(Z)** and **G(Z')**. The pixel-wise squared difference **D_i** between these images is computed. Shannon's entropy **H(D_i)** is estimated (via histogram) and averaged across features as loss **ℒ4**. Low **H(D_i)** indicates structured, meaningful changes; high entropy would mean random noise.
- **Core assumption:** Meaningful, physiologically plausible visual changes in the reconstructed CT (e.g., tumor size change when varying the tumor factor) will produce lower entropy in the difference map compared to random noise.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "entropy-based block for decoder refinement" to enable "sensitive qualitative intervention analysis."
  - [section III.B.3]: Equations (15)-(16) define the variation process and **ℒ4**. States that if visual transformations match tissue changes, "estimated entropy values would be far less than maximum entropy values."
  - [section IV.A]: Reports **ℒ4 = 0.3003**, which is "significantly lower than maximum entropy (approx. 2.322 with 5 bins)", indicating successful decoder sensitivity.
- **Break condition:** If **ℒ4** remains near the maximum possible entropy for the chosen binning (e.g., >2.0 for 5 bins), meaning that varying a causal factor produces imagery indistinguishable from random noise, invalidating visual intervention analysis.

## Foundational Learning
### Variational Autoencoder (VAE)
- **Why needed here:** The core encoder (**f1**) is a Convolutional VAE. Understanding the reparameterization trick, ELBO loss (**ℒ1**), and the trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and latent space regularization is essential to grasp how latent space **Z** is initially formed.
- **Quick check question:** What is the purpose of the KL divergence term in the VAE loss **ℒ1**, and how does it affect the learned latent space?

### Structural Causal Models (SCM) & DAGs
- **Why needed here:** The entire causal discovery objective (**ℒ2**) assumes the latent causal factors obey a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) as per Pearl's SCM. This defines the "physical causal mechanism" (Fig 1) they aim to recover.
- **Quick check question:** Why must the adjacency matrix **A** in equation (2) satisfy acyclicity (no cycles), and what does a cycle imply about the causal assumptions?

### Distance Correlation (dCor)
- **Why needed here:** It is the primary tool (**ℒ3**) for feature-wise disentanglement, replacing Mutual Information (MI) due to computational feasibility. Understanding how dCor measures general (linear/non-linear) dependence using Euclidean distances is key.
- **Quick check question:** How does dCor differ from Pearson's correlation, and why is it suitable for evaluating the dependence between a single latent feature and the cancer label **Y**?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (180x180 CT X, label Y) → CVAE Encoder (5 Conv2D + FC) → latent distribution q_φ(z|x) → reparameterization sample Z (dim=8) → split into Z_cs (3 dims) and Z_ncs (5 dims). Z_cs is used by the graph autoencoder (for causal graph discovery) and the entropy variation block (for decoder refinement). Z_ncs is used in dCor disentanglement with Y. The CVAE Decoder (FC + Conv2DTranspose) reconstructs X from the full Z. Total loss ℒ_total combines reconstruction ℒ1, GAE ℒ2, dCor ℒ3, and entropy ℒ4. After training, Z_cs is fed to a downstream lightweight DNN for classification.

**Critical path:** Data → CVAE encoding (get Z) → Split Z → Parallel/composite loss computation: (a) ℒ1 (recon), (b) ℒ3 (dCor disentanglement), (c) ℒ2 (GAE causal fit on Z_cs), (d) ℒ4 (decoder sensitivity on G) → Aggregate ℒ_total → update CVAE & GAE weights → (After training) Extract Z_cs → Train downstream DNN.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Disentanglement strength (φ3) vs. Reconstruction quality (φ1): High φ3 may force Z_cs to be predictive but could distort Z and harm X_hat quality.
- Causal accuracy (SHD) vs. Computational cost: The GAE with additive NNs and DAGMA constraint is more flexible than linear NOTEARS but adds significant parameters and complexity (masking, adjacency layer).
- Decoder sensitivity (φ4) vs. Mode collapse: Aggressive ℒ4 tuning might push the decoder to over-react to latent changes, generating unrealistic CT artifacts.

**Failure signatures:**
- High SHD (>5) or non-acyclic A: Causal discovery failed. Check GAE learning rates (0.007 for A, 0.002 for GAE), DAG penalty α, ρ scheduling (Alg 1), and mask implementation for additive models.
- Poor disentanglement: t-SNE of Z_cs shows overlapping clusters for different Y, or MI(Z_ncs,Y) is non-zero. Increase τ in ℒ3 or adjust φ3 schedule.
- High ℒ4 (near max entropy): Decoder is insensitive to causal factor variations. Ensure variation ς_i is sufficient (1-sigma) and that bin count in entropy computation is appropriate. May need to pre-train decoder longer.
- Blurry reconstructions: Intrinsic VAE limitation. Consider incorporating perceptual losses or transitioning to a hybrid VAE/diffusion decoder as suggested in conclusion.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **VAE Baseline Validation:** Train *only* the CVAE with ℒ1 (φ1=1, others=0) for 450 epochs. Verify reconstruction quality (visual inspection, BCE loss ~0.6 as reported) and that latent space Z is usable (e.g., t-SNE shows some separation by Y).
2. **Disentanglement Check:** Add ℒ3 (φ3=1, after epoch 50). Train and compute MI(Z_cs,Y) and MI(Z_ncs,Y). Goal: Z_cs MI should be high (>1.0), Z_ncs MI near zero. Visualize t-SNE of Z_cs—clusters for normal/benign/malignant should be clearly separated.
3. **Causal Graph Validation:** With full model, after training, extract binarized A (top 30% weights). Compute SHD against the 3-node ground truth DAG (tumor→angiogenesis→lymph). Target SHD ≤ 2.0. If higher, inspect learned graph: is the direction correct? Are all three factors connected?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the inherent blurriness in VAE-based CT image reconstructions within LungCRCT be mitigated to produce clinically sharp and detailed images for accurate treatment simulation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "Due to the intrinsic nature of VAE reconstructions from latent space, vagueness was consistently found within lung CT scan image reconstructions."
- **Why unresolved:** Vague reconstructions obscure subtle pathological features, reducing confidence in visual intervention analysis and limiting diagnostic utility in clinical settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace or enhance the VAE decoder with diffusion models or adversarial training, then quantitatively evaluate reconstruction fidelity (e.g., SSIM, PSNR) and qualitatively assess causal representation preservation via expert review.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What architectural modifications enable effective integration of non-imaging patient data (e.g., smoking history, genetic markers, pollution exposure) into LungCRCT's causal discovery process to form a comprehensive lung cancer model?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion identifies a key limitation: "as LungCRCT only relies on single-modal data usage (image data), non-physical risk factors... were not considered."
- **Why unresolved:** Excluding non-image factors likely yields incomplete causal graphs that fail to capture full etiology, impairing personalized intervention predictions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a multi-modal extension combining CT data with tabular patient information, train on integrated datasets, and measure improvements in causal graph accuracy (e.g., SHD) and downstream classification robustness.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is the fixed three-dimensional causal latent space in LungCRCT sufficient to encode all causally relevant factors in lung cancer progression, or are additional latent variables needed for elements like tumor heterogeneity or metastatic potential?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The methodology assumes three causal factors (tumor, angiogenesis, lymph node) based on domain knowledge but does not validate exhaustiveness.
- **Why unresolved:** An underspecified latent space may omit critical variables, leading to biased intervention simulations and reduced clinical applicability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform sensitivity analysis by varying latent dimensions, compare discovered causal graphs against expanded expert-validated DAGs, and assess downstream task performance with augmented latent representations.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does the choice of disentanglement metric—specifically distance correlation versus mutual information—affect the robustness and interpretability of causal representations in LungCRCT under conditions of data scarcity or noise?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses dCor for computational efficiency over MI but provides no comparative analysis of disentanglement quality.
- **Why unresolved:** The optimal metric for separating causal from non-causal features in medical VAEs is unclear; a suboptimal choice could weaken causal factor isolation and graph learning.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct ablation studies substituting dCor with MI or other metrics on synthetic and real datasets, quantifying disentanglement (e.g., MIG scores) and causal discovery performance (e.g., SHD).

## Limitations
- Fixed latent space dimensionality (8) and split (5 non-causal, 3 causal) may be insufficient to capture all causally relevant factors.
- Ground truth causal graph is assumed from prior biology, not derived from data; mapping from CT to latent factors is indirect and unverified.
- dCor-based disentanglement (ℒ3) is a heuristic; mutual information separation is computed on training data, with risk of overfitting and missed dependencies.
- Entropy-based decoder refinement (ℒ4) uses simple histogram entropy; low value (0.3003) suggests structured changes but lacks qualitative proof of physiological relevance.
- Evaluation appears limited to a single dataset; generalization across scanners and populations is unknown.

## Confidence
- **dCor disentanglement (Mechanism 1):** Medium.
- **GAE causal discovery (Mechanism 2):** Medium.
- **Entropy-based decoder refinement (Mechanism 3):** Low.
- **Overall framework:** Medium.
