# What do near-optimal learning rate schedules look like?

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.10301
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10301
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** For deep learning workloads (CIFAR-10, WikiText-103), near-optimal schedules consistently feature warmup and monotonic decay; flexible families (e.g., Two-Point Spline) outperform the standard cosine schedule, but highly flexible families (Smooth Non-Monotonic) are challenging to optimize via random search.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method to disentangle learning rate schedule shape from base learning rate optimization, enabling fair comparisons across schedule families. Applied to linear regression, CIFAR-10 image classification, and WikiText-103 language modeling, the search reveals that warmup and monotonic decay emerge as robust features of effective schedules in deep learning, even in families that do not explicitly enforce them. Flexible schedule families yield measurable improvements over the standard cosine schedule, though gains are limited by search inefficiency for highly parameterized families. Weight decay is found to strongly influence the optimal decay timing.

## Method Summary
The authors employ a two-step search procedure: first, they sample random schedule shapes from parameterized families (e.g., Two-Point Spline, Generalized Cosine); second, for each shape, they perform a grid sweep over base learning rates, training with a small set of seeds (10 for CIFAR-10, 5 for WikiText-103) and scoring by the median of the final training metric. The top-k shapes (100 for CIFAR-10, 50 for WikiText-103) are then re-trained with a larger seed set (100) for robust final ranking. This decoupling ensures that observed performance differences are attributable to shape rather than base learning rate scaling.

## Key Results
- Effective schedules on CIFAR-10 and WikiText-103 consistently exhibit warmup and monotonic decay, even in families (Smooth Non-Monotonic) that do not constrain these properties.
- Flexible families (Two-Point Spline, Generalized Cosine) outperform the standard cosine schedule: CIFAR-10 median error ~0.063–0.064 vs 0.092; WikiText-103 median perplexity ~26.6–26.7 vs 27.2.
- Weight decay magnitude strongly shifts optimal shape, delaying the decay phase; the Smooth Non-Monotonic family, despite flexibility, performs poorly under random search due to optimization difficulty.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Base Learning Rate Dominance
- **Claim:** The base learning rate is the primary determinant of training performance, with schedule shape variations having a smaller effect once base LR is tuned.
- **Mechanism:** By separately optimizing base LR for each shape, the analysis isolates shape effects. Base LR scales the entire schedule and has a dominant first-order impact on step size; shape fine-tunes the temporal profile.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the approach factors out base LR to avoid dominance in cross-schedule comparisons; Section 4.2 reports base LR as the most important factor, with extra tuning budget better spent on it than schedule hyperparameters.
- **Break condition:** Schedules lacking warmup/decay (e.g., Constant) underperform regardless of base LR tuning.

### Mechanism 2: Emergence of Warmup and Monotonic Decay
- **Claim:** Warmup and monotonic decay are robust, emergent features of near-optimal schedules for deep learning, even in unconstrained families.
- **Mechanism:** Random search on flexible families (Smooth Non-Monotonic) consistently discovers schedules with warmup and decay, indicating these properties are intrinsic to effective optimization in non-convex settings with stochastic gradients.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes effective schedules consistently feature warmup/decay; Section 4.2 states both are crucial and that Smooth Non-Monotonic "discovers" them via random search.
- **Break condition:** In convex settings (linear regression), optimal schedules lack warmup and have sharp decay.

### Mechanism 3: Flexibility vs. Search Efficiency Trade-off
- **Claim:** More flexible schedule families yield measurable improvements over simple ones, but gains diminish with excessive flexibility due to search inefficiency.
- **Mechanism:** Additional parameters allow adaptation of decay shape to workload-specific dynamics, lowering training metrics. However, highly flexible families (Smooth Non-Monotonic) become hard to optimize via random search, limiting realized gains.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports flexible families outperform cosine; Section 4.2 notes Smooth Non-Monotonic is difficult to optimize; Section 4.3 shows its ECDF lags despite more samples.
- **Break condition:** Inadequate search (e.g., poor priors) prevents flexible families from reaching their potential.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Decoupling Schedule Shape and Base Learning Rate**
  - **Why needed here:** To conduct fair cross-family comparisons; base LR confounds shape effects because different shapes may implicitly favor different base LR ranges.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the two-step search procedure (shape search followed by base LR sweep) ensure observed differences are due to shape rather than scale?
- **Concept: Optimization-Limited Regime**
  - **Why needed here:** To ensure training steps are insufficient for saturation, so schedule differences in final metrics are detectable; otherwise, all schedules would converge to similar losses.
  - **Quick check question:** Why were specific step budgets chosen (e.g., 1000 for CIFAR-10), and how do they prevent saturation?
- **Concept: Median Scoring Over Multiple Seeds**
  - **Why needed here:** To robustly evaluate schedules by averaging out randomness from weight initialization and data ordering, avoiding schedules that perform well only on specific seeds.
  - **Quick check question:** Why use the median of training loss minima across seeds instead of mean, and how many seeds were used in search vs. evaluation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Schedule families -> Search procedure (shape sampling -> base LR sweep -> median scoring) -> Top-k re-evaluation -> Analysis
- **Critical path:** 1. For each family, sample random shapes from predefined parameter distributions. 2. For each shape, sweep base learning rates and train with multiple seeds; compute median final training metric. 3. Rank shapes by median score; select top-k for full re-evaluation with more seeds. 4. Analyze best schedules' shapes, base LR distributions, and stability across hyperparameter variations.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Flexibility vs. searchability (more parameters increase representation power but reduce random search efficiency); seed count vs. computation (more seeds reduce noise but increase cost); prior design (uniform random sampling may not favor warmup/decay for unconstrained families).
- **Failure signatures:** Smooth Non-Monotonic: high variability in top schedules, suboptimal performance despite flexibility, and ECDF curve not converged even with 10x more samples; Base LR misalignment if optimal base LRs fall outside search grid; high variance in WikiText-103 indicating training instability or metric sensitivity.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1. **Linear regression validation:** Compare search results to theoretically optimal schedule; search captures qualitative features (decay, no warmup) but quantitative gaps exist. 2. **Deep learning workloads:** Search across families on CIFAR-10 and WikiText-103; all families exhibit warmup/decay, flexible families beat cosine, Smooth Non-Monotonic is poorly optimized. 3. **Hyperparameter ablation:** Vary AdamW β1, β2, weight decay; weight decay strongly shifts optimal shape (delays decay), while β1/β2 show minor effects.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Workload generality:** For which workloads beyond linear regression, CIFAR-10, and WikiText-103 do the identified schedule patterns (warmup, monotonic decay, flexible families outperforming cosine) hold? The authors state their conclusions are limited by the small number of workloads.
2. **Search for flexible families:** What search algorithms can efficiently optimize highly flexible schedule families (e.g., Smooth Non-Monotonic) that do not inherently promote warmup and decay? Random search required 10x more samples yet still underperformed, highlighting the need for improved strategies.
3. **Optimizer interactions:** How do other Adam hyperparameters (β₁, β₂) and optimizer choices (e.g., SGD with momentum) systematically influence optimal schedule shape? The paper only varied β₁, β₂, and weight decay on one family; interactions with other optimizers are unexplored.
4. **Theoretical account for warmup:** What theoretical explanation accounts for the presence of warmup in optimal schedules for deep learning but not for convex linear regression? The authors suggest non-convex curvature dynamics but provide no mechanistic account.

## Limitations
- **Search prior bias:** Uniform random sampling may inherently favor warmup/decay shapes even in unconstrained families (e.g., Smooth Non-Monotonic), potentially missing truly non-monotonic optima.
- **Generalization boundary:** The contrast between linear regression (convex, no warmup optimal) and deep learning (non-convex, warmup emerges) is suggestive but based on only one convex case; more convex/non-convex contrasts are needed.
- **WikiText-103 instability:** High variance in perplexity scores suggests the seed count (100) may still be insufficient for stable ranking, especially for subtle schedule differences.

## Confidence
- **Base LR dominance:** High (decoupled search design directly isolates effect; results consistent).
- **Warmup/decay emergence:** Medium (strong empirical support on two deep learning workloads, but limited to two; counterexample in linear regression weakens universality).
- **Flexible family gains:** Medium-Low (Two-Point Spline/Generalized Cosine beat cosine, but Smooth Non-Monotonic underperforms due to search inefficiency; unclear if true optimum would surpass simpler families).

## Next Checks
1. **Search method upgrade:** Re-run Smooth Non-Monotonic family with informed priors (e.g., biasing toward warmup regions) or Bayesian optimization to test if its performance gap closes, isolating search limitations from representation limits.
2. **Workload boundary test:** Extend comparison to additional convex problems (e.g., logistic regression) and other non-convex architectures (e.g., transformers on different datasets) to map where warmup/decay emergence fails.
3. **Weight decay interaction sweep:** Systematically vary weight decay magnitude across orders of magnitude while re-searching optimal schedules, to quantify and model the reported delay in decay onset.
