# Global Low-Rank, Local Full-Rank: The Holographic Encoding of Learned Algorithms
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18649  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18649  
- **Reference count:** 3  
- **Primary result:** Grokking trajectories lie in a 2–6 dimensional global subspace, with 3–5 trajectory principal components recovering >95% final accuracy; individual weight matrices remain effectively full-rank and static decompositions fail.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the internal weight structure of neural networks that exhibit **grokking**—a sudden transition from memorization to generalization—using multi-task modular arithmetic with Transformers. The authors compare three reconstruction methods for weight changes: per-matrix SVD, joint cross-matrix SVD, and trajectory PCA. Across model scales and weight-decay settings, they find that the optimization trajectory is confined to a very low-dimensional global subspace (2–6 dimensions), and projecting onto a few trajectory principal components nearly restores final performance. In stark contrast, truncating individual weight matrices or applying static joint SVD fails catastrophically even at high ranks. Simultaneously, task-specific circuits are nearly orthogonal in activation space but appear holographically entangled in weight space. This reveals a duality: learned algorithms are globally low-rank in learning directions but locally full-rank in parameter realization, explaining why static weight decompositions fail while trajectory-informed methods succeed.

## Method Summary
The authors train a shared-trunk Transformer on three modular arithmetic tasks (addition, multiplication, and quadratic modulo 97) with independent linear heads. Training uses full-batch AdamW (learning rate \(10^{-3}\), betas (0.9, 0.98)) across a sweep of weight-decay values (\(\lambda \in \{0.1,0.2,0.3,0.5,1.0\}\)) and three model scales (315K–2.2M parameters). Checkpoints are saved throughout training until all tasks achieve >95% test accuracy or a maximum step cap is reached. For analysis, only the trunk parameters are considered. The weight changes \(\Delta W\) from initial to final checkpoint are reconstructed using three methods: (1) **Per-matrix SVD**: each trunk matrix’s \(\Delta W\) is truncated to a fixed rank; (2) **Joint SVD**: all \(\Delta W\) matrices are flattened, zero-padded to equal length, stacked, and a joint low-rank approximation is computed; (3) **Trajectory PCA**: the sequence of trunk parameters across checkpoints is flattened, centered, and SVD is performed on the difference matrix; the top \(k\) trajectory PCs are used to reconstruct the final trunk. For each method and rank, test accuracy on all tasks is measured.

## Key Results
- Trajectory PCA with only 3–5 principal components recovers >95% accuracy across all model scales and weight-decay settings.
- Both per-matrix SVD (e.g., rank 64) and joint SVD (e.g., half rank) fail catastrophically, yielding near-chance accuracy despite capturing a large fraction of Frobenius energy (e.g., 88%).
- Task-specific circuits are nearly orthogonal in activation space (selectivity index >0.96) but holographically entangled in weight space, as evidenced by the failure of static SVD methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The grokking optimization trajectory is confined to a low-dimensional subspace.
- **Mechanism:** Gradient descent with weight decay implicitly biases the model toward solutions whose weight changes lie in an invariant low-dimensional manifold. The principal components of the trajectory (trajectory PCs) capture the directions of significant coordinated updates; projecting onto a few such PCs preserves nearly all performance.
- **Core assumption:** The loss landscape for multi-task modular arithmetic contains a low-dimensional attractor that governs learning dynamics.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "grokking trajectories lie in a 2–6 dimensional global subspace, with 3–5 trajectory principal components recovering >95% final accuracy." [Section 3.1] Table 2 and Figure 1 confirm that 3–5 PCs suffice across all model scales and weight decays. [corpus] Related subspace methods (e.g., Memory-Efficient LLM Training with Online Subspace Descent) also exploit low-rank structure but do not address the global-low-rank/local-full-rank duality.
- **Break condition:** If the tasks do not induce grokking (e.g., single-task or non-algorithmic data) or the architecture lacks a shared trunk, the trajectory may not be low-dimensional.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Individual weight matrices in the grokked solution require nearly full rank; low-rank truncation destroys function.
- **Mechanism:** The algorithmic computation is distributed across many singular values per matrix, with critical information residing in the spectral tail. Capturing most Frobenius energy (e.g., 88%) is insufficient because the task-relevant components are functionally essential but energetically small.
- **Core assumption:** The solution cannot be compressed within a single matrix because cross-matrix coordination demands contributions from many singular vectors.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "individual weight matrices remain effectively full-rank." [Section 3.2] Per-matrix SVD at rank 64 yields chance-level accuracy (3–87%) despite capturing ~88% Frobenius energy; full recovery requires ~80–100 singular components. [corpus] Work on low-rank weight training (Full-Rank No More) suggests low-rank structures can succeed for speech models, contrasting with the incompressibility of grokked algorithms.
- **Break condition:** For simpler tasks or non-grokking regimes, per-matrix low-rank might suffice; also, the linear heads are highly compressible, so this applies specifically to the shared trunk.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Cross-matrix correlations encoding task circuits are dynamically structured and only recoverable via the training trajectory; static decompositions fail.
- **Mechanism:** The learning process coordinates updates across all weight matrices in a way that is not apparent from final weights alone. Trajectory PCA captures the invariant subspace of these coordinated changes, while per-matrix and joint SVD on final weights miss the dynamic correlations because the final state is a sum of many coordinated updates that cancel out in a static snapshot.
- **Core assumption:** Task-specific information is holographically entangled: each weight matrix contributes to all tasks, and separation appears only in activation space.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "both per-matrix and joint SVD fail catastrophically at sub-full rank." [Section 3.3] Joint SVD at half rank gives 1–11% accuracy, while trajectory PCA with 3–5 PCs gives 93–99%. [Section 3.4] Task circuits are nearly orthogonal in activation space (selectivity index >0.96) but entangled in weight space. [corpus] SwitchLoRA attempts to learn full-rank information with low-rank adaptation, but the failure of joint SVD here indicates that static factors cannot capture the holographic structure.
- **Break condition:** With single-task learning or without grokking, cross-matrix entanglement may be weaker; static decompositions might then succeed.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Tasks (addition, multiplication, quadratic) -> Shared Transformer trunk (token embedding, positional embedding, pre-norm encoder layers, final layer norm) -> Three independent linear heads.

### Critical path
1. Train multi-task model until grokking; monitor test accuracy curves.  
2. Collect checkpoints throughout training.  
3. Compute initial and final trunk weights; flatten each checkpoint’s trunk into vector.  
4. For trajectory PCA: center by initial weights, compute SVD of difference matrix, select top k PCs, project final \(\Delta W\) onto PCs, reconstruct, evaluate.  
5. For per-matrix SVD: for each trunk weight matrix, compute SVD of \(\Delta W_i\), truncate to rank k, reconstruct, evaluate.  
6. For joint SVD: flatten each \(\Delta W_i\), stack as rows (with zero-padding to equal length), SVD, truncate to rank k, reshape back to original matrix shapes, evaluate.  
7. Compare mean test accuracy across tasks for each method and rank.

### Design tradeoffs
- Full-batch training ensures deterministic trajectories but is memory-intensive; avoids stochastic noise that could obscure low-dimensional structure.  
- Weight decay sweep reveals its role in grokking speed and compressibility but multiplies experiments.  
- Trunk-only reconstruction isolates the shared representation; heads are fixed because they are task-specific and compressible.  
- Joint SVD zero-padding is necessary to stack matrices of different shapes but introduces artificial dimensions; likely ignored by top singular vectors.  
- Trajectory PCA requires storing many checkpoints; tradeoff between memory and temporal resolution.  
- Accuracy threshold (>95% on all tasks) defines grokking; also examine >99% for fine structure.

### Failure signatures
- No grokking: test accuracy remains near chance after max steps—check learning rate, weight decay, model capacity.  
- Trajectory PCA high \(k^*\): >20 PCs needed for 95% accuracy—contradicts low-dimensional claim; model may not have fully grokked.  
- Per-matrix SVD succeeds at low rank (e.g., rank-64 yields >50% accuracy)—contradicts local full-rank claim.  
- Joint SVD succeeds at half rank—indicates static cross-matrix correlations exist, negating need for trajectory.  
- Activation circuits not separable: gradient-based ablation yields selectivity index <0.8—tasks not disentangled in activation space.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Reproduce grokking:** Train baseline model (d_model=128, 2 layers) with weight decay 0.5. Verify test accuracy abruptly rises from low to >97% on all three tasks. Plot accuracy curves to confirm the characteristic delay and jump.  
2. **Validate trajectory low-rank:** For the trained model, compute trajectory PCA on trunk checkpoints. Plot cumulative explained variance and reconstruction accuracy vs. number of PCs. Expect >95% accuracy with ≤6 PCs.  
3. **Test per-matrix SVD failure:** Reconstruct trunk by truncating each weight matrix’s \(\Delta W\) to rank 64. Evaluate on test set. Expect accuracy near chance (1–5%). If significantly higher, verify grokking status and truncation implementation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What theoretical mechanism explains why grokking trajectories lie in a low-dimensional subspace while final weight matrices remain full-rank?**  
   *Basis:* Section 7.2; calls for a formal connection between loss landscape geometry, weight decay, and the observed duality.  
2. **Does the holographic encoding principle hold for tasks beyond modular arithmetic?**  
   *Basis:* Second open problem; universality is unknown due to focus on a single task family.  
3. **Can the low-dimensional trajectory basis be recovered from final weights alone, without training checkpoints?**  
   *Basis:* Third open problem; trajectory-free compression is needed for pre-trained models.  
4. **What is the precise relationship between trajectory principal components and the Fourier circuits implicated in grokked modular arithmetic?**  
   *Basis:* Fourth open problem; mapping between task-agnostic basis and task-specific Fourier representations is missing.  
5. **How does the number of trajectory principal components required for reconstruction scale with model size, number of tasks, and task complexity?**  
   *Basis:* Fifth open problem; limited model sweeps prevent determination of scaling laws.

## Limitations
- The study is confined to a narrow multi-task modular arithmetic setting; generality to real-world algorithms (language, vision) or different architectures (CNNs, RNNs) is unknown.  
- Full-batch training may artificially enforce low-dimensional dynamics not present under stochastic minibatching.  
- Hyperparameter exploration is limited to weight decay; interactions with other regularizers or optimizer choices are unexplored.  
- The inference of “holographic entanglement” relies on the failure of static SVD; alternative explanations (e.g., ill-conditioned landscapes) are not fully ruled out.  
- Computational costs (dense checkpointing, large SVDs) restrict analysis to trunk parameters only; heads and biases are excluded.

## Confidence
- **Global low-rank trajectory:** High – consistently observed across model scales, weight-decay values, and tasks; 3–5 PCs recover >95% accuracy in all cases.  
- **Local full-rank requirement:** Medium – strong empirical support for the given architecture and tasks; generalizability to other networks or non-grokking regimes is uncertain.  
- **Holographic entanglement (weight-space) & activation-space orthogonality:** Medium – activation orthogonality is robustly measured; weight-space entanglement is inferred from static SVD failure and could be influenced by methodological choices (e.g., padding in joint SVD).

## Next Checks
1. **Checkpoint density ablation:** Increase checkpoint frequency (e.g., every 100 steps) during the grokking transition to confirm the 2–6 PC result is not an artifact of sparse sampling.  
2. **Stochastic training robustness:** Train the same model with minibatch SGD (batch size 256) and standard weight decay. If grokking occurs, compute trajectory PCA; failure to observe low-rank trajectories would indicate the result is specific to full-batch deterministic optimization.  
3. **Joint SVD on intermediate states:** Apply joint SVD to \(\Delta W\) from early (pre-grokking), mid-transition, and late checkpoints. If joint SVD never recovers accuracy at half rank—even on mid-training states—it strengthens the claim that cross-matrix correlations are dynamically formed and not capturable by any static snapshot.
