# On Catastrophic Forgetting in Low-Rank Decomposition-Based Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.09684
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09684
- **Reference count**: 9
- **Primary result**: Forgetting in low-rank PEFT for ViTs is governed by update subspace geometry; flexible parameterizations (LoRETTA, WeGeFT) mitigate forgetting while remaining parameter-efficient.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates catastrophic forgetting during sequential fine-tuning of vision transformers (ViTs) using parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods based on low-rank decomposition. The authors discover that the geometry and parameterization of the update subspace critically determine interference between tasks: methods that restrict updates to a small, shared subspace (e.g., PiSSA at low rank) cause severe forgetting, while approaches offering greater flexibility (higher-rank LoRA) or structural alignment (WeGeFT) or richer dependencies via tensor decomposition (LoRETTA) significantly reduce forgetting. On ViT-Base, LoRETTA and WeGeFT achieve low average forgetting (~0.07) and high final accuracy (>92%) with minimal additional parameters (~55K), far outperforming PiSSA (forgetting >0.20, accuracy ~81%). The work underscores that careful design of the update subspace is essential for balancing parameter efficiency and knowledge retention in continual learning with transformers.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates four low-rank PEFT methods—PiSSA (low-rank SVD approximation), LoRA (low-rank adaptation matrices), WeGeFT (structurally aligned updates), and LoRETTA (tensor decomposition)—in a sequential fine-tuning setting on a pretrained ViT-Base model. For each method, the backbone is frozen and only the PEFT parameters are updated per task. After sequentially fine-tuning on multiple vision tasks, catastrophic forgetting is quantified as the average drop in performance on previously seen tasks, alongside final accuracy on the last task. Experiments vary the rank (for PiSSA/LoRA) and compare the methods at comparable parameter budgets to isolate the effect of update subspace design.

## Key Results
- LoRETTA (57K params) and WeGeFT (54K params) achieve average forgetting of 0.0717 and 0.0752, respectively, with final accuracies of 92.65% and 92.30% on ViT-Base.
- PiSSA at low rank suffers severe forgetting (0.2089) and low final accuracy (81.25%).
- Forgetting systematically worsens as the update subspace becomes more constrained (e.g., lower rank or less flexible parameterization).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Forgetting occurs because updates for new tasks interfere with previously learned representations when the update subspace is small and shared across tasks. PiSSA uses a fixed low-rank subspace derived from the gradient SVD; this limited basis cannot accommodate distinct task-specific adjustments without overwriting prior knowledge. LoRETTA employs tensor decomposition to capture higher-order, multi-dimensional dependencies, enabling more expressive updates that are less likely to conflict. WeGeFT explicitly aligns the update basis with the singular vectors of the pretrained weights, preserving the original representation geometry and minimizing destructive interference. Higher-rank LoRA simply expands the capacity of the update subspace, providing more degrees of freedom to learn task-specific features without relying on the same coordinates. Thus, mitigating forgetting requires either sufficient subspace capacity or parameterizations that respect the pretrained model's structure.

## Architecture Onboarding
Pretrained ViT-Base → [PEFT Module (PiSSA/LoRA/WeGeFT/LoRETTA)] → Sequential Task Fine-tuning → Evaluation (Forgetting, Final Accuracy)

Critical path: For each new task, the PEFT parameters are updated while the pretrained backbone stays frozen; after all tasks, performance on every task is measured to compute forgetting (drop from peak) and final accuracy.

Design tradeoffs:
- Rank vs. parameters: Higher rank reduces forgetting but increases parameter count.
- Subspace flexibility: Shared low-rank basis (PiSSA) vs. independent (LoRA) vs. structurally constrained (WeGeFT/LoRETTA) directly impacts interference.
- Efficiency: All target <<0.1% of ViT-Base’s 86M parameters.

Failure signatures:
- Very low-rank PiSSA (rank ≤4) on diverse tasks → forgetting >0.20, accuracy collapse.
- Over-constrained subspace (e.g., rank-1 updates) fails to capture task-specific features.

Exactly 3 first experiments:
1. Train ViT-Base+PiSSA(rank=4) on Task A then Task B; compute forgetting on Task A.
2. Repeat with LoRA at ranks 4, 8, 16 to isolate rank effect.
3. Compare WeGeFT and LoRETTA vs. PiSSA/LoRA at matched parameter budget (~55K) on a 3-task sequence.

## Limitations
- **Architectural scope**: Experiments solely on ViT-Base; generalizability to CNNs (ResNet) or LLMs (Llama) is unvalidated.
- **Protocol opacity**: The summary does not specify the exact task sequences or datasets, limiting full reproducibility.
- **Confidence medium**: The core claim about subspace geometry is supported by a single model family, reducing certainty.

## Confidence
- Claim: The geometry and parameterization of the update subspace determine catastrophic forgetting in sequential ViT fine-tuning. Confidence: Medium (evidence from ViT-Base only).
- Claim: LoRETTA and WeGeFT achieve lower forgetting and higher final accuracy than PiSSA on ViT-Base at similar parameter counts. Confidence: High (direct experimental comparison).
- Claim: Results generalize to non-vision models (LLMs) and arbitrary task sequences. Confidence: Low (no validation provided).
