# Public Profile Matters: A Scalable Integrated Approach to Recommend Citations in the Wild

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17361
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17361
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Profiler reduces retrieval time by >32× and increases MRR by up to 63.85%; DA VINCI (110M) achieves new state-of-the-art, outperforming prior LCR systems and models 70× larger despite far fewer parameters.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles inefficiency and bias in local citation recommendation (LCR) by proposing Profiler, a non-learnable retrieval module that builds a "public profile" for each paper from its citation network, and DA VINCI, a reranker that fuses Profiler's confidence prior with semantic signals via adaptive vector-gating. It also introduces a rigorous inductive evaluation with strict temporal constraints to simulate real-world citation recommendation for new papers. Experiments across multiple datasets show that Profiler dramatically speeds up retrieval while improving accuracy, and DA VINCI sets a new state-of-the-art, significantly outperforming both prior LCR systems and much larger open-source rerankers. The work demonstrates that leveraging human citation patterns and proper evaluation yields scalable, high-performance citation recommendation.

## Method Summary
The proposed system consists of two stages. **Profiler** operates offline: for every paper in the corpus, it encodes title and abstract with a pre-trained model (specter2) to obtain a base vector, then enriches it by averaging vectors of citing papers and their citation contexts, yielding a static "public profile" vector that is indexed for fast retrieval. At query time, a query vector is formed by combining the citation context and the citing paper's metadata; cosine similarity against the indexed profiles retrieves candidate papers and raw scores. **DA VINCI** reranks these candidates: it transforms raw scores into a discriminative prior via ordinal abstraction and exponential remapping, encodes the query-candidate text pair with SciBERT to get a [CLS] embedding, and then fuses the semantic and prior signals using an adaptive vector-gating mechanism that learns to weight features per dimension. The full system is trained with a triplet loss. The authors advocate for an **inductive evaluation** setting where the candidate corpus strictly predates the test queries, preventing data leakage and better reflecting deployment.

## Key Results
- Profiler reduces retrieval time by over 32× on the largest dataset while increasing MRR by up to 63.85% compared to prior retrieval approaches.
- DA VINCI (110M parameters) establishes a new state-of-the-art across multiple benchmarks, significantly outperforming prior LCR systems.
- Despite having 70× fewer parameters, DA VINCI also surpasses massive-scale open-source rerankers (e.g., Qwen3-R-8B) in citation recommendation performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Public Profile Prior
Profiler enriches each paper's representation by aggregating the vectors of its citing papers and the specific citation context snippets. This creates a static "public profile" that captures how the scholarly community perceives the paper. The core assumption is that collective citation behavior forms a stable and generalizable relevance signal. By precomputing these profiles, retrieval becomes extremely fast and the signal is claimed to be bias-resistant because it discards raw citation counts and venue. Evidence includes the formalisation in Section 3.2 and statements about eliminating biases. The approach breaks for papers with no inbound citations, where it defaults to the base semantic vector and loses the network-derived advantage.

### Mechanism 2: Adaptive Vector Gating
DA VINCI fuses the Profiler's confidence prior with deep semantic features using an adaptive, per-dimension gating mechanism. The raw retrieval scores are first transformed into a robust prior via ordinal abstraction and exponential remapping to address poor calibration. This prior and the SciBERT [CLS] embedding are projected into a shared space, concatenated, and then element-wise multiplied by a gating vector computed from the original inputs. This allows the model to dynamically weight which fused features matter most. The core assumption is that the optimal integration is feature-specific, not scalar. Evidence includes the ablation study (Table 4) showing that scalar gating causes a significant performance drop. The mechanism fails if the first-stage Profiler retrieves an irrelevant candidate set, as the prior then becomes misleading.

### Mechanism 3: Inductive Evaluation with Temporal Constraints
The authors argue that realistic citation recommendation must use only literature published before the query paper. They define an inductive setting where the candidate corpus is disjoint from the evaluation set and every corpus paper predates every query paper. This prevents leakage of test papers into the corpus and simulates recommending citations for a newly authored manuscript. The core assumption is that transductive evaluation (allowing test papers as candidates) inflates performance and does not reflect deployment. Evidence includes the formal definitions (Section 3.1) and the dramatic reduction in corpus and test sizes observed in Table 1. The mechanism's applicability depends on reliable publication date metadata; without it, strict temporal separation cannot be enforced.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Information Retrieval (IR) evaluation metrics (MRR, Recall@K, NDCG@K)**
  - **Why needed**: These metrics measure ranking performance and guide optimisation (e.g., triplet loss).
  - **Quick check**: If a system always places the correct citation at rank 5, which metric(s) will reflect this perfect ordering: MRR, Recall@10, or NDCG@10?
- **Concept: Vector similarity search and indexing**
  - **Why needed**: Profiler relies on pre-computed document vectors and fast nearest-neighbour search to achieve millisecond retrieval.
  - **Quick check**: Why does pre-computing and indexing the profiled vectors (`ˆv_i`) for the entire corpus enable retrieval in milliseconds, whereas a model like SymTax's enricher requires processing the candidate list sequentially for each query?
- **Concept: Neural network design for fusion (gating, MLP towers)**
  - **Why needed**: DA VINCI’s adaptive vector-gating fuses heterogeneous signals (scalar prior and semantic vector) more expressively than scalar fusion.
  - **Quick check**: In DA VINCI's fusion, what is the functional purpose of the gating vector `g` applied via Hadamard product (`g ⊙ h_concat`) compared to simply averaging `h_score` and `h_text`?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Offline pipeline: Corpus → specter2 encoding → citation ego-network construction → profiled vector computation → FAISS indexing. Online retrieval: Query → specter2 query encoding → cosine search → top-k candidates with scores. Reranking: Candidates + scores + query → DA VINCI (SciBERT encoding, prior transformation, projection, gating) → final scores.

**Critical path**: Profiler retrieval must execute first; its output (candidate list and confidence scores) is the sole input to DA VINCI. The gating network inside DA VINCI depends on both the SciBERT [CLS] embedding and the transformed prior.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Profiler: Efficiency & bias reduction vs. cold-start (papers with no citations lose the profile benefit).
- DA VINCI: Parameter efficiency & specificity vs. limited context length (512 tokens) and generalism beyond scientific text.
- Inductive setting: Realistic evaluation vs. data scarcity (temporal split reduces training/eval sizes significantly).

**Failure signatures**:
- Very low Recall@300 suggests Profiler's static aggregation is ineffective (e.g., sparse/noisy citation network).
- Ablation A1 (semantics only) performing similarly to full DA VINCI indicates the prior is not being used effectively.
- Performance collapse when moving from transductive to inductive evaluation indicates overfitting to the static collection illusion.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Reproduce Profiler's efficiency and accuracy: measure retrieval time and MRR against Prefetcher+Enricher on ArSyTa; expect >32× speedup and MRR increase.
2. Validate gating mechanism: train ablations A1 (no prior), A3 (softmax norm), A4 (scalar gating) on ACL-200; confirm A4 shows largest drop relative to full DA VINCI.
3. Confirm inductive setting stringency: take a transductive model, re-evaluate under inductive constraints, document metric drop and corpus size reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalisation to non‑English and non‑CS domains**: The evaluation is limited to English CS papers; citation practices differ in other fields and languages, so it is unclear how well Profiler and DA VINCI transfer.
2. **Cold‑start for uncited papers**: Profiler defaults to pure semantics for papers with no inbound citations; the impact on recommendation quality for such "absolute cold‑start" papers is unresolved.
3. **Impact of 512‑token limit**: The reranker truncates inputs; for citations requiring long‑range or multi‑paragraph understanding, essential context may be lost. The severity of this limitation is unknown.
4. **Robustness to incomplete metadata**: The system relies on titles and abstracts; missing or malformed metadata could degrade performance, but no experiments simulate such degradation.

## Limitations
- **Data accessibility**: The raw datasets with citation contexts (e.g., ACL-200, ArSyTa) are not publicly available, blocking faithful reproduction of Profiler's enrichment.
- **Omitted hyperparameters and architectural details**: Critical values (α, β, γ, δ, λ, margin, MLP dimensions) and training settings are not specified, forcing assumptions that may deviate from the original.
- **Evaluation realism depends on temporal metadata**: The inductive split requires accurate publication dates; incomplete or inconsistent metadata could force approximations that affect results.
