# Representation Learning for Spatiotemporal Physical Systems

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2603.13227
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13227
* **Reference Count:** 7
* **Primary Result:** Latent-space predictive objectives (JEPA) significantly reduce physical parameter estimation error compared to pixel-level methods (VideoMAE), demonstrating superior sample efficiency and representation quality for scientific machine learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper reevaluates representation learning for physical systems by shifting the evaluation focus from generative next-frame prediction to downstream physical parameter estimation. The authors pretrain two self-supervised models—JEPA, which predicts future latent representations, and VideoMAE, a masked autoencoder that reconstructs pixels—on simulated PDE trajectories from The Well dataset. After freezing the encoders, they fine-tune lightweight probes for parameter regression. Across three physical systems (active matter, shear flow, Rayleigh-Bénard convection), JEPA consistently outperforms VideoMAE, achieving 28–51% lower mean squared error. Models that predict in latent space (JEPA, DISCO) also surpass the autoregressive foundation model MPP, which is trained on pixel-level prediction. The results suggest that latent predictive objectives yield representations more aligned with underlying physical parameters, with greater data efficiency.

## Method Summary
The study compares representation learning objectives for physical systems. Models are pretrained on short video clips (16 frames) from simulated PDE data. JEPA uses a CNN encoder and predictor trained with a VICReg loss to align future latent representations. VideoMAE uses a ViT encoder and decoder to reconstruct randomly masked video patches. DISCO and MPP are external pretrained baselines. For downstream parameter estimation, the encoders of JEPA, VideoMAE, and DISCO are frozen, and an attentive probe is trained to regress two physical parameters per system. MPP is fine-tuned end-to-end. Performance is measured by mean squared error on held-out test parameters. A data scaling experiment subsamples the fine-tuning set to assess sample efficiency.

## Key Results
* JEPA achieves lower parameter prediction MSE than VideoMAE on all three systems: active matter (0.079 vs 0.160), shear flow (0.38 vs 0.67), Rayleigh-Bénard (0.13 vs 0.18), representing 28–51% relative improvement.
* Latent-prediction models (JEPA, DISCO) outperform pixel-level models (VideoMAE) and the autoregressive foundation model MPP, which underperforms despite end-to-end fine-tuning.
* In data scaling on shear flow, JEPA with only 10% fine-tuning data (MSE 0.57) surpasses VideoMAE trained on 100% (0.67), demonstrating superior sample efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
* **Claim:** Latent-space predictive objectives produce representations that better capture governing physical parameters by avoiding overfitting to irrelevant pixel-level details.
* **Mechanism:** JEPA encodes input frames into a latent space and trains a predictor to forecast the latent of future frames. The VICReg loss (invariance, variance, covariance) enforces a structured, non-collapsed latent space. By not reconstructing pixels, the model focuses on abstract features correlated with dynamics.
* **Core Assumption:** Physical parameters manifest in latent dynamics but are obscured by high-variance pixel noise.
* **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states latent methods outperform pixel-level ones; Section 2.1 defines JEPA objective; Table 1 shows JEPA lower MSE than VideoMAE.
* **Break Condition:** If pixel reconstruction is required for downstream task (e.g., video synthesis), latent prediction may underperform due to discarded details.

### Mechanism 2
* **Claim:** VICReg regularization induces feature disentanglement and invariance, aligning with the low-dimensional parameter space.
* **Mechanism:** VICReg's invariance term aligns predicted and target latents; variance prevents collapse; covariance decorrelates dimensions. This yields a latent space where parameters are more linearly separable.
* **Core Assumption:** Governing parameters are among the few underlying factors of system evolution.
* **Evidence Anchors:** Section 2.1 details VICReg components; JEPA's consistent gains over VideoMAE suggest structural benefit.
* **Break Condition:** If enforced invariances conflict with true physical symmetries (e.g., conservation laws), relevant information may be discarded.

## Architecture Onboarding
* **Component Map:** Input frames -> JEPA encoder (CNN) -> latent z -> JEPA predictor (CNN) -> predicted latent ẑ; loss: VICReg(ẑ, z_target). VideoMAE: masked frames -> ViT encoder -> latent -> decoder -> pixel reconstruction. Fine-tuning: frozen encoder -> attentive probe -> parameter regression.
* **Critical Path:** 1) Pretrain JEPA/VideoMAE (6 epochs, VICReg/pixel MSE). 2) Freeze encoder. 3) Train attentive probe (100 epochs, MSE loss). 4) Evaluate test MSE.
* **Design Tradeoffs:** Latent methods discard visual noise but lose details; pixel methods preserve details but may overfit to transient dynamics. Encoder architecture: CNN (JEPA) vs. ViT (VideoMAE) may affect equivariance. Latent prediction is computationally cheaper.
* **Failure Signatures:** JEPA: VICReg variance near zero indicates latent collapse. VideoMAE: high reconstruction loss plateau. General: probe training loss does not decrease (encoder lacks parameter info); encoder gradients active during probe training (overfitting).
* **First 3 Experiments:** 1) **Baseline comparison:** Pretrain JEPA/VideoMAE on each dataset; fine-tune probes. Expect JEPA lower MSE (Table 1). 2) **Data scaling:** Vary fine-tuning data (10%, 50%, 100%). Expect JEPA to match VideoMAE with less data (Table 2). 3) **Objective ablation:** Compare latent (JEPA) vs. pixel (VideoMAE) methods directly; expect latent advantage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
* **Question:** What specific mechanisms cause latent-space predictive objectives to yield more physically informative representations than pixel-level ones?
* **Basis:** Results show JEPA reduces MSE by 28–51% (Table 1). The paper does not analyze *why* latent prediction better captures parameters.
* **Why unresolved:** No feature analysis or disentanglement evidence.
* **What evidence would resolve:** Comparative probing, representation similarity analysis, alignment with physical symmetries.

### Open Question 2
* **Question:** For which classes of physical systems does pixel-level reconstruction become competitive, and what properties determine this?
* **Basis:** Performance gap varies: 28% improvement on Rayleigh‑Bénard vs. 43–51% on others (Table 1). VideoMAE closes gap more on Rayleigh‑Bénard.
* **Why unresolved:** No investigation into why some systems yield smaller gaps.
* **What evidence would resolve:** Quantify pixel-parameter correlation across diverse PDEs.

### Open Question 3
* **Question:** Why does autoregressive foundation model MPP underperform on parameter estimation despite end‑to‑end fine‑tuning?
* **Basis:** MPP has higher MSE than JEPA/DISCO on all tasks (Table 1).
* **Why unresolved:** Causal explanation not validated; may stem from misaligned objective or representation biases.
* **What evidence would resolve:** Direct representation comparison (e.g., linear probes at layers); ablation of MPP's pretraining diversity.

### Open Question 4
* **Question:** How sensitive are latent method benefits to the fine-tuning strategy? Would full fine-tuning improve or degrade physical content?
* **Basis:** Authors use frozen encoders for JEPA/VideoMAE/DISCO; did not test encoder fine-tuning.
* **Why unresolved:** Unknown if representations robust to adaptation.
* **What evidence would resolve:** Experiments varying fine-tuned encoder layers; measure accuracy and generalization.

### Open Question 5
* **Question:** Do latent prediction representations transfer to other scientific tasks (e.g., phase classification, uncertainty quantification, long-term forecasting)?
* **Basis:** Evaluation limited to parameter regression; generality unknown.
* **Why unresolved:** Not tested.
* **What evidence would resolve:** Benchmark same pretrained models on diverse downstream physical tasks.

## Limitations
* Key implementation details missing: exact spatial resolution, number of fields, data splits, normalization, hyperparameters.
* No ablation of VICReg loss components or isolation of latent prediction effect from encoder architecture.
* Evaluation confined to three simulated PDE systems; real-world generalization untested.
* No direct latent space analysis (e.g., linear separability of parameters, invariance properties).

## Confidence
* **Latent prediction superiority:** High – Consistent MSE reduction across all systems (28–51%).
* **VICReg regularization yields physically informative representations:** Medium – Theoretical motivation exists, but lacking empirical disentanglement evidence.
* **Autoregressive pixel-level models underperform due to transient entanglement:** Medium – Results clear, but causal explanation unvalidated.
* **Sample efficiency of latent methods:** High – Data scaling experiment shows JEPA with 10% data surpasses VideoMAE with 100%.

## Next Checks
1. **Objective ablation:** Train a model with JEPA's architecture but a pixel-space predictor (next-frame MSE). If parameter estimation drops to VideoMAE levels, latent prediction is confirmed as key factor.
2. **Linear probing of latents:** Freeze pretrained encoder, fit linear regressors to parameters. High R² would directly confirm parameters are encoded linearly.
3. **Cross-system and real-world generalization:** Evaluate JEPA/VideoMAE on additional PDEs (e.g., Navier‑Stokes, wave) or experimental fluid videos to test robustness.
