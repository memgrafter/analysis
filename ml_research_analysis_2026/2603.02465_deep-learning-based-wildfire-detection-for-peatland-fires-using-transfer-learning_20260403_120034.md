# Deep Learning Based Wildfire Detection for Peatland Fires Using Transfer Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02465
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02465
- **Reference Count:** 18
- **Primary Result:** Transfer learning with a Walsh-Hadamard Transform-enhanced ResNet50 achieves 91.6% accuracy and 91.5% F1-score on Malaysian peatland fire detection, significantly outperforming a standard ResNet50 trained from scratch.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of detecting smoldering peatland fires, which produce subtle, low-contrast smoke plumes that are difficult for conventional models to identify. The authors propose a deep learning solution that combines transfer learning from a general wildfire dataset, a modified ResNet50 architecture incorporating Walsh-Hadamard Transform (WHT) layers for parameter efficiency, and a patch-based high-resolution image processing strategy. Their best model, WHT-ResNet50 initialized with weights from a general wildfire model and fine-tuned on peatland data, demonstrates robust performance under difficult conditions like partial occlusions and variable illumination.

## Method Summary
The method involves three key components. First, high-resolution input images are divided into overlapping 224x224 patches (via a tiling scheme) to preserve fine-grained smoke details that would be lost in full-image resizing. Second, the core classifier is a WHT-ResNet50, where standard convolutional layers are partially replaced by layers that perform operations in the orthogonal Walsh-Hadamard domain, reducing parameters and promoting decorrelated features. Third, a transfer learning pipeline is used: the network is first pretrained on the large GWFP wildfire dataset, then its weights are used to initialize a model that is fine-tuned on the smaller,target Malaysian peatland dataset for 25 epochs. Patch-level fire probabilities are aggregated to produce a final frame-level decision.

## Key Results
- Transfer learning from a general wildfire model (GWFP) to peatland data boosts standard ResNet50 performance from 71.4% accuracy / 70.1% F1 to 88.9% accuracy / 88.2% F1.
- The proposed WHT-ResNet50 architecture with transfer learning achieves the best results: 91.6% accuracy and 91.5% F1-score.
- The model shows robustness under challenging real-world conditions including low-contrast smoke, partial occlusions, and variable illumination.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Transfer Learning for Domain Adaptation
- **Claim:** Initializing the model with weights from a general wildfire dataset (GWFP) significantly improves accuracy on the limited Malaysian peatland dataset.
- **Mechanism:** The pretrained weights provide robust low-level feature detectors (e.g., for smoke texture, flame color) that are transferable. Fine-tuning then adapts primarily the high-level, task-specific representations efficiently, overcoming the limitation of small target data.
- **Core Assumption:** The feature hierarchy learned from general wildfires shares meaningful overlap with that required for peatland fires, especially in early convolutional layers.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states the strategy "enables effective learning despite the limited availability of labeled peatland fire data." Section 2.1 notes it "significantly reduced training effort while maintaining high performance." External literature on wildfire transfer learning supports the approach.

### Mechanism 2: WHT-Enhanced Architecture
- **Claim:** Integrating Walsh-Hadamard Transform layers into ResNet50 (WHT-ResNet50) improves detection robustness and parameter efficiency for subtle peatland smoke patterns.
- **Mechanism:** WHT performs convolution-like operations using orthogonal binary basis functions, reducing multiplicative operations and parameter count. This encourages learning compact, decorrelated feature representations better suited for distinguishing faint, persistent smoke from complex backgrounds.
- **Core Assumption:** The orthogonal, frequency-oriented basis of WHT provides a more effective representation for the subtle, textural patterns of smoldering smoke than standard spatial convolutions alone.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract notes the best model shows "robust detection under low-contrast smoke." Section 2.3 explains it reduces parameters and is "more effective than the regular ResNet in small datasets." Table 1 shows WHT-ResNet50 without TL (77.2% F1) already outperforms standard ResNet50 without TL (70.1% F1).

### Mechanism 3: Patch-Based High-Resolution Processing
- **Claim:** Dividing images into overlapping patches preserves small, subtle smoke cues that would be lost in conventional full-image resizing.
- **Mechanism:** This approach maintains native resolution for local analysis, preventing the dilution or aliasing of faint, low-contrast smoke signals during aggressive downsampling.
- **Core Assumption:** The spatial scale of relevant peatland fire cues (e.g., thin smoke plumes) is smaller than what survives typical full-image resizing.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Section 2.2 states resizing "can cause the little smoke to go undetected" and describes the tiling approach to "minimize the loss of information." Figure 1 shows detection localized to specific patches.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Preprocessor -> Backbone Classifier (WHT-ResNet50) -> Transfer Learning Pipeline -> Inference Aggregator
- **Critical path:** Data Pipeline (Peatland patches) -> Pretrained GWFP Model Weights -> Fine-tune on Peatland Patches -> Deploy WHT-ResNet50 for patch inference -> Aggregate to image/video decision.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Patch Overlap vs. Compute:** Higher overlap (50%) improves small-object detection but increases inference compute ~4x. Tradeoff: latency vs. sensitivity.
  - **WHT Depth vs. Representation:** Replacing all convolutions (HTMA-ResNet50) maximizes parameter reduction but may hurt small-dataset performance. Partial replacement (WHT-ResNet50) balances efficiency and accuracy.
  - **Transfer Learning Scope:** Fine-tuning all layers adapts thoroughly but risks overfitting on small data and forgetting general features. Common practice: freeze early layers, fine-tune later layers/classifier.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Persistent false negatives on low-contrast smoke: Likely indicates WHT layers discarding critical low-frequency spatial info or incompatible pretrained features.
  - Patch-level inconsistency (e.g., high fire probability in one patch, zero in adjacent patch with same smoke): Suggests insufficient patch overlap or boundary artifacts.
  - Degradation after transfer learning (target performance < source-only performance): Negative transfer; source/target domains too dissimilar. May require more target data, less aggressive fine-tuning (smaller LR, fewer unfrozen layers), or different source model.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Replication:** Train standard ResNet50 from scratch on peatland dataset (no patches, no WHT, no TL). Confirm baseline ~71% accuracy.
  2. **Transfer Learning Only:** Fine-tune standard ResNet50 (pretrained on GWFP) on peatland dataset using patch pipeline. Verify jump to ~89% accuracy/F1, isolating TL's effect.
  3. **WHT Integration Test:** Using transfer learning, compare full ResNet50 vs. WHT-ResNet50 on same peatland fine-tuning data. Verify incremental gain to ~91.5% F1 and note parameter count difference.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How well does the transfer learning approach generalize to peatland fires in geographic regions outside of Malaysia?
- **Basis in paper:** The study uses a dataset exclusively from Malaysian peatlands. Regional variations may affect model performance.
- **Why unresolved:** No testing on other countries' peatland data; generalizability unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluation on diverse international peatland fire datasets with cross-region performance metrics.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific visual features does the Walsh-Hadamard Transform (WHT) enhance that enable better detection of low-contrast smoke?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper states WHT-ResNet50 achieves higher accuracy but lacks feature-level analysis.
- **Why unresolved:** No interpretability studies (e.g., feature map visualization) to explain the mechanism.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Visualization of activated regions/feature maps from WHT layers vs. standard layers, especially for low-contrast smoke examples.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is the proposed model suitable for real-time monitoring on edge devices, considering computational efficiency and latency?
- **Basis in paper:** Authors claim practicality and scalability but provide no empirical timing/resource usage data.
- **Why unresolved:** Inference speed, memory footprint, and power consumption on target hardware are unmeasured.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmarks of inference time, FPS, and power draw on representative edge hardware vs. competing models.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How robust is the model to extreme or unseen variations in smoke density, occlusion, and illumination in real-world peatland fires?
- **Basis in paper:** Tests robustness under "challenging conditions" but dataset diversity in these conditions is unspecified.
- **Why unresolved:** Performance on rare/severe cases not evaluated; test set may not cover full spectrum of real-world extremes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Stress testing with augmented data simulating extreme conditions, or evaluation on a dataset with annotated difficulty levels.

## Limitations
- **Dataset Access:** The proprietary Malaysian peatland dataset prevents direct replication and independent verification; proxy datasets may not capture identical subtle smoke characteristics.
- **Architectural Specifics:** Exact WHT-ResNet50 implementation (which layers replaced, scaling layer details, kernel handling) is unspecified, requiring author clarification or reverse-engineering.
- **Pretraining Source:** GWFP dataset composition (sensor types, fire types, geographic diversity) relative to peatland fires is unclear, affecting assessed domain shift.

## Confidence
- **Transfer Learning Efficacy:** Medium Confidence. Strong internal evidence (21% F1 gain) but contingent on unverified domain similarity; external literature supports TL for wildfires but not peatland-specific transfer.
- **WHT-Enhanced Architecture Benefit:** Low-to-Medium Confidence. Standalone improvement shown (77.2% vs. 70.1% F1), but incremental gain with TL (~3% F1) could be within variance; break condition (loss of spatial detail) not experimentally ruled out.
- **Patch-Based Processing Necessity:** High Confidence. The risk of losing subtle smoke during resizing is logically sound and commonly addressed; tiling logic is clear and reproducible.
