# Metabolic cost of information processing in Poisson variational autoencoders

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13421  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13421  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Poisson VAEs intrinsically link coding rate to firing rate, enabling a tunable metabolic cost (sparsity) vs. reconstruction fidelity trade-off; Gaussian VAEs lack this coupling.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates energy-efficient computation in biologically plausible neural models by comparing Poisson variational autoencoders (P-VAE) with Gaussian VAEs using ReLU nonlinearities (Grelu-VAE). The key theoretical finding is that in P-VAE, the KL divergence term in the free energy objective is proportional to the learnable prior firing rates, creating an explicit metabolic cost that penalizes high baseline activity. This coupling makes the coding rate (information processing) directly tied to the biophysical variable of firing rate. Empirically, across a grid of KL weight β and latent dimensionality K on natural image patches, P-VAE shows a dramatic reduction in metabolic cost (up to 100×) and a monotonic increase in sparsity (from ~10% to >95%) as β increases. In contrast, Grelu-VAE exhibits no change in metabolic cost and its sparsity saturates at ~50%. Despite these differences, both models achieve comparable reconstruction accuracy at matched sparsity levels. The results position Poisson variational inference as a principled framework for theories of resource-constrained computation, where abstract information‑theoretic objectives naturally incorporate metabolic constraints.

## Method Summary
The authors train two types of variational autoencoders on small natural image patches: a Poisson VAE (P-VAE) where latent variables follow Poisson distributions and the decoder is linear, and a Gaussian VAE with ReLU nonlinearity (Grelu-VAE). Both models use a factorized approximate posterior and a linear decoder. The training objective is the variational free energy ℱ = ℒ_recon + β𝒟_KL, where β controls the trade‑off between reconstruction and KL divergence. ℒ_recon consists of a mean squared error term and a variance penalty derived from the linear decoder. For P-VAE, the KL divergence simplifies to λ₀ᵀf(δλ), where λ₀ are learnable prior firing rates and δλ are residuals; this term directly scales with λ₀, introducing a metabolic cost proportional to the coding rate. For Grelu-VAE, the KL is independent of prior mean. The authors sweep β from 0.01 to 8.0 and latent dimensionality K from 64 to 2048, measuring metabolic cost as mean spike count (average latent activity) and sparsity as fraction of zero spikes. They compare reconstruction accuracy (R²) at matched sparsity levels.

## Key Results
- In P-VAE, increasing β reduces metabolic cost by up to ~100× and monotonically increases sparsity from ~10% to >95%.
- Grelu-VAE shows no change in metabolic cost with β, and its sparsity saturates at ~50% regardless of β.
- At comparable sparsity levels, both models achieve similar reconstruction accuracy, but only P-VAE supports a smooth fidelity–energy trade‑off via β.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The KL divergence term in the Poisson VAE free energy objective is linearly proportional to the learnable prior firing rates (λ₀), creating an emergent metabolic cost that penalizes high baseline activity.
- **Mechanism:** Under a residual parameterization, the Poisson KL simplifies to λ₀ᵀf(δλ). Since f(·) ≥ 0, minimizing free energy directly reduces λ₀ when β is increased, making "silence cheap."
- **Core assumption:** Latent variables follow Poisson distributions, and the decoder is linear (or moments are tractable).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "the KL term of the free energy is proportional to prior firing rates, introducing a metabolic cost"
  - [page 3, eqs. (8)-(9)] Closed‑form derivation showing Poisson KL ∝ λ₀, Gaussian KL independent of prior mean.
  - [page 4, information geometry] "For the Poisson, the natural parameter is log λ, and the Fisher information metric ... yields a curvature scaled by the rate itself."
- **Break condition:** If the prior λ₀ is fixed and not learnable, the coupling to metabolic cost is static and cannot adapt to trade‑offs.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Only Poisson variational inference couples an abstract information‑theoretic quantity (coding rate/KL) to a concrete biophysical variable (firing rate), enabling a fidelity‑energy trade‑off.
- **Mechanism:** The KL divergence is the unique measure of information gain (Hobson, 1969). In the Poisson case, this information gain is multiplied by λ₀, converting bits‑per‑inference into spikes‑per‑inference.
- **Core assumption:** Information‑theoretic interpretation of KL as coding rate holds, and firing rate is a valid proxy for metabolic expenditure.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [page 5] "KL as a unique measure of information gain" and "Poisson KL: Information Rate ↔ Metabolic Rate"
  - [page 2] "This structure couples an abstract information‑theoretic quantity—the coding rate—to a concrete biophysical variable—the firing rate"
- **Break condition:** If metabolic cost does not scale linearly with firing rate in the biological substrate, the coupling is not metabolically meaningful.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The variance penalty from the linear decoder reconstruction term also promotes sparsity in both models, but only Poisson has a *direct* metabolic penalty in the KL term.
- **Mechanism:** For Poisson, Var[q(z)] = λ, so dimensions with large decoder weights (diag(ΦᵀΦ)ᵢ) are pressured to lower λ. For Grelu-VAE, rectification couples mean (μ) and variance, allowing μ → -∞ to silence units.
- **Core assumption:** Decoder is linear and likelihood is Gaussian with unit variance; posterior is factorized.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [page 6, eqs. (17)-(19)] Derivation of reconstruction loss with mean and variance penalties.
  - [page 7, Table 1] Summary showing m = v = λ for Poisson; for Grelu-VAE, m and v are functions of μ and σ that vanish as μ → -∞.
  - [page 6, Figure 3] Visualization of rectified Gaussian moments showing suppression via μ.
- **Break condition:** If the decoder is nonlinear or the likelihood non‑Gaussian, the closed‑form variance penalty may not hold, altering sparsity pressures.

## Foundational Learning

**Variational Inference & ELBO**  
- **Why needed here:** The entire framework optimizes ℱ = ℒ_recon + β𝒟_KL; understanding how ELBO decomposes into reconstruction and KL (coding rate) is essential.  
- **Quick check question:** Why does maximizing the ELBO minimize the original KL divergence to the true posterior?

**Exponential Family & Natural Parameters**  
- **Why needed here:** The key difference between Poisson and Gaussian emerges from their natural parameters (log λ vs. μ/σ²) and the resulting Fisher information metric.  
- **Quick check question:** For an exponential family distribution, why is the KL divergence locally quadratic in the natural parameters?

**Rate‑Distortion Theory Interpretation**  
- **Why needed here:** 𝒟_KL is the information rate (coding cost); the paper re‑frames β as trading off rate (information/metabolic cost) against distortion (reconstruction).  
- **Quick check question:** In the β‑VAE objective ℒ = ℒ_task + β𝒟_KL, what does β control in the rate‑distortion trade‑off?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Input x → Encoder (linear) → latent parameters (λ for Poisson; μ, σ for Grelu) → Sample z → h = identity(z) (Poisson) or h = ReLU(z) (Grelu) → Linear decoder Φ → Reconstruction loss: ||x - Φm||² + diag(ΦᵀΦ)ᵀv → KL divergence (Poisson: λ₀ᵀf(δλ); Grelu: ½[(δμ²/σ₀²) + g(δσ²)]) → Free energy ℱ = ℒ_recon + β𝒟_KL → Backpropagation (Poisson uses EAT relaxation).

### Critical Path
1. Input x → encoder → λ or (μ, σ)  
2. Sample z from q(z|x)  
3. Compute h = z (Poisson) or h = relu(z) (Gaussian)  
4. Compute reconstruction loss: mean penalty ||x - Φm||² and variance penalty diag(ΦᵀΦ)ᵀv  
5. Compute KL divergence: λ₀ᵀf(δλ) or ½[(δμ²/σ₀²) + g(δσ²)]  
6. Sum weighted terms ℱ = ℒ_recon + β𝒟_KL  
7. Backpropagate gradients (note: Poisson requires special gradient estimator like EAT relaxation).

### Design Tradeoffs
- Poisson: Direct metabolic cost coupling (λ₀ in KL) enables tunable sparsity via β, but requires discrete sampling and custom gradients.  
- Grelu-VAE: Simpler continuous reparametrization, but KL is decoupled from firing rate; sparsity limited to ~50% via rectification variance penalty.  
- Linear decoder: Enables closed‑form losses but limits representation capacity vs. nonlinear decoders.

### Failure Signatures
- Poisson model fails to reduce metabolic cost/sparsity with β: Check if λ₀ are truly learnable and not collapsing to zero; verify gradient flow through Poisson sampler.  
- Grelu-VAE shows no sparsity change with β: Expected behavior; indicates KL term does not act as metabolic cost.  
- Both models have poor reconstruction: May indicate insufficient latent dimensionality K or overly strong β causing posterior collapse (excessive silencing).

### First 3 Experiments
1. Sweep β and K: Train models across β ∈ {0.01,...,8.0} and K ∈ {64,...,2048}; measure mean activity (MC) and sparsity (P_Z).  
2. Match sparsity comparison: For P_Z ≈ 0.5 (Grelu-VAE plateau), find β in Poisson achieving same P_Z; compare reconstruction R².  
3. Analyze λ₀ evolution: Track learnable prior rates λ₀ during training; confirm they decrease with higher β in Poisson but remain stable in Grelu-VAE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does the Poisson KL's metabolic cost coupling extend to other count distributions like negative binomial or gamma?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Authors mention empirical tests for these are future work (page 8).  
- **Why unresolved:** Only Poisson vs Gaussian ReLU compared; other distributions not experimentally validated.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train NB-VAE and gamma-VAE, derive closed‑form KL proportionality, run β sweeps, measure sparsity and metabolic cost trends.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does Poisson variational inference preserve the coupling when applied to non‑perceptual tasks (e.g., decision‑making) with non‑Gaussian likelihoods?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Authors claim extension beyond perception (page 8) but provide no experiments.  
- **Why unresolved:** All experiments on image reconstruction; unknown for tasks like behavior or cognition.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement Poisson VAE in reinforcement learning or motor tasks, vary β, assess sparsity/cost trade‑off and task performance.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How do biological constraints on prior firing rates (e.g., minimum λ0) affect the sparsity‑metabolic trade‑off in Poisson VAE?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Model learns λ0 unconstrained; biology imposes lower bounds on firing rates not considered.  
- **Why unresolved:** λ0→0 may be biologically implausible; effect on trade‑off unknown.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Impose λ0 ≥ λ_min based on neural data, retrain models, compare sparsity and cost across β to unconstrained case.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How do nonlinear decoders impact the metabolic cost structure and sparsity induction in Poisson VAEs?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Analysis used linear decoder; nonlinear decoders common in practice but not tested.  
- **Why unresolved:** Reconstruction loss interactions may differ, altering the balance between KL and sparsity pressures.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train Poisson VAE with MLP decoder, approximate reconstruction via sampling, test β sweep for monotonic sparsity/cost changes.

## Limitations
- The analysis relies on a linear decoder and Gaussian likelihood, which may not generalize to nonlinear architectures, altering sparsity pressures.  
- Metabolic cost is simplified as mean spike count, ignoring biophysical details like nonlinear energy consumption or refractory effects.  
- Experiments are limited to small natural image patches; results may not scale to larger datasets or other tasks.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical coupling (Poisson KL ∝ λ₀):** High  
- **Empirical β‑driven cost/sparsity effects:** High  
- **Uniqueness of Poisson for fidelity‑energy trade‑off:** Medium  
- **Generality to nonlinear architectures/datasets:** Low
