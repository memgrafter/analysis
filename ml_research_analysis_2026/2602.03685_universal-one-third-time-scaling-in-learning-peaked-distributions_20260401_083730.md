# Universal One-third Time Scaling in Learning Peaked Distributions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03685
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03685
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Training loss in language models with a softmax output and cross-entropy loss decays as a universal power law \(L \sim \tau^{-1/3}\) when the target next-token distribution is sufficiently peaked (high inverse temperature), independent of data details.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the origin of the slow power-law convergence observed in language model training loss. By isolating the final linear layer (LM head) as a one-layer teacher-student model with softmax output and cross-entropy loss, the authors show via gradient flow analysis and a low-temperature expansion that learning a peaked target distribution forces the loss to decay as \(L \sim \tau^{-1/3}\), where \(\tau\) is dynamic time. The exponent \(1/3\) emerges from the algebraic vanishing of the gradient in the low-temperature regime and is independent of specific data properties. Empirical validation on Pythia models confirms that next-token distributions are indeed peaked (effective inverse temperature \(\beta^* \approx 6.87\)) and that the training loss follows a \(1/3\) scaling across model sizes. Logit standard deviation initially grows as \(\tau^{1/3}\) but later saturates, indicating that the latter phase of loss decay is driven by parameter rotation rather than norm growth.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a minimal teacher-student model: a teacher generates target probabilities via \(\text{softmax}(\beta^* W^* x)\) with \(x \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\), and a student learns with a linear layer and cross-entropy loss. They analyze continuous-time gradient flow and perform a low-temperature asymptotic expansion of the free energy for large \(\beta\) (peaked targets). This yields analytical expressions showing the loss \(L\) and effective temperature \(\beta\) follow \(L \sim \tau^{-1/3}\) and \(\beta \sim \tau^{1/3}\) under an aligned ansatz where student weights stay parallel to the teacher’s. The theory is extended to regimes where alignment fails, predicting the same exponent due to rotational dynamics with power-law gradients. For empirical validation, the authors load Pythia checkpoints, evaluate cross-entropy loss on held-out FineWeb tokens, compute logit standard deviation as a proxy for \(\beta\), and fit power laws \(L - L_\infty \propto \tau^{-\alpha_\tau}\) and \(\beta \propto \tau^{\gamma}\). They also estimate the teacher’s inverse temperature \(\beta^*\) from next-token entropy.

## Key Results
- Theoretical derivation demonstrates that in the low-temperature regime (\(\beta^* > \sqrt{2}\log n\)), the loss decays as \(L \sim \tau^{-1/3}\) regardless of data structure.
- Empirical analysis of Pythia models shows next-token distributions are peaked (\(\beta^* \approx 6.87 > \sqrt{2}\log n \approx 4.55\)) and the fitted scaling exponent \(\alpha_\tau \approx 0.33\) is universal across model sizes.
- Logit standard deviation grows initially with exponent \(\approx 1/3\) and later saturates, implying that later-stage loss reduction originates from parameter rotation rather than norm increase.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Aligned Student Dynamics in Low-Temperature Regime
- **Claim:** When the student's weight aligns with the teacher’s (\(W \propto W^*\)), the high-dimensional dynamics collapse to a scalar inverse temperature \(\beta\) obeying \(d\beta/d\tau \propto -dL/d\beta\). Low-temperature expansion of the free energy then yields \(L \sim \tau^{-1/3}\).
- **Mechanism:** For a peaked target (large \(\beta^*\)), the free energy \(F(\beta)\) has the asymptotic form \(F(\beta) = -c_0 - c_1/\beta - c_2/\beta^2 + \dots\). The internal energy \(U(\beta) = -c_0 + c_2/\beta^2 + \dots\) leads to a loss \(L \approx c_2/\beta\) for intermediate \(\beta \ll \beta^*\). Combining with \(d\beta/d\tau \propto -dL/d\beta \approx c_2 \beta^{-2}\) gives \(\beta \sim \tau^{1/3}\) and thus \(L \sim \tau^{-1/3}\).
- **Core assumption:** The student remains aligned throughout training (\(W = (1/\sqrt{m})\widehat{W} \beta(t)\)) and operates in the intermediate low-temperature regime (\(\beta\) large but \(\beta \ll \beta^*\)).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "softmax function (Boltzmann distribution) and cross-entropy loss together lead to power-law vanishing losses and gradients... exponent of 1/3." [section 3.1] Formal derivation and Eq. (9,17–19) show \(\beta \sim \tau^{1/3}\) and \(L \sim \tau^{-1/3}\). [section 4, Fig. 6b,c] LLM loss and logit std follow 1/3 scaling; inferred \(\beta^* > c_0\) confirms low-temperature regime.
- **Break condition:** Alignment fails initially (large initialization) or later (large weight decay causing misalignment), or temperature is too high (flat distributions) where the expansion breaks down.

### Mechanism 2: Parameter Rotation Power Laws Beyond Alignment
- **Claim:** Even when the aligned ansatz fails (student not parallel to teacher), loss can still decay as \(L \sim \tau^{-1/3}\) due to rotational dynamics in the low-temperature regime.
- **Mechanism:** In the low-temperature limit, softmax/cross-entropy produces power-law vanishing gradients for all parameter directions, not just the aligned one. If the student is "similar but not too close" to the teacher, parameter rotation driven by these power-law gradients can dominate loss decay with the same exponent.
- **Core assumption:** The student is in the low-temperature regime (large \(\beta\)) and neither perfectly aligned nor too misaligned (intermediate regime), with gradient flow approximately followed.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3, Fig. 5c,d] With weight decay, \(\beta\) saturates while loss still follows \(L \sim \tau^{-1/3}\), implying rotation-driven decay. [section 6] "loss decrease at the later stage is then mainly due to rotation of parameters rather than norm growth."
- **Break condition:** Student is too misaligned (large initial norm) leading to faster decay; or too aligned (\(\beta \approx \beta^*\)) where dynamics become exponential.

### Mechanism 3: Intrinsic Non-Linearity of Softmax/Cross-Entropy for Peaked Targets
- **Claim:** The combination of softmax output and cross-entropy loss *inherently* produces power-law loss and gradients when the target distribution is peaked (low entropy), independent of data structure.
- **Mechanism:** For a peaked target (large \(\beta^*\)), the partition function \(Z(\beta)\) and its derivatives have low-temperature asymptotics. The loss \(L(\beta) \sim (\beta^* - \beta)/\beta\) for \(\beta \to \infty\) (Eq. 15), and gradient \(-dL/d\beta \sim \beta^{-2}\). This algebraic vanishing of the gradient is the fundamental bottleneck forcing power-law time scaling upon integration.
- **Core assumption:** Target distribution is sufficiently peaked (\(\beta^* > c_0 \approx \sqrt{2}\log n\)); student operates in low-temperature regime; gradient flow dynamics.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "softmax and cross-entropy can lead to power-law training without power laws in data." [section 3.2, Eq. 13–19] Low-temperature expansion derivation of power-law loss and gradient. [section 4, Fig. 6a] LLM next-token entropy \(\leq 1.69\) implies \(\beta^* \approx 6.87 > c_0 \approx 4.55\), confirming peakedness.
- **Break condition:** Target distribution is flat (small \(\beta^*\)), where high-temperature expansion shows exponential convergence (Eq. 60–63).

## Foundational Learning
- **Gradient Flow Dynamics:** The theory models continuous-time training dynamics (\(dW/d\tau = -c_{\text{eff}} \nabla L\)) to derive scaling exponents. This ODE approximation of discrete optimization (taking infinitesimal steps) is essential for analytical integration and power-law extraction.

This expansion captures how the loss depends on the inverse temperature and reveals the critical constants (e.g., \(c_0 \approx \sqrt{2}\log n\)).  
  *Quick check:* What does the constant \(c_0 \approx \sqrt{2}\log n\) represent in the free energy expansion, and how is it related to extreme value statistics?
- **Teacher-Student Model & Aligned Ansatz:** The toy model uses a teacher generating \(\text{softmax}(\beta^*W^*x)\) and a student learning it. The aligned ansatz (\(W \propto W^*\)) reduces a high-dimensional problem to a 1D \(\beta\) dynamics, enabling analytical solution.  
  *Quick check:* Under what conditions might the aligned ansatz hold approximately during actual LLM training despite random initialization?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  LM head → isolated as one linear layer + softmax + cross-entropy loss (toy model).  
  Backbone/transformer layers → treated as a black box providing hidden states \(x \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\).  
  Optimizer (Adam/SGD) → mapped to gradient flow with effective constant \(c_{\text{eff}}\); learning rate controls \(\tau\) via \(d\tau = \eta dt\).  
  Weight decay → introduces additional force \(\propto -\omega W\), can cause misalignment.
- **Critical path:** Isolate LM head → analyze toy model (theory + small-scale experiments) → verify peakedness & 1/3 scaling in real LLMs (Pythia). The mechanism's validity depends on the LM head being the bottleneck.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Alignment vs rotation: Early training favors alignment (large updates on small norms). Later, weight decay or small LR may favor rotation. The 1/3 scaling holds in the intermediate "similar but not too close" regime.
  - Learning rate: Must be small enough to follow gradient flow (\(\tau\) well-defined) but large enough to avoid stagnation. Optimal LR balances noise and alignment speed.
  - Architecture extension: Adding more layers (residual MLPs) before the head does not break 1/3 scaling if logits still follow effective gradient flow.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High-temperature target: Next-token distribution too flat (\(\beta^* < c_0\)) → exponential, not power-law, decay.
  - Misalignment regime: Student far from teacher (large init norm) → faster than 1/3 decay; or too close (\(\beta \approx \beta^*\)) → exponential finish.
  - Large learning rate or noise: Prevents alignment/rotation, loss decay becomes noisy and slower than 1/3.
  - Architectural avoidance: If future architectures avoid full-vocabulary softmax (e.g., hierarchical output), the low-temperature mechanism may not apply.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Toy model temperature scan:** Vary \(\beta^*\) (teacher peakedness) at fixed LR. Verify: (a) power-law loss only for large \(\beta^*\); (b) fitted exponent → 1/3 as \(\beta^*\) increases.
  2. **Adam vs SGD dynamics:** Compare loss and \(\beta\) vs \(\tau\) across LR ranges. Verify: (a) SGD curves collapse on \(\tau\), following theory; (b) Adam requires smaller LR to collapse (follow gradient flow).
  3. **LLM logit analysis:** Measure logit std. (\(\beta(t)\)) and loss \(L(t)\) across Pythia checkpoints. Verify: (a) \(\beta^*\) inferred from next-token entropy > \(c_0\); (b) \(L - L_\infty\) vs \(\tau\) has exponent ≈ 0.33; (c) \(\beta(t) \sim \tau^{1/3}\) initially, then saturates.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Stochasticity effects:** How do stochastic gradients (finite batch size) and large learning rates affect the 1/3 time scaling exponent? The current theory assumes deterministic gradient flow; real training uses stochastic optimizers with noise that could alter the power-law dynamics.
- **Rotation origin:** Why does parameter rotation (rather than norm growth) also produce 1/3 time scaling, as observed with weight decay? The aligned student ansatz cannot explain this, and the dynamics of misaligned weights in the low-temperature regime remain incompletely understood.
- **Depth robustness:** Does the 1/3 scaling law hold for deeper teacher-student networks with softmax output, and how does network depth affect the intermediate regime? The theory is derived for a one-layer LM head, and extending it to multi-layer networks is an open challenge.

## Limitations
- **Proxy assumptions:** Mapping LLM logit standard deviation to an effective inverse temperature \(\beta^*\) assumes Gaussian-distributed logits, which may not hold in practice.
- **Dynamic time (\(\tau\)) computation:** For Pythia models, \(\tau\) requires unreported learning-rate scheduler details, limiting precise reproduction.
- **Universality scope:** The mechanism is restricted to full-softmax architectures and sufficiently peaked targets; it may fail for hierarchical output layers, alternative loss functions, or flat distributions.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical derivation (aligned ansatz, low-temperature expansion):** High  
- **Empirical validation on LLMs (power-law fits, \(\beta^*\) estimation):** Medium  
- **Extension to other settings (stochasticity, depth, non-softmax architectures):** Low
