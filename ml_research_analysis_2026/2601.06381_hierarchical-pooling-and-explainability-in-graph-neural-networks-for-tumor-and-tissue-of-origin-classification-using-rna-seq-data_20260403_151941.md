# Hierarchical Pooling and Explainability in Graph Neural Networks for Tumor and Tissue-of-Origin Classification Using RNA-seq Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06381
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06381
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A GNN with hierarchical pooling achieved a 0.978 F1-macro score on tumor vs. normal classification using RNA-seq and a STRING PPI network, with performance dropping as model depth increased.

## Executive Summary
This study explores graph neural networks (GNNs) that integrate RNA-seq gene expression data with a STRING protein-protein interaction (PPI) network for tumor/normal and tissue-of-origin classification. The core method uses Chebyshev graph convolutions and hierarchical graph pooling to aggregate genes into biologically meaningful supernodes, reducing dimensionality while preserving network structure. Counterintuitively, the best performance was achieved with a single pooling layer; deeper models suffered from over-smoothing. The approach's major strength lies in its interpretability: gradient-based saliency analysis on both input genes and pooled supernode embeddings successfully highlighted known cancer drivers and relevant biological processes.

## Method Summary
The pipeline starts with TCGA RNA-seq UQ-FPKM data and a STRING v11.6 PPI network for *Homo sapiens*. Genes are mapped to network proteins, retaining the largest connected component (~14,133 nodes). Graph coarsening is pre-computed via heavy-edge matching, generating 7 levels of cluster assignments (final ~111 supernodes, ~128 genes each). For each coarsening level, the model applies a Chebyshev convolution (K=2) followed by a weighted pooling layer that learns to aggregate node features into supernode embeddings. These embeddings are flattened and passed to a fully-connected classifier (one hidden layer with 256 units, dropout, batch norm). The model is trained with cross-entropy loss and evaluated using stratified F1-macro due to class imbalance.

## Key Results
- Highest test F1-macro (0.978) achieved with **a single pooling layer**; performance degraded monotonically with additional layers.
- Performance drop attributed to **over-smoothing**, where repeated graph convolutions homogenize node embeddings.
- **Gradient-based saliency maps** on input genes identified established cancer-related genes (e.g., *CTNNB1*, *LATS1*).
- Saliency analysis on **supernode embeddings** revealed clusters enriched for biologically relevant cancer processes (e.g., steroid metabolic process).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Increasing hierarchical coarsening and convolution layers degrades performance due to over-smoothing.
- **Mechanism:** Repeated neighborhood aggregation in graph convolutions causes node feature representations to converge toward similar values, diminishing discriminative power.
- **Core Assumption:** Graph convolutions mix node features with neighbors; excessive mixing leads to homogeneity.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.1 report downward performance trend with more layers and explicitly cite over-smoothing as the explanation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Gradient-based saliency on input genes highlights biologically relevant cancer biomarkers.
- **Mechanism:** The gradient of the class score w.r.t. input gene expression magnitudes indicates which genes most influence the classification decision.
- **Core Assumption:** Model relies on a consistent, diagnostically relevant subset of genes per class.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract notes identification of known cancer genes; Section 4.2.1 lists top saliency genes with established roles in literature.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Saliency on supernode embeddings reveals interpretable biological processes linked to tumor biology.
- **Mechanism:** Hierarchical pooling aggregates genes into clusters; gradient analysis on these aggregated embeddings identifies which gene groups (and their enriched GO terms) are most influential.
- **Core Assumption:** Pre-computed clusters (via heavy-edge matching) correspond to functionally coherent gene sets.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states hierarchical pooling provided tumor biology insights; Section 4.2.2 reports enriched GO terms like "steroid metabolic process" in high-saliency supernodes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Graph Neural Network Message Passing**
  - *Why needed here:* Chebyshev convolution (ChebConv) aggregates features from a gene's immediate neighbors in the PPI network, directly incorporating protein interactions.
  - *Quick check:* How does a ChebConv layer with K=2 differ from standard GCN in terms of receptive field and polynomial approximation?

- **Concept: Hierarchical Graph Pooling**
  - *Why needed here:* Pooling reduces the graph from ~14k genes to ~111 supernodes, aiming to capture higher-order gene modules while lowering computational cost.
  - *Quick check:* What is the trade-off between using pre-computed, topology-based clusters versus learned, differentiable pooling (e.g., DiffPool)?

- **Concept: Over-smoothing in Deep GNNs**
  - *Why needed here:* Explains the observed performance collapse when adding more convolution/pooling layers; it's a fundamental limitation of deep GNNs.
  - *Quick check:* What architectural strategies (e.g., skip connections, jumping knowledge) or training tricks can mitigate over-smoothing?

- **Concept: Gradient-based Saliency (Vanilla Grad)**
  - *Why needed here:* The primary tool for model interpretation, applied to both raw gene inputs and pooled supernode embeddings to identify important features.
  - *Quick check:* Why might saliency maps be noisy or misleading for highly non-linear models, and what are common pitfalls like gradient saturation?

## Architecture Onboarding
Input (gene expression) -> [ChebConv (K=2) -> Weighted Pooling -> ReLU] x L -> Flatten -> FC (256) -> Dropout/BatchNorm -> Output (sigmoid/softmax)

- **Critical Path:** RNA-seq features are processed through L coarsening levels (each convolution + pooling), then all supernode embeddings are concatenated and fed to a shallow classifier.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - *Fixed vs. Learnable Clustering:* Pre-computed heavy-edge matching is computationally stable but cannot adapt clusters to the classification task.
  - *Convolution Depth:* Adding layers risks over-smoothing; paper finds L=1 optimal.
  - *Saliency Granularity:* Interpretation can target individual genes (input gradients) or aggregated biological processes (supernode gradients + ORA).
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - Performance degrades sharply when L>1 (over-smoothing).
  - Interpretability fails if supernode clusters are too large/small for meaningful over-representation analysis (ORA).
  - Early-layer convolutions on the full ~14k-node graph may become a computational bottleneck.
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. Vary coarsening depth L=0..6. Expect peak F1-macro at L=1, decline thereafter.
  2. Ablate convolutions: For a fixed L, remove ChebConv layers to measure their contribution to preserving signal during pooling (performance should drop).
  3. Interpretability validation: For the best model (L=1), compute gene saliency ranks and test overlap with COSMIC Cancer Gene Census; perform ORA on top-saliency supernodes to recover cancer-related GO terms.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Over-smoothing causes:** What specific factors in RNA-seq/PPI integration cause deeper GNNs to over-smooth? The paper notes the effect but does not probe mechanisms like graph topology or feature initialization.
- **Trainable pooling:** Would differentiable, task-specific graph pooling improve performance and/or biological interpretability compared to fixed hierarchical clustering?
- **Disentangling processes:** How to separate multiple over-represented biological processes within a single high-saliency supernode to identify which are most relevant for classification?
- **Feature vs. interaction importance:** Is a supernode's high saliency driven by the intrinsic expression of its constituent genes or by its interactions with neighboring supernodes after convolutions?

## Limitations
- **Missing training details:** Critical hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, dropout rate, epochs, early stopping) are not specified.
- **Unclear data splits:** Exact cohort sizes in train/val/test splits and stratification strategy are not detailed.
- **Preprocessing ambiguities:** STRING edge weighting (raw scores vs. thresholded) and any gene filtering criteria beyond missingness are unspecified.

## Confidence
- **Over-smoothing explanation:** High (explicitly stated, consistent with GNN literature and experimental trend).
- **Saliency gene identification:** Medium (top genes are known cancer drivers, but independent validation against a gene census is not reported).
- **Supernode process enrichment:** Medium (enriched GO terms are biologically plausible, but depends on coherence of fixed clusters and ORA method).

## Next Checks
1. **Quantify over-smoothing:** Compute Dirichlet energy or average pairwise cosine similarity of node embeddings across pooling levels on a held-out set; expect monotonic increase correlating with F1 drop.
2. **Validate saliency genes:** Perform Fisher’s exact test for overlap between top-N saliency-ranked genes and the COSMIC Cancer Gene Census.
3. **Replicate supernode enrichment:** Using the provided cluster assignments, rerun ORA with an alternative tool (e.g., clusterProfiler) to confirm rediscovery of terms like “steroid metabolic process.”
