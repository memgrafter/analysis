# Total Recall QA: A Verifiable Evaluation Suite for Deep Research Agents
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.18516
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18516
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** Even with perfect retrieval (oracle), state-of-the-art LLMs like GPT-5.2 achieve at most 56.80% exact match on the simplest dataset, with over 90% of errors stemming from multi-document reasoning failures, not retrieval or memorization.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Total Recall QA (TRQA), a novel evaluation framework and benchmark suite designed to assess Deep Research Agents (DRAs) on the core challenge of retrieving *all* relevant documents to synthesize a correct answer. It achieves verifiable, large-scale benchmark construction by pairing a structured knowledge base (KB) with a text corpus via a deterministic entity-to-document mapping. This allows automatic generation of aggregation-based queries (e.g., `COUNT`, `MEDIAN`) whose answers require total recall. Three datasets are released: TRQA-Wiki1 (169 queries), TRQA-Wiki2 (1258), and a synthetic TRQA-Ecommerce (900) to eliminate data contamination. Comprehensive evaluation reveals that existing retrieval models exhibit low recall (BM25 R@1000 ≤ 67.10%), and DRAs perform poorly (<10.65% EM on Wiki1). Notably, even with oracle retrieval providing all necessary passages, top models fail dramatically (e.g., GPT-5.2: 56.80% EM on Wiki1, 1.67% on Ecommerce), with the vast majority of errors attributed to failures in multi-document reasoning.

## Method Summary
The framework constructs benchmarks by first sampling a filtered set of entities (`E'`) from a structured KB (Wikidata or synthetic). A one-to-one mapping links each entity to a document in an unstructured corpus. A query is generated as a natural language instruction to compute an aggregation (`θ`, e.g., `SUM`, `MEDIAN`) over a shared property (`p_j`) of all entities in `E'`. The gold answer is the deterministic result of `θ` applied to the complete set of property values. An LLM-based annotator verifies that each relevant corpus passage contains its corresponding property value, performing rewrites if necessary to ensure alignment. Evaluation measures retrieval via entity-level recall and end-to-end answer accuracy via exact match (EM) and soft match (SM) on normalized numerical answers. The TRQA-Ecommerce dataset uses a fully synthetic product KB and blog corpus, created via LLM-guided generation, to guarantee zero training-set contamination.

## Key Results
*   Retrieval models struggle with total recall: BM25's best R@1000 is 67.10% (Wiki2), demonstrating the difficulty of retrieving the complete relevant set.
*   DRAs perform poorly in end-to-end settings: Search-R1 achieves only 10.65% EM on TRQA-Wiki1, highlighting the combined challenge of retrieval and synthesis.
*   Reasoning is the primary bottleneck: With oracle retrieval, GPT-5.2 reaches 56.80% EM on Wiki1 but only 1.67% on Ecommerce; over 90% of oracle errors are reasoning failures, not retrieval or parametric bias.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Entity-centric pairing of a structured knowledge base (KB) and text corpus enables automatic, verifiable generation of total recall queries with gold relevance judgments.
*   **Mechanism:** A filtered set of entities (`E'`) from the KB defines the complete relevant set. For each entity, a corresponding document exists in the corpus. Queries are natural language formulations of an aggregation operation (e.g., `MEDIAN`, `COUNT`) over a shared property of all entities in `E'`. Answer correctness requires retrieving *all* documents linked to entities in `E'` and synthesizing their property values.
*   **Core assumption:** A one-to-one mapping between KB entities and corpus documents can be established and maintained, allowing passage-level relevance to be derived deterministically from entity membership.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "framework constructs single-answer, total recall queries with precise evaluation and relevance judgments derived from a structured knowledge base paired with a text corpus"; [section 2.1] "each entity in the structured knowledge base has a corresponding document in the corpus... enables straightforward relevance labeling".
*   **Break condition:** Inconsistent or missing KB-corpus links for an entity break the guarantee that retrieved passages contain the required property value, requiring manual rewrite/addition steps (section 2.1).

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Synthetic data generation (TRQA-Ecommerce) isolates evaluation from LLM parametric memory and data contamination.
*   **Mechanism:** A randomized product knowledge base and corpus are generated from scratch using LLM-guided metadata creation and blog synthesis. The model has no prior exposure to these specific products or their textual descriptions during training, forcing reliance on the provided retrieval context.
*   **Core assumption:** Synthetic data can be made sufficiently realistic and complex to challenge DRAs while being entirely free from training-set contamination.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "synthetically generated e-commerce knowledge base and corpus to mitigate the effects of data contamination"; [section 2.3] Detailed pipeline for synthetic product and blog generation; states purpose is to "measure model performance... where we cannot rely on LLM’s parametric memory".
*   **Break condition:** If synthetic text is too stylistically homogenous or superficial, models may learn to exploit generation artifacts rather than perform genuine multi-document reasoning.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Aggregation-based query design with a numerical single-answer output enforces *total recall* and provides a verifiable evaluation metric.
*   **Mechanism:** The answer is a deterministic function (`θ`) over the *complete set* of property values from all entities in `E'`. Missing even one entity alters the aggregate result (e.g., `MEDIAN`, `SUM`). The single numerical answer enables exact match (EM) and soft match (SM) metrics that are transparent and reproducible.
*   **Core assumption:** Aggregation operators (Table 3) are sufficiently sensitive to the presence/absence of individual entities to act as a total recall probe.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 2.1] "correctly resolving a query... requires recovering the full set of relevant entities... missing any relevant document would result in losing a piece of information"; [section 4] Evaluation uses EM and SM% over normalized numerical answers; [section 5] Oracle retrieval results show performance is far from 100%, confirming the total recall difficulty.
*   **Break condition:** An aggregation operator that is robust to missing values (e.g., `MAX` with a high outlier) would fail to penalize incomplete retrieval, violating the total recall requirement.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Total Recall Retrieval**
    *   *Why needed here:* The core task definition requires retrieving *all* documents relevant to the filtered entity set `E'`. Standard retrieval metrics like R@k are insufficient; the system must achieve recall approaching 100% with respect to entities.
    *   *Quick check question:* If a query's gold answer is `SUM` of property `p` over 10 entities, and your system retrieves documents for only 8 of them, what will the synthesized answer likely be?
*   **Concept: Entity-Centric Evaluation Anchoring**
    *   *Why needed here:* The framework's verifiability hinges on the deterministic link between KB entities and corpus documents. Understanding this mapping is crucial for interpreting relevance judgments and error analysis.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why is it acceptable to evaluate recall at the *entity* level (not passage level) in this framework?
*   **Concept: Contamination-Resistant Benchmarking**
    *   *Why needed here:* To measure a DRA's true research capability, not its ability to recall memorized facts. The synthetic dataset is the primary tool for this.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does performance on TRQA-Ecommerce vs. TRQA-Wiki1 indicate potential data contamination in a model?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Knowledge Base (KB) -> Corpus -> Query Generator -> Relevance Annotator -> Benchmark Assembly -> Evaluator. Critical path also includes: KB Entity Sampling -> Constraint Filtering (E') -> Aggregation & Answer Computation -> NL Query Generation -> Corpus Document Retrieval (for E') -> Passage-Level Relevance Labeling.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Synthetic vs. Real Data:* Synthetic (Ecommerce) guarantees no contamination but may lack nuanced linguistic complexity. Real (Wiki) has natural language but risks contamination and KB-corpus misalignment.
    *   *Automatic vs. Manual Annotation:* Fully automatic generation scales but relies on LLM judgment for passage verification/re-write, introducing potential noise.
    *   *Single Numerical Answer:* Maximizes verifiability and simplifies metrics but limits the benchmark to a specific query type, potentially excluding complex report-style synthesis.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Retrieval Failure:** Low R@k (especially R@1000) across models (Table 4) indicates the corpus/query design is challenging for first-stage retrieval.
    *   **Reasoning Failure:** High error rate even with *oracle* retrieval (Table 5, 6), especially on Ecommerce, indicates models fail to synthesize across many documents.
    *   **Contamination Signal:** Large performance drop on Ecommerce vs. Wiki1 for closed-book LLMs (e.g., GPT-5.2: 30.18% EM → 1.67% EM) suggests reliance on memorized Wiki facts.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Retrieval sanity check:** Run BM25 and one dense retriever (E5) on a small sample of TRQA-Wiki1. Verify that recall at entity level (`R@|E'|`) is low (<50%), confirming the total recall challenge.
    2.  **Oracle Retrieval test:** For 10 queries, manually provide the LLM (GPT-5.2) with the exact set of 8-12 gold passages (no distractors). Measure EM. Expect significant improvement over no-retrieval, but not 100%, isolating reasoning as the bottleneck.
    3.  **Error Type analysis:** For 50 oracle failures, categorize as (a) *Parametric Bias* (answer matches closed-book, ignoring context) or (b) *Reasoning Error* (different answer, incorrect synthesis). Use the method from section 5. Expect >90% reasoning errors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** What are the specific failure modes in multi-document reasoning that cause LLMs to underperform on total recall questions even with oracle retrieval, as over 90% of errors are attributed to reasoning?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Table 6 shows >90% of errors for oracle models (Claude 4.5, GPT-5.2) are reasoning errors, not retrieval or parametric bias. The authors state this underscores challenges in multi-document reasoning.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies reasoning as the primary bottleneck but does not categorize the types of reasoning failures (e.g., aggregation errors, inconsistency handling, long-context comprehension).
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-grained error analysis on oracle runs, such as probing LLMs’ intermediate reasoning steps, evaluating performance on sub-tasks (e.g., value extraction, numerical aggregation), and testing with controlled variations in document set size and noise.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** How reliable are the LLM-based automated relevance judgments and passage rewrites in the TRQA framework, and does noise in corpus-KB alignment affect benchmark validity?
*   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The framework uses GPT-4o-mini for passage-level labeling and rewriting to align corpus values with the knowledge base. The authors mention verifying alignment but do not quantify annotation accuracy or analyze residual noise.
*   **Why unresolved:** No evaluation of the annotation pipeline’s correctness is provided. Errors here could mislabel relevant passages, inflating or deflating retrieval metrics and compromising benchmark reliability.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Human evaluation of a sample of relevance judgments and rewritten passages, measuring agreement with LLM labels and assessing the impact of alignment errors on retrieval model performance.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** Beyond the absence of parametric knowledge (data contamination), what factors contribute to the drastic performance drop of LLMs on the synthetic TRQA-Ecommerce dataset compared to Wikipedia-based datasets?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Table 5 shows oracle GPT-5.2 achieves 56.80% EM on Wiki1 but only 1.67% on E-commerce. The authors attribute this to “new information items not observed during training” but do not isolate other potential causes.
*   **Why unresolved:** The synthetic domain differs in language style (blog-like product descriptions), entity density, and aggregation patterns. It is unclear whether failure is due solely to memorization gaps or also to domain shift in linguistic or structural features.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments varying domain-specific characteristics (e.g., sentence length, technical terminology, document structure) while holding contamination constant, and probing models’ ability to reason over synthetic vs. natural text.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Why do DRA sub-queries fail to incrementally retrieve new gold entities and instead collect mostly distractors, as shown in Figure 4b, and how can retrieval planning be improved for total recall?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Figure 4b shows that as DRAs issue more sub-queries, they retrieve few new gold entities but many distractors. Table 7 confirms low accumulated recall (<14% for ReAct/Search-R1 on Wiki1).
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper highlights the issue but does not analyze the sub-query generation process or why retrieval models fail to expand the result set effectively. Possible causes include poor query diversification, lexical mismatch, or retriever limitations on sparse results.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies on DRA query strategies (e.g., diversity-promoting techniques, query expansion using entity types, adaptive stopping criteria), and correlation analysis between sub-query semantics and retrieved entity recall.

## Limitations
*   **Unquantified KB-corpus mapping consistency:** The framework assumes a perfect one-to-one entity-to-document link, but the rate of mismatches requiring manual rewrite (section 2.1) is unreported.
*   **Unmeasured synthetic data realism:** TRQA-Ecommerce’s linguistic and factual complexity compared to real web text is unassessed; stylistic homogeneity could create artifacts.
*   **Proprietary model irreproducibility:** Exact API configurations (prompts, decoding) for GPT-5.2 and Claude 4.5 are unspecified, preventing exact result recreation.

## Confidence
*   **Entity-centric framework:** Medium. The design is logically sound and internally consistent, but link robustness is an unquantified assumption.
*   **Synthetic contamination resistance:** Medium. The performance gap on Ecommerce vs. Wiki1 suggests success, but without stylistic validation, the "no contamination" claim is inferential.
*   **Aggregation-driven total recall:** High. Oracle results (Tables 5,6) confirm that even with perfect retrieval, EM is far from 100%, proving aggregation operators effectively force total recall and expose reasoning failures.
