# Geometry of Uncertainty: Learning Metric Spaces for Multimodal State Estimation in RL

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12087
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12087
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: METRICMM learns a geometric latent metric space where distances correspond to action-based state transitions, enabling robust multimodal state estimation without explicit noise models, and consistently outperforms six baselines under seven corruption families.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning from multimodal observations under sensor noise typically requires explicit probabilistic models of corruption. METRICMM addresses this by learning a structured latent representation in which Euclidean distances directly correlate with the minimum number of actions needed to transition between environment states. This geometric interpretation of uncertainty enables a simple, noise-agnostic sensor fusion mechanism based on inverse distance weighting. The method combines per-modality encoders, a deterministic latent transition model, and contrastive losses to structure the space, and is trained end-to-end with SAC without any noise augmentation. Empirically, on MuJoCo and Fetch benchmarks with seven types of corruptions—including simultaneous multi-modality failures—METRICMM achieves higher average returns and flatter performance degradation compared to six strong baselines, demonstrating the effectiveness of the transition-aware metric space for scalable robust state estimation.

## Method Summary
METRICMM processes multiple sensor streams (e.g., RGB, depth, point clouds) through separate encoders into a shared 64-dimensional latent space. A deterministic transition model predicts the next latent state from the previous fused state and action. For sensor fusion, each modality’s current latent encoding is weighted inversely by its squared Euclidean distance from this transition prediction, producing a fused state estimate that the policy uses. The model is trained with four losses: latent transition prediction (L_T), temporal contrastive losses that encourage adjacent states to be unit distance apart and random states to be far (L⁺, L⁻), and an invariance loss (L_inv) that aligns all modality encodings. All losses are weighted equally and combined with the SAC objective; no noise augmentation is used during training, so robustness emerges from the learned metric structure.

## Key Results
- METRICMM achieves higher average returns than six baselines across MuJoCo and Fetch tasks under seven corruption families.
- Performance degradation under increasing noise is significantly flatter, especially when multiple modalities fail simultaneously.
- It maintains strong performance without requiring explicit noise augmentation during training, validating the transition-aware metric space as an effective solution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1**: **Distances in the learned latent space directly correlate with the minimum number of actions required to transition between environment states.**  
Contrastive (L⁺, L⁻) and transition (L_T) losses structure the space so successive states are close and temporally distant states are pushed apart, making small Euclidean neighborhoods correspond to states reachable within a few actions. The core Transition Error Assumption bounds transition model errors to such local balls. Evidence: abstract and section 4.1 define the metric space; ablations show metric losses are necessary for robustness. Break condition: highly stochastic dynamics violate the deterministic transition and Euclidean ball assumptions.

**Mechanism 2**: **Sensor fusion via inverse distance weighting adaptively integrates multimodal observations without explicit noise modeling.**  
Each modality’s latent encoding is weighted inversely by its squared distance from the transition prediction ẑ_t, effectively trusting modalities consistent with the learned dynamics and approximating a MAP estimate with the transition model as prior. Evidence: abstract and section 4.2 describe IDW fusion. Break condition: if corrupted modalities produce encodings systematically close to ẑ_t (adversarial or correlated noise) or if φ_T is inaccurate, IDW may upweight bad data.

**Mechanism 3**: **The three loss components jointly induce a structured latent space that preserves temporal dynamics and aligns modalities.**  
L_T aligns predictions with observations, L⁺/L⁻ create a Euclidean metric approximating action-based distance, and L_inv maps all modalities to the same space. This localizes transition errors and enables effective IDW fusion. Evidence: ablation studies (section 5.1) show removing any component hurts robustness. Break conditions: asymmetric distances (irreversible dynamics) or mismatched modality update rates harm the symmetric Euclidean metric and alignment loss.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Per-modality encoders -> Latent transition model -> Fusion module -> Policy network
- **Critical path**: At each timestep: encode observations -> compute transition prediction -> fuse encodings (IDW) -> feed fused state to policy -> act. Losses are computed on clean transitions from the replay buffer and backpropagated end-to-end.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Symmetric vs. quasimetric: Euclidean distance simplifies implementation but cannot exactly represent irreversible transitions; a learned quasimetric would be more expressive but geometrically less interpretable.
  - Deterministic vs. stochastic φ_T: Deterministic eases training and IDW computation but fails under highly stochastic dynamics; a stochastic model would require a distributional distance measure (e.g., KL divergence).
  - IDW parameter δ: A small constant avoids division by zero but affects gradient scale; requires tuning.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Sudden performance collapse when two modalities are simultaneously corrupted, indicating encoder alignment (L_inv) may be insufficient.
  - High variance in returns on stochastic dynamics tasks, suggesting the deterministic transition model limitation.
  - Over-reliance on a single modality if L_inv weight is too low; monitor per-modality weights.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Single-modality corruption on MuJoCo: Vary corruption probability per modality; METRICMM degrades slowest among baselines.
  2. Dual-modality corruption on Fetch: Corrupt two modalities at once; METRICMM maintains positive returns longer, testing robustness when a majority of sensors fail.
  3. Ablation on pendulum: Remove L_inv or metric losses (L⁺/L⁻); robustness to Gaussian noise vanishes, confirming necessity of each loss component.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to quasimetric spaces**: How can METRICMM be extended to capture asymmetric action-based distances? Basis: section 5.2 identifies symmetric metric assumption as a limitation. Unresolved: Euclidean distance is symmetric; irreversible actions require asymmetry. Evidence needed: replace Euclidean with learnable quasimetric and show improved robustness on irreversible tasks.
2. **Handling stochastic transition dynamics**: How to deal with multi-modal next-state distributions? Basis: section 5.2 notes deterministic assumption and suggests divergences. Unresolved: deterministic φ_T collapses multi-modality. Evidence: incorporate probabilistic transition (e.g., mixture density network) and use KL divergence; evaluate on stochastic MuJoCo.
3. **Adapting to non-stationary noise**: Can the method handle changing noise distributions without retraining? Basis: conclusion lists adapting to non-stationary environments as future work. Unresolved: training assumes stationary noise. Evidence: train on fixed noise, then deploy under gradually shifting noise types; assess robustness via meta-learning or online adaptation.
4. **Adversarial robustness**: How does IDW perform under adversarial perturbations? Basis: conclusion proposes evaluating adversarial robustness. Unresolved: IDW may trust adversarially crafted encodings close to transition prediction. Evidence: apply PGD attacks on sensor data; measure state estimation error and control performance; explore defenses.

## Limitations
- The Transition Error Assumption (ϵ-ball) fails under high stochasticity, limiting applicability to real-world systems with unpredictable dynamics.
- Ambiguity in L⁻ loss implementation (negative sampling strategy, distance scaling/margin) risks unstable training or an inconsistent metric structure.
- IDW fusion may erroneously upweight corrupted modalities if their latent encodings systematically align with the transition prior (e.g., under adversarial or correlated noise), a failure mode not evaluated in the paper.

## Confidence
- **High**: Empirical finding that METRICMM outperforms six baselines across seven corruption families on MuJoCo/Fetch; results are reproducible with full specifications.
- **Medium**: Mechanism 1 (metric space ↔ action distance) – strongly implied by losses and ablation studies, but causal link not directly proven (no validation of distance matching minimal action counts).
- **Low**: Mechanism 2 (IDW as noise-agnostic fusion) – plausible but unvalidated under correlated or state-dependent noise; relies on assumption that modality noise is independent.

## Next Checks
1. **Clarify L⁻ implementation & stability**: Re-implement with a margin-based contrastive loss (e.g., -log(σ(m - distance))) and varied negative sampling (in-batch vs. trajectory). Measure: convergence of metric losses, distribution of pairwise distances, sensitivity to margin.
2. **Stress-test transition model under stochasticity**: Inject increasing process noise (action noise, stochastic dynamics) in MuJoCo. Measure: return degradation vs. noise level, violation rate of ϵ-ball assumption (distance to true next state), performance when switching to a stochastic φ_T.
3. **Evaluate IDW under correlated modality failures**: Create synthetic corruptions where two modalities share identical bias (e.g., depth offset). Metric: fusion weight allocation vs. modality error; check if IDW erroneously upweights both bad inputs when transition prior is misaligned.
