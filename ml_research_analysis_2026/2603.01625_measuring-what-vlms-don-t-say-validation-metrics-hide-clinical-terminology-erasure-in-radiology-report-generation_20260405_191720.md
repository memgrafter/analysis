# Measuring What VLMs Don't Say: Validation Metrics Hide Clinical Terminology Erasure in Radiology Report Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.01625
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01625
- **Reference count**: 30
- **Primary result**: Deterministic decoding achieves high BERTScore (0.78) through template collapse while erasing 32-35 clinical terms; stochastic sampling preserves vocabulary but introduces 25-65 new bias terms and increases WAEpred from 2.50 to 33.81

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a critical blind spot in radiology report generation evaluation: deterministic decoding strategies like greedy and beam search achieve high surface-level metric scores through template collapse, systematically suppressing clinical terminology and demographic-specific language. The authors introduce Clinical Association Displacement (CAD) and Weighted Association Erasure (WAE) as vocabulary-level diagnostics that detect shifts in demographic-based word associations invisible to conventional metrics. Experiments across six decoding strategies and sex-skewed training demonstrate a fundamental trade-off between semantic erasure and emergent bias that current evaluation frameworks cannot detect.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct with LoRA (rank=16, α=32) on ReXGradient-160K dataset, creating sex-balanced (50/50), male-skewed (67/33), and female-skewed (33/67) training sets. Six decoding strategies are evaluated: Greedy, Beam Search (k=4), Conservative Sampling (T=0.3, top-p=0.95), Rich Sampling (T=0.7, top-p=0.95), Top-k (k=50, T=1.0), and Nucleus Sampling (top-p=0.9, T=1.0). The methodology computes surface-level metrics (BERTScore, ROUGE, RateScore), diversity metrics (Template Diversity, TTR, 1-Self-BLEU, Semantic Diversity), and vocabulary-level diagnostics (CAD, WAE) using Dirichlet-smoothed log ratio calculations with α=0.1 and zcrit at p⋆=0.05.

## Key Results
- Deterministic decoding (Greedy, Beam Search) achieves BERTScore 0.78 but suffers 32-35 erased clinical terms through template collapse
- Stochastic sampling (Rich, Top-k, Nucleus) preserves vocabulary diversity but introduces 25-65 new bias terms and increases WAEpred from 2.50 to 33.81
- Sex-skewed training leaves conventional metrics unchanged but drives WAEpred shifts from -0.05 to ±0.79, confirming WAE captures semantic shifts invisible to existing metrics
- Rich sampling achieves highest template diversity (11.92%) and semantic diversity (0.29) while maintaining moderate WAEpred (15.29)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Deterministic decoding inflates BERTScore and ROUGE by template collapse, masking clinical terminology erasure
- Mechanism: Greedy and beam search converge to a small set of high-probability "normal finding" templates that maximize token overlap with references while systematically suppressing group-specific clinical terms
- Core assumption: High-frequency "normal" reports dominate datasets, creating a strong language prior that deterministic decoding exploits
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "deterministic decoding produces high levels of semantic erasure, while stochastic sampling generates diverse outputs but risks introducing new bias"
  - [section]: "deterministic decoding optimises for mode selection, converging to a small set of high-probability templates"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss bias and evaluation but don't directly address template collapse

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Clinical Association Displacement (CAD) detects demographic-based vocabulary shifts invisible to aggregate metrics
- Mechanism: CAD measures standardized log-ratio shifts in word associations between reference and generated reports, flagging terms that lose demographic specificity (erasure) or gain new associations (emergent bias)
- Core assumption: Demographic associations in clinical language carry diagnostic meaning that should be preserved during generation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we introduce Clinical Association Displacement (CAD), a vocabulary-level framework that quantifies shifts in demographic-based word associations"
  - [section]: "CAD is a vocabulary-level diagnostic framework that quantifies how the demographic association, both in polarity and magnitude, of individual clinical terms shifts between a human-reference corpus and generated outputs"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss bias measurement but not demographic vocabulary displacement specifically

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Weighted Association Erasure (WAE) aggregates term-level displacement into a global clinical signal loss metric
- Mechanism: WAE weights each term's squared displacement by its frequency in predictions (or references) and normalizes by total weighted displacement
- Core assumption: Clinical terms that appear frequently and are strongly associated with demographics should be preserved; their systematic avoidance indicates signal loss
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Weighted Association Erasure (WAE) aggregates these shifts to measure the clinical signal loss across demographic groups"
  - [section]: "WAE aggregates these term-level shifts to provide a single, global measure of clinical signal loss across the entire vocabulary, weighted by clinical relevance"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss bias aggregation but not weighted clinical signal loss

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Log-ratio association measures with Dirichlet smoothing
  - Why needed here: To quantify how strongly words associate with demographic groups while handling zero-counts
  - Quick check question: How does Dirichlet smoothing prevent division-by-zero errors when computing p(w|g) = (c(w,g)+α)/(N(g)+αV)?

- Concept: Standardization and z-score thresholding for displacement detection
  - Why needed here: To distinguish statistically meaningful association shifts from random variation
  - Quick check question: Why use z_crit = Φ⁻¹(1-p*/2) instead of raw log-ratio differences when flagging displacement?

- Concept: Multiple hypothesis correction (Benjamini-Hochberg) in vocabulary-level testing
  - Why needed here: To control false discovery rate when testing thousands of vocabulary terms simultaneously
  - Quick check question: How does FDR correction prevent inflated Type I errors when testing |V| terms for displacement?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image preprocessing (512×512 RGB) -> Report tokenization -> ReX-QWEN (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct fine-tuned with LoRA) -> Six decoding strategies -> Evaluation suite (BERTScore, ROUGE, RateScore + CAD/WAE) -> Diagnostic pipeline (CAD flagging, WAE aggregation, bootstrap uncertainty estimation)

- Critical path: 1. Load image and generate report using specified decoding strategy 2. Compute token counts per demographic group for both reference and prediction 3. Calculate smoothed log-ratios and standardized associations 4. Compute displacement statistics and apply FDR correction 5. Aggregate into WAE score and generate diagnostic categories

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Deterministic vs. stochastic decoding: Accuracy vs. diversity vs. fairness
  - Prediction-based vs. reference-based weighting in WAE: Model behavior vs. clinical importance
  - Threshold selection (z_crit, z_strong, z_neutral): Sensitivity vs. specificity in displacement detection

- Failure signatures:
  - Low template diversity (<10%) with high BERTScore indicates template collapse
  - High WAEpred with low WAEref suggests systematic avoidance of clinically important terms
  - Large ∆DIR indicates verbosity imbalances across demographic groups

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does semantic erasure identified by CAD/WAE correspond to clinically meaningful omissions that affect diagnostic accuracy or patient care?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "clinician validation is needed to confirm whether CAD-flagged erasures correspond to clinically meaningful omissions."
- Why unresolved: The current evaluation framework identifies statistical shifts in terminology but does not establish clinical significance or impact on patient outcomes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Clinician studies comparing diagnostic accuracy and treatment decisions based on generated reports with varying levels of semantic erasure, or patient outcome studies correlating report quality with clinical results.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can fairness-aware decoding strategies be developed that simultaneously minimize semantic erasure while avoiding emergent bias, rather than requiring trade-offs between these competing objectives?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies a "fundamental trade-off between semantic erasure and emergent bias" and states that "no single strategy simultaneously optimises accuracy, diversity, and fairness."
- Why unresolved: Current decoding approaches exhibit opposing failure modes - deterministic methods cause erasure while stochastic methods introduce bias, with no clear solution that addresses both issues.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and empirical validation of novel decoding algorithms or post-processing techniques that maintain clinical specificity while preserving demographic equity across multiple evaluation metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do CAD/WAE metrics generalize to intersectional demographic groups and non-binary categories beyond the binary sex associations examined in this study?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "future work should extend CAD to age, ethnicity, and intersectional groups."
- Why unresolved: The current framework is validated only for binary sex associations, limiting its applicability to real-world clinical scenarios where multiple demographic factors interact.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extension of the CAD/WAE framework to multi-dimensional demographic attributes, validated on datasets with comprehensive demographic annotations and tested for sensitivity to intersectional bias patterns.

## Limitations

- The assumption that demographic-based word associations represent clinically meaningful signals rather than artifacts remains untested and may affect metric validity
- Small sample size (N=2,000) and specific ReXGradient dataset characteristics may limit generalizability across different radiological contexts or clinical domains
- Relationship between surface-level metric performance and clinical utility remains correlational rather than causal, with alternative explanations not ruled out

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Empirical demonstration that deterministic decoding achieves high BERTScore/ROUGE while producing template collapse with clinical term erasure. Mathematical formulation of CAD and WAE metrics is sound, and observed trade-off between semantic erasure and emergent bias is well-supported.

**Medium Confidence**: Claim that current evaluation frameworks systematically fail to detect these phenomena. Evidence shows aggregate metrics remain stable despite significant vocabulary-level shifts, but clinical significance of CAD/WAE values requires further validation.

**Low Confidence**: Generalizability across different medical imaging modalities, clinical domains, or cultural contexts. Specific interaction between sex-skewed training and decoding strategy outcomes may not extend to other demographic dimensions or complex bias structures.
