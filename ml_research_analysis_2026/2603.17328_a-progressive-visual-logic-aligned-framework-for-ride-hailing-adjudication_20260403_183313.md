# A Progressive Visual-Logic-Aligned Framework for Ride-Hailing Adjudication

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.17328
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17328
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RideJudge-8B achieves 88.41% accuracy on DiDi datasets, significantly outperforming 32B baselines (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct 65.55%).

## Executive Summary
Ride-hailing dispute adjudication requires transparent reasoning from multimodal data, but off-the-shelf MLLMs suffer from misalignment issues. The RideJudge framework introduces a progressive visual-logic-aligned approach. It leverages SynTraj to synthesize trajectory data with explicit violation semantics, Adaptive Context Optimization to prune and retrieve relevant regulations and precedents, a Chain-of-Adjudication reasoning loop to enforce evidence-based inquiry, and Ordinal-Sensitive Reinforcement Learning to calibrate severity-aware decisions. The three-stage training (vision alignment, reasoning finetuning, RL) yields a compact 8B model that surpasses larger baselines on real DiDi datasets and even generalizes to other multimodal tasks.

## Method Summary
The method consists of offline and online components. Offline, SynTraj programmatically generates compliant and mutated trajectories rendered as map images, forming a visual grounding dataset. The Chain-of-Adjudication (CoA) synthesizes reasoning chains via a three-agent loop (Adjudicator, Visual Analyst, Refiner) grounded in a scenario’s text and image, producing structured outputs (Info Analysis, Visual Evidence, Rule Grounding, Adjudication). Online, Adaptive Context Optimization (ACO) filters the massive rule base using scenario-aware classifiers and retrieves historical precedents to produce a condensed Meta-Insight. The model is trained progressively: Stage 1 aligns the vision encoder on SynTraj (LLM frozen); Stage 2 full-parameter SFT on CoA chains; Stage 3 applies DAPO RL with an ordinal-sensitive reward that penalizes severity-distance errors. At inference, the order context (text + image) along with ACO’s pruned rules and insight is fed to the model to produce a verdict and reasoning.

## Key Results
- RideJudge-8B achieves 88.41% accuracy on real DiDi datasets (Appeal, Driver-Cancel, Passenger-Cancel), beating 32B baselines such as Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct (65.55%) and DeepSeek-V3.1 (80.72%).
- Ablations confirm each module’s contribution: Ordinal-Sensitive (OS) reward improves accuracy by 21.35%; CoA increases malicious recall by over 20%; progressive training yields monotonic gains across stages.
- The framework generalizes to the PetFinder multimodal task, demonstrating broader applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SynTraj bridges the gap between general visual semantics and domain-specific trajectory violation patterns by synthesizing controlled, semantically rich trajectory data.
- **Mechanism:** A programmatic engine constructs a compliant navigation route (S_geo) and then applies deterministic mutation strategies (e.g., unintentional deviation, reverse driving) at specific spatial-temporal anchors. The visual rendering of the mutated trajectory (T_real) against the planned route explicitly encodes violation semantics (e.g., a right turn at an intersection where a left was required). This creates direct visual-linguistic pairs linking geometric anomalies to liability labels.
- **Core assumption:** Abstract platform violation concepts have consistent, learnable spatiotemporal signatures that can be programmatically instantiated and perceived by a vision encoder.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.1 details the pipeline; Section 6.2 ablation shows removing SynTraj hurts performance and scaling dataset size yields monotonic gains.
- **Break condition:** If synthetic patterns fail to capture long-tail real-world disputes, visual grounding will not transfer. The paper’s geographical stability test partially counters this but lacks formal distribution match.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Adaptive Context Optimization (ACO) resolves the conflict between massive, dynamic knowledge and limited context windows by distilling scenario-relevant information.
- **Mechanism:** A two-part system: (1) A scenario-aware rule calibrator (decomposed ensemble of binary classifiers) filters the vast rule base (K) to a scenario-specific subset (K'). (2) A retrieval-augmented insight extractor fetches top-K historical precedents and uses an LLM to synthesize a "Meta-Insight" (I_syn), condensing tacit expert knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** Rule applicability can be reliably predicted from order metadata/text, and statistical commonalities of retrieved precedents contain actionable wisdom.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 formalizes rule pruning; Section 4.2 describes retrieval and insight synthesis; Section 6.2 ablation shows 21.30% drop when removing KACR.
- **Break condition:** If classifiers suffer low recall, critical rules are omitted. The paper prioritizes recall but does not report precision-recall tradeoffs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Ordinal-Sensitive Reinforcement Learning (OS-RL) stabilizes training and calibrates decision boundaries by replacing sparse binary feedback with graded rewards respecting the hierarchical severity of liability errors.
- **Mechanism:** During RL, the reward R_ans assigns a score based on the ordinal distance d() between predicted and ground truth liability rank. Misclassifying "Malicious Fault" as "Normal Fault" incurs a larger penalty than misclassifying between two normal sub-types.
- **Core assumption:** The liability label space is meaningfully ordered by severity, and semantic proximity correlates with real-world error cost.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 5 defines ordinal labels and reward formula; Section 6.2 ablation shows 21.35% drop without OS reward.
- **Break condition:** If the ordinal hierarchy is incorrect, the reward becomes misleading. The hierarchy is defined but not validated against human expert cost matrices.

## Foundational Learning
### Vision-Language Alignment on Synthetic Schematics
- **Why needed:** Off-the-shelf MLLMs are pre-trained on natural photos. Adjudication requires precise interpretation of schematic map renderings where cues are geometric deviations, not object recognition.
- **Quick check:** Can the model distinguish a 30m detour (likely a violation) from a 10m GPS jitter (likely compliant) on a rendered map?

### Chain-of-Thought for Evidence Chaining
- **Why needed:** Adjudication is multi-hop: fact extraction, visual verification, rule mapping, logical deduction. Monolithic prediction leads to hallucination.
- **Quick check:** Does the model's reasoning trace explicitly reference a visual fact (e.g., "the trajectory deviates at intersection k2") before citing a rule?

### Ordinal Classification & Hierarchical Error Costs
- **Why needed:** Liability labels form a hierarchy: No Fault < Normal Fault < Malicious Fault. Treating them as flat ignores real-world cost asymmetry (penalizing a malicious driver as no-fault is worse than misclassifying between two normal subtypes).
- **Quick check:** Does the learning signal punish "Malicious Fault" → "Normal Fault" more severely than "Normal Fault A" → "Normal Fault B"?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
Input O (O_text, O_image) -> ACO (Scenario-Aware Rule Calibrator -> K' AND Retrieval-Augmented Insight -> I_syn) -> RideJudge (Vision Encoder + Projector + LLM) -> (CoA reasoning, liability verdict)

Offline: SynTraj engine -> synthetic dataset; CoA agents (Adjudicator, Visual Analyst, Refiner) -> reasoning chains.

Training pipeline: Stage1 (SynTraj) -> Stage2 (SFT on CoA) -> Stage3 (RL with DAPO + OS reward).

- **Critical path:** 1) SynTraj trains visual grounding. 2) CoA chains train logic decomposition. 3) ACO provides context at inference. 4) OS-RL fine-tunes severity awareness. Sequential; early failure cascades.

- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - SynTraj: high control vs. realism (deterministic mutations may lack real noise).
  - ACO: pruning risk (false negatives) vs. context efficiency (recall prioritized, fixed K=4).
  - CoA: structured rigor vs. latency (8-turn limit prevents infinite loops but may truncate inquiry).

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Visual hallucination: cites non-existent trajectory features -> Stage1/CoA failure.
  - Rule misapplication: cites irrelevant rule from K' -> ACO classifier precision failure.
  - Severity blunting: confuses "Normal" and "Malicious" -> OS-RL failure or Stage2 data lacking ordinal examples.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. SynTraj Transfer Test: Evaluate Stage1 model on real DiDi trajectory images; measure accuracy on basic pattern detection (e.g., deviation, reverse). Expect >90%.
  2. CoA Ablation - Turn Count: During Stage2 SFT, vary max Adjudicator-Analyst turns (2,4,8,∞); measure impact on Malicious Recall. Hypothesis: performance increases with turns, plateaus; ∞ causes instability.
  3. OS Reward Sensitivity: During Stage3, sweep λ_ans in Eq11 (0.2,0.5,0.8,1.0); measure accuracy and ordinal error distribution. Expect: too low loses severity calibration; too high destabilizes training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does SynTraj’s synthetic trajectory distribution adequately cover the diversity and long-tail of real ride-hailing disputes?
- **Basis:** SynTraj generates 12,585 samples via programmed mutations; the paper reports strong real-data accuracy but provides no distributional comparison between synthetic and real trajectories.
- **Why unresolved:** Real disputes may contain complex, interacting violations or rare edge cases not modeled by the existing mutation strategies, potentially leading to coverage gaps and performance degradation on unseen patterns.
- **What evidence:** Compute statistical feature distances (e.g., deviation angles, stop durations) between SynTraj and a large held-out set of real disputed orders. Evaluate model accuracy on clusters of rare real anomalies identified via outlier detection to quantify generalization deficits.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Is the four-stage Chain-of-Adjudication structure and the fixed 8-turn interaction limit optimal for balancing reasoning depth and efficiency across all dispute complexities?
- **Basis:** CoA enforces a standardized four-stage output and caps Analyst-Adjudicator turns at 8; ablations demonstrate significant gains but the design choices are not systematically studied.
- **Why unresolved:** Simple disputes may require fewer steps, while complex cases might benefit from more turns; the current handcrafted configuration may not be universally optimal.
- **What evidence:** Conduct an ablation varying the maximum turns (e.g., 4, 8, 12) and reorganizing stage granularity (e.g., merging analysis and evidence stages) on a validation set stratified by dispute complexity. Measure accuracy, reasoning coherence, and inference latency to identify the best trade-off.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the decomposed ensemble rule calibration handle overlapping and inter‑dependent regulations, and does the independence assumption degrade performance when rules co‑occur?
- **Basis:** The approach trains a separate binary classifier per rule without modeling dependencies; recall is prioritized during selection but rule interactions are ignored.
- **Why unresolved:** Ride‑hailing regulations often have logical relationships (e.g., one violation implies another). Independent classifiers may produce redundant or conflicting rule subsets, injecting noise into the reasoning context.
- **What evidence:** Construct a gold‑standard applicability set for a diverse test corpus using expert annotations. Compare selection precision/recall of the independent ensemble against a dependency‑aware selector (e.g., a graph‑based or sequence model). Correlate differences with downstream adjudication accuracy.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Is the proposed ordinal‑sensitive reward robust to the choice of semantic distance function, and does it consistently improve calibration for all severity levels, particularly mid‑tier errors?
- **Basis:** The OS reward uses a distance function d(⋅) to grade errors; the paper shows a 21.35% accuracy gain over binary reward but does not specify d or analyze its sensitivity.
- **Why unresolved:** The design of d directly shapes the reinforcement signal. An inappropriate metric may misguide learning, e.g., under‑penalizing adjacent misclassifications or over‑penalizing mid‑tier errors, leading to poor ordinal calibration.
- **What evidence:** Ablate candidate distance functions (absolute rank difference, squared difference, severity‑weighted variants). Evaluate ordinal calibration via mean absolute error, Kendall’s tau, and per‑level F1 scores. Track reward distributions and convergence stability during RL training.

## Limitations
- **Synthetic-to-Real Transfer Gap:** SynTraj’s clean, deterministic mutations may not capture real-world noise (e.g., complex multi-rule interactions, UI clutter on DiDi's maps). The geographical stability test is suggestive but not a formal distribution match.
- **Rule Classifier Recall:** The decomposed binary classifier ensemble’s per-rule recall is unreported. Low recall on rare but critical rules would silently omit key knowledge during ACO, a catastrophic failure mode not measured in aggregate accuracy.
- **Ordinal Hierarchy Validity:** The predefined liability severity order (No Fault < Normal < Malicious) is assumed but not validated against actual expert cost matrices or legal penalties. An incorrect hierarchy would misguide OS-RL.

## Confidence
- **High:** Empirical results on DiDi datasets (88.41% accuracy) and ablations (e.g., 21.35% drop without OS-RL) demonstrate clear performance gains.
- **Medium:** Mechanistic claims for SynTraj and ACO are primarily supported by internal ablations; no external corpus anchors or distributional validation exists for the core synthetic data or rule-pruning assumptions.
- **Low:** Unsupervised components like the CoA loop’s emergent query quality and the unconstrained Meta-Insight synthesis (I_syn) lack baseline comparisons; efficacy is inferred from final accuracy.

## Next Checks
1. **SynTraj Realism Stress Test:** Run Stage 1 (vision alignment) models on a manually annotated held-out set of real DiDi trajectory maps. Measure accuracy on detecting specific violation patterns (e.g., deviation, reverse). A significant drop vs. SynTraj test performance confirms the transfer gap.
2. **Rule Pruning Recall Audit:** On a validation set of real orders (with known applicable rules), compute per-rule recall for the ACO classifiers (f_i). Identify any rules with recall <50%. This directly tests the "missing rule" failure mode.
3. **Ordinal Hierarchy Human Alignment:** Conduct an expert survey presenting pairs of liability labels (e.g., "Malicious Fault" vs. "Normal Fault") and ask domain experts to judge which error is more severe in practice. Compare the derived pairwise preferences against the paper's assumed linear order Y. A misalignment invalidates the OS-RL reward design.
