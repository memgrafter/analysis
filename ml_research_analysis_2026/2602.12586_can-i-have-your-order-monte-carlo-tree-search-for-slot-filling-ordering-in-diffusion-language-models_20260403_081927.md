# Can I Have Your Order? Monte-Carlo Tree Search for Slot Filling Ordering in Diffusion Language Models

## Quick Facts
*   arXiv ID: 2602.12586
*   Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12586
*   Reference count: 40
*   Primary result: McDiffuSE improves average accuracy by 3.2% over autoregressive baselines and 8.0% over standard plan-and-infill, with particularly large gains on code generation (19.5% on MBPP, 16.3% on HumanEval).

## Executive Summary
Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) for text generation are highly sensitive to the order in which they fill predefined slots, with suboptimal orders degrading output quality. This paper introduces McDiffuSE, a training-free framework that formulates slot selection as a sequential decision-making problem solved via Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS). It uses the base model's slot-level confidence as a prior to guide search and combines immediate slot rewards with stochastic rollouts to evaluate long-term coherence. On six benchmarks, McDiffuSE yields consistent gains over autoregressive and plan-and-infill baselines, with dramatic improvements on code generation tasks. Analysis reveals that while the optimal order is often sequential, strategic non-sequential deviations are critical for performance, and that a large exploration constant in MCTS is essential to overcome the model's inherent bias toward myopic, sequential choices.

## Method Summary
McDiffuSE is applied to the ReFusion slot-and-infill MDM. It frames infilling as a Markov Decision Process where a state consists of the partially filled sequence and the history of selected slots, an action selects the next masked slot, and the immediate reward is the slot's confidence (mean token probability). The framework uses a modified PUCT criterion for MCTS selection, incorporating a confidence-derived prior. For simulation, it expands leaf nodes and estimates their value via a hybrid reward: a weighted mix of the immediate slot reward and the average return from a stochastic rollout that fills remaining slots using a temperature-scaled confidence policy. After a fixed number of simulations, the slot with the highest visit count at the root is selected. Key hyperparameters are λ=0.3 (immediate/rollout mix), exploration constant c=50, Nsim=256 simulations, and rollout temperature τ=0.5.

## Key Results
*   McDiffuSE improves average accuracy by 3.2% over autoregressive baselines and 8.0% over standard plan-and-infill.
*   It achieves substantial gains on code generation: +19.5% on MBPP and +16.3% on HumanEval.
*   Optimal generation orders are predominantly sequential, but crucial non-sequential decisions on hard cases drive the performance gains; large exploration constants—not more simulations—are necessary to discover them.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Using model confidence as a prior guides MCTS toward semantically plausible slot selections while still allowing exploration of lower-confidence but globally coherent orders.  
**Mechanism:** Slot-level confidence scores (mean token probability for a slot) are normalized to form a prior distribution P(a|s) over admissible actions. This prior biases the PUCT selection rule toward slots the model already deems locally high-quality.  
**Core assumption:** Model confidence, while often biased toward superficial patterns, provides a useful signal for initial search direction and reduces the combinatorial space that must be explored.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract states the method "uses model confidence as priors." Section 3.2 details how the prior is obtained from a forward pass and normalized (Equation 4). Related work in the corpus (e.g., value-guided MCTS) supports integrating model-derived signals.  
**Break condition:** If slot-level confidence is completely uncorrelated with true solution quality (e.g., due to severe model miscalibration), the prior becomes misleading and harms search efficiency.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** A hybrid reward function combining immediate slot confidence with stochastic rollout value captures both local token quality and long-term trajectory coherence, avoiding myopic slot selections.  
**Mechanism:** The value estimate V(s,a) for a leaf node mixes the immediate reward R(s,a) with the average reward from a temperature-scaled stochastic rollout that sequentially fills remaining slots: V(s,a) = λ·R(s,a) + (1-λ)·E[G]. The rollout policy samples actions proportionally to confidence, exploring diverse completion paths.  
**Core assumption:** Immediate slot confidence is a noisy predictor of global solution quality; simulating full completions provides a more reliable estimate of the long-term payoff of choosing a slot early.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions combining "immediate rewards with stochastic rollouts to evaluate long-term coherence." Section 3.2 details the rollout algorithm and mixing coefficient λ (Equation 5, Algorithm 1). Section 6 shows λ=0.3 was optimal, indicating strong benefit from rollout estimates.  
**Break condition:** If rollouts are too short (few remaining slots) or the stochastic rollout policy is too greedy (low temperature), the rollout estimate G becomes a biased or high-variance approximation of true long-term value, degrading the hybrid signal.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A large exploration constant (c_puct) in the PUCT criterion is essential to overcome the model's initial confidence bias and discover effective non-sequential orderings; increasing simulation count alone is insufficient.  
**Mechanism:** PUCT(s,a) = Q(s,a) + c·P(a|s)·√(N(s))/(1+N(s,a)). A high c amplifies the exploration bonus for actions with low prior but few visits, forcing the search to try slots that seem unpromising locally but may lead to globally better solutions.  
**Core assumption:** The model's confidence priors are systematically biased toward sequential, left-to-right generation patterns that are locally confident but globally suboptimal for some reasoning tasks.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract states "large exploration constants—not more simulations—are necessary to overcome biased confidence priors." Section 6 entropy analysis shows that under low c, increasing simulations reduces entropy (premature convergence), while high c maintains exploration. Figure 3 shows accuracy strongly increases with c.  
**Break condition:** If c is set too high relative to the action space size, the search wastes excessive effort on consistently poor actions, reducing computational efficiency without accuracy gains.

### Concept: Markov Decision Process (MDP)
**Why needed here:** The paper formally frames slot ordering as a sequential decision problem. Understanding states (partially filled sequence + chosen order prefix), actions (select next slot), deterministic transitions, and reward (slot confidence) is essential to grasp the search objective.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Tree node -> Tree edge (action) -> Node statistics (visit count N(s), action visit count N(s,a), accumulated value W(s,a), empirical mean Q(s,a)) -> Prior policy (from model confidence) -> Selection policy (PUCT) -> Rollout policy (temperature-scaled confidence) -> Root node.

### Critical path
For each simulation: (1) **Select** from root down to a leaf using PUCT; (2) if leaf is unexpanded and non-terminal, **Expand** by creating child nodes for all unfilled slots with priors; (3) **Simulate** from one expanded child by running a stochastic rollout to completion, computing V(s,a); (4) **Backpropagate** V up the visited path, updating N and W. After N_sim simulations, **choose** at root the action with highest N(s,a).

### Design tradeoffs
*   **c_puct vs N_sim:** High c with moderate N_sim (e.g., c=100, N_sim=30) often outperforms low c with high N_sim. Increasing N_sim under low c reinforces myopic biases.
*   **λ (immediate vs rollout weight):** λ=0.3 was best; lower λ trusts long-term rollouts more but risks noise; higher λ overfits to local confidence.
*   **Rollout temperature τ:** Low τ makes rollouts more greedy (faster but less exploratory); high τ increases diversity but may produce incoherent completions.
*   **Slot size:** Smaller slots (e.g., 4 tokens) increase the number of slots K, expanding the action space but allowing finer-grained ordering decisions.

### Failure signatures
*   **Premature convergence:** Under low c, all simulations follow the highest-confidence sequential path; accuracy plateaus or drops as N_sim increases (entropy decreases).
*   **Incoherent rollouts:** With high τ, rollouts generate invalid sequences (e.g., broken code syntax), making G meaningless.
*   **Computational blowup:** Large N_sim increases generation time linearly without accuracy gain if c is insufficient to escape local optima.
*   **Poor base model:** If the underlying MDM's confidence scores are completely uninformative (e.g., uniform), the prior provides no useful bias, and search becomes nearly random.

### First 3 experiments
1.  **Ablation on c_puct:** Fix N_sim=30, λ=0.3, τ=0.5. Sweep c ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 50, 100}. Measure accuracy and entropy (diversity of visited actions). Expect strong positive correlation between c and accuracy up to a point.
2.  **Ablation on N_sim:** Fix c=100, λ=0.3, τ=0.5. Sweep N_sim ∈ {10, 30, 90, 270}. Measure accuracy and time. Expect diminishing returns beyond N_sim=30; accuracy may even drop if c is low (repeat with c=2 to confirm).
3.  **Sequentiality analysis:** For a sample set, compute the "sequentiality rate" (fraction of slots filled in left-to-right order) for McDiffuSE vs baseline. Correlate sequentiality with correctness. Expect McDiffuSE to have a lower average sequentiality rate but higher accuracy, with non-sequential decisions concentrated on hard cases where sequential fails (as in Figure 2/G).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** Does extending the maximum token generation length of diffusion-based language models significantly improve their reasoning accuracy on complex benchmarks like MATH500 or GPQA?  
**Basis in paper:** The authors state: "We hypothesise that extending the maximum token length of diffusion-based models could substantially improve their reasoning capabilities" (Section D.3), noting ReFusion is limited to 1024 tokens while ARMs use 32768.  
**Why unresolved:** Implementation constraints of existing MDMs prevented testing this hypothesis; the paper compares at equal context lengths without extending diffusion models.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Implement longer-context support (e.g., via positional interpolation) for an MDM like ReFusion and evaluate on MATH500/GPQA with extended token budgets against standard-length baselines.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** What specific structural properties of code generation tasks cause them to benefit more from MCTS-based slot ordering than mathematical reasoning tasks?  
**Basis in paper:** Results show coding tasks (MBPP +19.45%, HumanEval +16.32%) gain substantially more than reasoning tasks (MATH500 +4.9%), but the paper provides no feature-level analysis linking task characteristics to the observed improvements.  
**Why unresolved:** The analysis only reports aggregate accuracy differences without examining how problem attributes (e.g., dependency density, modularity) interact with non-sequential ordering decisions.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a fine-grained study correlating successful non-sequential decisions on a diverse code/math dataset with measurable task features (e.g., AST complexity, variable interdependence, control flow branching) to identify predictors of MCTS benefit.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Does MCDIFFUSE's approach generalize to other masked diffusion models (e.g., LLaDA, Dream) that do not use the same slot-and-infill decoding framework?  
**Basis in paper:** MCDIFFUSE was evaluated exclusively on ReFusion, which the authors call "the only available slot-and-infill diffusion language model" (Section 4). Other MDMs employ different decoding strategies, making it unclear if the MDP formulation and MCTS adaptation are transferable.  
**Why unresolved:** No experiments with alternative MDMs were conducted; the reliance on ReFusion's specific two-phase (planning/infilling) iteration may be essential.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Adapt MCDIFFUSE's MCTS layer to another MDM by defining a compatible slot representation and selection mechanism, then compare accuracy gains on shared benchmarks against that model's standard decoding.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Can reward functions that incorporate external verification or learned value estimates improve MCDIFFUSE's accuracy and reduce reliance on large simulation budgets compared to confidence-based rewards?  
**Basis in paper:** The reward uses model slot-level confidence (Equation 3), which may be miscalibrated or myopic. While rollouts mitigate this, the paper does not explore whether more informative rewards could yield higher sample efficiency.  
**Why unresolved:** Only the confidence-based reward with mixing coefficient λ was evaluated; alternative designs (e.g., unit test outcomes for code, learned critic) remain untested.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Replace or augment the confidence reward with a verifier-based signal (e.g., pass/fail of generated code on unit tests) or train a value network from MCTS trajectories, then measure accuracy as a function of simulation count relative to the baseline.

## Limitations
*   **Base model dependency:** Results rely on the specific ReFusion architecture and its slot-level confidence calibration; unknown how McDiffuSE transfers to other MDMs or base LLMs.
*   **Slot partitioning:** The algorithm for splitting text into fixed-size contiguous slots (size 4/8) and handling token boundaries across code/math is unspecified, potentially affecting order sensitivity.
*   **Confidence prior bias:** The core assumption that model confidence is a useful prior may fail on miscalibrated models or tasks where high-confidence slots are locally plausible but globally detrimental.
*   **Computational cost:** While token-efficient, MCTS adds inference latency; scalability to very long sequences (large K) or complex reasoning tasks is untested.
*   **Benchmark scope:** Strong gains on coding/formal tasks, but marginal or inconsistent on others (e.g., ARC); unknown whether observed benefits generalize beyond the six evaluated benchmarks.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (Confidence-guided prior):** Medium. Directly supported by paper equations and ablations, but break condition (miscalibration) is theoretical; no tests with deliberately corrupted priors.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Hybrid reward with rollouts):** Medium. Paper shows λ=0.3 optimal, but rollout policy design (temperature τ) is heuristic; no comparison to learned value functions or longer rollouts.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Large c_puct > more simulations):** Medium-High. Strong empirical evidence in Section 6 (entropy/accuracy curves), but limited to their hyperparameter ranges; theoretical justification for *why* c=100 is sufficient for K≤32 is absent.
*   **Overall improvement claim (+3.2% avg):** Medium for generalizability. Strong on coding tasks (19.5% on MBPP), but modest/variable elsewhere; may not hold for different model sizes, domains, or slot granularities.
