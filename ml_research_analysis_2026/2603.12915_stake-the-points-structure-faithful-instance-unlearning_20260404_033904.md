# Stake the Points: Structure-Faithful Instance Unlearning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12915
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12915
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves average gains of 32.9%, 22.5%, and 19.3% over baselines on image classification, face recognition, and image retrieval, with near-perfect forgetting (100% misclassification) and improved retained/test accuracy, especially for larger forget sets.

## Executive Summary
Machine unlearning (MU) aims to remove designated data from a pretrained model while preserving performance on retained data. Existing methods often degrade the semantic relations among retained instances, leading to structural collapse and poor deletion-retention balance. This paper introduces a structure-faithful unlearning framework that employs semantic anchors ("stakes") generated from language-driven attribute descriptions (e.g., via CLIP). The framework enforces two key constraints: structure-aware alignment to preserve affinities between retained instance embeddings and anchors, and structure-aware regularization to limit updates to parameters critical for semantic organization. Evaluated on image classification (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet), face recognition (Lacuna-10), and image retrieval, the method yields substantial improvements over baselines (average gains of 32.9%, 22.5%, 19.3%) and consistently achieves near-perfect forgetting while significantly boosting retained-set and test accuracy, with performance gains increasing as the forget set grows.

## Method Summary
The method generates class-level semantic anchors by prompting an LLM for discriminative visual attributes and encoding them with a frozen CLIP text encoder. Since the true retained dataset is inaccessible, it constructs a surrogate set \(D_s\) by generating adversarial examples from the forget set \(D_f\) (via PGD) toward target classes. The "structure" is defined as the affinity matrix between embeddings of \(D_s\) and the fixed anchors. For unlearning, a learnable projector \(p_\omega\) maps the feature extractor's outputs to a space where affinities are computed. The total loss combines: (1) deletion loss (negative cross-entropy on \(D_f\)), (2) retention loss (cross-entropy on \(D_s\)), (3) structure-aware alignment loss (negative cosine similarity between original and current affinities), (4) structure-aware regularization (ℓ2 penalty on feature extractor parameters weighted by their gradient magnitude w.r.t. the alignment loss), and (5) elastic-net regularization on the classifier weights. Optimizing this loss effectively forgets the target data while preserving the semantic organization of the retained knowledge.

## Key Results
- Average performance gains: 32.9% on image classification (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet), 22.5% on face recognition (Lacuna-10), and 19.3% on image retrieval compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- Forgetting efficacy: Achieves ~100% misclassification on forget sets across all datasets.
- Retention improvement: Significantly higher retained-set accuracy and test accuracy than baselines, with the gap widening as the number of forgotten instances increases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's success stems from three synergistic mechanisms:

1. **Semantic anchors preserve relational structure.**  
   Class-wise semantic anchors, generated from LLM-described attributes, serve as stable reference points. The structure-aware alignment loss minimizes divergence between original and current affinities (via cosine similarity) between retained instance embeddings and these anchors, preserving the relative semantic organization. Evidence: Ablation shows removing alignment causes the largest drop in retention accuracy (Table 5). Break condition: If anchors do not correlate with the model's visual semantics (e.g., using language-only SBERT), alignment fails.

2. **Structure-aware regularization stabilizes critical parameters.**  
   An importance weight \(I_i\) for each feature extractor parameter \(\psi_i\) is estimated as the average magnitude of its gradient w.r.t. the alignment loss. An elastic-net penalty scaled by \(I_i\) strongly constrains updates to parameters most responsible for semantic structure while allowing flexibility elsewhere. Evidence: Ablation indicates regularization helps particularly on datasets with many classes (CIFAR-100). Break condition: Early in unlearning, gradient estimates may be noisy, leading to misestimated importance and either over- or under-constraint.

3. **Language-driven anchors provide a data-frugal, interpretable reference.**  
   Using LLM-generated attributes and a frozen CLIP encoder yields anchors that are stable across instances and classes, eliminating the need for access to retained data. These anchors capture human-interpretable semantics and align reasonably well with the pretrained model's representation space. Evidence: Replacing anchors with visual prototypes (averages of retained embeddings) degrades performance, highlighting the advantage of linguistic attributes (Table 4). Break condition: Anchor quality depends on LLM's ability to produce discriminative attributes and CLIP's alignment with the model's features; for fine-grained categories or OOD data, anchors may misguide.

## Foundational Learning
Understanding this paper requires three foundational concepts:

- **Representation Space & Affinity**  
  *Why needed:* The paper operationalizes "knowledge structure" as the matrix of affinities (cosine similarities) between instance embeddings and semantic anchors. Preserving this affinity matrix preserves the semantic organization.  
  *Quick check:* If you compute \(S = V \cdot A^T\) for a batch of images (embeddings \(V\)) and class anchors (\(A\)), what does a large positive entry \(S_{ij}\) indicate about image \(i\) and class \(j\)?

- **Unlearning Objectives & Trade-offs**  
  *Why needed:* The method operates under instance-level misclassification unlearning without access to retained data. The core tension is that forgetting the target set (via gradient ascent) can cause collateral damage to retained instances; the deletion-retention balance is the key metric.  
  *Quick check:* Why is achieving 100% misclassification on the forget set relatively easy, while maintaining high retention accuracy the real challenge in approximate unlearning without retained data?

- **Proxy/Reference Data for Unlearning**  
  *Why needed:* Because \(D_r\) is inaccessible, the method uses a surrogate \(D_s\) (adversarial examples from \(D_f\)) to define the original structure and compute alignment losses. This introduces assumptions about the relationship between \(D_f\) and \(D_r\).  
  *Quick check:* What implicit assumption justifies using adversarial examples derived from the forget set as a proxy for retained set embeddings?

## Architecture Onboarding
The framework consists of several interacting components:

- **Component Map:**  
  Semantic Anchor Module (LLM prompt → attribute strings → frozen CLIP text encoder → fixed anchor matrix \(A\)) → Model Framework (pretrained feature extractor \(g_\psi\) → learnable projector \(p_\omega\) → pretrained classifier \(h_\phi\)) → Structure Definition (compute \(S_{\text{ori}} = \text{norm}(g_\psi(D_s)) \cdot A^T\) and \(S_{\text{unl}} = \text{norm}(p_\omega(g_\psi(D_s))) \cdot A^T\)) → Losses (\(L_{\text{align}}\), \(L_{\text{reg}}\), \(L_{\text{ret}}\), \(L_{\text{del}}\)) → Optimize \(\theta' = \{\psi, \omega, \phi\}\).

- **Critical Path:**  
  1. Pre-unlearning: Generate class attribute descriptions (LLM), encode with CLIP to get \(A\). Create \(D_s\) by generating PGD adversarial examples from \(D_f\) toward random target classes.  
  2. Compute original structure \(S_{\text{ori}}\) using the original model on \(D_s\).  
  3. During unlearning: Forward pass through \(g_\psi\) and \(p_\omega\) on \(D_s\) to get \(S_{\text{unl}}\). Compute \(L_{\text{align}}\) between \(S_{\text{ori}}\) and \(S_{\text{unl}}\). Compute importance \(I_i = \mathbb{E}_{x_s} |\partial L_{\text{align}}/\partial \psi_i|\). Compute \(L_{\text{reg}} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_i I_i (\psi_i - \psi_i^{\text{ori}})^2\). Compute \(L_{\text{ret}}\) on \(D_s\) and \(L_{\text{del}}\) on \(D_f\) (bypassing \(p_\omega\)). Sum losses and backpropagate to update \(\psi, \omega, \phi\).  
  4. Output unlearned model \(f_{\text{unl}}(x) = h_\phi(p_\omega(g_\psi(x)))\).

- **Design Tradeoffs:**  
  - Anchor fidelity vs. generality: Using CLIP (multimodal) anchors outperforms SBERT (language-only) but ties performance to a specific encoder; more attributes per class could capture intra-class variance but risk noise.  
  - Surrogate proxy accuracy: \(D_s\) (adversarial from \(D_f\)) is cheap and accessible, but biased toward the forget distribution; this trade-off enables unlearning without \(D_r\) but risks misdefined structure if \(D_f\) and \(D_r\) are entangled.  
  - Computational cost: Alignment loss requires forward/backward passes on \(D_s\) (size \(N_f \times N_{\text{adv}}\)), which is cheaper than full retraining but costlier than simple gradient reversal. The projector \(p_\omega\) adds parameters and a forward pass.  
  - Stability of \(I_i\): The gradient-based importance estimate can be noisy early on; a poor estimate may over- or under-constrain parameters, harming flexibility or structure preservation.

- **Failure Signatures:**  
  1. Poor retention (\(A_r\) much lower than pre-unlearning test accuracy) indicates \(L_{\text{align}}\) or \(L_{\text{reg}}\) failed; check divergence between \(S_{\text{unl}}\) and \(S_{\text{ori}}\) (e.g., visual heatmap). Likely cause: poor anchors or unstable \(I_i\).  
  2. Incomplete forgetting (\(A_f < 100\%\)) suggests \(L_{\text{del}}\) is dominated; monitor loss magnitudes. Verify that bypassing \(p_\omega\) for \(D_f\) is effective.  
  3. Collapse on larger forget sets: As \(k\) grows, \(D_s\) becomes an even poorer proxy; retention should drop less than baselines. If not, structure preservation is failing under high deletion pressure; inspect \(I_i\) distributions—may need adaptive \(\lambda_{\text{reg}}\).  
  4. Predictable misclassification patterns (e.g., all "cat" forget samples misclassified as "dog") indicate biased decision boundaries; the paper's Figure 8 shows no such pattern, which is desirable.

- **First 3 Experiments:**  
  1. *Anchor Sanity Check:* On a small dataset (e.g., 3-class CIFAR-10), manually inspect LLM-generated attributes and CLIP anchors. Visualize anchor-instance affinity heatmaps to confirm anchors are discriminative.  
  2. *Component Ablation (Reproduce Table 5):* Run full method and ablate each component (structure-aware alignment, structure-aware regularization, classifier regularization) on CIFAR-10 with \(k=64\). Verify that removing alignment causes the largest drop in retention/test accuracy, confirming its primary role.  
  3. *Proxy Validation:* Compare \(S_{\text{ori}}\) computed with \(D_s\) (adversarial from \(D_f\)) versus \(S_{\text{ori}}\) computed with a small real sample from \(D_r\) (simulate having retained data). High cosine similarity between the two matrices supports the validity of the \(D_s\) proxy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization across architectures and modalities:** Does the observed correlation between structural collapse and deletion-retention trade-off hold for model families beyond ResNets (e.g., Vision Transformers) and for non-vision domains (e.g., language or multimodal models)? The paper's experiments are limited to convolutional networks on vision tasks.
2. **Role of instance-instance relations:** The framework defines structure solely via anchor-instance affinities, ignoring direct instance-instance relations. Would explicitly modeling instance-instance similarity improve performance on complex, multi-class datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100)?
3. **Scalability to large-scale settings:** How does the method scale in computational overhead and performance retention when applied to very large datasets (e.g., ImageNet-22k) and billion-parameter models? The reliance on LLM-generated anchors and per-parameter regularization may become cost-prohibitive.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (loss weights, learning rates, etc.) are not fully specified, requiring empirical tuning.
- Anchor quality depends heavily on LLM-generated attributes and CLIP's alignment with the model's feature space; it may fail for fine-grained or out-of-distribution categories.
- The surrogate set \(D_s\) (adversarial from forget set) may poorly approximate the true retained set structure if the forget and retained data are highly entangled.
- The gradient-based importance \(I_i\) can be unstable early in unlearning, potentially leading to misestimation of structural importance.

## Confidence
- Structure-aware alignment (\(L_{\text{align}}\)): **High** – Ablation studies (Table 5) consistently show its critical role; removing it causes the largest performance drop.
- Structure-aware regularization (\(L_{\text{reg}}\)): **Medium** – Ablation indicates benefits, but the gradient-based importance \(I_i\) may be noisy and less reliable.
- Language-driven semantic anchors: **Medium–Low** – Outperforms visual prototypes (Table 4), but anchor quality is hard to control and may degrade on fine-grained classes; reliance on LLM/CLIP introduces external dependencies.

## Next Checks
1. **Proxy correlation validation:** Compute \(S_{\text{ori}}\) using \(D_s\) (adversarial from \(D_f\)) and using a small real sample from \(D_r\) (if accessible). High cosine similarity would confirm the validity of the surrogate.
2. **Anchor robustness test:** Replace the LLM/CLIP pipeline with alternatives (e.g., SBERT for text, BLIP for multimodal) or use curated human-defined attributes. Measure performance changes to assess robustness to anchor quality.
3. **Importance weight stability:** Track \(I_i\) across unlearning iterations. Correlate with the actual impact of parameter perturbations on \(L_{\text{align}}\) to verify that gradient magnitude is a reliable proxy for structural importance.
