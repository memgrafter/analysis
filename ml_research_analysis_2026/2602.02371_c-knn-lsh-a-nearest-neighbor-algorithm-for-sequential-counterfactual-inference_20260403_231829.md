# C-kNN-LSH: A Nearest-Neighbor Algorithm for Sequential Counterfactual Inference
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02371
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02371
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** Combines VAE-based latent compression, LSH for scalable nearest-neighbor retrieval, and a doubly robust correction to estimate heterogeneous treatment effects in confounded longitudinal data, outperforming baselines on a real Long COVID cohort (N=13,511).

## Executive Summary
C-kNN-LSH (Latent Matchmaking Network with Locality-Sensitive Hashing) addresses sequential causal inference for high-dimensional, confounded longitudinal data. It compresses an individual's history into a low-dimensional latent state using a variational autoencoder with a frozen LLM backbone, ensuring the state is a sufficient statistic for potential outcomes. Efficient approximate nearest-neighbor search via LSH retrieves similar historical trajectories under the same treatment, enabling local doubly robust estimation. On a large real-world cohort with Long COVID (PASC) severity as the outcome and vaccination doses as the treatment, the method provides stable and plausible counterfactual estimates, particularly at higher dose levels where standard local AIPW becomes unstable.

## Method Summary
The method involves three stages: (1) Representation learning where a VAE (with decoder, outcome predictor, and treatment discriminator heads) is trained end-to-end on textual serializations of longitudinal histories to learn a latent state that balances history reconstruction, outcome prediction, and independence from the treatment policy; (2) Scalable neighbor retrieval using LSH to find k-nearest neighbors in the latent space from individuals who received a specific treatment; (3) Local doubly robust estimation where simple outcome and propensity models are fit on the retrieved neighbor set to compute an unbiased counterfactual mean for a query individual and treatment, under approximate ignorability in the latent space.

## Key Results
- The LMN estimator yields smoother, more clinically plausible dose-response curves for PASC severity compared to unstable Local AIPW estimates, especially at higher vaccine doses (a=3,4,6).
- Counterfactual predictions are robust to variations in the lookback window (30 vs. 180 days) and to different concept-based feature groupings (BREATHING, ACTIVITY, HEART, RECORDS), with relative effect patterns remaining stable.
- On a held-out test set, LMN demonstrates superior performance in estimating heterogeneous treatment effects and policy values relative to Outcome Regression, IPW, and Local AIPW baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Latent history compression via a variational autoencoder (VAE) creates a low-dimensional state that acts as an approximate sufficient statistic for confounders and outcomes, enabling feasible nearest-neighbor matching.
- **Mechanism:** A frozen LLM (ΨLLM) first embeds raw longitudinal history (Hi,t) into a semantic space. A VAE encoder (qϕ) then compresses this embedding into a Gaussian latent distribution. The VAE is trained with a balanced ELBO objective that combines reconstruction loss (log pψ(H|Z)), outcome prediction loss (log pω(Y|Z,A)), a KL divergence to a prior (enforcing smooth latent geometry), and a mutual information penalty I(Z;A) (discouraging policy leakage). This yields a latent space where similar histories map to nearby points and where treatment assignment is approximately independent of the latent state given the history.
- **Core assumption:** Approximate latent sufficiency (Assumption 3.1): There exists a mapping Φ⋆ such that Yi,t(a) ⊥⊥ Ai,t | Z⋆i,t = Φ⋆(Hi,t), and the learned representation Φϕ approximates Z⋆i,t with bounded representation error εrep.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...combines variational autoencoder-based latent history compression..."; [section 2.1] Describes the LLM-Augmented History Compression and the ELBO objective (Equation 6) explicitly designed to learn a latent state that is a sufficient statistic.
- **Break condition:** If the latent space fails to preserve outcome-relevant information or becomes entangled with treatment policy (high I(Z;A)), the nearest-neighbor comparisons will not approximate valid conditional ignorability, leading to biased counterfactual estimates. Detectable via poor reconstruction (pψ) or outcome (pω) model performance on validation data.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH) enables scalable approximate nearest-neighbor search in the learned latent space, making local counterfactual estimation computationally feasible for large longitudinal datasets.
- **Mechanism:** For a query latent state Zi,t and desired treatment action a, LSH (using p-stable hash functions) maps latent vectors to buckets such that nearby vectors collide with higher probability. This reduces the search for the k-nearest neighbors Nk(i,t,a) from the full dataset Da to a sublinear approximate search, with complexity O((N T)ρ log N T), ρ<1. The retrieved neighbors provide local samples with similar latent histories and observed treatment to weight outcomes.
- **Core assumption:** The latent space geometry induced by the VAE encoder is approximately Euclidean and respects the smoothness assumption (Assumption 3.2: θi,t(a) is L-Lipschitz in Z⋆i,t), so that neighbors in latent space have similar potential outcomes.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...locality-sensitive hashing for efficient nearest-neighbor retrieval..."; [section 2.2] Explicitly defines the LSH family h(a,w,c) and the ANN objective (Equation 7), linking it to the latent space distance d(·,·).
- **Break condition:** If the LSH approximation error is too high (many false negatives/positives due to poor bucketing or inappropriate hash parameters), the retrieved neighbor set Nk will not be the true k-nearest neighbors, violating the local smoothness guarantee and increasing variance/bias. Monitor via recall rate in validation ANN queries.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A doubly robust (DR) correction applied within the local neighbor set provides an estimator that is consistent if either the local outcome model or the local propensity model is correctly specified, mitigating bias from imperfect latent representation and nuisance estimation.
- **Mechanism:** For each neighbor (j,s) in Nk(i,t,a), the estimator combines a local outcome model bQ(Zj,s, a) and an estimated propensity score ê(a|Zj,s) via: bθi,t(a) = (1/k) Σ [ bQ(Zj,s, a) + I(Aj,s=a)/ê(a|Zj,s) * (Yj,s - bQ(Zj,s, a)) ]. This is the standard AIPW form applied locally. The local models are fit only on the retrieved neighbor set, making them adaptive to the local latent neighborhood.
- **Core assumption:** Sequential ignorability holds in the latent space (Yi,t(a) ⊥⊥ Ai,t | Zi,t) approximately (from Assumption 3.1), and the neighbor set is sufficiently large (k → ∞, k/(NT) → 0) for local averaging to work. Additionally, at least one of bQ or ê is consistent for its target in the local region.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...integrate neighborhood estimator with a doubly-robust correction."; [section 2.3] Presents Equation 8, the DR formula within neighbors; [section 3] Theoretical guarantee (Consistency paragraph): "if either the outcome model bQ or the propensity model ê is consistent, then bθi,t(a) → θi,t(a) + O(εrep)."
- **Break condition:** If both local nuisance models (bQ and ê) are poorly specified in the relevant latent neighborhoods (e.g., due to insufficient neighbors, high noise), the DR property fails and bias can be worse than using either alone. Diagnose via cross-validation performance of local models on held-out neighbors.

## Foundational Learning
### Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)
- **Why needed here:** To compress high-dimensional, irregular longitudinal histories into a low-dimensional latent state that preserves information relevant to potential outcomes and treatment assignment, enabling feasible nearest-neighbor search.
- **Quick check:** What are the roles of the reconstruction term (log pψ(H|Z)) and the mutual information penalty I(Z;A) in the ELBO objective (Equation 6) for learning a latent space suitable for causal matching?

### Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH)
- **Why needed here:** To achieve sublinear query time for nearest-neighbor retrieval in the latent space, which is essential for scaling C-kNN-LSH to large longitudinal datasets where brute-force search is infeasible.
- **Quick check:** How does the hash function h(a,w,c) = ⌊(w·a + c)/r⌋ (with w~N(0,1), c~Uniform(0,r)) ensure that points with small Euclidean distance are more likely to collide?

### Doubly Robust (DR) Estimation
- **Why needed here:** To provide an estimator that remains consistent even if either the outcome model or the propensity score model is incorrectly specified, offering robustness against model misspecification in the local latent neighborhoods.
- **Quick check:** In the DR formula bθ = (1/k) Σ [ bQ + I(A=a)/ê * (Y - bQ) ], what is the intuition for why the estimator is consistent if *either* bQ or ê is consistent?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Frozen LLM (ΨLLM) -> Text History Embedding (Ei,t) -> VAE Encoder (qϕ) -> Latent State (Zi,t) -> [Separate paths per treatment a]: LSH Index (from training Zj,s where Aj,s=a) -> Retrieved Neighbors Nk(i,t,a) -> Local Outcome Model (bQ) & Local Propensity Model (ê) -> DR Formula -> Counterfactual Estimate bθi,t(a)

### Critical path
1. **History construction & feature engineering:** Serialize longitudinal data into text summaries with safe leakage prevention (only use observations at or before time t).
2. **Representation learning:** Train LMN end-to-end on training set using objective L (Equation 6). Validate on holdout participants to avoid overfitting temporal patterns.
3. **Latent embedding extraction:** Pass all training and test histories through trained ΨLLM and qϕ to obtain fixed latent states Zi,t.
4. **ANN index building:** Construct LSH hash tables for all training (Zj,s, Aj,s, Yj,s) tuples.
5. **Counterfactual estimation:** For each test query (i,t,a), use LSH to get Nk(i,t,a), then fit local bQ and ê on that set, compute bθi,t(a) via DR formula.
6. **Policy evaluation:** Aggregate bθi,t(a) across individuals/times to estimate E[bY(a)] and compare treatments.

### Design tradeoffs
- **VAE capacity vs. latent smoothness:** A more expressive decoder pψ improves reconstruction but may learn a latent space where small perturbations change semantics, harming kNN reliability. The β and α hyperparameters in L trade off reconstruction/causal fidelity vs. latent smoothness/action invariance.
- **Look-back window L:** Longer L incorporates more history but increases sparsity and potential noise. The paper finds stability across L, but per-phenotype differences suggest optimal L may be phenotype-dependent.
- **k in kNN:** Larger k reduces variance but risks including dissimilar neighbors if latent space is imperfect. Theoretically k→∞ and k/(NT)→0, but practically choose k via validation.
- **LSH parameters (r, number of hash tables):** Affects approximation tradeoff (recall vs. query speed). Too few tables yield many false negatives; too many increase memory. Tune on validation ANN recall.

### Failure signatures
- **Unstable counterfactual trajectories:** Like Local AIPW in Table 2 (a=1 giving 15.50 vs LMN's 10.42), indicating poor neighbor matching due to high-dimensional noise or latent space not separating confounded regimes.
- **Poor validation reconstruction/outcome loss:** High pψ(H|Z) or pω(Y|Z,A) loss on held-out participants indicates the latent representation fails to preserve necessary information.
- **High mutual information I(Z;A):** If Dη can predict A from Z easily, the latent space encodes direct policy information, violating the invariance principle.
- **LSH recall < 80% on validation queries:** Significant drop from true k-NN set means approximation error will dominate.
- **DR estimator variance explosion:** If ê(a|Zj,s) is very small for some neighbors, the I(A=a)/ê term becomes huge. Check for extreme weights.
- **Concept set sensitivity:** Drastic swings in effect curves across concept sets (e.g., BREATHING vs HEART) suggest the latent space is not capturing a stable, sufficient representation.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline comparison (Table 2):** Train LMN and baselines (OR, IPW, Local AIPW) on 70% training participants, evaluate mean counterfactual PASC severity bθ(a) on held-out 20% test set for each treatment level a. Compare stability and plausibility of dose-response curves; LMN should show smoother, more clinically plausible non-monotonic pattern than unstable Local AIPW.
2. **Representation robustness (Figures 1, 3, 4):** Train LMN with different concept sets (ALL, ACTIVITY, BREATHING, HEART, RECORDS) on fixed 30-day and 180-day lookbacks. Plot estimated effects ˆθ(a) per phenotype and difference ˆθc - ˆθALL. Verify that (i) overall shape (peak at a=1) persists, (ii) concept-specific curves are smooth, (iii) relative differences are stable across lookback windows.
3. **Neighborhood quality ablation:** Ablate the LSH component: replace ANN with exact k-NN search (brute-force) on a validation subset. Compare bθ estimates and runtime. If LMN's results are similar to exact k-NN but much faster, it confirms LSH maintains neighbor quality while gaining scalability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What are the minimax optimal convergence rates for C-kNN-LSH under smoothness conditions on the latent counterfactual mean?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors explicitly state: "Our goal is not to establish sharp minimax rates, but rather to clarify the conditions..." (Section 3).
- **Why unresolved:** Sharp rates would quantify statistical efficiency, guide choice of hyperparameters like k, and enable theoretical comparisons with other methods; their absence leaves the method's optimality unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derivation of minimax lower and upper bounds in terms of sample size, latent dimension, and smoothness parameters, possibly under additional structural assumptions on the data-generating process.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Under what conditions does a low-dimensional latent representation exist that preserves conditional ignorability (approximate latent sufficiency) for sequential counterfactual inference in high-dimensional longitudinal biomedical data?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper posits Assumption 3.1 and notes that standard ignorability given full history does not guarantee such a compressible representation, highlighting a potential gap between theory and real-world data.
- **Why unresolved:** The entire C-kNN-LSH approach hinges on this assumption; without knowing when it holds, practitioners cannot assess applicability to new datasets or diagnose failures.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical results linking data properties (e.g., confounding strength, manifold structure) to the existence of sufficient low-dimensional summaries, or empirical validation protocols (e.g., tests for residual confounding after compression using held-out outcomes).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the approximation error from Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH) propagate through the doubly robust correction, and what are the optimal LSH configurations to balance computational efficiency with estimation accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** The method uses LSH for scalable nearest-neighbor search but provides no analysis of how its approximate nature affects the final estimator; the theoretical consistency assumes exact nearest neighbors.
- **Why unresolved:** LSH may retrieve neighbors that are not true nearest neighbors, potentially increasing bias; without understanding this trade-off, users cannot reliably configure LSH for different dataset sizes or dimensions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical studies on synthetic data with known true neighbors, varying LSH parameters to measure distortion in neighbor sets and resulting bias/variance; theoretical bounds on the propagation of neighbor approximation error through the doubly robust formula.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility blocked:** Critical hyperparameters (λ, β, α, k, LSH tables T, r) and exact text serialization template are unreported. Without these, exact replication is impossible and performance may be sensitive.
- **Single-cohort validation:** Claims are based solely on one real-world Long COVID dataset (N=13,511). Generalizability to other diseases or data structures is unestablished.
- **Limited theoretical validation:** Core assumptions (latent sufficiency, Lipschitz smoothness in Z) are posited but lack empirical falsification tests (e.g., testing Z ⊥⊥ A | H).

## Confidence
- **Cluster 1 (LMN vs. baselines):** Medium. Results are clear on the reported cohort, but single-dataset evidence and potential tuning bias reduce confidence.
- **Cluster 2 (Robustness to lookback/concepts):** Medium-Low. Stability across lookback windows and concept sets is shown, but the dependence on unreported concept definitions and possible overfitting to this specific feature grouping creates uncertainty.
- **Cluster 3 (High-dose stability):** Low-Medium. The comparison to unstable Local AIPW is compelling but relies on a single method’s failure mode; true robustness requires external validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Systematically vary λ (outcome loss weight), β (KL weight), α (MI penalty), and k across reasonable ranges on a validation fold. Confirm that LMN’s relative advantage over baselines persists across 80%+ of the tested grid.
2. **LSH recall benchmark:** For a held-out query sample, compute true k-NN via brute-force in the latent space. Measure recall@k for the LSH implementation. Acceptable performance requires >90% recall with <10% query-time speedup vs. brute-force.
3. **External replication test:** Apply the fully specified LMN pipeline (once hyperparameters are disclosed) to a second longitudinal observational dataset with a different causal structure (e.g., MIMIC-IV for sepsis treatment effects). Replicate the key finding: LMN’s counterfactual dose-response is smoother and more stable than Local AIPW.
