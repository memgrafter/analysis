# Robust Mean Estimation under Quantization
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.07074
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07074
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Minimax optimal robust mean estimation under individual quantization and adversarial corruption, with error O(max(σ,λ)√(log(1/δ)/n)) in 1D and O(max_i λ_i√(log(1/δ)/n) + √(Tr(Σ)/n)) in multivariate, plus O(ηλ) corruption.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies robust multivariate mean estimation in a distributed setting where each sample is individually quantized to bits and a fraction may be adversarially corrupted. The core method combines the statistical robustness of the trimmed mean with random dithering: uniform noise is added before sign quantization to control bias. In the fully quantized case, with only a rough bound on the outer quantiles of the centered distribution, the estimator attains minimax optimal error rates that scale with the standard deviation (or covariance) and include an additional linear term in the corruption fraction η. In the partial quantization setting, a small unquantized subset calibrates dither levels, rendering the error independent of the mean’s location and achieving near-minimax rates (e.g., O(K√(log n/n)) for sub-Gaussian data). A robustification variant incorporating a polynomial-time robust mean algorithm reduces the corruption dependence to an additive O(η) term. Theory is supported by numerical experiments confirming the behavior and robustness.

## Method Summary
The algorithm starts by selecting a mode: fully distributed (no extra samples) or partial quantization (a small random subset left unquantized). In partial mode, coordinate-wise empirical quantiles α,β are estimated from n₀ ≪ n unquantized samples; the mid μ₁ = (α+β)/2 and half-width Δ = (β-α)/2 are derived. All remaining samples (or all in full mode) are shifted by μ₁ (full mode uses zero shift) and dithered: uniform noise τ_i ∈ [-Δ,Δ] (or λ in 1D) is added, then sign-quantized. The quantized vector is scaled by Δ (or λ) to yield an unbiased clipped estimate of the original value. The final estimator is either the empirical mean of these clipped values plus μ₁, or—for robustness—the output of a robust mean algorithm (Depersin–Lécué) applied to the transformed samples. The method thus converts robust estimation under severe bit constraints into a problem with bounded coordinates, enabling trimmed mean techniques.

## Key Results
- Fully quantized distributed setting with known λ (bound on outer quantiles) yields minimax optimal error O(λ√(log(1/δ)/n) + σ√(log(1/δ)/n)) in 1D and O(max_i λ_i√(log(1/δ)/n) + √(Tr(Σ)/n)) in multivariate, plus O(ηλ) under η-corruption.
- Partial quantization, using o(n) unquantized samples to estimate quantiles, achieves error independent of μ and near-minimax rates (e.g., O(K√(log n/n)) for sub-Gaussian data), with corruption term reduced to O(η).
- Robustification via a polynomial-time robust mean algorithm (Depersin–Lécué) replaces the O(ηλ) corruption term with O(η) under the condition log(1/δ) ≳ 300ηn, albeit with larger constants.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dithering Realizes Trimmed Mean
- Claim: Random uniform dithering before sign quantization implements a clipped (trimmed) mean estimator in expectation.
- Mechanism: For any x and λ > 0, with τ ∼ U[-1,1], E[λ sign(x + λτ)] = ϕ_{-λ,λ}(x), where ϕ clips x to [-λ,λ]. This converts the quantized bits back into an unbiased estimate of the clipped original value.
- Core assumption: The dithering level λ upper-bounds the outer quantiles of the centered distribution X (i.e., λ ≥ max{Q_{1-ε}(X), -Q_ε(X)} for ε ≍ log(1/δ)/n).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "combines the trimmed mean from robust statistics with random dithering"
  - [section 2.1]: Identity (2.3) explicitly shows E[λ sign(x+λτ)] = ϕ_{-λ,λ}(x)
  - [section 3.1]: Theorem 3.1 uses this mechanism with λ depending on quantiles of X
- Break condition: If λ < Q_{1-ε}(X), the bias |E[λ˙X] - μ| becomes large because data outside [-λ,λ] is hard-clipped, losing information.

### Mechanism 2: Partial Quantization Removes Mean Dependence
- Claim: Using o(n) unquantized samples to estimate empirical quantiles α,β removes dependency of error rates on the location of μ.
- Mechanism: Compute α,β from n₀ ≪ n unquantized samples. Use these to define Δ = (β-α)/2 and shift the remaining samples by μ₁ = (α+β)/2 before dithering with level Δ. The estimator becomes empirical mean of Δ˙Xᵢ plus μ₁.
- Core assumption: n₀ is large enough that empirical quantiles concentrate: n₀ = Ω(√n log n) for sub-Gaussian X; and ε (quantile fraction) satisfies ε ≥ 8η under corruption.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "partial quantization setting... error becomes independent of the mean's location"
  - [section 4.1]: Theorem 4.1 gives rates depending on quantiles of X (not X) with n₀ = √n log n
  - [section 4.3]: Multivariate extension Theorem 4.4; error independent of ‖μ‖₂
- Break condition: If too few unquantified samples (n₀ too small), Chernoff bounds on binomial counts fail, so α,β may not bracket true quantiles, causing bias or wrong scaling Δ.

### Mechanism 3: Robustification via Heavy-Tailed Mean Algorithm
- Claim: Applying a robust mean estimator (Depersin-Lecué) to the dithered samples yields optimal corruption-dependent rates O(λ√log n/n + η) instead of O(ηλ).
- Mechanism: Instead of averaging the dithered samples, feed {Diag(Δ)˙Xᵢ} (or {λ˙Xᵢ} in 1D) to a polynomial-time robust mean algorithm tolerant to η-fraction contamination.
- Core assumption: The covariance of the dithered samples is diagonal with known scale (λᵢ² or Δⱼ²); the robust algorithm requires log(1/δ) ≳ 300ηn for the corruption term.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Robust variants using robust mean algorithms extend these guarantees to adversarial corruption with an additive O(η) term"
  - [section 3.2]: Theorem 3.5 combines dithering with Proposition 3.4 (Depersin-Lecué)
  - [section 4.5]: Theorem 4.5 extends to multivariate partial quantization with robust estimator
- Break condition: If corruption fraction η is too large relative to n (ηn large), the robust algorithm's additive term may dominate; also the condition log(1/δ) ≥ 300ηn may be violated in high confidence regimes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Trimmed Mean**
  - Why needed here: The estimator hinges on truncating extreme values; understanding how quantile trimming biases the mean is essential for error analysis.
  - Quick check question: "For X with mean μ, how does E[ϕ_{α,β}(X)] compare to μ when α,β are population vs. empirical quantiles?"
- **Concept: Dithering in Quantization**
  - Why needed here: Dithering randomizes quantization error to avoid bias; the identity E[λ sign(x+λτ)] = clipped value is the core constructive step.
  - Quick check question: "Why does adding uniform noise before scalar quantization allow unbiased recovery of the original (clipped) value?"
- **Concept: Concentration of Empirical Quantiles**
  - Why needed here: Partial quantization uses empirical quantiles from n₀ samples; binomial/Chernoff bounds show α,β concentrate around true quantiles iff n₀ε is large.
  - Quick check question: "How large must n₀ be relative to n and ε to ensure |α - Q_ε(X)| = O(?) with high probability?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map:
  Input Parser -> Mode Selector -> Subset Sampler -> Quantile Estimator -> Dither Generator -> Quantization Engine -> Estimation Engine -> Post-processor
  (Partial mode includes Subset Sampler; Full distributed skips it.)
- Critical path:
  1. Detect mode → if partial, randomly pick n₀ subset and compute α,β, μ₁, Δ.
  2. For remaining n - n₀ (or all n in full mode), generate dither τᵢ and compute ˙Xᵢ.
  3. Apply chosen estimator (mean or robust) to transformed samples.
  4. Return final μ̂. For full distributed with corruption, must use robust estimator.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - **Fully Distributed**: No extra unquantized bits; requires prior λ bound on quantiles; error O(λ√log n/n) and corruption O(ηλ). Use if n₀-bit cost is prohibitive.
  - **Partial Quantization**: Slight overhead (32 n₀ bits) but eliminates mean dependence; corruption term O(η) instead of O(ηλ). Prefer when n₀ ≪ n.
  - **Simple vs Robust**: Simple mean is computationally lighter (O(nd)) but corruption-sensitive; robust algorithm (Depersin-Lecué) is polynomial but heavier; adds log d factor.
- Failure signatures:
  - λ too small ⇒ bias dominates: |μ̂ - μ| ≈ |λ - μ| P(X > λ) large.
  - n₀ε ≤ C ⇒ quantile estimates α,β miss true quantiles; Δ wrong scale; error large even without corruption.
  - Pre-corruption in partial mode without random subset selection ⇒ all n₀ uncorrupted samples could be replaced; α,β derived from corrupted data; fail gracefully if n₀ > ηn.
  - Using simple mean under post-corruption in multivariate ⇒ error O(η√∑λᵢ²) which may worsen with dimension; need robust estimator.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. **Univariate corruption-free**: n=50..500, X~N(100,1), n₀=⌈√n⌉, ε=√(2/n). Compare |μ̂-μ| vs |¯μ-μ|; expect ratio ≤2.
  2. **Multivariate low-trace**: d=n/10, Σ low-rank (diag(i⁻²)), n=200..1000. Test that error ∼ √(Tr(Σ)/n) nearly independent of d.
  3. **Robustness comparison**: n=1000, X~N(100,1), η=0.005..0.2, pre-corruption flips largest ηn samples symmetrically. Compare proposed estimator vs trimmed mean (¯μ_{trim}) with optimal ξ; expect similar errors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Is there a practical robust mean estimator for quantized data without the large constant factors of Depersin–Lecue?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "We leave it as an important open problem." (Section 5)
- Why unresolved: The current multivariate robust estimator (Theorem 3.5) uses Depersin–Lecue with large constants, hindering practical use.
- What evidence would resolve it: A new polynomial-time algorithm with comparable minimax guarantees and experimental validation showing lower error and faster runtime.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the corruption term η√∑λ_i^2 in the fully distributed setting be reduced using tail assumptions, and can we find a polynomial-time algorithm achieving such improvement?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Although the factor max_i λ_i√η is sharp ... it may be improved under stronger assumptions on the tail of X. However, we do not know how to achieve that using a polynomial-time algorithm." (after Theorem 3.5)
- Why unresolved: No known efficient algorithm exploits light-tailed distributions to weaken the η dependence.
- What evidence would resolve it: An algorithm with an upper bound replacing √∑λ_i^2 by a term like η·max_i λ_i (or o(√∑λ_i^2)·η), plus experiments verifying the improvement for sub-Gaussian data.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the extra logarithmic factor in Theorem 3.2 be removed or reduced while staying within polynomial time, thereby attaining the minimax rate without exponential computation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "We also remark that it might be the case that the extra log-factor could be improved using a version of the multivariate trimmed mean estimator from [22], however such estimator is known to be computationally intractable." (after Theorem 3.2)
- Why unresolved: The current efficient algorithm incurs a log factor; it is unknown if this is inherent to efficient quantization or just an artifact.
- What evidence would resolve it: A polynomial-time algorithm achieving O(√(Tr(Σ)/n) + max_i λ_i/√n) (or with only log d factors) or a lower bound proving necessity of logarithmic factors for any polynomial-time method.

## Limitations
- Fully quantized setting depends critically on a correct prior bound λ on outer quantiles; too small λ yields bias, too large increases variance.
- Partial quantization requires an unquantized subset of size Ω(√n log n) and condition n₀ε ≥ constant, which may be costly in bit-constrained regimes and restrictive under high corruption.
- Robustification relies on the Depersin–Lécué algorithm with strong assumptions (diagonal covariance, known scales) and needs log(1/δ) ≳ 300ηn, limiting practical applicability and high-confidence regimes.

## Confidence
- **Fully quantized minimax rates**: High (theory based on trimmed mean and dithering identity, with explicit theorems).
- **Partial quantization independence of μ**: Medium-High (hinges on empirical quantile concentration, which needs n₀ = Ω(√n log n)).
- **Robust corruption additive term O(η)**: Medium (sound but Depersin–Lécué assumptions and condition log(1/δ) ≥ 300ηn may be restrictive).
- **Numerical experiments**: Low (claims lack detailed description, reproducibility uncertain).

## Next Checks
1. **Sensitivity to λ misspecification**: In fully quantized setting, vary λ by factors 0.5× and 2× relative to true quantile bound for Gaussian and heavy-tailed distributions; verify error bias grows when λ too small and variance increases when too large.
2. **Partial quantization with minimal n₀**: Test performance when reducing n₀ below √n log n (e.g., n₀ = log n, √n) for sub-Gaussian data under corruption; check if quantile estimation fails and error increases, validating the n₀ requirement.
3. **Robust algorithm under heterogeneous corruption**: Apply robustified estimator with worst-case arbitrary corruption (asymmetric shifts) rather than symmetric flips; compare to trimmed mean and test if additive O(η) term holds when diagonal covariance assumption is violated (using estimated scales).
