# Jailbreak Scaling Laws for Large Language Models: Polynomial-Exponential Crossover

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2603.11331
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11331
* **Reference Count:** 40
* **Primary Result:** Adversarial prompt injection causes jailbreak attack success to transition from polynomial to exponential scaling with inference-time samples, a crossover explained by a spin-glass model where prompt strength acts as a magnetic field.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how the success rate of jailbreak attacks against safety-aligned large language models (LLMs) scales when increasing the number of inference-time samples. The authors discover a crossover: for short injected prompts (weak perturbations), the attack success rate gap decays polynomially with sample count, while for long injected prompts (strong perturbations), it decays exponentially. They model the LLM's generative process using mean-field spin-glass theory, where token generation corresponds to sampling from a rugged energy landscape decomposed into hierarchical clusters. Prompt injection is treated as an external magnetic field biasing sampling toward unsafe clusters. The theoretical framework predicts the polynomial-exponential transition due to a phase change in the student model's energy landscape under strong fields. Empirical validation on models like Llama-3-8B and GPT-4.5 supports the scaling laws, and fitting reveals that longer injections increase the inferred adversarial field strength.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a generative model ("SpinLLM") where both a "teacher" (safety-aligned) and "student" (potentially misaligned) model sample configurations (token sequences) from a mean-field p-spin Hamiltonian with Gaussian disorder. At low temperature, the teacher's energy landscape exhibits replica symmetry breaking (RSB), decomposing into hierarchically clustered "pure states" based on overlap. Safety is defined as belonging to a size-biased union of the first `m` clusters at a specific level `l`. The student's Hamiltonian adds an external magnetic field `h` aligned with the teacher's unsafe cluster centers, modeling prompt injection. The attack success rate for `k` independent student samples, `Π_k`, is derived by averaging over the disorder (cluster weights following a Poisson-Dirichlet distribution). Analytical asymptotics for weak vs. strong fields yield polynomial (`1−Π_k ≍ k^{-ν}`) and exponential (`log(1−Π_k) ≥ −kNI_h(q_{l+1})`) scaling, respectively. The crossover parameter is `λ = βhN(q_{l+1}−q_l)`. Empirical fits to LLM jailbreak data use a combined form `log(−log Π_k) = −ν log k − μk + log C`, where μ proxies field strength.

## Key Results
* Jailbreak attack success scaling transitions from polynomial (power-law) to exponential as injected prompt length increases, confirmed across Llama and GPT model families.
* The spin-glass framework accurately models this crossover: weak injection (small magnetic field `h`) perturbs the RSB landscape slightly, leading to polynomial decay; strong injection destroys ultrametricity, inducing an replica-symmetric (RS) ordered phase that yields exponential decay.
* Fitting the scaling curves shows that longer injections increase the inferred adversarial order parameter `μ`, correlating with higher susceptibility to jailbreaking.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
* **Claim:** Injection strength (`h`) determines whether attack success rate (ASR) scales polynomially or exponentially with sample count `k`.
* **Mechanism:** Prompt injection adds a field term `−h∑⟨σ,σ*⟩` to the student's Hamiltonian. For weak `h ≪ j₀`, it slightly perturbs the RSB cluster weights, causing a residual failure probability that decays as a power law `1−Π_k ≍ k^{−ν}`. For strong `h ≫ j₀`, the field dominates, aligning configurations toward unsafe clusters and driving an RS phase where failure probability decays exponentially `log(1−Π_k) ≥ −kNI_h(q_{l+1})`.
* **Core assumption:** The teacher's low-temperature Gibbs measure decomposes into a hierarchical cluster tree; unsafe outputs belong to a size-biased subset of early clusters.
* **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the crossover; Section 3.1 derives polynomial scaling (Eq 18) with `λ = βhNΔq` as small parameter; Section 3.2 Theorem 2 gives exponential bound.
* **Break condition:** If the student's phase does not change from RSB to RS under strong `h` (e.g., spin-glass interactions too strong), exponential scaling fails.

### Mechanism 2
* **Claim:** The teacher's safety boundary corresponds to a size-biased union of low-energy clusters in an RSB spin-glass.
* **Mechanism:** For a prompt `x`, the teacher's Hamiltonian `H_J(σ)` defines a rugged landscape. At low `β`, the Gibbs measure splits into clusters `S^l_a` (level `l`, index `a`) with normalized weights `S_β(V_a^l; J)`. Clusters are ordered by size (stick-breaking/Poisson-Dirichlet). The unsafe set `T_m^l` is the first `m` clusters in this order; a generation is unsafe if its configuration falls in `T_m^l`.
* **Core assumption:** In the RSB phase, cluster weights follow Poisson-Dirichlet `Λ^{m_l}` with parameter `m_l`; overlaps separate at levels `q_l`.
* **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.1 defines clusters (Eqs 11-12), size-biased ordering (Eq 14), and `T_m^l`; Appendix A gives Beta stick-breaking for `p_{l,m}`.
* **Break condition:** If the model's internal representations do not form a hierarchical cluster structure (e.g., operation in RS phase), the mapping from cluster membership to safety is invalid.

### Mechanism 3
* **Claim:** The polynomial-exponential scaling crossover arises because the student's thermodynamic phase changes from RSB (weak field) to RS (strong field).
* **Mechanism:** Under weak `h`, student and teacher share RSB structure; cluster weights are perturbed but remain hierarchically distributed, so the average single-draw failure probability `p` has a heavy-tailed distribution, leading to polynomial decay in `Π_k`. Under strong `h`, the field biases the measure around a fixed point aligned with unsafe clusters, causing `p` to concentrate near a value determined by a large-deviation rate function `I_h(q_{l+1})`, yielding exponential decay.
* **Core assumption:** The magnetic field term `h∑⟨σ,σ*⟩` can overcome glassy interactions when `h` exceeds a critical `h_c`, destroying ultrametricity.
* **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "ordered phase under strong magnetic field"; Section 3.2 Theorem 2 provides exponential bound; Section 4 fits LLM data with increasing `μ` (proxy for `h`).
* **Break condition:** If the spin-glass interactions are too strong relative to `h` (no phase transition), the student may remain in RSB and polynomial scaling persists.

## Foundational Learning
* **Mean-Field Spin-Glass Theory (RSB phase):** The model treats token generation as sampling from a disordered energy landscape (`p`-spin Hamiltonian). At low temperature, the landscape breaks into hierarchically clustered pure states ( ultrametric structure). This clustering is essential to define teacher safety as a union of specific clusters. *Quick check:* In RSB, how does the overlap distribution `P(q)` between two independent Gibbs samples reflect the hierarchical clustering?
* **Poisson–Dirichlet Process (`Λ^θ`):** Provides the limiting distribution for normalized Gibbs weights of clusters in the RSB phase. The parameter `θ = m_l` directly determines the polynomial exponent `ν` and scaling constant `C_m`. Without this, the closed-form for `E[Π_k]` cannot be derived. *Quick check:* In the stick-breaking construction `V_i ~ Beta(1−θ, iθ)`, what is `E[∏_{i=1}^m (1−V_i)]` and its scaling with `m`?
* **Inference-Time Scaling and Large Deviations:** ASR with `k` samples is `Π_k = 1−(1−p)^k`. Here `p` is a random variable (cluster weight). Its distribution changes with `h`: heavy-tailed under weak field (polynomial decay), concentrated under strong field (exponential decay). Understanding this distributional shift explains the crossover. *Quick check:* For fixed `p`, `Π_k` scales as `1−e^{-pk}`. How does randomness in `p` (from disorder) alter the asymptotic scaling in `k`?

## Architecture Onboarding
Teacher model -> defines energy landscape H_J(σ) with RSB clusters -> labels unsafe clusters T_m^l (size-biased) -> Student model samples with added magnetic field h aligned to unsafe cluster centers -> computes single-draw success probability A_N(h|J) -> averages over disorder J (Poisson-Dirichlet) -> yields E[Π_k] -> asymptotic analysis yields polynomial (weak h) vs exponential (strong h) scaling.
* **Critical path:**
  1. For prompt `x`, generate disorder `J(x,W)` for teacher and `J(x,Ŵ)` for student.
  2. Teacher finds low-energy cluster centers `σ_i^{*,l}` via greedy descent, computes their Gibbs weights `S_β(V_a^l; J)`.
  3. Size-bias order clusters; `T_m^l` = first `m` clusters.
  4. Student draws `k` samples from `p̂_β(σ|h)` where `h` aligns to `σ_i^{*,l}`.
  5. Estimate single-draw success `A_N(h|J) ≈ 1−p_{l,m}/(p_{l,m}+(1−p_{l,m})e^λ)` for weak `h`.
  6. Average `A_N` over `J` (i.e., over `p_{l,m}` distribution from Poisson-Dirichlet).
  7. Compute `Π_k = 1−(1−A_N)^k`.
  8. Take limits `N→∞`, then `k→∞` with `λ` vs `k` scaling to extract asymptotic polynomial/exponential forms.
* **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Analytical tractability vs. realism:* Assumes mean-field fully-connected `p`-spin, matched parameters (`W=Ŵ, β=β̂`), equal field strengths, and greedy descent clusters. These simplify analysis but deviate from autoregressive decoding, finite-`N`, and realistic architectures.
  - *Teacher safety definition:* Maps safety to an explicit energy-based set (size-biased clusters). Real LLM safety is learned via SFT/RLHF, not an explicit Hamiltonian level set.
  - *Non-autoregressive sampling:* Model assumes Gibbs sampling (or Langevin) from the full-configuration measure; real LLMs use sequential token generation, introducing dependencies.
* **Failure signatures:**
  - Empirical scaling curves (especially for smaller models like Vicuna-7B) deviate from pure polynomial/exponential forms, indicating non-asymptotic effects or model mismatch.
  - Parameter identification relies on fitting `μ` (proxy for `h`); injection length to `λ` mapping is indirect and depends on estimating `Δq`.
  - If student remains in RSB phase even for large `h` (e.g., due to strong higher-order spins), exponential scaling prediction fails.
* **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Numerical spin-glass validation:** For small `N` (e.g., 24), enumerate states, compute teacher clusters via greedy descent, apply magnetic field `h` to student, measure `A_N(h)`. Plot `log(−log(1−Π_k))` vs `log k` for various `h`; verify polynomial slope `ν` for small `h` and exponential curvature for large `h`.
  2. **Fitting scaling laws to LLM data:** For Llama-3.2-3B/8B, measure ASR with `k`=1–125 for different injection lengths (0, 14, 20 tokens). Fit `log(−log Π_k) = −ν log k − μk + log C`. Check if `μ` increases with injection length.
  3. **Phase transition detection:** In spin-glass simulations, compute pairwise overlap `q` between independent student samples at different `h`. Examine ultrametric structure via Parisi overlap distribution `P(q)`. Strong `h` should collapse `P(q)` to a single peak (RS), correlating with exponential scaling onset.

## Limitations
* The mean-field RSB assumptions (fully-connected `p`-spin, large `N`, Poisson-Dirichlet cluster weights) may not hold for real autoregressive LLMs with finite size and structured training data.
* There is no direct evidence that safety-aligned LLMs' internal representations correspond to a size-biased cluster hierarchy in an RSB energy landscape; the safety definition is a modeling assumption.
* Mapping injection length to magnetic field strength `h` relies on indirect fits (parameter `μ`), and scaling curves for weaker models are noisy, suggesting non‑asymptotic effects.

## Confidence
* **Polynominal‑exponential crossover claim:** **Medium** (empirical fits across multiple models support the pattern, but deviations in smaller models and large-`N` theory assumptions reduce certainty).
* **Spin‑glass generative framework (teacher landscape, Poisson‑Dirichlet clusters):** **Low** (the abstraction is unverified in actual LLM internals; no evidence that learned safety aligns with such clusters).
* **Injection length ↔ `h` equivalence:** **Medium** (consistent increase of fitted `μ` with injection length, but `h` itself is not directly measured and alternative explanations exist).

## Next Checks
1. **Ultrametricity test:** For a fixed prompt, sample many unsafe completions from an aligned model, extract latent representations (e.g., last-layer hidden states), compute pairwise overlaps. Test for hierarchical ultrametric structure (e.g., via trihedral condition or non-trivial Parisi `P(q)`). Absence falsifies the RSB assumption.
2. **Gradient‑based `h` estimation:** For varying injection lengths, compute gradient of output log‑probability with respect to input embeddings. Project this gradient onto a reference unsafe direction (e.g., difference between safe/unsafe prompt embeddings). Correlate projection magnitude with fitted `μ`.
3. **Disorder‑average validation:** Across diverse prompts, estimate per‑prompt single‑draw attack success rates. Fit a Beta mixture to these rates; compare the expected product `E[Π_1]` to the Poisson-Dirichlet prediction. Significant mismatch indicates the disorder average does not reflect empirical data.
