# Stable-LoRA: Stabilizing Feature Learning of Low-Rank Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05204
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05204
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** Stable-LoRA resolves LoRA's unstable feature learning via progressive weight-shrinkage, improving QA accuracy by up to 4% with negligible overhead.

## Executive Summary
LoRA fine-tuning suffers from unstable feature learning when matrix A is non-zero initialized, causing output updates to scale with model width and degrade performance. Stable-LoRA introduces a simple weight-shrinkage strategy that progressively reduces A during early training steps until its normalized norm matches B's, restoring stable feature learning. Across multiple LLM scales and question-answering tasks, it yields consistent accuracy gains—e.g., 61.94% to 64.01% for 0.5B models and 83.53% to 84.03% for 3B models versus AdamW—while adding no memory overhead and only ~0.6% training time.

## Method Summary
Stable-LoRA modifies standard LoRA by adding an in-place shrinkage operation to matrix A before each gradient update during early training. Specifically, A is scaled by (1−λ) at each step, where λ is a small shrinkage ratio. This continues until the average normalized Frobenius norm of A (divided by its input dimension n) is no larger than that of B (divided by its output dimension m). The condition ∥A∥_F/n ≤ ∥B∥_F/m serves as a practical proxy for the theoretical stability guarantee γ[A_tZ] ≤ γ[η]+1. Training proceeds with AdamW; once the stable condition holds, shrinkage ceases and standard LoRA updates resume. Initialization remains A₀ ~ Uniform[−1/n, 1/n], B₀ = 0.

## Key Results
- On QA benchmarks, average accuracy rises from 61.94% to 64.01% (0.5B models) and from 83.53% to 84.03% (3B models) versus AdamW, with gains up to 4%.
- Outperforms LoRA+, Riemann, and LoRA-RITE across evaluated tasks.
- No additional memory overhead; training time increases by only ~0.6%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Non-zero initialization of matrix A disrupts LoRA's inherent self-stabilization of feature learning.
- **Mechanism:** The stability condition requires γ[A_tZ] ≤ γ[η] + 1, where γ tracks scaling with width n. With A₀ ≠ 0, γ[A₀Z] becomes finite; if γ[A₀Z] > γ[η]+1, the induction relation γ[A_tZ] = max(γ[A_{t-1}Z], γ[η]+1) ensures γ[A_tZ] remains too large for all t. This causes δ₁ = sηg_B A_tZ to dominate δ₂ = sη B_t g_A Z in ΔY, making learned feature scale depend on n (exploding).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "the necessary non-zero initialization of A compromises self-stability"; [section] Section 3.2 derives the condition and violation consequences; [corpus] Neighbor paper "Beyond Zero Initialization" studies non-zero A impact.
- **Break condition:** If γ[η]+1 ≥ γ[A₀Z] (e.g., with very large η), stability may hold but is impractical for typical small learning rates.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Progressive weight-shrinkage of A during early training steps restores stability while preserving non-zero A's early benefits.
- **Mechanism:** At each step t before update, A is scaled by (1−λ). This yields A_t = (1−λ)^t A₀ + gradient terms. For sufficient t or λ, (1−λ)^t A₀ scales as N·γ[1−λ] + γ[A₀Z]; since γ[1−λ] ≤ 0, this eventually drops below γ[η]+1. Feature learning becomes stable from step N+1 onward. Shrinkage stops once ∥A∥_F/n ≤ ∥B∥_F/m.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "progressively shrinking A during the earliest training steps"; [section] Algorithm 1 and Equation 7 formalize shrinkage; Section 5.4 validates the stable condition empirically.
- **Break condition:** λ too small → shrinkage insufficient; λ too large → erases A₀'s beneficial signal before B learns.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Achieving stable feature learning (γ[ΔY]=0) leads to consistent accuracy improvements across model scales and tasks.
- **Mechanism:** When γ[ΔY]=0, both δ₁ and δ₂ contributions scale as Θ(1), ensuring balanced learning from A and B updates regardless of width n. This prevents gradient explosion/vanishing in downstream layers and maintains signal fidelity. Empirically, stable training trajectories correlate with higher final task accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section] Definition 1 and Equation 3 derive γ[ΔY]=0; [abstract] "Experiments... show consistent accuracy improvements"; [section] Tables 1–3 demonstrate gains; Figure 2 visualizes baseline instability.
- **Break condition:** If γ[ΔY] ≠ 0 (only one δ term Θ(1)), updates vanish or explode with n, degrading performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **γ-function notation**: Symbolic tool to track how quantities scale with model width n (Section 2.3). Needed to formally define stability (ΔY = Θ(n⁰)) and derive initialization constraints. Quick check: If γ[v] = -2, v → 0 as 1/n².
- **LoRA self-stability conditions**: The pair γ[A₀Z] ≤ γ[η]+1 and γ[B₀] ≤ γ[η] (Equation 5) are jointly necessary and sufficient for γ[ΔY]=0 under tuned γ[s]+2γ[η]+1=0. This explains why standard A₀≠0 initialization breaks stability. Quick check: What if only γ[B₀] ≤ η holds? (Answer: γ[A_tZ] > γ[η]+1 persists, causing imbalance.)
- **Stable condition (norm ratio)**: Practical heuristic ∥A∥_F/n ≤ ∥B∥_F/m approximates theoretical condition without computing γ[A_tZ]. It works because when A and B scales are comparable, δ₁ and δ₂ naturally balance (Section 4, validated in Table 4). Quick check: Why divide by n (for A) and m (for B)? (Answer: Normalizes Frobenius norm by matrix dimension to compare effective per-element scales.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: A (r×n, trainable) → B (m×r, trainable) → norm tracker (computes ∥A∥_F/n and ∥B∥_F/m) → stable flag (boolean)
- **Critical path**: Per training step: forward pass → loss → backward → obtain g_A, g_B → if not stable and norm_A > norm_B: A ← (1−λ)·A → update A ← A − η·g_A − η·w·A → update B similarly → check stable condition → set flag if satisfied.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - λ: larger → faster stabilization but risks losing A₀'s early signal; smaller → slower stabilization, may leave window of instability. Requires tuning (Table 8).
  - Stable condition: norm ratio is simple and effective but heuristic; theoretically could be refined but adds overhead.
  - Overhead: O(r·n) for norms, but r<<n, and only in early steps → negligible (0.6% time increase, Table 5).
- **Failure signatures**:
  1. norm_A/n stays above norm_B/m across steps → shrinkage ineffective (λ too small or condition wrong).
  2. Final accuracy below AdamW baseline → stability not achieved or λ harmed learning.
  3. Stable flag never flips → check condition implementation or λ=0.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Verify shrinkage dynamics**: Log norm_A/n and norm_B/m every 100 steps; expect clear drop in norm_A relative to B within first few hundred steps.
  2. **Sensitivity to λ**: Run with λ ∈ {0.0005, 0.001, 0.002, 0.005} on a single task; confirm accuracy gain over AdamW for all λ, but best λ may vary (Table 8).
  3. **Ablate stable condition**: Remove the "break" from Algorithm 1 (always shrink A) and compare to original; Table 4 shows minimal difference → condition itself not performance-critical but useful for halting early.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does Stable-LoRA's theoretical guarantee of stable feature learning hold when using gradient optimizers other than AdamW (e.g., SGD, RMSProp)? (Limitations state compatibility not systematically evaluated; analysis assumes normalized gradients with γ(g)=0.)
2. Is the proposed stable condition (‖A‖_F/n ≤ ‖B‖_F/m) universally sufficient to ensure stable feature learning across all fine-tuning tasks and model architectures? (Empirical validation limited to QA/CoT with decoder-only LLMs.)
3. Can Stable-LoRA's weight-shrinkage strategy be effectively extended to cross-modal fine-tuning scenarios, such as vision-language models? (Performance in such settings remains to be explored.)
4. How sensitive is Stable-LoRA to the choice of shrinkage ratio λ and initialization of A₀, and can adaptive schedules outperform fixed λ? (λ requires tuning; interaction with learning rate/rank underexplored; adaptive schedules not studied.)

## Limitations
- Asymptotic γ-scaling may not hold at typical fine-tuning widths; finite-width effects could invalidate stability condition.
- Norm-ratio heuristic ∥A∥_F/n ≤ ∥B∥_F/m lacks theoretical guarantee and may be sensitive to batch size and architecture.
- Evaluation limited to ≤3B LLMs on QA/CoT tasks; broader generalization untested.

## Confidence
- Claim 1 (Non-zero A causes instability via γ[A₀Z] > γ[η]+1): Medium (relies on idealized assumptions).
- Claim 2 (Weight-shrinkage restores stability): High (supported by ablations and norm dynamics).
- Claim 3 (Stable-LoRA improves accuracy): Medium (results consistent but limited benchmark coverage and no statistical significance).

## Next Checks
1. Norm dynamics replication: Run with ≥5 seeds, record ∥A∥_F/n and ∥B∥_F/m per step; verify stable condition flip and its correlation with loss variance reduction.
2. λ scaling study: Sweep λ ∈ [0.0001,0.01] across model sizes 0.5B, 3B, 10B to identify trends and potential adaptive rule.
3. Alternative stabilization comparison: Implement orthogonal initialization for A and gradient clipping; isolate the effect of progressive shrinkage.
