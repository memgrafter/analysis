# Mitigating topology biases in Graph Diffusion via Counterfactual Intervention
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.02005
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02005
- **Reference Count**: 37
- **Primary Result**: FairGDiff achieves a superior fairness-utility trade-off by reducing topology bias ratios toward the unbiased target of 1, while preserving downstream task performance and offering improved computational efficiency.

## Executive Summary
Graph diffusion models can amplify existing topology biases linked to sensitive attributes (e.g., gender, race), leading to unfair synthetic graphs. Current fair generation methods often require sensitive attribute labels or simultaneously modify structure and attributes, limiting their applicability. This paper proposes FairGDiff, a one-step counterfactual intervention approach that directly mitigates topology bias. It builds a causal model to estimate an unbiased treatment for edge formation and integrates this into both the forward diffusion and backward denoising processes. Experiments on NBA, German Credit, and Pokec datasets demonstrate that FairGDiff attains a better balance between fairness (ΔDP, ΔEO) and utility (accuracy, NDCG@10) compared to baselines, significantly reducing biased edge ratios while maintaining efficiency.

## Method Summary
FairGDiff introduces a causal inference framework to address topology bias in graph diffusion. It first constructs a structural causal model (SCM) to represent the generative process of edges, incorporating sensitive attributes as exogenous variables. The core innovation is a counterfactual treatment estimation: for a node pair (i,j), the method identifies a bias-free reference context by finding similar node pairs where the sensitive attribute is independent of the edge, using a similarity threshold ξ. This yields an unbiased indicator T_CF^ij. This counterfactual treatment is then integrated into the diffusion process—guiding both the forward noising step (by adjusting edge probabilities) and the reverse denoising step (by modifying the denoising network's loss). This one-step intervention avoids iterative rebias-correction cycles and works within a latent diffusion model setup for scalability.

## Key Results
- FairGDiff achieves a superior fairness-utility trade-off, significantly lowering ΔDP and ΔEO compared to baselines while maintaining competitive accuracy and NDCG@10.
- Topology bias ratios (e.g., edge ratio between groups) are reduced toward the unbiased target of 1, demonstrating effective debiasing of graph structure.
- The method offers better computational efficiency than baselines, attributed to its one-step counterfactual intervention avoiding multiple re-training phases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The counterfactual intervention directly targets the root of topology bias by simulating what edge probabilities would be if sensitive attributes had no causal effect. By estimating an unbiased treatment via similar, bias-free contexts (within similarity threshold ξ), the method removes the spurious correlation between sensitive attributes and edge formation from the diffusion process. Integrating this treatment into both forward and backward diffusion ensures that bias is not reintroduced during generation. This causal "editing" of the diffusion dynamics allows the model to generate graphs where structure is independent of sensitive attributes, while preserving non-sensitive attribute utility because the treatment estimation respects non-sensitive feature similarity.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: 
Causal Estimator (finds similar bias-free pairs within ξ) -> Unbiased Treatment (T_CF) -> Diffusion Module (uses T_CF to adjust forward noising and reverse denoising loss) -> Generated Graph

**Critical Path**: 
1. For each node pair, compute similarity based on non-sensitive attributes. 
2. Identify reference pairs with similar context but different sensitive attribute. 
3. Estimate T_CF as average edge indicator from references. 
4. Modify diffusion forward step using T_CF to bias edge probabilities. 
5. Train denoising network with loss incorporating T_CF. 
6. Sample new graphs via reverse diffusion.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Fairness vs. Utility: Stronger debiasing (larger T_CF adjustment) may distort useful structural patterns. 
- Similarity Threshold ξ: Smaller ξ yields purer unbiased estimates but may have too few references; larger ξ risks including biased pairs. 
- Latent Diffusion: Used for scalability but may lose fine structural details compared to discrete diffusion.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- High topology bias ratios (>>1) after generation indicates inadequate reference pairs or poor similarity measure. 
- Significant drop in utility metrics (accuracy, NDCG) suggests over-correction harming predictive structure. 
- Training instability if ξ is too small, leading to sparse T_CF estimates.

**First Three Experiments**: 
1. Run FairGDiff on a small synthetic graph with known bias to verify that generated edges become independent of sensitive attribute (χ² test). 
2. Ablate the counterfactual injection: compare metrics with/without T_CF in diffusion to isolate its contribution. 
3. Sweep ξ on a validation set to identify a stable range where bias reduction plateaus without utility loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can FairGDiff be effectively integrated with other families of graph diffusion models (e.g., DDPMs, SGMs, SDEs) while maintaining fairness and potentially improving utility or efficiency?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state in the Limitations: "Although our experiments follow previous works and utilize a latent graph diffusion model for training due to computational constraints, this does not imply that other types of graph diffusion models cannot be easily adapted in FairGDiff. As computational resources advance, future work can explore the integration of alternative diffusion models to further enhance performance and flexibility."
- **Why unresolved**: The current implementation and experiments are limited to a latent diffusion backbone; it remains unknown whether other diffusion paradigms (e.g., discrete denoising, score-based, SDE-based) can incorporate the counterfactual treatment without degrading fairness-utility trade-offs or requiring substantial architectural modifications.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Comprehensive experiments replacing the latent diffusion backbone with multiple diffusion frameworks (DDPM, SGM, SDE) on the same benchmark datasets, comparing fairness metrics (ΔDP, ΔEO) and utility (accuracy, NDCG@10) against the current FairGDiff, to verify adaptability and identify any model-specific challenges.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can FairGDiff be extended to handle sensitive attributes that are multi-valued (more than two categories) or continuous, and how should fairness definitions be generalized accordingly?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The method assumes binary sensitive attributes: "We assume all attributes are categorical and binary with the first attribute as the sensitive attribute and the rest d−1 attributes as non-sensitive attribute." This restriction is not discussed as a limitation but implicitly bounds the applicability.
- **Why unresolved**: Real-world sensitive attributes often have multiple groups (e.g., race, ethnicity) or continuous ranges (e.g., age). The current counterfactual treatment definition (Eq. 8) and fairness metrics (ΔDP, ΔEO) are defined for binary splits. Generalizing to multiple or continuous sensitive attributes requires rethinking the causal model and possibly the treatment estimation process.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A generalized FairGDiff variant that defines treatment and counterfactuals for multi-valued or continuous sensitive attributes, validated on datasets with such attributes (e.g., multi-category race or age groups), demonstrating fairness metrics that extend naturally (e.g., multigroup ΔDP) and maintain utility.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the appropriate strategy for selecting the similarity threshold ξ used in the counterfactual treatment estimation, and how does its value affect the fairness-utility trade-off?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Section 4.2 describes: "If such a bias-free context exists within a certain similarity threshold ξ, we use T_F^ab to estimate the unbiased indicator T_CF^ij." However, the paper does not specify how ξ is chosen or whether it is tuned.
- **Why unresolved**: ξ controls the pool of candidate unbiased node pairs; an inappropriate threshold might yield too few estimates (reducing debiasing) or include biased pairs (undermining fairness). No analysis or sensitivity test of ξ is provided, leaving its selection ambiguous.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic evaluation over a range of ξ values on multiple datasets, measuring topology bias ratios, ΔDP/ΔEO, and utility metrics. The results would identify stable regions or an adaptive selection criterion (e.g., based on graph density or attribute distribution).

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Can FairGDiff be adapted to generate fair directed graphs, where edge directionality introduces asymmetric bias patterns not considered in the current undirected formulation?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The problem setup defines an undirected graph: "Let G = (U, A) be an undirected attributed graph." The adjacency matrix is treated symmetrically, and the bias definitions (Eq. 5) consider unordered node pairs. Directed graphs are not addressed.
- **Why unresolved**: Many high-stakes applications involve directed relationships (e.g., citation networks, financial transactions). The direction of an edge may carry distinct biases (e.g., bias in who cites whom). The current counterfactual treatment and diffusion process assume symmetric edge probabilities, which may not hold for directed settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An extension of FairGDiff to directed graphs by modifying the causal model to handle ordered pairs (i → j) and redefining treatment accordingly. Empirical validation on directed benchmark datasets with sensitive attributes, showing that the generated directed graphs achieve fairness (e.g., direction-aware ΔDP) while preserving downstream task performance.

## Limitations
- The approach requires access to sensitive attribute labels during training, which may be restricted due to privacy or legal constraints.
- Evaluation is limited to three standard datasets (NBA, German Credit, Pokec); generalizability to larger-scale or domain-specific graphs (e.g., biological networks) is not established.
- Claims of computational efficiency lack detailed benchmarking on very large graphs (>100k nodes), and robustness to latent confounders is untested.
