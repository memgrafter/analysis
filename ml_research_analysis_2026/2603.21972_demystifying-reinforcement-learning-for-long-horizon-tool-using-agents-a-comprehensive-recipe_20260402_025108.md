# Demystifying Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Tool-Using Agents: A Comprehensive Recipe

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21972
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21972
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introducing the STAR pipeline yields state-of-the-art TravelPlanner success rates (62.8% for 7B models), far surpassing prior LLMs (<15%) and matching specialized baselines on QA benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the absence of practical recipes for scaling reinforcement learning (RL) to train long-horizon, tool-using agents. It presents STAR—a unified pipeline for synthesizing controllable travel queries, applying supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and then optimizing policies via RL on the TravelPlanner benchmark. Through systematic evaluation of reward design, model scaling (1.5B–7B), data composition, algorithm choice, and environmental stability, the authors derive a concrete recipe. Applying it, their models dramatically improve agentic planning success, demonstrating that smaller models benefit from curriculum rewards and exploration-heavy algorithms, while larger models converge efficiently with dense rewards and standard GRPO. An optimal RL training set size of ~1K queries with balanced difficulty is identified, alongside sensitivity to environmental noise.

## Method Summary
The STAR pipeline consists of three stages. First, **Data Synthesis** generates >10k feasible travel queries with controllable difficulty (easy/medium/hard) by sampling elements (city, date) and validating feasibility via a sandbox. Second, **SFT** uses a strong teacher model (DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp-Thinking) to generate successful ReAct trajectories on a 5k query pool, which are used for full-parameter fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-Instruct models (1.5B/3B/7B). Third, **RL Training** employs the GRPO algorithm (or variants) on a disjoint 1k query set (difficulty mixed 4:3:3). Rewards are computed by a formatting model converting the agent's final plan to JSON, which is validated against TravelPlanner constraints. The best checkpoint is selected based on validation success and evaluated on the TravelPlanner test set and seven out-of-distribution QA benchmarks.

## Key Results
- RL training boosts TravelPlanner success from 19.7% (SFT) to 62.8% (7B model), dramatically exceeding prior LLMs (<15%).
- The optimal RL data regime is ~1K queries with a 4:3:3 easy:medium:hard balance, maximizing both in-domain and out-of-domain performance.
- Model scale dictates optimal reward shaping: smaller models (1.5B/3B) require curriculum rewards and exploration-heavy algorithms, while the 7B model converges best with dense SUM rewards and standard GRPO.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The optimal reward shaping strategy for long-horizon RL depends on model scale, with smaller models needing curriculum rewards and enhanced exploration while larger models efficiently use dense rewards.
- **Mechanism:** Smaller models struggle with credit assignment over long trajectories under sparse rewards; a staged curriculum (dense → sparse) provides intermediate guidance. Larger models have sufficient capacity to directly parse dense, fine-grained feedback across many steps.
- **Break condition:** If a 3B model still strongly prefers dense rewards on a similarly constrained long-horizon task, either the task complexity exceeds the scale threshold or the curriculum transition schedule is suboptimal.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 4.2; Fig 7; corpus context on tool-use failure irreversibility and long-horizon challenges.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** RL training data for long-horizon agents exhibits a "sweet spot" near 1K prompts with balanced difficulty (easy:medium:hard = 4:3:3), optimizing both in-domain and out-of-domain performance.
- **Mechanism:** Too little data lacks diversity; too much causes overfitting to the task distribution, harming generalization. Pure-easy data misses complex constraints; pure-hard data yields vanishingly rare successes, causing reward sparsity and instability. A balanced mixture provides dense intermediate rewards while gradually increasing complexity.
- **Break condition:** If the 1K/4:3:3 optimum persists across different long-horizon benchmarks, the mechanism is task-generalizable. If optimal scale scales linearly with model size, the sweet spot is primarily capacity-limited.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 4.4; Fig 5 & Table 9; corpus on synthetic trajectory quality.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Random tool execution failures during RL training cause progressive performance degradation, with agents tolerating ≤5% noise but experiencing significant instability when failure rates reach ≥10%.
- **Mechanism:** During exploration, successful trajectories provide reliable gradient signals. Random failures insert invalid observations, corrupting reasoning. At low rates (1–5%), the agent can learn implicit robustness (e.g., retries). At high rates (≥10%), the probability of a fully successful rollout drops below a viable learning threshold, starving the policy of meaningful gradients and increasing variance.
- **Break condition:** If adding explicit error-handling prompts or retry logic eliminates the 10% degradation, the mechanism is about observation robustness rather than fundamental sample inefficiency. If gradient clipping or KL penalties restore stability, the breakpoint is algorithmic.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.6; Fig 6; corpus on irreversible side effects of tool failures.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Policy Gradient RL (GRPO)**
  - **Why needed:** RL is the core optimization stage; understanding group-based advantage estimation and clipping explains algorithm-scale interactions.
  - **Quick check:** Why does a larger KL clip bound (ϵ_high) help exploration in GRPO for agentic tasks?
- **Concept: ReAct Paradigm**
  - **Why needed:** The entire pipeline uses interleaved Thought-Action-Observation cycles; misunderstanding this loop breaks comprehension of trajectory construction.
  - **Quick check:** How does the tool observation "Sorry, we cannot find..." influence the agent's next reasoning step?
- **Concept: TravelPlanner Evaluation**
  - **Why needed:** Success depends on satisfying both commonsense and hard constraints; knowing which rules fall into each category explains failure modes.
  - **Quick check:** If an itinerary repeats a restaurant, which constraint fails—commonsense or hard—and why?
- **Concept: Curriculum Learning**
  - **Why needed:** CURRICULUM reward is key for smaller models; understanding staged supervision clarifies how dense signals prevent early collapse.
  - **Quick check:** How would changing the CURRICULUM epoch schedule (e.g., 1/1/3 instead of 2/2/1) affect a 1.5B model's convergence?
- **Concept: Alignment Tax**
  - **Why needed:** Takeaway 2 highlights that dense rewards can degrade OOD generalization; this concept explains overfitting to task-specific formats.
  - **Quick check:** Why does SUM reward improve TravelPlanner scores but hurt NQ/TriviaQA performance in 7B models?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Synthesis (element sampler -> feasibility checker -> query generator -> difficulty classifier) -> SFT (teacher rollout -> filter Success -> full-param fine-tune base model) -> RL (rLLM framework: rollout buffer -> GRPO/DAPO/ARPO update -> checkpointing based on validation) -> Evaluation (formatting model -> JSON conversion -> TravelPlanner validator).
- **Critical path:** Synthetic data generation (10K+) -> SFT on 1.2K filtered successes -> RL training on 1K disjoint mixed-difficulty queries (4:3:3) -> select best checkpoint on validation -> test on 1K TravelPlanner test + 7 QA benchmarks. Format fidelity must be maintained at every stage.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Reward density: Dense (SUM) gives stronger gradients but risks alignment tax; sparse (SUCCESS) is robust but converges slowly for small models.
  - Exploration algorithm: ARPO/DAPO help small models but increase training time; GRPO is simpler and scales better.
  - Data size vs generalization: 1K balanced data is sweet spot; more data hurts OOD; harder data alone causes reward sparsity.
  - Environmental realism: Deterministic sandbox enables reliable RL; injecting noise >5% risks policy starvation.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Entropy collapse (early ↓ to near 0): overfitting, often from too-hard data or too small model.
  - Training instability (loss spikes, reward variance ↑): likely from high environmental failure rate or mismatched reward scale.
  - OOD performance drop while in-domain rises: alignment tax from overly task-specific dense rewards.
  - Zero-success rollouts throughout: reward sparsity—check RL data difficulty mixture and teacher model success rate.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline sanity check:** Run SFT checkpoint (no RL) on TravelPlanner test. Expect ~20% success for 7B. Confirms environment and evaluation pipeline.
  2. **Factor isolation - reward:** For target scale (e.g., 3B), fix data = Mixed-1K, algorithm = GRPO. Test SUM, MACRO, SUCCESS, CURRICULUM. Record success rates and OOD average. Verifies scale-dependent reward mechanism.
  3. **Factor isolation - data composition:** Keep reward = optimal from Expt 2 (e.g., CURRICULUM for 3B), algorithm = GRPO. Test 0.5K/1K/2K (balanced) and Easy-1K/Medium-1K/Hard-1K. Plot success + OOD. Verifies data sweet spot and difficulty balance.

## Limitations
- GRPO clipping bounds (ε_low/ε_high) unreported; DAPO defaults may not reflect original setup.
- DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp teacher model version/access unclear; substitutions risk SFT quality drift.
- Findings rely solely on TravelPlanner; cross-benchmark generalizability (e.g., to ToolGym) unvalidated.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (scale-dependent reward):** High. Multi-scale experiments show consistent patterns aligned with credit-assignment theory.
- **Mechanism 2 (1K data sweet spot):** Medium. Strong on TravelPlanner but lacks external validation; may be task-specific.
- **Mechanism 3 (noise sensitivity ≤5%):** Medium. Observed trend robust but only in sandbox; real-world API variability unknown.
