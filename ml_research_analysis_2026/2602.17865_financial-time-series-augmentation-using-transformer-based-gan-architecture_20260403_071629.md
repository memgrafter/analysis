# Financial time series augmentation using transformer based GAN architecture

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17865
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17865
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** TTS-GAN augmentation reduces financial time series forecasting MSE by 0.037–0.132 (p<0.05) on Bitcoin and S&P500 data across 40 windows.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TTS-GAN, a Transformer-based Generative Adversarial Network for augmenting scarce financial time series data. It enhances training stability through a simplified gradient penalty applied only to real samples and proposes the DTW DeD-iMs metric to evaluate temporal and distributional fidelity of generated sequences. By training LSTM forecasters on augmented datasets from Bitcoin and S&P500 across 40 distinct time windows, the method consistently achieves statistically significant reductions in Mean Squared Error compared to using real data only, demonstrating effectiveness in mitigating data scarcity.

## Method Summary
Financial data is preprocessed into fixed-length segments (K points) with sample-wise MinMax scaling based solely on the initial context (T points) to prevent future leakage. A Transformer-based GAN is trained using the simplified gradient penalty, with convergence monitored by the DTW DeD-iMs metric rather than traditional losses. After training, synthetic samples are generated to double the training set size (1:1 ratio). An LSTM forecaster is then trained on both original and augmented datasets, and performance is evaluated on held-out test sets using MSE across multiple window splits, with statistical significance assessed via paired t-tests.

## Key Results
- MSE improvements ranged from 0.037 to 0.132 across 40 time windows.
- All reductions were statistically significant (p < 0.05).
- Larger gains were observed for the volatile Bitcoin dataset compared to S&P500.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Transformer Architecture for Long-Range Dependencies
- **Claim:** Transformer-based architecture captures complex, long-range temporal dependencies more effectively than RNNs.
- **Mechanism:** Self-attention allows parallel processing of entire sequences, directly modeling non-linear relationships across arbitrary time lags without sequential compression, avoiding vanishing gradients.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and section 2.1 emphasize Transformer's advantage for long-range dependencies in financial data.
- **Break condition:** If data lacks long-range dependencies, Transformer complexity may cause overfitting without benefit.

### Mechanism 2: Simplified Gradient Penalty for Stability
- **Claim:** Applying gradient penalty only to real samples stabilizes adversarial training on volatile financial datasets.
- **Mechanism:** Constraining the discriminator's gradient norm on real data prevents it from overpowering the generator, mitigating mode collapse and vanishing gradients in non-stationary series.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.1 highlights its critical role in stabilizing training and accelerating convergence.
- **Break condition:** Excessively high penalty coefficient over-constrains the discriminator, leading to weak feedback and low-fidelity samples.

### Mechanism 3: DTW DeD-iMs for Temporal Fidelity
- **Claim:** The DTW DeD-iMs metric ensures synthetic data preserves temporal dynamics essential for forecasting, which distributional metrics alone miss.
- **Mechanism:** DTW measures elastic alignment between sequences, penalizing phase shifts; DeD-iMs extends this to dataset level by comparing distributions of nearest-neighbor DTW distances, guiding the GAN to produce temporally coherent samples.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.4.1 proposes the metric; section 5 shows it captures finer temporal improvements than Wasserstein distance.
- **Break condition:** DTW window constraints must be tuned; the metric is specific to forecasting tasks where temporal ordering matters.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
TTS-GAN Generator (noise vector z) -> Transformer decoder -> Synthetic K-point sequence
TTS-GAN Discriminator (real/synthetic K-point sequence) -> Transformer encoder -> Real/fake probability
Forecasting LSTM (first T points of K-point sequence) -> LSTM layers -> Predict next S points
DTW DeD-iMs Evaluator (batch of real and synthetic sequences) -> Compute pairwise DTW matrix -> Calculate DeD-iMs statistic

**Critical path:**
1. Preprocess financial data into fixed-length K samples with sample-wise MinMax scaling based on first T points.
2. Train TTS-GAN on training+validation set until DTW DeD-iMs converges.
3. Generate synthetic samples to achieve 1:1 real-to-synthetic ratio.
4. Train LSTM on original and augmented training sets; evaluate on held-out test set.
5. Compare MSE across 40 window splits using paired t-test.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Discriminator capacity (higher embedding size, attention heads) stabilizes training but risks suppressing generator; generator capacity is lower.
- Sample-wise normalization prevents future leakage but may erase inter-sample trends and heteroscedasticity.
- Fixed augmentation ratio (1:1) may not be optimal for all asset volatilities or dataset sizes.

**Failure signatures:**
- GAN instability: Discriminator loss plummets to zero, generator loss oscillates, DTW DeD-iMs fails to decrease.
- Poor synthetic quality: High variance in LSTM performance across retrains; low DTW DeD-iMs but no MSE improvement, indicating distribution match but temporal misalignment.
- Data leakage: If normalization uses future S-points, test MSE becomes artificially low and non-generalizable.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Ablate simplified gradient penalty: Train TTS-GANs with original WGAN-GP vs. simplified real-only penalty; compare discriminator loss stability and DTW DeD-iMs convergence.
2. Test augmentation ratio sensitivity: For a Bitcoin window, train LSTMs with ratios 1:1, 2:1, 3:1; measure MSE improvement to find saturation point.
3. Validate metric: For a trained GAN, compute Wasserstein, DTW DeD-iMs, and LSTM MSE on synthetic-only data; correlate DTW DeD-iMs with synthetic-only MSE to confirm predictive power.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How do transformer-based forecasting models benefit from TTS-GAN augmentation compared to LSTMs, and does the MSE reduction magnitude differ?
   - Basis: Paper suggests including transformer architectures in future work.
   - Why unresolved: Not tested; transformers may have different sensitivity to synthetic data.
   - Evidence needed: Replicate with state-of-the-art transformer models on same augmented datasets.

2. Can the discriminator detect market regime shifts unsupervised, and how does it compare to traditional methods?
   - Basis: Conclusion proposes using discriminator for regime detection.
   - Why unresolved: Hypothesis untested; discriminator's output on out-of-regime data unknown.
   - Evidence needed: Train GAN on known regime, feed data from various regimes, cluster representations, compare accuracy to HMMs.

3. What is the optimal augmentation ratio for financial time series, and does it vary with dataset size, volatility, or horizon?
   - Basis: Paper uses fixed 1:1 ratio without exploration.
   - Why unresolved: Trade-off between sample scarcity and GAN artifacts unknown; likely context-dependent.
   - Evidence needed: Test ratios (e.g., 0.5x, 1x, 2x, 4x) across datasets, find optimum, correlate with statistics.

4. Does sample-wise MinMax normalization preserve temporal dynamics and cross-sample properties, or distort scale relationships?
   - Basis: Preprocessing uses sample-wise MinMax on first T points.
   - Why unresolved: May erase trends and heteroscedasticity, distorting data manifold.
   - Evidence needed: Compare with global normalization, differencing, etc., on synthetic quality (DTW DeD-iMs) and forecasting MSE.

5. How well does DTW DeD-iMs correlate with human judgment of realism, and can it distinguish specific flaws?
   - Basis: Metric novel, not externally validated.
   - Why unresolved: No benchmark for controlled imperfections; sensitivity to flaw types unclear.
   - Evidence needed: Create synthetic data with controlled flaws (mode collapse, misalignment, drift), get expert ratings, analyze correlation and flaw differentiation.

## Limitations
- Architecture comparison gap: No direct RNN-GAN baseline to empirically validate Transformer superiority.
- Metric validation gap: DTW DeD-iMs lacks external validation for forecasting utility beyond decreasing during training.
- Forecaster obsolescence: Using LSTM may underestimate augmentation benefits; modern Transformers could yield different improvements.

## Confidence
- **Transformer captures long-range dependencies**: Medium (theoretical advantage but limited empirical ablation).
- **Simplified gradient penalty stabilizes training**: Medium (based on established method but not directly compared in results).
- **DTW DeD-iMs ensures temporal fidelity for forecasting**: Low (novel metric with no external validation for downstream task).

## Next Checks
1. Conduct architecture ablation: Train LSTM-based GAN on same datasets; compare forecasting MSE improvement to TTS-GAN.

3. Isolate simplified gradient penalty effect: Run controlled experiment with original WGAN-GP, simplified real-only penalty, and no penalty; track stability metrics and convergence.
