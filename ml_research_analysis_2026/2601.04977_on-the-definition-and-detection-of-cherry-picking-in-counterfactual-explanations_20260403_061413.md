# On the Definition and Detection of Cherry-Picking in Counterfactual Explanations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04977
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04977
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** Cherry-picking in counterfactual explanations is generally undetectable using standard quality metrics, even with full procedural access, due to inherent multiplicity and specification flexibility.

## Executive Summary
The paper formally defines cherry-picking in counterfactual explanations as the selective disclosure of favorable explanations from an admissible set ranked by a utility function. It evaluates detection feasibility under three information-access levels—full procedural, partial procedural, and explanation-only—and finds that standard metrics (proximity, plausibility, sparsity) fail to distinguish cherry-picked from baseline explanations. Empirical analysis on the Adult dataset shows that random seed variation often exceeds the effect of intentional restrictions (e.g., avoiding sensitive features). Under explanation-only access, detection is practically infeasible without restrictive assumptions. The work concludes that post-hoc metric-based detection is insufficient and advocates for ex ante safeguards like standardized specifications and reproducibility.

## Method Summary
The authors formalize cherry-picking relative to an admissible explanation space determined by a generation procedure (model class *F*, algorithm *A*, feasibility constraints) and a utility-based ranking. They analyze detection under three adversary knowledge levels: full procedural (knows *F*, *A*, *u*), partial procedural (knows *F*, *A* only), and explanation-only (only disclosed explanations). Detection methods compare the distribution of standard metrics from disclosed explanations against a baseline distribution derived from the full admissible space. The framework is tested on the Adult dataset using a variational autoencoder-based counterfactual generator, with cherry-picking simulated by restricting the explanation space (e.g., forbidding changes to sensitive attributes) and by varying random seeds.

## Key Results
- With full procedural access, cherry-picked explanations remain statistically indistinguishable from baseline ones on standard metrics due to the multiplicity of valid counterfactuals and flexibility in specification.
- Empirical tests on the Adult dataset show that variation from random seeds often exceeds differences caused by intentional restrictions, making detection unreliable.
- Under explanation-only access, detection is practically infeasible without imposing restrictive assumptions on the model class or explanation method.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's mechanism hinges on the non-identifiability of selection bias from observed metrics alone. Since multiple valid counterfactuals exist for a given instance, a provider can select a subset aligned with any plausible utility function without altering the distribution of standard metrics. The inherent flexibility in specifying feasibility constraints and utility weights creates a large admissible space where cherry-picked samples are drawn from the same distribution as random samples, making detection via aggregate statistics impossible without imposing external constraints. Even when the provider intentionally restricts the space (e.g., avoiding sensitive features), the resulting metric shifts are small and masked by natural variation from stochastic generation procedures.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Counterfactual Explanations:** Feature-based suggestions for how to change an input to obtain a different model prediction. *Why needed:* Provides actionable recourse for affected individuals. *Quick check:* Are proposed changes feasible and actionable for the user in their real-world context?
2. **Admissible Explanation Space:** The set of all valid counterfactuals consistent with the generation procedure (*F*, *A*, constraints). *Why needed:* Defines the pool from which selections are made, framing cherry-picking as a sampling bias. *Quick check:* Does the generation method enumerate or adequately sample from this space?
3. **Utility-Based Ranking:** A function *u* that orders counterfactuals by quality (e.g., proximity, plausibility). *Why needed:* Determines which explanations are "favorable" and thus likely to be cherry-picked. *Quick check:* Is *u* transparent, fixed, and aligned with domain ethics?
4. **Access Levels (Full/Partial/Explanation-Only):** Categories of adversary knowledge about the generation process. *Why needed:* Models realistic auditing scenarios with varying transparency. *Quick check:* What information does a regulator realistically have access to in practice?
5. **Standard Metrics (Proximity, Plausibility, Sparsity):** Quantitative properties used to evaluate counterfactuals. *Why needed:* Basis for detection via distribution comparison; commonly reported in practice. *Quick check:* Do these metrics capture all relevant ethical concerns (e.g., fairness, causality)?
6. **Statistical Indistinguishability:** When the distribution of metrics from cherry-picked explanations overlaps with that of baseline (unrestricted) explanations. *Why needed:* Core reason for detection failure under standard evaluation. *Quick check:* Are hypothesis tests between distributions non-significant even with large sample sizes?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Specification (*F*, *A*, *u*) -> Admissible Space *E* -> Provider Selection (cherry-picked subset) -> Disclosed Explanations -> Auditor Metrics Comparison -> Detection Decision

Critical path: Define cherry-picking formally -> characterize admissible space *E*<sub>*F*,*A*</sub>(*x*) -> simulate selection under utility ranking -> compute metric distributions -> compare under different access levels.

Design tradeoffs: Higher access (full procedural) enables exact replication of baseline but still may not detect cherry-picking due to flexibility in *u*; lower access (explanation-only) relies solely on output statistics, which are confounded by multiplicity and seed variation.

Failure signatures: Overlapping empirical cumulative distribution functions (ECDFs) for metrics between cherry-picked and baseline; small effect sizes (Cohen's *d* < 0.2) even under intentional restriction; high variance across random seeds.

First experiments:
1. Replicate Adult dataset results: Generate counterfactuals with multiple random seeds under baseline and cherry-picked settings (e.g., excluding sensitive attributes), compare metric distributions using Kolmogorov–Smirnov test.
2. Simulate partial procedural access: Auditor knows *F* and *A* but not *u*; attempt to infer plausible *u* from disclosed explanations and assess whether cherry-picking becomes detectable.
3. Test explanation-only detection with synthetic restrictions: Artificially constrain admissible space (e.g., only feature *A* allowed for changes) and measure if metric distributions become statistically distinguishable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can cherry-picking be formally defined when multiple stakeholders (e.g., providers, auditors, regulators) hold conflicting utility functions over counterfactuals?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The definition of cherry-picking in Section 3 relies on a single utility function *u*. The authors note that specification soundness is task-specific and mention fairness as an unaddressed issue, implying that different stakeholders may value different properties (e.g., plausibility vs. fairness).
- Why unresolved: Real-world scenarios involve divergent interests; a provider’s utility may prioritize avoiding sensitive features, while an auditor’s utility may prioritize detecting bias. The current definition assumes a unified *u*, which may not reflect multi-agent settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: A multi-criteria or multi-stakeholder framework that extends Definition 3.1, perhaps using Pareto optimality or a social welfare function. Empirical validation via user studies comparing stakeholder preferences on counterfactual datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are necessary and sufficient criteria for a counterfactual specification (*F*, *A*, *u*) to be considered "sound" such that deviations constitute detectable cherry-picking?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 3 states they give "general considerations for specification soundness" but do not address fairness of the specification. The paper treats specification soundness as domain-dependent and unresolved.
- Why unresolved: Without objective soundness criteria, providers can retrospectively justify any *u* or *A* as reasonable, enabling omittance. The gap allows plausible narratives to mask manipulation.
- What evidence would resolve it: A taxonomy of soundness properties (e.g., completeness, consistency, fairness alignment, invariance to seed choice) derived from domain regulations and expert consensus. Testing these properties on real-world counterfactual pipelines to assess their discriminative power.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Under explanation-only access, what specific, enforceable restrictions on the model class *F* or explanation method *A* make cherry-picking practically detectable using only the reported counterfactuals?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Proposition 4.1 establishes undetectability without restrictions. Section 4.3.2 explores VC dimension bounds as a theoretical side case but notes regulatory frameworks like GDPR do not constrain model complexity.
- Why unresolved: VC dimension is a coarse, often unknown proxy. Practical restrictions must be both technically meaningful (e.g., linearity, limited interactions) and legally enforceable, yet no concrete mapping exists between such restrictions and detection feasibility.
- What evidence would resolve it: Identify families of *F* (e.g., generalized linear models) and *A* (e.g., deterministic optimization) for which the admissible explanation space *E*<sub>*F*,*A*</sub>(*x*) is sufficiently constrained. Empirical evaluation on benchmark datasets with synthetic cherry-picking to measure detection rates under these restrictions.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What governance mechanisms can effectively establish and maintain domain-specific standards for counterfactual specifications to minimize hidden degrees of freedom?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 5 concludes that "domain-level convergence is especially important" and calls for standardisation of specifications, but provides no implementation roadmap.
- Why unresolved: Standardisation requires aligning technical choices (utility functions, feasibility constraints) across stakeholders with conflicting incentives. No existing models address how to achieve and enforce this in practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Case studies of standard-setting in regulated domains (e.g., credit scoring, healthcare) analyzing adoption drivers, compliance mechanisms, and impact on explanation variability. Development of a prototypical standard (e.g., a "counterfactual reporting template") and its pilot testing in an industry consortium.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to a single dataset (Adult) and a small set of standard metrics; generalizability to other domains and evaluation criteria is uncertain.
- Cherry-picking definition tied to a specific generation procedure and utility function; may not capture all forms of selective disclosure in practice.
- No robustness analysis against adversarial metric design, alternative utility functions, or varying degrees of restriction severity.

## Confidence
- **Formal definition and framework:** Medium (admissibility and utility choices may be arbitrary, affecting results)
- **Detection limits across access levels:** Medium (theoretical reasoning plausible but empirical validation is narrow)
- **Indistinguishability on standard metrics:** Medium (small differences observed; statistical significance and effect sizes not rigorously quantified)
- **Infeasibility under explanation-only access:** Low (based on strong theoretical assumptions without empirical testing)
- **Ex ante safeguards recommendation:** Medium (logical but unvalidated in real-world settings)
