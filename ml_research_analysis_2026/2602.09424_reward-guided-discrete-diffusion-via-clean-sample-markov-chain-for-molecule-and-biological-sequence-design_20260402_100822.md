# Reward-Guided Discrete Diffusion via Clean-Sample Markov Chain for Molecule and Biological Sequence Design

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.09424
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09424
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: CSMC achieves higher reward than Best-of-N on multiple tasks; e.g., on ZINC250K ring count: 8.703 ± 2.328 vs 4.184 ± 1.307.

## Executive Summary
Reward-guided generation of molecules and biological sequences is challenging when the reward function is non-smooth, as is typical in scientific domains. Standard approaches that rely on evaluating intermediate (noisy) samples produce noisy gradients or unreliable signals. This paper introduces the Clean-Sample Markov Chain (CSMC) sampler, which constructs a Markov chain over clean (denoised) samples using Metropolis-Hastings. The key innovation is a forward‑backward diffusion proposal: from a current clean sample, noise is added (forward diffusion) and then removed (reverse diffusion) to produce a new clean proposal. This construction makes the transition probability ratio tractable, eliminating the need for reward evaluations on noisy intermediates. Experiments on molecular datasets (QM9, ZINC250K) and a biological sequence dataset (MPRA) show that CSMC consistently outperforms prior methods, including Best-of-N, across a variety of reward functions. A batched variant, CSMC-B, further improves wall‑clock efficiency.

## Method Summary
CSMC operates on discrete data using any pre‑trained discrete diffusion model. Given a current clean sample \(x\), the proposal mechanism performs \(\tau\) steps of forward diffusion to reach a noisy state \(y\), then \(\tau\) steps of reverse diffusion from \(y\) to obtain a proposed clean sample \(x'\). The transition probability \(q(x'|x)\) is intractable, but the ratio \(q(x|x')/q(x'|x)\) becomes tractable due to the symmetric forward‑backward construction: the probability of going from \(x\) to \(x'\) via \(y\) mirrors that of the reverse path. The Metropolis‑Hastings acceptance probability is \(\alpha = \min\left(1, \frac{r(x')}{r(x)} \cdot \frac{q(x|x')}{q(x'|x)}\right)\), where \(r\) is the (unnormalized) reward. Since the ratio simplifies, the acceptance depends only on the reward ratio, avoiding evaluation on noisy samples. The method optionally uses multiple MCMC steps and a batched implementation (CSMC-B) to maximize GPU utilization.

## Key Results
- On ZINC250K ring count, CSMC achieves \(8.703 \pm 2.328\), substantially higher than Best‑of‑N's \(4.184 \pm 1.307\).
- Under matched wall‑clock time, CSMC‑B outperforms baselines on challenging rewards (ring count, HepG2 activity) and remains competitive on easier ones.
- Consistent improvements are observed across QM9 (property targets), ZINC250K (ring count, molecular weight), and MPRA (biological sequence activity).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The forward‑backward diffusion proposal creates a symmetric transition kernel: the process of forward‑then‑reverse from \(x\) to \(x'\) mirrors the reverse path, yielding a tractable ratio \(q(x|x')/q(x'|x)\). This allows Metropolis‑Hastings to accept or reject based solely on the reward ratio between clean samples, bypassing noisy intermediate states. By confining the Markov chain to clean samples, the method directly optimizes the target reward while preserving the correct stationary distribution. The MCMC framework naturally balances exploration (via diffusion steps) and exploitation (local moves), enabling effective climbing of the reward landscape even for non‑smooth objectives.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Current clean sample \(x\) → Forward diffusion (\(\tau\) steps) → Noisy sample \(y\) → Reverse diffusion (\(\tau\) steps) → Proposed clean sample \(x'\).
- **Critical path**: The core loop: for each MCMC iteration, generate proposal via forward‑backward diffusion, compute acceptance ratio (reward ratio × transition ratio), accept/reject, and update current sample.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Diffusion steps per proposal (\(\tau\)): Larger \(\tau\) yields more distant proposals (better exploration) but increases compute and may lower acceptance.
  - Number of MCMC steps (\(K\)): More steps can improve final reward but with diminishing returns and higher runtime.
  - Batch size (CSMC‑B): Parallel chains improve GPU utilization and wall‑clock efficiency but may require careful handling of proposal dependencies.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Acceptance rate below ~5% suggests proposals are too extreme; decrease \(\tau\) or adjust diffusion schedule.
  - High variance in final rewards across random seeds indicates poor mixing or unstable reward landscape.
  - No improvement over simple baselines (e.g., Best‑of‑N) under fixed compute may imply the proposal mechanism is unsuitable for that reward/dataset.
- **First experiments**:
  1. Implement CSMC on a toy dataset (e.g., binary strings) with a simple reward (e.g., number of 1s) to verify acceptance behavior and correctness.
  2. Sweep \(\tau\) on ZINC250K ring count to observe effects on reward, acceptance rate, and runtime.
  3. Compare CSMC‑B against Best‑of‑N and standard CSMC under a fixed time budget (e.g., 1 hour) on QM9 for a regression reward.

## Limitations
- High variance in results (e.g., ring count standard deviation 2.33) indicates instability, especially on challenging rewards.
- Evaluation limited to three datasets (QM9, ZINC250K, MPRA) restricts generalizability to other domains such as proteins or larger molecules.
- Computational cost and scaling behavior of the MCMC procedure are not thoroughly analyzed; baseline comparison may miss recent competitors.
