# Geometric separation and constructive universal approximation with two hidden layers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12482  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12482  
- **Reference count:** 15  
- **Primary result:** Networks with two hidden layers (depth 3) constructively approximate any continuous function on any compact subset of ℝⁿ for sigmoidal and ReLU activations; for finite sets, depth‑2 suffices.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a constructive solution to the universal approximation problem for neural networks with two hidden layers (depth three). Unlike classical theorems that assert existence without explicit construction, the authors develop an explicit geometric method that works for any continuous function on a compact set in ℝⁿ, using either sigmoid or ReLU activations. The core technique combines Urysohn‑style separation lemmas via “hole‑like” functions with an averaging process over the rotation group SO(n) to achieve radial symmetry. For general compact sets, an iterative oscillation‑reduction process (inspired by the Tietze extension theorem) aggregates these separators in a second hidden layer to achieve uniform approximation. The result demonstrates that depth three is sufficient for constructive universal approximation, while for finite input sets depth two suffices, revealing a trade‑off between minimal depth and constructivity.

## Method Summary
The construction begins with a one‑dimensional “hole” function (ψ for sigmoids, φ for ReLU) that is near zero on a small interval and near one outside a larger interval. Summing over coordinates yields a function Ψ(x) = Σ ψ(xᵢ) (or Σ φ(xᵢ)) that is small near the origin but large along axes. To obtain radial symmetry, Ψ is averaged over SO(n) using a Riemann sum; the average ¯Ψ is strictly increasing in radius and remains high outside a thin set (a union of slabs). This averaging is approximated by a finite sum to yield a function implementable in one hidden layer that separates a point from a disjoint closed set. For a continuous f on a compact K, the resulting separator is used to construct a function H in two hidden layers that takes high values on an upper level set and low values on a lower level set. Adding a scaled constant and H reduces the oscillation of the remainder by a factor c < 1. Iterating this step produces a series of two‑hidden‑layer functions whose sum converges uniformly to f. For finite K, the iteration is avoided and a single hidden layer suffices.

## Key Results
- Constructive depth‑3 universal approximation holds for all continuous functions on arbitrary compact subsets of ℝⁿ, for strictly monotone bounded sigmoids and for ReLU.
- For finite input sets, a depth‑2 (one hidden layer) construction achieves exact interpolation.
- The method is explicit but may lead to networks with very large width, highlighting a trade‑off between depth and constructivity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Hole‑like point‑separation.**  
A one‑dimensional function ψ (sigmoid) or φ (ReLU) is built to be low near 0 and high away from 0. By summing over coordinates, Ψ(x)=Σ ψ(xᵢ) (or Φ(x)=Σ φ(xᵢ)) is small in a neighbourhood of the origin but large on the coordinate axes. Scaling and translating yields a function h ∈ N₂ (one hidden layer) that is arbitrarily close to 0 near a given point p and close to 1 on a disjoint compact set A.

**Mechanism 2: SO(n) averaging for radial symmetry.**  
The function Ψ is averaged over the rotation group SO(n) with respect to Haar measure: ¯Ψ(x)=∫_{SO(n)} Ψ(Rx) dµ(R). Because SO(n) acts transitively on spheres and the set where Ψ is low has small volume, ¯Ψ becomes strictly increasing in radial distance and stays above a threshold outside a thin slab around the origin. A Riemann sum over a triangulation of SO(n) uniformly approximates ¯Ψ, yielding a concrete implementable function ˜Ψ ∈ N₂ with the same separation property.

**Mechanism 3: Oscillation reduction and iteration.**  
For non‑constant f ∈ C(K), the separator from Mechanism 2 is used in Lemma 3.2 (sigmoid) or Lemma 3.4 (ReLU) to build H ∈ N₃ that is >a on an upper level set E⁺ and <b on a lower level set E⁻, with a > b. Setting g₀ = (m_f + w_f) + w_f H reduces the oscillation of f – g₀ by a factor c = (3–a+b)/3 < 1. Repeating this construction on the remainder yields a series Σ g_k in N₃ that converges uniformly to f. For finite K, the iteration is unnecessary and a depth‑2 network directly interpolates the function values.

## Foundational Learning
- **Urysohn Lemma & Tietze Extension Theorem**  
  Why needed: Provide the topological foundation for separating closed sets by continuous functions and for extending partial functions; the iterative oscillation reduction mirrors Tietze’s proof.  
  Quick check: How does the construction of H in Lemma 3.2/3.4 mirror Urysohn’s lemma? *Answer: It aggregates point‑separators to separate two closed sets.*

- **Uniform convergence in C(K)**  
  Why needed: Approximation is in the supremum norm; uniform convergence guarantees that the limit of the constructed series is continuous and the error is uniformly bounded.  
  Quick check: Why is uniform convergence (vs. pointwise) necessary for the final approximation claim? *Answer: Pointwise convergence does not preserve continuity and may not control the maximum deviation over K.*

- **Haar measure on compact Lie groups**  
  Why needed: Invariant averaging over SO(n) creates radial symmetry; transitivity on spheres yields volume estimates that control the lower bound of the averaged function.  
  Quick check: Why does left‑invariance of µ imply ¯Ψ is radially symmetric? *Answer: Because for any rotation R, (R'·)· leaves the integral unchanged, so ¯Ψ(Rx)=¯Ψ(x).*

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input → Layer1: affine transform x ↦ w·x + b followed by activation (σ or τ) → Layer2: sum of N activated affine units followed by activation (σ or τ) → Output: linear combination.

**Critical path:**  
1. Implement the 1‑D hole‑like function (ψ for sigmoid, φ for ReLU).  
2. Extend to n‑D by summation over coordinates.  
3. Approximate the SO(n) average by a Riemann sum (triangulation) to obtain near‑radial symmetry.  
4. Use the separator to construct H ∈ N₃ that separates level sets of f.  
5. Apply iterative oscillation reduction to sum N₃ functions.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Depth vs. constructivity: Depth‑3 yields an explicit construction; non‑constructive classical results allow depth‑2.  
- Width: Potentially very large due to covering number N, triangulation of SO(n), and averaging.  
- Activation flexibility: Works for strictly increasing bounded sigmoids and ReLU; other activations may need modification.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Separation fails if d(p,A)=0 or δ is too small relative to d(p,A) and N.  
- Averaging fails if triangulation mesh size is too coarse, leading to violation of radial lower bound; check uniform error on balls.  
- Oscillation factor c ≥ 1 if separation parameters (e.g., ε in Lemma 3.2) are not sufficiently small; iteration does not converge.  
- For non‑compact K, the finite subcover step breaks down.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Approximate f(x)=‖x‖² on the unit ball in ℝ² with σ=sigmoid; compute N, δ, triangulation from proof inequalities and verify ‖f−ĥ‖∞<ε.  
2. Implement depth‑2 construction (Theorem 5.1) for K={(-1,0),(1,0),(0,1)} with arbitrary targets; compare required width with minimal interpolation width.  
3. Compare sigmoid vs. ReLU constructions on the same compact set; verify that both yield networks in N₃ satisfying the approximation guarantee.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Depth‑2 constructive approximation?** Is a depth‑2 constructive universal approximation theorem possible for continuous functions on arbitrary compact subsets of ℝⁿ? The paper shows depth‑3 constructive and depth‑2 for finite sets, but leaves open whether depth‑2 can be constructive for infinite compacts.
- **Quantitative width bounds:** What are the precise dependencies of the required network width on the dimension n, the geometry of K, and the tolerance ε? The current construction is explicit but does not provide explicit bounds; width may explode.
- **Beyond sigmoid and ReLU:** For which other activation functions does this geometric construction yield constructive universal approximation? The framework may extend after modifications, but no general characterization is given.

## Limitations
- No closed‑form parameter selection: Explicit formulas for choosing N, c, δ, and triangulation mesh size in terms of ε, n, and separation distance are not provided; only existence arguments are given.
- Unquantified geometric constant: The volume estimate for the thin set involves an unspecified constant C'(n, d(p,A)), making it difficult to determine how small δ must be relative to d(p,A) and ε/N.
- Potential width explosion: The construction may require extremely wide networks due to the Riemann sum over SO(n) and the covering of K; no polynomial bounds on width in terms of n and ε are established.
