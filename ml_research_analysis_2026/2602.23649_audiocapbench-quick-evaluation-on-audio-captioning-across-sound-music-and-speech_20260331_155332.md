# AudioCapBench: Quick Evaluation on Audio Captioning across Sound, Music, and Speech

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23649
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23649
- **Reference Count:** 29
- **Primary Result:** Gemini 3 Pro achieves the highest overall audio captioning score of 6.00/10, while OpenAI models hallucinate less; all models struggle most with music.

## Executive Summary
AudioCapBench establishes a benchmark for evaluating large multimodal models on audio captioning across three distinct domains: environmental sound, music, and speech. It uses a curated 1,000-sample test set and an LLM-as-Judge framework (GPT-4.1) to score predictions on accuracy, completeness, and hallucination (0-10), alongside traditional reference-based metrics. Evaluation of 13 models from OpenAI and Google Gemini reveals that Gemini models lead in overall quality, with a notable accuracy–hallucination trade-off: models producing more accurate and complete captions tend to hallucinate more. All models perform best on speech captioning and worst on music captioning. The weak correlation between lexical overlap metrics (e.g., METEOR) and LLM judgments underscores their inadequacy for semantically evaluating audio captions.

## Method Summary
The benchmark constructs a 1,000-sample test set by curating fixed splits from established datasets: 400 sound samples (Clotho v2, AudioCaps), 300 music samples (MusicCaps), and 300 speech samples (Emotional Speech Caption), ensuring balanced caption lengths. Thirteen multimodal models (OpenAI Chat/Realtime APIs, Google Gemini) generate captions using category-specific instruction prompts and fixed inference parameters (e.g., temperature 0.0 for most, 0.6 for Realtime). Each prediction is evaluated by GPT-4.1, which outputs integer scores (0-10) for accuracy, completeness, and hallucination based on reference captions; for speech, a transcript is also provided. Reference-based metrics (METEOR, BLEU-4, ROUGE-L) are computed offline. Scores are aggregated per model and category to produce leaderboards and analyze trends like the accuracy–hallucination trade-off.

## Key Results
- Gemini 3 Pro achieves the highest overall score (6.00/10), leading in accuracy and completeness; OpenAI models exhibit the lowest hallucination rates but lower overall quality.
- All models perform best on speech captioning and worst on music captioning, indicating domain-specific difficulty variations.
- A clear accuracy–hallucination trade-off exists: models with higher accuracy/completeness scores (e.g., Gemini) have higher hallucination, while conservative models (OpenAI mini) hallucinate less but are less informative.
- Reference-based metrics like METEOR show weak correlation with LLM judgments, confirming that lexical overlap fails to capture semantic correctness in audio captions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Domain Diversity for Holistic Assessment
**Claim:** Evaluating across environmental sound, music, and speech provides a discriminative assessment by isolating distinct audio understanding challenges.
**Mechanism:** The 1,000-sample dataset is curated from established domain-specific sources with balanced caption lengths. Each domain requires different expertise: sound focuses on event/environment identification; music requires describing genre, instrumentation, mood; speech combines transcription with paralinguistic analysis (e.g., emotion).
**Core assumption:** Challenges across these domains are meaningfully different, and strong performance in one does not guarantee competence in others.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "covers three distinct audio domains... with 1,000 curated evaluation samples"
- [section 2.1] Details dataset sources and curation criteria per category.
- [section 3.2] Per-category scores show music consistently hardest and speech easiest, confirming differential difficulty.

### Mechanism 2: LLM-as-Judge for Semantic Orthogonality
**Claim:** GPT-4.1 scoring accuracy, completeness, and hallucination provides a principled, semantically aligned assessment superior to lexical metrics.
**Mechanism:** For each prediction, GPT-4.1 receives a prompt with category-specific guidelines, all reference captions, and the prediction. It outputs three integer scores (0-10) mapped to information retrieval concepts: accuracy (precision), completeness (recall), hallucination (false positive rate). The overall score is their average. Dimensions like fluency were rejected for poor discrimination.
**Core assumption:** A powerful LLM with clear, domain-tailored instructions can reliably proxy human judgment on nuanced audio caption quality.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [section 2.2] Defines the three dimensions and their mapping to a confusion matrix; explains rejection of other dimensions.
- [section A.1 & A.2] Provides the exact judge prompt template, showing category-specific guidance injection.
- [section 3.1] Leaderboard shows models differentiating on these axes (e.g., Gemini leads in completeness/accuracy, OpenAI mini leads in hallucination).

### Mechanism 3: Observed Accuracy–Hallucination Trade-off
**Claim:** Current large multimodal models face a trade-off where generating more accurate and complete captions increases hallucination risk.
**Mechanism:** This is an empirical correlation from the leaderboard, not a designed component. Models with higher accuracy (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro: 6.18) have lower hallucination scores (5.42). Conversely, models with the highest hallucination scores (least hallucination), like OpenAI mini models (7.90), have the lowest accuracy and completeness.
**Core assumption:** The trade-off reflects a fundamental tension in generation strategy: conservative models avoid errors by producing generic, minimal captions; models aiming for high completeness must take semantic risks, sometimes inventing unsupported details.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [section 3.3] Directly describes and analyzes the pattern: "OpenAI mini models exhibit a striking pattern... these models minimize false positives by producing brief, generic descriptions."
- [Figure 4] Scatter plot visualizes the trade-off, showing clusters for Gemini, OpenAI mini, and Realtime models.
- [corpus] Neighbor work like "The MUSE Benchmark" probes similar reliability issues, suggesting a broader challenge.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Audio Captioning vs. ASR**
- **Why needed here:** The benchmark evaluates semantic description of audio (events, music attributes, speaker emotion), not mere transcription. Success requires multimodal understanding beyond speech-to-text.
- **Quick check:** What are two types of information a caption for a music clip must include that an ASR system would not produce?

**Concept: LLM-as-Judge Evaluation Paradigm**
- **Why needed here:** The core evaluation relies on this scalable but potentially biased method. Engineers must understand its strengths (semantic evaluation, flexibility) and weaknesses (cost, judge bias, prompt sensitivity).
- **Quick check:** Why might a reference-based metric like BLEU give a high score to a caption that is actually factually wrong about the audio?

**Concept: Information Retrieval Metrics (Precision, Recall, False Positive Rate)**
- **Why needed here:** The judge dimensions (accuracy, completeness, hallucination) are explicitly grounded in these concepts. Understanding this mapping is key to interpreting scores and the reported trade-off.
- **Quick check:** If a caption mentions 4 correct events from the reference and invents 1 incorrect event, how would its accuracy and hallucination scores likely compare to a caption that mentions only 2 correct events and invents none?

## Architecture Onboarding

Dataset -> Inference -> Evaluation (LLM Judge, Reference Metrics) -> Aggregation

**Critical Path:**
1. Prepare dataset: audio files, reference captions, transcripts (for speech).
2. For each model and sample, generate prediction using correct category-specific instruction prompt.
3. For each prediction, call GPT-4.1 judge API and parse JSON scores (accuracy, completeness, hallucination).
4. For each prediction, compute reference metrics (METEOR, BLEU-4, ROUGE-L).
5. Aggregate all scores by model and category; compute averages.
6. Generate leaderboards and trade-off visualizations.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- **LLM Judge vs. Human Evaluation:** LLM judge offers scalability and reproducibility but risks bias; human eval is gold-standard but expensive and slow.
- **Single Judge vs. Multi-Judge:** One judge (GPT-4.1) reduces cost and complexity but may introduce systemic bias; multi-judge ensembles improve reliability at higher cost.
- **Category-Specific Prompts:** Necessary for valid cross-domain evaluation but complicates the inference pipeline and requires maintaining three prompt variants.

**Failure Signatures:**
- **Judge API failures:** Non-JSON responses, scores outside 0-10, or consistently extreme scores (all 0 or 10) indicate prompt or API issues.
- **Model-specific failures:** Realtime models' higher temperature (0.6) may cause high prediction variance; mini models often produce empty/generic captions, yielding zero scores.
- **Metric mismatch:** A model ranking high on METEOR/BLEU but low on LLM judge indicates over-reliance on lexical overlap, an expected signature of reference metric inadequacy.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. **Judge Consistency Check:** Run GPT-4.1 judge on a 100-sample subset with 5 independent calls per prediction. Measure score standard deviation; >2 points indicates prompt instability.
2. **Single-Domain Sanity Check:** Evaluate all models on only the "Sound" category first. This isolates whether observed patterns (e.g., music hardest) are global or domain-specific.
3. **Human Correlation Pilot:** Manually evaluate 100 balanced samples using the same rubric. Compute Pearson correlation between human and GPT-4.1 scores; <0.5 would invalidate the primary evaluation method.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How does using multiple LLM judges or combining LLM with human judges affect reliability and bias compared to a single GPT-4.1 judge?
  - **Basis:** Limitations: "We use a single LLM judge (GPT-4.1), which may introduce systematic bias." Future Work: "Multi‑judge evaluation."
  - **Why unresolved:** Current results may be skewed by GPT-4.1's specific biases; without comparison, model rankings may not be robust.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Systematically evaluate predictions with multiple LLM judges (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini) and/or humans; measure inter‑judge agreement and ranking correlation.

- **Question 2:** Can reference‑free evaluation metrics for audio captioning achieve high correlation with human‑like quality judgments?
  - **Basis:** Future Work mentions "reference‑free metrics." The paper notes weak correlation of lexical metrics with LLM judgments.
  - **Why unresolved:** Current metrics rely on lexical overlap, failing to capture semantic correctness; reference‑free metrics would reduce cost and bias.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Propose new metrics (e.g., audio‑text embedding similarity, dedicated LLM scorer) and validate against LLM‑as‑judge or human evaluations across diverse models/domains.

- **Question 3:** How do open‑weight audio‑language models perform on AudioCapBench relative to proprietary OpenAI and Gemini models?
  - **Basis:** Limitations: "The benchmark currently only evaluated on API‑based models and does not include open‑weight audio models." Future Work: "inclusion of open‑weight models."
  - **Why unresolved:** The leaderboard reflects only closed API models; open‑weight models may have different strengths/weaknesses and could potentially surpass them.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Evaluate representative open‑weight models (e.g., SALMONN, Qwen‑Audio, Pengi) under the same protocol; compare scores and accuracy–hallucination trade‑offs.

- **Question 4:** How well do benchmark findings generalize to audio domains beyond environmental sound, music, and speech?
  - **Basis:** Future Work: "expansion to additional audio domains."
  - **Why unresolved:** Models' performance patterns may differ in specialized domains (e.g., biomedical sounds, animal vocalizations) not represented.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Curate evaluation sets from new domains with reference captions; measure model performance and compare with existing domain results to identify domain‑specific gaps or consistent trends.

## Limitations
- **LLM judge reliability:** Potential for positional, domain, or prompt bias not fully mitigated; unknown correlation with robust human judgments on this benchmark.
- **Dataset curation artifacts:** Possible leakage of domain-identifying features in the 1,000-sample set that could artificially amplify performance gaps between domains.
- **Model version drift:** Preview models (Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-4o Realtime) may have changed since evaluation, affecting reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **Holistic domain assessment (domain diversity as discriminative):** High — Balanced curation and consistent per-category score patterns provide strong evidence.
- **LLM-as-Judge orthogonality (semantic evaluation superiority):** Medium — Framework is well-specified and shows metric divergence, but lacks validated human correlation.
- **Accuracy–Hallucination trade-off (inherent tension):** Medium — Observed correlation is clear, but causal mechanism is speculative; future models may decouple these axes.
