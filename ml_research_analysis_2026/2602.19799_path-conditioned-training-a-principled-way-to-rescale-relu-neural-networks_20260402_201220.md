# Path-conditioned training: a principled way to rescale ReLU neural networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19799
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19799
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** One‑time rescaling of ReLU network parameters at initialization (PathCond) accelerates training, yielding up to 1.5× speedup on CIFAR‑10 MLPs and higher test accuracy on fully convolutional CIFAR‑NV.

## Executive Summary
PathCond is a novel initialization technique for feedforward ReLU neural networks. It exploits inherent neuron‑wise rescaling symmetries to improve the conditioning of the training geometry. Using the path‑lifting framework, the method defines a path kernel matrix whose spectrum governs gradient dynamics in a lifted parameter space. PathCond selects a rescaling that minimizes a log‑determinant divergence between a suitably scaled version of this kernel and the identity matrix, effectively aligning them. This convex optimization over scaling factors can be solved efficiently in linear time without explicitly constructing large matrices. Empirical results on CIFAR‑10, CIFAR‑NV and MNIST autoencoders demonstrate faster convergence and better generalization, especially for architectures with pronounced width variations. The analysis further identifies favorable regimes: architectural width imbalance and small initialization variance induce a spread in the diagonal of the path‑magnitude matrix, making conditioning by PathCond beneficial.

## Method Summary
PathCond applies a single rescaling to the network parameters immediately after initialization. The procedure consists of three steps:  
1. Compute the diagonal vector \(g = \text{diag}(G)\), where \(G = \partial\Phi(\theta)^\top\partial\Phi(\theta)\) and \(\Phi\) is the path‑lifting map. This is done via two linear‑time passes (forward and backward) on the computational graph using squared weights, avoiding any construction of the full \(G\) matrix.  
2. Solve a convex optimization problem over neuron‑wise scaling factors \(u\). The objective, derived from a log‑determinant divergence, can be minimized by alternating closed‑form updates (Algorithm 2) that touch only edges incident to the neuron being updated. The runtime is \(O(n p)\) where \(n\) is the number of alternating iterations and \(p\) the number of parameters.  
3. Rescale all parameters by \(w_i = \exp((B u)_i)\), where \(B\) encodes the neuron‑edge incidence relation, i.e. \(\theta' = w \odot \theta\).  

After this teleportation, the network is trained with standard stochastic gradient descent. The method is parameter‑free, incurs negligible overhead, and preserves the exact function class (because the rescaling lies in the invariance group).

## Key Results
- On a 3‑hidden‑layer MLP for CIFAR‑10, PathCond reaches 99% training accuracy up to **1.5× faster** than standard SGD.  
- On the fully convolutional CIFAR‑NV architecture, PathCond achieves **80% test accuracy**, while baseline and Equinormalization stall at 77%.  
- For MNIST autoencoders with high compression (larger width‑imbalance factor), PathCond yields **significantly lower final training loss**, confirming its benefit for architectures with unbalanced widths.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

PathCond’s effectiveness stems from three interconnected mechanisms:

1. **Path‑lifting invariance.** Any feedforward ReLU network admits a representation \(\Phi(\theta)\) that collects the products of weights (including biases) along all paths from inputs to outputs. This “path‑lifting” eliminates the inherent neuron‑wise rescaling redundancy: scaling all incoming weights and bias of a neuron by \(\lambda>0\) and all its outgoing weights by \(1/\lambda\) leaves \(\Phi(\theta)\) – and thus the implemented function – unchanged (Lemma A.1, Theorem D.7). This invariance defines a discrete group \(\mathcal{D}\) of admissible rescaling transformations.

2. **Preconditioning via the path kernel.** Let \(z = \Phi(\theta)\). Under a smooth loss \(L(\theta) = \ell(z)\), gradient flow in parameter space, \(\dot\theta = -\nabla L(\theta)\), induces a lifted dynamics \(\dot z = -P_\theta \nabla\ell(z)\), where \(P_\theta = \partial\Phi(\theta)\,\partial\Phi(\theta)^\top\) is the path kernel. Different choices of rescaling \(\theta \to D\theta\) leave \(z\) fixed but alter \(P_\theta\) (since \(\partial\Phi(D\theta) = \partial\Phi(\theta)D^{-1}\)). Hence, by selecting an appropriate \(D\in\mathcal{D}\), one can shape the effective pre‑conditioner of the lifted gradient flow, i.e., the geometry of optimization in the lifted space.

3. **Log‑determinant alignment.** PathCond chooses \(D\) to minimize the Bregman log‑determinant divergence \(d_{-\log\det}(\alpha P_{D\theta} \,\Vert\, I)\) over \(\alpha>0\) and \(D\in\mathcal{D}\). By spectral equivalence, this is equivalent to minimizing \(d(\alpha G_{D\theta} \,\Vert\, I)\) with \(G = \partial\Phi^\top\partial\Phi\). Because \(\mathcal{D}\) acts by per‑neuron scalings, the problem reduces to a convex objective (9) in the log‑scaling vector \(u\in\mathbb{R}^H\) (one entry per internal neuron). The objective takes the form \(F(u) = p\log(\sum_i e^{(Bu)_i} G_{ii}) - \sum_i (Bu)_i\), where \(B\) encodes the neuron‑edge incidence. Its strict convexity allows a closed‑form coordinate update (Lemma 4.1): for each neuron, solve a quadratic to obtain the optimal scaling factor. The alternating scheme converges in few iterations and runs in \(O(p)\) per sweep. The resulting rescaling aligns the path kernel with the identity, promoting well‑conditioned gradient flow in lifted space. Crucially, the entire procedure never constructs \(P_\theta\) or \(G\) explicitly; only the diagonal of \(G\) is needed, which is computable in linear time via the two‑pass algorithm described in Appendix G.

The method requires that the diagonal entries \(G_{ii}\) be positive (true for any connected ReLU DAG with at least one path per weight) and that the number of paths \(q\) be at least the number of parameters \(p\) (a generic condition). It also assumes that the loss factors through \(\Phi\) locally and that gradient flow is a good approximation of discrete SGD.

## Foundational Learning
- **Rescaling symmetries in ReLU networks**  
  *Why needed:* The entire PathCond method relies on the existence of per‑neuron scaling invariances; understanding them is essential to define the group \(\mathcal{D}\) and to guarantee that the rescaling does not change the network function.  
  *Quick check:* For a ReLU neuron with incoming weight vector \(w_{\text{in}}\), bias \(b\), and outgoing weights \(w_{\text{out}}\), if we multiply all \(w_{\text{in}}\) and \(b\) by \(\lambda>0\) and all \(w_{\text{out}}\) by \(1/\lambda\), does the neuron’s output remain unchanged for any input?

- **Path‑lifting framework**  
  *Why needed:* PathCond operates not in the original parameter space but in the lifted space defined by \(\Phi(\theta)\). This representation removes redundancy and allows the definition of the path kernel.  
  *Quick check:* How is \(\Phi(\theta)\) constructed from a directed acyclic graph of ReLU neurons? What does each entry of \(\Phi(\theta)\) represent?

- **Bregman matrix divergences**  
  *Why needed:* The rescaling criterion selects \(D\) by minimizing a matrix divergence (specifically the log‑determinant divergence) between \(P_{D\theta}\) and the identity. This choice directly promotes a low condition number of the kernel.  
  *Quick check:* Why use a matrix divergence instead of, say, the Frobenius norm? What advantage does the log‑determinant divergence offer for conditioning?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Compute g (forward/backward passes) -> Solve convex problem (Algorithm 2, alternating updates) -> Rescale parameters (θ ← diag(e^v) θ)

**Critical path:**  
Runtime dominated by \(O(p)\) computation of diag(\(G\)) plus \(O(n_{\text{iter}}\cdot p)\) for the alternating minimization. The algorithm reuses sparsity in the incidence matrix \(B\): each neuron update touches only its incident edges. Memory usage is \(O(p+H)\), where \(H\) is the number of hidden neurons.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
The log‑det divergence is parameter‑free and directly targets condition number, but it assumes that aligning \(P_\theta\) with the identity improves lifted gradient descent. Alternative divergences (e.g., Frobenius) are possible but may not condition as effectively. PathCond performs an “initialization‑only” teleportation rather than a continuous preconditioner during training, which reduces overhead but might miss later‑stage adaptation opportunities.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Numerical overflow/underflow in scaling factors \(w_i\): check \(\|\log w_i\|_\infty\); values > 50 indicate instability.  
- Rescaled network output differs from original: likely an incorrect assignment of edges to neuron “in/out” sets (e.g., missing bias edges).  
- Some \(g_i\) become zero: occurs only if a parameter does not lie on any input‑to‑output path; ensure the graph is fully connected.  
- Quadratic solve yields non‑real root: usually due to tiny negative discriminant from rounding; clamp to zero.  
- Alternating minimization fails to decrease objective: verify sign handling in coefficients and the special case \(A=0\).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **CIFAR‑10 fully connected MLP:** 3 hidden layers (500 units each) vs. baseline and Equinormalization; metric = epochs to 99% training accuracy. Expected ~1.5× speedup.  
2. **CIFAR‑NV fully convolutional:** Train CIFAR‑NV architecture; compare training/test accuracy curves; expect PathCond to reach ~80% test accuracy while others stall at 77%.  
3. **MNIST autoencoder:** Architectures \([784, 784/c, 784/c^2, 784/c, 784]\) with \(c\in\{1,2,4,7\}\); train 500 epochs; metric = final training loss; improvement should increase with compression (larger \(c\)).

## Limitations
- The method is validated only on simple MLPs and one fully convolutional network; architectures with skip connections or non‑ReLU activations may violate the core invariance assumption.  
- Integration with BatchNorm is not addressed; handling affine parameters while preserving the path‑kernel guarantee requires additional care.  
- The analysis assumes the number of paths \(q \ge p\) and that all diagonal entries of \(G\) are positive; degenerate DAGs or extreme compression could cause numerical issues.

## Confidence
- **Path‑lifting invariance:** High (formal theorem, Lemma A.1, Theorem D.7)  
- **Preconditioning via path kernel:** Medium (derived under smoothness; not directly measured empirically)  
- **Efficiency of alternating minimization:** High (convex solution, linear complexity per sweep)  
- **Empirical training acceleration:** Medium (consistent on small‑scale datasets, limited architectures)  
- **Applicability to CNNs/BatchNorm:** Low (no evidence; likely needs non‑trivial extension)
