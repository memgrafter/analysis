# A Hierarchical End-of-Turn Model with Primary Speaker Segmentation for Real-Time Conversational AI
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13379
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13379
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** A real-time voice AI front-end achieves 87.7% recall with 36 ms median latency, significantly outperforming Smart Turn v3 (58.9% recall, 900 ms latency) using only 1.14M parameters.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a real-time, edge-deployable system for managing turn-taking in two-speaker conversations. It addresses the challenge of correctly identifying when a speaker's turn ends amidst background speech and potential overlaps. The solution combines a streaming primary speaker segmentation module that isolates the engaged user's speech from background via online clustering of disentangled embeddings, with a hierarchical end-of-turn detector. The detector makes multi-horizon predictions using per-speaker features and incorporates the partner's near-future speech context while enforcing strict causality for immediate decisions. Task-specific knowledge distillation compresses powerful wav2vec 2.0 representations into a compact 32D MFCC-based feature stream, enabling efficiency.

## Method Summary
The system has two coupled neural modules. First, a primary speaker segmentation unit processes streaming audio to extract a sequence of embeddings for the active user. It uses a multi-scale causal encoder and a "peeling" mechanism to disentangle speaker embeddings from the mixture, then applies online centroid clustering to track the engaged speaker's embedding while filtering out others. Second, a hierarchical end-of-turn (EOT) detector takes per-speaker features (base MFCCs, pitch, VAD, plus a distilled 32D representation) for both the user and the bot. For each frame, it predicts 5 turn-state classes (Initial, Speech, Interim, Final, Backchannel) at four horizons (t+0, +10, +20, +30 ms). Horizon-0 uses only the speaker's own features; later horizons incorporate an adapted version of the partner's features and the speaker's own horizon-0 logits to anticipate near-future turns. The model is trained with multi-horizon cross-entropy, distillation losses between teacher (compressed wav2vec 2.0) and student (MFCC-based) features, and augmentations like label jitter and channel dropout to enforce causality and robustness.

## Key Results
- On a 350-hour dataset, the system achieves 82% multi-class frame-level F1, 70.6% backchannel F1, and 69.3% binary final-turn F1.
- In end-to-end evaluation, it reaches 87.7% turn-end recall with a median detection latency of 36 ms.
- It outperforms the Smart Turn v3 baseline (58.9% recall, 900 ms latency) while using only ~1.14M parameters and ≈111 MMAC/s, suitable for edge deployment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Streaming Primary Speaker Segmentation via Online Clustering
- **Claim:** The system robustly tracks the intended user in multi-speaker environments by online clustering of disentangled speaker embeddings.
- **Mechanism:** A streaming encoder produces frame-level features. A "peeling" mechanism recursively extracts up to K disentangled speaker embedding sequences from a residual signal. An online moving-average centroid (initialized from the first active frames) is updated via cosine similarity. Only frames whose embedding is closest to this centroid (and pass VAD) are forwarded as the primary user's speech.
- **Core assumption:** The engaged user's embedding will be sufficiently stable and distinct from background speakers to maintain a coherent centroid over short time scales.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "streaming primary speaker segmentation... via online clustering of disentangled embeddings"; [section III.C] "iteratively extract K disentangled embedding sequences... centroid... updated online"; [section III.F] average EER ≈10%, improving to <8% for SIR>10dB.
- **Break condition:** Performance degrades gracefully at low SIRs (Fig. 2), indicating brittleness when the primary speaker's signal is heavily interfered with or embeddings are not well separated.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical, Multi-Horizon EOT Detection
- **Claim:** A hierarchical, multi-horizon EOT model enables low-latency, accurate turn-end detection by enforcing strict causality at horizon 0 and incorporating partner-speech awareness only for near-future prediction.
- **Mechanism:** For each frame, the model predicts 5 classes for horizons t+0, +10, +20, +30 ms. Horizon-0 heads use *only* self-speaker features. Horizon-1 to 3 heads concatenate self features with an adapted version of the *other* speaker's features and the self horizon-0 logits.
- **Core assumption:** Turn-end decisions can be made accurately from a speaker's own acoustic/prosodic cues (horizon 0), while the partner's imminent speech (horizons >0) is a useful but non-essential contextual signal for anticipation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "hierarchical, causal EOT model... anticipates near-future states (t+10/20/30ms) through probabilistic predictions that are aware of the conversation partner's speech"; [section IV.B] "Horizon-0 heads predict immediate states using only self-features... Future heads (h1–h3) incorporate adapted partner features"; [section II.C] contrasts with VAP, noting "enforces that horizon-0 decisions for each speaker use only that speaker’s features".
- **Break condition:** The design includes "anti-shortcut data augmentation" (label jitter, channel dropout). The mechanism would break (learn a non-causal shortcut) if these augmentations were removed, as the model could then trivially predict "Final" for speaker A at the precise onset of speaker B's speech.

### Mechanism 3: Task-Specific Knowledge Distillation for Compact Representation
- **Claim:** Task-specific knowledge distillation compresses a high-dimensional, powerful teacher representation (wav2vec 2.0) into a compact, efficient student (MFCC + convolutions) with minimal task performance loss.
- **Mechanism:** A teacher network compresses wav2vec 2.0's 768D to 32D. A student network maps 20D MFCCs (per frame) to 32D. During training, the student's output is matched to the teacher's compressed output via L1, L2, and cosine losses. A schedule gradually transitions from using teacher to student features.
- **Core assumption:** The task-relevant information for EOT classification is preserved in a 32D space derived from MFCCs, and the student can learn to mimic the teacher's compressed representation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Task-specific knowledge distillation compresses wav2vec 2.0 representations (768 D) into a compact MFCC-based student (32 D)"; [section IV.B] "We train using... a loss combining L1, L2, and cosine similarity... Teacher targets are detached... transition from 100% teacher to pure student".
- **Break condition:** If the distillation weight (0.15) is too low, the student may not learn the desired representation. If the teacher's compressed representation itself is poorly suited for the EOT task, the student will inherit those limitations. The paper does not isolate the pure performance of the 32D student vs. raw wav2vec 2.0.

## Foundational Learning
- **Streaming Causal Convolutions & Multi-Scale Processing**
  - **Why needed here:** The system must process audio in real-time with strict latency constraints. Causal convolutions ensure no future context leaks into the current prediction. Multi-scale dilations capture patterns at different temporal resolutions (e.g., phoneme vs. syllable/prosodic) without increasing parameters linearly.
  - **Quick check question:** How does increasing the dilation factors in the MSCC blocks affect the maximum lookback context and the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies like inter-pausal intervals?
- **Disentangled Representation Learning (Peeling)**
  - **Why needed here:** To separate the acoustic contributions of overlapping speakers in a single-channel mixture. The peeling mechanism assumes the mixture can be decomposed into K distinct source embeddings sequentially.
  - **Quick check question:** What inductive bias does the residual update `r^i = r^{i-1} - z^i` impose, and what happens if the sources in the mixture are not statistically independent?
- **Task-Specific Knowledge Distillation**
  - **Why needed here:** wav2vec 2.0 is computationally heavy for edge deployment. The paper aims to transfer its representational power into a tiny model based on cheap-to-compute MFCCs for a specific downstream task (EOT).
  - **Quick check question:** Why not simply train the EOT model directly on wav2vec 2.0 features and distill those, instead of adding a separate "teacher compression" step?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input (16kHz audio) -> MFCC extraction (32D) -> Primary Speaker Segmentation (MSCC Encoder + Peeling + Online Clustering) -> Primary User Embedding & Activity Masks -> Per-Speaker Feature Prep (23D base + 32D distilled = 55D) -> Hierarchical EOT Core (MLP + LSTM + Multi-Horizon Heads) -> Frame-wise Turn-State Logits -> Decision Policy -> Turn-End Outputs.
- **Critical path (inference):** Raw audio → MFCCs → **Primary Segmentation** (tracks & filters to primary user) → per-speaker 55D features → **Hierarchical EOT** (LSTM + multi-horizon heads) → Final probability per frame → Thresholding & decision policy.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Latency vs. Accuracy:** Horizon predictions (+10/20/30ms) introduce lookahead but enable better anticipation. The system accepts this small, fixed lookahead for higher recall, trading off absolute minimal latency.
  - **Model Size vs. Performance:** Knowledge distillation enables a 1.14M parameter model to compete with ~10M parameter transformers (Smart Turn), prioritizing edge deployment.
  - **Primary Segmentation Independence vs. EOT Sensitivity:** Segregation ensures EOT isn't confounded by background speech, but any segmentation error (missing primary user speech or including background) directly corrupts EOT input.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High EER in Primary Segmentation (>15-20%):** Especially at low SIR (<5dB). Symptom: EOT fires on background talker speech → false positives.
  - **High Backchannel False Positives:** Model misclassifies interim pauses/hesitations as Backchannel → suppressed turn-switches when they shouldn't be.
  - **Latency Spike (>100ms):** Indicates buffering or processing delay in the streaming pipeline, breaking real-time requirement.
  - **Recall-Precision Imbalance:** Very high recall with very low precision (many false triggers) or vice-versa suggests decision threshold or horizon weighting is misconfigured for the deployment scenario.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Primary Segmentation EER vs. SIR sweep:** Replicate Fig. 2. Validate that the online centroid update (α=0.9) and threshold (θ=0.7) provide a stable operating point across target SIRs (e.g., 0, 5, 10, 15, 20 dB).
  2. **Ablation of Anti-Shortcut Augmentations:** Train EOT without label time-shift jitter and without channel dropout. Evaluate on a test set with clean turn boundaries. Expect significant drop in Final F1/recall if the model was relying on the partner-onset shortcut.
  3. **Distillation Weight Sensitivity:** Vary the distillation loss weight (0.0, 0.05, 0.15, 0.3). Monitor multi-class F1 and model size/throughput. Find the sweet spot where student features adequately mimic teacher without overwhelming the primary EOT classification loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does the hierarchical EOT model generalize to languages with different prosodic and turn-taking patterns beyond English?
- **Open Question 2:** What performance gains and latency trade-offs arise from integrating real-time ASR transcripts or LLM-based textual context into the EOT decision process?
- **Open Question 3:** How should predictive EOT models and traditional endpoint detectors be optimally combined in a hybrid system to maximize robustness to long pauses while maintaining responsiveness to prosodic cues?

## Limitations
- **Architecture Unknowns:** Exact MSCC block depths, kernel sizes, channel counts; teacher compression MLP layers and residual pattern; student conv net dilation schedule.
- **Training Hyperparameters:** Learning rates, batch sizes, warm-up steps, total epochs, augmentation strengths, distillation mixing schedule duration, label jitter and channel dropout probabilities.
- **Evaluation Robustness:** No variance estimates or confidence intervals; results from a single proprietary test split may not generalize.

## Next Checks
1. **Primary segmentation robustness:** Replicate the EER vs. SIR sweep (Fig. 2) on a public multi-speaker corpus (e.g., LibriVox) to verify online centroid stability across target SIRs.
2. **Causality enforcement:** Ablate anti-shortcut augmentations (label time-shift jitter and channel dropout) and evaluate horizon‑0 accuracy. A significant drop confirms the model was exploiting partner onset shortcuts.
3. **Distillation sensitivity:** Sweep distillation weight λ (0.0, 0.05, 0.15, 0.30) and report multi‑class F1 to ensure the chosen λ achieves the reported efficiency without degrading performance.
