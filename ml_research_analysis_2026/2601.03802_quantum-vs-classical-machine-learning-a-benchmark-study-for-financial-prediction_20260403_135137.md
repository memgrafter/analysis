# Quantum vs. Classical Machine Learning: A Benchmark Study for Financial Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03802
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03802
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Quantum ML models matched or exceeded classical baselines in specific financial tasks, with QNNs up to +4.9 AUC better, QLSTMs outperforming in volatile regimes, and QSVR matching RBF kernel performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive benchmark of quantum machine learning (QML) models against classical machine learning methods for financial prediction tasks. The study evaluates three key tasks: directional return prediction, live trading simulation, and volatility forecasting, using a standardized framework with parameter-matched architectures. Results show that quantum neural networks (QNNs) can outperform artificial neural networks (ANNs) by significant margins on specific equities; quantum LSTMs achieve higher risk-adjusted returns in volatile market regimes; and quantum support vector regression (QSVR) competes closely with the best classical kernels for volatility forecasting. The findings suggest that QML can be competitive when quantum circuit designs are well-aligned with data structures, particularly in high-dimensional and volatility-aware settings.

## Method Summary
The authors implement a rigorous experimental protocol across multiple financial assets (U.S. equities and Turkish stocks, S&P 500) using walk-forward cross-validation to avoid lookahead bias. For each task, quantum models (QNN, QLSTM, QSVR) are paired with classical counterparts (ANN, LSTM, SVR) having approximately matched parameter counts. Data preprocessing includes feature extraction (technical indicators, lagged returns), min-max scaling, and windowing. Hyperparameters are tuned via grid search or Bayesian optimization (Optuna) using validation metrics (AUC for classification, QLIKE for volatility). For live trading, threshold optimization on a hold-out slice maximizes Sharpe ratio before evaluation on the test slice. Quantum circuits are simulated using statevector methods (TorchQuantum/Pennylane) with angle or amplitude encoding as appropriate for dimensionality.

## Key Results
- QNNs surpassed parameter-matched ANNs by up to +4.9 AUC and +3.6 accuracy points on AAPL and KCHOL.
- QLSTMs achieved higher Sharpe ratios than LSTMs in two of four S&P 500 market regimes, particularly under volatile conditions.
- Angle-encoded QSVR attained the lowest QLIKE on KCHOL and performed within ~0.02–0.04 QLIKE of the best classical RBF SVR on other assets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: High-dimensional nonlinear capture with hybrid QNNs
- **Claim:** Hybrid QNNs with classical preprocessing and multi-qubit readout can outperform parameter-matched ANNs in high-dimensional feature regimes (e.g., 64-D) for certain equities, because the quantum embedding captures nonlinear interactions more parsimoniously.
- **Mechanism:** A classical preprocessing layer reduces dimensionality to match the number of qubits; the variational quantum circuit with multi-qubit readout explores the Hilbert space to model complex feature interactions, while maintaining comparable parameter counts to the ANN baseline.
- **Core assumption:** The quantum circuit depth and encoding scheme are well-aligned with the data structure; the parameter budget is similar between QNN and ANN.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "Quantum neural networks (QNNs) surpassed parameter-matched artificial neural networks (ANNs) by up to +4.9 AUC and +3.6 accuracy points on specific equities (AAPL, KCHOL)."
  - Table XI (Section IV-B): For AAPL (64-D), Hybrid-SQ QNN achieved AUC 0.635 vs ANN 0.597 and accuracy 0.606 vs 0.572; for KCHOL (3-D), Hybrid-MQ QNN achieved AUC 0.7968 vs 0.7475 and accuracy 0.7106 vs 0.6747.
- **Break condition:** If feature dimensionality exceeds qubit capacity or the data does not align with the quantum feature map, QNN performance degrades relative to ANN.

### Mechanism 2: Volatility-aware calibration with QLSTMs
- **Claim:** QLSTMs with quantum gating can improve risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe ratio) in volatile market regimes despite modest gains in directional accuracy, because the quantum transformations better calibrate prediction scores with return magnitudes.
- **Mechanism:** Each LSTM gate is replaced by a variational quantum circuit, introducing nonlinearities that may more effectively capture temporal dependencies and volatility dynamics; the resulting scores, when thresholded, yield trading signals with favorable risk-return trade-offs under high-volatility conditions.
- **Core assumption:** The QLSTM architecture (qubit width, circuit depth) is suitable for the temporal window; threshold optimization is regime-specific.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "Quantum LSTMs achieved higher risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe Ratio) in two of four S&P 500 market regimes."
  - Table XII (Section IV-C): QLSTM Sharpe > LSTM in F1 (0.3865 vs -0.0477) and F2 (1.2585 vs 0.4966); paper notes: "QLSTM’s score calibration aligns closely with return magnitudes under volatile conditions."
- **Break condition:** In low-volatility regimes (e.g., F4), QLSTM underperforms, indicating the advantage is regime-dependent and may not generalize.

### Mechanism 3: Nonlinear volatility clustering with QSVR
- **Claim:** Angle-encoded QSVR can match or exceed classical SVRs in volatility forecasting (as measured by QLIKE) for specific assets because the quantum kernel captures nonlinear volatility clustering more effectively than linear/polynomial kernels, approaching the performance of the best classical RBF kernel.
- **Mechanism:** The angle encoding with entangling layers maps input features into a high-dimensional Hilbert space; the quantum kernel inner product then implicitly models complex interactions in volatility dynamics, offering a middle ground between linear and highly flexible RBF kernels.
- **Core assumption:** The quantum feature map (qubit count, depth) is appropriately tuned for the asset's volatility characteristics; kernel matrix estimation is accurate.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "For volatility, angle-encoded quantum SVR attained the lowest QLIKE on KCHOL and performed within ~0.02–0.04 QLIKE of the best classical kernels on other assets."
  - Table XIII (Section IV-D): QSVR (10q) QLIKE -6.4352 on KCHOL (lowest), and on S&P 500/AAPL, QSVR QLIKE is within ~0.04 of RBF SVR; DirAcc remains competitive (~0.60).
- **Break condition:** Amplitude encoding degrades with high dimensionality (d=64) due to numerical instability; angle encoding remains more robust for moderate dimensions.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input (financial time series) → feature extraction (technical indicators, lagged returns) → scaling (min-max/z-score) → windowing → Model (QNN: classical preproc → quantum encoding → ansatz → readout; QLSTM: classical embedding → four VQCs for gates + two VQCs for state/output; QSVR: quantum feature map → kernel matrix → classical SVR) → training (hyperparameter search, walk-forward CV, early stopping) → model selection → calibration (threshold optimization for live trading) → evaluation (task-specific metrics).

- **Critical path:**
  1. Data pipeline: Acquire and clean prices, compute features, split walk-forward folds.
  2. Model selection: Grid/bayesian search over quantum/classical hyperparameters using validation metrics.
  3. Training: Fit model on training slice with early stopping.
  4. Calibration: For live trading, optimize thresholds on hold-out slice to maximize Sharpe.
  5. Testing: Evaluate final model on test slice, compute metrics, repeat across folds/assets.
  6. For volatility: Expanding window retraining every 120 days, one-step-ahead prediction.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Encoding: Angle encoding is simple but qubit-intensive; amplitude encoding compresses but introduces numerical instability.
  - Circuit depth: Deeper circuits increase expressivity but risk barren plateaus and noise; architectural search balances depth and performance.
  - Hybrid preprocessing: Reduces qubit requirements and can boost performance in high dimensions, but adds classical overhead.
  - Parameter matching: Matching total parameters between QNN and ANN isolates quantum effects, but quantum parameters have different inductive bias.
  - Threshold calibration: Optimizing thresholds on a hold-out slice improves Sharpe but may overfit to a specific regime.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - QNN: Performance collapse when qubits insufficient for feature dimension; erratic results with excessive depth.
  - QLSTM: Sharpe ratio lower than LSTM across most regimes; check if threshold grid is too narrow or overfitted.
  - QSVR: QLIKE degrades sharply when increasing amplitude-encoded dimension (d=64); monitor DirAcc for directional consistency.
  - Overfitting: Training loss decreases while validation AUC/Sharpe plateaus or drops; reduce depth or increase regularization.
  - Numerical issues: Amplitude encoding fails if feature vectors are not normalized; verify ‖x‖=1 before encoding.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Directional classification on KCHOL with 3-D features: run QNN (Hybrid-MQ, depth 6) vs ANN, confirm QNN AUC/accuracy improvement.
  2. Live trading on S&P 500 Pre-COVID regime: train QLSTM (5 qubits, depth 4) and LSTM (hidden dim 5, depth 2), optimize thresholds, compare Sharpe.
  3. Volatility forecasting on KCHOL: train angle-encoded QSVR (10 qubits, depth 2) vs RBF SVR, report QLIKE and DirAcc.

## Limitations
- Reproduction blocked: exact hyperparameters, circuit specifications, optimizer settings, and threshold calibration details are missing.
- Limited external corroboration: corpus provides QML benchmarks in other domains but no direct financial evidence; claims rely primarily on internal experiments.
- Regime/asset specificity: reported advantages are restricted to specific equities and two of four market regimes; break conditions indicate fragility when qubit capacity is exceeded or during low‑volatility periods.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (QNN vs. ANN)**: Medium. Internal results show up to +4.9 AUC and +3.6 accuracy points (Tables XI), but missing hyperparameters and encoding specifics prevent independent verification.
- **Mechanism 2 (QLSTM Sharpe advantage)**: Medium. Sharpe improvement in two regimes (Table XII) is promising, yet underperformance in low‑volatility periods and threshold‑calibration risks temper confidence.
- **Mechanism 3 (QSVR volatility forecasting)**: Medium. QLIKE on KCHOL is best (Table XIII) and within ~0.04 of RBF elsewhere, but advantage may be fragile to encoding choices and dimensionality.
