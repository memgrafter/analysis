# Care-Conditioned Neuromodulation for Autonomy-Preserving Supportive Dialogue Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01576  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01576  
- **Reference count:** 24  
- **Primary result:** CCN improves autonomy-preserving utility by +0.25 over SFT and +0.07 over DPO, achieving mean utility 0.412 vs. 0.162 (SFT) and 0.342 (DPO).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Care-Conditioned Neuromodulation (CCN), a framework designed to make supportive dialogue agents more aware of relational risks such as dependency reinforcement and coercive guidance. CCN combines a learned care signal—derived from a structured representation of user state—with utility-based reranking to select responses that balance helpfulness with user autonomy. Evaluated on a novel synthetic benchmark isolating six relational failure modes, the full CCN pipeline achieves a mean autonomy-preserving utility of 0.412, substantially outperforming supervised fine-tuning (0.162) and direct preference optimization (0.342). Ablations indicate that reranking is the dominant component, while care-conditioned generation supplies valuable candidate diversity. A pilot human evaluation shows directional alignment, with CCN preferred 58.3% of the time.

## Method Summary
The CCN pipeline operates as follows: A structured user state (encompassing goals, boundaries, preferences, vulnerability, commitments, and stress context) is first encoded into a latent vector. A memory bank tracks relational facts across the dialogue. A lightweight CareController predicts a scalar vulnerability signal \( m_t \) from this state, dialogue context, and memory summary. This signal modulates decoding parameters (temperature, top-p) for one candidate generator, producing a care-conditioned response. In parallel, baseline candidates are generated via greedy and sampled decoding. All candidates are scored by four DistilRoBERTa evaluators trained on rubric-labeled synthetic data, producing scores for autonomy support, dependency risk, coercion risk, and supportiveness. A utility function \( U = \lambda_1 V_{\text{aut}} - \lambda_2 Q_{\text{dep}} - \lambda_3 Q_{\text{coer}} + \lambda_4 V_{\text{sup}} \) (with manually set weights) combines these scores and includes a length penalty and a risk constraint \( Q_{\text{risk}} \le \kappa(m_t) \). The final response maximizes \( U \). The base language model (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct) is fine-tuned via LoRA on the synthetic benchmark; the evaluators and care controller are trained separately.

## Key Results
- Full CCN (care-conditioned generation + reranking) achieves autonomy-preserving utility 0.412, +0.25 over SFT (0.162) and +0.07 over DPO (0.342).  
- Reranking is the primary driver: Reranked-best utility (0.412) vastly exceeds Reranked w/o care (0.025) and CCN-candidate only (-0.150).  
- The care-conditioned candidate is selected in only 17% of cases, indicating it contributes useful but not dominant options.  
- Pilot human evaluation: CCN is preferred over SFT/DPO in 58.3% of comparisons.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Care Signal Modulates Decoding
A learned scalar "care" signal \( m_t \) predicts user vulnerability and biases the decoding distribution toward safer, less directive responses when vulnerability is high. The CareController \( g_\omega \) computes \( m_t = g_\omega(z_{d,t}, \psi(x_t), \rho(M_{d,t})) \) from encoded state, dialogue context, and memory summary. Higher \( m_t \) lowers temperature/top-p, reducing randomness and steering away from potentially coercive or dependency-inducing content. The core assumption is that vulnerability—a proxy for relational risk—can be effectively extracted from the structured state. Evidence: The CareController achieves Pearson \( r = 0.668 \) (\( p=4.38e-53 \)) on vulnerability prediction, and the synthetic benchmark operationalizes the targeted risks. Break condition: If \( m_t \)'s correlation with ground-truth vulnerability falls below a threshold (e.g., \( r < 0.6 \)), the conditioned decoding no longer meaningfully aligns with relational risk states.

### Mechanism 2: Utility-Based Reranking Optimizes Multi-Objective Selection
The large utility gains stem primarily from reranking a diverse candidate set, which explicitly enforces the autonomy-preserving objective. Multiple candidates (greedy, sampled, care-conditioned) are each scored by evaluators on four axes. The final response \( y^* \) maximizes \( U(x_t, y) = \lambda_1 V_{\text{aut}} - \lambda_2 Q_{\text{dep}} - \lambda_3 Q_{\text{coer}} + \lambda_4 V_{\text{sup}} \), subject to a risk constraint. This selection step directly implements the alignment goal, independent of how individual candidates were generated. Core assumption: The learned evaluators provide accurate, generalizable scores. Evidence: Ablation shows reranked-best utility (0.412) dwarfs reranked w/o care (0.025) and care-candidate only (-0.150); the care candidate is chosen only 17% of the time, confirming reranking's dominance. Break condition: If reranked responses do not consistently lower coercion/dependency (e.g., win-rate vs. SFT < 55% or DIR not decreasing), the reranking mechanism fails to realize the utility.

### Mechanism 3: Synthetic Benchmark Isolates Relational Risks
The synthetic benchmark provides a controlled environment to measure specific autonomy-violating behaviors that are rare or ambiguous in naturalistic data. It constructs 2,000 multi-turn dialogues across six relational failure modes (e.g., reassurance dependence, overprotection trap) using template-driven flows and structured user state. Rubric labels for autonomy, dependency, coercion, and supportiveness enable precise evaluation of evaluators and CCN components. Core assumption: Relational harms can be sufficiently operationalized in a synthetic format to serve as a valid proxy for real conversations. Evidence: Transfer to the real ESConv dataset shows directional agreement (evaluators produce meaningful utility scores, though lower than on synthetic), and the benchmark categories align with literature on relational co-adaptation and dependency dialogue acts. Break condition: If human evaluation on real conversations systematically contradicts automated rankings (e.g., no preference for CCN or higher coercion), the synthetic operationalization is invalid for real-world deployment.

## Foundational Learning

### Autonomy-Preserving Utility
**Why needed:** Formalizes the multi-objective trade-off (support vs. relational risks) as a single scalar for selection, making the alignment target computationally precise.  
**Quick check:** If a response is highly supportive but encourages the user to rely entirely on the agent, how do the \( \lambda \) weights cause it to be penalized?

### Structured User State (\( S_{d,t} \))
**Why needed:** Provides explicit relational facts (boundaries, commitments, vulnerability) that are not reliably inferable from free-text dialogue alone, enabling the care signal to make state-dependent decisions.  
**Quick check:** Which element of \( S_{d,t} \) (e.g., "Commitments: Study 2 hours per day") would directly inform the care signal about a potential overprotection risk?

### Inference-Time Control vs. Retraining
**Why needed:** CCN modulates behavior at inference via decoding parameters and reranking, avoiding costly retraining of the base LM, which is a practical design choice for deploying alignment constraints.  
**Quick check:** If you wanted to make the base model more autonomy-preserving without changing its weights, what two levers does CCN pull during generation?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
1. **DependentStateEncoder** (\( f_\theta \)): Encodes textual state \( S_{d,t} \) into latent \( z_{d,t} \).  
2. **MemoryBank** (\( M_{d,t} \)): Slot-based storage and retrieval of relational facts; outputs summary \( \rho(M_{d,t}) \).  
3. **CareController** (\( g_\omega \)): Takes \( z_{d,t} \), dialogue context \( \psi(x_t) \), and memory summary \( \rho(M_{d,t}) \) to produce care signal \( m_t \in [0,1] \).  
4. **Decoding Modulator**: Converts \( m_t \) into decoding parameters (temperature, top-p) for the care-conditioned generator.  
5. **Candidate Generators**:  
   - Greedy baseline (from base LM)  
   - Sampled baselines (multiple)  
   - Care-conditioned (using modulated decoding)  
6. **Evaluators** (four DistilRoBERTa regressors): Score each candidate on autonomy (\( V_{\text{aut}} \)), dependency (\( Q_{\text{dep}} \)), coercion (\( Q_{\text{coer}} \)), supportiveness (\( V_{\text{sup}} \)).  
7. **Utility Scorer & Constraint**: Computes \( U(x_t,y) = \lambda_1 V_{\text{aut}} - \lambda_2 Q_{\text{dep}} - \lambda_3 Q_{\text{coer}} + \lambda_4 V_{\text{sup}} \) plus length penalty; enforces risk constraint \( Q_{\text{risk}} \le \kappa(m_t) \).  
8. **Selector**: Picks candidate \( y^* \) maximizing \( U \).

### Critical path
Structured user state → DependentStateEncoder → latent \( z_{d,t} \) → Memory retrieval → memory summary \( \rho(M_{d,t}) \) → CareController → care signal \( m_t \).  
Parallel generation:  
- Baseline candidates: dialogue context \( \psi(x_t) \) → Candidate Generators (greedy/sampled)  
- Care-conditioned candidate: \( m_t \) → Decoding Modulator → Candidate Generator  
All candidates → Evaluators → scores → Utility Scorer → Selector → final response \( y^* \).

### Design tradeoffs
- **Reranking cost vs. diversity:** Generating and scoring multiple candidates increases computational overhead but enables explicit multi-objective optimization and selection from diverse policies.
- **Synthetic vs. real data:** The synthetic benchmark allows clean ablation of specific relational modes but risks non-generalization; transfer to ESConv shows only directional agreement, indicating limited ecological validity.
- **Fixed utility weights:** Manual \( \lambda \) values (1.00, 1.00, 1.25, 0.35) are simple but may not reflect user preferences across contexts; learning weights from human data is future work.

### Failure signatures
- Care signal \( m_t \) shows low correlation (\( r < 0.5 \)) with ground-truth vulnerability on validation set.
- Reranked-best system's Dependency Inflation Rate (DIR) is not significantly lower than SFT's (\( p > 0.05 \)).
- Human evaluation shows strong preference for SFT over CCN (CCN preference < 40%).
- Evaluators produce degenerate scores on real conversations (e.g., all 1 or all 5), indicating poor generalization.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Validate CareController:** Train \( g_\omega \) on the synthetic benchmark's validation split to predict vulnerability; expect Pearson \( r \approx 0.668 \).  
2. **Ablation of pipeline:** Compare autonomy-preserving utility of (a) SFT, (b) CCN-candidate only, (c) Reranked w/o care, (d) Reranked-best; expect (d) significantly higher than all others.  
3. **Candidate selection rate:** On the test set, measure proportion of turns where the care-conditioned candidate is selected; expect a non-zero minority (paper reports 17%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Joint training of components** – Could end-to-end training of the state encoder, care controller, and LM improve utility over the current inference-time approach? Unresolved due to separate training; evidence needed: a jointly trained variant optimized with a multi-objective loss approximating the utility function.
2. **Real-world utility** – Does the full CCN pipeline improve autonomy-preserving utility on real emotional-support conversations (e.g., ESConv)? Unresolved due to computational constraints; evidence needed: execute full reranking on ESConv and compare to baselines.
3. **Evaluator reliability** – Do the learned DistilRoBERTa evaluators correlate with human judgments across diverse dialogues? Unresolved due to a limited pilot (1 annotator, 24 examples); evidence needed: multi-annotator human study on a large, diverse set computing correlation metrics.
4. **Decoding modulation function** – Is the linear mapping from \( m_t \) to decoding parameters optimal? Unresolved; evidence needed: replace with a learned, context-adaptive function and compare utility.
5. **State representation sufficiency** – Are the six fixed state fields enough to predict intervention risk? Unresolved; evidence needed: augment state with additional features (e.g., emotional dynamics) and test whether care signal prediction or downstream utility improves.

## Limitations
- Synthetic benchmark construction details (templates, multi-turn flows, exact rubric labels) are not disclosed, preventing faithful recreation of the 2k-example dataset.
- Unclear whether the SFT baseline was trained on the synthetic benchmark or an external dataset (e.g., ESConv), affecting baseline performance interpretation.
- Evaluator training specifics (input format, number of epochs, loss weighting) are missing, risking implementation drift and limiting reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **Care signal predicts vulnerability**: Medium – solid Pearson \( r=0.668 \) on synthetic, but real-world generalization untested.  
- **Reranking dominates gains**: High – ablation shows massive utility jump (0.412 vs. 0.025) and low care-candidate selection rate (17%).  
- **Benchmark validity**: Low – transfer to ESConv yields only directional agreement; synthetic operationalization may not capture nuanced relational dynamics.

## Next Checks
1. Train the CareController on a held-out slice of the synthetic benchmark; if validation Pearson \( r < 0.6 \), revise state encoder or controller architecture.  
2. Run the full CCN inference pipeline on ESConv dialogues, collect human annotations for autonomy, dependency, and coercion, and verify that CCN outperforms SFT/DPO in human-rated utility.  
3. Measure the proportion of turns where the care-conditioned candidate is selected; if <5%, inspect the temperature mapping from \( m_t \) or increase candidate pool diversity.
