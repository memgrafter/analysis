# CTFS : Collaborative Teacher Framework for Forward-Looking Sonar Image Semantic Segmentation with Extremely Limited Labels
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21071
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21071
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary result:** With only 2% labeled data on FLSMD, CTFS achieves a 5.08% mIoU improvement over prior state-of-the-art semi-supervised segmentation methods.

## Executive Summary
Forward-looking sonar (FLS) image semantic segmentation under extremely limited labels is hindered by sonar-specific noise (speckle, shadows, low contrast) that produces unreliable pseudo-labels in standard teacher-student frameworks. The proposed Collaborative Teacher Framework for Sonar (CTFS) addresses this by introducing two key components: (1) a Collaborative Teacher mechanism (CBTS) that alternates guidance from one general teacher and two sonar-specific teachers, each applying distinct handcrafted augmentations to model sonar physics; and (2) a Multi-View Reliability Assessment (MVRA) module that dynamically scores pseudo-label reliability by evaluating stability across augmentations and consistency across teachers, filtering noise before loss computation. On the FLSMD dataset with just 2% labeled data, CTFS significantly outperforms existing methods, demonstrating the effectiveness of its domain-aware multi-teacher collaboration and reliability estimation.

## Method Summary
The CTFS framework employs a student model (DPT decoder with DINOv2-S encoder) and three teacher models of identical architecture, updated via exponential moving average (EMA). Teachers are distinguished by their weak augmentation policies: one general teacher uses standard geometric/color perturbations, while two sonar-specific teachers add handcrafted shadow simulation and energy attenuation, respectively. During training, after a brief warm-up, CBTS activates teachers in a fixed cycle (general → sonar_a → sonar_b), meaning the student is supervised by pseudo-labels from a different teacher each epoch, exposing it to diverse, domain-relevant perturbations. For unlabeled images, the MVRA module splits the image into a grid (e.g., 32×32). For each grid cell, it computes a per-teacher "stability" score (cosine similarity between predictions on the original and a weakly augmented view) and a "consistency" score (mean pairwise cosine similarity among all teachers' predictions on the original). These are combined into a pixel-level reliability score \(R\) after smoothing. The unsupervised loss then weights the cross-entropy between the student's strongly-augmented predictions and the active teacher's pseudo-labels by \(R\), and discards pixels below a threshold \(\psi\). The total loss is the sum of supervised cross-entropy on labeled data and this reliability-weighted unsupervised loss.

## Key Results
- On the FLSMD dataset with only 2% labeled data, CTFS achieves a **5.08% mIoU improvement** over prior state-of-the-art semi-supervised segmentation methods.
- CTFS demonstrates robust performance across label sparsity levels (2%, 5%, 10%) and generalizes to a new dataset (FSSG) collected in different conditions.
- Ablation studies confirm the contributions of both CBTS (multi-teacher collaboration) and MVRA (reliability filtering), with significant drops in mIoU when either component is removed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Collaborative Teacher Alternating Guidance (CBTS)
- **Claim:** Alternating guidance from one general teacher and two sonar-specific teachers helps the student learn both robust general features and sonar-specific representations, improving performance under extreme label scarcity.
- **Mechanism:** Three teachers share an architecture but apply distinct weak augmentations. The general teacher uses standard perturbations; sonar_a adds a simulated acoustic shadow (defined by random center, radius, and orientation); sonar_b applies distance-dependent energy attenuation (Eq. 11). The student is strongly augmented and trained on pseudo-labels from the active teacher in a fixed cycle. This exposes the student to a diverse set of perturbations that mimic real sonar distortions, encouraging features that are invariant to sonar-specific nuisances.
- **Core assumption:** Handcrafted augmentations (shadow, energy decay) adequately approximate key physical distortions in FLS images; learning from these simulated conditions yields features transferable to real sonar data.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "multi-teacher collaborative mechanism composed of one general teacher and multiple sonar-specific teachers... adopting a multi-teacher alternating guidance strategy"
  - Section 3.2: Formal definitions of shadow and energy augmentations (Eqs. 6–11).
- **Break condition:** If the handcrafted augmentations poorly match real sonar physics (e.g., shadow shape or attenuation model is inaccurate), the student may learn spurious correlations, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-View Reliability Assessment (MVRA)
- **Claim:** MVRA reduces the impact of noisy pseudo-labels by dynamically scoring their reliability via cross-teacher and multi-view consistency.
- **Mechanism:** For an unlabeled image, each teacher generates predictions for the original and one weakly augmented view. The image is divided into an \(m \times m\) grid. For each grid cell, compute: (1) per-teacher stability: mean cosine similarity between original-view and augmented-view class probability vectors; (2) cross-teacher consistency: mean pairwise cosine similarity among all teachers' original-view vectors. These are combined into a reliability score \(R_{ij} = \Pi(C_{ij}) \times \text{mean}(r_{t,ij})\), where \(\Pi(C_{ij}) = \delta + (1-\delta)C_{ij}\) smooths consistency scores (\(\delta=0.5\)). The grid-level \(R_{ij}\) is replicated to all pixels in that cell.
- **Core assumption:** High-quality pseudo-labels are stable under realistic augmentations and consistent across diverse teachers; low-quality labels are unstable and inconsistent.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "dynamically quantifies the reliability of pseudo-labels by evaluating the consistency and stability of predictions across multiple views and multiple teachers"
  - Section 3.3: Full MVRA pipeline and Equations (12)–(16).
- **Break condition:** If all teachers share a systematic bias (e.g., all miss the same class), consistency may be high for incorrect labels; or if augmentations overly distort features, stability scores become uninformative.

### Mechanism 3: Reliability-Weighted Unsupervised Loss
- **Claim:** Using MVRA's reliability scores to weight the unsupervised loss (and threshold low-reliability pixels) prevents error accumulation from noisy pseudo-labels.
- **Mechanism:** Unsupervised loss \(L_{\text{unsup}} = \frac{1}{N_u} \sum_{n,b} \text{CE}( \text{student\_pred}, \text{teacher\_pseudo} ) \times R_{nb} \times \Delta\), where \(\Delta=1\) if \(R_{nb} > \psi\) (reliability threshold \(\psi=0.4\)) else 0. Thus, pixels with high \(R\) contribute fully; low-reliability pixels are down-weighted or excluded.
- **Core assumption:** The reliability score \(R\) is a monotonic indicator of pseudo-label correctness; modulating loss accordingly yields a cleaner training signal.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "mitigating the negative impact caused by noisy pseudo-labels"
  - Section 3.4: Equation (17) and (18) define the reliability-guided adaptive constraint.
- **Break condition:** If \(R\) poorly correlates with label correctness (due to miscalibrated teachers), weighting could discard useful labels or retain noisy ones, harming convergence.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  Student (DPT + DINOv2-S) -> Teachers (3x EMA copies) -> MVRA module -> Reliability-weighted unsupervised loss. Labeled data -> Student -> Supervised loss. Total loss = Supervised loss + λ_u * Unsupervised loss. Student updated via backprop; Teachers updated via EMA.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Sample batch containing labeled and unlabeled images.
  2. Labeled images passed through student; compute supervised cross-entropy loss \(L_{\text{sup}}\).
  3. Unlabeled images undergo weak augmentation; all three teachers produce pseudo-labels. One teacher is active per epoch based on CBTS cycle.
  4. Unlabeled images also undergo strong augmentation; student produces predictions.
  5. MVRA uses each teacher's predictions on the original and one weak view over a grid to compute per-pixel reliability score \(R\).
  6. Compute unsupervised loss \(L_{\text{unsup}} = \text{CE}(\text{student\_strong}, \text{active\_teacher\_pseudo}) \times R \times \Delta\) (pixels with \(R > \psi\) kept).
  7. Total loss \(L = L_{\text{sup}} + \lambda_u L_{\text{unsup}}\); backpropagate to update student parameters.
  8. Update each teacher's parameters as an EMA of the student's parameters.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Teacher count: More teachers increase computational/memory cost but may improve diversity; too many could amplify correlated noise.
  - Grid size \(m\): Smaller grids capture finer reliability variation but increase variance; larger grids smooth over boundaries. Paper found 32×32 optimal.
  - Reliability threshold \(\psi\): Higher values filter more noise but reduce the number of usable pseudo-labels; lower values risk including erroneous labels.
  - Sonar augmentations: Handcrafted models (shadow, energy) are interpretable but may not cover all distortions (e.g., reverberation, multipath).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Ablation (Table 2) shows dropping CBTS or MVRA causes major mIoU drops, confirming their necessity.
  - Tail-class performance (e.g., "hook", "diver") is particularly sensitive; failure in MVRA leads to collapse on rare classes.
  - Mis-setting hyperparameters (\(\delta\), \(\psi\), \(m\)) causes performance degradation (Figure 8).
  - If sonar augmentations are unrealistic, sonar-specific teachers may diverge, harming cross-teacher consistency.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablate CBTS and MVRA: Compare baseline (supervised DINOv2-S), +EMA, +CBTS, +MVRA on FLSMD (2% labels) to show incremental gains.
  2. Ablate sonar-specific perturbations: Start with T_general only, then add T_sonar_a (shadow), then T_sonar_b (energy) to quantify individual contributions, especially on tail classes.
  3. Parameter analysis: Sweep consistency smoothing \(\delta\), reliability threshold \(\psi\), and grid size \(m\) to determine optimal values and sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Do other sonar-specific data augmentations beyond the handcrafted shadow and energy perturbations improve CTFS performance?
- **Open Question 2:** How well does CTFS generalize to forward-looking sonar images captured under different operating conditions (e.g., sonar frequency, underwater environment) beyond the two datasets used?
- **Open Question 3:** What is the impact of the number of specialized teachers on CTFS efficacy and computational cost?
- **Open Question 4:** Are the MVRA hyperparameters (grid size \(m\), consistency smoothing \(\delta\), reliability threshold \(\psi\)) robust across different label sparsity levels and datasets?

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters are unreported: unsupervised loss weight \(\lambda_u\), EMA decay rate, sonar augmentation intensities (\(\alpha, \gamma\)), MVRA weak views count, warm-up epoch count \(E\).
- Handcrafted sonar augmentations may not fully capture real physics (e.g., reverberation, multipath), risking spurious feature learning.
- Limited external validation: evaluation only on FLSMD and FSSG; no evidence of cross-dataset generalization to other sonar systems or environments.
- MVRA's reliability score assumes stability/consistency correlates with correctness; if teachers share biases, consistency may not indicate accuracy.

## Confidence
- **Primary result (5.08% mIoU gain on FLSMD with 2% labels): High** – Direct experimental comparison against SOTA under controlled conditions.
- **Mechanism 1 (CBTS alternating guidance): Medium** – Plausible and supported by ablation, but hinges on augmentation realism; no isolation of sonar-specific teacher contribution vs. teacher count diversity.
- **Mechanism 2 (MVRA reliability scoring): Medium** – Novel multi-view/multi-teacher fusion; core assumption (stability+consistency → correctness) not validated against alternative estimators or on a held-out labeled set.
- **Mechanism 3 (loss weighting by \(R\)): Medium** – Standard semi-supervised principle; break condition possible if \(R\) miscalibrated or threshold \(\psi\) mis-set.

## Next Checks
1. **Sonar augmentation fidelity test:** Replace handcrafted augmentations with physics-based models (e.g., BELLHOP acoustic propagation) and measure pseudo-label accuracy on a small validated subset; if gains diminish, original augmentations may be suboptimal.
2. **Reliability score correlation analysis:** On a held-out labeled set, compute pixel-wise correlation between MVRA's \(R\) and pseudo-label correctness (ground truth match). If correlation is low (<0.7), recalibrate \(R\)'s components or threshold \(\psi\).
3. **Teacher diversity ablation:** Substitute sonar-specific teachers with additional general teachers using identical augmentations; if performance drops, confirms domain-specific augmentations are key, not just increased teacher count.
