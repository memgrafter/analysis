# Conditional Generative Models for High-Resolution Range Profiles: Capturing Geometry-Driven Trends in a Large-Scale Maritime Dataset

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13297
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13297

- **Primary Result:** Conditional generative models (DDPM and GAN) trained on a large maritime HRRP dataset reproduce the theoretical length of object projection (TLOP) trend when conditioned on ship dimensions and aspect angle.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the generation of high-resolution range profiles (HRRPs) for radar target recognition using conditional generative models. Leveraging a large-scale maritime dataset comprising over 900,000 HRRP samples from more than 700 ships, the authors train both diffusion-based (DDPM) and generative adversarial (GAN) models. The key innovation is conditioning the generation on ship dimensions and aspect angle—the primary geometric drivers of HRRP signatures. The generated profiles accurately replicate the theoretical length of object projection (TLOP) trend observed in real data, demonstrating that acquisition geometry is central to producing physically plausible HRRPs. Quantitative results show the GAN achieving PSNR 27.0, MSEf 0.95, and cosf 0.87, while the DDPM attains PSNR 24.7, MSEf 1.51, and cosf 0.81 under full conditioning. The study underscores the importance of incorporating geometric constraints in HRRP synthesis for robust radar recognition.

## Method Summary
No reproduction notes were provided. The paper’s method involves training conditional generative models—specifically Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN)—on a large maritime HRRP dataset. The models are conditioned on ship dimensions (e.g., length, width) and the radar aspect angle, which are known to dictate the HRRP’s envelope structure. By learning the mapping from these geometric parameters to HRRP signatures, the models can generate synthetic profiles that adhere to the physical radar scattering phenomenology, particularly the TLOP trend. Evaluation is performed using standard metrics (PSNR, MSEf, cosf) comparing generated profiles against real ones under identical conditions.

## Key Results
- Both DDPM and GAN successfully reproduce the TLOP trend, confirming that geometry-driven conditioning captures essential HRRP structure.
- Under full conditioning, the GAN yields higher fidelity (PSNR 27.0, MSEf 0.95, cosf 0.87) compared to DDPM (PSNR 24.7, MSEf 1.51, cosf 0.81).
- The results demonstrate that acquisition geometry is a critical factor for robust HRRP generation, with conditioned models outperforming unconditional baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Conditioning on ship dimensions and aspect angle directly encodes the physical constraints that govern HRRP formation. The TLOP—the projected length of the target along the radar line of sight—is a deterministic function of these geometric parameters. By providing them as conditions, the generative models learn to map geometry to the corresponding HRRP envelope, ensuring that generated profiles respect the underlying scattering physics. This geometric grounding prevents the models from producing physically implausible signatures and leads to accurate replication of the TLOP trend observed in real data.

## Architecture Onboarding
The architecture comprises a conditional generator (DDPM or GAN) that ingests ship dimensions and aspect angle along with either noise (GAN) or a diffusion timestep (DDPM) to produce an HRRP. Component map: Conditioning Input (Dimensions, Angle) + (Noise/Timestep) -> Conditional Generator -> HRRP. The critical path flows from condition extraction through the generator network to the final HRRP output. Design tradeoffs: DDPM offers stable training and diverse outputs at the cost of slow iterative sampling; GAN provides fast single-pass generation but risks mode collapse and training instability. Failure signatures include deviation from the TLOP trend (indicating poor geometric conditioning), mode collapse in GAN (low diversity), and blurry outputs in DDPM (insufficient denoising steps). Three initial experiments: (1) Reconstruction test—condition on a real HRRP’s geometry and compare output; (2) Geometry sweep—vary aspect angle while holding dimensions constant to verify smooth TLOP adherence; (3) Metric replication—compute PSNR, MSEf, cosf on test set to match reported numbers.

## Limitations
- The large-scale maritime dataset is not publicly available, hindering independent verification and limiting assessments of generalizability.
- Conditioning is restricted to ship dimensions and aspect angle; other real-world factors (e.g., sea state, sensor noise, frequency band) are not considered, which may affect applicability.
- The paper does not clearly differentiate its contribution from prior work on geometry-aware HRRP generation, and the reported metrics lack established benchmarks for context.
