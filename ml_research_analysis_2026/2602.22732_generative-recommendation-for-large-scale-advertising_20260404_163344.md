# Generative Recommendation for Large-Scale Advertising

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.22732
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22732
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Deployed system achieves up to a 4.2% increase in ad revenue over a DLRM baseline with sub-100ms latency and 500+ QPS per server.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents GR4AD, a fully deployed generative recommendation system for large-scale advertising at Kuaishou. It addresses three core challenges: representing multimodal ad content with business signals via a Unified Advertisement Semantic ID (UA-SID); optimizing for list-level business metrics (e.g., eCPM) through value-aligned online learning (VSL & RSPO); and enabling efficient multi-candidate generation under tight latency budgets using a LazyAR decoder and Dynamic Beam Serving. Large-scale A/B tests confirm significant revenue gains while meeting stringent performance requirements.

## Method Summary
GR4AD co-designs tokenization, model training, and inference. First, a fine-tuned multimodal LLM (UAE) extracts embeddings from ad creatives and metadata. These are quantized into hierarchical semantic IDs (UA-SID), where the final layer hashes non-semantic business fields (e.g., account ID) to preserve value signals. The model uses a LazyAR decoder: initial transformer layers compute in parallel across all generation steps, delaying autoregressive conditioning to a later fusion layer. Training combines Value-Aware Supervised Learning (VSL)—which weights next-token prediction by user and interaction value—and Ranking-Guided Softmax Preference Optimization (RSPO), a list-wise RL loss that aligns generation probabilities with NDCG-based eCPM rewards. A dynamic per-sample weighting balances imitation and exploration. For serving, Dynamic Beam Serving adjusts beam width progressively and based on traffic to meet latency budgets.

## Key Results
*   +4.2% ad revenue in large-scale A/B test vs. DLRM baseline.
*   Sub-100ms end-to-end latency and >500 QPS per inference server (L20 GPU).
*   Consistent gains from both model scaling (more parameters) and inference scaling (larger beam).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Unified Tokenization via UA-SID
**Claim:** UA‑SID provides a compact, discriminative representation of ad content and business signals, enabling effective generative retrieval while preserving value‑related information.
**Mechanism:** A fine‑tuned multimodal LLM (UAE) produces embeddings from video, product, and advertiser data via instruction tuning and co‑occurrence contrastive learning. These embeddings are hierarchically quantized using MGMR RQ‑Kmeans: early levels use large codebooks for semantic fidelity; the final layer replaces vector quantization with a hash of non‑semantic business fields (e.g., conversion type, account ID) to reduce collisions.
**Evidence:** [abstract], [section 3.1].
**Break condition:** If the MLLM fails to extract fine‑grained business cues or the hash mapping produces many collisions, SID discrimination collapses.

### Mechanism 2: Value‑Aligned Learning with VSL and RSPO
**Claim:** VSL and RSPO jointly optimize a generative model for list‑level business metrics (e.g., eCPM), balancing imitation of historical interests with exploration toward high‑value items.
**Mechanism:** VSL augments next-token prediction with value‑aware sample weights and a discretized eCPM token. RSPO is a ranking‑aware RL loss that aligns generation probabilities with NDCG-based rewards via a softmax preference objective over candidate lists. A sample‑level alignment score dynamically weights VSL and RSPO per example.
**Evidence:** [abstract], [section 3.3–3.4].
**Break condition:** A poorly calibrated reward model or rapid market shifts can cause unstable updates and performance collapse.

### Mechanism 3: Efficient Inference via LazyAR and Dynamic Beam Serving
**Claim:** LazyAR decoder architecture and Dynamic Beam Serving together achieve sub‑100 ms latency and 500+ QPS per server for multi‑candidate generation, while preserving quality.
**Mechanism:** LazyAR delays autoregressive conditioning to an intermediate decoder layer, allowing the first K layers to be computed in parallel for all generation steps and shared across beams. Dynamic Beam Serving adjusts beam width across decoding levels and according to real‑time traffic (TABS), controlling compute under latency budgets.
**Evidence:** [abstract], [section 3.2 and 4.2].
**Break condition:** If generation length grows or traffic patterns change abruptly, parallel layers may lose predictive power and beam scheduling may misallocate, harming candidate quality.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Generative Retrieval with Semantic IDs
    *   *Why needed:* GR4AD treats recommendation as next‑token prediction over discrete UA‑SIDs; understanding how semantic IDs encode items and enable retrieval is essential.
    *   *Quick check:* What are the trade‑offs between hierarchical quantization (e.g., RQ‑VAE) and clustering‑based methods for semantic ID generation?
*   **Concept:** List‑wise Ranking Metrics
    *   *Why needed:* VSL and RSPO optimize for list‑level objectives like NDCG and eCPM, not per‑item accuracy; these metrics drive business value.
    *   *Quick check:* Why is directly optimizing NDCG challenging in a generative framework, and how does LambdaLoss approximate it?
*   **Concept:** Autoregressive Decoding and Beam Search
    *   *Why needed:* The inference pipeline uses autoregressive generation with beam search; efficiency optimizations (LazyAR, DBS) build directly on this process.
    *   *Quick check:* How does beam width affect latency and quality in multi‑candidate generation for advertising?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Realtime Serving -> Inference Server (LazyAR + DBS) -> Realtime Index (UA-SID ↔ item ID)
    Online Learning Module <- Logs -> Reward System -> Online Learning Module -> Parameter Sync -> Inference Server
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  User request hits Realtime Serving, forwarded to Inference Server.
    2.  Inference Server generates UA‑SID sequence via LazyAR & Dynamic Beam Serving.
    3.  Realtime Index translates UA‑SIDs to item IDs, returns ranked list.
    4.  User feedback logged, consumed by Online Learning and Reward System.
    5.  Reward System estimates eCPM values for candidates; RSPO logs created; model updated; parameters synced to Inference Server.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   UA‑SID: codebook size vs. collision rate; semantic fidelity vs. business‑signal fidelity.
    *   LazyAR: shared layers (K) vs. quality loss; more sharing improves throughput but risks representation impoverishment.
    *   DBS: beam width schedule vs. latency; aggressive reduction saves compute but may miss high‑value candidates.
    *   Unified VSL/RSPO weighting: imitation stability vs. value exploration; high RL weight can cause instability.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Surge in SID collisions in index (many items share same SID).
    *   Inference latency exceeding 100 ms budget during peak traffic despite DBS.
    *   Post‑update revenue drop suggesting online learning divergence.
    *   Reco Result Cache hit rate plummeting, indicating poor temporal locality.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Ablate LazyAR: Set K = L (full autoregression) and measure latency/QPS change versus baseline.
    2.  DBS sensitivity: Test progressive beam schedules (e.g., [64,128,256]) under fixed latency to find revenue‑optimal schedule.
    3.  Unified weighting study: Sweep the alignment score parameters (Eq. 20‑21) to assess revenue stability and convergence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **OQ1:** How can the unified VSL and RSPO learning framework be made more robust to rapid distribution shifts in non-stationary ad markets, such as sudden trending events or policy changes? The current unified weighting strategy assumes relatively平稳 learning dynamics; abrupt market changes may destabilize per-sample alignment scores.
*   **OQ2:** Can inference-time compute allocation be extended beyond latency to jointly optimize multiple cost dimensions (e.g., GPU memory, energy consumption) while preserving revenue gains? DBS adapts beam width for latency and QPS, but production systems face broader constraints.
*   **OQ3:** How can GR4AD incorporate secondary platform objectives (e.g., ad diversity, small advertiser fairness) into its list-wise optimization without compromising primary revenue metrics? RSPO optimizes eCPM via a single reward signal; other ecosystem goals are unaddressed.
*   **OQ4:** Does increasing the shared layer count K in LazyAR yield consistent quality improvements across all item popularity tiers, or are there diminishing returns for long-tail or semantically complex ads? LazyAR assumes later tokens are easier, which may not hold for rare or ambiguous ads.

## Limitations
*   **Underspecified Training Components:** Exact UAE MLLM fine-tuning details (data mix, hyperparameters) and the online reward model's architecture/features are not provided.
*   **Online Learning Loop Opacity:** Sampling rates, parameter sync frequency, and the reference gate threshold `δ` are unknown, hindering stability analysis.
*   **High Reported Collision Rate:** UA‑SID collision rate of ~18% raises concerns about the robustness of the hierarchical quantization scheme under distribution shifts.

## Confidence
*   **Revenue Lift (+4.2%):** High. Supported by large-scale A/B test with clear metrics.
*   **UA‑SID Representation:** Medium. Mechanism detailed, but high collision rate and lack of ablation on final-layer hash weaken confidence.
*   **Value‑Aligned Learning (VSL/RSPO):** Medium. Well-motivated, but online RL in non‑stationary markets is notoriously unstable; no variance/failure cases reported.
*   **Inference Efficiency (LazyAR/DBS):** High. Concrete latency/throughput numbers from production deployment; architectural changes clearly defined.

## Next Checks
1.  **UA‑SID Collision & Utilization Stress Test:** Replicate the MGMR RQ‑Kmeans pipeline on a sample ad corpus. Measure per-level codebook utilization and final-layer hash collision rate. Validate collision rate remains below 20% and early layers maintain >80% utilization under data distribution shifts.
2.  **RSPO Stability under Simulated Reward Drift:** In an offline simulation, inject a gradual shift in the reward model's eCPM predictions (e.g., ±10% bias on a subset). Train with VSL+RSPO and monitor for training instability (loss spikes, degenerate policy collapse).
3.  **LazyAR K-Parameter Ablation on Quality-Latency Trade-off:** Systematically vary the shared-layer depth `K` (from 1 to L-1). Measure latency change and a quality proxy (e.g., offline NDCG@10). Find the `K` that achieves <100ms latency with <1% quality drop vs. a full autoregressive baseline.
