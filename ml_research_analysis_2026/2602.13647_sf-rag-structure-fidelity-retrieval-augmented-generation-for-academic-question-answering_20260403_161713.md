# SF-RAG: Structure-Fidelity Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Academic Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13647
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13647
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SF-RAG achieves a 6.77-point F1(Answer) gain over the strongest baseline on QASPER (67.09% vs. 60.32%) while significantly reducing retrieval fragmentation and evidence misallocation metrics.

## Executive Summary
SF-RAG introduces a structure-fidelity framework for academic question answering that addresses the fundamental problem of fragmented contexts and token misallocation in standard RAG systems. Unlike approaches that flatten papers into unstructured chunks, SF-RAG preserves the native hierarchical organization of academic documents during indexing and employs a path-guided retrieval mechanism to select coherent root-to-leaf evidence paths under a fixed token budget. This design leads to superior answer quality, lower retrieval entropy, and reduced operational cost and latency compared to advanced baselines.

## Method Summary
SF-RAG operates in two core phases. First, it builds a **structure-fidelity index** by parsing a PDF into Markdown, inferring the hierarchical section tree via LLM validation with rule-based fallbacks, segmenting long sections contiguously, and generating **structure-anchored summaries** for each segment. These summaries are conditioned on the paper title, hierarchical path, current segment, and preceding segment summary. Second, for a query, it performs **path-guided retrieval**: it selects the top few relevant sections using a fused score of LLM-based semantic alignment and embedding similarity. Within those sections, it scores raw segments and their summaries, then uses beam search to find high-relevance-density root-to-leaf paths, filling a fixed token budget by adding segments in order of marginal relevance while preserving contiguity. The retrieved context is fed to an LLM to generate the final answer, with optional extensions for multi-hop and multi-document queries.

## Key Results
- On the QASPER benchmark, SF-RAG achieves an **F1(Answer) of 67.09%**, a 6.77-point gain over the strongest baseline (60.32%).
- It drastically reduces **Section Entropy** (0.44 vs. 2.27) and **Evidence Alignment Cross Entropy** (0.47 vs. 2.28), indicating far less retrieval fragmentation and more accurate evidence allocation.
- The system operates with **lower latency and cost** compared to advanced graph-based baselines like HippoRAG2.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Preserving Native Hierarchical Structure
- **Claim:** Preserving the native hierarchical structure during indexing prevents entropy increase at the source and provides a low-entropy foundation for retrieval.
- **Mechanism:** The PDF is parsed into Markdown; heading candidates are identified and validated via an LLM with fallback rules to build a tree. Long sections are split into contiguous segments respecting boundaries, and each segment gets a summary conditioned on title, path, segment text, and previous summary.
- **Core assumption:** Academic papers have a reliable hierarchical organization that encodes discourse intent and evidence locations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states SF-RAG "first inherits the native hierarchy to construct a structure-fidelity index, which prevents entropy increase at the source." Section 3.2 describes hierarchy inference and structure-anchored summarization.
- **Break condition:** If the PDF parser fails to detect headers accurately (e.g., non-standard formatting) or the paper lacks a clear hierarchy, the index may become corrupted, leading to erroneous path guidance.

### Mechanism 2: Path-Guided Retrieval Under Token Budget
- **Claim:** Path-guided retrieval selects coherent root-to-leaf evidence paths under token budget, reducing fragmentation and misallocation.
- **Mechanism:** After selecting top B sections via fused semantic and embedding scores, the system computes dual relevance (raw segment and summary) for each leaf. Paths are prioritized by relevance density (sum of scores divided by tokens) via beam search (P paths). Segments are added by marginal relevance per token until budget exhausted, preserving contiguity.
- **Core assumption:** Relevant evidence for a query tends to cluster along contiguous discourse paths within sections, and allocating tokens to such paths yields more usable context for the LLM.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "uses a path-guided retrieval mechanism to select coherent root-to-leaf evidence paths under a fixed token budget." Section 3.3 details the two-stage retrieval and optimization objective.
- **Break condition:** For queries requiring evidence from multiple non-adjacent sections, the single-document path selection may miss cross-section dependencies. Noisy relevance scores can also lead to suboptimal path choices.

### Mechanism 3: Structure-Anchored Summaries
- **Claim:** Structure-anchored summaries anchor segments to their discourse position, improving within-section evidence allocation and reducing Evidence Alignment Cross Entropy.
- **Mechanism:** Each segment summary is generated by an LLM with a prompt containing paper title, hierarchical path, current segment, and previous segment summary. This contextualizes the summary with positional awareness.
- **Core assumption:** The meaning of a segment is influenced by its location in the section's narrative; summaries that include this context are more discriminative for retrieval.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2.2 details the summarization prompt conditioned on four signals: title, path, segment, preceding summary.
- **Break condition:** If the preceding summary is inaccurate or the segment is very short, the summary may become noisy. If the hierarchical path is incorrect (from indexing failure), the summary conditioning propagates error.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Native hierarchical structure of academic papers**
  - *Why needed:* SF-RAG relies on the section hierarchy as a low-entropy scaffold; understanding this structure is key to seeing why flattening is harmful.
  - *Quick check:* What typical elements (e.g., Introduction, Methods, Results) appear in the hierarchy of a research paper, and how do they guide a reader to evidence?

- **Concept: Information entropy in retrieval**
  - *Why needed:* The paper uses Section Entropy and Evidence Alignment Cross Entropy to quantify fragmentation and misallocation; these metrics measure dispersion of retrieved tokens across sections.
  - *Quick check:* If a retrieval context draws tokens from many sections equally, would its Section Entropy be high or low? Why?

- **Concept: Token budget constraints in RAG**
  - *Why needed:* LLMs have limited context windows; RAG must allocate a fixed token budget to the most relevant evidence. SF-RAG's path-guided selection explicitly optimizes under this constraint.
  - *Quick check:* In a fixed-budget RAG system, what is the risk of selecting fragments from many different sections instead of coherent paths?

- **Concept: Structure-preserving vs. destructive indexing**
  - *Why needed:* SF-RAG's core innovation is "inherit-and-navigate" as opposed to "destroy-and-rebuild"; understanding this dichotomy clarifies the entropy argument.
  - *Quick check:* How does flattening a paper into unstructured chunks increase retrieval entropy compared to preserving its hierarchy?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** PDF Parser (MinerU) -> Structure-Fidelity Index Builder -> Section Selector -> Path-Guided Retriever -> Answer Generator. Optional: Multi-hop Decomposer and Multi-Document Transformer.
- **Critical path:** For a query, the system first selects relevant sections via fused semantic and embedding scores. Then, within those sections, it finds coherent root-to-leaf paths using beam search on relevance density, assembling segments until the token budget is exhausted before generating an answer.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Structure fidelity vs. flexibility: Strict hierarchy reduces entropy but may miss cross-section evidence.
  - Path coherence vs. coverage: Contiguous paths improve context utility but may exclude relevant non-contiguous fragments.
  - Indexing overhead vs. retrieval speed: Structure-anchored summarization adds cost but enables efficient, low-latency path-guided retrieval compared to graph-based methods.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High Section Entropy (>1.0): indicates scattered retrieval; check section selection or hierarchy parsing.
  - High Evidence Alignment Cross Entropy (>1.0): indicates misalignment with gold evidence; check relevance scoring or summarization quality.
  - Path-guided retriever returns too few segments: token budget may be exhausted by long summaries; adjust β or summary length.
  - Index building fails: MinerU parsing errors; fallback to rule-based header detection may be triggered.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce main result on QASPER: Compare SF-RAG vs. Naive RAG and HippoRAG2 in F1(Answer) and entropy metrics. Expect SF-RAG to show lower SE/EACE and higher F1.
  2. Ablate structure-anchored summaries: Run without summaries; measure increase in EACE and drop in F1(Answer). Validates summary contribution.
  3. Test robustness with smaller LLM: Replace GPT-4o-mini with Qwen2.5-7B; compare performance degradation relative to baselines. Expect SF-RAG to degrade less due to structured context.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What is the minimum hierarchical structure quality required for SF-RAG to maintain its advantage?** The paper shows gains correlate with structural integrity but does not define thresholds or test degradation with corrupted hierarchies.
- **Does SF-RAG scale efficiently to large document collections?** The efficiency analysis measures per-document time/cost but does not address indexing/retrieval overhead across thousands of documents.
- **How does coherent context impact LLM reasoning burden for multi-hop questions?** The paper claims reduced reasoning burden but does not directly measure hallucination rates or reasoning chain validity.

## Limitations
- PDF parsing and header inference are brittle; failures corrupt hierarchy and increase retrieval entropy.
- Hyperparameters (α, β) and token budget lack sensitivity analysis; optimal values may be dataset-specific.
- Evaluation is limited to academic QA datasets; generalizability to other domains is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Overall performance (F1 gain, entropy reduction):** High (strong QASPER results).
- **Hierarchy preservation indexing:** Medium (entropy reduction shown but parser-dependent).
- **Path-guided retrieval:** Medium (fragmentation metrics improved, not isolated).
- **Structure-anchored summaries:** Medium (theoretically sound, pending ablation).
