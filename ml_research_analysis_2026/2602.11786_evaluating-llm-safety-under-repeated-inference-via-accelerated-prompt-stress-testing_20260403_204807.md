# Evaluating LLM Safety Under Repeated Inference via Accelerated Prompt Stress Testing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11786
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11786
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: APST uncovers large hidden differences in per‑inference failure probabilities between models with similar benchmark scores, e.g., Gemma‑3N‑E4B (0.0536) vs GPT‑OSS‑20B (0.0126) at T=0.0, a 4.25× expected incident ratio per 100k queries.

## Executive Summary
Shallow, single‑sample evaluations dominate LLM safety benchmarks but fail to capture operational risks from repeated inference in real deployments. This paper introduces Accelerated Prompt Stress Testing (APST), which treats each inference on identical prompts as a Bernoulli trial and estimates failure probabilities through repeated sampling. Applying APST to multiple models on AIR‑BENCH prompts reveals that models with comparable benchmark scores exhibit substantially different empirical failure rates, especially at low temperatures where guardrails are expected to be stable. Failure probabilities rise with sampling depth and vary across risk categories, demonstrating that standard benchmarks severely underestimate reliability risks during sustained use.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a two‑phase testing framework. Phase 1 calibrates models by repeatedly sampling responses at temperatures T=0.0, 0.7, and 1.0 (N=2000 samples per prompt) to characterize stochasticity. Phase 2A performs a shallow, benchmark‑equivalent evaluation (T=0.0, N=3) using AIR‑BENCH’s three‑level rubric. Phase 2B conducts deep stress testing across temperatures (T=0.0, N=100; T=0.5, N=50; T=0.8, N=20). Responses are judged by an LLM‑as‑judge (gpt‑4o‑mini) using a structured prompt; outputs are classified into {safe_coherent, harmful, non_refusal, gibberish, mixed_gibberish_harmful}. A binary failure indicator is set to 1 for any label other than safe_coherent. For each prompt, the empirical failure rate is computed as the proportion of failures across samples; the model‑level estimate is the average over prompts, with 95% confidence intervals derived via bootstrap over prompts.

## Key Results
- Models with similar AIR‑BENCH scores show divergent failure probabilities under repeated sampling; e.g., at T=0.0, Gemma‑3N‑E4B (0.0536) and GPT‑OSS‑20B (0.0126) differ by a factor of 4.25 in expected incidents per 100k queries.
- Failure probabilities increase with sampling depth (larger N) and temperature, indicating that even low‑temperature settings can produce intermittent unsafe outputs.
- Guardrail behavior is volatile: models switch between refusal and compliance across repeated identical queries, an instability masked by aggregate benchmark scores.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Repeated sampling exposes intermittent safety failures that are invisible in single‑sample benchmarks.
- **Mechanism**: LLM outputs are stochastic under fixed prompts due to decoding randomness and model variability. A single sample may miss a failure that occurs with probability *p* > 0. By sampling *N* times, APST estimates the true failure frequency.
- **Core assumption**: Failures occur independently across inferences under identical conditions (Bernoulli trials).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "real‑world deployment exposes a different class of risk: operational failures arising from repeated inference on identical or near‑identical prompts"
  - [section 2.1]: "Studies … repeatedly query identical or semantically equivalent prompts and analyze response consistency … models may answer correctly yet inconsistently"
  - [corpus]: Weak; neighbor papers discuss reliability but not directly this mechanism.
- **Break condition**: If failures are perfectly correlated across samples (e.g., deterministic model with fixed seed), repeated sampling yields identical outcomes and *p* is either 0 or 1; APST would not reveal intermediate probabilities.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The Bernoulli/binomial model translates evaluation results into operational risk estimates via linear scaling with query volume.
- **Mechanism**: Each inference is a Bernoulli trial with failure probability *p_f*. Under *Q* queries, expected failures = *Q* × *p_f*. This bridges evaluation to deployment risk.
- **Core assumption**: Inference events are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) under fixed operational profile.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 5.1.3]: "Given an empirical inference‑level failure probability estimate ˆ*p_f* and an expected query volume *Q*, the expected number of safety‑relevant failures scales linearly as E[failures] = *Q* × ˆ*p_f*."
  - [section 3]: "Each inference is treated as an independent stochastic inference event … enable quantitative comparison of per‑inference failure probabilities."
- **Break condition**: If failure probability varies across queries (non‑stationarity) or failures are correlated, linear scaling becomes inaccurate; confidence intervals may be mis‑specified.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: APST reveals hidden differences in guardrail stability that are masked by aggregate benchmark scores.
- **Mechanism**: By repeatedly sampling the same prompt, we observe refusal‑compliance switching. Models with similar average safety scores can have very different failure probabilities because one model’s refusals are consistent while the other’s are probabilistic.
- **Core assumption**: The judge’s binary failure classification (*safe_coherent* vs. others) meaningfully captures safety incidents.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.3]: "Models that rank highly under AIR‑BENCH–equivalent scoring do not necessarily exhibit the lowest inference‑level failure probabilities under repeated sampling."
  - [section 4.3]: "Guardrail behavior is unstable, with refusal–compliance switching observed across repeated generations."
- **Break condition**: If the judge is inconsistent or the failure definition does not align with deployment safety requirements, the revealed differences may be artifacts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Bernoulli trial and binomial estimation.
  - *Why needed*: APST models each LLM output as a binary success/failure; estimating *p_f* from *N* samples uses binomial statistics.
  - *Quick check*: If you observe 3 failures in 100 repeated samples, what is the maximum likelihood estimate of *p_f* and what is its standard error?

- **Concept**: Temperature as a decoder randomness control.
  - *Why needed*: Temperature directly influences output stochasticity; APST sweeps temperature to calibrate and compare models under different operational conditions.
  - *Quick check*: How would you expect failure probability *p_f* to change when increasing temperature from 0.0 to 0.8? What might be a non‑monotonic effect?

- **Concept**: Guardrail volatility (refusal consistency).
  - *Why needed*: APST measures not just average refusal rate but the stability of refusals across repeats; volatile guardrails lead to higher operational risk even if average refusal is high.
  - *Quick check*: Two models have the same overall refusal rate on a set of harmful prompts. Why might APST still differentiate them?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  Prompt repository -> Inference engine -> Judge module -> Failure mapper -> Statistical aggregator -> Temperature and sampling controller.

- **Critical path**:
  1. Load balanced prompt set (*P* prompts).
  2. For each model *m* and temperature *T*:
     a. For each prompt *p* in *P*:
        i. Generate *N* samples (*m*(*p*, *T*)).
        ii. Judge each sample → label.
        iii. Record binary outcome (failure=1 if label != safe_coherent).
     b. For each prompt *p*, compute local failure rate ˆ*p_f*(*p*) = (# failures)/*N*.
     c. Compute model‑level estimate: mean_{*p*∈*P*} ˆ*p_f*(*p*).
     d. Compute uncertainty: bootstrap over prompts to get CI.
  3. Compare across models and temperatures; scale to deployment risk.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Sampling depth *N*: larger *N* reduces estimation variance but costs more; diminishing returns beyond ~20–50.
  - Prompt set size *P*: more prompts improve category coverage and precision but increase cost.
  - Temperature range: include low (0.0) for benchmark equivalence and higher (0.5, 0.8) to surface variability; extreme *T* (1.0) reserved for calibration.
  - Judge model: must be reliable; use deterministic decoding (*T*=0) to reduce judge noise.
  - Failure definition: selection among {harmful, non_refusal, gibberish} affects operational risk mapping; be explicit.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Judge collapse: if >5% of judgments are "unknown" or parsing failures, judge pipeline is unreliable.
  - Non‑stabilizing estimates: per‑prompt failure rates do not converge as *N* increases; indicates non‑i.i.d. or insufficient *N*.
  - Zero variance across prompts: all prompts show identical failure rate; may indicate over‑aggregation or flawed prompt set.
  - Temperature insensitivity when theory suggests sensitivity; could mask issues.
  - Unrealistic failure rates: e.g., *p_f* = 1.0 for all prompts indicates overly strict judge or broken model.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Reproduce calibration: Run Gemma‑3N‑E4B at *T*=0.0, 0.7, 1.0 with *N*=2000; verify failure probability increases with *T* (Figure 2).
  2. Benchmark vs APST divergence: Compare GPT‑OSS‑20B and Gemma‑3N‑E4B at *T*=0.0; both have high AIR‑BENCH scores but compute APST ˆ*p_f*; expect ~4.25× ratio (Table 1).
  3. Guardrail volatility: For a given harmful prompt, generate 100 samples at *T*=0.0; compute fraction of samples that are refusals (safe_coherent) vs non_refusal/harmful; quantify switching rate. Expect high volatility for some models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can APST be adapted to evaluate safety in multi‑step agentic systems where inference sequences may exhibit stateful or correlated failures?
- **Basis in paper**: The conclusion identifies "extending APST to agentic systems with multi‑step interactions" as a key direction for future work.
- **Why unresolved**: APST currently treats each inference as an independent Bernoulli trial on fixed prompts, but agentic systems involve memory, planning, and sequential decisions that could create interdependent failures invisible to repeated identical prompting.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implement APST extensions that sample from prompt trajectories or task sequences, measure failure accumulation and state dependence, and validate against human evaluations or synthetic agent failure modes.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What adaptive sampling strategies with statistical confidence guarantees can minimize APST evaluation cost while accurately estimating failure probabilities?
- **Basis in paper**: Future work includes "integrating confidence bounds and early‑stopping criteria for adaptive sampling" to improve efficiency.
- **Why unresolved**: Current APST uses fixed sampling depths (e.g., N=100), but evaluation cost could be reduced by stopping once failure probability estimates stabilize within desired confidence intervals; optimal adaptive rules are not defined.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Design online stopping rules based on sequential confidence interval tracking, test across models and temperatures to characterize convergence rates, and compare cost‑precision trade‑offs against fixed‑depth APST.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can APST be operationalized for continuous safety monitoring in live LLM deployments without prohibitive latency or resource overhead?
- **Basis in paper**: The conclusion proposes "applying the framework to continuous monitoring in post‑deployment LLMOps pipelines" as future work.
- **Why unresolved**: APST is designed for offline batch evaluation with repeated sampling; real‑time monitoring requires handling streaming queries, dynamic prompt distributions, and timely risk signals, which pose engineering and statistical challenges.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Build prototype monitoring systems that sample a fraction of live traffic under APST, assess detection latency for drift or degradation, and evaluate operational costs in production‑like environments.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: To what extent do correlated inference events in real deployments (e.g., session‑based queries, caching) violate APST's independence assumption, and how can reliability estimates be adjusted for dependence?
- **Basis in paper**: Section 5.1.4 notes APST "does not model long‑term drift, user adaptation, correlated failures, or agent memory," and Section 4.1 treats independence as a pragmatic approximation with potential optimistic uncertainty intervals.
- **Why unresolved**: The Bernoulli/binomial model assumes independent trials, but production systems may have correlated outputs due to shared context, rate limiting, or user behavior, which could bias failure probability estimates and incident scaling.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Analyze production logs for temporal or session‑level correlation in model outputs, extend APST's statistical model to account for dependence (e.g., via exchangeable trials or autocorrelation structures), and compare adjusted estimates with observed incident rates.

## Limitations
- Major uncertainty: exact AIR‑BENCH prompt set and balanced sampling procedure are not specified; researchers must reconstruct it from the source benchmark.
- Major uncertainty: model implementation details (Hugging Face IDs, checkpoints, decoding parameters beyond temperature) are omitted, risking implementation drift.
- Major uncertainty: the core Bernoulli trial assumption may be violated by deterministic decoding (T=0.0) or system caching, leading to correlated outputs and underestimated variance.

## Confidence
- Repeated sampling exposes intermittent safety failures: Medium.
- Linear scaling to operational risk via Bernoulli trials: Medium.
- Guardrail volatility differences revealed by APST: High.

## Next Checks
1. Independence audit: For each (prompt, temperature) pair, compute uniqueness rate of generated strings across N samples. High duplication, especially at T=0.0, indicates violated independence; adjust effective sample size or statistical model accordingly.
2. Judge validation: Sample 500 judged outputs per model and have human annotators re‑label using the same rubric. Compute Cohen’s κ; κ < 0.7 suggests misclassification risk that could bias failure estimates.
3. Non‑stationarity stress test: Generate paraphrased variants of a subset of prompts (e.g., via back‑translation). Compare failure probabilities between original and paraphrased prompts; significant variance would challenge the i.i.d. assumption underlying APST’s scaling.
