# Capability-Guided Compression: Toward Interpretability-Aware Budget Allocation for Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16440
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16440
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** Capability-Guided Compression (CGC) allocates differential compression budgets using SAE-derived capability density, which is orthogonal to weight-based importance metrics, but CGC underperforms uniform compression on GPT-2 Medium due to model functional uniformity and perplexity's insensitivity to reasoning loss.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Capability-Guided Compression (CGC), a method that uses Sparse Autoencoder (SAE)-derived component-level capability density to allocate compression budgets in large language models. Capability density measures the functional breadth of a component (e.g., an attention head) via feature activation statistics. The authors show that this density is orthogonal to existing weight-based importance metrics like Wanda, suggesting a novel signal for compression. However, when tested on GPT-2 Medium, CGC yields worse perplexity than uniform compression, a failure attributed to the model's functionally uniform heads, shallow SAE training, and the insensitivity of perplexity to reasoning capability degradation. The work underscores the difficulty of interpretability-aware compression, especially for smaller models where component functionality is homogeneous.

## Method Summary
CGC proceeds in several stages: First, for each compressible component (attention head or FFN), a separate TopK-Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) is trained on calibration activations to decompose the component's output into a sparse set of features. From each SAE, three sub-metrics are computed: feature breadth (number of active features), activation entropy (diversity of feature activations), and cross-input consistency (Jaccard similarity across input categories). These are combined into a normalized geometric mean, the capability density δ(c). This density map then guides compression budget allocation. The CGC-L scheme initializes per-component retention ratios proportionally to density, subject to ceiling constraints that protect high-density components. CGC-F further refines these budgets via evolutionary search. The final per-component ratios determine how much each component is compressed (e.g., pruned). The method is integrated into a broader framework that applies such guided allocation across compression dimensions (pruning, quantization, low-rank).

## Key Results
- Capability density is statistically independent of Wanda importance (Spearman ρ = −0.054, n = 384 heads), confirming an orthogonal compression signal.
- CGC-L yields higher perplexity than uniform compression at 50% global retention (27.87 vs. 27.57) on GPT-2 Medium.
- The negative result is diagnosed as due to GPT-2's functionally uniform heads (narrow density range), shallow SAE training, and perplexity's insensitivity to reasoning loss.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Orthogonal Signal via SAE-Derived Density**  
Capability density δ(c) is computed from SAE feature activation statistics (breadth, entropy, consistency), not weight magnitudes or activation variances, making it independent of existing importance metrics. The core assumption is that a component's functional breadth is distinct from its numerical salience. Evidence: Spearman ρ ≈ 0 between δ and Wanda. Break condition: If |ρ| > 0.3 consistently across models, orthogonality fails.

**Mechanism 2: Density Predicts Phase Transition Timing**  
Higher δ implies lower structural redundancy; random pruning destroys a larger expected fraction of a high-density component's active SAE features, causing its individual Phase Transition Point (PTP) to occur at lower compression ratios. Theorem 1 formalizes this monotonic relationship. Core assumption: SAE decoder directions faithfully represent features, and random weight removal intersects them proportionally to their spread. Break condition: If high-density components prove *more* resilient to pruning on heterogeneous models, the monotonicity assumption fails.

**Mechanism 3: CGC Delays Aggregate Collapse**  
By imposing density-induced budget ceilings, CGC protects high-density components from being compressed beyond their early PTPs. Theorem 2 proves that for a non-constant density map, the CGC-L allocation strictly reduces the sum of expected feature destruction versus uniform allocation, leveraging convexity of component-level destruction functions. Core assumption: Aggregate feature destruction predicts model-wide collapse. Break condition: If CGC-F does not outperform uniform on a heterogeneous model's reasoning benchmarks, the theoretical guarantee fails to translate empirically.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**  
  *Why needed:* SAEs extract monosemantic feature activation distributions per component, which are the sole input for computing capability density. Without trained SAEs, the density signal cannot be obtained.  
  *Quick check:* If an SAE with dictionary size F and TopK=k yields high activation entropy H across features, what does that imply about the component's functional role?

- **Phase Transitions in LLM Compression**  
  *Why needed:* CGC is designed to prevent crossing individual component Phase Transition Points (PTPs), beyond which capability collapses abruptly. The problem statement relies on the existence of such sharp transitions under uniform compression.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a 50% uniform pruning ratio cause catastrophic failure even if perplexity degrades only slightly? What does "phase transition" mean here?

- **Orthogonality of Compression Signals**  
  *Why needed:* The paper's key empirical contribution is proving capability density is a *new* signal, not a repackaging of Wanda or magnitude. This justifies CGC as a distinct research direction.  
  *Quick check:* If two metrics have Spearman ρ = -0.05 with n=384, what does that indicate about using one as a proxy for the other? What would a high |ρ| imply?

## Architecture Onboarding
Calibration data -> SAE Trainer (per component) -> Density Calculator -> Budget Allocator (CGC-L) -> Budget Refiner (CGC-F) -> Compression Application -> Evaluation.  
Critical path: Prepare calibration activations -> Train SAEs for all components -> Compute δ(c) map -> Compute CGC-L initial budgets -> Apply CGC-F refinement -> Generate final per-component retention ratios -> Prune/quantize accordingly -> Evaluate.  
Design tradeoffs: SAE training cost vs. accuracy (using pretrained SAEs may mismatch); φ function aggressiveness (controls protection of high-density components); evaluation metric choice (perplexity may miss reasoning loss).  
Failure signatures: Near-constant density map (low variance); non-monosemantic SAE features from shallow training; CGC-L underperforming uniform on perplexity.  
First 3 experiments:  
1. Replicate orthogonality on LLaMA-2-7B with pretrained SAEs, expecting |ρ| < 0.1.  
2. Ablate individual components on LLaMA-2-7B; test if high-δ components cause larger drops on ARC/GSM8K (not PPL).  
3. Compare CGC-F vs. uniform on zero-shot ARC-Challenge and GSM8K at 50% pruning retention; hypothesis: CGC-F retains more reasoning if model has heterogeneous heads.

## Limitations
- GPT-2 Medium's attention heads exhibit very narrow capability density range (0.63–0.80), indicating functional uniformity and limiting differential allocation.
- SAE training was shallow (5 epochs, small dictionary), likely producing non-monosemantic features and noisy density signals.
- The precise computation of cross-input consistency (Ψ) using "semantic categories" is ambiguous, hindering reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **Orthogonality (ρ ≈ −0.054):** High. Direct empirical result with large n=384; replication straightforward.  
- **CGC performance improvement over uniform:** Low. Paper reports CGC-L underperforms on tested model; contradicts core hypothesis.  
- **Theorem 2 empirical translation:** Medium. Proof is solid but failed on GPT-2 due to low density variance; may hold on heterogeneous models.
