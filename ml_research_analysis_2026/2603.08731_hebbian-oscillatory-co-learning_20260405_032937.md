# Hebbian-Oscillatory Co-Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08731
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08731
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** A unified two-timescale bio-inspired model (HOC-L) that couples slow Hebbian structural plasticity with fast Kuramoto oscillator synchronization, gated by global phase coherence, achieving stable convergence and O(n·k) complexity.

## Executive Summary
HOC-L introduces a novel framework that integrates hyperbolic sparse geometry (RSGN) with oscillatory synchronization attention (SSA) via a synchronization-gated Hebbian learning rule. The key innovation is a macroscopic order parameter *r(t)* derived from Kuramoto dynamics, which modulates a smooth sigmoid gate controlling when slow structural weight updates occur—plasticity is only permitted when oscillator coherence exceeds a threshold. This two-timescale separation (fast phase locking, slow weight evolution) is theoretically justified with a composite Lyapunov function and explicit separation bounds, yielding provable convergence to a stable equilibrium. Numerical simulations validate the theory, showing emergent cluster-aligned connectivity and monotonic Lyapunov decrease, while maintaining linear computational complexity relative to the sparse neighborhood size.

## Method Summary
HOC-L combines three core components: (1) a hyperbolic sparse geometry network (RSGN) that embeds nodes in the Poincaré ball and constructs a *k*-sparse graph via geodesic neighborhoods; (2) a selective synchronization attention (SSA) module where units are modeled as Kuramoto oscillators with intrinsic frequencies and phases, coupled only within their hyperbolic neighborhoods; and (3) a gated Hebbian plasticity rule where weight updates *dW<sub>ij</sub>/dt = -γW<sub>ij</sub> + ηx<sub>i</sub>x<sub>j</sub>G(r)* are multiplied by a sigmoid gate *G(r) = σ(β(r - r<sub>c</sub>))* based on the global coherence *r*. Training alternates between fast phase integration (to approximate oscillator equilibrium) and slow, gated weight updates, with hyperbolic embeddings updated via Riemannian gradients. The fast-slow timescale ratio is critical: the slow dynamics must be sufficiently slower than the fast to satisfy the quasi-static approximation bound from the convergence proof.

## Key Results
- Theoretical proof of convergence to a stable equilibrium using a composite Lyapunov function, with explicit timescale separation bounds.
- Demonstrated emergent cluster-aligned connectivity: oscillators with similar frequencies develop strong intra-cluster weights while desynchronized clusters remain weakly connected.
- Verified monotonic decrease of the Lyapunov function and O(n·k) computational complexity in numerical simulations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Synchronization-gated Hebbian plasticity ensures structural consolidation occurs only during meaningful computational patterns signaled by phase coherence.
- **Mechanism:** The macroscopic order parameter *r(t)* from Kuramoto dynamics (SSA) modulates a smooth sigmoid gate *G(r)* that multiplies the Hebbian update *ηx<sub>i</sub>x<sub>j</sub>*. When *r(t) > r<sub>c</sub>*, the gate opens (*G(r) ≈ 1*), allowing weight strengthening; otherwise, updates are suppressed (*G(r) ≈ 0*).
- **Core assumption:** Phase coherence *r(t)* is a reliable indicator of functionally relevant neural coactivation, consistent with "communication-through-coherence" biological principles.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "synchronization-gated plasticity: the macroscopic order parameter *r(t)* gates weight updates, so plasticity fires only when oscillator coherence exceeds a threshold."
  - [section IV.B] Formal definition of *G(r) = σ(β(r - r<sub>c</sub>))* and its role in Eq. (9).
  - [corpus] Limited direct evidence; corpus shows related work on attention gating (e.g., DGTN's diffusive attention gating) but not the specific coherence-gated plasticity mechanism. **Assumption:** The gating principle extends from biological findings cited in the paper (e.g., [5,6]).
- **Break condition:** If *r(t)* never exceeds *r<sub>c</sub>* (e.g., due to insufficient coupling *K* or pathological frequency distribution), structural plasticity never activates, leading to frozen connectivity. Also, if *r<sub>c</sub>* is set too low, spurious updates occur during transient synchrony.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Two-timescale separation (fast oscillatory synchronization, slow Hebbian plasticity) enables stable convergence via quasi-static approximation.
- **Mechanism:** Fast dynamics (Kuramoto phase updates) equilibrate on timescale *τ<sub>fast</sub>* relative to slow Hebbian weight updates on *τ<sub>slow</sub>*. The slow system effectively sees the fast system at equilibrium *θ*(W)*, allowing separate analysis and a composite Lyapunov function.
- **Core assumption:** The timescale ratio *α<sub>fast</sub>/α<sub>slow</sub> → ∞* satisfies the bound in Proposition 4, ensuring the quasi-static error is bounded.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "explicit timescale separation bounds" and "two-timescale dynamical framework."
  - [section V.D] Proposition 4 derives the bound *α<sub>slow</sub>/α<sub>fast</sub> ≤ ε / (L<sub>C</sub> · K · N + ηM²)*.
  - [section VI] Figure 2 empirically shows distinct frequencies in fast (phase updates) and slow (weight updates) signals, confirming separation.
  - [corpus] The Kuramoto model is well-studied (e.g., [18–20] in references), supporting the fast dynamics analysis; corpus paper "Kuramoto Orientation Diffusion Models" shows similar oscillatory principles in generation tasks.
- **Break condition:** Violating the ratio bound (e.g., *α<sub>slow</sub>* too large relative to *α<sub>fast</sub>*) causes the fast system to not equilibrate, invalidating the quasi-static approximation and potentially leading to instability or limit cycles (as per two-timescale theory [32,33]).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Hyperbolic sparse geometry (RSGN) provides an input-dependent, hierarchical sparse graph that confines oscillatory coupling to local neighborhoods, achieving *O(n·k)* complexity.
- **Mechanism:** Units are embedded in the Poincaré ball; connectivity is determined by hyperbolic distance *d<sub>H</sub>(z<sub>i</sub>, z<sub>j</sub>) < δ*, yielding sparse neighborhoods *N<sub>i</sub>* of size *k ≪ n*. Oscillatory coupling (Eq. 12) and Hebbian updates (Eq. 9) are restricted to these neighborhoods.
- **Core assumption:** Hierarchical data structure aligns with hyperbolic geometry, so geodesic neighborhoods capture functionally relevant local structure.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "integrates hyperbolic sparse geometry (RSGN) with Kuramoto oscillator attention (SSA)" and results in "*O(n·k)* complexity."
  - [section III.A] Defines hyperbolic distance and sparse graph construction.
  - [section IV.D] Restricts oscillatory coupling to neighborhoods: *Σ<sub>j∈N<sub>i</sub></sub>* in Eq. (12).
  - [corpus] Multiple corpus papers on hyperbolic embeddings (e.g., Nickel & Kiela [28], Chami et al. [30]) and sparse graphs support the geometric principles, but not specifically the HOC-L coupling.
- **Break condition:** If data lacks hierarchical structure, hyperbolic neighborhoods may not group semantically related units, causing oscillatory clusters to misalign with functional groups. Also, if *k* is too small, the graph becomes disconnected, isolating oscillator clusters.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Two-timescale stochastic approximation (Borkar framework).
  - **Why needed here:** HOC-L's convergence proof (Theorem 3) relies on this theory to analyze coupled fast (oscillatory) and slow (Hebbian) dynamics. It justifies separating timescales and guarantees almost-sure convergence under conditions.
  - **Quick check question:** What happens to the slow dynamics if the fast process does not have a locally attracting equilibrium for some *W*? (Answer: Theorem requires initial conditions within the synchronization basin; otherwise convergence fails.)
- **Concept:** Kuramoto model and order parameter *r(t)*.
  - **Why needed here:** Provides the fast oscillatory dynamics and a macroscopic coherence measure used for gating. Understanding the phase transition at critical coupling *K<sub>c</sub>* is essential for predicting synchronization behavior.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the frequency spread *Δω* affect the stable order parameter *r** and the gating threshold *r<sub>c</sub>*? (Answer: Larger *Δω* lowers the maximum achievable *r**; if *r<sub>c</sub>* exceeds this maximum, the gate never opens.)
- **Concept:** Poincaré ball model and hyperbolic geometry.
  - **Why needed here:** RSGN's sparse graph structure depends on hyperbolic distances. The exponential volume growth naturally yields hierarchical clusters and sparse neighborhoods, which are crucial for computational efficiency and biological plausibility.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the effect on neighborhood size *k* if the distance threshold *δ* is increased? (Answer: *k* increases, raising complexity and potentially mixing distinct functional groups.)
- **Concept:** Composite Lyapunov functions for coupled dynamical systems.
  - **Why needed here:** The proof constructs *V(W,θ) = V<sub>θ</sub>(θ) + λV<sub>W</sub>(W)* to simultaneously bound the fast oscillator energy and slow weight norm, showing monotonic decrease along trajectories.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is the cross-term between *θ* and *W* omitted in the Lyapunov candidate? (Answer: The gating mechanism *G(r)* decouples the systems sufficiently; adding a cross-term would complicate the derivative without improving the bound under the timescale separation.)
- **Concept:** "Communication-through-coherence" biological principle.
  - **Why needed here:** Justifies the core assumption that phase coherence *r(t)* signals functional relevance, linking the mathematical gate to known neural mechanisms.
  - **Quick check question:** If coherence is necessary but not sufficient for functional relevance, what false positives might the gate introduce? (Answer: Transient synchrony from noise or initial conditions could open the gate, causing spurious Hebbian updates between unrelated units.)
- **Concept:** Riemannian optimization on the Poincaré ball.
  - **Why needed here:** Hyperbolic embeddings *z<sub>i</sub>* are updated via retraction/exponential maps to remain within the ball, preserving geometric properties.
  - **Quick check question:** What numerical issue arises when embeddings approach the ball boundary, and how is it typically handled? (Answer: Distances explode; common fixes include clipping norms or using the Möbius gyrovector space operations.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** RSGN Stream (blue) -> Input -> Poincaré embedding z<sub>i</sub> -> hyperbolic distance d<sub>H</sub> -> sparse neighborhood N<sub>i</sub> -> Hebbian plasticity module (update W<sub>ij</sub>); SSA Stream (orange) -> Units -> oscillator encoding (θ<sub>i</sub>, ω<sub>i</sub>) -> phase-locking dynamics (Eq. 12 within N<sub>i</sub>) -> local attention A<sub>ij</sub><sup>local</sup>; Coupling Bridge (green) -> Global r(t) from all phases -> sigmoid gate G(r) -> modulates Hebbian update; weight matrix W defines sparse graph which feeds back into both RSGN message passing and oscillator coupling neighborhoods.
- **Critical path:** The gating signal *r(t)* is the single point of coupling: it depends on fast oscillator phases (SSA stream) and controls slow structural updates (RSGN stream). The structural updates then modify the sparse graph, which constrains future oscillator coupling and activations.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Gate threshold *r<sub>c</sub>*:** Low *r<sub>c</sub>* → frequent plasticity (risks spurious updates); high *r<sub>c</sub>* → rare plasticity (risks under-consolidation). Needs tuning to match typical coherence levels in the task.
  - **Neighborhood size *k*:** Larger *k* increases expressive capacity and oscillator coupling options but raises *O(n·k)* cost and may dilute cluster alignment. Smaller *k* enforces strict locality but may isolate functionally linked units.
  - **Timescale ratio:** Must satisfy Proposition 4 bound. Too slow a slow rate (*α<sub>s</sub>*) → plasticity lag; too fast → violates quasi-static assumption.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **No gate activation:** *r(t)* never exceeds *r<sub>c</sub>* → weight matrix decays to zero via *-γW<sub>ij</sub>* → degenerate connectivity.
  - **Desynchronization collapse:** Oscillators fail to phase-lock (*r ≈ 0*) due to excessive frequency spread *Δω* or weak coupling *K* → gate stays closed → no Hebbian learning.
  - **Weight explosion:** If gate is always open (e.g., *r<sub>c</sub> = 0*) and *η > γ*, weights grow unbounded, violating Lemma 2 assumptions.
  - **Cluster misalignment:** Strong weights form between desynchronized oscillators (e.g., Figure 3C shows only synchronized cluster strengthens), indicating gating is working but synchronization failed to identify correct functional groups.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate timescale separation:** Simulate a small Kuramoto-Hebbian system (N=50) as in Figure 2. Record characteristic frequencies of *|dθ<sub>i</sub>/dt|* (fast) and *|ΔW<sub>ij</sub>|* (slow). Compute ratio and verify it satisfies Proposition 4 bound for chosen *α<sub>f</sub>, α<sub>s</sub>*. Also check that Lyapunov *V* (Eq. 15) decreases monotonically.
  2. **Test gating mechanism:** Use the two-cluster oscillator system (N=8, Figure 3). Verify that (a) synchronized cluster (similar *ω<sub>i</sub>*) produces *r(t) > r<sub>c</sub>* and develops strong intra-cluster weights, while (b) desynchronized cluster remains weakly connected. Ablate by setting *r<sub>c</sub> = 0* and show that spurious inter-cluster weights appear.
  3. **Benchmark against baselines:** On a small graph classification dataset (e.g., MUTAG), compare HOC-L against RSGN-only and SSA-only ablations. Measure (a) accuracy, (b) final connectivity density (*||W||<sub>0</sub> / N²*), (c) average *r(t)* during training. Expect HOC-L to match or exceed baselines with higher sparsity and distinct *r(t)* trajectories indicating gating.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can a hierarchical gating mechanism using neighborhood-level order parameters *r<sub>N<sub>i</sub></sub>* improve performance in architectures with heterogeneous local structure compared to the single global order parameter *r(t)*?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that using a single global *r(t)* to gate all Hebbian updates "may be overly coarse for architectures with heterogeneous local structure" and suggest "a hierarchical gating mechanism using neighborhood-level order parameters *r<sub>N<sub>i</sub></sub>*" as a refinement.
  - **Why unresolved:** The proposed mechanism is not implemented or evaluated; the current theory and simulations use only the global gate.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation experiments comparing global vs. local gating on benchmark tasks (Section VII), measuring metrics like structural sparsity ratio and task accuracy, combined with a theoretical extension of the Lyapunov analysis to accommodate local gating signals.
- **Open Question 2:** Does the convergence guarantee (Theorem 3) and timescale separation bound (Proposition 4) hold for oscillator populations with broad or multimodal intrinsic frequency distributions, beyond the centered and narrowly distributed assumption (Assumption 4)?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note that Assumption 4 requires "centered" frequencies with "narrowly distributed spread relative to the coupling strength" and that relaxing this "requires extending the analysis beyond the co-rotating frame approximation, perhaps drawing on the Ott–Antonsen ansatz."
  - **Why unresolved:** The current convergence proof relies on the co-rotating frame and a perturbation argument that fails for large frequency spreads; no rigorous analysis exists for heterogeneous frequency spectra.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** (a) Theoretical extension using mean-field reductions (e.g., Ott–Antonsen) to derive new conditions for convergence with arbitrary frequency distributions; (b) numerical simulations with synthetic frequency clusters (as in Figure 3 but with wider gaps) showing whether the empirical timescale separation and Lyapunov decrease persist.
- **Open Question 3:** Is there a rigorous theoretical relationship between the composite Lyapunov function *V(W,θ)* and generalization performance (e.g., test error) on downstream tasks, or does Lyapunov stability merely guarantee dynamical convergence without predictive power for learning?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that "the relationship between the Lyapunov function *V(W,θ)* and downstream task performance remains indirect; establishing rigorous generalization bounds that connect Lyapunov stability to test-set error is an important open theoretical problem."
  - **Why unresolved:** The Lyapunov function is designed for stability of the coupled dynamics, not as a surrogate for task loss; no theory links its value or trajectory to generalization error.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** (a) Derive generalization bounds (e.g., using algorithmic stability or PAC-Bayes) that incorporate terms from *V* or its components *V<sub>θ</sub>, V<sub>W</sub>*; (b) empirical correlation studies tracking *V(t)* and validation loss across training runs on benchmark tasks (Section VII), controlling for task loss.
- **Open Question 4:** Does HOC-L achieve practical efficiency gains and performance improvements on large-scale benchmarks (e.g., Long Range Arena, graph classification) compared to its parent frameworks (RSGN, SSA) and other sparse transformers, and how sensitive is it to the critical threshold *r<sub>c</sub>* and timescale separation ratio *α<sub>slow</sub>/α<sub>fast</sub>*?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors propose "comprehensive experimental protocols" (Section VII) including benchmarks and ablations but note that "validation on full-scale machine learning benchmarks remains a critical next step." They also ask about "sensitivity of learning dynamics to the critical threshold *r<sub>c</sub>* and the timescale separation ratio."
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper presents only small-scale numerical simulations (N=50, N=8 oscillators) and theoretical analysis; no results on standard ML benchmarks are provided.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement HOC-L at scale, run the proposed experiments (Section VII.A–D) on all benchmarks, report accuracy/F1/AUC plus metrics like order parameter trajectory, sparsity ratio, and Lyapunov decrease; perform sensitivity analysis varying *r<sub>c</sub>* and learning rate ratios around the theoretical bound (Proposition 4).
- **Open Question 5:** Can the two-timescale, oscillator-based architecture of HOC-L be efficiently mapped to neuromorphic hardware (e.g., coupled analog oscillators with memristive plasticity) to achieve order-of-magnitude improvements in energy efficiency and latency over GPU implementations, and what are the key analog imperfections (e.g., oscillator mismatch, device variability) that must be modeled?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors discuss "compelling possibilities for neuromorphic hardware implementation," suggesting "fast analog oscillator circuits coupled to slower digital plasticity controllers" and that this "would bring HOC-L’s biologically inspired computational principles full circle." However, they state this "warrants future investigation with detailed hardware modeling."
  - **Why unresolved:** No hardware model, circuit design, or prototype exists; the proposal remains conceptual without analysis of non-idealities (e.g., phase noise, write variability in memristors) that could destabilize the proven dynamics.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** (a) Develop a SPICE-level or abstracted hardware model capturing oscillator coupling and memristive Hebbian updates; (b) simulate the full HOC-L dynamics under realistic device imperfections and quantify deviation from ideal Lyapunov decrease and convergence; (c) benchmark energy/latency against GPU baselines for a sample task (e.g., synthetic oscillator network).

## Limitations
- Major hyperparameters for standard datasets (embedding dimension *d*, neighborhood size *k*, learning rates *α<sub>f</sub>, α<sub>s</sub>*, etc.) are not provided, hindering reproducibility.
- Implementation specifics (exact Riemannian updates, phase integration details, boundary handling) are underspecified, which may affect stability and performance in practice.
- Convergence theory relies on strict timescale separation and the existence of an attracting equilibrium for the fast subsystem; if initial conditions or parameters cause the system to leave the synchronization basin (e.g., *r* never exceeds *r<sub>c</sub>*), the guarantee fails.
- The biological plausibility of coherence-gated plasticity is cited but not directly validated within HOC-L experiments; the link between mathematical gating and biological mechanisms remains assumptive.

## Confidence
- *Theoretical convergence*: **Medium**. Formal proof exists, but assumptions (Lipschitz constants, boundedness, strict timescale ratio) may be violated in practice; empirical tests focus on simple toy settings only.
- *Gating mechanism effectiveness*: **Medium**. Two-cluster simulations show intended cluster-aligned connectivity, yet robustness to noise, varying timescales, and complex data remains unproven.
- *Hyperbolic geometry benefits*: **Medium-Low**. Complexity and hierarchical sparsity are theoretically sound, but no comparative study against alternative sparse graph methods; the claim that hyperbolic neighborhoods naturally match functional groups is task-specific.
