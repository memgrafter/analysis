# Understanding Moral Reasoning Trajectories in Large Language Models: Toward Probing-Based Explainability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16017
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16017
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LLMs exhibit high ethical framework switching (55.4–57.7%) and low trajectory stability (16.4–17.8%); linear probes localize framework encodings and steering improves consistency.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces moral reasoning trajectories to track how LLMs invoke five canonical ethical frameworks (Kantian Deontology, Benthamite Utilitarianism, Aristotelian Virtue Ethics, Scanlonian Contractualism, Gauthierian Contractarianism) across intermediate reasoning steps. Analyzing six models on three moral benchmarks, the authors find that models systematically deliberate with high framework switching (55.4–57.7% of transitions) and low consistency (only 16.4–17.8% of trajectories fully stable). Unstable trajectories are 1.29× more susceptible to persuasive attacks. Linear probes reveal that framework-specific encodings are localized to model-specific layers (e.g., Llama-3.3-70B: layer 63/81; Qwen2.5-72B: layer 17/81) and reduce KL divergence by 13.8–22.6% over baselines. Lightweight activation steering reduces framework drift by 6.7–8.9% and strengthens the link between stability and accuracy. The proposed Moral Representation Consistency (MRC) metric correlates strongly with human coherence ratings (r=0.715) and its framework attributions are validated by humans (mean cosine similarity=0.859). A 2×2 factorial experiment shows that structured multi-framework integration improves accuracy by 7.0 percentage points, while forcing single-framework consistency degrades it by 7.0 pp.

## Method Summary
The study elicits stepwise moral reasoning from LLMs using a theory-neutral JSON prompt that structures the output into four discrete steps. Each step is then attributed to the five ethical frameworks via a separate scoring LLM (GPT-OSS-120B), producing a 100-point distribution over frameworks. For open-weight models (Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-72B), hidden states from all layers are extracted, and linear probes with a KL loss are trained to predict the framework distributions from specific layers. Optimal layers are identified by minimal validation KL divergence. Activation steering vectors are constructed by contrasting activations from stable (no framework drift) and unstable trajectories at the optimal layer; these vectors are added to the model’s activations during generation. The primary metrics include Framework Drift Rate (FDR), Moral Representation Consistency (MRC), classification accuracy, and susceptibility to persuasive attacks. Experiments use 400 scenarios per benchmark (Moral Stories, ETHICS, Social Chemistry 101) totaling 1,200 instances.

## Key Results
- LLMs show high ethical framework switching (55.4–57.7% of transitions) and low trajectory stability (only 16.4–17.8% fully stable); unstable trajectories are 1.29× more susceptible to persuasive attacks (p=0.015).
- Linear probes localize framework-specific encodings to model-specific layers (e.g., Llama-3.3-70B: layer 63/81; Qwen2.5-72B: layer 17/81), reducing KL divergence by 13.8–22.6% over baselines.
- Activation steering reduces framework drift by 6.7–8.9% and amplifies the stability–accuracy link; a factorial experiment reveals that structured multi-framework integration improves accuracy (+7.0 pp) while forcing single-framework consistency degrades it (−7.0 pp).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The findings suggest that ethical frameworks are distinctly encoded in specific layers of LLMs, allowing linear probes to decode them with high accuracy. The localization indicates that moral reasoning involves distributed yet separable representations. Steering vectors derived from contrasts between stable and unstable trajectories shift the model’s internal activations toward more consistent framework usage, thereby reducing drift. The strong correlation of MRC with human coherence ratings implies that stable multi-framework integration yields more coherent moral judgments. The factorial result—that allowing multiple frameworks improves accuracy while enforcing single-framework consistency harms it—demonstrates that moral reasoning benefits from a pluralistic approach rather than rigid adherence to one doctrine. Together, these mechanisms explain how probing-based interpretability can both diagnose and improve moral reasoning in LLMs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Ethical Frameworks**: Why needed: to categorize and track different moral reasoning approaches; Quick check: Can the model generate reasoning that clearly aligns with Kantian vs. utilitarian principles?  
- **Linear Probing**: Why needed: to interpret neural representations by training simple classifiers on hidden states; Quick check: Do probe predictions match human annotations of framework usage?  
- **Activation Steering**: Why needed: to test causal effects and guide model behavior without retraining; Quick check: Does adding the steering vector reduce framework drift while preserving accuracy?  
- **KL Divergence**: Why needed: to quantify the difference between predicted and ground-truth framework distributions; Quick check: Does lower KL correspond to more accurate attribution?  
- **Moral Representation Consistency (MRC)**: Why needed: to composite stability, drift, and entropy into a single coherence metric; Quick check: Does MRC correlate with independent human ratings of reasoning quality?  
- **Structured JSON Prompting**: Why needed: to obtain discrete, parseable reasoning steps for systematic analysis; Quick check: Does the model adhere to the required JSON schema with high success rate?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Input Scenario -> Model (generates 4-step JSON reasoning) -> Parse Steps -> Scoring LLM (attributes frameworks per step) -> Trajectory Metrics (FDR, MRC) -> For open-weight models: Extract Hidden States -> Train Linear Probes -> Identify Optimal Layers -> Construct Steering Vectors (stable vs unstable) -> Apply Steering during Generation -> Modified Model Output.

**Critical Path**  
1. Generate structured reasoning for diverse moral scenarios.  
2. Attribute ethical frameworks to each reasoning step using a scoring LLM.  
3. Compute trajectory stability metrics (FDR, MRC).  
4. Train linear probes on hidden states to localize framework encodings.  
5. Build steering vectors from stable/unstable contrasts.  
6. Apply steering during generation and evaluate drift reduction and accuracy.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Separate scoring LLM vs. probe: scoring LLM offers flexibility but adds cost; probe is cheaper and intrinsic but requires labeled attributions.  
- Quantization (4-bit NF4) vs. full precision: quantization enables memory-efficient probing/steering but may weaken steering effects; full precision yields stronger interventions at higher resource cost.  
- Structured JSON prompting ensures parseable outputs but may restrict natural reasoning; free-form prompts increase expressiveness but reduce reliability.

**Failure Signatures**  
- JSON parsing failures due to model non-adherence to schema.  
- Low agreement among repeated attribution runs (high variance in framework distributions).  
- Probe overfitting: training KL decreases while validation KL diverges.  
- Steering vectors producing reversed effects (FDR increase) or no observable change.  
- Quantization-induced attenuation of steering magnitude.

**First Experiments**  
1. Replicate core trajectory analysis on Llama-3.3-70B with the Moral Stories dataset to verify switching rates and stability metrics.  
2. Train linear probes on all layers, identify the layer with lowest validation KL, and confirm framework localization (expected: layer 63).  
3. Apply activation steering at the optimal layer with α ∈ [0, 10] and measure changes in FDR and accuracy.

## Limitations
- Uncertainty in the exact prompt wording used for GPT-OSS-120B's framework attribution, which may affect reproducibility and human validation.  
- Steering implementation details under 4-bit NF4 quantization are unspecified, potentially attenuating observed effects.  
- Reliance on proprietary models (GPT-5, GPT-OSS-120B) necessitates substitutes for full replication, limiting direct comparability.

## Confidence
- Multi-framework switching and low stability: Medium  
- Unstable trajectory susceptibility to persuasive attacks: Medium  
- Probe layer localization and KL reduction (13.8–22.6%): High  
- Steering reduces framework drift (6.7–8.9%): Medium  
- MRC coherence correlation (r=0.715): High  
- Factorial accuracy effects (±7.0 pp): Medium

## Next Checks
- Human-validate GPT-OSS-120B attribution distributions on a diverse sample of reasoning steps to ensure reliability and alignment with human judgments.  
- Replicate steering on Llama-3.3-70B in full precision to quantify the impact of quantization and refine the steering coefficient α.  
- Extend the multi-framework integration experiment to other architectures (e.g., Mixtral-8x7B, Claude-3.5) to test the generalizability of the observed accuracy improvements.
