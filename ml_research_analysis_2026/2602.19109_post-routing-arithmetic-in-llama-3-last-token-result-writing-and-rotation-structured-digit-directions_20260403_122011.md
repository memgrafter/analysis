# Post-Routing Arithmetic in Llama-3: Last-Token Result Writing and Rotation-Structured Digit Directions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19109
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19109
- **Reference count:** 26
- **One-line primary result:** Llama-3-8B writes three-digit addition results into the last token after layer ~16 using context-dependent digit-sum directions that are related by an orthogonal map in a low-rank subspace.

## Executive Summary
This paper mechanistically investigates how Llama-3-8B computes three-digit addition results under a one-token readout. Employing causal residual patching and cumulative attention ablations, the authors locate a sharp boundary near layer 16 where answer control shifts to the last token and subsequent self-attention becomes dispensable. Beyond this boundary, they learn digit-sum direction dictionaries for various higher-digit contexts and find these dictionaries are approximately aligned by an orthogonal transformation in a shared low-rank subspace, as shown by Procrustes alignment. Causal editing experiments confirm that within-context direct edits succeed, cross-context transfer fails, but applying the learned rotation restores success, demonstrating that the model writes arithmetic results via geometrically structured, context-sensitive representations in the last token.

## Method Summary
The core methodology combines causal and interpretability techniques. Causal residual patching swaps residual activations at specific layers and positions with those from another example, revealing where the answer becomes accessible via the last token. Cumulative attention ablation sequentially removes attention heads from later layers to test necessity. To characterize representations, the authors train linear dictionaries on the last token’s residual activations after the boundary, extracting directions corresponding to digit sums (e.g., ones+carry, tens+carry). Separate dictionaries are built for different higher-digit carry contexts. These dictionaries are compared using a low-rank Procrustes alignment, which finds an orthogonal map that best matches them; near‑perfect cosine similarity (0.99–1.00) and low relative Frobenius error (<0.11) indicate a shared low‑dimensional structure. Finally, causal digit editing manually perturbs the residual stream along these directions to test output changes, including cross‑context transfers with and without the learned rotation.

## Key Results
- **Sharp result‑writing boundary near layer 16:** Last‑token patching from this layer onward fully transfers the correct answer; ablating self‑attention from layer 17 onward leaves accuracy unchanged (~99.9%).
- **Context‑conditioned digit‑sum dictionaries linked by low‑rank orthogonal map:** Unaligned cosine decreases with depth, but after Procrustes alignment, aligned cosine remains near 1.0 (0.99–1.00) with relative Frobenius error below 0.11.
- **Causal digit editing confirms structured representation:** Within‑context direct edits succeed; naive cross‑context transfer fails; applying the learned rotation restores edit success to near‑direct levels, while control rotations or random mappings do not.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
After the result‑writing boundary, the last token’s residual stream becomes the sole locus of the final sum. The model deploys context‑specific basis vectors that encode the sum of each digit position plus any incoming carry. These basis vectors vary with the carry state of higher digits, reflecting the interdependence of digit sums. However, the dictionaries for different contexts are approximately related by an orthogonal transformation confined to a shared low‑rank subspace. This structure allows the model to compute the result using a common basis up to a rotation, effectively balancing context sensitivity with parameter efficiency. The sharp boundary at layer 16 indicates that earlier layers handle digit encoding, carry propagation, and routing, while post‑boundary layers merely assemble the answer from pre‑routed information, making later self‑attention redundant.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept 1: Causal Residual Patching
- **Why needed:** To pinpoint which layer and position the model relies on for the final answer, localizing the result‑writing boundary.
- **Quick check:** If patching the last token’s residual from layer N onward with that from a correct example preserves the model’s output, then the answer is determined by or accessible at that layer.

### Concept 2: Cumulative Attention Ablation
- **Why needed:** To assess whether attention heads in layers after the boundary are essential for maintaining accuracy.
- **Quick check:** Removing attention weights from layer L onward should not degrade performance if those heads are dispensable.

### Concept 3: Dictionary Learning for Digit‑Sum Directions
- **Why needed:** To extract linear directions from the residual space that correspond to the sum of individual digit positions (including carry), providing an interpretable basis.
- **Quick check:** The learned dictionary elements should correlate with their respective digit‑sum values across a diverse set of examples.

### Concept 4: Procrustes Alignment
- **Why needed:** To compare dictionaries obtained under different higher‑digit contexts by finding an orthogonal transformation that best aligns them, revealing a shared low‑rank subspace.
- **Quick check:** After alignment, corresponding dictionary vectors should have cosine similarity near 1 and the Frobenius error of the misalignment should be low.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Tokens (digits and special tokens) → Token Embedding Layer → Early Transformer Layers (digit value computation, initial carry) → Mid Transformer Layers (cross‑token routing, carry propagation) → Layer 16 (Result‑Writing Boundary) → Post‑Boundary Transformer Layers (last‑token MLP updates along digit‑sum directions; self‑attention largely inactive) → LM Head → Output Token

**Critical Path:**  
Digit embeddings are processed through early and mid layers to compute carries and route necessary information to the last token. Around layer 16, the answer becomes consolidated in the last token’s residual state. Post‑boundary MLPs then incrementally add the appropriate digit‑sum basis vectors, constructing the final representation that the LM head reads out.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
The single‑token readout simplifies generation but forces all result information into a bottleneck. The low‑rank orthogonal structure likely reduces the number of parameters needed for context‑dependent computations, trading off some flexibility for efficiency. The dispensability of late self‑attention suggests the model optimizes for minimal additional computation after consolidation.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Disrupting the boundary (e.g., ablating earlier layers) prevents the last token from containing the correct answer.  
- Perturbing post‑boundary MLPs or the digit‑sum directions leads to errors in specific digit positions.  
- Using an incorrect rotation for cross‑context edits causes failure, confirming the precise alignment is crucial.

**Three First Experiments:**  
1. Replicate the causal patching and attention ablation pipeline for subtraction, multiplication, and larger additions to test boundary generality.  
2. Apply the dictionary learning and Procrustes alignment to larger Llama‑3 variants (e.g., 70B) and other model families to evaluate robustness of the low‑rank structure.  
3. Perform targeted ablations of individual attention heads and MLP neurons within post‑boundary layers to map which components implement the digit‑sum directions and rotation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Do the sharp result‑writing boundary (near layer 16) and the post‑routing last‑token‑only behavior generalize to other arithmetic tasks (e.g., subtraction, multiplication) and to addition with more than three digits?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] ... Limitation: "Task and readout specificity... may change for other arithmetic tasks (e.g., subtraction/multiplication), longer numbers, different tokenizers, or multi‑token generation..."
- **Why unresolved:** The study only examined three‑digit addition under a strict one‑token numeric readout; variations could alter the causal boundary layer, the dispensability of late attention, or the representation format.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the causal localization pipeline (cross‑sample residual patching, cumulative attention ablations) across a diverse set of arithmetic tasks and digit lengths, including multi‑token readouts, to test whether similar boundaries and post‑routing regimes exist.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific neural circuit components (e.g., attention heads, MLP neurons) implement the low‑rank aligned subspaces observed in the post‑routing regime?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] ... Conclusion: "A natural next step is... to map the low‑rank aligned subspaces to more explicit circuit components."
- **Why unresolved:** The analysis characterizes geometric structure in the residual stream but does not attribute it to particular mechanistic modules; the link between abstract directions and fine‑grained circuit activity remains unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform targeted neuron‑level or head‑level causal interventions (e.g., activation patching, ablation) within post‑routing layers while measuring alignment metrics and editing success; identify components whose disruption collapses the Procrustes structure or counterfactual editability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How do the result‑writing boundary and the rotation‑structured digit dictionaries vary across model families, scales, training recipes, and fine‑tuned variants?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] ... Limitations: "We do not yet know how these phenomena vary across model families, scales, training recipes, or fine‑tuned/chat variants. Cross‑model replication and systematic comparisons are required..."
- **Why unresolved:** Evidence is currently limited to a single model (Meta‑Llama‑3‑8B base); architectural differences or training objectives could shift the boundary depth, the strength of context‑conditioning, or the quality of low‑rank alignment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply the same dictionary construction and Procrustes alignment to a broad suite of decoder‑only models (e.g., varying depth/width, training data, instruction tuning) and compare metrics like unaligned/aligned cosine and relative Frobenius error; test whether the patterns hold robustly or model‑dependent deviations emerge.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What is the full computational circuit that routes digit and carry information into the last token before the result‑writing boundary, and how are intermediate representations computed?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] ... Limitation: "Our methods do not provide a full end‑to‑end explanation of how earlier layers compute carries and intermediate representations, nor do we fully characterize the circuits that route information into the last token before the boundary."
- **Why unresolved:** The study isolates the post‑routing regime and does not trace the earlier stages where cross‑token interactions and carry propagation likely occur; these pre‑boundary mechanisms remain a black box.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Use causal tracing or mediation analysis across all layers and positions on arithmetic examples, tracking the flow of digit and carry information; identify attention patterns and MLP updates that crucially encode intermediate sums; validate via targeted ablations that selectively disrupt hypothesized pathways.

## Limitations
- The scope is limited to Llama‑3‑8B performing three‑digit addition with a one‑token readout; generalization to other tasks, longer numbers, and multi‑token readouts is unknown.
- The identification of the layer‑16 boundary relies on linear causal interventions that may overlook deeper distributed contributions.
- The low‑rank Procrustes alignment leaves residual error (up to 0.11) and does not establish the necessity of low rank; unexplained variance suggests additional structure.

## Confidence
- **Result‑writing boundary near layer 16:** High
- **Context‑conditioned direction dictionaries related by low‑rank orthogonal map:** Medium
- **Causal digit editing via learned rotation:** High
