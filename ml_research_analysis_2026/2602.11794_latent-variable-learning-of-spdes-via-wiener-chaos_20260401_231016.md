# Latent-Variable Learning of SPDEs via Wiener Chaos

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11794
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11794
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** Combines spectral Galerkin projection with truncated Wiener chaos to learn linear stochastic PDEs from spatiotemporal observations, achieving ~65% reduction in relative L2 error compared to baselines and accurate recovery of second‑order statistics.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the inverse problem of learning the governing stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) from observed solution trajectories, without access to the driving noise. The proposed method builds a structured latent‑variable model that merges spectral Galerkin projection with a truncated Wiener chaos expansion. This construction yields a finite set of deterministic propagator coefficients and stochastic chaos coordinates whose temporal evolution follows an explicit system of ordinary differential equations. By performing variational inference, both the latent variables and the modal parameters (eigenvalues and noise amplitudes) are learned jointly from data. Experiments on synthetic data for both bounded and unbounded domains show state‑of‑the‑art trajectory prediction accuracy (∼65% improvement in relative L2 error) and faithful recovery of second‑order statistics such as variance profiles and modal energy spectra. The approach demonstrates a principled way to uncover the intrinsic stochastic law underlying observed spatiotemporal phenomena.

## Method Summary
The method starts by projecting the SPDE onto an orthonormal spatial basis (e.g., Hermite or Fourier sine), reducing it to a system of modal stochastic differential equations. Each modal SDE is linear with additive Gaussian noise. A first‑order Wiener chaos expansion truncated at level L expresses the solution as a sum of deterministic propagators multiplied by independent standard Gaussians (chaos coordinates). This produces a finite‑dimensional latent ODE: the zero‑order coefficients evolve deterministically, while the first‑order coefficients are driven by the temporal basis functions scaled by the noise amplitudes. Observations are reconstructed by re‑expanding the modal coefficients in the spatial basis. Learning proceeds by encoding the spatiotemporal data with a multi‑time attention network (mTAN) to infer Gaussian posterior distributions over the initial propagator coefficients and the chaos coordinates. The evidence lower bound (ELBO) combines a Gaussian reconstruction loss with KL divergences to standard Gaussian priors, weighted appropriately. Model parameters—including the modal decay rates λ, noise amplitudes q, and decoder variance—are optimized via Adam with a warm‑up and cosine decay schedule. The architecture cleanly separates deterministic dynamics from stochastic excitation, enabling accurate inference of the underlying SPDE.

## Key Results
- Trajectory accuracy: ~65% reduction in relative L2 error compared to prior methods.
- Accurate recovery of second‑order solution statistics, including time‑dependent variance and modal energy spectra.
- Faithful estimation of true modal eigenvalues and noise amplitudes from data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The combination of spectral Galerkin and Wiener chaos provides a mathematically rigorous factorization of the solution into deterministic propagators and stochastic coordinates. This factorization turns the infinite‑dimensional SPDE into a tractable latent ODE with linear structure, making the learning problem well‑posed. Variational inference allows the model to infer latent variables from noisy observations without needing explicit noise samples, while the explicit ODE ensures that the learned dynamics respect the physical laws encoded in the projection. The use of a powerful encoder (mTAN) captures complex spatiotemporal dependencies, enabling accurate posterior approximations. As a result, the method can disentangle the underlying deterministic drift from the stochastic forcing and recover the true modal parameters, leading to high‑fidelity trajectory predictions and correct second‑order statistics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Spectral Galerkin projection**  
  *Why needed:* Reduces the PDE to a finite system of modal ODEs, making the problem finite‑dimensional while preserving orthogonality and energy properties.  
  *Quick check:* Verify that the spatial basis is orthonormal in L² and that modal equations match the weak form of the SPDE.

- **Wiener chaos expansion**  
  *Why needed:* Represents the Gaussian forcing as an explicit series of independent Gaussian variables times deterministic basis functions, enabling analytic evaluation of second‑order statistics and closure of the modal system.  
  *Quick check:* Ensure the chaos basis is complete and that truncation does not distort the noise statistics beyond acceptable tolerance.

- **Variational inference (ELBO)**  
  *Why needed:* Provides a scalable way to learn latent variables and parameters from data when the posterior is intractable, by maximizing a lower bound on the log‑likelihood.  
  *Quick check:* Monitor ELBO and reconstruction loss for convergence; check that KL divergences are positive and finite.

- **Latent ODE modeling**  
  *Why needed:* Encapsulates the temporal evolution of the propagator coefficients in a continuous‑time dynamical system, allowing exact integration and smooth trajectories.  
  *Quick check:* Integrate the ODE with known λ, q and compare against analytical OU solutions for a single mode.

- **Separable deterministic/stochastic structure**  
  *Why needed:* Distinguishes the zero‑order (deterministic drift) from first‑order (stochastic excitation) components, simplifying learning and regularization.  
  *Quick check:* Inspect learned posterior variances; both z₀ and ξ should have non‑zero KL contributions, indicating both parts are utilized.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Observations → Encoder (mTAN) → q(z₀), q(ξ) → Sample z₀, ξ → ODE solver (RK4) with learned (λ, q) → Reconstruction operator (spatial basis) → Output field

**Critical path:**  
During training, the encoder processes the full spatiotemporal input to produce latent posterior parameters. Sampling yields z₀ and ξ, which serve as initial conditions for the ODE. The ODE integrates the zero‑ and first‑order coefficients over time; the reconstruction combines them with the spatial basis to produce the predicted field. The reconstruction loss (Gaussian) and KL terms compute the ELBO, which is differentiated through the ODE solver (via backpropagation through time) to update all parameters.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Temporal basis choice (sine vs. cosine) affects non‑orthogonality and representation power; sine is simple but may miss certain phases.  
- Fixed‑step RK4 is fast and stable for moderate λ but may require small steps; adaptive solvers are more robust but slower.  
- Encoder capacity (mTAN vs. LSTM) trades sample efficiency for computational cost; a more expressive encoder may reduce KL weight tuning.  
- KL weighting (0.07 for z₀, 1.3 for ξ) carefully balances posterior collapse and flexibility; these values may need adjustment for different datasets.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Posterior collapse: KL(z₀) or KL(ξ) → 0, latent variables ignored.  
- ODE blow‑up: λ or q grow without bound, causing numerical overflow.  
- Encoder variance collapse: σ² → 0 for one latent variable, indicating encoder overfitting.  
- Reconstruction degradation: high relative L2 error, especially at later times.  
- Training instability: loss becomes NaN or fluctuates wildly.

**First experiments:**  
1. **Temporal basis ablation:** Train models with sine and cosine bases on identical data; compare validation ELBO and parameter recovery.  
2. **Encoder architecture comparison:** Replace mTAN with a GRU encoder while keeping other settings fixed; measure convergence speed and final trajectory error.  
3. **Parameter recovery test:** In the simplified single‑mode OU case (N=1, L=1), train to recover known λ and q; repeat across random seeds to assess consistency.
