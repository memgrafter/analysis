# Self-Supervised Learning from Noisy and Incomplete Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03244
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03244
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Survey of self-supervised learning (SSL) techniques for inverse imaging problems that eliminate the need for ground truth; SSL losses with known noise are unbiased, and for incomplete measurements they recover nullspace information via multiple operators or equivariance, achieving performance within a few dB of supervised learning.

## Executive Summary
This survey reviews self-supervised learning (SSL) approaches for inverse imaging problems where ground-truth data is unavailable. It categorizes SSL losses based on noise knowledge: methods like R2R and SURE provide unbiased estimation of the supervised loss when noise statistics are fully known; with partially known noise, techniques such as UNSURE or blind-spot networks introduce bounded performance gaps. For incomplete measurements, SSL leverages either multiple diverse forward operators or signal invariance under transformations (equivariance) to recover information in the nullspace, supported by theoretical guarantees under low-dimensional signal models. Applications in accelerated MRI, cryo-EM, and SAR demonstrate that SSL can match or closely approach supervised performance, often within a few dB of the 6 dB PSNR improvement that supervised deep learning provides over TV regularization. Sample complexity analysis suggests SURE requires roughly three times more unlabeled data than labeled data to achieve equivalent estimation variance at moderate noise levels. The survey also connects SSL to Stein’s lemma and Tweedie’s formula and outlines open challenges such as handling non-linear operators and generative modeling.

## Method Summary
The paper surveys a broad spectrum of SSL techniques for inverse problems. A concrete exemplar is the SSDU method for accelerated MRI, which trains a reconstruction network using a splitting loss: given an undersampled measurement y and its forward operator A, random disjoint splits A₁, A₂ are generated; the network predicts from the subset (y₁, A₁) and incurs a loss measuring consistency with the other subset (y₂, A₂). The network architecture typically comprises a U‑Net applied to the zero-filled inverse of y₁. At inference, predictions from multiple splits are averaged to stabilize results. This approach, falling under the multiple operators paradigm, yields an unbiased estimate of a weighted supervised loss under mild conditions on operator diversity. SSDU and related methods enable training without any paired ground-truth data, relying solely on the forward model and the statistics of the measurements.

## Key Results
- Unbiasedness & bounds: For fully known noise, SSL losses (R2R, SURE) are unbiased; for unknown noise, methods like UNSURE or blind-spot networks incur bounded performance gaps (on the order of σ²/√N).
- Incomplete data recovery: Using multiple operators or signal invariance (equivariance), SSL can recover missing information in many-to-one inverse problems, with guarantees under low-dimensional signal priors.
- Practical performance: Across accelerated MRI, cryo‑EM, and SAR, SSL attains results within a few dB of the typical 6 dB PSNR gain that supervised deep learning enjoys over TV; sample complexity is moderate (SURE needs ~3× more unlabeled data than labeled).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Pseudo‑labels from independent noisy samples (Noise2Noise & Recorrupted2Recorrupted)
- **Claim**: A reconstruction network can be trained to achieve unbiased performance relative to supervised learning without any clean data, by using two independent noisy observations of the same underlying signal.
- **Mechanism**: The loss ∥f(y₁)−y₂∥² is conditionally unbiased for the supervised ℓ₂ loss when y₁ is independent of y₂ given x and both have zero‑mean noise. If only one measurement y is available but the noise distribution is known (e.g., Gaussian), synthetic second samples can be generated by adding calibrated noise, yielding the Recorrupted2Recorrupted (R2R) loss. The expected loss matches that of a supervised learner.
- **Core assumption**: Independent noise realizations or full knowledge of the noise law to simulate them.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract]: "with fully known noise (e.g., R2R, SURE) they offer unbiased estimation of the supervised loss"  
  - [Section 2.1]: introduces Noise2Noise for independent pairs; [Section 2.2.1] derives R2R for Gaussian noise.  
  - [corpus]: The neighbor paper *Learning to Reconstruct Signals From Binary Measurements* (arxiv:2303.08691) presents a similar unbiased construction for binary operators.
- **Break condition**: The unbiasedness holds only if the synthetic noise matches the true noise statistics. Misspecifying the noise level or distribution can cause significant performance degradation (see Figure 2.2).

### Mechanism 2: Stein’s Unbiased Risk Estimate (SURE) and divergences
- **Claim**: By penalizing the divergence of the network output with respect to its input, one can construct a self‑supervised loss that is an unbiased proxy for the supervised loss *without* needing a second noisy copy or explicit resampling.
- **Mechanism**: Stein’s lemma gives the identity E_y|x[f(y)ᵀ(y−x)] = E_y[trace(∂f/∂y Σ)]. Substituting into the difference between the observed loss and the supervised loss yields the SURE formula L_SURE = ∥f(y)−y∥² + 2σ² trace(∂f/∂y) − nσ² (Gaussian case). The constant ensures that minimizing the expected SURE is equivalent to minimizing the supervised ℓ₂ risk.
- **Core assumption**: Exact parametric knowledge of the noise distribution (e.g., Gaussian with known covariance) and differentiability of the network.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract]: "SURE [...] offer unbiased estimation"  
  - [Section 2.2.2]: detailed derivation, extensions to Poisson (PURE) and mixed noise.  
- **Break condition**: The divergence term must be estimated accurately (e.g., via Monte‑Carlo or Hutchinson’s estimator). Unreliable estimation adds variance; moreover, if the noise model is unknown, the basic SURE becomes biased – remedies like UNSURE exist (see Mechanism 3).

### Mechanism 3: Learning from incomplete measurements via multiple operators or equivariance
- **Claim**: Even when the forward operator A is many‑to‑one (e.g., missing pixels, subsampled Fourier), self‑supervised learning can recover signals by either exploiting variations of A across samples or assuming invariance of the signal distribution under a group of transformations.
- **Mechanism**:  
  - *Multiple operators*: If the dataset contains measurements taken with several different operators {A_g}, each with a different nullspace, a splitting loss (MSPLIT) that predicts the missing subset of one operator using a non‑overlapping split of another yields an unbiased estimate of a weighted supervised loss provided a matrix Q_A1 is invertible (Section 3.1).  
  - *Equivariance*: If the signal distribution is invariant under a group {T_g}, then the combined operator A∘T_g provides virtual observations. An equivariant reconstructor that respects f(y,AT_g)=T_g⁻¹f(y,A) together with a splitting or consistency loss (EI) can learn the mapping in the nullspace (Section 3.2).  
- **Core assumption**: Either i) the set of operators has sufficient diversity and the statistics E[AᵀA] are invertible, or ii) the signal distribution possesses the required invariance and A is not itself equivariant to the full group (so that virtual measurements differ).
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract]: "For incomplete measurements, SSL exploits multiple operators or signal invariance (equivariance) to recover nullspace information"  
  - [Section 3.1] and [Section 3.2] formalize the two approaches.  
- **Break condition**: If all operators share the same nullspace (e.g., A equivariant to the group), or the invariance assumption does not hold in reality, the method fails to recover the missing components.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Inverse problem formulation (forward model and noise)**  
  Why needed: All self-supervised losses are derived from the observation model y = A(x) + ε. Understanding the operator A and the noise ε is essential to choose the appropriate loss (e.g., SURE for Gaussian, R2R for exponential family, splitting for unknown separable noise).  
  Quick check: Write down the forward model for accelerated MRI with a Cartesian mask and Rician noise.

- **Concept: Stein’s lemma and Tweedie’s formula**  
  Why needed: These statistical identities underpin unbiased risk estimation (SURE) and connect optimal denoisers to the score ∇log p_y(y). They appear in Algorithms 1 and 2 of the manuscript.  
  Quick check: State Stein’s lemma for a Gaussian vector y ~ N(x, σ²I) and a differentiable f. What does the RHS represent?

- **Concept: Equivariance and group invariance**  
  Why needed: For many‑to‑one A, equivariance of the reconstruction function and invariance of the signal prior are the only ways to learn in the nullspace. The EI and ESPLIT losses rely on this concept.  
  Quick check: If the signal distribution is translation invariant and A is a random mask operator, why does A break equivariance? How does this help?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  - Input module: Raw measurements y (and optionally operator specifications such as mask or kernel).  
  - Reconstruction network: Typically a U‑Net or ResNet; may incorporate a back‑projection layer A† for invertible A, or unrolled optimization steps.  
  - Self‑supervised loss: One of L_N2N, L_R2R, L_SURE, L_MSPLIT, L_EI etc., possibly with a random masking or transformation sampler.  
  - Training loop: For each batch, sample measurements (and optionally operators), generate auxiliary data (noisy pairs, splitted data), compute loss, back‑propagate.  
  - Test‑time: Apply the learned f directly; for R2R or splitting losses, optionally average over multiple synthetic perturbations.

- **Critical path**:  
  1. Data preparation: store measurement vectors y (and the forward operator A or its parameters).  
  2. Loss setup: choose a self‑supervised loss consistent with the noise model and measurement setup; if needed, implement Jacobian estimation (Hutchinson or finite differences) for SURE.  
  3. Network initialization: f initialized (U‑Net common).  
  4. Optimization: use Adam or SGD; monitor the self‑supervised loss on a validation split without ground truth.  
  5. Model selection: select the checkpoint with lowest validation self‑supervised loss.  
  6. Inference: for incomplete problems, if using splits, run f on J different perturbations and average as in Equation (2.23).

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Noise knowledge: Full knowledge enables SURE or R2R → unbiased but requires accurate parameters; partial knowledge forces constrained losses (UNSURE, CV) → sub‑optimal but robust.  
  - Multiple operators vs. equivariance: Multiple operators need diverse training data (e.g., different masks) and operator conditioning; equivariance requires carefully chosen group (e.g., translations) and may impose architectural constraints (blind‑spot networks or structured splits).  
  - Memory vs. accuracy: SURE requires Jacobian traces; auto‑diff with Hutchinson adds little overhead but Monte‑Carlo variance; finite‑difference approximation doubles forward passes.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Increasing validation loss while training loss decreases → overfitting (stop early).  
  - Noise2Self/CV methods failing to reconstruct edges → derivative constraints too strong for sparse signals.  
  - SURE producing biased reconstructions when noise covariance misspecified → check noise calibration.  
  - EI/EspLIT not improving over back‑projection → either the invariance assumption is violated or the group is too small (e.g., using rotations for a blur that is not rotation‑equivariant).

- **First 3 experiments** (for a new practitioner):  
  1. Gaussian denoising (known σ) – Implement SURE with the Monte‑Carlo trace estimator, compare against supervised baseline and Noise2Noise (generate two noisy copies). Expected result: SURE converges to near‑supervised performance; verification via Figure 2.2.  
  2. Image inpainting with random masks – Train a U‑Net with the splitting loss (MSPLIT) using two non‑overlapping masks per image. Test on held‑out masks; performance should match supervised if enough operator diversity.  
  3. Equivariant recovery in accelerated MRI – Use an unrolled network with Euclidean shifts as the group; enforce equivariance by averaging outputs over shifted measurements. Compare with a non‑equivariant baseline to see nullspace recovery (see Figure 3.3).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The survey identifies the following open challenges for SSL in inverse imaging:
- **Handling non-linear forward operators**: Most existing SSL theory assumes linear forward models; extending to non-linear operators (e.g., phase retrieval, blind deconvolution) requires new theoretical foundations.
- **Incorporating generative modeling**: Leveraging deep generative models (e.g., GANs, diffusion) within SSL to capture complex signal distributions is an promising but underexplored direction.

## Limitations
- Noise assumptions: SSL losses require exact noise distribution and independence; real-world noise (e.g., Rician, correlated) may violate these assumptions, leading to bias.
- Incomplete measurements: Theoretical guarantees for multiple operators/equivariance rely on low-dimensional signal models and operator diversity, which may not hold for complex natural signals.
- Reproducibility: The survey lacks standardized specifications (architectures, hyperparameters, mask patterns, test-time averaging), resulting in heuristic choices that affect performance and fair comparison.
