# AGRI-Fidelity: Evaluating the Reliability of Listenable Explanations for Poultry Disease Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18247
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18247
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: AGRI-Fidelity introduces a tiered cross-model consensus and cyclic temporal permutation method to compute empirical False Discovery Rate (FDR), enabling reliable separation of genuine bioacoustic disease cues from spurious stationary artifacts in poultry and swine audio.

## Executive Summary
Listenable explanations for animal disease detection models must reliably identify biologically relevant audio cues, but existing XAI metrics for audio fail to distinguish true signals from stationary artifacts like ventilation noise because they assess single-model faithfulness. AGRI-Fidelity addresses this by combining cross-model consensus across a committee of near-optimal models with cyclic temporal permutation to construct a null distribution, yielding an empirical False Discovery Rate (FDR). Low FDR indicates reliable, time-localized bioacoustic markers, while high FDR suppresses continuous artifacts. Evaluated on poultry and swine datasets, AGRI-Fidelity successfully separates genuine cues from spurious ones, outperforms masking-based metrics (AI, AD, AG), and provides orthogonal fidelity and reliability scores that expose shortcut learning in noisy environments.

The framework is explainer-agnostic and operates on binary importance masks derived from any attribution method. By jointly assessing statistical reliability (via FDR) and causal impact (via fidelity), AGRI-Fidelity offers a more robust evaluation of explanation quality in challenging real-world bioacoustic settings.

## Method Summary
AGRI-Fidelity evaluates explanation reliability by aggregating binary importance masks from a committee of *K* near-optimal classification models. First, each model’s mask (e.g., from Integrated Gradients) is generated and thresholded to retain the top-τ fraction of salient time-frequency pixels. Observed consensus *S_obs* is the average agreement across models; it is stratified into tier-specific masks *C_λ* for discrete λ values (e.g., 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0). To determine whether consensus arises from spurious stationary artifacts, a null distribution is built by independently cyclically shifting each model’s mask in time (Δₖ ~ uniform) and recomputing consensus for *B* iterations. The empirical False Discovery Rate for tier λ is *FDR(λ) = (E[N_null(λ)] + 1) / (N_obs(λ) + 1)*. Fidelity measures the drop in target-class confidence when masking *C_λ* regions. The final reliability score combines low FDR and high fidelity (e.g., 1 - FDR). The process decouples artifact-driven from genuinely useful explanations, with the permutation test providing statistical validation under stationarity assumptions.

## Key Results
- AGRI-Fidelity reliably separates genuine bioacoustic disease cues from stationary artifacts in poultry and swine datasets, overcoming limitations of single-model XAI metrics.
- It outperforms existing masking-based metrics (AI, AD, AG) and provides orthogonal fidelity and reliability scores that expose shortcut learning in noisy environments.
- The method’s permutation-based FDR provably suppresses strictly stationary artifacts while preserving sparse time-localized signals.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Tiered cross-model consensus across diverse architectures filters out model-specific and artifact-driven attributions, isolating features stable across the Rashomon set.
- **Mechanism:** For a committee of *K* near-optimal models, binary importance masks *E₁…Eₖ* are generated. Pixel-wise agreement *S_obs(t,f)* is computed and stratified into discrete tiers *C_λ* (e.g., λ = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0 for K=4). This preserves agreement granularity, distinguishing weak from robust consensus.
- **Core assumption:** Task-relevant bioacoustic cues (e.g., coughs, clucks) will elicit consistent, time-localized attributions across models with different inductive biases, whereas stationary artifacts (e.g., ventilation hum) may also show consensus but will be statistically distinguishable via permutation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...combines cross-model consensus with cyclic temporal permutation to construct null distributions and compute a False Discovery Rate (FDR), suppressing stationary artifacts while preserving time-localized bioacoustic markers."
  - [section 4.1] "...stratify S_obs into level-specific binary masks C_λ(t, f)... This tiered construction preserves the distinction between weak and strong inter-model agreement..."
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence is weak; neighboring papers discuss model consensus (e.g., CAMO [28]) but do not address tiered stratification for artifact suppression.
- **Break condition:** Fails if spurious cue is transient/time-localized (e.g., sensor click) rather than stationary; such artifacts may align with true signals under temporal permutation, yielding low FDR.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Cyclic temporal permutation constructs a valid null distribution that asymptotically yields FDR→1 for continuous stationary artifacts, while sparse time-locked signals yield FDR≈0.
- **Mechanism:** Each model's binary mask *Eₖ* is independently shifted along the time axis by a random offset Δₖ ~ U{0, ..., T-1}. The null consensus *S_null(b)* is recomputed for *B* iterations. The Empirical FDR for tier λ is the ratio of average null-positive pixels to observed positives. Stationary artifacts are invariant to shift → high null agreement → high FDR. Sparse signals lose alignment → low FDR.
- **Core assumption:** Stationary artifacts are time-invariant over the recording duration; genuine physiological events (coughs, squawks) are brief and temporally sparse (τ ≪ T).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...cyclic temporal permutation to construct null distributions and compute a False Discovery Rate (FDR)..."
  - [section 5.1] "We prove that the cyclic-shift null model (i) provably suppresses strictly stationary artifacts by yielding maximal False Discovery Rate (FDR) of 1.0..."
  - [section 5.2, Theorem 1] Formal proof that for time-invariant masks, E[N_null(λ)] = N_obs(λ) → FDR=1.
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; permutation tests are common in bioacoustics for noise modeling but not for explanation evaluation.
- **Break condition:** Fails if spurious cue is non-stationary but temporally correlated (e.g., periodic machinery cycle shorter than shift distribution), or if true signals have duration comparable to T (ρ not ≪ 1).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Joint evaluation of low FDR (statistical reliability) and high fidelity (causal impact) yields a reliability score that decouples artifact-driven from genuinely useful explanations.
- **Mechanism:** For each consensus tier λ, compute fidelity as the drop in target-class confidence when masking *C_λ* regions. A reliable feature must have FDR(λ) low (consensus not due to chance) *and* high fidelity (masking harms prediction). The final reliability score is typically 1 - FDR (or a joint function).
- **Core assumption:** An explanation is trustworthy only if the highlighted regions both (a) are consistently identified across models *and* (b) are causally necessary for the specific prediction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...integrates fidelity with tier-specific FDR analysis, ensuring consensus reflects statistically validated causal structure..."
  - [section 4.3] "A feature is reliable only if it satisfies both low FDR (statistical legitimacy of agreement) and high fidelity (causal impact on prediction)."
  - [section 7.1] "AGRI-Fidelity decouples causal influence from statistical reliability... AudioLIME achieves highest fidelity (0.9157) but lower reliability (0.3987), whereas CoughLIME shows inverse pattern."
  - [corpus] Corpus lacks evidence; related work separates fidelity/robustness but not via cross-model FDR.
- **Break condition:** Fails if fidelity metric is misestimated (e.g., OOD artifacts from zero-masking) or if committee models share a common but irrelevant bias not captured by stationarity (e.g., all use same frequency band for normalization).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Model Multiplicity (Rashomon Effect)**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's core premise is that single-model faithfulness metrics are insufficient because multiple near-optimal models can rely on different, potentially spurious features. Understanding that a committee of models can expose shortcuts is essential.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might two classifiers with similar accuracy produce completely different time-frequency explanations for the same poultry cough sound?

- **Concept: False Discovery Rate (FDR) in Explanation Validation**
  - *Why needed here:* AGRI-Fidelity's reliability hinges on FDR to quantify whether observed cross-model consensus exceeds what would occur by random temporal alignment. The engineer must grasp that FDR here is an empirical, permutation-based significance measure, not the classic multiple-testing definition.
  - *Quick check question:* In the cyclic permutation null model, what does an FDR of 0.9 for a consensus tier imply about the explanations in that region?

- **Concept: Stationary vs. Sparse Signals in Bioacoustics**
  - *Why needed here:* The theoretical guarantees depend on a clear distinction: continuous background noise (stationary) should be suppressed, while brief animal vocalizations (sparse) should be preserved. This distinction drives the permutation strategy.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the sparsity ratio ρ = τ/T affect the expected FDR for a perfectly aligned sparse signal according to Theorem 2?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  **Explainer Front-end:** Accepts pre-trained classification models *M₁...Mₖ* and an input spectrogram *X*. Generates binary importance masks *Eₖ* for each model (e.g., via Integrated Gradients, thresholded).
  2.  **Consensus Engine:** Computes observed consensus *S_obs* and stratifies into tiered masks *C_λ* for λ ∈ Λ (e.g., {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0} for K=4).
  3.  **Permutation Null Builder:** For *B* iterations, applies independent cyclic time shifts Δₖ to each *Eₖ*, recomputes null consensus *S_null(b)*, and stratifies into *C_null,λ(b)*.
  4.  **FDR Calculator:** Aggregates null positives and observed positives per tier to compute FDR(λ) = (E[∑ C_null,λ] + 1) / (∑ C_λ + 1).
  5.  **Fidelity Scorer:** For each tier λ, masks *C_λ* from *X*, runs forward pass(es) through the committee models (or a held-out model) to compute class confidence drop.
  6.  **Reliability Integrator:** Combines FDR(λ) and Fidelity(λ) into a final reliability score (e.g., mean of (1-FDR) * fidelity, or per-tier product). Outputs time-frequency maps of reliability and component scores.

- **Critical path:** Input Spectrogram → (1) Masks per Model → (2) Observed Consensus & Tiering → (3) Null Consensus via *B* Shifts → (4) FDR per Tier → (5) Fidelity per Tier → (6) Reliability Score. The permutation loop (step 3) is the computational bottleneck.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **B (Permutation count):** Larger *B* yields smoother FDR estimates but increases runtime O(B·K·T·F). Practical values 100-1000.
  - **Tier set Λ:** Finer granularity (e.g., include 0.33, 0.66 for K=3) provides more sensitivity but increases multiple-testing burden and noise.
  - **Fidelity implementation:** Computing fidelity on the original *M* vs. a separate validation model: using *M* tests whether the model relies on its own attributions; using a separate committee model tests whether the *consensus* is relevant to any near-optimal model.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High standard deviation in Reliability** across samples for same explainer/class → indicates consensus instability, possibly due to non-stationary artifacts or explainer stochasticity (see LIME in Table 1).
  - **FDR ≈ 0 but low Fidelity** → consensus on irrelevant but aligned features (e.g., all models highlight a quiet region).
  - **FDR ≈ 1 across all tiers** → no consensus structure; either all explanations are random or everything is a stationary artifact.
  - **Discrepancy between FDR trend classes:** In Table 3, "Near-1" trend for Unhealthy suggests artifact dominance; if this misaligns with domain knowledge, check if spurious cue is transient.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Spurious Artifact Rejection:** Run on "Poultry Spurious" dataset (injected high-freq noise). Verify AGRI-Fidelity assigns lower reliability to explanations for Unhealthy class vs. Denoised dataset, while masking metrics (AI, AD, AG) show minimal change.
  2.  **FDR Trend Analysis per Class:** For a given explainer, compute per-sample FDR curves (across λ). Expect "Downward" trend (FDR drops as λ increases) for genuine signals and "Near-1" trend for stationary artifacts. Validate with labeled synthetic/controlled data if available.
  3.  **Committee Architecture Robustness:** Train 4 committee models (CNN, LSTM, MLP, ResNet) on same dataset. Compare AGRI-Fidelity reliability distributions for a fixed explainer across committees. Expect consistent directionality (e.g., high-reliability samples remain high) despite architectural differences, as stated in conclusions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**  
How can standardized benchmarks with known spurious correlations be developed to calibrate and validate AGRI-Fidelity's False Discovery Rate estimates?  
*Basis in paper:* The paper states in Future Work: "develop statistically characterized artifact benchmarks to enable calibrated p-value estimation and principled rejection of unreliable consensus regions." Also, in Limitations: "lack of standardized spurious-correlation benchmarks in animal disease audio."  
*Why unresolved:* Current datasets lack ground-truth annotations for spurious artifacts, hindering assessment of whether AGRI-Fidelity correctly identifies unreliable explanations.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Creation of synthetic or controlled datasets with injected spurious artifacts of known characteristics, followed by systematic evaluation of AGRI-Fidelity's detection accuracy across diverse conditions.

**Open Question 2**  
What modifications to AGRI-Fidelity's null construction are necessary to suppress transient, time-localized shortcuts that evade temporal permutation?  
*Basis in paper:* In Limitations: "cyclic temporal permutation... remains vulnerable to transient, time-localized shortcuts (e.g., sensor clicks or microphone disturbances)."  
*Why unresolved:* The current method assumes stationary artifacts; transient shortcuts may not be suppressed by time shifts, risking false positives in reliability assessment.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Designing and implementing multi-axis permutations (e.g., frequency-axis or time-frequency joint shifts) or integrating anomaly detection, then testing on datasets with injected transient artifacts to measure suppression efficacy.

**Open Question 3**  
How can adaptive multi-resolution modeling preserve high-frequency diagnostic cues in poultry vocalizations while handling heterogeneous sampling rates from farm sensors?  
*Basis in paper:* Future Work: "investigate adaptive multi-resolution modeling to preserve clinically meaningful spectral information while maintaining deployment practicality."  
*Why unresolved:* Standard resampling may discard important high-frequency signals, and no adaptive method is proposed to handle varying rates without information loss.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Developing algorithms that process audio at multiple resolutions or learn sampling-rate invariant features, validated on datasets with diverse sampling rates and preserved disease detection accuracy.

**Open Question 4**  
What are the optimal criteria for selecting a committee of near-optimal models to maximize AGRI-Fidelity's discriminative power between genuine signals and artifacts?  
*Basis in paper:* The methodology uses a set M of near-optimal models but does not define how many models (K) or what diversity in architectures is required; experiments use K=4 without justification.  
*Why unresolved:* The choice of committee size and model diversity could affect consensus stability and FDR calculations; current implementation is ad-hoc and may not generalize.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic experiments varying K, model architectures, and performance homogeneity to identify configurations that yield the most reliable AGRI-Fidelity scores, potentially correlated with external biological validation.

## Limitations
- Relies on unspecified hyperparameters (binarization threshold τ, permutation count B, tier set Λ) that significantly affect reliability estimates and require careful tuning.
- Methodology assumes artifact stationarity; transient non-stationary shortcuts (e.g., sensor clicks) may evade detection and yield falsely low FDR.
- Strong dependence on committee diversity; low architectural heterogeneity can produce spurious consensus on artifacts, undermining the cross-model filtering premise.
