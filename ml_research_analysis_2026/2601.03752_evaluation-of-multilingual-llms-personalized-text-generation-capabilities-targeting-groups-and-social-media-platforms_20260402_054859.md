# Evaluation of Multilingual LLMs Personalized Text Generation Capabilities Targeting Groups and Social-Media Platforms
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.03752
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03752
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** Platform-targeted text personalization is easier to achieve than group-targeted personalization and significantly reduces detectability by multilingual AI detectors.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a large-scale evaluation of how well 16 multilingual large language models (LLMs) can generate personalized text for specific demographic groups and social media platforms across 10 languages. The study measures both the quality of personalization (via three LLM judges) and the resulting text's detectability as AI-generated (via three multilingual detectors). Key findings indicate that tailoring text to a platform's style is easier and more effective at evading detection than tailoring to a demographic group. English outputs achieved the highest personalization quality, but well-personalized texts in any language were generally less detectable. The Gemma-3 model family demonstrated superior personalization capability, while smaller models faced significant challenges in non-English languages.

## Method Summary
The study constructed 1,080 unique prompts combining 6 target groups, 3 platforms, and 60 topics in 10 languages. These were used to generate 17,280 texts from 16 different LLMs. Personalization quality was meta-evaluated on a 4-point scale by three LLM judges (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Llama-3.1-405B). Detectability was measured by the True Positive Rate (TPR) of three multilingual AI detectors (GPTZero, OpenAI text classifier, DetectGPT). The analysis compared personalization success across languages, models, and target types (group vs. platform), and correlated quality scores with detector TPR.

## Key Results
*   **Platform > Group Personalization:** Achieving high-quality personalization for social media platforms (e.g., "Twitter-style") was consistently easier and more successful than for demographic groups (e.g., "for teenagers").
*   **Quality Reduces Detectability:** Texts judged as higher quality personalization showed significantly lower detectability (TPR). Platform personalization drove the largest drop, from 0.93 to 0.67 for top-quality texts.
*   **Model & Language Gaps:** Gemma-3-1B-it and Gemma-3-27B-it led in personalization capability. Smaller models (e.g., Phi-3) struggled markedly in non-English languages, and Ukrainian text was the hardest for detectors to identify overall.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism enabling effective platform personalization is the distinct, learnable stylistic and structural conventions of social media platforms (e.g., character limits, hashtag use, informal syntax). An LLM can more reliably mimic these concrete, surface-level patterns than the nuanced, heterogeneous, and often implicit linguistic and cultural markers defining a demographic group's "voice." This stylistic mimicry, when executed well, aligns the text more closely with natural human platform usage, thereby reducing the statistical or linguistic artifacts that AI detectors are trained to identify.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Multilingual Model Scaling:** Understanding how model parameter count and training data diversity affect performance stability across languages, especially for lower-resource languages.
    *   *Why needed:* To interpret why smaller models fail catastrophically outside English.
    *   *Quick check:* Compare log-perplexity or generation coherence scores across languages for a small vs. large model.
*   **Prompt Engineering for Persona Adoption:** The techniques and limitations of using prompts to force an LLM to adopt a specific persona (group member) versus a specific style (platform post).
    *   *Why needed:* The prompt design is the primary intervention; its formulation dictates what "personalization" means to the model.
    *   *Quick check:* Ablate prompt components (e.g., remove "in the style of" vs. remove "for a [group]") and measure change in judge scores.
*   **AI Detection Reliance on Stylometrics:** The principle that most AI detectors identify generation by spotting deviations from expected human stylistic norms and textual statistics.
    *   *Why needed:* Explains why better stylistic mimicry (platform personalization) fools detectors more effectively.
    *   *Quick check:* Analyze detector feature importance (e.g., burstiness, vocabulary richness) on high-quality vs. low-quality generated texts.
*   **LLM-as-a-Judge Validation:** The methodology and biases inherent in using powerful but non-human LLMs to evaluate subjective qualities like "personalization."
    *   *Why needed:* The core quality metric is itself a potentially biased LLM judgment.
    *   *Quick check:* Run a small human evaluation subset and calculate correlation with LLM judge scores.
*   **Cross-Linguistic Transfer of Detectors:** How detectors trained primarily on English data generalize to other languages' structural and cultural norms.
    *   *Why needed:* Addresses the surprising result of Ukrainian text being hardest to detect.
    *   *Quick check:* Train a monolingual detector on Ukrainian human/AI text and compare TPR to the multilingual suite.

## Architecture Onboarding
The study's evaluation pipeline can be viewed as a multi-component architecture for stress-testing personalization.
*   **Component Map:** Prompt Template Generator -> Target Language/Group/Platform Selector -> LLM Generator -> LLM Judge (Quality Scorer) -> Multilingual AI Detector Suite -> Detectability Scorer (TPR Calculator) -> Analysis Engine.
*   **Critical Path:** The sequence from specific prompt construction (group/platform/topic/language) through generation to final scoring (quality + detectability) is the critical path. Any bias or limitation in prompt design propagates through all downstream results.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** 
    *   *Breadth vs. Depth:* The study prioritizes breadth (16 models, 10 languages, 1,080 prompts) over depth (e.g., extensive human validation, probing a single model's internal mechanics).
    *   *Automated vs. Human Evaluation:* Fully automated evaluation (LLM judges, detectors) enables scale but introduces risks of circularity and cultural bias.
    *   *Synthetic vs. Real-World:* Synthetic prompts offer control but lack the complexity of genuine disinformation campaigns, threatening ecological validity.
*   **Failure Signatures:** 
    *   A model scoring high on personalization quality but also high on detectability indicates a failure in the core hypothesis (style mimicry evades detectors).
    *   Consistently low scores across all models for a specific language indicates a systemic issue with multilingual capability or judge/detector bias for that language.
    *   High variance between the three LLM judges for the same text suggests the "quality" metric is unstable.
*   **First Three Experiments:**
    1.  **Prompt Ablation Study:** Systematically remove elements of the prompt (e.g., platform name, group descriptor, topic context) for a high-performing model (Gemma-3-27B-it) in English and one non-English language (e.g., Ukrainian) to isolate what drives the personalization signal for judges.
    2.  **Detector Feature Probing:** For the highest-quality platform-personalized vs. non-personalized texts in English and Ukrainian, extract the top-10 most influential features for each of the three detectors using a technique like Integrated Gradients. Compare feature distributions to understand *why* detectability drops.
    3.  **Judge-Detector Correlation Analysis:** For a stratified sample of 500 generated texts (balanced by language, model, target type), compute the correlation between the LLM judge's quality score and each detector's confidence score. A strong negative correlation supports the main claim; a weak or positive correlation invalidates it.

## Limitations
*   **Evaluation Metric Bias:** Personalization quality relies solely on three LLM judges, which may harbor cultural biases and lacks validation against human perception of personalization.
*   **Detector Suite Representativeness:** Detectability is measured using three specific multilingual detectors, which may not reflect the performance of current, more advanced, or specialized disinformation detection tools.
*   **Ecological Validity of Prompts:** The prompt design is synthetic and controlled, failing to capture the complexity, intent, and narrative structure of real-world disinformation campaigns.

## Confidence
*   Platform personalization is easier than group personalization: **Medium** (Based on automated LLM evaluation only).
*   English yields highest personalization quality but well‑personalized texts are less detectable: **Medium** (No human corroboration for "quality" or its link to detectability).
*   Ukrainian texts hardest to detect: **Low** (Likely influenced by uneven detector language coverage and training).
*   Platform personalization reduces detectability (TPR 0.93→0.67): **Medium** (Effect size is clear but dependent on the limited detector suite).
*   Gemma‑3 models lead in capability: **Medium** (Strong within this benchmark, but may not generalize to all personalization tasks).
*   Smaller models struggle in non‑English: **High** (Consistent with established scaling and multilingual performance literature).
