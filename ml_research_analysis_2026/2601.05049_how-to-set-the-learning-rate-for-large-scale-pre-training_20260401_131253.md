# How to Set the Learning Rate for Large-Scale Pre-training?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.05049  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05049  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: The Fitting Paradigm, using scaling laws to predict optimal learning rates, outperforms μTransfer-based Transfer on MMLU and CMMLU at scales up to 12B parameters and 500B tokens; module-wise tuning yields no significant gains, and modern features like QK-Norm undermine μTransfer's stability assumptions.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of optimally configuring learning rates for large-scale pre-training by introducing two paradigms: **Fitting**, which leverages scaling laws to model optimal learning rates as a function of model size and training data, drastically reducing hyperparameter search complexity; and **Transfer**, which extends μTransfer principles to Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures, incorporating depth, weight decay, and token horizon scaling. Through extensive experiments up to 12B parameters and 500B tokens, the authors find that Fitting consistently surpasses Transfer on downstream benchmarks like MMLU and CMMLU. Notably, fine-grained module-wise learning rate tuning provides no measurable benefit, and architectural innovations such as QK-Norm stabilize training in ways that invalidate core assumptions of μTransfer, suggesting that modern pre-training practices require rethinking of theoretical guidelines.

## Method Summary
The **Fitting Paradigm** trains small proxy models (500M–4B parameters) across a grid of learning rates on ~120B tokens, fits a quadratic loss-vs.-learning-rate curve to locate the optimum for each (model size, data) pair, and then fits a power-law scaling relation $L(D) = L_0 + A \cdot D^{-\gamma}$ to extrapolate to larger data regimes. This yields a global scaling law $\eta(N,D) = 38.46 \cdot N^{-0.22} \cdot D^{-0.35}$. The **Transfer Paradigm** searches hyperparameters on a 2B μP-proxy model and applies Complete-μP rules—including width/depth scaling, weight decay proportionality, and token horizon adjustments—to transfer these settings to larger target models. Both paradigms are evaluated on Qwen3-MoE architectures using the Warmup-Stable-Decay schedule, with final performance measured on MMLU and CMMLU after 500B tokens.

## Key Results
- At 4B scale after decay, Fitting achieves **43.51% MMLU** (1.28% absolute above Transfer's 42.23%) and **42.81% CMMLU** (2.23% above 40.58%).
- Module-wise learning rate tuning (per-module scaling) yields **no significant performance improvement** over a globally optimal learning rate.
- Modern architectural stabilizers like **QK-Norm maintain training stability** under standard parametrization, contradicting μTransfer’s assumption of inevitable “blow-up” in infinite-width limits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Scaling Laws for Search Factor Reduction
The Fitting Paradigm reduces hyperparameter search complexity from $O(n^3)$ to $O(n \cdot C_D \cdot C_\eta)$ by exploiting two predictive scaling laws. First, validation loss $L$ as a function of learning rate $\eta$ follows an invex profile well approximated by a quadratic $L(\eta) = L_{\min} + C \cdot (\log \eta - \eta_{\min})^2$, enabling accurate extraction of the optimal $\eta^*$ from a small number of training runs. Second, loss as a function of training tokens $D$ obeys a power law $L(D) = L_0 + A \cdot D^{-\gamma}$, allowing extrapolation to larger data regimes with minimal additional search. The combined law $\eta(N,D) = 38.46 \cdot N^{-0.22} \cdot D^{-0.35}$ emerges from fitting these relationships across scales.

*Break conditions*: Quadratic fits fail (R² < 0.95) if the learning rate search range is too narrow or training is unstable; power-law extrapolation fails when the proxy model is not near convergence within the fitting data range (e.g., extremely low LR like 8e-5).

### Module-Wise Tuning Ineffectiveness in MoE
Contrary to μP predictions, assigning different learning rates to individual modules (Embedding, LM Head, Router, Hidden) provides no tangible benefit in large-scale MoE pre-training. Under standard parametrization with modern stabilizing features (notably QK-Norm), the root-mean-square (RMS) of parameter updates remains uniformly around 0.2 across all modules throughout training, indicating balanced feature learning without any group being under-trained. This uniform update magnitude suggests that the “maximal feature learning” hypothesis—which posits that some modules require higher learning rates—does not hold for MoE architectures with QK-Norm at scale.

*Break condition*: Removing architectural stabilizers (e.g., ablating QK-Norm) re-introduces imbalance and instability, potentially resurrecting a need for module-wise tuning.

### Modern Architectures Undermine μTransfer Stability
μTransfer’s theoretical foundation relies on the property that under Standard Parametrization, certain internal states (e.g., attention logits) grow unbounded with model width in the infinite-width limit, necessitating specialized initialization and learning rate scaling. However, the presence of QK-Norm in modern Qwen3-MoE models suppresses this blow-up: embeddings, logits, and attention logits exhibit stable variation across widths. Ablation experiments confirm that removing QK-Norm reproduces the instability trends predicted by μTransfer. This indicates that μTransfer’s core stability guarantee is contingent on the absence of such normalizations, which are now commonplace, thus limiting the paradigm’s applicability to contemporary architectures.

*Break condition*: Future architectures that omit normalization layers or introduce new inductive biases could again alter infinite-width dynamics in unpredictable ways.

## Foundational Learning

- **Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) Schedule**:  
  *Why needed*: The paper’s experiments and scaling laws assume the WSD schedule (linear warmup → constant LR → decay), which is the industry standard for large-scale pre-training. The stable phase is critical for fitting the loss–LR quadratic and for the power-law data scaling.  
  *Quick check*: Why does WSD enable more predictable learning rate scaling compared to cosine annealing, and how does the duration of the stable phase affect sensitivity to the chosen learning rate?

- **Mixture of Experts (MoE) Sparse Activation**:  
  *Why needed*: The study uses Qwen3-MoE, where only a subset of experts activate per token. This sparse activation pattern influences gradient flow and may affect learning rate sensitivity differently than dense models.  
  *Quick check*: How does per-token expert routing change the effective learning rate dynamics for router parameters versus expert parameters?

- **Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) and μTransfer**:  
  *Why needed*: μTransfer is the primary baseline (Transfer Paradigm). Understanding its two preserved infinite-width properties—maximal feature learning and stable forward/backward signals—explains why it fails when modern stabilizers are present. Complete-μP extends these rules to depth scaling.  
  *Quick check*: What are the two infinite-width properties μP preserves, and how does Complete-μP transfer them when scaling model depth?

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map (plain text):
Model: Qwen3-MoE (total params: 550M–12B, active: 100M–1.3B, hidden size: 256–1280, layers: 3–30, 32 attention heads, 4 KV heads, intermediate size 768)
Optimizer: AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.95, ε=1e-8), weight decay 0.1
LR schedule: Linear warmup (1k steps) → constant LR stable phase → decay to 10% LR for last 100B tokens
Search space: η ∈ {8e-5, 1e-4, 3e-4, 5e-4, 8e-4, 1.5e-3, 2e-3}; init σ ∈ {0.0005–0.02}
Evaluation: MMLU (57 English subjects), CMMLU (67 Chinese subjects) via OpenCompass/LMDeploy

Critical path:
1. **Fitting Paradigm**: Train small proxy models (500M–4B) at multiple learning rates on ~120B tokens → fit quadratic $L(\eta)$ curves → extract optimal $\eta^*$ per $(N,D)$ → fit power-law $L(D)$ → extrapolate to target $D=500B$ → obtain global scaling law $\eta(N,D) = 38.46 \cdot N^{-0.22} \cdot D^{-0.35}$.
2. **Transfer Paradigm**: Search learning rate and initialization std on a 2B μP-proxy → apply Complete-μP rules (width/depth/weight decay/token horizon multipliers) to scale to target model → train from scratch.
3. **Comparison**: Train 4B and 12B models under both paradigms on 500B tokens → evaluate MMLU/CMMLU during stable and decay phases.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Fitting**: Requires training multiple scales and learning rates upfront (higher initial compute) but yields empirically optimal LR and superior final downstream performance (+1.28–2.23% absolute on MMLU/CMMLU).
- **Transfer**: Only a single proxy search is needed (theoretically cheaper) but accuracy degrades at extreme scales due to architectural shifts (e.g., QK-Norm) and data effects not captured by μP.
- **Module-wise tuning**: No gains observed; simplifies configuration to a global LR but may not generalize to non-MoE or highly customized architectures.

Failure signatures:
- Poor quadratic fit (R² < 0.95) → learning rate range too narrow or training unstable; expand search or verify convergence.
- Power-law extrapolation error (e.g., Figure 12, η=8e-5) → proxy model not near convergence within fitting tokens; increase $D_{\text{fit}}$ or use higher LR.
- μTransfer mismatch → large gap between predicted and empirical optimal LR; verify μP rules (initialization std, weight decay scaling), confirm QK-Norm presence, align token horizons.
- Training instability → “blow-up” in attention logits (rising std$(x_t - x_0)$ with width) indicates missing normalization or incorrect initialization.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Quadratic L(η) validation**: For each proxy size (500M, 1B, 2B, 3B, 4B), train at 7 learning rates to 120B tokens. Fit $L(\eta) = L_{\min} + C(\log \eta - \eta_{\min})^2$; ensure R² > 0.995 and a clear minimum.
2. **Power-law L(D) check**: Select one proxy (e.g., 2B) at its optimal $\eta$, train to 200B tokens. Fit the power-law $L(D)$ using only ≤120B-token data; predict loss at 200B. If prediction error > 0.05, adjust fitting range or increase $D_{\text{fit}}$.
3. **Module-wise search sanity check**: Using the global $\eta$ from step 1, greedily search separate learning rates for LM Head → Router → Hidden → Embedding. Compare final validation loss at 120B tokens to the global-LR baseline; if difference ≤ 0.01, conclude no benefit.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization across schedules and architectures**: How do the optimal learning rate relationships derived from the Fitting Paradigm generalize to other learning rate schedules (e.g., cosine annealing) and to dense (non-MoE) model architectures? The paper only evaluated WSD and MoE; broader applicability remains unverified.
2. **Extrapolation limits**: What are the practical extrapolation boundaries—in terms of maximum model size and training tokens—for which the Fitting Paradigm’s scaling law and Transfer Paradigm’s rules maintain accurate predictions? Experiments were capped at 12B parameters and 500B tokens; error characteristics beyond this scale are unknown.
3. **Theoretical characterization of module-wise failure**: Beyond QK-Norm, what specific architectural innovations or training dynamics cause the breakdown of module-wise learning rate tuning (μP principles) in large-scale pre-training, and can these be theoretically characterized? Only QK-Norm was explicitly ablated; other modern components (e.g., RMSNorm, SwiGLU, MoE router designs) may also play a role.

## Limitations
- Reproduction is hindered by undisclosed details of the InternLM2.5 corpus (deduplication/safety filters) and precise Qwen3-MoE implementation (router design, expert capacity, QK-Norm placement).
- Generalizability beyond Qwen3-MoE and the 500B-token regime is not established; results may not hold for dense transformers or multi-trillion-token training.
- The corpus analysis shows low citation impact for neighboring work, suggesting limited external validation of the paradigms.

## Confidence
- **Scaling Law Paradigm (Fitting)**: **High** – Strong internal empirical evidence (R² > 0.995 fits, clear extrapolation success) and a clear mechanistic reduction in search complexity.
- **Module-Wise Tuning Ineffectiveness**: **High** – Consistently negligible performance differences (ΔL ≤ 0.01) across scales, supported by stable update RMS norms and aligned with cited μP studies.
- **Architectural Undermining of μTransfer**: **Medium** – Ablation evidence (QK-Norm removal reproduces blow-up) is convincing for this architecture, but the claim that it invalidates μTransfer’s core assumptions may overreach without broader architectural testing.

## Next Checks
1. **Quadratic Fit Robustness**: Train a 2B proxy model with a narrower learning rate grid (e.g., 5 points within 1e-4 to 5e-4). Verify quadratic fit R² remains > 0.99; if it drops, the method’s sensitivity to search range is higher than claimed.
2. **Paradigm Generalization**: Apply both paradigms to a standard (non-MoE) Transformer at ~2B scale. If Fitting’s advantage persists, the result is not MoE-specific; if Transfer improves, the MoE architecture is a key confounder.
3. **QK-Norm Mechanism Isolation**: Train 1B models with identical data and hyperparameters except for QK-Norm (on/off). Measure the standard deviation of internal states (embeddings, attention logits) across width to quantify the exact stabilizing effect and its correlation with optimal LR divergence from μTransfer predictions.
