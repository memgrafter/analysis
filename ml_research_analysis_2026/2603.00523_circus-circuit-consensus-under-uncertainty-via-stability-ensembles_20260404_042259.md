# CIRCUS: Circuit Consensus under Uncertainty via Stability Ensembles

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00523
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00523
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** CIRCUS reframes circuit discovery as uncertainty quantification over pruning configurations, producing a consensus circuit that is ~40× smaller than the union while retaining comparable influence and achieving higher causal relevance in activation patching.

## Executive Summary
Mechanistic interpretability aims to reverse-engineer neural networks by identifying sparse causal subgraphs (“circuits”). However, the standard approach of pruning an attribution graph by cumulative-influence thresholds suffers from threshold dependence: different thresholds yield different circuits, with no principled way to separate robust structure from artifacts. CIRCUS addresses this by reframing circuit discovery as a problem of uncertainty over analysis choices. It applies a fixed set of non-nested pruning configurations to a single attribution graph, computes per-edge stability scores as empirical inclusion frequencies across views, and extracts a consensus circuit from edges that survive every configuration (s(e)=1). This yields a natural core/contingent/noise decomposition. Evaluated on Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.2-1B using CLT-based attribution, the consensus circuit is approximately 40 times smaller than the union of all configurations yet retains comparable Influence Retained (IR). It consistently outperforms influence-ranked and random baselines in win-rate evaluations (e.g., 47 out of 50 prompts against union-pruned circuits). Activation patching confirms the causal relevance of the consensus nodes, recovering 57% of an oracle intervention versus 20% for random controls. The method adds only 5.5% overhead beyond a single attribution run, making it a practical and robust solution to threshold dependence.

## Method Summary
CIRCUS operates on a precomputed attribution graph \(G = (V, E, W)\) for a given prompt and target token, typically produced by a cross-layer transcoder (CLT) and a path-patching attribution method. The analyst defines a family of \(B\) non-nested pruning configurations \(\{\pi_b\}\), each specifying a node cumulative-influence threshold \(\theta_n\) and an edge threshold \(\theta_e\). The configurations are designed such that their pruned edge sets are not subsets of one another (e.g., by varying thresholds in opposite directions). For each \(\pi_b\), the graph is pruned to retain only edges that satisfy both thresholds, yielding a view \(E^{(b)}\). The union of these sets \(E_{\text{union}}\) forms the candidate edge pool. For each edge \(e\) in the union, the stability score is computed as \(s(e) = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^B \mathbb{1}[e \in E^{(b)}]\). The strict consensus circuit \(C_1\) consists of edges with \(s(e)=1\). Optionally, a boosting layer can add a second‑tier circuit \(C_2\) from the residual graph if the core’s Influence Retained (IR) is low. The entire pipeline adds only about 5.5% computational overhead beyond the initial attribution because pruning is inexpensive compared to computing the attribution graph.

## Key Results
- The consensus circuit is ~40× smaller than the union of all configurations while retaining comparable Influence Retained (IR).
- In win‑rate evaluations, CIRCUS outperforms influence‑ranked and random baselines on 47/50 prompts (Gemma) and all 20 prompts (Llama) against union‑pruned circuits with non‑nested configs.
- Activation patching on held‑out prompts shows the consensus circuit recovers 57% of an oracle intervention, significantly above random controls (20%, p=0.0004) and matched‑influence controls.
- Stability scores exhibit a monotonic relationship with per‑edge influence, and the method incurs only 5.5% additional runtime beyond a single attribution run.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Reframing circuit discovery as an uncertainty quantification problem over analysis choices mitigates threshold dependence by converting analyst variability into a diagnostic score.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of a single arbitrary prune, CIRCUS applies a fixed set of *non-nested* pruning configurations (π₁...π_B) to one precomputed attribution graph. The variability in retained edge sets across these views is summarized per-edge as a stability score s(e) ∈ [0,1], which empirically quantifies how robustly an edge survives plausible threshold choices.
- **Core assumption:** Different non-nested configuration choices reveal distinct, complementary subsets of the underlying causal graph. The consensus of edges surviving *all* configurations (s(e)=1) isolates structure that is invariant to these analysis choices within the chosen family.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "reframes circuit discovery as a problem of uncertainty over explanations... assigns each edge an empirical inclusion frequency s(e)... and extracts a consensus circuit of edges present in every view."
  - [section 3.1] "s(e) = 1/B ∑ ⊮[e∈E(b)]... s(e) ∈ [0, 1]; s(e)=1 iff e appears in every view."
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on general ensembling/uncertainty but do not address *structural* uncertainty from pruning thresholds; CIRCUS's specific application to mechanistic circuits is novel.
- **Break condition:** If the chosen configuration family is poorly designed (e.g., nested, too narrow, or not crossing thresholds in opposite directions), the consensus may not represent robust structure but merely the intersection of similar views or an empty set.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The per-edge stability score s(e) correlates with causal salience, allowing a principled core/contingent/noise decomposition that separates robustly relevant edges from threshold-sensitive artifacts.
- **Mechanism:** Edges that frequently survive diverse pruning configurations (high s(e)) tend to be those with higher absolute influence (weight) in the full graph. This creates a monotonic relationship where strict-consensus edges (s=1) carry disproportionately high cumulative influence, making them strong candidates for a minimal "core" circuit.
- **Core assumption:** Within a family of non-nested pruning thresholds, the genuinely important causal pathways in the attribution graph are less sensitive to exact cutoff values than spurious or low-influence edges. Therefore, robustness across configurations is a proxy for importance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4, Fig 3] "Mean per-edge influence increases monotonically with stability: edges surviving all 25 configs carry ~70× higher mean influence than single-config edges."
  - [section 4, Table 1] Consensus circuits are ~40× smaller than the union while retaining comparable Influence Retained (IR).
  - [section 4, Fig 2] Activation patching shows consensus nodes recover 57% of oracle vs. 20% for random controls (p=0.0004), indicating causal relevance of high-s(e) edges.
- **Break condition:** The monotonic stability-influence relationship is empirical and specific to the models/prompts/CLTs tested. For different architectures or attribution methods, high-stability edges might not correspond to high-causal-salience edges, or the relationship could be non-monotonic.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The *non-nesting principle* ensures the consensus circuit is a genuinely new, aggregated explanation and not a trivial recovery of a single configuration's result.
- **Mechanism:** Configurations are designed so their pruned edge sets are not subsets of one another (e.g., by increasing node threshold while decreasing edge threshold, or vice versa). This forces each view to prune different graph regions. The intersection of all views (strict consensus) is therefore strictly smaller than any single view's edge set and cannot be obtained by simply choosing an extreme single configuration.
- **Core assumption:** Analyst choice uncertainty is meaningfully captured by varying two or more complementary threshold parameters in opposite directions. This creates "orthogonal" pruning pressure that reveals which edges are robust to *both* stringent node-level and edge-level filtering.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "If configurations are nested... then Cτ=1 = E(1)... We therefore require non-nested configurations, achieved by crossing node and edge thresholds in opposite directions."
  - [section 4, Table 1] "Match" column: With nested configs, consensus matches a single config on 50/50 (Gemma) and 20/20 (Llama) prompts. With non-nested configs (B=9), match drops to 8/50 and 0/20, confirming non-trivial consensus.
  - [section 4] "With non-nested B=9, mean pairwise Jaccard is 0.33 (Gemma) and 0.35 (Llama), confirming genuine view diversity."
- **Break condition:** If the configuration space is not thoroughly explored (e.g., B too small, thresholds not sufficiently separated), the consensus may still be dominated by a single view's structure, or critical robust edges might be missing if no configuration includes them due to an unlucky grid.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Mechanistic Interpretability (Circuit Discovery)**
  - *Why needed here:* CIRCUS operates on the output of a mechanistic interpretability pipeline: an attribution graph (edges = direct effects between features). Understanding the goal—finding a sparse, causal subgraph ("circuit") that explains a model's computation—is prerequisite.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the difference between an *attribution graph* and a *circuit* in this context?
- **Concept: Pruning by Cumulative Influence**
  - *Why needed here:* CIRCUS's inputs are pruned subgraphs derived from an attribution graph via cumulative-influence thresholds (node and edge). The paper's core problem is that the output circuit depends on these arbitrary thresholds.
  - *Quick check question:* How does increasing the node cumulative-influence threshold vs. the edge threshold affect the size and composition of a pruned circuit?
- **Concept: Stability Selection (Statistical Variable Selection)**
  - *Why needed here:* CIRCUS directly adapts the stability selection framework (Meinshausen & Bühlmann, 2010), replacing data subsampling with "config-bagging." Understanding the original goal (controlling false discoveries via selection frequency) clarifies what CIRCUS inherits and what formal guarantees it lacks.
  - *Quick check question:* Why does the paper state that CIRCUS's s(e) is *analogous* to a posterior inclusion probability but carries no formal FDR guarantee?
- **Concept: Non-Nested Sets**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire method hinges on generating views where pruned edge sets are not subsets of each other. This is a specific mathematical constraint on the configuration design.
  - *Quick check question:* Give an example of two pruning configurations (specifying node and edge thresholds) that would produce *nested* edge sets.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Attribution Graph (G):** Input. Directed weighted graph (V, E, W) from a CLT-based attribution method (e.g., circuit-tracer). Nodes = features/errors, edges = direct effects, weights = influence magnitude.
  - **Configuration Family ({πᵦ}):** A set of B pruning parameter pairs (node_thresh, edge_thresh). Must be designed to be non-nested (crossing thresholds).
  - **View Generator:** Applies each πᵦ to G, producing B pruned edge sets E^(b).
  - **Stability Scorer:** Computes s(e) = (1/B) * Σ [e ∈ E^(b)] for each edge e in the union of all views.
  - **Consensus Builder:** Filters edges by s(e) ≥ τ (typically τ=1) to output circuit C_τ. Also computes IR(C_τ).
  - **(Optional) Boosting:** Residual layer to add contingent edges (C₂) if core IR is low.
- **Critical path:** Attribution Graph → (for each config: prune → collect edges) → Compute s(e) for all union edges → Filter by τ=1 → Output C₁. The overhead is dominated by the B pruning operations (cheap, post-attribution).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Config Family Design:* Tradeoff between diversity (ensuring non-nesting) and meaningfulness (thresholds must still correspond to valid, interpretable prunes). A 5x5 grid as used is a heuristic.
  - *τ Selection:* Strict consensus (τ=1) maximizes robustness but may yield too small a circuit (low IR). Lower τ increases coverage but re-introduces threshold sensitivity. The stability-coverage curve (Fig 4) guides choice.
  - *Overhead vs. Benefit:* Adds ~5.5% runtime beyond one attribution run. Benefit is quantifiable robustness (smaller, more causally validated circuits) and a rejection taxonomy.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *High "Match" rate:* Consensus equals a single configuration on most prompts (Table 1). Indicates nested or insufficiently diverse configs.
  - *Low pairwise Jaccard similarity* between views (<0.3) might indicate configs are too extreme, pruning all common edges.
  - *Very small C₁ with IR << 0.5* suggests the config family is too stringent or the model's computation is inherently threshold-sensitive.
  - *Monotonicity violation* in Fig 3 (stability vs. influence) would challenge the core assumption.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Reproduce Stability-Coverage Tradeoff (B=25):** Implement the 5x5 threshold grid. For a set of prompts, compute s(e) and plot |C_τ| and IR(C_τ) vs. τ (Fig 4). Verify the elbow near τ=1 and tight bootstrap CIs.
  2.  **Validate Non-Nesting Principle:** Run with a *nested* config set (e.g., (0.6,0.95) ⊆ (0.8,0.98) ⊆ (0.9,0.99)) and a *non-nested* set (e.g., (0.6,0.99) vs (0.9,0.95)). Compute "Match" rate (fraction prompts where C₁ equals a single view). Expect near 100% match for nested, low match for non-nested (Table 1).
  3.  **Causal Validation via Activation Patching:** For C₁ nodes from a non-nested run, perform activation patching on a held-out set (source/target prompts with different answers). Measure recovery (logit shift toward source answer). Compare against two controls: random nodes (equal count) and matched nodes (equal count, similar total influence). Expect consensus recovery significantly higher than both (Fig 2, p<0.01).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does CIRCUS generalize to models >2B parameters and complex behaviors (reasoning, in-context learning)?
- **Basis:** [explicit] "Our experiments are limited to 1–2B models on short factoid prompts, and whether the stability structure generalizes to larger models or more complex behaviors remains open." (Sec 5, Conclusion)
- **Why unresolved:** Only two small models on simple factoid tasks were evaluated; larger models may have different circuit architectures, and complex behaviors could involve longer-range dependencies that affect stability patterns.
- **What evidence:** Apply the full CIRCUS pipeline to larger models (e.g., 10B, 70B) on tasks requiring multi-step reasoning or in-context learning. Compare core circuit size, influence retained (IR), and stability–coverage tradeoff curves to those observed here.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can CIRCUS be extended to provide formal statistical guarantees (e.g., false discovery rate control) similar to classical stability selection?
- **Basis:** [explicit] The Related Work and Section 3.2 note: "our deterministic configuration family does not satisfy [exchangeability], so s(e) is an empirical robustness score without formal guarantees."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper explicitly lacks FDR-type control because the configurations are not exchangeable; s(e) is only a conditional frequency, not a posterior inclusion probability, limiting its reliability for rigorous scientific claims.
- **What evidence:** Theoretical work to identify conditions under which a set of deterministic pruning thresholds yields exchangeable selection events or to derive concentration inequalities for s(e). Alternatively, empirical estimation of FDR on synthetic attribution graphs with known ground truth circuits.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How sensitive are stability scores and the core/contingent decomposition to the specific choice of configuration family? Can we optimize configuration selection to better distinguish robust from fragile edges?
- **Basis:** [inferred] The authors state the stability–coverage tradeoff is "specific to the chosen configuration family" (Fig. 4) and use a fixed 5×5 grid; no systematic exploration of design principles is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** Different threshold families might yield different s(e) distributions and consensus edges; the current non-nesting principle is necessary but not sufficient to guarantee informative diversity. Without guidance, analysts may arbitrarily pick thresholds that bias results.
- **What evidence:** Conduct an ablation varying threshold sets (range, density, random non-nested selections) and measure: consensus size, IR, monotonicity of stability vs. per-edge influence, and leave-one-out consistency. Identify families that maximize discriminative power and robustness across prompts.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the core/contingent/noise taxonomy align with human expert judgments of edge importance, and does it improve the efficiency of manual circuit interpretation?
- **Basis:** [inferred] The taxonomy is introduced as a principled decomposition, but all evaluations are automatic (IR, KL, patching). No user study or expert assessment of the output's interpretability is reported.
- **Why unresolved:** The ultimate goal of mechanistic interpretability is to aid human understanding; it is unknown whether stability-based tiering helps experts identify meaningful substructures or reduces cognitive load compared to traditional pruned graphs.
- **What evidence:** A controlled study with mechanistic interpretability researchers, comparing accuracy, time-to-insight, and confidence when analyzing circuits presented as (a) raw attribution graphs, (b) union-pruned circuits, and (c) CIRCUS core+contingent outputs. Collect qualitative feedback on the utility of the three-tier categorization.

## Limitations
- **Replication barriers:** Exact CLT checkpoints, attribution graph construction details, and the prompt datasets are unreleased, preventing full independent replication.
- **Underspecified configuration space:** The specific non-nested grid for B=9 is not provided, forcing implementers to design heuristic threshold sets that may not match the paper's.
- **Limited validation scope:** The core experiments are restricted to two small models (1–2B parameters) and short factoid prompts, using CLT-based attribution; performance on larger architectures or other attribution methods remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1** (Reframing as uncertainty quantification mitigates threshold dependence): **High**. Strong empirical evidence: non-nested configs yield non-trivial consensus (Match ≈ 0 for Gemma), and consensus is ~40× smaller than union while retaining comparable IR.
- **Claim 2** (Stability scores s(e) correlate with causal salience): **Medium**. Monotonicity shown and patching recovery > random/matched controls, but no formal guarantees; relationship is empirical.
- **Claim 3** (Non-nested principle ensures non-trivial consensus): **High**. Directly validated via Match rate experiments and pairwise Jaccard diversity.
- **Claim 4** (Findings generalize to other models/methods): **Low**. Tests only on Gemma-2-2B/Llama-3.2-1B with CLTs; no ablations on attribution method or model scale.
