# How Do Decoder-Only LLMs Perceive Users? Rethinking Attention Masking for User Representation Learning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10622
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10622
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** GG-SM achieves a new state average AUC of 0.7745 on 9 industrial user cognition benchmarks, outperforming causal, hybrid, and scheduler-only baselines.

## Executive Summary
Decoder-only large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used for user representation learning, but the impact of attention masking strategies on embedding quality remains underexplored. This paper introduces Gradient-Guided Soft Masking (GG-SM), a method that uses gradient norms during an initial warm-up phase to selectively allow future tokens to influence the current token’s representation, followed by a linear scheduler that smoothly transitions to fully bidirectional attention. The approach is evaluated on a large-scale Alipay user behavior dataset across nine real-world user cognition tasks, where GG-SM achieves state-of-the-art performance with an average AUC of 0.7745, while maintaining stable training dynamics.

## Method Summary
The method adapts a decoder-only LLM (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct) via LoRA to generate user embeddings. Input consists of heterogeneous user behaviors (e.g., bill payments, mini-program usage) encoded by modality-specific encoders (e.g., gte-base-zh) and concatenated into a single sequence with special tokens like `<USER>`. Training uses a contrastive InfoNCE loss with in-batch negatives and a margin mask to suppress false negatives. The key innovation is GG-SM: during a warm-up phase, future attention weights are set to σ(∥∇h_j L∥), where the gradient norm of each future token’s hidden state indicates its task relevance. After warm-up, a linear scheduler gradually blends these frozen gradient-derived weights with full bidirectionality, ensuring a smooth optimization trajectory. At inference, a fully bidirectional mask is used to obtain rich user embeddings.

## Key Results
- GG-SM attains an average AUC of 0.7745 across 9 industrial user cognition benchmarks, a new state-of-the-art.
- It consistently outperforms causal masking, hybrid static/dynamic masking (e.g., MLP or global-query variants), and a scheduler-only baseline.
- The training remains stable without loss spikes, and the resulting user embeddings exhibit stronger discriminative separation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Gradient norms during warm‑up provide a data‑driven signal to selectively open future attention to the most task‑relevant tokens, improving initial bidirectional adaptation.
- **Mechanism:** For each token position `j>i`, the soft mask weight `w_ij(t)` during warm‑up (`t < T_warm`) is set via `σ(∥∇h_j L∥)`. Higher gradient norms indicate tokens whose representation more strongly influences the current loss, granting them greater visibility ahead of the autoregressive boundary.
- **Core assumption:** Early in training, gradient magnitude correlates with token importance for the contrastive objective, and allowing selective future access to such tokens accelerates learning of bidirectional context.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract], [section 4]; neighboring works (Bitune, MAGNET) explore bidirectional augmentation but not gradient‑guided masking. The assumption that gradient‑based importance is novel in this context is supported by the lack of prior direct analogs.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A linear scheduler interpolating between the warm‑up mask and full bidirectionality ensures a smooth optimization trajectory, avoiding catastrophic interference from an abrupt mask change.
- **Mechanism:** After warm‑up, `w_ij(t)` is computed as `(1-α_t)·σ(∥∇h_j L_warm∥) + α_t`, where `α_t` linearly increases from 0 to 1. This gradually blends the learned importance prior with unrestricted attention.
- **Core assumption:** The representation space learned under the gradient‑guided mask is a good initialization for full bidirectionality; a gradual transition prevents destabilization.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract], [section 4], [section 6] (GG‑SM outperforms naive Bidirectional and Scheduler‑based methods, yielding sharper separation).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Freezing the gradient‑derived mask weights after warm‑up (`L_warm`) provides a stable, data‑specific prior that guides the later bidirectional phase, decoupling early importance estimation from later gradient noise.
- **Mechanism:** The term `∥∇h_j L_warm∥` uses the gradient norm computed at the final warm‑up step, not the instantaneous gradient during scheduling. This anchors the mask to a consolidated early‑learning signal.
- **Core assumption:** Early gradient statistics contain a robust signal about token relevance that should be preserved, not overwritten by later task‑specific fluctuations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4] (mask weights frozen after warm‑up), [section 6]; no direct analog in prior work; this design is presented as a key differentiator from a scheduler that adjusts based on ongoing gradients.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contrastive Learning with InfoNCE Loss**  
  - *Why needed here:* The training objective aligns user and answer embeddings by pulling positive pairs together and pushing apart negatives within a batch; this is what the attention masking strategies are ultimately optimizing for.  
  - *Quick check question:* How does the inclusion of "same-side" negative samples (other users/answers) in Eq. 7 affect the discriminative power of the learned embeddings?
- **Concept: Autoregressive vs. Bidirectional Attention Masking**  
  - *Why needed here:* GG‑SM transitions between strict left‑to‑right (causal) and all‑to‑all (bidirectional) connectivity; understanding this distinction is fundamental to grasping the core manipulation.  
  - *Quick check question:* Why is direct training with a full bidirectional mask from initialization potentially unstable for a decoder‑only LLM adapted from generative pretraining?
- **Concept: Gradient Norm as a Proxy for Parameter/Token Importance**  
  - *Why needed here:* GG‑SM’s novelty hinges on using `∥∇h_j L∥` to weight future attention; larger gradients indicate a token’s stronger influence on loss reduction.  
  - *Quick check question:* What are two potential pitfalls of using raw gradient norm as an importance metric during early training (before loss has meaningfully decreased)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Modality-specific encoders (e.g., `gte-base-zh`) → concatenated sequence → LLM backbone (`Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct`) with integrated soft mask generator → contrastive head → InfoNCE loss.

**Critical path:**  
User history logs are processed by modality encoders into token‑like embeddings, which are concatenated with special tokens into a single input sequence. During the forward pass, the LLM applies the soft attention mask `M_soft(t)`. The hidden state of the `<USER>` token is extracted, L2‑normalized, and used as the user embedding. The contrastive head computes similarity with answer embeddings; the InfoNCE loss (with in‑batch negatives and margin mask) is computed and backpropagated. During warm‑up, the gradient norms of future token hidden states are recorded to update `M_soft` for the next step.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Stability vs. speed:* GG‑SM adds warm‑up and gradient computation overhead but yields more stable convergence and better final AUC than a simple scheduler.  
- *Generative compatibility vs. representation quality:* Bidirectional masking gives best embeddings but breaks autoregressive inference; GG‑SM is a compromise that can revert to causal at inference if needed (paper uses full bidir at inference).  
- *Compute vs. data efficiency:* The frozen gradient‑derived prior adds minimal overhead, but the longer warm‑up phase consumes extra compute.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Training instability (loss spikes/NaN) during the scheduler phase if warm‑up is too short or gradient signal is chaotic.  
- Poor performance on some tasks (e.g., `Concert`) while others (e.g., `MAU`) excel, suggesting the gradient‑guided mask overfitted to dominant signal types in warm‑up batches.  
- No improvement over scheduler‑only baseline when `T_warm` is insufficient, indicating a noisy prior.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablation of Warm‑up Mechanism:** Compare GG‑SM against Scheduler‑only, Fixed Hybrid Mask, and Direct Bidirectional from step 0; measure loss stability and final average AUC.  
2. **Sensitivity to `T_warm`:** Vary warm‑up steps (0%, 5%, 10%, 20% of total steps) to identify optimal duration and trade‑offs.  
3. **Gradient Noise Robustness:** Add controlled noise to the gradient norms used in warm‑up (`∥∇h_j L + ε∥`) and observe performance degradation to assess robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does GG‑SM generalize to user behavior data from domains other than Alipay (e.g., e‑commerce, social media)? *Basis:* Study uses only Alipay data; cross‑domain validation is absent. *Why unresolved:* Behavior patterns, sparsity, and modality distributions vary across industries. *What evidence would resolve:* Replicate on publicly available or multi‑domain user behavior datasets and compare AUC gains and training dynamics.
- **Open Question 2:** Why do gradient norms from the loss function provide a more effective signal for soft masking compared to static or learned masking schemes (e.g., MLP or global‑query hybrids)? *Basis:* Paper empirically shows GG‑SM outperforms hybrid baselines but offers no deeper analysis. *Why unresolved:* Without understanding the correlation between gradient magnitudes and true token relevance, the method risks being heuristic. *What evidence would resolve:* Controlled experiments with synthetic data where token importance is known, measuring alignment between gradient norms and ground truth; ablation of gradient source.
- **Open Question 3:** How sensitive is GG‑SM to warm‑up duration (`T_warm`) and scheduler design (linear vs. non‑linear), and what are optimal settings for different model scales or data regimes? *Basis:* Paper uses fixed hyperparameters without sensitivity analysis. *Why unresolved:* Hyperparameters interact with data size, sequence length, and model capacity; suboptimal choices may destabilize training. *What evidence would resolve:* Hyperparameter sweeps across seeds, varying `T_warm` fraction and comparing scheduler types, then deriving robust recommendations.

## Limitations
- **Gradient Norm Validity:** Core assumption that early gradient magnitude robustly signals long‑term token importance lacks direct validation; noisy or misaligned gradients could mislead the mask.
- **Warm‑up Schedule Sensitivity:** Performance likely hinges on an optimal `T_warm`; too short yields a noisy prior, too long wastes compute and may overfit to transient patterns. Breakpoints are unknown.
- **Generalization Beyond Alipay:** Evaluation is restricted to nine proprietary Alipay tasks; effectiveness for other domains (e‑commerce, social networks) or larger model scales is unestablished.

## Confidence
- **Efficacy (AUC 0.7745):** High – based on reported internal industrial benchmarks with large‑scale data.
- **Mechanism (Gradient‑Guided Prior):** Medium – anchored in paper’s description but lacks direct ablation isolating the gradient signal from the scheduler.
- **Novelty (vs. Scheduler/Bitune):** Low – neighboring works (Bitune) explore bidirectional attention; GG‑SM’s specific gradient‑guided warm‑up is claimed but not contrasted in prior art.

## Next Checks
1. **Gradient Signal Ablation:** Replace `σ(∥∇h_j L∥)` with random uniform weights during warm‑up; if performance drops significantly, it validates the gradient’s role.
2. **Warm‑up Duration Sweep:** Systematically vary `T_warm` (e.g., 0%, 2%, 5%, 10% of `T_total`) and measure final AUC and convergence stability to identify sensitivity window.
3. **Linear Probe Fidelity Audit:** Replicate the exact linear evaluation protocol (random seeds, probe architecture, layers used) on a held‑out subset to confirm the reported AUC gap is reproducible and not an artifact of probe training.
