# From Questions to Trust Reports: A LLM-IR Framework for the TREC 2025 DRAGUN Track

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23125
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23125
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** The UR_Trecking system demonstrates that Chain-of-Thought query expansion and neural re-ranking significantly improve retrieval relevance and domain trustworthiness for news trust assessment, but LLM-based question generation and report synthesis show limited alignment with human rubrics.

## Executive Summary
The TREC 2025 DRAGUN Track tackles the challenge of assisting users in evaluating online news trustworthiness. This paper introduces UR_Trecking, an LLM-IR framework that automatically generates critical questions for a news article, retrieves supporting evidence from a large corpus, and synthesizes a trust report. The system leverages Chain-of-Thought query expansion to broaden search intents, a monoT5 cross-encoder for re-ranking, and domain trust scores to filter sources. Evaluation confirmed that CoT expansion combined with re-ranking substantially boosted both relevance and source credibility. However, generated questions had only moderate alignment with human assessor rubrics (low supportive scores), and final reports were of limited quality due to partial evidence coverage. Thus, the retrieval components are effective, but the generation components require further tuning.

## Method Summary
UR_Trecking operates as a multi-stage pipeline. Given a news article, GPT-4o-Nano generates 30 raw critical questions. These are filtered to remove compound or overly long questions, then clustered via K-means on embeddings to select 10 diverse questions. For each selected question, the query is expanded using one of four strategies (Baseline, Boolean, Chain-of-Thought, Structured). The expanded query is used with BM25 on the MS MARCO V2.1 corpus to retrieve 1000 passages. A monoT5 re-ranker reorders these passages. An LLM judge then labels each passage as relevant; passages from domains with a trust score ≥0.7 (from Lin et al.'s dataset) are retained. The final evidence set (top‑3 or top‑10) is passed to GPT‑4o‑Nano to synthesize Q&A pairs and a comprehensive trust report with citations.

## Key Results
- Chain-of-Thought query expansion with re-ranking significantly improved retrieval relevance and domain trustworthiness.
- Question generation achieved moderate overall scores but low supportive scores, indicating misalignment with human assessor priorities.
- Final report quality was limited; retrieved evidence often addressed article claims only partially.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Chain-of-Thought (CoT) query expansion improves retrieval relevance for news trustworthiness assessment.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM is prompted to reason step-by-step about the information needed to answer a critical question, generating expanded query terms that capture implicit aspects and missing facets of the information need beyond literal keyword matching.
- **Core assumption:** Reasoning‑oriented expansion exposes latent search intents related to source credibility, evidence, and context that are not fully represented in the original question text.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Chain-of-Thought query expansion with re-ranking significantly improved retrieval relevance"
  - [section 2.3] "CoT Expansion:... prompted a large language model to reason step-by-step about the information needed to answer a question and to generate expanded query terms accordingly."
  - [corpus] Neighbors discuss RAG for news trust (e.g., "Resources for Automated Evaluation of Assistive RAG Systems...") but provide no direct evidence for or against CoT expansion efficacy in this specific track.
- **Break condition:** If the LLM's reasoning degrades (e.g., hallucinates irrelevant aspects) or the expanded terms overly broaden the query, relevance could drop or noise could increase.

### Mechanism 2
- **Mechanism:** Neural re‑ranking (monoT5) applied to a large set of initially retrieved documents (k=1000) elevates passages that are both semantically relevant to the query and, as a side effect, originate from higher-trust domains.
- **Core assumption:** A cross‑encoder reranker fine‑tuned on MS MARCO passages promotes documents with strong lexical and semantic matching, which correlates with higher‑quality, more authoritative sources in the news domain.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "re-ranking substantially improved both relevance and domain trust"
  - [section 2.4] "mono T5 reranker... to rerank the first 1000 retrieved documents... Re-ranking consistently improved both relevance and domain trustworthiness"
  - [section 2.4] "domain-level trustworthiness dataset... assigned a continuous trustworthiness score to news and web domains"
- **Break condition:** If the reranker's training data (MS MARCO) is mismatched to the news trust domain, or if domain trust signals conflict with passage relevance, the re‑ranking could suppress relevant but lower‑trust sources needed for critical analysis.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** LLM‑based question generation and report synthesis currently show limited alignment with human assessor rubrics for trustworthiness.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM generates questions based on a prompt detailing trust principles (source bias, motivation, etc.). Later, an LLM synthesizes retrieved evidence into reports. Performance is moderate due to insufficient grounding in the specific assessment criteria used by human experts.
- **Core assumption:** Direct prompting of an LLM with general principles does not guarantee generation of questions that match the nuanced, priority‑weighted concerns (Have to Know vs. Nice to Know) captured in the assessor rubrics. Report synthesis similarly fails to optimally integrate retrieved evidence to address those specific rubric points.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "question-generation performance shows moderate quality with room for improvement... final report quality remained limited"
  - [section 4.2] "moderate overall qgen_scores... Low supportive scores, suggesting that many questions did not strongly align with what human assessors considered most helpful"
  - [section 4.3] "Supportive scores for these reports were generally low... retrieved evidence often addressed article claims only partially."
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; the claim is based solely on the paper's reported evaluation outcomes.
- **Break condition:** Without an iterative correction loop or explicit optimization for rubric coverage (as noted in the paper's discussion), generation components are unlikely to consistently produce high‑supportive, rubric‑aligned outputs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Query Expansion (Chain-of-Thought)**
  - *Why needed:* To transform a concise critical question into a richer set of search terms that capture the multifaceted nature of trustworthiness assessment (e.g., source investigation, evidence verification, logical fallacies).
  - *Quick check:* How does prompting an LLM to "reason step‑by‑step" differ in output from simple keyword synonym expansion, and why might the former better suit complex information needs?
- **Re‑ranking with Cross‑Encoders (monoT5)**
  - *Why needed:* To move beyond sparse lexical retrieval (BM25) and prioritize passages with deeper semantic relevance, which is crucial for finding nuanced evidence about a source's credibility or a claim's accuracy.
  - *Quick check:* What is the computational trade‑off between retrieving 1000 candidates with BM25 and re‑ranking them with monoT5 versus using a cross‑encoder for the entire corpus?
- **Domain‑Level Trust Signals**
  - *Why needed:* News trustworthiness is not only about topical relevance but also about source credibility. Incorporating a precompiled domain trust score allows post‑retrieval filtering toward higher‑quality publishers.
  - *Quick check:* If a highly relevant passage comes from a domain with a low trust score (e.g., 0.3), what are the arguments for and against filtering it out in a tool designed to help users *assess* trust?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  Article → Question Generator (GPT‑4o‑Nano) → Semantic Filter & Clustering → For each selected question: Query Expansion → BM25 Retriever → monoT5 Re‑ranker → LLM Judge & Domain Filter → (aggregated evidence) → Report Generator (GPT‑4o‑Nano).
- **Critical path:** The article enters the pipeline, generates 30 questions, filters and clusters to 10. Each question undergoes expansion, BM25 retrieval, re‑ranking, and relevance/trust filtering. The top passages across all questions are synthesized into the final trust report.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Cost/efficiency (GPT‑4o‑Nano) vs. quality.
  - Single‑run submission vs. iterative tuning.
  - Reliance on LLMs for internal scoring (relevance, quality) which may be uncalibrated.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low supportive/qgen scores.
  - High topic variance in generation outputs.
  - Poor correlation between LLM internal scores and human assessment.
  - Low report supportive scores indicating evidence does not address rubric priorities.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Retrieval sanity check:** Run BM25 with the original (unexpanded) question. Measure nDCG@10 or LLM‑based relevance on a small sample to establish a performance floor.
  2. **CoT Expansion isolation:** Compare retrieval quality (relevance, domain trust) using only CoT expansion vs. only Baseline expansion, holding all else equal (no re‑ranking, no filtering). Verify the claimed improvement.
  3. **Trust filter threshold sweep:** After re‑ranking, vary the domain trust threshold (e.g., 0.0, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9) and measure the trade‑off between the number of retained documents and the average domain trust score of the final set. Confirm that ≥0.7 is a meaningful cutoff.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Iterative correction loop for question generation:** Does adding an iterative correction loop improve question generation quality? The paper omitted this loop and recommends stabilizing generation; its impact on qgen_score and supportive scores remains untested.
2. **Alignment of LLM‑based relevance judges:** How can LLM judges be better aligned with human judgments? The paper notes low correlation between LLM and human‑aligned scores, calling for improved filtering.
3. **Incorporation of domain trust signals:** What is the best way to incorporate domain trust scores into retrieval to improve report trustworthiness? The paper used a fixed trust threshold and suggests strengthening coupling of trust signals.
4. **Real‑world user impact:** Do the system's technical improvements lead to better user trust judgments in real‑world news consumption? The paper states user studies are needed to test if gains translate to improved trust judgments.

## Limitations
- Exact prompts for GPT‑4o‑Nano (question generation, CoT expansion, relevance judging) are not disclosed, risking prompt‑sensitive divergence in reproduction.
- Access to the Lin et al. domain trust dataset is unspecified; substitutes may alter the trust‑filtering outcome.
- The LLM judge’s relevance assessments are uncalibrated against human labels; its correlation with assessor rubrics is assumed but not validated externally.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:**
  - Retrieval improvements from CoT expansion + monoT5 re‑ranking (direct experimental results).
  - Limitations in question/report generation alignment (explicit evaluation outcomes).
- **Medium confidence:**
  - The reasoning mechanism of CoT expansion exposes latent intents (assumed, no ablation).
  - Domain trust improvement via re‑ranking (correlation claimed, possible confounding).

## Next Checks
1. **Isolate CoT expansion:** Run BM25 retrieval with only CoT‑expanded queries vs. only baseline (no re‑ranking, no filtering) on a sample of DRAGUN topics; measure nDCG@10 and domain trust.
2. **Trust‑threshold sweep:** After re‑ranking, vary the domain trust cutoff (e.g., 0.0, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9) and log the trade‑off between retained documents and average domain score.
3. **Judge calibration:** Sample 100 passages judged relevant by the pipeline LLM and have a human assessor label them; compute agreement (Cohen’s κ) and adjust judge prompts if κ < 0.6.
