# LRConv-NeRV: Low Rank Convolution for Efficient Neural Video Compression

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18261
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18261
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Applying low-rank separable convolutions only to the final decoder stage reduces computational complexity by 68% (201.9 to 64.9 GFLOPs), model size by 9.3%, and bitrate by 9.2%, with negligible PSNR loss (<0.5 dB) and preserved temporal stability.

## Executive Summary
Neural video representations like NeRV suffer from high decoding complexity due to dense 3×3 convolutions. This work introduces LRConv-NeRV, which replaces selected dense convolutions with structured low-rank separable convolutions. When applied selectively to the final decoder stage—the most computationally heavy—the method achieves a major reduction in GFLOPs and model size with minimal impact on reconstruction quality (PSNR loss <0.5 dB) and temporal coherence (LPIPS ratio near 1.0). The approach offers a superior efficiency-quality trade-off compared to other efficient NeRV variants like Binary-NeRV and Ghost-NeRV, and remains robust under INT8 quantization.

## Method Summary
LRConv factorizes a dense 3×3 convolution kernel **W** ∈ ℝ^(C_out × C_in × 3 × 3) into two separable convolutions: a 3×1 projection (C_in → *r*) followed by a 1×3 expansion (*r* → C_out), where the rank *r* = ⌈ρ·min(C_in, C_out)⌉ (ρ=0.25). This structural decomposition reduces parameters and FLOPs. The factorization is applied progressively from the highest-resolution decoder stages (L4) backward. The NeRV decoder starts from a learned embedding of the frame index, processes it through multiple upsampling stages (each with convolution, ReLU, and pixel-shuffle), and outputs the frame. Training is end-to-end; post-training INT8 quantization is also evaluated.

## Key Results
- Final-stage-only LRConv reduces decoder FLOPs by 68% (201.9 → 64.9 GFLOPs).
- Model size reduced by ~9.3% and bitrate by ~9.2% with negligible PSNR loss (<0.5 dB).
- Temporal stability preserved: LPIPS ratio close to 1.0, outperforming Binary-NeRV and Ghost-NeRV.
- Under INT8 quantization, the final-stage configuration maintains quality near the dense baseline; aggressive earlier-stage factorization causes larger drops.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Replacing dense 3×3 convolutions with structured low-rank separable convolutions reduces computational complexity and model parameters while preserving reconstruction quality when applied to later decoder stages.
- **Mechanism:** A dense kernel **W** is approximated as **W ≈ A·B**, implemented as a 3×1 projection (C_in → *r*) and 1×3 expansion (*r* → C_out). Parameter count drops from 9·C_in·C_out to 3·*r*·(C_in + C_out). The rank *r* is chosen small (ρ=0.25).
- **Core assumption:** The intrinsic rank of later-stage convolutional operators is low; a small *r* captures essential transformations. Later stages, with larger channel counts, tolerate this approximation better than early stages.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: Reports 68% FLOP reduction with negligible PSNR loss for final-stage-only application.
  - [section III]: Factorization equations and example for last layer (C_in=96, C_out=384) showing ~9.6× parameter reduction.
- **Break condition:** Using *r* too small (ρ << 0.25) or applying LRConv to early stages (L0–L4) causes disproportionate quality loss (PSNR drop >1 dB) and increased temporal flicker.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Applying LRConv selectively to the final decoder stage yields the best trade-off because later stages dominate computational cost and are more tolerant to low-rank approximation.
- **Mechanism:** Later NeRV decoder stages operate at higher spatial resolutions and have larger channel dimensions (e.g., C_in=96, C_out=384), contributing the most to GFLOPs. Replacing their dense convolutions with LRConv reduces the dominant cost. Later stages refine fine details and can be modeled by separable filters with moderate rank; end-to-end training adapts the low-rank filters to preserve quality.
- **Core assumption:** Later stages are computationally dominant and suitable for low-rank approximation; early stages encode coarse structures requiring full capacity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: Final-stage-only configuration reduces FLOPs by 68% with negligible PSNR loss.
  - [section V.A]: Table 2 shows average PSNR differences <0.5 dB and ~9.2% bitrate reduction for final-stage-only; Figure 3 shows quality degrades as replacement extends earlier.
- **Break condition:** Extending LRConv to earlier stages (e.g., L3–L4) increases quality loss beyond computational benefit. Under INT8, early-stage factorization causes even larger drops (Figure 4).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** LRConv preserves temporal stability better than binary or ghost convolutions because it uses continuous-valued weights and structured factorization that maintains full channel mixing, avoiding discrete optimization pitfalls and heuristic feature generation.
- **Mechanism:** Temporal coherence in NeRV requires smooth output variations given continuous frame indices. Binary-NeRV binarizes weights, introducing noise and representational collapse, causing flicker. Ghost-NeRV generates channels from a small intrinsic feature set, potentially losing subtle temporal details. LRConv uses real-valued weights and decomposes the convolution into two continuous transformations (projection and reconstruction), preserving a global linear mapping between input and output channels and thus smoothness.
- **Core assumption:** Continuous-valued weights and structured low-rank factorization are compatible with smooth function approximation needed for temporal coherence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: LRConv-NeRV maintains temporal stability (LPIPS ratio close to 1.0) compared to other efficient variants.
  - [section V.C]: Figure 10 shows LRConv (final stage) LPIPS ratio ~0.85–0.9, close to dense NeRV baseline (1.0), while Binary-NeRV ratios reach 1.8.
- **Break condition:** Aggressive early-stage LRConv increases temporal flicker (LPIPS ratio >1.0), as seen in Figure 10 for L2–L4. Too low a rank may lose subtle motion cues, worsening flicker.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Low-rank matrix approximation  
  - **Why needed here:** Explains parameter reduction when approximating dense convolution kernels as products of two smaller matrices (A·B).  
  - **Quick check:** If a dense matrix is *M*×*N*, rank-*r* approximation requires *r*(*M*+*N*) parameters. Savings when *r* ≪ min(*M*,*N*).

- **Concept:** Separable convolutions  
  - **Why needed here:** LRConv implements low-rank factorization as two separable convolutions (3×1 then 1×3), directly reducing multiplicative operations per output pixel from *k*² to 2*k*.  
  - **Quick check:** How many multiplications per output pixel for a 3×3 separable convolution vs. standard? (Standard: 9·C_in·C_out; separable: 3·*r*·C_in + 3·*r*·C_out, with *r* << min(C_in,C_out)).

- **Concept:** Neural Representations for Videos (NeRV)  
  - **Why needed here:** LRConv modifies the NeRV decoder; understanding its sequential upsampling with dense convolutions is essential.  
  - **Quick check:** What is the primary computational bottleneck in NeRV decoding? (Dense 3×3 convolutions in the decoder, especially at high resolutions.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Frame index *t* → FC layer → L0 (Conv + ReLU + pixel-shuffle) → L1 (Conv + ReLU + pixel-shuffle) → L2 (Conv + ReLU + pixel-shuffle) → L3 (Conv + ReLU + pixel-shuffle) → L4 (Conv + ReLU) → output frame. LRConv replaces the Conv in a stage with two separable convolutions (3×1 then 1×3) with intermediate rank *r*.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Encode frame index *t* via a fully connected layer into initial feature map.  
  2. For stage *i* = L0 to L4:  
     a. If LRConv enabled: apply Conv(3×1, C_in → *r*) → ReLU → Conv(1×3, *r* → C_out).  
     b. Else: apply standard Conv(3×3, C_in → C_out).  
     c. Apply ReLU.  
     d. If not L4, apply pixel-shuffle (×2 upsampling).  
  3. Output the reconstructed frame.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Layer selection:** Replacing only L4 (final stage) gives best quality-efficiency; earlier replacements degrade quality disproportionately.  
  - **Rank ρ:** Smaller ρ increases compression but risks quality loss; paper uses ρ=0.25 for L4.  
  - **Quantization:** Under INT8, L4-only LRConv remains robust; early-stage factorization leads to larger quality drops.  
  - **Method choice:** LRConv offers better trade-off than Binary-NeRV (extreme compression, poor quality/flicker) and Ghost-NeRV (higher FLOPs at similar quality).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - PSNR drop >0.5 dB relative to baseline at similar bitrate.  
  - LPIPS ratio between adjacent frames significantly >1.0.  
  - Visual artifacts: blurring in high-texture areas or frame-to-frame flicker.  
  - Under INT8, quality collapse if LRConv applied to stages earlier than L4 or if rank too low.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline replication:** Implement standard NeRV decoder (dense 3×3 convs) and train on a small video (e.g., Bunny). Verify FLOPs ≈201.9 GFLOPs and PSNR ≈33 dB to ensure correct baseline.  
  2. **Single-stage LRConv:** Replace only L4 with LRConv (ρ=0.25). Train from scratch. Expect FLOPs ≈64.9 GFLOPs, ~9.3% parameter reduction, PSNR loss <0.5 dB, bitrate reduction ~9.2%. Also test INT8 quantization; quality should remain near dense baseline.  
  3. **Progressive replacement:** Apply LRConv to L3–L4, L2–L4, etc. Plot PSNR vs. FLOPs and verify diminishing returns and increasing quality loss. Measure LPIPS ratios to confirm temporal stability degrades only slightly for L4-only but worsens with earlier inclusions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How does the rank reduction factor ρ affect the efficiency-quality trade-off across decoder stages, and can stage-adaptive ρ improve over uniform ρ=0.25?** The paper fixes ρ=0.25 and notes early-stage degradation but does not explore per-stage ρ. Different stages have different sensitivity; uniform ρ may be suboptimal. Systematic experiments varying ρ per stage under GFLOP budgets could reveal Pareto improvements.
- **Can alternative training strategies (e.g., progressive fine-tuning, knowledge distillation) mitigate the quality loss when applying LRConv to early decoder stages?** The paper uses standard end-to-end training and reports severe early-stage degradation without investigating training modifications. Early layers are critical for global structure; reduced rank might not fully adapt during training. Comparing standard training with progressive LRConv introduction or distillation from dense NeRV would test if quality gains are possible.
- **How does LRConv-NeRV compare against conventional video codecs (H.264/AVC, HEVC, VVC) and modern learned compression methods in rate-distortion and complexity on high-resolution content?** The paper only compares to other NeRV variants; no benchmarks with standard or state-of-the-art learned codecs are provided. NeRV itself lags behind top codecs; absolute performance of LRConv-NeRV is unknown. Standardized evaluation on common datasets (e.g., UVG, MCL-JCV) using BD-rate and complexity metrics against reference codecs (HM, VTM, DVC, HNeRV) is needed.
- **Does low-rank factorization preserve temporal coherence for longer, high-motion videos, and how does it interact with explicit motion modeling in NeRV extensions?** The paper measures flicker on 132-frame clips but not on longer sequences or with motion compensation; NeRV lacks motion modeling. Longer videos may accumulate artifacts; motion-rich content could amplify issues. Evaluating LRConv-NeRV on longer, high-motion videos using temporal consistency metrics (LPIPS ratio, flicker) and integrating it into motion-aware variants (e.g., FFNeRV) would test robustness.

## Limitations
- Exact NeRV decoder channel dimensions per stage are not reported, preventing exact FLOP/parameter verification.
- Training hyperparameters (optimizer, learning rate schedule, batch size, loss details) and bitrate calculation method are omitted.
- Evaluation restricted to UVG dataset (Bunny-resolution); generalization to other content/resolutions is unknown.
- Rank reduction factor ρ=0.25 chosen ad hoc; no systematic search for optimal trade-off.
- Temporal stability assessed only via LPIPS ratio; other flicker metrics not considered.

## Confidence
- *LRConv reduces computational cost and model size with negligible quality loss when applied to later stages*: **Medium** – experimental evidence presented but reproducibility hampered by missing specs.
- *Applying LRConv only to the final decoder stage is optimal*: **Medium** – progressive replacement results support this, but boundary may shift with different architectures/datasets.
- *LRConv preserves temporal stability better than binary/ghost methods*: **Medium** – LPIPS comparisons favorable, but fairness of baselines and broader temporal metrics uncertain.
