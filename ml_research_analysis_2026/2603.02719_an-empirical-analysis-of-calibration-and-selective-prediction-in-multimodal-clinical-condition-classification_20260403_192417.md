# An Empirical Analysis of Calibration and Selective Prediction in Multimodal Clinical Condition Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02719
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02719
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Multimodal fusion improves discrimination (AUROC/AUPRC) but not calibration; severe class-dependent overconfidence on rare positives degrades selective prediction.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates uncertainty calibration and selective prediction in multilabel clinical condition classification using multimodal ICU data (EHR time-series and chest X-rays). The authors compare unimodal models with state-of-the-art multimodal fusion architectures (MedFuse, DrFuse, MeTra) and find that while fusion enhances discriminative performance (higher AUROC/AUPRC), it consistently fails to improve probability calibration. Moreover, models exhibit severe class-dependent miscalibration: they are overconfident on underrepresented positive conditions (high positive-class Expected Calibration Error). This miscalibration directly harms selective prediction—higher positive-class ECE reliably predicts lower selective AUROC/AUPRC. Simple loss upweighting reduces positive-class calibration error modestly but does not consistently improve selective reliability. The study highlights that aggregate calibration metrics (ECE) can mask these failures, underscoring the need for calibration-aware evaluation in multimodal clinical models.

## Method Summary
The study uses a paired dataset from MIMIC-IV (EHR) and MIMIC-CXR (frontal X-rays) to predict 25 clinical conditions (multilabel). Models include unimodal baselines (EHR-only LSTM, CXR-only ResNet-34) and multimodal fusion models (MedFuse, DrFuse, MeTra). All models are trained with binary cross-entropy loss, optionally with label-dependent upweighting to emphasize rare positives. Training involves hyperparameter search (50 configurations, each with 5 seeds), selecting the best by validation AUROC, and retraining on combined train/validation sets. Evaluation focuses on discrimination (AUROC, AUPRC), calibration (Expected Calibration Error, ECE), and class-stratified calibration (positive-class ECE<sub>c=1</sub>, negative-class ECE<sub>c=0</sub>). Selective prediction is assessed by rejecting predictions with high entropy; selective AUROC/AUPRC as a function of coverage is computed, and the area under these curves summarizes performance.

## Key Results
- Multimodal fusion significantly improves AUROC and AUPRC compared to unimodal models.
- Multimodal fusion does not improve, and often worsens, calibration (higher ECE), especially positive-class ECE.
- Positive-class ECE is consistently larger than negative-class ECE across conditions, indicating systematic overconfidence on rare positives.
- Higher positive-class ECE strongly correlates with lower selective AUROC/AUPRC, demonstrating that miscalibration directly degrades selective prediction reliability.
- Simple loss upweighting reduces positive-class calibration error modestly but fails to consistently enhance selective performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Multimodal fusion consistently improves discriminative performance (AUROC/AUPRC) but does not reliably improve, and can worsen, model calibration in multilabel clinical condition classification.
- **Mechanism:** Fusion combines complementary signals from EHR time-series and CXR images, enhancing the model's ability to discriminate between conditions (higher AUROC/AUPRC). However, the fusion operation (e.g., concatenation in MedFuse) and subsequent training with standard losses (binary cross-entropy) do not explicitly optimize for probability alignment. The model learns feature representations optimized for discrimination, not for ensuring predicted probabilities match true outcome frequencies. This creates a disconnect where a model can rank outcomes well (high AUROC) but assign poorly calibrated confidence scores.
- **Core assumption:** Standard training objectives for multimodal fusion are optimized for discrimination metrics, not calibration.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "we find that while multimodal fusion improves discrimination (higher AUROC/AUPRC), it consistently fails to improve calibration."
  - [section 5.1] Detailed per-condition results show MedFuse achieves higher AUROC/AUPRC than unimodal EHR but does not consistently achieve lower ECE; in some conditions, its ECE is higher than EHR's.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers like "Multi-pathology Chest X-ray Classification with Rejection Mechanisms" report similar overconfidence issues in multi-label medical imaging, suggesting this is a broader challenge where improved discrimination does not guarantee calibration. The specific link to multimodal fusion is an inference from this work's comparative setup.
- **Break condition:** If a multimodal model uses a calibration-aware training objective (e.g., with explicit regularization on probability outputs) or post-hoc scaling tailored per modality, the gap between discrimination and calibration might narrow. This paper's findings assume standard training pipelines.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Severe class-dependent miscalibration is the dominant error pattern, characterized by systematic overconfidence (high ECE) on underrepresented positive predictions (high `ECEc=1`), while negative-class calibration (`ECEc=0`) is often better.
- **Mechanism:** In multilabel classification with highly imbalanced conditions (many more negative than positive samples per label), the model is frequently exposed to negative examples. The decision boundary learned for rare positive conditions is under-constrained, leading to overconfident incorrect positive predictions. The binary cross-entropy loss, when applied naively, does not sufficiently penalize high-confidence false positives on rare classes. This manifests as a large gap between predicted confidence and empirical accuracy specifically when the true label is positive (`ECEc=1`).
- **Core assumption:** Class imbalance causes the model to be less frequently updated on positive examples, leading to overfitting on the majority negative class and poor uncertainty estimation for the minority positive class.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.1 & Table 2] "positive-class calibration error is consistently larger than negative-class error across conditions... indicating that overconfidence in positive predictions is the dominant source of miscalibration." Table 2 shows `ECEc=1` values often an order of magnitude higher than `ECEc=0` for the same condition/model.
  - [section 4.1] The dataset has 25 conditions with prevalence ranging from ~7% to ~45%; conditions like "Acute cerebrovascular disease" (7.99% prevalence) show extremely high `ECEc=1`.
  - [corpus] "Exposing and Mitigating Calibration Biases and Demographic Unfairness in MLLM Few-Shot In-Context Learning for Medical Image Classification" discusses calibration biases in medical image classification, aligning with the finding that standard metrics mask subgroup (here, class-level) failures.
- **Break condition:** If strong class-balancing techniques are applied during training (e.g., loss upweighting, oversampling) in a way that effectively equalizes the learning signal per class, `ECEc=1` could be reduced. This paper shows simple upweighting helps but does not eliminate the problem.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Class-dependent miscalibration (high `ECEc=1`) directly causes selective prediction to fail or degrade; higher positive-class ECE reliably predicts lower selective AUROC/AUPRC.
- **Mechanism:** Selective prediction relies on the model's uncertainty metric (often entropy) to rank predictions by confidence. If the model is overconfident on incorrect positive predictions, these high-risk cases will have low predicted uncertainty (high confidence) and will *not* be rejected. Conversely, some correct predictions may have high uncertainty and be unnecessarily rejected. This misranking means that rejecting the "most uncertain" cases does not preferentially remove errors, so performance does not improve (or degrades) as coverage decreases. The strong negative correlation between `ECEc=1` AUC and selective AUROC/AUPRC demonstrates that the severity of overconfidence on positives is a leading indicator of this failure.
- **Core assumption:** The selective prediction mechanism uses model confidence/entropy as the sole criterion for rejection, and this criterion is corrupted by the miscalibration described in Mechanism 2.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.2 & Figure 2] "Conditions with low, balanced positive and negative ECE exhibit stable and improving selective AUROC/AUPRC... By contrast, high ECE in the underrepresented class... produces noisy, unstable selective curves." Figure 2b shows a clear negative correlation (Spearman p<0.05) between `ECEc=1` AUC and selective AUROC/AUPRC across conditions.
  - [section 3] Defines selective prediction formally (equation 3.5) and uses predictive entropy as the uncertainty score.
  - [corpus] "Robust Decision Making with Partially Calibrated Forecasts" discusses how miscalibration impairs decision-making with forecasted probabilities, which is analogous to selective prediction's reliance on confidence scores. The direct causal link to selective *performance* in a multilabel clinical setting is a specific contribution of this paper.
- **Break condition:** If a model's uncertainty estimates are well-calibrated (low `ECEc=1`), or if the rejection criterion uses a more robust uncertainty measure less affected by class imbalance (e.g., disagreement in an ensemble), selective prediction could succeed. The paper tests a simple loss upweighting, which reduces `ECEc=1` but does not consistently fix selective performance, suggesting the relationship is necessary but not sufficient, or that upweighting alone is inadequate.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Calibration (Expected Calibration Error - ECE)**
  - *Why needed here:* Selective prediction's safety guarantee hinges on the model's confidence scores reflecting true probabilities. ECE quantifies the gap between predicted confidence and empirical accuracy. Without understanding calibration, one cannot diagnose why selective prediction fails.
  - *Quick check question:* If a model predicts "positive" with 90% confidence for a condition that only occurs 10% of the time, what would a well-calibrated model's actual accuracy be on such predictions? (Answer: ~90%, if the confidence is reliable).

- **Concept: Class-Dependent (Stratified) Calibration**
  - *Why needed here:* Aggregate ECE can mask severe miscalibration in minority classes. In multilabel clinical data, conditions have very different prevalences. A model can have a low overall ECE but be terribly overconfident on rare conditions, which is precisely where selective prediction is most needed. Analyzing `ECEc=1` and `ECEc=0` separately exposes this failure mode.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a model's calibration for the positive class of a rare condition be worse than for the negative class of the same condition? (Answer: Fewer positive examples lead to less constrained learning on the minority class boundary).

- **Concept: Selective Prediction (Reject Option)**
  - *Why needed here:* It is the proposed safety mechanism. Understanding its evaluation (selective AUROC/AUPRC curves) is essential to interpret the paper's core finding: that strong standard metrics (AUROC) do not guarantee selective prediction will work.
  - *Quick check question:* What does it mean if a model's selective AUROC curve is flat or decreasing as more cases are rejected? (Answer: The model's uncertainty ranking is poor—it is rejecting correct predictions and keeping wrong ones).

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Input (xehr, xcxr, y) -> EHR Encoder (LSTM) -> CXR Encoder (ResNet-34) -> Fusion (concatenation) -> Classification Head (FC + sigmoid) -> outputs.  
  Uncertainty metric: Shannon entropy of per-condition sigmoid outputs.  
  Gating: predictions with entropy <= τ are kept; others rejected.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Data preparation: Extract paired EHR-CXR samples from MIMIC-IV/CXR, patient-level train/test split, preprocess EHR (normalize vitals/labs) and CXR (resize, basic augmentation).  
  2. Model training: For each architecture (EHR-only, CXR-only, MedFuse, DrFuse, MeTra), train with BCE loss. Hyperparameters (LR, epochs) are searched (50 configs, 5 seeds), best selected by validation AUROC. Retrain on train+val with best config and 5 seeds.  
  3. Evaluation: On test set, for each condition and seed compute AUROC, AUPRC, ECE, class-stratified ECE. Generate selective prediction curves by sorting test samples by entropy, varying rejection threshold τ, computing AUROC/AUPRC on non-rejected set. Summarize selective performance via area under the selective curve.  
  4. Analysis: Correlate positive-class ECE AUC with selective AUROC/AUPRC across conditions and seeds. Repeat with loss-upweighted models.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Early vs. Late Fusion: MedFuse (late concatenation) is simple but may not capture fine-grained interactions. DrFuse/MeTra use more complex alignment (divergence-based, transformer) but the paper finds they do not solve calibration issues, suggesting fusion complexity is not the primary lever.  
  - Architecture Complexity: Adding more parameters (MeTra) or alignment objectives (DrFuse) improves discrimination marginally but does not consistently reduce class-dependent miscalibration.  
  - Calibration Intervention: Loss upweighting for rare positives is a lightweight, training-time intervention that reduces `ECEc=1` but does not guarantee better selective performance, indicating calibration and selective reliability are related but distinct objectives.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. High positive-class ECE (`ECEc=1`) for a condition, especially when its prevalence is low.  
  2. Selective AUROC/AUPRC curve that is flat, oscillatory, or decreasing as coverage increases (more rejections), particularly at high thresholds.  
  3. Large gap between standard AUROC and selective AUROC for a condition (i.e., selective AUROC is much lower than standard AUROC), indicating the model's uncertainty ranking is worse than random at identifying errors.  
  4. High variance across random seeds in selective curves and `ECEc=1`, indicating unstable uncertainty estimation.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Comparison:** Train EHR-only, CXR-only, and MedFuse models. Compute for each of the 25 conditions: standard AUROC, AUPRC, ECE, `ECEc=1`, `ECEc=0`. Verify that MedFuse improves AUROC/AUPRC but does not consistently lower `ECEc=1`.  
  2. **Selective Prediction Analysis:** For the MedFuse model, generate selective AUROC and AUPRC curves for all 25 conditions. Visually inspect for conditions where curves degrade. Quantify the correlation between the area under the `ECEc=1` curve and the area under the selective AUROC curve across conditions.  
  3. **Mitigation Attempt:** Train a MedFuse model with label-dependent loss upweighting (weights inversely proportional to label prevalence). Repeat Experiment 1 and 2. Check if `ECEc=1` decreases and if selective curves improve.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Generalizability to other datasets and tasks:** The analysis is conducted on a single ICU benchmark; it remains an open question whether similar patterns hold across other datasets, modality combinations, or clinical tasks.
2. **Effectiveness of advanced calibration techniques:** Can Bayesian methods, differentiable calibration, or other state-of-the-art approaches reliably mitigate class-dependent miscalibration and improve selective prediction performance in multimodal models?
3. **Interaction with clinical workflows:** How does selective prediction interact with human–AI decision-making in prospective deployment? Retrospective analysis cannot capture dynamics of workflow integration, workload shifts, or threshold tuning in real-time practice.
4. **Appropriate evaluation metrics:** What metrics best capture calibration quality for selective prediction in multilabel multimodal clinical models? Aggregate ECE obscures class-dependent failures; new metrics (e.g., class-weighted ECE) are needed.

## Limitations
- Missing reproduction details: Exact EHR feature extraction, imputation, CXR preprocessing, ECE binning, selective-rejection implementation, and loss-upweighting formula.
- Findings are limited to MIMIC ICU data and a few fusion models; external validity to other datasets, modalities, or tasks is unknown.
- The paper does not explore calibration across patient subgroups or other clinical settings.

## Confidence
- Multimodal fusion improves discrimination: **High**
- Multimodal fusion does not improve calibration: **Medium**
- Positive-class overconfidence degrades selective prediction: **High**
- Loss upweighting only modestly helps: **Low**

## Next Checks
- **Calibration-aware training:** Integrate explicit calibration regularization (e.g., temperature scaling, MMCE) into multimodal fusion and reassess ECE<sub>c=1</sub> and selective AUROC/AUPRC.
- **Cross-dataset replication:** Test all models on an external paired EHR-image dataset (e.g., eICU) to verify generalizability of class-dependent miscalibration and selective failures.
- **Alternative uncertainty metrics:** Replace predictive entropy with MC-Dropout variance or ensemble disagreement and examine whether selective performance decouples from ECE<sub>c=1</sub>.
