# SUREON: A Benchmark and Vision-Language-Model for Surgical Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06570
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06570
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** SureonVLM achieves 84‑85% MC accuracy on SUREON, beating GPT‑5.1 (68%) and Gemini (60%).

## Executive Summary
Surgical artificial intelligence lacks the ability to reason about intent, safety, and future steps, primarily due to the scarcity of explicitly annotated reasoning data. SUREON addresses this gap by extracting structured question‑answer pairs from narrated surgical lecture videos via a multi‑agent pipeline, defining 12 question categories spanning perception, decision rationale, and forecasting. The resulting dataset contains 206.8k QA pairs over 134.7k video clips. Models trained on this data (SureonVLM, 8B) undergo progressive supervised fine‑tuning followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with explicit reasoning rewards. On an expert‑validated benchmark of 354 samples, the models achieve 84–85% multiple‑choice accuracy, significantly surpassing larger general‑domain VLMs (GPT‑5.1: 68%, Gemini: 60%). Notably, reasoning interpretability improves while maintaining competence on standard surgical perception tasks.

## Method Summary
SUREON constructs its reasoning dataset using GPT‑5–based generator and validator agents. The pipeline identifies **Semantic Grounding Moments**—video segments where narration explicitly describes visible entities, actions, or decisions—and converts them into QA pairs across 12 categories. Specialized generator agents process aligned transcripts to propose candidates, while validator agents enforce visual grounding and clinical validity. The final dataset comprises 206.8k QA pairs derived from 134.7k lecture clips.

For model training, the authors start from Qwen3‑VL (8B) and apply a three‑stage supervised fine‑tuning regime that mixes SUREON (30%) with 18 public surgical datasets (70%). Stage 1 trains the MLP adapter only; stage 2 unfreezes the vision encoder; stage 3 unfreezes the LLM while keeping the vision encoder frozen, with 50% option dropout and where‑available chain‑of‑thought traces. After obtaining SureonVLM, they perform GRPO on SUREON alone, prompting the model to generate `<think>` reasoning tokens before answering multiple‑choice questions. Rewards combine correctness, format compliance, and encouragement of explicit reasoning patterns. The resulting SureonVLM‑R1 exhibits clearer reasoning traces without sacrificing accuracy.

## Key Results
- SureonVLM and SureonVLM‑R1 achieve 84–85% MC accuracy on the expert‑validated SUREON benchmark, substantially outperforming GPT‑5.1 (68%) and Gemini (60%).
- Both models maintain or improve performance on standard surgical perception tasks (phase recognition, tool detection, CVS score) compared to the base Qwen3‑VL and prior surgical VLMs.
- SureonVLM‑R1 explicitly generates reasoning traces (e.g., inferring surgical intent from visual cues), demonstrating improved interpretability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Expert-narrated surgical lectures provide a scalable source of reasoning supervision by encoding intent, rationale, and anticipation within spontaneous narration.
- Mechanism: The SUREON pipeline identifies **Semantic Grounding Moments (SGMs)**—video segments where narration explicitly describes visible entities, actions, or decisions—and converts them into structured QA pairs across 12 categories (e.g., decision reasoning, forecasting). Specialized generator agents (GPT-5) parse transcripts to locate SGMs and generate questions grounded in the transcript; validator agents then filter samples to ensure visual grounding and clinical validity.
- Core assumption: Pedagogically selective narration in lectures is semantically dense enough to serve as a proxy for expert reasoning annotations, despite being unstructured and noisy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "surgical video lectures already contain exactly this — explanations of intent, rationale, and anticipation, narrated by experts for the purpose of teaching."
  - [section 2] "We define Semantic Grounding Moments as video segments in which narration explicitly anchors specific visual entities, actions, reasoning, or decision-making cues."
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., GP-VLS, LLaVA-Surg) focus on general-purpose surgical VLMs but do not exploit lecture narration for reasoning, suggesting novelty. Evidence for scalability is indirect (206.8k QA pairs).
- Break condition: If narration frequently references off-screen events or abstract concepts without visual grounding, SGMs become unverifiable, leading to hallucinated or unanswerable QA pairs.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: A multi-agent pipeline with per-question-type generators and validators can convert noisy transcripts into high-quality, diverse QA pairs at scale.
- Mechanism: For each of the 12 question categories, a specialized GPT-5 generator agent processes the transcript to propose candidate QA pairs anchored to SGMs; a corresponding validator agent applies category-specific constraints (e.g., temporal coherence for forecasting, safety mention for safety questions) to filter invalid samples. This division of labor allows tailored quality control without manual annotation.
- Core assumption: LLM-based agents can reliably interpret and apply complex clinical and pedagogical criteria when properly instructed, and validator agents can compensate for generator imperfections.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2] "For each question type, paired specialized generator and specialized validator GPT-5 agents sequentially process the transcript to produce high-quality supervision samples."
  - [section 2] "The corresponding validator Vk then filters these candidates, producing the validated set by discarding samples that violate question-specific or global quality constraints."
  - [corpus] The neighboring "Memory-Augmented Multimodal LLMs for Surgical VQA" uses self-contained inquiry but lacks a multi-agent curation approach, implying SUREON's pipeline is a distinct solution to scalability.
- Break condition: If validator agents are under-specified (e.g., missing critical clinical constraints) or over-sensitive (e.g., discarding valid but nuanced samples), the dataset may suffer from low recall or high noise.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Progressive supervised fine-tuning (SFT) followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with explicit reasoning rewards yields a compact (8B) model that surpasses larger general-domain VLMs on surgical reasoning while preserving perception.
- Mechanism: 
  - **SFT**: Three stages—(1) train MLP only, (2) unfreeze vision encoder + MLP, (3) unfreeze MLP + LLM (freeze vision encoder)—using a mixture of SUREON (30%) and 18 public datasets (70%). Stage 3 omits multiple-choice options 50% of the time and includes CoT traces where available.
  - **GRPO**: Model generates `<think>` tokens before answering multiple-choice questions. Rewards combine correctness, format compliance, and for select tasks (temporal ordering, forecasting) encouragement of specific reasoning patterns. Group-normalized advantages update the policy with KL regularization.
- Core assumption: Staged SFT prevents catastrophic forgetting of perception skills while adapting to reasoning. GRPO encourages exploration of coherent reasoning trajectories without direct CoT supervision, improving interpretability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3] Describes the three-step SFT schedule and GRPO with reward design.
  - [table 2] Ablation: Progressive SFT (T+S) boosts accuracy from 0.66 to 0.83; including CoT (C) is necessary for stable GRPO training (as without it, models fail to generate `<think>` tokens).
  - [section 4] "SureonVLM-R1 exhibits explicit reasoning behavior, such as inferring surgical intent from visual cues."
- Break condition: If GRPO reward weights are misbalanced (e.g., format reward dominates), the model may prioritize well-formed `<think>` tags over clinical accuracy. If SFT stages are truncated, perception skills degrade (as seen in ablation without T).

## Foundational Learning
- Concept: **Semantic Grounding Moments (SGMs)**
  - Why needed here: SGMs are the atomic units linking narration to visual content. Misunderstanding SGM identification leads to ungrounded or irrelevant QA pairs.
  - Quick check question: How would you determine if a transcript sentence describing "careful dissection near the cystic duct" is a valid SGM for an *Instrument-Action Interaction* question?

- Concept: **Multi-agent validation loop**
  - Why needed here: The pipeline's quality hinges on generator-validator coordination. Without proper validation criteria, the dataset inherits LLM hallucinations or misalignments.
  - Quick check question: What validator constraints would you prioritize for a *Safety Practice Identification* question to ensure clinical relevance?

- Concept: **Progressive SFT schedule**
  - Why needed here: The staged parameter updates (MLP → vision encoder → LLM) are critical to adapt the base model without forgetting perception abilities. Changing this order likely causes instability.
  - Quick check question: Why freeze the vision encoder in SFT stage 3 when training on SUREON? What failure mode does this prevent?

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map:
  - Data pipeline: Narrated videos -> aligned transcripts -> per-question-type generator agents (GPT-5) -> candidate QA pairs -> validator agents -> filtered SUREON dataset (206.8k clips) -> expert validation -> benchmark (354 samples).
  - Model training: Qwen3-VL (8B) base -> 3-stage SFT (mixed SUREON + 18 public datasets) -> GRPO (SUREON only, with `<think>` prompting).
  - Evaluation: Models -> SUREON benchmark (multiple-choice & open-ended) + standard surgical perception datasets -> metrics.
- Critical path:
  1. Acquire narrated surgical videos (e.g., SurgLaVi) with accurate transcript-video alignment.
  2. Implement generator/validator agents for all 12 question types, using the provided prompts or replicating the GPT-5 based system.
  3. Run pipeline to generate full SUREON training set; conduct expert validation for benchmark (as in paper: ~20% discard rate).
  4. Preprocess 18 public datasets into conversational format (frame/clip-level QA).
  5. Execute three-stage SFT with exact data mixing ratios (30% SUREON, 50% images, 20% videos) and learning rates.
  6. Apply GRPO with 10 candidates per input, reward components as defined, and KL regularization.
  7. Evaluate on SUREON (MC accuracy) and standard tasks (e.g., Cholec80 phase F1).
- Design tradeoffs:
  - GPT-5 agents vs. cheaper LLMs: Using frontier models ensures quality but limits reproducibility and increases cost. Tradeoff: dataset quality vs. accessibility.
  - Mixed training data: Including standard datasets preserves perception but may dilute reasoning-specific learning. Paper shows marginal benefit (Table 2: P), but dropping them risks forgetting (implied by ablation without T).
  - GRPO with `<think>` tokens: Improves interpretability but reduces open-ended performance (SureonVLM-R1 EM lower than SureonVLM in Table 1). Tradeoff: reasoning transparency vs. generative fluency.
  - Progressive SFT: Carefully staged updates prevent forgetting but require multiple training runs and precise hyperparameter tuning.
- Failure signatures:
  - Data pipeline: Validation rate <10% or >90% indicates misconfigured validator prompts; manual review of 50 samples per question type reveals grounding errors.
  - SFT: After stage 1 (T+S), if MC accuracy on SUREON does not rise to ~0.8, the vision encoder may not be adapting (check LR, data mixing).
  - GRPO: Model fails to generate `<think>` tokens consistently → insufficient CoT supervision in SFT (paper notes this failure mode). Also, reward spikes without accuracy gain indicate reward hacking.
  - Evaluation: Performance drop on standard perception tasks (e.g., phase recognition F1 < base model) signals catastrophic forgetting; adjust data mixing or reduce SUREON proportion.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Pipeline sanity check: Run generator-validator loop on 5 videos, output 20 QA pairs per question type. Manually verify: (a) questions answerable from clip, (b) answers narrator-aligned, (c) options plausible.
  2. SFT stage 1 validation: Train only MLP (stage 1) on 10% SUREON + 10% public data for 1 epoch. Evaluate on 500 SUREON MC samples. Expect accuracy >0.75 (vs. base 0.66). If not, check vision feature extraction.
  3. GRPO stability pilot: Start GRPO with 100 steps, 2 candidates per input. Monitor: (a) `<think>` token generation frequency, (b) reward distribution, (c) accuracy on 200 held-out SUREON samples. Should see gradual improvement; if reward collapses or accuracy drops, reduce KL coefficient.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the pedagogical selectivity of surgical lecture videos—which emphasizes teaching moments over routine steps—be mitigated to create a more representative surgical reasoning dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The Limitations section states: "SUREON inherits the pedagogical selectivity of surgical lectures: narrators emphasize teaching moments, leaving routine operative steps underrepresented."
- Why unresolved: This bias may limit model robustness in real-world surgery, where routine steps are frequent and critical for safety.
- What evidence would resolve it: Compare model performance on routine steps extracted from unedited operative footage versus lecture-derived clips; or augment SUREON with annotations from raw OR videos to quantify coverage gaps.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can surgical reasoning evaluation move beyond LLM-as-judge metrics, which may prioritize linguistic fluency over clinical accuracy, toward surgeon-validated assessment?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The Limitations note: "Evaluation relies partly on LLM-based judgment, which may favor fluent over clinically accurate responses."
- Why unresolved: Fluent but incorrect reasoning could be dangerously misleading in clinical deployment; current benchmark may not reflect true clinical validity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct a large-scale study where multiple surgeons independently evaluate model responses on SUREON’s test set; correlate LLM-judge scores with surgeon consensus to measure bias and adjust metrics accordingly.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the surgical reasoning learned from SUREON’s 170 procedure types generalize to unseen procedures, or is it confined to the covered distribution?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] SUREON covers 170 procedure types, but the paper does not evaluate zero-shot transfer to entirely new procedures, focusing only on held-out videos within known procedures.
- Why unresolved: For broad surgical AI deployment, models must reason about procedures not seen during training; current results may overestimate generalization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Test SureonVLM on a held-out set of completely novel procedure types (e.g., from a different surgical specialty) and measure performance drop relative to in-distribution procedures.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can the multi-agent data curation pipeline be reproduced using open-source models instead of proprietary GPT-5 agents without sacrificing annotation quality?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The Data Curation Pipeline uses "specialized generator and validator GPT-5 agents" to extract QA pairs, making the approach dependent on a closed, costly API.
- Why unresolved: Proprietary dependence hinders reproducibility, scalability, and accessibility for broader research communities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Re-run the pipeline using state-of-the-art open-source VLMs (e.g., Llama-4, Qwen3) as generator/validator agents; compare the resulting dataset’s quality against GPT-5–derived SUREON via expert validation.

### Open Question 5
- Question: Why does Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision during SFT not improve multiple-choice accuracy yet is essential for stable GRPO training in SureonVLM-R1?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The Ablation study states: "CoT supervision (C) does not improve metrics, but is essential for stable GRPO training, as models trained without CoT during SFT failed to generate  during post-training."
- Why unresolved: This paradox—CoT stabilizing RL without boosting performance—suggests unknown interactions between SFT structure and RL reward shaping in surgical VLMs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyze training dynamics (e.g., token distributions, loss curves) with and without CoT during SFT; probe whether CoT shapes the latent reasoning space even when final accuracy is unchanged.

## Limitations
- Pedagogical selectivity of surgical lectures leads to underrepresentation of routine operative steps, potentially biasing the model.
- Evaluation relies on LLM-as-judge, which may reward linguistic fluency over clinical accuracy.
- The SUREON test set contains only 354 multiple-choice items, limiting statistical power on fine-grained reasoning distinctions.
