# Learning Pore-scale Multiphase Flow from 4D Velocimetry

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12516
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12516
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Pore-Scale GNS framework achieves near-perfect accuracy (R²=0.9999, Dice>0.98) and 10³–10⁴× speedup over DNS for predicting pore-scale multiphase flow from 4D velocimetry.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents Pore-Scale GNS, a multimodal machine learning framework that predicts pore-scale multiphase flow from time-resolved micro-CT velocimetry data. It couples a graph neural network for Lagrangian tracer-particle velocity prediction with a 3D U-Net for voxelized fluid interface evolution, using the imaged pore geometry as a boundary constraint. Trained on capillary-dominated flow (Ca≈10⁻⁶), the model accurately captures transient dynamics including Haines jumps over 30-step rollouts (~7.5 s physical time) and demonstrates partial zero-shot generalization to a different rock type. The framework provides a 10³–10⁴-fold speedup compared to direct numerical simulations, enabling rapid digital experiments for subsurface storage applications.

## Method Summary
The framework consists of two main components that iterate: a Graph Network Simulator (GNS) predicts tracer-particle velocities by encoding particle states and local pore geometry patches, applying message passing, and outputting velocities. An interpolation step scatters these velocities onto a voxel grid and max-pools them to a coarser resolution. A 3D U-Net then consumes a history of fluid interfaces, the pooled velocity field, and the pore geometry mask to predict the next interface. The updated interface feeds back to condition the GNS in the next step, forming a closed loop. Both networks are trained autoregressively on experimental sequences with noise injection to improve stability, while the static pore geometry is encoded once and used as a shared condition.

## Key Results
- Near-perfect velocity trajectory prediction (R² = 0.9999) and high interface fidelity (Dice > 0.98) on test data.
- Capable of 30-step autoregressive rollout (~7.5 s physical time) with sustained accuracy, capturing Haines jumps.
- Zero-shot transfer to Ketton limestone shows partial generalization (NRMSEp99 = 16.4%).
- Achieves 10³–10⁴× computational speedup relative to conventional direct numerical simulations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

1. **Multimodal coupling of GNS and U-Net**  
   - Claim: Coupling a Lagrangian GNS with an Eulerian 3D U-Net leverages complementary strengths to predict both velocity fields and interfaces accurately.  
   - Mechanism: GNS models particle motion via message passing on a dynamically reconstructed graph, capturing long-range correlations. The 3D U-Net predicts interface evolution with skip connections to preserve spatial details. Each step, GNS velocities are interpolated to a grid and conditioned into the U-Net; the updated interface constrains the next GNS step, with pore geometry as the shared boundary.  
   - Core assumption: Velocity and interface dynamics are tightly interdependent; decoupling causes artifacts.  
   - Evidence: Ablation studies show removing velocity conditioning in U-Net drops Dice from 0.94 to 0.84 in Haines jump regions; removing interface/geometry from GNS leads to particle escape.  
   - Break condition: Decoupling results in nonphysical artifacts and boundary violations.

2. **Pore geometry as a conditional prior**  
   - Claim: Conditioning on the imaged pore geometry enforces physical admissibility and captures geometry-controlled heterogeneities.  
   - Mechanism: The static dry CT scan is CNN-encoded and injected as a spatial bias into GNS node features and U-Net channels, masking solids and providing local context that modulates predictions according to void-space topology.  
   - Core assumption: Geometry is a first-order control on capillary-dominated flow.  
   - Evidence: Without geometry, GNS predicts particle motion that trespasses solids; zero-shot transfer to Ketton limestone degrades (NRMSEp99 rises from 12.5% to 16.4%).  
   - Break condition: Omitting geometry yields boundary violations and poor generalization.

3. **Learning from high-resolution 4D velocimetry**  
   - Claim: Autoregressive training on time-resolved experimental data implicitly learns complex non-equilibrium physics (e.g., Haines jumps) without explicit solvers.  
   - Mechanism: The model is trained to predict one-step future states given past frames and then rolled out. Experimental data include sub-pore effects (thin films, contact line dynamics) at micrometer scale, providing a physically grounded signal.  
   - Core assumption: High-resolution experimental measurements faithfully encode cumulative influence of fine-scale physics at the observed scale.  
   - Evidence: Model captures transient nonlocal perturbations and abrupt interface rearrangements; rollout matches experiments for 30 steps.  
   - Break condition: Errors accumulate beyond ~70 frames; zero-shot transfer limited.

## Foundational Learning

- **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)**  
  - Why needed: To model irregular Lagrangian particle dynamics in 3D pore space with message passing that naturally encodes long-range interactions, which are critical for capillary-dominated flow where perturbations propagate nonlocally.

  - Why needed: For accurate segmentation and prediction of fluid interfaces in voxel grids, leveraging encoder-decoder structure with skip connections to preserve fine details and multiscale morphology during evolution.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dry pore geometry + initial particle states + initial interface → GNS (predict velocities) → Interpolation (scatter to grid + max-pooling) → U-Net (predict interface) → Interface update → condition GNS (loop) [geometry injected throughout].
- Critical path: The multimodal inference loop (Algorithm 1) is the core runtime: GNS velocity prediction must complete before U-Net interface update, which in turn conditions the next GNS step; all steps depend on the shared geometry input.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Graph vs. grid: Graph excels at particle-based long-range correlations; grid excels at continuous interface morphology. Coupling adds computational overhead but ensures consistency.
  - Downsampling: Pooling preserves velocity extremes but loses fine details; slicing loses too much information.
  - Temporal depth: Using Nin=2 interface frames balances history and memory; longer histories may improve smoothness but increase cost.
- Failure signatures:
  - Unphysical particle trajectories crossing fluid interfaces (detected by tracking particle labels against interface mask).
  - Interface artifacts like disconnected fluid blobs or boundary violations (low Dice score in Haines jump regions).
  - Gradual velocity MAE increase and Dice score decrease in long rollouts beyond 30 steps.
  - Zero-shot performance drop on dissimilar pore structures (e.g., Ketton vs. sintered glass, NRMSEp99 increase).
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Single-step validation: Predict next frame from ground-truth history on test set; report velocity MAE, Dice score, and visualize sample predictions.
  2. Rollout stability: Autoregressively predict 30 steps from initial conditions; track error metrics over time and compare with ground-truth trajectories.
  3. Generalization probe: Apply trained model to a held-out experiment from the same sintered-glass sample (Exp. β) to test boundary-condition generalization, then to a different rock type (Ketton) for zero-shot capability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Generalization to diverse materials, wettability, and flow regimes**: The current study uses a single capillary-dominated dataset from sintered glass; only one zero-shot test on Ketton limestone; no variation in wettability or capillary number. Future work should validate across multiple 4D velocimetry datasets covering different rock types, wettabilities, and capillary numbers.
- **Reducing autoregressive error accumulation**: Errors increase beyond ~70 frames, limiting simulation horizon. Proposed mitigations include larger diverse training data, improved temporal coupling, or hybrid physics-informed constraints.
- **Upscaling to macroscopic properties**: The model outputs pore-scale fields but not constitutive relationships for continuum simulators. Needed evidence: virtual experiments with varying boundary conditions, computing effective properties over REVs, and validating against core-flood or upscaling results.

## Limitations
- Zero-shot transfer to a different rock type shows performance degradation (NRMSEp99 16.4% vs. 12.5% in-distribution), indicating sensitivity to geometric domain shift.
- Autoregressive rollout errors accumulate beyond ~30 steps, limiting simulation duration for rare or long-timescale events.
- Training relies on a single experimental sequence from one sintered-glass sample; limited diversity in flow regimes and rock types may affect robustness to unseen conditions.

## Confidence
- Multimodal coupling efficacy: **High** (ablation studies show clear degradation when decoupled).
- Pore geometry conditioning: **High** (particle trespass and zero-shot drop without geometry).
- Haines jump capture: **Medium** (compelling visuals but lacking quantitative jump-specific metrics).
- Speedup claim (10³–10⁴×): **High** (direct runtime comparison provided).
