# Neighborhood Stability as a Measure of Nearest Neighbor Searchability

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.16673
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16673
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Clustering-NSM and point-NSM measures enable assessing ANNS searchability from data alone, with clustering-NSM showing strong correlation (ρ up to 0.86) with ANNS accuracy and outperforming traditional clustering metrics

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of determining whether a dataset is "searchable" for clustering-based Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search (ANNS) without requiring empirical testing. The authors introduce two novel neighborhood stability measures: clustering-NSM (an internal clustering quality metric) and point-NSM (a dataset clusterability metric). Both measures leverage nearest neighbor relationships, making them applicable across different distance metrics including Euclidean, cosine, and inner product distances. Through extensive experiments on 11 ANNS datasets and 10 image clustering datasets, clustering-NSM demonstrates strong correlation with ANNS accuracy and external clustering metrics, significantly outperforming traditional indices like Dunn and Davies-Bouldin.

## Method Summary
The authors propose two related measures based on nearest neighbor stability. Clustering-NSM evaluates the quality of clustering assignments by measuring how consistently nearest neighbors are assigned to the same clusters. Point-NSM assesses the overall clusterability of a dataset by examining the stability of nearest neighbor relationships across the entire dataset. Both measures rely on the intuition that in a well-clustered dataset, nearest neighbors should belong to the same cluster and maintain stable relationships. The theoretical analysis shows that for spherical flat clusterings, the expected clustering-NSM equals the average point-NSM, with the point-NSM distribution concentrating around this mean. The measures are validated through correlation analysis with ANNS accuracy and established clustering metrics across multiple diverse datasets.

## Key Results
- Clustering-NSM achieves Spearman correlation up to 0.86 with ANNS accuracy across 11 datasets
- Clustering-NSM significantly outperforms Dunn and Davies-Bouldin indices in predicting ANNS performance
- Point-NSM correlates with HNSW accuracy, suggesting broader applicability beyond clustering-based ANNS
- Theoretical guarantee: for spherical flat clusterings, expected clustering-NSM equals average point-NSM

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of NSM measures stems from the fundamental relationship between data structure and nearest neighbor stability. When data forms well-separated clusters, nearest neighbors are more likely to belong to the same cluster and maintain consistent relationships across different distance computations. This stability directly translates to better performance in ANNS algorithms that rely on these neighborhood relationships for search. The measures capture this inherent structure without requiring external query sets or labels, making them practical tools for assessing searchability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Nearest Neighbor Stability**: Measures how consistently nearest neighbor relationships are maintained across different computations; needed to quantify data structure that impacts ANNS performance; quick check: stable neighbors indicate well-separated clusters
- **Spherical Flat Clusterings**: Assumes clusters are approximately spherical and well-separated; needed for theoretical guarantees connecting point-NSM and clustering-NSM; quick check: verify clusters have similar variance and are roughly spherical
- **Distance Metrics**: Euclidean, cosine, and inner product distances can all be used with NSM measures; needed to ensure broad applicability across different data types; quick check: confirm distance metric matches data characteristics

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data -> NSM Computation -> Stability Score -> Searchability Assessment

**Critical Path**: The core computation involves finding nearest neighbors, clustering data (if needed), then computing stability measures based on neighbor-cluster consistency. This path is O(n·d) for finding neighbors plus clustering cost.

**Design Tradeoffs**: NSM measures trade computational overhead for predictive power without requiring query sets. They provide interpretable scores but assume nearest neighbor relationships are meaningful indicators of searchability.

**Failure Signatures**: Low NSM scores indicate poor clustering structure or overlapping classes. High scores don't guarantee optimal ANNS performance but suggest good potential. The measures may struggle with non-spherical or nested cluster structures.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees only apply to spherical flat clusterings, limiting applicability to other clustering structures
- Assumes nearest neighbor queries are stable across different ANNS stages without thorough exploration of query stability under varying parameters
- Correlation does not establish definitive causation between NSM values and ANNS accuracy

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Strong correlation results between NSM measures and ANNS accuracy across multiple datasets; practical utility demonstrated through extensive experiments

**Medium confidence**: Theoretical guarantees for spherical flat clusterings; broader applicability beyond clustering-based ANNS suggested but not fully established
