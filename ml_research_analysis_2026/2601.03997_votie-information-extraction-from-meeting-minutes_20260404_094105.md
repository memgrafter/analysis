# VotIE: Information Extraction from Meeting Minutes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03997
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03997
- **Reference Count:** 17
- **Primary Result:** XLM-R-CRF achieves 93.2% exact-match macro F1 in-domain, but degrades sharply on cross-municipality generalization, while few-shot Gemini 2.5 Pro shows superior robustness at higher computational cost.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces VotIE, a novel task for extracting structured voting events (subjects, voter roles, stances, counts) from heterogeneous Portuguese municipal meeting minutes. Using the CitiLink corpus, it benchmarks fine-tuned discriminative encoders (with CRF) against generative LLMs. Results show XLM-R-CRF achieves state-of-the-art performance (93.2% F1) in standard in-domain evaluation. However, in a cross-municipality leave-one-municipality-out test, encoder performance collapses while few-shot Gemini 2.5 Pro degrades far less, indicating greater robustness to unseen administrative writing styles. Despite this, the high computational cost of generative models leads to the conclusion that lightweight fine-tuned encoders remain more practical for large-scale deployment when annotated target data exists.

## Method Summary
The study frames VotIE as a span extraction task, converting raw meeting minutes into structured voting event records. It evaluates two paradigms on the Portuguese CitiLink corpus: 1) discriminative models fine-tuned for token-level BIO sequence labeling, with XLM-RoBERTa as the encoder and a CRF layer for decoding; 2) generative LLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro) prompted with a schema and few-shot examples to produce JSON outputs. Models are tested under two evaluation regimes: a standard in-domain train/test split and a cross-municipality leave-one-municipality-out (LOMO) setting to assess generalization across heterogeneous administrative writing styles. Performance is measured using exact-match and relaxed-match macro-F1 for span alignment.

## Key Results
- XLM-R-CRF achieves SOTA 93.2% exact-match macro F1 in the in-domain evaluation, significantly outperforming other discriminative and generative baselines.
- In cross-municipality LOMO tests, encoder performance degrades sharply (e.g., F1 drops to 17.5% for municipality M06), while few-shot Gemini shows substantially smaller performance declines.
- Gemini’s robustness comes with a high spurious error rate (40% of its errors) and greater computational cost, leading to the conclusion that fine-tuned encoders are more practical for large-scale processing where annotated data is available.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Fine-tuned encoder-CRF models achieve state-of-the-art exact-match span extraction in homogeneous in-domain evaluations by effectively learning local syntactic patterns and transition constraints.
- **Mechanism:** The model learns a discriminative mapping from token embeddings (via XLM-R) to BIO tags, with a CRF layer explicitly modeling label transition probabilities to enforce valid sequence structures (e.g., `B-SUBJ` followed by `I-SUBJ`). This exploits the consistent lexical and syntactic patterns within a single municipality's minutes.
- **Core assumption:** The distribution of text segments and their annotation patterns (boundaries, types) is stationary within the training and test splits of a given municipality.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "fine-tuned encoders, specifically XLM-R-CRF, achieve the strongest performance, reaching 93.2% macro F1"
  - [section 5/Table 2] XLM-R-CRF's 93.2% exact-match F1 significantly outperforms other discriminative and generative models under standard in-domain split.
  - [corpus] The corpus signal notes related work on "metadata extraction" and "summarization" from the same CitiLink minutes, indicating a coherent, annotated dataset with consistent local style per municipality.
- **Break condition:** Performance degrades sharply when encountering a municipality with a distinct administrative writing style (lexical choices, narrative templates) not represented in training, as revealed by cross-municipality LOMO evaluations (e.g., F1 drop to 17.5% for M06).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Few-shot generative LLMs demonstrate superior robustness to cross-municipality domain shifts because they rely on high-level semantic understanding and instruction-following rather than surface-level pattern memorization.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) is prompted with a schema and a few examples to generate structured JSON outputs. It uses its pretrained knowledge to parse the *intent* of the voting description (e.g., identifying a "subject" and "voter-against" regardless of phrasing) rather than learning municipality-specific lexical cues. This zero/few-shot paradigm avoids overfitting to a single source domain.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM possesses sufficient parametric knowledge of administrative discourse and voting event semantics to abstract away from specific local terminologies and formatting conventions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "few-shot LLMs demonstrate greater robustness, with significantly smaller declines in performance" in cross-municipality tests.
  - [section 5/Table 3] Gemini's exact-match F1 drops are substantially smaller than DeBERTa-CRF's across held-out municipalities (e.g., M06: 45.4% vs 17.5%).
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors (e.g., "CitiLink-Minutes") highlight the "heterogeneous formats and writing styles" of municipal minutes, creating the domain shift that tests robustness.
- **Break condition:** The mechanism is constrained by the LLM's instruction-following accuracy and tendency toward spurious generation, leading to over-extraction (high recall, low precision) as seen in the error analysis (40% of Gemini's errors are spurious).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The practical deployment trade-off is between precision-boundary accuracy (encoder-CRF) and generalization robustness (LLM), with computational cost as a decisive factor for large-scale processing of historical archives.
- **Mechanism:** Encoder-CRF models, once fine-tuned, offer deterministic, low-latency inference optimized for a specific data distribution. LLMs, while adaptable, incur high per-call API costs and latency, making them expensive for processing thousands of documents. The choice hinges on whether annotated target-data exists (favoring encoder) or not (favoring LLM).
- **Core assumption:** For municipalities with sufficient annotated minutes, the performance ceiling of a fine-tuned encoder on in-domain data outweighs the generalization benefit of an LLM, given practical resource constraints.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "lightweight fine-tuned encoders remain a more practical option for large-scale, real-world deployment" and "high computational cost of generative models currently constrains their practicality."
  - [section 7] Conclusion reiterates the trade-off: encoders superior for "precise span extraction," LLMs for "low-resource or zero-shot settings."
  - [corpus] The corpus context involves a "CitiLink" project for enhancing "transparency and citizen engagement," implying a need for scalable, cost-effective processing of many municipal records.
- **Break condition:** The conclusion reverses if LLM inference costs drop dramatically or if the target deployment scenario involves hundreds of diverse municipalities with zero annotated data, where encoder performance would be unusably low.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Sequence Labeling (BIO) & CRF**
  - **Why needed here:** The VotIE task is framed as span extraction via token-level classification. Understanding BIO tagging (Begin, Inside, Outside) and how a CRF layer enforces valid transitions is essential to grasp why XLM-R-CRF achieves tight boundary precision.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might a plain linear classifier on top of XLM-R perform worse than XLM-R-CRF for this task, given the same embeddings?

- **Concept: Domain Generalization & Distribution Shift**
  - **Why needed here:** The core challenge is heterogeneity across municipalities. This concept explains why a model trained on M01–M05 fails on M06—the test data comes from a different *style* distribution, not just new topics.
  - **Quick check question:** If you fine-tune on five municipalities, what specific aspects of the text (e.g., phrasing, formatting, template usage) might change in the held-out sixth municipality to cause a performance drop?

- **Concept: Few-Shot Learning with LLMs**
  - **Why needed here:** The generative paradigm uses 5 examples in the prompt to adapt the LLM to the task without weight updates. This is key to its generalization ability.
  - **Quick check question:** In the few-shot setup, what is the risk if the provided examples inadvertently contain municipality-specific lexical quirks?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input Preprocessor -> Encoder Backbone -> Task Head -> Decoder/Extractor -> Evaluation Module
  For LLM path: Input Preprocessor -> Prompt Constructor -> LLM API -> JSON Parser -> Alignment Engine -> Evaluation Module

- **Critical path:** Data loading → Tokenization & windowing (512-token chunks, 50 overlap) → Forward pass through encoder → Head decoding (Viterbi for CRF / API call for LLM) → Span reconstruction (BIO-to-span or substring match) → Metric calculation (exact/relaxed macro-F1).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **CRF vs. Linear head:** CRF improves boundary consistency by modeling transitions but adds decoding complexity. Evidence: XLM-R-CRF gains 8.3 F1 over XLM-R-Linear.
  - **Encoder choice:** Multilingual (XLM-R) outperforms Portuguese-specific (BERTimbau) possibly due to better representation of diverse administrative phrasing.
  - **Generative vs. Discriminative:** Trade-off is precision/recall balance vs. robustness. Encoders have lower spurious errors; LLMs have higher recall but spurious rate (40% of errors).

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Boundary errors:** Most common, especially for long, complex `SUBJ` spans. Signal: model predicts `B-SUBJ` correctly but `I-SUBJ` tags drift or end prematurely.
  - **Overgeneration (LLM):** High spurious rate indicates LLM "hallucinates" voting arguments not present in text. Check: many predicted spans with no gold overlap.
  - **Municipality-specific degradation:** Sharp F1 drop on a single held-out municipality (e.g., M06 with DeBERTa: 17.5%) indicates overfitting to local style—review training data distribution.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate standard benchmark:** Train XLM-R-CRF on the provided train/val/test split. Verify you achieve ~93% macro F1 exact-match. This establishes baseline reproducibility.
  2. **Run LOMO for one municipality:** Hold out M01, train on M02-M06, evaluate on M01. Confirm significant drop (e.g., ~46-67% F1 for encoder) and that Gemini performs relatively better. This validates the generalization gap claim.
  3. **Error type ablation:** On a held-out set, manually inspect 50 errors from XLM-R-CRF and Gemini. Quantify % of Missing, Spurious, Boundary, Type errors. Expect: Encoder—mostly Boundary/Missing; Gemini—high Spurious. This confirms the error analysis pattern.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the VotIE extraction schema be extended to identify and disambiguate multiple distinct voting events occurring within a single text segment?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "meeting minutes occasionally describe multiple distinct votes within the same segment... a sequence labeling approach may successfully identify all entities but cannot explicitly link each VOTER span to its corresponding SUBJECT or OUTCOME span. Future work should explore Relation Extraction (RE) or Event Extraction paradigms." (Section 8)
- **Why unresolved:** The current span-based approach treats all arguments in a segment as one event, failing to capture event boundaries when multiple votes are discussed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Annotating the corpus with event-level links between arguments, implementing an RE/event extraction model, and evaluating its ability to separate and correctly assign arguments to distinct voting events compared to the baseline.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent can integrating coreference resolution improve VotIE's ability to consistently identify and link voter mentions (pronouns, roles) to specific individuals across document segments?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "voters are frequently referred to by role (e.g., 'the Councilor') or pronominal references rather than proper names, often relying on earlier context... Integrating coreference resolution is therefore a necessary next step." (Section 8)
- **Why unresolved:** The evaluation treats segments in isolation, missing cross-segment context needed to resolve coreferent mentions to real-world voters, limiting analysis of individual voting behavior.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Adding coreference annotations for voter mentions in the corpus, integrating a coreference resolver into the extraction pipeline, and measuring changes in voter mention consistency and the accuracy of aggregated voting records per individual.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can unsupervised domain adaptation techniques (e.g., domain adversarial training, style transfer) effectively reduce the performance gap for encoder models in cross-municipality generalization, and at what computational cost relative to few-shot LLMs?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Adrressing this may requires larger, geographically diverse datasets or unsupervised domain adaptation techniques to improve generalization." (Section 8). Also, LOMO results show encoders degrade sharply (e.g., M06: 17.5% vs 45.4% for Gemini).
- **Why unresolved:** Encoders overfit to municipal-specific phrasing; domain adaptation could promote style-invariant features but hasn't been tested for this task.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Applying domain adaptation methods (e.g., DANN, style augmentation) to XLM-R-CRF in LOMO settings, reporting exact-match F1 improvements on held-out municipalities, and comparing training/inference cost to the few-shot LLM baseline.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the observed advantage of generative LLMs in cross-municipality robustness generalize to other languages with different administrative writing conventions, or is it specific to Portuguese municipal records?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "the syntactic and stylistic patterns of administrative Portuguese differ significantly from other languages. Further research is needed to assess whether the architectural trends observed here... generalize to other linguistic contexts." (Section 8)
- **Why unresolved:** The study is monolingual (Portuguese); linguistic and administrative variation across languages could alter the encoder vs. LLM generalization trade-off.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicating the benchmark and LOMO experiments in at least two other languages (e.g., Spanish, English) using comparable corpora, then statistically comparing the relative performance drops of encoders vs. LLMs across languages.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** To what extent can advanced prompting, decoding constraints, or model scaling mitigate the boundary alignment issues of generative LLMs in VotIE, potentially closing the exact-match performance gap with fine-tuned encoders?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper shows a huge exact-match gap (LLM 64.1 F1 vs encoder 93.2) but a smaller gap in relaxed match (79.5 vs 95.9). The authors note LLMs "struggle to match the strict token-level boundaries" but only tested one model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) with basic decoding. The limitation discusses higher cost but not boundary alignment as a fundamental barrier.
- **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether the boundary issue is an inherent limitation of generative extraction for this task or a surmountable engineering problem (e.g., via better prompting, fine-tuning smaller LLMs, or constrained decoding).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments varying prompting strategies (e.g., character offsets, boundary tokens), decoding constraints (e.g., schema enforcement), and model scales (e.g., smaller fine-tuned LLMs) on exact-match F1, while measuring any increase in computational cost relative to the encoder baseline.

## Limitations
- **Encoder brittleness to domain shift:** The 93.2% in-domain F1 collapses (e.g., to 17.5% on M06) under cross-municipality shifts, indicating severe overfitting to local administrative templates and terminology. The specific stylistic factors driving this gap are not fully dissected.
- **LLM spurious generation:** Gemini’s robustness comes with a 40% spurious error rate, likely due to over-reliance on semantic priors without strict grounding. The prompt design’s role in mitigating this is unclear.
- **Cost-benefit trade-off assumptions:** The conclusion favoring encoders rests on current LLM inference costs and latency. This may not hold if API pricing drops or if processing very low-resource municipalities (zero annotations) where encoder performance is unusable.

## Confidence
- **Encoder-CRF in-domain SOTA:** **High** – Directly supported by Table 2 (93.2% F1) and consistent gains over baselines.
- **LLM cross-domain robustness:** **High** – Table 3 shows smaller LOMO performance drops for Gemini vs. discriminative models; error analysis confirms distinct error profiles.
- **Deployment practicality of encoders:** **Medium** – Based on computational cost arguments in the conclusion but lacks empirical scaling benchmarks (e.g., cost/hour for 10k documents).
