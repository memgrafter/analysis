# When Remembering and Planning are Worth it: Navigating under Change

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.15274
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15274
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A composite agent (ProbMap+LeastVisited) reduces mean steps to food by 2.5× on 15×15 grids (80 vs 200) and up to 30× on larger grids, approaching theoretical minima under low environmental change and motion noise.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates navigation strategies for agents operating in non-stationatory grid-world foraging environments where barriers and food locations change daily, and localization is uncertain due to motion noise. The authors evaluate a spectrum of strategies from memoryless (Random, Greedy) to those employing episodic memory and planning. The top-performing strategy, **ProbMap**, maintains per-location probability distributions over barrier and food presence by continuously updating episodic memories. It constructs probabilistic maps for A* path planning and switches to a **LeastVisited** exploration strategy when food location is unknown. In experiments, ProbMap+LeastVisited dramatically outperforms a mixed-greedy baseline, with its advantage scaling with grid size but diminishing under high noise or rapid environmental change. The study highlights the value of maintaining and updating structured episodic memories for efficient replanning in dynamic, partially observable worlds.

## Method Summary
The core method is the **ProbMap** strategy within a composite agent architecture. ProbMap uses **two-level non-stationary probability learning**: for each grid cell, it maintains memory-types based on observation age and object type (BARRIER, FOOD, EMPTY), with separate fast (within-day) and slow (daily) moving-average predictors updated from episodic memories. For navigation, it samples a barrier map and a food goal from these predictive distributions, then runs A* search. If execution fails (e.g., encounters an unsampled barrier), it immediately replans. When food location is unknown, the agent uses **LeastVisited**, a count-based exploration heuristic that biases action selection toward low-visitation areas via path integration. The composite agent cycles between strategies (round-robin with progressive time budgets) to handle both search and navigation phases. The evaluation compares this composite against baselines (Random, Greedy, Biased) and an Oracle in grid-worlds of varying size, barrier density, change-rate, and motion noise.

## Key Results
- ProbMap+LeastVisited achieves a **2.5× reduction** in mean steps to food compared to the mixed-greedy baseline on 15×15 grids (80 vs 200 steps).
- The performance **advantage scales with grid size**, growing to up to **30×** on 50×50 environments.
- Performance **approaches the theoretical minimum** (Oracle) when barrier change-rate and motion noise are low, but **degrades significantly** under high change-rate (>0.3) or high motion noise (>0.02).
- continual memory updating is essential; disabling adaptation causes rapid performance collapse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Adaptive Non-Stationary Probability Learning
- **Claim:** Continuous updating of location-specific probability distributions from episodic memories enables robust navigation despite daily changes in barrier and food locations.
- **Mechanism:** For each grid location, the agent maintains memory-types based on the age of observations (days since recorded) and object seen (BARRIER, FOOD, EMPTY). Two-level updating balances fast within-day adaptation (using a moving average with window K=10) and slower daily stability (window K=5), allowing predictions to reflect recent changes while resisting noise.
- **Core assumption:** The environment exhibits temporal regularity—recent memories are moderately predictive of current states—but changes slowly enough that daily aggregates remain useful.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "non-stationary probability learning techniques to keep updating its (episodic) memories"; [section 4.5.2] Describes two-level updating and moving average predictors; limited direct corpus evidence.
- **Break condition:** High barrier change-rate (e.g., >0.3) or motion noise (>0.02) corrupts the predictive distributions, causing frequent planning failures and performance degradation (Fig. 8, Fig. 9).

### Mechanism 2: Probabilistic Map Construction and Replanning
- **Claim:** Constructing barrier maps and selecting food goals from memory-derived distributions, then planning with A*, yields near-optimal paths even with incomplete and noisy knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** At each planning iteration, sample barrier presence from the best-performing memory-type predictor per location (based on logloss), and select a food goal from locations with food probability >0.01 (weighted by probability). Run A* search; if execution fails (e.g., unexpected barrier), immediately replan.
- **Core assumption:** The probabilistic map, though biased by limited exploration and localization errors, is sufficiently accurate for planning feasible paths most of the time.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "builds maps for A* path planning"; [section 4.5.5] Details sampling and iterative A* with replanning; corpus supports map-based navigation generally.
- **Break condition:** On day 1 or after large food shifts, no location meets food probability threshold, halting planning; sampled barriers may block all paths within time budget.

### Mechanism 3: Integrated Exploration via LeastVisited
- **Claim:** The LeastVisited strategy efficiently reduces search time when food location is unknown by promoting coverage of unexplored areas.
- **Mechanism:** Maintains a visit count per estimated location (via path integration), reset daily. When active, selects actions leading to adjacent cells with the lowest visit counts, implementing a self-avoiding walk bias.
- **Core assumption:** Unexplored regions have higher expected reward (food) or lead to food faster, given limited sensing.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "exploration via LeastVisited"; [section 4.3] Defines LeastVisited with visit-count mapping; corpus mentions exploration (e.g., Levy walks) but not this specific method.
- **Break condition:** In large, high-barrier environments, it may take many steps to discover food, but it complements planning by handling initial search phases.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Non-Stationary Environment Adaptation  
  **Why needed here:** The world changes daily; learners must update beliefs without catastrophic forgetting.  
  **Quick check:** How does two-level updating prevent a single noisy day from dominating long-term predictions?
- **Concept:** Path Planning under Partial Observability  
  **Why needed here:** Plans are made on probabilistic maps derived from limited, biased experiences.  
  **Quick check:** Why use A* on sampled barrier maps instead of a deterministic shortest-path algorithm?
- **Concept:** Strategy Switching and Composite Control  
  **Why needed here:** No single strategy handles both search (unknown food) and navigation (known food) optimally.  
  **Quick check:** What triggers a switch from LeastVisited to ProbMap during a day?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
EpisodicMemory -> MemoryTypePredictors (daily/within-day) -> AStarPlanner & GoalSelector; VisitCountMap -> LeastVisited; StrategyScheduler (round-robin with progressive budgets) orchestrates activation.

**Critical path:**  
Daily cycle: reset strategies (clear visit counts, clone daily->within-day predictors). Per tick: all strategies get `pre_action` update (record adjacent observations); active strategy (scheduler) selects action; all get `post_action`; execute. ProbMap triggers replanning on execution failure. Switch active strategy on failure or budget expiration.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Memory vs. performance: More episodic days stored improves predictions but costs memory.  
- Planning depth vs. speed: Longer A* budgets find better paths but delay execution.  
- Robustness vs. complexity: Using multiple strategies covers search/plan phases but increases control complexity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Sustained high step counts across days.  
- Frequent strategy switches (loggable).  
- High replanning counts (in ProbMap).  
- Persistent visit-count inflation (in LeastVisited).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline comparison:** Run all agent variants on 15×15 grids (Table 1) to establish ProbMap+LeastVisited superiority over greedy and random.  
2. **Scalability test:** Increase grid size (15×15 to 50×50) to show advantage grows with distance (Fig. 7b).  
3. **Robustness test:** Vary change-rate and motion noise to identify breakpoints where ProbMap’s gain diminishes (Fig. 8, Fig. 9).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How does an agent determine the appropriate spatial granularity for building internal representations?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] "How (and whether) an agent would carve and granularize its sensed and perceived space into locations, to serve its needs, remains open." (Introduction)  
  **Why unresolved:** Assumes fixed grid; real environments require dynamic partitioning.
- **Question:** Can the agent learn an effective ordering of strategies and adaptive time budgets?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] "Over the days, the agent could also learn aspects such as a better ordering of its given strategies or how much time to spent on each strategy." (Section 3.1)  
  **Why unresolved:** Current fixed round-robin may waste time; needs meta-learning.
- **Question:** How can the agent automatically learn and structure memory types beyond the hand-designed age+object scheme?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] "We leave exploring how memory types could be learned and further structured to future work." (Section 4.5.2)  
  **Why unresolved:** Fixed features may not capture predictive context; learning could improve robustness.
- **Question:** Would incremental memory access during planning (on-demand) maintain performance while reducing memory and being more biologically plausible?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] "An alternative implementation (possibly biologically more plausible) would access memories as needed during planning." (Section 4.5.4)  
  **Why unresolved:** Current pre-sampling of full map may be inefficient; on-demand tradeoffs unexplored.
- **Question:** What differential retention policy should prioritize which episodic memories to keep longer, especially for sparse food?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] "As food is important and sparse, it may be good to have a differential policy as to which memories are kept longer." (Section 5.5)  
  **Why unresolved:** Current equal retention up to fixed age window may be suboptimal under memory constraints.

## Limitations
- Algorithmic underspecification: Critical details (e.g., predictor merging, goal sampling, A* budgeting) are vague, risking inconsistent reimplementations.
- Repository dependency: The cited code may be unavailable or outdated; faithful reproduction without it relies on interpretation.
- Limited external validation: The work has low citation count and FMR, indicating minimal independent testing or extension.

## Confidence
- **Adaptive Non-Stationary Probability Learning (two-level updating):** Medium (plausible mechanism, breakpoints identified, but lacks direct ablation).
- **Probabilistic Map Construction and Replanning (A* on sampled maps):** Medium (sound idea but underspecified; performance sensitive to calibration).
- **Integrated Exploration via LeastVisited:** Low (simple heuristic, no independent evaluation, likely suboptimal in complex environments).
- **Overall performance claim (2.5×–30× gain):** Medium (supported by experiments but limited to moderate change/noise; scalability to real-world noise is open).
