# InftyThink+: Effective and Efficient Infinite-Horizon Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06960
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06960
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: InftyThink+ improves accuracy by 21% on AIME24 compared to supervised iterative reasoning, outperforms conventional long CoT RL by a clear margin, reduces inference latency by 32.8% on AIME25, and accelerates RL training by 18.2%.

## Executive Summary
InftyThink+ tackles the quadratic cost, context limits, and lost‑in‑the‑middle effects that plague large reasoning models when scaling chain‑of‑thought. It introduces an end‑to‑end reinforcement learning framework that optimizes the entire iterative reasoning trajectory, learning when to summarize, what to preserve, and how to resume. The two‑stage training first uses supervised learning to establish the iterative format, then RL to refine strategic decisions. On DeepSeek‑R1‑Distill‑Qwen‑1.5B, InftyThink+ achieves a 21% accuracy gain on AIME24 over supervised baselines, beats conventional long‑CoT RL by a clear margin, cuts inference latency by 32.8% on AIME25, and speeds RL training by 18.2%. The approach also generalizes better to out‑of‑distribution benchmarks, demonstrating that learned compression and adaptive iteration dramatically boost both effectiveness and efficiency.

## Method Summary
The method begins with a cold‑start supervised fine‑tuning phase. Long CoT data from OpenThoughts‑114K is transformed into an iterative format: reasoning is split into segments (max 6,000 tokens), and after each segment (except the final conclusion) a summary (max 1,000 tokens) is generated using Qwen3‑4B‑Instruct with task‑specific prompts. Special tokens (`<summary>`, `</summary>`, `<history>`, `</history>`) are added to the tokenizer. DeepSeek‑R1‑Distill‑Qwen‑1.5B is then fine‑tuned on this structured data for 3 epochs using ms‑swift/Megatron‑Core, with a maximum sequence length of 32k tokens.

Reinforcement learning fine‑tuning follows, using verl with SGLang for asynchronous rollouts. For each query, the model generates up to φ = 5 iterations; if it outputs a summary it continues, otherwise (conclusion or malformed output) it terminates. The reward multiplies a task reward (1 if the final answer matches ground truth) by an efficiency reward: 1 – ((n – 1)/φ)², where n is the number of iterations used. Optimization uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a group size of 8, clipping ratios (0.2, 0.26), token‑level loss averaging, and IcePop gradient masking (upper threshold 5.0, lower 0.5). Training runs for 1,000 steps with an actor learning rate of 1e‑6 and a global batch of 128. Evaluation uses SGLang, configurable max_new_tokens (8,192 for InftyThink vs. 32,768 vanilla), temperature 0.7, top_p 0.95, and 32 samples; accuracy is computed with CompassVerifier‑7B.

## Key Results
- +21% accuracy on AIME24 compared to supervised iterative reasoning.
- Outperforms conventional long CoT RL by a clear margin on multiple benchmarks.
- Reduces inference latency by 32.8% on AIME25 and accelerates RL training speed by 18.2%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The RL framework optimizes the full reasoning trajectory, teaching the model to balance correctness with computational cost. The task reward guarantees accuracy, while the efficiency reward encourages aggressive yet safe summarization, yielding fewer tokens and faster inference. Two‑stage training first instills the iterative pattern via supervised data, then refines the policy with RL, avoiding the instability of training from scratch. IcePop masking stabilizes updates by ignoring tokens where training and inference distributions differ. Together, these elements let the model overcome the lost‑in‑the‑middle effect and context limits by distilling intermediate reasoning into compact yet informative summaries that retain all necessary information for subsequent rounds.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
OpenThoughts‑114K → Cold‑start SFT → Actor Model → SGLang Rollout Engine → Reward Computation (Task + Eff) → GRPO Update → (back to Actor Model)

Critical path: The iterative reasoning loop—generate a segment, produce a summary, repeat until conclusion—followed by task verification, reward combination, and policy update.

Design tradeoffs:
- Summary length vs. information retention: shorter summaries compress more but risk losing critical steps; longer summaries preserve detail at the cost of context usage.
- Allowed iterations (φ) vs. latency and reward scaling: larger φ grants flexibility but lowers efficiency reward for small n, potentially disincentivizing summarization.
- Summarizer quality (via Qwen3‑4B) vs. dependence on an external model; errors in early summaries propagate.
- IcePop thresholds: too aggressive masking removes important gradients; too lenient masking fails to mitigate training‑inference distribution shift.

Failure signatures:
- Model never summarizes (always concludes early): reward signal too weak or cold‑start failed to teach format.
- Extremely long or degenerate summaries: summarization module broken or resampling ineffective.
- Training reward plateaus near zero/accuracy unchanged: possible RL misconfiguration or reward computation error.
- High variance in iteration counts: policy updates unstable.

First experiments:
1. **Format compliance check**: Confirm the cold‑start model reliably emits `<summary>` tags in correct positions; measure success rate on a sample.
2. **Baseline evaluation**: Evaluate the cold‑start model on a validation set (e.g., AIME24) without RL to establish accuracy, average iteration count, and token usage.
3. **Ablation of efficiency reward**: Train RL with only task reward; compare accuracy and latency to full reward to quantify R_eff’s contribution.

## Limitations
- Exact chat template used with `tokenizer.apply_chat_template` is not provided, hindering exact replication of tokenization.
- Rollout termination and summary extraction logic are underspecified, leaving ambiguity about how trajectories end and summaries are parsed.
- Task reward relies on proprietary PRIME‑Math verification scripts, which are not released, making full reproducibility of accuracy metrics difficult.

## Confidence
