# TTQ: Activation-Aware Test-Time Quantization to Accelerate LLM Inference On The Fly

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.19296
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19296
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** TTQ dynamically computes activation statistics per prompt during inference to eliminate offline calibration, achieving lower perplexity than AWQ/RTN at 2-5 bit widths and up to 6.3× speedup over FP16 on GPU.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the domain shift problem in activation-aware post-training quantization (PTQ), where offline calibration data may not match the test distribution. It introduces Test-Time Quantization (TTQ), a method that computes activation statistics online for each incoming prompt during inference. TTQ uses a diagonal approximation of the activation correlation matrix to keep overhead negligible, enabling adaptive per-prompt scaling and zero-points without any calibration data. Experiments on OPT, Qwen3, and Gemma3 models show TTQ achieves superior perplexity across 2-5 bit widths compared to static AWQ and RTN. Furthermore, TTQ integrated with low-rank decomposition enables stable extreme 2-bit quantization and delivers significant speedups on GPUs, demonstrating its practicality for accelerating large language and vision-language models.

## Method Summary
TTQ modifies the linear layer forward pass to operate online. For an input activation tensor **X** (dimension *d* × sequence length *T*), it computes a diagonal matrix **D** where each element *D*<sub>ii</sub> = (∥**X**<sub>i,:</sub>∥<sub>2</sub> + λ)<sup>α</sup>, using fixed hyperparameters (α, λ, p=2). The weight matrix **W** is temporarily scaled by **D**<sup>1/2</sup>, then quantized via per-group round-to-nearest (groupsize *g*=32) to obtain integer weights, scales **S**, and zero-points **Z**. The dequantized weights are rescaled by **D**<sup>−1/2</sup> before the matrix multiplication with the original **X**. The computational overhead is O(*dT* + *d'd*) versus O(*d'dT*) for the base operation, which is negligible for large models. An optional static low-rank factorization **W** ≈ **BA** (precomputed via SVD) is added to the quantized path to maintain accuracy under extreme (e.g., 2-bit) quantization.

## Key Results
*   **Perplexity:** TTQ (r=16) averages 15.5 vs AWQ's best 15.7 on OPT-2.7B at 3-bit across WT2/PTB/C4, with no calibration data.
*   **Extreme Quantization:** TTQ with low-rank (r=16) enables stable 2-bit quantization, e.g., on Gemma3-270M, achieving perplexity 19657 vs. 89188 for AWQ (C4 Calib).
*   **Speed:** TTQ (r=0) provides up to 6.3× speedup over FP16 on a Qwen3-32B model with an RTX4090 GPU, with comparable throughput to AWQ.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Elimination of Calibration Domain Shift
*   **Claim:** TTQ adapts quantization parameters online per prompt, eliminating the domain shift caused by mismatched offline calibration data.
*   **Mechanism:** For each input activation **X**, TTQ computes a per-channel diagonal matrix **D** from the L2 norms of each activation channel. This **D** approximates the diagonal of the activation correlation matrix **C** = **XX**<sup>⊤</sup>. The weights are scaled by **D**<sup>1/2</sup>, quantized, and then rescaled by **D**<sup>−1/2</sup>. This process yields activation-aware quantization scales that are tailored to the exact statistics of the current prompt.
*   **Core Assumption:** A diagonal approximation of the correlation matrix is sufficient for effective per-channel scaling, and per-prompt channel norms provide reliable estimates for the required scales.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   Abstract: "dynamically computes activation statistics per prompt during inference to adapt quantization scales and zero-points, eliminating offline calibration needs."
    *   Section 2: "we dynamically calculate the diagonal correlation **D** for incoming tokens **X** on the fly to adapt scale **S** and zero-point **Z**."
    *   Corpus: The AWQ method (218470) relies on offline activation statistics; prior work (248039) highlights domain shift risks from calibration data.

### Mechanism 2: Negligible Online Overhead
*   **Claim:** The extra computation for online statistic calculation is a vanishing fraction of the total matrix multiplication cost for large models and sequences.
*   **Mechanism:** The base linear operation **Y** = **WX** costs O(*d'**d**T*). TTQ adds: computing **D** (O(*dT*)), scaling **W** (O(*d'd*)), quantizing (O(*d'd*)), and rescaling (O(*d'd*)). Total extra overhead is O(*dT* + 3*d'd*). The overhead fraction ρ = O(1/*d'* + 3/*T*) approaches zero as output dimension *d'* and sequence length *T* grow large.
*   **Core Assumption:** Modern LLMs have large dimensions (*d'*, *T* ≫ 1) and are often bandwidth-bound, allowing the extra arithmetic to be hidden or efficiently fused.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   Section 2: Explicit complexity comparison showing ρ → 0.
    *   Section H (Runtime): Empirical speedups over FP16 show the overhead does not negate the benefits of lower precision computation.
    *   Corpus: Works like μ-MoE (32826) demonstrate feasibility of online computation during inference.

### Mechanism 3: Low-Rank Decomposition for Extreme Bits
*   **Claim:** Combining TTQ with a static low-rank approximation mitigates the severe accuracy loss when quantizing to very low bit-widths (e.g., 2-bit).
*   **Mechanism:** A low-rank factorization **W** ≈ **BA** is precomputed (e.g., via SVD). During inference, the forward pass computes **Y** = (**X** **A**<sup>⊤</sup>) **B**<sup>⊤</sup> + **W**<sub>*q*</sub> **X**, where **W**<sub>*q*</sub> is the extreme quantized weight (2-bit) via TTQ. The low-rank path **BA** captures the principal components of **W**, leaving a residual matrix that is less sensitive to extreme quantization noise.
*   **Core Assumption:** The dominant singular subspace of **W** is well-approximated by a low-rank factor, and the remaining residual matrix can be effectively quantized to ultra-low bits when combined with activation-aware online scaling.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   Section 2: Describes integration of TTQ with low-rank decomposition.
    *   Table 3: For Gemma3-270M at 2-bit, TTQ(r=16) has perplexity 19657.0 vs. TTQ(r=0) which diverges; AWQ (C4 Calib) gets 89188.4.
    *   Corpus: Mixed-precision quantization using SVD (9790) supports the principle of using low-rank for sensitive components.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Post-training Quantization (PTQ) and Calibration**
    *   **Why Needed:** TTQ is a PTQ method operating at test time. Understanding offline PTQ (RTN, AWQ) and the purpose of calibration (collecting representative activation statistics to set quantization parameters) is essential to grasp the domain shift problem TTQ aims to solve.
    *   **Quick Check:** "What is calibration in PTQ? Why might it cause domain shift for downstream tasks or varying input distributions?"

*   **Concept: Activation-aware Quantization (e.g., AWQ)**
    *   **Why Needed:** TTQ directly extends the activation-aware principle of AWQ to the online setting. Knowing how AWQ uses offline activation statistics (e.g., per-channel scaling factors derived from activation magnitudes) to protect salient weights is key to understanding TTQ's diagonal matrix **D**.
    *   **Quick Check:** "How does AWQ differ from simple round-to-nearest (RTN) quantization? What is the purpose of the diagonal matrix **D** in the AWQ formula?"

*   **Concept: Low-rank Model Compression**
    *   **Why Needed:** TTQ uses a static low-rank decomposition to handle extreme quantization. Understanding how a matrix **W** is approximated as **BA** (where **B** and **A** are thin) and how this reduces computation (via two smaller matrix multiplies) and the trade-off with reconstruction error is necessary.
    *   **Quick Check:** "How does low-rank decomposition reduce FLOPs? What is the trade-off between compression rank *r* and model accuracy?"

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Input Activation Buffer (X) -> Diagonal Correlation Calculator -> Weight Prescaler -> Groupwise Quantizer -> Rescaler -> (Optional: Low-rank Projection Units) -> Output Combiner -> Efficient GEMM Kernel (e.g., Marlin). The low-rank path (A, B) runs in parallel and combines at the output combiner.
*   **Critical Path** (per linear layer without low-rank):
    1.  Receive activation tensor **X**.
    2.  Compute per-channel diagonal **D** = (∥**X**<sub>i,:</sub>∥<sub>2</sub> + λ)<sup>α</sup>.
    3.  Temporarily scale weight **W** by **D**<sup>1/2</sup>.
    4.  Apply groupwise round-to-nearest quantization to scaled **W** to get integer weights **W**<sub>int</sub>, per-group scales **S**, and zero-points **Z**.
    5.  Dequantize: **Ŵ**′ = **W**<sub>int</sub> * **S** + **Z**.
    6.  Rescale: **Ŵ** = **Ŵ**′ * **D**<sup>−1/2</sup>.
    7.  Compute output **Y** = **Ŵ** **X** via integer-optimized GEMM.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Diagonal vs. Full Correlation:** Diagonal approximation reduces online cost from O(*d*<sup>2</sup>*T*) to O(*dT*), accepting potential loss of information about channel co-dependencies.
    *   **Hyperparameters (α, λ, p):** Fixed values avoid runtime search but may be suboptimal for all models or data distributions.
    *   **Groupsize:** Smaller groups (e.g., 16) improve accuracy by allowing finer-grained scaling but increase memory for **S**, **Z** storage. Paper uses *g*=32.
    *   **Low-Rank Rank *r*:** Higher *r* captures more of **W**'s energy, reducing the burden on extreme quantization, but increases memory and the cost of the low-rank path (**X** **A**<sup>⊤</sup>).
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   Severe accuracy collapse on very short sequences (*T* ≈ 1-2) due to high-variance norm estimates for **D**.
    *   Increased perplexity variance on datasets with heavy outliers if per-channel L2 norm is not robust to them.
    *   For 2-bit without low-rank, perplexity may diverge or become extremely high.
    *   If low-rank rank *r* is too small, the residual matrix **W** – **BA** may be too large/non-smooth for 2-bit quantization to handle.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Calibration Sensitivity Test:** OPT-350M, 3-bit. Vary AWQ calibration token count from 2<sup>11</sup> to 2<sup>17</sup> and compare perplexity on WT2/PTB/C4 to TTQ (no calibration). Expect AWQ performance to fluctuate with calibration set, while TTQ is stable.
    2.  **Groupsize Robustness Test:** Qwen3-1.7B, 3-bit. Evaluate perplexity for groupsizes {8,16,32,64,128,256}. Expect TTQ to be more tolerant to larger groupsizes than AWQ due to its online adaptation.
    3.  **Low-Rank Benefit at Extreme Bits:** Gemma3-270M, 2-bit. Compare TTQ(r=0) and TTQ(r=16). Expect a large perplexity improvement (orders of magnitude) with the low-rank residual.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question 1:** How can we dynamically adjust the hyperparameters (α, λ, p) of TTQ on a per-prompt basis to further improve quantization accuracy across diverse inputs?
    *   **Basis in Paper:** The authors state in the conclusion: "How to dynamically adjust hyperparameters is interesting to explore."
    *   **Why Unresolved:** Current implementation uses fixed hyperparameters, which may be suboptimal for varying input statistics.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** A method for online hyperparameter selection (e.g., learned policy, meta-optimization) that demonstrates consistent perplexity improvements on a suite of benchmarks.

*   **Question 2:** Can test-time pruning (e.g., µ-MoE) and decomposition be seamlessly integrated with TTQ to yield additional acceleration without sacrificing accuracy?
    *   **Basis in Paper:** The conclusion mentions: "we plan to integrate test-time pruning and decomposition into TTQ."
    *   **Why Unresolved:** Combining dynamic pruning, low-rank updates, and quantization on the fly poses significant challenges in computational overhead and coordination.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** A unified framework that jointly applies pruning, low-rank decomposition, and quantization on the fly, evaluated on end-to-end latency and accuracy, showing speedups beyond TTQ alone.

*   **Question 3:** Does the diagonal approximation of the activation correlation matrix in the online AWQ subcomponent of TTQ introduce a fundamental accuracy limit, and could a more accurate low-rank approximation be computed efficiently at test time?
    *   **Basis in Paper:** Inferred. The paper uses a diagonal for efficiency, acknowledging more powerful methods (e.g., full correlation used in GPTQ) are too costly.
    *   **Why Unresolved:** The impact of the diagonal approximation on quantization error is not theoretically analyzed; an online low-rank approximation could improve accuracy at a modest overhead.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** Analyze the eigenvalue spectrum of activation correlations; propose an online algorithm to compute a rank-k approximation (e.g., via incremental SVD) and integrate into TTQ, measuring perplexity improvements.

*   **Question 4:** How well does TTQ generalize to non-transformer architectures (e.g., convolutional networks, state space models) and to modalities beyond text and vision (e.g., audio, graphs)?
    *   **Basis in Paper:** Inferred. Evaluation limited to decoder-only LLMs and specific VLMs/VLAs.
    *   **Why Unresolved:** TTQ's assumptions about activation distributions and linear layer prevalence may not hold for other architectures (e.g., convolutions, SSMs).
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** Apply TTQ to CNNs for image classification, SSMs for sequence modeling, and models for audio/graph data, reporting accuracy and speed relative to static quantization baselines.

## Limitations
*   **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The choice of fixed (α, λ) is undisclosed and may be suboptimal; performance likely degrades if defaults differ across models or data distributions.
*   **Diagonal Approximation:** Assuming independence across activation channels may fail for layers with highly correlated activations, potentially limiting quantization quality.
*   **Sequence Length Dependency:** Overhead fraction ρ = O(1/*T*) suggests performance may degrade for very short sequences (e.g., streaming) where T is small.
*   **Architecture Scope:** Evaluation is primarily on decoder-only LLMs and a few VLMs/VLAs; generalizability to encoder-decoder, convolutional, or state-space models is unproven.

## Confidence
*   **Online adaptation eliminates calibration domain shift:** **Medium** (Strong evidence on standard language modeling benchmarks, but limited to 2-5 bits and specific models).
*   **Diagonal overhead is negligible for large models:** **High** (Complexity analysis and empirical speedups strongly support this; edge cases with small T are acknowledged).
*   **Low-rank + TTQ enables extreme (2-bit) quantization:** **Medium** (Results on Gemma3-270M are dramatic, but evidence is limited to one small model; stability on larger models needs confirmation).
*   **Generalization to VLM/VLA tasks:** **Low-Medium** (Positive results on Qwen3-VL and π0.5 are promising but based on a narrow set of tasks; broader validation is needed).
