# OptINC: Optical In-Network-Computing for Scalable Distributed Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28290
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28290
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** OptINC offloads gradient averaging and quantization to an optical ONN, eliminating ring all-reduce communication overhead while matching training accuracy (≤0.03% drop) and reducing end-to-end latency by 17–25%.

## Executive Summary
Distributed deep learning training suffers from high communication overhead in ring all-reduce, consuming 50–87.5% of time due to multiple synchronization rounds across servers. This paper introduces OptINC, an optical in-network computing architecture that places an Optical Neural Network (ONN) built from Mach-Zehnder Interferometers within the optical switch fabric. The ONN directly computes quantized gradient averages in a single pass, removing the need for Reduce-Scatter and All-Gather rounds. Hardware-aware training with matrix approximation reduces ONN area by ~50% while preserving accuracy. Evaluations on ResNet50/CIFAR-100 and a LLaMA-based model show that OptINC achieves near-identical training accuracy to ring all-reduce and reduces latency by 17–25%.

## Method Summary
OptINC employs an ONN to perform averaging and quantization of gradients across N servers. Each server encodes its B-bit gradient into M=⌈B/2⌉ PAM4 signals, sent to a preprocessing unit that averages groups of inputs to reduce the ONN's input dimension from M·N to K. The ONN, implemented as a multi-layer perceptron with linear transformations mapped to MZI arrays, is trained to map preprocessed inputs to the quantized average gradient. To mitigate hardware costs, matrix approximation partitions weight matrices into square blocks, each approximated as a diagonal matrix times a single unitary matrix (instead of two), cutting MZI count by nearly 50%. A two-stage training process first minimizes raw output error, then shifts to reconstructed gradient error, with periodic approximation enforcement. For scalability beyond N servers, a two-level cascading topology adapts the training dataset to include residual quantization terms from the first level, enabling exact global averaging.

## Key Results
- Training accuracy loss ≤0.03% compared to ring all-reduce on ResNet50/CIFAR-100 and LLaMA-based models.
- End-to-end training latency reduced by 17–25% by eliminating communication overhead.
- ONN achieves 100% accuracy on the averaging/quantization task even with hardware approximations.
- ONN area reduced by ~50% via matrix approximation without accuracy degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** OptINC eliminates the 50–87.5% communication overhead of ring all-reduce by offloading gradient averaging and quantization from servers into the optical network fabric itself.
- **Mechanism:** An Optical Neural Network (ONN), built from cascaded Mach-Zehnder Interferometers (MZIs), is placed in the optical switch fabric. It directly computes the quantized average of PAM4-encoded gradient chunks from all connected servers in a single pass, removing the need for multiple Reduce-Scatter and All-Gather rounds.
- **Core assumption:** The linear transformation (averaging) and nonlinear mapping (to discrete PAM4 levels) can be sufficiently approximated by an ONN with appropriately trained weights.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "offloads gradient averaging and quantization to an Optical Neural Network (ONN)... eliminating communication overhead." [section III-A] "The ONN fθ is used to approximate the quantized average... By offloading the computation from the servers to the optical interconnects, communication overhead... can be eliminated."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Hardware cost (number of MZIs) is reduced by ~50% via matrix approximation, while training accuracy is preserved through a hardware-aware training algorithm.
- **Mechanism:** Selected ONN layer weight matrices are partitioned into small square submatrices. Each submatrix is approximated as a product of a diagonal matrix and a single unitary matrix (instead of two unitary matrices from SVD). The training loss initially weights raw output errors and later shifts to reconstructed gradient errors. The approximation is periodically enforced during training to guide weights into the constrained structure.
- **Core assumption:** The accuracy loss from discarding one unitary matrix per approximated submatrix can be fully recovered by adjusting the remaining degrees of freedom (diagonal scaling and one unitary matrix) during training.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section III-B] "To reduce the area cost... Ws ≈ Wa = ΣaUa... With one unitary matrix eliminated, the area cost for each square matrix can be reduced by nearly 50%." [Table I] Shows area ratios of 39.3%-49.3% with 100% ONN training accuracy when matrix approximation is applied.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The OptINC architecture scales to support N² servers via a two-level cascading topology with minimal additional hardware overhead, by adapting the training dataset to account for compounded quantization errors.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple OptINCs (level 1) each handle N servers, and their outputs feed into a single OptINC (level 2). To prevent error accumulation from two-stage quantization (Q(Q(avg))), the training dataset for level 1 is constructed to include the "discarded" fractional parts from its own quantization as a residual term, which is carried in the last PAM4 signal. This allows the level 2 ONN to compute the true global average.
- **Core assumption:** The residual terms from level 1 quantization can be perfectly encoded into an extra signal bit-width and accurately processed by a slightly larger ONN in level 2.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section III-C] "By using these adapted datasets to train the ONNs in the OptINCs in the cascading topology, the actual averaged gradient Gnew can then become equivalent to G*." [section IV] "By cascading five such OptINCs in two levels... up to sixteen servers can be supported... The ONNs... can be trained to reach 100% accuracy on the modified dataset."

## Foundational Learning
- **Optical Neural Networks (ONNs) with MZIs:** ONNs use Mach-Zehnder Interferometers to perform linear transformations in the optical domain. Each MZI implements a 2×2 unitary matrix via programmable phase shifters. In OptINC, the entire ONN replaces digital gradient aggregation, enabling ultra-low-latency computation. *Quick check:* How does a single MZI's transformation matrix relate to the phase shifters' settings, and why is the overall MZI array unitary?
- **Mach-Zehnder Interferometer (MZI) fundamentals:** A MZI consists of two directional couplers and two phase shifters. It splits an optical signal, applies phase shifts, and recombines them to produce interference-based amplitude/phase changes. The 2×2 transfer matrix is unitary, preserving power. In ONNs, cascaded MZIs form a mesh that can implement any unitary matrix, which is essential for arbitrary linear operations. *Quick check:* What are the roles of the two directional couplers and the two phase shifters in a MZI's 2x2 transfer matrix?
- **Ring All-Reduce Communication Pattern & Overhead:** Ring all-reduce divides gradients into chunks that circulate among servers in a ring, performing Reduce-Scatter and All-Gather. The communication overhead is 2(N-1)/N, meaning for N servers, only 2/N of the time is spent on useful computation; the rest is communication. This overhead worsens as N increases. *Quick check:* For 8 servers, what is the relative communication overhead of ring all-reduce, and why does it occur?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Servers -> Preprocessing Unit -> Optical Neural Network -> Splitting Unit -> Servers
- **Critical path:**
  1. Encoding: Server computes gradient, splits into M PAM4 signals.
  2. Transmission & Preprocessing: Signals sent to OptINC; Preprocessing Unit averages groups to reduce to K inputs.
  3. ONN Computation: Preprocessed signals pass through MZI layers, performing linear transforms and activations.
  4. Splitting & Output: ONN outputs K PAM4 signals; Splitting Unit broadcasts to all servers.
  5. Decoding: Servers reconstruct B-bit gradient from K signals.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - ONN size vs. Servers (N): Larger N requires larger ONN input; cascading adds levels but increases total MZI count.
  - Area vs. Accuracy: More aggressive matrix approximation reduces area but risks accuracy loss if hardware-aware training fails.
  - Preprocessing Resolution (K): Smaller K reduces ONN size but increases input range, potentially needing larger layers.
  - Nonlinearity Implementation: Pure optical activations avoid O-E-O conversion but are harder to implement; hybrid designs add latency.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Training Failure: ONN accuracy <100% on mapping task after hardware-aware training.
  - Downstream Model Degradation: Final model accuracy drop >0.03% when using OptINC vs. perfect all-reduce.
  - Scalability Breakdown: Cascading topology fails to reach 100% ONN accuracy for large N or high bit-widths.
  - Latency Non-Improvement: OptINC latency not lower than ring all-reduce, due to ONN processing or O/E/O overheads.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Baseline ONN Training: Train a standard MLP-based ONN (without approximation) to 100% accuracy on the synthetic averaging/quantization dataset for given N and B.
  2. Hardware-Aware Training & Approximation: Apply matrix approximation to hidden layers during training with two-stage loss; verify 100% accuracy recovery and measure area reduction.
  3. Cascading Validation: For N² servers, train two ONNs (level 1 and level 2) on adapted datasets including residual terms; verify both achieve 100% accuracy and exact end-to-end gradient reconstruction.

- Could alternative network topologies (e.g., tree, butterfly, hierarchical) reduce quantisation error accumulation in multi-level OptINC when scaling beyond hundreds of servers?

## Limitations
- Omitted hyperparameters (learning rate schedule, batch size) may affect training stability and reproducibility.
- Real-device MZI non-idealities (loss, phase noise) could prevent achieving 100% ONN accuracy in practice.
- Cascading topology only validated for N ≤ 16; may fail for larger clusters or higher bit-widths.
- Downstream model accuracy claims are limited to ResNet50/CIFAR-100 and a LLaMA variant; broader validation is needed.

## Confidence
- Elimination of communication overhead: **Medium** (theoretically sound but unproven in hardware; requires flawless ONN training and minimal optical latency).
- Area reduction via matrix approximation: **Medium** (empirical evidence shows 40–50% savings on small tasks; scaling uncertain).
- Scalable cascading with adapted datasets: **Low** (only tiny clusters evaluated; residual encoding may hit PAM4 capacity limits).
