# Pre-Editorial Normalization for Automatically Transcribed Medieval Manuscripts in Old French and Latin

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.13905
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13905
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: A ByT5 model achieves 6.7% Character Error Rate (CER) on a gold evaluation set, far surpassing prior models (64.8% CER) and a zero-shot Llama baseline (139.9% CER).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Pre-Editorial Normalization (PEN), the task of converting graphemic automatic transcription (ATR) output of medieval manuscripts into an editorially normalized form while preserving an intermediate palaeographic layer. The authors create a large silver training dataset (4.66M pairs) by automatically aligning the CoMMA corpus (graphemic ATR) with digitized editions using the `passim` tool. A small gold test set (1.8k samples) is manually corrected for reliable evaluation. Fine-tuning a ByT5 model on this data yields a 6.7% CER on the gold set, dramatically improving over previous approaches. Moreover, integrating the PEN model into a downstream stylometry pipeline (lemmatization and POS-tagging) yields higher F1 scores compared to a prior specialized pipeline, demonstrating practical benefits for NLP on medieval texts.

## Method Summary
The methodology centers on generating parallel data through automatic alignment. The `passim 2.0` system uses character-level HMM alignment to match passages between the CoMMA corpus (graphemic ATR transcripts) and normalized digital editions after an initial n-gram filtering. Alignment pairs are filtered by minimum line count and match rate. This yields 4.66M silver training pairs spanning Old French and Latin, with Old French upsampled 10× to address language imbalance. A ByT5 model (small) is fine-tuned on this dataset. Evaluation uses a manually corrected gold test set of ~1.8k samples, reporting CER and WER. Downstream utility is assessed by feeding PEN outputs into an existing PoS-tagger/lemmatizer and comparing performance against a previous pipeline (Lambert) on the *Vie de Saint Lambert* corpus.

## Key Results
- ByT5 model achieves **6.7% CER** on gold test set.
- Prior specialized model: 64.8% CER; zero-shot Llama 3 8B Instruct: 139.9% CER.
- PEN-based downstream pipeline improves lemma and POS-tagging F1 scores compared to Lambert pipeline.
- The silver dataset contains 4.66M alignment pairs, enabling large-scale training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Large-Scale Silver Data via Automatic Alignment
- **Claim:** Automatic alignment of graphemic ATR transcripts with normalized editions creates a large-scale, weakly supervised training resource for PEN.
- **Mechanism:** The `passim` tool employs character-level HMM alignment to match passages between the CoMMA corpus and digitized editions, tolerating differences in page sequencing, spelling, and abbreviation. This bypasses manual annotation at scale.
- **Core assumption:** The normalized edition is a valid target "pre-editorial" form, and character n-gram overlap suffices to retrieve candidate passage pairs.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions 4.66M silver pairs; [section 4.2] details passim alignment process and filtering criteria (≥5 lines, ≥60% match rate).
- **Break condition:** Alignment quality degrades for texts with high non-abbreviation spelling variation (e.g., Old French) or severe, unrecoverable ATR noise, as the HMM may align mismatched lexical variants.

### Mechanism 2: Byte-Level Model Robustness
- **Claim:** A byte-level sequence-to-sequence model (ByT5) trained on the aligned silver data effectively learns the PEN mapping.
- **Mechanism:** ByT5's byte-level representation handles raw Unicode characters, decomposed diacritics, and scribal glyphs without explicit tokenization, making it robust to orthographic diversity. It learns to correct ATR confusions (e.g., `rn`/`m`), resolve abbreviations contextually, and apply editorial norms (e.g., `u/v` distinction).
- **Core assumption:** The mapping is learnable from parallel data despite silver noise; model capacity is sufficient to disentangle ATR errors from intentional scribal variation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] reports 6.7% CER; [section 5.1] justifies ByT5 choice for its wide character range and performance on spelling variation; [section 5.2] shows dramatic improvement over baselines.
- **Break condition:** Model fails on expansions requiring external knowledge (e.g., personal name abbreviation `S.`) or pervasive unrecoverable ATR noise. It also exhibits a "classical bias" for Latin, favoring standard spellings over medieval variants.

### Mechanism 3: Downstream Utility via Intermediate Normalization
- **Claim:** Applying PEN as a dedicated preprocessing step improves downstream NLP pipelines (lemmatization, POS-tagging) compared to raw graphemic ATR or more complex specialized pipelines.
- **Mechanism:** The normalized output provides consistent orthographic surface for downstream tools typically trained on standardized texts, reducing sparsity and OOV issues. The intermediate palaeographic fidelity is retained separately, allowing error attribution.
- **Core assumption:** Downstream tools are sensitive to orthographic variation and perform better on normalized text; PEN errors are less detrimental than raw ATR noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states improvement in lemma and POS-tagging F1; [section 6] compares with Lambert pipeline on *Vie de Saint Lambert*; [section 6.2] indicates annotation errors not strongly correlated with token normalization correctness.
- **Break condition:** Over-normalization may erase meaningful variation (e.g., `leurs` → `lors`), harming tasks where variation is the signal. Benefits diminish if downstream tool is itself robust to variation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Graphemic vs. Normalized Transcription**
  - *Why needed:* PEN sits between graphemic transcription (preserving scribal practices) and normalized transcription (adapting for modern readers). The task is defined by the gap between them.
  - *Quick check:* What is a key risk of training an ATR model to produce directly normalized output? (Answer: It may conflate ATR errors with intentional scribal variation, losing palaeographic information.)

- **Automatic Alignment (passim/HMM)**
  - *Why needed:* Core data creation technique; allows harvesting millions of training pairs without manual sentence-by-sentence alignment.
  - *Quick check:* Why is a character-level HMM alignment preferred over algorithms like Smith-Waterman for ATR alignment? (Answer: HMMs model the noisy channel of transcription and can handle gaps and variations more robustly at character level.)

- **Downstream Utility of Normalization**
  - *Why needed:* The paper argues PEN must improve practical NLP tasks like lemmatization; understanding when normalization helps vs. harms is key.
  - *Quick check:* According to Section 6, what was the net effect of the PEN pipeline on *recall* for the 100 most frequent words (MFW) in the stylometry task, and what does that suggest about its noise profile? (Answer: Recall improved, suggesting the pipeline reduces false negatives due to orthographic noise.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data Sources (CoMMA + digital editions) -> Alignment Engine (`passim 2.0`) -> Dataset Processor (filtering) -> Training Pipeline (ByT5 fine-tuning) -> Evaluation Suite (gold test set + external benchmark) -> Downstream Pipeline (PEN output + Clérice tagger/lemmatizer).

**Critical path:** Data Alignment (`passim`) -> Silver Dataset Creation -> Model Training (ByT5) -> Gold Evaluation -> Downstream Stylometry Test. The silver dataset quality is the primary gating factor.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Scale vs. Quality: Automatic alignment yields massive data but introduces noise and over-normalization; gold set is tiny.
- Language Balance: Severe Latin/Old French imbalance handled by 10× French upsampling; risks overfitting to French.
- Model Choice: ByT5 (byte-level) vs. subword models; avoids pre-tokenization biases but may need more data.
- Task Framing: PEN as standalone normalization vs. integrated ATR+normalization; allows error attribution but may miss document-level context.

**Failure signatures:**
- High CER on specific genres (e.g., administrative/archival texts, highly abbreviated).
- Systematic biases: "classical bias" in Latin (normalizing to `ph`, `-tio-`) and variant erasure in Old French (`leurs` → `lors`).
- Hallucination/Over-normalization: Inserting variants not in local context (e.g., `iterum` → `iustum`).
- Context-blind abbreviation expansion: Mis-expanding abbreviations like `S.` due to lack of external knowledge.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Alignment Sanity Check:** Manually verify 100 random silver pairs for passage correspondence, correct abbreviation expansion, and absence of spurious normalization; compute observed noise rate.
2. **Model Ablation - Language:** Train separate ByT5 models on Latin-only and French-only (upsampled) silver data; compare CER on respective gold subsets to isolate language-specific challenges and multilingual training effects.
3. **Downstream Sensitivity:** Apply PEN to a held-out manuscript with known intra-text spelling variation; quantify variation loss and correlate with changes in lemmatization/POS F1; assess whether all variation reduction helps or only error-type reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can PEN systems represent and output normalized variants when multiple valid expansions exist?** The current deterministic approach may erase meaningful variation; a probabilistic output distribution with variant recall metrics is needed.
2. **To what extent does PEN reduce over-normalization and preserve historically significant scribal features compared to end-to-end normalized ATR?** The gold test set is small and derived automatically; a fine-grained error analysis against multiple manuscript witnesses is required.
3. **How effective is PEN for a broader range of downstream NLP tasks?** Impact on NER, dependency parsing, or information retrieval remains unknown; benchmarking on standard medieval NLP datasets is needed.
4. **Can document-level or page-wide contextual information improve PEN accuracy?** The model operates on isolated chunks; context-aware architectures could better resolve ambiguous abbreviations and ensure consistency.

## Limitations
- Alignment noise and over-normalization in the silver dataset are not directly quantified; quality depends on `passim` HMM matching.
- The gold test set composition is ambiguous; it is unclear whether the HuggingFace dataset cleanly separates gold from silver examples.
- Systematic biases (classical bias, variant erasure) may limit utility for socio-stylistic analysis where variation is signal.
- Downstream utility is demonstrated only on a single stylometry pipeline; generalizability to other tasks is untested.
