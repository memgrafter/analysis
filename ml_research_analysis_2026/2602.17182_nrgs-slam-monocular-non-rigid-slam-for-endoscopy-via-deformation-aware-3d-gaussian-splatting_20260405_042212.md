# NRGS-SLAM: Monocular Non-Rigid SLAM for Endoscopy via Deformation-Aware 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.17182
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17182
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Reduces Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE by up to 50% compared to state-of-the-art methods and achieves higher rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS).

## Executive Summary
Monocular non-rigid SLAM in endoscopy faces the fundamental challenge of coupling between camera ego-motion and soft-tissue deformation, which leads to tracking drift and low-fidelity scene maps. NRGS-SLAM addresses this by introducing a deformation-aware 3D Gaussian representation, where each Gaussian primitive is augmented with a learnable deformation probability. This probability is projected to a dense 2D confidence map that guides a coarse-to-fine tracking module to prioritize low-deformation regions for pose estimation, effectively decoupling camera motion from scene deformation. The deformation probabilities are learned via a Bayesian self-supervision strategy that requires no ground-truth non-rigidity labels. Additionally, a unified robust loss integrates geometric priors from a foundation model. Evaluations on multiple endoscopic datasets demonstrate up to a 50% reduction in ATE RMSE and improved rendering quality over existing state-of-the-art approaches.

## Method Summary
NRGS-SLAM extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with a deformable scene representation: canonical Gaussians carry a learned deformation probability, and a temporal deformation field using Gaussian basis functions models motion. The system comprises four interrelated components: Measurement Preprocessing extracts geometric priors (depth, sparse 2D/3D tracks) from a foundation model and generates validity/co-visibility masks; Deformable Tracking performs coarse pose estimation via a deformation-weighted PnP solver on filtered correspondences, then refines pose and per-frame deformation residuals using a combined photometric-geometric loss weighted by the deformation confidence map; Deformable Mapping, triggered on keyframe creation, optimizes keyframe poses, extends the map with new Gaussians, and runs global bundle adjustment that includes Bayesian self-supervision for deformation probabilities and dynamic management of the deformation field (densify/merge/prune/freeze); Core Representation maintains the map `G_c` and deformation field `D_Θt`. The deformation probability map `M_def` is central, steering optimization away from highly deformable areas to mitigate pose-deformation coupling.

## Key Results
- Up to 50% reduction in Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE compared to state-of-the-art methods (e.g., NR-SLAM, 4DTAM, DDS-SLAM) on endoscopic datasets.
- Improved reconstruction quality with higher PSNR/SSIM and lower LPIPS.
- The deformation probability map effectively guides pose optimization, enabling robust tracking in the presence of tissue deformation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism is the deformation-aware Gaussian representation. Each Gaussian primitive carries a learnable deformation probability `w_d,i`, which is projected via differentiable rasterization to a dense pixel-wise deformation confidence map `M_def`. During pose optimization, this map down‑weights pixels predicted to be in high‑deformation regions, anchoring the estimation to low‑deformation (presumably rigid) anatomical structures. This mitigates the coupling between camera motion and scene deformation by providing a spatial prior that distinguishes photometric variations caused by deformation from those due to ego‑motion.

The coarse‑to‑fine tracking pipeline further enhances robustness. First, a coarse pose is estimated using a PnP solver on sparse correspondences (from a foundation model) filtered by `M_def` to exclude high‑deformation points. Then, the initial pose is refined by minimizing a combined photometric and geometric loss, with each pixel’s contribution scaled by `(1 - M_def)`. This two‑stage process yields a stable initialization and then refines it while suppressing residuals from deformable areas.

Learning the deformation probabilities relies on Bayesian self‑supervision without ground‑truth labels. For each Gaussian in a sliding window, two renderings are produced: one rigid (`w_d,i=0`) and one deformable (`w_d,i=1`). The photometric errors from these hypotheses are aggregated to compute a posterior probability via Bayes’ rule, favoring the deformable hypothesis if it significantly reduces error. This posterior supervises the learnable `w_d,i` through a binary cross‑entropy loss. The approach assumes that the error difference between the two hypotheses reliably indicates the true motion mode and that multiple views provide robust statistical evidence.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
- Measurement Preprocessing (Sec. V): Takes RGB frame `I_t` and outputs geometric priors (`D_t`, `T^2d`, `T^3d`), validity mask `M_val`, and co‑visibility mask `M_covis`.  
- Deformable Tracking (Sec. VI): Uses current frame, priors, and map state to produce refined camera pose `T_t` and per‑frame deformation residuals `δω_t`. Contains two sub‑stages: (a) coarse pose via deformation‑weighted PnP, (b) fine pose and deformation refinement.  
- Deformable Mapping (Sec. VII): Triggered when a new keyframe is selected. Optimizes keyframe poses, extends the map with new Gaussians, and performs global bundle adjustment that includes deformation probability estimation and dynamic deformation field management. Outputs updated canonical Gaussians `G_c` (with `w_d,i`), deformed field `D_Θt`, and optimized keyframe poses.  
- Core Representation (Sec. IV): The underlying model = `G_c` (canonical Gaussians with `w_d,i`) + `D_Θt` (temporal deformation field using Gaussian basis functions). Maintained by Tracking and Mapping.

**Critical Path**  
Frame Input → Measurement Preprocessing → Deformable Tracking (Coarse Pose) → Deformable Tracking (Refine Pose + Update Deform) → [Keyframe Selection?] → If yes: Deformable Mapping (Pose Opt → Extend Map → Global BA) → Next frame. The Bayesian self‑supervision for `w_d,i` occurs within the Global BA step of Mapping.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Accuracy vs. Speed: Using per‑frame residual updates (instead of full parameter updates) sacrifices a small amount of accuracy (Table VIII, Config C1 vs Ours) but gains ~36‑42% in FPS.  
- Representation Capacity vs. Stability: Dynamic deformation field management (densify/merge/prune/freeze) adapts to complex deformations but introduces algorithmic complexity and potential instability if thresholds are mis‑set.  
- Prior Reliability vs. Robustness: Geometric foundation model priors are essential for monocular ill‑posedness but are noisy; the unified robust loss (IRLS, annealing) trades gradient fidelity for outlier rejection.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Tracking drift/failure: Trajectory diverges from ground truth. Primary causes: `M_def` misclassification, lack of low‑deformation regions, or geometric prior failure (bad depth/tracks). Diagnose by visualizing `M_def` and checking posterior `w*_d` convergence.  
- Map degradation: Blurry or distorted renderings, especially in deformable areas. Signs: `w_d,i` stuck at prior (~0.6), diverging deformation parameters, or map extension adding Gaussians in wrong locations due to bad pose/depth. Monitor photometric loss and map size growth.  
- Memory/parameter explosion: Without dynamic management, number of active deformation parameters grows linearly, slowing optimization and risking numerical issues.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can computational efficiency be improved for real‑time performance? *Basis:* The paper notes lack of real‑time due to per‑primitive deformation parameters and suggests surface‑level modeling. *Why unresolved:* Current per‑primitive approach yields a high‑dimensional optimization. *Resolution evidence:* Implement a surface‑level deformation representation and benchmark runtime/accuracy on standard datasets.  
**Open Question 2:** How robust is Bayesian self‑supervision under severe visual degradation (specularities, low texture)? *Basis:* The method relies on RGB photometric errors, which may be unreliable in poor conditions. *Why unresolved:* Photometric inconsistencies may not reflect true deformation, leading to mislearned probabilities. *Resolution evidence:* Evaluate learned probabilities against ground truth or proxies on degraded datasets and correlate with tracking/mapping accuracy.  
**Open Question 3:** How well do geometric priors from foundation models generalize across diverse endoscopic datasets and setups? *Basis:* SpatialTrackerV2 priors may suffer domain gaps and noise in endoscopic imagery. *Why unresolved:* General‑scene training may not capture endoscopic specifics (illumination, occlusions, limited texture). *Resolution evidence:* Ablate different foundation models across datasets, correlate prior accuracy (e.g., depth errors) with ATE/rendering metrics, and identify failure modes.  
**Open Question 4:** Can the system handle scenes with minimal or absent rigid structures? *Basis:* Tracking weights optimization toward low‑deformation regions, assuming they provide reliable constraints. *Why unresolved:* If most of the scene is deformable, rigid constraints may be insufficient, causing coupling and drift. *Resolution evidence:* Test on extreme deformation sequences (e.g., C3VDv2), analyze deformation probability distribution over time, and correlate with tracking errors.

## Limitations
- Incomplete specification of deformation field hyperparameters (e.g., number of basis functions, learning rates, densification/pruning thresholds) hinders exact replication.
- Bayesian self‑supervision is sensitive to photometric noise (specular highlights, motion blur, illumination changes), which can mislead deformation probability learning.
- Tracking relies on the presence of sufficient low‑deformation regions; extreme global tissue deformation may cause failure.
