# CLFEC: A New Task for Unified Linguistic and Factual Error Correction in paragraph-level Chinese Professional Writing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23845
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23845
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Unified retrieval-augmented generation (U-RAG) achieves the best overall correction F1 of ~65% on the CLFEC benchmark, with factual correction F1 increasing from ~27% to ~50% using U-RAG.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces the CLFEC task for simultaneous correction of linguistic and factual errors in Chinese professional writing, addressing a gap where prior work treated these error types separately. To support this task, the authors construct a multi-domain dataset (current affairs, finance, law, medicine) with synthetically injected errors, creating diagnostic splits for linguistic error correction (LEC), factual error correction (FEC), mixed errors (MIX), and error-free controls. They evaluate several LLM-based approaches, including prompting, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) in sequential (S-RAG) and unified (U-RAG) forms, and an agentic framework with planning and verification. Key findings show that U-RAG significantly improves factual correction performance, mixed-error inputs are the most challenging, and the agentic workflow reduces over-correction on clean texts. The best system (Kimi-K2.5 with U-RAG) reaches an overall F1 of approximately 65%.

## Method Summary
The authors built the CLFEC dataset by selecting 925 paragraphs from four professional domains and injecting a diverse set of linguistic (spelling, grammar, punctuation) and factual errors. The injection process ensures a balanced distribution of error types and creates four diagnostic splits: LEC (only linguistic errors), FEC (only factual errors), MIX (both types), and error-free controls. For evaluation, they tested multiple LLM-based correction strategies: basic prompting, sequential RAG (S-RAG) where retrieval and correction are applied in separate steps for each error type, unified RAG (U-RAG) that jointly processes the entire paragraph with retrieved evidence in a single context, and an agentic framework that decomposes the task into planning, correction, and verification stages. Performance is measured primarily by token-level F1 against the annotated gold standard, with additional analysis of precision, recall, and over-correction rates.

## Key Results
- **U-RAG boosts factual correction:** F1 increases from ~27% (S-RAG) to ~50% on the FEC split; combined with agentic planning, the best system (Kimi-K2.5 U-RAG) reaches ~65% overall F1.
- **Mixed errors are hardest:** The MIX split yields notably lower recall than pure LEC or FEC, highlighting difficulty in handling co-occurring error types.
- **Agentic workflow reduces over-correction:** For advanced models (e.g., DeepSeek-V3.2), agentic correction cuts over-correction on clean texts by ~64% and improves FEC recall.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The superiority of U-RAG over S-RAG stems from its unified context, which avoids error accumulation and contextual misalignment that arise when processing errors sequentially. By presenting the entire paragraph and all retrieved evidence together, the model can make globally coherent corrections and resolve dependencies between linguistic and factual issues. The agentic framework further enhances performance by introducing explicit planning (to identify error types and required evidence) and verification (to validate corrections against evidence), which improves recall on factual errors and mitigates over-correction, especially on clean or lightly erroneous texts. These mechanisms align with the nature of professional writing, where errors often interact and correctness requires cross-checking against reliable sources.

## Foundational Learning
- **Unified error correction:** Why needed: Real-world writing contains mixed error types that must be corrected together. Quick check: Can a model correct both spelling and factual inaccuracies without introducing new errors?
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** Why needed: Factual correction requires up-to-date external knowledge. Quick check: Does retrieved evidence directly support the corrected claims?
- **Sequential vs. Unified RAG:** Why needed: To compare modular versus holistic processing. Quick check: Does sequential processing lead to error propagation?
- **Agentic workflows:** Why needed: Complex tasks benefit from decomposition and self-verification. Quick check: Does planning improve recall without sacrificing precision?
- **Synthetic error injection:** Why needed: To create large, controlled datasets with known error locations and types. Quick check: Do injected errors reflect natural error distributions?
- **Diagnostic splits (LEC/FEC/MIX):** Why needed: To isolate model strengths and weaknesses. Quick check: Does performance on pure error splits predict performance on mixed errors?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Paragraph -> Retrieval Module -> Unified Context Construction -> LLM Correction -> Output Paragraph  
(Agentic variant adds: Planning Module -> Error Analysis -> Correction Module -> Verification Module -> Output)

**Critical Path:**  
For U-RAG: The retrieval module fetches relevant documents based on the input paragraph; these documents and the original text are combined into a unified prompt; the LLM generates the corrected paragraph in one pass. For the agentic approach: the planning stage identifies potential error types and formulates retrieval queries; retrieval and correction are performed iteratively with verification loops.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- **U-RAG vs S-RAG:** U-RAG maintains full context but may introduce noise from irrelevant retrieved documents; S-RAG is simpler but risks error cascades.  
- **Agentic vs single-pass:** Agentic adds computational overhead and complexity but yields better control and reduced over-correction.  
- **Retrieval quality:** High-quality retrieval is crucial for factual correction; poor retrieval degrades performance significantly.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- **Over-correction on clean texts:** Model changes correct text due to over-aggressive generation or irrelevant retrieval.  
- **Low recall on factual errors:** Model misses factual inconsistencies, especially when evidence is ambiguous or missing.  
- **Grammatical/punctuation errors persist:** These error types are hardest to detect, leading to low recall on LEC splits.  
- **Contextual misalignment in S-RAG:** Corrections in later steps contradict earlier changes.

**First Experiments:**  
1. Compare U-RAG and S-RAG on the FEC split with a fixed retrieval source to quantify the impact of unified context.  
2. Evaluate the agentic framework against single-pass U-RAG on the MIX split, measuring recall and over-correction rates.  
3. Ablate retrieval quality by using gold-standard evidence vs. noisy retrieval to understand dependency on external knowledge.

## Limitations
- **External evidence dependency:** U-RAG's gains rely on high-quality, relevant retrieval; performance may drop with noisy evidence sources.
- **Dataset validity & generalizability:** Synthetic error injection may not capture real-world error distributions; the four domains may not represent all professional writing contexts.
- **Evaluation metric gaps:** Automated F1 against potentially noisy gold standards may not fully capture correctness or naturalness of corrections.

## Confidence
- Dataset construction & error injection methodology: High
- Factual correction gains from RAG (U-RAG > S-RAG): High
- Mixed-error inputs being hardest with lower recall: Medium
- Grammatical/punctuation errors hardest to detect: Low
- Agentic workflow reducing over-correction on clean text: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Human evaluation of factual correction accuracy:** Have domain experts judge the factual correctness of U-RAG outputs independently of the gold standard to validate true accuracy.
2. **Out-of-domain robustness test:** Evaluate top U-RAG systems on a held-out fifth professional domain (e.g., engineering) to assess generalizability.
3. **Error subtype ablation:** Re-run experiments on test sets containing only grammatical errors or only punctuation errors to quantify the difficulty hierarchy for linguistic error subtypes.
