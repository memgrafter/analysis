# Symbolic Pattern Temporal Numeric Planning with Intermediate Conditions and Effects

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.09798
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09798
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Extended Symbolic Pattern Planning (SPP) to temporal numeric planning with ICEs; the Patty planner solved 168/190 benchmarks, outperforming tamer (131) and anmlsmt (98).

## Executive Summary
This paper extends Symbolic Pattern Planning (SPP) to handle temporal numeric planning with Intermediate Conditions and Effects (ICEs), where durative actions can have conditions and effects at arbitrary offsets. The approach computes causal patterns via relaxed planning graphs and encodes them into an SMT formula that simultaneously reasons about action repetitions, timing, and causal constraints. An iterative pattern extension loop ensures completeness. The resulting Patty planner achieves state-of-the-art results on ICE domains and solves all 20 INSTraDi train-dispatching instances, often using tighter temporal bounds than other SMT planners.

## Method Summary
Patty follows a three-phase SPP loop. First, from the current state, it compiles durative actions with ICEs into instantaneous “snap” actions and builds an asymptotic relaxed planning graph (ARPg) to compute a pattern—a sequence of happenings (snap action occurrences) suggesting a causal order. Second, this pattern is encoded into an SMT formula `Π≺` that includes state transition constraints (using inductive formulas `σ_i` to track variable values), temporal constraints (durations, separation, plan-relative times), and rolling for eligible repeated actions. Third, an SMT solver searches for a model satisfying the formula; if unsatisfiable but progress was made, the pattern is compressed to its non-zero occurrences, the resulting intermediate state is extracted, and a fresh pattern is computed and concatenated. This repeats until goals are satisfied or timeout occurs.

## Key Results
- Patty solved 168/190 benchmark instances, beating tamer (131) and anmlsmt (98).
- Achieved best results in the majority of non-ICE domains and matched state-of-art on ICE domains.
- Solved all 20 InSTraDi train-dispatching instances.
- Used lower temporal bounds than other SMT planners on commonly solved instances.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Pattern Computation via Relaxed Planning Graphs
- **Claim**: Computing patterns via asymptotic relaxed planning graphs provides an initial causal ordering that can be iteratively extended to cover all needed happenings for temporal ICEs planning.
- **Mechanism**: The relaxed planning graph ignores negative effects and under-approximates reachability. By compiling durative actions' ICEs into snap actions, building layers of applicable snap actions from the current state, and linearizing them (ordering ICs before IEs within each layer), a pattern is obtained. Any valid plan’s relaxed version respects this under-approximation, so its happenings appear in some order in the graph.
- **Core assumption**: The graph contains a subsequence of the happenings in any valid plan, and the linearization respects necessary ordering constraints (e.g., plan-ics before plan-ies when they share offsets).
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "Computed patterns via relaxed planning graphs to guide SMT-based search"
  - Section 3.2.2: Details `ComputeSnapARPG` algorithm and linearization.
  - Corpus: Builds on prior work [253392] that introduced pattern computation for numeric planning.
- **Break condition**: When the computed pattern misses required happenings for a valid plan (non-well-orderable tasks with `H_left` non-empty and insufficient ordering constraints).

### Mechanism 2: SMT Encoding with Temporal and Causal Constraints
- **Claim**: Encoding the pattern as an SMT formula with variables for happening repetitions, absolute times, and durations allows the solver to simultaneously choose which happenings to apply and their timing while satisfying all causal and temporal constraints.
- **Mechanism**: The formula `Π≺ = I(X) ∧ S≺(X,H≺,X′) ∧ T≺(X,H≺,C≺) ∧ G(X′)` compactly represents all valid plans respecting the pattern's ordering. `S≺` computes the resulting state via inductive formulas `σ_i` that track variable values based on initial state and applied happenings. `T≺` enforces duration bounds, temporal separation (`ϵ`), and ensures the pattern's mutex order is respected. Rolling is encoded by treating repeated applications of an action's happenings as a single variable.
- **Core assumption**: SMT solvers can efficiently search the combined integer/numeric space when the pattern is sufficiently informative.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Section 3.3: Full specification of the encoding, including `S≺` and `T≺`.
  - Section 3.4 Theorem 3: Proves any model of `Π≺` corresponds to a valid plan.
- **Break condition**: SMT solver fails to find a model within time limits, meaning either no valid plan exists for the current pattern or the instance is too complex.

### Mechanism 3: Iterative Pattern Extension Guarantees Completeness
- **Claim**: Incrementally extending the pattern by concatenating newly computed patterns from intermediate states guarantees completeness: eventually the concatenated pattern will contain a subsequence of happenings from some valid plan.
- **Mechanism**: The `spp` algorithm (Alg. 2) starts with a simple pattern from the initial state. After each SMT solve, it checks which new goals are satisfied. If progress is made, it compresses the pattern to the "helpful" happenings (those with non-zero repetition count), extracts the resulting intermediate state, computes a fresh pattern from that state, and concatenates it to the compressed pattern. This iterative extension eventually covers all needed happenings.
- **Core assumption**: There exists a valid plan whose happenings can be covered by concatenating patterns from reachable intermediate states along that plan.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "If the suggestion by the pattern is inaccurate and no valid plan can be found, the pattern is extended until it contains the causal order of actions in a valid plan, making the approach complete."
  - Section 3.4 Theorem 4: Proves correctness and completeness of `spp`.
- **Break condition**: Theoretically none for well-posed instances given unlimited time; practically, timeouts may prevent completing the extension.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Relaxed Planning Graphs**
  - *Why needed*: Patterns are computed from relaxed planning graphs, which provide a heuristic ordering of actions/happenings that under-approximate valid plan structures.
  - *Quick check*: In an asymptotic relaxed planning graph for numeric planning, how do you handle numeric effects that involve variables assigned by other actions? (Answer: Supporters separate effects into directional cases `x < ψ`/`x > ψ` to maintain monotonicity.)

- **Concept: Planning-as-Satisfiability (Pas)**
  - *Why needed*: spp is a Pas approach; understanding SAT/SMT encodings clarifies how temporal and causal constraints are combined.
  - *Quick check*: What's the difference between a "rolled" action and a normal action in a Pas encoding? (Answer: Rolling allows representing multiple consecutive executions of the same action via a single variable counting repetitions, reducing plan length.)

- **Concept: Durative Actions & Intermediate Conditions/Effects (ICEs)**
  - *Why needed*: The paper targets temporal planning with ICEs, where conditions/effects can occur at arbitrary times during an action, not just start/end.
  - *Quick check*: In PDDL2.1, when can conditions be checked and effects applied? How does that differ from ICEs? (Answer: PDDL2.1 only at start/end; ICEs allow any offset during the action and also plan-relative times.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: 
  PatternComputer -> SMTEncoder -> SolverInterface -> PlanExtractor
  SPPController orchestrates loop: computes pattern, encodes, solves, compresses, extracts state, repeats.

- **Critical path**: 
  Parse Π → initial state I → ComputePattern(I, Π) → build Π≺ → Solver → if UNSAT and progress → Compress → GetState(plan) → ComputePattern(newState, Π) → ≺ ← ≺_g; ≺_h → repeat until goals satisfied.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Pattern length vs. solver time: Longer patterns encode more causal information but generate larger SMT formulas.
  - Rolling restrictiveness: Limiting rolling to well-orderable actions simplifies encoding but may miss some plans.
  - Complete vs. incomplete patterns: Always using complete patterns guarantees progress but may be overkill; incomplete patterns risk losing completeness unless carefully managed.
  - SMT solver choice: Z3 is general but not specialized for temporal constraints; could be slower than dedicated temporal planners.

- **Failure signatures**:
  1. `ComputeSnapARPG` produces huge pattern: monitor `|H_left|`; if pattern length explodes, consider limiting iterations or heuristic pruning.
  2. SMT formula too large: model with ~1000 happening variables may cause memory blowup; consider sparser patterns or incremental solving.
  3. Solver returns UNSAT immediately with simple pattern: likely the pattern does not respect necessary ordering (e.g., mutex constraints); check well-orderability conditions.
  4. Extracted plan violates ICEs: likely bug in `GetPlan` or `σ_i` formulas; verify that `t_i` and `t_∅` assignments correctly capture intermediate timing.
  5. Infinite loop in `spp` without progress: likely compression is too aggressive or pattern recomputation yields same pattern; ensure `|SatG(μ, G)|` strictly increases or fallback to extending pattern.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Single-action InSTraDi**: One train moving from entry to exit with no ICEs. Verify that `bound n=1` yields correct plan; check timing variables.
  2. **Pour domain with rolling**: Instance requiring multiple `pour` actions from same bottle. Verify that rolling variable `h_i > 1` generates correct total effect (`x += ψ * h_i`).
  3. **Pack domain concurrency**: Instance where two `pack` actions must overlap (one starts before the other ends). Check that pattern includes both `pack[start]` happenings in correct interleaved order and SMT assigns times satisfying `no-overlap` for same action but allowing concurrency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can heuristic search strategies from heuristic search planners (e.g., progress measures, landmark heuristics) be integrated into the SPP approach to improve pattern selection and compression without sacrificing completeness?
  - *Basis*: Sec. 5 states they plan to investigate encapsulating search strategies inside spp and note pattern `≺h` is recomputed from scratch at intermediate states.
  - *Why unresolved*: SPP is purely SMT-based; integrating heuristics could accelerate solving but risks completeness if not carefully designed.
  - *What evidence would resolve*: Systematic empirical comparison of modified spp variants incorporating specific heuristics (e.g., preferred operators, landmarks) on domains where Patty times out, measuring coverage vs. planning time trade-offs.

- **Open Question 2**: For which classes of temporal numeric planning problems with ICEs does there exist a valid plan with bound `n=1`, and can these problems be characterized combinatorially or by structural properties?
  - *Basis*: Sec. 5 observes many domains have all instances solvable with `n=1` and asks to investigate this property.
  - *Why unresolved*: No theoretical characterization exists; understanding could lead to fast-solving subclasses and better pattern heuristics.
  - *What evidence would resolve*: Formal analysis linking `n=1` solvability to properties (e.g., univariate numeric effects, strong temporal constraints, absence of interfering ICEs across actions) followed by empirical validation on broader benchmarks.

- **Open Question 3**: What is the precise impact of the well-orderability assumption on completeness and efficiency, and can the approach be extended to handle durative actions with non-well-orderable ICEs without compromising scalability?
  - *Basis*: Sec. 3.4 defines well-orderable tasks and relies on this for translation (Thm. 1); actions with variable durations violating `max(K start_b) + max(K end_b) < L` are ineligible for rolling.
  - *Why unresolved*: Many real-world durative actions have duration ranges where ICE timing matters; current approach may lose completeness or require more complex encodings, but extent is unknown.
  - *What evidence would resolve*: Identify/generate benchmarks with non-well-orderable actions; compare Patty’s coverage and encoding size against alternative encodings that do not rely on well-orderability (e.g., explicit unrolling of time points).

## Limitations
- **Pattern compression (`Compress`) ambiguity**: unclear whether result is a contiguous substring or subsequence; impacts pattern length and solver efficiency.
- **Non-well-orderable task handling**: how `H_left` is integrated into linearized patterns is underspecified; may overgenerate patterns and affect scalability.
- **Max-SMT strategy**: exact weighting/incremental approach for maximizing satisfied goals is not detailed; could impact solved instances and runtime.

## Confidence
- **High**: Empirical performance claims (solved 168/190, best in non-ICE domains) — supported by Table 2 results.
- **Medium**: Theoretical completeness (Theorem 4) — proof exists but relies on well-orderability and unlimited time; practical termination not guaranteed.
- **Low**: Scalability to very large instances — SMT formula growth from pattern concatenation may cause solver blowup, though not observed in reported benchmarks.
