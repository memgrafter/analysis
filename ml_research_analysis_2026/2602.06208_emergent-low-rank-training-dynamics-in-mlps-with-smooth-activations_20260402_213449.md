# Emergent Low-Rank Training Dynamics in MLPs with Smooth Activations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06208
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06208
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Weight updates in MLPs with smooth activations concentrate in invariant low-dimensional subspaces of dimension 2K (K = output dimension); low-rank MLPs (rank 2K) achieve comparable test accuracy to full-rank models on Fashion MNIST and CIFAR-10.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the theoretical gap in understanding low-rank training dynamics in nonlinear neural networks. Focusing on multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) with smooth activation functions (ELU, GELU, SiLU), the authors analyze gradient descent training and prove that weight updates become confined to low-dimensional subspaces determined at initialization. These invariant subspaces have dimension 2K, where K is the network's output dimension. The theoretical characterization is established for two-layer networks, and empirical evidence extends the phenomenon to deeper architectures, various optimizers (SGD, Adam, Muon), unwhitened data, and cross-entropy loss. Experiments show that low-rank MLPs, with rank constrained to 2K and properly initialized, perform nearly as well as full-rank models on Fashion MNIST and CIFAR-10, indicating that essential learning dynamics occur within these subspaces.

## Method Summary
The authors combine rigorous theoretical analysis with empirical validation. Theoretically, they consider a two-layer MLP with a smooth activation and semi-orthogonal initialization. Under gradient descent on squared-error loss with whitened data, they prove that the weight update direction for the first layer remains within a fixed subspace spanned by the top 2K singular vectors of the data covariance and the second-layer weight matrix; this subspace is invariant throughout training. For deeper networks, they recursively define similar subspaces and verify through experiments that weight updates concentrate in the corresponding low-dimensional blocks. Empirically, they use synthetic data to track the concentration of updates (via normalized block distances) and real datasets (Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10) to compare full-rank vs. low-rank (rank 2K) MLPs, employing various optimizers and loss functions.

## Key Results
- Theoretical guarantee: For two-layer MLPs with smooth activations, gradient descent updates concentrate in an invariant subspace of dimension 2K (where K is output dimension), with the subspace determined at initialization.
- Empirical observation: In deeper MLPs (up to 4 layers), weight updates similarly concentrate in recursively defined low-dimensional subspaces, even with SGD/Adam/Muon, unwhitened data, and cross-entropy loss.
- Low-rank MLPs (rank 2K) with proper initialization achieve near-identical test accuracy on Fashion MNIST and comparable performance on CIFAR-10 relative to full-rank models, demonstrating that learning dynamics are effectively captured by these subspaces.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism hinges on the smoothness of activations and the semi-orthogonal initialization. For a two-layer network, the gradient of the first-layer weight matrix factorizes into a product involving the second-layer weights and the data covariance. Under whitened data, this gradient becomes approximately low-rank, with its range confined to the span of the top 2K left singular vectors of W2 and the data matrix. Because the initialization is semi-orthogonal, these singular vectors remain nearly constant during training, creating an invariant subspace. The gradient updates thus mostly affect components within this subspace, while the orthogonal complement sees negligible change. For deeper networks, the same principle recurses: each layer's gradient depends on the next layer's weights, which themselves evolve in a low-rank subspace, leading to a chain of low-rank updates. Smooth activations ensure higher-order terms do not disrupt this structure.

## Foundational Learning
- **Smooth activations (ELU, GELU, SiLU):** Infinitely differentiable functions enabling clean gradient analysis and bounded Jacobians. *Why needed:* Smoothness bounds higher-order terms and preserves low-rank structure. *Quick check:* Verify activation derivative is bounded and continuous.
- **Semi-orthogonal initialization:** Weight matrices are scaled orthogonal (or close). *Why needed:* Ensures singular vectors defining the small-update subspace are stable and well-aligned. *Quick check:* Check initial W satisfies W^T W ≈ scaled identity.
- **Whitened data:** Inputs with isotropic covariance (identity). *Why needed:* Diagonalizes data covariance, making low-rank gradient structure explicit. *Quick check:* Compute empirical covariance of data; should be near identity.
- **Gradient descent (GD):** Full-batch gradient updates. *Why needed:* Theoretical analysis assumes exact gradients; avoids stochastic noise that could break concentration. *Quick check:* Use full-batch training for synthetic experiments.
- **Low-rank approximation:** Representing a matrix as a product of thin matrices. *Why needed:* Core claim is that weight matrices can be compressed to rank 2K without loss. *Quick check:* Compute SVD of trained weight; rank-2K reconstruction error should be small.
- **Invariant subspace:** A subspace unchanged by the weight update map. *Why needed:* If updates stay within a fixed subspace, effective degrees of freedom are reduced. *Quick check:* Track span of top singular vectors over time; should be stable.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input -> Linear(W1) -> φ -> Linear(W2) -> φ -> ... -> Linear(W_L) -> Output  
**Critical path:** (1) For two-layer case, show gradient w.r.t. W1 factorizes with low-rank components derived from W2 and data; (2) Prove subspaces (S_small, S_big) are invariant because underlying factors change little orthogonally; (3) Extend recursively to deeper networks via layer-wise dependence.  
**Design tradeoffs:** Theoretical analysis assumes whitened data and full-batch GD for clean proofs; empirical extension to unwhitened data and stochastic optimizers works but with some fragility. Practical use requires careful initialization and subspace estimation from data batches.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- Misaligned low-rank basis: If learned eU/eV deviate from true invariant subspaces (alignment ∥sinΘ(eU, ĤU)∥_F > 0.5), low-rank training fails.  
- Large gradient tail: If σ_{K+1}(G1) / σ_K(W2^T Y X^T) > 0.1, low-rank assumption breaks.  
- Optimizer sensitivity: Adam/Muon can weaken concentration; monitor normalized block distances (fWl1 share) — below 80% at convergence indicates breakdown.  
**First three experiments:**  
1. Two-layer synthetic: 2-layer MLP (m=72, GELU) on whitened data (d=64, K=4, N=1000). Initialize W1 as ϵ-scaled orthogonal (ϵ=0.01), fix W2. Full-batch GD (η=1e-2, squared loss, 100 epochs). Plot W1 singular values and normalized block distances (fW11 vs others) to verify Theorem 3.5.  
2. Deeper dynamics: 4-layer MLP (m=72, ELU/GELU) on same synthetic data (no whitening). Initialize first 3 layers ϵ-scaled orthogonal. Define S_small recursively (Eq. 8). Compute fWl1,…,4 per Eq. 9 for each layer; plot normalized distances to show concentration.  
3. Low-rank validation on Fashion MNIST: 4-layer MLP (m=d=784, GELU/SiLU). Rank r=2K. Full-rank: initialize all layers ϵ-scaled orthogonal (ϵ=0.1), full-batch GD (η=1e-5). ## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees are restricted to two-layer MLPs with specific assumptions (semi-orthogonal initialization, no normalization); extension to deeper networks and practical settings lacks rigorous proof.
- The unquantified "unbiased estimator" for first-layer gradient in CIFAR-10 experiments introduces irreproducibility; batch size and sampling protocol are unclear.
- Empirical results on real data sometimes degrade under Adam/Muon or unwhitened data, suggesting the phenomenon may be fragile to common training choices.

## Confidence
- Two-layer theoretical characterization (subspace dimension = 2K, invariant update direction): High.
- Emergent low-rank dynamics in deeper MLPs (empirically observed, not proven for l>1): Medium.
- Generalization to standard deep learning pipelines (CIFAR-10 with Adam, unwhitened data): Low.

## Next Checks
