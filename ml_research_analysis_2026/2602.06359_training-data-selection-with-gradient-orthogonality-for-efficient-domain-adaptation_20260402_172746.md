# Training Data Selection with Gradient Orthogonality for Efficient Domain Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06359
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06359
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** OGS matches full-data domain performance with 10% data while preserving general reasoning and reducing selection overhead 15× via Navigator-Target transfer.

## Executive Summary
OGS is a data selection method for efficient domain adaptation of large language models that mitigates catastrophic forgetting. It selects training samples whose gradients are orthogonal to a precomputed general-knowledge anchor, ensuring updates lie in a "safe subspace." A lightweight Navigator model computes orthogonality scores, and a reinforcement learning policy dynamically mixes orthogonal domain samples with conflict-aware replay. This approach achieves Pareto improvements: it reaches full-data domain performance using only 10% of the data, significantly outperforms prior methods in preserving general reasoning (e.g., GSM8K), and cuts selection overhead by 15× through cross-model gradient geometry transfer.

## Method Summary
OGS shifts gradient projection from the optimizer to the data selection stage. A compact anchor dataset defines an average general gradient direction `g_ref`. Candidate domain samples are scored for orthogonality `Orth(x) = 1 - |cos(g, g_ref)|`; those above a threshold `τ_orth` are deemed safe. A lightweight Navigator model (from the same family as the Target) computes these scores and cluster geometries. An RL policy (PPO-Lagrangian) is trained on the Navigator to dynamically balance domain learning (reward) and forgetting prevention (cost) by selecting data clusters over time. The learned policy is then applied to the Target model, assuming Navigator-Target gradient geometry transferability, enabling efficient selection without costly online projections.

## Key Results
- Matches full-data domain performance (e.g., 62.1% average on Qwen3-8B) using only 10% of the domain data.
- Preserves general reasoning: GSM8K accuracy 68.5%, significantly higher than LESS (60.1%) and GrADS (66.8%).
- Reduces selection overhead by 15× via Navigator-Target transfer, with <0.3% performance drop compared to using the Target directly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Offline Geometric Awareness via Orthogonal Gradient Selection
- Claim: Selecting training samples whose gradients are orthogonal to a precomputed general-knowledge anchor prevents catastrophic forgetting by ensuring updates lie in the "safe subspace" where general loss does not increase (first-order).
- Mechanism: A compact anchor dataset (e.g., GSM8K, MMLU) defines an average gradient direction `g_ref`. For each candidate domain sample, its gradient computed on a Navigator model is evaluated for orthogonality: `Orth(x) = 1 - |cos(g, g_ref)|`. Samples above a threshold `τ_orth` are selected, guaranteeing that during fine-tuning, the expected inner product with general gradients is near zero.
- Core assumption: The first-order Taylor approximation holds: forgetting is dominated by the inner product of domain and general gradients (Theorem 4.1). Orthogonality (zero inner product) thus eliminates first-order forgetting.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [Abstract] "OGS shifts the geometric insights of gradient projection from the optimizer to the data selection stage... By leveraging a lightweight Navigator model... OGS dynamically identifies training samples whose gradients are orthogonal to a general-knowledge anchor."
  - [Section 3.3] Formal definitions of Orthogonality and Conflict scores, with Conflict = `-cos(g, g_ref)`.
  - [Section 4.1] Theorem 4.1: "∆L_gen = -η⟨∇L_gen, ∇L_med⟩ + O(η²)" establishes the interference term.
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related gradient-based data selection methods (LESS, GrADS) but none explicitly enforce orthogonality to a general anchor; their focus is similarity or magnitude.
- Break condition: If the anchor gradient `g_ref` is poorly representative (e.g., missing key general skills) or `τ_orth` is set too high, the selected subset may lack sufficient domain diversity, limiting domain performance.

### Mechanism 2: Navigator-Target Transferability
- Claim: Geometric relationships (cosine similarities) between data gradients and the anchor direction are preserved across model scales, allowing a small Navigator to guide selection for a much larger Target.
- Mechanism: A lightweight Navigator (e.g., 0.6B) from the same family as the Target (e.g., 8B) computes orthogonality/conflict scores for all domain samples. These scores are used to train an RL policy offline. The same policy is then applied to the Target, assuming the ranking of sample safety is consistent.
- Core assumption: The semantic orientation of gradients relative to `g_ref` is a property of the data distribution and model family, not absolute scale; thus, the Spearman correlation between Navigator and Target cosine rankings is sufficiently high (observed γ ∈ [0.4, 0.7]).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [Abstract] "using a lightweight Navigator model and reinforcement learning techniques... avoid costly online projections."
  - [Section 3.2] "A key insight is that gradient geometric features exhibit remarkable transferability across model scales... This phenomenon arises because models are from the same architectural family..."
  - [Section 5.3] Table 8: "w/o Navigator (Upper Bound)" vs. full OGS shows <0.3% performance difference with 15× cost reduction.
  - [corpus] Corpus includes efficient data selection works (e.g., CHIPS) but does not address cross-model geometry transfer; OGS's Navigator idea is novel in this context.
- Break condition: If the Navigator is too small (e.g., <0.1B) or architecturally mismatched (different tokenizer, layer types), the correlation may drop below useful levels, causing false positives (samples safe on Navigator but harmful on Target).

### Mechanism 3: RL-Driven Dynamic Policy for Constrained Selection
- Claim: A reinforcement learning policy trained on the Navigator can dynamically balance domain learning speed and forgetting prevention by adaptively mixing orthogonal selection and conflict-aware replay.
- Mechanism: Data selection is cast as a Constrained MDP: state includes current performance metrics and cluster-level geometry stats; action selects a data cluster; reward = domain improvement; cost = general degradation. PPO-Lagrangian optimizes the policy while a Lagrange multiplier λ enforces the forgetting constraint `E[∑ cost] ≤ ϵ`. The policy learns a time-varying mixing ratio α(t).
- Core assumption: The optimal trade-off between plasticity and stability is non-stationary over training steps; an RL agent can learn this dynamic curriculum from interactions on the Navigator.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [Abstract] "By leveraging... reinforcement learning techniques, OGS dynamically identifies..."
  - [Section 3.5] Detailed CMDP formulation and PPO-Lagrangian update: "L(π, λ) = Eπ[∑ γ_t r_t] - λ(Eπ[∑ γ_t c_t] - ϵ)".
  - [Section 5.3] Ablation: "w/o RL (Static)" underperforms full OGS by 1.4% on general accuracy, demonstrating the benefit of a learned dynamic policy.
  - [corpus] Corpus includes reinforced data selection (Liu et al., 2019) but not for gradient orthogonality constraints; OGS adapts RL to a new objective.
- Break condition: RL training may be unstable or sample-inefficient; if the state representation fails to capture relevant dynamics (e.g., forgetting trends), the policy may not generalize to the Target training trajectory.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Gradient Interference Theory  
  **Why needed:** OGS's core premise is that catastrophic forgetting is caused by negative inner products between domain and general gradients. Understanding this geometric view explains why orthogonality is a sufficient first-order safety condition.  
  **Quick check:** According to Theorem 4.1, what term in the Taylor expansion determines whether fine-tuning increases general loss?
- **Concept:** Data Selection for Efficient Fine-Tuning  
  **Why needed:** OGS is a data-centric alternative to gradient surgery. Knowing prior methods (LESS, GrADS) clarifies OGS's novelty: using orthogonality instead of similarity or magnitude to avoid forgetting.  
  **Quick check:** Why do gradient magnitude-based methods like GrADS fail to prevent catastrophic forgetting reliably?
- **Concept:** Reinforcement Learning for Constrained Optimization  
  **Why needed:** OGS uses PPO-Lagrangian to solve a constrained selection problem where the constraint is forgetting. This allows automatic balancing of domain gain and stability.  
  **Quick check:** In PPO-Lagrangian, what effect does increasing the Lagrange multiplier λ have on the policy's actions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Anchor construction -> Navigator model -> RL policy -> Target model (with LoRA)
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Preprocessing: Build anchor, compute `g_ref`, cluster domain/general pools.  
  2. Phase 1 (Policy Learning on Navigator): For each episode, run T steps: compute cluster geometries → construct state → sample action → update Navigator → compute reward/cost → update policy via PPO-Lagrangian.  
  3. Phase 2 (Policy Application on Target): For each Target step: compute state (using cached Navigator scores or proxy metrics) → select cluster → sample batch → update Target with standard SFT.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Navigator size: Smaller reduces preprocessing cost but may weaken geometry correlation. Choose smallest model that maintains Spearman γ > 0.5 (empirically 0.6B for 8B Target).  
  - Orthogonality threshold τ_orth: Higher ensures safety but may discard useful domain data; lower risks forgetting. Tune based on validation set (e.g., monitor GSM8K during selection).  
  - Replay frequency: Adding conflict-aware replay improves stability but adds general data loading overhead. Hybrid α(t) learned automatically vs. fixed schedule.  
  - Anchor diversity: Must cover all critical general capabilities; narrow anchor leads to incomplete protection (e.g., only math, no world knowledge).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Domain performance plateaus below full-data baseline: Check τ_orth too high or anchor misaligned; try lowering threshold or expanding anchor.  
  - GSM8K still drops significantly: Navigator-Target transfer failure (low correlation); verify by computing a small subset on Target and comparing rankings; if correlation < 0.4, use larger Navigator or different base.  
  - RL policy unstable (λ oscillating, constraint violated): Increase PPO batch size, adjust reward scaling, or add more state features (e.g., recent loss trends).  
  - Selection overhead still high: Navigator too large; reduce navigator size or use gradient checkpointing; also consider fewer clusters.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Anchor gradient stability: Bootstrap sample anchor sets, compute `g_ref` multiple times; ensure cosine similarity > 0.95 between iterations.  
  2. Navigator-Target correlation test: Randomly sample 500 domain examples, compute orthogonality scores on both Navigator and Target (via a few forward passes). Compute Spearman rank correlation; if < 0.5, consider a larger Navigator.  
  3. Policy convergence validation: During Phase 1, plot average cost (forgetting proxy) per episode; it should decrease and stabilize below constraint budget ϵ within 10-20 episodes. Also monitor λ to ensure it converges.

## Limitations
- Anchor set size and coverage may not fully represent general abilities; poor choice leads to unprotected skills.
- Orthogonality threshold τ_orth requires careful tuning; values that are too high reduce domain relevance, too low risks forgetting.
- Navigator-Target gradient geometry transfer is validated only for Qwen models; its generalizability to other families or extreme scale gaps is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Orthogonal gradient selection** (first-order forgetting prevention): High
- **Navigator-Target transferability** (cross-model geometry): Medium
- **RL-driven dynamic policy** (adaptive trade-off): Medium

## Next Checks
1. Anchor stability test: Resample anchor sets (5 bootstraps) and recompute selected subsets; aim for <1% variance in domain performance and GSM8K accuracy.
2. Cross-family correlation test: Compute Navigator-Target orthogonality ranking correlations for 0.5B → 7B models across at least two architectures (e.g., Llama, Mistral). Expect correlation ≥0.5 for same family, quantify drop for different families.
3. RL policy robustness test: Train with 3 random seeds and varying λ initializations; report average constraint violation (<5%) and domain gain standard deviation (<0.5%). If violations exceed budget, explore curriculum-based λ scheduling.
