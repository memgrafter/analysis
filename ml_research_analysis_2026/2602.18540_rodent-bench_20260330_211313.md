# Rodent-Bench

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18540
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18540
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Multimodal Large Language Models show modest success on rodent behavior annotation tasks but lack robustness for practical scientific use

## Executive Summary
Rodent-Bench is a benchmark for evaluating Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on rodent behavioral annotation tasks. It comprises two versions: Rodent-Bench-Short (videos up to 10 minutes) and Rodent-Bench-Long (videos up to 35 minutes), covering diverse datasets including social interactions, grooming, scratching, and freezing behaviors. The study evaluates three state-of-the-art MLLMs—Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-2.5-Flash, and Qwen-VL-Max—using standardized metrics such as second-wise accuracy, macro F1, mean average precision, mutual information, and Matthew's correlation coefficient. Results show that none of the models perform strongly enough for practical use, with modest success on some datasets (e.g., grooming detection) but significant challenges in temporal segmentation, handling extended video sequences, and distinguishing subtle behavioral states. The findings highlight key limitations in current MLLMs for scientific video annotation and provide insights for future model development.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates MLLMs on rodent behavioral annotation through zero-shot prompting using standardized JSON output schemas across multiple datasets. Three models were tested: Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-2.5-Flash, and Qwen-VL-Max, with the latter requiring frame-by-frame processing. The benchmark includes both short videos (up to 10 minutes) and long videos (up to 35 minutes) across five datasets covering social interactions, grooming, scratching, and freezing behaviors. Performance was measured using five standardized metrics including second-wise accuracy, macro F1, mean average precision, mutual information, and Matthew's correlation coefficient. The evaluation process involved running models on video inputs and comparing their JSON-formatted predictions against ground truth annotations.

## Key Results
- None of the evaluated MLLMs achieved strong enough performance for practical rodent behavior annotation
- Modest success was observed for grooming detection tasks, but significant challenges remained in temporal segmentation
- Models struggled with subtle behavioral states and long video sequences (up to 35 minutes)
- Qwen-VL-Max showed limitations with JSON output generation, sometimes stopping mid-segment

## Foundational Learning
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): AI systems that can process both text and visual inputs simultaneously, needed for understanding video content; quick check: can process image/video frames with text prompts
- Behavioral annotation in neuroscience: The process of labeling specific actions or states in animal behavior videos, crucial for understanding brain-behavior relationships; quick check: involves time-series classification of video frames
- Temporal segmentation: The ability to identify when specific behaviors start and end in continuous video, essential for behavioral analysis; quick check: requires fine-grained time resolution
- Zero-shot evaluation: Testing models without any task-specific training, providing baseline performance assessment; quick check: uses only prompt engineering without model adaptation
- Video frame extraction: The preprocessing step of converting videos into individual frames for model processing, critical for frame-by-frame models; quick check: typically 30 frames per second for standard video
- JSON output schemas: Standardized structured formats for model predictions, enabling consistent evaluation across different models; quick check: defines specific fields and formats for behavior labels and timestamps

## Limitations
- Current MLLMs are not robust enough for practical scientific use in rodent behavior annotation
- Access to private freezing behavior dataset is restricted, preventing full reproducibility
- Frame extraction and preprocessing details for Qwen-VL-Max are not fully specified
- Zero-shot prompting may not represent optimal achievable performance

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Benchmark creation methodology and standardized evaluation metrics are clearly described and reproducible
- Medium Confidence: Performance comparisons between models are reliable, though absolute performance levels may be affected by dataset access limitations
- Medium Confidence: Identification of specific failure modes (temporal segmentation, subtle behaviors, long video handling) is well-supported by results
