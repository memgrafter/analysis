# Draft-and-Prune: Improving the Reliability of Auto-formalization for Logical Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17233
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17233
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Draft-and-Prune achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on logical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., 78.43% on AR-LSAT with GPT-4) and pruning alone provides a 33.30% absolute boost on AR-LSAT.

## Executive Summary
Draft-and-Prune (D&P) is an inference-time framework that addresses the brittleness of auto-formalization (AF) pipelines for logical reasoning, where generated programs often fail to execute or encode incorrect semantics. D&P first drafts multiple high-level natural-language plans and generates formalizations conditioned on each plan, introducing semantic diversity while maintaining syntactic stability. It then prunes executable but contradictory or ambiguous formalizations via solver-based well-definedness checks, retaining only those yielding a singleton set of feasible hypotheses. Finally, it aggregates surviving answers by majority voting. Evaluated on AR-LSAT, ProofWriter, PrOntoQA, and LogicalDeduction, D&P delivers substantial gains: on AR-LSAT, it achieves 78.43% accuracy with GPT-4 (far surpassing prior methods), and near-ceiling performance (98.33–100%) on the other benchmarks. Ablation studies confirm that pruning is critical, alone increasing AR-LSAT accuracy by over 33 percentage points.

## Method Summary
D&P operates purely at inference without model retraining. For each input problem, it samples *k* high-level natural-language plans using an LLM with temperature *T_draft* > 0. Each plan conditions the generation of a formal program (e.g., Z3Py code) via greedy decoding (*T_gen*=0) to ensure syntactic correctness. Syntax errors trigger up to *R* repair rounds using solver feedback. The program is executed to obtain the set of feasible hypotheses *S_i*. Only paths with |*S_i*| = 1 survive pruning; others are discarded as ill-defined (contradictory if |*S_i*|=0, ambiguous if |*S_i*|>1). Majority voting over singleton answers from surviving paths yields the final prediction; if no paths survive, the system abstains. Key hyperparameters include *k*=20 for main results, *T_draft*=1.0, *T_gen*=0, and *R*=2.

## Key Results
- On AR-LSAT, D&P with GPT-4 achieves 78.43% accuracy, and with GPT-4o, 78.00%, significantly outperforming MAD-LOGIC (53.25%) and CLOVER (46.8%).
- On ProofWriter, PrOntoQA, and LogicalDeduction, D&P reaches near-ceiling performance (98.33% to 100%).
- Pruning alone boosts AR-LSAT accuracy from 45.13% to 78.43%, a 33.30% absolute improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
D&P's reliability stems from three synergistic mechanisms:

1. **Plan-conditioned generation for semantic diversity**: Drafting multiple natural-language plans before formalization injects stochasticity at the semantic level, increasing the likelihood that at least one plan correctly encodes the problem. The mapping from natural language to symbolic logic is ambiguous; diverse plans cover different interpretations. Evidence: Ablations show plan drafting improves accuracy even without pruning.

2. **Pruning executable but ill-defined formalizations**: Formalizations that execute but yield zero (contradictory) or multiple (ambiguous) feasible hypotheses are removed. This filters out programs that, despite syntactic correctness, misrepresent the problem constraints. Evidence: The 33.30% gain from pruning alone on AR-LSAT validates its critical role in eliminating semantic errors.

3. **Majority voting over surviving answers**: After pruning, correct answers appear more frequently among survivors due to diversity and filtering. Majority voting aggregates these singleton answers, leveraging statistical dominance to predict the correct conclusion. Evidence: High end-to-end accuracy across benchmarks and increasing coverage@k with more samples confirm voting efficacy.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Plan Drafter -> Formalization Generator -> Solver (with repair) -> Pruner -> Aggregator

- **Critical path**: For each of *k* paths: (1) draft plan *p_i* with *T_draft*=1.0; (2) generate code *z_i* conditioned on *p_i* via greedy decoding; (3) repair syntax up to *R*=2 times if errors occur; (4) execute *z_i* to obtain *S_i*; (5) prune if |*S_i*| ≠ 1. After all paths, aggregate surviving singleton answers by majority vote; if none survive, abstain.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Sampling budget k*: Larger *k* increases coverage but linearly raises compute cost; diminishing returns observed beyond *k*=20 on AR-LSAT.
  - *Pruning vs. survivors*: Aggressive pruning removes noise but risks too few paths for reliable voting, especially on harder instances.
  - *Plan diversity vs. quality*: Higher *T_draft* increases diversity but may produce incoherent plans; greedy code generation mitigates syntax errors.
  - *Solver completeness*: Pruning assumes solvers enumerate all satisfying assignments; incomplete search could misclassify correct formalizations as ambiguous.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Low execution rate (<50%): Many programs fail to parse/execute → issues in code generation or repair.
  - Low accuracy among executable (<50%): Among running programs, few are semantically correct → systematic translation failures.
  - High pruning rate (>80%): Most paths are contradictory/ambiguous → poor semantic diversity or overly strict well-definedness.
  - High variance across runs: Unstable results with random seeds → insufficient *k* or excessive stochasticity.
  - Frequent ties in majority vote: Uniform vote distribution → survivors lack consensus; may need diversity control or weighting.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Plan drafting ablation**: Compare single-path AF with vs. without drafting a plan on AR-LSAT to isolate the contribution of plan-level diversity.
  2. **Pruning impact**: Run D&P with *k*=20 on AR-LSAT with and without pruning to quantify the accuracy gain from filtering ill-defined paths.
  3. **Coverage growth**: Measure cover@k (fraction of instances with at least one correct path) as *k* increases across benchmarks to demonstrate that more samples elevate the chance of encountering a correct encoding.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Inference-time cost reduction**: How can computational cost be reduced while preserving D&P's accuracy gains? The paper notes cost as a limitation but offers no solutions.
2. **Integration of model adaptation**: Can fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, or distillation improve the base auto-formalizer within D&P? The framework treats LLMs as black boxes; adaptation might boost performance or reduce sampling needs.
3. **Equivalence-aware aggregation**: Current majority voting ignores symbolic equivalence among formalizations, potentially overweighting redundant programs. How can aggregation be made equivalence-aware?
4. **Optimizing plan diversification**: Naive temperature sampling is used; what guided diversification strategies (e.g., diverse beam search, prompt variation) maximize coverage of correct formalizations without semantic drift?

## Limitations
- Pruning assumes exactly one correct answer per instance; problems with multiple or no valid conclusions may have correct formalizations erroneously discarded.
- Missing experimental specifics (ICL demonstrations, repair prompt templates, exact solver timeouts) may hinder exact replication.
- Evaluation is limited to four narrow logical reasoning benchmarks; generalization to broader logical constructs or domains remains unproven.

## Confidence
- **Plan diversity**: Medium – ablations support its role, but coverage of plan quality landscapes is unclear.
- **Pruning**: High – the 33.30% absolute gain on AR-LSAT is strong evidence, contingent on the |S_i|=1 assumption holding.
- **Majority voting**: Medium – effective when many survivors exist, but vulnerability to ties and aggregation failures is not fully measured.

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate D&P on a dataset with known multi-answer instances to quantify the false pruning rate and assess robustness to varied well-definedness.
2. Conduct a prompt perturbation study: vary the three in-context learning examples (templates, seeds) to measure output stability and sensitivity to prompt design.
3. Systematically measure accuracy versus sample budget *k* (e.g., from 5 to 40) across all benchmarks to identify cost-accuracy inflection points and optimal *k*.
