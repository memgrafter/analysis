# IndicFairFace: Balanced Indian Face Dataset for Auditing and Mitigating Geographical Bias in Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12659
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12659
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A balanced, ethically sourced dataset of 14,400 Indian faces across 28 states and 8 Union Territories exposes significant intra-state geographical bias in CLIP-based VLMs; debiasing via Iterative Nullspace Projection (INLP) reduces inter-state similarity variance by ~37% with minimal (<1.5%) utility loss on 35 external benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the "monolithic Indian" bias in vision-language models (VLMs), where prompts like "Indian person" retrieve faces disproportionately from a few states. The authors create **IndicFairFace**, a balanced dataset of 14,400 face images (100 per gender per state/UT), to audit this bias. Using prompt-based retrieval, they demonstrate that CLIP-based models exhibit strong geographical skew. They then apply **Iterative Nullspace Projection (INLP)** to remove state-specific bias directions from the joint embedding space, complemented by SLERP interpolation and similarity compensation to preserve utility. Debiased models show a ~37% reduction in inter-state variance and near-perfect accuracy for previously underrepresented states in a job-application simulation, all while losing less than 1.5% accuracy on standard vision-language benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The reproduction workflow follows four steps: (1) Acquire the IndicFairFace dataset (images, state/gender labels) and pre-trained CLIP variants; (2) Extract image and text embeddings for target prompts; (3) Audit bias by computing per-state average cosine similarities and Jensen-Shannon divergence from uniform; (4) Apply INLP: train linear classifiers to distinguish state embeddings, iteratively project embeddings onto nullspaces of classifier weights, blend with original embeddings via SLERP (strength α), and add a similarity-compensation vector aligned with the prompt. Debiased embeddings are re-evaluated for fairness (σ, JSD) and utility (top-1 accuracy on 35 external datasets).

## Key Results
- **Dataset:** 7,200 unique images balanced across 28 states, 8 UTs, and binary gender; augmented to 14,400 with mild transforms.
- **Bias reduction:** Debiasing via INLP lowers inter-state standard deviation of similarity scores by ~37% and Jensen-Shannon divergence toward uniform distribution.
- **Utility preservation:** Average zero-shot accuracy on 35 external benchmarks drops by <1.5%.
- **Simulation success:** In a job-application task, debiased models achieve near-100% accuracy for previously low-scoring "at-risk" states.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach succeeds through three interconnected mechanisms. **First**, constructing a geographically and gender-balanced dataset removes data imbalance as a confounder, enabling a clean audit. Images are ethically sourced (Wikimedia, open web), filtered with a rigorous pipeline (face detection, texture/skin validation), and exactly balanced (100 per gender per state) with mild augmentations. This guarantees that any retrieval skew reflects model bias, not dataset skew [section 3]. **Second**, a prompt-based retrieval audit uses cosine similarity between text prompts (e.g., "An Indian person") and balanced face embeddings to quantify bias. The distribution of top-k retrieved states and metrics like standard deviation (σ) and Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD) measure deviation from fairness. The balanced dataset ensures over-representation indicates encoded model bias [abstract, section 4.5]. **Third**, INLP debiasing removes linear state-discriminatory directions from the joint embedding space. A logistic regression classifier distinguishes a target state's embeddings; its weight vector defines a bias direction. Embeddings are projected onto the nullspace orthogonal to this direction. Iterating captures multiple bias directions. SLERP blends original and projected embeddings to control strength, and a similarity-compensation vector (aligned with the prompt) restores semantic alignment lost during projection. This reduces bias with minimal utility loss [abstract, section 4.4].

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data Curation (Wikimedia SPARQL, SerpAPI; OpenCV/YOLOv8 cleaning; balancing with augmentations) -> Bias Audit (encode prompts/images with VLM; compute cosine similarities; aggregate by state; calculate σ, JSD) -> Debiasing (INLP: train logistic regressions iteratively; project embeddings; blend via SLERP; add similarity compensation) -> Evaluation (test on 35 external benchmarks; job-application simulation).

Critical path: (1) Build IndicFairFace, (2) Audit baseline VLM bias, (3) Apply INLP debiasing, (4) Re-audit for bias reduction, (5) Evaluate utility on downstream tasks.

Design tradeoffs: Mild augmentations risk distorting facial features but are needed for balance; INLP modifies embeddings at test time without full retraining, saving compute but possibly less integrated; SLERP and compensation introduce hyperparameters (α, β) requiring tuning; evaluation on 35 benchmarks is thorough but expensive.

Failure signatures: After debiasing, σ and JSD show <10% reduction; external benchmark accuracy drops >1.5%; job simulation still shows low accuracy for at-risk states; projection matrices miscomputed (e.g., classifier fails to converge).

First 3 experiments:
1. **Baseline bias quantification**: For a CLIP variant, compute per-state mean similarities for "An Indian person" prompt; record σ and JSD. Expect high σ (>0.1) and JSD (>0.5).
2. **Debiasing iteration test**: Apply INLP with K=1,5,10 iterations; measure Δσ and ΔJSD. Identify plateau where further iterations yield negligible gains; verify projection matrices are orthogonalizing bias directions.
3. **Generalization check**: After debiasing (e.g., K=5), evaluate on ImageNet-1k; compute top-1 accuracy drop. Acceptable drop: <1.5%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: Does incorporating non-binary gender identities into IndicFairFace alter observed geographical bias or introduce new fairness considerations?  
  Basis: The dataset uses binary gender categories and explicitly notes this limitation [Section 13].  
  Why unresolved: No data on non-binary individuals, so bias against them cannot be assessed.  
  Evidence needed: Annotate a non-binary inclusive dataset and repeat audits.

- **Question 2**: Is INLP debiasing equally effective for non-CLIP VLMs (e.g., BLIP, Flamingo)?  
  Basis: Experiments only cover CLIP-based models [Abstract, Section 4.1].  
  Why unresolved: Different architectures/training objectives may yield different embedding spaces.  
  Evidence needed: Apply INLP to diverse VLMs and measure bias reduction/utility.

- **Question 3**: How much geographical bias remains within states when accounting for finer-grained socio-cultural attributes like caste, religion, or language?  
  Basis: State-level balance may obscure intra-state disparities; dataset lacks such annotations [Section 13].  
  Why unresolved: Current metrics cannot detect subgroup biases.  
  Evidence needed: Annotate IndicFairFace with caste/religion/language and audit at subgroup level.

- **Question 4**: Does removing geographical bias directions inadvertently amplify other biases (e.g., skin tone, age) due to data correlations?  
  Basis: Debiasing targets only state-level bias; utility evaluation does not examine intersectional attributes.  
  Why unresolved: Projection might affect correlated dimensions.  
  Evidence needed: Post-debiasing, measure embeddings for skin tone/age biases using appropriate annotations.

## Limitations
- **Dataset construction**: Label noise possible from ambiguous geographic associations (birthplace vs. residence); mild augmentations might alter skin textures.
- **Bias audit**: Prompt selection and top-k cutoff are not exhaustively justified; cosine similarity may be influenced by non-facial cues (background, lighting).
- **Debiasing**: INLP assumes linear separability of bias; hyperparameters (iterations, SLERP α, compensation β) are not fully disclosed; reported utility drop is averaged and may not generalize to all tasks.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1 (Balanced, ethical dataset)**: Medium. Pipeline is transparent but lacks external validation of state labels or demographic representativeness.
- **Claim 2 (Significant intra-state bias in CLIP models)**: High. Consistent across variants and prompts, aligned with literature.
- **Claim 3 (INLP reduces bias with minimal utility loss)**: Medium. Results are substantial, but hyperparameter ablation is missing and linear projection assumptions may not hold universally.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Sweep INLP iterations (K=1–20), SLERP α ∈ [0.1,0.9], and logistic regularization (C); measure Δσ, ΔJSD, and utility change on benchmarks to find robust settings.
2. **Label-noise audit**: Sample 20 images per state, have annotators verify geographic association; compute agreement and correlate error rates with retrieval performance.
3. **Prompt robustness**: Audit with diverse prompts (e.g., "person from [State]", regional language translations) and varying top-k (100–1000); report stability of σ and JSD.
