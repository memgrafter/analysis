# Beyond Hard Constraints: Budget-Conditioned Reachability For Safe Offline Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.22292  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22292  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: BCRL achieves safe policies in all 38 DSRL tasks, outperforms SOTA in 16 tasks, attains highest average normalized reward on SafetyGym, BulletGym, and MetaDrive; in maritime navigation, reduces close‑quarter events to 26%, achieves 88% success, and lowers average displacement error to 52 m, with training speeds of minutes versus hours.

## Executive Summary
Safe offline reinforcement learning under cumulative cost constraints is challenging due to unstable min‑max optimization in existing methods. This paper introduces Budget‑Conditioned Reachability (BCR), which augments the state with a dynamic remaining budget and precomputes a budget‑conditioned persistent safety set. By decoupling safety enforcement from reward maximization, BCR avoids adversarial training. Integrated with Implicit Q‑Learning (IQL) as BCRL, the approach learns from fixed datasets while guaranteeing cost constraint satisfaction. Extensive experiments on 38 DSRL tasks and a real‑world maritime navigation dataset demonstrate BCRL’s superior safety, performance, and training efficiency.

## Method Summary
BCR transforms a Constrained MDP (CMDP) into a Budget‑Adaptive MDP (BAMDP) by adding a remaining budget variable that tracks allowable future discounted cost. From offline data, a cost critic estimates the minimal cost‑to‑go. Using this critic, the algorithm precomputes a persistent safety set: for any state‑budget pair, actions with estimated Q‑value below the budget are safe. The offline dataset is then augmented by sampling budgets within this set and updating budgets via a deterministic (or soft) function. Finally, an in‑sample offline RL algorithm (IQL) trains a policy on the augmented data, restricted to safe actions. This decoupled pipeline yields stable learning, provable constraint satisfaction, and no need for online interaction or min‑max optimization.

## Key Results
- BCRL produces safe policies in all 38 DSRL tasks, outperforms state‑of‑the‑art baselines in 16 tasks, and attains the highest average normalized reward across SafetyGym, BulletGym, and MetaDrive.
- On real maritime navigation (AIS data), BCRL reduces close‑quarter events from 30% to 26%, achieves 88% success rate, and lowers average displacement error to 52 m, while maintaining realistic acceleration profiles.
- Training completes in minutes, significantly faster than baseline methods that take hours.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BCR’s effectiveness stems from three key mechanisms:

1. **Budget‑augmented state representation** – The remaining budget is part of the state, converting the CMDP into a BAMDP. This allows tracking cumulative cost offline: after each transition, the budget is updated by subtracting the incurred cost (discounted). Restricting the policy to actions whose cost‑ Q‑value does not exceed the current budget guarantees the cumulative cost stays under the initial threshold. (Assumption: accurate cost critic; break condition: cost underestimation leads to overly optimistic budget updates and possible violations.)

2. **Precomputed persistent safety set** – For each state‑budget pair, the set of safe actions is defined as those with estimated cost‑Q ≤ current budget. This set is computed once from the cost critic and does not depend on the reward‑maximizing policy. Decoupling safety from reward eliminates the instability of Lagrangian or min‑max methods. (Assumption: cost critic approximates the optimal cost‑to‑go; break condition: poor estimation yields either over‑restriction or constraint violations.)

3. **Integration with in‑sample offline RL** – The original dataset is augmented with budget‑conditioned transitions: for each original transition, a budget δ is sampled uniformly between the cost‑Q and a maximum, and the next budget δ’ is computed. The augmented dataset lies entirely within the feasible subspace. Standard IQL (expectile regression) then learns a reward‑maximizing policy that never queries out‑of‑distribution actions because the safe action set is already enforced. (Assumption: sufficient coverage of feasible actions in the original data; break condition: lack of feasible actions leads to narrow support and poor reward performance.)

## Foundational Learning
- **Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP)**  
  *Why needed*: Formalizes the problem of maximizing cumulative reward under a cumulative cost constraint. Understanding CMDPs is essential to grasp the challenge of safe RL and alternatives like Lagrangian relaxation or reachability‑based methods.  
  *Quick check*: Given a CMDP with threshold δ_init, what does it mean for a policy to be feasible?

- **Reachability / Persistent Safety**  
  *Why needed*: BCR’s safety mechanism is based on a forward‑invariant set of state‑budget pairs from which a safe policy exists. This reachability concept extends to cumulative costs.  
  *Quick check*: How does V_C^*(s) ≤ δ guarantee the existence of a policy that stays within budget?

- **Offline RL with In‑Sample Learning (e.g., Implicit Q‑Learning)**  
  *Why needed*: The algorithm must learn from a fixed dataset without querying out‑of‑distribution actions. IQL’s expectile regression provide stability by avoiding over‑estimation.  
  *Quick check*: Why does the max operator in standard Q‑learning cause problems in the offline setting, and how does IQL address it?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**  
  Data ingestion → Cost‑critic network (Q_C, V_C) → Budget sampler (draw δ ~ Uniform[Q_C(s,a), δ_max]) → Augmented transition generator (update δ via function g) → Reward‑critic network (Q_R, V_R) → Policy network (AWR) → Action selector constrained by A_P(s,δ).

- **Critical path**  
  1. Learn cost‑to‑go on original dataset (minimize cost prediction error).  
  2. Dynamically build augmented dataset by sampling budgets within the precomputed persistent safety set and updating budgets.  
  3. Train reward critic and policy on augmented data using IQL losses; all actions remain within the safe set throughout.

- **Design tradeoffs**  
  - Deterministic vs. soft budget tracking: deterministic simpler and optimal in deterministic MDPs; soft tracking provides invariance in stochastic settings but can be more conservative.  
  - Number of budget samples n per transition: larger n improves coverage but increases computational cost.  
  - Expectile τ_C for cost critic: lower values (e.g., 0.2) yield more conservative cost estimates, enhancing safety at potential reward cost.

- **Failure signatures**  
  - Sudden spikes in normalized cost > 1 during evaluation indicate cost critic underestimation or budget update leaving the safe set.  
  - Very low reward while cost is safe may signal over‑conservative feasible set (e.g., too high τ_C or inaccurate Q_C).  
  - Training instability if the reward critic receives transitions that effectively require querying OOD Q‑values (ensure δ always ≥ Q_C).

- **First 3 experiments**  
  1. **Grid‑world**: Compare BCRL against Linear Programming optimal solutions under varying transition stochasticity; validates budget‑tracking correctness and safe set coverage.  
  2. **DSRL benchmarks (38 tasks)**: Evaluate safety (cost ≤ threshold) and normalized reward against baselines (CDT, CAPS, CCAC, LSPC); establishes general‑purpose performance.  
  3. **Maritime navigation (AIS data)**: Test real‑world applicability; measures close‑quarter rate, average displacement error, success rate, and acceleration realism.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does BCRL achieve near‑optimal performance compared to the true CMDP solution in stochastic environments?  
   *Basis*: Tested on a simple grid‑world with moderate stochasticity; gap may widen in more complex stochastic MDPs. *Evidence needed*: Evaluation on diverse stochastic MDPs with exact optimal solutions for small‑to‑medium instances.

2. Can BCRL consistently outperform state‑of‑the‑art offline safe RL baselines across diverse benchmark tasks?  
   *Basis*: BCRL was safe in all 38 tasks but did not always achieve the highest reward; conditions for success are not fully analyzed. *Evidence needed*: Cross‑task analysis linking performance differences to dataset characteristics (coverage, environment dynamics).

3. How sensitive is BCRL’s performance to hyperparameter choices like expectiles and policy temperature?  
   *Basis*: Ablation cover only narrow ranges for IQL parameters; interactions with other components not explored. *Evidence needed*: Comprehensive sensitivity analysis across wider ranges, multiple offline RL backbones, and adaptive schedules.

4. Does BCRL produce safe and realistic policies on other real‑world maritime navigation datasets?  
   *Basis*: Evaluation limited to the Singapore Strait AIS data; different traffic patterns or environmental conditions unverified. *Evidence needed*: Application to additional maritime regions and comparison against expert demonstrations and historical safety records.

## Limitations
- Safety guarantee hinges on an accurate cost critic; underestimation may lead to budget overruns and constraint violations.
- Requires sufficient coverage of feasible actions in the offline dataset; otherwise policy collapse or excessive conservatism can occur.
- Evaluation is limited to 38 DSRL tasks and a single real‑world maritime scenario; broader domain generalizability remains unverified.
