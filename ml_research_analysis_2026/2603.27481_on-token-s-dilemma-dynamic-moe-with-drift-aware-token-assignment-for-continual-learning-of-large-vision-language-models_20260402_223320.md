# On Token's Dilemma: Dynamic MoE with Drift-Aware Token Assignment for Continual Learning of Large Vision Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.27481  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27481  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: LLaVA‑DyMoE improves mean final accuracy by 7.2% (57.70% vs. 49.50%) and reduces forgetting by 12% (backward transfer −4.67% vs. −16.67%) compared to IncMoELoRA.  

## Executive Summary
Continual learning of large vision‑language models (LVLMs) suffers from catastrophic forgetting when adapting to new multimodal tasks. This paper reveals a "token’s dilemma": ambiguous and old tokens in new‑task data are often misrouted to newly added Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) experts, providing minimal learning benefit while inducing forgetting. To address this, the authors propose LLaVA‑DyMoE, a dynamic MoE framework with two regularizations. Token Assignment Guidance (TAG) identifies token types from routing scores and steers ambiguous/old tokens away from new experts. Routing Score Regularization (RSR) enforces exclusive token‑to‑group routing and promotes specialization of new experts. Evaluated on the CoIN benchmark of eight VQA tasks, LLaVA‑DyMoE significantly outperforms prior incremental MoE methods, improving final accuracy and reducing backward transfer. The approach is orthogonal to existing continual learning techniques and scales to larger LVLM variants.

## Method Summary
LLaVA‑DyMoE extends the LLaVA architecture with a continuously expanding MoE module. As new tasks arrive, additional experts are incorporated while maintaining a shared router. The router computes a score for each token across all experts. TAG leverages these scores to classify tokens (ambiguous, old, new) and introduces a bias that discourages ambiguous and old tokens from being routed to newly added experts. RSR adds a regularization term to the router loss that encourages a sparse, exclusive assignment pattern, thus fostering specialization among new experts. The overall training loss combines the standard task loss with TAG and RSR contributions. This design directly targets routing‑drift, ensuring that new experts learn novel information without overwriting previous knowledge.

## Key Results
- Mean final accuracy on CoIN: **57.70%** vs. **49.50%** for IncMoELoRA (+7.2%).
- Backward transfer: **−4.67%** vs. **−16.67%**, a 12% reduction in forgetting.
- The method is orthogonal to existing CL techniques and demonstrates scalability to larger LVLM models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The token’s dilemma arises because newly added experts initially attract tokens with low routing confidence (ambiguous or old tokens). These tokens contribute little to learning the new task yet interfere with expert specialization, leading to catastrophic forgetting. TAG mitigates this by explicitly preventing ambiguous/old tokens from selecting new experts, thereby preserving old task representations. RSR reinforces TAG by encouraging the router to produce more peaked scores, which reduces overlap between experts and ensures that new experts develop clear, distinct responsibilities. Together, these regularizations maintain a stable routing distribution that respects task boundaries, enabling incremental capacity growth without forgetting.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE)**  
  *Why needed*: Enables dynamic model expansion while keeping computational cost bounded.  
  *Quick check*: Verify that each token activates only a small subset of experts (e.g., top‑2).
- **Token Routing**  
  *Why needed*: Determines which expert processes each token, driving specialization.  
  *Quick check*: Compute routing entropy per token type; lower entropy indicates more discriminative assignments.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting**  
  *Why needed*: Core challenge in continual learning; neural networks tend to overwrite prior knowledge.  
  *Quick check*: Evaluate performance on old tasks before and after training on a new task.
- **Continual Learning (CL)**  
  *Why needed*: Allows models to learn sequentially from diverse data without retaining all past data.  
  *Quick check*: Track per‑task accuracy over time; aim for minimal negative backward transfer.
- **Routing Drift**  
  *Why needed*: Misrouting of old‑task tokens to new experts undermines specialization and causes forgetting.  
  *Quick check*: Quantify the fraction of old‑task tokens assigned to new experts; should decrease after regularization.
- **Regularization (TAG/RSR)**  
  *Why needed*: Provides explicit guidance to the router to avoid harmful assignments.  
  *Quick check*: Monitor regularization loss terms; ensure they converge and correlate with improved retention.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Input → Vision Encoder → LLM Backbone → MoE Layers (Router + Expert Pool) → Token‑Expert Assignment → Output  

**Critical Path**  
During training on a new task, for each token the router produces scores over all experts. TAG uses score statistics to label tokens as ambiguous/old/new and biases the softmax to keep ambiguous/old tokens from picking newly added experts. RSR adds a sparsity‑inducing regularization term to the router loss. The router’s softmax yields routing probabilities, and tokens are assigned (e.g., top‑1) to the corresponding experts for processing. The total loss combines the primary task loss with TAG and RSR terms, scaled by hyperparameters. At inference, only the top‑1 expert per token is used, preserving efficiency.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *TAG threshold*: Too tight may over‑restrict new experts; too loose may fail to prevent drift.  
- *RSR strength*: Excessive regularization can under‑utilize experts; insufficient may not enforce exclusivity.  
- *Overhead*: Dynamic routing and extra losses increase training time, though inference remains sparse.

**Failure Signatures**  
- High proportion of old‑task tokens routed to new experts (persistent routing‑drift).  
- Significant negative backward transfer (e.g., >10% drop on old tasks).  
- Regularization losses diverge or dominate the primary loss.  
- High token‑type entropy for new experts, indicating poor specialization.

**First Experiments**  
1. **Routing distribution analysis**: Compute the fraction of old‑task tokens assigned to new experts across incremental steps; expect a substantial drop after applying TAG/RSR.  
2. **Ablation study**: Remove TAG and RSR individually to measure their isolated impact on final accuracy and forgetting.  
3. **Efficiency benchmark**: Compare training time, peak memory, and FLOPs of LLaVA‑DyMoE against IncMoELoRA on identical hardware to quantify overhead.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to CoIN’s eight VQA tasks; generalizability to other modalities, longer task sequences, or more diverse datasets is unclear.  
- TAG relies on fixed thresholds for routing scores, which may be sensitive and require task‑specific tuning.  
- Computational overhead from dynamic MoE routing and regularizations is not quantified; efficiency at scale remains uncertain.

## Confidence
- Routing‑drift phenomenon and token dilemma: **High**  
- Effectiveness of TAG and RSR regularizations: **High**  
- Quantitative performance (final accuracy & backward transfer): **Medium**  
- Orthogonality and scalability: **Medium**
