# Gap-Dependent Bounds for Nearly Minimax Optimal Reinforcement Learning with Linear Function Approximation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20297
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20297
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The paper establishes the first gap‑dependent regret bound for LSVI‑UCB++ with linear function approximation, achieving Õ(d²H⁴/Δ_min) expected regret and a correspondingly improved PAC sample complexity, and introduces a concurrent multi‑agent variant that attains linear speedup.

## Executive Summary
This work provides a gap‑dependent analysis for reinforcement learning with linear function approximation. Focusing on the nearly minimax‑optimal LSVI‑UCB++ algorithm, the authors prove an expected regret bound of Õ(d²H⁴/Δ_min), where Δ_min is the minimum suboptimality gap. This improves the dependence on the feature dimension d and horizon H compared to prior gap‑dependent results. The bound translates into a PAC sample complexity of Õ(d²H⁴/(Δ_min ε) + d⁶H⁷/(δ ε)), which improves from the worst‑case Õ(1/ε²) to Õ(1/ε). Moreover, leveraging the low policy‑switching property of LSVI‑UCB++, the paper proposes Concurrent LSVI‑UCB++, a multi‑agent extension that achieves a gap‑dependent sample complexity with linear speedup in the number of agents M.

## Method Summary
The core algorithm, LSVI‑UCB++, maintains optimistic and pessimistic value estimates (Q and ˇQ) through weighted ridge regression using adaptive variance weights σ̄². The covariance matrices Σ are updated with these weights, and the policy is updated only when det(Σ) doubles, resulting in low switching. For the concurrent setting, M agents share a single instance of LSVI‑UCB++; their trajectories are fed sequentially into the same buffer, and updates occur at the same deterministic trigger. Any remaining episodes in a round are discarded upon an update. The analysis introduces two key technical tools: (i) a surrogate covariance matrix to bound partial sums of exploration bonuses, and (ii) a recursive decomposition to control the sum of estimated variances over the episodes where suboptimal actions are taken.

## Key Results
- Expected regret: Õ(d²H⁴/Δ_min) (ignoring logarithmic factors).
- PAC sample complexity: Õ(d²H⁴/(Δ_min ε) + d⁶H⁷/(δ ε)), improving from worst‑case Õ(1/ε²) to Õ(1/ε).
- Concurrent LSVI‑UCB++ attains gap‑dependent sample complexity with linear speedup in M agents.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The algorithm can bound the partial sum of exploration bonuses even when the covariance matrices no longer follow a simple one‑step recursion.  
**Mechanism:** Introduce a surrogate covariance matrix Σ′ that is updated only on the selected indices kᵢ (the episodes counted in the partial sum) and satisfies Σ′ᵢ ⪯ Σ_{kᵢ,ₕ}. Because Σ′ inherits a one‑step recursive form, standard matrix concentration (e.g., Lemma A.4) yields a tight bound on the sum of bonuses β √ϕᵀΣ⁻¹ϕ. This bound then translates back to the original sum via the domination property.  
**Core assumption:** The selected episodes {kᵢ} form a subsequence of the historical data, so that Σ_{kᵢ,ₕ} includes at least the same samples as Σ′ᵢ. Also, feature vectors are bounded (∥ϕ∥ ≤ 1) and the bonus scaling β is chosen appropriately.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- The abstract explicitly states “bounding partial sums of bonuses … using surrogate matrices.”  
- Section 5.1 and Lemma C.3 (page 24) present the construction Σ′ and the inequality Σ′ᵢ ⪯ Σ_{kᵢ,ₕ}.  
- The corpus includes “[201110] Variance-Dependent Regret Bounds for Linear Bandits…” which uses covariance matrices for bonus control but does not address partial sums, suggesting the surrogate trick is novel in this context.  
**Break condition:** If the episode selection {kᵢ} included indices not present in the data up to kᵢ (e.g., future or out‑of‑order samples), the domination Σ′ ⪯ Σ would fail. Moreover, if the estimated variance clipping is too loose, the bound may become vacuous, undermining the gap‑dependent improvement.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The partial sum of the estimated variance σ² over the critical episodes {kᵢ} can be controlled via a recursive relationship across time steps.  
**Mechanism:** Decompose σ²_{k,ₕ} (Equation 3) into components involving the squared value estimates and the optimism‑pessimism gap. The gap V_{k,ₕ} −  V_{k,ₕ} can be telescoped using the Bellman residuals P_{s,a,ₕ}(V_{k,ₕ₊₁} −  V_{k,ₕ₊₁}) and the bonus terms. Summing over the selected episodes and steps, the martingale differences are bounded via Azuma‑Hoeffding (Lemma B.1), while the recursive structure yields a closed‑form bound (Lemma D.1).  
**Core assumption:** The optimism and pessimism events E₁∩E₂ hold, guaranteeing V ≥ V* ≥  V. The bonus terms are themselves bounded by the techniques in Mechanism 1.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- The abstract notes “bounding … estimated variances using … recursive structures.”  
- Lemma C.5 (page 26) establishes the recursive inequality for ∑ P(V‑ V).  
- Lemma D.1 (page 28) combines the recursive bound with bonus control to finalize the variance sum.  
- In the corpus, “[123413] Nearly Minimax Optimal Regret for Learning Linear Mixture…” uses variance‑aware techniques, but the explicit recursion for partial sums appears unique.  
**Break condition:** Violation of high‑probability events E₁, E₂, or E₄ disrupts the guarantee that V and V track V* closely. Also, if the bonus β or β̄ are mis‑specified, the recursion may produce a super‑linear dependence.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The low policy‑switching property of LSVI‑UCB++ enables a concurrent multi‑agent variant (Concurrent LSVI‑UCB++) that attains linear speedup in the number of agents M for gap‑dependent sample complexity.  
**Mechanism:** In the concurrent algorithm, all M agents follow the same policy derived from a single instance of LSVI‑UCB++. Their collected trajectories are fed sequentially into the single‑agent buffer. The policy is updated only when the determinant‑trigger (originally designed for infrequent switching) fires, and any remaining trajectories in the current concurrent round are discarded. Because the number of policy switches N_ε is O(d H log K) (He et al., 2023), the total number of concurrent rounds is at most N_ε + K_ε/M, yielding a near‑linear speedup when M is sufficiently large.  
**Core assumption:** LSVI‑UCB++ exhibits a low number of policy updates in the single‑agent case (Theorem 5.1 of He et al. 2023). The MDPs faced by all agents are i.i.d. copies, so a shared policy is valid. The trigger condition remains deterministic.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- The abstract: “Leveraging the low policy‑switching property … we introduce a concurrent variant … achieving linear speedup.”  
- Section 4.3 (page 11) and Theorem 4.3 present the concurrent algorithm and its round complexity bound.  
- The corpus lists “[64958] Q‑Learning with Fine‑Grained Gap‑Dependent Regret” and others that study multi‑agent RL, but Concurrent LSVI‑UCB++ is the first to combine gap‑dependency with linear speedup under linear function approximation.  
**Break condition:** If the underlying MDPs are not identical across agents (e.g., heterogeneous rewards or transitions), the shared policy may be far from optimal for some agents, destroying the gap‑dependent guarantee. Additionally, if the trigger condition updates too often (e.g., due to overly sensitive determinant threshold), the number of rounds would approach K_ε, eliminating speedup.

## Foundational Learning

- **Gap‑dependent regret analysis**: Regret bounds that depend on the minimum suboptimality gap Δ_min rather than just the time horizon. Needed because it yields tighter guarantees that reflect problem difficulty; a larger Δ_min means the optimal action is much better than suboptimal ones, so fewer samples are needed to identify it.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**
- Feature extractor ϕ(s,a): Known mapping to ℝᵈ.
- Covariance matrices {Σ_{k,ₕ}}: d×d positive‑definite, initialized λI, updated with clipped variance weights.
- Weight estimators: bwk,ₕ (optimistic) and ˇw_{k,ₕ} (pessimistic) via weighted ridge regression on past trajectories, using inverse variance weights 1/ σ̄².
- Value functions: Qk,ₕ(s,a) and ˇQk,ₕ(s,a) with bonuses β and β̄, and clipping to ensure boundedness.
- Policy: πk(h)(s)=argmax_a Qk,ₕ(s,a).
- Variance estimate: σ²_{k,ₕ} (Eq 3) with estimated variance of V and bonus terms; then σ̄² = max(σ², H, 2d³H² ∥ϕ∥_{Σ⁻¹}).
- Update trigger: if det(Σ) ≥ 2 det(Σ_last) for any h, recompute Q and  Q (policy switch).
- Concurrent layer: Shared global Σ, Q, ; agents act in parallel; trajectories are enqueued sequentially into the single‑agent update routine; updates trigger only when the determinant condition is met; remaining episodes in the round are dropped.

**Critical path**
1. Initialize all matrices (Σ=λI, Q=H, ˇQ=0).
2. Policy selection: agents request current policy π_k.
3. Interaction: for each step h, agent computes σ̄², selects action a_k^h = argmax_a Q_k,h(s_k^h,a), observes reward and next state.
4. Update Σ: Σ_{k+1,h} ← Σ_{k,h} + σ̄^{-2} ϕϕᵀ.
5. Check switch: if for any h, det(Σ_{k+1,h}) ≥ 2·det(Σ_last,h), recompute Q and ˇQ via weighted regression and set new policy; discard any remaining episodes in the current concurrent round.
6. Loop to next episode/concurrent round.

**Design tradeoffs**
- β, β̄: Larger values increase exploration guarantees but may inflate bonuses, worsening gap‑dependent constants.
- λ: Regularization; too small risks ill‑conditioning, too large shrinks estimates. λ=1/H² chosen to balance.
- Variance clipping: Enforces σ̄² ≥ H and a term depending on ∥ϕ∥_{Σ⁻¹} to maintain lower bounds on weights, but may over‑estimate variance for low‑variance samples, biasing regression.
- Memory vs. communication: Storing all past trajectories gives exact regression but costs O(K d²) memory. Low‑switch property keeps N_ε small, so recomputation is infrequent.
- Concurrency waste: When a switch occurs mid‑round, the remaining agents’ experiences in that round are discarded, potentially reducing data efficiency.

**Failure signatures**
- Numerical instability: Σ becomes nearly singular (det near 0) if λ is too small or data scarce; matrix inverses blow up.
- Insufficient optimism: If β is too small, Q estimates fall below Q*, leading to suboptimal actions and larger regret.
- Excessive switching: If the determinant threshold is lowered, policy updates become frequent, increasing communication and possibly degrading concurrent speedup.
- Gap‑dependent regime breakdown: When Δ_min is extremely tiny, the bound becomes huge; observed regret may appear worst‑case, indicating the improvement only manifests for moderate gaps.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **Regret vs. Δ_min scaling:** Run LSVI‑UCB++ on a linear MDP with fixed d, H and vary Δ_min (by scaling reward offsets). Plot cumulative regret; verify slope scales roughly as 1/Δ_min and constant improves over prior bounds.
2. **Policy switch count:** Measure the number of policy updates N_ε as a function of K for different d and H. Confirm N_ε = O(d H log K), matching the theoretical bound used in the concurrent analysis.
3. **Concurrent speedup:** Use Concurrent LSVI‑UCB++ with M = 1,2,4,8 agents and a fixed total episode budget K_total = M·R (so R is the number of concurrent rounds). Measure the achieved error ε after R rounds. Expect ε to decrease linearly with M up to the point where switch frequency dominates. Plot rounds R vs. achieved ε on a log‑log scale; a slope of ‑1 indicates linear speedup.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly discuss open questions. However, based on its contributions and limitations, several natural directions for future work emerge:
- Extending the gap‑dependent analysis to nonlinear function approximation or kernel methods.
- Tightening the dependence on the feature dimension d and horizon H, possibly matching lower bounds more closely.
- Removing the assumption of known feature mappings or handling misspecified linear models.
- Conducting empirical evaluations of Concurrent LSVI‑UCB++ on realistic multi‑agent benchmarks to validate the linear speedup.
- Investigating optimal switching thresholds to minimize discarded data in the concurrent setting.

## Limitations
- Implicit constants in confidence radii may require conservative tuning, potentially weakening gap‑dependent gains.
- Lack of a canonical linear MDP benchmark makes reproduction difficult; custom constructions might violate assumptions (e.g., norm bounds, conditioning).
- The concurrent algorithm discards partial data upon policy switch, which could reduce practical efficiency.
- PAC guarantee for concurrent LSVI‑UCB++ does not specify how to select or verify the final policy in a fully online manner.
