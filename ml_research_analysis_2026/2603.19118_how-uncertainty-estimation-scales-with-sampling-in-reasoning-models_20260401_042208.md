# How Uncertainty Estimation Scales with Sampling in Reasoning Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19118
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19118
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Hybrid combination (SCVC) with just two samples yields dramatic AUROC gains (+12.9 in math, +6.4 in STEM/humanities) over single-sample VC and already exceeds scaled VC/SC at K=8.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates how uncertainty estimation scales with parallel sampling in reasoning language models (RLMs). Evaluating verbalized confidence (VC) and self-consistency (SC) across three models and 17 tasks, both signals improve with more samples, but SC lags VC in moderate sample regimes. The key finding is that combining VC and SC (SCVC) with only two samples provides large improvements: AUROC increases by up to +12.9 in mathematics and +6.4 in other domains over single-sample VC, and already outperforms VC or SC scaled to K=8. Mathematics shows the strongest gains and complementarity, likely due to RLVR post-training. The results suggest that for costly reasoning traces, early combination of signals is far more effective than deepening a single signal.

## Method Summary
The study uses three reasoning-optimized models (gpt-oss-20b, Qwen3-30B-A3B, DeepSeek-R1-8B) evaluated on 17 tasks spanning mathematics, STEM, and humanities. For each input question, the model generates K independent reasoning traces (chains of thought) using temperature >0. From each trace, two uncertainty signals are extracted: verbalized confidence (VC), a self-reported confidence score, and self-consistency (SC), computed as the frequency of the majority answer among the K samples. The per-question VC signal is aggregated as the average VC across samples (VCavg). A hybrid estimator combines SC and VCavg: SCVC = λ·SC + (1−λ)·VCavg (default λ=0.5). The quality of each signal is measured by macro-AUROC, which ranks questions by correctness. The authors plot scaling curves (AUROC vs. K) for VC and SC, and compare SCVC at small K against scaled single signals. They also analyze domain differences and the correlation between VC and SC across K.

## Key Results
- Both VC and SC improve with more samples, but SC consistently underperforms VC in the moderate sample regime (K≤8).
- SCVC with K=2 yields dramatic AUROC gains: +12.9 in mathematics and +6.4 in STEM/humanities over single-sample VC, and already exceeds VC@K=8 and SC@K=8.
- Scaling beyond K=2 shows diminishing returns, especially outside mathematics; mathematics exhibits the steepest scaling curves and strongest VC-SC complementarity, likely due to RLVR training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Verbalized Confidence (VC) scales with parallel sampling in Reasoning Language Models (RLMs) because extended chain-of-thought traces allow a single sample to integrate multiple hypotheses, and aggregating VC over samples refines the introspection.
- **Mechanism**: Each sample produces a reasoning trace and a self-reported confidence value. Aggregating VC over samples that predict the majority answer (`VCavg`) reduces noise from any single trace’s idiosyncratic introspection, leveraging the model’s internal deliberation to produce a more stable uncertainty signal.
- **Core assumption**: The model’s verbalized confidence, even from a single extended trace, contains some discriminative signal about correctness that can be sharpened by averaging across independent samples.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Both self-consistency and verbalized confidence scale in reasoning models... Verbalized confidence is a strong baseline that can be further improved via sampling, particularly in mathematics."
  - [section 3.1] Reports AUROC gains for VC from K=1 to K=8 (e.g., +10.1 in mathematics) and earlier saturation in STEM/humanities.
  - [corpus] Corpus shows strong focus on verbalized confidence calibration (e.g., "Calibrating Verbalized Confidence with Self-Generated Distractors"), supporting that VC is a recognized but often miscalibrated signal; this paper's finding is about its scaling behavior under parallel sampling in RLMs, which is not directly addressed in corpus.
- **Break condition**: If the model’s introspection becomes saturated after one extended trace (i.e., all candidate solutions are internally explored in a single pass), additional samples yield no new information for VC aggregation, leading to immediate saturation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Combining Verbalized Confidence (VC) and Self-Consistency (SC) yields front-loaded, superlinear gains because they capture transiently distinct forms of uncertainty during early sampling, with convergence at higher K.
- **Mechanism**: VC reflects the model’s introspective assessment of its *current* trace’s soundness, while SC reflects inter-sample agreement across independent traces. With few samples (K=2), these signals are minimally correlated and thus complementary; their additive combination (`SCVC`) merges intra-trace quality assessment with inter-trace stability. As K increases, SC becomes more stable and its correlation with VC rises, reducing marginal gains from combination.
- **Core assumption**: VC and SC measure partially orthogonal aspects of uncertainty in RLMs under moderate sampling—VC is sensitive to trace-specific flaws, SC to solution diversity—and their joint presence provides more information than either alone.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Most uncertainty gains... arise from signal combination: with just two samples, a hybrid estimator improves AUROC by up to +12 on average and already outperforms either signal alone even when scaled to much larger budgets."
  - [section 3.2] Table 2 shows SCVC@K=2 exceeds VC@K=8 and SC@K=8 across domains. Figure 3 shows Kendall’s τ between VC and SC increases with K, and is lower in mathematics (strongest complementarity).
  - [section 3.4] Figure 2 shows SCVC is robust to weighting λ over a wide range, confirming that mere presence of both signals drives gains, not precise tuning.
  - [corpus] Papers like "Combining confidence elicitation and sample-based methods..." (Rivera et al.) explore hybrid signals in non-reasoning LLMs; this paper extends to RLMs and isolates the *front-loaded* nature of the synergy.
- **Break condition**: If VC and SC become perfectly correlated early (e.g., if the model’s introspection perfectly predicts agreement across samples), the hybrid provides no benefit over the stronger single signal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Uncertainty signals scale fastest and combine most effectively in mathematics because RLVR post-training optimizes models for this domain, leading to richer introspection and more diverse error modes that are distinguishable by both VC and SC.
- **Mechanism**: RLVR training on math problems teaches the model to generate longer, more structured reasoning with verifiable outcomes. This yields: (1) more reliable VC (the model better assesses its own math reasoning), (2) more varied failure modes across samples (boosting SC’s discriminative power), and (3) greater complementarity (VC and SC respond to different failure patterns—e.g., trace-level logical errors vs. sample-level answer dispersion).
- **Core assumption**: The benefits of RLVR training for uncertainty quality are domain-specific and strongest in mathematics, the primary training domain.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Mathematics showed the strongest gains and complementarity, likely due to RLVR training."
  - [section 3.3] Scaling curves for VC, SC, and SCVC are steeper and show less saturation in mathematics vs. STEM/humanities. Initial SCVC@K=2 gain is +12.9 AUROC in math vs. +6.4 elsewhere.
  - [section 2.2] Notes that mathematical tasks are "the native domain of RLVR-style post-training" and that models (gpt-oss-20b, Qwen3-30B-A3B) are RLVR-trained, while DeepSeek-R1-8B is a fine-tuned dense model—all show consistent math advantage.
  - [corpus] Corpus includes papers on RLVR for reasoning (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 reference) but does not directly link RLVR to uncertainty scaling patterns; this is an inferred domain-specific effect from the paper’s cross-domain comparison.
- **Break condition**: If a model is RLVR-trained on non-math domains or if the mathematical tasks used do not reflect the model’s RLVR optimization distribution, the domain gap in uncertainty scaling may not appear.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)
  - *Why needed here*: The paper’s key finding—domain-dependent uncertainty scaling—hinges on RLVR being the post-training method for the studied models, with mathematics as its native domain. Understanding RLVR explains why math shows stronger signals.
  - *Quick check question*: Why might RLVR training lead to better verbalized confidence in mathematics compared to humanities?
- **Concept**: Parallel Sampling & Test-Time Scaling
  - *Why needed here*: The entire study manipulates the number of parallel samples (K) to observe scaling behavior. This is the independent variable and the practical lever for uncertainty improvement.
  - *Quick check question*: What is the cost implication of increasing K in a reasoning model versus a non-reasoning LLM?
- **Concept**: AUROC as a Discrimination Metric
  - *Why needed here*: The primary evaluation metric is AUROC, chosen for its scale-invariance given miscalibrated VC and coarse SC. Understanding AUROC is necessary to interpret the results (e.g., +12.9 AUROC means a significantly better ranking of correct vs. incorrect examples).
  - *Quick check question*: Why is AUROC preferred over calibration metrics like ECE for this study’s confidence signals?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Parallel Sampler -> Signal Extractors (VC Extractor, SC Extractor) -> Combiner -> Aggregator -> Evaluator
- **Critical path**: Input → Sample K traces → Extract VC/SC per trace → Aggregate to per-question signal → Bootstrap over pools → Compute AUROC → Compare across K, signals, domains.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Token budget vs. signal quality*: Longer traces (up to 60K tokens) enable richer introspection but make each sample costly, making early combination (K=2) highly valuable.
  - *Generalization vs. domain fit*: VC/SC prompts are domain-agnostic, but performance varies by domain due to model training, not prompt design.
  - *Judge vs. elicitation*: Judge methods (second-pass reading) add an extra costly reasoning pass; this study finds them inferior to simple elicitation + combination.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - VC fails to scale (flat AUROC curve with K) in non-math domains, indicating saturation of introspective signal after single trace.
  - SC significantly lags VC at all K, especially K<8, showing poor sample-efficiency for agreement-based uncertainty in RLMs.
  - SCVC gains diminish after K=2 in STEM/humanities, signaling early convergence of VC and SC signals.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Reproduce Table 2 (main scaling curves)**: Run the full protocol on one model (e.g., gpt-oss-20b) across 3-4 tasks (one math, one STEM, one humanities) for K∈{1,2,5,8}. Confirm VC scales, SC lags, and SCVC@2 outperforms others.
  2. **Vary λ for SCVC (Section 3.4)**: For K=2, sweep λ∈[0,1] on a math and a humanities task. Verify performance stability over a wide interior range (0.3<λ<0.7) and drop at extremes.
  3. **Correlation analysis (Figure 3)**: Compute Kendall’s τ between VC and SC values *per question* across K. Confirm τ increases with K and is lower in math than other domains. This validates the complementarity decay explanation.

## Limitations
- Domain causality: The math advantage is attributed to RLVR training, but tasks may inherently differ in difficulty/answer structure.
- Model scope: All tested models are reasoning-optimized; findings may not generalize to base LLMs or different architectures.
- Prompt sensitivity: VC relies on a fixed elicitation prompt; its scaling behavior could vary with prompt engineering, which was not systematically tested.

## Confidence
- **High**: VC and SC both improve with more samples; SCVC with K=2 outperforms single-signal scaling.
- **Medium**: Domain differences (math > others) stem primarily from RLVR training.
- **Low**: Extrapolating findings to non-reasoning models or untested prompt designs.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate RLVR**: Run the protocol on a base LLM (e.g., Llama 3.1) vs. its RLVR-tuned variant on overlapping math/non-math tasks to isolate training effect.
2. **Prompt variation**: For a single model/task, sweep VC prompt phrasings (e.g., "How sure are you?" vs. "Probability correct?") at K=2 to test SCVC stability.
3. **Cross-paradigm test**: Apply the same sampling protocol to a non-reasoning LLM (e.g., factual QA) to see if VC/SC scaling patterns hold outside chain-of-thought contexts.
