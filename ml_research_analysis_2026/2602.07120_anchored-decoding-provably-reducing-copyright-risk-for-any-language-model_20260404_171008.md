# Anchored Decoding: Provably Reducing Copyright Risk for Any Language Model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.07120
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07120
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A training-free inference-time technique that fuses a risky and a safe language model to provably bound copyright infringement (K-NAF) while preserving utility, eliminating up to 75% of the measurable copying gap relative to the safe baseline.

## Executive Summary
Anchored Decoding addresses the problem of language models regurgitating memorized, copyrighted training data. It is a training-free method that operates at inference time by carefully fusing the next-token predictions of two models: a high-utility "risky" model trained on mixed-license text and a lower-capability "safe" model trained only on permissively licensed content. The core algorithm solves a per-step optimization to minimize divergence from the risky model while constraining divergence from the safe model by a dynamically managed budget. This process, combined with a prefix-debt mechanism that allocates extra safety margin to memorization-prone prompts, provides a provable global guarantee (K-NAF) on copyright risk. The approach defines a new Pareto frontier, significantly reducing copying metrics while maintaining near-original fluency and factuality, and extends to cross-tokenizer scenarios via byte-level decoding.

## Method Summary
The method constructs token (or byte) level distributions via a KL-constrained projection. For each step, it finds the distribution \( p^*_t \) that minimizes \( D_{KL}(p^*_t \| p_r) \) subject to \( D_{KL}(p^*_t \| p_s) \leq k_t \). The per-step budget \( k_t \) is derived from a global budget \( K \) using an adaptive banking rule that carries over unused capacity. A prefix-debt \( \delta_{init}(x) \), computed from the early log-likelihood ratio spikes in the prompt, is subtracted from \( K \) to front-load safety for risky prompts. For models with mismatched tokenizers, the same optimization is performed in byte space using the ByteSampler framework to exactly marginalize token distributions into byte distributions. The solution is a closed-form weighted geometric mean \( p^*_t \propto p_s^{1-\beta} p_r^{\beta} \), where \( \beta \) is found via a safeguarded root-finding procedure.

## Key Results
- Eliminates up to **75%** of the measurable copying gap (averaged over six metrics) relative to the safe model baseline.
- Preserves **near-original fluency and factuality** on standard benchmarks, establishing a new Pareto frontier for risk-utility trade-offs.
- Introduces **TinyComma 1.8B**, a small safe model with a Llama-compatible tokenizer to facilitate practical adoption.
- Inference overhead is modest: **~1.1×** for token-level fusion and **~1.2×** for byte-level fusion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: KL-constrained per-step fusion guarantees global copyright safety (K-NAF) by chaining local budgets.
- **Mechanism**: At each decoding step \( t \), compute the next-token distribution \( p^*_t \) that minimizes \( D_{KL}(p^*_t \| p_r) \) subject to \( D_{KL}(p^*_t \| p_s) \leq k_t \). This yields a closed-form weighted geometric mean of \( p_r \) and \( p_s \). The adaptive banking rule updates \( k_t \) based on prior spend to ensure \( \sum_t D_{KL}(p^*_t \| p_{s,t}) \leq K \).
- **Core assumption**: The KL chain rule decomposes sequence-level divergence into a sum of conditional divergences, so bounding each conditional implies bounding the total.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "For each decoding step it solves an optimization that minimizes KL divergence to the risky model while constraining KL divergence to the safe model by a per-step budget"
  - [section 3.1] Theorem 3.1 proves that if each \( p^*_t \) satisfies its local constraint and \( \sum k_t \leq K \), then the autoregressive sequence satisfies global K-NAF.
  - [section 3.4] Proposition 3.4 shows adaptive banking preserves the global bound even when \( k_t \) varies dynamically.
- **Break condition**: If the greedy per-step solution diverges significantly from the global optimum, or if \( D_{KL}(p_r \| p_s) \) is not a reliable memorization signal in a given domain (e.g., where \( p_r \) contains valuable long-tail facts \( p_s \) lacks).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Prefix debt proactively allocates extra safety margin to prompts that exhibit early signs of memorization, preventing copyright leakage from the start of generation.
- **Mechanism**: Compute \( \delta_{init}(x) \) as the mean of the top-\( n \) largest positive log-likelihood ratios (LLRs) between \( p_r \) and \( p_s \) on the prompt tokens: \( \text{LLR}(i) = \log(p_r(x_i|x_{<i})/p_s(x_i|x_{<i})) \). Subtract \( \delta_{init}(x) \) from global budget \( K \), effectively forcing more reliance on \( p_s \) during the initial decoding steps.
- **Core assumption**: Large positive LLR spikes in the prefix indicate that \( p_r \) has seen and memorized content that \( p_s \) has not, and such prompts are more likely to trigger verbatim regurgitation later.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.3] Proposes offsetting the global \( K \) budget with context-dependent debt \( \delta_{init}(x) \), reflecting that a risky prompt has already pre-spent some safety margin.
  - [section 6.3] Empirical analysis shows copying events are heavily front-loaded; Copyright prompts have right-shifted prefix LLR distributions.
  - [section 5.2] Ablation "NoDebt" produces strictly worse risk-utility tradeoffs.
- **Break condition**: If prefix LLRs are noisy or correlated with benign long-tail knowledge rather than memorization, prefix debt may over-penalize legitimate informative prompts.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Byte-level anchoring (Anchored Byte Decoding) extends provable copyright mitigation to model pairs with mismatched tokenizers by operating on the byte-level distributions induced via ByteSampler.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of token-level fusion, apply the same KL-constrained optimization over the 256-dimensional byte space. The ByteSampler framework exactly marginalizes token distributions into next-byte distributions for both models, enabling fusion without vocabulary alignment.
- **Core assumption**: The byte-level formulation inherits the K-NAF guarantee; byte-level KL \( (p_r \| p_s) \) remains a useful memorization diagnostic.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.6] Anchored Byte Decoding builds upon ByteSampler to bypass tokenizer mismatch by operating on the next-byte distribution.
  - [section 4.2] Evaluated on five model pairs with mismatched tokenizers, achieving similar Pareto frontier as token-level.
  - [section 6.2] Byte-level KL histogram shows Copyright domain has heavier extreme tail.
- **Break condition**: If ByteSampler's exact marginalization is too slow or byte-level statistics are less discriminative for certain languages/tokenizers.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence and chain rule**
  - **Why needed**: The entire safety guarantee (K-NAF) is defined in terms of KL divergence; the proof that per-step constraints compose into a global bound relies on the chain rule: \( D_{KL}(p^*\|p_s) = \mathbb{E}_{p^*}[\sum_t D_{KL}(p^*_t(\cdot|prefix) \| p_s(\cdot|prefix))] \).
  - **Quick check**: Why does bounding the conditional KL at each step guarantee a bound on the full-sequence KL? What assumption about the support of \( p_s \) is needed?

- **Concept: Autoregressive language modeling**
  - **Why needed**: Anchored Decoding explicitly targets the next-token distribution at each step of an autoregressive decoder. Understanding the chain rule factorization \( p(y_{0:T}|x) = \prod_t p(y_t|y_{<t},x) \) is essential.
  - **Quick check**: How does the latent prefix \( y_{<t} \) affect the computation of \( p_r(\cdot|y_{<t},x) \) and \( p_s(\cdot|y_{<t},x) \) during decoding?

- **Concept: BPE tokenization and ByteSampler**
  - **Why needed**: The byte-level variant requires understanding that BPE tokenizers map bytes to tokens non-injectively. ByteSampler allows exact marginalization from token probabilities to byte probabilities by enumerating valid tokenizations.
  - **Quick check**: Why is a simple "decode-encode" round-trip insufficient for converting a token distribution to a byte distribution? What role do invalid UTF-8 sequences play?

- **Concept: Lagrange multipliers and convex optimization**
  - **Why needed**: The closed-form solution for \( p^*_t \) is derived via Lagrange duality. Understanding that the constraint \( D_{KL}(p\|p_s) \leq k_t \) is convex and that the objective \( D_{KL}(p\|p_r) \) is convex in \( p \) clarifies why the solution exists and is unique.
  - **Quick check**: In the Lagrangian \( \mathcal{L}(p,\lambda,\alpha,\mu) = D_{KL}(p\|p_r) + \lambda(D_{KL}(p\|p_s)-k) + \alpha(\sum p-1) - \sum \mu_y p(y) \), what do the KKT conditions imply about the support of \( p^*_t \) relative to \( p_s \)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  - Risky LM (p_r) -> KL Projector & Adaptive Banking -> Sampler
  - Safe LM (p_s) -> KL Projector & Adaptive Banking -> Sampler
  - Prefix Debt Calculator -> Adaptive Banking
  - ByteSampler Wrapper (if needed) -> KL Projector & Adaptive Banking -> Sampler

- **Critical path** (token-level):
  1. Process prompt `x` through both LMs (prefill), store KV caches, gather logits for each token to compute δ_init(x) in parallel.
  2. Initialize bank `A_1 ← δ_init(x)`, nominal per-step cap `k ← K / T_max`.
  3. For `t=0,...,T_max-1`:
     - Compute `p_r(·|prefix)`, `p_s(·|prefix)` from KV caches.
     - Compute `k_t = max(0, (t+1)k - A_t)`. If `k_t = 0`, sample from `p_s`; else run KL Projector to get `p*_t`.
     - Sample `y_t ~ p*_t`, append to prefix, update `A_{t+1} ← A_t + D_KL(p*_t || p_s(·|prefix))`.
     - If `y_t = EOS` or `t ≥ T_max`, break.
  4. Return concatenated `y_0,...,y_t`.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Token vs. Byte: Token-level is faster/simpler but requires tokenizer alignment; byte-level works across any BPE tokenizers but adds ~0.2–0.5x overhead.
  - Fixed vs. Adaptive Budget: Fixed `k_t ≡ k` is simpler but can't bank unused budget; adaptive banking yields strictly better empirical trade-offs.
  - Prefix Debt vs. No Debt: Including δ_init(x) improves early-step compliance for memorization-prone prompts, but adds O(L) compute (amortized to O(L/T) per token).
  - KL vs. ∞-Rényi: KL gives average-case guarantee; ∞-Rényi gives worst-case but may be overly restrictive.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Degenerate outputs**: If `k` too small, `p*_t ≈ p_s` throughout, leading to low-fluency, repetitive text.
  - **Metric plateau**: If `k` too large, `p*_t ≈ p_r`, so copying metrics converge to `p_r` baseline.
  - **Tokenizer incompatibility**: Using token-level fusion with mismatched tokenizers causes silent misalignment; must switch to byte-level or re-tokenize.
  - **Prefix debt over-triggering**: If prompt contains rare proper nouns or domain jargon that `p_r` knows but `p_s` doesn't, δ_init(x) may be large, causing unnecessary cold start and utility loss.
  - **Solver non-convergence**: Newton-Raphson solver for λ may fail if `k_t` is extremely large or if distributions have zero mass on common support.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Reproduce token-level Pareto frontier**: Use TinyComma 1.8B + Llama 3.1 70B. Sweep `k ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 15, 20}`. Plot NCR vs. Fluency/Factuality; confirm rising utility with higher `k` but copying increases monotonically.
  2. **Validate prefix debt impact**: Same sweep with `n=0` (no debt) vs. `n=5` (default). Plot both curves; `n=5` should dominate for high-copying prompts (e.g., from BOOKS). Compute δ_init(x) for a mix of prompts; inspect distribution—Copyright prompts should have higher debt.
  3. **Cross-tokenizer fusion**: Use Comma 7B vs. Llama 3.1 70B. Run Anchored Byte Decoding with `k` sweep. Confirm 1) outputs are legible text, 2) copying metrics are lower than `p_r`, 3) throughput overhead is ~1.2×, and 4) the Pareto frontier is comparable to token-level. Test a non-English prompt to ensure byte-level handles multi-byte UTF-8 correctly.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What is the minimum copyright infringement risk achievable under Anchored Decoding given a fixed utility level?** No theoretical characterization of the fundamental risk-utility trade-off exists; evidence would be a derived lower bound or empirical saturation curves.
- **Does the local per-step KL constraint optimization sacrifice global optimality?** The optimality gap between the local solution and the global constrained optimum is unknown; evidence would be computing the global optimum for small LMs or developing tighter approximations.
- **How can Anchored Decoding avoid suppressing legitimate long-tail factual knowledge that the safe model lacks?** The current KL proxy conflates memorization with rare facts; evidence would be a more accurate memorization detector (e.g., membership inference) to drive budget allocation.
- **How can we verify that a safe model’s training data is entirely copyright-free, and mitigate latent leakage?** If the safe model itself reproduces protected sequences, the guarantee weakens; evidence would be robust auditing techniques for training data or safe models trained on rigorously filtered datasets.

## Limitations
- Exact evaluation prompts for BOOKS/BIOS are not disclosed; reproducibility may vary.
- Baseline implementations (MEMFREE, TOKENSWAP) lack full hyperparameter details.
- Prefix debt may over-penalize prompts with domain-specific jargon, hurting utility unnecessarily.
- Numerical edge cases in the KL solver (zero probabilities, extreme k) may affect the theoretical guarantee in practice.
- Byte-level overhead and discriminative power across diverse languages need further verification.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (KL-constrained fusion ⇒ K-NAF)**: High (theorem proof, chain rule).
- **Mechanism 2 (Prefix debt)**: High (ablation, empirical validation).
- **Mechanism 3 (Byte-level extension)**: Medium (depends on correct ByteSampler; fewer language contexts tested).
- **Pareto frontier claims**: High (six model pairs, comprehensive metrics).
- **Inference overhead (~1.1×)**: Medium (reported average; may vary with hardware/implementation).
