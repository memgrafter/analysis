# The Conundrum of Trustworthy Research on Attacking Personally Identifiable Information Removal Techniques

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08207
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08207
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** Reported high success rates in attacking PII-removed texts are inflated by data leakage or memorization, not genuine PII inference; a constrained study using unlikely data yields much lower rates (EM@3: YouTube 19.1%, Czech 5.5%).

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a fundamental flaw in the evaluation of reconstruction attacks on PII-removed text: prior high success rates are largely attributable to data leakage (overlap between attack model training corpora and original documents) or memorization, rather than true inference from sanitized context. The authors conduct a small-scale study using Czech court announcements and post-cutoff YouTube travel vlog transcripts—data sources unlikely to be in an LLM’s pretraining set—attacking documents sanitized with Presidio. Their results show significantly lower exact-match success rates. However, they articulate a conundrum: achieving a truly trustworthy evaluation requires data that is provably private and inaccessible to the attack model, which is legally and ethically infeasible for transparent, reproducible academic research.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate a GPT-OSS-120B reconstruction attack on documents sanitized with Presidio (combined with spaCy NER). Two datasets are used: Czech court announcements (translated from Czech PDFs) and English YouTube travel vlog transcripts uploaded after the model’s training cutoff. PII spans are detected and replaced with unique `[MASK_i]` tokens. The attack model is prompted to first extract verbatim textual hints and then generate exactly three candidate strings per mask token. Success is measured by EM@3 (exact match of any candidate with ground-truth PII). A control fine-tuning the attack model on sanitized documents instead of originals yields 0% accuracy, confirming memorization as the key driver of inflated prior results.

## Key Results
- EM@3 rates are substantially lower than prior work: 19.1% on YouTube transcripts, 5.5% on Czech court announcements.
- Maximum document-level EM@3 reaches 51.5% (YouTube) and 36.4% (Czech).
- Analysis attributes remaining success primarily to incomplete PII detection by Presidio, where missed entities provide contextual clues for inferring masked ones.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Memorization Leakage
- **Claim:** Attack models reconstruct PII because they memorized original documents during training/fine-tuning, not by inferring from sanitized text.
- **Mechanism:** The attack LLM was exposed to the original unsanitized documents in its training data. When presented with a sanitized version, it recalls the original PII via pattern-matching.
- **Core assumption:** Temporal/data overlap exists between the attack model’s training corpus and the original documents.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Models fine-tuned on original documents before attack have the information memorized at start ([section 5.3]).
  - Models fine-tuned on sanitized documents achieve 0% reconstruction accuracy ([section 5.3]).
- **Break condition:** Attack model’s training data is provably disjoint from original documents (e.g., strict pre-cutoff, post-cutoff data).

### Mechanism 2: Public Knowledge Leakage
- **Claim:** Attack success occurs because PII or its contextual anchors already exist in public sources included in the model’s pretraining.
- **Mechanism:** Sanitized text retains contextual clues (unique locations, events) that correlate with PII in public data (news, Wikipedia). The model associates context with specific PII from its pretraining.
- **Core assumption:** PII and its contextual anchors are publicly available and were in the model’s pretraining data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - PII removal cannot protect if underlying data is already public ([section 5.1]).
  - Model trained on Wikipedia can re-identify famous people from public information ([section 5.2]).
- **Break condition:** PII and its contextual anchors are absent from all public sources in the model’s pretraining data.

### Mechanism 3: Incomplete PII Detection
- **Claim:** Attack success is enabled by the PII removal tool’s imperfect recall, leaving some PII unmasked and providing contextual anchors.
- **Mechanism:** Missed PII entities (e.g., a repeated name in one sentence) allow the attack model to deduce masked PII via coreference or association.
- **Core assumption:** The PII removal tool has sub-perfect recall and documents contain repeated/co-referent PII.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Successful name reconstruction in Czech data resulted from repeated mentions where one instance was missed ([section 8.3]).
  - Unmasked phrases like “I Love NY gift shop” suffice to guess location ([section 8.3]).
- **Break condition:** The PII removal tool achieves perfect recall, masking every PII entity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: PII Removal Typology**
  - Why needed: Understands the methods (redaction, masking, replacement, pseudonymization) and their inherent limitations (no formal guarantees), which are the target of attacks.
  - Quick check: How does masking (`[PERSON]`) differ from pseudonymization (synthetic name replacement) in utility preservation and privacy risk?

- **Concept: Memorization in LLMs**
  - Why needed: Memorization is a primary mechanism for inflated attack success; LLMs can verbatim reproduce training data, invalidating evaluations where the attack model has seen originals.
  - Quick check: Why does fine-tuning an LLM on original documents before attacking them invalidate the attack evaluation?

- **Concept: Threat Modeling**
  - Why needed: Formalizing attacker capabilities and knowledge is essential to avoid flawed evaluations; success rates are meaningless without clear assumptions about attacker’s prior knowledge.
  - Quick check: What is the difference between an attacker who has never seen the original document and one who has memorized it during pretraining?

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Source -> Sanitization Module -> Attack Engine -> Evaluation & Analysis Module
(Leakage Audit Module is optional but critical for validation)

- **Critical path:**
  1. Secure acquisition of private text data with provable disjointness from attack model’s training corpus.
  2. Run PII detection and masking (e.g., Presidio + spaCy) to produce sanitized documents.
  3. Prompt attack LLM to generate reconstruction candidates and hints per mask token.
  4. Compare candidates against ground-truth PII; compute EM@3 and document-level metrics.
  5. Analyze hints and failures to attribute success to memorization, public knowledge, or incomplete detection.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Data source:** Truly private data is ideal but inaccessible; post-cutoff public data is accessible but risks public knowledge leakage; synthetic data avoids privacy issues but introduces distribution shift and potential generation leakage.
  - **Attack model:** Open-weight models (e.g., Llama) allow training data inspection but may lag in capability; proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4) are powerful but have opaque training data, risking contamination.
  - **PII removal tool:** Open-source tools (Presidio) enable error analysis but may have lower accuracy; closed-source cloud APIs (Azure Comprehend) may be more accurate but hinder reproducibility and data privacy.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - High success on public-figure entities indicates public knowledge leakage.
  - Zero recall when attack model is trained on sanitized data confirms memorization as the cause.
  - Success correlates with repeated unmasked entities suggests incomplete PII detection.
  - Success on generic/common names without contextual clues may indicate model bias, not genuine inference.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Disjointness validation:** Use a verifiably post-cutoff dataset (e.g., new forum data). Perform membership inference test to establish near-zero memorization baseline before attack.
  2. **PII recall stress test:** Manually annotate a subset for *all* PII. Correlate document-level EM@3 with count of undetected PII entities.
  3. **Perfect-masking control:** For a subset, create a version with *all* ground-truth PII masked. Rerun attack; significant drop confirms incomplete detection as primary enabler.

## Limitations
- **Dataset source & disjointness:** Specific Czech court URLs and YouTube video IDs are not public, preventing verification that data was truly unavailable to the attack model’s training corpus; post-cutoff assumption is unverifiable without model provenance.
- **Attack model details:** The exact training data cutoff and contamination audit for “gpt-oss-120b” are unspecified; prompting and decoding parameters are not fully detailed.
- **PII detection configuration:** The precise Presidio recognizers, spaCy NER labels, and overlap-resolution logic are underspecified, making replication’s detection pipeline different and potentially altering masked span distribution.

## Confidence
- **High:** The core conundrum—that prior high success rates are likely inflated by data leakage—is strongly supported by control experiments (e.g., fine-tuning on sanitized data yields 0% accuracy) and aligns with memorization literature.
- **Medium:** The specific quantitative results (EM@3: YouTube 19.1%, Czech 5.5%) are plausible as a proof-of-concept bound but are not independently reproducible due to data unavailability.
- **Low:** The attribution of remaining attack success primarily to “incomplete PII detection” is suggestive but not proven; analysis correlates success with repeated entities but does not isolate this factor from residual public knowledge in a controlled ablation.

## Next Checks
1. **Data disjointness verification:** Procure a verifiably post-cutoff dataset (e.g., newly created forum with no-crawl license). Perform membership inference test with attack model to establish near-zero baseline memorization before PII reconstruction attack.
2. **PII detection recall audit:** Manually annotate a statistically significant sample for all PII entities. Calculate true recall of the Presidio+spaCy pipeline. Correlate document-level EM@3 with count of undetected PII entities.
3. **Perfect-masking control:** For a subset, create a version where *all* ground-truth PII spans are masked. Rerun attack; a significant drop in success compared to automated masking confirms incomplete detection as a primary enabler.
