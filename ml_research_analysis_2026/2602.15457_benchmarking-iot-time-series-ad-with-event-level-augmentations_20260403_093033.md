# Benchmarking IoT Time-Series AD with Event-Level Augmentations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15457
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15457
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** No single model universally robust; graph-structured models excel under sensor dropout and long events, flow-based models collapse under monotone drift, and sensor vetting can boost detection F1 by +54% on industrial data.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a critical gap in anomaly detection (AD) benchmarking: point-level metrics do not reflect real-world deployment needs where models must handle sensor failures, drift, and noise without test-time calibration. The authors introduce an event-level evaluation protocol with offline-calibrated stress augmentations (dropout, drift, noise, window shifts) and a sensor-level probing technique (mask-as-missing zeroing) to estimate channel influence. Evaluating 14 models across seven datasets reveals that model robustness is stressor-dependent—graph neural networks handle dropout best, density flows fail under monotonic drift, and removing "toxic" sensors dramatically improves performance on industrial systems. The work provides concrete design trade-offs (e.g., Gaussian vs. flow densities, fixed vs. learned graphs) and a reproducible stress-testing framework for reliable IoT AD.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 14 time-series AD models (reconstruction, predictive, graph, spectral CNN, density/flow) on seven datasets (SWaT, WADI, SMD, SKAB, TEP, plus two proprietary industrial sets). Models are trained exclusively on normal training windows. Stressors are applied as preprocessing to validation and test tensors: additive noise (scaled by feature std), sensor dropout (random channel zeroing), linear/log drift (monotonic transforms), and window shifts. Stress severity is normalized per-dataset using validation statistics (e.g., noise std = severity × feature’s training std). A fixed threshold, chosen to maximize event-level F1 on the validation set, is applied to test predictions. Event aggregation merges overlapping window predictions into discrete anomaly events; primary metric is event-level F1. Sensor probing zeroes one channel at a time on the test set to measure F1 change, identifying channels whose removal improves detection ("toxic" sensors).

## Key Results
- Graph models (GBAD, STGAT) are most robust to sensor dropout and long anomalous events; e.g., on SWaT with additive noise, GBAD drops only 16% (0.804→0.677) while some models collapse.
- Normalizing flow-based detectors (GANF) are fragile to monotone drift (linear/log), showing near-zero F1 on SKAB and nuclear plant data under log drift.
- Replacing normalizing flows with Gaussian density estimates (GDE) reduces high-stress F1 by ~0.18 (SWaT log drift) but lowers computational cost.
- Fixing a learned DAG yields small clean-set gains (+0.5–1.0 F1 points) but increases drift sensitivity ~8×.
- Sensor vetting via zeroing "toxic" channels boosts F1 by +54% on a turbogenerator dataset.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Explicit graph-structured priors improve model robustness to sensor dropout and long anomalous events by maintaining information flow through learned dependencies.
- **Mechanism:** Graph neural networks encode inter-sensor relationships; when some channels fail (dropout), messages propagate via alternate edges, preserving temporal patterns needed for event detection.
- **Core assumption:** Sensor dependencies are relatively stable and can be learned from normal data; dropout events are spatially uncorrelated across sensors.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "graph-structured models (e.g., GBAD, STGAT) transfer best under dropout and long events"
  - [section] "On SWaT with additive noise a graph autoencoder falls from 0.804 to 0.677 (-16%)... hybrid graph attention stays nearly flat at 0.762 to 0.756 (-0.8%)"
- **Break condition:** If dropout is highly targeted (e.g., all sensors in a critical subsystem) or the learned graph topology is incorrect, the redundancy benefit vanishes and performance degrades sharply.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Normalizing flow-based density models collapse under monotone drift because they rely on stationary distribution assumptions and invertible transforms that fail under sustained unmodeled shifts.
- **Mechanism:** Flows learn a bijective mapping to a Gaussian base distribution; monotone drift (linear/log) pushes data outside the support of the learned distribution, yielding arbitrarily low likelihood scores for normal-seeming points.
- **Core assumption:** Test-time data distribution is identical to training normal distribution; monotone transforms are not present in the normal manifold.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "density/flow models... can be fragile to monotone drift, as log drift collapses a flow-based detector on SKAB and nuclear power plant (NPP) dataset"
  - [section] "Under log drift, flow-based density models can collapse... on SKAB and NPP scores collapse toward 10.0 at small drift"
- **Break condition:** If drift is non-monotonic or the flow is replaced with a robust density estimator (e.g., Gaussian), the collapse is mitigated, as shown by the GDE ablation in Fig. 5.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Sensor-level zeroing probing identifies "toxic" channels whose removal improves overall detection by eliminating misleading signals that dominate anomaly scores.
- **Mechanism:** Zeroing a channel simulates its permanent failure; a significant F1 increase indicates that channel's normal variations were causing false positives or masking true anomalies. Removing it reduces noise in the model's residual or likelihood.
- **Core assumption:** Some sensors exhibit behavior inconsistent with the learned normal manifold (e.g., faulty calibration, external interference) and their inclusion degrades detection.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "sensor vetting (zeroing toxic channels) boosts F1 by +54% on industrial data"
  - [section] "On the Turbogenerator dataset, disabling it lifts GBAD scores from ≈0.38→0.58 (+54%)"
- **Break condition:** If all sensors are equally informative or the "toxic" channel is actually the primary indicator of anomalies, zeroing would reduce performance instead.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Event-level aggregation**
  - *Why needed here:* Paper argues deployment success depends on detecting entire anomalous episodes, not isolated points; metrics must aggregate overlapping window predictions into events.
  - *Quick check question:* How does point-adjust scoring differ from true event-level F1 in reflecting detection latency and false alarms?
- **Concept: Offline-calibrated stress**
  - *Why needed here:* Stressors (dropout, drift, noise) are calibrated on validation set statistics (e.g., relative to nominal variance) and *fixed* before testing, ensuring zero test-time adaptation.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is normalizing stress severity by dataset-specific validation metrics crucial for fair comparison?
- **Concept: Graph neural networks for multivariate TS**
  - *Why needed here:* Top-performing models under dropout use explicit or learned graph structures to model sensor dependencies, a key inductive bias for robustness.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a graph autoencoder handle missing sensors better than a standard LSTM autoencoder?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw sensor streams → Preprocessing (windowing, normalized stress augmentations) → Model (e.g., graph encoder, flow density estimator) → Anomaly scoring → Event aggregation (merge overlapping window predictions) → Evaluation (event-level F1 with fixed threshold)
- **Critical path:** Windowing → Model forward pass on test windows → Threshold comparison (threshold set on validation) → Event merging (overlap logic) → Metric computation. Stress is injected in preprocessing; no model retuning after onset.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Graph models (GBAD, STGAT) scale with sensor count (O(N²) attention) but gain dropout robustness.
  - Flow models (GANF) offer causal insights via DAG but are computationally heavy and drift-fragile.
  - Replacing normalizing flows with Gaussian density (GDE) cuts cost but reduces high-stress F1 by ~0.18 (SWaT log drift).
  - Fixing learned DAG yields +0.5–1.0 clean points but increases drift sensitivity ~8×.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - Under additive noise: variable-attention models (GDN) collapse; graph hybrids (MTAD-GAT) stay flat.
  - Under monotone drift: flow models (GANF) collapse on SKAB/NPP; graph models degrade moderately.
  - Without sensor vetting: reconstruction autoencoders (MLPREC) show large per-channel swings; toxic channels dominate failure.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Clean baseline: Train on normal training set, evaluate on clean test set with event-level F1 to establish reference rankings.
  2. Stress sweep: Apply one stressor (e.g., additive noise at 10% of feature std) to test set, re-evaluate with same threshold. Record F1 drop per model.
  3. Sensor zeroing probe: For each channel, zero it across entire test set, re-evaluate F1, and compute influence as score difference. Identify channels where zeroing increases F1 (>5% boost) as "toxic"; optionally retrain with those channels removed.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can stress severity normalization be made independent of validation statistics? The authors note that "stress severity is normalized by validation statistics" as a limitation and suggest extending to embedded data-specific stress testing.
- How stable are the model rankings and derived design rules under different event aggregation definitions? The protocol fixes event definitions, while prior work shows point-adjust and other metrics can alter rankings.
- How accurate is mask-as-missing zeroing for identifying truly influential (toxic) sensors? The method is used for sensor-level probing and performance improves when disabling certain channels, but its diagnostic accuracy is not validated.

## Limitations
- Proprietary datasets (steam turbine, nuclear turbogenerator) prevent full independent verification of reported results.
- Stress severity mapping (drift coefficients, noise scaling) and window shift implementation are underspecified, requiring access to authors' code for faithful reproduction.
- Event aggregation algorithm (overlap rules, tolerance) and sensor "toxicity" ranking method lack detailed documentation.
- Validation set composition and threshold selection procedure are not fully described, introducing ambiguity in reproduction.

## Confidence
- **Graph models (GBAD, STGAT) robust to sensor dropout/long events:** Medium (specific numbers but stress calibration uncertain).
- **Flow models (GANF) collapse under monotone drift:** Medium (consistent but drift formula vague).
- **Sensor zeroing yields +54% F1 boost on turbogenerator:** Low (proprietary, no public replication).
- **No universal winner across stressors:** Medium.
- **Trade-offs (Gaussian vs flow, fixed DAG) quantified:** Medium.
