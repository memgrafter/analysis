# Reactive Knowledge Representation and Asynchronous Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05625
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05625
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Achieves several orders of magnitude speedup in exact inference for dynamic autonomous systems via frequency‑based partitioning and reactive incremental evaluation.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the inefficiency of exact inference in dynamic autonomous systems, where traditional methods redundantly recompute entire probabilistic models on any input change, ignoring heterogeneous update rates of real‑world data streams. The authors introduce Resin, an asynchronous probabilistic programming language, and Reactive Circuits (RCs), which partition computations based on the estimated Frequency of Change (FoC) of each input signal. RCs dynamically restructure a time‑dynamic directed acyclic graph over algebraic circuits, memoizing stable sub‑problems and only re‑evaluating components affected by meaningful updates. In high‑fidelity drone swarm simulations, this approach yields orders‑of‑magnitude speedups over frequency‑agnostic inference while preserving exact semantics.

## Method Summary
Resin programs declare source signals, probabilistic logic rules, and target signals. Compilation first translates the program into an ASP representation; a solver (Clingo) finds stable models, which are interpreted as a weighted model counting formula. This formula is compiled into an initial Algebraic Circuit (AC). At runtime, the system estimates each source’s FoC using a Kalman filter and clusters signals into frequency bands of width *h*. The RC manager then restructures the circuit via *lift* and *drop* operations (exploiting the distributive law) so that signals in the same band belong to common sub‑circuit nodes. Upon a meaningful update, only the nodes dependent on that signal (its *Dep* set) are recomputed in reverse topological order, preserving exactness while avoiding full recomputation.

## Key Results
- Orders‑of‑magnitude speedup over full recomputation in drone swarm safety monitoring.
- Exact inference semantics are maintained by the RC adaptation and evaluation.
- Speedup scales with heterogeneity of signal update frequencies; FoC‑guided partitioning is essential.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Frequency‑of‑Change guided partitioning
**Claim:** Estimating each signal’s Frequency of Change (FoC) and partitioning computations accordingly reduces redundant evaluations.  
**Mechanism:** A Kalman filter estimates inter‑arrival times of meaningful updates; signals are binned into discrete frequency bands (width *h*). The RC DAG is restructured via *lift* and *drop* operations so that same‑band signals feed common sub‑formula nodes. When a signal updates, only its band’s nodes and ancestors are recomputed.  
**Core assumption:** FoC estimates accurately reflect relative volatility, enabling grouping of low‑frequency signals for maximal memoization.  
**Evidence anchors:** Sections 3.2–3.3 describe the estimator and partitioning; Section 4 provides empirical validation.  
**Break condition:** Persistent large FoC estimation errors (e.g., due to non‑Poisson streams or high noise) cause mis‑partitioning, leading to either excessive restructuring or lost memoization gains.

### Mechanism 2: Resin compilation to Reactive Circuits
**Claim:** Compiling a Resin program into an RC bridges high‑level probabilistic logic with an adaptive, semiring‑general inference engine.  
**Mechanism:** The Resin program is compiled to an ASP representation; Clingo finds stable models, which are interpreted as a weighted model counting formula. This formula is translated into an initial Algebraic Circuit (AC). The AC becomes the root of an RC DAG, with source signals as leaves. The RC then adapts online as per Mechanism 1.  
**Core assumption:** The ASP compilation correctly captures Resin’s probabilistic semantics (Eq. 1) and the AC accurately represents the target semiring computation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 2.3 defines the compilation; Section 3.3 defines RC structure; prior work validates AC foundations.  
**Break condition:** ASP solver failures or exponential AC growth during compilation; errors in Resin’s type coercion corrupt the AC.

### Mechanism 3: Reactive incremental evaluation
**Claim:** Incrementally re‑evaluating only invalidated sub‑computations matches update cost to the true scope of change.  
**Mechanism:** On a meaningful update to signal *s*ₙ, the RC computes *Dep(s*ₙ*) , the set of all formula nodes whose memoized values are invalidated. These nodes are processed in reverse topological order, using fresh child values and memoized results to recompute only the affected sub‑DAG.  
**Core assumption:** Dependency tracking (*Dep*) is exact and the topological order ensures correct recomputation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Definition of *Dep(s*ₙ*) and update queue *Q* in Section 3.3; Theorem 3.9 proves computational gain.  
**Break condition:** Incorrect dependency metadata (missing/extra invalidations) or topological order violations produce stale results or wasted work.

## Foundational Learning
### Algebraic Circuits (ACs)
**Why needed here:** ACs are the computational substrate for RCs, providing exact, semiring‑general representation of formulas (e.g., weighted model counting) as DAGs of sum (⊕) and product (⊗) gates. RCs extend a single AC into a time‑dynamic DAG of ACs.  
**Quick check:** For the formula *P(A)*·*P(B)* + *P(¬A)*·*P(C)*, identify leaf nodes and gate types. How would you restructure the AC if B and C have similar update frequencies?

### Commutative Semirings
**Why needed here:** The *lift* and *drop* operations and reactive evaluation rely on the distributive property *a*⊗(*b*⊕*c*) = (*a*⊗*b*)⊕(*a*⊗*c*). This allows moving a signal between nodes without changing the result and generalizes beyond probabilities (e.g., gradients, max‑product).  
**Quick check:** Why is distributivity critical for the *drop* operation? What breaks if ⊗ does not distribute over ⊕?

### Asynchronous Data Streams & Frequency of Change (FoC)
**Why needed here:** Real‑world data streams update at heterogeneous, non‑stationary rates. FoC (Definition 3.6) quantifies the rate of “meaningful” updates, driving the partitioning that makes RCs efficient.  
**Quick check:** If a drone’s position updates at 50 Hz but its “safe/unsafe” derived atom changes once per minute, how should these be partitioned? What FoC would you estimate for the derived atom?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Resin Compiler → RC Manager → DDS  
*Resin Compiler* parses Resin programs into initial Algebraic Circuits; *RC Manager* maintains the RC DAG, runs FoC estimators, and triggers *lift*/*drop* adaptations; *DDS* (Data Distribution Service) routes source updates and publishes target values.

**Critical path:** A source signal update arrives via DDS → the RC Manager’s FoC estimator updates → if the signal’s estimated FoC crosses a partition boundary, the RC is restructured (*lift*/*drop*); otherwise, the signal’s dependency set is marked invalid → the update queue processes invalidated nodes in reverse topological order, recomputing only the affected sub‑DAG → the new target value is published via DDS.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Partition width (*h*):* Smaller *h* increases granularity and potential speedup but also circuit depth, memory usage, and adaptation frequency. Larger *h* simplifies the circuit but reduces speedup.  
- *Memory vs. speed:* Finer partitions (more memoization) lower per‑update cost at the expense of higher memory; the trade‑off is tunable via *h*.  
- *FoC estimation tuning:* Kalman filter process/measurement noise covariances affect responsiveness versus lag; poor tuning leads to too many or too few adaptations.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Semantic drift:** Target outputs diverge from correct WMC; likely due to incorrect *lift*/*drop* violating distributivity or mishandling negation.  
- **No speedup / slowdown:** Runtime matches or exceeds the flat baseline; check whether all FoC estimates collapse to one band, update rate is too high relative to adaptation cost, or dependency tracking is over‑inclusive.  
- **Excessive memory use:** Circuit depth or node count far exceeds expectations; likely *h* set too small or noisy FoC estimates causing over‑partitioning.

**First experiments:**  
1. **Synthetic FoC tracking and partitioning:** Inject artificial signals with known update frequencies; verify Kalman filter tracks FoC, partitioning assigns signals to correct bands, and *lift*/*drop* occur at expected times. Measure circuit depth and memory vs. *h*.  
2. **UAS safety assertion (drone swarm):** Use AirSim‑generated drone positions; compute pairwise distances as Density sources. Verify that FoC of distance correlates with proximity, RC restructures during maneuvers, and reactive runtime shows orders‑of‑magnitude speedup over flat inference.  
3. **Ablation of adaptation and reactiveness:** On a fixed weighted model counting problem (e.g., 10⁵ models, 10³ sources), compare three configurations: flat (no adaptation), adapted (structurally optimized but full recompute), and reactive (full RC). Isolate the contributions of frequency‑guided compilation vs. incremental evaluation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Heterogeneous computation integration:** How can RCs incorporate diverse models (e.g., neural networks) within formula nodes, and what performance trade‑offs emerge? The paper suggests future work but provides no implementation.  
- **Proactive data acquisition:** Can RCs use FoC estimates to request upstream models or sensors to adapt their update rates, thereby reducing overall system latency? The paper mentions active interactions but has not explored them.  
- **Automatic partition width selection:** What is the optimal strategy for setting the partition width *h* online to balance speedup against memory overhead? The paper treats *h* as a hyperparameter and shows non‑monotonic trade‑offs but offers no automatic method.  
- **Robustness to non‑Poisson streams:** How robust are RCs to bursty or correlated signal updates that violate the independent Poisson assumption? The model assumes Poisson updates and Gaussian‑noisy FoC estimation; real sensor streams often deviate, potentially affecting correctness or speedup guarantees.  
- **Generalization beyond ASP/WMC:** Can the Resin compilation pipeline support other probabilistic programming paradigms (e.g., deep probabilistic programming, stochastic variational inference) while retaining RC efficiency? The paper proposes extending RCs to a wider range of models but currently only demonstrates exact weighted model counting.

## Limitations
- FoC estimation relies on Kalman filter tuning; performance may degrade for non‑Poisson or bursty streams.
- Partition width *h* is a heuristic; no principled method to select it for a given problem’s memory‑speed trade‑off.
- ASP‑based compilation may not scale and evaluation is limited to simulation; real‑world overheads (communication, scheduling, hardware) remain unmeasured.
