# CMAD: Cooperative Multi-Agent Diffusion via Stochastic Optimal Control

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10933
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10933
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CMAD achieves up to 99.90% accuracy on MNIST digit composition with two agents and 99.71% with three agents, substantially outperforming a DPS-style baseline in both accuracy and terminal loss.

## Executive Summary
CMAD introduces a cooperative multi-agent framework for compositional generation by treating pre-trained diffusion models as agents whose trajectories are jointly steered via stochastic optimal control (SOC). Instead of combining probability densities algebraically, the method formulates composition as an SOC problem where a shared objective guides control policies. Each agent's reverse diffusion process is perturbed by a learned control, and the aggregated output is optimized. The control policies are trained using iterative diffusion optimisation with coordinate-wise updates and a Tweedie-based running cost that provides dense supervision. Experiments on MNIST digit generation show significant improvements over a baseline, demonstrating the effectiveness of the cooperative SOC approach.

## Method Summary
CMAD redefines compositional generation as a cooperative stochastic optimal control (SOC) problem. Each pre-trained diffusion model acts as an agent following a reverse-time SDE; a learned control \(u_i\) perturbs its drift. The agents' states are aggregated (e.g., via non-overlapping masks) into a joint state \(Y_t\). The SOC objective minimizes the sum of control energy, a running cost \(c(Y_t,t)\), and a terminal loss \(\Psi(Y_T)\). The running cost uses Tweedie's formula to denoise intermediate states, providing dense supervision. Control policies are optimized with iterative diffusion optimisation (IDO): an outer loop alternately updates one agent's control while freezing others (coordinate-wise), and an inner loop simulates coupled SDE trajectories, estimates the objective via Monte Carlo, and backpropagates through time (BPTT) to update parameters. This decouples the high-dimensional joint problem and scales to multiple agents.

## Key Results
- **Digit '3' with three agents:** accuracy 99.71% (CMAD) vs 97.17% (DPS baseline); terminal loss 0.131 vs 0.638.
- **Digit '3' with two agents:** accuracy 99.90% vs 95.70%; terminal loss 0.064 vs 0.420.
- Across all tested settings, CMAD achieves higher classification accuracy and lower terminal loss, validating the cooperative SOC approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Treating multiple diffusion models as cooperative agents in a stochastic optimal control (SOC) framework enables composition without explicit density algebra.
- **Mechanism:** Each agent follows a reverse-time SDE perturbed by a learned control \(u_i\). The agents' states are aggregated via \(\phi\) into \(Y_t\), and a shared objective \(J = \mathbb{E}\left[\int_0^T (\sum_i \lambda_i\|u_i\|^2 + c(Y_t,t)) dt + \Psi(Y_T)\right]\) is minimized over all controls. This steers trajectories jointly toward minimizing \(\Psi\) on the aggregated output.
- **Core assumption:** The task can be specified by a differentiable loss \(\Psi\) on the aggregated final state, rather than requiring an explicit product or mixture of the agents' marginal densities.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "formulates compositional generation as a cooperative Stochastic Optimal Control problem. Rather than combining probability densities, we treat pre-trained diffusion models as interacting agents whose diffusion trajectories are jointly steered."
  - [section 2] The SOC objective (2) with control \(u_i\), aggregator \(\phi\), and costs \(c\), \(\Psi\) defines the cooperative optimization.
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., "Stochastic Optimal Control for Diffusion Bridges") explore SOC for single-agent diffusion, but CMAD's cooperative multi-agent formulation is not directly mirrored in the corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the aggregation operator \(\phi\) (e.g., non-overlapping mask) does not align with the task structure, or if \(\Psi\) is not well-approximated by the running cost \(c\), the control may fail to coordinate agents meaningfully, leading to incoherent outputs or poor task performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A coordinate-wise optimization scheme with iterative diffusion optimisation (IDO) enables scalable learning of cooperative control policies.
- **Mechanism:** The outer loop alternates updates over agents; for each agent \(i\), the other agents' controls are frozen, and IDO performs stochastic gradient descent on \(u_i\) by simulating coupled SDE trajectories, computing Monte Carlo estimates of \(J\), and backpropagating through time. This decouples the high-dimensional joint problem into a sequence of single-agent SOC subproblems.
- **Core assumption:** The control objective \(J\) is sufficiently separable that coordinate descent converges to a stationary point despite non-convexities and agent coupling.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.1] Algorithm 1 details the control-wise outer loop (lines 2–14) and inner IDO updates (lines 5–11), showing how agents are updated alternately.
  - [paper text] "The control-wise update is able to optimise the SOC objective" (Section 3) and comparative results in Table 1 show control-wise often matches or exceeds joint optimization.
  - [corpus] Related works (e.g., "Data-Driven Stochastic Optimal Control") consider single-agent SOC; CMAD extends IDO to multi-agent via coordinate-wise updates, a structural novelty not present in the corpus.
- **Break condition:** If agents' trajectories are highly interdependent (e.g., through overlapping aggregation regions), fixing other controls may cause oscillatory behavior or convergence to poor local equilibria, especially if the number of IDO inner steps is insufficient.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Using a Tweedie-based running cost provides dense temporal supervision, mitigating the credit assignment problem inherent in terminal-only objectives.
- **Mechanism:** At each diffusion step \(t\), each agent's state \(X^i_t\) is denoised via Tweedie's formula (\(\hat{X}^i_0 = X^i_t + \sigma(t)^2 S(X^i_t,t;\theta)\)), aggregated to \(\hat{Y}_0\), and the running cost is set to \(c(Y_t,t) = \alpha_t \Psi(\hat{Y}_0)\). This propagates terminal reward information backward along the trajectory.
- **Core assumption:** The pre-trained score network \(S(x,t;\theta)\) is accurate enough that Tweedie estimates \(\hat{X}^i_0\) are reliable proxies for clean samples, making \(\Psi(\hat{Y}_0)\) a meaningful surrogate for \(\Psi(Y_T)\) during training.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2] Equation (5) defines \(c(Y_t,t) = \alpha_t \Psi(\hat{Y}_0)\) with \(\hat{Y}_0\) from Tweedie estimates.
  - [appendix D] Algorithm 3 line 14 implements the Tweedie look-ahead; the text notes it acts as a stabilizer.
  - [corpus] "Stochastic Interpolants" and diffusion bridge papers use Tweedie-like corrections, but CMAD's specific use as a running cost in a multi-agent SOC context is unique.
- **Break condition:** If the score model \(S\) is poorly trained (common in low-data regimes), Tweedie estimates become biased, causing the running cost to misguide the control, potentially destabilizing optimization or converging to incorrect distributions.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Pre-trained score network \(S(x,t;\theta)\) (frozen) → provides score gradients; Agent control networks \(u_i(X^i_t, Y_t, t; \phi_i)\) → output control perturbations; Aggregator \(\phi\) (e.g., fixed non-overlapping mask) → maps agent states to \(Y_t\); SDE integrator (Euler–Maruyama) → simulates controlled forward-in-time reverse diffusion; Objective calculator → computes Monte Carlo estimate of \(J\): control energy, running cost (Tweedie aggregate), terminal loss \(\Psi\).

- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize control parameters \(\phi_i\), aggregator (if learnable), hyperparameters (\(\lambda, \alpha\)).
  2. For each outer iteration (control-wise or joint):
     - Simulate \(K\)-step coupled SDE trajectories for all agents using current controls.
     - At each step, compute Tweedie estimates, aggregate to \(\hat{Y}_0\), accumulate running cost \(c\).
     - At terminal step, compute \(\Psi(Y_T)\).
     - Form total objective \(\hat{J} = \lambda \ell_u + \alpha \ell_c + \ell_\Psi\).
     - Backpropagate through time (BPTT) to obtain \(\nabla_{\phi_i} \hat{J}\) (and \(\nabla_\vartheta\) if learning aggregator).
     - Update control parameters (and aggregator parameters) via Adam.
  3. Repeat until convergence or fixed iterations; evaluate generation accuracy/loss.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Joint vs. control-wise optimization:** Joint updates are simpler but memory- and compute-intensive for many agents; control-wise scales better but may require more iterations and careful tuning to avoid suboptimal equilibria.
  - **Fixed vs. learned aggregator \(\phi\):** Fixed masks (e.g., non-overlapping regions) are interpretable and stable; learned aggregators could adapt to tasks but add complexity and overfitting risk.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Training instability:** Exploding/NaN gradients in BPTT; check SDE step size, gradient clipping, learning rate.
  - **Incoherent outputs:** Seam artifacts or blurry digits; inspect intermediate \(Y_t\); adjust seam loss weights or control regularisation \(\lambda\).
  - **Poor coordination despite low loss:** Verify classifier accuracy on clean samples; ensure \(\Psi\) is meaningful; test different aggregation masks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the CMAD framework be scaled to high-dimensional applications, and what modifications are needed to extend path-wise gradient estimators or adjoint matching techniques to the multi-agent setting?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "In future work, we want to scale this framework to more challenging, high dimensional applications. This requires extending common path-wise gradient estimators (Clark et al., 2023; Schulman et al., 2017) or techniques such as adjoint matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024a) to our setting."
- **Why unresolved:** The current implementation is limited to small-scale MNIST experiments; scaling to high-resolution images or complex data would require efficient gradient estimation to avoid memory bottlenecks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Developing a memory-efficient variant of IDO (e.g., using adjoint methods) and demonstrating successful composition on high-dimensional benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet, text-to-image).

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Under what conditions does the control-wise (coordinate-descent) optimization scheme guarantee convergence, and how does its performance compare to joint optimization in terms of sample complexity and stability?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Our numerical results in Section 3 show that the control-wise update is able to optimise the SOC objective. We want to study under which conditions the control-wise update actually leads to a provable convergent algorithm."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only provides empirical evidence; no theoretical analysis ensures convergence or optimality of the alternating updates, especially given the coupled SDE system.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Proving convergence rates or conditions (e.g., convexity-like assumptions) for the control-wise update, and conducting large-scale experiments comparing convergence speed and solution quality between joint and control-wise schemes.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the proposed cooperative SOC framework relate to differential games and fictitious-play dynamics, and can insights from game theory improve the multi-agent coordination?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Further, we want to expand the connection to differential games and fictitious-play dynamics (Hu, 2019)."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper frames composition as a cooperative control problem but does not exploit game-theoretic perspectives that might yield better algorithms or equilibrium concepts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Formulating the multi-agent diffusion composition as a stochastic differential game and testing fictitious-play or Nash equilibrium approaches against the current SOC formulation.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the aggregation operator \(\phi\) be parameterized and learned jointly with the control policies, and would such end-to-end learning improve composition quality on tasks with complex or overlapping spatial constraints?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Finally, it would be interesting to investigate settings in which the aggregation operator is itself parametrised and learned jointly with the control."
- **Why unresolved:** The current experiments use fixed, hand-designed non-overlapping masks. Learned aggregation could adapt to data and tasks but introduces additional complexity and the risk of overfitting.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Designing a differentiable parameterization of \(\phi\) (e.g., soft masks, attention maps) and optimizing it together with controls; evaluating on tasks where optimal aggregation is unknown and showing improvements.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Does the cooperative SOC approach provide a principled alternative to algebraic composition (e.g., product of experts) across diverse generative tasks beyond MNIST digit classification, and under what conditions does it outperform such methods?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The abstract and introduction claim that algebraic composition is unrealistic and that CMAD avoids explicit density combinations; however, experiments are limited to a single dataset and simple spatial composition. The paper does not directly compare to algebraic composition methods (PoE, geometric average) as baselines, only to DPS-style guidance.
- **Why unresolved:** The advantage of SOC over algebraic composition is asserted conceptually but not empirically validated against those methods; it's also unclear when one should be preferred.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting direct comparisons against product-of-experts and geometric average samplers on a range of compositional tasks (e.g., multi-prompt text-to-image, multi-domain translation) and analyzing when SOC yields better sample quality, diversity, or coherence.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to MNIST; performance on higher-dimensional or diverse datasets is unknown.
- Aggregation limited to fixed non-overlapping masks; applicability to overlapping or learned aggregators remains untested.
- Critical hyperparameters (control network architecture, scaling function \(\alpha_t\), seam loss weights) are not fully disclosed, hindering faithful reproduction.

## Confidence
- Cooperative SOC formulation enables density-free composition: **Medium** (theoretically sound but empirically narrow).
- Coordinate-wise IDO yields effective multi-agent control: **Medium** (experimental support lacks convergence guarantees).
- Tweedie-based running cost mitigates credit assignment: **Medium** (plausible design, no direct ablation).
- Reported MNIST accuracy/loss: **High** for the exact setting, but **Low** for broader generalization.
