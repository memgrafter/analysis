# Weber's Law in Transformer Magnitude Representations: Efficient Coding, Representational Geometry, and Psychophysical Laws in Language Models
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.20642
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20642
*   **Reference count:** 6
*   **Primary result:** Magnitude representations in three major instruction-tuned LLMs (Llama, Mistral, Qwen) consistently exhibit a log-compressive (Weber-law) geometry across numerical, temporal, and spatial domains, yet this geometry is dissociated from behavioral discrimination performance and functionally localized to early layers.
## Executive Summary
This study resolves prior conflicting reports on whether transformer language models represent magnitude linearly or logarithmically. Applying rigorous psychophysical toolkit—representational similarity analysis (RSA), behavioral discrimination tests, precision gradients, and causal activation patching—across numerical, temporal, and spatial domains in three 7–9B parameter models, it establishes that log-compressive geometry (consistent with Weber's Law) is universal (RSA correlations 0.68–0.96). However, behavioral Weber fractions (a measure of discrimination precision) were model- and domain-specific: only Llama achieved human-range performance numerically, while both models performed at chance on temporal and spatial tasks despite possessing the logarithmic geometry. Causal intervention further revealed a dissociation: early layers showed strong functional involvement in comparison (4.1× specificity), whereas later layers with the strongest geometric signal were causally inert (1.2×). Corpus analysis confirmed the efficient coding precondition: integer frequencies in the training data followed a power-law distribution (α=0.77). These findings separate representational geometry from behavioral readout and functional locus within the transformer architecture.
## Method Summary
The study employs four interconnected paradigms across three 7–9B instruction-tuned decoder-only models (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct). **Paradigm A (Geometry)** extracts hidden states at magnitude token positions, averages across contextual carrier sentences, computes pairwise distance matrices (cosine/Euclidean), and tests competing geometric models (Linear, Weber/Log, Stevens) via fit statistics (R², AIC) and RSA against theoretical dissimilarity matrices (Spearman ρ, Mantel permutation test). **Paradigm B (Behavior)** presents forced-choice comparison prompts (e.g., "Which is larger: A) [expr1] or B) [expr2]?"), generates greedy decodes, computes accuracy as a function of magnitude ratio, and estimates Weber fractions by fitting psychometric functions and performing nested model deviance tests. **Paradigm C (Precision)** computes local precision as the inverse norm of the representation difference between adjacent magnitudes and analyzes its gradient. **Paradigm D (Causal Intervention)** trains linear ridge probes to predict log(magnitude), validates the extracted "magnitude direction" via PCA (requiring PC1 correlation >0.80), then additively patches activations along this direction at various layers during behavioral tasks, measuring accuracy changes (Δp) and specificity relative to random direction controls.
## Key Results
*   **Universal log-compressive geometry:** Representational geometry was consistently best described by a Weber-law (logarithmic) model across all 96 tested model-domain-layer combinations (RSA ρ = 0.68–0.96); a linear model was never preferred.
*   **Geometry-behavior dissociation:** Only Llama produced a behavioral Weber fraction in the human range (WF ≈ 0.20) for numerical comparison. Both Llama and Mistral performed at chance (~50%) on temporal and spatial discrimination tasks, despite exhibiting strong logarithmic geometry in those domains.
*   **Layer-specific functional dissociation:** Causal activation patching implicated early layers (e.g., Llama layer 5: specificity 4.07×) as necessary for comparison performance, while later layers where geometric signals peaked showed minimal causal impact (e.g., Llama layer 23: specificity 1.22×).
## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
The training distribution's power-law statistics (α≈0.77) drive the emergence of log-compressive magnitude representations via the efficient coding principle. Under capacity constraints, an information-maximizing encoder for a 1/s prior produces a logarithmic transformation, yielding Weber's Law. This occurs in transformers via standard cross-entropy minimization on natural text, without biological constraints. The finding is robust: no model-domain-layer cell showed a statistically significant preference for linear geometry (all Weber RSA ρ > Linear RSA ρ, Mantel p < 0.017).
### Mechanism 2
Logarithmic geometry is necessary but not sufficient for Weber-like behavioral discrimination. Behavioral competence requires a functional readout mechanism established during instruction tuning. The geometric encoding exists independently of the downstream pathways that translate it into comparison decisions. Evidence: Llama and Mistral both possess strong logarithmic geometry, but only Llama shows human-range Weber fractions numerically, and both models perform at chance on temporal/spatial discrimination. The base (non-instruct) Llama, with equal geometry, cannot follow the comparison instruction, confirming the readout dissociation.
### Mechanism 3
The layers where geometry is strongest (late layers) are not functionally implicated in approximate magnitude comparison; early layers with weaker geometry provide the causal substrate. Early layers encode magnitude in an operational format directly used by the comparison circuit, while late layers may encode magnitude as part of a structured summary for next-token prediction, which is geometrically organized but not accessed by the specific comparison pathway. Causal patching shows early layers have significantly higher specificity ratios than late, peak-geometry layers.
## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Efficient Coding**
*   **Why needed here:** Provides the theoretical link between the observed power-law input distribution (α=0.77) and the predicted logarithmic representational geometry, forming the core hypothesis.
*   **Quick check:** If training data number frequencies follow f(n) ∝ n^{-α} with α≈0.77, what compression does efficient coding predict, and what psychophysical law does that yield?

**Concept: Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA)**
*   **Why needed here:** The primary method for testing geometric hypotheses (linear vs. logarithmic) by correlating model pairwise distance matrices with theoretical model dissimilarity matrices using rank correlation (Spearman's ρ), with Mantel permutation tests to handle non-independence of pairs.
*   **Quick check:** Why is a Mantel permutation test necessary when correlating two RDMs, and what would violating its assumption of non-independence inflate if uncorrected?

**Concept: Causal Intervention (Activation Patching)**
*   **Why needed here:** Probing (e.g., RSA) reveals correlation, not necessity. Patching tests whether manipulating a putative magnitude subspace actually shifts behavioral outcomes, establishing functional relevance and revealing layer dissociation.
*   **Quick check:** If patching along a "magnitude direction" changes comparison accuracy by Δp, but patching along 10 random directions produces a similar |Δp| distribution, what does that imply about the specificity of the magnitude subspace?
## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Corpus (OpenWebText) -> Pretraining -> Model Hidden States (per layer) -> RSA Geometry Test -> Behavioral Readout (Instruction Tuning) -> Causal Patching
**Critical path**: The study's logic flow hinges on connecting corpus statistics (power-law) to pretrained geometry (RSA), then dissociating that from instruction-tuned behavior (Weber fraction), and finally localizing function via causal intervention (patching). The key insight is the disconnect between geometric structure (ubiquitous and strong) and behavioral competence (model/domain-specific), with causality traced to early, not late, layers.
**Design tradeoffs**:
*   **RSA metrics:** Using both cosine and Euclidean distances; complete agreement on model preference increases robustness.
*   **Behavioral tasks:** Using cross-format (B1) prompts to isolate approximate comparison from exact arithmetic; this caused unit-boundary failures for temporal/spatial domains.
*   **Intervention direction:** Using a probe-derived direction (perfect R²=1.0, degenerate) but validating it against PCA's PC1 (requiring r>.80) to ensure it captures the dominant magnitude axis.
*   **Stimulus format:** Using mixed units for temporal/spatial to increase ecological validity, which inadvertently created a unit-conversion barrier explaining the behavioral null.
**Failure signatures**:
*   **Tokenization misalignment:** Mistral's multi-token numbers require extracting the final token; using the wrong token corrupts geometry. Diagnostic: verify tokenization offsets.
*   **Behavioral ceiling/floor:** Llama's symbolic control hits 99.9% (no room for patching), Mistral's hits 50% (broken readout). Diagnostic: always compute symbolic control baseline first.
*   **Probe overfitting:** With p=4096 >> n=26, probe R²=1.0 is guaranteed. Diagnostic: rely on PCA validation (PC1 correlation >0.80).
*   **RSA model selection ambiguity:** The hypothesis fails if any cell shows Linear AIC lower or Linear RSA not significantly lower (Mantel p>0.017). The paper reports zero such failures; any deviation indicates implementation differences.
**First 3 experiments**:
1.  **Corpus distribution analysis:** Extract all integer mentions (1–1000) from 500k OpenWebText documents; fit a power-law; verify α≈0.77.
2.  **Representational geometry sweep:** For each model, domain, and layer, compute RDM from magnitude centroids; fit Linear/Weber/Stevens models; run RSA with Mantel (10k permutations); record if Weber is strictly preferred.
3.  **Behavioral discrimination (B1):** Run cross-format forced-choice for all baseline×ratio pairs; compute accuracy by ratio; fit logistic psychometric; estimate Weber fraction; perform nested model ∆deviance test (ratio vs. absolute difference).
## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  Do the findings generalize to larger models, encoder-decoder architectures (e.g., T5), or models trained on substantially different data distributions? The study only tested three 7–9B decoder-only instruction-tuned models. Evidence: test representative models from these other categories using the same RSA geometry sweep.
2.  For temporal and spatial magnitudes, does forcing a single unit (e.g., all durations in seconds, all distances in meters) produce Weber-like discrimination? The mixed-unit format may have caused the chance-level behavioral null. Evidence: re-run behavioral discrimination with single-unit stimuli.
3.  Does systematically varying the power-law exponent of the training data distribution causally determine the Stevens exponent β of the resulting geometry? The current evidence is correlational. Evidence: train models on synthetic corpora with controlled α (e.g., 0.5, 1.0, 1.5) and measure β.
4.  Do the logarithmic geometry findings replicate for word-form number representations (e.g., "twenty-three") and for representations extracted from the sentence-final token? Two pre-registered robustness checks were not conducted. Evidence: run geometry analysis with word-form numbers and with final-token extraction.
## Limitations
*   **Stimulus & protocol details:** Exact baseline jitter parameters and full carrier sentence lists are not provided, making exact replication dependent on author code. The temporal/spatial behavioral null is confounded by mixed-unit format, as the pre-registered single-unit fallback was not executed.
*   **Causal intervention specifics:** The probe direction suffers from degeneracy (R²=1.0), and the precise patching implementation (e.g., additive vs. subtractive, dose scaling) is unspecified, though PCA validation provides a safeguard.
*   **Potential confounds:** Tokenization differences (single- vs. multi-token numbers) and extreme behavioral ceiling/floor effects (99.9% vs. 50%) could influence geometry readouts and the feasibility of causal tests.
## Confidence
*   **Universal log-compressive geometry (H1):** High. RSA correlations 0.68–0.96 across all 96 model-domain-layer cells, robust to distance metric and carrier sentence variance, with zero cells preferring linear geometry.
*   **Geometry-behavior dissociation (H2):** Medium. Strong dissociation in numerical domain (Llama human-range WF, Mistral not) and clear nulls in temporal/spatial, but the latter may be due to stimulus format (mixed units) rather than a true lack of accessible representation.
*   **Early-layer functional specificity (H3):** Medium. Causal pattern (early > late specificity) is consistent across models, but relies on a probe-derived magnitude direction validated by PCA; robustness to alternative extraction methods is not guaranteed.
## Next Checks
1.  **Exact replication with full stimulus set:** Recreate all carrier sentences and jitter parameters from the paper's description or code; confirm the universal Weber-preferred geometry (0/96 cells preferring linear) and replicate the behavioral dissociation pattern.
2.  **Causal robustness test:** Re-implement activation patching using PCA-only magnitude directions (without the linear probe) or independent component analysis; verify that early-layer specificity remains significantly higher than random controls and exceeds late-layer effects.
3.  **Single-unit temporal/spatial experiments:** Convert all temporal magnitudes to seconds and all spatial magnitudes to meters; re-run the behavioral discrimination (B1) analysis to determine if Weber fractions become positive and within human-range, resolving the unit-conversion confound.
