# On the Necessity of Learnable Sheaf Laplacians

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05395
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05395
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** An Identity Sheaf Network (ISN) with fixed identity restriction matches the performance of learnable Sheaf Neural Networks (SNNs) on standard heterophilic benchmarks, challenging the necessity of learned sheaf Laplacians for oversmoothing mitigation.

## Executive Summary
This paper questions the central design choice of Sheaf Neural Networks (SNNs): learning edge-wise restriction maps to construct a sheaf Laplacian. It introduces the Identity Sheaf Network (ISN), a trivial baseline where all restriction maps are fixed to the identity, simplifying the sheaf Laplacian to a structure akin to a Graph Isomorphism Network. Across five standard heterophilic node classification benchmarks, ISN achieves performance statistically indistinguishable from various SNN variants. Furthermore, using a normalized Rayleigh quotient to measure oversmoothing in the sheaf space, the study finds that trained ISNs do not oversmooth more than SNNs. This contradicts the theoretical diffusion-based argument that non-identity maps are needed to control the kernel of the sheaf Laplacian. The authors suggest the benchmark graphs possess a "good" heterophily character, where standard message-passing suffices, implying the learned maps may be unnecessary on these datasets and the theoretical link requires re-evaluation.

## Method Summary
The work conducts an empirical ablation study comparing SNNs (with learnable restriction maps defined by MLPs on node pairs) against the proposed ISN baseline (with restriction maps fixed as identity matrices). All models use a shared residual GNN-like propagation scheme based on the sheaf Laplacian. Models are trained and evaluated on five heterophilic graph datasets (Texas, Wisconsin, Squirrel, Chameleon, Cornell). Performance is measured by node classification accuracy. To assess oversmoothing, the normalized Rayleigh quotient (trace of representation times Laplacian divided by trace of representation squared) is computed layer-wise for both the sheaf Laplacian and the standard graph Laplacian on the trained models' node representations. The heterophily character of each dataset is quantified using the gain metric from Wang et al. (2024).

## Key Results
- The ISN baseline achieves accuracy comparable to SNN variants across all five heterophilic benchmarks, with no statistically significant performance gap.
- The Rayleigh quotient analysis shows that for both SNNs and ISNs, the sheaf-space smoothing measure is not consistently lower than the standard graph-space measure, contradicting the theoretical prediction that learned maps reduce smoothing in the sheaf space.
- All five benchmark datasets are classified as having "good" heterophily according to the Wang et al. (2024) measure, suggesting the datasets may not require the edge-aware transformations provided by learned restriction maps.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Performance parity between ISN and SNN suggests learnable restriction maps are empirically unnecessary for oversmoothing mitigation on these specific heterophilic graphs.
- **Mechanism:** ISN's identity restriction maps reduce the sheaf Laplacian to a form equivalent to a GIN with specific weighting. The paper posits that other architectural features (residual connections, normalization) or the intrinsic "good" heterophily of the benchmarks may be sufficient to prevent severe oversmoothing, making learned map complexity redundant.
- **Core assumption:** The selected benchmarks are representative of the target scenarios for sheaf learning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Comparable performance across five heterophilic benchmarks.
  - [section 3, Table 1] Quantitative results show ISN within one standard deviation of most SNN variants on all datasets.
- **Break condition:** Significant ISN performance degradation on a more diverse set of heterophilic graphs, especially with "bad" or "mixed" heterophily.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The empirical Rayleigh quotient trends do not support the theoretical diffusion-based motivation that non-identity maps control the sheaf Laplacian kernel to prevent constant representations.
- **Mechanism:** The hypothesis that `R_ΔF` (sheaf-space quotient) would be lower than `R_ΔI` (graph-space quotient) for SNNs is tested. Observed trends for both SNNs and ISNs are similar and do not show this systematic pattern, indicating the trained dynamics diverge from the theory.
- **Core assumption:** The normalized Rayleigh quotient is a valid, architecture-normalized proxy for oversmoothing.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "behavior predicted by the diffusion-based analysis... is not reflected empirically."
  - [section 5, Figure 1] Plots show `R_ΔF` (red) generally not lower than `R_ΔI` (blue) for SNNs; ISN trends are similar.
- **Break condition:** Consistent, significant difference in Rayleigh quotient trends between ISNs and SNNs aligning with original theory across multiple seeds.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** ISN's success can be explained by the "good" heterophily of the benchmark datasets, where adjacent node label distributions are discriminative enough for standard GNNs to work.
- **Mechanism:** Applying Wang et al.'s heterophily gain measure, all datasets have min_gain > 0.2, classifying them as "good". This property may reduce the need for directional, edge-aware information flow from learned restriction maps.
- **Core assumption:** Wang et al.'s gain metric accurately predicts when sheaf learning is essential.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Relates observation to Wang et al. (2024) heterophily characterization.
  - [section 4, Table 2] Lists gain values and "Good" classification for all datasets.
- **Break condition:** ISN performance drops significantly on datasets classified as "bad" or "mixed" while SNNs maintain performance.

  - **Why needed here:** The core motivation is oversmoothing on heterophilic graphs. The quantitative gain measure from Wang et al. directly explains the ISN's empirical success and problem scope.
  - **Quick check question:** How does Wang et al. (2024) quantitatively distinguish "good" from "bad" heterophily, and why is this distinction critical for interpreting the paper's results?
- **Concept: Oversmoothing & Dirichlet Energy**
  - **Why needed here:** Oversmoothing is the addressed problem. Theoretical link is via Dirichlet Energy (`xᵀΔx`) minimization under diffusion, extended to the sheaf space. Rayleigh quotient is a normalized version.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the mathematical relationship between the Dirichlet Energy of a graph signal and the concept of oversmoothing, and how is it modified for a sheaf?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Graph G, Node Features X -> Sheaf Construction (ISN: F=I; SNN: F=MLP) -> Sheaf Laplacian Δ_F -> Update Rule: X^(l+1) = X^(l) - σ(Δ_F (I ⊗ W₁) X^(l) W₂) -> Output Representations
- **Critical path:** (a) Implement ISN (fixed identity maps) and SNN (learnable maps) using same backbone. (b) Train both on five heterophilic benchmarks with identical hyperparameter protocols (or use provided configs). (c) Evaluate test accuracy and compute layer-wise Rayleigh quotient `R_Δ(X) = trace(XᵀΔX)/trace(XᵀX)` for Δ_F and Δ_I on trained models.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **ISN:** Minimal parameters (no restriction map learning), simpler, faster, comparable performance on "good" heterophily. Lower expressive capacity for edge-specific transformations.
  - **SNN:** High parameter count from restriction maps, theoretically more expressive for edge-aware routing. May overfit or be unnecessary on simpler heterophilic patterns.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **ISN underperformance:** Accuracy drops >1 std dev on datasets with "bad"/"mixed" heterophily per Wang et al.
  - **Misaligned theory:** Rayleigh quotient shows `R_ΔF` consistently and significantly lower than `R_ΔI` for SNNs.
  - **Reproducibility failure:** Cannot match published SNN results on benchmarks (e.g., Film dataset issues in Appendix).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline replication:** Train ISN and SNN (Bodnar et al. code/configs) on Texas, Wisconsin, Squirrel, Chameleon, Cornell. Report mean ± std accuracy. Verify ISN ≈ SNN.
  2.  **Heterophily correlation:** Compute Wang et al.'s gain metric for all datasets. Correlate performance gap (SNN - ISN) with "min gain". Expect no/positive correlation (good heterophily -> small gap).
  3.  **Oversmoothing quantification:** For best trained models, compute average `R_ΔF` and `R_ΔI` per layer across test nodes. Plot trends. Confirm `R_ΔF` not systematically lower than `R_ΔI` for SNNs; ISN trends similar.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Are learnable restriction maps necessary for SNNs to mitigate oversmoothing on heterophilous graphs?
  - **Basis:** [explicit] Abstract asks whether additional complexity of learning restriction maps is necessary.
  - **Why unresolved:** ISN matches SNN on five "good" heterophily benchmarks. Unknown if learnable maps help on "bad"/"mixed" heterophily graphs.
  - **Resolving evidence:** Evaluate ISN/SNNs on diverse graphs spanning heterophily types, measuring accuracy and oversmoothing.
- **Open Question 2:** Does the diffusion-based theoretical framework accurately predict oversmoothing in trained SNNs?
  - **Basis:** [explicit] Test Hypothesis 5.1 and find contradictions; theory not supported empirically.
  - **Why unresolved:** Empirical Rayleigh quotients show ISNs do not oversmooth more than SNNs, contrary to prediction that non-identity maps prevent constant convergence. Link between sheaf Laplacian kernel and oversmoothing unvalidated.
  - **Resolving evidence:** Measure Rayleigh quotients for various SNNs on multiple datasets, compare with theory. Investigate role of residual connections/normalization for alternative explanation.
- **Open Question 3:** Do results generalize to other sheaf-based architectures and additional heterophilic benchmarks?
  - **Basis:** [explicit] Future work: evaluate remaining datasets (Ribeiro et al., Fiorini et al.); analyze Bamberger et al. (2025) approach.
  - **Why unresolved:** Study covers only five benchmarks and specific SNN variants. Other sheaf constructions or datasets might show different patterns.
  - **Resolving evidence:** Replicate ISN on Ribeiro/Fiorini datasets; implement Bamberger et al. with identity sheaf. Compare performance/oversmoothing.

## Limitations
- Findings rely on five specific heterophilic benchmarks, all classified as having "good" heterophily; generalizability to other heterophily types or topological patterns is unproven.
- The normalized Rayleigh quotient's sensitivity as a cross-architecture oversmoothing proxy and the precise computation details (pre/post-activation) involve some uncertainty.
- SNN hyperparameters are inherited from prior work (Bodnar et al.), and exact replication details (splits, optimizer settings) are not fully specified, introducing potential reproducibility variance.

## Confidence
- **Performance Parity Claim:** ISN matches SNN accuracy on standard benchmarks. **Confidence: Medium.** Strong Table 1 support but depends on exact replication of hidden SNN settings and is limited to five "good" heterophily graphs.
- **Oversmoothing Discrepancy Claim:** Trained models do not exhibit predicted lower Rayleigh quotient in sheaf space. **Confidence: Medium.** Figure 1 evidence, but reduced by unknowns in quotient computation and normalization robustness.
- **Heterophily Characterization Claim:** Success due to datasets' "good" heterophily. **Confidence: Medium.** Measure is well-founded and applied consistently, but claim is contingent on all benchmarks falling in one category; needs validation on "bad"/"mixed" graphs.
