# A Role-Based LLM Framework for Structured Information Extraction from Healthy Food Policies

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2604.01529
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01529
- **Reference Count:** 25
- **Primary Result:** A role-based prompting framework using three specialized LLM agents with embedded domain definitions and step-by-step reasoning significantly outperforms zero-shot, few-shot, and chain-of-thought baselines in extracting structured data from heterogeneous healthy food policy documents.

## Executive Summary
Extracting structured information from diverse policy documents is challenging for large language models (LLMs) due to hallucinations and misclassifications. This study introduces a framework that decomposes the extraction task into three specialized roles—Policy Analyst, Legal Strategy Specialist, and Food System Expert—each with tailored prompts containing explicit domain definitions and mandated step-by-step reasoning. Evaluated on 608 healthy food policies using Llama-3.3-70B, the framework achieves substantial improvements in accuracy for legal strategies and food system stages compared to standard prompting baselines, demonstrating the value of role-based decomposition and schema-grounded prompt engineering.

## Method Summary
The proposed method partitions the extraction task into three sequential subtasks, each handled by a dedicated LLM agent. The Policy Analyst extracts metadata (state, effect year, policy type). The Legal Strategy Specialist identifies up to two applicable legal strategies from a taxonomy of seven. The Food System Expert classifies coverage across six binary food system stages. Each role prompt embeds detailed definitions for every label, explicit decision criteria, and an instruction to reason step-by-step before outputting structured JSON. The pipeline processes each policy’s title and summary in sequence, aggregating results into a single structured record. Performance is measured against gold-standard annotations using exact-match, partial-match, and per-category accuracy metrics.

## Key Results
- Legal strategy exact-match accuracy reached 44.16% vs. 27.24% for the best baseline; partial-match accuracy was 96.45% vs. ~61%.
- For the “Get” food category, accuracy improved to 63.13% from 36.01% (zero-shot).
- The percentage of policies with all six food categories correctly predicted increased to 28.78% under the proposed framework.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Task decomposition into specialized analytical roles reduces cognitive load and contextual ambiguity, improving accuracy on complex, multi-dimensional extraction.
- **Mechanism:** Isolating distinct extraction goals (metadata, legal strategies, food system stages) into separate prompts confines each role to a narrower semantic subspace, preventing interference from disparate reasoning modes required by different attributes.
- **Core Assumption:** An LLM’s performance improves when a complex task is partitioned into subtasks with homogeneous reasoning requirements, each supported by a domain-specific prompt.
- **Evidence Anchors:** The framework assigns three specialized agents and mimics expert workflows; each role-specific prompt follows a structured format with clear expertise specification.
- **Break Condition:** If information for a single attribute is heavily distributed across disjoint text segments requiring cross-role context, the isolated role approach may fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Embedding explicit, structured domain definitions and decision criteria into prompts provides a constrained, shared taxonomy that reduces label ambiguity and hallucination.
- **Mechanism:** Prompts include exhaustive definitions (e.g., for “Executive Order,” “Creates an incentive,” or the “Get” category) and precise inclusion/exclusion rules, grounding the LLM’s knowledge to the specific operational definitions of the annotation schema.
- **Core Assumption:** Hallucinations in niche domains often stem from a mismatch between the model’s general knowledge and the specialized, sometimes counter-intuitive, definitions of the target schema; providing the schema’s logic explicitly mitigates this.
- **Evidence Anchors:** The framework incorporates structured domain knowledge; prompt figures show multi-sentence definitions preceding extraction tasks.
- **Break Condition:** If policy content falls into a genuine edge case not covered by the provided definitions, the model may force-fit it to an incorrect category or hallucinate a new one.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Mandating step-by-step reasoning forces the model to make its inference process explicit and sequential, improving consistency and reducing premature conclusions.
- **Mechanism:** The instruction “carefully reason step-by-step before extracting” compels the model to generate intermediate reasoning tokens before the final JSON output, creating a verifiable chain of thought that constrains the model to follow procedural logic aligned with prompt definitions.
- **Core Assumption:** Many LLM errors in complex classification stem from jumping directly to an answer based on superficial pattern matching; explicit reasoning steps prevent this.
- **Evidence Anchors:** The framework embeds step-by-step reasoning; all three role prompt examples include the phrase “carefully reason step-by-step before extracting.”
- **Break Condition:** For extremely short or unclear policy texts, step-by-step reasoning may become circular or verbose without converging on a correct answer, increasing computational cost without accuracy gain.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Domain Task Decomposition**
  - **Why needed here:** The extraction task combines factual lookup, multi-label classification, and binary multi-label classification. A single prompt must reason across these different logics, increasing error rates. Decomposition assigns each subtask to a specialist prompt.
  - **Quick Check:** What are the fundamental differences in reasoning between extracting a “policy type” and identifying “food system stages,” and why might a single prompt struggle with both?
- **Concept: Schema-Grounded Prompt Engineering**
  - **Why needed here:** The specialized annotation schema (7 legal strategies, 6 food categories) may not align with the LLM’s internal vocabulary. The prompt must override generic associations by providing the schema’s operational logic as a premise.
  - **Quick Check:** Why is providing a detailed definition like “Get: …affect people’s ability to obtain and consume healthy food…” more effective than just listing the label “Get”?
- **Concept: Multi-Label Classification with Abstention**
  - **Why needed here:** Legal strategies (0–2 applicable) and food stages (0–6 applicable) are multi-label problems with variable cardinality. The framework must instruct how to handle empty slots to avoid hallucination.
  - **Quick Check:** In a multi-label task with unknown label count, what are two risks of not explicitly telling the model how to represent “no label applies”?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** Input Parser -> Dispatcher -> Policy Analyst -> Legal Strategy Specialist -> Food System Expert -> Output Aggregator
- **Critical Path:** Input Text → Dispatcher → Policy Analyst → Legal Strategy Specialist → Food System Expert → Aggregated JSON Output. Each module depends on the prior one’s completion;Dispatcher logic is central control.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - Modularity vs. Latency: Three separate LLM calls increase time/cost versus a monolithic call but improve per-attribute accuracy and debuggability.
  - Prompt Complexity vs. Maintainability: Long, definition-heavy prompts improve accuracy but are harder to update; schema changes require careful template edits.
  - Strictness vs. Recall: Restrictive definitions reduce false positives but may increase false negatives for borderline cases (e.g., “Get” accuracy ~63%).
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - High exact-match error on Legal Strategies (>50%): Prompt definitions insufficiently discriminative or model ignores “max two strategies” constraint.
  - Consistently low partial-match (~60%) on Legal Strategies: Model frequently omits applicable strategies, possibly due to inadequate step-by-step scrutiny.
  - Bimodal distribution on food categories: Model performs perfectly on some policies and poorly on others, indicating failure on genuinely ambiguous documents.
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. **Role Isolation Test:** Run each role (Analyst, Specialist, Expert) alone on the full dataset and compare its subtask accuracy to its performance within the full pipeline.
  2. **Ablation of Definitions:** Create a “Role-Only” variant removing all detailed category definitions; compare to full framework to test necessity of embedded domain knowledge.
  3. **Baseline Equivalence Test:** Replicate exact Zero-Shot, Few-Shot, and CoT baseline prompts on the same 608-policy test set using Llama-3.3-70B to verify reported performance gaps.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) be integrated into the Legal Strategy Specialist role to improve disambiguation between adjacent legal strategies (e.g., “creates an incentive” vs. “expressly allows something”)?
- **Open Question 2:** To what extent does the role-based framework generalize to other policy domains (e.g., environmental, housing), and what adaptations are necessary?
- **Open Question 3:** Can uncertainty-aware abstention mechanisms be designed for the role-based agents to improve reliability by withholding predictions when evidence is insufficient, and does this trade recall for precision beneficially?
- **Open Question 4:** What specific linguistic or structural features cause the “Distribute” food system category to remain a consistent performance bottleneck (~70% accuracy) across all methods, and can prompt engineering overcome this?
- **Open Question 5:** What explains the bimodal distribution of correct food category predictions for Llama-3.3 (23.19% with all 6 correct, 28.29% with 0 correct), and can this be made more consistent?

## Limitations
- Train/test split and few-shot examples not disclosed, blocking exact baseline replication.
- Evaluation limited to two LLMs (Llama‑3.3‑70B, Qwen‑3‑80B); performance on other models unknown.
- Inputs are title+summary only; full policy text may contain crucial details.
- Findings may not generalize beyond healthy food policies.
- “Get” category accuracy (63%) shows definitions remain insufficient for ambiguous cases.

## Confidence
- **Framework outperforms baselines:** High
- **Role decomposition & definition grounding effectiveness:** Medium
- **Generalization across models/domains:** Low
