# Attention Sinks Are Provably Necessary in Softmax Transformers: Evidence from Trigger-Conditional Tasks

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11487  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11487  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** For softmax attention transformers solving a trigger‑conditional averaging task, achieving vanishing ℓ∞ error provably requires placing near‑unity attention on a fixed token (BOS) at all non‑trigger positions; this necessity does not extend to ReLU attention models.

## Executive Summary

This paper investigates whether attention sinks—the phenomenon where transformer heads strongly attend to the beginning‑of‑sequence (BOS) token—are an optimization artifact or a functional necessity. Focusing on softmax attention, the authors introduce a synthetic trigger‑conditional task: at a designated trigger position, output the mean of all preceding non‑BOS tokens; elsewhere output zero. They prove theoretically that any single‑layer softmax model achieving vanishing ℓ∞ error must place near‑unit attention on BOS at every non‑trigger position. This necessity extends to multi‑layer models, where at least one layer must exhibit such sink behavior. In contrast, they show that a ReLU‑based attention mechanism (without normalization) can solve the same task perfectly without any sink, via an explicit parameter construction. Experiments on single‑ and multi‑layer architectures confirm these predictions: softmax models develop strong BOS sinks while ReLU models avoid them, both achieving low loss. The results demonstrate that the softmax normalization constraint fundamentally drives attention sink formation for trigger‑conditional computations.

## Method Summary

The study combines theoretical analysis with controlled experiments on a synthetic task. The task defines sequences containing a BOS token, a trigger indicator (1 at a random position, 0 elsewhere), and continuous content tokens. The target output is zero at all positions except the trigger, where it equals the mean of preceding non‑BOS tokens. Theoretically, the authors analyze a single‑layer softmax attention model and prove that to achieve arbitrarily low ℓ∞ error, attention weights at every non‑trigger position must concentrate almost entirely on BOS; otherwise, continuous input variations force the value projection to produce non‑zero outputs at those positions. They extend this to multi‑layer models by considering effective attention coefficients, showing that at least one layer must sink. For ReLU attention—where scores are passed through ReLU and scaled by the number of previous tokens—they construct explicit parameters that yield zero BOS attention and perfect task performance. Empirically, they train both softmax and ReLU models (single‑layer and 4‑layer 4‑head) on sequences of length 16, measuring attention on BOS at non‑trigger positions and task loss.

## Key Results

- **Theoretical necessity:** Single‑layer softmax models with vanishing ℓ∞ error must place near‑unity attention on BOS at all non‑trigger positions (Theorem 1). Multi‑layer softmax models must have at least one layer that exhibits such sink behavior at some non‑trigger position (Theorem 2).
- **Constructive alternative:** ReLU attention can solve the task with zero attention on BOS at all positions (Theorem 3).
- **Empirical confirmation:** Softmax models develop strong BOS sinks (mean αᵢ,₁ ≥ 0.95 at non‑trigger positions) across single and multi‑layer settings, while ReLU models maintain near‑zero BOS attention; both families achieve comparable low task loss.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** In single‑layer softmax attention models solving the trigger‑conditional task, achieving vanishing ℓ∞ error necessarily places near‑unity attention on a fixed token (BOS) at all non‑trigger positions.
- **Mechanism:** The softmax normalization constraint (weights sum to 1 over all previous tokens) forces the model to concentrate probability mass on a stable, content‑agnostic anchor (BOS) whenever it must output a default zero vector. At non‑trigger positions, the target is zero, so the weighted sum of values must be zero. If attention were distributed over content‑varying tokens, their value projections would need to cancel exactly for all inputs—impossible with continuous content. Instead, the model collapses attention onto BOS, whose value projection can be driven to zero (via the value map), achieving the zero output without sensitive cancellation.
- **Core assumption:** The content coordinates are drawn from a continuous distribution, making exact cancellation across varying inputs statistically impossible unless attention is concentrated on a fixed token whose value is constant (or driven to zero).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - abstract: "normalization over a probability simplex must force attention to collapse onto a stable anchor to realize a default state"  
  - section 3.5: Theorem 1 and its proof (pages 5‑6) formally show that if a non‑trigger position does not place near‑unit attention on BOS, the value map must crush a positive‑probability set of tokens, leading to a contradiction at the trigger position where the target is sensitive.  
  - corpus: While neighboring papers (e.g., Barbero et al. 2025; Guo et al. 2024) empirically observe sink behavior, this paper provides the first provable necessity result for softmax in a specific computational setting.
- **Break condition:** If a softmax model achieves low ℓ∞ error on the task without placing ≥ 1‑ε attention on BOS at some non‑trigger position, Theorem 1 is contradicted. This would require either discontinuous value mappings or a non‑continuous input distribution, violating the setup.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** In multi‑layer softmax attention models solving the trigger‑conditional task, at least one layer must exhibit sink behavior (near‑unit attention on BOS) at some non‑trigger position.
- **Mechanism:** The necessity propagates through layers via the unrolled effective attention coefficients (β coefficients). If no layer places high attention on BOS, then at some pre‑trigger position (e.g., position 2), the effective coefficient on the non‑BOS token (β₂,₂) remains bounded away from zero across all layers. This forces the combined value map to crush that token’s representation to near‑zero (to achieve zero output at pre‑trigger positions). With continuous content, this leads to the same contradiction as in the single‑layer case when considering the trigger position.
- **Core assumption:** The task’s target outputs zero at all non‑trigger positions, and the model must achieve uniformly low ℓ∞ error across all sequence lengths and trigger positions.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - abstract: "for multi‑layer models… at least one layer must exhibit such sink behavior"  
  - section 3.5: Theorem 2 and its proof (pages 6‑7) extend the single‑layer argument by considering the product of per‑layer attention weights and using Lemma 8‑11 to bound combined value projections.  
  - section 4.2: Experimentally, 4‑layer 4‑head softmax models show sink behavior in at least one head per layer, consistent with the existential guarantee.
- **Break condition:** If a multi‑layer softmax model achieves low loss and yet every layer has αᵢ,₁ < 1‑ε for all non‑trigger positions i, Theorem 2 fails. This would likely require the model to use non‑causal attention or violate the bounded density assumption.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** ReLU attention (without normalization) can solve the trigger‑conditional task perfectly with zero attention on BOS at all positions.
- **Mechanism:** ReLU attention eliminates the simplex constraint: scores are passed through ReLU and scaled by the number of previous tokens (excluding BOS). The model can set query/key weights so that scores are zero for all non‑trigger positions (since trigger indicator = 0 there), making all attention weights zero. At the trigger position, scores become positive for all preceding non‑BOS tokens, yielding uniform positive weights that compute the mean exactly after scaling.
- **Core assumption:** The ReLU scaling is chosen as 1/(number of previous non‑BOS tokens) to enable averaging; alternatively, the task could be redefined as summing.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - abstract: "ReLU attention… solves the same task without any sink"  
  - section F: Theorem 3 provides an explicit construction: WQ = e₂(e₂+e₃)⊤, WK=I, WV=I, WO=I yields zero attention on BOS everywhere and perfect computation at the trigger.  
  - section 4: Experiments (figures 3c,d, 5, 8) show ReLU models achieve low task loss with near‑zero BOS attention.
- **Break condition:** If a ReLU model fails to achieve zero loss or develops high BOS attention, the construction may be degenerate (e.g., poor initialization) or the scaling may be mismatched. The theorem guarantees existence but not that gradient descent finds it.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Softmax attention and probability simplex constraint**  
  *Why needed here:* The core mechanism relies on the fact that softmax weights must sum to 1 over all previous tokens. This geometric constraint forces attention to collapse onto a single token when the desired output is zero across varying inputs.  
  *Quick check question:* Why can’t softmax assign small, distributed attention weights to multiple tokens to achieve zero output, given that their value projections might sometimes cancel?
- **Concept: Trigger‑conditional computation pattern**  
  *Why needed here:* The task defines a specific computational motif—output zero except at a trigger position where an aggregation (mean) of past tokens is required. Understanding this pattern is essential to see why a default state (no‑op) is needed.  
  *Quick check question:* How does the presence of a trigger indicator coordinate (coordinate 2) allow the model to condition its behavior, and why must non‑trigger positions output zero?
- **Concept: Value projection (WV, WO) and residual stream manipulation**  
  *Why needed here:* The necessity proofs hinge on bounding ∥Vx(i)∥₂ (where V = WO WV). The value map must map certain tokens (non‑trigger) to near‑zero vectors to achieve zero output when attention is not fully on BOS, leading to contradictions.  
  *Quick check question:* In the proof of Theorem 1, why does a lower bound on αᵢ,ₕ for some non‑BOS token h force ∥Vx(h)∥₂ to be small when the loss is low?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Input: sequence x¹,…,xᴸ ∈ ℝᴸ×ⁿ with indicator channels (BOS, trigger) and continuous content.  
  Query/Key: WQ, WK ∈ ℝⁿ×ⁿ compute scores sᵢ,ₖ = x⁽ⁱ⁾WQ (x⁽ᵏ⁾WK)ᵀ.  
  Attention weights: Softmax: αᵢ,ₖ = exp(sᵢ,ₖ)/∑_{ℓ≤i} exp(sᵢ,ℓ); ReLU: αᵢ,ₖ = ReLU(sᵢ,ₖ)/nᵢ where nᵢ = max{i‑1,1} (excluding BOS).  
  Value projection: WV ∈ ℝⁿ×ⁿ maps tokens to value vectors.  
  Output: f(x)⁽ⁱ⁾ = WO ∑_{k≤i} αᵢ,ₖ WV x⁽ᵏ⁾.  
  Loss: ℓ∞ over all positions and sequences.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Compute Q/K for all tokens → apply causal mask.  
  2. Compute attention weights (softmax or ReLU).  
  3. Compute weighted sum of WV‑projected tokens.  
  4. Apply WO to obtain output per position.  
  5. Compare output to target (zero except at trigger where target = mean of preceding non‑BOS tokens).  
  6. Compute ℓ∞ loss and backpropagate to update WQ, WK, WV, WO.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Softmax: Normalization guarantees well‑defined weights but introduces the sink necessity for trigger‑conditional no‑op behaviors.  
  - ReLU: Avoids sink by allowing zero weights, but loses probabilistic interpretation and may require careful scaling to compute averages.  
  - Multi‑head: Allows multiple computation pathways; Theorem 2 only requires at least one head/layer to sink, so other heads can be sink‑free.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Softmax model failing to develop a sink: Loss remains high (≫ η) on non‑trigger positions because output ≠ 0; attention distributed over content tokens cannot consistently produce zero due to varying content.  
  - ReLU model failing to aggregate at trigger: If ReLU weights are not properly aligned with trigger indicator, the model may not sufficiently attend to preceding tokens, causing high loss at trigger position.  
  - Multi‑layer softmax without any sink: Contradicts Theorem 2; likely indicates optimization failure or architectural deviation (e.g., non‑causal attention).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Single‑layer softmax sink formation:** Train on sequences with L=16, varying trigger positions. Measure mean αᵢ,₁ for all non‑trigger i. Expect near‑1 values with low variance across samples.  
  2. **ReLU sink avoidance:** Replace softmax with ReLU, same initialization/training. Expect αᵢ,₁ ≈ 0 for all i, and similar final loss.  
  3. **Multi‑layer existential sink:** Train a 4‑layer 4‑head softmax model. For each layer/head, compute max_{i≠j} αᵢ,₁ over test set. Expect at least one head in some layer to have near‑1 values, while others may be lower. For ReLU, all heads should remain near‑zero.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What broader class of tasks beyond the trigger‑conditional averaging task provably require attention sinks in softmax transformers?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state in the Limitations section: *"We leave the formal characterization of the full class of tasks necessitating sinks to future work."*  
- **Why unresolved:** Their theoretical analysis is restricted to one synthetic task; while they conjecture extension to similar tasks like key‑query retrieval, the necessary and sufficient conditions on task structure that force sinks remain unidentified.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A general theorem linking sink necessity to properties such as the need for a default zero output and trigger detection across a family of functions, verified on additional synthetic tasks and correlated with real‑world circuit analysis.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What determines the specific layers and heads that develop attention sinks in multi‑layer transformers?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Theorem 2 guarantees existence of a sink but not its location; the Limitations note: *"understanding exactly where sinks emerge would likely require a dynamical analysis… which we leave to future work."*  
- **Why unresolved:** Empirically, sinks appear in some heads/layers but not others, yet the theoretical principles guiding this selection (e.g., depth, initialization, optimization) are not understood.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretical framework that predicts sink placement based on architecture and training dynamics, or large‑scale empirical mapping of sink emergence across diverse models with controlled variables.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do other special tokens (e.g., `<|think|>`) and secondary attention sinks also arise necessarily from softmax normalization?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations propose: *"it would be interesting to investigate whether other special tokens… exhibit similar sink behavior, and to investigate the relatively newly discovered phenomenon of 'secondary attention sinks'."*  
- **Why unresolved:** The analysis focuses exclusively on BOS tokens as sinks; the necessity of sinks for other tokens and the mechanisms behind multiple sinks (secondary) are unexplored.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extensions of the necessity proof to arbitrary stable tokens, and empirical/theoretical study of when and why multiple attention sinks form within single heads or across layers.

## Limitations

- **Task specificity:** The provable necessity is established for a highly specific synthetic task (computing a conditional mean). It remains open whether the exact sink structure is necessary for more complex, real‑world trigger‑conditional computations or for natural language modeling where "default" outputs are less clearly zero.
- **Assumption sensitivity:** The theorems assume a continuous input distribution and bounded value map projections; disruptions (e.g., discontinuities, extreme norms) might circumvent necessity, though such solutions may be unstable under standard training.
- **Architectural scope:** Results assume standard decoder‑only, causal, single‑head‑per‑layer softmax attention. The interplay with multi‑head attention, layer norms, RoPE, or modern variants remains unaddressed.

## Confidence

- Single‑layer softmax sink necessity (Theorem 1): **High**
- Multi‑layer softmax existential sink (Theorem 2): **Medium‑High**
- ReLU explicit construction (exists parameters solving task without BOS attention): **High**
- ReLU solution discovered by gradient descent in practice: **Medium**

## Next Checks

1. **Complexify Trigger Computation:** Test if the sink necessity persists for trigger‑conditional tasks requiring a non‑linear aggregation (e.g., output `max` or a small neural network of preceding tokens at the trigger) instead of a linear mean.
2. **Natural Language Pilot:** Train a small softmax transformer (e.g., 4‑layer, 4‑head) on a next‑token prediction task with a clearly defined BOS token and analyze attention patterns at positions *preceding* a synthetically inserted trigger token (e.g., a rare word that cues a specific in‑context retrieval). Measure whether heads that consistently attend to BOS at non‑trigger positions are *causally necessary* for correct prediction at the trigger position via ablation (zeroing their output).
3. **Alternative Normalization Ablation:** Replace softmax with a constrained normalization that does not enforce a probability simplex (e.g., `αᵢ,ₖ = positive(sᵢ,ₖ) / (∑ positive(sᵢ,ℓ) + ε)` with a small learned `ε`, or use `αᵢ,ₖ = softmax(sᵢ,ₖ).pow(γ)` for `γ≠1`). Train on the trigger‑conditional task. If models learn to avoid strong BOS sinks while maintaining low loss, it would directly challenge the claim that the *simplex constraint* is the fundamental driver.
