# Koopman-based surrogate modeling for reinforcement-learning-control of Rayleigh-Benard convection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.28074  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28074  
- Reference count: 31  
- Primary result: Pretraining RL agents on Koopman-based surrogates reduces total training time by >40% while achieving near‑DNS control performance (mean Nusselt number Nu≈2.75 vs DNS‑only Nu=2.74).  

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the use of Koopman‑based Linear Recurrent Autoencoder Networks (LRANs) as surrogate models to accelerate reinforcement learning (RL) control of two‑dimensional Rayleigh‑Bénard convection at Rayleigh number Ra=10⁴. Direct numerical simulations (DNS) with Oceananigans.jl are computationally expensive; the LRAN surrogate provides fast state predictions. Two surrogate training strategies are evaluated: (i) a random‑action model trained on a static dataset of 3300 random episodes, and (ii) a policy‑aware iterative scheme (model‑based policy optimization style) that adapts the surrogate to the state distribution induced by the current policy. Surrogate‑only RL training yields inferior control (best mean Nu=2.97) compared to DNS‑only (Nu=2.74). However, a simple pretraining approach—first training the agent on the surrogate and then finetuning on DNS—recovers state‑of‑the‑art performance (mean Nu≈2.75) with >40% reduction in total training time. The policy‑aware surrogate specifically improves prediction accuracy in policy‑relevant regions, albeit with a risk of overfitting to the current policy.

## Method Summary
The control task uses 12 thermal actuators to adjust temperature and minimize the Nusselt number (Nu). The state comprises temperature and velocity fields on a 96×64 grid. The LRAN surrogate employs a convolutional encoder to map high‑dimensional states to a 200‑dimensional latent space where dynamics are linear (a Koopman approximation). The latent vector is decoded back to the physical grid. Training uses sequence length T=10 with a discounted loss (δ=0.9) and data augmentation (horizontal translations, vertical reflections). PPO with an MLP policy interacts with the environment. Two regimes are tested: (1) random‑action surrogate trained on precomputed random trajectories, and (2) policy‑aware surrogate that is iteratively updated using data from the current policy (similar to MBPO). Pretraining runs PPO on the surrogate for a fixed interaction budget (e.g., 120k for random‑action, 400k for policy‑aware) and then switches to DNS for the remaining steps (280k or 200k respectively). Performance is measured as mean Nu over 20 DNS test episodes and total training time.

## Key Results
- Surrogate‑only RL control achieves best mean Nu=2.97, worse than the DNS‑only baseline (2.74).  
- Pretraining on a surrogate followed by DNS finetuning attains near‑DNS performance (mean Nu≈2.75) while cutting total training time by >40%.  
- Policy‑aware iterative training reduces distribution shift; the surrogate exhibits higher accuracy on policy‑relevant states (e.g., two‑cell flow configurations) than the random‑action surrogate.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Koopman‑based LRAN surrogate accelerates RL by replacing expensive DNS with fast linear rollouts in a learned latent space. Pretraining allows the policy to learn effective actions on the cheap model, discovering good strategies early. Finetuning on DNS then corrects model errors and adapts the policy to the true dynamics. The policy‑aware scheme further minimizes distribution shift by continuously adapting the surrogate to the state distribution visited by the current policy, ensuring that model errors remain small precisely where the policy operates. Together, these techniques yield substantial computational savings without sacrificing control performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Koopman operator**: Linear operator describing evolution of observables in a nonlinear system; justifies linear latent dynamics. Quick check: The Koopman matrix in the LRAN governs latent transitions.
- **Linear Recurrent Autoencoder Network (LRAN)**: Neural network that encodes states to a latent space with linear dynamics and decodes back. Quick check: The decoder reconstructs physical fields from latent vectors.
- **Rayleigh‑Bénard convection (RBC)**: Fluid layer heated from below, forming convective rolls; the control testbed. Quick check: Nusselt number quantifies convective heat transport.
- **Nusselt number (Nu)**: Ratio of total to conductive heat transport; the minimization objective. Quick check: For Ra=10⁴, uncontrolled Nu is around 3.0–3.5.
- **Reinforcement Learning (PPO)**: Proximal Policy Optimization, a stable on‑policy algorithm. Quick check: PPO uses a clipped surrogate objective.
- **Surrogate modeling**: Learning a fast approximation of the true environment. Quick check: Surrogate predictions are orders of magnitude faster than DNS.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
InitialStates -> RandomPolicy -> DNS_sim -> RawTrajectories -> Augmentation -> TrainingSet -> LRAN (Encoder -> LinearLatent -> Decoder) -> SurrogateEnv -> PPO_Agent -> Policy  
Policy‑aware loop: Policy -> (SurrogateEnv) ImaginedTrajectories + (DNS_sim) RealTrajectories -> NewData -> Retrain_LRAN (within budget)  
Pretraining switch: PPO_Agent (on SurrogateEnv) for K steps -> switch -> PPO_Agent (on DNS_sim) for remaining steps  

**Critical path**  
1. Collect random‑action data via DNS.  
2. Train LRAN surrogate.  
3. Train PPO policy on surrogate (pretraining).  
4. Finetune policy on DNS.  
*Policy‑aware variant inserts iterative surrogate retraining using policy‑collected real data during pretraining.*

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Surrogate speed vs. accuracy: LRAN is fast but may miss fine nonlinearities, especially in out‑of‑distribution states.  
- Random‑action vs. policy‑aware data: random covers diverse states; policy‑aware focuses on relevant states but risks overfitting.  
- Data augmentation: improves sample efficiency but may break physical constraints if transformations misalign actuators.  
- Pretraining vs. end‑to‑end: pretraining yields early progress but model errors can mislead; finetuning recovers at DNS cost.

**Failure signatures**  
- Surrogate error spikes on two‑cell configurations, indicating distribution shift.  
- Policy performance plateaus when trained solely on surrogate.  
- Policy‑aware retraining increases error on held‑out random‑action data (overfitting).  
- PPO produces NaNs or unstable returns, often due to surrogate non‑determinism or reward scaling.

**First three experiments**  
1. Train an LRAN on the random‑action dataset (with/without augmentation) and report one‑step L2 error on a held‑out test set; establish baseline accuracy.  
2. Execute pretraining: PPO on random‑action surrogate for 120k interactions, then DNS for 280k; compare final mean Nu and total wall‑time to DNS‑only (400k).  
3. Implement the policy‑aware scheme: after every 50k surrogate interactions, collect 1k steps from the current policy on DNS, augment the data, and retrain the LRAN; evaluate prediction error separately on two‑cell and four‑cell states to confirm reduced distribution shift.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**  
*Can LRANs generalize to higher Rayleigh numbers (Ra > 10⁴) and to other fluid systems?*  
Basis: Explicitly stated in the conclusion.  
Why unresolved: Study limited to 2D RBC at Ra=10⁴; Koopman spectra may change with increased turbulence.  
Evidence needed: Train/evaluate LRANs on data from higher Ra (e.g., 10⁵, 10⁶) or other PDEs (channel flow, Kuramoto‑Sivashinsky); success measured by prediction error and ability to train effective RL controllers.

**Open Question 2**  
*How can policy‑aware surrogate training avoid overfitting to the current policy while still adapting to policy‑relevant distributions?*  
Basis: Explicitly noted; policy‑aware surrogate overfits to optimized actions, failing on early four‑cell states.  
Why unresolved: Continuous addition of latest policy data gradually shifts distribution, causing forgetting of less‑visited states.  
Evidence needed: Algorithms that maintain diverse coverage (e.g., mixture of random and policy data, prioritized replay, ensembles). Evaluate by tracking surrogate error across state clusters and RL stability.

**Open Question 3**  
*What is the optimal allocation between surrogate pretraining and DNS finetuning to minimize computational cost for a target performance?*  
Basis: Inferred from the pretraining experiments; the 400k/200k split was heuristic.  
Why unresolved: Optimal split depends on surrogate error growth and policy learning curve; without systematic exploration, efficiency gains may be suboptimal.  
Evidence needed: Sweep over pretraining/finetuning step ratios, possibly with early stopping based on surrogate validation error; derive trade‑off curves between DNS‑equivalent time and achieved Nu.

**Open Question 4**  
*How to formally quantify the distribution shift between policy‑induced state probabilities and surrogate training data, and how does this metric correlate with surrogate error and policy degradation?*  
Basis: Inferred; paper discusses shift qualitatively (Fig. 4) but provides no quantitative metric.  
Why unresolved: Without a measurable definition, it is hard to diagnose unreliability or compare strategies.  
Evidence needed: Define a distributional distance (e.g., MMD, Wasserstein) between policy state distribution and training data; track during RL; correlate with surrogate error on policy states and performance drop when using surrogate alone.

## Limitations
- Key DNS parameters (Prandtl number, time step, exact boundary implementation) are unspecified, risking physical regime mismatches.  
- Action mapping in Eq. (1) contains ambiguous clipping/scaling logic; horizontal data augmentation may misalign fixed actuators if not implemented as wrap‑around.  
- Missing PPO hyperparameters (batch size, gradient steps) may affect training stability and final performance.

## Confidence
- **High**: Pretraining reduces training time by >40% while achieving Nu≈2.75 (near DNS‑only) – well‑supported by reported metrics.  
- **Medium**: Policy‑aware training improves surrogate accuracy in policy‑relevant states – plausible but depends on unverified implementation details.  
- **Low**: Exact reproducibility of surrogate‑only Nu=2.97 and full policy‑aware results – due to missing DNS/PPO specifics and potential distribution‑shift handling differences.
