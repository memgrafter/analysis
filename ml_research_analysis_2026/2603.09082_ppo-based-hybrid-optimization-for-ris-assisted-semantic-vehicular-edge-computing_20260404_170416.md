# PPO-Based Hybrid Optimization for RIS-Assisted Semantic Vehicular Edge Computing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09082  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09082  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A PPO-based hybrid scheme reduces average end-to-end latency by 40–50% compared to Genetic Algorithm (GA) and Quantum-behaved Particle Swarm Optimization (QPSO) in an RIS-assisted semantic vehicular edge computing system.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of minimizing end-to-end latency for latency-sensitive Internet of Vehicles (IoV) applications in dynamic environments with intermittent connectivity. It introduces an Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS)-aided semantic-aware vehicular edge computing (VEC) framework that jointly optimizes task offloading ratios, the number of semantic symbols, and RIS phase shifts. A two-tier hybrid solution is proposed: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) handles high-dimensional discrete decisions for RIS phase shifts and semantic symbol count, while a Linear Programming (LP) solver optimally determines continuous offloading ratios. Simulations demonstrate significant latency reductions and strong scalability, suggesting the approach’s effectiveness for real-time semantic offloading in mobile networks.

## Method Summary
The system model considers vehicles generating computational tasks that can be offloaded locally, to a Roadside Unit (RSU) via V2I, or to a neighboring Service Vehicle (SV) via V2V. Semantic communication reduces data size by extracting semantic symbols. The joint optimization of offloading ratios (continuous), semantic symbol count (discrete), and RIS phase shifts (discrete) to minimize total latency is a mixed-integer non-convex problem. The proposed solution decouples this into two layers: an upper layer uses PPO with continuous action space relaxation (actions later rounded to feasible discrete values) to search for near-optimal RIS configurations and symbol counts; a lower layer solves a convex LP (via epigraph formulation) to compute the optimal offloading ratios for the current discrete decisions. During training, the LP returns the minimal achievable latency as the reward for the PPO agent. This separation reduces PPO’s action dimensionality and ensures the continuous subproblem is solved optimally and efficiently.

## Key Results
- The PPO-based hybrid scheme reduces average system latency by approximately 40–50% compared to GA and QPSO baselines.
- The solution scales effectively, maintaining low latency even as the number of vehicles increases up to 30.
- The two-tier approach ensures stable convergence and dynamically compensates for task size fluctuations and channel variations via the LP layer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The performance gain stems from three design choices. First, decoupling the original joint problem into a discrete (PPO) and a continuous (LP) subproblem makes the optimization tractable and scalable; the LP subproblem is convex when discrete decisions are fixed, guaranteeing a globally optimal offloading solution per time slot. Second, PPO employs a continuous relaxation strategy: its Actor outputs continuous actions that are deterministically mapped to feasible discrete values for phase shifts and symbol counts, avoiding the training instability of high-dimensional discrete action spaces while respecting hardware constraints. Third, the LP layer provides instantaneous, optimal compensation for dynamic channel conditions and task sizes, feeding an accurate latency signal back to PPO. Evidence includes the formal problem reformulation from P1 to P2/P3 in Sections 4.1–4.2 and the two-tier scheme description in the abstract. Limitations arise if the LP subproblem becomes non-convex under extreme interference, or if rounding errors are significant when discrete feasible sets are sparse, potentially destabilizing PPO’s policy updates.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Environment Simulator → PPO Agent (Upper Layer) → LP Solver (Lower Layer) → Reward Calculator → back to Environment Simulator  

**Critical path:**  
At each time step t: (1) Environment provides state s_t (vehicle positions, AoAs, etc.); (2) PPO Actor outputs continuous action a_t; (3) Mapping functions convert a_t to discrete ν and Θ; (4) LP solver, given {ν, Θ} and current channel, computes optimal offloading ratios ρ and minimal latency T_sys; (5) Reward r_t = -T_sys returned to PPO; (6) Transition (s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}) stored; (7) Periodic minibatch updates to PPO networks using clipped objective (Eq 26).  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *PPO action space dimension:* Larger RIS or more vehicles explode discrete actions; continuous relaxation trades exactness for trainability.  
- *LP solve frequency:* Calling LP every step adds overhead but enables rapid reaction to channel dynamics.  
- *Assumption of optimal LP:* PPO’s policy gradient relies on LP providing true minimal T_sys; solver tolerances or non-convexities could corrupt rewards.  

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Reward stagnation/decreasing:* Indicates poor action mapping (Eqs 20–21), non-convex LP subproblem, or misconfigured PPO hyperparameters (e.g., clip ϵ).  
- *High variance in test delay:* Suggests PPO lacks robustness, possibly due to insufficient exploration (low entropy coefficient) or mismatched semantic model f(ν,γ).  
- *LP infeasibility:* Means current {ν, Θ} cannot satisfy constraints (C1, C2, C6–C8) for the given task sizes and channel, rendering the action invalid.  

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Convergence & reward validation:** Train PPO for E_max episodes; plot average cumulative reward per episode to confirm monotonic improvement and plateau (like Figure 3).  
2. **Ablation – power & density scaling:** Replicate Figures 4 and 7; vary transmit power (0.1–0.3 W) and vehicle count (5–30). Verify PPO’s latency remains below GA/QPSO and grows slower with density.  
3. **Discretization impact test:** For a fixed trained PPO model, compare performance using exact discrete {ν, Θ} (via Eqs 20–21) vs. raw continuous actions (ignoring hardware constraints). A large gap confirms rounding is necessary but lossy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Dynamic knowledge base updates:** How can a dynamic semantic knowledge base be updated in real-time to maintain consistency across highly mobile IoV environments without prohibitive overhead?  
   *Basis:* Paper admits static knowledge base is a limitation; future work needed on real-time updates and associated latency/overhead.  
   *What would resolve:* A prototype of a lightweight synchronization protocol (e.g., federated learning) measuring update latency and semantic drift.  

2. **Multi-RIS cooperative coverage:** Can multi-RIS cooperation extend benefits to wider urban areas, and what are optimal coordination strategies for joint phase shifts?  
   *Basis:* Future work notes investigating multi-RIS cooperative coverage.  
   *What would resolve:* Simulation/testbed results comparing single- vs. multi-RIS, showing latency reduction per added RIS and PPO convergence in larger action spaces.  

3. **Multimodal semantic tasks:** How does the framework perform under multimodal data (images, sensor fusion) beyond text, and does DeepSC generalization hold?  
   *Basis:* Paper explicitly focuses on text-based tasks via DeepSC; multimodal extensions unaddressed.  
   *What would resolve:* Adaptation with multimodal semantic encoders (e.g., vision-language models), measuring end-to-end latency for mixed modalities and optimal ν trade-offs.  

4. **Sensitivity to CSI imperfections:** What is the sensitivity to inaccurate/delayed channel state information (CSI) in high-mobility, and can robustness be improved?  
   *Basis:* Model assumes perfect CSI; no analysis of estimation error or feedback delay.  
   *What would resolve:* Injecting controlled CSI errors (noise on positions, phase quantization) and measuring performance degradation; comparison with robust RL training.  

5. **Heterogeneous SV capacities:** Does the three-path offloading with nearest SV selection remain optimal under non-uniform vehicle distributions or heterogeneous SV computing resources?  
   *Basis:* Model assumes equal SV capacity and nearest selection; simplistic load balancing.  
   *What would resolve:* Simulations with varying SV CPU frequencies and clustered vehicles, comparing proposed policy against oracle or learned assignment.

## Limitations
- **Semantic mapping unreported:** The exact function f(ν, γ) → δ (semantic similarity) is not described; reproduction of absolute latency values is blocked.  
- **Baseline details unclear:** Implementation specifics for GA and QPSO baselines are missing; it is uncertain whether they share the same LP sub-routine, raising fairness concerns.  
- **Lack of statistical validation:** No variance or confidence intervals across multiple random seeds; reported gains may be seed-dependent.

## Confidence
- **Latency reduction (40–50%)**: Medium (strong simulation evidence but baseline specifics unclear, no statistical tests).  
- **Scalability to 30 vehicles**: Medium (density tests promising but real-world dynamics unexplored).  
- **Two-tier stability and optimality**: Low–Medium (convex LP theoretically sound, but continuous-relax-then-round may bias gradients without ablation proof).

## Next Checks
1. **Statistical comparison:** Train/test PPO, GA, QPSO with ≥5 random seeds; report mean latency and 95% confidence intervals to confirm significance.  
2. **Rounding impact ablation:** Replace continuous-relax-then-round mapping with a discrete policy (e.g., Gumbel–Softmax); measure performance difference to quantify rounding suboptimality.  
3. **Semantic model integration:** Implement or approximate the DeepSC model to derive f(ν, γ); re-evaluate all schemes under identical semantic accuracy constraints to validate reported gains.
