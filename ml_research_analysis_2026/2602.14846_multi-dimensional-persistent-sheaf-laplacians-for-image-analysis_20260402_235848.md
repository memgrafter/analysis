# Multi-dimensional Persistent Sheaf Laplacians for Image Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14846
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14846
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MPSL achieves substantially higher classification accuracy than PCA on COIL20 and ETH80, with stable performance across a wide range of reduced dimensions.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a multi-dimensional persistent sheaf Laplacian (MPSL) framework for image analysis. It constructs dataset-level simplicial complexes and leverages persistent sheaf Laplacians to extract multiscale topological spectral features. These features are aggregated across multiple reduced dimensions and neighborhood scales to produce robust image representations. Experiments on COIL20 and ETH80 show that MPSL (single-dimension) achieves average classification accuracies of 90.91% and 63.06%, far surpassing PCA's 36.75% and 35.76%. Multi-dimension aggregation yields further improvements to 91.67% and 66.22%. The method exhibits stability over a broad range of reduced dimensions where PCA performance degrades sharply.

## Method Summary
The MPSL framework begins by representing the dataset as a simplicial complex, typically via an ε-neighbor graph extended to higher-dimensional simplices. Instead of assigning scalar weights to simplices, a sheaf structure is employed, allowing richer algebraic data (e.g., vector spaces and linear maps) to capture multi-dimensional relationships. A multi-dimensional persistence filtration is then applied, generating a module over a polynomial ring. The Laplacian operators of the sheaf are computed at each filtration step, and their spectral features (eigenvalues/eigenvectors) are extracted. These features are aggregated across multiple filtration dimensions (i.e., considering different combinations of dimensions in the persistence) and across various neighborhood scales. The aggregated features serve as input to a classifier (e.g., SVM). The approach yields representations that are stable under perturbations and less sensitive to the choice of reduced dimension compared to linear methods like PCA.

## Key Results
- MPSL (single-dimension) achieves 90.91% accuracy on COIL20 and 63.06% on ETH80, vastly outperforming PCA (36.75% and 35.76%).
- Multi-dimension aggregation improves results to 91.67% (COIL20) and 66.22% (ETH80).
- Performance remains robust across a wide range of reduced dimensions (1–50), while PCA accuracy fluctuates dramatically.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The persistent sheaf Laplacian encodes topological information at multiple scales and dimensions, capturing both geometric and higher-order structural relationships in the data. By aggregating spectral features across different persistence dimensions and neighborhood scales, the representation becomes less sensitive to any single parameter choice. The sheaf construction allows for richer encoding than a standard combinatorial Laplacian, potentially leading to more discriminative features. The stability of persistent homology carries over to the sheaf setting, ensuring that small perturbations in the data do not drastically alter the extracted features.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: 
Data -> Simplicial Complex Construction -> Sheaf Assignment -> Persistent Sheaf Laplacian Computation -> Spectral Feature Extraction -> Feature Aggregation -> Classifier

Critical path: The core workflow follows the sequence above; the most computationally intensive step is computing persistent sheaf Laplacians across the filtration. Sheaf assignment and aggregation parameters are crucial for performance.

Design tradeoffs: 
- Neighborhood scale (ε) affects complex connectivity: too small leads to disconnected components, too large oversmooths.
- Dimensionality of persistence (e.g., 2D or 3D) increases expressiveness but complicates computation and representation.
- Aggregation strategy (e.g., pooling across eigenvalues) trades off information richness for stability.

Failure signatures: 
- Degenerate sheaf assignments (e.g., all trivial) yield performance similar to PCA.
- Overly large ε leads to loss of discriminative local structure; overly small ε yields noisy complexes.
- Multidimensional persistence may produce too few points for stable statistics if the filtration is coarse.

First three experiments:
1. **Synthetic manifold test**: Generate data on a known shape (e.g., circle) and verify that persistent sheaf spectra recover expected topological signatures.
2. **Parameter sweep**: Vary ε and reduced dimension independently to reproduce the reported stability curve; confirm that accuracy remains within a band.
3. **Sheaf vs. constant sheaf comparison**: Replace the learned sheaf with a constant coefficient system and measure accuracy drop to isolate the contribution of sheaf structure.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to two small, simple image datasets (COIL20, ETH80); generalizability to larger, more complex benchmarks is unproven.
- Comparisons are only with PCA; modern nonlinear methods (UMAP, t-SNE) and deep learning baselines are absent.
- The exact contribution of multi-dimension aggregation versus neighborhood scales is not disentangled via ablation.
