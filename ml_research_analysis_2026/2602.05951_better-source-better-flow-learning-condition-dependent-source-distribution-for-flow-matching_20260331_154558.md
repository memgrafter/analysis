# Better Source, Better Flow: Learning Condition-Dependent Source Distribution for Flow Matching

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05951
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05951
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** Learning a text-dependent source distribution reduces intrinsic variance, improving FID from 2.925 to 2.453 and CLIP Score from 0.3396 to 0.3420 on ImageNet-1K, with up to 3.01× faster FID convergence.

## Executive Summary
Flow matching for conditional generation typically uses a fixed Gaussian source distribution, ignoring conditioning signals. This paper introduces Condition-dependent Source Flow Matching (CSFM), which learns a conditional source distribution \( p_\phi(X_0|C) \) to reduce the intrinsic variance term in the flow matching objective. Key innovations include a variance regularization term that prevents source collapse while allowing the mean to adapt to target modes, and a directional alignment loss that stabilizes training when the flow model absorbs too much conditioning. Experiments on ImageNet-1K and large-scale models show consistent improvements in sample quality, text alignment, and convergence speed, with straighter flows that degrade less under few-step sampling.

## Method Summary
CSFM replaces the fixed Gaussian source \( \mathcal{N}(0,I) \) with a learnable conditional Gaussian \( \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(C), \sigma_\phi^2(C)I) \), where \( \mu_\phi \) and \( \sigma_\phi \) are produced by a source generator (a Perceiver-style network) from the text condition \( C \). The flow backbone \( v_\theta \) is trained on the standard flow matching loss \( L_{FM} = \|v_\theta(X_t,t,C) - (X_1 - X_0)\|^2 \), with \( X_t = (1-t)X_0 + tX_1 \) and \( X_0 \sim p_\phi(C) \). Two auxiliary losses are added: \( L_{VarReg} \) (a KL divergence that penalizes variance deviation from 1 while leaving the mean unconstrained) to prevent collapse, and \( L_{align} \) (negative cosine similarity between \( X_0 \) and \( X_1 \)) to encourage directional alignment and stabilize source learning. The model is trained end-to-end on latents from a RAE (DINOv2) encoder, using an AdamW optimizer (\( lr=2\times10^{-4} \), batch 1024) for 100K steps.

## Key Results
- Reduces FID on ImageNet-1K from 2.925 (fixed Gaussian) to 2.453; improves CLIP Score from 0.3396 to 0.3420.
- Achieves up to 3.01× faster FID convergence and 2.48× faster CLIP convergence during training.
- Produces straighter flows that degrade less with fewer sampling steps.
- Scales to 1.3B parameter models: GenEval improves from 0.77 to 0.80; DPG-Bench from 78.31 to 81.11.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A learnable condition-dependent source distribution reduces the *intrinsic variance* term in the flow matching loss decomposition, leading to cleaner supervision signals and faster convergence.
- **Mechanism:** The FM loss decomposes into approximation error and intrinsic variance, where the latter is irreducible under a fixed source-target coupling (Eq. 6). By introducing a conditional source \( p_\phi(X_0|C) \), the coupling \( \pi_\phi(X_0,X_1,C) \) becomes learnable, transforming the intrinsic variance from a fixed constant into a term that can be minimized via training. This adaptive coupling reduces path intersections and the multi-valuedness of \( \Delta \) at each \( (x,t) \).
- **Core assumption:** The intrinsic variance can be reduced by aligning source means \( \mu_\phi(C) \) with target modes, without requiring the full coupling structure of optimal transport.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "learning a text-dependent source distribution to reduce intrinsic variance and improve optimization."
  - [section 2.2 & 3.1]: Decomposes FM loss and introduces learnable coupling \( \pi_\phi \).
  - [corpus]: Related works on OT couplings (e.g., C2OT) also target intrinsic variance reduction, but via minibatch OT, which CSFM replaces with end-to-end learning. **Note:** Corpus papers discuss similar goals but not CSFM's specific mechanism; evidence for CSFM is primarily internal.
- **Break condition:** Gains diminish when the target representation space lacks structure (Sec. 4.3), e.g., when a single condition maps to highly multimodal targets, making \( \mu_\phi(C) \) ambiguous.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Variance regularization (KL divergence between the conditional source and a unit-variance Gaussian *with fixed mean*) prevents distributional collapse while allowing \( \mu_\phi(C) \) to adapt freely, maintaining sufficient source support.
- **Mechanism:** Joint training drives \( \sigma^2_\phi(C) \rightarrow 0 \) to minimize intrinsic variance. Standard KL regularization \( \mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2I) \| \mathcal{N}(0,I) \) penalizes both mean and variance deviations, anchoring \( \mu_\phi(C) \) to the origin. Instead, \( L_{VarReg} \) penalizes only variance deviation from 1 (Eq. 9), letting \( \mu_\phi(C) \) move toward target modes without collapse.
- **Core assumption:** Sufficient source support (non-zero variance) is necessary to recover the target distribution (Lee et al., 2025); collapsing to deterministic \( \delta(X_0-\mu_\phi(C)) \) causes path entanglement.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "variance regularization to prevent collapse."
  - [section 3.2 & Fig. 2]: Shows collapse without regularization, failure with full KL, success with variance-only regularization.
  - [corpus]: CAR-Flow (Chen et al., 2025) also addresses collapse via reparameterization, but is not in the provided corpus. **Note:** No direct corpus counterpart; evidence is from paper's own experiments.
- **Break condition:** If \( \lambda_{VarReg} \) is too high, it may over-constrain variance, hindering adaptation; too low risks collapse.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A directional alignment loss (\( L_{align} \)) between source and target samples stabilizes source learning when the flow model \( v_\theta \) is strongly conditioned on \( C \), preventing the source from becoming uninformative.
- **Mechanism:** When \( v_\theta(X_t,t,C) \) already encodes most conditional information, gradients through \( X_t = (1-t)g_\phi(C) + tX_1 \) provide weak learning signal for \( g_\phi \). \( L_{align} \) (negative cosine similarity) directly encourages \( X_0 \) and \( X_1 \) to be directionally similar, providing an explicit training signal for \( \mu_\phi(C) \) that is independent of the flow model's capacity.
- **Core assumption:** The flow model can over-absorb conditioning, creating a "credit assignment" problem where the source generator receives diminishing gradients.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "directional alignment between source and target are critical for stable and effective learning."
  - [section 3.3 & Fig. 11]: Shows source distributions become discriminative only with alignment; FM loss and gradient variance improve (Fig. 3).
  - [corpus]: "Conditioning Matters" (diffusion policies) shows conditioning affects training efficiency, analogous but in a different generative framework. **Note:** Corpus provides indirect support via analogous phenomena.
- **Break condition:** If \( \lambda_{align} \) is too high, it may over-constrain the source distribution, reducing flexibility under the FM objective.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Flow Matching (FM) objective and its decomposition.
  - *Why needed here:* CSFM's core innovation modifies the intrinsic variance term via adaptive coupling; understanding this decomposition is essential to grasp what is being optimized.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the two components of the FM loss in Eq. (6), and which one does a fixed Gaussian source make irreducible?
- **Concept:** Conditional probability paths and source support.
  - *Why needed here:* The source must have sufficient support (non-zero variance) to allow trajectories to avoid intersections and recover the target distribution.
  - *Quick check question:* Why does a deterministic source (\( \delta(X_0-\mu_\phi(C)) \)) fail, according to Fig. 2B?
- **Concept:** Variance regularization via KL divergence.
  - *Why needed here:* Prevents collapse to deterministic source during joint training, a key failure mode identified.
  - *Quick check question:* How does \( L_{VarReg} \) differ from a standard Gaussian KL prior, and why is that difference critical?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Text Encoder -> Source Generator \( g_\phi \) (Perceiver) -> \( \mu_\phi(C), \sigma_\phi(C) \) -> Sample \( X_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2 I) \) -> Concatenate with \( X_1, t \) -> Flow Backbone \( v_\theta \) (UnifiedNextDiT + DDT head) -> Velocity prediction -> Compute \( L_{FM}, L_{VarReg}, L_{align} \) -> Joint optimization of \( \phi, \theta \).
- **Critical path:** End-to-end training: Sample \( C \) → sample \( X_1 \) → compute \( g_\phi(C) \) → construct \( X_t \) → compute \( v_\theta(X_t,t,C) \) → compute \( L_{FM} \) → backpropagate to update both \( \phi \) and \( \theta \) alongside \( L_{VarReg} \) and \( L_{align} \). The source update directly shapes the effective coupling and intrinsic variance.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Variance regularization:* \( \lambda_{VarReg} \) trades off collapse prevention (low \( \sigma^2 \)) vs. mean mobility (free \( \mu \)). Too low → collapse; too high → source remains near origin.
  - *Alignment loss:* \( \lambda_{align} \) trades off directional guidance vs. flexibility under FM. Cosine similarity preserves scale-invariance; MSE over-constrains magnitude.
  - *Target representation:* RAE (DINOv2) spaces have more separable class structure, making \( \mu_\phi(C) \) more informative; SD-VAE spaces are more entangled, reducing gains.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Source collapse:** \( \sigma^2_\phi(C) \rightarrow 0 \) during training (detect by monitoring variance stats). Remedy: increase \( \lambda_{VarReg} \).
  2. **Entangled trajectories:** t-SNE shows source and target overlapping (Fig. 6A). Remedy: add \( L_{align} \) or use more structured target space.
  3. **Training instability:** FM loss plateaus or increases; gradient variance spikes (Fig. 3). Remedy: ensure \( L_{align} \) is active; check that source generator capacity matches backbone.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Toy validation (Fig. 2):** Train on 2D synthetic data with known conditional geometry. Verify: (a) collapse without \( L_{VarReg} \), (b) restricted mean with full KL, (c) disentangled trajectories with \( L_{VarReg} \) only, (d) improved with \( L_{align} \) in complex condition cases.
  2. **ImageNet component ablation (Tab. 1):** Incrementally add \( p_\phi \), \( L_{VarReg} \), \( L_{align} \) to baseline. Check: FID/CLIP improvements, NaN without \( L_{VarReg} \), limited gains without \( L_{align} \).
  3. **Gradient variance tracking (Fig. 3):** Monitor \( \text{Var}(\nabla_\theta L_{FM}) \) across \( t \). Expect lower variance, especially near \( t=0 \), confirming cleaner supervision from reduced intrinsic variance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can we design source distributions that work well even when target latents are not well-structured (e.g., SD-VAE)?
  - *Basis in paper:* Sec 4.3 shows CSFM benefits strongly depend on structured target representation; entangled spaces show minimal gain.
  - *Why unresolved:* No proposal to overcome this limitation; the paper treats it as a fundamental restriction.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* A method that adapts source learning to any latent space, with consistent improvements across diverse autoencoders.
- **Open Question 2:** Why does adding a directional alignment loss improve flow matching optimization, beyond simply reducing intrinsic variance?
  - *Basis in paper:* Fig 3 shows CSFM with alignment has lower gradient variance; the paper suggests it stabilizes training but lacks theoretical explanation.
  - *Why unresolved:* The interplay between alignment regularization and gradient dynamics is not formalized.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Derivation linking alignment to variance reduction in the FM gradient estimator.
- **Open Question 3:** Do conditional source models more expressive than Gaussian (e.g., mixtures, flows) improve performance in highly multimodal conditioning settings?
  - *Basis in paper:* Sec 4.3 and Fig 9 show that when a condition maps to multiple distant modes, CSFM's learned source becomes Gaussian-like and offers little benefit.
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper assumes a Gaussian source and does not explore richer parametrizations.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Extending CSFM to flexible source families and demonstrating gains on difficult multimodal prompts.

## Limitations
- CSFM gains depend heavily on a well-structured target latent space (e.g., RAE-DINOv2); benefits vanish with entangled spaces like SD-VAE.
- Reproduction is hindered by unspecified details: exact RAE checkpoint, DDT head design, Qwen3-VL captioning pipeline, and training schedule nuances.
- Performance is sensitive to hyperparameters \( \lambda_{VarReg} \) and \( \lambda_{align} \); improper tuning risks collapse or over-constraint.

## Confidence
- **Intrinsic variance reduction via adaptive coupling:** Medium (derivation plausible, but empirical validation mostly internal; break condition suggests limits).
- **Variance regularization preventing collapse:** High (clear ablations in Fig. 2 show failure without it).
- **Directional alignment stabilizing training:** Medium (empirical support strong, but mechanism is indirect and sensitive to \( \lambda_{align} \)).

## Next Checks
1. **Variance collapse test:** Train with \( \lambda_{VarReg}=0 \), monitor average \( \sigma^2_\phi(C) \). Expect rapid collapse (\( \sigma^2 \rightarrow 0 \)) and FID drop; with \( \lambda_{VarReg}=5 \), expect stable positive variance.
2. **Source discriminability probe:** Train a linear classifier to predict conditions from \( \mu_\phi(C) \). Without \( L_{align} \), accuracy near chance; with \( L_{align} \), accuracy should rise significantly (as in Fig. 11).
3. **Gradient variance profiling:** Compute per-\( t \) variance of \( \nabla_\theta L_{FM} \) across a minibatch. CSFM (with both losses) should reduce variance, especially near \( t=0 \), confirming cleaner supervision (replicate Fig. 3).
