# Corruption-robust Offline Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning From Human Feedback

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28281
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28281
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves corruption-robust offline MARLHF with Nash gap \(O(\varepsilon^{1-o(1)} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m})\) under uniform coverage and \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m})\) under unilateral coverage, extending to coarse correlated equilibria via efficient computation.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies offline multi-agent reinforcement learning from human feedback (MARLHF) where a fraction \(\varepsilon\) of pairwise trajectory preferences is adversarially corrupted. Assuming a linear Markov game structure, the authors develop robust algorithms under two data coverage regimes: uniform coverage (all policy directions represented) and weaker unilateral coverage (covering only a Nash equilibrium and its single-agent deviations). For uniform coverage, they use a trimmed maximum likelihood estimator for rewards and backward induction with pessimistic/optimistic bounds to achieve a Nash gap of \(O(\varepsilon^{1-o(1)} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m})\). Under unilateral coverage, they employ projected gradient ascent on reward parameters with a biased yet tractable gradient surrogate derived from behavior policy feature differences, yielding a Nash gap of \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m} + nH^2/\sqrt{T_1})\). To avoid computational intractability of Nash equilibria, they relax to coarse correlated equilibria (CCE) and use Optimistic Hedge per stage game, attaining a CCE gap of \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m} + nH^2/\sqrt{T_1} + nH^2/T_2)\). These are the first corruption-robust guarantees for offline MARLHF, with optimal \(\varepsilon\)-dependence under uniform coverage and matching rates to single-agent RLHF under unilateral coverage.

## Method Summary
The approach splits data into two parts: one for robust reward estimation and one for value estimation. Under uniform coverage, a trimmed MLE yields a reward confidence set; optimistic and pessimistic reward bounds are constructed and plugged into a robust Q-learning (Rob-Q) algorithm to compute value bounds for all policies. The final policy minimizes an estimated Nash gap over the policy class. Under unilateral coverage, reward estimation again uses trimmed MLE but defines a confidence set via likelihood divergence. A projected gradient ascent (PGA) refines reward parameters for \(T_1\) steps, using a gradient surrogate based on feature expectation differences between behavior policies (which cover the Nash equilibrium and deviations). After obtaining refined rewards, Rob-Q is run with an additional coverage-adjusted bonus, and the gap is minimized. For computational tractability, the Nash gap minimization is replaced by per-stage Optimistic Hedge to compute an approximate coarse correlated equilibrium (CCE) in quasi-polynomial time, composing stage policies into a history-dependent correlated policy.

## Key Results
- Under uniform coverage: Nash equilibrium gap \(O(\varepsilon^{1-o(1)} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m})\).
- Under unilateral coverage (Nash): Gap \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m} + nH^2/\sqrt{T_1})\).
- Under unilateral coverage (CCE): Gap \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m} + nH^2/\sqrt{T_1} + nH^2/T_2)\) with quasi-polynomial computation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Uniform Coverage with Trimmed MLE and Backward Induction
- **Claim:** Achieves Nash gap \(O(\varepsilon^{1-o(1)} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m})\).
- **Mechanism:** Trimmed MLE robustly estimates rewards from corrupted preferences. Optimistic/pessimistic reward confidence sets are built. Backward induction (Rob-Q) computes value bounds for all policies. The final policy minimizes the estimated gap.
- **Core assumption:** Uniform coverage (\(\Sigma_{\mu,\mu^{\text{ref}}}^{-} \succeq \xi_R I\), \(\Sigma_{\mu}^{(h)} \succeq \xi_P I\) for all \(h\)).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the bound; Section 3 details Algorithm 1; single-agent RLHF literature shows \(\varepsilon^{1-o(1)}\) rates under uniform coverage.
- **Break condition:** If uniform coverage fails (small \(\xi_R, \xi_P\)), bound degrades to \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\).

### Mechanism 2: Unilateral Coverage with Projected Gradient Ascent
- **Claim:** Achieves Nash gap \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m} + nH^2/\sqrt{T_1})\) via PGA.
- **Mechanism:** Rewards estimated via Trimmed MLE; confidence set via likelihood divergence. PGA on reward parameters for \(T_1\) steps uses a biased gradient surrogate: difference of feature expectations under behavior policies \(\mu\) and \(\mu^{\text{ref}}\) (which cover Nash equilibrium and deviations). Final policy minimizes gap from Rob-Q with coverage-adjusted bonus \(\Gamma\).
- **Core assumption:** Unilateral coverage: \(\Sigma_{\rho,\rho'}^{-} \succeq C_R \Sigma_{(\pi_i^*, \pi_{-i}^*)}^{-}\), \(\Sigma_{\mu}^{(h)} \succeq C_P \Sigma_{(\pi_i^*, \pi_{-i}^*)}^{(h)}\) for \(\rho,\rho' \in \{\mu, \mu^{\text{ref}}\}\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\); Section 4 details Algorithm 4; single-agent RLHF and two-player zero-sum games show \(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\) under weak coverage.
- **Break condition:** Insufficient \(T_1\) or small coverage constants \(C_R, C_P\) cause surrogate gradient error or blowup.

### Mechanism 3: Coarse Correlated Equilibria via Optimistic Hedge
- **Claim:** Under unilateral coverage, computes CCE in quasi-polynomial time with gap \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon} + nH d^{3/4}/\sqrt{m} + nH^2/\sqrt{T_1} + nH^2/T_2)\).
- **Mechanism:** Same reward estimation as Mechanism 2. For each stage game backward, compute Q estimates with Rob-Q+\(\Gamma\). Define per-state loss and run Optimistic Hedge for \(T_2\) steps to obtain stage policy \(\tilde{\pi}_h\). Compose stage policies into history-dependent correlated policy.
- **Core assumption:** Unilateral coverage (same as Mechanism 2); relaxation from Nash to CCE enables efficient computation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions CCE gap \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\) and quasi-polynomial time; Section 5 describes Algorithm 5; Optimistic Hedge is standard for CCE in online Markov games.
- **Break condition:** Large action spaces or small \(T_2\) impair Hedge convergence; correlation limited to stage games.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data split (D1 for reward, D2 for value) -> RewardEst (Trimmed MLE + PGA) -> ValueEst (Rob-Q with bonus \(\Gamma\)) -> Gap minimization (search over \(\Pi^{PP}\) or Optimistic Hedge per stage) -> Output policy \(\hat{\pi}\).
- **Critical path:** Reward estimation (Step 2) must precede value/gap estimation (Step 3); value estimates depend on robust reward parameters and coverage-adjusted bonus.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Uniform coverage vs. \(\varepsilon\)-dependence: Strong coverage gives \(O(\varepsilon^{1-o(1)})\) but unrealistic; unilateral gives \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\) but requires gradient surrogate.
  - NE vs. CCE: Exact NE gap minimization is combinatorial (exponential in agents); CCE via Hedge is polynomial/quasi-polynomial but may not find pure NE.
  - Bonus choice: \(\Gamma\) must balance corruption (\(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\)) and sample complexity (\(1/\sqrt{m}\)); uses scaled covariance \(\Lambda_h\) with regularization \(\lambda\).
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - Coverage assumptions fail (small \(\xi_R, \xi_P\) or \(C_R, C_P\)); compute empirical feature covariances to diagnose.
  - Inadequate tuning of \(\lambda\), learning rates, or insufficient \(T_1, T_2\); track gGap or true gap over iterations.
  - Robust estimation sensitive to \(\varepsilon\); evaluate on clean validation set; check RobMean stability.
  - Computational intractability of enumerating all policies; restrict to tiny instances for theoretical guarantees.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Validate Trimmed MLE: On synthetic corrupted preference data, compare \(\|\hat{\theta} - \theta^*\|_2\) vs. \(\varepsilon\); should show \(O(\varepsilon)\) under uniform coverage.
  2. Test gradient surrogate: For known Nash \(\pi^*\), compute true gradient \(\nabla_\theta \text{Gap}(\pi^*, \theta)\) vs. feature difference proxy; should be correlated under unilateral coverage.
  3. Measure CCE convergence: Run Optimistic Hedge for increasing \(T_2\); plot per-stage CCE gap vs. \(1/T_2\); should decrease as \(O(1/T_2)\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the data corruption problem in MARLHF be formulated and solved under general function approximation? Basis: The paper states it is not clear how to formulate corruption with general function approximation and design robust algorithms. Why unresolved: Current theory relies on linearity; general approximation introduces complexities in robust statistics and optimization.
- **Open Question 2:** Is the \(O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\) bound on the Nash gap under unilateral coverage tight? Basis: The paper explicitly asks if this bound is tight. Why unresolved: Upper bound is provided but matching lower bound is unknown; it might be improved with refined analysis.
- **Open Question 3:** How do the proposed robust algorithms perform in practice on MARL benchmarks? Basis: The paper notes implementing and experimentally testing them is an exciting direction. Why unresolved: Algorithms are theoretical only; practical issues like scalability, hyperparameter sensitivity, and real-world corruption remain unaddressed.

## Limitations
- Coverage assumptions (uniform/unilateral) require unknown positive constants; may not hold in practice.
- Robust oracles (RobEst, RobMean) are abstract; implementation details missing, affecting practical performance.
- Nash gap minimization over all policies is combinatorial; CCE relaxation yields only coarse equilibria.
- Requires prior knowledge of corruption fraction \(\varepsilon\); sensitivity to mis-specification not analyzed.
- Finite optimization steps (\(T_1, T_2\)) introduce additional errors not tightly bounded.
