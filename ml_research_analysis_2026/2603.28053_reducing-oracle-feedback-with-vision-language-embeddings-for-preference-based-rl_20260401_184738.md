# Reducing Oracle Feedback with Vision-Language Embeddings for Preference-Based RL

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.28053
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28053
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: ROVED reduces oracle queries by 50–80% on Meta-World tasks while matching or exceeding prior PbRL methods, and its adapted VLE generalizes to new tasks, achieving cumulative annotation savings of up to 90%.

## Executive Summary
Preference-based reinforcement learning (PbRL) typically relies on expensive human oracles to provide pairwise trajectory preferences, limiting scalability. This paper introduces ROVED, a hybrid framework that leverages a noisy but scalable vision-language embedding (VLE) to automatically generate initial preferences. ROVED selectively queries the oracle only for uncertain samples, identified via a KL-divergence-based filter between the reward model's predictions and VLE labels. To improve the VLE's alignment with task progress, ROVED fine-tunes it parameter-efficiently using a combination of sparse oracle feedback and a dynamics-aware self-supervised loss (inverse dynamics). Evaluated on Meta-World robotic manipulation tasks, ROVED achieves comparable or superior success rates to state-of-the-art PbRL baselines while reducing oracle queries by 50–80%. Moreover, the adapted VLE transfers effectively to new tasks, yielding cumulative annotation savings of up to 90% in cross-task experiments.

## Method Summary
ROVED integrates a pre-trained vision-language model (e.g., LIV) into the PbRL loop. For each pair of trajectory segments, the VLE computes image-text similarity scores to derive segment returns and auto-assign preferences. A reward model (ensemble MLP) is trained on these noisy labels, but samples where the KL divergence between the reward model's prediction and the VLE label exceeds dynamic thresholds are flagged as uncertain. These uncertain samples are submitted to the oracle within a fixed budget. The reward model updates on all labeled data (clean VLE, flipped noisy, and oracle-labeled). Concurrently, the VLE is fine-tuned using two objectives: (1) a preference loss on oracle-labeled pairs, and (2) an inverse dynamics loss that predicts actions from consecutive VLE embeddings, encouraging alignment with environment dynamics. Small adapter layers are added to the frozen VLE encoders for parameter efficiency. After adaptation, the VLE’s improved preference generation reduces future oracle needs, and its weights can be transferred to new tasks for further savings.

## Key Results
- Oracle query reduction of 50–80% on Meta-World tasks compared to PEBBLE and other baselines, without performance loss.
- Cross-task transfer: VLE fine-tuned on one task generalizes to related tasks, cutting cumulative annotation needs by 75–90%.
- Ablations confirm the importance of both the KL-based uncertainty filtering and the dynamics-aware VLE fine-tuning for maintaining performance and query efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Uncertainty-Based Oracle Filtering
**Claim:** ROVED reduces oracle dependency by using a lightweight VLE to generate initial preference labels, reserving costly oracle queries for uncertain cases.  
**Mechanism:** For a trajectory segment pair, the VLE computes image-text similarity to derive segment returns and auto-assign preferences (Eqs. 3–4). A KL-divergence-based filter (Eqs. 7–9) identifies samples where the reward model's prediction diverges significantly from the VLE label, marking them uncertain for oracle review. Clean samples (KL ≤ τlower) and flipped noisy samples (KL ≥ τupper) train the reward model; uncertain samples are sent to the oracle within a budget.  
**Core assumption:** The VLE's noisy preferences are sufficiently aligned with true preferences to provide a useful training prior, and KL divergence correlates with label noise.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions VLE generates preferences and defers to oracle for high uncertainty; [section IV-C] details τ thresholds and sample categorization; [corpus] references active learning and noisy-label robustness literature.  
**Break condition:** If VLE noise dominates early, KL thresholds may misclassify clean samples as uncertain, leading to unnecessary queries; if thresholds too lenient, noisy labels corrupt reward learning.

### Mechanism 2: Dynamics-Aware VLE Fine-Tuning
**Claim:** Parameter-efficient VLE fine-tuning with a dynamics-aware self-supervised loss improves the quality and generalization of VLE-generated preferences over time.  
**Mechanism:** Small adapter layers (GL, GI) are added to frozen VLE encoders. The VLE is fine-tuned on (i) sparse oracle preferences (Eq. 2) and (ii) an inverse dynamics loss (Eq. 6) predicting actions from consecutive adapted embeddings. This aligns VLE embeddings with environment dynamics without full model fine-tuning.  
**Core assumption:** Consistency between visual embeddings and action transitions is a valid self-supervised signal for task-relevant representation learning in manipulation domains.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions dynamics-aware objective; [section IV-B] describes adapters and inverse dynamics head; [corpus] supports self-supervised robotic representation learning.  
**Break condition:** If dynamics are not captured by simple action prediction (e.g., contact-rich tasks), the inverse dynamics loss may misguide VLE adaptation.

### Mechanism 3: Cross-Task Transfer via Adapted VLE
**Claim:** The adapted VLE transfers across related tasks, enabling cumulative annotation savings because fine-tuning on one task's oracle feedback improves its preference generation on new tasks.  
**Mechanism:** Adapter weights fine-tuned on a source task are reused to initialize the VLE for a target task. The same uncertainty-aware feedback loop then requires minimal additional oracle queries because the VLE already encodes transferable visual-linguistic and dynamic priors.  
**Core assumption:** Task families (e.g., Meta-World manipulations) share sufficient visual and dynamic structure for VLE adaptation to generalize.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] reports cumulative savings up to 90%; [section V-B] cross-task transfer experiments (Fig. 4); [corpus] lacks direct evidence, empirical from paper.  
**Break condition:** If source and target tasks are semantically/dynamically dissimilar, transferred VLE may produce misleading preferences, requiring near-full oracle budgets.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**
Policy πϕ (SAC actor-critic) interacts with environment and stores transitions in replay buffer. Reward model rθ (ensemble MLP) is trained on preferences from VLE and oracle. VLE encoder (pre-trained, e.g., LIV) has added adapter layers GL, GI for fine-tuning. Oracle filter computes KL divergence between rθ predictions and VLE labels and applies thresholds τlower, τupper to categorize samples. Dynamics head f (linear layer) provides inverse dynamics loss on adapted embeddings.

**Critical path**
1. Sample trajectory pairs from replay buffer.
2. Compute VLE returns and preferences via image-text similarity.
3. For each pair, compute KL divergence between rθ's predicted return and VLE's return.
4. Partition samples: clean (KL ≤ τlower), uncertain (τlower < KL < τupper), high-confidence noisy (KL ≥ τupper). Flip noisy labels.
5. Query oracle for a subset of uncertain samples within budget.
6. Update reward model rθ on all labeled pairs (clean, flipped noisy, oracle).
7. Update VLE adapters (GL, GI) using (a) preference loss on newly oracle-labeled pairs and (b) inverse dynamics loss on recent transitions predicting actions from consecutive embeddings.
8. Update policy πϕ using rθ as reward (e.g., via SAC).

**Design tradeoffs**
- VLE choice (LIV vs. DecisionNCE): Stronger pre-trained VLEs may require less adaptation but could be less controllable; ROVED shown robust across both.
- Threshold sensitivity (τlower, τupper): Tight thresholds risk over-filtering; loose thresholds risk noise. Adaptive τunc (βt decay) helps but requires tuning.
- Adaptation budget: More oracle feedback improves VLE faster but increases cost; the paper's 250 initial + 0.25N per iteration is a heuristic.

**Failure signatures**
- VLE reward predictions on expert trajectories do not increase monotonically after adaptation.
- Oracle query rate remains high (>50%) without performance gain, indicating ineffective filtering.
- Cross-task transfer fails (success rate near zero) for dissimilar task pairs.

**First 3 experiments**
1. Ablate VLE adaptation: Compare ROVED with fixed VLE (no fine-tuning). Expect: Fixed VLE performance degrades over time due to distribution shift.
2. Ablate inverse dynamics loss: Train VLE only on oracle preferences. Expect: Initial gain then plateau/decline as policy improves and dynamics misalignment grows.
3. Ablate selective feedback: Replace KL-based selection with random oracle sampling at same budget. Expect: Lower final success rate due to redundant/noisy oracle labels.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **What quantitative task similarity metrics determine VLE transfer effectiveness?**  
   Basis: Cross-task transfer shown (e.g., door-open → drawer-open) with 75–90% savings, but no similarity metrics.  
   Why unresolved: Conditions for generalization unexplored; applying to new tasks requires trial-and-error.  
   Evidence needed: Controlled experiments varying similarity (visual features, action space, goal semantics) to establish bounds.

2. **Does an adaptive oracle query budget reduce total annotations more than fixed per-iteration budget?**  
   Basis: ROVED uses fixed budget (250 initial + 0.25N per iteration).  
   Why unresolved: Fixed budget may waste queries on low-uncertainty batches and starve high-uncertainty ones.  
   Evidence needed: Ablation comparing fixed vs. uncertainty-driven budgets (e.g., allocate more when mean KL high).

3. **How reliable is KL divergence as a universal indicator of noisy preferences across VLE backbones and domains?**  
   Basis: Filtering uses KL with dynamic thresholds, evaluated only on LIV/DecisionNCE in manipulation tasks.  
   Why unresolved: Thresholds are hyperparameter-tuned; unclear if generalizes to other VLEs or complex environments.  
   Evidence needed: Evaluate filtering precision/recall against ground truth on multiple VLEs/domains, correlate KL with label error.

4. **What is the theoretical lower bound on oracle preferences needed to adapt a VLE to a new task?**  
   Basis: ROVED uses small initial set (250) and sparse feedback, achieving 50–80% reduction, but no analysis of minimal budget.  
   Why unresolved: Knowing minimal viable budget would improve cost efficiency.  
   Evidence needed: Systematic studies varying total oracle budget, measuring success rate plateaus; possible theoretical bounds.

## Limitations
- KL filtering thresholds are task-dependent and underspecified; sensitivity may undermine query reduction.
- Inverse dynamics loss assumes deterministic transitions; may fail on stochastic or contact-rich tasks.
- Results limited to specific VLE backbones (LIV/DecisionNCE); generalization to other pre-trained models unclear.

## Confidence
- Query reduction (KL filtering): Medium
- VLE adaptation (dynamics loss): Medium
- Cross-task transfer: Low-Medium
