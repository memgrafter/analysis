# Scaling Laws of Machine Learning for Optimal Power Flow
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.02706
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02706
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: Systematic scaling study reveals power-law relationships for ML-OPF error with data and compute, with PINNs reducing constraint violations but increasing prediction error, exposing an accuracy-feasibility trade-off.

## Executive Summary
This paper conducts the first systematic scaling study for machine learning-based optimal power flow (OPF). Using deep neural networks (DNN) and physics-informed neural networks (PINN), it varies training data size (0.1K–40K samples) and computational resources (FLOPs) across grid sizes from 118 to 2000 buses. Key findings include strong power-law scaling of active power prediction error with data (MAE_Pg ∝ D^{-0.389}, R²=0.992), weaker scaling with compute (MAE_Pg ∝ C^{-0.25}, R²=0.6), and a feasibility bottleneck where constraint violations—especially reactive power—scale poorly with data. PINNs mitigate violations by orders of magnitude but at the cost of higher prediction error, quantifying a fundamental trade-off. The results offer empirical guidance for designing resource-efficient ML-OPF systems.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates DNN and PINN models on synthetic power grids ranging from 118 to 2000 buses. Training data is scaled from 0.1K to 40K samples, and computational effort is varied by adjusting model FLOPs through architectural changes. Performance is assessed using mean absolute error for generator active power (MAE_Pg) and magnitudes of constraint violations, including reactive power and voltage limits. PINNs incorporate physical constraints directly into the loss function, while DNNs rely on post-hoc feasibility corrections. The scaling exponents are derived by fitting power laws to error metrics across data and compute regimes.

## Key Results
- Data scaling law: MAE_Pg scales as D^{-0.389} with high confidence (R²=0.992) across grid sizes.
- Compute scaling law: MAE_Pg scales as C^{-0.25} with moderate confidence (R²=0.6), indicating unexplained variance.
- Feasibility bottleneck: Constraint violations, particularly reactive power, do not scale favorably with data, limiting practical deployment.
- PINN trade-off: Physics-informed training reduces constraint violations by up to 42× compared to DNNs with less data, but increases prediction error.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide a theoretical mechanism for the observed scaling laws, suggesting they emerge empirically from data properties and model capacity. For PINNs, the mechanism is the explicit enforcement of physical constraints (e.g., power flow equations) via loss function penalties, which directly minimizes violations during training. This contrasts with DNNs that learn mappings without constraint adherence, leading to higher feasibility errors.

## Limitations
- Generalization beyond tested grids: Scaling laws derived from synthetic grids (118–2000 buses) may not hold for real-world transmission or distribution networks with different topologies.
- Compute scaling confounders: The moderate R² (0.6) for FLOPs scaling suggests unaccounted factors like optimizer choices or hardware efficiency.
- PINN robustness: Gains in violation reduction are shown on limited scenarios; performance under N-1 contingencies or high renewable variability is unknown.

## Confidence
- Data scaling law (MAE_Pg ∝ D^{-0.389}): High confidence (R²=0.992, consistent across sizes)
- Compute scaling law (MAE_Pg ∝ C^{-0.25}): Medium confidence (R²=0.6, possible omitted variables)
- Constraint violation scaling: Medium confidence (empirically observed but not formulated as a law)
- PINN accuracy-feasibility trade-off: High confidence (direct experimental comparison)
