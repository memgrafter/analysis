# Sim-to-Real Fruit Detection Using Synthetic Data: Quantitative Evaluation and Embedded Deployment with Isaac Sim

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28670
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28670
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** Hybrid training with as few as 1k synthetic and 50 real fruit images recovers near real-only detection performance (mAP@0.5 0.974 vs. 0.983) and, after TensorRT FP16 optimization, achieves real-time inference (53 FPS) on an embedded Jetson Orin NX.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the use of synthetic data from NVIDIA Isaac Sim to train fruit detection models under constraints of limited real images and embedded deployment. It compares real-only, synthetic-only, and hybrid (synthetic pre-training plus real fine-tuning) regimes using YOLOv8s. Hybrid training with minimal real data (e.g., 50 images) attains performance almost identical to models trained on much larger real-only sets on in-domain tests. Under domain shift to real-world conditions, hybrid models show significantly better robustness than synthetic-only models. The final model, optimized with TensorRT in FP16, meets real-time requirements (53 FPS inference, 28 FPS end-to-end) on a Jetson Orin NX, demonstrating a practical pipeline for sim-to-real transfer in resource-constrained agricultural robotics.

## Method Summary
Synthetic datasets (1,000 or 5,000 images) are generated in Isaac Sim using domain randomization over lighting, object pose, textures, and occlusions. Real training data consists of 50 or 100 manually annotated images. Three training regimes are evaluated: real-only, synthetic-only, and hybrid (synthetic pre-training followed by real fine-tuning). All models use YOLOv8s with identical hyperparameters. Evaluation is performed on two fixed test sets: T1-100 (in-domain, similar to training distribution) and T2-100 (domain-shifted, real-world fruit and backgrounds). The best hybrid models are exported to ONNX, optimized with TensorRT in FP16 precision, and deployed on a Jetson Orin NX to measure inference latency and frames-per-second (FPS).

## Key Results
- **Hybrid efficacy:** With just 1k synthetic images and 50 real images, hybrid training achieves mAP@0.5 of 0.974 on the in-domain test set, comparable to the real-only model (0.983) and far above synthetic-only (0.656).
- **Domain shift robustness:** Under domain shift (T2-100), hybrid models (e.g., H1000+50: mAP@0.5 0.747) substantially outperform synthetic-only models (S1000: 0.565).
- **Embedded deployment:** TensorRT FP16 optimization yields 53 FPS for inference-only (18.9 ms latency) and 28 FPS end-to-end on the Jetson Orin NX, meeting real-time constraints.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Hybrid training (synthetic + limited real data) recovers detection performance near real-only models while drastically reducing real annotation needs.
- **Mechanism:** Synthetic data provides vast, automatically labeled coverage of geometric and visual variations via domain randomization. Limited real data then "anchors" the model to the true data distribution, calibrating it against the simulator's representational gaps (e.g., texture, lighting). The model learns invariant features from synthetic diversity while aligning decision boundaries to real examples.
- **Core assumption:** The domain gap is primarily a distribution shift in low-level feature statistics rather than high-level semantics. Limited real samples are sufficient to correct this if synthetic data covers the relevant pose/occlusion space.
- **Evidence:** Hybrid models (e.g., H1000+50) achieve mAP@0.5 0.974 vs. real-only (R50) 0.978 and synthetic-only (S1000) 0.626 on in-domain test (Table 1). On domain shift, hybrid (H1000+50: 0.747) outperforms synthetic-only (S1000: 0.565) (Table 2).
- **Break condition:** If synthetic randomization fails to cover a critical real-world mode (e.g., a novel fruit ripeness stage) not present in the small real set, the hybrid model will inherit that failure.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Domain randomization within Isaac Sim improves model robustness to unseen real-world conditions compared to a deterministic synthetic dataset.
- **Mechanism:** Randomizing lighting, pose, textures, and occlusions forces the model to learn features stable across variations, reducing overfitting to simulator-specific cues and encouraging reliance on robust shape/structural cues.
- **Core assumption:** Real-world variability can be approximated by a predefined randomization distribution in simulation; the model's representations are malleable enough that wide synthetic exposure acts as a regularizer.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.2.2 details randomization parameters. Hybrid models show better robustness under domain shift than synthetic-only (Table 2), aligning with established domain randomization literature.
- **Break condition:** If the real-world shift lies entirely outside the randomization bounds (e.g., outdoor sunlight vs. indoor simulation), randomization provides no benefit and may hurt.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** TensorRT FP16 optimization on Jetson Orin NX preserves sufficient accuracy while achieving real-time inference.
- **Mechanism:** TensorRT performs layer fusion, kernel auto-tuning, and precision calibration for the Jetson GPU, reducing memory bandwidth and computation. YOLOv8s's single-stage, anchor-based architecture is amenable to such optimizations, with FP16 causing minimal accuracy loss for this task.
- **Core assumption:** The accuracy degradation from FP16 is acceptable for operational requirements; the model's weights and activations have enough dynamic range to withstand 16-bit precision.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 4.5 report 53 FPS inference and 18.9 ms latency on Jetson Orin NX.
- **Break condition:** If the model is sensitive to quantization noise, FP16 could cause a significant mAP drop (>5%), making deployment infeasible—this was not measured.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Sim-to-Real Transfer Gap**
  - **Why needed:** The core problem is that models trained purely in simulation fail on real fruit images. Understanding the nature of this gap (e.g., photorealistic texture vs. motion blur) is critical to designing mitigation (hybrid training, domain randomization).
  - **Quick check:** If a synthetic image is photorealistic but the real camera adds motion blur, is the gap primarily in texture fidelity or motion characteristics?

- **Concept: Domain Randomization**
  - **Why needed:** It is the primary tool used to bridge the sim-to-real gap. Its goal is not to create a perfect simulator but to create a hardened model that ignores simulator-specific cues.
  - **Quick check:** Should you randomize the fruit's *color* if ripeness is a critical detection feature? What is the trade-off?

- **Concept: Embedded Model Optimization (TensorRT)**
  - **Why needed:** The deployment target is a resource-constrained Jetson Orin NX. Achieving real-time FPS requires understanding how framework changes (PyTorch -> ONNX -> TensorRT) and precision changes (FP32 -> FP16) impact latency and accuracy.
  - **Quick check:** What is the typical latency breakdown (preprocess / inference / postprocess) for a YOLO model on Jetson, and which stage is most amenable to optimization?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Isaac Sim -> Synthetic Dataset -> YOLOv8s Training (PyTorch) -> Hybrid Fine-tuning -> Evaluation (T1/T2) -> ONNX Export -> TensorRT FP16 -> Jetson Orin NX Inference -> Benchmarking (FPS/latency)
- **Critical path:** Synthetic dataset generation -> Model training -> ONNX export -> TensorRT optimization -> Jetson deployment & benchmarking. Evaluation (T1/T2) provides the feedback loop for regime comparison.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Synthetic data size vs. Real data size: ~1k synthetic + 50 real images is a viable minimum; 5k synthetic gives negligible gains; 100 real helps only in-domain.
  - Model size vs. FPS: YOLOv8s chosen for embedded feasibility; larger models (YOLO26s) handle domain shift better but not deployment-tested.
  - Precision vs. Accuracy: FP16 used for deployment; paper assumes negligible loss without direct measurement.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Training: Hybrid performance plateaus or degrades with more synthetic data (e.g., H5000+100 worse than H1000+100 on T2), indicating synthetic quality is the bottleneck.
  - Deployment: End-to-end FPS (28) far below inference FPS (53), pointing to preprocessing/postprocessing as the system bottleneck.
  - Domain Shift: All models show >20% absolute mAP drop from T1 to T2; if T2 performance is inadequate, the solution is better synthetic realism or different adaptation techniques.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate synthetic-only baseline: Train S1000 and S5000 models to confirm large performance gap on T1 (~0.63 mAP).
  2. Validate hybrid benefits: Train H1000+50 and H5000+50 to confirm they recover >95% of real-only (R50) performance on T1 and show most robust T2 performance.
  3. Deployment sanity check: Export best hybrid model (e.g., H1000+50) to ONNX, optimize with TensorRT FP16, and measure 18.9 ms / 53 FPS on Jetson Orin NX.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can the realism and variability of synthetic fruit data be improved to better capture complex textures and non-uniform appearances?**  
   The "Further Work" section calls for advanced material modeling and lighting simulation, as current domain randomization did not fully bridge the gap for natural objects.
2. **To what extent do the findings on hybrid training trade-offs generalize to structured industrial objects with uniform geometry and materials?**  
   The study only evaluated fruit; generalization to manufactured parts (with more consistent appearances) is proposed as future work.
3. **Can the accuracy benefits of hybrid training with limited real data be preserved when using more computationally efficient model architectures or lower precision (e.g., INT8) on embedded platforms?**  
   Only YOLOv8s with TensorRT FP16 was benchmarked; YOLO26s showed better accuracy on desktop but was not deployment-tested, and INT8 quantization was not evaluated.
4. **How does the number of target classes in a multi-class detection task influence the required ratio of synthetic to real images in effective hybrid training?**  
   The paper used 7 fruit classes and noted that a reduced class set would likely improve performance due to decreased ambiguity. The optimal synthetic-to-real ratio may change with task complexity.
5. **What specific aspects of the domain randomization in Isaac Sim are most critical for closing the sim-to-real gap for deformable/natural objects, and can automatically learned domain gaps inform randomization strategies?**  
   No ablation isolated which randomization parameters (lighting, material, deformation) were most important; the paper concluded quality over quantity but did not quantify contributions.

## Limitations
- **Unreported implementation details:** Exact Isaac Sim randomization parameter ranges and the specification of the real test sets (T1/T2) are not provided, hindering faithful replication.
- **Deployment specifics:** Jetson Orin NX power mode, SDK versions, TensorRT version, and preprocessing/postprocessing implementation details are unspecified, introducing variability in latency/accuracy measurements.
- **Inferred accuracy preservation:** The paper assumes negligible accuracy loss from FP16 optimization but does not directly measure mAP@0.5 before and after TensorRT conversion, a key gap for embedded trust.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid training efficacy (recovering real-only performance with minimal real data):** High  
  Strongly supported by in-domain mAP comparisons (Table 1).
- **Synthetic-only failure under domain shift:** High  
  Clear mAP drop in Table 2, consistent with sim-to-real literature.
- **Real-time deployment metrics (53 FPS inference):** Medium  
  Latency numbers are reported, but accuracy preservation post-TensorRT FP16 is not validated.
- **Domain randomization as primary robustness mechanism:** Medium  
  Correlational evidence from Table 2; no ablation isolating randomization effects.

## Next Checks
1. **Quantify FP16 accuracy impact:** Re-measure mAP@0.5 on T1/T2 for the same model in FP32 (PyTorch) vs. FP16 (TensorRT) to confirm negligible degradation.
2. **Characterize domain gap:** Train a domain classifier or use t-SNE on model embeddings to visualize the synthetic-real feature shift; validate that hybrid’s robustness correlates with reduced domain discriminability.
3. **Profile deployment pipeline bottleneck:** Time preprocessing (resize/normalize), inference (TensorRT), and postprocessing (NMS) separately on Jetson Orin NX to confirm whether the 28 FPS end-to-end limit stems from non-inference stages.
