# Navigating the Concept Space of Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23524
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23524
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** Concept Explorer enables scalable exploration of SAE features via hierarchical neighborhood embeddings (HUMAP), revealing coherent concept clusters and rare features.

## Executive Summary
Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) decompose language model activations into interpretable features, but analyzing thousands of these features remains challenging due to the lack of scalable tools. This paper introduces Concept Explorer, a system that organizes SAE feature explanations using hierarchical neighborhood embeddings (HUMAP). By generating textual explanations for each feature via an LLM and embedding them, Concept Explorer constructs a multi-resolution manifold that supports progressive navigation from broad concept clusters to fine-grained neighborhoods. Applied to features from SmolLM2, the tool reveals coherent high-level clusters (e.g., punctuation, markers), meaningful subclusters (e.g., dash variants), and distinctive rare features that are difficult to identify with standard workflows.

## Method Summary
Concept Explorer operates in three stages: (1) For each SAE feature, the system collects top-activating contexts and uses GPT-5-mini to produce a concise explanation. (2) These explanations are embedded using `nomic-embed-text-v1.5`. (3) The embeddings are fed into HUMAP, which builds a hierarchy of landmarks and fuzzy similarity graphs to produce multi-level 2D projections. The frontend visualizes each level as an interactive canvas; clicking a landmark drills down to its finer-level region, and triage signals (proximity, influence-region size, text similarity) help identify rare concepts. The pipeline is fully automated after the initial explanation and embedding steps, enabling scalable exploration without manual feature inspection.

## Key Results
- Organizes thousands of SAE features into hierarchical clusters, overcoming manual inspection limits.
- Reveals coherent subclusters, such as punctuation variants (e.g., dashes, list markers).
- Highlights distinctive rare concepts through triage signals, enabling discovery of specialized features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Hierarchical neighborhood embedding (HUMAP) preserves local and global feature relationships across multiple resolutions, enabling progressive navigation from broad concept clusters to fine-grained neighborhoods.
- **Mechanism:** HUMAP constructs a hierarchy of subsets via landmark selection from random walks on a fuzzy similarity graph derived from explanation embeddings. Each level `ℓ` produces a 2D embedding `Y^(ℓ)` where landmarks from level `ℓ+1` define regions of influence on level `ℓ`. This allows reprojection of a landmark’s neighbourhood to a finer level while maintaining manifold relations.
- **Core assumption:** Explanation embeddings capture sufficient semantic similarity such that spatial proximity in HUMAP correlates with conceptual relatedness, and the hierarchical landmark sampling preserves this structure across scales.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...organizes concept explanations using hierarchical neighborhood embeddings... enables progressive navigation from coarse concept clusters to fine-grained neighbourhoods, supporting discovery, comparison, and relationship analysis among concepts."
  - [section 3] "HUMAP constructs a hierarchy of subsets based on landmarks and the corresponding fuzzy similarity graphs... random walks create a region of influence at level X^(ℓ) for each landmark in level X^(ℓ+1), enabling hierarchical drill-down."
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers use single-level UMAP or lack hierarchical navigation, making HUMAP’s multi-resolution approach a novel differentiator but not externally validated in this corpus.
- **Break condition:** If hierarchical landmark selection fails to preserve neighbourhood structure (e.g., due to embedding noise or poor similarity graph), drill-down would reveal discontinuities, breaking coherent navigation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Textual explanations generated by an LLM for SAE features, when embedded, provide a scalable semantic proxy for feature meaning, allowing automated grouping without manual inspection of activation contexts.
- **Mechanism:** For each SAE feature, top-activating contexts are fed to an LLM (GPT-5-mini) with a prompt to produce a concise explanation. These explanations are embedded (`nomic-embed-text-v1.5`) and used as input to HUMAP. Semantic similarity in explanation embedding space clusters features with conceptually related roles.
- **Core assumption:** LLM-generated explanations accurately and consistently summarize the activating patterns of SAE features, and the embedding model captures this semantics such that geometric proximity reflects functional similarity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1] "We embedded the explanations using `nomic-embed-text-v1.5`... These metadata are the only inputs required by Concept Explorer: the interface uses the explanation embeddings to group semantically similar explanations..."
  - [section 5] Manual annotation of clusters (e.g., "punctuation", "markers") aligns with explanation content, suggesting explanations are meaningful (e.g., Feature #12378 activates on sentence-end patterns).
  - [corpus] Several neighbors (e.g., "ConceptViz", "Visual Exploration of Feature Relationships...") also use LLM-generated explanations or embeddings, indicating community adoption but not proof of accuracy.
- **Break condition:** If LLM explanations become generic, inconsistent, or fail to capture key distinctions (e.g., conflating subclusters), embedding-based grouping would produce semantically incoherent clusters, requiring manual re-annotation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Visual cues derived from HUMAP’s structure—proximity to dense groups, influence-region size, and explanation similarity—enable rapid triage of rare or outlier concepts in a large feature set.
- **Mechanism:** HUMAP outputs landmark-centric influence regions whose size (number of features) and position (isolation from dense clusters) are visualized. Combined with fast text-similarity search over explanation embeddings, users can filter for small regions (specialized features) or isolated points (rare concepts).
- **Core assumption:** Rare or highly specific SAE features have low activation frequency or unique contexts, leading to small influence regions and/or spatial isolation in the embedding manifold, which correlates with human notions of rarity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1] "To answer Q2, Concept Explorer exposes three lightweight signals... (i) projection proximity (points far from any dense group are candidate outliers), (ii) influence-region size (small regions often indicate specialized, rare features)..."
  - [section 5.1] "By focusing on a small influence region, we discovered features that specialize on dashes / em-dashes and on list-boundary markers... a single rare feature discovered inside that cluster."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; neighboring papers focus on clustering or search, not multi-resolution outlier detection.
- **Break condition:** If rare features have broad influence (e.g., due to noisy training) or cluster with common concepts, these signals would fail to highlight them, requiring alternative heuristics like activation statistics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs):**  
  *Why needed here:* SAEs are the source of the features being explored; understanding that they produce a high-dimensional, sparse code from LLM activations is essential to grasp why thousands of features exist and why they need organizing.  
  *Quick check:* What does the sparsity constraint in SAE training encourage in the learned latent features?
- **Hierarchical Neighborhood Embedding (HUMAP):**  
  *Why needed here:* HUMAP is the core algorithm enabling multi-resolution visualization; understanding landmarks, influence regions, and drill-down is necessary to interpret the interface’s hierarchical canvases.  
  *Quick check:* How does HUMAP generate a finer-level embedding from a coarser-level landmark’s region of influence?
- **Explanation Generation via LLM Prompting:**  
  *Why needed here:* The entire system hinges on textual explanations as a semantic representation; knowing the prompt template and its limitations is key to assessing explanation quality and potential biases.  
  *Quick check:* What specific instruction in the prompt template ensures explanations start consistently and capture context around the token?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Feature Metadata Store → HUMAP Engine → Frontend UI (with Embedding Index supporting similarity search)
- **Critical path:** Feature extraction from SAE → LLM explanation generation → embedding computation → HUMAP hierarchy build → UI render → user interaction (drill-down/select) → detail view update.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Scalability vs. semantic fidelity:* Using explanation embeddings (rather than activation statistics) scales but depends on LLM explanation quality.  
  - *Interactivity vs. recomputation:* HUMAP hierarchy is precomputed; dynamic reprojection of regions is fast, but changing embedding model or parameters requires full recompute.  
  - *Automation vs. manual effort:* Automated triage signals reduce browsing but may miss concepts not captured by explanation semantics or HUMAP structure.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Cluttered or overlapping clusters at all levels, indicating explanation embeddings lack discriminative power.  
  - Drill-down reveals disjoint or discontinuous neighborhoods, suggesting HUMAP hierarchy failed to preserve local structure.  
  - Rare concepts not appearing in small/isolated regions, implying activation patterns don’t align with explanation embeddings.  
  - High annotation effort needed to label clusters, pointing to poor explanation quality or embedding mismatch.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **HUMAP parameter sweep:** Vary number of landmarks per level and `n_neighbors` in UMAP to measure cluster coherence (via silhouette on manual labels) and navigation depth.  
  2. **Explanation validation:** Sample features from different cluster types; have annotators judge if explanations match top-k contexts, comparing to baseline (no explanation) or alternative prompt templates.  
  3. **User navigation study:** Task users with finding concepts of a specified type (e.g., "punctuation variants") using Concept Explorer vs. manual search; measure time, success rate, and discovered subcluster richness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How consistent and generalizable are the high-level concept clusters discovered by Concept Explorer across different language models, SAE architectures, and training datasets?
- **Basis:** The evaluation is limited to a single SAE from SmolLM2 (layer 27) with explanations generated by GPT-5 mini. The paper does not test whether the observed cluster structure replicates for other models, layers, or SAE configurations.
- **Why unresolved:** Without cross-model or cross-SAEs validation, it is unclear whether the hierarchical organization reflects universal properties of language model representations or idiosyncrasies of the specific SAE and data used.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply Concept Explorer to SAEs from multiple model families (e.g., GPT, Claude, Llama), various layers, and different training corpora. Compare the resulting hierarchies to identify stable top-level clusters versus model-specific groupings.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent do the LLM-generated feature explanations accurately represent the true causal semantics of SAE features, and how sensitive is the clustering to explanation quality?
- **Basis:** The system relies on GPT-5 mini to produce explanations from top-activating contexts, with no human verification or comparison to alternative explanation methods. The clustering is directly based on these explanation embeddings, so errors or biases could distort the concept map.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not evaluate explanation fidelity or explore how different explanation strategies affect the resulting hierarchical structure.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a human evaluation of explanation accuracy for a sample of features. Recluster using explanations from multiple sources (e.g., different LLMs, human annotators) and measure cluster coherence via intra-cluster activation similarity or expert assessments. Also, perform causal checks to see if features within a cluster indeed influence model behavior similarly.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does Concept Explorer quantitatively improve human analysts' speed, coverage, and insightfulness in exploratory SAE analysis compared to existing workflows?
- **Basis:** The paper claims the system enables discovery of rare concepts and supports open-ended exploration, but provides no controlled user study or comparative metrics. The presented use case is anecdotal and lacks baseline comparisons.
- **Why unresolved:** It is unknown whether the hierarchical interface actually enhances discovery or merely repackages existing information. Potential downsides such as cognitive load from multi-level navigation or redundancy with simpler tools are not addressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A user study with interpretability researchers performing open-ended tasks (e.g., "find all rare concept types" or "compare related concept families") using Concept Explorer versus baselines (e.g., flat UMAP with search, manual inspection). Measure objective outcomes (time, number of distinct clusters found) and subjective feedback (usability, perceived utility).

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can HUMAP scale to the millions of features typical of modern SAEs while preserving computational feasibility and human-interpretable hierarchical navigation?
- **Basis:** The paper suggests scalability to millions but only demonstrates up to 36,864 features. The HUMAP algorithm's complexity and memory usage at larger scales are not analyzed, and the UI may become cluttered or slow.
- **Why unresolved:** No benchmarks for larger SAEs are provided. The hierarchical approach might break down if lower levels contain too many landmarks or if rare features become indistinguishable in crowded projections.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark HUMAP's runtime and memory on synthetic and real SAE datasets ranging from 100k to 1M+ features. Test the Concept Explorer interface with such large datasets to evaluate navigation intuitiveness and the identifiability of rare concepts. If performance degrades, explore optimizations like adaptive sampling or level-specific simplification.

## Limitations
- Unknown HUMAP hyperparameters and SAE training details block exact reproduction and may affect generalizability.
- The system’s reliance on LLM-generated explanations introduces risks of generic, inconsistent, or biased summaries that distort clustering.
- Triage signals for rare concepts are heuristic and lack quantitative validation across the full feature set.

- **Mechanism 2 (Explanation embeddings):** Medium

## Next Checks
1. **HUMAP structure preservation test:** Compute pairwise activation-based similarity (e.g., cosine on max-activated activations) for a subset of features and correlate with pairwise distances in the HUMAP embedding. A significant correlation would support the core assumption that explanation embeddings preserve functional relationships.
2. **Explanation-context alignment audit:** Sample features from distinct HUMAP clusters; have annotators score (1–5) whether the LLM explanation faithfully summarizes the top-k activation contexts, controlling for prompt variations. Compare scores to a baseline with no explanation or alternative prompts.
3. **Triage signal quantification:** Define "rare" features via low activation frequency or unique contexts, then measure precision/recall of Concept Explorer’s signals (small influence regions, isolation, text-similarity filter) in retrieving them. This will validate the heuristic triage approach.
