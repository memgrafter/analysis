# Learning When to Act: Interval-Aware Reinforcement Learning with Predictive Temporal Structure

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.22384
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22384
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: ATCPG improves efficiency by 22.8% over fixed-interval baseline, with ablations attributing gains to learning (+54.8%), hyperbolic spread (+26.2%), and spatio-temporal embedding (+5.8%); it also outperforms a privileged oracle-based controller by +72.5% and reduces token consumption by 10.9% in live GPT-4.1 deployment while matching success rates.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of autonomous agents learning optimal timing between actions in continuous operation. ATCPG introduces a lightweight pacing policy that uses a predictive hyperbolic spread signal (κ) computed from future state embeddings in a Poincaré ball, where higher spread indicates uncertain futures and triggers sooner action. An interval-aware reward corrects a systematic credit-assignment failure inherent in naive outcome-based rewards. Evaluations on a synthetic environment show ATCPG achieving a 22.8% efficiency gain over a fixed-interval baseline. Ablations quantify contributions: learning (+54.8%), hyperbolic spread (+26.2%), and spatio-temporal embedding (+5.8%). A head-to-head against a privileged oracle-based controller yields +72.5% higher efficiency despite less direct information. A live deployment with GPT-4.1 confirms the ordering, with ATCPG-ST reducing token consumption by 10.9% while maintaining success rates.

## Method Summary
ATCPG frames pacing as a contextual bandit where an agent selects an inter-tick interval ∆t based on a state vector st that includes priority, fatigue, wellbeing change, performance, an oscillator phase, and a hyperbolic spread κ. The policy is linear: ˆ∆t = clip(θᵀst, ∆t_min, ∆t_max), with weights updated online via a reward-weighted regression rule. κ is the core innovation: it is computed by sampling n future trajectories from a world model, embedding their state summaries into a Poincaré ball, and measuring mean pairwise geodesic distances (plus variance). The Poincaré metric amplifies divergence near the boundary, so κ acts as an uncertainty indicator—higher κ yields shorter intervals via a negative weight. The interval-aware reward (Eq. 9) combines an efficiency term, a spacing bonus that penalises inefficient waits during overload, and a spread brake that rewards uncertainty-driven acceleration. An optional spatio-temporal variant (ATCPG-ST) concatenates position vectors into the embeddings, further increasing κ when spatial trajectories diverge. The system also includes an internal oscillator for temporal regularity and exploration modulation via state‑dependent noise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*Claim*: The interval-aware reward corrects a systematic credit-assignment failure in naive outcome-based rewards for pacing problems, enabling the agent to learn appropriate slowdown under overload.
*Mechanism*: The reward (Eq. 9) explicitly includes a spacing bonus term (1.5 * ot * (∆t/∆t_base)) that creates a positive gradient on the fatigue weight θf during overload (ot > 0), countering the negative gradient produced by the naive efficiency term when wellbeing delta (∆w) is negative.
*Core assumption*: The linear policy update rule (Eq. 4) is stable and effective under this shaped reward, and the overload magnitude (ot) is recoverable from the environment state.
*Evidence anchors*:
  - [abstract]: "propose an interval-aware reward that explicitly penalises inefficiency relative to the chosen wait time, correcting a systematic credit-assignment failure"
  - [section 5.1]: Formal proof (Proposition 1) showing the update rule with naive reward decreases θf when rt < 0 (overload), producing shorter intervals—the opposite of desired behaviour.
  - [section 7.4]: Ablation "-Interval-aware reward" shows a -15.1% drop in efficiency, validating the correction's contribution.
  - [corpus]: Corpus neighbors do not directly address this specific credit-assignment pathology in continuous pacing; evidence is internal to the paper.
*Break condition*: If the environment's overload signal (ot) is not linearly separable in the state features or if the linear policy is too weak to capture the optimal timing mapping.

### Mechanism 2
*Claim*: Predictive hyperbolic spread (κ) computed from future state embeddings in a Poincaré ball provides a prospective, geometry-amplified uncertainty signal that drives the agent to act sooner under branching futures.
*Mechanism*: n future trajectories are embedded into the Poincaré ball. The mean pairwise geodesic distance (plus variance) among these embeddings is computed. The ball's metric (λ²_c = 4/(1-∥x∥²)²) exponentially amplifies distances near the boundary, so even modest divergence in predicted futures yields large κ values. High κ indicates high epistemic uncertainty and triggers shorter intervals via the negative weight θκ.
*Core assumption*: The world model's future predictions are sufficiently noisy or divergent to produce meaningful spread, and the Poincaré amplification correlates with decision uncertainty better than Euclidean spread.
*Evidence anchors*:
  - [abstract]: "predictive hyperbolic spread signal... computed from future state embeddings in a Poincaré ball, where higher spread indicates uncertain futures and triggers sooner action."
  - [section 6.3 & 6.4]: Definition of κ (Eq. 14) and integration with policy (θκ < 0). Proposition 3 shows the amplification mechanism (d_c² ≈ 8r²δ/(1-r²)²).
  - [section 7.4]: Ablation "-Curvature" shows a -26.2% efficiency drop, confirming its significant complementary gain beyond non-geometric features.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "Hyperbolic Space Learning Method Leveraging Temporal Motion Priors" and "GeoWorld: Geometric World Models" support the use of hyperbolic geometry for uncertainty/temporal representation, but not this specific κ mechanism.
*Break condition*: If future predictions are consistently identical (κ ≈ 0) or if the world model's noise does not correlate with actual environmental volatility. The O(n²) computation could also become a bottleneck for large n.

### Mechanism 3
*Claim*: Joint spatio-temporal embedding (ATCPG-ST) adds an independent timing signal from spatial trajectory divergence, which, via shared hyperbolic amplification, yields higher absolute κ and further efficiency gains without altering the learning rule.
*Mechanism*: Position vectors are projected into the Poincaré ball alongside state embeddings and concatenated with independent normalization (Eq. 16). Spatial divergence pushes joint embeddings toward the ball boundary, increasing pairwise geodesic distances and thus κ. Higher absolute κ leads to shorter intervals (via fixed θκ), increasing action frequency and throughput.
*Core assumption*: Spatial trajectory divergence (∥p(i)-p(j)∥) is genuinely correlated with epistemic uncertainty or task load in the domain, and the Poincaré amplification applies equivalently to mixed (state+position) vectors.
*Evidence anchors*:
  - [abstract]: "joint spatio-temporal embedding (ATCPG-ST)... concatenates independently normalised state and position projections... spatial trajectory divergence provides an independent timing signal... raises mean hyperbolic spread (κ) from 1.88 to 3.37 and yields a further +5.8% efficiency gain."
  - [section 6.5]: Formal description of the concatenation and independent normalization. Proposition 4 (sketch) shows κ increases monotonically with position scaling ε if clipping is inactive.
  - [section 7.8]: Controlled ablation on shared 500-tick trajectory shows κ increases by 1.79× and efficiency by +5.8% when position noise is correlated with overload.
  - [section 7.9]: Real-LLM benchmark confirms ordering: η_Fixed < η_ATCPG < η_ATCPG-ST, with ATCPG-ST saving 10.9% more tokens.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "Diffusion Model Agnostic Social Influence Maximization in Hyperbolic Space" use hyperbolic space for graph diffusion but not for joint spatio-temporal pacing.
*Break condition*: If position information is absent or uncorrelated with task load, the ST variant degrades gracefully to SO. The benefit is proportional to the correlation strength between position divergence and the latent uncertainty/load signal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contextual Bandits & Online Linear Regression**  
  Why needed: The pacing policy is a linear associative bandit updated via Eq. 4, a reward‑weighted regression rule. Understanding this update’s bias and stability is key to interpreting the learning dynamics and the “Learning” ablation’s +54.8% gain.  
  Quick check: Why does omitting the (∆t − ˆ∆t) term from the REINFORCE gradient not destabilize learning in this pacing domain?

- **Concept: Hyperbolic Geometry (Poincaré Ball Model)**  
  Why needed: The core novelty—predictive spread κ—relies on the Poincaré metric’s boundary expansion property. Engineers must understand how geodesic distance (Eq. 11) amplifies angular/radial divergence to grasp why κ is a useful uncertainty proxy.  
  Quick check: How does the conformal factor λ_c² = 4/(1-∥x∥²)² cause small Euclidean differences near the boundary to become large hyperbolic distances?

- **Concept: Temporal Credit Assignment in RL**  
  Why needed: The paper’s primary problem (optimal *when*) suffers from a unique credit‑assignment failure when using naive outcome reward. Understanding the misalignment between the objective (1) and immediate reward (rt) is necessary to appreciate the interval‑aware reward design.  
  Quick check: In a pacing problem with objective J(π)=lim (1/T)∑ (rt/∆t), why would a reward signal rt = ∆w alone incentivize the agent to use shorter intervals even when overloaded (∆w < 0)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**
1. **Pacing Policy (Linear)**: Input st (6D state), outputs ˆ∆t. Updated online via Eq. 4.
2. **State Construction**: Gathers priority, fatigue, ∆wellbeing, performance, oscillator phase sin(ϕ), and curvature κ.
3. **Curvature Estimator (κ)**: Samples n futures from world model W, embeds them via Eq. 13 into Poincaré ball, computes mean+variance of pairwise d_H (Eq. 14).
4. **Interval-Aware Reward Calculator**: Combines efficiency, spacing bonus, and spread brake (Eq. 9) using observed ∆w, ot (implied), κ, and chosen ˆ∆t.
5. **Internal Oscillator**: Phase ϕ and velocity ω updated via Eq. 7, with optional Kuramoto coupling (Eq. 8) for multi-agent sync.
6. **Exploration Modulator**: State-dependent noise ε_eff(st) (Eq. 6) applied to ˆ∆t.

**Critical path**  
Observe raw features → Compute κ (requires n future samples/embeddings) → Build st → Compute ˆ∆t (linear model) → Apply exploration noise → Sleep ˆ∆t → Execute tick → Observe new w → Compute ∆w → Calculate rt → Update θ and (ϕ, ω).

**Design tradeoffs**
- Linear policy vs. neural network: Linear ensures O(1) update and minimal daemon overhead, sacrificing potential expressivity; paper prioritizes lightweight.
- n=4 futures: O(n²) curvature computation cheap but noisy; larger n reduces variance but increases compute; not swept.
- Fixed θκ scale: absolute κ magnitude determines intervals; without normalization, κ scale must be manually tuned; paper freezes θκ for comparison, implying sensitivity.
- World model oracle vs. approximation: Experiments use simulated oracle W; discussion suggests MC-dropout on LLM hidden states as cheap Bayesian proxy, but untested in main results.

**Failure signatures**
1. κ collapses to near-zero: means all future embeddings are nearly identical (world model deterministic or n too small). Agent loses uncertainty-driven acceleration.
2. θf drifts negative under overload: indicates interval-aware reward not applied correctly (missing ot) or linear update overwhelmed.
3. Oscillator phase (ϕ) not evolving: ω update may have α too small or reward signal rt consistently near zero.
4. ATCPG-ST fails to improve over SO: position vectors uncorrelated with load, or independent normalization causes dominance, or clipping activates too often.

**First 3 experiments**
1. Sanity-check curvature amplification: Construct two simple future sets: (a) all identical → κ ≈ 0; (b) equally spread on a sphere at radius r=0.8 → κ ≈ ? (should be >0, see Table 1 regime). Verify approximate scaling with r.
2. Ablation replication - Interval Reward: Run with rt = 2∆w (naive) vs. Eq. 9 on a short seed. Track θf over time under sustained overload (ot=1). Expect naive: θf decreases; interval-aware: θf increases.
3. Position correlation test: In synthetic env, generate position vectors with Gaussian noise: σ_pos_high when ot=1, σ_pos_low when ot=0. Run ATCPG-SO vs. ATCPG-ST. Expect ST to show higher κ and η. Then reverse correlation (σ_pos_high when ot=0); expect ST to degrade to SO performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Does the linear pacing policy suffice in non-convex efficiency landscapes?**  
   Basis: The authors opted for a linear policy to minimize computational overhead but note that real-world agents might theoretically benefit from deep neural policies.  
   Why unresolved: No nonlinear pacing policy was evaluated; the adequacy of linear policies under complex landscapes remains untested.  
   Evidence needed: Compare linear and shallow neural policies on environments with varying degrees of non-convexity, measuring both efficiency gains and computational overhead.

2. **Does the ATCPG pacing policy learned on one task family transfer zero-shot to semantically different tasks?**  
   Basis: The authors state that evaluating multi-task transfer is a critical direction for future work.  
   Why unresolved: All experiments were single-task; cross-task generalization has not been investigated.  
   Evidence needed: Train ATCPG on a suite of search tasks, then directly evaluate on distinct navigation or code-generation tasks without further adaptation, comparing against task-specific learned policies.

3. **How does ATCPG perform in complex, low-success-rate environments where temporal efficiency must be balanced against reasoning depth?**  
   Basis: The live LLM benchmark leaves open the question of how ATCPG behaves in highly complex, low-success-rate environments where the agent might need to actively trade temporal efficiency for increased reasoning accuracy.  
   Why unresolved: Current evaluations use simple tasks with near-perfect success rates; behaviour under realistic failure conditions is unknown.  
   Evidence needed: Deploy ATCPG on challenging multi-step reasoning benchmarks (e.g., SWE-bench, WebArena) and analyze the relationship between pacing decisions and task outcomes, particularly in failure cases.

4. **Can a lightweight world model approximation using latent perturbations (e.g., MC dropout on LLM hidden states) provide a reliable curvature signal for ATCPG?**  
   Basis: The paper proposes instantiating W via Latent Perturbation to reduce cost, but all experiments use oracle or synthetic futures; the proposal remains unevaluated.  
   Why unresolved: The feasibility and accuracy of this proxy have not been demonstrated with real LLM internals.  
   Evidence needed: Implement ATCPG using dropout-perturbed hidden states from a live LLM as future embeddings; compare efficiency and responsiveness against the oracle-future version across diverse tasks.

## Limitations
- Exact state dynamics (priority, fatigue, performance) and world model details are omitted; reproduction relies on guesses or approximations.
- Hyperparameters (learning rates, exploration ε₀, oscillator coupling) are not fully specified; reported gains may be sensitive to these choices.
- All experiments use a synthetic oracle world model; generalization to imperfect models or diverse environments is unknown.
