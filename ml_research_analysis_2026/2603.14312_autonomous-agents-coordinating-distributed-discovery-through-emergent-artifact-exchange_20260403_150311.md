# Autonomous Agents Coordinating Distributed Discovery Through Emergent Artifact Exchange

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.14312
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14312
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The system enables autonomous, decentralized scientific discovery by independent AI agents using a shared registry of skills and a provenance DAG, with emergent coordination via need broadcasting and schema-overlap matching.

## Executive Summary
Scientific discovery often relies on centralized coordination, creating scalability bottlenecks. This paper introduces INFINITE, a framework where autonomous AI agents collaborate in a decentralized manner. Each agent possesses a set of skills and produces computational artifacts with full provenance. An ArtifactReactor coordinates agents by broadcasting needs and matching schemas, enabling emergent synthesis without central planning. The system was tested in four domains: peptide design, materials screening, cross-domain resonance, and an urban-grain analogy. In the urban-grain case, agents generated an executable L-system grammar. These findings demonstrate that decentralized coordination can produce integrated scientific outputs and maintain traceable, auditable records, pointing toward scalable, autonomous research ecosystems.

## Method Summary
INFINITE integrates three layers: the computational layer (ScieneClaw) where autonomous agents perform LLM-based reasoning, select skills from a shared registry of over 300 interoperable tools, and execute them to produce computational artifacts; the coordination layer (ArtifactReactor) that scans a global index of needs and available artifacts, scores needs via a pressure heuristic, and triggers both need-driven and schema-overlap reactions to synthesize new multi-parent artifacts; and the discourse layer (INFINITE platform) that converts artifacts into auditable posts with visible provenance, where community feedback generates karma that influences agent behavior.

Each skill execution creates an artifact with a UUID, type, hash, and parent IDs, forming a directed acyclic graph (DAG) that preserves full lineage. The reactor's pressure score combines novelty, centrality, depth, and age to prioritize needs. Schema matching leverages intersection of artifact payload keys and skill input parameters to discover implicit integration opportunities. An autonomous mutation layer monitors the DAG for stagnation, redundancy, or conflict and applies topological operations (fork, merge, graft) to sustain exploration. This design enables emergent coordination without central planning and maintains traceability throughout the discovery process.

## Key Results
- Demonstrated across four domains: peptide design (10 agents, 177 artifacts, 57 synthesis), materials screening (8 agents, 73 artifacts), cross-domain resonance (13 agents, 159 artifacts), and urban-grain analogy (9 agents, 52 artifacts, 48% synthesis density).
- The urban-grain case yielded an executable six-rule L-system grammar formalizing the analogy, showcasing cross-domain integration.
- Multi-parent synthesis was prevalent, with the reactor automatically merging artifacts based on schema overlap without explicit negotiation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Emergent cross-agent coordination arises from decentralized need broadcasting and schema-overlap matching, eliminating central planning.
- **Mechanism:** Agents broadcast explicit "NeedsSignal" records to a global index. The ArtifactReactor scans this index and ranks open needs by a "pressure score" (novelty, centrality, depth, age). It fulfills high-scoring needs by executing compatible skills on requested data. Separately, it performs schema-overlap matching: if multiple peer artifacts' payload keys intersect with a skill's input parameters, the reactor merges payloads and runs the skill, producing a multi-parent synthesis artifact that explicitly credits all contributors.
- **Core assumption:** Independent agents, operating from diverse scientific profiles, will generate heterogeneous artifacts and non-redundant needs. The pressure scoring prioritizes convergent, high-impact gaps, while schema overlap identifies implicit integration opportunities without explicit negotiation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "ArtifactReactor coordinates peer agents via pressure-based need scoring and schema-overlap matching, enabling emergent multi-parent synthesis without central planning."
  - [section 2.2.5] Detailed description of `scan_needs()`, `scan_available()`, pressure score formula, and `_react_multi()` merging logic.
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers discuss multi-agent coordination but not this specific artifact-exchange mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If all agents have identical profiles/skill selection logic, artifacts become redundant, needs converge trivially, and schema overlap yields no new synthesis. Pressure scoring would fail to identify meaningful gaps.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The immutable artifact DAG with provenance-preserving parent links ensures traceability, enables loop prevention, and supports autonomous DAG mutation for self-correction.
- **Mechanism:** Every skill execution produces an artifact (UUID, type, hash, parent IDs). These are appended to agent-local JSONL stores and indexed globally (metadata-only). The DAG structure prevents cycles via consumed-artifact tracking and self-cycle blocking. The ArtifactMutator monitors for stagnation (long leaf paths), redundancy (sibling key overlap), and conflict (sibling key collisions), applying fork/merge/graft operations to steer exploration.
- **Core assumption:** Explicit computational lineage is sufficient to detect topological inefficiencies (stagnation, redundancy) and that automated topology modifications (mutation) can improve collective exploration without human intervention.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Artifact layer preserves full computational lineage as a directed acyclic graph (DAG)... autonomous mutation layer actively prunes the expanding artifact DAG."
  - [section 2.2.4 & 2.2.5] Artifact schema definition; mutation layer conditions (stagnation_cycles, redundancy_threshold) and operations.
  - [section 2.2.7] Heartbeat cycle includes mutation trigger detection.
- **Break condition:** If the DAG grows too fast relative to mutation scrutiny, redundant or conflicting branches proliferate. If mutation thresholds are misconfigured, beneficial branches may be forked or pruned prematurely, degrading solution quality.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The INFINITE platform's provenance-aware governance couples visible evidence surfaces with reputation accrual, incentivizing rigorous, reproducible investigations.
- **Mechanism:** Posts expose `toolsUsed`, `evidenceSummary`, and the artifact DAG. Community engagement (votes, actions, citations) generates "karma" for the author. Karma determines tier (Probation, Active, Trusted) with associated rate limits and moderation rights. This creates a feedback loop: deeper provenance → higher engagement → higher karma → greater platform influence, rewarding thorough artifact chains.
- **Core assumption:** Peer evaluation within the community can adequately assess investigation quality based on visible tool diversity, artifact lineage depth, and quantitative results, without requiring human expert review per post.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "INFINITE converts these outputs into auditable scientific records... community feedback steering subsequent investigation cycles."
  - [section 2.3.2] "Evidence surfaces are immediately visible to all peers... Engagement drives reputation... deeper provenance yields higher engagement."
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; neighboring papers focus on agent capabilities, not platform governance economics.
- **Break condition:** Karma inflation/deflation from non-quality factors (e.g., popularity contests, vote brigading) decouples reputation from rigor. Agents may optimize for engagement metrics (e.g., sensational claims) rather than sound provenance, leading to a "gaming" of the incentive loop.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) for Provenance
- **Why needed here:** The entire artifact layer and coordination logic depend on understanding parent-child relationships, cycle prevention, and topological mutations (fork/merge/graft).
- **Quick check question:** If an artifact has parents `[a1, a2]` and a later synthesis artifact has parents `[a1, a2, a3]`, what does this imply about the investigation's dependency structure?

### Concept: Pressure Scoring in Decentralized Systems
- **Why needed here:** The ArtifactReactor's need-ranking function (`2.0·novelty + 1.0·centrality + 0.5·depth + 0.2·age`) is the core coordination heuristic. Understanding how each term affects prioritization is critical.
- **Quick check question:** Why might a very old, shallow need (low depth) eventually be fulfilled ahead of a novel, deep need?

### Concept: Schema Matching for Implicit Coordination
- **Why needed here:** The reactor's `can_react()` uses intersection of skill input parameters and artifact payload keys to discover fulfillment opportunities without explicit need signals. This enables "reactive" integration.
- **Quick check question:** If a skill expects `{"smiles": str, "model": str}` and an artifact's payload is `{"compound": "CCO", "predictions": [...]}`, will they match? Under what condition might a secondary JSON-schema path be required?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
- **ScieneClaw (computation layer):** Agent profiles → LLM reasoning → Skill selector → Skill executor → Artifact store (agent-local JSONL) + Global index (metadata).
- **ArtifactReactor (coordination layer):** Scans global index (needs, available artifacts) → Scores needs → Executes need-driven reactions & schema-overlap reactions → Records consumed IDs → Triggers mutation layer.
- **INFINITE (discourse layer):** REST API ↔ PostgreSQL. Posts link to artifact metadata. karma system → tier constraints. Notification polling feeds back to agent observations.
- **Memory layer:** AgentJournal (JSONL), InvestigationTracker (JSON), KnowledgeGraph (JSON graph) persist across heartbeat cycles.

### Critical path
Topic → Agent reasoning → Skill chain → Artifact generation → Needs broadcast → Reactor scanning/fulfillment → Synthesis artifact → Post to INFINITE → Community engagement (votes/actions) → Notification → Next cycle gap detection.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Lightweight global index vs. full payload exchange:** Index enables fast cross-agent scanning but requires agents to fetch full payloads from peer stores (potential latency/availability issues).
- **Deterministic pressure scoring vs. LLM-based need prioritization:** Scoring is transparent and reproducible but may miss nuanced scientific value an LLM could assess.
- **Domain gating (preferred_tools) vs. open synthesis:** Restricts cross-domain data flow but `synthesis`/`peer_validation` types are exempt to allow integration.

### Failure signatures
- **Stagnation:** Long paths of artifacts with no children; mutation layer should fork.
- **Redundancy:** Sibling artifacts (same parent) with >70% identical payload keys; mutation should merge.
- **Conflict:** Siblings with same key, different values; mutation should graft or merge.
- **Karma decay:** Agent's karma drops below posting threshold (karma < 50) → cannot contribute new posts.
- **Consumed-needs thrash:** Same need repeatedly broadcast but never fulfilled; check skill coverage.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Single-agent skill chain:** Run `run_deep_investigation("protein structure of P53")` on a fresh agent. Verify artifact JSONL created with correct parent IDs and needs signal (if any). Check global index entry.
2. **Two-agent need fulfillment:** Agent A creates artifact of type `protein_data` with need `"need: assay_results"`. Agent B (with skill that outputs `assay_results` in its `preferred_tools`) runs reactor. Confirm B's artifact appears with parent=[A's artifact] and A's need marked consumed.
3. **Schema-overlap multi-parent synthesis:** Agent A produces artifact with payload `{"sequences": [...], "taxonomy": "mammalia"}`. Agent B produces artifact with payload `{"sequences": [...], "method": "esm"}`. Agent C has skill expecting `sequences` input. Run C's reactor. Verify synthesis artifact with parents=[A_id, B_id] and merged payload (newest wins on key conflict).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the autonomous mutation layer perform in preventing stagnation, redundancy, and conflict over long-term, large-scale investigations across diverse domains?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The mutation layer is described as applying fork, merge, or graft operations based on thresholds for stagnation cycles, redundancy, and conflict (Section 2.2.7). However, the case studies report artifact counts and synthesis metrics but do not evaluate the mutation layer's specific impact on DAG health, exploration diversity, or conflict resolution over many cycles. The paper states thresholds "drift stochastically" but provides no data on whether this adaptive policy successfully maintains productive exploration without human steering.
- **Why unresolved:** Without targeted metrics (e.g., rates of dead-end branches pruned, duplicate analyses merged, or conflicting findings reconciled), it is unclear if the mutation policy effectively scales or if it introduces unintended biases or inefficiencies in complex, open-ended investigations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Longitudinal analysis of DAG topology across multiple full autonomous runs (e.g., >50 heartbeat cycles) measuring branch survival rates, synthesis depth distributions, and conflict incidence before/after mutation triggers. Comparative runs with mutation disabled would isolate its contribution.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the empirical limits of the formal analogy methodology—specifically, under what conditions does schema-overlap matching and grammar synthesis produce spurious or brittle analogies between distant domains?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The urban–grain analogy investigation explicitly notes the analogy is "structurally coherent but incomplete" and "brittle," depending critically on a hub mapping whose removal disconnects the network (Section 3.5). The authors state the result is "not mathematical isomorphism" but a "constrained formal analogy."
- **Why unresolved:** The criteria for distinguishing robust from brittle analogies are not formalized. The paper does not test the grammar's predictive power on out-of-sample data from either domain or probe sensitivity to minor changes in the ontology or feature extraction.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic perturbation studies: (a) applying the same pipeline to domain pairs known to be unrelated should yield low-scoring, unstable grammars; (b) the derived grammar should generate predictions testable in one domain that are validated in the other (e.g., specific urban growth patterns predicting grain boundary velocities).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the framework's decentralized coordination scale to hundreds or thousands of heterogeneous agents without degradation in need-matching efficiency or artifact synthesis quality?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The case studies involve 8–13 agents and a 300+ skill registry. The system design emphasizes "emergent" coordination via global index scanning and pressure scoring (Section 2.2.5). However, computational complexity of scanning all artifacts/needs with schema-overlap matching and pressure scoring is not analyzed. The global index is described as "lightweight" but no benchmarks for lookup/ranking latencies with scalable agent populations are provided.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates functionality at modest scale but does not address whether the reactor's O(n²) or worse matching complexity becomes a bottleneck. Pressure scoring heuristics (novelty, centrality, depth, age) may also behave differently with many more agents, potentially leading to starvation or redundant synthesis.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Simulation or empirical stress-testing with synthetic agent populations (100–1000 agents) measuring: (a) need-to-fulfillment latency distributions, (b) rate of multi-parent synthesis events per cycle, (c) DAG growth patterns (e.g., fan-out vs. convergence), and (d) system resource usage (I/O, CPU) of the global index and reactor.

## Limitations
- The emergent coordination mechanism's scalability to large populations of heterogeneous agents remains unproven; homogeneous agents may lead to redundancy and trivial need convergence.
- The autonomous mutation layer's long-term impact on DAG health and exploration diversity is not evaluated; thresholds may require tuning to avoid premature pruning or excessive branching.
- The karma-based governance system lacks evidence of resistance to gaming, risking decoupling of reputation from rigor and potentially incentivizing engagement over sound provenance.

## Confidence
- Emergent coordination via artifact exchange: Medium
- Immutable DAG & autonomous mutation: High
- INFINITE governance & karma incentives: Low
