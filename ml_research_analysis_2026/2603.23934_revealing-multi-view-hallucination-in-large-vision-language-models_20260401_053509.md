# Revealing Multi-View Hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23934
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23934
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** RSCD, a training-free decoding technique, reduces multi-view hallucination, improving MVH-Score by up to 34.6 points on LLaVA-OneVision-7B and 21.1 points on Qwen2.5-VL-7B compared to existing methods.

## Executive Summary
Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) often struggle with multi-view hallucinations (MVH), where they confuse visual information across different viewpoints or instances. This paper introduces MVH-Bench, a benchmark of 4.8k question-answer pairs designed to isolate two types of MVH: cross-instance and cross-view hallucination. Evaluating 15 state-of-the-art LVLMs reveals uniformly poor performance, highlighting the prevalence of MVH. To mitigate this, the authors propose Reference Shift Contrastive Decoding (RSCD), a training-free method that suppresses visual interference by masking text-to-text attention in intermediate layers and contrasting original and negative logits. Experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-OneVision-7B show RSCD substantially outperforms prior hallucination mitigation techniques, with absolute gains of up to 34.6 points on the MVH-Score metric.

## Method Summary
MVH-Bench constructs multi-view QA pairs by swapping descriptors across instances and views, creating binary and multiple-choice questions that require consistent answers across paired queries to succeed. This isolates grounding errors where the model binds the wrong visual reference to the query. RSCD operates by first identifying a critical range of middle layers (Λ⋆) where text-to-text attention is essential for query context formation. During decoding, it masks a fraction (ρ) of the most-attended text tokens in these layers, generating a negative distribution that reflects responses based on an incomplete query. The original and negative logits are combined via `logit_rscd = (1+α)*original_logit - α*negative_logit`, amplifying predictions that are consistent with full query context. The method is training-free and reuses the KV cache of visual tokens for efficiency.

## Key Results
- All 15 evaluated LVLMs performed poorly on MVH-Bench, with the best model achieving only 50.2% pair accuracy, confirming widespread MVH.
- RSCD significantly outperforms existing hallucination mitigation methods (VCD, ICD, AvisC), yielding absolute improvements of up to 21.1 points on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 34.6 points on LLaVA-OneVision-7B on the primary MVH-Score.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that masking in middle layers (e.g., layers 13–20) and tuning ρ around 0.9 yields optimal performance, while disrupting text-to-text attention increases reference errors and shifts attention to irrelevant visual regions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Disrupting Incomplete Query Context
RSCD mitigates MVH by generating a negative distribution that corresponds to responses arising from a partially parsed textual query. It applies a reference shift mask to text-to-text attention in a selected range of middle layers (Λ⋆) to disrupt the formation of a complete referential context. The final logits combine the original and negative logits, amplifying predictions that rely on the full query versus those susceptible to visual interference. Evidence: The authors show that blocking text-to-text attention in middle layers reduces reference accuracy (Figure 4), confirming these layers are critical for grounding. The method’s superiority over visual-corrupting approaches (e.g., VCD) suggests MVH originates from textual context deficits rather than over-reliance on visual priors.

### Mechanism 2: Isolating MVH via Paired Question Design
MVH-Bench isolates two MVH types by constructing pairs of questions that swap descriptors between instances (cross-instance) or across views (cross-view). The metrics (p-Acc, q-Acc) require consistent correctness across both questions, penalizing models that rely on a single reference. This design ensures that failures stem from incorrect binding of query to visual evidence rather than general multimodal capability gaps. Evidence: The benchmark’s explicit decoupling of instance and view descriptors (Section 2.1) and the low scores even for top models demonstrate that current LVLMs cannot robustly resolve referential ambiguity in multi-view settings.

### Mechanism 3: Visual Interference from Partial Parsing
MVH is driven by competition among multiple valid visual references when the query is incomplete. The paper shows that disrupting text-to-text attention causes models to attend to irrelevant regions and increases yes-error/adversarial-error ratios. Moreover, replacing distractors with non-existent descriptors in MVH-Bench reduces these errors, proving that models latch onto visible but wrong objects due to insufficient query grounding. Evidence: Figures 6 and 7 and Tables 2,5 illustrate the shift in attention and error patterns under different ablations, supporting the claim that MVH is a distinct hallucination mode from single-view object hallucination.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Transformer Self-Attention & Causal Masking**
  - *Why needed:* RSCD modifies attention patterns within decoder layers; understanding how tokens mix information via self-attention and causal masking is essential to see how masking text-to-text pathways disrupts context formation.
  - *Quick check:* In a decoder-only transformer, how does the causal mask restrict information flow between tokens, and what would happen if text tokens could not attend to each other?

- **Concept: Contrastive Decoding**
  - *Why needed:* RSCD is a form of contrastive decoding; it constructs a negative distribution (via masked forward pass) and subtracts it from the original distribution to suppress predictions arising from incomplete query parsing.
  - *Quick check:* Why might amplifying the difference between original logits and negative logits reduce certain types of errors, and what is a risk of this approach?

- **Concept: Multi-View Vision-Language Integration**
  - *Why needed:* MVH arises when multiple views are present; understanding how LVLMs process interleaved image tokens from different viewpoints and fuse them with text is necessary to see why interference occurs.
  - *Quick check:* In a typical LVLM (e.g., LLaVA), how are visual features from multiple images combined with text tokens before being fed to the language model, and where might interference between views first manifest?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Multi-view input processor (extracts frames, encodes images) → Fusion transformer (concatenates image tokens and text query into a single sequence for LLM) → RSCD module (identifies Λ⋆, applies reference shift mask, runs parallel forward pass, combines logits) → MVH-Bench validator (computes p-Acc/q-Acc on paired questions)

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Receive multi-view image pair and text query.  
  2. Run base forward pass through the LLM to obtain original logits.  
  3. For layers in Λ⋆, apply the reference shift mask (top ρ% text-to-text attention per token) during a second forward pass to generate negative logits.  
  4. Compute adjusted logits: `logit_rscd = (1+α)*original_logit - α*negative_logit`.  
  5. Decode and compare answers to both paired questions to compute MVH metrics.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Masking strength (ρ) vs. usefulness:* ρ too low yields weak negative distribution; ρ too high (e.g., 1.0) masks necessary context and degrades performance (Section 5.2). Optimal around 0.9.  
  - *Layer range selection:* Middle layers (e.g., 13–20 in LLaVA-OneVision) are most effective; early layers damage foundational representations, later layers may have already formed query context (Figure 5a).  
  - *Efficiency:* RSCD avoids altering visual tokens, so KV cache for image tokens is reused; only text-to-text attention in selected layers is recomputed, making it cheaper than methods that modify image inputs (Section C.4).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High yes-error/adversarial-error ratios persist after RSCD → interference source not adequately disrupted by text-context masking (maybe visual feature entanglement).  
  - Performance drops on non-MVH tasks (e.g., single-image QA) → Λ⋆ selection overlapped with layers critical for general reasoning rather than specific multi-view grounding.  
  - No improvement when applying masking to late layers → query context formed earlier; masking too late is ineffective.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Layer sensitivity validation:** On a held-out MVH-Bench validation set, vary the start and end of Λ⋆ and measure changes in MVH-Score to confirm middle-layer sensitivity.  
  2. **Ablation on ρ and α:** Sweep ρ in [0.3, 0.9] and α in [0.5, 2.0] on Qwen2.5-VL-7B to reproduce the trend in Figure 5c and identify a stable operating region.  
  3. **Baseline comparison replication:** Implement VCD, ICD, AvisC and compare against RSCD on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-OneVision-7B with fixed random seed; report mean MVH-Score over 3 runs to verify the claimed improvements (Table 3).

## Limitations
- **Benchmark generalizability:** MVH-Bench uses synthetic descriptor-swapping; it is unclear if findings transfer to real-world multi-view scenes with natural language variation.
- **Architectural dependence:** The identified layer range (Λ⋆) and masking hyperparameters may not generalize to LVLMs with different vision-language fusion schemes.
- **Mechanism assumptions:** The premise that disrupting mid-layer text-to-text attention uniquely reveals “incomplete query parsing” lacks direct causal probing (e.g., attention flow visualization across views).

## Confidence
- **High:** MVH-Bench’s design systematically isolates grounding errors via paired questions; the benchmark provides reliable measurement.
- **Medium:** RSCD’s consistent gains across two models suggest broad efficacy, but the method depends on hyperparameters (Λ⋆, ρ, α) that require tuning.
- **Low:** The claim that MVH is primarily driven by visual interference from partial query parsing—alternative explanations (e.g., dataset bias, tokenization issues) are not fully ruled out.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-architecture sweep:** Apply RSCD to at least five additional LVLMs (e.g., InternVL, CogVLM) with varying fusion patterns; report success rate of transferring the optimal Λ⋆ and hyperparameters.
2. **Real-world MVH probe:** Curate a small set of unscripted multi-view videos (e.g., from Egocentric datasets), manually annotate hallucination instances, and compare model performance on this set with MVH-Bench scores.
3. **Attention causality test:** For a fixed model, intervene on text-to-text attention in Λ⋆ layers by masking versus amplifying and measure changes in object-attention maps (e.g., via GradCAM) to verify whether query grounding improves.
