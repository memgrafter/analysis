# Kareus: Joint Reduction of Dynamic and Static Energy in Large Model Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.17654
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17654
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Kareus reduces training energy by up to 28.3% at equal time, or time by up to 27.5% at equal energy, outperforming Perseus+Nanobatching.

## Executive Summary
Large model training is increasingly energy-constrained, but existing systems optimize dynamic or static energy in isolation. Kareus tackles the joint optimization problem by co-tuning fine-grained kernel scheduling (SM allocation, launch timing) and GPU frequency. It introduces a partitioned overlap execution model that breaks the overwhelming global search into manageable per-partition optimizations, each solved via multi-objective Bayesian optimization to characterize the time-energy frontier. The resulting schedules achieve up to 28.3% lower energy for the same training time, or up to 27.5% faster training for the same energy budget, on Transformer models with tensor/context parallelism, surpassing prior state-of-the-art approaches.

## Method Summary
Kareus operates in two phases. First, an offline optimizer scans the model’s execution graph to identify repeating partitions—each consisting of a communication kernel overlapped with a sequence of independent computations. For every partition type, it evaluates candidate configurations (GPU frequency, SM allocation, launch timing) using thermally stable profiling and employs multi-pass multi-objective Bayesian optimization (with XGBoost surrogates) to trace the Pareto frontier of execution time and dynamic energy. Second, during training, a specialized execution engine enforces the chosen configurations: it launches kernels with precise CUDA stream/event timing, controls SM usage via grid size, and adjusts GPU frequency asynchronously. A fallback switch selects between partitioned overlap and sequential execution depending on workload characteristics.

## Key Results
- Energy reduction: up to 28.3% (iso-time) compared to Nanobatching+Perseus.
- Time reduction: up to 27.5% (iso-energy) compared to Nanobatching+Perseus.
- Outperforms both baseline Megatron-LM and prior energy-aware systems across multiple model scales.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Interdependent impact of SM allocation, launch timing, and GPU frequency
- **Claim**: These three knobs jointly and interdependently affect both dynamic and static energy consumption.
- **Mechanism**: Frequency changes alter kernel characteristics (compute-bound vs. memory-bound), which in turn modify the optimal overlap partners and SM distribution; SM allocation governs resource contention during overlap, while launch timing determines which kernels compete.
- **Core assumption**: Kernel behavior (resource utilization) is sufficiently sensitive to frequency to shift the optimal schedule at different frequencies.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: “fine-grained kernel scheduling and frequency scaling jointly and interdependently impact both dynamic and static energy consumption.”
  - Section 3.2.4: Energy-optimal SM allocation and launch timing depend on GPU frequency.
  - Section 3.2.3: At lower frequencies, kernels become more compute-bound, changing the optimal schedule.

### Mechanism 2: Partitioned overlap model decomposes global optimization
- **Claim**: Decomposing the execution graph into repeating partitions transforms an intractable global search into independent local subproblems.
- **Mechanism**: Each partition overlaps one communication kernel (e.g., AllReduce) with a contiguous sequence of computation kernels that have no data dependencies. Optimizing each partition separately and then composing the partial frontiers yields the overall frontier without exponential enumeration.
- **Core assumption**: The graph contains many identical or similar partitions that can be separately optimized and combined additively.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: “Kareus addresses this by introducing a partitioned overlap execution model to decompose the intractable global optimization into local subproblems.”
  - Section 4.2: Definition of a partition and its flexibility in overlap choices.
  - Section 4.4: Hierarchical composition of partition frontiers into microbatch and iteration frontiers.

### Mechanism 3: Multi-pass Bayesian optimization efficiently explores mixed-parameter space
- **Claim**: Multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MBO) discovers accurate time-energy frontiers for each partition despite costly evaluations and mixed discrete/continuous parameters.
- **Mechanism**: XGBoost surrogate models predict time and dynamic energy from SM allocation, launch timing, and frequency. A multi-pass acquisition strategy samples candidates via hypervolume improvement (exploitation), uncertainty (exploration), and separate passes targeting total/dynamic/static energy. Static energy is derived from predicted time × measured static power.
- **Core assumption**: Surrogates can generalize from limited measured configurations to rank candidates in the continuous time-energy space.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: “applies multi-objective Bayesian optimization to each partition’s time-energy frontier.”
  - Section 4.3: Details of surrogate model and multi-pass candidate selection.
  - Section 6.4: Breakdown of contributions; random init ~35%, passes ~65% of selected candidates.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Dynamic vs. Static GPU Power
- **Why needed here**: The optimization targets both dynamic (workload-dependent) and static (idle) energy. Misunderstanding their split leads to suboptimal frequency-time trade-offs.
- **Quick check**: If you halve execution time while keeping average frequency constant, by what factor does static energy change?
  - **Answer**: Halved (static energy = static power × time).

### Concept: Nanobatching and Kernel Overlap
- **Why needed here**: Nanobatching is the baseline technique for overlapping communication and computation in model-parallel training. Kareus generalizes it by adding fine-grained control over SM allocation and frequency.
- **Quick check**: Why does splitting a microbatch into nanobatches create overlap opportunities?
  - **Answer**: Because data dependencies between nanobatches are removed, allowing communication from one nanobatch to run concurrently with computation from another.

### Concept: Pareto Frontier and Multi-Objective Optimization
- **Why needed here**: Kareus does not choose a single point but constructs a frontier of time-energy trade-offs, enabling users to pick configurations under constraints.
- **Quick check**: What does it mean for a configuration to dominate the frontier?
  - **Answer**: Configuration A dominates configuration B if A is at least as good as B in all objectives (time and energy) and strictly better in at least one objective. The Pareto frontier consists of all configurations that are not dominated by any other configuration.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Partition Detector → MBO Optimizer (using Thermally Stable Profiler) → Frontier Composer → Partitioned Overlap Execution Engine (with Frequency Controller). Fallback Switch chooses between partitioned and sequential execution per workload.

### Critical Path
1. Workload is analyzed offline: Partition Detector identifies partitions.
2. For each partition type, MBO Optimizer uses Thermally Stable Profiler to measure candidates and build a frontier.
3. Frontier Composer merges partition frontiers into microbatch-level and iteration-level fronts.
4. During training, Partitioned Overlap Execution Engine schedules kernels with the selected SM allocations, launch timings, and Frequency Controller sets GPU frequencies accordingly. The Fallback Switch may bypass partitioning for small or irregular workloads.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Partition granularity**: Finer partitions increase flexibility but explode composition complexity. Kareus groups short memory-bound kernels and fuses consecutive communications to keep partitions coarse.
- **Frequency uniformity**: To avoid switching overhead, a single frequency is enforced across all partitions within a microbatch; this slightly shrinks the frontier.
- **Sequential vs. partitioned**: For very small models, sequential execution may be more energy-efficient due to nanobatching’s reduced arithmetic intensity. Kareus automatically includes sequential candidates in the frontier.

### Failure Signatures
- **Poor frontier expansion**: MBO passes yield few new candidates; check thermal stability of profiler and acquisition hyperparameters.
- **Excessive overhead**: Profiling dominates runtime; ensure sufficient cooldown and inspect measurement window settings.
- **Unexpected slowdown**: Selected configuration may violate performance expectations; verify partition definitions for missed dependencies and check whether the uniform-config composition heuristic is too restrictive.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline comparison**: Run end-to-end training with Megatron-LM, Nanobatching+Perseus, and Kareus on a small model (e.g., Llama 3.2 3B); confirm Kareus achieves a superior time-energy frontier.
2. **Partition validation**: For a fixed partition (e.g., Attention–AllReduce), sweep frequency and SM allocation and measure energy; verify that the optimal launch timing shifts with frequency.
3. **Profiler stability test**: Repeatedly profile an identical configuration with and without thermal cooldown; demonstrate that cooldown reduces energy measurement variance below 1%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Cross-partition resource interactions**: How do concurrent partitions contend for shared resources, and does the additive composition assumption hold under interference? The paper does not model or evaluate this effect.
2. **Frequency switching overhead**: Uniform frequency within a microbatch is adopted due to switching costs; can overhead be lowered to enable per-partition scaling, and how much energy would that save?
3. **Profiling overhead reduction**: MBO profiling dominates cost (~97% of 2 hours). Can transfer learning or cheaper surrogates reduce the number of required measurements for many partitions?
4. **Online adaptation**: Kareus performs offline optimization and assumes static conditions; can it adapt to dynamics like network congestion or power caps without costly re-profiling?

## Limitations
- **Reproduction barriers**: Exact partitioning rules and MBO hyperparameters are unspecified, hindering precise replication.
- **Generalizability**: Evaluation limited to Transformer models on NVIDIA A100 GPUs; applicability to other architectures (e.g., Mixture-of-Experts, diffusion) or newer hardware is unknown.
- **Decomposition assumptions**: The partitioned model presumes repeating execution patterns; irregular graphs may break the pattern and inflate composition cost.

## Confidence
- Interdependence of frequency, SM allocation, and launch timing: Medium (evidence from frequency-dependent experiments but limited model scales).
- Partitioned overlap model’s decomposition: Medium (empirical validation on Transformer graphs; theoretical guarantees unknown).
- Multi-objective Bayesian optimization accuracy: Medium (surrogate generalization not thoroughly analyzed across diverse kernels).
- Achieved energy reductions (up to 28.3% iso‑time, 27.5% iso‑energy): Low (evaluated on limited hardware and models; transferability uncertain).

## Next Checks
1. **Partition sensitivity test**: For a fixed partition (e.g., attention + AllReduce), sweep GPU frequency and SM allocation and identify the energy‑optimal launch timing at each frequency; confirm that optimal timing shifts with frequency.
2. **Decomposition loss quantification**: Construct a synthetic microfbenchmark with a known global optimum; apply Kareus’s partitioning and composition; compute the hypervolume difference between composed frontier and true global optimum to measure suboptimality.
3. **Thermal profiling consistency**: Profile the same configuration repeatedly with (a) no cooldown, (b) 5‑s cooldown, and (c) 10‑s cooldown; show that increasing cooldown reduces energy variance below 1%.
