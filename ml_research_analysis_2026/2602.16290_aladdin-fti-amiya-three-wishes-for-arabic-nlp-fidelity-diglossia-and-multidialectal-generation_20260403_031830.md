# Aladdin-FTI @ AMIYA Three Wishes for Arabic NLP: Fidelity, Diglossia, and Multidialectal Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16290  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16290  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Joint MT+GEN training balances diglossia (ChrF++) and dialectal fidelity (Macro ADI2), with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct achieving ChrF++ 35.09 and Macro ADI2 0.233—comparable to GPT-OSS-120B (Macro ADI2 0.237) despite far fewer parameters.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the under-representation of Arabic dialects in natural language processing, where dominance of Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) in training data suppresses dialectal variation. It introduces Aladdin-FTI, a joint training framework that simultaneously learns machine translation (MT) between MSA, dialects, and English (addressing *diglossia*) and instruction-based dialectal generation (ensuring *fidelity*). Using closed-track data from the AMIYA shared task covering five dialects, the authors show that MT alone yields strong translation (ChrF++) but near-zero dialectal fidelity (Macro ADI2), while generation alone improves fidelity but degrades translation. The combined objective (MT+GEN) achieves a favorable balance: a 3B parameter model (SmolLM3-3B) and an 8B instruction-tuned model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) match or exceed the fidelity of a 120B model (GPT-OSS-120B), demonstrating the efficiency of the joint approach.

## Method Summary
Aladdin-FTI employs multi-task learning with a weighted loss: λ·L_MT + (1-λ)·L_GEN. The MT task uses instruction-formatted parallel sentences (e.g., “Translate from X into Y: <source> <target>”) from bilingual corpora (MADAR, SauDial, Shami, etc.) spanning five dialects and English. The generation task uses instruction-formatted sentence completion (prefix + dialectal full sentence) from monolingual dialectal corpora. Two model families are explored: SmolLM3-3B (full fine-tuning) and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (LoRA). Training uses 4 epochs, AdamW, cosine LR scheduling, and a LR sweep {2e-5, 3e-5, 5e-5, 6e-5}. Checkpoint selection on the dev set relies on macro-average ChrF++ across translation tasks, with a decoding hyperparameter sweep (top-p and temperature). Final evaluation uses the AL-QASIDA benchmark, which provides monolingual and cross-lingual prompts for dialectal generation and translation.

## Key Results
- **MT-only (λ=1):** High ChrF++ but near-zero Macro ADI2 → strong translation quality but poor dialectal authenticity.
- **GEN-only (λ=0):** High Macro ADI2 but low ChrF++ → dialectal generation at the cost of translation.
- **Joint MT+GEN (λ=0.5):** Balances both metrics: SmolLM3-3B reaches ChrF++ 33.65 / Macro ADI2 0.067; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct reaches 35.09 / 0.233, matching GPT-OSS-120B (0.237) despite 15× fewer parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Joint training forces the model to learn complementary representations from both objectives. The MT task provides parallel links between MSA and dialects, grounding the model in the standard-dialect continuum and improving diglossia handling (ChrF++). The generation task exposes the model to natural dialectal text without MSA interference, reinforcing dialect-specific patterns and boosting fidelity (Macro ADI2). The weighted loss (λ) balances their influence; empirically, λ=0.5 yields the best trade-off. This synergy allows smaller models to acquire capabilities that typically require much larger MSA-centric models, as confirmed by the near-parity with GPT-OSS-120B on Macro ADI2.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diglossia:** Coexistence of MSA and regional dialects in Arabic. *Why needed:* The paper’s core challenge is translation across the MSA-dialect divide without losing authenticity. *Quick check:* Does the model translate between MSA and a dialect while preserving meaning?
- **Dialectal Fidelity:** Authenticity of generated text to a specific dialect’s lexicon, syntax, and phonology. *Why needed:* MSA-trained models often produce “MSA-ized” outputs; fidelity (Macro ADI2) quantifies deviation. *Quick check:* Given a prompt, does the output sound like natural dialect rather than MSA?
- **Multi-task Learning (Joint Training):** Training a single model on multiple objectives with a combined loss. *Why needed:* The paper hypothesizes that combining MT and generation mitigates their individual weaknesses. *Quick check:* Does a joint model improve both translation quality and dialectal fidelity compared to single-task models?
- **Instruction Tuning:** Formatting tasks as natural language instructions to guide model behavior. *Why needed:* Enables one model to perform diverse tasks without task-specific heads. *Quick check:* Does the model correctly interpret and follow instructions for MT vs. generation?
- **Metrics: ChrF++ and Macro ADI2:** ChrF++ is a character-level F-score for translation; Macro ADI2 is a sentence-level metric for dialect match. *Why needed:* Provide orthogonal evaluation of diglossia and fidelity. *Quick check:* Do the metrics correlate with human judgments for their respective aspects?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data (MT parallel & GEN monolingual) → Instruction formatters → Combined dataset → Model (SmolLM3-3B / Llama-3.1-8B) → Compute L_MT & L_GEN → Weighted loss (λ) → Optimizer → Checkpoint.  
**Critical path:** (1) Collect parallel and monolingual dialect corpora; (2) Format into instruction templates; (3) Initialize model; (4) Train with weighted loss; (5) Select best checkpoint via dev set; (6) Evaluate on AL-QASIDA test sets.  
**Design tradeoffs:** λ weighting (MT vs. generation balance), model size (capacity vs. compute), LoRA vs. full fine-tuning (efficiency vs. capacity), decoding hyperparameter sweep (fluency vs. diversity).  
**Failure signatures:** (i) High ChrF++ but low Macro ADI2 → MT dominance (λ too high); (ii) High Macro ADI2 but low ChrF++ → GEN dominance (λ too low); (iii) Overfitting → dev losses diverge from training losses.  
**First three experiments:** 1) Train MT-only (λ=1); 2) Train GEN-only (λ=0); 3) Train joint (λ=0.5) and compare both metrics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
No explicit open questions are raised in the paper. The authors note the evaluation is limited to five dialects and the AL-QASIDA benchmark, leaving broader generalization unexplored.

## Limitations
- The exact λ value for the reported MT+GEN result is unspecified, hindering precise replication.
- Data splits, tokenization details, and preprocessing nuances are insufficiently described, affecting reproducibility.
- Generalizability is restricted to the five covered dialects and the AL-QASIDA benchmark; performance on other dialects or domains remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** MT-only improves ChrF++ but not Macro ADI2. **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Claim:** GEN-only improves Macro ADI2 but degrades ChrF++. **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Claim:** Joint MT+GEN balances both metrics. **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Claim:** Smaller models (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B) achieve performance comparable to GPT-OSS-120B on Macro ADI2. **Confidence:** Low.

## Next Checks
1. Re-run joint training with explicit λ=0.5 and report both metrics; also perform a fine-grained λ sweep (0.1–0.9) to locate the optimal trade-off.
2. Evaluate the final models on an external Arabic dialect test set (e.g., a held-out portion of MADAR or a new social-media corpus) to assess generalizability beyond AL-QASIDA.
3. Compute per-dialect Macro ADI2 breakdowns to identify which dialects benefit from joint training and whether any are systematically under-performed.
