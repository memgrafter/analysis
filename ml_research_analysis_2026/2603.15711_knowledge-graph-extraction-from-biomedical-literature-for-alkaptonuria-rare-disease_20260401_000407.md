# Knowledge Graph Extraction from Biomedical Literature for Alkaptonuria Rare Disease

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.15711  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15711  
- Reference count: 38  
- Primary result: Built two knowledge graphs from PubMed for alkaptonuria; validated against external databases; identified drug repurposing candidates (Vitamin C, Metformin, Romosozumab) and systemic interactions in inflammation, bone remodeling, oxidative stress.

## Executive Summary
Alkaptonuria (AKU) is a rare disease poorly represented in biomedical knowledge graphs. This study applies automated text mining (PubTator3) on PubMed literature to construct two knowledge graphs: an extended network capturing a broad set of interactions and a high-confidence network filtered by multi-publication support. Validation against STRING, DGIdb, and KEGG demonstrates that the high-confidence network accurately covers known biological pathways (e.g., tyrosine metabolism) while the extended network reveals potential comorbidities. Graph-based analyses highlight roles of inflammation (TNF, IL1B), bone remodeling (TTR, SOST), and oxidative stress, and suggest drug repurposing opportunities. The approach provides a scalable pipeline for rare-disease knowledge extraction.

## Method Summary
The authors first retrieved PubMed articles using seed terms ("Alkaptonuria", "HGD", "homogentisic acid") and annotated entities (genes, diseases, chemicals, variants) and relations via PubTator3, restricting to primary research. They then extended the dataset by using 188 high-confidence entities (confidence >0.7) as new seeds, querying up to 2000 recent articles per entity, and aggregating annotations. Relations were filtered by removing "Comparison" type and keeping those with average confidence >0.7, or average >0.5 with support from >3 publications. Variants were mapped to genes, and overly generic high-degree nodes (e.g., "Disease") were manually removed. Networks were built as undirected weighted graphs, with edge weights reflecting publication support. The high-confidence network further required edges with ≥2 publications and retained only the connected component containing "Alkaptonuria". Validation compared subnetworks to STRING (experimental gene-gene), DGIdb (gene-drug), and KEGG pathway hsa00350 via direct edge overlap and shortest paths. Knowledge extraction employed graph metrics, community detection (Leiden), centrality measures, and meta-path analysis.

## Key Results
- Constructed two KGs: extended (27,252 nodes, 261,649 edges) and high-confidence (1,450 nodes, 3,307 edges).
- High-confidence KG shows 22.2% direct edge overlap with KEGG tyrosine pathway and average shortest path 2.79 for indirect coverage; also covers STRING/DGIdb interactions via 3–4 step paths.
- Network analysis highlights inflammation (TNF, IL1B), bone remodeling (TTR, SOST), oxidative stress as systemic interactions; identifies repurposing candidates: Vitamin C, Metformin, Romosozumab.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The methodology succeeds because PubTator3 provides accurate, high-throughput annotation of biomedical literature, capturing a wide range of entity types and relations. Filtering by multi-publication support and confidence thresholds reduces noise, while manual curation of generic hubs further refines network specificity. Validation against established databases (STRING, DGIdb, KEGG) confirms that the high-confidence network retains known biological mechanisms (e.g., tyrosine metabolism) and that the extended network expands into comorbidities without sacrificing relevance. The graph-based extraction then reliably surfaces novel associations and candidate drugs by leveraging global network topology and community structure.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Graph (KG)**: A graph-structured representation of entities and relations; needed to integrate heterogeneous biomedical data. Quick check: Can define nodes, edges, and types.
- **Text Mining**: Automated extraction of information from text; PubTator3 is a tool for biomedical entity/relation extraction. Quick check: Knows PubTator3 outputs entities like genes, diseases, and relations with confidence.
- **Network Validation**: Comparing a constructed KG against gold-standard databases to assess correctness. Quick check: Understands direct overlap and shortest path metrics.
- **Graph Metrics**: Measures like degree distribution, clustering coefficient, assortativity that characterize network structure. Quick check: Can explain what high clustering implies.
- **Community Detection**: Algorithms (e.g., Leiden) to find clusters of densely connected nodes; used to identify functional modules. Quick check: Knows that communities may correspond to biological pathways.
- **Drug Repurposing**: Using existing drugs for new indications; KG provides hypothesis generation. Quick check: Recognizes that network proximity between drug and disease targets can suggest repurposing.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Seed Selection -> PubMed Query -> PubTator3 Annotation -> Entity/Relation Extraction -> Filtering (confidence, publication count) -> Manual Curation (generic nodes, variant mapping) -> Network Construction (weighted) -> Validation (STRING/DGIdb/KEGG) -> Knowledge Extraction (metrics, communities, centrality, meta-paths)

Critical path: The core workflow follows the sequence above, with the initial seed retrieval and annotation feeding the extension iteration. The most critical decisions are confidence and publication thresholds, and manual curation steps.

Design tradeoffs: (1) Automated PubTator3 versus manual curation: Speed and scale versus precision; (2) Extended network breadth versus high-confidence network specificity: The extended network captures broader comorbidity signals but includes more noise; (3) Undirected weighted edges simplify analysis but lose directed mechanistic information (e.g., activation/inhibition).

Failure signatures: (1) Many high-degree generic nodes (e.g., "Disease", "Cell") remain after curation, indicating incomplete filtering; (2) Unusually high number of variant nodes not linked to genes, suggesting mapping failure; (3) Validation overlap with KEGG/STRING is near zero, implying annotation or filtering is too strict/lenient.

First three experiments:
1. Replicate the initial seed-based retrieval and PubTator3 annotation on a small subset (e.g., 10 articles) to verify extraction pipeline.
2. Perform variant-to-gene mapping on a sample of variant nodes and cross-check against dbSNP to confirm correctness.
3. Recompute node degrees on the extended network and list top-degree nodes; evaluate whether they are biologically specific or generic.

## Limitations
- Uncertainties: PubTator3 access method and parameters not specified; manual node removal lacks explicit list and degree calculation details; variant-to-gene mapping rules unclear; no reported quality assessment (e.g., inter-annotator agreement).
- Generalizability: The pipeline is applied only to AKU; performance for other rare diseases may vary.
