# Structural Stress and Learned Helplessness in Afghanistan: A Multi-Layer Analysis of the AFSTRESS Dari Corpus

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27233
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27233
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Created AFSTRESS, a 737-narrative Dari corpus with 12 stress/emotion labels; structural stressors dominate (uncertain future 62.6%, education closure 60.0%); high label cardinality (5.54) indicates chronic multi-dimensional stress; character TF-IDF with Linear SVM and tuned thresholds achieved Macro-F1=0.651.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the absence of Dari NLP resources for crisis stress analysis by constructing AFSTRESS, a multi-label corpus of 737 self-reported narratives from Afghans, annotated with 5 emotional and 7 structural stressor labels. Analysis reveals structural stressors—particularly uncertain future and education closure—are more prevalent than primary emotional states like stress. The high mean label cardinality (5.54) and density (0.462) suggest a chronic, multi-domain burden consistent with allostatic load. Strong co-occurrence between hopelessness and uncertain future aligns with learned helplessness theory. Computational experiments show character-level TF-IDF features with a Linear SVM, combined with per-label threshold tuning, outperform transformer models on this small dataset.

## Method Summary
The authors collected 737 Dari narratives via an anonymous survey, with respondents self-selecting applicable labels from a predefined schema of 5 emotions (stress, anxiety, sadness, burnout, hopelessness) and 7 stressors (uncertain future, education closure, economic issues, family, social, displacement, health). After addressing a Unicode normalization bug that corrupted some label columns, the data was split 70/15/15% (515/111/111). The primary model used character 2–5-gram TF-IDF vectors (max_features=30,000, min_df=2, sublinear_tf=True) with a one-vs-rest Linear SVM. A critical step involved optimizing classification thresholds per label on the validation set (search range [0.35, 0.80]) to address class imbalance, which substantially improved Micro-F1.

## Key Results
- Structural stressors (uncertain future: 62.6%, education closure: 60.0%) were more prevalent than primary emotional labels (stress: 58.1%, hopelessness: 55.9%).
- High mean label cardinality (5.54) and density (0.462), with 65% of respondents selecting ≥4 labels, indicate chronic multi-dimensional stress.
- Strong co-occurrence between hopelessness and uncertain future (Jaccard J=0.388, Pearson r=0.40) is consistent with learned helplessness; an emotional cascade pattern (Stress → Anxiety → Sadness → Burnout → Hopelessness) was observed in the label network.
- Character TF-IDF with Linear SVM achieved Macro-F1=0.651; per-label threshold tuning improved Micro-F1 by 10.3 percentage points over the default 0.5 threshold.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Structural Stressors as Primary Drivers
- **Claim:** External, uncontrollable social structures (e.g., education closure, uncertain future) are the primary sources of reported distress, not internal emotional states.
- **Mechanism:** Chronic policy-driven deprivations create pervasive, multi-source stress that manifests in self-reports. The prevalence and co-occurrence of structural and emotional labels indicate the stress source is environmental.
- **Core Assumption:** Self-reported labels reliably indicate perceived sources (stressors) and affective states (emotions). High co-occurrence (e.g., hopelessness–uncertain future) reflects a causal attribution where structural conditions shape cognitive-emotional outcomes.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract and Section 5.1 state structural stressors exceed emotional prevalence. Table 2 shows "UNCERTAIN 461 (62.6%)", "EDUCLOSE 442 (60.0%)", "STRESS 428 (58.1%)". Figure 5 reports Pearson r=0.40 between hopelessness and uncertain future.
- **Break Condition:** If a replication with a different Afghan cohort (e.g., rural, majority male) found emotional labels exceeding structural ones, the claim of structural dominance would weaken.

### Mechanism 2: High Label Cardinality as Chronic Stress Signature
- **Claim:** The high mean label cardinality (5.54) and density (0.462) are computational signatures of chronic, multi-dimensional stress (allostatic load).
- **Mechanism:** Chronic stress involves persistent activation across multiple life domains (education, economy, health, family). The multi-label schema captures this concurrent pressure, where selecting 5+ labels reflects cumulative burden.
- **Core Assumption:** The annotation schema validly captures distinct stress domains; a respondent with many active labels experiences simultaneous pressure from multiple structural and emotional sources.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states high cardinality "indicated chronic multi-dimensional stress." Section 6.2 notes mean cardinality 5.54 as the signature, with 65% of respondents having ≥4 labels. Figure 3 shows the distribution.
- **Break Condition:** If longitudinal data showed cardinality dropping sharply after an initial wave, the chronicity interpretation would be challenged.

### Mechanism 3: Emotional Cascade via Co-occurrence Pattern
- **Claim:** The Jaccard similarity network among emotion labels (Stress → Anxiety → Sadness → Burnout → Hopelessness) reflects an emotional cascade where poorly regulated stress sequentially amplifies into more severe states.
- **Mechanism:** Initial stress triggers anxious anticipation; failed coping leads to sadness; prolonged sadness depletes resources into burnout; final collapse yields hopelessness. Decreasing Jaccard similarity from Stress outward reflects this deteriorating sequence.
- **Core Assumption:** Co-occurrence patterns in cross-sectional data can be interpreted as a temporal or developmental sequence. Stronger adjacency (Stress-Anxiety, Sadness-Burnout) versus non-adjacency supports a cascade.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract mentions the cascade pattern. Section 6.3 states the sequence matches emotional cascade theory; strongest edge is Stress–Anxiety (J=0.49). Figure 6 provides edge weights showing a monotonic decrease pattern.
- **Break Condition:** If experience sampling found no temporal precedence or that hopelessness often preceded anxiety, the cascade model would be invalidated.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structural Stress Theory (Pearlin, 1989)**
  - **Why Needed:** To interpret why structural stressors (education closure, uncertain future) dominate emotional labels; it frames stress as originating in macro-level conditions (policy, economy) rather than individual appraisal.
  - **Quick Check:** If a stressor is "structural," what differentiates it from an "emotional" label? (Answer: Structural stressors are external conditions imposed by social/political systems; emotions are internal affective states.)
- **Concept: Learned Helplessness & Hopelessness Theory (Abramson et al., 1989)**
  - **Why Needed:** To explain the strong co-occurrence (r=0.40, J=0.388) between *Hopelessness* and *Uncertain future*. It links a cognitive attribution style (stable, global, uncontrollable causes) to hopelessness when facing an unchangeable structural barrier.
  - **Quick Check:** Why would an indefinite education ban specifically foster "stable and uncontrollable" attributions?
- **Concept: Allostatic Load / Chronic Stress (McEwen, 2007)**
  - **Why Needed:** To interpret the high mean label cardinality (5.54) as the computational fingerprint of chronic, multi-domain burden, distinguishing it from acute, single-source stress.
  - **Quick Check:** How would label cardinality differ between acute stress (post-disaster) and the chronic crisis context of Afghanistan?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** Raw Dari Survey Responses -> Unicode Normalization & ZWNJ Handling -> Character N-gram TF-IDF Vectorization -> Linear SVM (One-vs-Rest) -> Per-Label Threshold Optimization -> Evaluation (Micro/Macro-F1, Hamming Loss) -> Co-occurrence & Cardinality Analysis.
- **Critical Path:** 1. Unicode normalization (Arabic to Persian kaf/ya) and ZWNJ replacement. 2. Character 2–5-gram TF-IDF vectorization. 3. Linear SVM training. 4. Per-label threshold search on validation set. Skipping normalization/ZWNJ causes silent label corruption; skipping threshold tuning costs ~10 pp Micro-F1.
- **Design Tradeoffs:** 
  - *TF-IDF vs. Transformers:* Character TF-IDF wins on this small corpus (N=515 train) by avoiding overfitting and capturing Dari morphological patterns; transformers (ParsBERT, XLM-R) underperform due to data scarcity.
  - *Default vs. Tuned Thresholds:* Default 0.5 severely under-predicts positives; tuning is non-optional and yields larger gains than model selection.
  - *Character vs. Word N-grams:* Character n-grams are essential due to Dari's clitic attachment and orthographic variation; word unigrams are too sparse.
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - **Silent label corruption:** Six all-zero label columns from Unicode kaf/ya mismatch. Diagnostic: check label sums against Table 2 (e.g., EDUCLOSE should be ~442, not 0).
  - **ZWNJ-induced tokenization errors:** Tokenizers splitting words incorrectly at ZWNJ. Diagnostic: verify token count sanity (avoid extreme sparsity).
  - **Threshold mis-specification:** Using default 0.5 leads to low recall on minority labels (e.g., HEALTH F1~0.48). Diagnostic: always report tuned vs. default thresholds.
  - **Transformer overfit:** ParsBERT/XLM-R training loss drops but validation F1 stalls; early stopping is critical.
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce baseline:** Run Char TF-IDF (2–5-gram) + Linear SVM with default threshold 0.5. Expect Micro-F1 ~0.55, Macro-F1 ~0.58. Confirm the gap to tuned results.
  2. **Threshold tuning ablation:** For each label, sweep threshold ∈ [0.1,0.9] on validation to maximize Micro-F1; apply to test. Expect Micro-F1 improvement to ≥0.66, Macro-F1 to ≥0.65.
  3. **Error analysis by label:** For the tuned model, compute per-label precision/recall/F1. Predict: High-F1 labels (STRESS, EDUCLOSE, UNCERTAIN) have distinctive Dari keywords; low-F1 label (HEALTH) has varied phrasing.

## Limitations
- The AFSTRESS dataset is not yet publicly available, preventing independent verification.
- Small sample size (N=737) and single-cohort design limit generalizability across Afghanistan’s diverse contexts.
- Cross-sectional design precludes causal claims about structural stress dominance or emotional cascades.

## Confidence
- *Structural stressors dominate emotional states* – **Medium**. Prevalence data support the claim, but data integrity and external validity remain uncertain.
- *High label cardinality signals chronic multi-dimensional stress* – **Medium**. Descriptive statistics align with allostatic load theory, but without longitudinal data the chronicity inference is tentative.
- *Emotional cascade pattern* – **Low**. Co-occurrence patterns are theory-consistent but cross-sectional data cannot establish temporal precedence.

## Next Checks
1. After dataset release, reproduce the exact preprocessing pipeline (Unicode normalization, ZWNJ handling) and verify that each label’s positive count matches Table 2; re-calculate mean cardinality and density.
2. Replicate the Char TF-IDF + Linear SVM baseline with per-label threshold search, confirming Macro-F1 ≥ 0.65 and the reported 10.3-point Micro-F1 gain over default threshold.
3. Test generalizability by collecting a new Dari narrative sample from a distinct Afghan subgroup (e.g., rural, offline) and comparing structural vs. emotional label prevalences; a consistent pattern would strengthen confidence in structural dominance.
