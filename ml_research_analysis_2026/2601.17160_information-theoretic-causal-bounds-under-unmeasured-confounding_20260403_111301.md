# Information-Theoretic Causal Bounds under Unmeasured Confounding
## Quick Facts
ArXiv ID: 2601.17160. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17160. References: 40. Result: $D_f(P(Y|A=a,X)\|P(Y|do(A=a),X)) \le B_f(e_a(X))$, enabling sharp partial identification without auxiliary inputs.

## Executive Summary
We propose an information‑theoretic framework for partial identification of causal effects under unmeasured confounding. A key bound based on $f$‑divergence depends only on the propensity score, yielding valid intervals for any conditional functional (e.g., CATE) without outcome boundedness or instruments. A debiased semiparametric estimator attains $\sqrt{n}$ consistency even when nuisances are learned via machine learning, and experiments confirm coverage and tight intervals across varied settings.

## Method Summary
The bound defines an ambiguity set; the causal effect is optimized via convex duality, reducing to a finite‑dimensional problem in parameters $\lambda>0$ and $u$. Estimation cross‑fits the propensity, trains a neural network to learn $(\lambda,u)$ by minimizing a debiased loss (ensuring Neyman orthogonality), then regresses a pseudo‑outcome $Z=g^*(\phi(Y)-u/\lambda)$ to compute the bound. Results across several $f$‑divergences are aggregated via $k$‑th order statistics for robustness.

## Key Results
- Proposition: $D_f \le B_f(e)$ where $B_f(e)=e f(1/e)+(1-e)f(0)$.  
- Sharp conditional intervals for any $\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\phi(Y)]$ (including CATE) without outcome restrictions.  
- $\sqrt{n}$‑consistent inference despite ML‑estimated nuisances; experiments show valid coverage and narrow intervals across propensities, heavy‑tailed outcomes, and sample sizes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Data‑processing inequality**: $D_f(P\|Q) \le B_f(e_a(x))$. Assumes mutual absolute continuity and positivity; bound uses only $e_a(x)$.  
2. **Convex duality**: Optimizing over $Q$ with $D_f\le B_f$ yields a finite‑dimensional dual problem in $(\lambda,u)$, giving explicit primal‑dual formulas for bounds.  
3. **Debiasing + cross‑fitting**: Correction term eliminates first‑order bias from propensity estimation; orthogonality ensures $\sqrt{n}$ convergence even when nuisances converge at slower ML rates.

## Foundational Learning
- *$f$‑divergences*: discrepancy measures (e.g., KL, TV). They define the ambiguity set. **Quick check**: How does the shape of $f(t)$ affect $B_f(e)$?  
- *Propensity score & positivity*: The bound is a function of $e_a(x)$; $e\in(0,1)$ is essential. **Quick check**: Why do $e=0$ or $e=1$ break sharpness?  
- *Convex duality*: Converts infinite‑dimensional supremum over $Q$ into dual variables $(\lambda,u)$. **Quick check**: Derive $g^*$ for the $\chi^2$ divergence $f(t)=\frac12(t-1)^2$.  
- *Neyman orthogonality*: Makes estimator robust to nuisance estimation errors. **Quick check**: What is the Gateaux derivative condition for orthogonality?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Propensity estimator → $f$‑generator → Dual net → Cross‑fit → Pseudo‑outcome regressor → Aggregator → Evaluation.  
Critical path: (1) Fit $\hat e$ per fold (XGBoost). (2) Train dual MLP with debiased loss to obtain $\lambda,u$. (3) Compute $Z_i=g^*(\phi(Y_i)-u/\lambda)$. (4) Regress $Z$ on $(A,X)$ for $m$. (5) $\hat\theta=\lambda(B_f(\hat e)+m)+u$. (6) Aggregate over $f$’s via $k$‑agg.  
Tradeoffs: $f$‑choice (KL tight vs. TV robust), network capacity, number of folds, $k$, propensity model complexity.  
Failure signatures: $\lambda$ overflow, $g^*$ domain errors, poor $\hat e$, $m$ overfitting, aggregation discarding all $f$’s, mutual absolute continuity violations, heavy‑tailed $Y$ instability.  
First experiments: (1) Synthetic heavy‑tailed noise; (2) Varying $n$ and injected $\hat e$ noise; (3) Semi‑synthetic IHDP.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Extending the framework to continuous treatments.  
2. Incorporating auxiliary data (IVs, proxies, sensitivity parameters) to sharpen bounds.  
3. Characterizing conditions for tightness of $B_f(e)$.  
4. Relaxing mutual absolute continuity to handle discrete outcomes with point masses.  
5. Developing an optimal aggregation strategy for multiple $f$‑divergences.

## Limitations
- Unspecified implementation details (network heads, aggregation scope, pseudo‑outcome cross‑fitting) hinder replication.  
- Mutual absolute continuity is a strong, untestable assumption that may fail in practice.  
- Numerical instability from $g^*$ domain violations can collapse training.  
- Finite‑sample coverage may fall below nominal for small $n$ or low overlap.

## Confidence
- Core bound (Theorem 1): High  
- Partial ID without auxiliary inputs: High  
- Debiased semiparametric inference: Medium  
- Algorithm reproducibility: Low  
- Finite‑sample coverage: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Validate all $B_f(e)$ and $g^*$ formulas, especially domain restrictions.  
2. Cross‑fit pseudo‑outcome: compute $Z$ using $\lambda,u$ trained on the opposite fold; compare coverage/width to current approach.  
3. Resolve aggregation scope: test per‑point vs. global $k$ selection; choose method that maintains coverage while minimizing width.
