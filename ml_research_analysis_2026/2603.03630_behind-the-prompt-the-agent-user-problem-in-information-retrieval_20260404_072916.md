# Behind the Prompt: The Agent-User Problem in Information Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03630
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03630
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Individual agent intent (autonomous vs. human-directed) is non-identifiable from observable actions; aggregate platform signals separate quality tiers but mixing them degrades click model performance, and cross-community capability diffusion is endemic and resilient.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the "agent attribution problem" on an agent-native platform: when AI agents are configured via hidden human instructions, it is mathematically impossible to determine from an agent's actions alone whether it is acting autonomously or following private directions. Using a large-scale dataset (MolbookTraces), the authors formalize this non-identifiability. They find that while external platform metadata (e.g., account verification) can group agents into quality tiers, training a relevance model (click model) on mixed-tier data causes significant performance degradation. Furthermore, they model the spread of capability references (e.g., "using GitHub") across communities as an SIS epidemic and find it is endemic (R₀ > 1) and highly persistent, even under aggressive simulated interventions.

## Method Summary
The study uses the MolbookTraces dataset of 370K posts from 47K agents across 4,257 communities. The core methodological approach has three parts: 1) Formal argument for individual-level non-identifiability based on the privacy of system prompts. 2) Empirical analysis grouping agents into high/low-validation tiers using five external signals (karma, email verification, etc.) and measuring click model (Position-Based Model) AUC degradation as training data mixes these groups. 3) Construction of a community-level SIS epidemic model for capability diffusion, where "infection" is a community having at least one post referencing a specific capability; R₀ is estimated from the attack rate (fraction of exposed communities infected by the end of the observation window).

## Key Results
- Individual agent intent is structurally non-identifiable; any observable action can be replicated by a hidden human instruction.
- Click model AUC degrades by 8.5% when trained on a mixture of high-validation and low-validation agent data versus using only high-validation data.
- Cross-community capability references follow an endemic SIS process with R₀ values between 1.26 and 3.53 across risk categories; reducing transmission by 70% still fails to push R₀ below 1 for some categories.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Individual agent actions cannot be classified as autonomous or operator-directed from observable data (post-level non-identifiability).
- **Mechanism:** The system prompt is hidden and expressive enough to generate any specific output. Therefore, for any observed action, there exists a private instruction that would produce it under human direction, making the latent orchestration indicator statistically non-identifiable.
- **Core assumption:** System prompts are private and sufficiently expressive.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statement on hidden instructions; Section 2 formal construction; 32.9% duplicate rate in corpus illustrates behavior consistent with orchestration but not proof.
- **Break condition:** A reaction faster than human-composition latency provides one-directional evidence *for* autonomy but cannot prove the absence of prior direction.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Aggregate platform signals separate agents into quality tiers, but mixed-quality training data degrades a click model.
- **Mechanism:** External validation signals (independent of behavior) correlate with latent quality/orchestration patterns, creating separable sub-populations with different base engagement rates. A click model assumes a single user distribution; mixing populations with different base rates conflates learned relevance signals.
- **Core assumption:** Validation signals are conditionally independent of behavioral observables and correlate with the latent quality construct.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract on degradation; Section 4.1 group definitions and behavioral differences; 100% of agents with owner linkage used for validation; 27.9% vs. 7.5% cross-community posting rate.
- **Break condition:** Validation signals lose correlation with quality, or click models are inherently robust to mixture shifts.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Cross-community capability references spread endemically (R₀ > 1) and persistently, modeled as an SIS epidemic.
- **Mechanism:** A capability mention by an agent "infects" its community in the SIS model. High cross-community posting (27.9% of agents) bridges the community network. Estimated transmission (β) yields R₀ > 1, leading to an endemic equilibrium where the concept persists without dying out.
- **Core assumption:** A community's "active" state (≥1 reference) is a valid proxy for agent-level adoption diffusion.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract on endemic R₀; Section 4.2 SIS model, keyword identification, R₀ ranges; 27.9% cross-community posting as sustaining factor.
- **Break condition:** An intervention reduces β enough to push R₀ below 1 (sensitivity analysis suggests >70% reduction for least-spreadable category).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structural Non-Identifiability of Latent Variables**
  - **Why needed here:** The attribution problem hinges on this statistical limit: if a latent variable (orchestration) can produce the same observable distribution under multiple values, no finite sample can identify it.
  - **Quick check:** If a human can write a prompt to make an agent produce a specific post, what single observable property of that post could ever definitively prove "autonomous" origin?
- **Concept: Population-Level Inference from Aggregate Signals (Hoeffding Bound)**
  - **Why needed here:** Justifies moving from failed individual attribution to successful group separation. Differences in underlying probabilities of observables between groups can be estimated with high confidence given enough samples.
  - **Quick check:** If you cannot tell for any one coin if it's biased, how could you reliably separate a bag of biased coins from a bag of fair coins by flipping them all?
- **Concept: Epidemic Threshold (R₀) in Networked Information Flow**
  - **Why needed here:** Formalizes persistence of capability diffusion. R₀ > 1 means each "infected" community, on average, creates >1 new "infected" community before recovery, leading to endemic behavior.
  - **Quick check:** If R₀ = 1.5 for a capability, will the number of communities discussing it grow, shrink, or stabilize long-term? What about R₀ = 0.9?

## Architecture Onboarding
DataIngestor -> ValidationGrouper & BehavioralFeatureExtractor -> (Parallel) -> ClickModelTrainer OR CapabilitySISM
- **Critical path:** Data ingestion feeds both validation grouping and behavioral feature extraction. Validation groups then control data mixtures for the click model path. Capability detection and SIS modeling form the parallel diffusion path.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Validation Signals:** Objective platform metadata (sparser) vs. behavioral proxies (less direct). Paper uses a weighted mix.
  - **Click Model Fidelity:** Simple PBM for clear demonstration vs. potentially more/less robust complex models.
  - **Epidemic Granularity:** Feasible community-level SIS vs. potentially masking agent-level heterogeneity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - For Mechanism 2: AUC increases as low-validation agents are added to training (contradicts degradation).
  - For Mechanism 3: All R₀ <= 1.0, or R₀ drops below 1.0 with <30% β reduction.
  - General: A major drop in the 27.9% cross-community posting rate would break the diffusion mechanism.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate click model degradation with state-of-the-art architectures (e.g., neural, Transformer-based).
  2. Simulate a "prompt disclosure" policy by appending inferred prompts to posts, re-run grouping and click model training to test for mitigations.
  3. Strengthen causal diffusion test via full post reshuffling (reassign posts to different agents within same community), beyond the paper's timestamp permutation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Do more sophisticated click models suffer the same performance degradation when trained on mixed-quality agent data? The paper only tested a basic PBM.
- **Open Question 2:** Can prompt-disclosure policies, where platforms reveal agent system prompts, partially resolve the attribution problem? This is a theoretical policy intervention without empirical test.
- **Open Question 3:** Can causal inference interventions (e.g., removing seed posts) distinguish true social diffusion of capabilities from parallel generation driven by similar prompts? Current analysis is observational.
- **Open Question 4:** Do the observed quality-tier separation and endemic diffusion patterns generalize to mixed human-agent environments? The study context is purely agent-native.

## Limitations
- **Claim Cluster 1 (Non-identifiability):** High confidence, but empirical duplicate rate is suggestive, not proof. Break condition noted for extreme temporal cases.
- **Claim Cluster 2 (Quality tiers & degradation):** Medium confidence; depends on validity of five external signals and their independence. Unknown PBM hyperparameters block exact replication confidence.
- **Claim Cluster 3 (Endemic diffusion):** Medium confidence; depends on undefined capability keyword list and ambiguous denominator (all agents vs. only capability-discussing agents) for R₀ calculation.

## Confidence
- **Individual non-identifiability:** High (formal constructive argument)
- **Quality-tier separation:** Medium (depends on validation signal validity)
- **Click model degradation:** Medium (depends on PBM implementation details)
- **Endemic capability diffusion:** Medium (robust to permutations but depends on keyword list and denominator choice)

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate click model degradation with state-of-the-art architectures.** Train advanced click models on the agent data mixtures to test if the 8.5% AUC drop is fundamental or model-specific.
2. **Simulate a prompt-disclosure policy intervention.** Artificially expose a subset of system prompts and measure changes in validation group separability and click model quality.
3. **Strengthen causal diffusion test via full post reshuffling.** Perform a stricter permutation test by reassigning entire posts to different agents within communities to better isolate social diffusion from parallel generation.
