# ALPACA: A Reinforcement Learning Environment for Medication Repurposing and Treatment Optimization in Alzheimer's Disease
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19298
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19298
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** RL policies in ALPACA significantly outperformed no-treatment and clinician baselines, with PPO achieving cumulative reward of 3.38 and final ADNI-Mem score of -0.46.

## Executive Summary
The authors present ALPACA, a reinforcement learning environment designed to explore medication repurposing and treatment optimization for Alzheimer's disease. At the core of ALPACA is the CAST model, a medication-conditioned forecaster based on a mixture-of-experts transformer, trained on longitudinal ADNI data to generate realistic patient trajectories under different drug interventions. By training standard RL algorithms (PPO, A2C, SAC, BDQ) within this simulator, the study demonstrates that learned policies can achieve superior cumulative rewards and better memory outcomes compared to both no-treatment and behavior-cloned clinician baselines. SHAP analysis shows these policies rely on clinically meaningful features, and CAST validation confirms that simulated trajectories do not significantly drift from real patient data. This work offers a promising avenue for safe, virtual trials of AD treatments, though concerns remain about the simulator's generalizability, safety, and real-world translatability.

## Method Summary
The ALPACA environment consists of a patient simulation engine driven by the CAST model. CAST is trained on ADNI longitudinal data, taking as input a patient’s current state (clinical scores, imaging biomarkers) and a medication action to predict the next state. This enables the environment to emulate the Markov decision process of AD progression and treatment response. The state space includes cognitive measures such as ADNI-Mem and structural markers like hippocampal volume; the action space encompasses common AD drugs. A composite reward function encourages improvements in memory while penalizing adverse events. RL agents (PPO, A2C, SAC, BDQ) are trained by interacting with this environment. Performance is benchmarked against a no-treatment baseline and a behavior-cloned clinician policy derived from historical ADNI prescriptions.

## Key Results
- RL agents, particularly PPO, achieved markedly higher cumulative reward (PPO: 3.38 vs. clinician -13.72, no-treatment -17.81) and better final ADNI-Mem scores (PPO: -0.46 vs. clinician -1.36, no-treatment -1.53).
- SHAP analysis confirmed that policies depend on clinically relevant features, notably memory scores and hippocampal volume.
- CAST validation using MMD and Mantel tests showed no significant distributional drift between simulated trajectories and real ADNI data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ALPACA addresses the impracticality of conducting large-scale, personalized AD treatment trials by providing a high-fidelity simulated environment. CAST’s mixture-of-experts transformer captures the heterogeneity of patient responses to medications, generating plausible longitudinal outcomes. This allows RL agents to safely explore a vast space of treatment sequences without risking patient harm. The reward structure explicitly balances cognitive benefits and potential side effects, guiding policies toward clinically favorable regimens. The combination of a robust simulator and well-established RL algorithms yields policies that not only improve on baselines but also exhibit interpretable decision-making patterns, as evidenced by SHAP.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning**: Enables sequential decision-making in uncertain environments. *Why needed*: To discover optimal treatment policies through trial-and-error in simulation. *Quick check*: Does the agent’s cumulative reward increase over training?
- **Mixture-of-Experts Transformer**: A neural architecture that activates specialized subnetworks for different input patterns. *Why needed*: CAST uses it to model diverse patient trajectories under various medications. *Quick check*: Does the model outperform a single-expert transformer on prediction accuracy?
- **ADNI Dataset**: Longitudinal Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative data including clinical assessments and imaging. *Why needed*: Provides the real-world trajectories for training and validating the simulator. *Quick check*: Are there sufficient samples across disease stages to ensure generalizability?
- **SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)**: Technique to attribute feature importance in model predictions. *Why needed*: To interpret RL policy decisions and verify they rely on medically sound factors. *Quick check*: Are top SHAP features consistent with clinical knowledge?
- **Markov Decision Process (MDP)**: Mathematical framework for modeling decision problems with state transitions. *Why needed*: Formalizes the treatment planning as states, actions, rewards, and transitions. *Quick check*: Are chosen state variables Markov with respect to future outcomes?
- **Cumulative Reward**: Sum of per-step rewards over an episode. *Why needed*: The optimization objective for RL agents, reflecting overall treatment effectiveness. *Quick check*: Does the reward correlate with desirable clinical endpoints (e.g., memory preservation)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
ADNI Data → CAST (Medication-Conditioned Forecaster) → ALPACA (RL Environment) ↔ RL Agent (Policy)  
Baseline Policies: No-Treatment, Behavior-Cloned Clinician

**Critical Path:**  
1. Preprocess ADNI data into patient trajectories.  
2. Train CAST model to predict next state given current state and medication.  
3. Integrate CAST into ALPACA, defining state/action spaces and reward function.  
4. Train RL agents (PPO, A2C, SAC, BDQ) via environmental interaction.  
5. Evaluate policies against baselines using cumulative reward and final ADNI-Mem score.  
6. Conduct SHAP analysis to interpret policy feature reliance.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Simplified state representation vs. completeness of clinical picture.  
- Reward function simplicity vs. nuanced clinical validity.  
- Restricted action space (ADNI medications) vs. coverage of real-world regimens.  
- CAST fidelity vs. computational cost.  
- Exploration during RL training vs. risk of unsafe actions in simulation.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- CAST trajectories diverge significantly from real data (high MMD/Mantel).  
- RL agents fail to outperform random or baseline policies.  
- SHAP highlights irrelevant features, indicating simulation exploitation.  
- Training instability (e.g., reward collapse).  
- Policies suggest implausible or dangerous medication combinations.

**First Experiments:**  
1. Evaluate CAST accuracy on a held-out ADNI set, reporting prediction errors and distributional metrics (MMD, Mantel).  
2. Run a single RL agent with random actions to sanity-check environment dynamics and reward computation.  
3. Train all four RL agents and compare them against no-treatment and clinician baselines to verify the expected performance hierarchy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The authors do not discuss open questions or future work in the paper. Based on the identified limitations, potential open questions include the generalizability of the simulator to diverse populations, the safety and interpretability of learned policies for real-world use, and the translatability of simulated outcomes to clinical practice.

## Limitations
- CAST is trained solely on ADNI data; its ability to generalize to diverse, real-world populations is unknown.  
- The medication action space is limited to ADNI prescriptions and may not capture off-label, polypharmacy, or drug interaction complexities.  
- Optimizing a composite reward does not guarantee that policies are clinically safe, interpretable, or ethical, especially for novel drug combinations.

## Confidence
- **Functional performance:** RL policies achieve higher cumulative reward and improved ADNI-Mem scores compared to baselines *within the simulation*. **Confidence: High**  
- **Interpretability and simulator fidelity:** SHAP feature importance aligns with clinical knowledge; CAST trajectories show statistical similarity to real data. **Confidence: Medium**  
- **Real-world applicability:** It remains uncertain whether these results translate to actual patient outcomes or safe clinical decision support. **Confidence: Low**

## Next Checks
1. **External Dataset Holdout Test:** Re-train CAST on a subset of ADNI and evaluate policy performance and trajectory fidelity on a separate cohort (e.g., NACC) to test generalization.  
2. **Comparative Simulator Benchmark:** Replace CAST with an alternative simulator (e.g., GAN or simpler predictive model) trained on the same data and measure the sensitivity of RL policy performance.  
3. **Clinician-in-the-Loop Safety Audit:** Present a diverse set of state-action pairs, including edge-case recommendations, to a panel of AD clinicians for blinded assessment of plausibility, safety, and novelty. Compute inter-rater agreement.
