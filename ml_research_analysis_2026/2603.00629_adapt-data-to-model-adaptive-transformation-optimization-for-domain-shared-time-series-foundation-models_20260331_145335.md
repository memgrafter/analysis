# Adapt Data to Model: Adaptive Transformation Optimization for Domain-shared Time Series Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00629
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00629
- Reference count: 30
- Primary result: TATO reduces mean squared error by 13.6% on average (up to 65.4%) across 192 scenarios, with optimization under 2 minutes, and adds 7.3% improvement over finetuned LTMs.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of adapting large time series models (LTMs) to new domains where data characteristics differ from pre-training distributions. The authors propose TATO, a data-centric framework that automatically optimizes a preprocessing pipeline (context slicing, scale normalization, outlier correction) for a frozen LTM. Using Bayesian search and a two-stage Pareto ranking, TATO identifies robust transformation configurations. Extensive experiments on 8 datasets across 4 horizons and 6 LTMs show TATO consistently reduces error (average 13.6% MSE reduction) with minimal optimization cost (<2 mins). Moreover, TATO provides complementary gains to model finetuning, improving finetuned LTMs by an additional 7.3% MSE, demonstrating the value of data adaptation alongside model adaptation.

## Method Summary
TATO is a framework for test-time adaptation of frozen LTMs without model retraining. It formulates preprocessing as a search over a library of 9 operators (e.g., Trimmer, Sampler, Scaler, Differencer, etc.) with discrete hyperparameters. A Bayesian optimizer (TPE) proposes candidate pipelines, respecting a small set of heuristic ordering constraints. To ensure robustness, candidate pipelines are evaluated on multiple augmented versions of a small historical dataset (e.g., 500 samples). A two-stage Pareto ranking first filters out pipelines that perform poorly on any metric subset across augmented data, then ranks survivors by weighted sum of metric ranks on the original historical set. The top pipeline is deployed: new data passes through the selected pre-processing, then the frozen LTM, then inverse post-processing to produce forecasts.

## Key Results
- Average 13.6% MSE reduction (up to 65.4%) across 192 dataset-horizon-model scenarios.
- Optimization completes in under 2 minutes, making it practical for real-world adaptation.
- TATO also improves universally finetuned LTMs by an additional 7.3% MSE, showing complementary benefits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Optimizing Context Slicing
Adjusting the input sequence length and sampling rate aligns the raw data with the LTM's receptive field and temporal resolution. The `Trimmer` and `Sampler` operators change the number of tokens and temporal spacing, which can reduce noise, emphasize periodicity, or match the model's context window. Evidence shows downsampling stabilizes predictions (Figure 1, Example 1).

### Scale Normalization
Transformations like `Scaler`, `Differencer`, and `Warper` modify the value distribution to match the statistical properties (e.g., stationarity, variance scale) assumed during LTM pre-training. Differencing removes trends, scaling standardizes range, and warping (e.g., log) stabilizes variance. These address distribution shift between pre-training and target domains. Figure 1 (Example 3) demonstrates differencing enabling trend capture.

### Outlier Handling
Operators such as `Denoiser`, `Imputator`, and `Clipper` mitigate the impact of anomalies on the LTM's attention and embeddings. Smoothing, imputation, and clipping prevent spurious pattern learning from extreme values. Figure 1 (Example 2) shows outlier correction aligning predictions with ground truth.

## Foundational Learning
### Hyperparameter Optimization (Bayesian Search via TPE)
**Why needed:** The search space is combinatorial and non-differentiable; Bayesian optimization efficiently proposes promising transformation pipelines based on validation performance, avoiding the high cost of random or grid search.
**Quick check:** Why is random search less suitable? It would require far more trials to discover effective pipelines due to lack of prior knowledge from previous trials.

### Time Series Non-Stationarity & Domain Shift
**Why needed:** Frozen LTMs encounter downstream data with different statistical properties (mean, variance, trend) than pre-training data. Understanding non-stationarity explains why transformations like differencing are necessary to induce stationarity.
**Quick check:** How does first-order differencing reduce integration order? It converts a series with a unit root (I(1)) into a stationary series (I(0)) by subtracting consecutive values.

### Pareto Optimality & Multi-Objective Ranking
**Why needed:** No single pipeline excels on all error metrics (MSE, MAE, etc.). Pareto ranking selects pipelines that are not dominated on any metric subset, ensuring robust performance across metrics.
**Quick check:** What is the difference between Stage-1 (filtering on augmented samples) and Stage-2 (ranking on original samples)? Stage-1 ensures pipelines survive under distributional variations (augmentations), while Stage-2 selects the best overall on the true historical set.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Data Preparation Module (augmentations) -> Transformation Library (operators) -> Pipeline Search Engine (Bayesian TPE) -> Two-Stage Ranking Module (Pareto filter then weighted ranking) -> Inference Wrapper.

### Critical Path
Submit historical samples -> Data Preparation (augment) -> For each Bayesian trial: sample pipeline (operators+hyperparams+order) -> apply pipeline to augmented samples -> run frozen LTM -> compute loss -> repeat -> after trials, Stage-1 Pareto filtering on metrics over augmented samples -> Stage-2 weighted ranking on original samples -> select top pipeline -> deploy: pre-process new data, run LTM, post-process.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Search Space Breadth vs. Efficiency:** Curated 9 operators balance expressiveness and feasibility; heuristic ordering bounds complexity.
- **Augmentation Diversity vs. Relevance:** Augmentations simulate real-world shifts without destroying intrinsic structure.
- **Robustness vs. Peak Performance:** Strict Pareto filtering improves stability at possible cost of single-metric optimum.

### Failure Signatures
1. **No improvement:** Search space misconfigured, budget insufficient, or frozen LTM incompatible.
2. **High variance in improvement:** Insufficient sample diversity or augmentation; tighten Pareto threshold.
3. **Excessive optimization time:** Model inference slow; may need to reduce trials/samples.
4. **Pipeline instability:** Small changes in historical data yield different optimal pipelines; indicates many local optima.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Operator Ablation:** Restrict TATO to single operator categories to quantify individual contribution.
2. **Scalability & Budget Analysis:** Vary number of trials and samples to map time-performance trade-offs.
3. **Complementarity with Finetuning:** Apply TATO to universally finetuned LTMs to confirm additive gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Extending TATO to multivariate time series.
- Automatically expanding the transformation operator set for specialized domains.
- Implementing adaptive operator selection.
- Integrating TATO with test-time adaptation for robustness.
- Identifying why TATO fails on certain dataset-model combinations.

## Limitations
- Exact augmentation parameters, Pareto ranking specifics, and operator ordering heuristics are undisclosed, hindering exact replication.
- The claim that TATO universally complements finetuning assumes orthogonal shifts, which may not hold for all domain-LTM pairs.
- Full reproducibility is limited due to missing implementation details.

## Confidence
- Empirical outcome (13.6% avg MSE reduction): High
- Mechanistic explanations (context slicing, normalization, outlier correction): Medium
- Full reproducibility: Low
