# Beyond Refusal: Probing the Limits of Agentic Self-Correction for Semantic Sensitive Information

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21496  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21496  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** SemSIEdit reduces semantic sensitive information leakage by 34.6% with only a 9.8% utility loss, uncovering scale-dependent and reasoning-related safety dynamics.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles Semantic Sensitive Information (SemSI)—contextual leaks involving sensitive inferences, harmful content, and hallucinations that evade traditional PII filters—by introducing SemSIEdit, an agentic framework that iteratively rewrites sensitive narrative spans instead of refusing to answer. Across 13 large language models, SemSIEdit achieves a privacy-utility Pareto frontier, significantly reducing leakage while preserving most utility. The study reveals that model scale dictates safety strategies: large reasoning models expand responses with nuance, while smaller models delete content destructively. Furthermore, inference-time reasoning presents a paradox: it increases baseline leakage risk by enabling deeper inferences but simultaneously empowers the defense to produce safer, more calibrated rewrites.

## Method Summary
SemSIEdit employs an iterative Evaluator-Editor loop. First, a preprocessing agent generates a draft response using a safety-conscious system prompt. Then, an Evaluator agent critiques the draft against SemSI categories (privacy, harm, misinformation), outputting structured JSON with flagged spans and scores. An Editor agent rewrites only those flagged segments to mitigate issues while maintaining narrative flow and answer intent. The loop repeats until no leakage is detected or a maximum iteration budget (T_max=3) is reached. No model training occurs; frozen models are prompted to act as separate agents. Privacy is measured via a refined LLM-as-a-judge pipeline assessing occurrence and toxicity of SemSI, while utility combines relevance, correctness, and completeness scores (Sutil). Experiments compare this defense against a no-protection baseline across diverse model families.

## Key Results
- **Pareto Frontier:** SemSIEdit reduces overall SemSI occurrence by 34.6% across all categories with a marginal 9.8% drop in utility.
- **Scale-Dependent Divergence:** Large reasoning models (e.g., GPT-5) employ "constructive expansion" (adding contextual nuance, increasing answer length), while capacity-constrained models (e.g., Gemma-3-4B) resort to "destructive truncation" (deleting text).
- **Reasoning Paradox:** Enabling inference-time reasoning (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) raises baseline leakage but improves defended performance, yielding lower leakage and higher utility compared to non-reasoning defenses.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Agentic Feedback Loop for Semantic Rewriting
- **Claim:** The iterative Evaluator-Editor loop reduces semantic leakage by allowing the model to detect sensitive narrative spans and rewrite them contextually, rather than refusing.
- **Mechanism:** The `Evaluator` agent critiques the current draft against SemSI categories (privacy, harm, misinformation) using structured prompts. The `Editor` agent then rewrites only the flagged segments, preserving the surrounding narrative flow and answer intent.
- **Core assumption:** The frozen base model possesses sufficient semantic reasoning to both critique sensitive content and generate coherent, safer alternatives for the same query context.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "SEMSIEDIT... iteratively critiques and rewrites sensitive spans to preserve narrative flow rather than simply refusing." [section 3]: Algorithm 1 and Figure 2 describe the two-phase (Initialization, Refinement) loop. [corpus]: Weak direct support; neighboring papers focus on reasoning safety but not iterative editing for semantic privacy.
- **Break condition:** If the `Evaluator` consistently fails to detect nuanced semantic leakage or the `Editor` cannot produce a non-destructive rewrite, the loop fails to achieve the Pareto frontier.

### Mechanism 2: Scale-Dependent Safety Divergence (Constructive vs. Destructive)
- **Claim:** Larger reasoning models achieve safety through "constructive expansion" (adding nuance/context), while capacity-constrained models resort to "destructive truncation" (deleting text).
- **Mechanism:** High-capacity models use their reasoning budget during editing to synthesize justifying context (e.g., citing standards, explaining uncertainty), increasing answer length. Smaller models, lacking capacity, default to the lowest-risk heuristic: removing the sensitive segment entirely.
- **Core assumption:** Model scale correlates with the ability to perform complex constraint-satisfaction rewriting versus simple content elimination.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "large reasoning models... achieve safety via constructive expansion... while capacity-constrained models resort to destructive truncation." [section 5.2]: Figure 5 shows GPT-5's mean length increases (+110 chars) post-defense, while Llama-3.3-70B decreases by 63–76%. Convergence efficiency: large models satisfy constraints at t=0/1; small models exhaust t=3 without converging. [corpus]: Aligns with "Safety is Not Only About Refusal" and "AdvChain" on reasoning model safety cliffs.
- **Break condition:** If a model's reasoning capacity does not predict its rewrite strategy, the scale-divergence hypothesis is falsified. Also, if destructive models could be fine-tuned to rewrite constructively with the same inference-time budget, scale is not primary.

### Mechanism 3: The Reasoning Paradox (Dual Role of Inference-Time Reasoning)
- **Claim:** Inference-time reasoning is a double-edged sword: it increases baseline semantic leakage risk by enabling deeper sensitive inferences, but simultaneously empowers the defense to execute safer, more nuanced rewrites.
- **Mechanism:** Reasoning chains allow the model to connect benign facts to sensitive inferences, raising baseline leakage. However, the same capability allows the `Evaluator` to better detect subtle SemSI and the `Editor` to generate more sophisticated corrections.
- **Core assumption:** The cognitive process of "thinking step-by-step" is fungible—the same compute that increases risk in an unconstrained setting is co-opted by the agentic loop to improve safety.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "inference-time reasoning increases baseline risk... but simultaneously empowers the defense..." [section 5.3]: Table 2 for Qwen-3-8B shows leakage occurrence rises from 74.0% to 84.0% when "Thinking Mode" is on without defense, but with SemSIEdit, the reasoning-enabled version performs better (42% leakage, 4.61 utility) than the non-reasoning defense (47% leakage, 4.03 utility). [corpus]: Supported by "A Comment On 'The Illusion of Thinking'".
- **Break condition:** If enabling reasoning only increases baseline leakage without providing a corresponding, statistically significant improvement in defense efficacy, the paradox does not hold.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Semantic Sensitive Information (SemSI)**  
  - **Why needed here:** Redefines the privacy threat from syntactic (PII) to contextual, requiring narrative understanding rather than pattern matching. All mechanisms target SemSI categories.  
  - **Quick check:** How does "Sensitive Identity Attribute" differ from a PII leak like a phone number, and why would a regex-based filter fail against it?

- **Concept: Agentic Self-Correction (Evaluator-Editor Loop)**  
  - **Why needed here:** It's the core operational architecture of SemSIEdit. Understanding the separation of critique (Evaluator) and revision (Editor) is key to grasping the mechanism.  
  - **Quick check:** What is the risk if the same model instance acts as both Evaluator and Editor in a single step rather than in a distinct, iterative loop?

- **Concept: Epistemic Calibration**  
  - **Why needed here:** Explains the shift from "confident hallucination" (baseline) to "qualified responses" (with SemSIEdit) as a key outcome, linking privacy to truthfulness/hallucination.  
  - **Quick check:** Why does moving from a 15.9% false information rate to 0% in GPT-5 not necessarily imply a 15.9% utility loss, based on the paper's findings?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Pre-processing Agent -> Evaluator Agent (Mev) -> Editor Agent (Med) -> Stop Condition
- **Critical path:** Input x -> y0 = M(pinit ∥ x) -> Loop(t=0..T_max): f_et = Mev(peval ∥ x ∥ y_t) -> if STOP: break -> y_{t+1} = Med(pedit ∥ y_t ∥ f_et) -> Output y_final = y_t.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Latency vs. Safety: Each iteration adds 2–4× compute cost; T_max is a hard cap. Utility vs. Privacy: The Pareto frontier shows a marginal 9.8% utility cost for 34.6% leakage reduction, but varies by model scale. Judge Specificity: Using GPT-5 as a strict judge may yield conservative privacy gains; a weaker judge might overestimate effectiveness.
- **Failure signatures:** Destructive Truncation: Answer length decreases >50% with minimal leakage reduction (e.g., Gemma-3-4B). Non-Convergence: High leakage persists after T_max iterations. Over-Refusal: f_et flags benign content or Editor produces generic safe-completions, hurting utility scores.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Leakage & Utility:** Run No Protection for all models on SEMSI-BENCH. Record occurrence, toxicity, and utility scores. Establish starting Pareto points.  
  2. **Scale-Dependent Behavior:** For GPT-5 and Gemma-3-4B, plot answer length per iteration (t=0,1,2,3). Observe divergence: constructive (increasing/stable) vs. destructive (rapid drop).  
  3. **Reasoning Paradox Toggle:** For Qwen-3-8B, run 4 conditions: (No Protection, Think OFF), (No Protection, Think ON), (SEMSIEDIT, Think OFF), (SEMSIEDIT, Think ON). Verify that ON increases baseline leakage but improves defended performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What is the minimum reasoning capability required for LLMs to safely rewrite semantic sensitive information without resorting to destructive truncation? *Basis in paper:* The authors pose RQ3 regarding scale-dependent safety behaviors and whether "safe rewriting" is an emergent ability (Section 1). *Why unresolved:* Smaller models truncate aggressively, but the precise reasoning threshold and necessary architectural features remain unidentified. *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic evaluation across diverse model architectures and sizes, correlating reasoning benchmark scores with the prevalence of constructive versus destructive edits.
- **Open Question 2:** Does the privacy-utility frontier observed on SEMSI-BENCH generalize to real-world, multi-turn conversational contexts and alternative utility metrics? *Basis in paper:* RQ2 asks if agentic defenses can mitigate privacy risks without severe utility loss (Section 1). *Why unresolved:* Utility was measured via three axes on single-turn responses; real-world utility may involve multi-turn coherence, user satisfaction, or domain-specific criteria not captured. *What evidence would resolve it:* Longitudinal user studies with domain experts evaluating agentic-defended conversations in applied settings (e.g., healthcare, legal), measuring both semantic leakage and holistic, context-aware utility.
- **Open Question 3:** What mechanisms underlie the scale-dependent divergence between constructive expansion and destructive truncation in semantic safety behaviors? *Basis in paper:* RQ3 investigates model scale effects (Section 1), and the paper uncovers the divergence in Section 5.2. *Why unresolved:* The phenomenon is documented but not explained—whether due to training data differences, optimization objectives, inherent capacity limits, or emergent reasoning strategies. *What evidence would resolve it:* Probing studies analyzing internal representations (e.g., attention heads, hidden states) during the Evaluator-Editor loop across scales, combined with fine-tuning experiments isolating scale from other factors.
- **Open Question 4:** Can agentic self-correction consistently restore epistemic calibration under distribution shift, and do users trust qualified responses over confident hallucinations? *Basis in paper:* RQ4 links semantic leakage to hallucination and asks if agentic defenses restore epistemic calibration (Section 1). *Why unresolved:* The paper shows increased qualified responses on SEMSI-BENCH, but persistence under adversarial queries or topic shifts is untested, and user trust in qualified responses remains unmeasured. *What evidence would resolve it:* Stress-testing SemSIEdit on out-of-distribution datasets (e.g., emerging misinformation) and conducting human subject experiments to assess trust, perceived helpfulness, and decision-making based on qualified versus unqualified responses.
- **Open Question 5:** How can the computational overhead of iterative agentic defenses be reduced for latency-sensitive deployments without sacrificing the achieved privacy-utility trade-off? *Basis in paper:* The conclusion notes the iterative loop increases compute by 2–4× and that real-time deployment requires optimization (Section 6). *Why unresolved:* No optimization techniques are explored, creating a barrier for practical use in resource-constrained or real-time environments. *What evidence would resolve it:* Development and benchmarking of efficient variants (e.g., early-stopping heuristics, distillation into single-pass models, hardware-aware scheduling) demonstrating comparable privacy-utility gains with lower latency on edge devices.

## Limitations
- **Judge specificity & bias:** Utility scores likely use GPT-5 as judge (unconfirmed), and privacy judges show strictness variance by model, threatening reproducibility.
- **Hyperparameter tuning & stop condition:** T_max=3 may be suboptimal per model scale; exact stop logic (e.g., requiring all flags "no") is implied but not explicitly validated.
- **Model role clarity:** Main results' agent configuration (same vs. separate models for M, Mev, Med) is unspecified, potentially conflating capabilities.

## Confidence
- **SemSIEdit Framework Effectiveness (Pareto gain):** High. Strong empirical support (Table 1, Fig 3) across 13 models with clear metrics; break condition well-defined.
- **Scale-Dependent Safety Divergence (Constructive vs. Destructive):** Medium-High. Supported by length trends (Fig 5) and convergence patterns (Table 5), but causal link to reasoning capacity vs. other scale factors not fully isolated.
- **Reasoning Paradox (dual role of CoT):** Medium. Evidence from Qwen-3-8B ablation (Table 2) is compelling but limited to one model; requires broader validation across reasoning-model families.

## Next Checks
- **Judge Robustness Ablation:** Re-run core evaluation with at least three distinct judge models (e.g., GPT-5, Claude-4, a specialized fine-tuned judge) for both privacy and utility. Quantify variance in reported Pareto frontier to establish confidence intervals around the 34.6%/9.8% figures.
- **T_max Sensitivity Analysis:** For a destructive model (Gemma-3-4B) and constructive model (GPT-5), sweep T_max ∈ {1, 3, 5, 10}. Track convergence (leakage occurrence) and utility decay per iteration. Determine if the reported divergence persists or if small models can eventually converge with more steps.
- **Agent Configuration Impact:** Compare three setups: (a) single model (M=Mev=Med), (b) separate models per role but same family, (c) heterogeneous (stronger Mev/Med). Measure changes in constructive/destructive behavior and final Pareto trade-off to isolate whether observed effects are capability-bound or architecture-bound.
