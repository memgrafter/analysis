# Authorship Impersonation via LLM Prompting does not Evade Authorship Verification Methods

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29454  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29454  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** GPT-4o-generated impersonation texts across emails, SMS, and tweets consistently produced negative log-likelihood ratios, indicating reliable rejection by state-of-the-art authorship verification systems.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates whether GPT-4o can generate texts that impersonate specific authors well enough to evade modern authorship verification (AV) systems. Using four prompting strategies—from naive instruction to linguistically structured Tree-of-Thoughts—the authors created impersonation texts in three forensic genres (Enron emails, BOLT SMS, Twitter). These were tested against six AV methods (both classic and neural). The impersonations were uniformly rejected, with most methods yielding strongly negative likelihood ratios indicating correct identification as non-authors. Remarkably, two neural methods (STAR, LUAR) showed higher true negative rates on LLM impersonations than on genuinely different human authors. The core detection mechanism is attributed to the higher lexical diversity and entropy of LLM outputs, which systematically deviates from the stable, low-entropy patterns of human idiolect. Prompt sophistication did not alter this outcome.

## Method Summary
The research followed a controlled forensic framework. For each corpus, a fixed "source text" was taken from one author. For multiple "target authors," the LLM (GPT-4o) was prompted to rewrite that source text in the target author's style, using four escalating prompting strategies. Each generated impersonation was then scored by six AV methods—three non-neural (using POSNoise to mask topic) and three neural (operating on raw text). Raw scores were calibrated into forensic log-likelihood ratios (LLRs). The primary evaluation compared LLR distributions between impersonations and genuine negative samples (different-author test pairs), supplemented by lexical diversity metrics (Type-Token Ratio, entropy, compression size) on the generated texts.

## Key Results
- **Uniform rejection:** All six AV methods produced mean negative LLRs for GPT-4o impersonations across all three corpora and prompting strategies.
- **Enhanced detection:** STAR and LUAR achieved higher True Negative Rates (TNR) on LLM-generated impersonations than on genuine human negative pairs, indicating the model-generated texts formed a distinct, easily rejectable cluster.
- **Prompt independence:** No prompting strategy (Naive, Self, Role-play, Tree-of-Thoughts) significantly improved impersonation success; all were equally detectable.
- **Diversity signal:** LLM outputs exhibited significantly higher lexical diversity (Type-Token Ratio), entropy, and compressed size compared to human-authored baselines, a property leveraged by AV methods for detection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Entropy and Diversity Discrepancy
LLM-generated text, under default sampling, exhibits systematically higher lexical diversity and entropy than human idiolect. Human writing is characterized by stable, low-entropy habits (repeated phrasings, preferred words), while LLMs produce text from a broad training distribution, leading to less repetition and larger compressed size. AV methods sensitive to distributional properties (e.g., n-gram tracing, STAR) detect this deviation as an outlier signal. The effect holds across all three genres, suggesting a fundamental model property. A break condition would require an adversary to constrain LLM output entropy to match human redundancy, e.g., via strict decoding or fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 2: Contrastive Model Outlier Sensitivity
State-of-the-art neural AV methods (STAR, LUAR) are contrastively trained to cluster an author's texts in embedding space. LLM-generated impersonations, due to their high-entropy nature, form a cluster distinct from the target author's human cluster and even from genuine human negatives. This makes them stronger outliers, leading to higher true negative rates and more negative LLRs compared to human negatives. If an LLM were fine-tuned on a specific author's corpus to replicate their exact statistical distribution, this outlier advantage might diminish.

### Mechanism 3: In-Context Learning Cannot Replicate Latent Idiolect
Prompt engineering sophistication (from naive instructions to linguistically informed prompts) fails because authorial identity is a complex, distributed pattern learned over a lifetime, not a set of explicit rules. Few-shot in-context learning cannot internalize these deep, stable lexical and grammatical habits; the model defaults to its own higher-entropy generation strategy regardless of prompt structure. Overcoming this would likely require shifting from prompting to persona fine-tuning with substantial target author data.

  *Why needed:* Standard output for evaluating AV systems; positive LLR supports same-author, negative supports different-author.

  *Why needed:* Underlies STAR/LUAR; explains sensitivity to outliers like LLM text.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Corpora Loader -> LLM Impersonator (GPT-4o API) -> Prompt Manager (Naive/Self/Role-play/ToT) -> AV Engine (6 methods) -> Evaluator & Calibrator (logistic regression to LLR) -> Analysis Suite (lexical metrics, TNR, LLR stats)

**Critical path:**  
1. Load and preprocess corpus (Enron, BOLT, Twitter).  
2. Select a global source text S from an author.  
3. For each target author and prompting strategy: generate impersonation Q using target author's known texts.  
4. For each AV method M: score (known texts of target author, Q).  
5. Calibrate raw scores to LLRs using held-out pairs.  
6. Aggregate LLR distributions, compute TNR vs. genuine negatives, and analyze lexical metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Genre selection:* Three forensically relevant genres (email, SMS, Twitter) chosen at cost of unified text length (Twitter truncated).  
- *Topic control:* Single global source text per corpus isolates stylistic imitation from content variation.  
- *Non-neural AV:* Used POSNoise to mask content; neural methods used raw text to preserve semantics.  
- *Adversary model:* Default GPT-4o decoding (temperature=1) simulates an average user, not an optimized attacker.

**Failure signatures:**  
- LLR mean near zero for a method → impersonation is confusing/evading that AV.  
- Positive TNR degradation (impersonation TNR < genuine negative TNR) → method's rejection weakened.  
- No diversity difference between LLM and human text → core detection mechanism absent.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline impersonation test:** Use Naive prompt to generate impersonations for all authors in Enron. Run all 6 AV methods. Expect predominantly negative LLRs.  
2. **Prompting strategy ablation:** For one target author in BOLT, generate impersonations under all 4 prompts. Compare LLR distributions per method. Expect no significant difference across prompts.  
3. **Lexical diversity validation:** For generated vs. human texts in Twitter, compute TTR, entropy, compressed size. Expect all three metrics significantly higher for LLM text.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can adaptive adversarial prompting, using feedback from AV system outputs to iteratively refine prompts, enable LLM impersonations to evade state-of-the-art AV? (Limitations)
- Does parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) on a target author's minimal samples produce impersonations more effective than prompting? (Limitations)
- Are human readers more susceptible to LLM impersonations than automated AV systems, creating a forensic gap? (Limitations)
- Why do specific neural architectures (e.g., AdHominem) show higher vulnerability (LLRs near zero) while others (STAR, LUAR) show increased robustness and confidence? (Inferred from results)
- To what extent does higher lexical diversity/entropy directly cause improved detection rates in methods like n-gram tracing and STAR, versus serving as a proxy for other cues? (Inferred from correlation analysis)

## Limitations
- AV method hyperparameters, training details, and calibration dataset construction are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Only GPT-4o with default decoding was tested; other models or parameters (e.g., lower temperature) might reduce entropy and alter detectability.
- Prompt specifics (number/length of example snippets, source text continuity) are unclear and could influence generation.
- Generalizability to other LLMs, languages, or additional genres remains untested.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** LLM impersonations are reliably rejected by current AV systems. **Label:** High (consistent across methods/corpora).
- **Claim:** Higher lexical diversity/entropy is the primary cause of detection. **Label:** Medium (strong correlation but not causal proof).
- **Claim:** Prompt sophistication does not improve impersonation success. **Label:** Medium (four prompts tested; more exotic prompting might yield different results).
- **Claim:** Findings generalize to other LLMs and domains. **Label:** Low (single LLM, three corpora only).
