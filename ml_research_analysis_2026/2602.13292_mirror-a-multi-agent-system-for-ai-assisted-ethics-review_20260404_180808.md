# Mirror: A Multi-Agent System for AI-Assisted Ethics Review
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13292
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13292
- **Reference Count:** 30
- **Primary Result:** A fine-tuned 8B-parameter ethics LLM achieves 76.07% QA accuracy and, when integrated into a multi-agent system with an executable rule graph, reaches F1 0.8447 on real-world ethics review dossiers while identifying implicit risks missed by other models.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles scalability and consistency limitations in institutional AI research ethics review by introducing **Mirror**, a multi-agent framework. Its core is **EthicsLLM**, an 8B model fine-tuned on 41K ethics question-chain-of-thought triples. Mirror operates in two modes: **Mirror-ER** automates expedited review via an executable rule graph derived from regulations, and **Mirror-CR** simulates full committee deliberation across ten ethical dimensions using specialized expert agents. Evaluations on 100 real dossiers show Mirror-ER achieves 94.44% recall and 76.40% precision (F1 0.8447), outperforming baselines. Case studies demonstrate the system's ability to surface implicit, non-rule-based risks.

## Method Summary
The method constructs **EthicsQA**, a supervised dataset of 41K question-rationale-answer triples distilled from authoritative ethics and regulatory texts via a two-stage process. **EthicsLLM** is created by full-parameter supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of Qwen3-8B on this dataset, using a dual-mode training strategy that interleaves chain-of-thought and non-chain-of-thought samples. The system then implements two distinct pipelines: **Mirror-ER** converts regulations into an executable, scenario-partitioned rule graph (expressed as canonical quadruples), matches research dossiers to scenarios, retrieves applicable rules and supporting evidence, and uses EthicsLLM to judge compliance. **Mirror-CR** instantiates six role-specialized expert agents plus a PI agent and an Ethics Secretary to conduct a three-stage deliberative review (independent analysis, iterative debate, synthesis) across ten predefined ethical dimensions.

## Key Results
- EthicsLLM (8B) achieves 76.07% accuracy on an ethics QA benchmark, surpassing larger generalist LLMs.
- On rule-based expedited review of 100 real dossiers, Mirror-ER attains 94.44% recall, 76.40% precision (F1 0.8447), and 79.17% professionalism.
- Case studies show Mirror identifies implicit, non-rule-based risks (e.g., familial implications in genetic studies) that are missed by other models and are not explicitly stated in the dossiers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Domain adaptation via supervised fine-tuning on curated ethics corpora produces an LLM with specialized ethical reasoning capability superior to generalist LLMs on ethics-specific tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Creating `EthicsQA` (41K question-Chain-of-Thought-answer triples) from authoritative sources provides supervised signals that teach the model normative principles, regulatory structures, and expert reasoning patterns. Fine-tuning (`EthicsLLM`) distills this knowledge into the model's parameters, enabling accurate interpretation of ethics guidelines and context-sensitive judgment.
- **Core assumption:** Ethical review tasks require knowledge and reasoning patterns distinct from general-purpose NLP; this specialized knowledge can be effectively transferred via supervised fine-tuning on a high-quality, focused dataset.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...fine-tuned on `EthicsQA`, a specialized dataset of 41K question–chain-of-thought–answer triples distilled from authoritative ethics and regulatory corpora... Mirror (8B) achieves 76.07% accuracy on ethics QA, surpassing larger generalist LLMs."
  - [section 3] Details the construction of `EthicsCorpus` from "authoritative ethics literature, regulatory documents" and the two-stage CoT annotation process to ensure quality.
  - [corpus] Weak neighbor citation signal (max 0) indicates limited direct prior work on this specific fine-tuning approach for ethics review, making the claimed mechanism novel but not independently corroborated by citation impact.
- **Break condition:** If the curated corpus (`EthicsCorpus`) fails to capture the full diversity of real-world ethical dilemmas or contains subtle biases, `EthicsLLM`'s reasoning may be inconsistent on out-of-distribution protocols, despite high benchmark scores.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Automating expedited review via an executable, scenario-partitioned rule graph derived from regulations ensures comprehensive, transparent, and consistent compliance checking for minimal-risk studies.
- **Mechanism:** Regulatory clauses are parsed into atomic, canonical rule quadruples `(condition, subject, deontic word, action)` and organized into a graph. During review, the `Rule Matcher` selects all rules for the identified scenario and subjects (prioritizing recall), and the `Rule Checker` uses `EthicsLLM` to evaluate compliance against retrieved evidence from the dossier. This enforces systematic, auditable application of rules.
- **Core assumption:** Many ethics review decisions, especially for expedited categories, are fundamentally rule-based and can be formalized into a machine-readable structure without losing essential normative nuance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Mirror-ER automates expedited review through an executable rule base that supports efficient and transparent compliance checks."
  - [section 4.1.1] "We design a pipeline that converts heterogeneous regulatory texts into standardized, canonical rules. Each rule is expressed as a quadruple... The resulting rule base spans a broad range of research domains."
  - [section 4.1.2] "Rule matching aims to ensure **comprehensive coverage**... all rules relevant to the selected scenario and subjects are forwarded... No similarity-based pruning... missing an applicable obligation is more costly."
- **Break condition:** If the rule extraction process fails to correctly deonticize complex, context-dependent regulations, or if the scenario partitioning is erroneous, the system may either miss applicable rules (low recall) or misapply rules to inappropriate studies (low precision), despite high recall on the tested benchmark.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Simulating committee deliberation via coordinated multi-agent interaction among role-specialized experts surfaces non-rule-based ethical risks and produces committee-level assessments more comprehensive than single-agent evaluation.
- **Mechanism:** `Mirror-CR` instantiates agents (Ethics Reviewer, Life Sciences Expert, Medical Expert, Legal Expert, Disciplinary Expert, Social Representative) aligned with regulatory committee composition. Agents perform dimension-guided review, engage in structured debate with a PI agent, and have concerns synthesized by an Ethics Secretary. This process mimics human committee dynamics to identify latent risks (e.g., misattributed parentage in genetic studies).
- **Core assumption:** Complex, ambiguous ethical dilemmas require multi-perspective scrutiny and interactive justification that cannot be reliably captured by a single monolithic LLM reasoning in isolation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Mirror-CR simulates full-board deliberation through coordinated interactions among expert agents... producing structured, committee-level assessments across ten ethical dimensions."
  - [section 4.2] "Based on these requirements, Mirror-CR instantiates a set of expert agents corresponding directly to the legally mandated categories... The deliberation process... is organized into three sequential stages."
  - [section 5.4] Case study: " Mirror’s genetic-medicine expert agent highlights the possibility that testing may reveal non-biological relationships... This issue does not appear explicitly in the dossier... Mirror flags this as an unresolved ethical concern."
- **Break condition:** If agent role definitions are too rigid or the debate protocol is poorly structured (e.g., insufficient rounds, poor synthesis), the simulation may produce redundant or incoherent outputs, fail to resolve genuine disagreements, or miss risks that require deep integration across domains, reducing utility over a highly capable single agent.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Distillation**
  - *Why needed here:* Ethics review requires transparent, step-by-step normative reasoning, not just an answer. The paper's `EthicsQA` construction explicitly creates CoT rationales to teach `EthicsLLM` the *structure* of expert ethical deliberation.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a two-stage CoT annotation process (model generation then human review + leakage filtering) critical for the dataset's utility in training an ethics model?
- **Concept: Domain Adaptation via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
  - *Why needed here:* General LLMs lack deep, structured knowledge of ethics regulations and principles. SFT on `EthicsQA` bridges the gap between a general pre-trained model and the specialized knowledge required for reliable review.
  - *Quick check question:* The authors use a "dual-mode training strategy" interleaving CoT and non-CoT samples. What is the likely trade-off they are managing with this approach?
- **Concept: Agentic Workflow Design**
  - *Why needed here:* Ethics review is a multi-stage, multi-role process. A single LLM call cannot replicate the separation of concerns (rule matching, evidence retrieval, debate, synthesis) inherent in institutional procedures.
  - *Quick check question:* In `Mirror-ER`, why is the `Rule Matcher` designed for high recall (forwarding all plausible rules) rather than high precision (only the top-k most similar rules)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** EthicsLLM (Qwen3-8B fine-tuned on EthicsQA) -> Mirror-ER: Rule Graph (offline, expert-curated) -> Rule Matcher (scenario/subject ID) -> Rule Checker (evidence retrieval + EthicsLLM judgment) -> Report Generator. EthicsLLM -> Mirror-CR: Role-specialized Expert Agents + PI Agent + Ethics Secretary, orchestrated through a 3-stage deliberation protocol.
- **Critical path:** Mirror-ER: Regulatory doc -> Rule extraction -> Rule graph -> Research dossier -> Scenario match -> Rule retrieval -> Evidence retrieval -> Per-rule EthicsLLM check -> Aggregation. Mirror-CR: Dossier -> Parallel expert dimension review -> Issue list -> Iterative PI debate per issue -> Secretary synthesis.
- **Design tradeoffs:** **Recall vs. Precision** in rule matching: System biases toward recall (comprehensive coverage) at potential cost of precision. **Scalability vs. Depth:** Mirror-ER is scalable for high-volume minimal-risk studies; Mirror-CR is depth-oriented for complex cases but more computationally expensive. **Automation vs. Human Oversight:** System is designed as an *assistant*; final decisions and nuanced judgments remain with human reviewers (especially in Mirror-CR).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Mirror-ER: High recall but consistently low precision in evaluations suggests rule extraction or condition parsing errors. Report lacks specific evidence spans indicates retrieval failure. Inability to map a dossier to a scenario signals a gap in the rule graph's coverage.
  - Mirror-CR: Experts repeatedly fail to raise domain-specific risks (e.g., no legal agent flags jurisdictional issues) indicates poor role specialization or prompting. Debate cycles never resolve issues suggests inadequate PI agent capability or debate protocol parameters. Secretary report is a simple consensus average indicates failure to synthesize nuanced expert disagreement.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **EthicsQA Benchmark Validation:** Run EthicsLLM on the held-out EthicsQA and EthicsQA-ER test sets. Verify accuracy matches reported ~76% (No-Thinking mode). A significant drop suggests fine-tuning degradation or data leakage issues.
  2. **Rule Graph Inspection & Spot-Check:** Manually review a sample of extracted rules from the graph (e.g., from the "biomedical experimentation" scenario). Check if quadruple (condition, subject, deontic, action) correctly captures a known regulatory clause. Test Rule Matcher by feeding a simple, known minimal-risk protocol and verifying it selects the correct scenario subgraph and retrieves relevant rules.
  3. **End-to-End Mirror-ER on a Synthetic Dossier:** Create a minimal-risk study dossier (e.g., anonymous survey). Run the full Mirror-ER pipeline. Inspect the compliance report: (a) Do cited rules plausibly apply? (b) Are retrieved evidence spans actually in the dossier? (c) Is the rationale for each judgment logically sound? This tests integration of rule matching, retrieval, and EthicsLLM reasoning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How well does Mirror generalize to research domains beyond life-science studies, such as high-risk clinical trials, multi-center studies, and sensitive social/behavioral research?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "The 100-case ethics-review benchmark is similarly skewed toward relatively standard life-science studies and does not yet capture the full breadth of ethics review settings, including high-risk clinical trials, multi-center studies, and sensitive social or behavioral research."
- **Why unresolved:** The current evaluation lacks coverage of these more complex and ethically fraught domains, so we do not know if Mirror's performance holds across the full spectrum of research that ethics committees review.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Construct a diverse benchmark covering those underrepresented domains and evaluate Mirror's performance (e.g., recall, precision, professionalism) and conduct case studies with domain experts to assess its ability to identify domain-specific ethical risks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does integrating Mirror into the actual workflow of institutional ethics committees improve review consistency, efficiency, and quality compared to human-only review, and how do human reviewers perceive the AI assistance?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "This work opens several promising directions for future research, including ... deeper investigation of human–AI collaborative decision-making."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only evaluates Mirror in isolation against LLM baselines; it does not study real-world deployment with human reviewers interacting with the system. The effectiveness in a collaborative setting remains unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a controlled field study where ethics committees use Mirror as an assistant, measuring outcomes such as time per review, inter-rater reliability, and review quality via expert evaluation, alongside qualitative feedback from reviewers.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does scaling EthicsLLM to larger model sizes (e.g., >70B parameters) improve its performance on complex ethics reasoning tasks that require long-context understanding and multi-step deliberation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "EthicsLLM is built on an 8B-parameter backbone. While domain adaptation yields substantial performance gains, the model’s scale inherently constrains long-context reasoning and complex deliberative capabilities."
- **Why unresolved:** The current evaluation uses an 8B model; larger models might better handle lengthy research proposals and multi-faceted ethical analysis, but this has not been explored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train EthicsLLM with larger backbones (e.g., 70B) on the same EthicsQA dataset, and compare performance on ethics QA, expedited review, and committee review tasks, especially on cases requiring long context (>10k tokens) and multi-dimensional analysis.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the rule extraction pipeline be automated without expert human refinement while maintaining the accuracy and interpretability required for reliable compliance checking?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Applicability conditions remain the most error-prone component, so domain experts review and refine these fields to ensure correct scope and prevent misapplication across research contexts."
- **Why unresolved:** The reliance on expert input limits scalability; if we could automate the extraction fully, the system would be more easily adaptable to new regulations. But automation risks errors that could lead to incorrect compliance decisions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct an ablation study comparing rule bases produced with and without human refinement, evaluating correctness, coverage, and misapplication rates in downstream review tasks. Additionally, develop advanced NLP techniques for extracting precise applicability conditions and test them against the expert-refined baseline.

## Limitations
- **Proprietary data:** Original `EthicsCorpus` source texts and Doubao-1.6-seed prompts for `EthicsQA` construction are unreleased, preventing exact replication of the training dataset.
- **Implementation gaps:** Full orchestration logic for `Mirror-CR` (debate protocol, synthesis algorithm) and retrieval system specifics (embedding/cross-encoder models) are underspecified.
- **External validation weak:** Corpus signals show zero citations for neighboring work, indicating limited independent corroboration of the core approach.

## Confidence
- **Domain adaptation via SFT:** Medium – Strong internal benchmark (76.07% accuracy) but no external replication; dataset construction details are high-level.
- **Executable rule graph:** Medium – Detailed pipeline and strong expedited-review metrics (F1 0.8447), yet rule extraction errors could silently degrade real-world performance.
- **Multi-agent simulation:** Medium-Low – Compelling case studies, but no ablation studies isolating agent coordination effects; risk of prompt-hacking or loop failure.

## Next Checks

2. **Rule graph audit:** Manually sample 50 extracted quadruples from key scenarios; confirm correct deontic/scoping against source regulations (e.g., 45 CFR 46).
