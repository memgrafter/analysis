# Hybrid Pooling with LLMs via Relevance Context Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.08457
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08457
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: On TREC-8, RCL achieves a 24% relative F1 improvement over standard ICL, with gains also seen in MCC.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the high cost of manual relevance judgments and the limitations of standard in-context learning (ICL) for IR evaluation. It introduces Relevance Context Learning (RCL), where an Instructor LLM generates explicit relevance narratives from a small set of human-judged examples. These narratives guide an Assessor LLM to label documents without lengthy context prompts. RCL is combined with a hybrid depth-k pooling strategy: humans judge only the top-ranked documents, while LLMs label the rest. Experiments on TREC-8 show RCL outperform zero-shot and standard ICL, achieving a 24% relative F1 improvement. The approach also reduces token usage, scales better with long documents, and mitigates circularity risks by grounding narratives in diverse human judgments.

## Method Summary
RCL operates in two phases. First, an Instructor LLM processes a curated set of human-judged query-document pairs to produce a concise relevance narrative that explicitly states the criteria for relevance. Second, an Assessor LLM uses this narrative as context to evaluate new documents for the same query. The evaluation pipeline employs hybrid depth-k pooling: an initial ranker retrieves documents; the top-k (e.g., based on rank) are set aside for human judgment, while the remaining documents are automatically labeled by the Assessor using the relevance narrative. This hybrid design minimizes human effort while maintaining evaluation quality, and it avoids the long prompt lengths required by standard ICL.

## Key Results
- RCL achieves a 24% relative improvement in F1 compared to standard ICL on TREC-8.
- Improvements extend to Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC).
- RCL reduces token consumption and demonstrates better scalability to longer documents than ICL.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Standard ICL merely appends raw judgment examples to the prompt, which fails to distill topic-specific relevance factors and leads to long, inefficient contexts. RCL’s Instructor compresses the essence of relevance into an explicit narrative, giving the Assessor a clear, concise criterion. This explicitization not only cuts token usage but also improves the assessor’s focus and consistency. Moreover, the hybrid depth-k pooling focuses human effort on the most promising documents (top-k), reducing the pool size and mitigating circularity—where LLMs might otherwise replicate biases from limited human data—because the narratives are built from diverse human judgments.

## Foundational Learning
1. **In-Context Learning (ICL)**  
   - *Why needed*: Allows LLMs to perform tasks without fine-tuning by providing examples in the prompt.  
   - *Quick check*: Can the model correctly classify a new instance after seeing a few labeled examples in the prompt?

2. **Relevance Judgment**  
   - *Why needed*: Provides ground truth for evaluating IR systems; defines document usefulness for a query.  
   - *Quick check*: Do human annotators agree when labeling the same query-document pairs?

3. **Depth-k Pooling**  
   - *Why needed*: Reduces the number of required judgments by focusing on the top-k retrieved documents, where relevant docs are most likely.  
   - *Quick check*: Does the chosen k capture the majority of relevant documents in a typical run?

4. **LLM-as-a-Judge**  
   - *Why needed*: Automates relevance labeling at scale, cutting costs compared to human-only evaluation.  
   - *Quick check*: How well do LLM judgments correlate with human judgments across multiple queries?

5. **Narrative-based Prompting**  
   - *Why needed*: Distills relevance criteria into an explicit story, improving over raw example lists by providing interpretable guidance.  
   - *Quick check*: Does the generated narrative capture the key aspects of relevance for the given query?

6. **Hybrid Human-LLM Evaluation**  
   - *Why needed*: Combines the reliability of human judgments on critical documents with the efficiency of LLMs on the long tail.  
   - *Quick check*: Does mixing human and LLM labels preserve overall evaluation quality?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**  
  Human-judged examples → Instructor LLM → Relevance narrative → Assessor LLM → Document judgments  
  Hybrid depth-k pooling: Initial ranker → Documents sorted → Top-k → Human judgment; Rest → Assessor LLM.

- **Critical Path**  
  1. Select a small set of diverse human-judged query-document pairs.  
  2. Instructor generates a relevance narrative summarizing criteria.  
  3. For a new query, retrieve documents.  
  4. Apply depth-k: top-k documents sent to humans; others to Assessor with the narrative.  
  5. Combine human and LLM judgments; compute evaluation metrics.

- **Design Tradeoffs**  
  - *Narrative length vs. informativeness*: Too brief loses key criteria; too long defeats token-saving.  
  - *Choice of k*: Larger k increases human effort but may improve quality; smaller k relies more on LLM accuracy.  
  - *Instructor diversity*: Using many judges reduces circularity risk but raises narrative generation cost.

- **Failure Signatures**  
  - Narratives are vague or contradictory, causing inconsistent Assessor outputs.  
  - Assessor ignores the narrative and reverts to zero-shot behavior.  
  - Pooling misses relevant documents because they fall beyond depth-k and are mislabeled by the LLM.  
  - LLM bias from training data leads to systematic misjudgments on certain topics.

- **First 3 Experiments**  
  1. **Main replication**: Reproduce TREC-8 results with the same Instructor/Assessor models and depth-k settings.  
  2. **Ablation study**: Compare RCL against (a) raw human labels in context, (b) zero-shot, and (c) varying narrative sources (single vs. multiple judges).  
  3. **Generalization test**: Apply RCL to a modern dataset (e.g., MS MARCO) to measure performance transfer and token efficiency.

## Limitations
- Generalizability is limited to TREC-8 (newswire, short docs); unverified on modern web-scale or long-document collections.
- Claims about token efficiency and long-document scalability lack direct empirical measurement.
- Reduction of circularity risks is plausible but not experimentally disentangled from the mere presence of any human guidance.

## Confidence
- F1 improvement on TREC-8 vs. ICL/zero-shot: **High**
- Generalization to other datasets/tasks: **Medium**
- Token efficiency & long-document scalability: **Low**
- Reduction of circularity risks: **Low**

## Next Checks
1. Replicate on diverse modern IR benchmarks (e.g., MS MARCO, BEIR, or long-document datasets like TREC Deep Learning) using at least two newer LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude 3) to test robustness.
2. Conduct an explicit token-efficiency analysis: quantify total token consumption (prompt + completion) for RCL vs. standard ICL across varying numbers of in-context examples and document lengths.
3. Perform an ablation on narrative source: replace the Instructor-generated relevance narratives with (a) raw human judgment labels only, and (b) narratives from a single judge, to measure the specific contribution of explicit, diverse narratives versus any human-derived signal.
