# Semantic Segmentation and Depth Estimation for Real-Time Lunar Surface Mapping Using 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18218  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18218  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Achieves ~3 cm height accuracy on a 120-meter lunar traverse via 3D Gaussian Splatting, outperforming a dense point cloud baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a real-time lunar surface mapping system that fuses dense stereo depth estimation and semantic segmentation with an incrementally updated 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation. Targeting the challenges of poor texture, high contrast, and limited onboard compute, the pipeline uses RAFT-Stereo for metric depth and MANet for segmentation, combining their outputs with ground-truth poses to build a geometrically accurate and semantically rich 3DGS map. The resulting map supports novel view synthesis and is proposed as the foundation for a full SLAM system. On a 120-meter traverse, the method yields a height error of approximately 3 cm, demonstrating significant improvement over a traditional point cloud baseline.

## Method Summary
The pipeline operates in two concurrent loops. The perception frontend runs at 1 Hz: for each stereo frame, it employs a pre-trained RAFT-Stereo model to compute dense depth and a MANet model to produce a semantic segmentation mask. Using provided ground-truth camera poses, valid (non-sky, in-range) pixels are back-projected to 3D points, each spawning a Gaussian with position, scale, color, and semantic label. An incremental mapper adds these Gaussians only to previously unobserved voxels to control memory growth. An asynchronous optimization backend periodically renders a buffer of keyframes, computes a multi-term loss (photometric, geometric, semantic, depth, empty-space), and updates Gaussian parameters via gradient descent. The system is evaluated on synthetic lunar data from the LuPNT simulator, with metrics including Chamfer-L2 accuracy, height error, and semantic mIoU.

## Key Results
- Reconstructed a 120-meter traverse with a height accuracy of ~3 cm, surpassing a dense point cloud baseline.
- The 3DGS map enables novel view synthesis and provides a continuous, optimizable representation.
- Semantic integration via MANet yields a high mIoU (83.1%) on test data, though it contributes minimally to geometric improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1**  
*Claim:* 3D Gaussian Splatting provides an explicit, incrementally updatable scene representation that optimizes for multi-view consistency and supports real-time novel view synthesis.  
*Mechanism:* Depth and pose inputs initialize 3D points as Gaussians. A keyframe buffer stores past views; asynchronous optimization renders them, computes photometric, geometric, and semantic losses, and updates Gaussian parameters via gradient descent. The differentiable splatting enforces coherence across all stored viewpoints.  
*Core assumption:* Dense depth input provides sufficient metric scale for consistent world coordinates, and optimization smooths perception noise over many views.  
*Evidence anchors:* [abstract] incremental 3DGS map building; [section 3] incremental mapping and async optimization; [section 4] Table 3 shows 3DGS outperforms point cloud baseline.

**Mechanism 2**  
*Claim:* Dense stereo depth (RAFT-Stereo) supplies the primary metric-scale geometric signal, overcoming feature-based method limitations in poorly-textured lunar scenes.  
*Mechanism:* RAFT-Stereo builds a 3D correlation volume and iteratively refines a high-resolution disparity field with a GRU. Combined with stereo baseline and intrinsics, this yields dense depth. The depth map back-projects pixels to 3D using the rover’s pose, forming the initial point cloud for Gaussian spawning.  
*Core assumption:* The pre-trained RAFT-Stereo generalizes to lunar domain high-contrast shadows and low texture.  
*Evidence anchors:* [abstract] selection of GRU-based stereo model; [section 2.1] Table 1 shows RAFT-Stereo leads in δ25% (0.45) at 4.4 FPS; [section 4] Table 3: estimated depth pipeline achieves 2.8 cm height error.

**Mechanism 3**  
*Claim:* Semantic segmentation (MANet) integrated via per-Gaussian labels and a dedicated loss creates a semantically-rich map and may indirectly improve geometry by masking invalid regions (e.g., sky).  
*Mechanism:* The segmentation network assigns a class to each pixel. New Gaussians inherit the label. During rendering, a semantic probability field accumulates analogously to color. The Negative Log Likelihood loss (L_semantic) supervises this field against segmentation masks. A dense mask (M_dense) excludes sky pixels from geometric losses.  
*Core assumption:* Semantic model errors do not correlate harmfully with depth errors, and the semantic loss regularizes rather than conflicts with other objectives.  
*Evidence anchors:* [abstract] selection of CNN for semantic segments; [section 2.2] MANet chosen for best rock detection; [section 3.3] semantic loss and masking; [section 4] negligible geometry difference between estimated and GT segmentation (Table 3).

## Foundational Learning
- **Dense Stereo Matching**  
  *Why needed:* It is the chosen source of metric depth; understanding rectification, disparity computation, and cost-volume aggregation (e.g., RAFT-Stereo's GRU updates) is key to diagnosing depth failures.  
  *Quick check:* What is the purpose of the 3D correlation volume in RAFT-Stereo, and how does the GRU-based update operate on it?

- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) Fundamentals**  
  *Why needed:* The entire mapping backend is built upon 3DGS. Prerequisites include Gaussian parameterization (mean, covariance via scale/rotation, opacity, SH coefficients), differentiable rasterization, and adaptive densification/pruning.  
  *Quick check:* How is the 3D covariance matrix Σ guaranteed to be positive semi-definite during optimization, and what is the role of the scale regularization loss L_scale?

- **Semantic Segmentation Architectures**  
  *Why needed:* To understand why MANet was selected and how its multi-scale attention helps detect sparse lunar rocks; also to grasp training losses (Cross Entropy, Dice, Focal) for class imbalance.  
  *Quick check:* How does the position-wise and channel-wise attention in MANet differ from U-Net skip connections, and why might it be better for detecting small, sparse objects like rocks?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Perception Frontend (RAFT-Stereo depth + MANet segmentation + GT poses) -> Incremental Mapper (back-project, voxel filter, initialize Gaussians) -> Global 3DGS model. Concurrently, Optimization Backend (async, keyframe buffer, render, loss, update) modifies the global model.

**Critical path:** Real-time loop is Perception Frontend → Incremental Mapper. Optimization runs asynchronously and does not block frame processing. The frontend must produce depth and segmentation faster than 1 Hz.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Depth model: RAFT-Stereo (accurate, 4.4 FPS) vs. SGBM (faster, less accurate). RAFT chosen for accuracy, enabled by 1 Hz mapping rate.  
- Memory vs. scale: Voxel filtering limits map growth but may cull detail in revisited areas. 3DGS memory (~2.4 GB for 24M Gaussians) is ~7× a point cloud (331 MB), trading memory for smoother geometry and renderability.  
- Semantic integration: Using MANet’s output directly adds negligible cost but inherits its errors; GT segmentation improves semantics but is unavailable online.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Large Hausdorff/Chamfer distances at rock boundaries (e.g., Rock Accuracy 12.5 cm vs. GT Depth 5.3 cm). Cause: Depth failure in shadows + no loop closure.  
2. Memory exceeds projected growth. Cause: Voxel filtering mesh too coarse or threshold too high; ineffective pruning.  
3. L_empty high and persistent. Cause: Depth errors placing Gaussians at infinite distance, or poor semantic masking.  
4. Semantic mIoU significantly lower than MANet’s 83.1%. Cause: Inconsistent per-Gaussian labeling or optimization overriding segmentation signal.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce depth benchmark: Run the four stereo models (BM, SGBM, RAFT-Stereo, CREStereo) on LuPNT Spirals test set. Compute MAE, AbsRel, δ25%, confirm RAFT-Stereo’s lead in δ25% and ~4–5 Hz FPS.  
2. Isolate mapping with GT inputs: Run full 3DGS pipeline on 120 m traverse using GT depth, segmentation, and poses. Should reproduce Table 3 row “+ GT Depth + GT Segmentation” (Accuracy ~4.8 cm, F1 ~72%).  
3. Ablate semantic loss: Run full pipeline with estimated depth/segmentation but set λ_semantic = 0. Compare final rendered semantic mIoU to default run; expect significant drop, confirming semantic loss necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Pose Estimation Integration:** How does integrating a visual-inertial tracking front-end to create a full SLAM system affect geometric accuracy and global consistency under realistic lunar conditions with pose noise and loop closures?  
   *Basis:* Conclusion states: “The most critical next step is to integrate a tracking front-end to create a complete Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) pipeline.” Current pipeline assumes ground-truth poses.

2. **Spaceborne Hardware Suitability:** What is the scalability and memory efficiency of the 3DGS mapping pipeline on radiation-hardened spaceborne hardware, and can model quantization or pruning maintain 3 cm height accuracy while reducing computational demands?  
   *Basis:* Conclusion: “future efforts must address the limited computational resources typical of planetary missions, investigating model quantization and hardware acceleration.”

3. **Alternative Neural Representations:** How do alternative neural scene representations (e.g., NeRF, 2D Gaussian Splatting, Neural Point Clouds) compare to 3DGS in reconstruction quality (geometry and semantics), memory consumption, and real-time performance for large-scale lunar mapping?  
   *Basis:* Conclusion: “We also plan to evaluate alternative neural representations, such as NeRF, 2DGS, and Neural Point Clouds, to assess their trade-offs.”

4. **Surface Extraction Improvement:** Can a density-based surface extraction method significantly improve geometric fidelity, particularly for fine structures like small rocks, compared to the current point-based evaluation using Gaussian means?  
   *Basis:* Conclusion: “Future work will also focus on density-based techniques for surface extraction from the Gaussians to better capture the continuous geometry.”

5. **Generalization and Uncertainty:** How does the framework generalize to unseen lunar environments (e.g., permanently shadowed regions, high-relief craters) and real rover imagery, and how can incorporation of perception uncertainty improve map robustness?  
   *Basis:* Conclusion: “a more rigorous characterization of the system—including its performance with different camera configurations and effects, additional datasets, and the incorporation of perception uncertainty—is necessary.”

## Limitations
- **Ground-truth pose reliance:** The current pipeline assumes accurate camera poses, separating perception from localization; performance with noisy or drifting poses is unknown.
- **Synthetic data dependency:** Evaluations rely solely on the LuPNT simulator, which may not fully capture real lunar photometric anomalies (e.g., dust, extreme shadows, lens flare).
- **Long-term consistency unvalidated:** The design omits a global opacity reset; map quality over very long traverses (beyond 120 m) may degrade due to accumulated errors.

## Confidence
- **3DGS Representation Efficacy:** High – clear geometric improvement over point cloud baseline (Table 3).
- **RAFT-Stereo Depth Accuracy:** Medium – benchmark leads in δ25% but acknowledged failures in high-contrast shadow regions (Fig 8).
- **Semantic Integration Utility:** Low – MANet integration achieves high mIoU but provides negligible geometric benefit vs. GT segmentation (Table 3); indirect geometry improvement via masking is speculative.

## Next Checks
1. **Pose Estimation Sensitivity Test:** Replace ground-truth poses with a typical lunar rover VIO pipeline (e.g., ORB-SLAM3 with stereo input). Re-run mapping and measure degradation in height error and Chamfer-L2 accuracy.
2. **Out-of-Distribution Depth Failure Analysis:** Evaluate RAFT-Stereo and resulting 3DGS map on a held-out LuPNT trajectory with extreme lighting (low sun angle, high contrast). Quantify depth MAE increase and corresponding Hausdorff distance in reconstruction.
3. **Long-Term Memory and Forgetting Check:** Run incremental mapper on a synthetically generated 1 km traverse. Track Gaussian count vs. distance and periodically re-evaluate metrics on fixed, early keyframes. A significant drop in old-view accuracy would indicate catastrophic forgetting.
