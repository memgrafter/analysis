# Reservoir Subspace Injection for Online ICA under Top-n Whitening

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02178
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02178
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: RSI causes crowd-out in top-n whitening, but guarded control preserves passthrough and improves SI-SDR by +1.7 dB under nonlinear mixing.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies reservoir subspace injection (RSI) as a critical bottleneck in online independent component analysis (ICA) when using top-n whitening with nonlinear feature expansion. Stronger injection of reservoir features can displace passthrough directions from the whitening subspace, degrading separation performance despite higher injected energy. The authors formalize this crowd-out effect using diagnostics like Injections Energy Ratio (IER) and passthrough retention (ρ_x). A guarded RSI controller adaptively regulates injection to preserve passthrough (ρ_x ≥ 0.95), enabling the proposed RE-OICA method to outperform vanilla online ICA by +1.7 dB under nonlinear mixing and match baseline performance on super-Gaussian benchmarks.

## Method Summary
RE-OICA expands the input space with high-dimensional features from a fixed echo state network (reservoir). These features are scaled by an adaptive factor α_t and concatenated with original inputs. Online whitening applies exponential moving average covariance updates and periodic top-n eigendecomposition to the augmented vector. At each whitening refresh, diagnostics (IER, SSO, ρ_x) assess whether injected features occupy the top-n subspace without displacing passthrough variance. A guarded controller updates α_t to target a desired IER while penalizing drops in ρ_x below a threshold. The whitened signals then undergo natural gradient ICA with periodic re-orthonormalization.

## Key Results
- Stronger reservoir injection increases IER but crowds out passthrough energy (ρ_x: 1.00→0.77), degrading SI-SDR by up to 2.2 dB.
- With passthrough preservation (ρ_x≥0.95), guarded RSI allows RE-OICA to improve over vanilla online ICA by +1.7 dB under nonlinear mixing.
- On super-Gaussian benchmarks, RE-OICA achieves positive SI-SDR_sc (+0.6 dB) and recovers mean performance within 0.1 dB of baseline 1/N scaling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Crowd-out in Top-n Whitening
- **Claim**: Stronger reservoir injection can crowd out passthrough directions from the top-n whitening subspace, harming separation performance despite higher injected-feature energy.
- **Mechanism**: Under top-n selection, the retained eigenspace competes between passthrough (C_xx) and reservoir (α_t² C_pp) block spectra. When α_t increases so that reservoir eigenvalues displace passthrough eigenvalues (λ_max(α_t² C_pp) > λ_n(C_xx)), ρ_x drops. In block-diagonal case, displacement is direct; with cross-block coupling, Davis–Kahan perturbation allows crowd-out via eigengap narrowing.
- **Core assumption**: Passthrough directions are necessary for accurate demixing; displacing them reduces SI-SDR even if injected features enter the subspace.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract reports IER↑ and ρ_x↓ (1.00→0.77) with SI-SDR degradation up to 2.2 dB. Section II.D shows block-diagonal analysis and stable cross-block coherence (0.38–0.45), implying shifts are driven by relative block energies.

### Mechanism 2: Adaptive Guarded Controller
- **Claim**: An adaptive injection controller can preserve passthrough retention, allowing beneficial reservoir expansion without crowd-out.
- **Mechanism**: At each whitening refresh, IER_t and ρ_x,t are computed from V_n. α_t is updated via a tracking rule (Equation 11): increased when IER_t < IER^⋆ but penalized when ρ_x,t < ρ_x^⋆. Clip bounds constrain α_t.
- **Core assumption**: A fixed ρ_x^⋆ (e.g., 0.95) is conservative; performance degrades when ρ_x < 0.9.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states guarded controller preserves ρ_x and recovers performance within 0.1 dB of baseline. Table II shows RE-OICA-RSI achieves ρ_x≈0.98 with SI-SDR_sc ≈ base. Ablation: unguarded RSI yields ρ_x=0.77 and SI-SDR_sc = −9.2 dB vs. baseline −7.0 dB.

### Mechanism 3: Reservoir Expansion Benefit Condition
- **Claim**: High-dimensional reservoir expansion helps online ICA under nonlinear mixing only if injected features are retained in the top-n subspace and passthrough is preserved.
- **Mechanism**: Reservoir features provide nonlinear lifting via random features, but top-n whitening acts as a rank filter. If crowd-out occurs, net benefit is negative even with positive IER.
- **Core assumption**: Nonlinear mixing requires nonlinear feature expansion for effective linear demixing, but expansion must be compatible with the rank budget.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract: with passthrough preserved, RE-OICA improves by +1.7 dB under nonlinear mixing. Section III: baseline 1/N scaling (IER≈0, ρ_x≈1) performs similarly to guarded RSI (ρ_x≈0.98), while unguarded with high IER (ρ_x=0.77) degrades severely. This shows IER alone is insufficient; ρ_x preservation is necessary.

## Foundational Learning
- **Top-n whitening and eigendecomposition**: Why needed: Defines the RSI bottleneck—only top-n eigenvectors are retained, making subspace inclusion a competition. Quick check: How does increasing α_t change the eigenvalue spectrum of C_t(α_t) relative to C_xx, and what condition causes a reservoir eigenvector to enter the top-n set?
- **Reservoir computing (Echo State Network)**: Why needed: Provides fixed, high-dimensional nonlinear lifting without training. The echo state property ensures fading memory. Quick check: What role do spectral radius ρ and leak rate α_r play in the echo state property, and how does sparse W_res affect computational cost?
- **Natural gradient ICA**: Why needed: The demixing update is equivariant and operates on whitened z_t; whitening quality directly impacts convergence. Quick check: Why is natural gradient preferred over vanilla gradient for ICA, and what periodic re-orthonormalization is applied and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input x_t -> Reservoir encoder (ESN) -> Readout projection -> RSI injection controller -> Concatenation u_t = [x_t; α_t p_t] -> Online whitening (EMA covariance, eigendecomposition) -> Whitened z_t -> Natural gradient demixing -> Output y_t.
- **Critical path**: The whitening refresh loop (every Δ_w samples) computes V_n, IER, SSO, ρ_x, and updates α_t. This must complete before subsequent ICA updates use the new subspace.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Reservoir size N: Larger N increases nonlinear capacity but computational cost (sparse multiply).
  - Scaling c_N: 1/N yields low IER but safe ρ_x; 1/√N increases IER but risks crowd-out.
  - Refresh intervals Δ_w and Δ_o: Larger Δ_w slows α_t adaptation; Δ_o balances ICA drift.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High IER (>0.2) with ρ_x < 0.9 indicates crowd-out, often with SI-SDR degradation >1 dB.
  - Low IER (≪0.25) with ρ_x≈1 shows under-injection; minimal gain.
  - Stable cross-block coherence (~0.4) suggests crowd-out is driven by block energy competition.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **N-sweep under nonlinear mixing**: Plot SI-SDR_sc vs. N for 1/N, 1/√N, and guarded RSI. Expect 1/N and guarded to track; 1/√N to drop at higher N.
  2. **Inject α_t step response**: Simulate constant α_t increase (no guard), track IER_t and ρ_x_t over refreshes. Expect ρ_x to drop as α_t passes crowd-out threshold.
  3. **Cross-regime validation**: Compare RE-OICA (guarded) vs. vanilla on static, time-varying, and nonlinear mixing. Expect +1.7 dB gain only in nonlinear regime.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can we increase useful cross-block structure between passthrough and reservoir features to improve RE-OICA? Future work should optimize readout matrices for cross-block informativeness while preserving passthrough.
2. What precise eigenvalue gap and eigenvector overlap conditions guarantee that injection augments the top-n subspace without crowd-out? Currently, only sufficient conditions for block-diagonal case are known; general quantitative condition is lacking.
3. How does cross-block coherence affect the trade-off between IER and ρ_x? Cross-block coherence was stable (~0.38–0.45) in experiments, but its causal role is unclear.
4. Does the RSI crowd-out trade-off scale with problem dimension n, and how should controller parameters adapt? All experiments used n=3; fixed thresholds may not generalize to higher dimensions.

## Limitations
- Exact source signal implementations (e.g., discrete-time Lorenz, Mackey-Glass) are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Eigensolver details (accuracy, symmetry enforcement, handling near-degenerate spectra) are not provided, potentially affecting diagnostics.
- Controller parameters (ρ_x^⋆, IER^⋆, κ, κ_g) are empirically chosen; their robustness to varying n, N, or mixing statistics is unclear.

## Confidence
- Crowd-out mechanism (M1): High – supported by theory and strong empirical trends.
- Guarded controller (M2): High for presented setup, but Medium for broader applicability due to empirical parameters.
- Nonlinear mixing benefit (M3): Medium – gains shown on synthetic data, but lacking real-world validation.
