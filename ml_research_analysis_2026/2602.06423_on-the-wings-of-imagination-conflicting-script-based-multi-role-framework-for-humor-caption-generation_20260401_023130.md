# On the Wings of Imagination: Conflicting Script-based Multi-role Framework for Humor Caption Generation

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.06423
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06423
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** HOMER framework achieves average pass@1 improvements of +8.62% (GPT-4o) over SOTA on New Yorker caption benchmarks, with human-evaluated funniness at 3.54/5.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces HOMER, a novel framework for generating humorous image captions that addresses the limited creativity and interpretability of standard large language model (LLM) approaches. Inspired by the General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH), HOMER structures the task among three specialized LLM roles: a conflicting-script extractor, a hierarchical imaginator that builds association trees and retrieves from a joke database, and a final caption generator. Evaluated on two New Yorker caption contest datasets, HOMER demonstrates significant performance gains over state-of-the-art baselines and is rated as funnier by human evaluators. Ablation studies confirm the value of both the humor-theory guidance and the hierarchical imagination process.

## Method Summary
HOMER decomposes humor caption generation into a pipeline of three LLM-driven stages. First, a "conflicting-script extractor" analyzes the image to identify opposing semantic scripts (e.g., "doctor" vs. "butcher") based on GTVH principles. Second, a "hierarchical imaginator" uses these scripts as anchors to construct a tree of imaginative associations via LLM prompting and retrieves relevant jokes from a database to populate potential humorous angles. Finally, a "caption generator" synthesizes these elements into a coherent, funny caption. This structured, theory-grounded approach aims to inject systematic creativity and explanatory power beyond end-to-end LLM prompting.

## Key Results
*   HOMER yields average **pass@1 improvements of +8.62% with GPT-4o** and +7.0% overall against prior SOTA on New Yorker caption benchmarks.
*   **Human evaluation** assigns a mean funniness score of **3.54/5**, reported as a significant improvement over baselines.
*   **Ablation studies** confirm that both the GTVH-based script conflict extraction and the hierarchical imagination tree components contribute substantively to the framework's performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HOMER works by imposing a structured, theory-informed process on a generative task that is otherwise prone to randomness or clichés in standard LLMs. The GTVH provides a formal model for the core mechanism of verbal humor (script opposition), which the first role explicitly extracts. This anchors the subsequent imagination phase in a recognized humorous structure rather than free-form association. The hierarchical imagination tree then systematically explores related concepts and retrieves concrete joke examples, providing material that is both novel and aligned with the identified conflict. This multi-stage decomposition improves interpretability (each role's output can be inspected) and guides the final generator toward captions with a higher likelihood of containing a legitimate humorous opposition, thus enhancing both creativity and funniness.

## Foundational Learning
*   **General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH):** A linguistic theory modeling humor as a conflict between cognitive scripts. *Why needed:* Provides a formal, abstract structure for identifying what makes a text funny, moving beyond surface patterns. *Quick check:* Can the framework identify the primary script opposition in a known joke?
*   **Script Opposition:** The core GTVH concept where two incompatible scripts (e.g., real vs. unreal, possible vs. impossible) are juxtaposed. *Why needed:* Defines the target of the extractor role and the seed for imagination. *Quick check:* Does the extracted opposition clearly distinguish two different interpretations of the image/scenario?
*   **Multi-Role LLM Framework:** Decomposing a complex task into specialized prompts/roles for extraction, imagination, and generation. *Why needed:* Compartmentalizes expertise, reduces conflation of tasks, and improves traceability. *Quick check:* Does each role's output serve as a clear, constrained input for the next role?
*   **Hierarchical Imagination Tree:** A tree-structured exploration of semantically related concepts from the script opposition, built via iterative LLM prompting. *Why needed:* Moves beyond flat retrieval or single-step brainstorming to explore a broader, more nuanced space of humorous connections. *Quick check:* Does the tree demonstrate branching from the core opposition into diverse, relevant sub-concepts?
*   **Humor-Specific Database Retrieval:** Augmenting LLM associations with a curated database of existing jokes. *Why needed:* Grounds imagination in empirically funny material and provides concrete templates or phrases. *Quick check:* Are retrieved jokes demonstrably relevant to the extracted script opposition?
*   **Pass@1 & Human Evaluation for Humor:** Using both automated (pass@1 on benchmark solutions) and subjective (human funniness scores) metrics. *Why needed:* Captures objective task success and subjective quality, which are distinct for creative tasks. *Quick check:* Is there a meaningful correlation between automated and human scores in the results?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Image Input -> Conflicting-Script Extractor (GTVH Analysis) -> Script Opposition -> Hierarchical Imaginator (LLM Association + Joke DB Retrieval) -> Imagination Tree -> Caption Generator -> Humorous Caption Output

**Critical Path:** The sequence is strictly linear: Script extraction must precede imagination, as the opposition seeds the tree; the populated tree must precede final generation, as it provides the content and constraints.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
1.  **Theoretical Rigor vs. Generality:** The GTVH constraint improves coherence for script-based humor but may stifle humor forms that lack clear binary opposition (e.g., absurdism).
2.  **Computational Depth vs. Efficiency:** The hierarchical imagination tree and database query introduce significant latency compared to a single LLM call, trading speed for explore-exploit balance in idea generation.
3.  **Structured Creativity vs. Spontaneity:** The pipeline's structure enhances interpretability and targeted creativity but may filter out serendipitous, cross-domain associations a freer model might find.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  **Extraction Failure:** The identified "script opposition" is vague, incorrect, or not truly oppositional (e.g., "animal" vs. "person").
2.  **Imagination Failure:** The imagination tree remains shallow, diverges from the opposition, or retrieved jokes are irrelevant, yielding a poor set of candidate concepts.
3.  **Generation Failure:** The final caption is incoherent, fails to incorporate the identified opposition, or is simply not funny despite good inputs from prior stages.

**Exactly 3 First Experiments:**
1.  **Sanity Check - Role Isolation:** Run each LLM role in isolation with perfect inputs from the previous stage (e.g., provide a correct script opposition to the imaginator) to verify each component functions as intended.
2.  **Database Ablation:** Disable the joke database retrieval, relying only on LLM associations for imagination, to quantify the specific contribution of retrieved examples to final funniness scores.
3.  **Opposition Complexity Stress Test:** Feed the extractor with images known to have subtle or multi-faceted script oppositions and measure pipeline failure rates, identifying the limits of GTVH applicability.

## Limitations
*   **Domain Specificity:** Performance is validated only on the stylized, verbal humor of The New Yorker caption contests; generalizability to other image types or humor genres is unproven.
*   **Theory Constraint:** The reliance on GTVH's script opposition model may not capture humor forms that do not fit this binary structure (e.g., absurdist or ironic humor without clear opposition).
*   **Computational Opacity & Cost:** The hierarchical imagination process is LLM-driven and associative, remaining largely opaque in its "creative" leaps, while also introducing significant inference overhead compared to single-prompt baselines.

## Confidence
*   **Performance Improvement on Benchmarks:** **High**. Results are on established datasets with clear pass@1 metrics and a described, replicable setup.
*   **Superior Human-Evaluated Funniness:** **Medium**. Subjective scores are promising but lack reported inter-annotator agreement and direct baseline comparisons in the summary.
*   **Contribution of Theory & Imagination Components:** **Medium-High**. Ablation studies directly support their additive value, though the precise mechanistic reason for the imagination tree's superiority over simpler methods is inferred.
*   **Claim of Enhanced Creativity & Interpretability:** **Low-Medium**. "Creativity" lacks a formal metric (e.g., novelty, diversity). "Interpretability" is improved over a black box but the LLM's associative reasoning within roles is still a proxy explanation.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-Domain Generalization Test:** Replicate HOMER on at least one distinct humor caption dataset (e.g., HumorDB or FunnyQA) to measure performance transfer beyond the New Yorker style.
2.  **Fine-Grained Theory Ablation & Error Analysis:** Manually categorize failures on the New Yorker benchmark into (a) incorrect script extraction, (b) imagination failure given correct extraction, or (c) generation failure, to pinpoint the primary bottleneck and GTVH's failure modes.
3.  **Efficiency-Efficacy Trade-off Benchmark:** Rigorously measure and report inference time/cost per caption for HOMER vs. the strongest single-prompt LLM baseline. Systematically vary imagination tree parameters (depth, breadth, retrieval top-k) to chart the explicit trade-off curve between computational overhead and output quality (pass@1, human funniness).
