# Deep Learning-Based Anomaly Detection in Spacecraft Telemetry on Edge Devices
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.29375
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29375
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Pareto-optimized forecasting & threshold anomaly detection reduces RAM by 97.1% to 59 KB and operations by 99.4% while retaining 88.8% CEF0.5, fitting within CubeSat memory budgets.

## Executive Summary
This research tackles the critical challenge of deploying deep learning-based anomaly detection on resource-scarce spacecraft edge computers, such as those on CubeSats. It applies Pareto-optimal multi-objective neural architecture search (NAS) to optimize three distinct modeling approaches—forecasting & threshold, direct classification, and image classification—using the ESA Anomaly Dataset. The optimized forecasting & threshold model achieves dramatic reductions in RAM (97.1%) and computational operations (99.4%) while maintaining high detection performance (88.8% CEF0.5). Deployment analysis confirms these optimized models consume only a small fraction (0.36%–6.25%) of typical CubeSat RAM, demonstrating the practical feasibility of sophisticated on-board AI for spacecraft health monitoring without exceeding hardware constraints.

## Method Summary
The study employs a multi-objective neural architecture search (NAS) framework to generate Pareto-optimal model architectures. This search simultaneously optimizes for two primary objectives: anomaly detection performance (measured by CEF0.5 on the ESA dataset) and resource efficiency (measured by peak RAM usage and inference operations). Three anomaly detection paradigms are explored: a forecasting model with a threshold-based detector, a direct classifier, and an image-based classifier treating telemetry as an image. The NAS process iteratively evaluates candidate architectures on these metrics to identify a set of models representing the best possible trade-offs (the Pareto front). The most promising models from this front are then analyzed for their specific resource footprints relative to CubeSat hardware specifications.

## Key Results
*   The baseline forecasting & threshold model achieves a CEF0.5 of 92.7%, outperforming the direct classification and image classification baselines.
*   After NAS optimization, the forecasting & threshold model preserves 88.8% CEF0.5 while reducing RAM usage by 97.1% to 59 KB and operations by 99.4%.
*   Optimized models require only 0.36% to 6.25% of the RAM available on a representative CubeSat's on-board computer, confirming feasible deployment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism succeeds by formalizing model design as a multi-objective optimization problem, where detection accuracy and resource consumption are competing goals. Multi-objective Pareto-optimal NAS systematically explores a vast architecture space to discover models that are *non-dominated*—meaning no other model exists that is simultaneously better in performance *and* more efficient in resources. This yields a portfolio of models (the Pareto front) from which a mission planner can select the optimal point based on the specific, tight constraints of the target edge hardware (e.g., exact RAM limit, acceptable latency). The forecasting & threshold approach's dominance stems from its inherent structural efficiency for sequential telemetry data, requiring less complex networks than image-based methods while providing robust, interpretable error signals.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Pareto Optimality:** Why needed: Forms the mathematical basis for finding the best possible trade-off models when two objectives (accuracy, efficiency) conflict. Quick check: Ensure no single model on the final front is outperformed by another on *both* objectives.
*   **Neural Architecture Search (NAS):** Why needed: Automates the design of efficient neural network topologies, which is infeasible to do manually for a complex multi-objective space. Quick check: Verify the search space includes operations known to be parameter/FLOP efficient (e.g., depthwise convolutions).
*   **Anomaly Detection via Forecasting & Threshold:** Why needed: Well-suited for univariate or low-dimensional time-series telemetry; simple to interpret and tune. Quick check: Confirm the model predicts the next timestep(s) and anomalies are flagged when prediction error exceeds a calibrated threshold.
*   **Resource-Aware Deep Learning:** Why needed: Standard cloud-scale model designs are wholly unsuitable for microcontroller-class hardware. Quick check: All candidate architectures must have their peak RAM and MACs profiled on the *target* hardware profile during search.
*   **CEF0.5 Metric:** Why needed: A cost-sensitive evaluation metric for anomaly detection that balances precision and recall at a specific, mission-critical false positive rate (FPR = 0.5%). Quick check: Compute metric on a held-out validation set to compare models fairly.
*   **CubeSat On-Board Computer (OBC) Constraints:** Why needed: Defines the absolute, non-negotiable design envelope (e.g., 256 KB – 1 MB RAM, limited flash, no OS). Quick check: All final selected models must have estimated peak RAM < (OBC RAM * allocated budget fraction, e.g., 10%).

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** ESA Telemetry Dataset -> NAS Search Space (Candidate Architectures: Forecasting, Classifier, Image) -> Multi-Objective Evaluator (Performance: CEF0.5; Resources: RAM, MACs) -> Pareto Front Generator -> CubeSat OBC Hardware Specs -> Final Model Selection -> Deployed Anomaly Detector.
*   **Critical Path:** The NAS loop (candidate generation -> training/evaluation -> Pareto front update) is the critical path. Its duration depends on search efficiency, proxy metric reliability, and parallelization capability.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** The primary tradeoff is between detection performance (CEF0.5) and resource efficiency (RAM/MACs). A secondary, implicit tradeoff exists between NAS search time/compute and the quality of the final Pareto front. The paper prioritizes the former, accepting the computational cost of NAS as a one-time design-phase burden.
*   **Failure Signatures:** 1) A model on the Pareto front that uses >90% of OBC RAM, leaving no buffer for other processes. 2) A model with a sharp drop in CEF0.5 (>5% relative) when moving from the validation set to a simulated realistic noise/test set. 3) An architecture whose inference latency exceeds the telemetry acquisition cycle time.
*   **First Three Experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Benchmark:** Train and evaluate the three canonical model types (forecasting, direct classification, image classification) with standard architectures on the ESA dataset to establish initial performance/resource baselines.
    2.  **NAS Validation:** Run the multi-objective NAS with a small, restrictive search space (e.g., only varying depth/width of the forecasting model) to verify the search correctly identifies known efficient architectures and produces a coherent Pareto front.
    3.  **Hardware Stress Test:** Take the top 3 models from the preliminary Pareto front and measure their *actual* peak RAM and inference time on the specific CubeSat OBC emulator/prototype board to validate the profiler's estimates used during search.

## Limitations
*   **Dataset Generalizability:** Evaluation is confined to the ESA Anomaly Dataset; real telemetry may exhibit different noise, failure modes, and dimensionality, potentially degrading performance.
*   **Holistic Resource Assessment:** Optimization focused solely on RAM and MACs, omitting critical embedded constraints like power consumption, thermal load, firmware integration overhead, and real-time latency requirements.
*   **Safety & Operational Validation:** The assertion of "not compromising mission safety" is inferred from hardware limits. No evidence is provided for model robustness to sensor drift, communication errors, or integration with existing fault management protocols.

## Confidence
*   **Resource Reduction (RAM/Operations):** High. Quantifiable percentages are direct outputs of the defined NAS process.
*   **On-board Feasibility (Hardware Limits):** Medium-High. contingent on the accuracy of the CubeSat RAM budget figures and completeness of the model's memory footprint (e.g., excluding runtime library overhead).
*   **Performance Retention & Mission Safety:** Medium-Low. The 88.8% CEF0.5 is a solid in-dataset result, but extrapolating to operational safety involves unvalidated assumptions about robustness and system integration.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-Dataset Robustness Test:** Re-evaluate the optimized forecasting & threshold model on at least two additional, publicly available spacecraft/multivariate time-series anomaly datasets (e.g., SMAP, MSL) to measure performance consistency.
2.  **End-to-End Embedded Benchmarking:** Deploy the optimized model on the *actual* target CubeSat OBC hardware to measure true peak RAM, average inference latency, and energy per inference, comparing against paper estimates.
3.  **Safety-Case Simulation:** Conduct hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) or high-fidelity spacecraft simulator tests where the model's anomaly outputs trigger simulated fault responses, measuring false negative/positive rates under realistic communication dropouts and sensor degradation to assess operational safety impact.
