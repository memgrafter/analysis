# Interpretable Operator Learning for Inverse Problems via Adaptive Spectral Filtering: Convergence and Discretization Invariance

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20602
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20602
- **Reference count**: 28
- **Primary result**: SC-Net learns an adaptive spectral filter achieving the minimax optimal convergence rate \(O(\delta^{0.5})\) for \(s=p=1.5\) and exhibits zero-shot super-resolution, maintaining stable reconstruction errors when trained on coarse grids and tested on much finer grids.

## Executive Summary
SC-Net introduces a neural network framework for solving linear ill-posed inverse problems by learning a pointwise adaptive spectral filter directly in the singular value decomposition (SVD) domain of the forward operator. The filter reweights spectral coefficients based on their signal-to-noise ratio, effectively damping noisy high-frequency components while preserving signal. Theoretically, the learned operator achieves the minimax optimal convergence rate \(O(\delta^{s/(s+p)})\) under standard source and smoothness assumptions. A key property is discretization invariance: a model trained on a coarse spatial grid generalizes to significantly finer grids without retraining, as the learned mapping operates on function-space coefficients rather than fixed-resolution vectors. Empirical results on 1D synthetic problems confirm the optimal rate, visualize interpretable sharp-cutoff filters, and demonstrate stable zero-shot super-resolution from \(N=256\) to \(N=2048\).

## Method Summary
SC-Net processes noisy measurements \(y_\delta\) by first projecting them onto the first \(N\) left singular vectors \(\{u_n\}\) of the forward operator \(K\) to obtain spectral coefficients \(y_n = \langle y_\delta, u_n \rangle\). A shared multi-layer perceptron \(\Psi_\theta\) takes each pair \((y_n, \sigma_n)\) (where \(\sigma_n\) is the corresponding singular value) and outputs a multiplicative factor in \([0,1]\). The filtered coefficient is \(\hat{f}_n = \Psi_\theta(y_n, \sigma_n) \cdot (y_n / \sigma_n)\). The reconstruction \(f_\theta\) is synthesized via \(\sum_{n=1}^N \hat{f}_n v_n\) using the right singular vectors \(\{v_n\}\). Training minimizes a Sobolev-weighted mean squared error loss \(\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{M}\sum ( \|f_\theta - f^\dagger\|^2 + \gamma \|\nabla f_\theta - \nabla f^\dagger\|^2 )\) over a dataset of \((f^\dagger, y_\delta)\) pairs generated with controlled noise levels \(\delta\).

## Key Results
- Achieves the theoretical minimax optimal convergence rate \(O(\delta^{s/(s+p)})\); for \(s=p=1.5\) the rate is \(O(\delta^{0.5})\) with empirical slope 0.50, matching the lower bound.
- Learns interpretable, sharp-cutoff spectral filters that outperform Oracle Tikhonov regularization in reconstruction error.
- Demonstrates zero-shot super-resolution: a model trained on \(N=256\) maintains stable relative \(L^2\) error around 0.23 when tested on grids up to \(N=2048\) without retraining.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Adaptive Spectral Filtering
SC-Net stabilizes the ill-posed inversion by learning a pointwise filter \(\Psi_\theta(y_n, \sigma_n)\) that reweights spectral coefficients according to their signal-to-noise ratio. Projecting \(y_\delta\) onto the left singular vectors yields coefficients \(y_n = \sigma_n f^\dagger_n + \epsilon_n\). Dividing by \(\sigma_n\) amplifies noise where \(\sigma_n\) is small. The learned factor \(\Psi_\theta \in [0,1]\) attenuates these amplified noisy coefficients while preserving those with high signal-to-noise ratio. The core assumption is a computable SVD for \(K\) and a source condition where \(f^\dagger\)'s spectral coefficients decay faster than \(\sigma_n\). Evidence includes the paper’s description of the three-step process (projection, filtering, synthesis) and the corpus context of spectral filter algorithms. The method breaks down if the singular system is unavailable or if the training distribution does not cover the relevant SNR ranges encountered at test time.

### Mechanism 2: Optimal Convergence and Discretization Invariance
The total error is decomposed into stability (boundedness of \(\Psi_\theta\)), approximation (universal approximation of the optimal filter), and truncation (choice of \(N\)). Balancing these, with \(N \asymp \delta^{-p/(s+p)}\), yields the minimax rate \(O(\delta^{s/(s+p)})\). Discretization invariance holds because the spectral basis \(\{u_n\}, \{v_n\}\) is independent of the spatial mesh; once \(N\) is chosen based on \(\delta\), the same filter \(\Psi_\theta\) applies on any grid where the basis can be evaluated. The core assumptions are polynomial decay \(\sigma_n \asymp n^{-p}\) and \(f^\dagger \in H^s\) with spectral decay \(| \langle f^\dagger, v_n \rangle | \lesssim n^{-(s+p)}\). Evidence is the formal Theorem 4.3 and the abstract claim of discretization invariance. The guarantees fail if the true smoothness \(s\) is lower than assumed or if basis interpolation on complex geometries introduces error.

### Mechanism 3: Zero-Shot Super-Resolution
SC-Net generalizes across spatial resolutions because it learns a mapping between function spaces (via spectral coefficients) rather than fixed-dimensional vectors. The filter \(\Psi_\theta\) depends only on \((\sigma_n, y_n)\), not on grid size. The truncation index \(N\) is chosen relative to \(\delta\), not absolute resolution. On finer grids, additional high-frequency modes become available, but the learned filter still appropriately damps those with low SNR. The assumption is that the singular vectors \(v_n\) can be evaluated/interpolated at any resolution and that the SNR distribution across modes has consistent structure. Evidence is the abstract claim and Figure 3 showing stable errors from \(N=256\) to \(N=2048\). Failure occurs if training resolution \(N_{\text{train}}\) is too small to capture relevant SNR patterns or if the operator’s spectral decay changes with resolution.

## Foundational Learning
- **Spectral Theory of Compact Operators**: Needed to understand ill-posedness (singular values \(\sigma_n \to 0\)) and how spectral filtering stabilizes inversion. Quick check: If \(\sigma_n \sim n^{-p}\), what does \(p\) imply about inversion difficulty? Higher \(p\) (faster decay) means more severe ill-posedness.
- **Ill-posedness and Spectral Regularization**: Needed to grasp the bias-variance trade-off in filtering (TSVD cutoff). Quick check: In TSVD filter \(g(\sigma_n)=1_{\sigma_n \geq \sqrt{\alpha}}\), what happens if \(\alpha\) is too large vs. too small? Too large preserves too few modes (high bias); too small admits too much noise (high variance).
- **Operator Learning vs. Fixed-resolution CNNs**: Needed to appreciate SC-Net’s resolution invariance. Quick check: Why does a U-Net trained on \(256\times256\) fail on \(512\times512\) for inverse problems, whereas SC-Net does not? U-Nets learn resolution-specific pixel mappings; SC-Net learns a function-space operator via spectral coefficients.

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Spectral Projection Layer -> Learnable Spectral Filter Block (shared MLP \(\Psi_\theta\)) -> Spectral Synthesis Layer
- Critical path:  
  1. For measurement \(y_\delta\), compute coefficients \(y_n = \langle y_\delta, u_n \rangle\) for \(n=1..N\).  
  2. For each \(n\), input \((y_n, \sigma_n)\) to \(\Psi_\theta\) to get factor \(\psi_n\).  
  3. Compute filtered coefficient \(\hat{f}_n = \psi_n \cdot (y_n / \sigma_n)\).  
  4. Reconstruct \(f_\theta = \sum_{n=1}^N \hat{f}_n v_n\).
- Design tradeoffs:  
  - Truncation index \(N\): Larger \(N\) captures more signal but risks noise amplification if filter mis-learns; theory suggests \(N \asymp \delta^{-p/(s+p)}\).  
  - Boundedness of \(\Psi_\theta\): Output must be in \([0,1]\) (e.g., via sigmoid) for stability guarantee.  
  - Network capacity vs. overfitting: A small MLP (e.g., 2 layers, 64 neurons) balances approximation and generalization; too large may overfit training noise.
- Failure signatures:  
  - Instability on test data (reconstruction norm blows up) → likely unbounded \(\Psi_\theta\) or mismatched \(N\).  
  - Poor zero-shot transfer (error increases on finer grids) → likely grid-dependent features in network or poor basis interpolation.  
  - Suboptimal convergence slope (< \(s/(s+p)\)) → insufficient network capacity or misaligned training loss.
- First 3 experiments:  
  1. **Convergence Rate Validation**: Train at fixed \(N=256\), test across noise levels \(\delta\), plot log(error) vs. log(\(\delta\)), fit slope.  
  2. **Filter Visualization**: For a trained model, plot \(\psi_n\) vs. \(n\) (or \(\sigma_n\)) and compare to Oracle Tikhonov and TSVD filters.  
  3. **Zero-Shot Resolution Test**: Train exclusively on \(N=256\) data, evaluate on \(N \in \{512, 1024, 2048\}\) without retraining, report relative \(L^2\) error.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can SC-Net be extended to nonlinear inverse problems where the spectral basis is data-dependent rather than fixed a priori?
- How can SC-Net be efficiently applied to high-dimensional inverse problems (e.g., 3D CT/MRI) where computing the full singular value decomposition of the forward operator is computationally intractable?
- What are the theoretical performance bounds of SC-Net under distributional shifts between training and testing data (e.g., changes in noise level, signal class, or measurement operator)?

## Limitations
- Relies on a computable SVD with polynomial decay, limiting applicability to nonlinear or non-self-adjoint operators.
- Evidence is restricted to 1D synthetic experiments; no validation on real-world 2D/3D imaging problems.
- Key hyperparameters (network depth, loss weight \(\gamma\), optimizer settings) are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction.

## Confidence
- **Convergence rate**: High (theorem + empirical match).
- **Discretization invariance**: Medium (theoretical basis but only 1D evidence).
- **Zero-shot super-resolution**: Medium (stable errors across resolutions in limited test).
- **Interpretability**: Medium-High (visual sharp cutoffs, no statistical variability reported).

## Next Checks
1. **2D generalization**: Train SC-Net on 1D (\(N=256\)), test on 2D deblurring across grids \(64\times64 \rightarrow 512\times512\); error variation should be <10%.
2. **\(\delta\)-robustness**: Train at \(\delta=0.05\), test at \(\delta \in \{0.01, 0.1\}\); error ratio to lower bound should stay within \(2\times\).
3. **Filter reproducibility**: Over 10 random seeds, compute transition sharpness (width of filter cutoff) and correlation with Oracle TSVD; report mean and variance to assess reliability.
