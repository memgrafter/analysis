# Latent Matters: Learning Deep State-Space Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23050  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23050  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** EKV AE achieves state-of-the-art prediction accuracy (MSE 1.99e-4 on pendulum) and near-perfect correlation (R²>0.98) with ground-truth states.

## Executive Summary
Deep state-space models (DSSMs) trained via the evidence lower bound (ELBO) often fail to learn underlying system dynamics, instead focusing on reconstruction. This paper introduces a constrained optimization (CO) framework that balances reconstruction quality (distortion) and dynamics learning (rate) by enforcing a constraint on distortion during training. Additionally, the authors propose the Extended Kalman VAE (EKV AE), which combines amortized variational inference with extended Kalman smoothing and a neural linearization approach to replace recurrent neural networks in the transition model. Applying CO to existing DSSMs (DKS, DVBS) yields substantial improvements in system identification and multi-step prediction. The EKV AE achieves the best results, with the lowest prediction MSE (e.g., 1.99e-4 vs. 3.53e-4 on pendulum) and R²>0.98 correlation with ground-truth states, demonstrating accurate disentangled representations for model-based RL.

## Method Summary
The CO framework reformulates the ELBO as a Lagrangian: ℒ(θ,ψ,ϕ;λ)=R(ψ,ϕ,ψ₀,ϕ₀)+λ(D(θ,ϕ)−D₀), where D₀ sets a target reconstruction quality. The Lagrange multiplier λ is adaptively updated to enforce D(θ,ϕ)≤D₀. Training proceeds in two phases: initially only the observation model (encoder and decoder) is optimized to meet the distortion constraint; once satisfied, the transition dynamics and variational hierarchical prior (VHP) are also optimized. The VHP learns a flexible prior for the initial latent state p(z₁) by matching it to the empirical marginal posterior, avoiding over-regularization from a fixed Gaussian. The EKV AE implements this framework with a specific architecture: an encoder maps observations xₜ to auxiliary variables aₜ; the transition model is locally linear Gaussian, with linearization parameters (F,B,Q) given by a weighted combination of learned base matrices using a gating network; and the auxiliary model ties aₜ linearly to zₜ. The posterior over latent states is computed exactly via extended Kalman smoothing, yielding Markovian zₜ and closed-form inference. This design eliminates RNNs, leading to more accurate dynamics learning.

## Key Results
- EKV AE significantly outperforms all baselines in prediction MSE, achieving 1.99e-4 on pendulum vs. 3.53e-4 for the next best.
- It achieves near-perfect correlation (R²>0.98) with ground-truth states, indicating precise system identification.
- Applying CO to DKS and DVBS also improves their performance, demonstrating the generality of the constrained approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper identifies that standard ELBO optimization can lead to models that sacrifice dynamics learning for high likelihood (e.g., by using trivial constant predictions). Three innovations address this:

1. **Constrained Optimization (CO) Framework**: By reformulating the ELBO as a Lagrangian with a distortion constraint D(θ,ϕ)≤D₀, the model is forced to achieve a minimum reconstruction quality before being penalized for rate (KL divergence). This prevents early collapse into a reconstruction-only solution and ensures dynamics are learned. An adaptive λ schedule (REWO) enforces the constraint during training. Evidence: Without CO, DKS fails to learn angular velocity despite high ELBO; with CO it succeeds (Sec. 6.1).

2. **Variational Hierarchical Prior (VHP)**: Replacing the fixed Gaussian prior p(z₁) with a learned hierarchical prior pψ₀(z₁)=∫pψ₀(z₁|ζ)p(ζ)dζ prevents over-regularization and broken generative models. The VHP is optimized to match the empirical marginal posterior of z₁ from the training data, ensuring the prior covers plausible states. Evidence: Non-VHP models generate incoherent sequences, while VHP produces valid rollouts (Sec. 6.1).

3. **Extended Kalman VAE (EKV AE) Architecture**: Instead of RNN-based transitions, EKV AE uses a locally linear transition model p(zₜ₊₁|zₜ,uₜ)=N(Fψ(zₜ,uₜ)zₜ+Bψ(zₜ,uₜ)uₜ, Qψ(zₜ,uₜ)). The Jacobians F and B are approximated by a gating network over a set of base matrices (neural linearization), avoiding costly Jacobian computations. Combined with a linear Gaussian auxiliary model p(aₜ|zₜ)=N(H zₜ,R) (with H often an identity for the first Dₐ dimensions), the posterior over zₜ can be computed exactly via extended Kalman smoothing, yielding Markovian latents and accurate inference. Evidence: EKV AE outperforms RNN-based models in prediction MSE and shows much higher correlation with ground-truth velocity (Sec. 6.2, 6.3).

## Foundational Learning
- **Amortized Variational Inference**: Enables learning an encoder network qϕ(z|x,u) that maps sequences to latent distributions, avoiding per-sequence optimization. *Why needed here*: It provides efficient inference for training DSSMs on large datasets. *Quick check*: How does amortization affect the trade-off between learning generalizable dynamics and overfitting to specific sequence patterns?
- **ELBO as Rate–Distortion Trade-off**: The ELBO decomposes into distortion (reconstruction quality) and rate (KL divergence to prior). *Why needed here*: Understanding this trade-off is key to why naive ELBO maximization fails for dynamics learning: a model can achieve high ELBO by either compressing well or reconstructing well, but dynamics require a balanced objective. *Quick check*: Why might a model with a lower ELBO sometimes learn better dynamics than one with a higher ELBO?
- **Bayesian Filtering/Smoothing**: Bayesian methods for state estimation in latent variable models, such as the Kalman filter/smoother for linear-Gaussian systems. *Why needed here*: The EKV AE’s core innovation is using extended Kalman smoothing to compute exact posteriors for its locally linear model, replacing approximate RNN-based posteriors. *Quick check*: What are the limitations of using extended Kalman methods when the model’s transition is only piecewise linear and the posterior is non-Gaussian?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
1. Encoder Network (qϕ(at|xt)): Maps observation xt to Gaussian parameters for at.
2. Auxiliary Variable Model (pψ(at|zt)): Linear Gaussian mapping from latent state zt to at, with matrix H and covariance R.
3. Transition Model (pψ(zt+1|zt,ut)): Locally linear Gaussian; base matrices F(m), B(m), Q(m) are learned; gating network gψ outputs weights αψ to combine them.
4. Empirical Bayes Prior (pψ0(z1)): Hierarchical prior p(ζ)=N(0,I) -> pψ0(z1|ζ) via a network, with inference network qϕ0(ζ|z1).
5. Decoder Network (pθ(xt|at)): Reconstructs xt from at.
6. Kalman Backend: Computes filtered/smoothed distributions p(zt|a1:T,u1:T-1) for the linearized system.

**Critical path**: Data (x1:T,u1:T) -> Encoder -> a1:T -> Extended Kalman Smoothing (using locally linear transition and linear observation model) -> smoothed state distributions -> sampling z1 -> unrolling with transition model and decoder to generate predictions and compute loss. The CO Lagrange multiplier λ is updated based on distortion D(θ,ϕ) to enforce D≤D₀.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Computational cost vs. accuracy: Kalman steps are efficient per-step but require covariance maintenance; neural linearization adds forward cost but avoids Jacobian.
- Markovian vs. expressive dynamics: Locally linear transition yields Markovian zt but may limit expressiveness for highly nonlinear dynamics.
- Disentanglement vs. reconstruction: Using identity H for first Dₐ dimensions enforces disentanglement but may constrain representation if Dₐ is poorly chosen.
- CO constraint (D₀) sensitivity: D₀ must be set appropriately; too high infeasible, too low under-constrains dynamics.

**Failure signatures**:
1. High test ELBO but poor prediction MSE: dynamics not learned; check λ schedule and constraint satisfaction.
2. Broken generative sequences from prior: VHP failure or prior mismatch.
3. Non-Markovian latent code: velocity correlated with RNN state not zt (in RNN baselines).
4. Persistent constraint violation (D>D₀): model cannot achieve required reconstruction; increase D₀ or adjust ν.
5. Divergence during VHP training: hierarchical prior optimization unstable.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Reconstruction validation**: Train EKV AE with CO and verify D(θ,ϕ)≤D₀ on validation set; inspect reconstructions for fidelity.
2. **Constraint ablation**: Ablate CO (fix λ=1) and compare state-space visualization and R² of inferred states vs. ground truth; expect failure to represent velocity.
3. **Baseline comparison**: Train DKS, DVBS, and EKV AE with CO+VHP; evaluate prediction MSE conditioned on initial segments; expect EKV AE to achieve lowest MSE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Sensitivity of CO to D₀**: How robust is the CO framework to the choice of D₀, and can a principled method for setting D₀ be derived? The paper uses a heuristic with no theoretical guidance.
2. **Validity of neural linearization**: For what classes of nonlinear dynamics does the weighted base matrix approximation provide an accurate enough model for extended Kalman smoothing? The approach is heuristic and lacks error analysis.
3. **Automatic disentanglement**: Can EKV AE automatically determine the dimensionality of disentangled static features (Dₐ) without manual specification? Currently Dₐ must be chosen a priori.
4. **Generality across architectures and data**: Does the CO framework consistently improve other DSSM architectures and real-world datasets with partial observability? Evaluations so far are limited to two DSSMs and two simulated environments.

## Limitations
- The CO framework’s performance depends critically on hyperparameters D₀ and λ schedule; their robustness across tasks is unclear.
- EKV AE’s locally linear transition assumption may fail for highly nonlinear or chaotic dynamics, limiting its applicability.
- Evaluation is restricted to synthetic pendulum and reacher tasks; generalization to real-world systems with complex noise and partial observability remains unproven.
- Baseline comparisons use CO-enhanced versions; it is uncertain whether improvements stem primarily from CO or architectural innovations.

## Confidence
- CO framework efficacy (preventing ELBO collapse): High
- VHP necessity (avoiding broken generative models): Medium
- EKV AE advantages (neural linearization + Kalman smoothing): Medium
- Overall SOTA performance on benchmarks: Medium
