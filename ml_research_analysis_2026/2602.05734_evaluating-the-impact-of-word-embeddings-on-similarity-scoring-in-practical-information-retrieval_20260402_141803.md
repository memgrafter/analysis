# Evaluating the impact of word embeddings on similarity scoring in practical information retrieval

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.05734
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05734
*   **Reference count:** 0
*   **Primary result:** WMD with GloVe embeddings achieved 100% recall in the top 20 results and 89.83% top-1 accuracy on a legal document retrieval task, significantly outperforming centroid-based methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the lexical gap in information retrieval by evaluating Word Mover's Distance (WMD) combined with pre-trained word embeddings (Word2Vec, FastText, GloVe) for semantic similarity scoring. Unlike centroid-based approaches (e.g., Doc2Vec, LSA) that aggregate text into a single vector, WMD computes the minimal "travel distance" between individual word distributions. Experiments on a legal prospectus corpus reveal that the WMD + GloVe combination yields superior accuracy, returning 100% of correct matches within the top 20 and nearly 90% as the top-ranked result. The study concludes that WMD with robust, general-purpose embeddings offers a domain-agnostic, high-accuracy solution for semantic retrieval, though it notes significant computational scalability challenges.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct a comparative evaluation on a legal document corpus (8,127 statements from a prospectus) with 59 query variations. Pre-processing involves lowercasing, tokenization, and stop-word removal. They test WMD using three pre-trained embeddings (Word2Vec, FastText, GloVe) against centroid-based Doc2Vec (three variants) and LSA baselines. For each query, the system ranks all document statements by similarity score (WMD distance or cosine similarity for centroids). Performance is measured by the recall of the known correct target statement within the top 20, top 3, and top 1 positions. The core finding is that WMD, particularly with GloVe vectors, dramatically improves ranking accuracy over aggregated vector methods.

## Key Results
*   WMD combined with GloVe embeddings achieved 100% recall in the top 20 results and 89.83% (53/59) top-1 accuracy.
*   The same WMD approach with Word2Vec embeddings yielded only 58.98% top-1 accuracy, demonstrating a significant performance gap tied to the embedding model.
*   All WMD variants (with any embedding) achieved 100% top-20 recall, while Doc2Vec and LSA methods performed substantially worse, especially on short queries.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Fine-Grained Alignment via Optimal Transport
**Claim:** Word Mover's Distance (WMD) improves semantic matching by considering the minimum "travel distance" between individual query and document word vectors, rather than aggregating words into a single centroid vector.
**Mechanism:** WMD treats each text as a distribution of word embeddings. It solves an optimization problem (a relaxed Earth Mover's Distance) to find the minimal cumulative pairwise cosine distances required to transform the query's word distribution into the document's word distribution, capturing fine-grained semantic alignment.
**Core assumption:** Semantic similarity is better estimated by aligning individual constituent words than by summarizing the entire text into an average vector, which can lose distinctions, especially for short queries.
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract states similarity is evaluated using distance between individual words. Section 2.2 explains WMD measures similarity by calculating the minimum "travelling distance" between text objects, contrasting it with centroid struggles on multi-topic documents.
**Break condition:** The original O(n³ log n) complexity becomes prohibitive for very long documents or large corpora, forcing a switch to approximate methods like Relaxed WMD.

### Mechanism 2: Robust, Domain-Agnostic Representations from Large-Scale Training
**Claim:** Pre-trained, large-scale word embeddings (GloVe, Word2Vec, FastText) provide robust, domain-agnostic semantic representations because they capture general linguistic patterns from vast, diverse corpora.
**Mechanism:** Trained on billions of tokens (e.g., Common Crawl, Google News) using the distributional hypothesis, these models learn vectors where semantically related words reside in close proximity, regardless of downstream domain.
**Core assumption:** General linguistic semantics are stable enough across domains that embeddings trained on generic web data will transfer effectively, avoiding expensive domain-specific retraining.
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract claims pre-trained embeddings "result in domain agnostic language processing solutions." Section 3 states the models were trained over vast amounts of data to ensure they "were not over sensitive to the test dataset."
**Break condition:** Domain-specific jargon, specialized terminology, or unique syntactic constructions may fall outside the training distribution, leading to poor vector quality without fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 3: GloVe's Global Co-occurrence Advantage for This Task
**Claim:** GloVe embeddings, when combined with WMD, yield superior retrieval accuracy compared to Word2Vec or FastText in this experimental context due to its global matrix factorization approach capturing different statistical regularities.
**Mechanism:** GloVe combines global count-based co-occurrence matrix factorization (like LSA) with local context-window prediction goals. For the task of matching short, rephrased legal statements, the global co-occurrence statistics produce a more meaningful geometric space for WMD distance calculation.
**Core assumption:** The semantic relationships critical for matching (e.g., synonymy, conceptual association) are better preserved in GloVe's vector space geometry than in the purely local context-prediction spaces of Word2Vec or the subword-influenced space of FastText.
**Evidence anchors:** Table 3 shows WMD-GloVe with 53/59 (89.83%) top-1 matches versus WMD-Word2Vec's 18/59 (58.98%). Section 5.1 states pre-trained GloVe vectors "proved successful in accurate retrieval of both short and long query-statement searches."
**Break condition:** The advantage is dataset/task-specific. For tasks reliant on subword morphology or local word order, FastText or a different similarity measure might outperform.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Distributional Semantics & Vector Space Model**
    *   *Why needed here:* The entire approach relies on the principle that word meaning is approximated by its distributional context in a large corpus, explaining why similar words have nearby vectors.
    *   *Quick check question:* If two words frequently appear in similar sentences during training, what does the Vector Space Model predict about their vector representations?

*   **Concept: Word Mover's Distance (WMD) as an Optimal Transport Problem**
    *   *Why needed here:* WMD is the core mechanism. Understanding it as a constrained optimization (transport plan) that respects word frequencies is key, not a simple average distance.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why is solving an optimal transport problem between word distributions potentially more informative for sentence similarity than computing the cosine similarity between their mean (centroid) vectors?

*   **Concept: Trade-offs between Count-based (GloVe/LSA) and Prediction-based (Word2Vec) Embeddings**
    *   *Why needed here:* The paper's key result hinges on GloVe's superior performance. Understanding the theoretical difference—global co-occurrence matrix factorization vs. local context prediction—explains potential performance divergence.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does GloVe's use of a global word-word co-occurrence matrix theoretically differ in what it captures compared to Word2Vec's local window-based prediction objective?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Pre-trained Embedding Lookup Table -> Document Store (pre-processed corpus) -> WMD Similarity Engine -> Ranker -> Evaluation Harness
*   **Critical path:** For a new query: Query Text -> Tokenize & Remove Stopwords -> Vector Lookup (per word) -> Form Query Word Bags -> For each Candidate Document: Compute WMD(Query_Vectors, Doc_Vectors) -> Store Score -> Sort all Docs by Score -> Return Top-K.
    *   *Bottleneck:* The "For each Candidate Document: Compute WMD" loop. WMD computation per pair is expensive (O(p*q*³) for p,q unique words), making naive full-corpus search infeasible for 8,127 statements.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Accuracy vs. Scalability:** Full WMD is accurate but scales poorly. Production use requires approximations (Relaxed WMD, inverted indexing) or a two-stage retrieval (fast lexical filter then WMD re-rank).
    *   **Embedding Choice vs. Task:** GloVe gave best results here, but FastText is better for OOV words. The tradeoff depends on expected domain jargon and query characteristics.
    *   **Query Length Sensitivity:** The paper notes Doc2Vec struggles with short queries. WMD+embeddings handled them well. Choose mechanism based on expected query length distribution.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **High WMD scores for intuitively similar pairs:** Suggests embedding space geometry is misaligned for the task (try different pre-trained embeddings or fine-tune).
    *   **Process hangs or times out on moderate-length documents:** Indicates the unapproximated WMD algorithm is hitting its cubic complexity limit. Switch to Relaxed WMD or use a two-stage retrieval pipeline.
    *   **Poor recall on queries with rare/domain-specific terms:** Check if terms are Out-Of-Vocabulary (OOV). FastText might help via subwords, but true domain terms may need fine-tuning or glossary enrichment.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Sanity Check:** Replicate the paper's experiment using the Foxtons prospectus dataset and specified pre-trained models (GoogleNews Word2Vec, CommonCrawl GloVe/FastText). Verify Table 1's 100% top-20 recall for all WMD variants.
    2.  **Component Isolation Test:** Swap only the embedding model while keeping WMD identical. Compare WMD+GloVe vs. WMD+Word2Vec vs. WMD+FastText on a subset of queries. Confirm the performance gap shown in Table 3, isolating the embedding's contribution.
    3.  **Scalability Stress Test:** Use a larger public corpus (e.g., Wikipedia subset). Measure query latency and throughput for full WMD, Relaxed WMD, and a centroid+cosine baseline as document count grows. Identify the corpus size where full WMD becomes impractical.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question:** How can the computational efficiency of Word Mover's Distance (WMD) be improved to enable scalable use on large, full-text document repositories?
    *   *Basis:* Explicit. Section 5.1 states scalability is challenging due to O(n³ log n) complexity and cites Relaxed WMD as a potential solution.
    *   *Why unresolved:* The paper demonstrates accuracy on a small corpus but provides no empirical scalability benchmarks or production-ready approximation evaluation.
    *   *Needed evidence:* Benchmarks comparing original WMD vs. approximations (RWMD, inverted indexing) on a large-scale collection, measuring both relevance and resource usage.

*   **Question:** Under what specific conditions do pre-trained, domain-agnostic word embeddings fail, necessitating domain-specific fine-tuning?
    *   *Basis:* Inferred. The conclusion claims "domain agnostic" portability but concedes a disadvantage for "particularly nuanced and specific" business domains.
    *   *Why unresolved:* The claim is based on one legal corpus; thresholds for failure (e.g., in biomedical or slang-heavy domains) are undefined.
    *   *Needed evidence:* A systematic multi-domain evaluation comparing generic vs. fine-tuned embeddings, identifying linguistic/statistical features that predict when fine-tuning is required.

*   **Question:** To what extent can WMD's gains be integrated into a hybrid IR framework with traditional lexical matching (e.g., BM25) and user signals without prohibitive overhead?
    *   *Basis:* Explicit. The conclusion proposes a hybrid framework combining semantic approaches with BM25 and click-through data.
    *   *Why unresolved:* The paper evaluates WMD in isolation; the mechanism for blending its costly scores with fast lexical signals and dynamic feedback is unexplored.
    *   *Needed evidence:* Implementation and evaluation of a system where WMD scores are combined (e.g., via learning-to-rank) with BM25 and implicit feedback, measuring the relevance-latency trade-off.

*   **Question:** What is the specific factor causing GloVe embeddings to significantly outperform Word2Vec and FastText in the WMD framework for this task?
    *   *Basis:* Explicit. Tables 2 and 3 show a large margin (89.83% vs. 58.98% top-1) for WMD-GloVe over WMD-Word2Vec.
    *   *Why unresolved:* The paper does not analyze whether the discrepancy is due to algorithms (global co-occurrence vs. local context), training corpora, or interaction with WMD.
    *   *Needed evidence:* A controlled ablation comparing WMD on different embedding sets trained on the same corpus, plus analysis of embedding space properties and legal corpus vocabulary coverage.

## Limitations
*   **Missing Ground Truth:** The complete mapping of all 59 queries to their exact target statement IDs is not provided, blocking full quantitative replication.
*   **Hyperparameter Vagueness:** Exact Doc2Vec training parameters (epochs, window size, negative sampling) are unspecified, relying on assumptions that introduce replication variance.
*   **Scalability Claim Untested:** The computational bottleneck of full WMD is noted theoretically but not empirically validated on the 8,127-statement corpus or larger; reported runtimes are absent.

## Confidence
*   **WMD Mechanism Superiority (vs. Centroid):** High. Theoretical argument and internal results (top-20 accuracy) strongly support this for the specific legal corpus.
*   **GloVe + WMD Performance Edge:** High. The paper's own data shows a large, clear margin in top-1 accuracy.
*   **Domain-Agnostic Portability:** Medium. Supported by a single-domain experiment but lacks validation on other domains and is contradicted by the noted break condition for specialized jargon.
*   **Scalability & Practical Deployment:** Low. Computational cost is a stated limitation with no empirical stress test or evaluated production-ready approximation.
