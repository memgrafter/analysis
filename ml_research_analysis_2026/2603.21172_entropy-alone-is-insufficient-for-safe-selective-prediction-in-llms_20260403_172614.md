# Entropy Alone is Insufficient for Safe Selective Prediction in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21172  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21172  
- **Reference count:** 26  
- **Primary result:** Entropy-based uncertainty quantification fails in "confidently wrong" hallucinations; combining entropy with a supervised correctness probe improves safe deployment metrics (E-AURC, TCE) across multiple models and QA benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies a critical failure mode in entropy-based uncertainty methods for selective prediction in LLMs: they often assign low entropy to incorrect answers (confidently wrong hallucinations), leading to unreliable abstention at low target risk levels. To address this, the authors propose combining entropy scores with a supervised correctness probe trained on model activations. Evaluated on three QA benchmarks and four model families, the combined approach consistently lowers Excess Area Under the Risk–Coverage Curve (E-AURC) and Target Calibration Error (TCE), demonstrating better risk–coverage trade-offs and calibration to safety thresholds. The results highlight that global detection metrics like AUROC can mask failures in strict safety regimes, emphasizing the need for deployment-facing evaluation.

## Method Summary
The task is selective prediction for question answering: given an LLM's answer, decide whether to accept it or abstain, aiming to keep the hallucination rate among accepted answers below a target α. The authors generate answers for 1000 questions per dataset (TriviaQA, BioASQ, MedicalQA) using four instruction-tuned models (Llama, Qwen, Gemma, Ministral). For each question, they produce one low-temperature answer (T=0.1) and ten high-temperature samples (T=1) to compute uncertainty. Uncertainty signals include: (1) entropy-based scores such as Sequence NLL (single-pass) and Semantic Entropy (SE, which clusters semantically similar high-temperature samples using mutual entailment), and (2) a supervised Correctness Probe (PC) – a logistic regression trained on frozen last-token-before-generation activations to predict binary correctness (determined by an LLM-as-judge). The combined risk score is produced by training a second logistic regression on the pair (entropy score, PC risk proxy). For each method, an acceptance threshold τ is tuned on a calibration set to achieve the largest coverage while keeping selective risk ≤ α. Performance is evaluated on a held-out test set using deployment-facing metrics: E-AURC (area above the ideal risk-coverage curve) and TCE (mean deviation from target risk across α ∈ [0.05,0.3]).

## Key Results
- Entropy-based methods (e.g., Semantic Entropy) exhibit a "confidently wrong" regime where hallucinations receive low entropy, creating a risk floor that prevents achieving low target risk levels.
- Combining entropy with the PC probe consistently improves E-AURC and TCE across most (model, dataset) pairs, with the exception of SE on MedicalQA.
- Global metrics like AUROC can be high even when methods fail to meet strict safety thresholds (α ≤ 0.15), underscoring the need for deployment-focused evaluation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Entropy-based signals fail in the confidently wrong regime.
- **Claim:** Entropy-based uncertainty signals exhibit a model-dependent failure mode where confidently wrong hallucinations receive low entropy, leading to a risk floor in selective prediction.
- **Mechanism:** The model's output distribution for certain incorrect answers is sharp (low entropy) because it generates a single, fluent but wrong response with high confidence, decoupling model certainty from correctness.
- **Core assumption:** Low entropy implies certainty but not necessarily correctness; they can decouple.
- **Evidence:** For Qwen on TriviaQA, Semantic Entropy collapses to zero for both correct and incorrect answers, while a correctness probe distinguishes them (Section 3.2). Breaks when base accuracy is very low (MedicalQA).

### Mechanism 2: Combining entropy with a supervised correctness probe mitigates failure.
- **Claim:** The two signals are complementary: entropy captures distributional dispersion, while the probe learns a direct mapping from activations to correctness, identifying confidently wrong cases where entropy is low.
- **Mechanism:** Model activations contain information about forthcoming correctness not reflected in output entropy. The probe can flag low-entropy hallucinations.
- **Core assumption:** Model activations contain correctness information not captured by output distribution entropy.
- **Evidence:** Combined method improves E-AURC and TCE across most (model, dataset) pairs; Figure 2 shows probe separates hallucinations even when SE=0. Fails on very hard tasks like MedicalQA where probe calibration suffers.

### Mechanism 3: Deployment-facing metrics reveal failures hidden from AUROC.
- **Claim:** Metrics like E-AURC and TCE are necessary because they evaluate performance at strict, operational risk thresholds, whereas AUROC aggregates over all thresholds and can mask failures in high-trust regimes.
- **Mechanism:** Real deployment requires meeting a predefined maximum acceptable error rate α, not just ranking hallucinations above correct answers globally. E-AURC penalizes inability to achieve low selective risk at any coverage; TCE measures deviation from target α across a relevant range.
- **Core assumption:** Operational safety requires meeting a specified risk bound at a given coverage, not just good ranking.
- **Evidence:** Entropy-only methods hit a risk floor and fail to enter α≤0.15 regime; combined methods track the calibration diagonal. TCE captures this divergence (Figure 3). Breaks if α range is misaligned with true safety constraints.

## Foundational Learning
#### Selective Prediction Setup and Risk–Coverage Trade-off
- **Why needed:** The evaluation framework revolves around tuning an abstention threshold to achieve a target hallucination rate α among answered questions; understanding coverage vs. selective risk is essential for interpreting E-AURC and TCE.
- **Quick check:** If a system abstains on all examples, coverage=0 and selective risk is undefined. The oracle ranking achieves the lower envelope of the risk–coverage curve.

#### Entropy-Based vs. Supervised Correctness Uncertainty Signals
- **Why needed:** The core technical contribution combines a cheap, distribution-based entropy signal with a learned, supervision-dependent correctness probe. Understanding their distinct computation and assumptions is key to diagnosing complementary behavior.
- **Quick check:** Why might Semantic Entropy assign a score of 0 to a hallucination? Because all high-temperature samples are semantically equivalent (or clustered into one group) despite being incorrect. How does the PC probe differ? It uses internal activations to directly predict correctness, not output distribution.

#### Deployment-Facing Calibration (TCE vs. ECE)
- **Why needed:** The paper argues against global probability calibration (ECE) in favor of TCE, which measures calibration at specific, operationally relevant risk targets. This conceptual shift is crucial for interpreting results.
- **Quick check:** Why would a system with perfect AUROC still be unsafe at α=0.05? Because AUROC measures ranking quality across all thresholds; the top-ranked examples (which would be answered at low coverage) might still contain many hallucinations. TCE differs by measuring absolute deviation from target α over a range, directly testing whether the system can meet specified safety bounds.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (question + model) → Entropy Calculator → u_ent(x)  
Input → Model forward pass (extract last-token-before-generation activations) → Correctness Probe (logistic regression) → u_pc(x) = 1 - p_pc(x)  
[u_ent(x), u_pc(x)] → Combiner (logistic regression) → r(x)  
r(x) on calibration set → Threshold Tuner (for target α) → τ  
r(x) on test set + labels → Evaluation (E-AURC, TCE)

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate answers and correctness labels (LLM-as-judge) for train/cal/test splits (70/15/15 stratified).  
2. For Semantic Entropy: generate K=10 high-temperature samples per question and cluster using mutual entailment (DeBERTa).  
3. Train PC probe: select best layer via 5-fold CV on train split, then fit logistic regression on TBG activations to predict binary correctness.  
4. Compute entropy scores (Sequence NLL, SE, or SEP) for all examples.  
5. Train combiner logistic regression on train split using u_ent and u_pc.  
6. For each α in [0.05,0.3], tune τ on calibration set (largest τ with selective risk ≤ α), evaluate test selective risk and coverage, compute E-AURC and TCE.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Cost vs. Robustness:* Sequence NLL/Probe are cheap (single pass); Semantic Entropy expensive (K passes + entailment) but more robust to lexical variation.  
- *Supervision dependency:* PC probe requires binary correctness labels; in low-resource settings, unsupervised proxies may be needed.  
- *Combiner complexity:* Simple logistic regression suffices; deeper models add hyperparameter sensitivity without consistent gains.  
- *Operational range:* α range should cover from the hardest achievable target to near base hallucination rate.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Risk floor in RC curve: Entropy-only methods plateau above target α, never entering high-trust regime (α ≤ 0.15).  
- TCE spike at low α: Realized risk systematically exceeds target for α < 0.1.  
- Collapsed entropy distribution: Large mass at SE=0 despite mixed correctness.  
- MedicalQA degradation: All methods show higher TCE; combination fails to improve over SE alone.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce confidently wrong regime: For Qwen/TriviaQA, plot SE vs. PC probe logit; verify vertical cluster at SE=0 contains both correct and incorrect answers.  
2. Test combination benefit in high-trust regime: For Ministral/BioASQ, plot RC curves for SE, PC probe, and combined; show combined enters α ≤ 0.15 regime at meaningful coverage while entropy-only does not.  
3. Evaluate on hard dataset: For Gemma 3 4B/MedicalQA, run all methods and record that SE alone has lower TCE than combined, confirming the exception.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to long-form/reasoning tasks**: Does the confidently wrong failure mode extend beyond short-form QA? The paper admits its evaluation is limited to short-form QA, leaving this open. Resolution requires systematic replication on long-form, reasoning, and open-ended benchmarks with appropriate hallucination labels.

2. **Variation across model scale, architecture, and training**: How does the efficacy of entropy-based signals vary with model scale, architecture, and training recipe beyond the 3B–8B range? The paper's narrow evaluation (four families, 3B–8B) leaves this open. Evidence: extend experiments to sub-1B to 100B+ models, diverse architectures, and RLHF-trained models.

3. **Eliminating supervised correctness labels**: Can the reliance on supervised correctness labels for the PC probe be reduced or eliminated using unsupervised proxies without sacrificing performance? The paper calls this a limitation. Evidence: develop self-supervised correctness proxies (e.g., consistency across rationales, internal state metrics) and evaluate on selective prediction metrics.

4. **Failure on MedicalQA**: Why does the combination with Semantic Entropy fail to improve TCE on MedicalQA? The paper links this to low base accuracy making the probe hard to calibrate but lacks a causal mechanism. Evidence: error analysis comparing signal distributions on MedicalQA vs. easier datasets; controlled experiments varying base accuracy.

## Limitations
- **Unknown implementations:** The specific LLM-as-judge model/prompt and DeBERTa checkpoint for Semantic Entropy clustering are unspecified, risking label/proxy drift and hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Supervision dependency:** The correctness probe requires costly binary correctness labels for training, limiting applicability in low-resource or specialized domains.
- **Evaluation scope:** Results are confined to short-form QA; the "confidently wrong" regime and effectiveness of combination may not generalize to long-form, reasoning, or open-ended tasks.

## Confidence
- **Entropy failure mode (confidently wrong):** Medium (strong internal evidence, weak corpus support).
- **Combination superiority:** High (consistent improvement across 12 (model, dataset) pairs with one documented exception).
- **Deployment-metric necessity:** High (clear empirical demonstration that AUROC masks low-α failures).
