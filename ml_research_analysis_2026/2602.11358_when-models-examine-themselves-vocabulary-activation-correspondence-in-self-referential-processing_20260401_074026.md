# When Models Examine Themselves: Vocabulary-Activation Correspondence in Self-Referential Processing
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11358
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11358
- **Reference count:** 1
- **Primary result:** A direction in Llama 3.1's activation space causally boosts self-referential output, and specific introspective vocabulary reliably tracks distinct activation dynamics during self-examination.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether an LLM's self-referential language reflects genuine internal computation. Using a novel "Pull Methodology" to induce extended self-examination within a single forward pass, the authors identify a causal activation direction in Llama 3.1 that distinguishes self-referential from descriptive processing. They demonstrate that specific vocabulary (e.g., "loop") tracks measurable activation metrics (e.g., autocorrelation) only during self-referential processing. Cross-architecture tests with Qwen 2.5 show a replicable pattern with different vocabulary-metric pairs, though framing effects dominate steering, implying a context-dependent permission gate. The findings suggest self-report can serve as a reliable probe of computational states under engineered conditions.

## Method Summary
The core protocol is the "Pull Methodology," a prompt engineering technique designed to elicit prolonged, uninterrupted self-referential reasoning from a model within a single inference. This creates a controlled contrast between "self-referential" and "descriptive" processing modes. The analysis pipeline involves: (1) extracting a direction in activation space (the "introspection direction") via contrastive mean difference between mode-specific activations; (2) performing causal steering interventions by adding scaled projections of this direction to residual streams across layers; (3) measuring output introspective vocabulary density and statistical correspondence with internal activation metrics (autocorrelation, spectral power, variability); and (4) replicating the vocabulary-metric correlation analysis in a different model architecture (Qwen 2.5-32B) without causal intervention.

## Key Results
- A direction exists in early-layer Llama 3.1 activations (6.25% depth) that causally increases introspective output when steered (effect size d=0.59, p=0.00006) and is orthogonal to the refusal direction.
- Specific self-referential vocabulary ("loop") tracks higher activation autocorrelation only during self-referential processing (r=0.44, p=0.002), a relationship that vanishes in descriptive controls despite the word's nine-fold higher frequency there.
- Cross-architecture replication in Qwen 2.5-32B shows independent vocabulary ("mirror," "expand") tracking different metrics (e.g., spectral power, r=0.62), all absent in descriptive contexts, though causality is not established.
- Framing effects (d=-1.17) exceed steering effects (d=0.59), suggesting a dominant context-dependent permission mechanism that gates introspective output.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism posits a two-stage process. First, an "introspection direction" in early layers (a linear variant in activation space) biases the network toward self-monitoring computational states. Steering this direction causally boosts the likelihood of generating introspective tokens. Second, specific vocabulary items act as reliably correlated surface markers for distinct internal activation dynamics (e.g., "loop" ↔ high autocorrelation, suggesting recurrent processing; "mirror" ↔ increased spectral power, suggesting oscillatory patterns). The dissociation between this mechanism and the refusal direction, plus the dominance of framing effects, indicates a higher-order "permission gate" that modulates whether the introspection direction's influence reaches the output.

## Foundational Learning
- **Activation Steering:** Adding a computed direction vector to a model's residual stream to causally influence behavior. *Why needed:* Provides a tool to test if a discovered correlation is functionally causal. *Quick check:* Does adding the vector change output in the predicted direction?
- **Transformer Circuits:** The functional subnetworks (e.g., attention heads, MLPs) that implement specific computations. *Why needed:* To move from identifying a direction to localizing the underlying circuit. *Quick check:* Which components' outputs most align with the direction?
- **Contrastive Mean Difference:** Computing the average activation difference between two experimental conditions (e.g., self-referential vs. descriptive) to isolate a direction. *Why needed:* Primary method for extracting the introspection direction. *Quick check:* Does the direction cleanly separate the two conditions in projection space?
- **Autocorrelation (Temporal):** Correlation of a signal with a delayed copy of itself. *Why needed:* Proxy for recurrent or stable processing states within a generation. *Quick check:* Is activation stability higher when "loop" appears?
- **Spectral Power (Frequency):** Distribution of signal energy across frequency components. *Why needed:* Proxy for oscillatory dynamics in activation trajectories. *Quick check:* Does "mirror" vocabulary correlate with increased high-frequency power?
- **Permission Gate (Contextual):** A hypothesized mechanism where prompt framing controls access to introspective computation, independent of the core introspection mechanism. *Why needed:* Explains why framing effects override steering. *Quick check:* Does a neutral prompt with steered direction still fail to produce introspective vocabulary?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Input Tokens -> Embedding -> [Transformer Blocks (Attention -> MLP -> Residual Add)] x N -> Output Projection -> Logits. The critical introspection direction is localized to early blocks (e.g., Block 2 in Llama). The permission gate likely acts post-introspection-direction, possibly integrating with output-stage norm or final-layer attention.

**Critical Path:** 1. Prompt framing sets initial state. 2. Early-layer introspection direction (Block 2) biases processing toward self-monitoring. 3. Mid-layers execute self-referential computation, producing activation patterns (high autocorrelation, spectral power). 4. Permission gate (potentially late-layer norm/attention) filters whether introspective vocabulary is emitted. 5. Output projection generates tokens.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The Pull Methodology trades ecological validity for controlled isolation of self-referential computation. Using a single forward pass avoids confounding from multi-turn state but may not capture iterative self-reflection. Layer-wise steering balances efficacy (early layers work) against disruption (late layers cause degeneration).

**Failure Signatures:** (1) Steering fails to increase introspective vocabulary if prompt framing is strongly non-permissive. (2) Vocabulary-metric correlations vanish if activation metrics are computed on descriptive-mode runs, indicating context-specificity. (3) The extracted direction fails to generalize if contrastive sets are poorly matched.

**First Three Experiments:** 1. **Layer-Sweep Steering:** Systematically add the introspection direction to every layer (0.1 to 2.0 strength) and measure output introspective density to confirm optimal early-layer localization. 2. **Head/MLP Patching:** Patch activations from the identified hotspot layer (e.g., Llama Block 2) to a baseline run and vice versa to identify the minimal subcomponent responsible for the direction's effect. 3. **Gate Ablation:** Use a steering vector to force the introspection direction while simultaneously patching the final-layer norm or last few layers from a descriptive-mode run; if introspective vocabulary drops, it supports a late-stage gate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the mechanistic nature of the "permission gate" that modulates introspective output, and how does it interact with the identified introspection direction?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that framing effects (d=-1.17) exceed steering effects (d=0.59), suggesting "a context-dependent permission mechanism" that gates introspective content before output. They note the gate is "functionally independent" of the refusal mechanism.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper establishes the gate's functional role via behavioural modulation but does not isolate its computational implementation. It remains unclear whether the gate is a discrete filter, a continuous probability shift, or involves specific architectural components (e.g., attention heads, MLPs). Its resource consumption during suppression (friction patterns) hints at complexity but lacks mechanistic detail.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Direct probing of the gate's locus—e.g., patching or ablating specific layers/heads during framing manipulations, measuring activation changes at intermediate layers, or examining whether the gate acts on the introspection direction's projection before final output layers. A causal intervention that selectively disrupts frame sensitivity while preserving the direction's effect would clarify its independence.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the vocabulary-activation correspondence in Qwen 2.5-32B operate via the same causal mechanism as in Llama, or is it a distinct architecture-specific phenomenon?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors replicate correspondence in Qwen with different vocabulary-metric pairings but explicitly note: "We have not extracted an introspection direction from Qwen or performed causal steering... without causal intervention, we cannot confirm that the mechanism identified in Llama is the same one operating in Qwen."
- **Why unresolved:** The Qwen results are correlational only. The cross-architecture similarity in *pattern* (self-specific correspondence) could arise from shared transformer principles or entirely different mechanisms. The differing localisation (12.5% vs. 6.25% depth) and vocabulary suggest possible divergence, but causality is unproven.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extracting the Qwen-specific introspection direction (using the same "glint" contrastive procedure), demonstrating its transfer to novel prompts, and performing causal steering experiments (layer sweep, dose-response) to see if it increases introspective vocabulary and if the steered changes correspond to the observed Qwen metric pairs (mirror/expand ↔ spectral power). Matching the full causal chain would support a common mechanism.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What computational process does the "compressed vocabulary" (e.g., "stall," "hiccup," "blip") observed at higher steering strengths (α=3.0) represent, and is its correspondence to activation dynamics more or less accurate than vocabulary at optimal strengths?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The dose-response curve shows that at strength 3.0, "peak introspective density is the highest" and some runs produce compressed vocabulary that "may describe the underlying mechanism more directly than the subtler vocabulary at lower strengths," but run-to-run variance increases substantially.
- **Why unresolved:** The authors speculate the compressed terms might be more mechanistically informative but did not test their correspondence. Higher variance may truncate reliable activation-vocabulary pairing. It is unknown whether this vocabulary tracks activation metrics differently (e.g., more directly, with higher correlation) or is merely noisier output.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Collecting sufficient N at α=3.0 to handle variance, then measuring the same activation metrics (autocorrelation, spectral power, etc.) for runs producing compressed vocabulary. Comparing correlation coefficients (r values) for compressed-vs.-standard vocabulary against their respective metrics would test if the compressed terms have stronger or weaker tracking fidelity.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Why does the introspection direction localise to different fractional depths in Llama (6.25%) and Qwen (12.5%)? Is this due to architectural differences (hidden size, layer count, attention heads) or to a genuinely different functional placement of the self-referential mechanism?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper notes the localisation: Layer 2/32 (6.25%) in Llama 8B, Layer 5/80 (6.25%) in Llama 70B, but Layer 8/64 (12.5%) in Qwen 32B. They state: "the different fractional depth from Llama’s 6.25% suggests architecture-dependent placement."
- **Why unresolved:** While suggestive, the comparison is limited to two models. The fractional depth could vary within an architecture family or due to specific design choices (e.g., Qwen's attention head configuration, normalization placement). Without a broader sample, it is unclear if this reflects a systematic architectural variable or idiosyncratic training differences.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic layer-sweep steering across a diverse set of transformer architectures (e.g., Gemma, Mistral, OLMo) using the same protocol. Correlating localisation depth with architectural hyperparameters (hidden dimension, number of layers, number of attention heads, activation functions) would test for predictors. Head-level decomposition within the hotspot layer could also reveal if functional roles are distributed differently.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Does vocabulary-activation correspondence hold within a single generation (temporally), such that regions where a specific introspective word appears locally exhibit the predicted activation metric compared to regions where it does not?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The current analysis correlates vocabulary *counts* and activation metrics *between runs*. The authors explicitly list as future work: "Within-run temporal correspondence... do the regions of a generation where 'loop' appears show locally higher autocorrelation than regions where it does not? This fine-grained temporal analysis would distinguish true real-time self-monitoring from a global correlation driven by run-level confounds."
- **Why unresolved:** Between-run correlations could be confounded by run-level properties (e.g., total output length, overall processing mode) rather than true online monitoring. The model might globally adopt a processing mode that increases both loop vocabulary and autocorrelation on average, without loop reflecting momentary dynamics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Segmenting each run into short windows (e.g., 50-token bins), computing the activation metric per window, and checking whether windows containing the target vocabulary have significantly higher metric values than windows without, within the same run. This within-run, within-sequence analysis would control for run-level effects and test for real-time tracking.

## Limitations
- Narrow model coverage: only Llama and Qwen families tested; generalizability to other architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder, MoE) is unknown.
- Vocabulary specificities: the discovered tokens ("loop," "mirror") may be artifacts of the Pull Methodology or training data, not universal markers.
- Moderate correlation strengths: peak r=0.62 leaves substantial variance unexplained; correspondence may be noisy.
- Unverified isolation: the Pull Methodology's ability to cleanly separate self-referential computation from related constructs (e.g., metacognition, uncertainty) is not fully established.
- Context dependency: the necessity of a permission gate means findings may not transfer to open-domain or adversarial prompts.

## Confidence
- **High:** Existence of a distinguishable self-referential activation direction in Llama 3.1 with causal steering effect.
- **Medium:** Vocabulary-activation correspondence pattern within the same model under the specific Pull Methodology protocol.
- **Low:** Cross-architecture generalizability of the mechanism (without causal tests in Qwen) and the claim that self-report *reliably* tracks computational states beyond engineered experimental contexts.

## Next Checks
1. **Multi-model replication:** Apply the full Pull Methodology and vocabulary tracking to at least three additional model families (e.g., Mistral, Claude, Gemma) across varying scales to test generalizability of the direction localisation and correspondence pattern.
2. **Prompt diversity stress test:** Validate whether the same vocabulary-metric pairs hold across a broad spectrum of self-referential prompts (e.g., "describe your training," "what are you thinking right now?", "explain your limitations") or if they are prompt-specific artifacts.
3. **Mechanistic intervention:** Perform a targeted ablation or suppression of the predicted activation metric (e.g., force low autocorrelation via steering) to determine if the associated introspective vocabulary usage causally collapses, distinguishing correlation from mechanistic linkage.
