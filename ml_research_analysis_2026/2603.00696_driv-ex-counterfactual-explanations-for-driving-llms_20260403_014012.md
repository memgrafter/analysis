# DRIV-EX: Counterfactual Explanations for Driving LLMs
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00696
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00696
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: DRIV-EX outperforms baselines (61.3% success for Llama3) while maintaining high fluency (>98%) and similarity (>98% BERTScore), and exposes biases that reduce collisions by up to 16%.

## Executive Summary
LLM-based driving planners lack interpretability and may rely on unsafe biases, hindering auditability and safety improvement. DRIV-EX addresses this by generating counterfactual explanations—minimal input changes that flip the model’s driving decision—while ensuring the outputs remain linguistically valid and semantically similar to the original. On the LC-LLM dataset with textual highD data, DRIV-EX achieves a 61.3% aggregated success rate for Llama3, surpassing baselines like DAB and PEZ, and maintains >98% fluency and >98% BERTScore similarity. The method uncovers latent biases (e.g., over-reliance on lateral velocity) and demonstrates that removing such features reduces collisions by up to 16%, highlighting its utility for auditing and enhancing safety-critical models.

## Method Summary
DRIV-EX uses a two-stage pipeline. First, it optimizes continuous token embeddings of the input text via gradient-based methods to find minimal perturbations that cause the driving LLM to change its decision. The optimization employs a decision loss and a straight-through estimator to propagate gradients through the discrete token projection. Second, the optimized embeddings are fed to a separate fluency model (an autoregressive LM) whose decoding is biased by these embeddings, producing fluent and semantically proximate counterfactual text. The method assumes a fixed-length, template-based input structure (as in the highD dataset) and restricts edits to a predefined token set to preserve domain relevance. It requires white-box access to the target driving model for gradient computation.

## Key Results
- Higher aggregated success rates (61.3% for Llama3) than baselines DAB and PEZ on LC-LLM.
- Maintains high linguistic fluency (>98%) and semantic similarity (>98% BERTScore) across generated counterfactuals.
- Exposes latent biases (e.g., over-reliance on lateral velocity); removing such features reduces collisions by up to 16%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DRIV-EX works by separating the counterfactual search into a continuous optimization phase and a fluency-constrained generation phase. Gradient-based optimization in the embedding space efficiently locates minimal perturbations that maximize the decision loss, thereby increasing the chance of flipping the driving output. The straight-through estimator approximates gradients through the non-differentiable token projection, enabling end-to-end learning. The fluency model then decodes the optimized embeddings with a bias term that steers generation toward natural language, preventing degenerate outputs. This decoupling ensures that the counterfactuals satisfy both the decision-flip objective and linguistic quality, while the template and restricted token set keep edits within meaningful driving scenario boundaries.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Input Text → Driving LLM Embedding Layer → Continuous Embedding Optimizer → Optimized Embeddings → Fluency Model Decoder (biased) → Counterfactual Text.

**Critical Path**: The optimization loop computes the driving model’s decision loss, backpropagates through the embedding layer to update the continuous embeddings, and repeats until a decision flip is achieved or steps exhausted. The decoding loop then processes the optimized embeddings with the fluency model, applying a bias derived from the embeddings to influence token selection and produce the final counterfactual.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Success rate vs. fluency (controlled by bias strength), minimality vs. success (number of optimization steps), computational cost (dual loops), and flexibility vs. domain relevance (fixed template restricts edits to predefined slots). The white-box requirement also limits applicability to black-box models.

**Failure Signatures**: Low aggregated success rate (<50%), low fluency (high perplexity), high semantic drift (BERTScore <0.8), or counterfactuals that are invalid driving scenarios (e.g., impossible maneuvers). Additionally, failure to identify known biases in aggregation suggests poor feature sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can DRIV-EX be extended to handle vision-language models (VLMs) for generating counterfactual explanations from image inputs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "First, the method currently operates solely on textual inputs. Extending DRIV-EX to Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to directly handle image-based counterfactuals is a critical avenue for future research."
- Why unresolved: DRIV-EX relies on gradient-based optimization over discrete text tokens within a unimodal embedding space. VLMs process multimodal inputs where visual features are continuous and not directly tied to a discrete vocabulary, making the current projection and biasing mechanisms inapplicable.
- What evidence would resolve it: A scalable adaptation of DRIV-EX to a VLM (e.g., LLaVA) that optimizes over visual feature embeddings while using the language branch for fluent textual generation, validated on a multimodal driving dataset with image inputs and demonstrating decision flips with semantic plausibility.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can DRIV-EX be adapted for black-box LLMs without gradient access, and what alternative optimization strategies could replace the current gradient-based search?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Second, DRIV-EX can only be applied to white-box models."
- Why unresolved: The method's core uses gradients from the decision loss to update continuous embeddings via a straight-through estimator. Black-box models (e.g., API-only services) provide no gradients, breaking this loop and forcing reliance on less efficient gradient-free methods that may not ensure minimality or success rates.
- What evidence would resolve it: A black-box variant of DRIV-EX using zeroth-order optimization (e.g., evolutionary strategies or Bayesian optimization) that maintains comparable aggregated success rates and minimality on the same driving tasks, with documented trade-offs in computational cost.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can DRIV-EX generate counterfactuals that modify sequence length (inserting or deleting tokens) rather than only substituting existing ones?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Third, the optimization assumes a fixed sequence length; the method cannot currently generate counterfactuals that require inserting or deleting tokens relative to the original input."
- Why unresolved: The algorithm maintains a fixed-length embedding sequence, projecting each position to a token. Insertions or deletions would require altering sequence structure, which the current projection mechanism does not support, limiting counterfactuals to within-slot edits and missing scenarios where adding/removing elements (e.g., a new vehicle) is necessary.
- What evidence would resolve it: An extended DRIV-EX that incorporates dynamic sequence operations (e.g., via pointer networks or edit-step optimization) and evaluates on cases where meaningful counterfactuals require structural changes, showing improved decision flip rates on such instances.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Does DRIV-EX generalize to free-form natural language and diverse NLP tasks beyond the rigid, template-based driving descriptions of highD?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Fourth, our evaluation is limited to the textual highD dataset and a specific rigid template. While the framework is generic, broader validation across diverse NLP tasks and free-form text remains to be done."
- Why unresolved: The method's design—including template fitness metrics and restricted token sets for optimization—depends on the structured driving prompt. Free-form text lacks fixed slots, complicating the definition of minimal edits, fluency enforcement, and similarity evaluation, potentially breaking the decoupled search-generation balance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Application of DRIV-EX to standard counterfactual benchmarks (e.g., sentiment flipping, toxicity reduction) with unstructured inputs, using adapted metrics for minimality and fluency, and demonstrating performance on par with or better than task-specific baselines.

### Open Question 5
- Question: How can individual DRIV-EX counterfactuals be aggregated into global insights about model biases when the method is local and current aggregation relies on structured templates?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Finally, as a local interpretability method, aggregating individual counterfactuals into global insights is challenging. The aggregation strategies proposed in Sec. 4.3 rely on the structured nature of the textual highD template and may not generalize to unstructured free text."
- Why unresolved: The histogram-based token analysis in Sec. 4.3 assumes semantically aligned token positions across all inputs (e.g., 'vy' always at a fixed slot). Unstructured text lacks this alignment, making it difficult to identify consistent edit patterns across counterfactuals, thus hindering discovery of model-wide shortcuts or biases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of aggregation techniques for unstructured counterfactuals, such as semantic role labeling or embedding clustering to group edits by meaning, validated by uncovering known biases in free-form text models and correlating with performance drops on perturbed datasets.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is restricted to a single textual driving dataset (LC-LLM/highD), limiting generalizability to other scenarios or modalities.
- Claims about causal latent biases and collision reduction are correlational; the ablation study does not fully isolate causality.
- Fluency and similarity metrics rely on automated scores and a shared fluency model, risking metric overfitting and potential lack of human validation.

## Confidence
- **High**: DRIV-EX achieves superior aggregated success rates vs. DAB/PEZ on the reported test set.
- **Medium**: Generated counterfactuals maintain high semantic similarity and linguistic fluency per automated metrics.
- **Low**: Claims about exposing causal latent biases and that removing them directly causes up to 16% fewer collisions; requires external validation.
