# Diagnostic Benchmarks for Invariant Learning Dynamics: Empirical Validation of the Eidos Architecture
## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2602.13322
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13322
- References: 9
- Primary result: Eidos achieves >99% accuracy on PolyShapes-Ideal and 81.67% zero-shot font transfer without pre-training; learning exhibits phase transitions and differential stability.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates the problem of texture-based shortcuts in vision benchmarks, which confound statistical correlations with true topological invariance. To isolate geometric reasoning, the authors introduce the PolyShapes-Ideal (PSI) dataset and three complementary diagnostic probes: polygon classification under controlled noise, zero-shot font transfer from MNIST to unseen typefaces, and progressive affine deformation to map collapse thresholds. The core contribution is an empirical validation of the Eidos architecture—a discrete-commitment system employing ternary states—using these probes. Results demonstrate that Eidos attains >99% accuracy on PSI, achieves 81.67% zero-shot font transfer without any pre-training, and exhibits phase transitions when statistical shortcuts are explicitly invalidated. Moreover, the model shows differential stability: digits with closed loops (0,6,8,9) maintain robust classification under deformation while open-form digits (1,7) collapse rapidly. A 3D polyhedra extension reveals hyper-rapid convergence (97.64% by epoch 4), hinting at a native volumetric representation. These findings suggest that discrete-commitment mechanisms can effectively disentangle topology from texture, offering a path toward more invariant vision systems.

## Method Summary
The evaluation framework centers on three diagnostic probes designed to isolate topological invariance from texture-based correlations. The PolyShapes-Ideal (PSI) dataset generates synthetic polygon images (3–10 sides) in filled, outline, and ideal variants with additive noise; training on PSI v0.2 (filled+outline) tests the model’s ability to learn boundaries beyond mass cues. Zero-shot font transfer trains Eidos on MNIST until >99% training accuracy, then periodically evaluates on a curated suite of 30 unseen system fonts spanning handwriting, stylized, and monospace categories. The collapse mapping protocol applies incremental affine transformations (shear/rotation) to held-out Comic Sans digits, measuring accuracy degradation to locate the 50% collapse threshold per digit class. All experiments use the “pure Eidos” variant, which removes classical interpolation layers and relies on discrete-commitment modules with ternary activations. Key unknowns include exact hyperparameters and the precise font list, while common failure modes involve inadvertently using continuous-activation layers or mis-specifying dataset versions.

## Key Results
- PSI classification: >99% accuracy on ideal polygon variants after training on filled+outline (v0.2) data.
- Zero-shot font transfer: 81.67% aggregate accuracy across 30 fonts when transferring from MNIST without any target-domain training.
- Learning dynamics: Phase transition observed on PSI v0.1 (filled only) but immediate convergence on v0.2; differential stability shows closed-loop digits resist affine deformation longer; 3D polyhedra achieve 97.64% accuracy by epoch 4.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Eidos’s discrete-commitment mechanism, which employs ternary states (-1,0,1) and eschews continuous activation interpolation, forces the network to make categorical decisions at each layer. This design inhibits the formation of texture-based shortcuts by preventing the blending of conflicting features, thereby promoting representations that capture topological properties. The phase transition phenomenon—from reliance on superficial cues (e.g., mass in PSI v0.1) to robust geometric reasoning—emerges when such cues are systematically invalidated (e.g., by adding outline data). The differential stability under deformation reflects intrinsic topological complexity: closed-loop digits possess wider attractor basins in the discrete representation space, allowing tolerance to affine distortions. Hyper-rapid convergence on 3D polyhedra suggests that discrete-commitment may naturally encode volumetric relationships, reducing structural tension compared to 2D projections.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Discrete-commitment**: Ternary state quantization that forces categorical decisions.
   - *Why needed*: Avoids interpolation of features, encouraging invariant representations.
   - *Quick check*: Ensure activations are limited to {-1,0,1} and no smooth activation functions are present.
2. **Topological invariance**: Robustness to deformations and style changes, focusing on shape connectivity.
   - *Why needed*: Generalization across domains (e.g., fonts) and resistance to spurious correlations.
   - *Quick check*: Evaluate on transformed images (rotations, shears, different fonts); accuracy should degrade gracefully.
3. **Phase transition**: Abrupt improvement when statistical shortcuts are removed.
   - *Why needed*: Indicates the network can overcome biases and learn true structure.
   - *Quick check*: Compare training curves on PSI v0.1 (delayed jump) vs v0.2 (smooth).
4. **Attractor basins**: Regions of stable classification in representation space; wider for closed-loop digits.
   - *Why needed*: Explains robustness to deformations and differential stability.
   - *Quick check*: Incrementally deform digits and record accuracy drop; closed loops should persist longer.
5. **Volumetric representation hypothesis**: 3D tasks are solved faster due to native 3D understanding.
   - *Why needed*: Connects discrete-commitment to potential inherent 3D bias.
   - *Quick check*: Compare convergence speed on matched 2D/3D datasets; inspect internal representations for 3D structure.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input Image -> Feature Encoder -> Discrete Commitment Blocks -> Feature Aggregation -> Linear Classifier.

**Critical path**:  
1. Normalize and possibly augment input.  
2. Extract features through a backbone (likely CNN).  
3. Apply discretization (e.g., sign function with hysteresis) to produce ternary states.  
4. Pool or flatten discrete features.  
5. Pass to final fully connected layer for class probabilities.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Discreteness*: Improves invariance but may lose fine-grained information.  
- *Ternary states*: Balance between binary (too restrictive) and continuous (too permissive).  
- *No interpolation*: Reduces optimization smoothness but forces categorical abstraction.

**Failure signatures**:  
- High accuracy on PSI v0.1 but drop on v0.2 indicates reliance on mass shortcut.  
- Uniform collapse thresholds across digits suggest lack of topological encoding.  
- Poor zero-shot transfer (<60%) implies insufficient invariance.  
- Slow convergence on 3D (<50% after 10 epochs) contradicts volumetric hypothesis.

**First three experiments**:  
1. Train on PSI v0.2, evaluate on ideal variants; expect >99% accuracy.  
2. Train on MNIST, zero-shot evaluate on the 30-font suite; expect ~81.67%.  
3. Apply affine deformation to Comic Sans digits; verify closed-loop digits (0,8) outlast open-form (1,7) before collapse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Asymmetric zero-shot transfer**: Why does MNIST→fonts (81.67%) outperform fonts→MNIST (~64.89%)? Could reflect dataset bias or architectural limits in capturing invariant forms across domains.  
- **Hyper-rapid 3D convergence**: Why does Eidos achieve 97.64% on 3D polyhedra by epoch 4? The native volumetric representation hypothesis remains speculative without mechanistic evidence.  
- **Generalization to complex shapes**: Does Eidos’s topological invariance extend beyond simple convex polygons and digits to shapes with holes, fractals, or organic topologies? PSI’s limited shape set may not capture full geometric reasoning.  
- **Phase transition triggers**: What specific data attributes (e.g., boundary clarity, noise distribution) or architectural factors cause the shift from texture heuristics to topological reasoning? The paper observes but does not isolate causal conditions.

## Limitations
- **Missing training hyperparameters**: Learning rates, batch sizes, optimizer choices for PSI and MNIST experiments are unreported, hindering exact replication.  
- **Undisclosed evaluation specifics**: The exact list of 30 zero-shot fonts and the affine deformation schedule (step sizes, ranges) are not provided, affecting protocol consistency.  
- **Synthetic benchmark limitations**: PSI’s simple polygons and digit-only tests may not generalize to real-world geometric reasoning challenges with richer textures and shapes.
