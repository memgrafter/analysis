# Do 3D Large Language Models Really Understand 3D Spatial Relationships?

## Quick Facts

- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.23523
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23523
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** Real-3DQA filtering causes SOTA 3D-LLMs to drop >30% on EM, all models show near‑zero VRS (<0.5%), and 3DR‑FT yields ~10–15% absolute gains on Real-3DQA while increasing 3D token attention by ~20%.

## Executive Summary

Recent 3D-LLMs report near‑human accuracy on situated QA (SQA3D). This paper questions whether such performance reflects genuine 3D spatial reasoning or exploitation of textual shortcuts. The authors construct Real-3DQA by systematically removing questions answerable by blind (text‑only) models and by GPT‑4o, leaving a set that truly requires 3D understanding. They also introduce a Viewpoint Rotation Score (VRS) to test robustness to egocentric perspective changes. Furthermore, they propose 3D‑reweighted fine‑tuning (3DR‑FT), which upweights training samples where a blind model fails, forcing the model to rely on 3D context. Results show that on Real-3DQA, top models’ exact‑match scores collapse by more than half (e.g., GPT4Scene 60.6%→33.1%), VRS scores are essentially zero, and 3DR‑FT substantially recovers performance while increasing attention on 3D tokens. The work highlights the need for better benchmarks and training methods to achieve real 3D understanding.

## Method Summary

The authors first train “blind” versions of three 3D‑LLMs (LEO, Chat‑Scene, 3D‑LLM) by fine‑tuning them on SQA3D without any 3D point‑cloud input. Questions correctly answered by both the full model and its blind counterpart are deemed solvable via textual priors and are removed from the test set; the union across the three model pairs yields a large set of 3D‑independent items. An additional pass with GPT‑4o‑mini filters out any remaining guessable questions, producing the final Real‑3DQA benchmark. To evaluate viewpoint robustness, they generate three rotated variants (±90°, 180°) of each remaining question by transforming the situation description and adjusting directional terms; the Viewpoint Rotation Score measures the fraction of these four perspective‑consistent correct answers. For training, 3D‑reweighted fine‑tuning (3DR‑FT) dynamically upweights tokens where the blind model’s surprise (negative log‑likelihood) is higher than the current model’s, emphasizing samples that truly depend on 3D context. The loss becomes a weighted cross‑entropy with per‑token reweighting factor w_j = S_ϕ(y_j)/S_θ(y_j). This simple modification yields higher Real‑3DQA performance and increased attention on 3D tokens.

## Key Results

- **Big performance drop on Real-3DQA:** SOTA models lose >30% absolute exact‑match compared to SQA3D (e.g., LEO 49.4%→14.3%, Chat‑Scene 54.4%→17.0%).
- **Near‑zero rotation robustness:** All models achieve VRS < 0.5% on the four‑viewpoint consistency test.
- **3DR‑FT improves 3D awareness:** Gains of 10–16% absolute on Real‑3DQA (Chat‑Scene 22.1→33.9, LEO 13.6→29.3) and ~20% absolute increase in 3D token attention.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Filtering 3D‑independent questions**  
- **Claim:** Removing questions answerable by blind models creates a benchmark that genuinely requires 3D spatial reasoning.  
- **Mechanism:** For each 3D‑LLM, train a blind variant using only textual prompts. If both models answer a test question correctly, the question is labeled “3D‑independent” and discarded. Taking the union across models eliminates a large set of shortcut‑driven items; a final filter with GPT‑4o catches remaining guessable questions.  
- **Core assumption:** When 3D input does not change the answer distribution, the question does not need genuine 3D reasoning; the 3D model’s success on such items likely stems from textual cues.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Blind models achieve ~50% EM on SQA3D (Fig. 2), and Real‑3DQA causes >30% drops (Table 2).  
- **Break condition:** If blind models’ correctness does not correlate with human‑identified shortcut questions, the filtering might remove truly hard items or retain shortcut‑dependent ones.

**Mechanism 2: Viewpoint Rotation Score (VRS)**  
- **Claim:** VRS exposes lack of rotation‑invariant spatial understanding by measuring answer consistency across four rotated egocentric perspectives.  
- **Mechanism:** For each seed question, three rotated variants (±90°, 180°) are generated by applying the opposite transformation to the observer’s viewpoint and adjusting directional terms in both the situation description and the answer. The model must answer all four variants correctly to score highly on VRS; any inconsistency yields a lower score, forcing reasoning about absolute spatial layouts rather than textual pattern matching.  
- **Core assumption:** True 3D understanding produces stable answers under viewpoint changes; inconsistency indicates reliance on viewpoint‑specific cues or failure to internalize the scene’s geometry.  
- **Evidence anchors:** VRS scores are near zero (<0.5%) for all tested models (Table 3), confirming poor rotation robustness.  
- **Break condition:** Models could learn to cheat by memorizing rotation augmentation patterns without spatial grounding, rendering VRS unreliable.

**Mechanism 3: 3D‑reweighted fine‑tuning (3DR‑FT)**  
- **Claim:** Upweighting samples where a blind model performs poorly relative to a 3D model forces the full model to rely on 3D context, improving genuine spatial reasoning.  
- **Mechanism:** During training, for each token y_j the loss weight is set to the ratio of the blind model’s surprise (negative log‑probability) to the current model’s surprise: w_j = S_ϕ(y_j) / S_θ(y_j). Tokens that surprise the blind model more (i.e., harder to guess from text alone) receive higher weight, increasing the penalty for ignoring 3D information. The weighted cross‑entropy loss is back‑propagated to update only the 3D model parameters.  
- **Core assumption:** The blind model’s difficulty on a token correlates with that token’s dependence on 3D context; emphasizing such tokens pushes the 3D model to use the point‑cloud evidence.  
- **Evidence anchors:** 3DR‑FT improves Real‑3DQA EM by 10–16% and raises average 3D token attention by ~20% absolute (Fig. 5).  
- **Break condition:** If the surprise ratio does not reliably reflect 3D‑dependency, or if it encourages overfitting to 3D artifacts, gains may be superficial.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept 1: Blind Fine‑Tuning as Diagnostic**  
- **Why needed:** It reveals the upper bound of performance achievable without any 3D input, exposing textual shortcut vulnerability in the benchmark and model.  
- **Quick check:** What percentage of SQA3D can a blind model trained on text‑only QA pairs answer correctly? (Answer: ~50% for top models—paper Fig. 2.)

**Concept 2: Conditional Independence in Multimodal Models**  
- **Why needed:** The paper assumes a token’s probability should change when 3D context is added if it truly depends on 3D. This underpins the 3D‑dependency definition and 3DR‑FT.  
- **Quick check:** If p(y|text) = p(y|text,3D) for a token, what does that imply about that token’s need for 3D information? (Answer: conditional independence; the token is guessable from text alone.)

**Concept 3: Surprise‑Based Reweighting**  
- **Why needed:** It operationalizes “how much a sample needs 3D” via the blind model’s uncertainty, guiding the loss to focus on hard, 3D‑dependent cases.  
- **Quick check:** Why use the ratio of surprise (log probs) rather than absolute surprise difference? (Answer: Ratio normalizes across tokens of varying base probabilities, focusing on relative difficulty.)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input (textual situation + question + 3D point cloud/object identifiers) → Point cloud encoder (point‑wise or object‑centric) → 3D tokens → Concatenation with text tokens → LLM (autoregressive decoder) → Answer tokens. Auxiliary: Blind model copy used for reweighting during 3DR‑FT.

**Critical path:** For 3DR‑FT: (1) Forward pass with 3D+text to get model distribution p_θ; (2) Run blind model on the same text to get distribution p_ϕ; (3) Compute per‑token weight w_j = S_ϕ(y_j)/S_θ(y_j); (4) Compute weighted cross‑entropy loss ℒ = - Σ w_j log p_θ(y_j); (5) Back‑propagate to update only θ.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Point‑wise encoding preserves local geometry but may lack object‑level semantics; object‑centric groups features but can lose fine details.  
- 3DR‑FT adds negligible computational overhead but requires a pre‑trained blind model and careful tuning of reweighting factors.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High SQA3D EM but near‑zero VRS.  
- >30% performance drop from SQA3D to Real‑3DQA.  
- Low 3D token attention scores pre‑3DR‑FT.  
- SQA3D performance degrades after 3DR‑FT (indicating over‑correction).  
- Unusually low reweighting factors (w_j ≈ 0) due to overfitted blind model.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline blind model reproduction:** Fine‑tune each target 3D‑LLM architecture on SQA3D text‑only (remove 3D tokens). Verify that it reaches ~45‑60% EM, confirming the shortcut problem.  
2. **Real‑3DQA construction:** Using the filtered pipeline (union of correctly answered pairs + GPT‑4o‑mini filtering), produce the final test set. Report the size of the filtered set and compare against paper Table 7.  
3. **3DR‑FT application:** Apply reweighted loss to LEO or Chat‑Scene, train on SQA3D train split, and evaluate on Real‑3DQA test (EM Refined) and VRS (mean P_k). Verify improvements and increased 3D token attention.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can arbitrary viewpoint rotations (beyond fixed 90° increments) be systematically incorporated into 3D QA benchmarks without prohibitive annotation costs?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors note in Section 7 that considering more rotation angles (e.g., 12 o’clock direction) is valuable but requires "more complex rules" and "re‑annotation," increasing manual effort.
- **Why unresolved:** Manual annotation for continuous rotations is expensive and impractical at scale; automatic methods may introduce errors or fail to preserve spatial semantics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A scalable, reliable method (e.g., programmatic or LLM‑based) to generate rotation‑consistent QA pairs for arbitrary angles, validated by human evaluation and correlation with model performance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can egocentric spatial reasoning evaluation be extended to 3D grounding and captioning tasks, which currently use allocentric (scene‑centric) benchmarks?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state in Section 7 that existing grounding/captioning datasets lack situated information and require "redefining these tasks with egocentric situation descriptions" under viewpoint changes.
- **Why unresolved:** Current 3D grounding/captioning benchmarks do not incorporate observer‑centric context or test perspective consistency, leaving a gap in evaluating embodied spatial understanding.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** New benchmark suites for 3D grounding/captioning that include egocentric descriptions, viewpoint rotation tests, and curated questions requiring genuine 3D reasoning—similar to Real‑3DQA’s pipeline but adapted for these tasks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What architectural modifications can endow 3D‑LLMs with inherent robustness to viewpoint rotation, as measured by the Viewpoint Rotation Score (VRS)?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper shows all tested models (point‑wise, object‑centric, hybrid) exhibit near‑zero VRS (<0.5% for four rotations), indicating a systemic lack of rotation‑invariant spatial representations (Section 5.2).
- **Why unresolved:** Existing architectures are not explicitly designed or trained for rotation invariance; no prior work has targeted VRS as an optimization objective.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies with modified architectures (e.g., equivariant neural networks, rotation‑augmented training) showing significant VRS improvement without sacrificing overall QA accuracy, coupled with analysis of learned representations.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Is the performance gap between a blind (text‑only) model and a full 3D‑LLM a reliable proxy for a question’s 3D‑dependency, as assumed by the 3D‑reweighted fine‑tuning (3DR‑FT) strategy?
- **Basis in paper:** 3DR‑FT weights samples based on the blind model’s "surprise" relative to the 3D model (Eq. 1). The paper validates 3DR‑FT on Real‑3DQA but does not analyze whether the gap reliably correlates with true 3D reasoning difficulty across all question types.
- **Why unresolved:** Blind models may exploit their own (non‑3D) biases; a large gap could reflect linguistic complexity rather than spatial reasoning needs. The paper’s ablation on LEO (Table 6) shows some questions remain answerable without 3D input, but the weighting scheme’s general validity is untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A fine‑grained analysis of upweighted vs. downweighted samples, using human annotations or controlled experiments to correlate the gap with independent measures of 3D‑dependency (e.g., necessity of geometric computation).

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Does Real‑3DQA fully eliminate linguistic shortcuts, or do residual biases (e.g., corpus‑level priors, object salience) persist in the filtered question set?
- **Basis in paper:** Real‑3DQA removes questions correctly answered by any blind model and GPT‑4o (Section 3.1). However, blind models themselves may learn spurious correlations from text‑only data; filtering based on their correctness might not guarantee freedom from all shortcuts.
- **Why unresolved:** The filtering criterion is model‑dependent and may not capture unseen biases; GPT‑4o’s capabilities could also evolve. The paper does not conduct adversarial testing or human evaluation on the final set to confirm absence of guessable patterns.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Human studies measuring human performance on Real‑3DQA with and without 3D scene access; or adversarial perturbations to questions that maintain surface text but alter 3D context, checking if model performance drops as expected.

## Limitations

- The Real‑3DQA construction relies on blind models and GPT‑4o‑mini, both of which may have their own biases; residual shortcuts could remain.
- Hyperparameters for 3DR‑FT (e.g., reweighting schedule, learning rate) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- Rotation augmentation quality is unverified; invalid rotated variants could invalidate VRS scores.
- The link between increased 3D token attention and actual spatial reasoning remains correlational.
