# DSC2025 -- ViHallu Challenge: Detecting Hallucination in Vietnamese LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04711
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04711
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** Introduced ViHallu, a 10k-sample Vietnamese hallucination detection benchmark; top system achieved macro-F1 84.80% on private test set using instruction-tuned LLMs with structured prompting and ensembles.

## Executive Summary
The ViHallu Challenge addresses the lack of hallucination evaluation benchmarks for low-resource languages by releasing the UIT-ViHallu dataset, which contains 10,000 human-annotated triplets of (context, prompt, response) spanning three types of hallucination: no, intrinsic (contradiction), and extrinsic (unsupported information). The challenge attracted 111 teams, and the best submission reached a macro-F1 score of 84.80% on the private test set, far surpassing an encoder-only baseline (32.83%). Results show that instruction-tuned large language models, when guided by carefully structured prompts and ensemble strategies, are highly effective at this task, although detecting intrinsic contradictions remains particularly difficult.

## Method Summary
The task is a three-way classification: given a context passage, a user prompt, and an LLM response, predict whether the response contains no hallucination, an intrinsic hallucination (contradicting the context), or an extrinsic hallucination (adding unsupported information). The UIT-ViHallu dataset provides training, public, and private test splits. Top-performing systems employ instruction-tuned models (e.g., Qwen3-4B-Instruct) that are either directly prompted or fine-tuned with LoRA adapters. A structured prompt template is crucial: it describes the task, defines the three labels, supplies few-shot examples, and then presents the input triplet. Many teams also use ensembles—multiple models or multiple sampling temperatures—to aggregate predictions and improve robustness. Performance is measured by macro-F1.

## Key Results
- The ViHallu benchmark comprises 10,000 annotated (context, prompt, response) triplets with prompt types including factual, noisy, and adversarial.
- 111 teams participated; the winning system achieved a macro-F1 of 84.80% on the private test set, while a PhoBERT encoder-only baseline only reached 32.83%.
- Intrinsic (contradiction-based) hallucinations are identified as the most challenging sub-problem, with lower detection accuracy compared to extrinsic and no-hallucination cases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Instruction-tuned LLMs with structured prompting significantly outperform encoder-only classifiers for Vietnamese hallucination detection.
- **Mechanism:** The model receives a prompt that explicitly defines the task, describes the three hallucination labels (no, intrinsic, extrinsic), provides illustrative examples, and then presents the (context, prompt, response) triplet. This structure leverages the model’s instruction-following capabilities to reason about faithfulness relative to the provided context.
- **Core assumption:** The instruction-tuned model has sufficient competence in Vietnamese and can follow structured guidance to perform nuanced textual entailment and contradiction detection.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "instruction-tuned LLMs with structured prompting and ensemble strategies are highly effective"
  - [section 5.2.1] "structured prompt that first describes the task and the three hallucination labels, then provides several illustrative examples, and finally presents the instance to classify."
  - [corpus] Corpus signals show related benchmarks (e.g., HuDEx, MedHallu) also focus on structured evaluation, but do not directly validate this prompting technique for Vietnamese.
- **Break condition:** Model may fail if prompt instructions are ambiguous, if the model’s Vietnamese instruction-following is weak, or if examples are unrepresentative of test distribution.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Ensemble strategies that aggregate predictions from multiple models and multiple sampling temperatures improve robustness and performance, especially on adversarial and noisy prompts.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple instruction-tuned LLMs (possibly different families) are run with varying temperature settings to generate diverse predictions. A voting or weighted combination scheme then yields the final label, reducing variance and mitigating individual model biases.
- **Core assumption:** Errors made by individual models are not perfectly correlated; diversity in model architecture and sampling introduces independent signals that can be combined to reduce overall error.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "ensemble strategies are highly effective"
  - [section 5.2.3] "each model is run with ten different temperature values... aggregated by majority vote"
  - [section 5.2.2] "35 LoRA adapters... weighted combination"
- **Break condition:** If all models share similar training data or architecture, diversity may be low, leading to correlated errors and no ensemble gain. Computational cost increases linearly.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Decomposing the three-way classification into binary sub-tasks (e.g., "is there any hallucination?" and "is there a contradiction?") with rule-based fusion can achieve competitive performance.
- **Mechanism:** Train specialized binary classifiers for simpler decisions, then combine their outputs via deterministic rules to produce the final three-way label. This simplifies learning and allows focused optimization on each sub-problem.
- **Core assumption:** The binary subtasks are easier to learn than the joint three-way task, and the combination rules can correctly handle most edge cases.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.2.7] "trained three binary classifiers... A rule-based module combines these binary outputs"
  - [section 5.2.7] "reaches 84.01% macro-F1"
- **Break condition:** Rules may fail on ambiguous or overlapping cases, and error propagation from early binary mistakes cannot be corrected by later stages.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Hallucination taxonomy (intrinsic vs extrinsic)
  - **Why needed here:** The task definition hinges on distinguishing between responses that contradict the context (intrinsic) and those that add unsupported information (extrinsic). Misunderstanding this distinction leads to incorrect labeling and model evaluation.
  - **Quick check:** "If a model response states 'The capital of France is Paris' but the context does not mention Paris, is this intrinsic or extrinsic? Why?"

- **Concept:** Instruction tuning and structured prompting
  - **Why needed here:** The top systems rely on instruction-following models that can interpret detailed prompts. Understanding how to format the task description, examples, and input is critical for replicating the success.
  - **Quick check:** "How does providing few-shot examples within the prompt help the model understand the classification criteria for hallucinations?"

- **Concept:** Ensemble methods and aggregation
  - **Why needed here:** Most top submissions use ensembles—either multiple models or multiple temperatures. Grasping why and how to combine predictions is essential for building high-performing systems.
  - **Quick check:** "Why might running the same model at different temperatures and aggregating the results improve stability on adversarial prompts?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (context, prompt, response) → Prompt engineering module (formats into structured instruction) → Model inference (instruction-tuned LLM(s)) → Aggregation module → Output label.

**Critical path:** For a single-model system: (1) construct structured prompt → (2) run model inference → (3) parse output to label. For ensemble: parallelize (2) across models/temperatures → (4) aggregate predictions.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Single model vs ensemble: Ensemble yields higher accuracy but increases latency and resource usage.
- Model size: Larger models (7B vs 4B) may capture nuances better but require more memory; smaller models with LoRA are efficient.
- Prompt length: Detailed prompts improve clarity but consume context window and may slow inference.
- Temperature: Higher temperature increases diversity but may produce inconsistent outputs; low temperature is deterministic but may miss edge cases.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low macro-F1 on intrinsic hallucinations, suggesting the model struggles with contradiction detection.
- High variance in predictions when using different temperatures, indicating instability.
- Disagreement among ensemble members without clear majority, leading to ambiguous final label.
- Prompt parsing errors (e.g., model output not matching expected label format).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline reproduction: Fine-tune PhoBERT as a three-way classifier on the training set to verify the reported 32.83% macro-F1 on private test. This establishes a lower bound and sanity check.
2. Prompt ablation: Systematically vary the structured prompt—remove examples, change order of label definitions, or simplify wording—to measure impact on a validation split. Identifies minimal effective prompt structure.
3. Ensemble diversity analysis: Train multiple LoRA adapters on the same base model with different preprocessing (e.g., spelling correction) and test combinations. Compare performance of uniform vs. weighted voting to assess diversity contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Contrastive learning for intrinsic hallucinations:** What contrastive learning architectures are most effective for improving detection of intrinsic hallucinations in Vietnamese LLMs? The paper notes this as future work due to the challenge of subtle semantic contradictions.
- **Retrieval-augmented verification for extrinsic detection:** How can retrieval-augmented verification be integrated into hallucination detectors to effectively reduce extrinsic hallucinations in Vietnamese RAG systems? Current systems rely solely on fine-tuned LLMs without retrieval.
- **Span-level annotations:** Would span-level annotations of hallucinations yield more explainable and accurate detection systems compared to sentence-level labels? Current sentence-level labels obscure the exact location of hallucinations.

## Limitations
- Exact structured prompt template and LoRA hyperparameters are not disclosed, blocking faithful replication of the top system.
- The annotation protocol and inter-annotator agreement for the UIT-ViHallu dataset are not described, raising potential label reliability concerns.
- Intrinsic hallucinations (contradictions) are reported as the most challenging sub-problem, indicating a fundamental limitation in current models' ability to detect subtle inconsistencies.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (structured prompting):** High confidence. The performance leap (84.80% vs 32.83% macro-F1) robustly supports the claim, despite missing exact prompt wording.
- **Mechanism 2 (ensemble strategies):** Medium confidence. The paper reports ensemble benefits, but the top system used a single model; without ablation, the magnitude of ensemble gains is uncertain.
- **Mechanism 3 (binary decomposition):** High confidence. The reported 84.01% macro-F1 demonstrates that this simpler approach is nearly as effective as the top system.

## Next Checks
1. Acquire the winning team's exact prompt template and LoRA configuration (rank, alpha, dropout, preprocessing) from the challenge organizers to attempt faithful reproduction.
2. Conduct a systematic prompt ablation on the public test set: remove few-shot examples, reorder label definitions, and simplify task description; measure macro-F1 drop to identify critical components.
3. For ensemble-based submissions, quantify member diversity via prediction disagreement and compare uniform majority vote against learned weighted combination for statistical significance.
