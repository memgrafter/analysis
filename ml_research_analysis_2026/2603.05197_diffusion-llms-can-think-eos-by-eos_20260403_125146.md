# Diffusion LLMs can think EoS-by-EoS
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05197
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05197
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** Diffusion LLMs use end-of-sequence (EoS) tokens as a latent scratchpad for intermediate reasoning, improving accuracy on complex tasks when generation length is increased far beyond the answer length, and this effect is causal and more token-efficient than explicit chain-of-thought.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether diffusion large language models (LLMs) leverage end-of-sequence (EoS) tokens as a hidden computational space—termed "thinking EoS-by-EoS"—to perform multi-step reasoning. Through behavioral experiments and causal interventions across three diffusion models and three reasoning tasks (Addition, Entity Tracking, Sudoku), the authors demonstrate that model accuracy systematically improves with increased generation length, which adds more EoS tokens. Controlled experiments isolating the number of EoS tokens confirm they boost performance independently. Causal intervention by patching EoS token hidden states frequently flips outputs, directly showing these representations encode task-relevant information. Compared to chain-of-thought prompting, a small number of EoS tokens achieve similar performance with far greater token efficiency. However, models with block-causal attention show limited benefits, highlighting an architectural dependency.

## Method Summary
The study employs three experimental protocols. First, a prompting experiment varies the generation length during inference on three reasoning datasets, measuring accuracy and analyzing output composition (semantic vs. EoS tokens). Second, a controlled prompting experiment fixes the number of masks (predictions) to the expected answer length while manually appending varying quantities of EoS tokens (1 to 128), then measures accuracy changes. This isolates the effect of EoS tokens from the number of decoding steps. Third, a causal intervention experiment creates counterfactual generation pairs (e.g., different arithmetic operators) and patches the hidden states of the last 16 EoS tokens from the counterfactual run into the original generation at a middle layer (e.g., layer 15), observing whether the output switches to the counterfactual answer. These are compared against autoregressive baselines (Llama3.1, Qwen3) and chain-of-thought prompting.

## Key Results
- Accuracy on complex reasoning tasks improves systematically as generation length increases, with diffusion models surpassing autoregressive baselines at high lengths.
- In the controlled experiment, adding EoS tokens while fixing the number of masks significantly boosts accuracy for most models and tasks, indicating EoS tokens serve as latent reasoning space.
- Causal intervention via activation patching of EoS token hidden states frequently causes the model's output to flip to the counterfactual answer, providing direct evidence that EoS representations encode task-relevant information.
- A small number (e.g., four) of EoS tokens can yield performance close to lengthy verbal chain-of-thought, achieving greater token efficiency.
- The model with block-causal attention (LLaDA2.0-mini) shows degraded generation and limited accuracy gains from added EoS tokens, suggesting architectural constraints are critical.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Mechanism 1:** Diffusion models use EoS tokens as a latent scratchpad for intermediate reasoning computations. During generation, padded EoS tokens' representations are leveraged to encode step-by-step reasoning, akin to chain-of-thought but covertly, allowing the model to handle complex, interdependent sub-goals. Core assumption: EoS tokens, though semantically minimal, can be repurposed by the model's bidirectional attention to store task-relevant intermediate states due to training with padded sequences. Evidence: Controlled prompting shows accuracy improves with added EoS tokens while decoding steps are fixed; corpus evidence is indirect.
- **Mechanism 2:** Full bidirectional attention in diffusion models enables tokens to attend to far-away positions, including trailing EoS tokens, facilitating their integration into reasoning. Unlike autoregressive left-to-right attention, bidirectional attention allows any token to reference EoS tokens throughout the sequence, using their representations as part of the context for computation. Core assumption: Attention patterns are not order-constrained, permitting global context inclusion. Evidence: Models with full bidirectional attention (LLaDA1.5, Dream-v0) benefit from EoS tokens, while the block-causal variant (LLaDA2.0-mini) does not.
- **Mechanism 3:** Generation length acts as a hyperparameter for reasoning budget; more EoS tokens expand computational capacity, improving performance on harder tasks until saturation. Increasing generation length pads more EoS tokens, providing additional representational dimensions for multi-step reasoning. Core assumption: The volume of EoS tokens correlates with the complexity of latent computations. Evidence: Accuracies rise with generation length across tasks while semantic token count stays constant or declines; diffusion models surpass autoregressive baselines at high lengths.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input embeddings → bidirectional transformer encoder (with full or block-causal attention) → iterative decoding loop (mask prediction, confidence-based token fixing) → output with EoS padding.
- **Critical path:** Start with masked tokens and padded EoS tokens → forward pass to predict all masked positions → fix high-confidence tokens (including possible EoS) → repeat until all masks filled, with EoS tokens potentially influencing earlier predictions via attention.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Higher generation length improves reasoning but increases compute; full bidirectional attention enables EoS use but may be less efficient than block-causal for long sequences.
- **Failure signatures:** Model outputs broken or minimal semantic content when too many EoS tokens are appended (e.g., LLaDA2.0-mini at 128 EoS tokens); no accuracy gain from EoS tokens under block-causal attention.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Prompting Experiment: Vary generation length while holding decoding steps proportional; measure accuracy and output composition to correlate performance with EoS token count.
  2. Controlled Prompting Experiment: Fix initial mask count and decoding steps, manually append varying EoS tokens; assess accuracy changes to isolate EoS effect.
  3. Intervention Experiment: Perform activation patching on EoS token hidden states from counterfactual generations; evaluate output flips to confirm causal role of EoS representations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What are the underlying reasons that diffusion LLMs outperform autoregressive models on complex reasoning tasks? Basis: The introduction states this as an open question; the paper provides evidence that EoS-by-EoS reasoning contributes but does not fully explain the advantage. Why unresolved: Limited to three models and tasks; other factors (e.g., bidirectional attention, iterative decoding) may also play a role. Resolution requires large-scale comparative evaluations and controlled ablations.
- **Open Question 2:** How does block‑causal attention in diffusion models like LLaDA2.0‑mini affect the utilization of trailing EoS tokens as a latent scratchpad? Basis: LLaDA2.0‑mini shows limited benefit from added EoS tokens, and its generation degrades with many EoS tokens; the authors hypothesize block‑causal attention restricts access. Why unresolved: The difference may also stem from other training specifics; no experiment directly manipulates attention pattern while keeping other factors constant. Resolution requires training models with explicitly controlled attention masks under identical conditions.
- **Open Question 3:** What specific computational role do the hidden states of EoS tokens play during the iterative decoding process? Basis: Causal interventions show EoS representations encode task‑relevant information, but the paper does not characterize what this information represents or how it evolves. Why unresolved: No fine‑grained analysis of layer‑wise or timestep‑wise content. Resolution requires probing EoS states across layers and decoding steps to predict intermediate reasoning variables and visualize attention flow.

## Limitations
- Incomplete disclosure of model checkpoints, training data, and decoding hyperparameters impedes exact replication.
- Evidence is restricted to three medium-sized diffusion models on narrow tasks; generalizability to larger models or complex reasoning is uncertain.
- Autoregressive baselines may be suboptimally prompted or limited by context length, potentially understating their capabilities.

## Confidence
- **EoS as latent scratchpad:** Medium – Behavioral trends and causal patching support the claim, but reproducibility gaps and narrow model coverage reduce certainty.
- **Architecture dependence:** Medium – Contrast between full-bidirectional and block-causal models is suggestive but requires broader architectural sampling.
- **Token efficiency vs. CoT:** Medium – Performance equivalence hinges on specific prompt lengths and tasks; longer CoT might narrow the gap.

## Next Checks
1. **Full independent replication** using released code/checkpoints to confirm all experiments, especially accuracy curves and intervention outcomes.
2. **Ablation on EoS representations**: systematically vary patched token count (1–all) and layer depth (early/mid/late) to map reasoning information distribution.
3. **Cross-architecture extension**: test models with hybrid attention (e.g., sliding window) to refine the boundary of architectures that can leverage EoS tokens.
