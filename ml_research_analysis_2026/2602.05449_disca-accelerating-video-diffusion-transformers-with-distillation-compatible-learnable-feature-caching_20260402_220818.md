# DisCa: Accelerating Video Diffusion Transformers with Distillation-Compatible Learnable Feature Caching

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05449
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05449
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: DisCa achieves 11.8× acceleration on HunyuanVideo with near-lossless quality (only 5.7% semantic score and 0.5% quality score drop compared to the original 50-step model).

## Executive Summary
DisCa addresses the quality drop that occurs when applying feature caching to video diffusion transformers that have been distilled to fewer sampling steps. Traditional caching relies on handcrafted heuristics (e.g., Taylor series) that assume smooth feature evolution, but sparse, distorted trajectories from step-distillation break these assumptions. To overcome this, DisCa proposes a learnable feature caching mechanism: a small neural predictor trained to accurately forecast high-dimensional feature changes across skipped steps, combined with a Restricted MeanFlow distillation that avoids overly aggressive compression targets, thereby stabilizing training on large video models.

Evaluated on the HunyuanVideo model, DisCa yields an 11.8× acceleration (50 steps to 10 steps) with minimal quality loss: only a 5.7% drop in semantic score and 0.5% in quality score compared to the original 50-step model. It outperforms existing caching and distillation approaches, particularly at high acceleration ratios. Moreover, by caching only the final DiT layer, DisCa incurs a VRAM overhead of just ~0.4 GB and remains efficient in distributed parallel generation settings.

## Method Summary
DisCa combines two innovations: Restricted MeanFlow distillation and a learnable feature caching predictor. Restricted MeanFlow modifies standard MeanFlow by restricting the interval I = (t−r) to a maximum R<1 during training, preventing the numerical instability that occurs with extremely aggressive one-step distillation targets on large video models. For inference, a lightweight predictor (two DiT blocks, <4% of the base model) is trained to map cached final-layer features and current timestep/rotation information to the model output. The predictor uses both MSE and multi‑scale adversarial losses to preserve high‑frequency details. Instead of caching every layer, only the final DiT block output is stored, drastically reducing memory usage while the predictor compensates for the missing intermediate information. This design enables high‑resolution, long‑video generation with minimal VRAM overhead and high acceleration.

## Key Results
- 11.8× acceleration on HunyuanVideo (50→10 steps) with only 5.7% semantic score and 0.5% quality score loss.
- VRAM overhead as low as ~0.4 GB, far below multi‑layer caching methods (>30 GB).
- Outperforms prior feature caching and distillation methods, especially at high acceleration ratios, and works well with distributed parallel generation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Learnable Predictor Handles Sparse Trajectories
- **Claim:** A lightweight learnable neural predictor can accurately model high‑dimensional feature evolution in step‑distilled video diffusion models where traditional handcrafted heuristics fail.
- **Mechanism:** The predictor \(P\) takes cached features \(C = (x_t, r, t)\) from a full computation step and current timestep \((t', r', t')\) to predict the model output. It is trained via MSE loss against ground‑truth outputs and refined with adversarial loss from a multi‑scale discriminator to preserve high‑frequency details. The predictor uses only 2 DiT blocks (<4% of base model size), enabling fast inference.
- **Core assumption:** Cached final‑layer features contain sufficient temporal trajectory information, and a small network can learn the complex non‑linear mapping between sparse sampling steps.
- **Break condition:** If the sparse sampling interval exceeds the predictor’s capacity or cached features lose critical information due to extreme compression.

### Mechanism 2: Restricted MeanFlow Stabilizes Distillation
- **Claim:** Pruning overly aggressive compression ratios during MeanFlow distillation stabilizes training for large‑scale video models and prevents catastrophic quality degradation.
- **Mechanism:** Original MeanFlow aims for one‑step distillation by sampling \(I = (t - r) \in [0,1]\). Restricted MeanFlow restricts \(I \in [0,R]\) with \(R < 1\) (typically 0.2–0.4), avoiding the most compression‑heavy training scenarios where numerical errors (JVP operations) compound and destabilize learning.
- **Core assumption:** Training instability correlates directly with the magnitude of \(I\); smaller intervals yield more stable, learnable mean velocity targets.
- **Break condition:** If \(R\) is too small, acceleration potential is limited; if too large, instability returns. Optimal \(R\) likely depends on model scale and video complexity.

### Mechanism 3: Single‑Final‑Layer Caching for Memory Efficiency
- **Claim:** Single‑final‑layer feature caching with a learnable predictor reduces VRAM overhead dramatically while maintaining quality, enabling practical high‑resolution long‑video generation in distributed parallel settings.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of maintaining per‑layer cache tensors (as in TaylorSeer, FORA), DisCa caches only the final DiT block output. The predictor, trained to handle temporal evolution, compensates for the loss of multi‑layer information. This reduces extra VRAM to ~0.4 GB vs >30 GB in multi‑layer methods.
- **Core assumption:** Higher‑level semantic features are sufficient for predicting evolution; lower‑level details can be reconstructed by the predictor or are less critical.
- **Break condition:** Would fail if lower‑layer feature changes are essential for fidelity (e.g., fine textures) and the predictor cannot infer them from high‑level cache alone.

## Foundational Learning

#### Feature Caching in Diffusion Transformers
- **Why needed:** DisCa redefines caching from handcrafted reuse/interpolation/Taylor‑series to learnable prediction. Understanding prior caching limits (error accumulation, handcrafted assumptions) is necessary to appreciate the shift.
- **Quick check:** Why do Taylor series‑based methods (TaylorSeer) degrade under high compression from step‑distillation?  
  **Answer:** They assume smooth local feature evolution via derivatives, but sparse distilled steps create large, non‑linear jumps where Taylor approximations fail.

#### MeanFlow Distillation
- **Why needed:** Restricted MeanFlow is a core component; understanding its original one‑step aggressive target explains why it fails on large video models and why restriction helps.
- **Quick check:** What causes MeanFlow to diverge on large video models but work on smaller image models?  
  **Answer:** JVP numerical errors compound with model complexity and sequence length; aggressive one‑step target provides insufficient gradient signal for high‑dimensional video features.

#### Adversarial Training for Perceptual Quality
- **Why needed:** DisCa uses GAN training (beyond MSE) to recover high‑frequency details lost in caching. This is crucial for near‑lossless quality.
- **Quick check:** Why might MSE loss produce blurry predictions even if L2 error is low?  
  **Answer:** MSE favors average/mean outputs and penalizes high‑frequency deviations, leading to over‑smoothed results that lack sharp details.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Base DiT (frozen backbone) generates ground‑truth outputs and provides feature extractor \(F\) for the discriminator. Cache buffer stores a single final‑layer feature tensor from the last full computation step. Predictor (2 DiT blocks) takes the cached features and current timestep/rotation information to predict the model output. Discriminator (multi‑scale with spectral normalization) receives features from Base DiT and Predictor to compute adversarial loss. Restricted MeanFlow controls the sampling of \((t, r)\) pairs during distillation, restricting \(I = (t - r) \le R\).

**Critical path (inference):**  
1. At timestep \(t_i\), run full DiT forward pass, save cache \(C = M(x_{t_i}, r_i, t_i)\).  
2. For next \(N-1\) steps \(j = 1..N-1\): run predictor \(P(C, x_{t_i-j}, r_{i-j}, t_{i-j})\) to get cached output.  
3. After \(N\) cached steps, repeat step 1.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Predictor capacity vs speed: More DiT blocks improve accuracy but increase overhead; paper finds 2 blocks sufficient.  
- Cache interval \(N\) vs quality: Larger \(N\) gives higher speedup but error accumulates; \(N=4\) achieves 11.8× with minimal drop.  
- Restrict factor \(R\) vs stability/acceleration: Lower \(R\) (e.g., 0.2) gives stable distillation but may limit max speedup; need balance.  
- Single‑layer cache vs multi‑layer: Saves VRAM but relies entirely on predictor; adversarial training helps bridge gap.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Semantic score drop >5%: Predictor underfitting, or MeanFlow not sufficiently restricted (try lower \(R\)).  
- Temporal inconsistency/flickering: Predictor not capturing temporal dynamics; increase predictor capacity or adversarial loss weight \(\lambda\).  
- VRAM exceeds expectations: Accidentally implemented multi‑layer caching; verify only one cache tensor per step.  
- Actual speedup far below theoretical: Heavy memory access patterns; ensure predictor operations fully parallelized and avoid small kernel launches.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Restricted MeanFlow ablation:** Train with different \(R\) (0.2, 0.4, 0.6) on video model; verify stability and quality at target steps before adding caching.  
2. **Predictor initialization:** Pre‑train predictor with 500 iterations of MSE‑only loss to stabilize before introducing GAN loss; monitor \(L_D\) and \(L_P\) curves for healthy adversarial dynamics.  
3. **Cache interval sweep:** Test \(N=2,3,4\) on distilled model; measure quality drop vs speedup to find sweet spot for deployment constraints (e.g., \(N=4\) for 11.8×).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Optimal restrict factor \(R\) across datasets and architectures:** What is the optimal restrict factor \(R\) for Restricted MeanFlow across diverse video datasets and model architectures, and how does it interact with the target number of sampling steps? The paper only reports \(R=0.2\) and \(0.4\) on HunyuanVideo; systematic experiments across datasets and model scales are needed.
2. **Feature extractor \(F\) in adversarial training:** How does the choice of feature extractor \(F\) affect Predictor performance and generalization, and could a lighter or alternative extractor reduce training overhead? Using full \(M\) as \(F\) is expensive; alternatives (smaller network, LPIPS) unexplored.
3. **Failure modes of multi‑layer caches under sequence parallelism:** What are the precise failure modes of traditional multi‑layer caches (e.g., TaylorSeer) under sequence parallelism, and how universally does single‑final‑layer caching outperform them across different parallelization strategies? Evidence is based on one model and one parallel setup.
4. **Transfer to other diffusion tasks and model families:** To what extent can the DisCa framework (Restricted MeanFlow + Learnable Predictor) be transferred to accelerate other diffusion‑based generation tasks (e.g., text‑to‑image, audio generation) and other model families (e.g., UNet‑based diffusion, non‑DiT transformers)? The paper exclusively experiments on large‑scale video DiTs.

## Limitations
- Predictor generalization gap: The neural predictor is validated on specific cache intervals (\(N=4\)) and \(R \le 0.4\); its accuracy on significantly sparser trajectories (e.g., \(N \ge 8\)) or diverse video motion complexities remains unproven.
- Restricted MeanFlow optimal \(R\): \(R\) likely scales with model parameter count and video temporal resolution; no clear scaling rule provided, so applying DisCa to larger future models may require re‑tuning.
- Single‑final‑layer cache robustness: The drastic VRAM saving assumes cached final‑layer features suffice; this may fail for videos requiring fine‑grained low‑level detail (e.g., fast motion, texture), where intermediate‑layer information is critical.

## Confidence
- Predictor achieves low MSE on trained distribution: High
- Restricted MeanFlow prevents divergence and improves semantic score vs. original MeanFlow: Medium
- Single‑final‑layer caching reduces VRAM overhead to ~0.4 GB: High
- Near‑lossless quality at 11.8× acceleration: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Predictor extrapolation test:** Train DisCa with \(N=2,3,4\); evaluate quality drop when increasing \(N\) to 6/8 without retraining predictor. Monitor semantic/quality score degradation to quantify generalization horizon.
2. **Restricted MeanFlow scaling law:** Apply DisCa distillation to a larger video DiT (e.g., 2× HunyuanVideo parameters). Sweep \(R \in [0.1,0.6]\) and record training stability (loss curves, gradient norms) to verify if optimal \(R\) shifts with scale.
3. **Single‑vs‑multi‑layer cache failure mode:** For high‑motion/high‑texture video sets (e.g., Kinetics‑600), compare DisCa (single‑layer) against a multi‑layer cache variant (e.g., last 3 layers). Measure failure rate (e.g., FVD jump >10%) to identify content types where single‑layer insufficient.
