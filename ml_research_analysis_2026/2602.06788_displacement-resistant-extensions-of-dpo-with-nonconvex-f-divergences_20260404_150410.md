# Displacement-Resistant Extensions of DPO with Nonconvex $f$-Divergences

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.06788  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06788  
- **Reference count:** 38  
- **Primary result:** SquaredPO (using $f(t)=0.5(\log t)^2$) maintains validation win rate near 51% after 4 epochs on Llama-3-8B/TL;DR, while DPO collapses to 34.7%, and matches DPO on AlpacaEval 2 and MT-Bench.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates probability displacement in Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), where during training the probabilities of both chosen and rejected responses can collapse to zero, degrading performance. The authors show that the KL divergence penalty in the RLHF objective can be replaced by a broad class of nonconvex $f$-divergences while retaining a tractable DPO-style loss. They characterize DPO-inducing functions by the condition $\lim_{t\to0^+} f'(t)=-\infty$ and displacement-resistant ones by $\arg\min_t f(t) \ge 1$. As a concrete instantiation, SquaredPO uses $f(t)=\frac12(\log t)^2$, yielding an adaptive coefficient $\beta_\theta(y,x)=\beta/(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\text{ref}})$ that increases when the model probability drops below the reference, counteracting displacement. Experiments on Llama-3-8B with TL;DR summarization show SquaredPO maintains a validation win rate near 51% after four epochs, while DPO falls to 34.7%. SquaredPO also matches DPO on AlpacaEval 2 (length-controlled 29.2%) and MT-Bench (7.92), and histograms confirm reduced likelihood displacement and broken monotonicity of chosen probabilities.

## Method Summary
The authors derive a family of $f$-DPO losses by substituting the closed-form optimal policy from regularized RLHF, $\pi_\theta(y|x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)\exp(r(x,y)/\beta)$, into the Bradley-Terry preference model. This substitution is valid for any continuous $f$-divergence satisfying $\lim_{t\to0^+} f'(t)=-\infty$ (interior-inducing), ensuring the optimal policy lies in the simplex interior. To avoid displacement, the function must additionally satisfy $\arg\min_t f(t) \ge 1$. SquaredPO selects $f(t)=\frac12(\log t)^2$, meeting both criteria. The resulting loss per preference pair $(x,y_w,y_l)$ is  
\[
\mathcal{L} = -\log\sigma\Big(\beta_\theta(y_w,x)\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w|x)} - \beta_\theta(y_l,x)\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l|x)}\Big)
\]  
with $\beta_\theta(y,x)=\beta\,\exp\big(\min\{\log\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)-\log\pi_\theta(y|x),\,50\}\big)$ and $\beta=0.01$. Training uses LoRA on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, AdamW with learning rate $5\cdot10^{-7}$, batch size 64, and 4 epochs. Evaluation employs GPT-4 as a judge on the TL;DR validation set, AlpacaEval 2, and MT-Bench.

## Key Results
- SquaredPO maintains a validation win rate near 51% after 4 epochs on TL;DR, while DPO collapses to 34.7%.  
- On AlpacaEval 2 (length-controlled 29.2%) and MT-Bench (score 7.92), SquaredPO matches DPO’s performance.  
- Histograms of chosen response log-ratios show SquaredPO reduces likelihood displacement and breaks the monotonic decrease observed in DPO.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Tractability with Nonconvex $f$-Divergences
The RLHF objective with $f$-divergence penalty has a closed-form optimal policy $\pi_\theta \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}\exp(r/\beta)$. Substituting this into the Bradley-Terry loss yields an $f$-DPO loss containing terms $\beta f'(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\text{ref}})\cdot(\log\pi_\theta-\log\pi_{\text{ref}})$. The substitution is valid only if the optimal policy assigns positive probability to all responses (interior of the simplex). This holds exactly when $\lim_{t\to0^+} f'(t)=-\infty$ (Theorem 1), allowing a wide range of nonconvex $f$.

### Mechanism 2: Displacement Resistance via Minimum Location
Lemma 2 shows that under the mild assumption that some out-of-sample response has higher reward than any in-sample response, any optimal solution to regularized RLHF with $f$ having minimum at $c$ satisfies $\pi_\theta(y|x) \le c\cdot\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)$ for every in-sample $y$. If $c<1$, this forces a multiplicative decrease, causing displacement. Therefore, preventing displacement requires $\arg\min_t f(t) \ge 1$.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Regularization in SquaredPO
For $f(t)=\frac12(\log t)^2$, $f'(t)=\frac{\log t}{t}$, so the effective coefficient is $\beta_\theta(y,x)=\beta\frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)}{\pi_\theta(y|x)}$. When $\pi_\theta(y|x)$ falls below $\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)$, the ratio exceeds 1 and $\beta_\theta > \beta$, automatically strengthening the penalty for that response and pulling its probability back toward the reference. This adaptive mechanism directly counteracts the downward spiral.

## Foundational Learning

- **f-divergences**  
  Why needed: Generalize KL divergence; replacing the KL penalty in RLHF changes optimization behavior.  
  Quick check: What two properties must $f$ satisfy to be both tractable and displacement-resistant? (Answer: $\lim_{t\to0^+} f'(t)=-\infty$ and $\arg\min_t f(t)\ge 1$.)

- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**  
  Why needed: DPO provides the derivation backbone; SquaredPO is an instance of $f$-DPO.  
  Quick check: How does the KL penalty in RLHF become part of the DPO loss? (Answer: Substituting $\pi_\theta \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}\exp(r/\beta)$ into the Bradley-Terry likelihood yields terms $\beta(\log\pi_\theta-\log\pi_{\text{ref}})$.)

- **Probability displacement**  
  Why needed: Displacement is the core problem the paper addresses.  
  Quick check: Why does the KL penalty (i.e., $f(t)=t\log t$) cause displacement? (Answer: Because $\arg\min_t t\log t = e^{-1}<1$, forcing $\pi_\theta(y)\le e^{-1}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y)$ for in-sample responses.)

- **Interior-inducing**  
  Why needed: Ensures the closed-form policy lies in the simplex interior, enabling the substitution step.  
  Quick check: What condition on $f'$ guarantees interior-inducing? (Answer: $\lim_{t\to0^+} f'(t)=-\infty$.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
TL;DR dataset → LoRA adapter ($\pi_\theta$) on frozen Llama-3-8B-Instruct ($\pi_{\text{ref}}$) → SquaredPO loss with adaptive $\beta_\theta$ → AdamW optimizer (lr $5\cdot10^{-7}$) → checkpoint → evaluation (GPT-4 judge on TL;DR, AlpacaEval 2, MT-Bench)

### Critical Path
1. Load SFT checkpoint as $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ and initialize LoRA modules for trainable $\pi_\theta$.  
2. For each batch $(x, y_w, y_l)$:  
   - Tokenize $x\oplus y_w$ and $x\oplus y_l$; compute log-probabilities of response tokens only under $\pi_\theta$ and $\pi_{\text{ref}}$.  
3. Compute log-ratios $r_w = \log\pi_\theta(y_w|x)-\log\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w|x)$ and $r_l$ analogously.  
4. Compute effective $\beta_\theta(y) = \beta \cdot \exp\big(\min\{\log\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)-\log\pi_\theta(y|x),\,50\}\big)$ with $\beta=0.01$.  
5. Form scores $s_w = \beta_\theta(y_w)\cdot r_w$, $s_l = \beta_\theta(y_l)\cdot r_l$.  
6. Loss $\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B \log\sigma(s_w - s_l)$. Backward pass and optimizer step.  
7. After each epoch, generate responses on a fixed validation set and compute win rate against $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ using GPT-4.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Nonconvexity vs stability**: Nonconvex $f$ broadens design space but risks unstable loss landscapes; SquaredPO is empirically stable but theory lacks global optimum guarantees.  
- **Adaptive $\beta$**: Per-response scaling emerges from $f$; however, very small $\pi_\theta$ yields huge $\beta_\theta$, risking numerical overflow. Clipping the exponent at 50 is a pragmatic fix.  
- **Parameter-efficient fine-tuning**: LoRA reduces memory and may slow displacement dynamics; results might differ under full fine-tuning.  
- **Hyperparameter $\beta$**: Fixed at 0.01 based on theory; sensitivity not fully explored.

### Failure Signatures
- **NaNs in loss**: Caused by overflow in $\exp(\cdot)$ when $\log\pi_{\text{ref}}-\log\pi_\theta$ is too large; ensure clipping is active and monitor $\beta_\theta$ statistics.  
- **Persistent displacement**: Chosen log-ratios keep dropping; indicates $f$ may not be displacement-resistant or $\beta$ is too small.  
- **Over-optimization**: Validation win rate declines after early epochs (as with DPO); implement early stopping.  
- **Memory overflow**: Reduce batch size or use gradient accumulation.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Displacement histograms**: After each epoch, compute distribution of $\log(\pi_\theta(y_w|x)/\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w|x))$ over all training pairs. Compare DPO vs. SquaredPO; expect SquaredPO to avoid extreme negatives and break monotonic decrease.  
2. **Validation win-rate trajectories**: Evaluate checkpoints at epochs 1, 2, 4 on 512 held-out TL;DR prompts with GPT-4 judge. Plot win rate vs. epoch; DPO should decline while SquaredPO stays near 50%.  
3. **Generalisation benchmarks**: After one epoch, run AlpacaEval 2 (report raw and length-controlled win rates) and MT-Bench. Verify SquaredPO’s scores are comparable to DPO’s (e.g., AlpacaEval LC ~29.2%, MT-Bench ~7.92).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Is the displacement-resistance condition both necessary and sufficient?**  
   The paper proves necessity but leaves sufficiency open. A proof that $\arg\min_t f(t)\ge1$ guarantees absence of displacement for all DPO-inducing $f$ would complete the picture; a counterexample would show it is not sufficient.

2. **What other nonconvex $f$ meet the desiderata and how do they perform?**  
   The characterization admits many $f$, yet only SquaredPO was tested. Systematically exploring families (e.g., $f(t)=\frac12(\log t)^2(1+\alpha\sin(\log t))$) and comparing empirical performance could identify better divergences.

3. **How do training dynamics influence displacement across $f$-divergences?**  
   The theory focuses on optimal solutions, but observed monotonic decrease in DPO is a dynamic phenomenon. A dynamic stability analysis (e.g., gradient flow) or experiments varying optimizer, fine‑tuning scope, and learning rates could explain why DPO collapses and SquaredPO does not.

4. **Does SquaredPO’s benefit generalize beyond LoRA and TL;DR?**  
   All experiments used LoRA on Llama-3-8B with the TL;DR dataset. Extending to full fine‑tuning, other model architectures (e.g., Qwen, Mistral), and diverse preference datasets (HH‑RLHF, UltraFeedback) is needed to establish broad applicability.

## Limitations
- Theoretical displacement-resistance condition is proved necessary but sufficiency remains unestablished.  
- Experimental evaluation is limited to LoRA fine‑tuning of a single model on one dataset; broader generalizability is unknown.  
- Baseline comparison includes only DPO; other $f$-DPO instantiations and recent preference methods (e.g., IPO, KTO) were not evaluated.
