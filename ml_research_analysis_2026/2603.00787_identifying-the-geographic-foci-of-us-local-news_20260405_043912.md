# Identifying the Geographic Foci of US Local News

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00787
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00787
- **Reference Count:** 23
- **Primary Result:** A model (NLGF) using LLM-disambiguated toponyms and engineered spatial-semantic features predicts US local news geo-focus levels with macro F1 0.89 and specific geo-foci with F1 0.86.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework, NLGF, to identify the geographic scope (geo-focus level and specific foci) of US local news articles. The core challenge is that traditional geoparsers fail on ambiguous local toponyms. The solution involves manually labeling a diverse dataset, using GPT-4o for highly accurate toponym disambiguation, and extracting spatial-semantic features (e.g., positional emphasis of toponyms) to train an XGBoost classifier. The resulting system significantly outperforms baselines, enabling large-scale measurement of newspaper nationalization trends.

## Method Summary
The method operates in a pipeline. First, a manually labeled dataset of 1,250 articles from all 50 US states, with geo-focus levels (`local`, `state`, `national`, `international`, `none`) and specific place names (foci), provides ground truth. For prediction, an article's text and publisher location are processed: 1) Named Entity Recognition (spaCy) extracts candidate toponyms (GPE, LOC, FAC); 2) Each entity is disambiguated by GPT-4o, prompted with the publisher context to resolve ambiguities and return coordinates/administrative level; 3) An Initial Geo-focus Level (IGL) is assigned per toponym via rules based on its admin level relative to the publisher's state. From these, 15 features are engineered, counting toponyms by IGL category in the title, among leading mentions, by entity type (GPE), and counting unique identifiers. An XGBoost classifier, trained with specific hyperparameters, predicts the single primary geo-focus level. Finally, a heuristic filters toponyms matching the predicted level, scores them by a combination of title, body, and positional signals, and selects those above a threshold as the article's geo-foci.

## Key Results
- The NLGF classifier achieves a macro-averaged F1 score of **0.89** for geo-focus level prediction.
- The heuristic for identifying specific geo-foci achieves a sample-averaged F1 of **0.86**.
- The system substantially outperforms baselines: GPT-4o alone (level F1 0.62) and the frequency-based Cliff-Clavin system (level F1 0.62).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A manually labeled, geographically diverse dataset provides a reliable ground truth for training and evaluating the geo-focus classifier.
- **Mechanism:** Human experts annotate articles with geo-focus levels and specific geo-foci. Strong inter-rater reliability (Krippendorff’s α of 0.81) indicates consistent label application across the US.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...we manually labeled US local news articles from all 50 states... Krippendorff’s α of 0.81." [section 3.1] "two domain experts independently annotated... Cohen’s κ and Krippendorff’s α of 0.83."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Using LLMs (GPT-4o) for toponym disambiguation yields significantly higher accuracy than traditional geoparsers, especially for ambiguous place names.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs use contextual reasoning, including publisher location, to resolve ambiguities (e.g., "Paris" in a Texas paper vs. France). This produces more accurate coordinate and administrative level assignments.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "LLMs outperformed all eight geographic entity disambiguation methods we evaluated." [section 3.3] Table 1 shows GPT-4o F1 scores of 0.948 (GPE), 0.813 (LOC), 0.964 (FAC), far exceeding traditional methods (max ~0.543).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Engineering spatial-semantic features from disambiguated toponyms and their IGLs allows a classifier to robustly distinguish closely related geo-focus levels.
- **Mechanism:** Features capture not just frequency but **positional emphasis** (toponyms in leading positions), **prominence** (title mentions), **diversity**, and **entity-type specificity**. XGBoost learns non-linear interactions between these cues.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.4.1] Lists feature classes (title counts, GPE counts, leading toponyms, diversity). [section 4 & Figure 2] Feature importance plot shows leading toponym features as most influential. [Table 2] NLGF (macro F1 0.89) significantly outperforms Cliff-Clavin (0.62).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Geoparsing & Toponym Disambiguation**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire pipeline depends on accurately extracting where a place name refers. The paper's key innovation is using LLMs for this step.
  - **Quick check question:** What are the core failure modes of a tool like Mordecai on a sentence like "The spring in Paris is lovely," from a *Paris, Texas* newspaper, and how might an LLM's contextual reasoning resolve it?
- **Concept: Feature Engineering for Spatial-Semantic Signals**
  - **Why needed here:** Raw toponym lists are insufficient. The model needs signals about *how* geography is used (e.g., a local story often mentions the town in the first sentence).
  - **Quick check question:** Why would "count of toponyms appearing in the first five mentions" be more predictive of "local" geo-focus than "total toponym count"?
- **Concept: Multi-Label vs. Single-Label Classification**
  - **Why needed here:** Geo-foci (specific places) is multi-label (0, 1, or many foci). Geo-focus level is single-label (one primary scope). The paper uses different strategies and metrics for each.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is sample-averaged F1 more appropriate for evaluating geo-foci prediction than micro-averaged F1?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Article (title, body, publisher location) -> spaCy NER (extract GPE/LOC/FAC entities) -> GPT-4o Disambiguation (assign lat/long, admin level) -> IGL Assignment (map admin level to local/state/national/international) -> Feature Extraction (compute 15 spatial-semantic features) -> XGBoost Classifier (predict primary geo-focus level) -> Geo-foci Heuristic (filter by level, score, threshold) -> Output (predicted level, set of foci).
- **Critical path:** Article -> NER -> Disambiguation -> IGL -> Features -> Level Prediction -> Foci Selection. Cascading failure occurs if disambiguation returns invalid coordinates/admin levels.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Chose GPT-4o for accuracy over cost/latency. Used a heuristic for foci (simplicity/interpretability) instead of a learned multi-label model. Used a single-label level classifier for simplicity, discarding articles with genuine multi-level foci.
- **Failure signatures:** Mis-disambiguation cascades (e.g., "Springfield" → wrong state → wrong IGL → wrong features). Feature sparsity for toponym-poor articles. High `α` threshold yields empty foci for marginal articles. Concept drift on new slang or hyponyms.
- **First experiments:** 1) Replicate the disambiguation benchmark on the 300-toponym gold set; verify GPT-4o F1 matches Table 1 (~0.9+). 2) Sanity-check feature correlations; ensure no |r| > 0.85 and that leading toponym features are highly important. 3) Run the full pipeline on 50 random articles; compare level predictions to Cliff-Clavin and a human spot-check, focusing on borderline local/state/national cases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Can NLGF be extended to predict multiple geo-focus levels per article?** The current model outputs one primary level, but articles often cover multiple scopes (e.g., local and national) simultaneously.
- **How effective is NLGF in non-US contexts?** The framework relies on US administrative structures; applicability to other countries with different hierarchies and naming conventions is unknown.
- **What feature enhancements reduce misclassification between local, state, and national?** Confusion persists due to semantic overlap and contextual nuance; hierarchical or graph-based models may help.
- **How do LLM disambiguation errors propagate to geo-focus prediction?** No analysis links disambiguation mistakes to downstream accuracy; integrating confidence scores or consistency checks could mitigate.

## Limitations
- Unclear final evaluation protocol (fixed test set vs. CV average).
- Feature extraction specifics are not fully detailed (handling of title repeats, definition of "unique" toponyms, leading-toponym logic).
- Unstated spaCy model version, GPT-4o parameters, and prompt robustness may cause replication variance.
- Potential break conditions include IRR drops on new article types, LLM failure on dialectal text, concept drift, and sparse-toponym edge cases.

## Confidence
- **Manual labeling reliability: High** – Strong IRR (Krippendorff α 0.81) across all 50 states.
- **LLM disambiguation superiority: High** – GPT-4o F1 >0.81 on all entity types, far exceeding traditional geoparsers (Table 1).
- **Feature-based classifier effectiveness: Medium** – Reported macro F1 0.89, but ambiguous feature definitions and heuristic α-tuning create replication risk.
