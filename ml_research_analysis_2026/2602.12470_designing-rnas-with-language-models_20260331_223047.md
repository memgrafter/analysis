# Designing RNAs with Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12470
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12470
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** An autoregressive language model with supervised and reinforcement learning achieves state-of-the-art Boltzmann probability and Normalized Ensemble Defect across four RNA design benchmarks, while being 1.7× faster than prior methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles RNA inverse folding—designing sequences that adopt a target secondary structure—by reformulating it as conditional sequence generation with an autoregressive language model. The approach involves three stages: (1) supervised learning (SL) on a large corpus of random-induced structure-sequence pairs, (2) reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning on a small, intelligently filtered subset of structures to directly optimize thermodynamic ensemble metrics, and (3) a constrained decoding algorithm that enforces Watson-Crick pairing during generation. The final model, SL+RL, sets new state-of-the-art results on four test datasets (Eterna100, Eterna100-v2, Rfam-Taneda-27, RNAsolo-100), matching or exceeding prior ensemble quality metrics while being significantly faster due to efficient GPU-based decoding.

## Method Summary
The authors use a decoder-only Transformer (Qwen2.5-0.5B) pretrained on general text, modify its embedding and output layers for RNA-specific tokens, and first train it via maximum likelihood on 10 million (structure, sequence) pairs generated by a prior solver (SAMFEO) on random-induced RNA structures. To improve ensemble quality, they fine-tune the model with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a form of RL, on a filtered subset of ~2,800 Eterna-web structures. The filtering selects structures where the SL model shows moderate but variable performance (average Boltzmann probability ≥ 0.1, normalized standard deviation > 0.5). During both training and inference, a constrained decoding procedure dynamically masks invalid nucleotides based on the target dot-bracket structure, ensuring every generated sequence is syntactically valid (i.e., base-paired positions are complementary).

## Key Results
- The SL+RL model achieves state-of-the-art Boltzmann probability and Normalized Ensemble Defect (NED) on all four test datasets, outperforming SAMFEO, RNAinverse-pf, and other competitors.
- It matches or exceeds prior ensemble metrics while being 1.7× faster, attributed to efficient GPU-based constrained decoding.
- Reinforcing on a small, filtered subset (~2.8K structures) yields better quality and training speed than using a larger, uncurated set (~13.1K structures).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Reframing RNA design as conditional sequence generation with an autoregressive LM amortizes the inverse folding problem into a reusable feedforward mapping.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of solving each target structure from scratch via per-instance optimization, the model learns a shared parameter function \( p_{\theta}(x|y^{\star}) \) that directly generates candidate sequences conditioned on the dot-bracket structure. This transforms the combinatorial search over sequence space into a tractable, parallelizable sampling process.
- **Core assumption:** A sufficiently expressive autoregressive LM, when trained on an appropriate distribution of structure-sequence pairs, can internalize the mapping from structural constraints to viable sequences, generalizing to unseen targets.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "We instead reframe RNA design as conditional sequence generation and introduce a reusable neural approximator... that maps target structures directly to sequences."
  - [section 3.1] Formalizes the conditional distribution \( p_{\theta}(x|y^{\star}) = \prod_{t} p_{\theta}(x_t | x_{<t}, y^{\star}) \).
  - [corpus] Papers like "BAnG" and "Eterna is Solved" also explore generative approaches but with different architectures/conditioning, suggesting the paradigm shift is recognized but the specific LM+RL+constrained decoding pipeline is novel.
- **Break condition:** If the training distribution \( Y_{X}^{\text{train}} \) (random-induced MFE structures) is too disjoint from test distribution \( Y_{\text{test}} \) (human-designed/native structures), the learned prior may not transfer, causing poor generalization. The paper's success mitigates this but doesn't prove universal transfer.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Constrained decoding enforces biochemical validity (Watson-Crick/Wobble pairing) during autoregressive generation, guaranteeing outputs lie in the feasible design space \( \mathcal{X}(y^{\star}) \).
- **Mechanism:** A precomputed pairing map \( m(t) \) for structure \( y^{\star} \) dynamically masks logits at each decoding step. For a paired position \( t \) (symbol ')'), the model's choice is restricted to \( \text{Comp}(x_{m(t)}) \), the nucleotides compatible with the nucleotide already placed at its partner index \( m(t) \). This eliminates post-hoc filtering.
- **Core assumption:** Enforcing validity at generation time is more sample-efficient than generating unrestricted sequences and discarding invalid ones, especially for long structures where invalidity rates explode.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 3.2] Provides the exact masking rule \( A_t \) and implementation details.
  - [section 6.2] Empirically shows naive decoding yields high invalid rates (>80% for long targets) while constrained decoding guarantees 0% invalidity with only ~30% overhead.
  - [corpus] No direct corpus anchor; this is a technical contribution specific to this work's decoding setup.
- **Break condition:** The constraint is hardcoded based on \( y^{\star} \)'s dot-bracket. If the target structure itself is thermodynamically unrealistic or contains pseudoknots (not handled), generated sequences may still fold incorrectly even if syntactically valid.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reinforcement learning on a small, intelligently filtered subset of structures (\( \mathcal{Y}_{\text{RL}} \)) fine-tunes the SL model to directly optimize end-to-end thermodynamic metrics (Boltzmann probability, NED), yielding superior ensemble quality.
- **Mechanism:** After SL produces plausible designs, GRPO uses rewards \( R(x, y^{\star}) \) based on folding evaluation. Critically, they select \( \mathcal{Y}_{\text{RL}} \) via two statistics computed from SL model samples: Average Probability (AoN) ≥ 0.1 and Normalized Standard Deviation (NSD) > 0.5. This identifies structures where the SL model is neither too poor nor too deterministic, maximizing RL's potential to improve.
- **Core assumption:** RL on a small, high-potential subset is more compute-efficient and yields better generalization than RL on a large, uncurated set, as the latter may degrade performance on many structures.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "We develop methods to select a small subset for RL that greatly improves RL efficiency and quality."
  - [section 5.3] Details the filtering criteria (Eq. 7) and the size reduction from 13.1K (\( \mathcal{Y}_{\text{RL}}^{\text{large}} \)) to ~2.8K (\( \mathcal{Y}_{\text{RL}} \)).
  - [section 6.4] Shows RL on \( \mathcal{Y}_{\text{RL}} \) (16 hrs) is faster than on \( \mathcal{Y}_{\text{RL}}^{\text{large}} \) (47 hrs) and yields positive generalization, while RL on the large set harms many structures.
  - [corpus] Related work like LEARNA uses RL from scratch; this paper's RL is a fine-tuning stage on top of SL, and the subset selection heuristic appears novel.
- **Break condition:** The filtering criteria (AoN ≥ 0.1, NSD > 0.5) are heuristics based on the SL model's initial performance. If the SL model's sampling distribution is poorly calibrated or the metrics are noisy, the selected subset may not be optimally "learnable," limiting RL's ultimate gains.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Autoregressive language modeling (Transformer decoder-only architecture).
  - *Why needed here:* The core generator is an autoregressive LM that factorizes \( p_{\theta}(x|y^{\star}) = \prod_t p_{\theta}(x_t | x_{<t}, y^{\star}) \). Understanding next-token prediction, causal attention, and training via maximum likelihood is essential.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the autoregressive factorization enable conditioning on the full prefix \( x_{<t} \) during generation, and why is this important for capturing dependencies required by base-pairing constraints?
- **Concept:** Thermodynamic RNA folding models and ensemble-based evaluation metrics.
  - *Why needed here:* The reward function and all key metrics (Boltzmann probability \( p(y^{\star}|x) \), NED) are defined in terms of the RNA folding ensemble \( \mathcal{Y}(x) \) and free energies \( \Delta G^{\circ}(x,y) \). Without grasping partition functions and MFE, the objectives are meaningless.
  - *Quick check question:* Why are ensemble metrics (Boltzmann probability, NED) considered more important than MFE-solve rate for assessing RNA design quality?
- **Concept:** Constrained generation for context-free/regular language hybrids.
  - *Why needed here:* The valid design space \( \mathcal{X}(y^{\star}) \) is a context-free language (long-distance dependencies via base pairs). The paper implements a stack-free, precomputed masking scheme. Understanding why vanilla autoregressive generation violates the pairing constraints is key.
  - *Quick check question:* For a given structure \( y^{\star} \) and current prefix \( x_{<t} \), what specific information is needed to compute the set of admissible nucleotides \( A_t \) at position \( t \)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Encoder (structure tokenization) -> Transformer Backbone (Qwen2.5-0.5B, frozen) -> Modified Input Embedding Layer (RNA-specific) -> Modified LM Head (RNA-specific) -> Constrained Decoding Callback (logit masking) -> Folding Engine (ViennaRNA, external for RL rewards)
- **Critical path:**  
  Training: Load pretrained Qwen2.5-0.5B -> Replace embedding and LM head with RNA-specific layers -> Supervised learning (MLE) on 10M (structure, sequence) pairs -> Filter ~2.8K RL structures from Eterna set using SL model statistics -> Reinforcement learning (GRPO) on filtered set with ViennaRNA-based rewards.  
  Inference: Given new target structure -> Tokenize with special tokens -> Autoregressive decode with constrained callback -> Output sequence.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Pretrained vs. Scratch:* Pretrained backbone provides optimization stability and sample efficiency; scratch training converges slower and underperforms.  
  - *Decoder-only vs. Encoder-Decoder:* Decoder-only enables high-throughput parallel sampling critical for RL and evaluation; encoder-decoder has higher per-step cost.  
  - *Small RL subset vs. Large:* Small subset (~2.8K) is faster (2.9×) and yields positive generalization; large subset (~13.1K) risks destructive updates.  
  - *Constrained decoding overhead:* ~30% slower than naive decoding but eliminates invalid outputs, improving effective sample efficiency.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High invalid sequence rate (>5%) → Constrained decoding not properly integrated or pairing map incorrect.  
  - RL degrades performance on large RL set → Filtering criteria too lenient/strict; reward signal too noisy.  
  - Poor generalization to test sets despite good SL performance → Training distribution (random-induced) too mismatched with test distribution.  
  - Slow decoding → Constrained callback inefficient; consider beam search or grammar-specific acceleration.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Validate constrained decoding:** For 100 random structures (50-400 nt), generate 100 sequences with constrained vs. naive decoding. Measure % invalid and wall-clock time. Expect: constrained ~0% invalid, naive invalid ↑ with length.  
  2. **SL from scratch vs. pretrained:** Train two models (identical architecture) on 10M SL data: one from Qwen2.5-0.5B init, one random init. Compare best-of-1000 Boltzmann probability on Eterna100 after same compute. Expect: pretrained >> scratch.  
  3. **RL subset selection ablation:** Run RL on three subsets: (a) filtered 2.8K (AoN≥0.1, NSD>0.5), (b) full 13.1K (distance-filtered only), (c) random 2.8K from (b). Compare final AoN on 13.1K and training time. Expect: (a) best gain & fastest, (c) worst, (b) intermediate but possibly negative.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Can beam search reduce the ∼30% overhead of constrained decoding while maintaining validity?  
  *Basis:* Explicit in conclusion. *Why unresolved:* Proposed method remains unimplemented and unevaluated. *Resolving evidence:* Integrate beam search into constrained decoding pipeline, measure time and invalid rates across lengths on Eterna100.
- **Question 2:** Does scaling reinforcement learning to larger structure sets and more samples improve performance?  
  *Basis:* Explicit in conclusion. *Why unresolved:* RL was restricted to ∼2.8K structures due to computational limits; impact of more data and samples is unknown. *Resolving evidence:* Train RL on increasingly large structure subsets (up to 13.1K) with varying samples per structure; compare Boltzmann probability, NED, and training time.
- **Question 3:** Does Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL) enhance design quality by adapting the model per test structure?  
  *Basis:* Explicit in conclusion. *Why unresolved:* Amortized model may not be optimal for every puzzle; TTRL could tailor parameters but cost is speculative. *Resolving evidence:* For a subset of test structures, fine-tune SL+RL model briefly with RL on that structure; compare best-of-N Boltzmann probability and total time vs. standard SL+RL.
- **Question 4:** Why does supervised learning on random-induced, test-unrelated structures yield competitive performance on human-designed puzzles?  
  *Basis:* Inferred from Sec 6.1. *Why unresolved:* Unclear whether random structures capture universal folding principles, provide sufficient diversity, or if model learns generalizable biases. *Resolving evidence:* Ablate training data sources (random-induced vs. native-derived), measure performance on diverse test sets; analyze attention patterns for generalizable structure rules; test on out-of-distribution puzzles.

## Limitations
- Missing critical details: hyperparameters, SAMFEO setup, ViennaRNA version, and the exact 10M training pairs are unreleased.
- Distribution shift: SL trained on random-induced MFE structures; test puzzles are human-designed; generalization may suffer.
- RL filtering thresholds (AoN≥0.1, NSD>0.5) are heuristic; their optimality outside Eterna puzzles unknown.
- Constrained decoding assumes no pseudoknots; overhead ~30% may limit scalability to long RNAs.

## Confidence
- **Amortized conditional generation:** Medium. Depends on pretraining and data distribution; transfer uncertain.
- **Constrained decoding validity:** High. Guarantees 0% invalid; empirical overhead ~30%.
- **RL filter gains:** Medium. Improves over large-set RL; heuristic may not generalize.
- **SOTA results:** Medium. Reported on four benchmarks; not independently verified.
