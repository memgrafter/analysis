# Regularized Online RLHF with Generalized Bilinear Preferences

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23116
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23116
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proves the dual gap of the greedy policy is bounded by the square of estimation error under GBPM, enabling Greedy Sampling with polylogarithmic, $e^{O(\eta)}$-free regret $\tilde{O}(\eta d^4 (\log T)^2)$ and Explore-Then-Commit with poly$(d)$-free regret $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\eta r T})$ by exploiting low-rank structure.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses online reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) under intransitive preferences modeled by the Generalized Bilinear Preference Model (GBPM). It introduces a framework using any strongly convex regularizer $\psi$ and analyzes two algorithms: Greedy Sampling (GS) and Explore-Then-Commit (ETC). The core theoretical insight is a quadratic bound linking the dual gap of a greedy policy to the estimation error of the preference parameter matrix $\Theta^\star$. This yields the first polylogarithmic, $e^{O(\eta)}$-free regret guarantee for GS and the first statistically efficient (poly($d$)-free) guarantee for high-dimensional online RLHF via ETC, both relying on feature diversity and, for ETC, low-rank structure of $\Theta^\star$.

## Method Summary
The problem is an online two-player zero-sum game where at each round $t$, a context $x_t$ is sampled. The max-player selects action $a_1^t \sim \hat{\pi}_1^t(\cdot|x_t)$, the min-player selects $a_2^t \sim \hat{\pi}_2^t(\cdot|x_t)$, and a binary preference feedback $r_t \sim \text{Ber}(\mu(\phi(x_t,a_1^t)^\top \Theta^\star \phi(x_t,a_2^t)))$ is observed. The feature map $\phi: \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{A} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ is known; $\Theta^\star \in \text{Skew}(d; 2r, S)$ is skew-symmetric, low-rank ($\text{rank}(\Theta^\star) \le 2r$), and unknown. The objective is to minimize Max-Best-Response (MBR) or Average-Best-Response (ABR) regret with respect to the symmetric Nash equilibrium of the regularized game $J_\eta(\pi_1,\pi_2) = J(\pi_1,\pi_2) - \eta^{-1}\psi(\pi_1) + \eta^{-1}\psi(\pi_2)$, where $\psi$ is any $\beta^{-1}$-strongly convex function w.r.t. $\|\cdot\|_1$.

*Greedy Sampling (GS)*: The max-player plays the symmetric Nash equilibrium (SNE) of the regularized game computed from the current MLE $\hat{\Theta}_t$, which is obtained by minimizing the logistic loss over a norm-and-skew-constrained set. The min-player plays a fixed, diverse exploration policy $\rho$. *Explore-Then-Commit (ETC)*: Both players play $\rho$ for an exploration horizon $T_0$ to collect diverse data. Then a nuclear-norm regularized MLE $\hat{\Theta}$ is computed, and both players commit to the SNE derived from $\hat{\Theta}$.

The regret analysis hinges on Theorem 3.1, which bounds the dual gap $\text{DGap}_\eta(\hat{\pi})$ of any greedy policy quadratically in the estimation error $\|(\Theta^\star - \hat{\Theta})\phi\|_2$. For GS, this is combined with a martingale concentration argument and a Coverage Lemma that converts sums over greedy policies to elliptical sums under $\rho$, yielding $\tilde{O}(\eta d^4 (\log T)^2)$. For ETC, the low-rank structure and the Coverage Lemma convert the operator-norm error term into a Frobenius norm term independent of $d$, yielding $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\eta r T})$.

## Key Results
- **Quadratic Dual Gap:** For any $\hat{\pi}$ and any $\beta^{-1}$-strongly convex $\psi$, under GBPM with skew-symmetric $\Theta^\star$, $\text{DGap}_\eta(\hat{\pi}) \lesssim (1+\eta\beta) \cdot \mathbb{E}_{\phi \sim \hat{\pi}}[\|(\Theta^\star - \hat{\Theta})\phi\|_2^2]$.
- **Greedy Sampling Regret:** With feature diversity (Assumption 1) and logistic link, GS achieves with high probability $\text{MBR-Reg}_\eta(T) = \tilde{O}(\eta d^4 (\log T)^2)$, which is polylogarithmic and free of $e^{O(\eta)}$ factors.
- **Explore-Then-Commit Regret:** With feature diversity and $\text{rank}(\Theta^\star) \le 2r$, ETC (with nuclear-norm MLE) achieves $\text{MBR-Reg}_\eta(T) = \tilde{O}(\sqrt{\eta r T})$, a rate polynomial in $r$ but free of explicit $d$-dependence, providing the first statistically efficient guarantee for high-dimensional online RLHF.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The dual gap of any greedy policy is bounded quadratically by the estimation error of $\Theta^\star$, enabling fast (polylogarithmic) regret.
- **Mechanism:** For a $\beta^{-1}$-strongly convex regularizer $\psi$ and skew-symmetric $\Theta^\star$, a Taylor expansion of the dual gap yields a linear term in $E_t = \Theta^\star - \hat{\Theta}_t$ and a quadratic term. The linear term is bounded by $\frac{L_\mu}{2} \|\hat{\pi} - \tilde{\pi}\|_1 \cdot \mathbb{E}_{\phi \sim \hat{\pi}}[\|E_t \phi\|^2]$ via Lemma 3.2. Lemma 3.2 crucially uses the integral probability metric representation of $\|\cdot\|_1$ and the skew-symmetry/central symmetry of GBPM. Strong convexity (Lemma 3.3) then gives $\text{DGap}_\eta(\hat{\pi}) \ge (2\eta\beta)^{-1} \|\hat{\pi} - \tilde{\pi}\|_1^2$, linking $\|\cdot\|_1$-distance to the dual gap. Combining these produces a self-bounding quadratic inequality in $\text{DGap}_\eta(\hat{\pi})$.
- **Core assumption:** Strong convexity of $\psi$ and skew-symmetry of $\Theta^\star$.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the quadratic bound result derives from strong convexity and skew-symmetry. Section 3 derives Lemma 3.2 using the IPM representation. The proof does not require feature diversity.
- **Break condition:** If $\psi$ is not strongly convex, the $\|\cdot\|_1$ bound fails, leading to a linear error term and $\sqrt{T}$ regret. If $\Theta^\star$ is not skew-symmetric, Lemma 3.2 fails due to the symmetry argument.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Under feature diversity and low-rank structure, ETC with nuclear-norm regularized MLE attains poly($d$)-free regret by converting operator-norm error into Frobenius error and using the Coverage Lemma.
- **Mechanism:** ETC uses an exploration policy $\rho$ satisfying $\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb{E}_{\phi \sim \rho}[\phi \phi^\top]) \ge C_{\min} > 0$. The nuclear-norm regularized MLE yields a Frobenius error bound $\|\hat{\Theta} - \Theta^\star\|_F \lesssim (\tilde{\kappa} C_{\min}^2)^{-1} \sqrt{r \log d / T_0}$ (Lemma C.1). The $\eta$-independent dual gap bound $\text{DGap}_\eta \lesssim \mathbb{E}[\|E_t \phi\|^2] + \mathbb{E}[\|E_t\|_{\text{op}}]$ (from Theorem 3.1 applied with $\eta \to \infty$) is then analyzed. The low-rank structure converts the operator norm term to $\sqrt{r}$ times the Frobenius norm. The Coverage Lemma (Theorem B.2) replaces the expectation over the greedy policy $\hat{\pi}$ with an elliptical sum over features drawn from $\rho$. Balancing the exploration and commitment phases with $T_0 \asymp \sqrt{T \eta r \log d}$ gives $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\eta r T})$.
- **Core assumption:** Feature diversity ($C_{\min} > 0$) and $\text{rank}(\Theta^\star) \le 2r$.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the poly($d$)-free rate by exploiting low-rank structure. Section 5 details the ETC algorithm and the use of the Coverage Lemma to bound the sum of elliptical potentials. Theorem B.2 provides the Coverage Lemma.
- **Break condition:** Poor feature diversity ($C_{\min}$ too small) breaks the Coverage Lemma, reverting to $d$-dependent rates. If $\Theta^\star$ is full-rank, the nuclear-norm regularization does not improve the error rate beyond $O(d/\sqrt{T_0})$.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Greedy Sampling with norm+skew-constrained MLE achieves polylogarithmic, $e^{O(\eta)}$-free regret under feature diversity, improving upon prior work.
- **Mechanism:** GS plays the SNE w.r.t. the MLE $\hat{\Theta}_t$ at each round. The proof follows: (1) Apply the $\eta$-dependent dual gap bound (Theorem 3.1) to get $\text{MBR-Reg}(T) \lesssim \eta \sum_t \text{DGap}_\eta(\hat{\pi}_t)$. (2) Decompose $\text{DGap}_\eta(\hat{\pi}_t)$ via Cauchy-Schwarz w.r.t. the regularized Hessian $\mathbf{cH}_t$ and use the confidence sequence $\|(\Theta^\star - \hat{\Theta}_t)\|_{\mathbf{cH}_t}^2 \lesssim \gamma_t(\delta)$. (3) Apply the Coverage Lemma to replace the sum over greedy policies with an elliptical sum under $\rho$. (4) Bound the expected elliptical sum $S_T$ using a martingale (Freedman's inequality), leading to a linear self-bounding inequality that resolves to $\tilde{O}(d^4 (\log T)^2)$. The $e^{O(\eta)}$-free rate is achieved by avoiding log-density ratio arguments specific to KL-regularization.
- **Core assumption:** Feature diversity; logistic link $\mu(z) = (1+e^{-z})^{-1}$ for the confidence sequence; MLE constrained to skew-symmetric norm ball.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract gives the $\tilde{O}(\eta d^4 (\log T)^2)$ rate. Section 4 presents Theorem 4.1 and the martingale argument. The paper notes (Sec 1.1) that Wu et al. (2025a) achieved $\tilde{O}(e^{9\eta} (\log T)^2)$, and this work removes the $e^{O(\eta)}$ factor.
- **Break condition:** If $C_{\min}^{-1}$ scales with $d$ (e.g., $C_{\min} \asymp 1/d$), the bound becomes $\tilde{O}(d^5 \eta \log^2 T)$. Non-logistic links may degrade the confidence sequence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Regularized Nash Equilibrium (SNE):** The solution concept for the game $J_\eta(\pi_1,\pi_2)$. The value of the regularized game is always $1/2$ (Lemma 2.4), simplifying the dual gap definition and regret decomposition.
  - *Why needed:* Defines the target policy and the suboptimality measure (dual gap).
  - *Quick check:* Why does $\max_{\pi_1} \min_{\pi_2} J_\eta(\pi_1,\pi_2) = 1/2$ for all $\Theta$, and how does that simplify regret analysis?
- **GBPM (Generalized Bilinear Preference Model):** The preference model $P(a_1 \succ a_2 | x) = \mu(\phi(x,a_1)^\top \Theta^\star \phi(x,a_2))$. The skew-symmetry of $\Theta^\star$ ensures $P(a_1 \succ a_2) + P(a_2 \succ a_1) = 1$.
  - *Why needed:* Parametrizes intransitive preferences; skew-symmetry is critical for Lemma 3.2's symmetry argument.
  - *Quick check:* How does $\Theta^{\star\top} = -\Theta^\star$ manifest in the preference probabilities, and why is it used in Lemma 3.2?
- **Dual gap and strong convexity:** The dual gap $\text{DGap}_\eta(\hat{\pi}) = 1/2 - \min_{\pi_2} J_\eta(\hat{\pi}, \pi_2)$ measures suboptimality. Strong convexity of $J_\eta(\cdot, \pi_2)$ w.r.t. $\|\cdot\|_1$ gives $\text{DGap}_\eta \ge (2\eta\beta)^{-1} \|\hat{\pi} - \tilde{\pi}\|_1^2$, linking $\ell_1$-distance to the gap.
  - *Why needed:* Enables the self-bounding technique where $\|\hat{\pi} - \tilde{\pi}\|_1$ is bounded by $\sqrt{\text{DGap}_\eta}$, converting linear error terms to quadratic.
  - *Quick check:* In the self-bounding inequality (Eqn. 9), why is pairing Theorem 3.1 (quadratic bound) with Lemma 3.3 (strong convexity) necessary to avoid a dominating linear error term?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Feature map $\phi$ -> GBPM with unknown $\Theta^\star$ (skew-symmetric, low-rank) -> Estimator (norm+skew MLE for GS; nuclear-norm MLE for ETC) -> SNE oracle (computes equilibrium of $J_\eta$) -> Policies ($\hat{\pi}_1^t, \hat{\pi}_2^t$) -> Feedback $r_t$.
- **Critical path:** Theorem 3.1 (quadratic dual gap bound) is the central lemma. For GS: Theorem 3.1 + MLE confidence + Coverage Lemma + martingale concentration $\to$ polylogarithmic regret. For ETC: Lemma C.1 (nuclear-norm MLE error) + Theorem 3.1 ($\eta \to \infty$) + low-rank conversion + Coverage Lemma $\to$ poly($d$)-free regret.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *GS:* Simple, no separate exploration phase, but incurs a $d^4$ factor and requires $C_{\min} > 0$. Suitable for large $T$ and moderate $d$.
  - *ETC:* Requires choosing $T_0$, but yields rates independent of $d$ (only depending on rank $r$). Better for high-dimensional settings with low-rank $\Theta^\star$. Still needs feature diversity via $C_{\min}$ for the Coverage Lemma.
  - *Regularizer choice:* Theory holds for any strongly convex $\psi$, but constants (e.g., $\beta$) and potential $\eta$-dependence without diversity (Section B.4) vary. Reverse KL (common in practice) may incur $e^{\eta}$ factor without diversity; $\chi^2$ incurs $\eta$; mixed can give $1+\eta$.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. Feature diversity fails: $\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[\phi\phi^\top]) \ll 1$ $\to$ Coverage Lemma fails $\to$ regret scales polynomially in $d$ (GS: $\tilde{O}(d^5 \eta \log^2 T)$; ETC loses poly($d$)-free property).
  2. Low-rank structure violated: $\text{rank}(\Theta^\star) \approx d$ $\to$ nuclear-norm MLE error $\|\hat{\Theta} - \Theta^\star\|_F = O(d/\sqrt{T_0})$ $\to$ ETC regret becomes $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\eta d T})$.
  3. Strong convexity lost: Using non-strongly convex $\psi$ (e.g., pure entropy) $\to$ Lemma 3.3 fails $\to$ dual gap bound becomes linear in error $\to$ regret $\Omega(\sqrt{T})$.
  4. Skew-symmetry violated: Standard BTL or non-antisymmetric preferences $\to$ Lemma 3.2 fails $\to$ quadratic bound does not hold.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce Theorem 4.2: Set $d=10, r=2$, generate random skew-symmetric low-rank $\Theta^\star$, use logistic $\mu$, ensure $C_{\min} \approx 1$ (well-conditioned $\rho$). Run GS, plot cumulative MBR-Reg vs. $T$, verify $\tilde{O}(\eta d^4 (\log T)^2)$ scaling.
  2. Verify quadratic dual gap bound: For each round of GS, compute $\text{DGap}_\eta(\hat{\pi}_t)$ and $\mathbb{E}_{\phi \sim \hat{\pi}_t}[\|(\Theta^\star - \hat{\Theta}_t)\phi\|_2^2]$. Plot their relationship and check consistency with Theorem 3.1 for several $\psi$ (e.g., $\chi^2$, Tsallis).
  3. Test GS vs. ETC in high dimensions: Set $d=50, r=3$, $C_{\min} \approx 1/d$ (ill-conditioned). Compare cumulative MBR-Reg for GS (should blow up with $d^4$) and ETC (should scale as $\sqrt{\eta r T}$). Vary $T_0$ to observe the trade-off between exploration cost and commitment error.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can we obtain polylogarithmic regret with Greedy Sampling under GBPM while relaxing the feature diversity assumption (e.g., using a local anti-concentration property instead of a uniform $C_{\min}$)? The paper states in Section H.1 that this is an important open question, as the current analysis relies heavily on Assumption 1.
- **Open Question 2:** Can we derive instance-specific regret bounds that adapt to the geometry of the Nash equilibrium set (e.g., achieving logarithmic regret when the NE is unique and pure)? Section H.2 notes that their unregularized bound is worst-case $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ and adapting instance-dependent guarantees from tabular games to contextual GBPM is non-trivial.
- **Open Question 3:** Can we design computationally efficient algorithms that match the statistical guarantees of oracle-based GS or ETC without requiring a population NE oracle? Section H.3 explicitly identifies developing efficient variants as a practical priority, as Oracle 3 may be computationally expensive.

## Limitations
- The results critically depend on (i) $\Theta^\star$ being skew-symmetric and low-rank, (ii) a well-conditioned exploration distribution with $\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[\phi\phi^\top]) \ge C_{\min} > 0$, and (iii) the regularizer $\psi$ being strongly convex. Violations can cause regret to scale polynomially in $d$ (up to $d^4$ or $d^5$) or as $\sqrt{T}$.
- The analysis assumes an efficient oracle for computing symmetric Nash equilibria; without it, the practical implementation of the algorithms is unclear.
- The claim that the framework works for "any" strongly convex regularizer is theoretically broad, but the interplay between the strong convexity constant $\beta$, $\eta$, and feature diversity is not fully characterized for common choices like KL divergence; some $\psi$ may still incur $\eta$-dependent factors without feature diversity.

## Confidence
- **Quadratic dual gap bound (Theorem 3.1):** Medium. Proof structure is credible under the stated assumptions, but its fragility to skew-symmetry and strong convexity is explicit.
- **Greedy Sampling polylogarithmic regret:** Medium. Achieves the stated rate under feature diversity and logistic link, but the $d^4$ factor and sensitivity to $C_{\min}$ suggest high-dimensional fragility.
- **Explore-Then-Commit poly($d$)-free regret:** Medium. Requires accurate low-rank assumption and $C_{\min} > 0$; if $r$ is large or features are ill-conditioned, the improvement vanishes.
- **Generality to arbitrary strongly convex $\psi$:** Low-Medium. The framework is general, but concrete constant-level characterizations for common $\psi$ are lacking; Section B.4 indicates some $\psi$ still incur $\eta$-dependence without diversity.
