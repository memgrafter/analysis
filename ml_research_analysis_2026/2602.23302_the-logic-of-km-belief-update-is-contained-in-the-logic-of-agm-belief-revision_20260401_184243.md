# The logic of KM belief update is contained in the logic of AGM belief revision

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23302  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23302  
- **Reference count:** 10  
- **Primary result:** The modal logic of KM belief update (L_KM) is syntactically contained in the modal logic of AGM belief revision (L_AGM); every KM axiom is a theorem of AGM.

## Executive Summary
This paper compares two major approaches to belief change: Katsuno and Mendelzon’s belief update (KM) and Alchourrón, Gärdenfors, and Makinson’s belief revision (AGM). Both are translated into a unified modal language with operators for belief (B), conditional supposition (>), and necessity (□). The translation yields two modal logics: L_KM (for KM update) and L_AGM (for AGM revision). The central theorem states that L_KM is contained in L_AGM – every modal axiom corresponding to KM update can be derived within L_AGM. Consequently, AGM revision emerges as a special case of KM update, specifically when new information is not “surprising.” For the stronger variant of KM update (using axiom (K⋄9s)), the only remaining difference is a single axiom handling unsurprising inputs: (A⋄2) in KM versus (A∗4) in AGM. This containment clarifies the logical landscape and opens avenues for studying iterated change and higher-order beliefs in a modal framework.

## Method Summary
The authors work with Kripke-Lewis semantics, where belief is represented by a serial accessibility relation and a selection function f picks the closest states consistent with a given proposition. A belief change operator K⋄ϕ is defined by a Ramsey condition: Bψ holds after update exactly when ψ holds in all f-selected states satisfying ϕ. Set-based axioms for KM and AGM are systematically translated into modal formulas (e.g., (K⋄2) becomes Bϕ → (Bψ ↔ B(ϕ>ψ))), producing L_KM and L_AGM. Containment is proved by deriving each non-shared KM modal axiom (A⋄2, A⋄6w, A⋄7s) from the AGM axioms within the base modal logic L. Key derived rules (RM_B> and RM_¬□¬) enable the derivations. Additionally, frame correspondence results from the literature indicate that the semantic constraints imposed by AGM frames entail those of KM frames, reinforcing the syntactic inclusion.

## Key Results
- **L_KM ⊆ L_AGM:** Every modal axiom that characterizes KM belief update is a theorem of L_AGM.  
- **AGM as a special case:** AGM revision corresponds to KM update when the new information is not initially disbelieved (i.e., “un-surprising”).  
- **Strong update distinction:** For strong KM update, the only difference from AGM is the axiom (A⋄2) versus (A∗4), which governs the inclusion of beliefs when the input is unsurprising.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Syntactic containment via modal axioms**  
- **Claim:** L_KM ⊆ L_AGM because each KM modal axiom can be derived within L_AGM.  
- **Mechanism:** The KM set-based axioms are translated into modal axioms (e.g., (K⋄1)→(A∗2_⋄1)). The paper shows that while some of these are already theorems of the base logic L, the remaining ones – (A⋄2), (A⋄6w), (A⋄7s) – are derivable from the AGM axioms. Notably, (A⋄2) is proved using (A∗4) and (A∗3); (A⋄6w) and (A⋄7s) follow directly from AGM theorems.  
- **Core assumption:** The modal translation is sound and complete relative to Kripke-Lewis semantics; the derivation rules of L are sufficient.  
- **Evidence:** Section 4, Proposition 1 establishes the containment; Lemmas 4–6 give the derivations; Figures 2 and 4 list the modal axioms.  
- **Break condition:** If any KM modal axiom (e.g., (A⋄2)) could not be derived from L_AGM, the claim would fail. This is avoided because AGM’s axioms are strictly stronger.

**Mechanism 2: Semantic frame inclusion**  
- **Claim:** The frame properties characterizing KM axioms are weaker or equal to those of AGM; every KM frame property is entailed by the AGM frame properties.  
- **Mechanism:** Each modal axiom corresponds to a property of Kripke-Lewis frames (Figure 2). For AGM, the corresponding frame properties (from the literature) are stronger. For example, (P∗4) (for AGM’s (A∗4)) implies (P⋄2) (for KM’s (A⋄2)). Hence any frame satisfying the AGM conditions automatically satisfies all KM conditions, ensuring that L_AGM’s frame class is contained in that of L_KM.  
- **Core assumption:** Standard frame correspondence results hold: a modal axiom is valid on a frame class iff the frame satisfies its property.  
- **Evidence:** Lemma 7 shows (P∗4) characterizes (A∗4) and implies (P⋄2); Figure 2 provides KM correspondences; Figure 4 gives AGM correspondences (via reference [4]).  
- **Break condition:** If a KM frame property were not logically implied by the set of AGM frame properties, L_KM would not be semantically captured by L_AGM. The AGM properties are shown to be strong enough.

**Mechanism 3: Strong KM update difference**  
- **Claim:** For strong KM update (axiom (K⋄9s) replacing (K⋄6)/(K⋄7)), the only difference from AGM revision reduces to one axiom handling “un-surprising” information.  
- **Mechanism:** Strong KM update is axiomatized by (K⋄0)–(K⋄5) and (K⋄9s). It is shown that (K⋄9s) is equivalent to AGM’s (K∗8). The remaining gap is between KM’s (A⋄2) and AGM’s (A∗4): (A⋄2) permits revised beliefs to include worlds outside the original belief set B(s) when ϕ is believed, while (A∗4) restricts them to B(s)∩∥ϕ∥ when ¬ϕ ∉ K.  
- **Core assumption:** (K⋄8) is superfluous because its role is already enforced by the Kripke-Lewis semantics; the modal equivalences are valid.  
- **Evidence:** Section 4, Definition 9; Lemma 3 shows (A∗3) is a theorem of L_KM; the paper’s final paragraph explicitly states the reduction.  
- **Break condition:** If (P∗4) did not imply (P⋄2) or if (K⋄8) were not captured by the semantics, the reduction would not hold. The proof avoids this break by deriving the implication.

## Foundational Learning
- **Modal Logic with Conditional Operator (>)**  
  *Why needed:* The entire translation hinges on using > to represent conditional supposition, linking belief change to a unified logical language.  
  *Quick check:* In the Kripke-Lewis semantics, when is ϕ > ψ true at a state s? How does this differ from material implication?  

- **AGM Postulates for Belief Revision**  
  *Why needed:* The containment claim compares KM update to AGM revision; knowing the standard AGM axioms (K∗1)–(K∗8) is prerequisite to seeing which are shared and which differ (e.g., (K∗4) vs (K⋄2)).  
  *Quick check:* What does AGM axiom (K∗4) (inclusion when input is not disbelieved) require that KM axiom (K⋄2) does not?  

- **Kripke-Lewis Semantics (Selection Functions)**  
  *Why needed:* This semantics provides the bridge between set-based belief change functions and modal formulas; understanding f(s,E) as “closest states to s consistent with E” is key.  
  *Quick check:* How does frame property (P⋄2) constrain f(s,E) when B(s) ⊆ E, and what KM axiom does it characterize?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
L (base modal logic) → L_KM (adds KM modal axioms)  
L → L_AGM (adds AGM modal axioms)  
L_AGM ⊢ L_KM (syntactic containment)  
Kripke-Lewis frames ⟨S,B,f⟩ ↔ modal axioms (via Figures 2 & 4)  
Belief change K⋄ϕ defined by (RI) using f and B  

**Critical path:**  
1. Translate KM set-based axioms → frame properties (Figure 1) → modal axioms (Figure 2).  
2. Recall AGM set-based axioms → their modal translation (Figure 4).  
3. For each KM modal axiom, prove it in L_AGM: (A⋄2) via Lemmas 4–6, (A⋄6w) and (A⋄7s) are direct AGM theorems.  
4. For strong update, show (K⋄9s) ≡ (K∗8) and argue the only difference is (A⋄2) vs (A∗4).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Expressivity vs. containment:* Using a modal language with > gives precise translation, but can obscure the original set-based intuitions.  
- *Strong vs. weak update:* Strong KM update aligns more closely with AGM, differing only on the axiom for unsurprising information; the paper treats (K⋄8) as superfluous to simplify the comparison.  
- *Frame constraints:* The semantics imposes minimal conditions (serial B, non‑empty f). Adding stricter constraints (e.g., Weak Centering) would collapse the distinctions the paper wants to isolate.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Semantic mismatch:* An AGM‑compliant frame that violates a KM frame property would contradict the claim that AGM frames entail KM properties.  
- *Derivation gap:* If any KM modal axiom (e.g., (A⋄2)) cannot be proved from L_AGM axioms, syntactic containment fails.  
- *Misinterpreting (K⋄8):* If (K⋄8) is not actually captured by the semantics, the strong update comparison may be incomplete.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Modal axiom derivation:** Encode L_AGM (base + AGM axioms) in a theorem prover (e.g., Isabelle/HOL) and attempt to derive (A⋄2). Success confirms syntactic containment; failure reveals hidden assumptions.  
2. **Frame property implication test:** Enumerate all Kripke-Lewis frames (|S| ≤ 3) satisfying the AGM frame conditions (from the literature). Verify that each also satisfies the KM property (P⋄2). Any counterexample would refute the semantic containment claim.  
3. **Strong update distinction model:** Build a frame where (P⋄2) holds but (P∗4) fails. Construct a model on this frame that satisfies all strong KM axioms but falsifies AGM’s (A∗4). This concretely exhibits that L_KM (strong) is strictly weaker than L_AGM.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Iterated change and introspection:** How can the modal framework be extended to capture iterated belief revision and update? What are the logical properties of introspection for suppositional beliefs (e.g., B(ϕ > ψ) → B B(ϕ > ψ)) in iterated settings? The current axioms only handle single‑step change with shallow nesting; higher‑order nestings like B(ϕ > (ψ > χ)) remain unexplored.  
- **Complex nestings of > and B:** What modal axioms govern formulas such as B(ϕ > (ψ > χ)) or B(ϕ > Bψ)? These operators, mixing belief and conditionals in the consequent, are beyond the restricted axioms studied here and may be key to characterizing iterated change.  
- **Inconsistent initial belief sets:** Can the results be extended to inconsistent belief states (as considered by Katsuno and Mendelzon)? The representation K_{s,M} = {ϕ | B(s) ⊆ ∥ϕ∥} relies on the seriality of B to ensure consistency; allowing inconsistency likely requires a revised semantics or additional axioms.

## Limitations
- The derivations of the derived rules (RM_B>) and (RM_¬□¬) are omitted; their provability from the base logic L is assumed but not shown.  
- The exact set of AGM frame conditions is not provided (only referenced), making independent verification of the semantic containment difficult.  
- The claim that axiom (K⋄8) is superfluous in strong update because its role is “captured by the semantics” lacks a formal justification.

## Confidence
- **Syntactic containment (L_KM ⊆ L_AGM):** High – explicit derivations for the non‑shared axioms are given.  
- **Derivation of (A⋄2) from L_AGM:** Medium – depends on the unproven derived rules (RM_B> and RM_¬□¬) and the availability of B(ϕ∨¬ϕ).  
- **Semantic containment (AGM frame conditions imply KM frame conditions):** High – the frame correspondence approach is standard, though the missing list of AGM conditions slightly weakens the evidence.  
- **Strong KM update difference reduces to one axiom:** Medium – rests on the redundancy of (K⋄8) and the equivalence of (P∗4) → (P⋄2); both points require further support.
