# Tempora: Characterising the Time-Contingent Utility of Online Test-Time Adaptation
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06136
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06136
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: Tempora framework reveals that offline rankings of TTA methods do not predict time-contingent performance, with ETA underperforming in 41.2% of temporal evaluations.

## Executive Summary
Tempora introduces a systematic evaluation framework for test-time adaptation (TTA) under temporal constraints, defining three utility metrics: discrete utility for hard deadlines, continuous utility for latency-decaying value, and amortised utility for budget-constrained deployments. Evaluating seven TTA methods on ImageNet-C across 240 scenarios shows that offline rankings fail to predict temporal performance; the offline best method, ETA, underperforms in 41.2% of evaluations. The highest-utility method varies with corruption and temporal pressure, and decompositions expose failure modes: availability collapse (gradient-based methods skip batches), responsiveness penalties (late predictions discounted), and unproductive overhead (budget consumed without lasting gains).

## Method Summary
Tempora evaluates online test-time adaptation by simulating data streams with temporal constraints and measuring time-contingent utility. The framework defines three metrics: (1) discrete utility multiplies the fraction of batches served before hard deadlines by their mean accuracy; (2) continuous utility discounts prediction accuracy by a hyperbolic latency penalty; (3) amortised utility weights adapted accuracy against frozen accuracy under a cumulative overhead budget. Experiments use ImageNet-C with ResNet-50, measuring per-batch adaptation overhead and accuracy. Seven TTA methods (AdaBN, LAME, NEO, Tent, ETA, SHOT-IM, SAR) are compared against standard inference. The evaluation protocol varies parameters like utilization ρ, HCI threshold T, and budget B across 15 corruptions × 5 severities to expose rank instability.

## Key Results
- Offline rankings do not predict time-contingent performance; ETA (offline best) underperforms in 41.2% of temporal evaluations.
- The highest-utility method depends on corruption type and temporal pressure (utilization, latency threshold, or budget).
- Failure modes: availability collapse (gradient-based methods skip many batches), responsiveness penalties (late predictions heavily discounted), and unproductive overhead (budget exhausted without lasting accuracy gains).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Discrete Utility for Hard Deadlines
- **Claim:** Discrete utility quantifies the accuracy-latency trade-off when batches arrive asynchronously with fixed intervals and missed batches are lost, by multiplying availability (fraction of batches served) by mean served accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** The protocol uses a buffer to simulate a single-model pipeline. Batches arrive every γ ms; if processing time δi exceeds γ, subsequent batches are evicted and lost. Utility U_discrete = α × ¯a_served, where α = |P|/N (availability) and ¯a_served is mean accuracy on served batches.
- **Core assumption:** The system cannot serve a batch after its deadline; temporal pressure creates sample starvation, not delayed value.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "discrete utility for asynchronous streams with hard deadlines"
  - [section 3.1]: Formal definition in Equation 1 and protocol description with buffer.
  - [corpus]: Weak; neighbor papers focus on general TTA, not discrete temporal evaluation.
- **Break condition:** If adaptation overhead δi never exceeds γ (i.e., α=100%), discrete utility reduces to offline accuracy; the mechanism collapses to conventional evaluation.

### Mechanism 2: Continuous Utility for Latency Decay
- **Claim:** Continuous utility models interactive settings where late predictions incur a hyperbolic penalty on accuracy, reflecting user experience degradation rather than binary failure.
- **Mechanism:** The stream is greedy: next batch arrives only after current prediction is emitted. Utility U_continuous = mean(ai × κi), with κi = (1 + di/(T−λ))^{-1}, where di is effective response delay beyond baseline λ, and T is an HCI threshold. This penalises late predictions multiplicatively.
- **Core assumption:** Prediction value decays continuously with latency; there is no hard deadline, only diminishing returns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "continuous utility for interactive settings where value decays with latency"
  - [section 3.2]: Derivation of κi, decomposition into responsiveness ¯κ and Cov(a,κ).
  - [corpus]: Weak; neighbor papers do not address continuous latency-discounted utility.
- **Break condition:** If T → ∞ (no penalty) or all di = 0 (no overhead), continuous utility equals offline accuracy; the decay factor κi becomes unity.

### Mechanism 3: Amortised Utility for Budget Constraints
- **Claim:** Amortised utility evaluates TTA under a fixed cumulative overhead budget B, where adaptation proceeds until budget exhaustion, then freezes; utility is a weighted average of adapted-phase and frozen-phase accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** Let ci = δi − λ be per-batch overhead. Adaptation runs until batch m where Σ_{i=1}^m ci ≤ B. For i ≤ m, updates occur; for i > m, inference is frozen at θ_m. Utility U_amortised = β·¯a_adapt + (1−β)·¯a_frozen, with β = m/N.
- **Core assumption:** Total computational resource (e.g., battery, compute quota) is constrained, but per-prediction latency is acceptable as long as the overall budget is respected.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "amortised utility for budget-constrained deployments"
  - [section 3.3]: Formal definition and explanation of β cutoff.
  - [corpus]: Weak; neighbors discuss continual adaptation but not budget-constrained amortisation.
- **Break condition:** If B → ∞, all batches adapt (β=1) and utility equals ¯a_adapt; if B=0, no adaptation occurs (β=0) and utility equals standard inference accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Test-Time Adaptation (TTA)
- **Why needed here:** Tempora evaluates TTA methods; understanding that TTA updates models during inference using only unlabelled test data (Fully TTA) is foundational. The paper contrasts "offline" (unbounded time) with "online" (sequential commitment) settings.
- **Quick check question:** What is the key constraint that distinguishes Fully TTA from other adaptation approaches?

### Concept: Time-Contingent Utility
- **Why needed here:** The core innovation of Tempora is conditioning accuracy on latency. This requires understanding that utility is a functional of both correctness and timeliness, not mean accuracy alone.
- **Quick check question:** How does continuous utility differ from discrete utility in handling late predictions?

### Concept: Latency-Accuracy Trade-off
- **Why needed here:** All three metrics formalise this trade-off. Awareness that adaptation overhead (δi) consumes time that could cause missed batches (discrete), discounted value (continuous), or budget exhaustion (amortised) is essential.
- **Quick check question:** Why might a method with higher offline accuracy underperform under temporal pressure?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Data stream -> Processing (single-model pipeline) -> Timing engine -> Utility calculator -> Output

**Critical path:**
1. Simulate stream processing according to protocol (discrete/continuous/amortised).
2. Record per-batch accuracy ai and latency δi.
3. Derive scenario-specific factors (α, κi, β) from δi and parameters (γ, T, B).
4. Compute U and decompose into interpretable terms.
5. Aggregate across N batches and corruptions to compare methods.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Choice of metric depends on deployment: discrete (hard real-time), continuous (interactive), amortised (resource-budgeted).
- Buffer size in discrete protocol: larger buffers absorb bursts but increase staleness.
- Metric sensitivity: discrete utility is binary, continuous smooth (requires HCI threshold T), amortised ignores intermediate latency.

**Failure signatures:**
- Availability collapse (discrete): α severely reduced, e.g., SAR at ρ=100%: α=20.6%.
- Responsiveness penalty (continuous): ¯κ very low, e.g., SAR at T=50ms: ¯κ=6.2%.
- Unproductive overhead (amortised): ¯a_frozen ≈ 0% after budget exhaustion (Tent/ETA/SAR at B=1s).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Discrete sweep:** Vary utilization ρ ∈ {100%, 70%, 50%, 35%, 25%} for all 15 corruptions; measure U_discrete and α. Expect gradient-based methods (Tent, ETA, SHOT-IM, SAR) to collapse at ρ=100% but recover as ρ decreases.
2. **Continuous sweep:** Vary HCI threshold T ∈ {50, 100, 200, 400, 1000} ms; measure U_continuous and ¯κ. Expect rank instability to decrease as T increases (penalty relaxes).
3. **Amortised sweep:** Vary budget B ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32} s; measure U_amortised and β. Expect SHOT-IM to dominate at tight B (due to durable frozen accuracy) and ETA to dominate at relaxed B (due to higher ¯a_adapt).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we automatically estimate the difficulty of a corruption without supervision?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Deployable adaptation requires corruption-conditioned compute allocation: ... However, estimating corruption difficulty without supervision remains an open question."
- **Why unresolved:** Current TTA methods use fixed compute budgets across corruptions, ignoring that different corruptions require differing amounts of adaptation to recover accuracy. Without a supervised signal, it is unclear how to measure corruption hardness to dynamically scale adaptation effort.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A method that correlates corruption characteristics (e.g., pixel-level statistics, frequency domain cues) with the amount of adaptation needed, validated on held-out corruptions showing improved utility under temporal pressure.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can Tempora’s discrete protocol be extended to model variable-rate (bursty) data streams?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Modelling variable-rate streams is a derivative temporal scenario that we leave to future work."
- **Why unresolved:** Real-world deployments often exhibit non-uniform inter-arrival times, yet Tempora assumes fixed γ. Bursty arrivals could alter availability and responsiveness, demanding different buffer strategies and utility definitions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A simulation study varying arrival patterns (e.g., Poisson, self-similar) showing how existing TTA methods fail under burstiness, and an extended utility metric that accounts for dynamic queuing delays.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can Tempora be integrated with distributionally realistic test-time adaptation benchmarks that model continual, non-stationary target distributions?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Though out of scope, integrating Tempora with such evaluations could model complex deployments and inform application-specific methods."
- **Why unresolved:** Current Tempora evaluation uses i.i.d. batches within a stationary corruption. Many real deployments have evolving target distributions, which may interact with temporal pressure in ways uncovered by Tempora.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Running Tempora’s temporal scenarios on a continual TTA benchmark (e.g., UniTTA’s Markov streams) and reporting new insights about method selection and failure modes under combined temporal and distributional drift.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How can wrapper-like test-time adaptation methods be incorporated into Tempora’s evaluation framework without combinatorial explosion?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "We also exclude wrapper-like methods ... which would combinatorially expand the evaluation space and are left as future work."
- **Why unresolved:** Wrapper methods (e.g., ensembling, confidence thresholds) can be combined with base TTA methods, potentially altering their time-contingent behaviour. Tempora currently does not evaluate such compositions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A taxonomy of wrapper types and a systematic protocol for sampling combinations, perhaps using Pareto efficiency analysis across wrapper parameters, to identify promising wrapper-TTA pairs without exhaustively testing all.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters for TTA methods are not fully reported, potentially affecting overhead-accuracy trade-offs.
- Timing instrumentation details are lacking, risking inconsistent latency measurements.
- Evaluation is limited to ResNet-50 on ImageNet-C; generalizability to other architectures or data types is unverified.

## Confidence
- Metric definitions (discrete, continuous, amortised): High
- Offline ranking instability: Medium
- Failure mode decompositions: Medium
