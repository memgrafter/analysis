# Cochain Perspectives on Temporal-Difference Signals for Learning Beyond Markov Dynamics
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06939
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06939
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces a topological framework where TD errors are 1-cochains; HFPS minimizes the topological residual to achieve robust learning under non-Markovian dynamics.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning in non-Markovian environments violates the Bellman equation, leading to unstable or suboptimal learning with standard TD methods. This paper reinterprets TD errors through algebraic topology: they form 1-cochains in a Hilbert space over the occupancy measure, and Markovian dynamics correspond to topological exactness (all TD errors are gradients of a global potential). A Hodge decomposition separates the exact (integrable) component from a topological residual. The proposed HodgeFlow Policy Search (HFPS) uses a potential network to approximate the exact component, minimizing the residual during training. Theoretically, this yields stability under non-Markovian noise; empirically, HFPS outperforms DQN, PPO, and variants on continuous control tasks with hidden memory, observation noise, or sticky actions, with the residual magnitude serving as a diagnostic for non-integrability.

## Method Summary
HFPS maintains two networks: a value (or Q-) network \(V_\theta\) (or \(Q_\theta\)) and a potential network \(U_\phi\), both sharing an MLP backbone. For each batch from a replay buffer, compute TD errors \(\delta_i = r_i + \gamma V_\theta(s'_i) - V_\theta(s_i)\) (or Q-learning target) and the differential \(\Delta_i = U_\phi(s'_i) - \gamma U_\phi(s_i)\). The potential network is updated to minimize the mean squared loss \(\hat{L}_{\text{topo}}(\theta,\phi) = \frac{1}{B}\sum_i (\delta_i - \Delta_i)^2\), often with an inner loop of \(K\) steps. The integrable TD component is defined as \(\tilde{\delta}_i = \text{clip}(\Delta_i, -\Delta_{\text{max}}, \Delta_{\text{max}})\); the residual is \(\delta^{\text{res}}_i = \delta_i - \tilde{\delta}_i\). The value network is updated via a semi-gradient along a direction that adaptively mixes \(\tilde{\delta}\) and raw \(\delta\) using a weighting \(\lambda_{\text{eff}}\) based on residual magnitude, followed by norm rescaling to preserve update scale. For actor-critic settings, the policy is updated using projected advantages \(\tilde{\delta}_i\). The topological residual \(\|\delta^{\text{res}}\|_{C^1}\) is tracked as a diagnostic.

## Key Results
- HFPS significantly improves final returns and sample efficiency (AUC) on non-Markovian control tasks (LunarLander, Acrobot, Pendulum-discrete, PointMass, BipedalWalker) compared to DQN, PPO, and TD3 baselines.
- The topological residual \(\|\delta^{\text{res}}\|_{C^1}\) reliably distinguishes Markovian vs. non-Markovian regimes and correlates with performance degradation in standard TD methods.
- Theoretical analysis provides stability and sensitivity guarantees for HFPS under non-Markovian perturbations, assuming exact minimization of the topological loss.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core insight is that the Bellman equation’s failure in non-Markovian MDPs manifests as TD errors that are not exact 1-cochains (i.e., not in the image of the differential operator \(d\)). The Hodge decomposition in the Hilbert space \(L^2(\nu_\pi)\) of square-integrable functions over the state-action occupancy measure \(\nu_\pi\) uniquely splits any TD error \(\delta\) into \(\delta = du + \delta^{\text{res}}\), where \(du\) is exact (arising from a global potential \(u\)) and \(\delta^{\text{res}}\) is coexact (orthogonal to all exact forms). The coexact part captures topological obstructions—cycles in the state-transition structure that cannot be expressed as potential differences—and persists even under optimal policies. HFPS explicitly learns a potential \(U_\phi\) to capture the exact component \(du\). By minimizing \(\|\delta - dU_\phi\|_{\nu_\pi}\), it forces the residual to be as small as possible given the network capacity, thereby extracting the most integrable signal from the TD errors. The adaptive weighting ensures updates remain stable even when a residual remains, preventing divergence that plagues pure TD methods when Bellman equations are inconsistent.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hilbert space \(L^2(\nu_\pi)\)**: Square-integrable functions over occupancy measure \(\nu_\pi\) with inner product \(\langle f,g\rangle_{\nu_\pi} = \int f g \, d\nu_\pi\). *Why needed*: Provides a geometric setting for orthogonal decomposition of TD errors. *Quick check*: Verify \(\langle f,g\rangle\) is well-defined for bounded TD errors and \(\nu_\pi\) normalized.
- **Differential operator \(d\)**: Maps 0-cochains (potentials \(u\)) to 1-cochains via \(d u(s,a) = u(s') - \gamma u(s)\). *Why needed*: Encodes the Bellman operator’s action; exactness means \(\delta = d u\) for some \(u\). *Quick check*: For a known value function \(V^*\) in a Markovian MDP, compute \(d V^*\) and confirm it equals TD error under greedy policy.
- **Codifferential \(d^*\)**: Adjoint of \(d\) under \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\nu_\pi}\); maps 1-cochains to 0-cochains. *Why needed*: Defines the coexact subspace \(\ker d^*\) orthogonal to \(\operatorname{im} d\). *Quick check*: In a finite MDP, compute \(d^*\) via solving a linear system and verify \(\langle d u, \delta \rangle = \langle u, d^* \delta\rangle\).
- **Hodge decomposition**: Any 1-cochain \(\delta = d u + \delta^{\text{res}}\) with \(\delta^{\text{res}} \in \ker d^*\). *Why needed*: Separates Bellman-consistent from inconsistent components. *Quick check*: In a tabular MDP, compute \(d\) and \(d^*\); project a random \(\delta\) onto \(\operatorname{im} d\) and \(\ker d^*\) to verify orthogonality.
- **Occupancy measure \(\nu_\pi(s,a)\)**: State-action visitation frequency under policy \(\pi\). *Why needed*: Weights states by how often they are visited, defining the inner product. *Quick check*: Estimate \(\hat{\nu}_\pi\) from replay buffer counts; ensure \(\sum_{s,a} \hat{\nu}_\pi(s,a) \approx 1\).
- **Topological residual \(\|\delta^{\text{res}}\|_{C^1}\)**: Norm of coexact component in \(L^2(\nu_\pi)\). *Why needed*: Measures Bellman non-integrability; diagnostic for non-Markovianity. *Quick check*: In a synthetic non-Markovian MDP, compute \(\|\delta\|\) before and after approximating \(d u\); the residual should remain nonzero.

## Architecture Onboarding
Replay Buffer -> Sample Batch -> Compute TD error δ and differential Δ (via Uϕ) -> Update potential ϕ by minimizing (δ-Δ)^2 (inner loop K steps) -> Compute integrable TD ˜δ = clip(Δ) and residual δ_res = δ - ˜δ -> Update value θ with semi-gradient in mixed direction (λ_eff weighting) -> (Optional) Update actor policy with projected advantages ˜δ -> Update target networks periodically.

**Critical path**: The potential update must precede the value update in each iteration to compute ˜δ correctly; the adaptive mixing λ_eff is computed from residual statistics after potential update.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Inner-loop steps K: Larger K better approximates the Hodge projection but increases compute per batch. 
- Clipping threshold Δ_max: Prevents extreme potential updates that destabilize value learning, but too small biases the exact component. 
- λ_eff and gating (gate_power): Heuristically scale the residual’s influence; aggressive mixing can reintroduce instability.

**Failure signatures**: 
1. Diverging L_U or L_V: reduce learning rates or increase Δ_max. 
2. Residual remains high in clean MDPs: increase network width, inner-loop K, or use a more expressive backbone. 
3. HFPS underperforms baseline: λ_max too high; try reducing topo_weight.

**Three first experiments**:
1. Hyperparameter ablation over Δ_max ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}, K ∈ {1,5,10}, λ_max ∈ {0.1,0.5,1.0}, gate_power ∈ {1,2} on LunarLander Nonmarkov; report final return and residual trajectory.
2. Tabular ring MDP with known transition matrix: analytically compute exact and residual components; train HFPS with linear features and compare learned potentials/ residuals to ground truth.
3. Extend evaluation to Pommerman (partially observable), MiniGrid-KeyCorridor (memory), and noisy MuJoCo (e.g., HalfCheetah with delayed observations); compare HFPS against R2D2 and SR-DQN.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What classes of non-Markovian dynamics are capturable by the Bellman framework? i.e., when does the TD error lie in the exact subspace \(\operatorname{im}(d)\)?
- How can the Hodge decomposition be efficiently approximated with deep neural networks given that consistency assumptions (function class denseness, i.i.d. sampling from exact occupancy) may not hold?
- Does HFPS maintain theoretical guarantees (stability, convergence) when the policy—and thus the occupancy measure \(\mu_\pi\)—changes during learning, altering the cochain spaces?
- Under what conditions on the MDP and policy is the Hodge Laplacian \(\Delta_0 = d^* d\) invertible on \((\ker \Delta_0)^\perp\), ensuring a unique canonical potential \(u^*\)?
- Can the topological residual \(\|\delta^{\text{res}}\|_{C^1}\) reliably predict Bellman non-integrability and be used adaptively (e.g., gating updates, step sizes) to improve robustness?

## Limitations
- Key hyperparameters (Δ_max, K, λ_max, gate_power) are not specified, hindering exact replication.
- Actor-critic integration for continuous control lacks details (policy update steps, KL penalties).
- Residual-adaptive weighting formula is vaguely described; implementation ambiguity remains.
- Theoretical guarantees assume exact topological loss minimization and linear features, which may not hold for deep networks with finite samples.

## Confidence
- Topological reinterpretation & Hodge decomposition: **High** (rigorous Hilbert-space formulation).
- HFPS stability/sensitivity guarantees: **Medium** (proofs rely on strong assumptions like exact minimization and fixed policy).
- Empirical gains on non-Markovian benchmarks: **Medium** (positive results but limited environments; reproducibility uncertain due to missing details).
