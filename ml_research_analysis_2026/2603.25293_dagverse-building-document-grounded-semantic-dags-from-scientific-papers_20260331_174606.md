# DAGverse: Building Document-Grounded Semantic DAGs from Scientific Papers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.25293
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25293
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: DAGverse-1 dataset of 108 expert-validated semantic causal DAGs; the pipeline achieves 88% DAG classification precision (vs. 72-78% VLMs), 88-93% end-to-end retention, and near-perfect (~100%) evidence alignment.

## Executive Summary
Scientific papers often contain directed acyclic graph (DAG) figures that capture causal knowledge. However, extracting these structures along with their textual evidence into a semantic DAG (SemDAG) format is challenging due to variation in visual presentation and implicit grounding. This paper introduces DAGverse, a framework that leverages such figures as supervision to build document-grounded semantic DAGs. The core is DAGverse-Pipeline, a semi-automatic, modular five-stage system (collection, parsing, classification, annotation, validation) designed for high precision. The pipeline produces DAGverse-1, a dataset of 108 expert-validated SemDAGs with multi-level evidence. Experiments show that DAGverse-Pipeline significantly outperforms zero-shot vision-language models: it achieves 88% DAG classification precision (compared to 72-78% for top VLMs), end-to-end retention of 88% (arXiv) and 93% (bioRxiv), and nearly perfect evidence alignment (~100%). This demonstrates that a staged, precision-optimized pipeline effectively overcomes key bottlenecks in DAG classification and structural reconstruction.

## Method Summary
The Doc2SemDAG task aims to reconstruct a semantic DAG (graph structure plus document-grounded evidence) from a scientific paper. The pipeline begins by collecting candidate papers from arXiv/bioRxiv using keywords related to causality and graphical models. Each PDF is parsed with the Marker OCR system into structured text blocks and extracted figures. A vision-language model (GPT-5.1) classifies each figure as a single, static, clearly directional DAG, rejecting non-DAG figures with high precision. For retained figures, a multimodal model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) receives the figure and parsed text, re-validates the DAG, extracts nodes and edges, and grounds each element (and graph-level context) to specific text block IDs, outputting structured JSON. Finally, a two-stage validation applies human expert review (via a UI allowing limited edits) and a conservative LLM-as-a-Judge for scalability. The entire pipeline is modular and precision-focused, allowing component upgrades while ensuring high-quality outputs.

## Key Results
- **DAGverse-1 dataset**: 108 expert-validated semantic causal DAGs with multi-level evidence.
- **Precision advantage**: Pipeline achieves 88% DAG classification precision, outperforming zero-shot VLMs (72-78%).
- **High end-to-end performance**: 88% (arXiv) and 93% (bioRxiv) retention with near-perfect (~100%) evidence alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Leveraging Explicit DAG Figures as Supervision
- **Claim**: Using scientific papers that already contain a DAG figure provides a natural, high-quality source of ground-truth structure, bypassing the need for experts to manually design graphs from scratch.
- **Mechanism**: The DAG figure externalizes the author's preferred structural abstraction, and the accompanying text provides the semantic grounding. This converts the problem from *ab initio* graph construction into supervised extraction and alignment from a document that already contains both the graph and its justification.
- **Core assumption**: A paper with a well-drawn DAG figure implies the authors have already performed the expert abstraction, and the text sufficiently supports interpreting that specific DAG.
- **Evidence**: Abstract: "We leverage scientific papers containing explicit DAG figures as a natural source of supervision." Section 2.1: "In such papers, the DAG figure provides the structure, the accompanying text often describes its structure, semantics, and application setting."

### Mechanism 2: Staged, Precision-Optimized Pipeline
- **Claim**: Decomposing the task into sequential specialized stages with high-precision filtering early on prevents error propagation and yields a high-quality dataset more reliably than an end-to-end model.
- **Mechanism**: Early classification uses conservative thresholds to reject borderline cases (e.g., flowcharts), ensuring downstream annotation operates on a clean candidate pool. Human validation is reserved for a manageable set for final quality control.
- **Core assumption**: False positives in early stages are more costly than false negatives; precision-first filtering is efficient for building a trusted benchmark.
- **Evidence**: Abstract describes the modular system designed for high precision; Section 3 outlines design principles. Results show classification precision (88%) surpasses VLMs and end-to-end retention remains high (88-93%), confirming the staged approach.

### Mechanism 3: Multimodal Annotation with Textual Grounding
- **Claim**: Providing a multimodal model with the parsed paper text and the figure, and prompting for structured output with explicit evidence citations, successfully aligns graph elements to specific text snippets, achieving near-perfect evidence alignment.
- **Mechanism**: The annotation stage instructs the model to reconstruct the graph from the figure and then search the provided text blocks to find supporting evidence for each node, edge, and graph-level context, outputting evidence as specific block IDs for traceability.
- **Core assumption**: Modern multimodal models can reliably match a given graph element to its supporting explanation once the structure is correctly identified.
- **Evidence**: Section 3 describes the DAG Annotation stage. Section 5.1 reports evidence alignment at 98-100% for GPT-5.1, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and the pipeline, indicating the grounding sub-task is nearly solved.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Paper Collection -> Paper Parsing -> DAG Classification -> DAG Annotation & Enrichment -> Validation

**Critical path**: Paper Collection → Parsing → Classification → Annotation → (Human/LLM) Validation. The classification stage is the primary bottleneck/gate; its precision directly determines downstream workload and final yield.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Precision vs. Recall**: Pipeline is tuned for high precision (accepts ~0.002% of initial papers). Yields a small, high-trust dataset but misses many potential valid cases.
- **Automation vs. Expert Trust**: Heavy reliance on final human validation ensures quality but limits scalability. LLM-as-a-Judge attempts scalability but is too conservative.
- **Modularity vs. Integration**: Stages are plug-and-play, but may miss end-to-end context; a specialized joint model could outperform later but risks error propagation.

**Failure signatures**:
- **High rejection at Classification**: Dominant failure is rejecting "DAG-like negatives" (flowcharts, model diagrams) incorrectly classified as DAGs by VLMs. This is the main error source for baselines.
- **Low retention post-Validation**: Candidates fail due to misaligned node identities, incorrect edge directions, or lack of textual evidence, often traces back to poor figure parsing or ambiguous source figures.
- **Source sensitivity**: Performance may drop if layout/notation conventions differ, but pipeline is robust across arXiv and bioRxiv.

**First 3 experiments**:

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question**: Given a document, can we automatically construct the semantic DAG that a domain expert would draw from it?  
**Basis in paper**: The authors pose this as the central research question in the Introduction.  
**Why unresolved**: The Doc2SemDAG task is complex due to multiple plausible abstractions, implicit structure, and scattered evidence. While DAGverse-Pipeline makes progress, it is not fully automatic and relies on human validation, leaving the core question partially answered.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: A fully automated system that, given a new scientific paper with a DAG figure, reconstructs a semantic DAG with precision comparable to expert annotations across diverse domains.

### Open Question 2
**Question**: How can we systematically determine the "preferred" semantic DAG when a document admits multiple valid structural interpretations?  
**Basis in paper**: Desideratum 2 (Preferred Canonicalization) notes multiple semantic DAGs may be compatible, but one canonical representation is preferred depending on the intended problem; the paper does not provide a method to decide this automatically.  
**Why unresolved**: The current pipeline uses expert validation to select the preferred DAG, but for scaling an automated criterion is needed. The authors acknowledge that abstraction level depends on purpose, but no operational definition is given.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: A study or framework that defines metrics for "preferred" DAGs (e.g., based on parsimony, utility for downstream tasks) and demonstrates consistent agreement with experts across cases.

### Open Question 3
**Question**: Can DAGverse-Pipeline be scaled to process a larger fraction of scientific papers while maintaining its high precision?  
**Basis in paper**: The pipeline is designed for high precision, but retention from raw metadata to validated DAGs is very low (e.g., 0.0021% for arXiv). The conservative design limits coverage.  
**Why unresolved**: The current approach discards many borderline cases to avoid errors. Scaling requires either relaxing precision constraints (risking noise) or improving early-stage classification to capture more true DAGs without increasing false positives.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: An enhanced pipeline that processes a significantly larger candidate pool (e.g., 10x more papers) with minimal drop in precision, perhaps via better classifiers or confidence-based filtering.

### Open Question 4
**Question**: To what extent can the DAGverse methodology be generalized beyond causal DAGs to other graph types and scientific domains?  
**Basis in paper**: The paper focuses on causal DAGs as a case study and notes in Section 8 that extending to other graph classes (e.g., undirected networks, dynamic graphs) and broader domains is future work. The current dataset is skewed toward specific fields.  
**Why unresolved**: Different graph types may have different visual syntax and textual grounding patterns. The pipeline's components are tuned for causal DAGs.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: A replication study applying DAGverse-Pipeline to, say, Bayesian networks or biological pathway diagrams, showing comparable validity and inter-annotator agreement.

### Open Question 5
**Question**: How do advances in foundation models (e.g., multimodal LLMs) impact the performance bottlenecks in DAG reconstruction?  
**Basis in paper**: The pipeline uses specific models (GPT-5.1, Gemini 2.5 Pro), and the authors suggest that better OCR, VLM, and LLM components could improve results. However, the relative importance of each component is not fully analyzed.  
**Why unresolved**: Experiments compare zero-shot VLMs to the pipeline but don't ablate the contribution of each stage or test how newer models affect each stage's accuracy. It is unclear which components are the limiting factors.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic benchmarks where each pipeline stage is replaced with newer models, measuring impact on end-to-end metrics, especially DAG classification precision and structural accuracy.

## Limitations
- Small final dataset (108 DAGs) limits statistical power and domain diversity; may not generalize beyond causal inference papers.
- Use of proprietary models (GPT-5.1, Gemini 2.5 Pro) hinders reproducibility and may inflate performance relative to publicly available alternatives.
- The 100-pair test set for classification and human validation criteria are not publicly disclosed, limiting independent verification and gold standard reliability.
