# Rel-Zero: Harnessing Patch-Pair Invariance for Robust Zero-Watermarking Against AI Editing

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.17531
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17531
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** TPR@0.1% FPR exceeding 95% across diverse AI editing models (e.g., 97.43% on ControlNet-Inpainting, 96.55% on UltraEdit) with perfect fidelity (LPIPS=0.000).

## Executive Summary
Robust digital watermarking for AI-edited images traditionally requires modifying image pixels, risking quality loss. Rel-Zero introduces a zero-watermarking approach that extracts a stable relational signature without altering the host image. It leverages the insight that L2 distances between Vision Transformer patch embeddings remain nearly invariant under generative edits. A trained predictor identifies the most stable patch pairs (the watermark). Verification computes overlap between watermarks of original and suspect images. Rel-Zero achieves >95% true positive rate under stringent 0.1% false positive rate across multiple editing models, maintains perfect perceptual fidelity, and significantly outperforms prior zero-watermarking methods. The approach demonstrates that patch-pair invariance is a robust foundation for AI-edit-resistant watermarking.

## Method Summary
Rel-Zero’s method comprises two phases: training and watermarking. For training, it uses a pre-trained VAE to simulate generative edits: each image and its VAE reconstruction yield ViT patch embeddings; the L2 distances between all patch pairs are computed, and pairs with minimal distance change (top-K) are marked as stable (positive labels). A lightweight MLP predictor Φ takes the concatenated embeddings of a pair and their L2 difference and is trained via binary cross-entropy to classify stable vs. unstable pairs. During watermark generation, the trained Φ scores all patch pairs of an image; the top-K pairs form the zero-watermark (a set of edge indices). Verification applies the same process to a suspect image; the Jaccard overlap between the two watermarks determines authenticity against a calibrated threshold. No pixel modifications occur, guaranteeing zero perceptual impact.

## Key Results
- **Robustness:** TPR@0.1% FPR > 95% across diverse AI editors; e.g., 97.43% on ControlNet-Inpainting, 96.55% on UltraEdit.
- **Fidelity:** Perfect LPIPS = 0.000 (no perceptual distortion).
- **Uniqueness:** Negligible inter-image overlap η < 0.05.
- **Superiority:** Outperforms prior zero-watermarking baselines (TPR < 10%) and competes with embedding-based methods without quality degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*Claim:* Patch-pair relational distances (in feature space) are invariant under generative AI editing, providing a robust zero-watermarking basis.  
*Mechanism:* For an image divided into N patches with feature vectors v₁…v_N, the L2 distance d_ij = ||v_i – v_j|| remains linearly correlated (d_after ≈ α d_before + β, α≈1, β≈0) after editing, even when absolute features change drastically.  
*Core assumption:* Generative edit models preserve high-level relational geometry due to content-preservation losses and the low-dimensional nature of edit operations in latent space.  
*Evidence anchors:*  
- [abstract] discovers "patch-pair relational distances remain invariant" as the key insight.  
- [section 3.1] empirical analysis shows tight distribution of |d_after – d_before| near zero, R² > 0.95, Spearman ρ > 0.98.  
- Corpus: Invariant feature learning for watermarking is active; Rel-Zero’s specific patch-pair invariance is novel.  
*Break condition:* Edits that explicitly randomize relational geometry (e.g., extreme style transfer replacing textures uniformly, adversarial perturbations scrambling pairwise distances) break invariance; deterministic regeneration is most challenging.

### Mechanism 2
*Claim:* A ViT-based predictor can reliably identify stable patch pairs from a single image without needing the original or edited version.  
*Mechanism:* The predictor Φ takes ViT patch embeddings f_i, f_j and their difference ||f_i – f_j|| as input, outputs a stability score p_ij. It is trained on "ground-truth" stable pairs identified by simulating edits with a VAE. The top-K predicted pairs form the zero-watermark.  
*Core assumption:* The relational stability pattern learned on VAEs generalizes to real generative edits because both operate under similar fidelity constraints.  
*Evidence anchors:*  
- [section 4.1] uses VAE reconstructions to define training targets.  
- [section 5.4 (Ablation)] replacing ViT with ResNet-18/50 drops TPR by ~12%, showing ViT’s patch-native architecture and self-attention are critical.  
- "Robust Watermarking Using Generative Priors" (arxiv:2410.18775) uses generative priors for embedding; Rel-Zero’s zero-watermarking with patch pairs is distinct.  
*Break condition:* If ViT’s patch embeddings become unstable under certain edits (e.g., severe semantic distortion), the predictor fails; CNNs are less suitable.

### Mechanism 3
*Claim:* The zero-watermark (Ep, the index set of top-K patch pairs) exhibits high uniqueness across images and survives common distortions because relational geometry is scale-invariant.  
*Mechanism:* The watermark is a set of edges in a fully-connected patch graph. Verification computes Jaccard overlap η between Ep from original and Ep' from suspect. Linear scaling of distances (α factor) does not change the relative ordering of pairwise distances, preserving the top-K set. Small uniform distortions (JPEG, noise) similarly preserve ordering.  
*Core assumption:* Top-K selection is based on relative ranking, robust to monotonic transformations.  
*Evidence anchors:*  
- [section 5.3] inter-image similarity η_a,b negligible (<0.05) across datasets.  
- [section 5.2 & Appendix D] high TPR (>95%) on common distortions (Gaussian noise, scaling, contrast).  
- Neighbors FGPCET and ConZWNet claim uniqueness but fail on generative edits; Rel-Zero’s relational basis addresses that gap.  
*Break condition:* Distortions causing non-monotonic, pairwise-specific changes (selective cropping, geometric warping) could alter the top-K ordering.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Zero-Watermarking**  
  *Why needed here:* The entire method is a zero-watermarking scheme—it extracts a signature from the original image without modifying pixels. Understanding this paradigm explains perfect fidelity (LPIPS=0) and the verification process.  
  *Quick check question:* If a watermarking method does not alter the host image, what are the implications for verification and storage?

- **Concept: Vision Transformer (ViT) Patch Embeddings**  
  *Why needed here:* Rel-Zero uses ViT to extract patch-level features; its non-overlapping tokens map directly to the patch-pair graph, and self-attention captures contextual relations for stability assessment.  
  *Quick check question:* Why might a CNN backbone (e.g., ResNet) be less suitable than ViT for this patch-pair relational task?

- **Concept: Relational Graph Learning**  
  *Why needed here:* The watermark is defined on a fully-connected graph of patches (edges = pairs). The predictor is an edge classifier; understanding graph sparsification (top-K) and edge prediction is key.  
  *Quick check question:* How does selecting the top-K most stable pairs trade off robustness and uniqueness?

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Input Image -> ViT Feature Extractor (ϕ_vit) -> Fully-Connected Pair Construction -> Pair Predictor (Φ, MLP) -> Top-K Selector -> Watermark (Ep). Verification: Suspect Image -> same pipeline -> E'p -> Overlap η -> Decision.

Critical path: Image -> ViT -> all patch pairs -> predictor scores -> top-K indices -> Ep.

Design tradeoffs:
- Patch size (16×16 vs 32×32): Finer patches capture more detail but increase graph size; coarser patches reduce computation but may lose invariants. Paper finds 16×16 optimal.
- Top-K: Larger K increases robustness (more redundancy) but decreases uniqueness (higher collision risk). K=50 saturates performance.
- Predictor capacity: Simple MLP suffices; adding attention (Transformer/GAT) blurs pairwise differences and harms performance.

Failure signatures:
- Low TPR on specific editors (e.g., deterministic regeneration DPM-Solver) indicates relational geometry disruption.
- High inter-image overlap η suggests K too large or predictor not discriminative.
- Sensitivity to rotation (>5°) and severe cropout reveals dependence on patch neighborhood relations.

First 3 experiments:
1. Validate core invariance: For an image, compute all patch-pair RGB distances before/after an edit (e.g., ControlNet-Inpainting). Plot d_after vs d_before; expect α≈1, R²>0.95.
2. Ablate ViT backbone: Replace ViT-B/16 with ResNet-18 (use last-layer feature maps as patches). Retrain predictor. Expect TPR@0.1%FPR on ControlNet-Inpainting to drop from ~97% to ~84%.
3. Sweep K: Train predictor once. Generate watermarks for 1000 images with K={10,30,50,70,100}. Compute average inter-image overlap η and TPR on UltraEdit. Expect TPR increases with K but saturates; η increases with K.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
*Question:* What fundamental factors cause patch-pair relational invariance to break down under deterministic regeneration compared to other generative editing methods?  
*Basis:* Table 1 shows deterministic regeneration yields significantly lower TPR (85.13%) than other edits (e.g., 97.43% for ControlNet-Inpainting). Authors attribute this to large-scale semantic changes but do not analyze mechanisms.  
*Why unresolved:* Understanding degradation is critical for extending robustness to all edit types. Without identifying precise failure modes, improvements may rely on trial-and-error.  
*What evidence would resolve:* A detailed ablation correlating edit characteristics (global vs local, content preservation ratio) with patch-pair distance variance and correlation metrics. Visualizing which patch pairs fail under deterministic regeneration could reveal structural weaknesses.

### Open Question 2
*Question:* To what extent does the VAE-based training surrogate generalize to unseen or future generative editing models beyond those evaluated?  
*Basis:* Training uses VAE reconstruction to simulate generative edits (Section 4.1), justified by PCA similarity between VAE and edit-induced feature shifts (Appendix A). Evaluation covers only a subset of current diffusion-based editors.  
*Why unresolved:* Rel-Zero’s predictor is trained on VAE-mimicked invariances; if new editing paradigms induce different feature-space manipulations, the learned relational signature may not transfer, limiting real-world applicability.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Cross-evaluate a Rel-Zero model trained exclusively on VAE data against a broad suite of editing models (including GAN-based, autoregressive, hybrid). Measure TPR drops and correlate with latent-space distances between VAE and actual edit transformations to quantify surrogate fidelity.

### Open Question 3
*Question:* Are there more effective relational encoding schemes beyond top-K patch-pair selection and MLP-based prediction that could further improve robustness without increasing computational cost?  
*Basis:* The framework uses a simple MLP predictor and selects top-K pairs. Ablations show adding Transformer or GAT layers yields no gain (Table 2), and K=50 is near saturation (Fig. 6a). Authors hypothesize attention blurs relational differences but do not explore alternatives like hierarchical or adaptive pairing.  
*Why unresolved:* The current design might be suboptimal; for instance, leveraging graph neural networks for higher-order relations or content-adaptive pair selection could capture more invariant structures. The negative result for attention may be architecture-specific rather than fundamental.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Evaluate alternative relational learners (e.g., sparse-attention transformers, set-based networks, dynamic pair selection via learned masks) on the same training setup. Compare TPR, efficiency, and robustness across diverse image content to determine if more sophisticated encodings yield consistent gains.

## Limitations
- Patch-pair invariance may break under deterministic or adversarial edits that scramble relational geometry.
- VAE-based training surrogate may not generalize to unseen generative models, introducing distribution gaps.
- Top-K selection sensitivity to non-monotonic transformations (e.g., cropping, rotation) can reduce robustness.

## Confidence
- Patch-pair invariance: High
- ViT predictor generalization: Medium
- Uniqueness & robustness (simple distortions): High
- Uniqueness & robustness (AI edits): Medium
- Reported TPR>95% for evaluated editors: High
- Generalizability to unseen models: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Simulation gap test:** For 500 images, compute ground-truth stable pairs via actual ControlNet edits and compare overlap with predictor’s top-K (trained on VAE). Quantify VAE realism.
2. **Failure mode profiling:** On worst-performing editors (e.g., DPM-Solver), analyze flipped stable pairs using semantic segmentation. Check if failures cluster in specific regions (e.g., faces, textures).
3. **Threshold calibration replication:** With 10k unwatermarked images, fit binomial model per editor to derive τ for FPR=0.1%. Report calibrated τ and achieved FPR; contrast with paper’s unspecified thresholds.
