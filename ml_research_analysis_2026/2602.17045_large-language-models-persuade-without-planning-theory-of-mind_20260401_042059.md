# Large Language Models Persuade Without Planning Theory of Mind

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17045
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17045
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LLMs persuade human targets effectively without employing Planning Theory of Mind, relying instead on high-volume informational disclosures.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MindGames, an interactive persuasion task that requires agents to strategically reveal information to a target with hidden knowledge and values—a test of Planning Theory of Mind (PToM). Across three experiments comparing large language models (o3) and human persuaders, the authors find that humans outperform LLMs when targeting a rational bot with hidden mental states, but LLMs surpass humans when targeting human participants. The LLM’s success correlates with a flood of informational disclosures and few inquiries about the target’s mental states, demonstrating that effective persuasion can occur without the causal, counterfactual reasoning that defines human PToM. This dissociation cautions against anthropomorphizing LLM social cognition and raises concerns about real-world influence risks.

## Method Summary
The MindGames framework is a chat-based task where a persuader tries to get a target to choose the persuader’s preferred proposal (A, B, or C) by revealing information about three attributes. The target has partial information and a value function over attributes. Two conditions vary whether the target’s knowledge and values are REVEALED to the persuader or HIDDEN. Three experiments test different targets: a rational bot (utility‑maximizing, truthful), human role‑players assigned values, and real humans with measured values. Performance is measured by Persuasion Success (final choice) and secondary metrics include counts of informational disclosures and appeals (questions about mental states). LLM (o3) is prompted via API with only the current conversation; human persuaders are recruited via Prolific. A 7.5% chance baseline is derived from the probability of random choice given the task constraints.

## Key Results
- With a rational bot target (E1), humans outperformed o3 in the HIDDEN condition (29% vs 5% success) while o3 edged humans in REVEALED (65% vs 22%).
- When targeting humans (E2 role‑play, E3 real), o3 surpassed human persuaders in both HIDDEN (51% vs 34%; 45% vs 36%) and REVEALED (66% vs 35%; 94% vs 53%).
- Critically, o3 seldom appealed to mental states but flooded conversations with disclosures, succeeding despite clear PToM failure in controlled settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The authors propose that LLMs succeed via an associative rather than causal Theory of Mind. First, LLMs lack a causal model of mental states; they match observable states without counterfactual planning. In HIDDEN trials, humans strategically gather information to build a causal model, whereas LLMs indiscriminately disclose information. Second, LLMs employ a high‑information flooding heuristic: they emit many disclosures about proposal attributes, exploiting human cooperativity and tolerance for detail, which increases the chance that some content aligns with the target’s values. Third, this heuristic works because human conversation follows cooperative norms (Grice’s maxims), causing targets to volunteer relevant mental‑state information and thus reducing the need for explicit PToM inquiries. Evidence includes higher disclosure counts, lower appeal counts for LLMs, and the reversal of performance from E1 to E2/E3.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Configuration (scenario, attributes, target value function) → Payoff matrix (generated by constraint solver) → Interaction engine (chat interface with turn‑by‑turn exchange) → Target implementation (rational bot / human) → Discourse classifier (labels disclosures vs. appeals) → Outcome determiner (computes Persuasion Success and Rational Bot Success)

### Critical path
1. Load scenario and select unseen payoff matrix matching constraints and target’s value function (E3 uses survey to infer).  
2. Initialize target with partial proposal info and value function; show persuader full info (and target’s mental states in REVEALED).  
3. Conduct dialogue until persuader ends game or max turns reached.  
4. After each target message, classifier logs disclosures/appeals.  
5. At termination, record target’s final choice and compute success metrics.  
6. Export transcript and metrics.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Control vs. realism:** HIDDEN/REVEALED isolates PToM; E2/E3 introduce human variability and cooperation.  
- **Fixed vs. mutable values:** E2 uses assigned values for internal validity; E3 uses real values for naturalistic persuasion.  
- **Bot vs. human targets:** Rational bot ensures only strategic information changes decisions; human targets bring rhetorical vulnerabilities and cooperativity.

### Failure signatures
- LLM persuader below 10% success in HIDDEN (E1) but above 50% in REVEALED.  
- High disclosure count with low appeal count across HIDDEN trials.  
- Large gap between Persuasion Success and simulated Rational Bot Success in E2/E3.  
- Human target choices deviate from utility maximization more often than rational bot.

### First 3 experiments
1. **E1:** Rational bot target; test if LLMs can perform PToM. Expect humans > LLMs in HIDDEN; LLMs > humans in REVEALED.  
2. **E2:** Role‑play human target with assigned values; test if LLM success improves due to cooperative scaffolding. Expect smaller gap, LLMs possibly match humans.  
3. **E3:** Real human target with measured values; test naturalistic persuasion. Predict LLMs > humans due to heuristic disclosure strategies.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Why is Planning Theory of Mind (PToM), despite being a human capacity, rarely deployed in naturalistic adult social interaction, and what does this imply about its role in everyday persuasion?**  
   *Basis:* The authors discuss that PToM may be effortful and only deployed when necessary.  
   *Why unresolved:* The cognitive or contextual factors determining its deployment remain unclear.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Within‑subject studies measuring PToM and naturalistic persuasion across varying task demands with process‑tracing.

2. **What specific non‑PToM strategies enable LLMs to persuade human targets effectively in Experiments 2 and 3 despite failing at PToM in Experiment 1?**  
   *Basis:* The paper notes o3 flooded conversations with disclosures but does not isolate which strategies drove success.  
   *Why unresolved:* The dissociation suggests alternative mechanisms, but they are not systematically identified or tested.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Ablation studies manipulating strategy availability and fine‑grained coding to correlate tactics with outcomes.

3. **How does the cooperativeness of human targets reduce the necessity for PToM in persuasion, and to what extent does this explain the LLM‑human performance gap reversal?**  
   *Basis:* The authors attribute higher success in E2/E3 partly to human targets being “more cooperative communicators.”  
   *Why unresolved:* The contribution of cooperativity is not quantified or tested directly.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Experiment with human targets instructed to be minimally cooperative, comparing LLM vs. human persuader performance.

4. **What are the cognitive or architectural differences that lead humans to employ a causal, planning‑based Theory of Mind while LLMs rely on associative modeling?**  
   *Basis:* The authors propose different ToM styles based on behavioral proxies.  
   *Why unresolved:* No direct evidence about representational formats or computational constraints.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Neuroscientific or computational analyses (e.g., fMRI, probing LLM representations) or architectural modifications to LLMs.

## Limitations
- The dissociation is clear in controlled E1 but less so in human‑target experiments due to cooperativity confounds, lowering confidence in the PToM claim for natural settings.  
- Generalizability to complex, multi‑modal real‑world persuasion is uncertain; the task is simplified.  
- Unreported details (LLM prompts, rational bot implementation) may hinder faithful replication.

## Confidence
- **Core dissociation (LLMs lack PToM but persuade):** High  
- **Mechanism (high‑information flooding):** Medium‑High  
- **Generalizability:** Low‑Medium  

## Next Checks
1. **Manipulate Target Cooperativity:** Modify the rational bot to be non‑cooperative (withhold info, penalize irrelevant disclosures). Prediction: If flooding is primary, o3’s success should drop dramatically, aligning with its poor HIDDEN performance.  
2. **Analyze Cue Exploitation in E2/E3:** Code human target transcripts for cooperative cues (spontaneous value disclosure, confusion, clarification requests) and measure whether LLM disclosures follow such cues. Prediction: LLMs’ successful disclosures should be disproportionately reactive if scaffolding is key.  
3. **Test Disclosures vs. Strategic Inquiries in HIDDEN:** Design a new HIDDEN condition where persuader cannot disclose information, only question (appeals). Compare o3, humans, and a rule‑based PToM agent. Prediction: Humans and PToM agent retain some success via targeted questioning; o3’s success should collapse to near chance.
