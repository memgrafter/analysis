# Federated Learning for Privacy-Preserving Medical AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.15901
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15901
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: ALDP achieved 80.4±0.8% accuracy with ε=2000, outperforming fixed-noise DP by 5-7 percentage points; FedProx matched or exceeded centralised accuracy (81.4±3.2% with 3 clients) and improved Alzheimer’s sensitivity from 64% to 74%.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses critical gaps in federated learning for medical AI: unrealistic data partitioning and inflexible privacy-utility trade-offs. It introduces a site-aware partitioning strategy that preserves institutional boundaries to better simulate real-world multi-centre heterogeneity, and an Adaptive Local Differential Privacy (ALDP) mechanism that dynamically adjusts noise per tensor and over training rounds. Evaluated on Alzheimer’s disease classification using ADNI 3D MRI, FedProx with site-aware data matches or surpasses centralised accuracy, while ALDP substantially outperforms fixed-differential privacy, establishing quantitative standards for privacy-preserving collaborative healthcare AI.

## Method Summary
The system uses a 3D CNN classifier on preprocessed ADNI 3T T1-weighted MRI (resampled to 1mm³, MNI-normalised, skull-stripped). Data are partitioned via a greedy algorithm assigning entire institutional sites to 2-4 clients, creating realistic non-IID distributions. Federated training employs FedProx regularisation to mitigate client drift. For privacy, Local Differential Privacy (LDP) adds Gaussian noise to clipped updates; the proposed ALDP enhances this with an exponential temporal privacy budget schedule and per-tensor noise scaling based on parameter standard deviations. Experiments compare FedAvg, FedProx, fixed LDP, and ALDP across client counts, with accuracy, F1, sensitivity, and stability as key metrics.

## Key Results
- FedProx matched or exceeded centralised accuracy (81.4±3.2% with 3 clients) and improved Alzheimer’s sensitivity from 64% to 74%.
- ALDP achieved 80.4±0.8% accuracy with ε=2000, outperforming fixed-noise DP by 5-7 percentage points and demonstrating substantially greater training stability.
- Site-aware partitioning created meaningful heterogeneity that FedProx effectively managed, while fixed DP caused divergence at low ε (≤1000), which ALDP avoided.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Site-Aware Data Partitioning
- **Claim:** Partitioning data by institutional site creates a realistic non-IID distribution that enables meaningful evaluation under multi-institutional heterogeneity.
- **Mechanism:** A greedy load-balancing algorithm assigns entire sites to clients. It counts samples per site, sorts descending, then iteratively assigns each site to the client with the fewest samples so far. This ensures each client's data originates from distinct institutions, simulating real-world federations where hospitals contribute complete local datasets.
- **Core assumption:** Institutional differences (scanners, demographics, protocols) are the primary heterogeneity source; preserving site integrity is necessary to evaluate robustness in real deployments.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.1.2 (Algorithm 1) describe the strategy; corpus papers typically use random client assignment, not site-aware.
- **Break condition:** Sites with extremely small datasets (<100 samples) may leave some clients with insufficient data. The mechanism also assumes sites are meaningfully heterogeneous; identical sites yield less realistic heterogeneity.

### Mechanism 2: Adaptive Local Differential Privacy (ALDP)
- **Claim:** ALDP improves the privacy-utility trade-off by dynamically adjusting temporal privacy budgets and per-tensor noise scales, enabling stable convergence under high-dimensional medical data constraints that break fixed-noise DP.
- **Mechanism:** Two adaptations per training round t: (1) Temporal scheduling: εₜ = ε₀ * (1/α)^(t-1) (exponential growth), clamped between ε_min and ε_max. Larger budgets in later rounds when gradient magnitudes decrease. (2) Per-tensor scaling: Base noise scale σ_base (from εₜ and sensitivity Δ) is scaled per tensor i by clip(std_i / std_mean, 0.1, 1.0), where std_i is the tensor’s standard deviation. This injects less noise into small-variance parameters that may carry critical features.
- **Core assumption:** Early rounds tolerate more noise due to large gradients; different parameter tensors have heterogeneous noise sensitivity, so uniform injection wastes budget or harms utility.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.2.2 (Algorithm 2) provide details; Section 6.2.4 shows 5–7 percentage point improvements over fixed DP; Figures 6.3–6.4 show fixed DP divergence vs. ALDP smooth convergence; corpus lacks per-tensor scaling.
- **Break condition:** Misconfigured ε₀/α risks privacy violation or over-privatisation. Incorrect per-tensor statistics (e.g., unstable early std estimates) could harm valuable parameters.

### Mechanism 3: FedProx for Non-IID Medical Federations
- **Claim:** FedProx’s proximal regularisation mitigates client drift in heterogeneous federations (created by site-aware partitioning), enabling accuracy that matches or exceeds centralised training while improving Alzheimer’s sensitivity.
- **Mechanism:** Each client’s local objective becomes min_w F_k(w) + (μ/2) * ||w - w_t||², where w_t is the global model at round t and μ is a hyperparameter. This penalises local updates that deviate too far from the global model, reducing the impact of outlier distributions.
- **Core assumption:** Institutional non-IID from site-aware partitioning causes divergent local updates; a proximal term constrains this while allowing useful local adaptation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports results; Section 2.3.3 describes FedProx; Table 6.1 shows FedProx outperforms FedAvg and SecAgg+; Section 6.1.2 confusion matrices show improved AD recall (74% vs. 64% centralised); corpus papers use FedAvg or other methods, not FedProx for ADNI.
- **Break condition:** μ too low (<10⁻⁵) fails to prevent drift; μ too high (>0.1) overly constrains local adaptation. Extreme heterogeneity (e.g., one site only MCI data) might still overwhelm FedProx.

## Foundational Learning

### Differential Privacy
- **Concept:** A formal framework guaranteeing that inclusion/exclusion of a single individual’s data does not significantly change output distribution, expressed as (ε,δ)-DP where ε is the privacy budget and δ a failure probability.
- **Why needed here:** The system implements local DP via ALDP for provable privacy on ADNI data. Understanding DP is essential to interpret privacy-utility trade-offs in Section 6.2 and ALDP design.
- **Quick check:** What is the effect of increasing ε on the privacy guarantee and the amount of noise added?

### Non-IID Data in Federated Learning
- **Concept:** When data distributions differ across clients (non-Independent and Identically Distributed), standard federated averaging (FedAvg) can suffer from client drift—local updates move in directions misaligned with the global optimum.
- **Why needed here:** Site-aware partitioning intentionally creates non-IID conditions mimicking real medical federations. FedProx addresses this. Performance differences between FedAvg and FedProx in Table 6.1 directly reflect non-IID challenges.
- **Quick check:** How does non-IID data cause slower convergence or lower accuracy in FedAvg compared to centralised training?

### 3D Convolutional Neural Networks for Medical Imaging
- **Concept:** CNNs with 3D kernels process volumetric data (like 3D MRI) while preserving spatial relationships in all three dimensions.
- **Why needed here:** The classifier is a 3D CNN (Table 5.3). Understanding its architecture (pooling, channel expansion) explains its capacity to extract features from brain volumes. Replacement with GroupNorm (for DP compatibility) is a key design decision.
- **Quick check:** Why are 2D CNNs generally insufficient for Alzheimer’s classification from 3D MRI, and what architectural choices compensate for increased computational cost?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Central Server (Flower ServerApp) initialises global model and aggregates updates. Clients (ClientApp per institution) load site-specific ADNI data, run the 3D CNN (with GroupNorm if DP), apply ALDP noise if enabled, train locally, send updates. Data Pipeline uses MONAI transforms (DICOM→NIfTI, resampling, MNI normalization, skull stripping, augmentation). Privacy Module implements ALDP (Algorithm 2). Security Layer handles optional SecAgg+ with quantisation. Tracking integrates Weights & Biases. Flow: Server → Clients → (Data → Training → Privacy/Security) → Server.

### Critical path
One training round: (1) Server initialises global 3D CNN weights. (2) Server selects all clients (client_fraction=1.0) and sends current global model. (3) Each client: loads local batches, computes gradients, clips l2_norm=1.0, applies DP/ALDP noise or SecAgg+ masking/quantisation, sends update. (4) Server aggregates via chosen strategy (FedAvg/FedProx) and updates global model. (5) Repeat for 100 rounds. Breakpoints: server timeout and min_fit_clients handling.

### Design tradeoffs
- Privacy vs. Utility: ALDP adds complexity (per-tensor stats) but recovers utility lost by fixed DP (Table 6.2: ε=2000 ALDP matches centralised, fixed DP lags 5 points).
- Communication vs. Security: SecAgg+ quantisation reduces bandwidth; in single-GPU simulation it was 7% faster than centralised due to smaller payloads, but network may bottleneck in distributed settings.
- Heterogeneity vs. Simplicity: Site-aware partitioning increases realism but harms reproducibility; random IID is simpler but less generalisable.
- FedProx μ-tuning: Requires careful search (Table A.2); too small: drift; too large: underfitting.

### Failure signatures
- Training loss diverges (Figure 6.3 for ε=100/500): likely fixed DP with excessive noise or misconfigured ALDP ε₀/α. Check privacy settings and noise scale equations.
- High variance across runs (e.g., FedProx 4-client μ=0.1 in Table A.2): indicates instability from poor μ or extreme heterogeneity. Tune μ smaller.
- AD sensitivity ~50% while accuracy acceptable: model biased toward CN class. Check class weighting and confusion matrix (Section 6.1.2).
- Per-round time increases linearly with client count: aggregated payload too large. Consider gradient compression.
- W&B logs missing: verify API key/project settings and network connectivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we formally quantify the cumulative privacy loss of ALDP over multiple training rounds using Privacy Loss Distribution or Rényi Differential Privacy?
- **Basis:** Section 7.1 notes privacy guarantees for ALDP remain incompletely characterised; Section 7.3 calls for formal privacy analysis and composition bounds.
- **Why unresolved:** Adaptive temporal scheduling may weaken overall privacy; without rigorous accounting, total (ε,δ) is unknown, limiting compliance and trust.
- **Needed evidence:** Derive formal privacy accounting theorem for ALDP; validate with empirical privacy auditing (e.g., membership inference) across rounds; compare cumulative loss against fixed-DP baselines.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How robust is the framework against membership inference and model inversion attacks in realistic medical imaging scenarios?
- **Basis:** Section 7.1 notes absence of privacy attack analysis; Section 7.3 identifies adversarial resilience as critical.
- **Why unresolved:** DP’s effectiveness in high-dimensional medical imaging under adaptive mechanisms is untested; adversarial resilience is essential for healthcare deployment.
- **Needed evidence:** Implement membership inference and model inversion attacks on ALDP and competing methods; measure attack success rates across privacy budgets; correlate with formal privacy audits.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does performance and fairness degrade when client datasets exhibit realistic demographic and clinical heterogeneity (varying sample sizes, class imbalances, protocols)?
- **Basis:** Section 7.1 acknowledges site-aware partitioning used balanced datasets and not real-world imbalances; Section 7.3 recommends exploring heterogeneity.
- **Why unresolved:** Simplified partitioning may overestimate utility; heterogeneity can cause unfairness and reduced collaboration effectiveness.
- **Needed evidence:** Simulate federations with skewed distributions and varied scanners; evaluate global accuracy per demographic group; measure fairness metrics (e.g., equalized odds).

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are comparative advantages and scalability limits of newer FL algorithms (e.g., Scaffold, FedDyn, neuroimaging attention networks) relative to FedAvg/FedProx under communication and privacy constraints?
- **Basis:** Section 7.3 identifies algorithmic expansion and scaling to larger client networks as future work, noting current benchmarking is restricted.
- **Why unresolved:** Advanced algorithms may better handle non-IID and communication, but empirical performance on neuroimaging tasks is unknown; scaling effects uncharacterised.
- **Needed evidence:** Implement Scaffold/FedDyn/attention models within the same framework; compare accuracy, convergence speed, communication efficiency, stability across increasing client counts (2,5,10,20); evaluate under DP.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can interpretable AI techniques (e.g., 3D attention maps, saliency) be integrated into privacy-preserving federated models without increasing privacy vulnerability, and do such explanations improve radiologist trust?
- **Basis:** Section 7.3’s future work on clinical generalisability and interpretability motivates this, noting need for interpretability to promote clinician confidence.
- **Why unresolved:** Current work lacks interpretability; explanations may leak private information under FL/DP; impact on decision-making unknown.
- **Needed evidence:** Develop DP-compatible interpretability modules; evaluate privacy impact of sharing explanations (e.g., membership inference on local vs. global explanations); conduct radiologist user studies on trust and usefulness.

## Limitations
- Exact ADNI site-to-client assignment used in site-aware partitioning is not disclosed, hindering full reproducibility of partitions.
- Cumulative privacy budget for ALDP over 100 rounds is not formally bounded; total (ε,δ) composition remains unknown.
- Generalizability beyond ADNI (other modalities, diseases) is untested; hyperparameter sensitivity under extreme heterogeneity is not fully explored.

## Confidence
- Site-aware partitioning realism: Medium (algorithm described but no quantitative heterogeneity metrics or comparisons to random partitioning).
- ALDP utility improvement: High (strong empirical evidence: 5-7% points gain, stable convergence, consistent outperformance of fixed DP).
- FedProx effectiveness: High (repeatedly matches/exceeds centralised accuracy and improves sensitivity in multi-client trials).
- Overall framework standards: Medium (validated on a single dataset; broader claims need external verification).
