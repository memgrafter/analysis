# Learning from Similarity/Dissimilarity and Pairwise Comparison

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19713  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19713  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A unified risk estimator combining similarity/dissimilarity and pairwise comparison labels yields higher classification accuracy and AUC compared to using either alone under weak pairwise supervision.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses binary classification when only weak pairwise supervision is available: instance labels are absent, but we have similarity/dissimilarity (SD) labels indicating whether two instances share the same class, and pairwise comparison (Pcomp) labels indicating which instance is more likely positive. The authors propose the SD-Pcomp framework with two unbiased risk estimators: a convex combination of existing SD and Pcomp estimators, and a novel unified estimator that explicitly models the generative relationship between SD and Pcomp. Risk correction (e.g., ReLU or absolute value) is introduced to mitigate overfitting from negative empirical risk values. Theoretical analysis confirms consistency, robustness to label noise, and bounded sensitivity to class prior misestimation. Extensive experiments on multiple datasets under varying class priors show that the unified estimator, especially with risk correction, achieves superior accuracy and AUC.

## Method Summary
The task is binary classification with only pairwise weak supervision: each training example is a pair \((x, x')\) accompanied by an SD label \(s\) (same/different class) and a Pcomp ordering (ensuring \(p(y=+1|x) > p(y=+1|x')\)). The core idea is to derive unbiased risk estimators from these pairwise labels. Two estimators are proposed: (i) a convex combination \(\gamma \, \widehat{R}_\text{SD}(g) + (1-\gamma) \, \widehat{R}_\text{PC}(g)\) of separate SD and Pcomp risks, and (ii) a unified estimator \(\widehat{R}_\text{SD-PC}(g)\) that integrates SD and Pcomp through class‑prior‑dependent coefficients. To prevent overfitting when the raw risk becomes negative, a Lipschitz correction (e.g., ReLU or absolute value) is applied to each component term. Training proceeds by generating pairwise data from a controlled training set (adjusted to a desired class prior \(\pi_+\)) using a confidence model to provide ordering, then minimizing the corrected risk with a neural network (MLP or ResNet) via Adam.

## Key Results
- The unified estimator achieves higher test accuracy and AUC than using SD or Pcomp alone, and often outperforms the convex combination.
- Risk correction (ReLU/absolute value) stabilizes training and reduces overfitting caused by negative risk values.
- Theoretical guarantees: the estimators are consistent, robust to independent label noise in SD/Pcomp, and their excess risk includes additive bias terms proportional to noise rates and to the error in class prior estimation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Unified Estimator
- **Claim:** A unified risk estimator integrates SD and Pcomp labels by explicitly modeling their generative relationship to the classification risk.
- **Mechanism:** The classification risk is decomposed into two terms derived from the conditional distributions of similar and dissimilar pairs, while incorporating the Pcomp‑weighted class‑conditional densities. This yields a single risk formula (Eq. 7) that combines contributions from SD and Pcomp with coefficients that depend on the class prior \(\pi_+\).
- **Core assumption:** The joint distribution of instance pairs follows the generative processes for SD (Eqs. 2, 3) and the Pcomp‑induced mixture distributions derived from class priors.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 defines the decomposition; Theorem 3.2 gives the exact coefficients \(\alpha_S^t\), \(\alpha_D^t\); abstract mentions “explicitly models the relationship between SD and Pcomp”.
- **Break condition:** If the assumed relationship between SD (class agreement) and Pcomp (ordering of positive class probabilities) is misspecified—e.g., dependencies not captured by the independence assumptions—the unified risk may become biased or high‑variance.

### Mechanism 2: Risk Correction
- **Claim:** Applying a Lipschitz function (ReLU or absolute value) to component risks mitigates overfitting from negative empirical risk values while preserving consistency.
- **Mechanism:** The raw unified risk estimator can be negative for some classifiers. By applying a Lipschitz correction \(f\) (e.g., \(f(z)=\max(z,0)\) or \(|z|\)) to each term, the corrected risk remains non‑negative and retains an (approximately) unbiased structure under boundedness assumptions.
- **Core assumption:** Positive lower bounds exist on the expected component risks; \(f\) is Lipschitz with constant \(L_f\); classifier outputs are bounded.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes mitigation via ReLU/absolute; Section 3.6 defines corrected risk \(eR_\text{SD-PC}(g)\) and Theorem 3.7 shows estimation error decays.
- **Break condition:** If \(f\) is not Lipschitz or lower bound assumptions fail (component risks arbitrarily close to zero), the introduced bias may dominate and harm generalization.

### Mechanism 3: Robustness to Noise and Prior Errors
- **Claim:** The framework is robust to label noise in SD/Pcomp and to class prior estimation errors, with degradation bounded by noise rates and prior error magnitude.
- **Mechanism:** Under a pairwise corruption model, observed distributions are mixtures of true SD/Pcomp distributions. The risk estimator is adjusted, and Theorem 3.5 gives an excess risk bound with additive bias terms \(\propto \rho_S, \rho_D, \rho_C\) (noise rates). For prior errors, Theorem 3.6 shows bias scaling linearly with \(|\hat{\pi}_+ - \pi_+|\), which inflates when \(\pi_+ \approx 0.5\).
- **Core assumption:** Label noise is independent of instances and constant across pairs; class prior estimation error is bounded.
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorems 3.5 and 3.6 provide the formal bounds.
- **Break condition:** High noise rates or a class prior near 0.5 cause the bias terms to become large, leading to significant performance loss.

## Foundational Learning

#### Unbiased Risk Estimation from Pairwise Supervision
- **Why needed:** Instance labels are unavailable; the classifier must be trained using only relational information (SD, Pcomp). An unbiased risk estimator ensures the expected empirical risk equals the true classification risk.
- **Quick check:** How does the risk expression in Eq. (7) ensure unbiasedness given only pairs \((x, x', s)\) and Pcomp info?

#### Class Prior Reconstruction from Pairwise Statistics
- **Why needed:** The risk formulas require the class prior \(\pi_+\) (e.g., in coefficients). When \(\pi_+\) is unknown, it must be estimated from pairwise data. The relation \(\pi_S - \pi_D = (2\pi_+ - 1)^2\) enables recovery.
- **Quick check:** Why does this relationship allow estimating \(\pi_+\) from proportions of similar/dissimilar pairs?

#### Uniform Convergence via Rademacher Complexity
- **Why needed:** To prove consistency (Theorem 3.3), the paper bounds the estimation error of the empirical risk minimizer. Rademacher complexity quantifies the capacity of the classifier class \(\mathcal{G}\).
- **Quick check:** What assumptions on \(\mathcal{G}\) (bounded outputs, Lipschitz loss) are necessary for the \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n_S} + 1/\sqrt{n_D})\) rate?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
- Input pipeline: Generates pairwise dataset \(\mathcal{D}_{\text{SD-PC}} = \{(x_i, x'_i), s_i \mid p(y_i=+1|x_i) > p(y'_i=+1|x'_i)\}\). SD label \(s_i\) indicates class agreement; ordering encodes Pcomp.  
- Risk computation module: Implements either the convex combination (Eq. 6) or the unified estimator (Eq. 8). Computes \(\widehat{R}_\text{SD}(g)\) and \(\widehat{R}_\text{PC}(g)\) using batch statistics over similar/dissimilar pairs.  
- Risk correction wrapper: Optionally applies \(f\) (ReLU/abs) to each component term in \(\widehat{R}_\text{SD}\), \(\widehat{R}_\text{PC}\) before summation.  
- Optimization loop: Minimizes the corrected risk \(eR_\text{SD-PC}(g)\) via gradient descent on model parameters (e.g., neural network weights). Class prior \(\pi_+\) is either fixed or estimated from \(\pi_S\) (Eq. 13).

**Critical path**  
Pair generation → risk forward pass → (optional correction) → loss → backpropagation. The unified estimator’s coefficients \((\alpha_S^t, \alpha_D^t)\) depend on \(\pi_+\), so prior estimation/assignment must precede risk computation.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Convex vs. unified: The convex combination (Eq. 6) is simpler and may reduce variance by averaging, but the unified estimator (Eq. 8) explicitly captures the SD‑Pcomp dependency, potentially lowering bias.  
- Risk correction: Applying ReLU/abs stabilizes training when unadjusted risk becomes negative but may introduce a small bias (Theorem 3.7); absolute correction is more aggressive than ReLU.  
- Prior source: Using true \(\pi_+\) (oracle) vs. estimated \(\pi_+\) from \(\pi_S\)—estimation error degrades bounds (Theorem 3.6), especially for balanced classes.

**Failure signatures**  
- Negative risk divergence: Without correction, the optimizer may chase large‑margin solutions that produce negative risk values, leading to poor test performance.  
- High variance on one pair type: If \(n_S \gg n_D\) or vice versa, the term with fewer pairs dominates the estimation error (Theorem 3.3).  
- Prior sensitivity collapse: When \(\pi_+ \approx 0.5\), the denominator \(|\pi_+ - \pi_-|\) becomes small, amplifying the effect of prior misestimation (Theorem 3.6) and making \(\pi_S\) estimation unreliable.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Baseline comparison:** Train SD‑only, Pcomp‑only, convex (\(\gamma \in \{0.2,0.5,0.8\}\)), and unified (with/without correction) on MNIST/Fashion‑MNIST with \(\pi_+ \in \{0.1,0.4,0.7\}\). Compare test accuracy and AUC; expect unified (especially with ReLU) to outperform.  
2. **Noise robustness:** Inject label noise (\(\rho_S, \rho_D, \rho_C \in \{0.1,0.3\}\)) into SD/Pcomp. Measure accuracy drop relative to noiseless case; verify bias terms \(B_1,B_2\) in Theorem 3.5 correlate with performance loss.  
3. **Prior estimation error:** Replace true \(\pi_+\) with inaccurate estimates (e.g., via small‑sample \(\pi_S\)). Compare test accuracy for tuned (oracle) vs. estimated prior; expect degradation matching Theorem 3.6’s bound, worse near \(\pi_+=0.5\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**  
- **Question:** How can the proposed SD‑Pcomp framework be extended to multi‑class classification problems?  
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state in the conclusion that “Extensions to richer relational supervision and multi‑class classification remain open.”  
- **Why unresolved:** The current theory and experiments are limited to binary classification; extending to multi‑class would require new risk estimators and theoretical analysis.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of a multi‑class SD‑Pcomp risk estimator, consistency proofs, and validation on standard multi‑class datasets would demonstrate feasibility.

**Open Question 2**  
- **Question:** What is the most accurate and robust method for estimating the class prior \(\pi_+\) directly from SD‑Pcomp data, given that inaccurate priors can harm performance?  
- **Basis in paper:** Section 3.5 acknowledges the difficulty of obtaining accurate class priors and analyzes the effect of estimation errors, but does not provide a specific estimation procedure or recommendations.  
- **Why unresolved:** While the analysis quantifies the impact of prior misestimation, practical estimation techniques tailored to SD‑Pcomp data are not discussed.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Propose an estimator for \(\pi_+\) within the SD‑Pcomp framework, derive its statistical properties (bias, variance, consistency), and compare its performance empirically against naive estimators.

**Open Question 3**  
- **Question:** Does the proposed SD‑Pcomp approach retain its theoretical robustness guarantees when the weak labels are noisy in practice, and how does it compare empirically to alternative noise‑robust methods?  
- **Basis in paper:** Section 3.4 provides a theoretical analysis under label noise models, but the experimental evaluation uses noise‑free synthetic labels and does not test noisy conditions.  
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical bounds suggest robustness, but empirical validation under varying noise levels and types is lacking; also, practical noise mitigation strategies beyond risk correction are unexplored.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments with artificially injected noise rates (\(\rho_S, \rho_D, \rho_C\)) on multiple datasets, measure accuracy, AUC, and compare against other weakly supervised methods; also evaluate the tightness of the theoretical bounds.

**Open Question 4**  
- **Question:** What explains the observed superior AUC of the unified estimator compared to the convex combination, despite similar classification accuracy? Is the unified estimator capturing complementary information more effectively?  
- **Basis in paper:** In experimental results (Section 4.2), the unified estimator (SD‑Pcomp‑Unbiased) achieves higher AUC than convex combinations, but accuracy differences are not pronounced. The paper does not delve into why this occurs.  
- **Why unresolved:** The theoretical properties of the unified estimator versus the convex combination are similar; the empirical AUC advantage suggests a potential difference in calibration or pairwise relational learning, which is not understood.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze the estimation bias/variance of both estimators, evaluate on metrics like Brier score, examine learned representations or pairwise score distributions, and possibly derive theoretical properties linking AUC to risk estimator structure.

**Open Question 5**  
- **Question:** Can the SD‑Pcomp framework incorporate richer forms of relational supervision beyond binary similarity/dissimilarity and pairwise comparison, such as graded similarity scores or multi‑way comparisons?  
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion mentions “Extensions to richer relational supervision … remain open.” The current work only uses binary SD and ordinal Pcomp.  
- **Why unresolved:** Incorporating more complex relational labels (e.g., similarity as a real number, triple comparisons) would require re‑deriving unbiased risk estimators and might involve more complex generative models.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Define a generalized risk formulation for such relational labels, prove consistency under appropriate assumptions, and validate on datasets with richer annotations (e.g., human similarity judgments).

## Limitations
- Pair generation details (e.g., split between similar/dissimilar pairs \(n_S\) vs \(n_D\), exact method for setting \(\pi_+\) in training data) are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Training of the confidence model and handling of ties in Pcomp ordering are not described.
- Strong assumptions (independent label noise, bounded classifier outputs, known class prior) may not hold in real‑world applications.
- Experimental diagnostics (e.g., empirical \(\pi_S\), \(\pi_D\) variance, sensitivity to sample size) are insufficient to fully assess estimator behavior.

## Confidence
- **Unified estimator:** High confidence – sound theory and consistent empirical gains.  
- **Risk correction:** Medium confidence – theorem holds but empirical bias of correction is unmeasured.  
- **Robustness bounds:** Medium confidence – proved under assumptions that real noise may violate.
