# ItinBench: Benchmarking Planning Across Multiple Cognitive Dimensions with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.19515
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19515
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** LLMs show severe performance trade-offs when integrating verbal (multi-rule preference filtering) and spatial (route optimization) reasoning, with validated plan rates jumping from ~5% to ~66% only when pre-filtered data is provided, and spatial gaps remaining high (7–24%) except when given textual spatial clustering cues.

## Executive Summary
ItinBench is a novel benchmark for evaluating Large Language Models (LLMs) on integrated trip itinerary planning, which requires simultaneous verbal reasoning (filtering businesses against complex user preferences) and spatial reasoning (optimizing travel routes). Built from real Philadelphia Yelp data and human queries, it defines four tasks to isolate these cognitive dimensions. Testing models like GPT-4o and o1 reveals that LLMs struggle significantly when both reasoning types are required concurrently. Performance improvements are observed only when the verbal filtering burden is removed or when spatial relationships are provided as explicit textual cues, suggesting current LLMs rely on semantic shortcuts rather than genuine geometric computation.

## Method Summary
The benchmark constructs a realistic planning scenario using a database of Philadelphia businesses (restaurants, hotels, attractions) with extracted review attributes. Five hundred human-like travel queries, each containing 6-10 preference constraints, are generated. Four tasks are defined by varying two factors: (1) whether the model receives the full business database or a pre-filtered subset matching the query's preferences, and (2) whether spatial clustering information is provided as text. Models generate natural language itineraries, which are parsed into structured lists. Evaluation combines **verbal metrics** (e.g., Validated Rate, which checks adherence to all preferences and data validity) with **spatial metrics** that compare the model's proposed route against an optimal Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP) solution computed via dynamic programming, measuring distance gaps and cluster-hopping inefficiencies.

## Key Results
*   LLMs suffer significant performance **trade-offs** when required to perform both verbal filtering and spatial optimization simultaneously; validated plan rates plummet and spatial distance gaps remain high (7–24%).
*   The **filtering bottleneck** is primary: providing pre-filtered candidate businesses improves validated plan rates dramatically (~5% to ~66%), isolating spatial reasoning as the remaining challenge.
*   Spatial reasoning gains appear **contingent on textual cues**: performance on distance gaps only improves when spatial clusters are described in the prompt, not when route optimization is simply requested, indicating reliance on semantic rather than computational shortcuts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Cognitive Trade-off in Dual-Domain Integration
*   **Claim:** LLMs exhibit significant performance trade-offs when required to simultaneously handle verbal reasoning (multi-rule preference matching) and spatial reasoning (route optimization).
*   **Mechanism:** The benchmark tasks force concurrent execution of two cognitively distinct processes. Verbal reasoning involves parsing, filtering, and matching complex, multi-attribute preferences from a large database. Spatial reasoning requires visualizing and optimizing Euclidean distances between points of interest. The cognitive load of switching between these modalities exceeds the models' integrated processing capacity.
*   **Evidence:** [abstract, section 4.1 & 4.2] Validated plan rates drop sharply (~18% to ~4-5%) when route optimization is requested without pre-filtered data, while spatial metrics (Total-DG) remain high (~20-25%).
*   **Break Condition:** If a model maintains high VR (<5% absolute drop) and low Total-DG (<10%) on the most demanding task (All Data with Route Optimization), the core trade-off claim would be weakened.

### Mechanism 2: Semantic Shortcuts over Genuine Spatial Computation
*   **Claim:** LLMs' apparent success on spatial reasoning tasks is often mediated by semantic/symbolic shortcuts (e.g., using provided cluster labels) rather than genuine geometric computation.
*   **Mechanism:** When spatial relationships are encoded as human-readable textual cues (e.g., "Cluster_4: ..."), LLMs can leverage pattern matching to select group-appropriate items without performing coordinate-based distance calculations, bypassing the need for an internal spatial representation.
*   **Evidence:** [abstract, section 4.2, section 2.1] Total-DG improves significantly only when clustering information is provided in the prompt (Task 3 vs. Task 2). Case studies show correct cluster selection but failure in ordering clusters by actual distance.
*   **Break Condition:** If LLMs achieve low Total-DG (<10%) without any pre-computed spatial clustering information (only raw coordinates), the semantic shortcut explanation would be falsified.

### Mechanism 3: Filtering as the Primary Bottleneck
*   **Claim:** The validated plan rate (VR) is primarily bottlenecked by the LLM's ability to perform detailed, multi-rule filtering from unstructured data, not by its spatial planning ability.
*   **Mechanism:** VR measures successful adherence to all user preferences. The dramatic VR increase from ~5% (Task 2) to ~66% (Task 3) when pre-filtered data is provided isolates the filtering bottleneck. The spatial optimization component adds a secondary, additive difficulty but the dominant failure mode is selecting incorrect items from the full database.
*   **Evidence:** [abstract, section 4.1 & Table 4] Task 2 VR: 0-18%. Task 3 VR: 15-66.7%. The ~50% absolute gain points to filtering as the main hurdle.
*   **Break Condition:** If providing pre-filtered data yields only a minor VR improvement (<10% absolute) while spatial metrics remain poor, the filtering bottleneck claim would be challenged.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Multi-Rule Reasoning / Constraint Satisfaction**
    *   **Why needed:** The benchmark requires matching a user's 6-10 diverse preferences (cuisine, hotel quality, attraction type) against a large database—a conjunctive, multi-attribute filtering problem that overwhelms many LLMs, seen in high MI and OOP rates.
    *   **Quick check:** Given a list of 500 restaurants with attributes (cuisine, flavor_rating, freshness_rating), how would you algorithmically filter for "French restaurants with good flavor AND good freshness"? What makes this hard for a text-generation model?
*   **Spatial Cognition & Route Optimization (TSP)**
    *   **Why needed:** The spatial reasoning dimension requires converting latitude/longitude into a mental model of distances and ordering POIs to minimize travel. This tests non-symbolic, geometric reasoning distinct from text processing.
    *   **Quick check:** If given (lat, long) for 5 points and a hotel, how do you determine the optimal order to visit all points and return to the hotel? What intuitive spatial concept (e.g., "cluster") is key here?
*   **Tool-Use & ReAct Framework**
    *   **Why needed:** Task 4 requires the LLM to iteratively call search functions (AccommodationSearch, BusinessClusterSearch) to gather information before final planning, testing reasoning *about* which external processes are needed.
    *   **Quick check:** In a ReAct loop, what is the difference between a 'Thought' (reasoning) and an 'Action' (tool call)? How does this change the model's problem-solving approach versus direct generation?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Database & Pre-processor -> Query Constructor -> Task Prompt Builder -> LLM Planner -> Extractor -> TSP Evaluator -> Metric Calculator
*   **Critical Path:** Task Prompt -> LLM Generation -> Extraction -> TSP Evaluation -> Metrics. The evaluation loop is compute-heavy but offline; the online generation is a single API call.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Realism vs. Controllability:** Uses real Philadelphia Yelp data for authenticity but limits scale (500 restaurants, 105 hotels) to keep TSP evaluation feasible.
    *   **Cognitive Separation vs. Integration:** Explicitly isolates verbal vs. spatial metrics but forces their integration in Tasks 2-4. The pre-filtered data task (3) isolates spatial reasoning by removing the verbal filtering bottleneck.
    *   **Text-based Spatial Cues vs. Raw Coordinates:** Provides clustering as text to diagnose semantic shortcut use, but this concedes that LLMs cannot natively process raw coordinate lists effectively.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   **High OOP (>30%):** Model hallucinates businesses not in the provided dataset.
    *   **High MI (>20%):** Model fails to specify required itinerary slots.
    *   **High Total-DG (>20%) with Clustering Info:** Model fails at spatial ordering even with explicit cluster labels.
    *   **Low Delivery Rate in Tool Use (<80%):** Model fails to call tools correctly or enters dead loops.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Replicate Table 4 (Core Results):** Run all four tasks with GPT-4o and o1. Verify the VR jump from Task 2 to Task 3 and the Total-DG improvement only with clustering text.
    2.  **Ablation on Clustering Presentation:** For Task 3, vary spatial info delivery: (a) text clusters (original), (b) raw coordinates, (c) no hints. If Total-DG only improves in (a), it supports the semantic shortcut mechanism.
    3.  **Parameter Scaling on Filtered Task:** Run Task 3 across model sizes (Llama 8B, 70B; GPT-4o, o1). If VR scales but Total-DG does not, spatial reasoning remains the harder bottleneck post-filtering.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** Do LLM spatial reasoning gains in ItinBench reflect true spatial cognition or reliance on textual semantic shortcuts (e.g., clustering cues)?
*   **Basis:** Authors explicitly question this in Section 5, noting performance improves only when spatial clustering information is provided in text.
*   **Why unresolved:** The benchmark may conflate semantic processing with spatial computation, as models succeed when relationships are described textually rather than computed from coordinates.
*   **Evidence:** Compare performance with coordinate-only inputs versus textual cluster descriptions; use probing to examine internal representations for spatial invariants.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** What specific failures in multi-rule verbal reasoning cause the ~61% drop in validated plan rates when LLMs filter data themselves versus receiving pre-filtered candidates?
*   **Basis:** Validated plan rate jumps from ~5% to ~66% with pre-filtered data, indicating LLMs struggle to apply multiple constraints from a large pool.
*   **Why unresolved:** The nature of the failure—whether in constraint parsing, integration, or working memory—remains unclear; different constraint types may pose varying difficulties.
*   **Evidence:** Decompose queries by constraint type and measure accuracy; conduct controlled experiments varying constraint complexity and interdependencies.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** Why are hotel and restaurant preferences more error-prone in tool-use tasks, and does this stem from semantic ambiguity or tool-interface design?
*   **Basis:** Error analysis shows frequent mistakes in hotel/restaurant parameters; the paper notes such preferences "appear diffusely across the query."
*   **Why unresolved:** Unclear if ambiguity in terms like "good service" or tool parameter design causes errors, impacting real-world applicability.
*   **Evidence:** Redesign tool parameters for specificity; map natural language preferences to structured attributes via user studies and measure accuracy changes.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** How does urban spatial structure (e.g., grid vs. organic layouts) affect LLM spatial reasoning performance on ItinBench?
*   **Basis:** The benchmark uses only Philadelphia and a small Santa Barbara subset; trends are consistent but geographic diversity is limited.
*   **Why unresolved:** Without varied city layouts, findings may not generalize; spatial reasoning likely depends on environmental factors like street network entropy.
*   **Evidence:** Replicate in cities with differing spatial characteristics (e.g., Manhattan’s grid, Boston’s organic layout); correlate performance metrics with spatial indices.

### Open Question 5
*   **Question:** What new challenges emerge when extending ItinBench to multi-city itineraries with inter-city transportation options?
*   **Basis:** Authors propose this extension in Section 5; the current benchmark is limited to single-city routing.
*   **Why unresolved:** Multi-city planning introduces transportation mode selection, cross-city time budgeting, and preference consistency—untested dimensions that could exacerbate dual-domain trade-offs.
*   **Evidence:** Develop a multi-city version with realistic travel times/costs; evaluate LLMs on holistic itineraries spanning multiple cities.

## Limitations
*   **Prompt/Data Presentation Ambiguity:** The exact format of database injection (full list vs. search results) and extraction parsing logic are underspecified, risking replication drift.
*   **Clustering Implementation Gaps:** K-means parameters, coordinate normalization, and multi-day TSP adaptation details are pseudocoded, requiring careful re-implementation that may deviate.
*   **Model/Hardware Reproducibility:** Minor API settings and potential dataset versioning introduce uncontrolled variance, especially for smaller models.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (Cognitive Trade-off):** High. Strong empirical support from cross-task VR/Total-DG degradation.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Semantic Shortcut):** Medium. Supported by clustering ablation and case studies, but inference-based.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Filtering Bottleneck):** Medium. Clear Task 2→3 VR jump isolates filtering difficulty, but spatial confounds in Task 2 prevent pure isolation.

## Next Checks

2.  **Clustering Presentation Ablation:** For Task 3, systematically vary spatial info delivery: (a) text clusters, (b) raw coordinates only, (c) no spatial hints. A large Total-DG gap only in (b) would falsify Mechanism 2.
