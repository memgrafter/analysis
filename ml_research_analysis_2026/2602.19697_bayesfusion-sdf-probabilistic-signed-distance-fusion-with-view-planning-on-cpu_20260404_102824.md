# BayesFusion-SDF: Probabilistic Signed Distance Fusion with View Planning on CPU

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.19697  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19697  
- **Reference count:** 31  
- **Primary result:** BayesFusion-SDF improves geometric accuracy over TSDF baselines, reducing Chamfer distance to 0.00373 and increasing F-score@20mm to 0.6532 on a controlled scene, and achieves higher completeness (0.2500 vs. 0.2436) on the CO3D dataset.

## Executive Summary
Bayesian volumetric fusion has traditionally relied on heuristically weighted averaging (e.g., TSDF) without explicit uncertainty modeling, while state-of-the-art neural implicit methods require GPUs. BayesFusion-SDF bridges this gap by modeling the signed distance field as a sparse Gaussian random field on the CPU. It uses a coarse TSDF reconstruction to define an adaptive narrow band of voxels, then fuses depth observations via a heteroscedastic Bayesian formulation with a Markovian smoothness prior. The resulting sparse linear system is solved by preconditioned conjugate gradients. Posterior uncertainty is estimated via randomized probes to enable effective next-best-view planning for active sensing. The approach yields improved geometric accuracy over deterministic TSDF and demonstrates practical CPU-based inference with uncertainty-driven view planning. On a controlled scene, the anchored version reduces Chamfer distance from 0.00458 to 0.00373 and increases F-score@20mm from 0.3790 to 0.6532. On CO3D, it achieves a completeness of 0.2500, surpassing the TSDF baseline.

## Method Summary
BayesFusion-SDF treats the signed distance field (SDF) as a Gaussian random field with a sparse precision matrix encoding a smoothness prior. The pipeline begins with a fast, deterministic TSDF fusion to obtain a rough surface estimate. Voxels within a narrow band around this surface are activated for probabilistic refinement. For each depth image, raycasting generates observation samples; each sample’s contribution is weighted by a heteroscedastic variance derived from sensor noise and pose uncertainty. The observations and smoothness prior combine into a joint Gaussian posterior whose maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate is the solution of a sparse linear system $Q\mu = h$, where $Q = Q_0 + A^\top W A$. The system is solved iteratively using preconditioned conjugate gradients on the CPU. Marginal variances $\text{diag}(Q^{-1})$ are approximated via randomized Hutchinson probes, providing per‑voxel uncertainty estimates without full matrix inversion. These uncertainties drive a next‑best‑view planner to select informative new camera poses for active sensing.

## Key Results
- On a controlled scene, anchoring reduces Chamfer distance from 0.00458 to 0.00373 and improves F‑score@20mm from 0.3790 to 0.6532 compared to TSDF.  
- On the CO3D dataset, BayesFusion-SDF achieves a completeness of 0.2500, higher than the TSDF baseline’s 0.2436.  
- Posterior uncertainty estimates enable effective next‑best‑view planning, as shown by improved reconstruction with fewer views.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Heteroscedastic Bayesian Fusion Replaces Heuristic Averaging
- **Claim:** Replacing heuristic TSDF weighted averaging with heteroscedastic Bayesian fusion improves geometric accuracy by incorporating explicit noise models and spatial smoothness priors.  
- **Mechanism:** Sensor observations and a sparse Gaussian Markov Random Field (GMRF) smoothness prior are combined into a joint Gaussian posterior. The Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimate solves a sparse linear system $Q\mu = h$, where $Q = Q_0 + A^\top W A$ fuses data fidelity ($A^\top W A$) with Markovian smoothness ($Q_0$).  
- **Core assumption:** The true SDF is a smooth field; observation noise is independent and heteroscedastic (variance $\sigma_i^2$ per ray sample); the narrow‑band contains the surface.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states “depth observations are combined using a heteroscedastic Bayesian formulation”; Section 3.6 describes the posterior precision matrix $Q$.  
- **Break condition:** If the sparse system $Q$ is ill‑conditioned or too large for CPU memory, PCG may fail to converge to a useful $\mu$, degrading geometry.

### Mechanism 2: Narrow‑Band Activation via Coarse TSDF
- **Claim:** Using a coarse TSDF reconstruction to define an adaptive narrow‑band activation region makes large‑scale Bayesian inference computationally feasible on CPU.  
- **Mechanism:** An initial deterministic TSDF fusion provides a rough surface $\hat{S}_0$. Voxels within a band $|\text{TSDF}(v)| \le \alpha\tau$ around this surface are activated for the probabilistic refinement step, ignoring distant, irrelevant space.  
- **Core assumption:** The initial TSDF surface is a reasonable proxy for the final surface; significant geometric correction occurs only in a narrow band around it.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “a rough TSDF reconstruction is used to create an adaptive narrow‑band domain”; Section 3.3 defines the activation band $B$.  
- **Break condition:** If the initial TSDF is catastrophically biased, the narrow‑band may exclude correct surface regions or include large erroneous ones, harming accuracy.

### Mechanism 3: Randomized Probe‑Based Variance Estimation
- **Claim:** Randomized probe‑based diagonal approximation of the posterior covariance ($\text{diag}(Q^{-1})$) enables efficient uncertainty estimation for active view planning without full matrix inversion.  
- **Mechanism:** Solve $K$ linear systems $Q u^{(k)} = z^{(k)}$ with Rademacher probe vectors $z^{(k)}$. An unbiased estimator $\text{diag}(Q^{-1}) \approx \frac{1}{K} \sum z^{(k)} \odot u^{(k)}$ approximates marginal variances.  
- **Core assumption:** The probe‑based stochastic estimator converges with sufficient $K$; the diagonal of the inverse is a meaningful measure of per‑voxel uncertainty for NBV utility.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes “Randomized diagonal estimators are a quick way to get an idea of posterior uncertainty”; Section 3.7 presents Equation (12) and references Hutchinson’s estimator.  
- **Break condition:** If $K$ is too small, the variance estimate is noisy, leading to suboptimal NBV selections. If $Q$ is extremely large, even $K$ probe solves become computationally prohibitive.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
1. Input Preprocessor: Filters depth, computes per‑pixel $\sigma_i^2$ from sensor/pose models.
2. TSDF Bootstrap: Fast, coarse volumetric fusion (e.g., voxel hashing) to get $\hat{S}_0$.
3. Narrow‑Band Activator: Queries TSDF volume to mark active voxels in band $B$.
4. Sparse Hierarchy Builder: Allocates voxel nodes, defines connectivity stencil, maps global $x$ index.
5. Observation Builder: For each depth pixel, raycasts, samples, computes $a_i$ (trilinear weights) and $y_i$.
6. Linear System Assembler: Constructs sparse $Q_0$ (smoothness + anchors), $A$, $W$, and RHS $h = A^\top W y + b_0$.
7. PCG Solver: Iteratively solves $Q\mu = h$ to get MAP SDF values.
8. Uncertainty Estimator: Runs $K$ randomized probe solves, accumulates $\text{diag}(Q^{-1})$.
9. Surface Extractor: Marching Cubes or Dual Contouring on $\mu$ to get mesh $\hat{S}$.
10. NBV Planner: Computes utility $U(v)$ over candidate viewpoints using surface‑proximal variance.

**Critical path:** 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7 → (8) → 9. Steps 2 and 3 define the computational domain; 6 and 7 are the core inference; 8 is optional for planning.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Narrow‑band width ($\alpha\tau$) vs. completeness: Wider bands capture more potential surface but increase system size and memory. Anchoring ($\lambda_b$) stabilizes but may bias toward TSDF.  
- Probe count $K$ vs. uncertainty quality/planning time: More probes yield smoother, more accurate variance maps but linearly increase solve time.  
- Sparse structure (hash vs. octree) vs. solver access pattern: Hash tables offer flexible scaling but can cause poor memory locality for PCG; octrees have more predictable access but fixed refinement.

**Failure signatures:**  
- PCG fails to converge → Check $Q$ symmetry/positive‑definiteness; inspect conditioning (perhaps $\lambda$ too low or $\sigma_i^2$ too small/large); verify anchor term $\lambda_b$.  
- Mesh has holes/fat surfaces → Narrow‑band may be too narrow; check TSDF bootstrap quality; inspect anchoring weight.  
- NBV selects redundant views → Probe‑based variance estimate may be noisy ($K$ too small) or utility function $U(v)$ may not properly account for occlusions/information gain.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Synthetic 2D Bayesian update validation:** Construct a 1D chain of voxels with known prior precision and observe a single noisy measurement; verify that the computed posterior mean matches the analytical Gaussian posterior for a small chain.  
2. **Anchor term ablation ($\lambda_b$):** Run on the controlled scene with $\lambda_b=0$ vs. paper’s value. Measure change in Chamfer distance and completeness (Tab 2) to confirm anchoring stabilizes accuracy.  
3. **Probe convergence test:** For a fixed scene, increase $K$ (e.g., 10, 50, 200) and monitor variance estimate stability at a voxel near the surface. Plot variance vs. $K$ to find knee point for acceptable utility.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Scalability to large environments:** How can the PCG solver scalability be improved for large environments? The current formulation increases memory and solver time with probabilistic inference; hierarchical or multigrid solvers need investigation.  
- **Automatic hyperparameter selection:** Can hyperparameters (e.g., truncation distance $\alpha$, smoothness weight $\lambda$) be set automatically? Manual tuning is scene‑dependent; a meta‑learning or cross‑validation approach could automate selection.  
- **Integration of learned priors:** How can learned priors be integrated to enhance geometric fidelity while maintaining CPU efficiency? Hand‑designed smoothness may not capture complex details; lightweight neural modules might help.  
- **Extension to dynamic scenes:** Can the framework be extended to handle dynamic scenes with moving objects? The static GMRF assumption violates moving elements; temporal consistency terms (e.g., optical flow) are needed.

## Limitations
- Narrow‑band activation depends on initial TSDF; errors in the coarse reconstruction may exclude true surface voxels or retain erroneous ones, limiting accuracy.  
- The heteroscedastic Gaussian noise model does not account for outliers or spatially correlated errors present in real depth sensors.  
- PCG solver convergence is sensitive to the conditioning of $Q$; on large or poorly initialized scenes the system may become ill‑conditioned, causing failure or excessive iterations.
