# Beyond State-Wise Mirror Descent: Offline Policy Optimization with Parametric Policies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23811
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23811
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: First theoretical guarantees for offline RL with parametric policies via CFA-based updates (LSPU, DRPU), avoiding contextual coupling and achieving vanishing regret under compatibility and sufficient data.

## Executive Summary
Offline reinforcement learning (RL) with continuous or parameterized policies faces a fundamental challenge: prior state-wise mirror descent methods (e.g., PSPI) fail due to contextual coupling—distribution shift between the data and comparator state occupancies when aggregating per-state updates. This paper replaces direct mirror descent with a compatible function approximation (CFA) principle for policy updates. Two novel actor updates are proposed: least-square policy update (LSPU) minimizing squared regression of advantage on policy gradient features, and distributionally robust policy update (DRPU) using a CVaR-like objective that matches advantage conditional means. The regret decomposes into optimization error (O(1/√K)), actor-critic incompatibility bias (ϵ_CFA), and estimation error (scaling with coverage C and sample size N). LSPU achieves vanishing regret when ϵ_CFA=0 and N is large; DRPU unifies offline RL and imitation learning, driving the bias to zero when the offline distribution equals the comparator’s. This work provides the first theoretical guarantees for standalone parametric policies in offline RL over general action spaces.

## Method Summary
The framework operates in an offline RL setting with a fixed dataset D sampled from distribution d_D. A critic oracle O takes a policy π_k and returns a pessimistic Q-function f_k satisfying a pessimism condition and bounded Bellman error under the comparator state-occupancy d_{π_cp}. The policy is parameterized by θ, with smooth log-density (Assumption 5). At each iteration k, the advantage A_k(s,a)=f_k(s,a)-f_k(s,π_k) is estimated on D. The actor update computes a direction v_k in the space of parameters using either:
- LSPU: v_k = argmin_{∥v∥≤V_max} E_{d_D}[(A_k - v^T ∇_θ log π_k)^2] (closed-form linear regression).
- DRPU: v_k = argmin_{∥v∥≤V_max} max_{w∈W} |E_{d_D}[w(A_k - v^T ∇_θ log π_k)]|, where W encodes a bound on density ratios (e.g., W_∞ with constant C). This reduces to a CVaR-type convex problem.
The parameters are updated: θ_{k+1} = θ_k + η v_k (with step size η). After K iterations, the output is a mixture of the iterates. The theory yields a regret decomposition (Lemma 3) and specific bounds for LSPU (Theorem 4) and DRPU (Theorem 5).

## Key Results
- Contextual coupling in state-wise mirror descent is identified and formally shown to cause constant per-step suboptimality (Proposition 2).
- A new regret decomposition (Lemma 3) separates optimization error (O(1/√K)), actor-critic incompatibility bias (ϵ_CFA), and estimation error (O(√(C·dim/N))).
- LSPU attains vanishing regret when the compatible function approximation condition holds (ϵ_CFA=0) and the sample size N is sufficiently large relative to feature dimension (Theorem 4).
- DRPU unifies offline RL and imitation learning: when d_D = d_{π_cp}, DRPU reduces to behavior cloning and provably drives the CFA error to zero (Theorem 5 and related propositions).
- The analysis provides the first theoretical guarantees for offline RL with arbitrary parametric policies over general (continuous) action spaces, without requiring explicit density estimation or state-wise updates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Avoiding Contextual Coupling via CFA
**Claim:** Using CFA-based policy updates instead of state-wise mirror descent avoids the *contextual coupling* failure.
**Mechanism:** Instead of updating a policy independently per state (which couples states through the policy parameters when the policy is parametric), the update aggregates information across states using policy gradient features ϕ_k(s,a)=∇logπ_k(a|s). The target is the advantage A_k(s,a)=f_k(s,a)-f_k(s,π_k). By minimizing a global loss (squared error or DRO) under the data distribution d_D, the resulting direction v_k respects the single policy parameterization. The regret then matches the quadratic decomposition in terms of a linear error under d_{π_cp}, which can be transferred via coverage.
**Core assumptions:** Density coverage ∥d_{π_cp}/d_D∥∞ ≤ C (Assumption 1) and smoothness/Lipschitz of log-policy (Assumption 5).
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 4.1 (Proposition 2) show failure of contextual mirror descent; section 4.2 (Lemma 3) provides the new decomposition.
**Break condition:** If coverage C is large or the linear span of ϕ_k does not cover A_k (ϵ_CFA > 0), the error terms do not vanish.

### Mechanism 2: LSPU Achieves Vanishing Regret under Compatibility
**Claim:** LSPU achieves vanishing regret under actor-critic compatibility (ϵ_CFA=0) and sufficient samples via least-squares regression of advantage on policy gradient features.
**Mechanism:** LSPU computes v_k = argmin_{∥v∥≤V_max} E_{d_D}[(A_k - v^T ϕ_k)^2] (closed-form via Eq. 7). If the regression is realizable (A_k = v_k^*^T ϕ_k), then ϵ_CFA=0. The regret then decomposes into O(1/√K) optimization error, zero bias, and estimation error O(√(C·dim/N)).
**Core assumptions:** ϵ_CFA=0 (Assumption 6), invertible covariance Σ_D and bounded leverage (Assumption 8), enough samples N ≥ feature dimension d.
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5.1 (Eq. 7, Theorem 4); Appendix D.1 (Propositions 8,9) show ϵ_CFA=0 for canonical softmax or compatible linear features.
**Break condition:** Large ϵ_CFA introduces constant bias; ill-conditioned Σ_D requires N >> d for low statistical error.

### Mechanism 3: DRPU Unifies Offline RL and Imitation Learning
**Claim:** DRPU unifies offline RL and imitation learning; when d_D = d_{π_cp} it reduces to behavior cloning and drives the CFA error to zero.
**Mechanism:** DRPU uses distributionally robust optimization: v_k = argmin_{∥v∥≤V_max} max_{w∈W} |E_{d_D}[w(A_k - v^T ϕ_k)]|. With weight class W_∞ (bounded density ratio) or W_χ², the loss equals a CVaR objective (Eq. 9). Under d_D = d_{π_cp}, the weight class W={1} is realizable, and DRPU becomes min_v |E_{d_{π_cp}}[A_k] - v^T E_{d_{π_cp}}[ϕ_k]|, i.e., matching the mean of the advantage. This provably reduces model mis-specification bias (eϵ_CFA → 0 under mild conditions).
**Core assumptions:** Realizability of density ratio in W (Assumption 7); for no-shift, W={1} suffices. Smoothness and boundedness as before.
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5.2 (DRPU algorithm, Theorem 5); unification claim in abstract; Proposition showing eϵ_CFA ≤ √(Cϵ_CFA) (section 5.3) and Theorem 21 for known d_{π_cp}.
**Break condition:** Requires accurate knowledge of coverage constant C to set the DRO bound; mis-specification harms performance if distribution shift is present.

## Foundational Learning
- **Policy Mirror Descent (PMD)**  
  *Why needed:* The paper’s starting point is prior work (PSPI) that uses state-wise PMD; understanding its per-state aggregation reveals the *contextual coupling* limitation when moving to parametric policies.  
  *Quick check:* Why does state-wise PMD avoid distributional mismatch while contextual PMD does not?

- **Compatible Function Approximation (CFA)**  
  *Why needed:* The regret decomposition hinges on representing the advantage via policy gradient features; the CFA error (ϵ_CFA) captures actor-critic misalignment and directly influences the bias term.  
  *Quick check:* What does ϵ_CFA = 0 mean, and why does it matter for offline RL with parametric policies?

- **Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) / CVaR**  
  *Why needed:* DRPU employs DRO to handle the unknown density ratio between d_{π_cp} and d_D without explicit estimation; the CVaR dual form yields an efficiently solvable convex problem.  
  *Quick check:* How does the CVaR representation (Eq. 9) reduce the dependency on the coverage constant C from linear to √C?

- **Coverage and Pessimism**  
  *Why needed:* Assumptions 1 and 2 standardize offline RL theory—coverage bounds the distribution shift; pessimism controls Bellman error. Both are essential for transferring errors and bounding suboptimality.  
  *Quick check:* If ∥d_{π_cp}/d_D∥∞ = 100, how does that affect the regret bounds?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Critic oracle O → (pessimistic f_k); Offline actor dataset D → (samples for advantage estimation); Coverage estimator (optional) → bound C for DRPU; Actor update module (LSPU/DRPU) → v_k; Policy parameter updater → θ_{k+1} = θ_k + η v_k; Mixture policy ← {π_{θ_1}, ..., π_{θ_K}}.

**Critical path:** Initialize θ_1. For k = 1 to K: query oracle O for f_k; compute advantage A_k(s,a)=f_k(s,a)-f_k(s,π_k) on samples from D; compute update v_k via LSPU or DRPU; set θ_{k+1} = θ_k + η v_k. Return the uniform mixture of {π_{θ_1},...,π_{θ_K}}.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *LSPU*: Closed-form linear regression; computationally cheap; requires N ≥ d for low estimation error; sensitive to incompatibility (ϵ_CFA).  
- *DRPU*: Solves a convex program (SOCP/LP); requires knowledge of C (or a bound); more robust to incompatibility (eϵ_CFA ≤ √(Cϵ_CFA)); under no-shift (d_D = d_{π_cp}), bias vanishes and it reduces to behavior cloning.  
- Both require smooth policies (Assumption 5) and degrade if coverage C is large.

**Failure signatures:**
- Large ϵ_CFA / eϵ_CFA → persistent regret plateau.  
- Small N or rank-deficient Σ_D → ill-conditioned regression in LSPU; high variance in DRPU.  
- Violating smoothness (β) → higher-order terms not controlled; Lemma 3 fails.  
- Critic oracle not providing sufficiently bounded Bellman error → critic-side error dominates.  
- For DRPU, mis-specified coverage bound C → suboptimal weight region, increased regret.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Tabular verification**: Run the two-state MDP from Appendix C.1 with known d_{π_cp}. Compare empirical regret of LSPU vs uniform mixture; validate O(1/√K) scaling and confirm PSPI stagnates (constant per-step regret).
2. **Coverage sensitivity**: Vary the data distribution d_D (e.g., change ε in the contextual bandit) to alter C. Measure increase in regret for LSPU and DRPU to confirm the √C dependence in the bounds.
3. **Actor-critic misalignment**: Introduce feature mismatch (e.g., policy network has extra hidden layer not spanned by critic linear features). Compare LSPU’s bias (large ϵ_CFA) vs DRPU’s bias (eϵ_CFA) and verify DRPU’s bias vanishes when d_D = d_{π_cp} (becomes behavior cloning).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can the theoretical guarantees be extended to deterministic or implicit generative policies (e.g., diffusion policies)? The analysis relies on density-based assumptions that such policies do not satisfy; new analytical tools are required.
- **Open Question 2:** Can the algorithms avoid requiring exact knowledge of the coverage constant C? In practice, C is unknown and mis-specification harms performance; an adaptive method is needed.
- **Open Question 3:** Can the framework accommodate non-differentiable or non-smooth policy classes (e.g., ReLU policies, decision trees)? The current decomposition uses gradient smoothness; extending to non-smooth policies would require different proof techniques.
- **Open Question 4:** When policy parameters far exceed dataset size (d >> N), can the sample complexity be improved beyond O(d/N)? The analysis does not account for implicit regularization or low-dimensional structure in overparameterized settings.

## Limitations
- Strong critic oracle assumptions (Assumption 2) and exact knowledge of the coverage constant C are required but difficult to obtain in practice.
- Parametric policy smoothness (β) and gradient bounds (G) are demanded; these may be loose or violated for deep networks.
- Finite-sample guarantees assume N ≥ feature dimension d and a well-conditioned covariance matrix Σ_D, conditions that may not hold for high-dimensional policies.
- The analysis does not address overparameterized regimes where d >> N, leaving sample complexity potentially prohibitive.

## Confidence
- Contextual coupling failure: **High** (well-established in theory and experiments).  
- LSPU vanishing regret under compatibility: **Medium-High** (solid assumptions but requires ϵ_CFA=0 and sufficient data).  
- DRPU unification and bias reduction: **Medium** (theoretically sound but hinges on accurate C).  
- General regret decomposition: **High** (robust across both algorithms).
