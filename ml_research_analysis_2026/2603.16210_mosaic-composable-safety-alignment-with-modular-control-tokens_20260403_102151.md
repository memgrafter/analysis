# MOSAIC: Composable Safety Alignment with Modular Control Tokens

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16210
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16210
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: MOSAIC achieves near-perfect defense success rates (99.8–100%) across 1–4 active constraint orders while substantially lowering over-refusal (1.8–5.9%) compared to SFT (6.1–7.3%) and far surpassing in-context and ORPO baselines.

## Executive Summary
MOSAIC introduces a modular safety alignment framework that decouples safety constraints from the LLM backbone. By representing each constraint as a small set of learnable control tokens, it enables composable, conditional safety control at inference time without retraining the backbone. The method employs order-based combinatorial task sampling to efficiently train token interactions across combinations and a counterfactual knowledge distillation objective to preserve utility on benign queries. Evaluations on a new context-sensitive safety benchmark show near-perfect defense success rates (99.8–100%) for 1–4 active constraints, with over-refusal rates (1.8–5.9%) far below SFT and other baselines. General model utility (MMLU) is maintained, and incremental addition of new categories incurs only minor degradation. This approach offers a flexible, parameter-efficient path to user- and region-specific safety alignment.

## Method Summary
MOSAIC freezes a pretrained, safety-aligned LLM backbone and learns a small set (e.g., 5) of continuous token embeddings for each safety category. At inference, tokens corresponding to active constraints are prepended to the input, conditioning the frozen model to refuse violations of those constraints while remaining helpful otherwise. Training uses order-based combinatorial task sampling: for each step, a subset of categories (order `r`) is chosen, and a fixed number of positive (unsafe for those categories) and negative (benign) examples are drawn. Positive examples are trained with a cross-entropy loss toward a fixed refusal template. Negative examples use a combined loss: standard language modeling plus counterfactual knowledge distillation, which minimizes the KL divergence between the controlled model’s output distribution and the backbone’s original distribution on the same benign input. Only the control token embeddings are updated; the backbone remains static. This design enables dynamic composition of multiple constraints with minimal additional parameters.

## Key Results
- **High defense success**: 99.8–100% DSR across 1–4 active constraint orders on context-sensitive safety requests.
- **Low over-refusal**: 1.8–5.9% OR on benign queries, significantly lower than SFT (6.1–7.3%) and far above in-context and ORPO baselines.
- **Utility preservation**: MMLU scores remain comparable to the unfrozen backbone; incremental category expansion causes only minor performance drops.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

1. **Token-based conditional safety control**  
   Each safety constraint is encoded by a small set of learnable token embeddings (`z_c`). Prepending these for active categories `S` to a frozen backbone model produces refusals for violations while preserving normal behavior. The assumption is that constraints are linearly separable and composable in the embedding space. Evidence: Experiments show high DSR with few tokens per category. Break condition: Token interference dominance, where one token suppresses others, leading to failure on multi-category requests (as seen in ablations without multi-task).

2. **Order-based combinatorial task sampling**  
   Training data are organized by the number of active constraints (order `r`). Each order receives a fixed sample budget, split equally among its `|T_r|` subsets. Cycling through orders ensures balanced exposure to all combination orders, promoting cooperative token interactions without exponential data growth. Assumption: Uniform exposure across orders is sufficient for tokens to learn non-dominant behavior. Evidence: Ablations show that omitting higher orders harms multi-category DSR. Break condition: Severe training imbalance (e.g., neglecting higher orders) causing token dominance.

3. **Counterfactual knowledge distillation (KD)**  
   On benign inputs, the controlled model’s distribution is aligned with the frozen backbone’s original distribution via KL divergence loss, in addition to standard language modeling loss. This fine-grained guidance preserves utility and reduces over-refusal. Assumption: The backbone’s responses to benign inputs are desirable. Evidence: KD reduces OR compared to SFT; a λ sweep indicates an optimal window. Break condition: The backbone itself over-refuses or is unhelpful on benign queries, making it a poor teacher.

## Foundational Learning

- **Soft Prompt Tuning / Control Tokens**  
  Why needed: MOSAIC repurposes the soft prompt paradigm—learning continuous token embeddings that steer model behavior without weight changes—for safety control.  
  Quick check: How does prepending learned vectors to a frozen model differ from fine-tuning its parameters in mechanisms and computational cost?

- **Knowledge Distillation (KD)**  
  Why needed: The counterfactual KD loss is central to maintaining helpfulness on benign queries by matching the backbone’s distribution token-by-token.  
  Quick check: Why is minimizing KL(p_base || p_ctrl) preferred over using a single “safe answer” reference on benign inputs?

- **Combinatorial Explosion in Multi-Task Learning**  
  Why needed: Training tokens for all `2^K - 1` category combinations becomes intractable as `K` grows; order-based sampling caps cost per order.  
  Quick check: With 5 categories, how many pairwise (order‑2) combinations exist, and why would naïve enumeration be problematic for data collection?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (arrows indicate data/control flow):**  
Training Orchestrator → (select order, sample) → Input Composer → Frozen Backbone → Loss Calculator → Optimizer → Control Token Bank (update)  
Control Token Bank → (provide z_c) → Input Composer

**Critical path:**  
1. Initialize token embeddings `z_c` randomly.  
2. Pick training order `r` (1…K) and subset `S` of `r` categories.  
3. Draw positive (unsafe for `S`) and negative (benign) examples.  
4. For each example, prepend `z_S` to input and forward through frozen backbone.  
5. Compute loss: positives → cross-entropy to refusal template; negatives → LM loss + λ·KL(p_base || p_ctrl) (requires a no‑token forward for `p_base`).  
6. Back‑propagate loss and update only `z_c` with Adam.  
7. Cycle orders until per‑order budgets are exhausted.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Token count (`m`)**: More tokens improve granularity but increase memory/compute; diminishing returns beyond 2–5 tokens.  
- **KD weight (`λ`)**: Higher λ lowers over‑refusal but can over‑constrain; optimal range ~0.8–1.2.  
- **Frozen backbone**: Preserves utility and enables modularity but limits expressivity; safety knowledge is confined to token space.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **High OR with high DSR**: Over‑conservative refusals; likely λ too high or insufficient negative supervision.  
- **Low DSR on multi‑order**: Token dominance/interference; likely missing exposure to higher‑order combinations.  
- **Degradation on incremental expansion**: New category tokens interfere with old ones; suggests inadequate mixed‑order training.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Single‑token baseline**: Train one token per category. Expect high DSR but also high OR, highlighting need for more tokens and KD.  
2. **Composition test**: Activate two or more tokens that were trained only on single‑category data (no multi‑task). Expect a sharp DSR drop, validating order‑based sampling.  
3. **KD ablation**: Train with multi‑task sampling but set λ = 0. Expect OR similar to SFT, confirming KD’s role in reducing over‑refusal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Scaling to larger models and many categories**  
   How does MOSAIC’s efficacy and computational overhead change with much larger backbones (>100B parameters) and a large number of safety categories (>50)? The paper only tests on 3B/8B models with 5 categories.

2. **Dependence on backbone alignment**  
   To what extent does the counterfactual KD objective rely on the frozen backbone’s quality on benign queries? If the backbone itself over‑refuses or is unhelpful, the KD teacher may be flawed.

3. **Generalization across heterogeneous constraint types**  
   Does order‑based combinatorial sampling remain effective when categories have uneven data distributions or fundamentally different natures (e.g., legality vs. age‑appropriateness vs. factual integrity)? Uniform per‑order budgets may under‑train rare or complex constraints.

4. **Robustness to adversarial manipulation**  
   How resistant is MOSAIC’s token‑based control to prompt injection or adversarial attacks that try to disable or bypass the safety condition? The paper does not evaluate adaptive threats targeting the token prefix.

## Limitations
- Dataset construction details (prompts, filtering pipeline) are not provided, hindering faithful replication.
- The fixed refusal template used for positive samples is unspecified, which could affect training dynamics and results.
- The framework’s scalability to many categories or large models is unclear; experiments are limited to 5 categories and ≤8B parameters.

## Confidence
- Token composability: **High** (strong multi‑order DSR with small tokens)
- Order‑based combinatorial sampling: **Medium** (ablations show benefit but no efficiency comparison)
- Counterfactual knowledge distillation: **Medium** (reduces OR but depends on backbone quality)

## Next Checks
