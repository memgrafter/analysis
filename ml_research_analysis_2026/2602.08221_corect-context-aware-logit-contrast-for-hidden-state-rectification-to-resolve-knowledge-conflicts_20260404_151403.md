# CoRect: Context-Aware Logit Contrast for Hidden State Rectification to Resolve Knowledge Conflicts

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08221
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08221
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** A training-free inference intervention that mitigates "parametric suppression" in RAG, improving Exact Match on NQ-Swap from 70.54% to 80.15% (Qwen2.5-7B) and AlignScore on CNN-DailyMail from 91.48 to 92.88.

## Executive Summary
CoRect addresses a critical failure mode in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) where large language models ignore retrieved evidence in favor of incorrect parametric memory, a phenomenon termed "parametric suppression." The authors identify that deep feed-forward network (FFN) layers overwrite context-sensitive representations with memorized priors. Their solution is a two-stage, training-free framework: first, it selects a trustworthy target token by contrasting logits from a full-context pass versus a null-context pass and filtering with attention evidence; second, it rectifies hidden states in layers where the FFN output negatively projects onto the target's unembedding vector. Across question-answering and summarization benchmarks, CoRect consistently enhances faithfulness to retrieved evidence without requiring target answers during inference, outperforming decoding-time baselines like CAD and AdaCAD.

## Method Summary
CoRect operates in two stages per generation step without any training. **Stage 1 (Trustworthy Token Selection):** The model performs a full forward pass with the retrieved context and a separate "null-context" pass with only the instruction. For each vocabulary token, it computes a *contextual mutual information* score by aggregating the difference in layer-wise logits (last *k* layers) between the two passes. This highlights tokens whose probability increases due to the context. It then filters the top-*M* candidates using an *attention evidence* score: the average attention in the final layer directed at positions where the candidate token appears in the context. The final target token *t̃** maximizes a weighted sum of these scores. **Stage 2 (Hidden State Rectification):** For the selected *t̃**, the method identifies FFN layers where the output *u<sub>l</sub>* negatively projects onto the target's unembedding vector *w<sub>t̃*</sub>*. In these "suppressive" layers, it computes a patch *φ<sub>l</sub>* that neutralizes this negative component and adds it to the FFN output during the original generation forward pass, thereby disinhibiting the correct answer.

## Key Results
- On the high-conflict **NQ-Swap** benchmark, CoRect raises Exact Match from 70.54% (greedy decoding) to 80.15% with Qwen2.5-7B.
- On **CNN-DailyMail** summarization, AlignScore improves from 91.48 to 92.88.
- CoRect outperforms decoding-time baselines (CAD, AdaCAD) and a direct decoding baseline that forces the selected target token, confirming the efficacy of hidden state rectification over forced decoding.
- Ablations show optimal performance when intervening on the last ~10 layers for QA and ~5 for summarization, with a rectification strength *α*=1 (neutralization) being most effective.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Deep FFN layers cause "parametric suppression," overwriting context-sensitive representations with memorized priors, leading to unfaithful outputs under knowledge conflict.
- **Mechanism:** Using Logit Lens, the authors observe that the correct answer token's rank can be high in intermediate layers but drops in final layers. They attribute this to specific FFN modules (acting as key-value memories) that add a suppressive component (*u<sub>l</sub>*) to the residual stream, negatively projecting onto the correct answer's unembedding vector *w<sub>t*</sub>*. The residual stream is treated as a linear channel where the final logit shift is approximately the sum of projections from each layer's output.
- **Evidence anchors:** Layer-wise analysis shows a "flip" pattern in 282/500 conflict cases where the gold answer's rank drops. The hypothesis is that a negative projection *u<sub>l</sub>ᵀ w<sub>t*</sub>* suppresses the target.
- **Break condition:** If the model's internal gold answer never achieves a high rank in intermediate layers (no "flip"), the suppression hypothesis does not apply.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A trustworthy target token *t̃** for rectification can be selected without ground-truth answers by contrasting contextualized vs. null-context logits (contextual mutual information) and filtering with attention-based evidence.
- **Mechanism:** Two forward passes compute per-layer logits. The difference (*S<sub>info</sub>*) measures context-induced boost for a token. This is aggregated over the last *k* layers and normalized. A candidate set *C* (top-*M* by this score) is filtered by attention mass directed at the candidate's occurrences in the context (*S<sub>attn</sub>*). The final *t̃** maximizes *S<sub>info_total</sub> + λ S<sub>attn</sub>*.
- **Evidence anchors:** Ablations show that including the attention filter (λ > 0) improves performance, with optimal λ per task.
- **Break condition:** If the retrieved context is entirely irrelevant or model attention is misaligned, *S<sub>attn</sub>* may filter out the correct token. Strong parametric priors could also weaken *S<sub>info</sub>* signals.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Rectifying hidden states in layers identified as suppressive (where *u<sub>l</sub>ᵀ w<sub>t̃*</sub>* < 0) by neutralizing their projection (*φ<sub>l</sub> = -α (u<sub>l</sub>ᵀ w<sub>t̃*</sub> / ||w<sub>t̃*</sub>||²) w<sub>t̃*</sub>*) disinhibits the correct answer without requiring target-specific supervision.
- **Mechanism:** For each suppressive layer in *L<sub>supp</sub>*, a patch *φ<sub>l</sub>* is added to the FFN output *u<sub>l</sub>*, canceling the component that negatively impacts *t̃**'s logit. Scaling factor *α* controls intensity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Ablations show rectification outperforms direct forcing of *t̃**. Optimal performance at *α*=1 (neutralization), with degradation for *α*>1.
- **Break condition:** If *L<sub>supp</sub>* includes layers critical for general language modeling (false positives), fluency may degrade. If *t̃** is incorrect, rectification may promote a wrong answer.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Transformer residual stream & Logit Lens**
  - **Why needed:** CoRect's analysis and intervention operate on the residual stream (*h<sub>l</sub>*). Logit Lens projects intermediate states to vocabulary space to observe rank evolution and compute *S<sub>info</sub>*.
  - **Quick check:** If a layer's hidden state *h<sub>l</sub>* is projected via *W<sub>U</sub>*, what does a high probability for token *t* at that layer suggest about the model's internal state at that depth?
- **Concept: FFN as key-value memory**
  - **Why needed:** The mechanism assumes FFN layers store factual associations. Suppression occurs when an FFN's output adds a conflicting memory to the residual stream.
  - **Quick check:** In the ROME editing framework, how is the FFN's operation (*u<sub>l</sub> = W<sub>down</sub>(σ(W<sub>up</sub> * input))* analogous to a key-value memory lookup?
- **Concept: Contrastive decoding & context-aware logits**
  - **Why needed:** Stage 1's token selection relies on contrasting logits from a full pass vs. a null-context pass to isolate context-derived information.
  - **Quick check:** What does a positive *S<sub>info</sub>(v)* indicate about token *v*'s relationship to the retrieved context?
- **Concept: Activation engineering**
  - **Why needed:** CoRect is a form of instance-specific activation editing, related to methods like ITI but using a target-derived direction rather than a static "truth" direction.
  - **Quick check:** How does CoRect's use of *w<sub>t̃*</sub>* as the rectification direction differ from ITI's use of a "truth" direction computed across many examples?

## Architecture Onboarding
Dual Forward Pass -> Token Selector -> Layer Localization -> Rectification Engine

- **Critical path:** Token selection (Stage 1) must complete before rectification (Stage 2) for the current decoding step, as *t̃** defines *w<sub>t̃*</sub>* for layer localization and patch computation. Both stages happen per generation step.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *k* (last layers): Intervening on more layers captures more potential suppression but risks false positives or over-corruption. Optimal *k* is task-dependent (QA: ~10, summarization: ~5).
  - *λ* (attention weight): Higher *λ* increases reliance on attention-grounded tokens. Too high may over-extract or ignore abstraction (bad for summarization); too low may select plausible but context-absent tokens.
  - *α* (rectification strength): *α*=1 (neutralization) balances removal of suppression with preservation of other knowledge. *α*>1 risks over-correction and semantic distortion.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Token selection failure:** *t̃** is irrelevant or incorrect. Symptom: Rectification promotes wrong token, EM drops. Check: *S<sub>info</sub>* scores are noise-dominated; *S<sub>attn</sub>* is zero for all candidates.
  2. **Localization failure:** *L<sub>supp</sub>* is empty (no detected suppression) or very large (broad parametric bias or wrong *t̃**). Check: Distribution of *u<sub>l</sub>ᵀ w<sub>t̃*</sub>* across layers.
  3. **Rectification failure:** Fluency drops despite higher EM. Check: Compare output entropy/perplexity with baseline. Likely *α* too high or *k* too large, corrupting syntax-critical layers.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate suppression pattern:** Track gold answer rank vs. layer using Logit Lens on a small set of conflict examples. Confirm >50% show a "flip" (gold rank high in middle layers but drops in final layers).
  2. **Isolate Stage 1 contribution:** Run only token selection and **directly decode** *t̃** (no rectification). Compare EM/AlignScore to greedy baseline. Significant gain confirms *t̃** selection is better than model's native choice.
  3. **Measure rectification necessity:** For cases where *t̃** is correct, compare direct decoding vs. hidden state rectification. If rectification performs better, it confirms that even with a correct target, forcing it as next token hurts quality, and disinhibition is superior.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** For what range of model architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder models, different layer norms, or non-transformer paradigms) does the identified "parametric suppression" phenomenon and CoRect's layer-localization strategy remain valid and effective?
- **Basis in paper:** The study focuses exclusively on decoder-only LLMs with Pre-LN architecture (e.g., Qwen, LLaMA, Mistral). The core mechanism—localizing suppressive FFN layers via logit contrast—relies on the residual stream and FFN-key-value memory hypothesis, which may not directly translate to other architectures.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates effectiveness on several decoder-only models but does not test or theorize about architectural boundaries.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation on diverse architectures (e.g., T5, BART) with adapted localization metrics. Causal tracing experiments similar to Appendix C would verify if FFN layers in those models also exhibit negative Average Indirect Effects (AIE) under knowledge conflict.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the theoretical and practical trade-off between the computational overhead of CoRect's two-stage inference and its benefits in faithfulness, and can this overhead be reduced without sacrificing accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** The "Limitations" section explicitly states: "CoRect introduces non-negligible computational overhead... necessitating a multi-pass inference scheme... additional latency compared to vanilla decoding." It suggests KV cache reuse as a mitigation but frames the trade-off as inherent.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper quantifies performance gains but does not analyze the computational cost in detail (e.g., FLOPs, wall-clock time per token) or explore more efficient alternatives.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A rigorous benchmark measuring latency, memory, and throughput vs. greedy decoding and CAD/AdaCAD across model sizes. Developing and testing approximations (e.g., caching logit-lens projections, early-exit during layer selection) could identify minimal viable interventions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does the optimal number of intervened layers (*k*) and attention weight (*λ*) vary significantly between tasks (e.g., *k*=10 for QA, *k*=5 for summarization; *λ* peaks at 0.5 for XSum vs. improving with higher values for NQ), and what do these variations reveal about the underlying dynamics of knowledge conflict in different generation scenarios?
- **Basis in paper:** Ablation studies (Figure 5) show clear task-dependent optimal hyperparameters. The paper speculates that summarization requires "broader understanding" while QA is "extractive," but does not provide a mechanistic explanation.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper treats hyperparameters as tunable knobs without connecting their optimal values to task-specific conflict characteristics. It does not analyze whether different conflict types correlate with distinct suppression patterns across layers or attention distributions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-grained analysis of layer-wise suppression strength across tasks. Correlating optimal *k* with the depth at which the gold answer's Logit Lens rank stabilizes or flips. Studying attention patterns in the final layer for correct vs. incorrect generations to explain why strong attention grounding (*λ*) hurts abstractive summarization but helps extractive QA.

## Limitations
- The core "parametric suppression" hypothesis is based on Logit Lens trends; causal proof is absent.
- Hyperparameters (*k*, *M*, *λ*, *α*) are task-tuned; their robustness across models is unknown.
- Requires full internal access (FFN outputs, unembedding), restricting use with closed LLMs.
- Evaluated only on Qwen models for QA/summarization; broader validation lacking.

## Confidence
- **Parametric suppression hypothesis (Mechanism 1): Medium** (internal evidence only, no causal tracing).
- **Token selection strategy (Mechanism 2): Medium** (ablations supportive but not extensively benchmarked).
- **Hidden state rectification (Mechanism 3): Medium** (optimal *α*=1 shown, theoretical limits unclear).
