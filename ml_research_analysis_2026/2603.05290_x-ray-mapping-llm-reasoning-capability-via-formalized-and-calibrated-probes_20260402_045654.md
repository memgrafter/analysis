# X-RAY: Mapping LLM Reasoning Capability via Formalized and Calibrated Probes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05290
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05290
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** X-RAY uncovers that LLMs are robust to constraint refinement but fragile under solution-space restructuring, and solver-verified CoT training yields large gains (e.g., +34pp on GSM8K).

## Executive Summary
LLM reasoning evaluation often confuses pattern matching with genuine reasoning and suffers from data contamination, leaving true capabilities poorly understood. X-RAY addresses this by constructing formalized, calibrated reasoning probes: natural language problems are autoformalized into executable SMT code, with difficulty parameterized by structural descriptors (conjunctive width, compositional depth, cross-coupling, dependency length). Controlled structural operators generate probe families: constraint refinement (tightens solution space) and solution-space restructuring (alters manifold geometry). Each probe is formally verified for correctness and uniqueness. Evaluating models across this structured space reveals a systematic asymmetry: models remain robust to constraint refinement but degrade sharply under solution-space restructuring. Moreover, the calibrated probes distinguish subtle model differences that standard benchmarks miss, and training with solver-verified chain-of-thought substantially improves reasoning performance, e.g., +34 percentage points on GSM8K for GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking.

## Method Summary
X-RAY begins by converting natural-language problems into formal executable code (e.g., SMT) using an LLM-based autoformalizer, which also produces a binding map aligning natural-language variables with formal symbols. The resulting artifact undergoes tri-verification: static syntax checks, dynamic execution with cross-validation across multiple solvers, and semantic alignment via an LLM judge to ensure both syntactic and semantic fidelity.

The formal representation is captured as an intermediate representation (IR) tuple (V, C, G, S) that exposes the problem’s structure. Reasoning difficulty is characterized by four structural descriptors: conjunctive width (c), compositional depth (d), cross-coupling (κ), and dependency length (ℓ). Two classes of structural operators manipulate the IR: constraint refinement (e.g., adding constraints) primarily increases c, while solution-space restructuring (e.g., introducing nesting or cross-variable coupling) alters d, κ, or ℓ. By applying these operators while calibrating parameters to keep other descriptors fixed, X-RAY generates families of probes with controlled variation. Each probe is then fed to formal solvers (Z3, CVC5, Mathematica) to compute a canonical answer and verify existence and uniqueness. Finally, target LLMs are evaluated on these probes, and success rates are aggregated across structural dimensions to produce interpretable heatmaps of reasoning capability.

## Key Results
- Systematic asymmetry: LLMs maintain performance under constraint refinement but degrade sharply under solution-space restructuring across GSM8K, MATH, Physics, and Chemistry domains.
- Calibrated probes distinguish nuanced capability differences (e.g., QwQ vs GPT-4o) that standard benchmarks fail to capture.
- Solver-verified chain-of-thought training yields large, domain-specific gains (e.g., GLM-4.1V-9B +34pp on GSM8K; Qwen3-14B +30pp on Chemistry).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Formalization as Canonicalization
- **Claim:** Autoformalization transforms natural language problems into a formal intermediate representation that removes surface variations and exposes the underlying reasoning structure.
- **Mechanism:** An LLM-based autoformalizer converts natural language to executable formal code (e.g., SMT) and produces a binding map aligning variables. The formal artifact undergoes tri-verification (static syntax checks, dynamic solver execution with cross-validation, and semantic alignment via an LLM judge) to ensure correctness. This canonical form allows systematic structural manipulation while holding superficial features constant.
- **Core assumption:** The formal representation captures all essential constraints and reasoning steps, and verification ensures semantic fidelity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statements about modeling reasoning capability via formal properties; Section 3.1 details autoformalization and tri-verification; related work acknowledges novelty but limited direct corpus evidence for this exact mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Frequent misrepresentation due to ambiguous language or insufficient expressiveness of formal logic invalidates the capability mapping.

### Mechanism 2: Controlled Structural Variation via Operators
- **Claim:** By defining two classes of structural operators (constraint refinement and solution-space restructuring), X-RAY can systematically vary difficulty along explicit structural dimensions while holding others constant, enabling precise measurement of reasoning capacity.
- **Mechanism:** Each probe is represented by an IR tuple (V, C, G, S). Constraint refinement operators (e.g., adding constraints) primarily increase conjunctive width (c) without altering dependency topology. Solution-space restructuring operators (e.g., introducing nesting, cross-variable coupling) modify the dependency graph G or structural skeleton S, affecting depth (d), coupling (κ), or dependency length (ℓ). By composing operators that isolate one coordinate of θ = (c, d, κ, ℓ), X-RAY traverses a structured probe space.
- **Core assumption:** The four dimensions are sufficiently orthogonal to be varied independently and capture the key aspects of reasoning difficulty relevant to LLMs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statement on controlled operators; Section 3.3 formal definitions and examples; corpus evidence limited to general ideas of structured difficulty.
- **Break condition:** Operators inadvertently affect multiple dimensions or the structural descriptors do not map monotonically to human-perceived difficulty, making isolated variation impossible.

### Mechanism 3: Formal Verification for Contamination-Resistant Ground Truth
- **Claim:** Formal verification (existence and uniqueness checks) ensures each probe is well-posed and has an unambiguous correct answer, providing a contamination-resistant evaluation substrate.
- **Mechanism:** For each instantiated probe, formal solvers (Z3, CVC5, Mathematica) compute a canonical answer and verify that at least one solution exists and is unique. Cross-validation across solvers further increases reliability. This process fixes ground truth independent of model predictions, avoiding annotation noise or data leakage.
- **Core assumption:** The formal solvers are sound and complete for the problem class (e.g., decidable fragments of arithmetic), and the autoformalization correctly encodes the intended problem.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 3.4 details verification; Table 4 shows >81% tri-verification pass rates across domains, confirming high reliability.
- **Break condition:** Solver bugs, undecidability within the chosen logic, or autoformalization errors that produce a different but well-formed problem with a unique solution could evaluate the wrong construct.

### Autoformalization and Binding Map
- **Why needed here:** Autoformalization is the first step that converts natural language into a manipulable formal structure; the binding map B:V_NL ↔ V_Code ensures traceability and semantic alignment, critical for interpreting structural variations.
- **Quick check:** Given a natural language problem, what are the key constraints and variables that must appear in the formal code? How would you verify that the formal code matches the problem's intent?

### Structural Descriptors (c, d, κ, ℓ)
- **Why needed here:** These four dimensions (conjunctive width, compositional depth, cross-constraint coupling, dependency length) quantify the "extractable structure" that X-RAY varies to probe reasoning capacity. Understanding them is essential to interpret heatmaps and capability boundaries.
- **Quick check:** For a given formal code, how would you compute conjunctive width (c) and compositional depth (d)? Give an example where increasing d does not increase c.

### Constraint Refinement vs Solution-Space Restructuring
- **Why needed here:** These two operator classes explain the systematic asymmetry observed in model performance (robustness to refinement, fragility to restructuring). They operationalize distinct types of structural change.
- **Quick check:** Design a refinement operator and a restructuring operator for a simple algebra problem (e.g., solving for x in linear equations). How would each affect the solution space geometry?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Autoformalizer → Tri-verification → Intermediate Representation (IR) → Structural Operators → Probe Generator → Solver Engine → Evaluator → Capability Mapper

### Critical Path
1. Input natural language problem.
2. Autoformalize → formal code + binding map.
3. Tri-verification; if fail, iterate or discard.
4. Generate probe family: apply structural operators to IR while calibrating parameters to keep skeleton fixed.
5. For each probe, use solver to compute canonical answer (ensure existence/uniqueness).
6. Evaluate target LLM on each probe (multiple random seeds) and compare to canonical answer.
7. Aggregate success rates by structural parameters (θ) and visualize as heatmaps.

### Design Tradeoffs
- LLM-based autoformalization: flexible across domains but introduces noise; mitigated by tri-verification.
- Multi-solver verification: cross-validation increases reliability but adds computational cost.
- Calibration vs randomization: calibration isolates structural effects but requires careful design; randomization provides statistical power but may confound with surface features.
- Two operator classes: simplifies interpretation of asymmetry but may not capture all structural dimensions (e.g., temporal reasoning).

### Failure Signatures
- Low tri-verification pass rate (<80%) indicates autoformalization quality issues or domain mismatch.
- Heatmaps with large grey (missing) regions: probes may be too hard or incorrectly generated (e.g., unsolvable or non-unique).
- Checkerboard instability (alternating high/low success): suggests brittle reasoning or insufficient probe variation within bins.
- All models failing on a probe family: possible that restructuring operator made the problem unsolvable or changed the problem type entirely.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Autoformalization Verification:** Run the autoformalizer and tri-verifier on 100 GSM8K problems. Report pass rates per verification stage and manually inspect 10 failures to identify common error patterns (e.g., misaligned binding, solver mismatch).
2. **Single-Dimension Probe Family:** For a stamp coverage problem, generate a probe family by varying only the number of denominations (structural operator: adding a denomination) while keeping other parameters calibrated. Evaluate o4-mini and GPT-4o on this family. Expect monotonic decline for both, but sharper for GPT-4o? Compare to refinement family (e.g., tightening coverage range).
3. **Asymmetry Test:** For the same base problem, create two families: (a) constraint refinement (add conditions like "sum of digits is prime") and (b) solution-space restructuring (introduce nesting or cross-coupling). Measure success rate decay for GPT-4o and o4-mini. Expect robust performance on refinement but sharp drop on restructuring, confirming the asymmetry.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** What underlying mechanistic factor causes LLMs to be relatively robust to constraint refinement yet degrade sharply under solution-space restructuring?  
**Basis in paper:** Abstract and Section 4.4.2 document the systematic asymmetry.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates this phenomenon empirically but does not probe the representational or computational reasons behind the asymmetry.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative analysis of model internal states (e.g., attention maps, activation patterns, or latent space trajectories) during refinement versus restructuring tasks, possibly using probing classifiers or mechanistic interpretability methods.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** Why do certain models (e.g., QwQ, o4-mini) exhibit pronounced checkerboard instability—alternating high and low success rates across neighboring structural difficulty bins?  
**Basis in paper:** Section 4.4.4 describes this visual pattern in heatmaps, especially on Chemistry and Physics, and speculates it reflects brittle reasoning strategies.  
**Why unresolved:** The cause of this oscillatory sensitivity to small structural perturbations is unknown; it could stem from prompt sensitivity, discrete reasoning jumps, or unstable search heuristics.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation varying only surface phrasing while holding structure constant, combined with fine-grained step-wise error analysis to see if failures correlate with specific reasoning templates or stochastic path choices.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Why does training with solver-verified Chain-of-Thought yield highly domain-specific performance gains (e.g., +34 pp on GSM8K for GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking but only +1.6 pp on MATH)?  
**Basis in paper:** Table 3 shows varied improvements across models and domains; the discussion notes gains are consistent but not uniform.  
**Why unresolved:** It is unclear which aspects of verified traces (e.g., explicit dependencies, domain symbolism, step atomicity) transfer and why some domains benefit more.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled cross-domain transfer experiments where verified CoT traces from one domain are used to train models on another, along with ablations on trace properties (e.g., removing formal variables, flattening dependency depth).

### Open Question 4
**Question:** What additional structural dimensions beyond conjunctive width, compositional depth, cross-coupling, and dependency length significantly impact LLM reasoning capacity?  
**Basis in paper:** Section 3.2 defines four descriptors, and the limitations explicitly state these are not exhaustive.  
**Why unresolved:** Other factors like temporal sequencing, probabilistic uncertainty, multi-modal grounding, or non-Markovian dependencies may create distinct bottlenecks but are not captured by X-RAY’s current operator set.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Extending X-RAY’s structural operators to generate probes targeting hypothesized new dimensions (e.g., introducing time-series constraints or belief updates) and measuring whether they induce failure modes independent of the existing four axes.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** How do LLMs’ reasoning capabilities on formally specified, unambiguous problems translate to ambiguous, real-world tasks where problems are under-specified or admit multiple interpretations?  
**Basis in paper:** The limitations note that formalization abstracts away natural-language nuances, implying a gap between calibrated probe performance and real-world reasoning.  
**Why unresolved:** X-RAY’s contamination-resistant probes eliminate ambiguity, but human reasoning often operates under uncertainty; it is unknown whether strength on formal probes predicts robustness to ambiguity.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Parallel evaluation of models on X-RAY probes and on matched natural-language versions engineered to include pragmatic ambiguity or multiple valid solutions, comparing error distributions and consistency.

## Limitations
- Autoformalization may cause semantic drift; binding map fidelity not fully checked.
- Structural descriptors might not be orthogonal; operators could affect multiple dimensions.
- Grey heatmap regions show coverage gaps or unsolvable probes.
