# Perturbing the Phase: Analyzing Adversarial Robustness of Complex-Valued Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06577
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06577
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: CVNNs generally more robust to standard attacks, but iterative Phase Attacks are most effective, causing 0% F1 at ε=0.01 on PolSAR.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the adversarial robustness of complex-valued neural networks (CVNNs) compared to real-valued neural networks (RVNNs) when processing complex-valued image data. The authors introduce novel Phase Attacks that perturb only the phase component of complex inputs, as well as adapted versions of standard attacks (FGSM, PGD) using Wirtinger calculus for the complex domain. Experiments on PolSAR and FastMRI Prostate datasets reveal that while CVNNs show greater robustness to standard attacks, iterative Phase Attacks are surprisingly effective, degrading performance more than unrestricted attacks despite having a smaller search space. The findings suggest that both CVNNs and RVNNs are highly susceptible to phase perturbations, indicating a fundamental vulnerability in processing complex-valued data.

## Method Summary
The study compares CVNNs and RVNNs using ResNet and ConvNeXt architectures on PolSAR and FastMRI Prostate datasets. Models are trained with Adam optimizer (lr=1e-4, 50 epochs) with and without adversarial training. Attacks are implemented using Wirtinger calculus, including standard complex adaptations (CFGSM, CIFGSM, CMIFGSM) and novel Phase Attacks (PFGSM, PIFGSM, PMIFGSM) that constrain optimization to phase-only perturbations while maintaining magnitude. Magnitude Attacks serve as controls. Performance is measured via normalized F1 score relative to clean model performance across varying attack strength ε.

## Key Results
- CVNNs demonstrated greater robustness to standard unrestricted attacks than RVNNs on both datasets.
- Iterative Phase Attacks (PIFGSM/PMIFGSM) achieved 0% normalized F1 at ε=0.01 on PolSAR, outperforming unrestricted attacks at the same perturbation level.
- Magnitude Attacks were consistently less effective than both Phase and unrestricted attacks.
- Both CVNNs and RVNNs showed high susceptibility to phase-only perturbations, suggesting a fundamental vulnerability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Phase Attack Effectiveness Despite Smaller Search Space
Phase Attacks, which perturb only phase information while maintaining constant magnitude, achieve greater effectiveness than unrestricted attacks despite having a theoretically smaller search space. The constrained optimization (searching on a 1D circle per pixel rather than a 2D complex plane) may allow the optimizer to avoid problematic gradients or barren plateaus encountered in the full complex space. This constraint potentially leads to a smoother loss landscape with more exploitable directional gradients for iterative methods like PIFGSM/PMIFGSM.

### Mechanism 2: CVNN Robustness via Native Complex Structure
CVNNs process complex inputs using complex weights and Wirtinger calculus, preserving inherent phase-magnitude relationships. This native handling of complex algebraic structure may lead to more stable internal representations where small complex perturbations (altering both phase and magnitude) do not cause disproportionate output changes. Unlike RVNNs that split complex data into real/imaginary channels, CVNNs maintain coherent phase-sensitive operations throughout the network.

### Mechanism 3: Fundamental Susceptibility to Phase Perturbations
Both CVNNs and RVNNs exhibit high susceptibility to pure phase perturbations because phase encodes crucial structural information in complex-valued signals. Neural network layers treat phase as a first-class citizen, and altering phase changes the sign and interaction patterns of complex values across channels and spatial locations, causing cascading errors. Magnitude-only attacks are less effective because magnitude is often more redundant and robustly encoded in learned features.

- **Concept: Adversarial Attack Formulation as Constrained Optimization**
  - Why needed here: All attacks (FGSM, PGD, Phase Attacks) are defined by an ε-ball constrained maximization of the loss. The paper modifies the constraint (e.g., constant magnitude for Phase Attacks), requiring comprehension of the original optimization problem.
  - Quick check question: How does the constraint for a Phase Attack (|Z| = |X| pointwise) change the feasible set compared to the standard ∞-norm ball? What is the shape of this set per pixel?

- **Concept: Wirtinger Calculus for Complex Derivatives**
  - Why needed here: Standard real-valued gradient descent does not apply to complex-valued loss functions with complex inputs. Wirtinger derivatives (δ/δz, δ/δz̄) provide the correct gradient-like direction for optimization in ℂⁿ, analogous to the real gradient.
  - Quick check question: Given a complex scalar z = a + bi and a real-valued loss L(z, z̄), what are the Wirtinger derivatives ∂L/∂z and ∂L/∂z̄? Which one points in the direction of steepest ascent of L?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Question 1**: What techniques can enhance robustness of CVNNs and RVNNs specifically against phase-only adversarial attacks?
  - Basis: Authors propose investigating how to make models more robust to phase changes.
  - Why unresolved: Phase Attacks cause drastic performance drops, creating safety risks in critical applications.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Development of phase-invariant training regularizers, data augmentations, or architectural modifications showing reduced susceptibility to Phase Attacks.

- **Question 2**: Is the high susceptibility to phase perturbations consistent across a wider range of complex-valued datasets and neural network architectures?
  - Basis: Authors propose testing the hypothesis of susceptibility to phase perturbations on a wider scale.
  - Why unresolved: Current study only uses two datasets and two architectures; broader applicability remains unknown.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments on multiple complex-valued modalities and modern CVNN designs measuring robustness under Phase Attacks.

- **Question 3**: How do phase perturbations interact with other robustness properties such as noise tolerance or distribution shift robustness?
  - Basis: Authors note the influence of phase changes on other robustness definitions should be investigated.
  - Why unresolved: Phase is just one dimension of variability; understanding its relationship with other robustness facets is crucial.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying phase and magnitude corruption independently, combined with evaluation on corrupted or distribution-shifted benchmarks.

- **Question 4**: Why do iterative phase-constrained attacks (e.g., PIFGSM, PMIFGSM) achieve greater effectiveness than unrestricted iterative attacks (e.g., CIFGSM, CMIFGSM) despite having a more restricted search space?
  - Basis: Paper observes this counterintuitive result without concrete explanation.
  - Why unresolved: This points to unknown properties of the loss landscape or optimization dynamics in complex-valued networks.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Analytical study of loss curvature along phase vs. unrestricted directions, comparison of gradient norms and optimization trajectories.

## Limitations

- The study is limited to two complex-valued datasets (PolSAR, FastMRI Prostate) and two architectures (ResNet, ConvNeXt), potentially restricting generalizability.
- Exact preprocessing details for PolSAR and FastMRI Prostate are not fully specified in the paper, creating reproducibility challenges.
- The counterintuitive effectiveness of iterative Phase Attacks versus unrestricted attacks lacks a theoretical explanation, representing a fundamental gap in understanding.

## Confidence

- **High confidence** in the theoretical foundation: The mechanisms for Phase Attack effectiveness and CVNN robustness are well-supported by mathematical derivations and experimental results.
- **Medium confidence** in the practical implementation: Successful reproduction depends on code repository completeness and correct handling of complex tensor operations in PyTorch.
- **Low confidence** in the generalizability of phase-specific vulnerability: Susceptibility to phase perturbations may not extend to other complex-valued data domains or architectures beyond those tested.
