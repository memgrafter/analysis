# Marker-Based 3D Reconstruction of Aggregates with a Comparative Analysis of 2D and 3D Morphologies

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12667
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12667
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Volume Mean Percentage Error (MPE) of +2.0% against water-displacement ground truth for 40 riprap aggregates.

## Executive Summary
The study develops a low-cost, marker-based photogrammetry system for accurate 3D reconstruction of aggregate particles, addressing the limitations of expensive scanners or unreliable 2D analysis. Using a smartphone camera, turntable, white backdrop, and drawn markers, the method integrates a deep learning foreground mask with Structure-from-Motion to suppress background noise and employs object and background markers for robust multi-view stitching and absolute scaling. Validation against water-displacement volumes confirms high accuracy (MPE +2.0%). Comparative analysis reveals systematic and view-dependent discrepancies between 2D and true 3D morphological metrics, demonstrating that 2D measurements are inadequate for inferring 3D shape.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with a fixed smartphone capturing ~30 images per visible side of an aggregate placed on a turntable against a white backdrop. Colored object markers are drawn on the aggregate, and four background markers of known spacing are fixed at the turntable corners. A pre-trained U2-Net model generates foreground masks for each image. A modified Structure-from-Motion (SfM) engine then reconstructs sparse point clouds while ignoring masked background regions. For aggregates requiring multi-side capture, manual labeling of object markers on a few views aligns partial clouds via rigid transformation. Background marker pixel coordinates and their known physical distances provide an absolute scale factor. A dense mesh is generated, from which volume, surface area, and principal dimensions are computed to derive 3D morphological descriptors (Flat & Elongated Ratio, sphericity). For 2D comparison, silhouettes from individual views yield circularity and FER.

## Key Results
- Volume reconstruction achieves a Mean Percentage Error (MPE) of +2.0% compared to water-displacement ground truth across 40 riprap aggregates.
- 3D morphological metrics (FER, sphericity) are systematically higher than averages of corresponding 2D metrics computed from multi-view images.
- 2D metrics exhibit substantial view-dependent variation, confirming that single or averaged 2D measurements do not reliably represent true 3D morphology.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Object Markers for Robust Point Cloud Stitching
**Claim:** Drawn colored object markers (head-tail pattern) enable accurate registration of partial 3D point clouds when a full 360° capture in a single pass is impossible.
**Mechanism:** The invariant marker pattern is manually labeled on a few images from each partial scan. This establishes corresponding 3D coordinates that allow aligning the separate sparse point clouds via a rigid transformation, overcoming the failure of automatic feature matching on texture-poor aggregate surfaces.
**Core assumption:** Markers remain visible and unaltered on the aggregate during all acquisitions; the aggregate’s pose is stable between side-specific captures.
**Break condition:** Markers become occluded, smudged, or ambiguously labeled; the aggregate is inadvertently moved or reoriented between scans.

### Mechanism 2: Deep Learning Mask for Background Suppression
**Claim:** A U2-Net salient object mask suppresses background noise during SfM bundle adjustment, eliminating manual cleaning of reconstructed models.
**Mechanism:** The binary mask is incorporated into the SfM reprojection error minimization, forcing the optimization to ignore feature points from background regions (e.g., turntable edges). This prevents irrelevant structures from being reconstructed into the point cloud.
**Core assumption:** The U2-Net model, fine-tuned once on ~100 image-mask pairs for a specific lab environment, generalizes robustly to new images under similar lighting and background conditions.
**Break condition:** Significant environmental changes (background color, lighting) occur that are not represented in the training set; the aggregate’s color closely matches the background, confusing the salient object detector.

### Mechanism 3: Background Markers for Absolute Scale
**Claim:** Color-coded background markers at known positions provide an absolute scale reference, converting the arbitrary-scale SfM model to true physical dimensions.
**Mechanism:** The fixed inter-marker distances are measured beforehand. During reconstruction, the pixel coordinates of these markers in multiple views allow solving for the scale factor and global coordinate transformation, analogous to using Ground Control Points in photogrammetry.
**Core assumption:** Background markers are rigidly fixed with accurately known distances; they are clearly visible and sharply focused in multiple acquired images.
**Break condition:** Markers are obscured, blurred, or their measured distances are inaccurate; insufficient parallax exists in views containing the markers.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structure-from-Motion (SfM) Bundle Adjustment**
  - **Why needed:** The core reconstruction engine jointly optimizes camera poses and 3D point positions by minimizing reprojection error. Understanding this is key to seeing how masks and markers inject constraints into the optimization.
  - **Quick check:** If background feature points are included in bundle adjustment, what effect does it have on the reconstructed model’s scale and accuracy?
- **Concept: 3D Morphological Descriptors (FER, Sphericity)**
  - **Why needed:** The study’s comparative analysis depends on correctly computing these metrics from the reconstructed mesh (via principal dimensions and surface/volume ratios) versus from 2D silhouettes.
  - **Quick check:** How would the 3D Flat and Elongated Ratio (FER) of a prolate spheroid compare to its 2D FER measured from a silhouette taken along its major axis?
- **Concept: Photogrammetric Scale Determination**
  - **Why needed:** SfM produces models at an arbitrary scale. The background markers implement a standard photogrammetric solution (resection/intersection using known control points) to resolve absolute scale.
  - **Quick check:** What is the minimum number of non-collinear background markers with known distances required to define both scale and 3D orientation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Hardware (smartphone on tripod, motorized turntable, white backdrop, colored object/background markers) -> Software Pipeline (U2-Net mask generator, modified SfM engine, manual marker labeling interface, point cloud stitching/scale alignment module) -> Data Flow (Raw images -> Foreground masks -> Masked SfM -> Sparse point clouds per side -> Marker-assisted registration -> Scaled dense model -> Mesh -> Morphological analysis).

**Critical path:**  
1. One-time U2-Net fine-tuning on ~100 images of the specific lab setup.  
2. Per-sample: prepare aggregate (draw markers), capture multi-view images, generate masks, run masked SfM for each side, manually label object markers, stitch partial clouds, apply scale from background markers, extract mesh and metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Fixed camera vs. moving camera: Fixed camera simplifies geometry (known rotation axis) but limits aggregate size; moving camera increases size range but requires more complex SfM and marker placement.  
- Manual vs. automatic marker labeling: Manual labeling (3 views/side) is robust for small batches; automatic detection would be needed for high throughput but is challenging with variable surfaces.  
- Deep learning mask vs. chroma-key: Deep learning adapts to variable conditions but requires training; chroma-key is deterministic but less flexible in field settings.  
- Marker-based vs. marker-less stitching: Markers guarantee alignment for feature-poor aggregates; marker-less automatic registration may fail and require more manual intervention.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Noisy point cloud with background structures: Mask failure → inspect U2-Net output, retrain if environment changed.  
- Disjointed partial point clouds: Inconsistent marker labeling or occlusion → verify marker visibility and labeling consistency.  
- Incorrect scale (volume mismatch): Background marker distance error or poor visibility → re-measure distances, ensure markers are in focus.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Validation replication:** Reconstruct all 40 riprap samples and compute volume MPE against water displacement to confirm the +2.0% result; record per-sample error distribution.  
2. **Mask robustness test:** Change backdrop color (e.g., white to blue) *without retraining* U2-Net and capture a few samples; quantify increase in background noise points in sparse clouds.  
3. **Marker dependency test:** Reconstruct a sample *without* object markers, attempting automatic multi-side registration via general feature matching; document failure rate and additional manual intervention time compared to marker-assisted method.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Can a consistent correction factor be established to convert 2D morphological metrics (e.g., average 2D FER, circularity) to their true 3D counterparts across different aggregate size categories (e.g., IDOT RR1–RR7)?** The study only validated one size group (RR3 riprap); 2D metrics showed view-dependent variation, and average 2D FER tended to fall between intermediate-to-shortest and longest-to-intermediate 3D ratios, suggesting size-specific biases. Further research across all size categories is needed.
- **How does geological composition and surface texture (e.g., porosity, mineral graininess) affect reconstruction accuracy and the 2D–3D morphology discrepancy?** The study used only dolomite riprap. Different rock types (e.g., granite, basalt) may impact feature extraction, mask generation, and volume measurement due to varying reflectance and texture.
- **Can the manual steps of object marker labeling and partial point cloud stitching be automated for high-throughput reconstruction, especially for aggregates with low surface texture?** Manual labeling is time-consuming and introduces variability. Automation would require robust automatic marker detection or alternative feature-based stitching methods that work on feature-poor surfaces.
- **To what extent can the approach be applied directly in field conditions (e.g., quarries, stockpiles) with uncontrolled lighting, natural backgrounds, and without a turntable?** The authors suggest potential extensibility, but all experiments used a controlled lab setup. The U2-Net’s generalizability to natural scenes without retraining and alternative scale reference methods in the field remain untested.

## Limitations
- U2-Net training specifics (augmentation, epochs, dataset) are unreported, hindering mask reproducibility across different environments.
- Manual object/background marker labeling workflow (number of views, error minimization) is vague, limiting automation potential.
- Validation restricted to one aggregate type (riprap) and a narrow size range; generalizability to other shapes (e.g., flaky, angular) and lithologies is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Volume accuracy (±2% MPE):** High (robust ground truth, n=40).
- **2D vs. 3D morphology differences:** Medium (consistent trends but limited to one aggregate class).
- **Marker-based stitching robustness:** High (validated in paper, though manual).
- **Deep learning mask generalizability:** Low (environment-dependent training, no cross-condition tests).
- **Scale referencing via background markers:** High (standard photogrammetric GCP approach).
