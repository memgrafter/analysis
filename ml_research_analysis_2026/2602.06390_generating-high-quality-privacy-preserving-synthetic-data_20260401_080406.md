# Generating High-quality Privacy-preserving Synthetic Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06390
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06390
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A model-agnostic post-processing framework improves distributional fidelity and empirical privacy of synthetic tabular data with minimal downstream utility loss.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the core challenge of simultaneously achieving high distributional fidelity, downstream utility, and privacy in synthetic tabular data generation. It introduces a two-step, model-agnostic post-processing framework: first, a layer-frozen mode-patching procedure repairs missing or underrepresented categorical modes; second, a HEOM–kNN filter rejects synthetic records that are too close to real data points, enforcing a minimum distance threshold. Evaluated on CTGAN and TVAE across three datasets, the method with moderate thresholds (τ ≈ 0.2–0.35) yields significant gains in categorical fidelity (up to 36% reduction in Jensen–Shannon divergence) and pairwise dependence preservation (10–14% improvement), while predictive utility remains within ~1% of the unprocessed baseline. Distance-based privacy metrics improve, though attribute inference attack success is largely unchanged.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework operates as a post-hoc repair on synthetic data from any generative model. Step one, **layer-frozen mode-patching**, identifies categorical columns where the synthetic data under-represents modes present in the real data. It then selectively replaces synthetic values with real values from the corresponding column, preserving row-wise correlations by applying changes within layers defined by mixed-type variables. Step two, the **HEOM–kNN filter**, computes the Heterogeneous Euclidean-Overlap Metric (HEOM) distance between each synthetic record and its nearest neighbor in the real dataset. Synthetic records with a distance below a threshold τ are rejected, enforcing a minimum proximity buffer to mitigate memorization. The process is tunable via τ, and rejected records can optionally be resampled from the generator.

## Key Results
- Reduced categorical Jensen–Shannon divergence by up to 36% with τ ≈ 0.2–0.35, indicating better distributional matching.
- Improved a composite measure of pairwise dependence preservation by 10–14%, suggesting enhanced multivariate fidelity.
- Downstream predictive performance (accuracy/AUC) stayed within ~1% of the unprocessed synthetic baseline, demonstrating minimal utility loss; distance-based privacy indicators (e.g., RPR) improved.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by addressing two common failure modes of tabular generators. **Mode-patching** directly combats mode collapse in categorical columns, a known weakness of models like CTGAN and TVAE, by borrowing observed modes from real data while attempting to preserve correlations through layer-based selection. This boosts fidelity without retraining. **HEOM–kNN filtering** then reduces the risk of exact or near-exact memorization by removing synthetic records that are too proximate to real records, thereby increasing empirical privacy as measured by distance-based metrics. The two steps are complementary: mode-patching enriches the synthetic distribution, while filtering sanitizes it, together yielding a better trade-off.

## Foundational Learning
- **Jensen–Shannon divergence**: A symmetric measure of similarity between two probability distributions; used here to quantify categorical distribution fidelity between synthetic and real data.
- **HEOM distance**: Heterogeneous Euclidean-Overlap Metric; a distance function for mixed-type (continuous and categorical) data, enabling nearest-neighbor search in tabular settings.
- **Mode-patching**: A repair technique for categorical under-generation; replaces synthetic values with real ones to recover missing modes.
- **kNN filtering**: A proximity-based rejection rule; used to enforce a minimum distance τ between synthetic and real records as a privacy heuristic.
- **Downstream utility**: Evaluation of synthetic data via performance on predictive tasks (e.g., accuracy, AUC); ensures synthetic data remains useful for ML.
- **RPR (Record of Proximity Risk)**: A distance-based privacy metric; lower RPR indicates synthetic records are less likely to be too close to real ones.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Synthetic data generator -> Mode-patching module -> HEOM–kNN filter -> Filtered synthetic data  

Critical path: Synthetic records first undergo mode-patching to fix categorical deficiencies; the corrected records are then passed to the HEOM–kNN filter, where each record is compared to the real dataset. Records with HEOM distance < τ are rejected (and optionally resampled), while others are retained.  

Design tradeoffs:  
- Threshold τ directly balances fidelity (higher τ retains more records) vs. privacy (lower τ rejects more close matches).  
- Mode-patching must avoid over-correction that distorts multivariate distributions; the layer-frozen approach attempts to preserve local correlations but may not capture all dependencies.  
- kNN search is computationally intensive for large datasets, though approximate methods could mitigate this.  

Failure signatures:  
- τ set too high: insufficient rejection, leading to poor privacy (high RPR) due to memorization.  
- τ set too low: excessive rejection causes utility degradation from data loss and potential distribution shift.  
- Mode-patching applied without layer constraints: destroys row-wise correlations, reducing downstream utility.  

First experiments:  
1. Sweep τ across 0.1–0.5 on CTGAN/TVAE for all datasets, plotting JS divergence, RPR, and downstream AUC to identify Pareto-efficient regions.  
2. Compare the post-processed outputs against a differentially private generator (e.g., DP-CTGAN) on fidelity, utility, and formal privacy bounds (ε).  
3. Apply the exact pipeline to a modern generator like TabDDPM and an LLM-based synthesizer; report whether fidelity/privacy gains generalize.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the HEOM–kNN filter be endowed with formal privacy guarantees (e.g., differential privacy)?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state: "This is a post-hoc, distance-based heuristic; it is not a formal (ε, δ)-DP mechanism and carries no DP accounting."
- **Why unresolved**: The filter is used to enforce proximity but lacks any theoretical privacy bound, relying solely on empirical proxies.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A proof that the rejection-with-replacement process satisfies differential privacy under certain conditions, or a tight bound on privacy loss as a function of τ and dataset geometry.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Does the proposed post-processing pipeline remain effective when applied to modern tabular generative models (e.g., diffusion models, LLM-based synthesizers) that have different architectures and failure modes?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The evaluation was restricted to CTGAN and TV AE; the authors mention in the introduction and future work that "diffusion models and row-serialization approaches using large language models are emerging as promising alternatives" but were not tested.
- **Why unresolved**: The filter's mechanism assumes certain geometric properties of the synthetic data that may not hold for other generators; evidence of efficacy is missing for these newer approaches.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Reimplementing the mode-patching and HEOM–kNN filter on at least two recent tabular generators (e.g., TabDDPM and an LLM-based synthesizer) and systematically comparing fidelity, utility, and privacy trade-offs.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can the acceptance threshold τ be automatically tuned in a privacy-utility Pareto-efficient manner rather than by manual sweep?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors note they "did not formulate a single composite metric for the privacy–utility trade-off" and that "without a unified metric... we cannot definitively say τANY = 0.1 is optimal." They also call for formalizing the trade-off in future work.
- **Why unresolved**: Threshold selection currently relies on ad-hoc observation of multiple curves; this does not scale to diverse applications with differing privacy/utility requirements.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Development of a multi-objective optimization framework (e.g., generating a Pareto front of (privacy, utility) pairs) that can propose τ values given stakeholder constraints, and validation that such τ choices outperform heuristic picks on held-out datasets.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How does the HEOM–kNN filter affect membership inference vulnerability and the stability of L-diversity as a privacy metric?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors report "L-Diversity Instability" and acknowledge that "our evaluation of privacy might be incomplete if L-diversity can swing so much". They also state that "we did not explicitly compute membership inference directly" as a limitation.
- **Why unresolved**: L-diversity showed large fluctuations across thresholds, and membership inference—a standard attack—was not measured, leaving gaps in the privacy assessment.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: (a) A thorough analysis of L-diversity under varying τ to identify patterns or alternative metrics that are more stable; (b) Implementation of membership inference attacks (e.g., shadow models, Yeom's attack) on filtered synthetic data to directly quantify memorization risk and correlate it with existing proxies.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to 3 datasets and 2 generative models (CTGAN, TVAE); generalizability to other models/data modalities is unknown.
- Privacy assessment relies on distance-based metrics (RPR); attribute inference attack success remained largely unchanged, suggesting incomplete privacy guarantees.
- Threshold τ selection is ad-hoc and dataset-dependent; no unified metric guides optimal choice.

## Confidence
- **Distributional fidelity improvement**: High – Consistent JS divergence reduction across datasets with clear algorithmic steps.
- **Minimal downstream utility loss**: Medium – Utility preserved within ~1% on tested models/tasks, but limited task/dataset diversity.
- **Enhanced empirical privacy**: Medium – RPR improved, but attack resilience mixed; privacy gains may be narrow.
- **Model-agnostic post-processing**: Low – Only two models evaluated; applicability to newer architectures (e.g., diffusion models) unverified.

## Next Checks
