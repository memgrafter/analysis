# VAD4Space: Visual Anomaly Detection for Planetary Surface Imagery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.13993  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13993  
- **Reference count**: 19  
- **Primary result**: Feature-based VAD with a MobileNetV2 backbone achieves near-supervised anomaly detection on planetary imagery with 10–17 MB memory and provides interpretable anomaly maps.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles automated discovery of rare geological phenomena in planetary surface images, where manual inspection is infeasible and labeled anomalies are scarce. It evaluates seven state-of-the-art feature-based Visual Anomaly Detection (VAD) methods on two novel benchmarks—lunar LROC imagery and Mars Curiosity Mastcam—using a lightweight MobileNetV2 backbone for edge deployment. Under balanced test conditions, the best VAD models approach supervised accuracy while using dramatically less memory. The study also reveals a critical vulnerability: when anomalous samples contaminate the normal training set, memory-bank methods like PatchCore collapse on Mars data (where anomalies are globally distinct) but remain robust on lunar data (where anomalies are locally subtle). These results highlight both the promise and the deployment challenges of VAD for open-world planetary exploration.

## Method Summary
The work assesses seven feature-based VAD algorithms (PatchCore, PaDiM, CFA, STFPM, RD4AD, SimpleNet, FastFlow) on two planetary image benchmarks. The lunar dataset consists of ~5 k LROC chipouts with "none" as normal and fresh/old craters as anomalies. The Mars dataset contains 477 Mastcam images tiled to 64×64, yielding 9 302 normal training tiles and 856 test tiles across 9 classes (426 normal, 430 anomalous). Models use a frozen MobileNetV2 backbone pretrained on ImageNet to extract multi‑scale features; only normal images are used for training (except in contamination experiments). Three evaluation settings test (1) balanced test, (2) 5% anomalies in test, and (3) 5% anomalies in both training and test. Metrics include image‑level AUROC, F1, PR‑AUC, accuracy, and resource usage (memory, latency). Anomaly maps are generated for interpretability.

## Key Results
- **Near‑supervised accuracy in clean setting**: PaDiM reaches 73 % image accuracy vs. 82 % supervised on lunar; PatchCore attains 0.81 image‑ROC vs. 0.705 supervised on Mars.
- **Extreme memory efficiency**: VAD models require only 10–17 MB, far below supervised counterparts (34–507 MB).
- **Contamination robustness divergence**: With 5% anomalous training samples, PatchCore stays robust on lunar (0.64 image‑ROC) but collapses on Mars (0.40 image‑ROC), exposing sensitivity of memory‑bank methods to globally distinct anomalies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper identifies three key mechanisms. First, feature‑based VAD achieves edge‑deployable efficiency by reusing a pretrained lightweight backbone (MobileNetV2) and scoring anomalies via deviations in semantic feature embeddings rather than costly pixel‑space reconstruction; this hinges on the assumption that natural‑image pretraining transfers to planetary domains. Second, memory‑bank methods (e.g., PatchCore, PaDiM) store representative normal feature statistics—either a coreset of patch embeddings or per‑location Gaussians—and flag test samples whose features deviate strongly; this enables localization without pixel labels but assumes the normal feature space is sufficiently dense that a finite sample captures it. Third, robustness to training‑set contamination depends on the spatial character of anomalies: for locally subtle anomalies (lunar craters), anomalous patches are few and their corruption of the memory bank is limited; for globally distinct anomalies (Mars spectral novelties), many contaminated patches enter the bank, causing catastrophic failure for exemplar‑based methods while statistical models degrade more gracefully.

## Foundational Learning
- **Open‑world anomaly detection**: Needed because planetary missions must discover unanticipated rare phenomena without labeled examples. *Quick check*: How can a model trained solely on normal terrain detect a fresh crater it has never seen? It learns a distribution of normal features; anomalies produce out‑of‑distribution embeddings.
- **Feature embedding space normality modeling**: Needed because direct pixel comparison is brittle to lighting, angle, and noise; semantic features from pretrained CNNs offer a stable, abstract representation. *Quick check*: What is the risk of using an ImageNet‑pretrained backbone for Martian patches? Potential domain shift—Martian textures may not align well with natural‑image features, reducing discriminativity.
- **Memory bank vs. statistical modeling**: Needed because VAD families trade off memory, accuracy, and contamination resistance; memory banks store exemplars (flexible but memory‑heavy), while statistical models compactly parameterize distributions (efficient but distribution‑shape assumptions). *Quick check*: How would a Gaussian‑based model (PaDiM) behave if anomalous patches mix into its training statistics? The estimated means and covariances become biased, likely increasing false positives on normal data that now appear anomalous relative to the corrupted statistics.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Input → MobileNetV2 backbone → Multi‑scale feature extraction → [Memory bank (PatchCore) / Gaussian statistics (PaDiM)] → Scoring (distance metric) → Image aggregator (max/mean) → Anomaly map generation

**Critical Path**  
*Training*: Normal images → forward pass through frozen backbone → extract selected layer features → build/update memory bank or compute per‑location statistics.  
*Inference*: Test image → same feature extraction → query memory/statistics → compute per‑patch anomaly scores → aggregate to image‑level decision → upsample patch scores to anomaly map.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Memory vs. accuracy*: PatchCore stores exemplar patches (higher memory, may capture diverse normals better); PaDiM stores Gaussian parameters (lower memory, assumes unimodal per location).  
- *Contamination sensitivity*: Memory‑bank methods store anomalous patches directly, making them highly vulnerable; statistical methods dilute contamination but still suffer performance loss.  
- *Backbone choice*: MobileNetV2 reduces compute/memory but may lose fine‑grained details needed for small anomalies; deeper backbones could improve accuracy at higher resource cost.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Catastrophic AUROC drop under contamination (e.g., PatchCore on Mars: 0.81 → 0.40).  
- Noisy or uniform anomaly maps, indicating poor feature separation.  
- High false positive rate on rare normal terrain types absent from training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multispectral fusion**: How can full multispectral information beyond RGB be effectively exploited when current models rely on RGB‑pretrained backbones? The paper discards six narrow‑band filters, limiting detection of spectrally novel anomalies.
2. **Robust memory mechanisms**: Why do memory‑bank methods collapse under contamination with globally distinct anomalies, and how can memory be made robust for realistic deployment where clean training data is unattainable?
3. **Anomaly subtype analysis**: How do VAD methods perform across specific anomaly sub‑types (spectral vs. morphological) in planetary imagery, and can method selection be tailored accordingly? The paper aggregates all anomalies, obscuring subtype‑specific behavior.
4. **Optimal edge architectures**: What neural network architectures beyond MobileNetV2 best balance accuracy, memory, and latency for onboard planetary VAD? The paper fixes MobileNetV2 but does not explore the design space.

## Limitations
- **Implementation details missing**: Exact MobileNetV2 layers used, training hyperparameters, and score aggregation method are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **Preprocessing ambiguity**: Input resolution and normalization (e.g., ImageNet mean/std) are unclear, especially for 64×64 Mars tiles.
- **Contamination protocol underspecified**: How anomalous samples are selected and injected (percentage, class distribution, randomness) is not described, making Setting 3 reproduction uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Edge‑efficient VAD framework (MobileNetV2, low memory, interpretable maps)**: High  
- **Balanced test performance (approaching supervised)**: Medium (depends on unreported implementation choices)  
- **Contamination robustness (PatchCore robust on lunar, collapses on Mars)**: Medium‑Low (based on single scenario; generalizability untested)
