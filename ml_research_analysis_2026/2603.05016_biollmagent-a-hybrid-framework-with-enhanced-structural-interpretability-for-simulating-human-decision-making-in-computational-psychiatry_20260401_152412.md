# BioLLMAgent: A Hybrid Framework with Enhanced Structural Interpretability for Simulating Human Decision-Making in Computational Psychiatry

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.05016
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05016
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: BioLLMAgent combines an internal RL engine (Outcome-Representation Learning) with an external LLM shell to produce interpretable, behaviorally realistic simulations of human decision-making, validated on Iowa Gambling Task (N=350) and Delay Discounting with high parameter identifiability (>0.67) and controllable therapeutic effects.

## Executive Summary
Computational psychiatry seeks models that are both interpretable and behaviorally realistic. BioLLMAgent bridges the trade-off by fusing an interpretable reinforcement learning engine based on Outcome-Representation Learning (ORL) with a flexible LLM shell that encodes cognitive strategies via prompts. The ORL engine tracks distinct components—reward sensitivity, frequency weighting, and perseveration—while the LLM generates a stable prior by averaging simulated responses. A weighted fusion (ω) combines their utilities, yielding a hybrid agent. Validation across six Iowa Gambling Task datasets (N=350) shows accurate reproduction of human choice patterns and excellent parameter identifiability (Pearson r > 0.67). The framework exhibits controllable responses to prompts (e.g., CBT principles), simulates intervention effects, and generalizes to Delay Discounting. BioLLMAgent thus provides a structurally interpretable, clinically relevant sandbox for psychiatric hypothesis testing.

## Method Summary
The BioLLMAgent architecture comprises three modules: (1) Internal RL Engine: Implements the ORL model, which maintains separate traces for expected value (EV) and expected frequency (EF) updated by asymmetric learning rates (A_rew, A_pun), plus a perseveration score (PS) with decay K. Utility is computed as U_RL = EV + β_F·EF + β_P·PS. (2) External LLM Shell: A persona prompt is used to generate action probabilities over many simulated IGT trials; these are averaged into a static prior Π_prob, which is linearly scaled to Π_util on a utility scale. (3) Decision Fusion: The combined utility U_combined = (1-ω)·U_RL + ω·Π_util is passed through a softmax to select actions. Parameters of the ORL model are fit to human data via Bayesian inference; ω is fixed at 0.25 based on sensitivity analysis. The system is validated by comparing simulated vs. human trajectories, conducting parameter recovery tests, performing LLM prompt ablations, and running multi-agent network simulations of interventions.

## Key Results
- Validated on the Iowa Gambling Task across six datasets (N=350), BioLLMAgent accurately reproduces human advantageous choice trajectories with high parameter identifiability (Pearson r > 0.67 for core ORL parameters β_F and β_P).
- Prompt ablations demonstrate that large language models (e.g., GPT-4o) reliably follow instructions to produce controllable behavioral priors (e.g., uniform random, CBT principles), and these priors yield simulated performance shifts consistent with therapeutic goals.
- Multi-agent network simulations show community-wide educational interventions achieve higher health scores (0.950) than targeted individual CBT (0.750), illustrating the framework’s capacity to explore systemic intervention effects. Cross-task generalization to Delay Discounting further supports broader applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Internal RL Engine provides structurally interpretable value learning
The ORL model decouples reward sensitivity, frequency weighting, and habit formation via separate EV, EF, and PS traces, each updated by distinct parameters. Utility is a linear sum of these components. This separation aligns with dual-process theories and allows each cognitive factor to be measured independently. Evidence includes formal equations in the paper and high parameter recovery correlations (r > 0.67). Identifiability fails if the core parameters (β_F, β_P) show recovery r < 0.6, indicating overfitting or failure to isolate components.

### Mechanism 2: External LLM Shell encodes high-level cognitive strategies as a stable prior
By simulating many trials under a fixed persona prompt, the LLM’s dynamic responses are averaged into a static probability distribution Π_prob, which is scaled to a utility prior Π_util. This assumes that trait-like dispositions can be approximated by a consistent exogenous drive. Evidence includes prompt ablation studies showing large models comply with uniform or CBT instructions. The prior becomes invalid if the LLM’s distribution is extremely skewed regardless of prompt, indicating memorization rather than persona simulation.

### Mechanism 3: Decision Fusion balances internal experience and external guidance via linear weighting
The combined utility U_combined = (1-ω)·U_RL + ω·Π_util integrates the RL engine’s learned values with the LLM’s strategic prior. With ω = 0.25, the fusion improves behavioral fit without degrading RL parameter identifiability. The linear model reflects dual-process theories and requires only one tuneable parameter. Break conditions arise if ω exceeds 0.3 (causing identifiability collapse) or if alternative fusion functions yield substantially better fits without sacrificing interpretability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning parameter interpretation**  
  *Why needed*: To understand what the ORL parameters (A_rew, A_pun, β_F, β_P) represent cognitively and to validate the Internal RL Engine’s outputs against human data.  
  *Quick check*: If a participant has high β_P but low β_F, what does this suggest about their decision-making in the IGT? (Answer: They are more influenced by recent choices (perseveration) and less by reward frequency, indicating a habit-driven strategy.)

- **LLM prompt engineering for behavioral control**  
  *Why needed*: The External LLM Shell’s output depends entirely on prompt design; precise prompts are required to generate consistent, distinguishable priors that reflect the intended cognitive strategy.  
  *Quick check*: Why does instructing an LLM to "choose uniformly" require a specific, explicit prompt structure? (Answer: Because LLMs may not produce truly uniform distributions without explicit constraints; the prompt must enforce probability normalization and uniform distribution.)

- **Dual-process theory in decision-making**  
  *Why needed*: The fusion architecture assumes two interacting systems (fast, experience-based vs. slow, strategy-based). This theoretical grounding justifies the linear combination and gives meaning to the ω parameter.  
  *Quick check*: In BioLLMAgent, which component (RL Engine or LLM Shell) corresponds to the "habitual" system? (Answer: The RL Engine, as it updates values based on repeated experience, akin to a habitual system.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: IGT Environment → RL Engine (ORL) → U_RL; LLM Shell (offline) → Π_util; Fusion (weighted sum) → U_combined → Softmax → Action → IGT Environment.

- **Critical path**: For a new simulation: (a) Select or fit ORL parameters to target population; (b) Define LLM persona prompt; (c) Generate static prior Π_util by simulating 100 IGT trials with the LLM; (d) Choose fusion weight ω (default 0.25); (e) Run simulation loop: for each trial, compute U_RL from ORL, combine with Π_util via ω, sample action via softmax, observe outcome, update ORL.

- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - *Fusion mechanism*: Linear (simple, one parameter, interpretable) vs. multiplicative or gated (potentially better fit but less interpretable). Chose linear for clinical phenotyping.
  - *Prior representation*: Static average (trait-like, easier to disentangle from RL learning) vs. dynamic per-trial (more realistic but confounded with RL updates). Static chosen for proof-of-concept.
  - *LLM scale*: Large models (>70B) ensure instruction compliance but are proprietary and resource-intensive; small open-source models are accessible but often fail to follow prompts accurately.

- **Failure signatures**: 
  - LLM prior contamination: Π_util is extremely skewed (e.g., >0.9 on one deck) regardless of prompt, suggesting the LLM is using memorized IGT strategies rather than simulating the persona.
  - Parameter identifiability collapse: Core ORL parameters (β_F, β_P) show recovery correlation r < 0.8 in synthetic data tests, indicating overfitting or insufficient separation of RL components.
  - ω sensitivity mismatch: Expected improvement with moderate ω is absent or negative, especially in clinical groups, implying mis-specified prior or population-specific ω not accounted for.

- **First three experiments**:
  1. *Pure RL validation*: Fit ORL to human IGT data and check that parameter recovery (synthetic data) yields r > 0.8 for core parameters; also compare model trajectories to human data.
  2. *LLM prompt compliance test*: Set ω = 1 (LLM-only) and verify that with a "uniform selection" prompt, GPT-4o produces a choice distribution within 0.05 of uniform (0.25 per deck). If not, prompt engineering is required.
  3. *Fusion sanity check*: With ω = 0.25 and a neutral LLM prior (e.g., uniform), confirm that BioLLMAgent trajectories closely match pure ORL (high correlation) and that mean squared difference (MSD) and cumulative B.Diff are minimal (<0.05), indicating the fusion does not disrupt underlying RL dynamics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can BioLLMAgent generalize to other cognitive domains (e.g., working memory, social cognition) beyond decision-making tasks like IGT and Delay Discounting?
2. Is the fusion weight ω a reliable, estimable individual-difference parameter that predicts susceptibility to external guidance and treatment outcomes in clinical populations?
3. Do the behavioral shifts induced by CBT-simulated prompts correspond to actual therapeutic mechanisms, and can these simulations inform real-world CBT design?
4. Can BioLLMAgent reduce dependence on large-scale LLMs (>70B parameters) to improve accessibility in resource-limited settings without compromising controllability or identifiability?

## Limitations
- The scaling method for converting LLM probability distributions to utilities (Π_util) is unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Verbatim prompt templates and full details of the Bayesian inference for ORL parameters are omitted, requiring assumptions for reproduction.
- The framework relies on large, proprietary LLMs for reliable instruction following, limiting accessibility, reproducibility, and applicability in low-resource environments.

## Confidence
- Internal RL Engine: High (robust validation, strong parameter recovery across multiple datasets)
- External LLM Shell: Medium (prompt compliance shown for large models but inconsistent for smaller ones; prior stability not fully verified)
- Decision Fusion: High (at ω=0.25, improves fit without harming interpretability; boundary conditions explored)

## Next Checks
1. *Scale conversion experiment*: Test alternative scaling methods (e.g., matching RL utility ranges) for Π_util and measure impact on trajectory fit and parameter recovery.
2. *Cross-model prompt stress test*: Evaluate CBT and uniform prompts across several LLMs (including open-source) with multiple runs each to quantify Π_util variance and instruction adherence.
3. *ω boundary mapping*: Conduct systematic simulations varying ω from 0 to 0.5 on synthetic clinical vs. healthy profiles to pinpoint the ω threshold where RL parameter identifiability degrades and to refine population-specific ω values.
