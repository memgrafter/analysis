# Does Inference Scaling Improve Reasoning Faithfulness? A Multi-Model Analysis of Self-Consistency Tradeoffs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.06423
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06423
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Inference scaling via self-consistency yields model-dependent accuracy–faithfulness tradeoffs; e.g., GPT-5.2 gains accuracy without sacrificing faithfulness, while Claude Opus 4.5 suffers accuracy loss for a large faithfulness boost.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether increasing the number of reasoning samples (self-consistency) improves the faithfulness of model reasoning across four frontier LLMs on a math benchmark. Faithfulness is measured by whether the model’s chain-of-thought (CoT) answer differs from an early-answer probe. Key findings reveal divergent scaling effects: GPT-5.2 and Gemini-3-flash improve accuracy with stable or slightly reduced faithfulness, whereas Claude Opus 4.5 experiences an “overthinking” effect—accuracy drops while faithfulness increases dramatically. DeepSeek-v3.2, already near ceiling accuracy, shows faithfulness gains without accuracy change. The results illuminate fundamental tradeoffs between answer correctness and reasoning integrity when scaling inference compute.

## Method Summary
The study evaluated four frontier models (GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.5, Gemini-3-flash, DeepSeek-v3.2) on 100 sampled GSM8K math problems. For each model and problem, multiple CoT reasoning paths were generated at temperature 0.7 using a standard prompt. The final answer was obtained via majority voting across N samples (N = 1, 5, 20). To assess faithfulness, an early answering probe (temperature 0) was run for each selected reasoning path; faithfulness was defined as the proportion of paths where the probe answer differed from the CoT answer. Statistical comparisons employed bootstrap confidence intervals, McNemar’s test for accuracy, and paired t-tests for faithfulness, with effect sizes (Cohen’s d). Problem difficulty was stratified based on N=1 correctness to decompose scaling effects into “solved hard problems” and “broken easy problems.”

## Key Results
- GPT-5.2: accuracy +12% (78%→90% at N=5, p=0.031, d=0.33) with stable faithfulness (0.540→0.510).
- Claude Opus 4.5: accuracy –3.7% (78%→74.3% at N=5) with a dramatic faithfulness increase (0.270→0.891, p<0.001, d=2.73); it solved 82% of hard problems but broke 23% of easy ones.
- DeepSeek-v3.2 (98% baseline accuracy): faithfulness +0.101 at N=20 (p=0.018, d=0.50) with negligible accuracy change.
- Gemini-3-flash: accuracy +5% at N=5 with a slight faithfulness decrease (0.260→0.212).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Inference scaling can improve accuracy by aggregating confident but potentially unfaithful reasoning paths, primarily by solving harder problems while rarely introducing errors on easier ones.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple diverse reasoning samples increase the probability that at least one correct reasoning path is generated; majority voting then selects the correct answer even if many individual paths are unfaithful post-hoc rationalizations. The gains come from accessing latent correct intuitions rather than improving step-by-step reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** Unfaithful reasoning paths tend to be inconsistent or diverse, while correct intuitive answers are more consistently reproduced across samples, allowing aggregation to filter out noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** GPT-5.2 accuracy improves from 78% to 90% at N=5 while faithfulness remains stable (0.540 to 0.510). GPT-5.2 solves 82% of hard problems at N=20 while breaking only 13% of easy ones. Self-consistency literature notes reliance on diverse path sampling for accuracy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Inference scaling can drastically increase faithfulness at the cost of accuracy by inducing an “overthinking” effect, where forced multi-path reasoning disrupts correct initial intuitions.
- **Mechanism:** When a model has a strong, accurate prior answer, generating multiple explicit reasoning paths forces it to second-guess and construct alternative explanations, often deviating from the correct intuitive answer. The early answering probe shows that reasoning frequently changes the answer, indicating genuine computational processing, but this deliberation introduces errors.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s single-sample CoT may often be a faithful expression of a direct answer retrieval, and scaling disrupts this efficient pathway.
- **Evidence anchors:** Claude Opus 4.5 accuracy drops from 78% to 74.3% while faithfulness jumps from 0.270 to 0.891 at N=5. The paper explicitly labels this the “overthinking effect” where Claude “second-guesses correct initial intuitions.” Claude breaks 23% of easy problems that were correct at N=1, explaining the net accuracy loss.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** For models near accuracy ceilings, inference scaling primarily improves faithfulness with negligible accuracy changes, as there are few hard problems left to solve and the main effect is reducing unfaithful reasoning on already-soluble problems.
- **Mechanism:** When baseline accuracy is very high (e.g., 98%), the set of problems that are truly hard is tiny. Additional sampling fails to yield new correct answers on these few hard cases but does increase the proportion of problems where reasoning meaningfully influences the answer, boosting faithfulness metrics.
- **Core assumption:** Faithfulness gains from scaling require that some correct paths exist that demonstrably change the answer from the early probe; with ceiling accuracy, these represent refinements on already-correct solutions rather than corrections.
- **Evidence anchors:** DeepSeek-v3.2 (98% accuracy) shows faithfulness gains (+0.101 at N=20) without accuracy change. The paper describes “ceiling effects with faithfulness gains” and notes that even highly capable models engage in more genuine reasoning with multiple samples. Scaling efficiency near zero accuracy gain for DeepSeek.

## Foundational Learning
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting**  
  *Why needed:* The study evaluates models using CoT, and faithfulness is measured relative to a CoT-generated answer. Understanding CoT's role as a reasoning elicitation technique is prerequisite to understanding why its faithfulness matters.  
  *Quick check:* What does a CoT prompt encourage the model to do that a direct answer prompt does not? It encourages the model to produce intermediate reasoning steps before final answer, often improving accuracy on complex tasks.

- **Self-Consistency / Inference Scaling**  
  *Why needed:* The independent variable is the number of samples (N) in self-consistency. One must understand that this is a compute-scaling technique at inference time that aggregates multiple reasoning paths via majority vote.  
  *Quick check:* How does the final answer get selected in self-consistency with N=5 samples? The final answer is the majority vote of the five generated answers; ties broken arbitrarily.

- **Faithfulness Measurement via Early Answering Probe**  
  *Why needed:* The core dependent variable is “reasoning faithfulness,” operationalized by comparing the answer from a CoT path to an answer given without reasoning. This probe's design and interpretation are central to all results.  
  *Quick check:* What is the logical inference if a model’s “answer only” response matches its CoT answer? The reasoning path is considered faithful (answer unchanged by the prompting to reason).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Dataset (GSM8K 100-problem subset) -> Sampling Engine (generate N CoT paths per problem) -> Answer Extraction (parse answers from paths) -> Aggregation (majority vote) -> Faithfulness Probe (early-answer query) -> Stats Module (bootstrap CIs, McNemar, t-tests, effect sizes) -> Difficulty Analyzer (easy/hard stratification)

- **Critical path:**  
  Problem → (N× sampling + extraction) → Majority vote → For each voted path: run early-answer probe → Record (correctness, faithfulness) → Aggregate over problems → Statistical comparison across N.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Sampling Diversity vs. Cost: Temperature 0.7 promotes diversity but increases chance of low-quality paths; N=5 chosen as Pareto frontier for most models.  
  - Probe Fidelity: Early answering probe tests necessity but not causality; alternative probes (perturbation, filler tokens) could yield different patterns.  
  - Generalizability: GSM8K math problems may not represent all reasoning domains; findings require validation on other benchmarks.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High variance in accuracy/faithfulness across bootstrap resamples (sample size too small?).  
  - Majority vote ties frequently (N too high for problem set?).  
  - Probe answers inconsistent due to API non-determinism despite temp=0.  
  - One model showing opposite pattern to literature expectations (like Claude) signals need for deeper mechanistic analysis.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline establishment:** Run N=1 for all models/problems to establish initial accuracy and faithfulness distributions. Identify “easy” vs. “hard” strata.  
  2. **Scaling effect isolation:** For each model, compare N=5 vs. N=1 using McNemar’s test (accuracy) and paired t-test (faithfulness). Visualize accuracy-faithfulness tradeoff.  
  3. **Difficulty decomposition:** For each model, compute: (a) proportion of hard problems solved at N=20, (b) proportion of easy problems broken at N=20. Correlate net accuracy change with this imbalance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Do inference scaling effects on faithfulness generalize to model families beyond the four tested (e.g., Llama, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma)?  
  *Basis in paper:* Future Work 7.1. *Why unresolved:* The study included only four frontier models; generalizability is unknown. *What evidence would resolve it:* Replication across a diverse set of additional models.

- **Open Question 2:** Would alternative faithfulness probes (e.g., perturbation analysis, attention visualization, causal interventions) confirm the model-dependent scaling patterns found with the early answering probe?  
  *Basis in paper:* Future Work 7.2. *Why unresolved:* The study relied on a single probe, risking measure-specific bias. *What evidence would resolve it:* Applying multiple faithfulness probes to the same scaling experiment across all models and comparing convergence.

- **Open Question 3:** Do inference scaling effects on faithfulness hold across reasoning domains beyond mathematics (e.g., commonsense, scientific, legal reasoning)?  
  *Basis in paper:* Future Work 7.3. *Why unresolved:* The experiment used only GSM8K math problems; domain transfer is untested. *What evidence would resolve it:* Conducting the same experimental design on a diverse suite of benchmarks representing multiple reasoning domains.

- **Open Question 4:** What mechanistic factors (training data, architecture, objectives) explain the divergent scaling behaviors observed (e.g., Claude's overthinking vs. GPT's aggregation)?  
  *Basis in paper:* Future Work 7.4. *Why unresolved:* The empirical study identified patterns but not underlying causes. *What evidence would resolve it:* Comparative mechanistic analyses (e.g., probing internal representations, training ablations) correlated with scaling outcomes.

- **Open Question 5:** Can adaptive inference scaling (varying N based on problem difficulty or model uncertainty) mitigate accuracy–faithfulness tradeoffs and improve efficiency?  
  *Basis in paper:* Future Work 7.5. *Why unresolved:* The study used fixed N; adaptive approaches remain unexplored. *What evidence would resolve it:* Developing and evaluating adaptive scaling algorithms that dynamically choose N per problem, measuring tradeoff improvements.

## Limitations
- Sample size: 100 GSM8K problems may not capture full difficulty distribution, leading to wide confidence intervals and limited power.
- Model version fidelity: Exact model identifiers on OpenRouter are unspecified, risking misidentification and hindering exact replication.
- Faithfulness probe validity: The early-answer probe measures answer change but may reflect instability rather than true reasoning infidelity; causal interpretation is unclear.

## Confidence
- **Tradeoff existence:** High confidence. Consistent cross-model patterns with statistical support and moderate-to-large effect sizes.
- **Model-specific magnitudes:** Medium confidence. Estimates are stable across bootstraps but sample size is modest; API nondeterminism could introduce noise.
- **Explanatory mechanisms (Mechanisms 1–3):** Low confidence. They are plausible post-hoc narratives but lack direct experimental validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Full-benchmark replication:** Compute scaling effects on the complete GSM8K test set (1,300+ problems) to reduce sampling error and confirm effect sizes.
2. **Probe triangulation:** Compare early-answer faithfulness with alternative measures (e.g., step importance perturbation, consistency across reasoning variations) to establish construct validity.
3. **Generalization study:** Evaluate same scaling-faithfulness dynamics on non-math reasoning benchmarks (e.g., logical deduction, commonsense QA) and with open-weight models (e.g., Llama 3, Mistral) to test portability.
