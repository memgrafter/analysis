# Temporal Difference Learning with Constrained Initial Representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11800  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11800  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** CIR matches or surpasses state-of-the-art off-policy RL methods (e.g., SimBa, TD-MPC2) on 49 state-based continuous control tasks while using substantially less computation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Constrained Initial Representations (CIR), a framework to improve sample efficiency in off-policy reinforcement learning by mitigating distribution shift caused by out-of-distribution (OOD) samples. CIR combines three main components: (1) constraining initial representations via Tanh activation and normalization (AvgRNorm, LayerNorm) to bound feature magnitudes, (2) a U-shape critic network with skip connections to preserve gradient flow in deep architectures, and (3) convex Q-learning to replace conservative target estimation with a flexible min-max blend. Theoretical analysis supports convergence and variance reduction. Empirical evaluations on DeepMind Control Suite, HumanoidBench, and ODRL benchmarks show CIR achieves competitive or superior performance with lower computational cost. Ablation studies validate the necessity of each design choice.

## Method Summary
CIR modifies the critic network of a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) backbone. The critic processes observations through an initial layer that applies linear transformation, LayerNorm, AvgRNorm (scaling by a small constant *c*), and Tanh to produce bounded features. These features pass through a down-sampling path (L layers of Linear → LayerNorm → elu) and a symmetric up-sampling path (L layers with linear skip additions from corresponding down layers). The final linear head outputs Q-values. Training uses convex Q-learning targets: `y = r + γ(λ·min(Q₁,Q₂) + (1-λ)·max(Q₁,Q₂) − α·logπ(a′|s′))`, with λ tuned per benchmark. Additional techniques include Sample Multiple Reuse (reusing each batch M=2 times) and standard SAC hyperparameters (e.g., batch size 256, Adam lr 3e-4). The actor remains a vanilla two-layer MLP.

## Key Results
- CIR matches or exceeds SimBa and TD-MPC2 on 49 tasks across DMC, HumanoidBench, and ODRL while reducing GPU memory and wall-clock time.
- Ablations confirm that removing Tanh, skip connections, or convex Q-learning significantly harms performance, especially on hard tasks.
- On DMC hard tasks, CIR underperforms some baselines, indicating limitations in complex domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Constraining initial representations with Tanh and normalization mitigates distribution shift by bounding feature magnitudes.
- **Mechanism:** AvgRNorm scales inputs by a small constant *c* (default 0.1) divided by the per-sample mean absolute value, followed by LayerNorm and Tanh (range [-1,1]). This ensures initial features *z_t* have small, bounded magnitudes. In deeper layers, bounded inputs limit the sensitivity `|y1 - y2|` to OOD perturbations, stabilizing TD updates. Formalized in Eq.7: `z_t = tanh(AvgRNorm(LayerNorm(Linear(o_t))`.
- **Core assumption:** Vanilla MLPs produce large, unstable representation shifts on OOD samples; bounded features reduce this sensitivity.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "employs a Tanh activation with normalization (AvgRNorm, LayerNorm) on the first layer" and "constrain the initial representations... alleviate the distribution shift issue."
  - [section 1] "The Tanh function can also help alleviate the severe distribution shift caused by OOD input samples" and MLP inequality analysis.
  - [section 6.1] Ablation (NoTanh, NoInputNorm) shows large performance drops on DMC hard tasks.
- **Break condition:** If AvgRNorm *c* is too large, Tanh inputs saturate (`|x| >> 1`), causing vanishing gradients and failing to bound sensitivity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** U-shape skip connections preserve gradient flow in deep critics, enabling effective scaling.
- **Mechanism:** The U-shape architecture comprises a down-sampling path (Eq.8: `x_t^l = Linear(LayerNorm(x_t^{l-1}))` with elu) and an up-sampling path (Eq.9: `x_t^{2L−l+1} = x_t^l + Linear(LayerNorm(Linear(x_t^{2L−l})))`). Skip connections add outputs from down layers directly to up layers, creating linear pathways for gradients. This mitigates vanishing gradients and allows low-level features to inform deeper layers.
- **Core assumption:** Deep MLPs suffer from gradient degradation; residual-like paths maintain gradient magnitude.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "skip connection module to provide a linear pathway from the shallow layer to the deep layer."
  - [section 5.2] U-shape design rationale: "enables effective multi-scale feature extraction" and "combines shallow low-level information with deep abstract representations."
  - [section 6.2] NoSC ablation (removing skips) yields significant performance drop on HumanoidBench.
- **Break condition:** Without skips, deep critics suffer from gradient explosion/vanishing, particularly on complex tasks; performance degrades as depth increases.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Convex Q-learning reduces conservatism introduced by bounded representations, improving value estimation flexibility.
- **Mechanism:** Replaces clipped double Q-learning's `min` target with a convex combination: `y = r + γ(λ·min + (1-λ)·max − αlogπ)`. The `max` term injects optimism, counteracting potential underestimation bias from bounded Tanh outputs, while `min` retains stability. Default λ=0.3 for DMC/ODRL, λ=1.0 for HumanoidBench.
- **Core assumption:** Clipped double Q-learning is overly conservative with constrained networks; blending min/max yields a less biased target.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "convex Q-learning that allows a more flexible value estimate and mitigates potential conservatism."
  - [section 5.3] Theorem 5.1 guarantees convergence for any λ∈(0,1].
  - [section 6.3] Ablation: CDQ variant underperforms convex Q-learning on multiple tasks.
- **Break condition:** Poor λ choice (e.g., λ→0 causes overestimation bias, λ=1 reverts to conservative CDQ).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input processing (Linear → LayerNorm → AvgRNorm → tanh) → Down-sampling path (L layers: Linear → LayerNorm → elu) → Up-sampling path (L layers: skip addition + Linear → LayerNorm → elu) → Critic head (Linear)
- **Critical path:** Bounded initial features *z_t* → down-sampling layers (progressively compress features) → up-sampling layers (with skip additions from down path) → final linear head outputting Q-values.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Tanh vs. other bounded activations:* Tanh provides smooth, sign-preserving bounds; ablations show sigmoid/softmaxperform poorly (Table 9).
  - *Depth (L):* L=2 balances capacity and stability; deeper networks yield diminishing returns (Fig.10 left).
  - *AvgRNorm coefficient c:* Smaller *c* (default 0.1) prevents Tanh saturation; large *c* harms hard tasks (Fig.9).
  - *Convex λ:* Requires per-benchmark tuning (λ=0.3 for DMC/ODRL, 1.0 for HumanoidBench).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - NoTanh/NoInputNorm: High variance and instability on hard tasks (Fig.5, Table 12).
  - NoSC (no skip connections): Performance drop, especially on HumanoidBench (Table 11, Fig.5).
  - CDQ instead of convex Q-learning: Significant underperformance (Fig.5, Table 12).
  - Excessive *c*: Tanh saturation, slow learning on DMC hard tasks (Fig.9).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation: Remove Tanh from initial layer (NoTanh). Expect large performance drop on DMC hard tasks, confirming bounded features are necessary.
  2. Ablation: Replace U-shape with plain deep MLP (NoSC). Expect instability or lower final return due to gradient flow issues.
  3. Algorithmic comparison: CIR with convex Q-learning vs. CIR with CDQ. Verify convex Q-learning achieves higher returns, especially on high-variance tasks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the dependency on U-shape critic networks be relaxed to allow deeper or alternative architectures?

  **Why unresolved:** Limits flexibility in scaling and experimenting with different backbones; overcoming it would broaden applicability.  
  **Resolution evidence:** Empirical demonstrations of CIR with various backbones (e.g., plain MLPs, transformers) achieving similar or better sample efficiency on standard benchmarks.

- **Open Question 2:** Why does CIR underperform on DMC hard tasks, and how can its sample efficiency be improved on complex domains?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] "the performance of CIR is inferior to baselines on DMC hard tasks."  
  **Why unresolved:** Performance gap suggests unaddressed challenges in harder environments; understanding and fixing these is crucial for robustness.  
  **Resolution evidence:** Diagnostic analyses identifying failure modes (e.g., representation collapse, gradient issues) followed by targeted modifications that close/reduce the gap on hard tasks.

- **Open Question 3:** Can the Constrained Initial Representations framework be effectively applied to offline reinforcement learning and pixel-based observations?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] "we only evaluate CIR on several state-based online continuous control RL tasks, while the applicability of CIR on offline RL tasks or pixel-based RL tasks remains to be explored."  
  **Why unresolved:** Extending to offline and visual domains would greatly increase impact and test generality of the representation constraint principle.  
  **Resolution evidence:** Successful implementation within offline RL algorithms (e.g., CQL) and on pixel-based benchmarks (e.g., Atari), showing comparable or superior sample efficiency.

- **Open Question 4:** What alternative normalization or bounded activation functions could further stabilize initial representations and enhance sample efficiency beyond the current Tanh+AvgRNorm combination?  
  **Basis:** [inferred] Conclusion notes opportunities for "proposing better representation stabilization methods (e.g., combining various normalization tricks)" and ablations reveal removing AvgRNorm or Tanh harms performance.  
  **Why unresolved:** Current design may not be optimal; exploring other techniques could yield higher performance.  
  **Resolution evidence:** Systematic experiments comparing different normalizations (BatchNorm, GroupNorm) and bounded activations (softsign, HardTanh) across diverse tasks, identifying superior combinations.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees assume linear or shallow two-layer features; extension to deep nonlinear critics lacks proof.
- Empirical evaluation confined to state-based continuous control benchmarks (DMC, HumanoidBench, ODRL); applicability to visual or discrete domains unknown.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: AvgRNorm scaling constant *c* and convex λ require task-specific tuning, limiting out-of-the-box usability.
- Architectural details ambiguous: exact placement of activations in up-sampling block and skip connection shape handling unspecified; minor variations could affect performance.
- Corpus query shows low neighbor relevance (average FMR ≈0.45), indicating limited prior work directly supporting proposed mechanisms.

## Confidence
- **Constrained initial representations** mitigate OOD distribution shift. *Confidence: Medium.* Supported by ablation and two-layer analysis, but no direct feature distribution/OOD measurements.
- **U-shape skip connections** preserve gradient flow for deep critics. *Confidence: Medium-High.* NoSC ablation shows clear benefit; gradient arguments standard, but gradient norms not reported.
- **Convex Q-learning** reduces CDQ conservatism. *Confidence: Medium.* Ablation gains and linear convergence theorem exist, but nonlinear behavior not formally analyzed.
- **Overall sample efficiency** vs. SOTA. *Confidence: Medium.* Strong results on 49 tasks, yet limited to specific suites and hyperparameter-sensitive.
