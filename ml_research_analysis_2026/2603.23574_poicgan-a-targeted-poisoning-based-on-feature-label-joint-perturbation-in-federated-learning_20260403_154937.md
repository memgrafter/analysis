# PoiCGAN: A Targeted Poisoning Based on Feature-Label Joint Perturbation in Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.23574
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23574
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A CGAN-based targeted poisoning attack achieves an attack success rate 83.97% higher than baselines while reducing main task accuracy by less than 8.87%, and remains effective (average ASR 67.17%) under standard FL defenses.

## Executive Summary
PoiCGAN is a novel targeted poisoning attack against federated learning systems. It employs a modified conditional generative adversarial network (CGAN) to generate stealthy poisoned samples through joint perturbation of features and labels. By training the discriminator to associate images from a source class with a target label, the generator can produce samples that automatically flip the label while maintaining visual realism. Evaluated on industrial image datasets, PoiCGAN significantly outperforms prior attacks in success rate and stealthiness, and evades common aggregation defenses such as Krum, RLR, and FLAME.

## Method Summary
The attack framework consists of a CGAN tailored for the federated poisoning context. The generator receives a source image and a target label, producing a perturbed image that retains the semantics of the source but is classified as the target by the discriminator. The discriminator is trained not only to distinguish real from generated images but also to enforce correct label association. After local training on benign data, malicious clients generate poisoned samples and include them in their model updates, which are aggregated by the FL server. This joint feature-label perturbation makes the poisoning samples difficult to detect by statistical anomaly detectors, thus preserving stealth.

## Key Results
- Attack success rate (ASR) 83.97% higher than baseline poisoning methods.
- Main task accuracy drop limited to <8.87%, indicating high stealth.
- Average ASR of 67.17% under defenses (Krum, RLR, FLAME), demonstrating robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PoiCGAN succeeds because it produces poisoned samples that are both effective (cause misclassification of target class) and inconspicuous (resemble normal data). The feature-label joint perturbation ensures that the generated images are semantically consistent with the source class while the discriminator’s label association training forces the generator to embed the target label into the output. This yields samples that evade detection methods relying on feature or label anomalies.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Malicious Client -> CGAN (Generator + Discriminator) -> Poisoned Samples -> Federated Aggregation (server) -> Global Model

**Critical path**: 1. Malicious client trains CGAN on local dataset; 2. Generator produces perturbed images conditioned on target label; 3. Poisoned samples are mixed with benign updates and sent to server; 4. Server aggregates updates, poisoning the global model; 5. Global model exhibits targeted misclassification.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Perturbation magnitude: Larger perturbations increase attack effectiveness but raise detection risk.
- Sample quality: Higher fidelity improves stealth but may require more training resources.
- Attack scale: More malicious clients boost ASR but may trigger client-based defenses.

**Failure signatures**:
- Generated samples exhibit artifacts detectable by visual inspection or statistical tests.
- Discriminator fails to enforce label association, resulting in samples retaining original labels.
- Poisoned updates cause significant accuracy drops on main task, alerting defenders.

**First three experiments**:
1. *CGAN training validation*: Assess generated sample quality via FID/IS and label-flipping accuracy on a held-out set.
2. *FL poisoning simulation*: Integrate PoiCGAN into a FL environment with varying numbers of malicious clients and measure main task accuracy and ASR.
3. *Defense evasion test*: Run FL with Krum, RLR, and FLAME defenses, record ASR under different poisoning fractions.
