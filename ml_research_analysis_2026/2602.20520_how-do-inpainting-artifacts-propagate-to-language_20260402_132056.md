# How Do Inpainting Artifacts Propagate to Language?
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.20520
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20520
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: Diffusion inpainting artifacts systematically affect vision-language model outputs; reconstruction fidelity correlates with caption quality for semantically rich captions, smoother masking mitigates effects, and the propagation depends on caption richness.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how visual artifacts introduced by diffusion-based inpainting influence language generation in frozen vision-language models. Using a two-stage diagnostic pipeline, images are degraded with various masks (center removal, Gaussian blur, low-dimensional compression), reconstructed by Stable Diffusion (1.5/2/3), and then captioned by models such as BLIP, LLaVA, and Qwen2.5-VL. Across multiple datasets with natural captions, better reconstruction fidelity (lower MSE, LPIPS) consistently predicts higher caption quality (BLEU, METEOR, SBERT). Smoother masking preserves caption quality better than hard center masking. However, on datasets with linguistically impoverished captions (GTZAN, X‑ray), large fidelity variations produce no measurable caption changes. Analysis of a frozen ViT encoder reveals layer‑dependent attention drift localized to the reconstructed regions, suggesting a mechanistic pathway for artifact propagation.

## Method Summary
Authors employ a controlled two‑stage framework. First, input images from Flickr, RefCOCOg, TRUCE, ROCOv2, GTZAN, and X‑ray are degraded using three masking strategies: hard center removal, Gaussian blur, and a low‑dimensional degradation (color quantization, downsampling, JPEG). Second, degraded images are inpainted using Stable Diffusion 1.5, 2.0, and 3.0 (50 denoising steps, guidance 7.5, strength adjustments). Original and reconstructed images are fed to frozen captioning models (BLIP, LLaVA, Qwen2.5‑VL) to generate captions. Caption quality is evaluated against references via lexical metrics (BLEU‑1–4, METEOR, ROUGE‑L) and semantic metrics (SBERT, SimCSE). Reconstruction fidelity is measured by MSE, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. Additionally, a frozen ViT‑Base encoder is analyzed: CLS token cosine similarity and layer‑wise attention drift (Total Variation Distance) are computed, with spatial partitioning to localize drift to inpainted regions. Correlations (Pearson) across all (SD × mask) configurations assess systematic relationships.

## Key Results
- Reconstruction fidelity (lower MSE, LPIPS) strongly correlates with caption quality (higher SBERT, BLEU) across four rich‑caption datasets; SSIM shows erratic correlation.
- Smooth masking strategies (Gaussian blur, low‑dimensional degradation) yield higher caption quality and smaller representational drift than hard center masking.
- On caption‑impoverished datasets (GTZAN, X‑ray), large variations in reconstruction fidelity produce no measurable change in caption metrics, highlighting the necessity of semantic richness for artifacts to propagate.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Diffusion inpainting artifacts alter the visual representations processed by the vision encoder, leading to measurable changes in language generation.
- **Mechanism:** Artifacts (e.g., pixel errors, perceptual distortions) shift the CLS token embedding and induce layer‑dependent attention drift in the frozen ViT encoder. These representational changes propagate to the language decoder, altering caption content. The captioning model, unaware of the reconstruction, treats synthesized content as genuine visual evidence.
- **Core assumption:** The vision‑language pipeline has no mechanism to detect or compensate for upstream reconstruction errors when models are frozen.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "inpainting artifacts lead to systematic, layer‑dependent changes in model behavior."
  - [section 4] Figure 5 shows increasing attention TVD with depth; Figure 7 shows cosine similarity drop; Figure 8 shows spatial localization of drift to reconstructed regions.
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence on artifact detection in 3D/medical reconstructions (e.g., "Puzzle Similarity," "Clinical Impact of Generative Inpainting") supports that inpainting introduces detectable changes, but direct propagation to language is not established in these neighbors—relevance is indirect.
- **Break condition:** If the vision encoder is fine‑tuned or if the captioning model includes reconstruction‑aware modules (e.g., uncertainty estimation), the observed drift may be mitigated.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The type of masking/degradation applied before inpainting modulates the severity of artifact propagation.
- **Mechanism:** Hard center masking creates sharp spatial discontinuities that remove semantic cues, forcing the inpainting model to hallucinate content with larger representational shifts. Smoother degradations (Gaussian blur, low‑dimensional compression) preserve coarse structure, leading to less disruptive reconstructions and smaller attention/embedding drifts.
- **Core assumption:** Abrupt spatial changes are more disruptive to the vision encoder’s hierarchical feature extraction than gradual degradations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Smoother masking strategies (Gaussian blur, low‑dimensional degradation) preserve caption quality better than hard center masking."
  - [section 4] Figures 6–8 show that Gaussian/low‑dimensional masking yields closer UMAP alignment, higher cosine similarity, and lower attention drift than center masking.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus neighbors, which focus on artifact reduction in other modalities (CT, MRI) rather than masking strategies’ effect on downstream language.
- **Break condition:** If the captioning model is highly robust to local image corruptions (e.g., trained on augmented data), the masking‑type effect may diminish.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Propagation of inpainting artifacts to language is contingent on the semantic richness of the caption ground truth.
- **Mechanism:** When captions contain fine‑grained, visually grounded details (e.g., object attributes, relationships), changes in reconstructed regions directly impact captioning metrics. With impoverished captions (e.g., genre labels, repetitive clinical phrases), the evaluation metrics have low sensitivity, so even large reconstruction errors do not produce measurable caption differences.
- **Core assumption:** Caption quality metrics require linguistic variability to detect changes; static/near‑constant outputs yield flat metric scores regardless of input quality.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "datasets with linguistically impoverished captions (GTZAN, X‑ray) show no meaningful reconstruction‑caption correlation."
  - [section F] Figures 12–13 and text describe failure cases where caption metrics remain nearly constant across reconstruction variants.
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors study artifact impact on *diagnostic* tasks (e.g., bone age estimation), not language generation; no direct evidence on caption richness as moderator.
- **Break condition:** If new datasets have both high reconstruction error and semantically rich captions, the correlation should reappear.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Diffusion‑based inpainting fundamentals (noise scheduling, latent space manipulation, artifact modes).
  - *Why needed here:* To understand why reconstructions are perceptually plausible but semantically unstable.
  - *Quick check question:* What typical artifact (e.g., texture smearing, object merging) might cause a “woman” to replace a “man” in a reconstructed region?
- **Concept:** Vision‑language model architecture (frozen ViT encoder + autoregressive/decoder LLM, attention flow from vision to language).
  - *Why needed here:* To trace how visual disturbances affect language generation via intermediate representations.
  - *Quick check question:* How does a shift in the CLS token embedding influence the first generated token in an autoregressive captioner?
- **Concept:** Reconstruction and captioning evaluation metrics (MSE/LPIPS for fidelity; BLEU/METEOR/SBERT for language).
  - *Why needed here:* To interpret correlation results and understand metric limitations (e.g., SSIM weakness, SBERT sensitivity).
  - *Quick check question:* Why might LPIPS correlate better with caption quality than SSIM in natural images?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input loader → Masking module (center/Gaussian/low-dim) → Diffusion inpainting model (SD1.5/2/3) → Frozen captioning model (BLIP/LLaVA/Qwen2.5‑VL) → Metric calculators (reconstruction & captioning) → Analysis tools (embedding similarity, attention drift).
- **Critical path:** For each image: (1) Apply degradation mask; (2) Run inpainting; (3) Generate captions for original & reconstructed; (4) Compute fidelity & caption metrics; (5) Extract ViT embeddings/attention for drift analysis.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Frozen vs. fine‑tuned models: Frozen isolates artifact effects but ignores potential adaptation.
  - Masking strategy: Hard masks maximize artifact severity; smoother masks test milder perturbations.
  - Dataset selection: Must include both rich and poor caption datasets to test boundary conditions.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - High reconstruction error but no caption drop → likely impoverished captions.
  - Low caption quality drop despite poor reconstruction → possible model robustness or masking too mild.
  - Non‑spatial attention drift → may indicate global degradation rather than localized artifact effect.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce Flickr correlation: Run all SD variants with center/Gaussian/low‑dim masks; verify MSE/LPIPS negatively correlates with BLEU/SBERT.
  2. Masking strategy ablation: Fix SD model, compare caption drop across masking types; expect center > Gaussian > low‑dim.
  3. Failure case validation: Run on GTZAN and X‑ray; confirm flat caption metrics despite LPIPS variations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### 1. Fine‑tuning sensitivity
How does end‑to‑end fine‑tuning of vision‑language models alter their sensitivity to inpainting artifacts? The current frozen‑model setup isolates reconstruction effects but does not capture potential adaptation in end‑to‑end trained systems. *Basis:* Systematic comparison of frozen vs. fine‑tuned VLMs using the same framework, measuring shifts in reconstruction‑caption correlations and internal attention patterns.

### 2. Generalization to other reconstructions
To what extent do findings generalize beyond diffusion inpainting to other visual reconstruction methods (e.g., GAN‑based inpainting, super‑resolution)? Different artifact profiles may interact differently with VLMs. *Basis:* Replicate the diagnostic framework with alternative inpainting architectures and evaluate consistency of correlations.

### 3. Linguistic determinants
Which specific linguistic properties of captions (e.g., length, detail, object specificity) determine whether reconstruction artifacts propagate? Datasets with impoverished captions showed no correlation, but the mediating properties remain unclear. *Basis:* Controlled caption augmentation experiments on failure datasets, varying specificity/detail, or analyzing caption syntax/semantics across successful vs. failing datasets.

### 4. Hyperparameter sensitivity
How sensitive are the observed correlations to inpainting hyperparameters (guidance scale, inference steps)? The study used fixed settings; varying them could change artifact severity. *Basis:* Extended evaluation across wider ranges of guidance scales and diffusion steps, correlating fine‑grained reconstruction metrics with caption quality.

## Limitations
- Masking parameters (central region size, Gaussian blur sigma, low‑dim specifics) are not precisely specified, risking replication variability.
- Model checkpoints (ViT‑Base source, captioning model sizes/resolutions) are underspecified.
- All evaluations used frozen models; fine‑tuned pipelines could yield different propagation dynamics.
- Only two linguistically impoverished datasets were tested; broader generalization of the caption‑richness boundary condition is uncertain.
- Observed correlations do not establish causation; attention drift may be epiphenomenal.

## Confidence

|---------------|------------|
| Reconstruction fidelity ↔ caption quality (on rich‑caption datasets) | High |
| Smooth masking > hard masking | High |

## Next Checks
1. **Mask parameter sweep:** Vary mask size (10–40% area), Gaussian σ, and low‑dim compression factors to map robustness of correlation and identify effect thresholds.
2. **Fine‑tuning experiment:** Fine‑tune the vision encoder on a mixture of original and inpainted images, then re‑run diagnostic; a weakened correlation would support that frozen models lack compensation.
3. **Causal embedding perturbation:** Artificially inject CLS embedding drift (measured between original and reconstructed) into original embeddings and measure resulting caption changes; matched effect would directly link representation shift to language output.
