# Benchmarking GNN Models on Molecular Regression Tasks with CKA-Based Representation Analysis
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.20573
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20573
- **Reference count**: 29
- **Primary result**: GNN+FP fusion consistently outperforms standalone GNNs and ML baselines, achieving >7% RMSE improvement across datasets, with CKA revealing low similarity between GNN and fingerprint embeddings (≤0.46).
## Executive Summary
The study benchmarks four Graph Neural Network (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE) on molecular regression tasks and introduces a hierarchical fusion framework (GNN+FP) that combines graph embeddings with ECFP4 fingerprints. Using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) with an RBF kernel, the authors analyze representational similarity across architectures and between GNN and fingerprint modalities.

Results show that GNN+FP fusion significantly improves performance, reducing RMSE by more than 7% compared to standalone GNNs and traditional ML baselines. CKA scores indicate that GNN and fingerprint embeddings capture largely independent information (CKA ≤0.46), explaining the fusion benefit. Among GNNs, isotropic models (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN) exhibit high convergence (CKA ≥0.88), while attention-based GAT learns more distinct representations (CKA 0.55–0.80). These findings highlight the complementary value of expert-crafted fingerprints and the divergent inductive bias of attention mechanisms on small molecular datasets.
## Method Summary
The authors used four molecular regression datasets (ESOL, Lipophilicity, RT, B3DB), each downsampled to 1,000 molecules. Inputs were SMILES strings converted to graphs via RDKit; node features included atomic number, degree, valence, charge, hybridization, and aromaticity (6-dimensional). ECFP4 fingerprints (1024-bit, radius 2) were generated. Four single-layer GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE) were trained with global mean pooling and a two-layer MLP regressor. The proposed GNN+FP hybrid concatenated the GNN graph embedding with a linearly transformed fingerprint embedding (linear layer + ReLU) before the MLP head. Traditional ML baselines (linear regression, SVM, random forest, XGBoost) were trained on fingerprints alone. Models were optimized per dataset using Adam optimizer and MSE loss for 100 epochs, with hyperparameter search (details unspecified). Performance was evaluated on hold-out test sets (20% split) via RMSE with 95% confidence intervals from bootstrap. Representational similarity was quantified using CKA with an RBF kernel; bandwidth σ was set to the median of squared Euclidean distances among embeddings.
## Key Results
- GNN+FP fusion achieved >7% RMSE improvement over standalone GNNs on all datasets.
- CKA between GNN and fingerprint embeddings was low (≤0.46), indicating互补 information.
- Isotropic GNNs (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN) converged highly (CKA ≥0.88), while GAT showed moderate divergence (CKA 0.55–0.80) from them.
## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Hierarchical fusion of GNN embeddings with ECFP4 fingerprints improves molecular regression performance on small datasets.
- **Mechanism**: The GNN learns structure-aware, data-driven embeddings from molecular graphs, while ECFP4 provides fixed, expert-knowledge-driven structural motifs. Fusion via concatenation merges these complementary latent spaces, allowing the regression head to leverage both learned topological patterns and pre-encoded chemical knowledge.
- **Core assumption**: The two embedding spaces are sufficiently orthogonal (non-redundant) to provide additive predictive value, especially when training data is limited.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "fusion framework consistently outperforms or matches the performance of standalone GNN (RMSE improvement > 7%)" and "CKA ≤0.46" indicating low similarity.
  - [section 3] Table 2 shows consistent RMSE reductions across datasets for all GNN+FP models; Figure 4 heatmap shows low GNN-FP CKA scores (avg ~0.3).
  - [corpus] Weak corpus support; neighboring papers explore multimodal fusion but do not directly benchmark this specific GNN+FP hierarchy or use CKA to justify it.
- **Break condition**: If GNNs were trained on very large datasets where they could autonomously learn all relevant structural motifs, the marginal benefit from fixed fingerprints might diminish. The current single-layer GNN design intentionally limits learning capacity, making fusion critical.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) with an RBF kernel can reliably quantify representational similarity between heterogeneous molecular embeddings (GNN vs. fingerprint) and between different GNN architectures.
- **Mechanism**: CKA computes the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) between two sets of neural activations (or embeddings). The RBF kernel captures non-linear similarities. The "median trick" for bandwidth σ ensures scale-invariance, making scores comparable across embedding dimensions. A low CKA score (near 0) suggests the models encode largely independent information; a high score (near 1) suggests convergent representations.
- **Core assumption**: The RBF kernel's σ, set via the median of pairwise squared distances, is an appropriate heuristic for the feature distribution of each model's embeddings, and CKA is a valid metric for comparing representations from fundamentally different sources (learned graph embeddings vs. fixed binary fingerprints).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] Mentions "CKA with RBF kernel" and reports specific scores (e.g., ≤0.46 for GNN-FP, ≥0.88 for isotropic GNNs).
  - [section 2.4] Details the implementation: "setting σ to the median of the squared Euclidean distances" and the goal of comparing "high-dimensional latent manifolds."
  - [corpus] [paper_id: 181797] directly discusses CKA for network similarity but focuses on Bayesian DL; [paper_id: 192617] uses kernel methods on SMILES but not CKA. No strong direct corpus validation for this specific application.
- **Break condition**: If the embedding dimensionality or distribution drastically differs (e.g., 1024-bit FP vs. 64-dim GNN embedding), the median trick might fail to normalize kernel scales appropriately, leading to misleading CKA scores. The metric assumes the compared layers are from networks trained on the same task/data.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Among standard message-passing GNNs, attention-based (GAT) architectures learn more distinct representations from isotropic aggregators (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN) on small molecular datasets.
- **Mechanism**: Isotropic models (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN) apply uniform or simple, non-attentional aggregation over neighbors, leading them to converge on similar topological feature summaries. GAT's learnable attention weights allow it to assign different importance to neighbors, enabling it to capture relational patterns that are not easily replicated by uniform averaging, resulting in a more divergent latent manifold.
- **Core assumption**: The representational divergence observed via CKA is primarily due to the architectural inductive bias (attention vs. isotropic aggregation) and not other factors like random seed, hyperparameters, or dataset specifics. The single-layer design prevents deeper hierarchical differences from dominating.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "high convergence between isotopic models like GCN, GraphSAGE and GIN (CKA ≥0.88), with GAT learning moderately independent representation (CKA 0.55–0.80)."
  - [section 3] Figure 5 heatmap shows consistently lower GAT vs. others CKA scores across all datasets, while other pairs are very high.
  - [corpus] [paper_id: 86597] explores multimodal contrastive learning, implying different modalities (like graph vs. sequence) are complementary, but does not analyze intra-GNN divergence. No direct corpus evidence on GAT divergence.
- **Break condition**: If dataset size increases significantly or GNN depth is increased, the isotropic models might also begin to diverge in representation due to different struggles with optimization or overfitting. The attention mechanism's advantage might diminish if global structural information (well-captured by fingerprints) becomes less critical.
## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Graph Neural Network (GNN) Message Passing  
  - Why needed here: It is the core mechanism by which the models process molecular graphs. Understanding how nodes aggregate neighbor information (GCN's normalized sum, GAT's weighted sum, GIN's MLP+sum, GraphSAGE's sampling/aggregation) is essential to interpret *why* their learned embeddings might differ or converge.  
  - Quick check question: How does the permutation invariance of the sum aggregator in GIN/GCN/GraphSAGE differ conceptually from the permutation-equivariant attention weights in GAT?

- **Concept**: Molecular Fingerprints (ECFP4)  
  - Why needed here: They are the standard, fixed-length, expert-derived representation against which GNNs are benchmarked and with which they are fused. Their high dimensionality and sparsity, and their basis in circular atomic neighborhoods, define the "traditional" baseline.  
  - Quick check question: What specific chemical information (e.g., atom counts, ring systems, functional groups) is explicitly encoded in a 1024-bit ECFP4 fingerprint that a single-layer GNN with only 6 atomic features might struggle to learn from scratch on small data?

- **Concept**: Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)  
  - Why needed here: It is the primary analysis tool used to move beyond performance metrics (RMSE) and investigate *how* and *what* the models are learning. It quantifies the core claim about representation complementarity and convergence.  
  - Quick check question: What is the mathematical difference between CKA and a simpler metric like cosine similarity of average embedding vectors, and why is CKA more suitable for comparing deep network layers?
## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  `Input SMILES` → `RDKit Preprocessing` → `Graph Construction (nodes=atoms, edges=bonds, features=atomic props)` + `ECFP4 Generation (1024-bit)`.  
  `Graph` → `GNN Layer (GCN/GAT/GIN/GraphSAGE)` → `Global Mean Pooling` → `Graph Embedding (h_g)`.  
  `ECFP4` → `Linear Layer + ReLU` → `Fingerprint Embedding (h_fp)`.  
  `Concat(h_g, h_fp)` → `Two-Layer MLP Head` → `Property Prediction`.  
  `All embeddings` → `CKA Analysis (RBF kernel, median σ)`.

- **Critical path**: The most crucial sequence for the novel contribution is: `Train standalone GNNs` → `Extract graph-level embeddings (h_g)` → `Generate ECFP4` → `Train GNN+FP fusion model` → `Extract all embeddings` → `Compute pairwise CKA`. A failure in the CKA computation (incorrect σ, mismatch in sample indices) invalidates the core analysis.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *GNN Depth*: Paper uses 1 layer to isolate architectural inductive bias. Deeper GNNs might improve pure GNN performance but obscure the fusion effect.  
  - *Fusion Strategy*: Simple concatenation is used. More complex fusion (e.g., attention-based, tensor product) might yield better results but increases parameter count, risk on small data.  
  - *CKA Kernel*: RBF is more expressive but introduces σ tuning. Linear CKA is simpler but may miss non-linear similarities. Median trick is a heuristic.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - *No Fusion Gain*: If GNN+FP does not outperform standalone GNNs, the complementarity assumption (low CKA) is false for that dataset/model.  
  - *Uniformly High CKA*: If all GNN-FP CKA scores are >0.8, embeddings are redundant, and fusion is just adding noise.  
  - *Unexpected GAT Convergence*: If GAT's CKA with others is also >0.9, the attention mechanism is not inducing distinct representations under these constraints.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Baseline Establishment**: Train and evaluate the four classical ML models (LR, SVM, RF, XGB) on ECFP4 features for all datasets. Establish the fingerprint-only performance ceiling.  
  2. **GNN Architecture Benchmark**: Train the four single-layer GNNs (no fusion) on the molecular graphs. Compare their RMSE to the ML baselines. Verify the initial claim that GNNs underperform on these small datasets.  
  3. **Fusion Efficacy Test**: Train the four GNN+FP hybrid models. Compute the percentage RMSE improvement over their standalone GNN counterparts (as in Table 2). Confirm the >7% average improvement claim.

## Limitations
- The paper does not disclose hyperparameter search spaces and optimal values for any model, hindering reproducibility.
- Critical details of the CKA implementation (sample size, layer used, median computation) are missing, raising concerns about the validity of similarity scores.
- The comparison of GNN architectures may be confounded by uncontrolled factors such as random seeds and dataset splits; no efforts to control these are reported.
## Confidence
- **Fusion effectiveness**: High  
- **CKA reliability**: Medium  
- **GAT distinctiveness**: Medium
## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter disclosure**: Supply the full search ranges and final hyperparameters for every model, or perform a sensitivity analysis demonstrating that the fusion gains hold across a broad hyperparameter grid.  
2. **CKA reproducibility test**: Implement a synthetic benchmark (e.g., embeddings that are identical, orthogonal, or linearly related) to verify that the median-bandwidth RBF CKA yields expected similarity scores, and report this as part of the supplementary material.  
3. **Ablation on model depth and data scale**: Evaluate the architectures with increased GNN layers (e.g., 3) and without downsampling (full dataset sizes) to test the hypotheses that fusion benefits diminish with capacity and that GAT divergence grows with task complexity.
