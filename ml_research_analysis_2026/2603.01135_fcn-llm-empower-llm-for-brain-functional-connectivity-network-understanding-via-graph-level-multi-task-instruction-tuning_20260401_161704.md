# FCN-LLM: Empower LLM for Brain Functional Connectivity Network Understanding via Graph-level Multi-task Instruction Tuning

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.01135
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01135
*   **Reference Count:** 21
*   **Primary Result:** Achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot disease classification accuracy (~54% for 6 classes) on external datasets (ABIDE II, CNP), significantly outperforming both supervised FCN models and prior FCN foundation models, which perform near random.

## Executive Summary
Brain functional connectivity network (FCN) analysis has been limited by a modality divide: FCNs are graph-structured data, while Large Language Models (LLMs) operate on text. This work proposes FCN-LLM to bridge this gap. It uses a multi-scale encoder to convert an FCN matrix into a sequence of feature tokens that are projected into an LLM's semantic space. The model is then trained on diverse, graph-level instruction tasks (predictive, judgment, comparative) over 19 subject attributes. A two-stage training regimen first aligns the FCN encoder's outputs with the LLM, then jointly fine-tunes on high-quality data. This framework enables the LLM to directly "read" and reason over FCNs, achieving strong zero-shot generalization to new datasets without site-specific fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
FCN-LLM employs a two-stage, multi-task instruction tuning pipeline. First, a multi-scale FCN encoder extracts hierarchical features from a static 116x116 correlation matrix: 116 ROI-level row vectors, 7 subnetwork-level averages (based on the Yeo atlas), and 1 whole-brain feature from a 2-layer GCN. These 124 features are concatenated and passed through an MLP to project them into the LLM's (Qwen2.5) input embedding dimension, replacing a special `<FCN>` token in a text prompt. In Stage I, only this encoder+projector is trained on a large volume (2.3M pairs) of augmented data to learn LLM-compatible representations while the LLM remains frozen. In Stage II, the entire model is fine-tuned on a smaller, high-quality dataset (426K pairs) of original FCNs, allowing the LLM to integrate the new modality. The instruction tasks span three paradigms: predicting a single attribute, judging the truth of a statement, and comparing two FCNs, which collectively teach both attribute grounding and relational population knowledge.

## Key Results
*   On external zero-shot test sets (ABIDE II, CNP), FCN-LLM (3B/7B) achieves SOTA disease classification accuracy (~54%), while supervised FCN models and foundation models like BrainNPT and BrainMass perform near random (~16-17%).
*   Internal test set performance remains competitive with supervised models, demonstrating effective cross-dataset transfer without site-specific adaptation.
*   Ablation studies confirm the necessity of the multi-scale encoder (each scale contributes uniquely) and the comparative instruction paradigm (its removal degrades disease classification by 2.25%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Multi-Scale Hierarchical Feature Extraction
The multi-scale encoder captures non-redundant information at different granularities. ROI features capture local connectivity patterns of each brain region. Subnetwork features (averaged over 7 functional modules) capture mid-level coordinated dynamics. The whole-brain GCN feature captures global topological properties. Concatenating these provides a comprehensive representation that the LLM can interpret. Ablation shows all scales are critical; removing any significantly harms performance on specific tasks (e.g., subnetwork for disease, global for gender), confirming the hierarchical signal is essential.

### Mechanism 2: Graph-Level Multi-Paradigm Instruction Tuning
Training on predictive, judgment, and comparative tasks forces the LLM to learn more than simple attribute classification. The comparative paradigm, which presents two FCNs and asks for a relational judgment (e.g., which has higher connectivity), explicitly teaches the model inter-subject contrasts and relational patterns within the population. This builds a more robust, generalizable understanding of how FCN variations map to attributes, which is crucial for zero-shot transfer to new datasets with different distributional shifts.

### Mechanism 3: Two-Stage Alignment and Joint Fine-Tuning
Stage I (frozen LLM, augmented data) learns to map the multi-scale FCN features into the LLM's expected input space without risking catastrophic forgetting or overfitting to augmentation artifacts. Stage II (unfrozen full model, original data) allows the LLM to deeply integrate the now-aligned FCN tokens and adapt the encoder based on cleaner data. This staged approach prevents the model from overwriting its core language knowledge. Empirically, extending Stage II beyond one epoch reduces zero-shot performance (Figure 3a), confirming the risk of over-tuning to the fine-tuning distribution.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Multi-Scale Graph Representation Learning**
    *   **Why needed:** FCNs are graphs. Disease/phenotype signals may reside at local (ROI), modular (subnetwork), or global topological levels. A single graph embedding is insufficient; a hierarchical representation is necessary to capture all potential sources of signal.
    *   **Quick check:** If you only used the whole-brain GCN feature vector and omitted the ROI and subnetwork features, what specific capacity would be most impaired, and what evidence supports this?

*   **Concept: Instruction Tuning for Non-Language Modalities**
    *   **Why needed:** FCNs lack a natural language description. Instruction tuning with synthetic Q&A pairs "speaks" about FCNs, teaching the LLM to reason over them as a context modality rather than generating them. This requires a curated dataset linking FCNs to attributes.
    *   **Quick check:** Why is the "comparative" paradigm considered crucial for learning cross-subject associations, and how does this differ from just doing many predictive tasks?

*   **Concept: Zero-Shot Transfer via Semantic Alignment**
    *   **Why needed:** The goal is to generalize to new sites/scanners without retraining. By aligning FCN features into the LLM's pre-trained semantic space (where "autism" and "male" have meaning), the model can generalize to any dataset where the text label semantics are identical, even if the FCN data distribution shifts.
    *   **Quick check:** What is the fundamental architectural reason FCN-LLM beats supervised models on zero-shot transfer, and why do supervised FCN foundation models fail at zero-shot?

## Architecture Onboarding
FCN Matrix (116x116) -> Multi-Scale Encoder -> [ROI features (116), Subnet features (7), Global feature (1)] -> Concatenation (124-dim) -> MLP Projection -> 124 Projected Tokens -> LLM Input Sequence (replaces `<FCN>` token) -> LLM Core (Qwen2.5) -> Text Output Generation

**Critical Path:** The FCN data is transformed into a sequence of continuous token embeddings that are inserted into a text prompt template. The LLM processes this mixed sequence of text and "FCN tokens" and generates a text answer via standard next-token prediction.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **MLP Projection vs. Cross-Attention (Q-Former):** MLP is simpler and more parameter-efficient for Stage I alignment but is a fixed map that may lose structural nuances. Cross-attention could learn queried, adaptive representations but increases complexity.
*   **Fixed Atlas (AAL116/Yeo):** Simplifies the encoder and ensures consistent feature dimensions. However, it discards nuances of alternative parcellations and limits adaptability to new atlases.
*   **Static FCN Input:** Uses a single correlation matrix per subject. Ignores dynamic FCN (time-varying), which may contain critical state-dependent information for certain tasks.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **Zero-shot performance near random:** Likely failed Stage I alignment (encoder didn't produce LLM-compatible features) or catastrophic forgetting from excessive Stage II epochs.
*   **Poor continuous attribute prediction (e.g., age):** Multi-scale features may lack precise numerical signal, or the LLM struggles with regression in this setup.
*   **Gibberish/refusal outputs:** LLM backbone wasn't sufficiently adapted to accept the foreign token type; the MLP projection may be inadequate.
*   **Stage II overfitting:** As shown in the paper, validation loss on zero-shot sets increases after 1 Stage II epoch, indicating over-tuning to the fine-tuning distribution.

**First 3 Experiments (for validation):**
1.  **Ablate Multi-Scale Encoder:** Train versions using only ROI, only Subnetwork, only Global, and all combinations. Validate on internal test for Disease/Gender to confirm each scale's unique contribution.
2.  **Stage I Data/Epoch Sensitivity:** Vary the proportion of Stage I augmented data (10%, 50%, 100%) and number of Stage I epochs (1, 3, 5). Measure resulting zero-shot ABIDE II accuracy to find the alignment threshold.
3.  **Prompt & Parsing Robustness:** For a fixed trained model, systematically reword prompts and shuffle candidate labels. Measure output consistency (e.g., self-BLEU) and metric stability to identify fragile prompt dependencies.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Dynamic Functional Connectivity:** How can dynamic (time-varying) FCNs be incorporated, and would this improve performance on tasks requiring temporal understanding?
*   **Multimodal Integration:** How can FCN-LLM be extended to integrate other modalities (structural MRI, diffusion MRI) for a more comprehensive brain representation?
*   **Leveraging Neuroscience Knowledge:** How can richer language-based knowledge (neuroscience literature, biomedical LLM pretraining) be used to enhance understanding and generalization?
*   **Biomarker Reliability:** Are the attention-based biomarkers derived from the model reliable, reproducible, and clinically meaningful?

## Limitations
*   **Reproducibility Gaps:** Exact prompt templates (50 per attribute) and MLP projection architecture details are omitted, hindering faithful recreation.
*   **Fixed Architecture & Modality:** Locked to AAL116 atlas and static FCNs. Unknown if it generalizes to other parcellations, dynamic connectivity, or multimodal inputs.
*   **Unmeasured Alignment:** The quality of semantic alignment between FCN embeddings and the LLM's space is inferred but not directly probed or visualized.

## Confidence
*   **Zero-shot SOTA on ABIDE II/CNP:** **High.** Clear external test results against strong baselines.
*   **Multi-scale Encoder Necessity:** **High.** Ablation study (Fig 3b) empirically validates each scale's contribution.
*   **Two-stage Training Rationale:** **High.** Empirical validation (Fig 3a) shows over-tuning in Stage II harms generalization.
*   **Broader Transfer Beyond Tested Datasets:** **Low.** No validation on new scanners, populations, or atlas variants; site-specific effects remain an assumption.

## Next Checks
1.  **Ablate Multi-Scale Components:** Isolate and evaluate ROI-only, subnetwork-only, and global-only encoder versions on zero-shot disease classification to quantify the unique information captured by each hierarchical level.
2.  **Stage I Alignment Sufficiency:** Train with reduced Stage I data (e.g., 10% of 2.3M pairs) and epochs. Monitor if zero-shot performance collapses, confirming a minimum alignment requirement.
3.  **Prompt Parsing Sensitivity:** For a fixed model, perturb prompt wording (rephrase, shuffle labels) and parsing rules. Measure output consistency and metric variance to diagnose brittleness in the instruction interface.
