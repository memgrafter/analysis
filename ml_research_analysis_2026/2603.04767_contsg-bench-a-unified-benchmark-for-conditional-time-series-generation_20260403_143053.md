# ConTSG-Bench: A Unified Benchmark for Conditional Time Series Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.04767  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04767  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Introduces the first unified benchmark for conditional time series generation (ConTSG) with aligned datasets and comprehensive metrics, revealing that high fidelity does not guarantee condition adherence and that models struggle with fine-grained control and compositional generalization.

## Executive Summary
Conditional time series generation (ConTSG) lacks a standardized evaluation framework, impeding progress. The authors present ConTSG-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark that systematically evaluates ConTSG across three conditioning modalities (class label, attribute, text) and two semantic abstraction levels (morphological, conceptual). It comprises large-scale, well-aligned datasets and a dual-dimensional metric suite: generation fidelity (e.g., FID, statistical moments) and condition adherence (e.g., CTTP Score, DTW, CRPS). Benchmarking ten state-of-the-art models yields three key insights: (1) fidelity and adherence are orthogonal; text conditioning attains the highest ceiling but with high variance; (2) all models fail to match simple retrieval baselines on fine-grained local control and compositional generalization; (3) downstream utility (classification drop rate) varies across datasets and is not predicted by fidelity alone. These findings expose critical bottlenecks and suggest future work on inductive biases for structural controllability. The benchmark is publicly released to enable reproducible research.

## Method Summary
ConTSG-Bench constructs aligned multimodal conditions for eight diverse time series datasets (e.g., healthcare, meteorology). Raw series are normalized; an LLM generates morphological captions for each instance. A discovered attribute schema extracts structured attributes from captions; class labels are derived from unique attribute combinations. For PTB-XL and Weather, both morphological and conceptual descriptions are provided. The benchmark zoos ten conditional generators (e.g., diffusion, GAN, transformer-based) trained under standardized protocols (AdamW, grid search over learning rates/batch sizes, early stopping). A Contrastive Time-Text Pre-training (CTTP) encoder, trained per dataset, provides a joint embedding space for semantic alignment metrics. Evaluation computes fidelity (FID, Precision/Recall, statistical moments) and adherence (CTTP Score, J-FTSD, DTW, CRPS). Additional analyses assess fine-grained control, compositional generalization, and downstream utility via classification drop rate.

## Key Results
- **Orthogonality**: Models with similar fidelity scores exhibit widely varying condition adherence, demonstrating that realism does not imply controllability.
- **Text conditioning ceiling**: Text-based guidance achieves the highest potential performance but suffers from large variance across models, indicating architectural sensitivity.
- **Generalization failure**: All models struggle with fine-grained segment control and novel attribute combinations, often underperforming naive retrieval baselines; downstream utility is dataset-dependent and unpredictable from fidelity metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The benchmark’s effectiveness stems from three interconnected mechanisms:

### Mechanism 1: Dual-axis evaluation uncovers orthogonality of fidelity and adherence
The benchmark defines two separate metric groups: fidelity measures marginal distribution similarity (e.g., FID, statistical moments), while adherence quantifies semantic alignment between generated series and conditions (e.g., CTTP Score, DTW). Reporting both dimensions reveals that high-quality generation does not guarantee condition following, as evidenced by models with similar fidelity but divergent adherence in experiments. This separation is valid as long as adherence metrics capture true semantic correspondence; the benchmark’s reference-based distances (DTW, CRPS) and joint embedding similarities (CTTP, J-FTSD) provide that evidence.

### Mechanism 2: Aligned multimodal datasets enable cross-modality comparison
For each time series instance, an LLM produces a morphological caption. A discovered attribute schema extracts structured attributes; class labels are derived by indexing unique attribute combinations. This pipeline ensures that class, attribute, and text conditions all encode the same underlying semantics, allowing fair comparison across conditioning modalities. The LLM’s accuracy in capturing temporal patterns and the preservation of meaning during attribute extraction are critical assumptions.

### Mechanism 3: Disentangling semantic abstraction exposes model inductive biases
Providing both morphological (surface-level) and conceptual (higher-level) conditions for PTB-XL and Weather isolates the effect of abstraction. Models must bridge abstract semantics to temporal structure. Observed performance gaps indicate a model’s ability to generalize from concrete to abstract conditions. If the two condition types were semantically equivalent, no gap would appear; the empirical gaps validate the distinction and highlight the need for condition-adaptive architectures.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Dataset Construction Module (LLM captioning, attribute schema discovery, label mapping) -> Model Zoo (10 conditional generators) -> Evaluation Engine (fidelity/adherence metrics, CTTP encoder training per dataset) -> Analysis Module (RQ-specific protocols)

**Critical path**:  
1. Preprocess raw time series (normalization).  
2. Build aligned multimodal conditions via LLM pipeline.  
3. For each model: train on training split, validate, generate K samples per test condition.  
4. Compute metrics (embedding-based require CTTP; statistical use raw series).  
5. Aggregate results by research question, normalize/rank, interpret.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *CTTP vs. statistical metrics*: CTTP captures semantics but requires training a separate encoder; statistical metrics are interpretable but may miss nonlinear patterns.  
- *Best-of-K vs. full distribution*: Best-of-K highlights top samples but may overestimate adherence; full distribution reflects uncertainty.  
- *LLM annotation*: Scalable vs. potential noise; human validation limited.  
- *Cross-dataset ranking*: Normalizing to ranks enables comparison but can obscure dataset-specific difficulty.

**Failure signatures**:  
- High fidelity + low adherence → condition ignored.  
- Large accuracy drop from head to tail in compositional splits → poor attribute disentanglement.  
- Near-chance fine-grained control → lack of segment-aware architecture.  
- Negative downstream drop rate → generated data harms classifier training (mode collapse).  
- Text-conditioned models show highest variance → architectural sensitivity to text encoder design.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Overall benchmarking (RQ1)** – Run all models on all eight datasets, compute both metric groups, produce per-model rankings. Establishes baseline capability gap.  
2. **Semantic abstraction comparison (RQ2)** – On PTB-XL and Weather, generate under morphological and conceptual conditions, evaluate DTW/CRPS against source series, compare rank stability. Tests sensitivity to condition semantics.  
3. **Fine-grained control (RQ3)** – On Synth-U and TelecomTS-Segment, assess segment-level pattern adherence via classifier accuracy and retrieval, and measure temporal order preservation. Diagnoses local controllability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question**: What architectural inductive biases can enable models to dynamically adjust their conditioning mechanisms based on the semantic abstraction level (morphological vs. conceptual) of natural language descriptions?  
**Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors observe dataset-dependent variation in model performance on morphological vs. conceptual conditions and suggest future work on condition-adaptive architectures.  
**Why unresolved**: Current models treat text condition uniformly regardless of abstraction level; no known mechanism for automatic adaptation.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluation on paired morpho-conceptual datasets showing consistent performance across abstraction levels when using such adaptive interfaces.

### Open Question 2
**Question**: How can we design training objectives or architectural constraints that achieve precise fine-grained (e.g., segment-level) control while preserving global time series coherence?  
**Basis in paper**: [explicit] Models fail to achieve segment-level alignment comparable to retrieval baselines; motivates segment-aware objectives and architectures with explicit positional control.  
**Why unresolved**: Existing diffusion/GAN models optimize global alignment but lack explicit mechanisms for local pattern placement.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: A model that significantly exceeds retrieval accuracy on Synth-U and TelecomTS-Segment without sacrificing global fidelity.

### Open Question 3
**Question**: What architectural innovations enable robust compositional generalization in ConTSG, so models correctly synthesize novel attribute combinations by disentangling individual semantics?  
**Basis in paper**: [explicit] Models with strong condition adherence are more sensitive to novel combinations, indicating need for architectures that generalize attribute semantics beyond memorized correlations.  
**Why unresolved**: Performance drops on OOD combinations; higher adherence increases sensitivity.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Normalized retrieval accuracy on novel attribute combinations that does not degrade and absolute accuracy surpasses weaker models.

### Open Question 4
**Question**: Why are standard generation fidelity metrics poor predictors of downstream utility, and how can we design alignment-aware generative objectives that optimize discriminative utility?  
**Basis in paper**: [inferred] Downstream utility varies substantially across datasets and is not reliably predicted from fidelity metrics alone.  
**Why unresolved**: Theoretical link between marginal distribution matching and conditional discriminability is unclear; possibly missing alignment in representation space.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Correlation analysis across datasets showing which fidelity/adherence metrics correlate with utility, or a generative loss that directly minimizes downstream task loss.

## Limitations
- **Incomplete description of CTTP encoder**: Architecture and hyperparameters (e.g., PatchTST dimensions, contrastive temperature) are not fully specified, risking inconsistent adherence metric computation.
- **LLM-based alignment potential noise**: Automated captioning and attribute extraction lack systematic human validation; semantic drift could undermine cross-modality comparability.
- **Missing reproducibility details**: Random seeds, environment specifications, and data splitting protocols are not provided, limiting stable replication and variance estimation.

## Confidence
- **Orthogonality of fidelity and adherence** – Medium (supported by benchmark experiments but contingent on CTTP robustness).  
- **Alignment across conditioning modalities** – Medium (pipeline enables scaling but accuracy unverified).  
- **Semantic abstraction gap** – Medium (demonstrated on two datasets; generalizability uncertain).
