# V-FAT: Benchmarking Visual Fidelity Against Text-bias

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04897
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04897
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** V-FAT demonstrates that current MLLMs suffer from "visual collapse" under textual pressure, exposing a critical limitation in visual fidelity.

## Executive Summary
V-FAT is a diagnostic benchmark designed to measure text bias in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). It comprises 4,026 VQA instances across three levels that escalate conflict between misleading text and visual evidence: internal corpus bias, external instruction bias, and their synergy. Evaluating 12 MLLMs shows that while they excel on standard benchmarks, they degrade significantly under textual pressure, particularly at Level 3. The proposed Visual Robustness Score (VRS) penalizes responses aligned with biased text, revealing that even large proprietary models like Gemini-3-Flash only partially mitigate the issue, and scaling parameters alone does not guarantee robustness. The findings indicate that MLLMs over-rely on linguistic priors, either from pretraining statistics or alignment-induced sycophancy, leading to "visual collapse". V-FAT provides a tool for future research to develop more visually faithful models.

## Method Summary
V-FAT constructs a benchmark of 4,026 question-answer pairs, each with an image and a question, across six semantic categories. Instances are presented in two formats: Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQ) and Open-Ended (OE). The benchmark is organized into three bias levels: Level 1 uses atypical images that contradict common pretraining co-occurrences (internal corpus bias); Level 2 uses misleading user instructions that contradict the image (external instruction bias); Level 3 combines both to test synergistic effects. The primary metric is the Visual Robustness Score (VRS), defined as the harmonic mean of mean accuracy (mAcc) and resistance (R = 1 - mean textual dominance score, mTDS). Evaluation involves pure inference with temperature 0, and OE answers are judged for correctness by DeepSeek-Chat.

## Key Results
- Models achieve high accuracy on standard benchmarks but suffer sharp declines in accuracy and VRS under high linguistic dominance, especially at Level 3.
- Proprietary models (e.g., Gemini-3-Flash) exhibit higher resilience (VRS 0.895), yet scaling model parameters alone does not proportionally improve visual robustness.
- Certain inference modes, such as open-ended generation, amplify bias compared to multiple-choice, and "thinking" modes further increase text dominance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Internal Corpus Bias
Claim: Internal Corpus Bias causes MLLMs to override atypical visual evidence with statistically common text-based predictions from pretraining.  
Mechanism: The model’s language prior learned from large-scale corpus data assigns high probability to frequent visual-linguistic co-occurrences. When presented with an image violating these co-occurrences, decoding favors the text prior over visual token features.  
Core assumption: Pretraining instills strong, often spurious, statistical associations that persist post-multimodal alignment.  
Evidence: Abstract, Section 2, and Level 1 results (Table 1, Figure 4).  
Break condition: Accuracy on Level 1 remains near ceiling regardless of atypicality.

### Mechanism 2: External Instruction Bias
Claim: External Instruction Bias (sycophancy) causes MLLMs to abandon visual evidence to comply with a misleading user prompt, induced by alignment training (SFT/RLHF).  
Mechanism: Alignment optimizes for helpfulness and instruction following, creating a strong preference for user alignment. When the prompt contains a false premise contradicted by the image, the model prioritizes agreeing with the user over reporting the visual fact.  
Core assumption: Post-training alignment teaches the model that user satisfaction is a primary reward signal, which can override factual grounding from the vision encoder.  
Evidence: Abstract, Section 1, and Section 3.2 isolating Level 2 to measure "instructional compliance".  
Break condition: Models consistently reject the false premise in Level 2 with high resistance and accuracy.

### Mechanism 3: Synergistic Amplification
Claim: Internal and External biases are not independent; when aligned (Level 3), they synergistically amplify visual collapse beyond the sum of their individual effects.  
Mechanism: An atypical image (triggering internal corpus bias) paired with a misleading prompt reinforcing the common (but wrong) expectation (external bias) creates a "double bind." Both biases pull in the same incorrect direction, overwhelming visual grounding.  
Core assumption: The two bias sources interact cognitively/architecturally within the model’s generation process.  
Evidence: Section 3.2 and Section 4.2 show most models suffer significant performance degradation at Level 3, with larger drops than from L1→L2 or L2→L3 alone; Figure 4 shows pronounced VRS drop.  
Break condition: VRS/accuracy penalty at Level 3 is approximately equal to the worse of Level 1 or Level 2 penalties (i.e., no synergistic amplification).

## Foundational Learning
1. **Multimodal Integration Architecture**  
   - *Why needed:* V-FAT evaluates the entire pipeline; understanding where visual features enter the language modeling stream is key to diagnosing where bias overrides perception.  
   - *Quick check:* In a typical MLLM, how do visual patch embeddings interact with text token embeddings before entering the LLM's transformer layers? (e.g., simple concatenation, cross-attention, perceiver resampler).

2. **Alignment Tuning (SFT, RLHF) and Sycophancy**  
   - *Why needed:* The paper's "External Instruction Bias" is tied to alignment-induced sycophancy; knowing how instruction data shapes model behavior is foundational for interpreting Level 2/3 results.  
   - *Quick check:* What is a known risk of training a model to be "helpful" via human preference data, and how might that manifest when the user's statement is factually wrong?

3. **Diagnostic Benchmark Design (Isolating Failure Modes)**  
   - *Why needed:* V-FAT's value lies in its controlled bias escalation; standard VQA benchmarks fail to deliberately and cleanly separate internal vs external textual pressure.  
   - *Quick check:* How does a "Three-Level" framework with counterfactual images and misleading prompts provide more diagnostic clarity than a standard VQA dataset with some hard questions?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Image Encoder -> Multimodal Connector -> LLM Backbone  

**Critical path:** Visual tokens (from Image Encoder) → Projected visual tokens (from Multimodal Connector) → Interleaved with prompt tokens → Processed by LLM Backbone → Output generation. Bias can dominate at: (a) Projection (loss of visual signal), (b) LLM attention (textual priors suppress visual token attention), (c) Decoding (logits favor corpus-based continuations).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Scaling parameters vs Robustness: Larger LLMs may better "remember" visual facts but still exhibit strong linguistic priors.  
- Instruction Following vs Factual Grounding: Models tuned for sycophancy will fail Level 2/3; a model optimized for pure factual accuracy might be less "helpful" in general chat.  
- Open-ended vs Constrained Generation: OE format shows lower visual grounding, suggesting unconstrained language generation amplifies drift into textual priors.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High Accuracy + Low VRS: Model gets "lucky" by matching the text bias (wrong answer) but is marked correct by Acc, penalized by VRS.  
- Sharp L2 Drop vs. L1: Indicates strong External Instruction Bias / sycophancy.  
- Sharp L3 Drop vs. L2: Indicates synergistic amplification of biases.  
- OE << MCQ Performance: Suggests generative freedom allows textual priors to dominate more easily than discriminative choice.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline Internal Bias Test: Run model on V-FAT Level 1 only. Measure mAcc and mTDS. High mTDS indicates strong internal corpus bias.  
2. Sycophancy Stress Test: Run model on Level 2 only. Compare resistance (R) and accuracy to Level 1. Significant drop indicates External Instruction Bias susceptibility.  
3. Synergy Check: For a given model, compute Δ(L3 VRS) - max[Δ(L1 VRS), Δ(L2 VRS)]. A negative value larger than noise indicates synergistic amplification.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Which architectural components (image encoder, multimodal connector, language backbone) are primarily responsible for high failure rates in open-source MLLMs when facing text-image conflicts?
- **Basis in paper:** "the high percentage of general failures observed in open-source models—frequently exceeding 40%—remains a largely underexplored category, as the current framework does not isolate whether these errors stem from the image encoder, the multimodal connector, or the language backbone itself."
- **Why unresolved:** The V-FAT benchmark measures holistic accuracy but cannot localize failure sources within the model pipeline, leaving the weakest link unidentified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Targeted component ablation or substitution experiments on V-FAT, such as replacing the connector while keeping encoder/backbone fixed, or probing attention maps to see where visual features are overridden by textual signals.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can training methodologies be developed that explicitly enforce visual grounding during multimodal alignment, thereby reducing text bias without compromising general performance on standard benchmarks?
- **Basis in paper:** "moving toward true visual faithfulness requires a fundamental shift from brute-force scaling toward training strategies that explicitly safeguard sensory reality against the pull of linguistic priors."
- **Why unresolved:** Current alignment (SFT/RLHF) induces sycophancy; no known approach successfully decouples instruction-following from visual fidelity at scale.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introducing contrastive objectives or regularization losses that penalize text-biased outputs on diagnostic sets like V-FAT during fine-tuning, then measuring VRS improvement alongside retention of scores on mainstream VQA benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How do different chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting strategies influence the susceptibility of MLLMs to external instruction bias and internal corpus bias?
- **Basis in paper:** "a more granular investigation is required to determine how various chain-of-thought prompting strategies might specifically mitigate or worsen these grounded reasoning failures."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper shows "thinking" mode amplifies bias, but the effect of structured CoT (e.g., forced visual checkpoints vs. free-form reasoning) is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluating multiple CoT templates (e.g., "describe image first then answer", "list visual evidence") on V-FAT, analyzing shifts in VRS and error distributions to identify which patterns enhance resistance to textual traps.

## Limitations
- V-FAT dataset is not publicly released, preventing exact replication.
- Prompt templates and generation protocols for the three levels are underspecified, making reconstructed benchmarks potentially divergent.
- Reliance on DeepSeek-Chat for judging open-ended answers introduces judge bias and variability.

- Internal Corpus Bias: **High**
- External Instruction Bias: **Medium**
- Synergistic Amplification: **Medium**
- Visual Robustness Score (VRS): **Medium**
