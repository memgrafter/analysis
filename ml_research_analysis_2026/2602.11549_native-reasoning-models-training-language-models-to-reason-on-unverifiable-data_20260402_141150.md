# Native Reasoning Models: Training Language Models to Reason on Unverifiable Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11549  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11549  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** NRT achieves state-of-the-art among verifier-free methods, improving average score by +10.2 over SFT and +5.4 over prior best RLPR on Llama-3.1-8B, with particularly strong gains on GSM8K (29.0 → 76.0) and MATH.

## Executive Summary
Native Reasoning Training (NRT) addresses a key limitation in training language models for reasoning: existing methods like supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) with chain‑of‑thought or reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) rely on costly human‑annotated reasoning traces or external reward functions, confining them to domains with ground‑truth verification. NRT reframes the problem by treating the reasoning process as a latent variable and learning directly from question‑answer pairs. It introduces an intrinsic reward that measures how much a generated reasoning trace increases the model’s own confidence in the correct answer, and a robust Weighted Sum aggregation that focuses on tokens the model finds uncertain. This combination enables models to develop reasoning abilities without any external supervision beyond the final answer. Empirically, NRT achieves state‑of‑the‑art performance among verifier‑free approaches across multiple model families (Llama, Mistral), delivering large gains on challenging math and reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MATH) while maintaining training stability and resisting the policy collapse that plagues simpler RL methods.

## Method Summary
NRT’s training procedure operates as follows: Given a batch of questions and their ground‑truth answers, the algorithm samples multiple reasoning traces from a stale copy of the policy (π_old) for each question. For each trace, it computes the token‑level conditional probabilities of the answer sequence given the trace. These probabilities are aggregated into a scalar reward using a function f (e.g., geometric mean or weighted sum). The reward is clipped against a baseline (empty reasoning) and normalized across sampled traces to form advantages. The policy gradient is then applied to both the trace generation and the answer prediction, using the advantage to weight the gradient contributions; this encourages traces that improve answer confidence. A small format‑supervision loss encourages the use of special tokens to delimit reasoning. The model is updated with AdamW, and the stale policy is refreshed. The core innovation lies in using the model’s own predictive confidence as a reward and in the weighted‑sum design that targets uncertain tokens, which together avoid the rapid degeneration (policy collapse) observed in prior RL approaches that maximize average token probability.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art among verifier-free methods on Llama and Mistral backbones.  
- On Llama-3.1-8B, NRT-WS(-logp) improves average score by +10.2 points over SFT and +5.4 over prior best RLPR.  
- Large gains on hard benchmarks: GSM8K from 29.0 to 76.0, and similar improvements on MATH; training remains stable without policy collapse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper introduces three interlocking mechanisms that enable training on unverifiable data:

1. **Latent variable formulation**: Reasoning trace `z` is treated as a latent variable. The model optimizes the marginal likelihood `p(y*|x)` by integrating over `z` using policy gradient. This allows discovery of effective reasoning traces without human supervision. Core assumption: there exist traces that increase confidence in the correct answer. Evidence: formal objective (Eq. 6) and gradient estimator (Eq. 8). Break condition: if such traces do not exist or RL fails to find them.

2. **Intrinsic reward from predictive confidence**: The reward `R(z,θ) = f(c_1,...,c_T)` aggregates the conditional probabilities of answer tokens given the trace. This provides a self‑supervised signal that does not require external verifiers. Core assumption: higher confidence in correct answer tokens correlates with better reasoning. Evidence: abstract, Section 3.2.1's aggregation functions. Break condition: miscalibration or mis‑specified aggregation could incentivize superficial confidence.

3. **Weighted Sum aggregation to target uncertainty**: To avoid collapse, NRT uses `R = Σ w_i c_i` where weights `w_i` are based on baseline uncertainty (e.g., `w_i ∝ -log c_{i,base}`). This focuses learning on tokens the model finds difficult, preventing degenerate short traces. Evidence: abstract, Table 2, and Section 4.3's stability analysis (Figure 3). Break condition: if baseline uncertainty is noisy or misaligned, weighting may amplify irrelevant signals.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: π_θ (current policy) → generates reasoning z; π_old (frozen copy) → supplies off‑policy samples; f (aggregation) → computes reward R(z,θ) from token probs; GRPO → stabilizes advantages; format loss → injects structural bias; optimizer (AdamW) → updates θ; then π_old is synchronized.

**Critical path**:
1. Sample minibatch of (x, y*).
2. For each prompt, sample K traces {z_k} from π_old. Append t_end if missing.
3. Compute baseline reward R_base = f(c_base) with empty reasoning (just t_start, t_end).
4. For each z_k, compute R_k = f(c_k) using current π_θ.
5. Clip: R'_k = max(0, R_k - R_base). Normalize advantages: A_k = (R'_k - mean(R')) / std(R').
6. Compute token reward signals S_i,k = (∂f/∂c_i) c_i,k.
7. Compute NRT gradient: ∇L_k = -A_k [∇ log π_θ(z_k|x) + Σ_i S_i,k ∇ log π_θ(y*_i | x, y*_<i, z_k)].
8. Add format loss gradient (coefficient 0.3).
9. Average over batch and update θ.
10. Copy π_θ to π_old.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Reward aggregation: Arithmetic Mean collapses; Geometric Mean is harsher; Weighted Sum adds hyperparameters but targets uncertainty.
- GRPO clip ratios: need tuning; too loose unstable, too tight slow.
- Format supervision weight: 0.3 used; higher risks over‑enforcement.
- Trace length limit: 2048 tokens balances depth vs. compute.

**Failure signatures**:
- Entropy collapse: sharp drop in token entropy or average length of z.
- Reward stagnation: group‑wise advantages near zero or variance collapse.
- Benchmark regress: validation scores fail to exceed SFT or degrade.
- Formatting issues: missing special tokens or excessive closing tokens.
- High gradient variance: spiking loss or gradient norms.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline reproducibility**: Run SFT on the 200k subset for same steps; record average scores (expected ~46.0 on Llama‑3.1‑8B) to match Table 3.
2. **NRT‑GM validation**: Train with Geometric Mean; monitor entropy and length (should stay stable/increase); verify final scores exceed SFT (e.g., ~54.9 on 8B).
3. **Ablation of weighting schemes**: Compare NRT‑WS(1/p) and NRT‑WS(‑logp) vs. NRT‑GM. Implement token‑level analysis: bin answer tokens by SFT entropy, measure relative probability improvement. Expect NRT‑WS(‑logp) to show strongest positive slope on high‑entropy tokens, matching Figure 3b.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Automating reward aggregation**: The authors call for exploring adaptive or fully learned reward schemes instead of handcrafted Weighted Sum. This remains open because the paper only evaluated fixed functions; a parameterized reward network trained jointly would provide evidence.
2. **Improving sample efficiency**: NRT's sampling‑based approach is computationally heavy; future work on more sample‑efficient optimization (e.g., variance reduction) is needed to reduce the number of traces and steps.
3. **Integrating reasoning into pre‑training**: Applying NRT’s principles during pre‑training could instill reasoning from the start, but this is unexplored. Evidence would involve designing a pre‑training objective that combines next‑token prediction with a latent reasoning reward and training a model from scratch.
4. **Dependence on base model capabilities**: The method assumes the model can generate non‑trivial traces; its performance on very weak models (e.g., random initialization or tiny models) is unknown. Testing on such models would clarify this dependence.
5. **Reliability of baseline uncertainty**: The Weighted Sum uses baseline probabilities to identify difficult tokens; it is unclear whether this correlates with true reasoning difficulty. Human annotation of token difficulty and correlation analysis would resolve this.

## Limitations
- Incomplete replication details: Ambiguities in the baseline model for Weighted Sum (π_old vs. fixed checkpoint), format supervision implementation, and reward normalization prevent faithful reproduction.
- No direct evaluation of reasoning trace quality: The paper does not assess whether generated traces are semantically meaningful or human‑like.
- Potential sensitivity to base model reasoning ability: All experiments use models with some prior reasoning capability; performance on models lacking such priors is untested.

## Confidence
- **NRT performance claims**: Medium – strong empirical results but rely on undisclosed implementation choices and seeds; independent replication needed.
- **Weighted Sum aggregation effectiveness**: Medium – internal analysis shows stability, but sensitivity to baseline calibration is unknown.
- **Latent variable + intrinsic reward sufficiency**: Low – the core assumption that predictive confidence alone yields useful reasoning lacks direct validation; risk of reward hacking.
