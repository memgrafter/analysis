# Mitigating Preference Leakage via Strict Estimator Separation for Normative Generative Ranking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20800
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20800
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a two-judge framework with strict estimator separation (Judge B for supervision, Judge A for evaluation) and a unified intersection pool, yielding a distilled BGE-M3 model that substantially outperforms its cross-encoder teacher under leakage-free evaluation on the SSGEN benchmark (33,052 culturally grounded children's stories).

## Executive Summary
LLM-as-a-judge evaluations for normative generative tasks often suffer from preference leakage, where the same judge supervises training and evaluates results, creating circularity and inflated metrics. This paper addresses this by proposing a two-judge framework with strict separation: Judge B (Llama-3.1-8B) provides supervision during training, while Judge A (Yi-1.5-9B) independently evaluates final performance. A unified intersection pool ensures all methods rank the same candidate set per query, eliminating missing-label confounds. Applied to a new benchmark of 33,052 culturally grounded children’s stories (SSGEN), the authors employ teacher-student distillation: a cross-encoder teacher fine-tuned on Judge B’s scores is distilled into a bi-encoder student (BGE-M3). The student’s dot-product bottleneck acts as a regularizer, filtering judge-specific noise and achieving significant improvement over the teacher under Judge A. This work demonstrates that strict estimator separation and careful pool design can yield more reliable normative ranking.

## Method Summary
The task is within-query ranking of culturally grounded children’s stories given triple constraints (Age, Moral, Culture). For each query, a generator produces multiple candidate stories. To prevent preference leakage, the framework uses two independent judges: Judge B scores candidates for training supervision, while Judge A scores them for final evaluation. An intersection filter retains only candidates validly scored by both judges, forming S∩_q, which all rankers must rank. Training proceeds via teacher-student distillation: a high-capacity cross-encoder is first fine-tuned on Judge B’s scores to capture fine-grained interactions; then a bi-encoder student (BGE-M3) is trained to mimic the teacher’s logits via mean squared error. The student’s bottleneck forces it to retain only the most consistent normative signals, suppressing judge-specific overfitting. Evaluation uses nDCG@5 and ERR@5 on held-out queries, with Judge A’s scores on the intersection pool providing leakage-free assessment.

## Key Results
- Introduces a two-judge framework with strict separation and a unified intersection pool, effectively breaking circular preference leakage.
- The distilled BGE-M3 student model substantially outperforms its cross-encoder teacher under Judge A evaluation, demonstrating the benefit of the bottleneck regularizer.
- The framework is evaluated on SSGEN, a new benchmark of 33,052 culturally grounded children’s stories, showing robust improvement across multiple ranking methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Independent Judges
Preference leakage is eliminated by strictly separating supervision (Judge B) from evaluation (Judge A), ensuring no gradient flow or hyperparameter feedback from Judge A influences training. This breaks the circular dependency that causes models to overfit to a single judge’s idiosyncrasies. Core assumption: leakage arises when the same or correlated estimators serve both roles; orthogonal estimators remove this dependency. Evidence: explicit information barrier in Section 3.1 and citation of Li et al. (2025) formalizing leakage. Break condition: residual correlation may persist if judges share model families or prompts.

### Teacher-Student Distillation
A cross-encoder teacher, fine-tuned on Judge B’s scores, captures rich token-level interactions but also overfits to Judge B’s scoring style. Distilling into a bi-encoder student imposes a dot-product bottleneck that retains only the most consistent directions across the teacher’s logits, acting as a regularizer that filters high-frequency judge-specific noise. The student then generalizes better under Judge A. Core assumption: the true normative signal is present in the teacher and fits within the student’s representational capacity. Evidence: abstract and Section 5 describe the noise-filtering effect; Kaplun et al. (2022) supports that smaller models can outperform larger ones under noisy supervision. Break condition: if teacher noise dominates or student capacity is insufficient, distillation fails.

### Intersection Pool
The intersection pool S∩_q includes only candidates with valid scores from both judges. This guarantees every method ranks exactly the same set per query, controlling for missing-label confounds that could bias comparisons. Core assumption: missing labels are independent of the model’s predictions, so fixing the candidate set isolates the scoring function’s effect. Evidence: abstract and Section 3.1 define S∩_q; IR literature (Carterette 2012) underscores the need for such control. Break condition: if |S∩_q| is too small (< k) or one judge systematically rejects certain story types, metrics become unstable or biased.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Query sampler -> Generator G (Gemma-2-9B) -> candidate stories S_q -> Judge B (Llama-3.1-8B) scores y_B (supervision) and Judge A (Yi-1.5-9B) scores y_A (evaluation); Intersection filter builds S_q^∩; Training module (pointwise MLP, pairwise RankNet, or cross-encoder teacher fine-tuned on y_B); Distillation trains bi-encoder student (BGE-M3) to mimic teacher logits; Evaluation computes nDCG@5, ERR@5 on held-out queries using y_A.

### Critical Path
1. Enforce strict separation: Judge A never influences training (no gradients, no hyperparameter tuning).
2. Build intersection pool before any train/test split to avoid query-level leakage.
3. Train a high-capacity cross-encoder on y_B as teacher.
4. Distill teacher into bi-encoder student.
5. Evaluate all rankers on S_q^∩ with Judge A labels.
Failure at any step reintroduces bias or weakens the signal.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Judge independence vs. cost: Two LLMs double inference and introduce variance but are essential for unbiased evaluation.
- Pool size: Intersection reduces candidates (~96 → ~52 per query), trading statistical power for fairness.
- Capacity vs. efficiency: Cross-encoder captures fine interactions but is slow; bi-encoder is efficient but coarser; distillation aims to combine benefits.
- Selective supervision: Judge B’s higher invalid rate discards many candidates, potentially biasing training toward clearer cases.

### Failure Signatures
- Leakage: Large gap between performance on y_B vs. y_A (e.g., B-Score >> any learned model) suggests overfitting to Judge B.
- Intersection collapse: Many queries have |S_q^∩| < 5 → unstable metrics; may need to filter queries or adjust cutoff.
- Distillation failure: Student does not beat teacher or baselines; check teacher quality or distillation hyperparameters.
- Unexpected judge agreement: High correlation on out-of-domain data (e.g., SSGEN) may indicate shared biases rather than true normative alignment.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Judge independence check:** Compute Spearman ρ between Judge A and B on SSGEN. Expected ≈ 0.04; higher would challenge independence.
2. **Distillation transfer:** Train cross-encoder teacher on y_B, distill to BGE-M3 student. Under Judge A, expect Δ nDCG@5 > 0.1 (student > teacher). If not, revisit teacher training.
3. **Intersection robustness:** Bucket test queries by |S_q^∩| (small ≤35, medium 36–50, large >50). Compute nDCG@5 of best model in each bucket. Significant variation indicates results driven by dense pools; consider stratified reporting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we achieve complete evaluator independence in LLM-as-a-judge frameworks when judges, even from different families, share pre-training data and latent societal biases?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "We acknowledge, however, that 'leakage-free' refers to the experimental framework, not the pre-training distribution. Since Yi-1.5 and LLaMA-3 likely share training data (e.g., CommonCrawl), they inevitably share latent societal priors." (Section 5)
- **Why unresolved:** The authors identify this as a limitation but do not propose methods to eliminate shared biases, which could undermine the strict separation goal.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Studies using judges with disjoint pre-training corpora or probing shared biases via adversarial prompts to measure residual correlation beyond task-specific signals.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why does the distilled bi-encoder student model consistently outperform its cross-encoder teacher under independent evaluation, and is this phenomenon generalizable to other normative ranking tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Table 3 shows BGE-M3 (Distilled) nDCG@5=0.771 vs. Cross-Encoder (Teacher)=0.577. Section 5 hypothesizes that the student's bottleneck acts as a regulariser filtering judge-specific noise.
- **Why unresolved:** The hypothesis is suggestive but not causally proven; it may depend on specific judge noise patterns, dataset characteristics, or model architectures.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation experiments varying teacher capacity, student architecture, and supervision noise across multiple normative datasets to isolate the conditions where student superiority holds.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does the size of the intersection pool affect the reliability and generalizability of the reported improvements?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper acknowledges that the intersection pool reduces the candidate set size (from ~96 to ~52 per query) and can lead to unstable metrics if many queries have small pools (Limitations section; also break condition in Why This Works).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not systematically analyze how variations in pool size across queries impact the measured performance differences between ranking methods. It remains unclear whether the observed gains hold uniformly across all pool sizes or are driven by queries with denser pools.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An analysis stratifying results by intersection pool size (e.g., small, medium, large) to check for performance consistency; possibly also experiments with controlled pool size variations to isolate the effect.

## Limitations
- Judge independence may be compromised by shared pretraining biases even across different model families, potentially allowing residual preference leakage.
- Distillation superiority is contingent on teacher signal quality and student capacity; if the teacher overfits to Judge B or the student is too limited, the expected gains may not materialize.
- The intersection pool reduces candidate size per query (from ~96 to ~52), which can lead to unstable metrics if many queries have small pools or if the filtering introduces systematic bias.

## Confidence
- **Framework design (strict separation, intersection pool):** High
- **Judge A/B independence:** Medium
- **Distillation superiority under Judge A:** Low
