# CLEAR-Mamba:Towards Accurate, Adaptive and Trustworthy Multi-Sequence Ophthalmic Angiography Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.20601
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20601
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Primary result**: Achieves 59.1% overall accuracy and 22.7% macro F1 on a 43-disease ophthalmic angiography dataset (15,524 FFA/ICGA images), exceeding MedMamba by 4% OA and 6-8% F1; also reaches 94.5% OA on OCT-C8 and 56.8% OA on RetinaMNIST with superior calibration.

## Executive Summary
Accurate diagnosis of ophthalmic diseases from angiography sequences is challenging due to temporal dynamics, device variability, and the need for reliable confidence estimates. CLEAR-Mamba introduces a hypernetwork-based adaptive conditioning (HaC) module and an evidential uncertainty head (RaP) to the MedMamba backbone, addressing these issues. The method achieves state-of-the-art classification performance and well-calibrated uncertainty on a large in-house dataset and public benchmarks, demonstrating its potential for trustworthy clinical decision support.

By dynamically generating instance-specific parameters, HaC mitigates cross-domain shifts without full fine-tuning, while RaP's evidential learning models both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty, enabling selective prediction and human-in-the-loop review. Extensive ablations confirm the complementary benefits of both modules, and the model shows improved feature separability and calibration over prior arts.

## Method Summary
CLEAR-Mamba leverages MedMamba, a visual state-space model backbone that uses 2D selective scan (SS2D) blocks to efficiently capture long-range temporal and spatial dependencies in angiography sequences with linear computational cost. The backbone processes input images through a series of SS-Conv-SSM blocks, producing hierarchical feature representations.

Two complementary modules enhance MedMamba: the Hyper-adaptive Conditioning (HaC) module and the Reliability-aware Prediction (RaP) head. HaC introduces a lightweight hypernetwork that takes globally pooled backbone features and generates per-channel affine parameters (γ, β); these are applied via FiLM modulation, with a gated update α = σ(a(z)) to prevent disruptive feature shifts. RaP replaces the standard linear classifier with an evidential layer that outputs evidence e via SoftPlus; the resulting Dirichlet parameters α = e + 1 allow uncertainty quantification. Training optimizes a combined loss: negative log-likelihood of the true label under Dir(α) plus a KL divergence toward a non-informative prior Dir(1), optionally using an adaptive coefficient λ_e. Standard data augmentations and Adam optimization complete the training pipeline.

## Key Results
- On a curated in-house dataset of 15,524 FFA/ICGA images covering 43 diseases, CLEAR-Mamba achieves 59.1% overall accuracy and 22.7% macro F1, outperforming MedMamba by 4% OA and 6–8% F1.
- It attains state-of-the-art results on public benchmarks: 94.5% OA on OCT-C8 and 56.8% OA on RetinaMNIST.
- The model exhibits significantly better calibration and feature separability; the RaP head yields reliable uncertainty estimates, and the full model shows a clear gap in confidence between correct and incorrect predictions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hyper-adaptive Conditioning (HaC) for Cross-Domain Adaptability
- **Claim**: The HaC module improves cross-domain adaptability by dynamically generating instance-specific affine parameters, mitigating performance degradation from inter-device variability and lesion heterogeneity.
- **Mechanism**: A lightweight hypernetwork processes pooled backbone features to predict per-channel scaling (γ) and shifting (β) factors, which are then applied via a FiLM-style modulation. A gating mechanism α = σ(a(z)) stabilizes updates, preventing disruptive shifts.
- **Core assumption**: Input feature distributions (e.g., contrast, texture) indicative of acquisition device or lesion stage can be captured by global pooling and used to condition the backbone’s channel-wise responses without full fine-tuning.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "introduce HaC, a hypernetwork-based adaptive conditioning layer that dynamically generates parameters according to input feature distributions, thereby improving cross-domain adaptability."
  - Section "Hyper-adaptive Conditioning": details the FiLM formulation and gated update, noting it enables "lightweight case-wise adaptation."
  - Corpus: Neighboring papers on ophthalmic AI (e.g., "FusionFM") also explore dynamic adaptation, but no direct evidence validates *this specific* hypernetwork design for angiography; assumption is inferred from general hypernetwork literature.
- **Break condition**: If the hypernetwork’s conditioning signal (pooled z) fails to correlate with meaningful domain shifts (e.g., device noise patterns), or if the gating α becomes degenerate (saturated), HaC may add noise without benefit or cause instability.

### Mechanism 2: Reliability-aware Prediction (RaP) for Calibrated Uncertainty
- **Claim**: The RaP head using evidential uncertainty learning produces calibrated confidence estimates and prevents overconfident errors by modeling epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty via a Dirichlet distribution over class probabilities.
- **Mechanism**: The head outputs non-negative evidence *e* via SoftPlus, which is converted to Dirichlet concentration parameters α = *e* + 1. Training minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the true label under Dir(α) plus a KL divergence toward a non-informative prior Dir(1). Predictive entropy H(ˆp) and total evidence S = Σα_k quantify uncertainty.
- **Core assumption**: The evidential framework properly separates uncertainty due to insufficient evidence (epistemic) from inherent class ambiguity (aleatoric) when applied to hierarchical medical image features, and that minimizing the specified loss encourages the model to be "honest" about low-confidence cases.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "evidential uncertainty learning, which encourages the model to emphasize low-confidence samples and improves overall stability and reliability."
  - Section "Reliability-aware Prediction": explicitly states: "the RaP head produces Dirichlet parameters α... yielding both class probabilities and an associated measure of uncertainty" and uses the loss from Eq. 9.
  - Corpus: Evidential learning is established in general (Kendall & Gal 2017 cited), but its efficacy for *multi-disease ophthalmic angiography* specifically is not externally validated; evidence is from in-house experiments.
- **Break condition**: If the feature extractor (MedMamba) produces representations that are not sufficiently discriminative, the evidence *e* may not meaningfully correlate with actual ambiguity, leading to miscalibrated uncertainty (e.g., high confidence on hard cases). The KL prior can also overwhelm learning if λ is too high.

### Mechanism 3: MedMamba Backbone for Efficient Temporal Modeling
- **Claim**: The MedMamba backbone, based on structured state space models (SSMs), efficiently captures long-range temporal dependencies in angiography sequences with linear computational complexity, overcoming CNN receptive field limits and ViT memory costs.
- **Mechanism**: 2D selective scan (SS2D) propagates information across spatial locations in a data-dependent manner, while a lightweight gate fuses the scanned global-context features with local residual features. This allows modeling of hemodynamic progression (e.g., filling, leakage) across frames.
- **Core assumption**: The state-space parameterization (Δ, B, C matrices) can be learned to selectively propagate relevant temporal-spatial patterns (e.g., late-phase leakage) while forgetting irrelevant variation, and that linear scanning is sufficient for the sequential cues in FFA/ICGA.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "enhancing MedMamba... to efficiently capture long-range dynamics."
  - Section "Preliminaries": explains SS2D and gate fusion, noting it "captures both fine-grained local cues and global structural dependencies, which is crucial for angiography images."
  - Corpus: Foundational SSM work (Gu 2023) is cited, but no corpus paper directly compares SSMs to CNNs/ViTs *on angiography sequences*; the mechanism's advantage is inferred from general sequence modeling properties and the paper's superior results vs. MedViT/MambaVision.
- **Break condition**: If the temporal dynamics in angiography are highly non-Markovian or require modeling very long-context (> sequence length) beyond SS2D's scan range, or if the selective scan parameters fail to learn meaningful temporal weights (e.g., all near zero), MedMamba degrades toward a local feature extractor.

## Foundational Learning
### State Space Models (SSMs) for Vision
- **Why needed here**: MedMamba's core is a Visual SSM; understanding continuous/discrete SSM parameterization (A, B, C, Δ) and the 2D selective scan is essential to grasp how it compresses temporal context.
- **Quick check**: How does SS2D's direction-aware scanning differ from a flattened Transformer's global attention in terms of computational complexity and inductive bias for image sequences?

### Hypernetwork Conditioning (FiLM/Adapter)
- **Why needed here**: HaC generates affine parameters from input features. One must understand hypernetworks (H(z) → θ), FiLM (γ⊙X+β), and low-rank adapters to see how instance-wise modulation is achieved without full fine-tuning.
- **Quick check**: Why might a gated update (α⊙(˜X−X)) be more stable than directly adding the hypernetwork output?

### Evidential Deep Learning (Dirichlet Uncertainty)
- **Why needed here**: RaP's uncertainty quantification hinges on interpreting Dirichlet parameters as evidence. Key concepts: evidence *e*, concentration α = *e*+1, NLL loss, KL prior, and separating total/aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty.
- **Quick check**: What is the expected effect on predictive entropy H(ˆp) when the model sees an out-of-distribution (e.g., unseen modality) image versus a clear in-distribution image?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input → Patch Embedding → Stacked SS-Conv-SSM Blocks (MedMamba backbone) → Global AvgPool → HaC Module (hypernetwork from pooled z → γ,β; gating α) → Modulated features → RaP Head (Linear → SoftPlus → α = e+1) → Output (ˆp, uncertainty).

### Critical Path
1. **Backbone extraction**: MedMamba must produce sufficiently discriminative hierarchical features from temporal sequences.
2. **Adaptive conditioning**: HaC's hypernetwork must generate meaningful (γ,β) from z that adapt features without destroying discriminative structure.
3. **Uncertainty parameterization**: RaP's evidence e must correlate with actual sample difficulty; training loss must balance likelihood and regularization.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **HaC capacity vs. stability**: Larger hypernetwork (higher `had_feat_dim`, lower `r`) increases adaptivity but risks over-conditioning on spurious correlations (ablation shows CLEAR-B sensitive to high `r`). Gating α mitigates but doesn't eliminate risk.
- **RaP calibration vs. confidence**: Pure evidential learning (without adaptive weighting) can become overly conservative (confidence ~0.6 even for easy cases). The "adaptive" update strategy balances this by weighting samples.
- **Sequence modeling depth**: More SS-Conv-SSM blocks improve temporal modeling but increase memory; the paper uses ~4 blocks, but optimal depth may vary with sequence length.

### Failure Signatures
- **HaC-only variant**: Overconfident incorrect predictions; confidence distributions for correct/incorrect samples show minimal separation (median ~0.87 vs 0.47).
- **RaP-only variant**: Overly conservative predictions (confidence clustered ~0.6); low separability; potentially high uncertainty on easy cases.
- **Full model imbalance**: If λ_e is too high (>0.1), model may output uniform Dirichlet (high uncertainty, low accuracy). If too low (<1e-4), model becomes overconfident like HaC-only.
- **Generalization failure**: On public benchmarks, overfitting to in-house domain causes limited gains (e.g., MedMamba ~55% → CLEAR-B 56.8% on RetinaMNIST).

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Replicate MedMamba baseline**: Train MedMamba-B on your dataset with identical preprocessing (224×224, random aug, Adam, LR=1e-3, 150 epochs). Verify you match ~55% OA, ~18% F1.
2. **Add HaC only**: Integrate HaC (with `had_feat_dim=96, r=4`) and train with standard cross-entropy. Measure OA/F1 and plot confidence distribution for correct/incorrect samples. Expect slight OA gain but overconfidence (median conf_incorrect > 0.6).
3. **Add RaP only (no HaC)**: Replace final linear layer with RaP head (λ=5e-3). Use fixed weighting initially. Expect lower OA but better calibrated uncertainty; compare confidence separation to step 2.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does CLEAR-Mamba generalize to unseen patient cohorts and diverse clinical settings in a prospective multi-center validation?
- **Basis in paper**: The conclusion states: “Future work will focus on multi-center prospective validation...”
- **Why unresolved**: Current evaluations are retrospective on a single-center dataset and public benchmarks, lacking prospective assessment across multiple hospitals with varying devices and populations; real-world deployment challenges remain unaddressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A multi-center clinical trial recruiting patients from diverse hospitals, collecting new angiography data with different imaging systems, and evaluating CLEAR-Mamba’s accuracy, calibration, and impact on clinical decision-making compared to standard care.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can CLEAR-Mamba be effectively extended to integrate multiple ophthalmic imaging modalities (e.g., combining FFA/ICGA with OCT, fundus photography) for improved diagnostic performance?
- **Basis in paper**: The conclusion identifies “extending to multimodal fusion” as future work.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper focuses exclusively on single-modality angiography; multimodal fusion remains unexplored within the CLEAR framework, leaving its potential benefits and design challenges unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Development of a multimodal architecture that fuses features from diverse modalities, collection of paired multimodal datasets, and systematic comparison against unimodal and existing multimodal baselines.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can the uncertainty estimates from the RaP head be integrated into clinical workflows to enable selective prediction and human-in-the-loop review, and what is the impact on diagnostic accuracy and clinician workload?
- **Basis in paper**: The conclusion mentions “integrating uncertainty with selective prediction and human-in-the-loop review” as future work.
- **Why unresolved**: While RaP produces calibrated uncertainty, the paper does not investigate its practical use in clinical decision-making; the design of interfaces, thresholds, and protocols for leveraging uncertainty remains open.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: User studies with ophthalmologists testing interactive systems that use uncertainty to trigger manual review, measuring outcomes such as diagnostic accuracy, time efficiency, and clinician trust.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Would sequence-aware pretraining on large-scale unlabeled temporal ophthalmic angiography sequences improve the downstream classification performance of CLEAR-Mamba?
- **Basis in paper**: The future work includes “exploring sequence-aware pretraining”.
- **Why unresolved**: The backbone MedMamba is either randomly initialized or pretrained on natural images, not leveraging the temporal structure inherent in angiography; potential gains from self-supervised pretraining on unlabeled sequences are untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Pretrain MedMamba on a large corpus of unlabeled FFA/ICGA sequences using objectives like contrastive learning or masked modeling, then fine-tune on classification tasks; compare performance against standard pretraining and ablations of pretraining design.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified architecture details**: Exact MedMamba configuration (number of SS2D blocks, channel dimensions) and precise HaC insertion points are not described, hindering exact replication.
- **Ambiguous temporal modeling**: The paper treats angiography frames as independent images, yet claims efficient temporal modeling; it remains unclear whether and how sequence order is exploited.
- **Modest cross-domain gains**: Performance improvements on public benchmarks are limited (e.g., +1.8% on RetinaMNIST), suggesting the model’s adaptability may be constrained to the in-house domain.
