# Calibrate-Then-Act: Cost-Aware Exploration in LLM Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16699
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16699
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CTA consistently improves cost-aware decision-making, achieving near-oracle performance in QA and adaptive behavior in coding tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the cost-uncertainty trade-off faced by LLM agents when exploring environments (e.g., retrieving information or testing code). It introduces Calibrate-Then-Act (CTA), a framework that equips LLMs with explicit probabilistic priors over latent environment variables. By separating prior estimation from policy learning, CTA enables agents to reason optimally about when to explore and when to commit. Evaluated on a Pandora’s Box toy task, a knowledge QA task with retrieval, and a coding task with selective testing, CTA variants (both prompted and RL-trained) demonstrate superior cost-aware performance, often aligning with oracle policies and achieving Pareto-optimal trade-offs across varying cost regimes.

## Method Summary
CTA formalizes exploration as an MDP with a latent state. A separate prior estimator produces calibrated probabilities \( \hat{p}(z|x) \) summarizing the agent’s initial belief about the hidden variable (e.g., answer correctness, file format). This prior is injected into the agent’s prompt or RL state. The agent then decides sequentially whether to take an exploratory action (incurring cost) or commit, using the prior to compute expected value of information per the Bellman equation. Two instantiations are evaluated: **CTA-PROMPTED** uses chain-of-thought prompting with the prior; **CTA-RL** further fine-tunes the LLM with RL (GRPO) while conditioning on the prior. Prior estimators differ by task: for QA, isotonic regression calibrates the model’s verbalized confidence; for coding, a tiny BERT (MBERT) predicts format priors from filenames.

## Key Results
- On knowledge QA, CTA-PROMPTED achieves the highest discounted reward (0.293), closely matching the oracle and significantly outperforming baselines by balancing accuracy and retrieval cost.
- In the coding task, CTA-RL attains a reward of 0.268 versus 0.259 for standard RL, demonstrating adaptive exploration across cost ratios and Pareto-optimal performance.
- On the Pandora’s Box toy task, CTA-PROMPTED achieves a 94% optimal match rate, while baselines without priors achieve at most 23%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Explicit priors enable LLMs to compute optimal exploration-commitment trade-offs by quantifying expected value of information.
- **Mechanism:** The agent uses the provided prior to form posterior beliefs after observations and compares immediate commit value (posterior success probability) with the discounted expected value of verifying, following the Bellman recursion.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can perform probabilistic reasoning when given numerical priors in a structured prompt.
- **Evidence anchors:** CTA-PROMPTED achieves 94% optimal match rate on Pandora’s Box, while baselines without priors achieve at most 23% (section 4.2). The abstract states priors enable optimal decision-making.
- **Break condition:** The prior is miscalibrated or the model ignores provided numbers during reasoning.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Decoupling uncertainty estimation from policy learning yields policies that generalize better across varying cost regimes.
- **Mechanism:** A separate, pre-trained prior estimator produces calibrated priors, which are then fed to the policy. This separation avoids entanglement, allowing the policy to focus on action selection given reliable uncertainty estimates.
- **Core assumption:** The prior estimator is accurately trained and remains valid on test distributions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Prior estimators are detailed (isotonic regression for QA, MBERT for CODE) in section 6. CTA-RL consistently outperforms RL across cost ratios in CODE, achieving Pareto-optimal reward, while RL collapses to a static “test-first” policy (section 8, Figure 4).
- **Break condition:** Prior estimator suffers distribution shift (e.g., new filename patterns in CODE) leading to inaccurate priors.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Prompting with explicit priors induces chain-of-thought reasoning that approximates optimal policies without training.
- **Mechanism:** The prompt includes numerical priors, triggering step-by-step reasoning about the trade-off between exploration cost and reward, effectively simulating the Bellman equation internally.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs possess latent decision-making abilities activated by structured probabilistic information.
- **Evidence anchors:** CTA-PROMPTED achieves 94% optimal match on Pandora’s Box without RL, while PROMPTED achieves only 23% (section 4.2). Model thinking traces explicitly compare expected values (Figure 8).
- **Break condition:** Chain-of-thought is superficial or misapplies probability rules.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Sequential Decision-Making under Uncertainty  
  *Why needed:* CTA formalizes exploration as an MDP with latent states; understanding Bellman equations and discounting is crucial to grasp the optimization objective.  
  *Quick check:* In Pandora’s Box, how does the discount factor γ influence the optimal verification order? (Higher γ favors earlier commitment; lower γ allows more verification.)
- **Concept:** Calibration of Uncertainty Estimates  
  *Why needed:* Verbalized confidence is often uncalibrated; isotonic regression aligns it with true accuracy, ensuring priors reflect actual success probabilities.  
  *Quick check:* What does Expected Calibration Error (ECE) measure, and why was it reduced from 0.618 to 0.029? (ECE measures deviation between confidence and accuracy; reduction improves prior reliability.)
- **Concept:** Reinforcement Learning with Conditional Policies  
  *Why needed:* CTA-RL conditions the policy on estimated priors, altering the state representation; understanding conditioning effects on credit assignment is key.  
  *Quick check:* Why does end-to-end RL fail to learn adaptive exploration in CODE, while CTA-RL succeeds? (Entanglement of uncertainty estimation and action selection leads to static policies; conditioning disentangles them.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Prior Estimator -> Agent Policy -> Environment -> RL Trainer. Prior Estimator outputs \( \hat{p}(z|x) \) (isotonic regression or MBERT). Agent Policy (prompted LLM or RL-trained model) takes \( (x, A, D_{\theta}, o_{0:t}, \hat{p}) \) and outputs actions. Environment provides observations and rewards. RL Trainer (GRPO) updates policy using discounted reward, with fixed prior estimator.
- **Critical path:** Prior estimation → agent decision loop (observe, act) → commit → reward calculation with discounting. In RL training, policy gradients update the agent; prior estimator remains fixed.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Explicit vs. implicit priors: Explicit priors improve interpretability and adaptivity but require reliable estimation; implicit learning may fail to capture trade-offs.  
  - Prompting vs. RL: Prompting is zero-shot and effective; RL can further optimize at the cost of training complexity.  
  - Prior estimator choice: Simple calibrators (isotonic) are data-efficient but limited; neural predictors (MBERT) capture complex patterns but need more data.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Agent disregards prior (retrieval rate doesn’t vary with \( p_{\text{no context}} \) or γ).  
  - High ECE in prior estimator (raw verbalized confidence had ECE 0.618).  
  - RL without conditioning adopts static exploration pattern (e.g., always test first).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Pandora’s Box: CTA-PROMPTED vs. baselines; verify optimal match rate >90% and reward close to oracle (0.625 vs. 0.649).  
  2. QA: Plot retrieval decisions against \( p_{\text{no context}} \) and γ; expect boundary aligning with oracle threshold \( p_{\text{ret}} \cdot γ = p_{\text{no context}} \) (Figure 3).  
  3. CODE: Across ρ values (relative test cost), measure “guess-and-go” rate; CTA-RL should show decreasing guess rate as ρ increases, while RL remains constant (Figure 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the accuracy and calibration of the estimated prior affect CTA's ability to achieve Pareto-optimal decisions across varying cost regimes? (The paper notes CTA works "even with imperfect prior estimates" but doesn't systematically vary prior quality.)
- Can CTA scale to environments with high-dimensional, continuous, or interdependent latent variables where explicit priors are difficult to specify or estimate reliably? (Studied tasks involve low-dimensional discrete latents.)
- To what extent does CTA's benefit depend on the inherent reasoning capabilities of the base LLM, and does it interact with model scale or architecture? (All experiments use Qwen3-8B; no cross-model comparison.)
- What are the mechanistic reasons why end-to-end RL fails to internalize uncertainty-cost trade-offs while CTA-RL succeeds? (No analysis of internal representations or training dynamics.)

## Limitations
- Generalization beyond studied tasks (e.g., real-world coding with complex repositories) is unproven.
- Robustness of explicit priors to severe miscalibration or out-of-distribution inputs is unclear.
- Overhead of prior estimation (e.g., training MBERT) may limit practicality in low-data regimes.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Explicit priors enable optimal trade-offs):** High for toy/Pandora’s Box; Medium for complex tasks due to reliance on accurate priors.
- **Mechanism 2 (Decoupling improves generalization):** Medium; supported by CODE experiments but prior estimator quality is critical.
- **Mechanism 3 (Prompting induces optimal reasoning):** Medium; strong in controlled settings, but chain-of-thought fidelity not externally validated.
- **Foundational Learning Claims:** High; standard RL/MDP concepts well-established.
