# Adaptive Decoding via Test-Time Policy Learning for Self-Improving Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18428
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18428
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** A lightweight RL policy dynamically adjusts decoding parameters (temperature, top-p) at test time while keeping the LLM frozen, achieving up to +88% relative improvement on BookSum and +79% on WikiHow.

## Executive Summary
Static decoding strategies like greedy decoding or fixed-temperature sampling are suboptimal across diverse text generation tasks. This paper proposes Adaptive Decoding, a method that uses a lightweight reinforcement learning policy to dynamically adjust decoding parameters (temperature, top-p) at test time. The policy interacts with a frozen LLM, observing a state representation built from the model's internal states and generation history, and outputs actions that modify the token distribution. Training uses PPO with a composite reward combining ROUGE-L and auxiliary signals (length, coverage, repetition, completeness) to guide generation toward higher quality.

Experiments on summarization with Granite-3.3-2B and Qwen-2.5-0.5B show consistent improvements over static baselines, with up to +88% relative improvement on BookSum and +79% on WikiHow. Ablation studies confirm that the full composite reward is crucial: ROUGE-only yields minimal gains (+6.96% vs. greedy), while the shaped reward enables stable and sustained learning.

## Method Summary
Adaptive Decoding treats the test-time generation process as a reinforcement learning problem. A lightweight policy network (2-layer MLP) observes a state vector that concatenates mean-pooled final hidden states, top-k logits, prefix length, and normalized entropy from the frozen LLM. At each generation step, the policy outputs continuous actions that adjust temperature and top-p sampling parameters. The LLM uses these to sample the next token, and the process repeats until termination. The policy is trained via PPO using a final reward computed after sequence completion. The reward combines ROUGE-L (overlap with reference) with auxiliary shaping terms: length penalty, coverage bonus, repetition penalty, and completeness penalty. This composite design provides dense, multi-faceted supervision that stabilizes learning and improves generation quality.

## Key Results
- Consistent gains over static baselines on three summarization datasets (BookSum, arXiv, WikiHow) with two small LLMs (≤2B).
- Large relative improvements on BookSum (+88%) and WikiHow (+79%).
- Ablations reveal that the full composite reward yields +60.45% gain over greedy, while ROUGE-only gives only +6.96%, confirming the importance of structured reward shaping.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Adaptive decoding emerges from a closed-loop reinforcement learning (RL) process where a lightweight policy dynamically adjusts sampling parameters (temperature, top-p) at each generation step based on the evolving context.
- **Mechanism:** The policy observes a state representation (prompt, generated prefix, model logits, hidden states, entropy) and outputs actions that modify the LLM's next-token distribution. These actions influence future states, creating a feedback loop within a single generation episode. The LLM weights remain frozen; only the policy is updated via PPO using a final episode reward.
- **Core assumption:** Decoding hyperparameters can be optimized per-step to improve end-to-end generation quality, and the state representation sufficiently captures relevant context for decision-making.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "learns a lightweight policy to adjust sampling parameters at test-time while keeping the LLM weights frozen"
  - [section 3.1]: Explicit MDP formulation with state, action, reward; state includes "mean-pooled final hidden states, top-k logits, prefix length, normalized entropy"
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "Learning Adaptive LLM Decoding" (arxiv:2603.09065) explore adaptive decoding but do not directly evidence the specific test-time RL loop; indirect support exists for adaptive parameter control.
- **Break condition:** If the reward signal fails to correlate with true output quality or the state representation lacks critical cues (e.g., long-range dependencies), the policy may learn spurious correlations or fail to adapt meaningfully.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Stable and sustained policy learning is enabled by a composite reward function that combines overlap-based metrics (ROUGE-L) with structured shaping terms (length penalty, coverage bonus, repetition penalty, completeness penalty).
- **Mechanism:** The reward aggregates multiple quality dimensions—faithfulness (ROUGE), conciseness (length), informativeness (coverage), fluency (repetition), and structural completeness (punctuation). Ablations show ROUGE-only leads to negligible gains or instability, while the full composite yields robust improvements.
- **Core assumption:** Single automatic metrics like ROUGE are insufficient proxies for summary quality; auxiliary shaping terms provide denser, multi-faceted supervision that guides the policy toward more desirable generation patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Reward ablations show that overlap-only objectives underperform compared to composite rewards, while structured shaping terms (length, coverage, repetition, completeness) enable stable and sustained improvements."
  - [section 4.2]: Table 2 ablations: ROUGE-only yields minimal gains (+6.96% vs. greedy) vs. Proposed Reward (+60.45%); core shaping shows negative early→late trend, indicating instability.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors emphasize reward design challenges (e.g., "Better Instruction-Following Through Minimum Bayes Risk" uses MBR), but direct evidence for this specific shaping combination is absent from corpus.
- **Break condition:** If shaping terms are poorly weighted or contradictory (e.g., coverage bonus rewards source copying over abstraction), the policy may optimize for proxy metrics that degrade true quality.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Test-time adaptation is achieved without retraining the LLM by decoupling the decoding controller (policy network) from the frozen generative model, enabling reversible and low-overhead improvements.
- **Mechanism:** The policy is a separate 2-layer MLP (256 hidden units) that consumes LLM-generated state features and outputs Gaussian over 2D action (temperature, top-p). Actions squashed via sigmoid and scaled to ranges [0.2,1.2] and [0.8,1.0]. PPO updates only this network during training; at inference, the policy runs alongside the LLM, adjusting decoding parameters on the fly. No LLM gradients are computed.
- **Core assumption:** Decoding behavior can be steered by manipulating sampling hyperparameters alone, and a lightweight policy can learn effective mappings from LLM-internal states to these hyperparameters.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "enabling domain-aware and user-controllable generation without retraining large models"
  - [section 3.2]: "policy is parameterized as a lightweight multi-layer perceptron... ensures that the additional learnable component is compact, trainable with modest resources, and decoupled from the frozen LLM backbone."
  - [section 3.3]: "decouples the sampling capabilities... from the LLM training process"
  - [corpus]: "Controlled Decoding" (Mudgal et al., 2024) uses a learned prefix scorer but trains offline; this paper's RL test-time loop is distinct. Corpus provides no direct counterevidence.
- **Break condition:** If the policy's action space (temperature, top-p ranges) is too limited to express necessary decoding strategies, or if state features fail to capture uncertainty cues, adaptation may be ineffective.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
1. **Frozen LLM:** Generates token probabilities and hidden states; unchanged throughout.
2. **State extractor:** Concatenates mean-pooled final hidden states, top-k logits (k=50), prefix length, normalized entropy; linear projection + layer norm.
3. **Policy network:** 2-layer MLP (hidden size 256, GELU) outputs Gaussian over 2D action (temperature, top-p). Actions squashed via sigmoid and scaled to ranges [0.2,1.2] and [0.8,1.0].
4. **Reward calculator:** Computes normalized composite score from generated summary vs. reference (ROUGE-L) plus penalties/bonuses.
5. **PPO trainer:** Updates policy using rewards, advantages (GAE λ=0.95, γ=0.99), clipped objective, value loss, entropy bonus.

### Critical Path
1. Given prompt, initialize generation.
2. At each step: LLM forward pass → extract state → policy selects (temp, top-p) → sample next token → append to prefix.
3. Repeat until EOS or max length → compute final reward.
4. After collecting trajectories, PPO update using stored (state, action, reward) tuples.

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Action space size:* Limited to 2D (temp, top-p) for tractability; adding more knobs (top-k) increases expressivity but may require more RL samples.
- *State feature richness:* More features (e.g., full hidden states) could improve policy but increase compute; top-k logits balance informativeness and cost.
- *Reward complexity:* Composite rewards improve quality but risk instability; ablation needed per task.

### Failure Signatures
- *Reward collapse:* Policy converges to a single static parameter setting (e.g., always high temperature) early in training; check early→late change and action distribution entropy.
- *Overfitting to reward proxy:* High ROUGE but low human-rated quality; inspect generated examples for repetition or hallucination.
- *Policy instability:* Large variance in reward trajectories; may indicate poor reward scaling (e.g., consider sigmoid normalization as in ablations).

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Verify learning signal:** Train with Proposed Reward on a single dataset (e.g., WikiHow + Granite) and plot reward vs. PPO iteration (reproduce Figure 2). Expect steady upward trend.
2. **Baseline comparison:** Evaluate final policy against greedy and static (temp=0.3) baselines on all datasets. Compute relative gains (as in Table 1); expect consistent improvements, especially on BookSum/WikiHow.
3. **Reward ablation:** Run full ablation suite (ROUGE-only, core shaping, no coverage, etc.) on a held-out dataset (e.g., arXiv + Granite). Compare absolute rewards and early→late changes to confirm Proposed Reward's balance (Table 2).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the adaptive decoding framework be effectively applied to other text generation tasks beyond summarization, such as dialogue or story generation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "applying adaptive decoding to broader generative tasks such as dialogue or story generation" from the conclusion.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only validates on summarization datasets. Tasks like dialogue have different objectives (e.g., engagement, persona consistency) and reward design is non-trivial.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement the RL decoder sampler for dialogue generation (e.g., using PersonaChat), define suitable task-specific rewards (e.g., engagement, coherence), and compare against static decoding baselines.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Do LLM-based or preference-based reward signals improve the stability and performance of the learned decoding policy compared to the current hand-crafted composite reward?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "exploring preference-based or LLM-as-a-judge reward signals" from the conclusion.
- **Why unresolved:** Current rewards rely on heuristic metrics (ROUGE, length penalties). LLM-as-judge might better capture nuanced human preferences but could introduce noise, cost, or bias.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace the reward function with scores from a strong LLM (e.g., GPT-4) or human preference data, train policies, and evaluate both summary quality (automatic/human) and training stability (reward trajectories, early-late trends).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the learned decoding policy generalize to out-of-distribution domains or prompts not encountered during training?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The conclusion states "out-of-distribution robustness of the learned policy is an open empirical question." Training is limited to BookSum, arXiv, WikiHow with no cross-domain tests.
- **Why unresolved:** The policy may overfit to the training domains' reward functions and fail on new styles (e.g., poetry, legal text) without adaptation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train the policy on the original three datasets, then test on completely new domains (e.g., PubMed abstracts, Reddit posts) without retraining, measuring performance drops relative to static baselines.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the action space be expanded to include additional decoding parameters (e.g., top-k, repetition penalties) without compromising training stability or computational efficiency?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "extending this framework to richer action spaces (e.g., top-k, top-p, penalties)" from the conclusion.
- **Why unresolved:** Currently only temperature and top-p are used. More parameters increase dimensionality, risking PPO instability, slower convergence, or higher per-token latency.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Modify the policy to output actions for top-k and repetition penalties, train on summarization tasks, and monitor reward trajectories, convergence speed, and final performance vs. the 2D baseline.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Does adaptive decoding yield greater relative improvements for smaller language models compared to larger ones, and is there a model size beyond which benefits diminish?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Results show larger relative gains for Qwen-2.5-0.5B vs. Granite-3.3-2B, and the conclusion notes tested models are "the lower end" of scale.
- **Why unresolved:** It is hypothesized that smaller models benefit more due to higher sensitivity to decoding, but this is untested on larger models (e.g., 7B+).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate experiments across a spectrum of model sizes (0.5B, 2B, 7B, 70B), compute relative gains over static baselines, and analyze scaling trends to identify diminishing returns thresholds.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to summarization; applicability to other tasks (dialogue, translation) remains unverified.
- Experiments on small LLMs (≤2B parameters); potential scaling effects and benefits for larger models are unclear.
- Reliance on automatic metrics (ROUGE-L) and heuristic penalties risks misalignment with human preferences; possible reward hacking.

## Confidence
- Adaptive decoding improves quality: Medium
- Composite reward stabilizes learning: Medium
- Decoupling enables test-time adaptation without LLM retraining: High
