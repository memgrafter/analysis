# Ordinal Semantic Segmentation Applied to Medical and Odontological Images
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26736
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26736
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** Combining ordinal losses with cross-entropy reduces contact surface by up to 90% while maintaining or slightly improving Dice (up to ~1% gain).

## Executive Summary
Conventional semantic segmentation ignores ordinal relationships among classes, a critical oversight in medical imaging where labels represent ordered states such as disease severity or anatomical depth. This paper introduces three ordinal loss functions—Expanded Mean Squared Error (EXP_MSE), Quasi-Unimodal Loss (QUL), and Contact Surface Loss with Signed Distance Function (CSSDF)—that can be integrated with any deep segmentation model (U-Net) to enforce ordinal consistency. The losses operate at two levels: pixel-wise unimodal probability shaping and spatial smoothing of ordinal transitions. Evaluated on five medical and odontological datasets, the approach reduces the contact surface metric (invalid ordinal jumps between adjacent pixels) by up to 90% compared to a cross-entropy baseline, while Dice coefficients remain stable or improve by at most ~1%. The results demonstrate that ordinal constraints enhance structural coherence and anatomical plausibility without sacrificing segmentation accuracy.

## Method Summary
The method augments a standard U-Net segmentation pipeline with an additional ordinal loss term. The network outputs per-pixel softmax probabilities over K ordinal classes. The total loss is L = L_CE + λ·L_ord, where L_CE is cross-entropy and L_ord is one of three ordinal losses: (1) **QUL** enforces a quasi-unimodal shape in each pixel's probability distribution by comparing probabilities of adjacent ordinal classes and ensuring a peak at the true class with margin δ; (2) **EXP_MSE** treats class indices as numeric values, penalizing the squared deviation between the expected ordinal value (computed from probabilities) and the true label while regularizing the predictive variance; (3) **CSSDF** constructs signed distance functions for high-confidence regions of each class and penalizes the proximity of high-probability regions belonging to non-adjacent ordinal classes (|k1−k2|>1) with weighting γ. The model is trained with Adam (lr=1e-4), batch size 16, early stopping, and 5-fold cross-validation on each dataset. Evaluation uses Dice (accuracy), Contact Surface (spatial ordinal consistency), and Unimodal Pixels (pixel-level coherence). Results show consistent improvements in Contact Surface and Unimodal Pixels, with negligible impact on Dice.

## Key Results
- Contact surface reduced by up to 90% compared to cross-entropy alone.
- Dice coefficient maintained or improved by at most ~1%.
- Ordinal losses increased unimodal pixel percentage and enhanced anatomical fidelity across all five datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Quasi-Unimodal Loss (QUL)
QUL encourages the predicted probability distribution for each pixel to form a quasi-unimodal ridge aligned with the ordinal class order. For the true class k*, the loss includes ReLU terms that (i) ensure p(k*) exceeds the probabilities of its immediate left and right neighbors by at least δ, guaranteeing a peak, and (ii) enforce that any class left of k* has higher probability than any class further left (and similarly on the right), preserving monotonicity away from the peak. This allows small local deviations while maintaining global coherence. The margin δ and weight λ control the strictness of the constraint.

### Mechanism 2: Expanded Mean Squared Error (EXP_MSE)
EXP_MSE encodes ordinality by treating class indices as numeric values. For each pixel, it computes the expected ordinal label E[Y] = Σ k·p_k and the variance Var[Y] = Σ p_k·(k−E[Y])². The loss is |E[Y]−y|² + λ·Var[Y]. The first term forces the expected value to match the true ordinal label y; the second term encourages low variance, i.e., confident and compact probability distributions. This directly ties the error to ordinal distance while regularizing certainty.

### Mechanism 3: Contact Surface Loss with Signed Distance Function (CSSDF)
CSSDF reduces anatomically implausible ordinal transitions between neighboring pixels. For any class pair (k1, k2) with |k1−k2| > 1, a base cost ReLU(|k1−k2|−1) is applied. For each spatial location, the loss multiplies the probability of one class by the signed distance to the high-confidence region of the other class. The high-confidence region is defined by thresholding the predicted probability at δ=0.05 and computing an SDF; a boundary weighting factor γ modulates the penalty. This penalizes the proximity of high-probability regions belonging to non-adjacent ordinal classes, encouraging smooth ordinal transitions in the segmentation.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input image → U-Net → softmax probability tensor ˆp → loss calculation (CE + λ·L_ord) → backward pass → optimizer step.

**Critical path:**
1. Data loading: images and ordinal masks (integers 1..K).
2. Preprocessing: normalize images, augment (rotation, flip, intensity shifts).
3. Model forward: U-Net output logits → softmax.
4. Loss calculation: CE + λ·L_ord.
5. Backward: compute gradients, optimizer step.
6. Validation: monitor Dice, contact surface, unimodal pixels.
7. Repeat until early stopping.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- λ balances categorical accuracy vs. ordinal consistency; too high may hurt Dice, too low may not improve structural metrics.
- QUL/EXP_MSE add minimal compute; CSSDF requires distance transforms per class pair, increasing memory and time.
- Combining unimodal + spatial losses may yield best contact surface but needs careful λ scheduling.
- U-Net architecture is fixed; loss weighting must be tuned per dataset.

**Failure signatures:**
- Contact surface remains high → CSSDF hyperparameters (γ, δ) or λ may be mis-set.
- Dice coefficient drops significantly → ordinal loss over-regularizing; reduce λ or relax δ.
- Unimodal pixels metric low despite QUL/EXP_MSE → δ margin too large or model capacity insufficient.
- Training instability (loss spikes) → large λ or aggressive spatial penalties; consider gradient clipping.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline: Train U-Net with CE only; record Dice, contact surface, unimodal pixels on validation.
2. Unimodal ablation: Train with CE+QUL, sweep λ ∈ {0.1,1,10,100,1000} and δ ∈ [0.05,0.7]; measure changes in unimodal pixels and contact surface.
3. Spatial integration: Train with CE+CSSDF, vary γ ∈ [0.05,1.0] and δ=0.05; evaluate contact surface and Dice. Then combine best QUL + CSSDF with separate λ values.

## Limitations
- Architectural details (e.g., U-Net depth, filter counts, upsampling method) are unspecified, risking performance variance and reproducibility.
- Optimal hyperparameters (λ, δ, γ) per dataset are not reported, limiting practical deployment.
- The method lacks comparison to contemporary ordinal segmentation techniques; reported improvements may be incremental.
