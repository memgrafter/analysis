# Improving Search Agent with One Line of Code
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10069
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10069
- Reference count: 31
- One-line primary result: SAPO achieves absolute +10.6% EM (relative +31.5%) over Search‑R1 by adding a conditional KL penalty to GRPO.

## Executive Summary
Tool-based agentic reinforcement learning (TARL) methods such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) enable search‑augmented LLMs but suffer from importance sampling distribution drift (ISDD). ISDD causes vanishing gradients and catastrophic model collapse during training. This paper introduces Search Agent Policy Optimization (SAPO), a lightweight modification to GRPO that adds a token‑level KL penalty only for tokens with positive advantage and probability ratio below a threshold. The change stabilizes training while preserving informative gradient updates. Across seven QA benchmarks, SAPO improves exact match by 10.6% absolute (31.5% relative) over the Search‑R1 baseline. The approach shows consistent gains across model scales (1.5B–14B) and families (Qwen, LLaMA), with training dynamics confirming reduced ISDD and sustained stability.

## Method Summary
SAPO augments the standard GRPO objective with a conditional KL penalty. GRPO samples a group of G responses per query, computes per‑response rewards (F1 score), and normalizes advantages within the group. The policy gradient loss is the usual clipped PPO surrogate (`pg_loss`). SAPO adds: `kl_term_loss = -mean( log(ratio) * mask )`, where `ratio = exp(log_prob - old_log_prob)`, `mask = (advantage ≥ 0) & (ratio ≤ τ)`, and `τ=1.0`. Only tokens with positive advantage and ratio ≤ τ contribute; the penalty is scaled by coefficient `γ=0.1`. The total loss is `pg_loss + γ * kl_term_loss`. During rollout, the agent generates tokens in `<think>`, `<search>`, and `<answer>` blocks; retrieved documents (from `<documents>`) are produced by the environment and must be excluded from the KL computation. An `eos_mask` indicates which tokens belong to the agent’s output (1 for agent tokens, 0 for padding and retrieval tokens). Training uses a Qwen or LLaMA backbone, E5‑base‑v2 retriever, DPR Wikipedia corpus, max search turns T=5, group size G=10, and a total of 420 steps.

## Key Results
- Absolute EM improvement of 10.6% (relative 31.5%) over Search‑R1 averaged over seven QA benchmarks.
- Gains hold across model sizes (1.5B to 14B) and architecture families (Qwen, LLaMA).
- Training dynamics show stabilized importance sampling ratios, lower clipping frequency, and no catastrophic collapse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
In GRPO, all tokens in a response share the same advantage, so tokens with extreme importance sampling ratios (new/old) dominate the gradient update. This leads to ISDD: ratios can collapse toward zero or explode, causing vanishing gradients and training instability. SAPO mitigates by applying a one‑sided KL penalty only to tokens with positive advantage and ratio ≤ τ. This prevents the policy from increasing probability too much on already likely positive tokens (thereby bounding the ratio from below), while allowing large increases for tokens with ratio > τ (which are likely under‑represented in the old policy) and large decreases for negative‑advantage tokens (unpenalized). The selective constraint keeps the importance sampling distribution tighter, stabilizes variance, and maintains useful gradients for adaptation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: Normalizes advantages within each query’s response group (mean‑centered, divided by group std) to provide a stable baseline. *Why needed*: Avoids reward scaling issues in multi‑response settings. *Quick check*: Compute normalized advantages from a list of rewards for a single query.
- **Importance Sampling Distribution Drift (ISDD)**: The mismatch between the old (behavior) policy and the current policy leads to high variance in gradient estimates when ratios become extreme. *Why needed*: Understanding ISDD is key to designing stable on‑policy RL methods. *Quick check*: Write the importance sampling ratio and explain why it should stay near 1.
- **Token‑level KL penalty**: Regularizes the policy by penalizing the average log‑ratio over selected tokens, rather than a full sequence KL. *Why needed*: Allows targeted control over which tokens are penalized, offering more flexibility than a global KL term. *Quick check*: Derive the gradient of a token‑level KL penalty and discuss its effect on policy updates.
- **Retrieval‑augmented agent**: An LLM that can initiate search actions to retrieve external documents, interleaving generation with retrieval steps. *Why needed*: Handles knowledge‑intensive tasks requiring up‑to‑date or extensive information. *Quick check*: Describe how a `<search>` action is represented in the prompt and how retrieved documents are injected.
- **Group‑normalized advantage**: In GRPO, advantages are computed per response by subtracting the group mean and dividing by the group standard deviation of rewards. *Why needed*: Ensures each query contributes equally regardless of absolute reward magnitudes. *Quick check*: Given a tensor of rewards for a query, implement advantage normalization.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Query → Agent (policy network) → Generation (with `<think>`, `<search>`, `<answer>`) → Retriever (E5‑base‑v2) → Wikipedia corpus → Retrieved documents (inserted into context) → Agent continues → Final answer → Reward (F1) → Replay buffer (stores old log‑probs, actions, rewards) → Loss computation (PG loss + SAPO KL term) → Policy update. Old policy network is used for rollouts and log‑prob replay.

**Critical path**: For each training iteration: (1) Sample G responses per query using current policy with search (max T turns). (2) Compute F1 reward per response. (3) Normalize advantages within each query’s group. (4) Compute token‑level log‑probabilities under current and old policies (replay with same context). (5) Compute PPO clipped surrogate loss (`pg_loss`). (6) Compute SAPO KL term loss: identify tokens with advantage ≥ 0 and ratio ≤ τ, compute `-mean(log(ratio))` on those. (7) Total loss = `pg_loss + γ * kl_term_loss`. (8) Backpropagate and update policy.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Conditional KL penalty vs. standard global KL: Conditional approach allows large updates on “risky” tokens (high ratio or negative advantage), potentially improving exploration, but introduces two hyperparameters (τ, γ) that need tuning.
- Group advantage normalization reduces inter‑query bias but increases variance within a group; requires sufficient group size G.
- Masking retrieval tokens is essential to avoid penalizing non‑agent tokens, but constructing `eos_mask` precisely is non‑trivial and implementation‑specific.

**Failure signatures**:
- `kl_term_loss` becomes NaN or extremely large, indicating empty mask or ratio explosion.
- Importance sampling ratios collapse to near zero or diverge significantly from 1 (check histograms).
- Training reward plateaus or drops after a few steps, often preceded by a spike in clip ratio.
- Entropy of the policy drops rapidly, signaling premature convergence.

**First experiments**:
1. **Verify SAPO integration**: Train on a small subset (e.g., 100 queries) and plot `pg_loss` and `kl_term_loss` separately; ensure the latter is non‑zero and varies with advantage.
2. **Compare IS ratio distributions**: After equal training steps, plot histograms of importance sampling ratios for GRPO and SAPO; SAPO should show tighter concentration around 1.
3. **Ablate τ and γ**: Run a grid search on a single model size (τ ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.5}, γ ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}); record EM and number of collapsed runs to identify robust settings.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can we mitigate optimization conflicts when the current policy diverges significantly from the old policy on positive tokens?
- **Open Question 2**: What is the optimal strategy for selecting the KL penalty coefficient γ and IS ratio threshold τ in SAPO across different model scales and tasks?
- **Open Question 3**: Would replacing group‑level advantage with token‑level credit assignment further reduce ISDD and improve training stability in search agents?

## Limitations
- The exact definition of `eos_mask` for masking retrieval tokens is unspecified, risking implementation inconsistencies.
- The training data mixing ratio for NQ and HotpotQA (and any pre‑filtering) is not detailed, which could affect reproducibility and final performance.
- The claim of a “one line” code change underestimates the complexity of correctly integrating masking, advantage grouping, and old‑policy log‑prob replay.

## Confidence
