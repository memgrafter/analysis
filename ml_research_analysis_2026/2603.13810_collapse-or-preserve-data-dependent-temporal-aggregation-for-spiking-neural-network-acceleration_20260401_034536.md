# Collapse or Preserve: Data-Dependent Temporal Aggregation for Spiking Neural Network Acceleration
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13810
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13810
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** Temporal Aggregated Convolution (TAC) accelerates SNN inference on GPUs by pre-aggregating spikes, achieving up to 13.8× speedup with accuracy gains on rate-coded data, while TAC-TP preserves temporal information for event-based data with 50% fewer convolution calls.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the fundamental inefficiency of spiking neural networks (SNNs) on GPUs caused by spike sparsity, which conflicts with SIMD architectures' preference for dense operations. It introduces two complementary techniques: Temporal Aggregated Convolution (TAC) for rate-coded data, which collapses K temporal spike frames into a single convolution call, and TAC with Temporal Preservation (TAC-TP) for event-based data, which shares a convolution across K frames but broadcasts results to independent LIF steps to retain timing. The core finding is that the optimal strategy is data-dependent: collapsing time denoises rate-coded inputs and boosts speed, while preserving time is essential for motion-sensitive event data. Speedups are demonstrated on NVIDIA V100 and claimed to be hardware-agnostic.

## Method Summary
TAC groups consecutive spike frames (a hyperparameter K) into an aggregated tensor by summing along the temporal dimension before a single convolution, exploiting linearity to replace K small convolutions with one larger one. For event-based data where temporal resolution carries critical information (e.g., motion from DVS cameras), TAC-TP computes one shared convolution per K frames but then splits the result and applies it separately to each of the K temporal steps via independent LIF neuron updates, thus preserving fine-grained timing while still reducing convolution calls by a factor of K.

## Key Results
- **TAC on rate-coded data (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST):** 13.8× training speedup, +1.6% and +5.4% accuracy improvements respectively.
- **TAC-TP on event-based data (DVS128-Gesture):** 95.1% accuracy (vs 96.3% baseline) with 50% fewer convolution calls.
- **Hardware validation:** 11.0× speedup on NVIDIA V100; speedups argued to be architecture-agnostic.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GPUs with SIMD architectures inefficiently execute sparse, irregular spike operations due to scattered memory access and low arithmetic intensity. TAC circumvents this by pre-aggregating K spike frames into a dense tensor, converting many small convolution operations into a single larger but compute-dense one, thereby increasing throughput and utilization. For event data, TAC-TP shares the convolution's compute across the K frames but decouples it from temporal integration, avoiding the information loss that full collapsing would cause while still cutting the number of convolution calls. The linearity of convolution is key: summing inputs before convolution equals summing results after, enabling exact pre-aggregation.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Spike Frames -> K-Frame Grouper -> Aggregated Tensor -> Convolution Layer -> [TAC: Direct Output / TAC-TP: Broadcast to K Independent LIF Steps] -> Output
- **Critical path:** Convolution operations dominate compute; TAC reduces convolution calls by factor K; TAC-TP adds a broadcast and separate LIF steps post-convolution but shares the heavy convolution.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Collapsing time (TAC) maximizes speed and denoises but destroys temporal resolution; preserving time (TAC-TP) retains motion/timing information at the cost of slightly more post-processing and minor accuracy loss on event data.
- **Failure signatures:** Applying TAC to event data causes significant accuracy drop due to motion information loss; applying TAC-TP to rate-coded data may not fully leverage denoising potential, yielding smaller gains than TAC.
- **First three experiments:** 1) Train baseline SNN vs TAC on MNIST to measure speedup and accuracy; 2) Run TAC-TP on DVS128-Gesture and compare accuracy/conv-calls to baseline; 3) Test both methods on Fashion-MNIST and a second event dataset (e.g., CIFAR10-DVS) to validate the collapse-vs-preserve dichotomy.

## Limitations
- **Dataset diversity:** Only two rate-coded (MNIST variants) and one event-based dataset (DVS128-Gesture) evaluated; performance on complex or diverse data unknown.
- **Hardware scope:** Speedups validated solely on NVIDIA V100; behavior on other GPUs or neuromorphic chips not tested.
- **Overheads unquantified:** Memory/scheduling costs of K-frame grouping and broadcasting not measured; net wall-time gains may be less than FLOP reduction suggests.

## Confidence
- **Spike sparsity inefficiency on GPUs:** High – aligns with known SIMD constraints.
- **TAC speedup & accuracy gains on rate-coded data:** High – strong empirical results on two benchmarks.
- **TAC-TP preserving temporal info for event data:** Medium – single-dataset validation; motion-sensitive tasks may vary.
- **Data-dependent optimal strategy (collapse vs. preserve):** Medium – logically sound and supported by contrast, but needs broader validation.
- **Hardware-agnostic speedups:** Low – tested on one GPU generation; architectural dependencies likely.

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset Diversity:** Reproduce on at least two additional event-based datasets (e.g., CIFAR10-DVS, GEN1) and one high-dimensional rate-coded dataset (e.g., CIFAR-100) to test robustness of the collapse/preserve dichotomy.
2. **Cross-Architecture Benchmarking:** Implement TAC on at least two other hardware platforms (e.g., NVIDIA A100, AMD MI100) and measure ROI (speed vs. memory overhead) to evaluate hardware agnosticism.
3. **Energy-Performance Profiling:** Use power monitoring during inference to quantify Joules/epoch or Joules/accuracy, comparing TAC against baseline SNN and ANN equivalents to validate net energy savings.
