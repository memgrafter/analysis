# Eigenmood Space: Uncertainty-Aware Spectral Graph Analysis of Psychological Patterns in Classical Persian Poetry

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv:** 2602.16959
*   **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16959
*   **References:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** An uncertainty-aware LLM annotation pipeline with abstention flags and confidence scores yields a robust poet×concept matrix; Jensen-Shannon divergence quantifies poet individuality, and Laplacian spectral decomposition of a confidence-weighted concept co-occurrence graph produces an "Eigenmood" embedding for directional psychological contrast.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a scalable framework for profiling the psychological patterns of classical Persian poets. It addresses the challenge of metaphorical density by using an LLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash) for multi-label annotation with per-label confidence scores and an explicit abstention flag for uncertain verses. These annotations are aggregated into confidence-weighted Poet×Concept distributions. Poet individuality is measured as Jensen-Shannon divergence from a corpus baseline. The relational structure between psychological concepts is captured via a confidence-weighted co-occurrence graph; its Laplacian spectral decomposition yields a low-dimensional "Eigenmood" space where axes represent interpretable concept couplings. Applied to 61,573 verses from 10 poets, the method identifies Khayyam and Parvin as the most psychologically distinct, while Hafez and Shahriar are baseline-adjacent. The framework demonstrates high inter-annotator agreement (κ=0.818), robustness to confidence thresholding, and supports verse retrieval aligned with scholarly interpretation.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with verse-level annotation by an LLM, producing sets of psychological concepts, per-concept confidence scores, and an abstention flag. Aggregation sums confidence-weighted, non-abstained evidence for each poet-concept pair, then row-normalizes to form a distribution Pᵢ. A global baseline distribution P₀ is computed across all poets. Individuality is quantified by the symmetric Jensen-Shannon divergence D_JS(Pᵢ ∥ P₀). To capture concept relations, a graph is built where nodes are concepts (filtered by baseline frequency ≥10⁻³) and edge weights aggregate confidence-weighted co-occurrence. The unnormalized graph Laplacian L = D - W is spectrally decomposed. The first non-trivial eigenvectors define "Eigenmood" axes; poet coordinates are projections of (Pᵢ - P₀) onto these axes. The same eigenvectors score individual verses for retrieval along an axis.

## Key Results
*   On 61,573 verses from 10 poets, the LLM abstained on 22.2% of verses. Macro agreement with two human annotators is κ=0.818, and model calibration error is ECE=0.0346.
*   Khayyam shows the highest divergence from the corpus baseline (D_JS=0.0901), followed by Parvin (0.0459). Hafez and Shahriar are closest to the baseline.
*   Divergence rankings are highly robust to confidence thresholding (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.964). The Eigenmood axes reveal interpretable bipolar couplings (e.g., EM2: Internal Projection vs. Identity Fragmentation), enabling verse retrieval that aligns with domain knowledge.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Confidence-weighted aggregation with explicit abstention produces a robust Poet×Concept distribution that mitigates over-counting ambiguous verses.
*   **Mechanism:** For each poet, aggregate over non-abstained verses by summing (1−aᵥ)·pᵥ,꜀ for each concept c, where aᵥ is the abstention flag and pᵥ,꜀ is the confidence. Row-normalize to obtain a probability distribution Pᵢ(c).
*   **Core assumption:** Model confidence (pᵥ,꜀) and abstention (aᵥ) are meaningful signals of evidential strength and non-commitment; aggregating only non-abstained evidence with confidence weighting prevents spurious signal from forced or uncertain labels.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] “Each verse is associated with a set of psychological concepts, per-label confidence scores, and an abstention flag… aggregates confidence-weighted evidence into a Poet×Concept matrix.”
    *   [Section 4.2] Formal definition of Xi,c and Pi(c) with additive smoothing.
    *   [corpus] 22.2% abstention rate; macro agreement κ=0.818; calibration ECE=0.0346 (Section 5.1, 8.5).
*   **Break condition:** If abstention is not randomly distributed but systematically correlated with poet identity or verse characteristics (e.g., high abstention for small-subcorpus poets), then Pᵢ may be biased by selection effects.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Jensen–Shannon divergence from a global baseline distribution quantifies a poet’s distributional individuality in a way that is robust to corpus-size confounds and interpretable as rhetorical-affective distinctiveness.
*   **Mechanism:** Compute baseline P₀(c) by summing all poets’ confidence-weighted concept mass and normalizing. For each poet i, compute D_JS(Pᵢ∥P₀) as a symmetric, bounded measure of how far the poet’s concept distribution deviates from the corpus-wide norm.
*   **Core assumption:** The baseline P₀ captures the “default” rhetorical-affective configuration of the tradition; poets with higher D_JS use the ontology’s constructs in systematically different proportions, reflecting distinctive poetic strategies rather than sampling noise.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] “quantify individuality as divergence from a corpus baseline using Jensen–Shannon divergence.”
    *   [Section 4.3] Formal definitions of D_KL and D_JS; note that D_JS is symmetric and bounded.
    *   [corpus] Divergence rankings are robust under confidence thresholding (Spearman ρ≥0.964; Table 5) and stabilize in bootstrap for poets with sufficient non-abstained verses (Table 6).
*   **Break condition:** If the baseline P₀ is dominated by a few high-volume poets, then D_JS may reflect deviation from a non-representative norm. Also, if abstention rates vary widely, treating only non-abstained mass in Pᵢ but including all mass in P₀ could create a systematic comparison bias.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Laplacian spectral decomposition of a confidence-weighted concept co-occurrence graph yields an “Eigenmood” embedding that captures relational (pairwise) structure among psychological concepts, enabling directional contrast and verse retrieval aligned with coupling patterns.
*   **Mechanism:** Build an undirected graph where nodes are concepts (filtered to baseline share ≥10⁻³). Edge weight W_{cd} aggregates confidence-weighted co-activation across verses. Compute unnormalized Laplacian L = D − W. Eigenvectors u_k of L (ordered by increasing eigenvalue) define axes; project baseline-centered poet distributions onto these axes to obtain Eigenmood coordinates zᵢ^{(k)} = Σ_c (Pᵢ(c)−P₀(c)) u_k(c). Verse-level scores s_v^{(k)} similarly score alignment with axis k.
*   **Core assumption:** The graph Laplacian’s spectral modes encode a notion of “affective geometry” where concepts that co-occur frequently form clusters; eigenvectors highlight contrasts between coupled regions of the concept space, which are stable across poets and can be used for retrieval.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] “captures relational structure beyond marginals… Laplacian spectral decomposition yields an ‘Eigenmood’ embedding for directional contrast.”
    *   [Section 4.4–4.5] Formal construction of W, L, and zᵢ^{(k)}; justification for unnormalized Laplacian.
    *   [corpus] Top edges (Table 7) show dense core (Melancholia, Emotional Dependency, Romantic Obsession). EM2 contrasts Internal Projection vs. Identity Fragmentation; EM3 contrasts Ambivalent Attachment vs. Identity Fragmentation/Internal Projection (Table 8). Poet coordinates form interpretable clusters (Fig. 8, 9). Retrieval exemplars for axes align with scholarly interpretations (Section 7).
*   **Break condition:** If the concept graph is too sparse (e.g., after filtering), early eigenvectors may capture noise or artifacts of filtering rather than meaningful coupling. Also, changing Laplacian normalization (e.g., symmetric normalized) alters geometry substantially (correlations drop to ~0.5 for EM3), implying that the specific choice of Laplacian shapes the embedding.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Uncertainty-aware multi-label annotation (abstention + confidence).
    *   **Why needed here:** The entire pipeline rests on treating model non-commitment (abstention) and graded evidence (confidence) as first-class signals. Without these, aggregation would force labels on ambiguous verses and produce spurious poet-level patterns.
    *   **Quick check:** If a verse is abstained, should its (zero) evidence contribute to a poet’s concept distribution? How does confidence weighting change the contribution of a low-confidence label versus a high-confidence one?
*   **Concept:** Graph Laplacian and spectral embedding.
    *   **Why needed here:** The Eigenmood space is defined via Laplacian eigenvectors of the concept co-occurrence graph. Understanding why the Laplacian (not the adjacency matrix itself) is decomposed is key: it encodes smoothness over the graph, making eigenvectors highlight cuts or contrasts between densely connected concept clusters.
    *   **Quick check:** What is the relationship between the second eigenvector of an unnormalized Laplacian and the “most balanced cut” of a graph? How might that relate to contrasting two groups of co-occurring psychological concepts?
*   **Concept:** Jensen–Shannon divergence for distributional comparison.
    *   **Why needed here:** D_JS is the primary measure of poet individuality. It is symmetric, bounded, and more robust to zero probabilities than KL divergence. Knowing its properties (e.g., square root is a metric) helps interpret distance values.
    *   **Quick check:** Why might KL divergence be problematic for comparing a poet’s distribution to the baseline if the poet has near-zero mass on some concepts that are common in the baseline?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Annotation Module -> Aggregation Module -> Baseline & Divergence Module -> Graph Module -> Spectral Module -> Validation & Diagnostics
*   **Critical path:** Annotation → Aggregation → Graph construction → Spectral decomposition → Poet/verse embeddings → Retrieval & validation. The graph must be built from the same non-abstained, confidence-weighted evidence used for Pᵢ; the spectral basis is fixed before projecting poets/verses.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Confidence weighting vs. uniform:** Weighting emphasizes high-certainty evidence; uniform treats all predicted labels equally. Paper shows rankings are stable (ρ=1.000) but confidence weighting is kept for epistemic fidelity.
    *   **Graph filtering:** Excluding low-frequency concepts (e.g., `idealization`) avoids sparsity-driven eigenmodes. Trade-off: losing potentially meaningful but rare constructs.
    *   **Laplacian normalization:** Unnormalized (used) emphasizes absolute co-activation mass; normalized reweights by degree and changes geometry. Unnormalized is chosen to highlight contrasts supported by high-weight edges.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **High abstention + small subcorpus:** e.g., Khayyam (356 verses, 38.5% abstention) yields wide bootstrap intervals. Any poet with <~500 non-abstained verses will have unstable D_JS and Eigenmood coordinates.
    *   **Ontology sparsity:** `idealization` occurs only 3 times globally; including it in the graph would make eigenvectors dominated by noise.
    *   **Laplacian sensitivity:** Switching to normalized Laplacian changes EM3 correlations to ~0.335, so results are not invariant to this choice.
    *   **Selection bias:** If abstention is correlated with poet identity, then treating abstention as “missing” could misstate individuality; rank changes occur when abstention is included as a category.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Validate abstention distribution:** Check that abstention rate is not trivially predicted by verse count. Compute correlation between poet verses and abstention rate; expect moderate (paper finds r=0.644, N=10, p=0.044 but small N).
    2.  **Robustness to confidence thresholding:** Recompute D_JS and Eigenmood coordinates after filtering labels with pᵥ,꜀ < τ (τ=0.5, 0.7). Expect Spearman ρ≥0.964 for D_JS ranking (Table 5) and high correlation for eigenvectors (>0.999 for EM1–EM3 per paper).
    3.  **Graph construction sanity check:** Rebuild co-occurrence graph W using simple co-occurrence counts instead of confidence-weighted edges. Compute correlation of resulting eigenvectors with the paper’s unnormalized eigenvectors; expect >0.999 (paper reports this).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  **Alternative graph constructions:** How do normalized Laplacians or PMI-based edge weighting influence the Eigenmood embedding's structure and interpretability? The paper notes that switching Laplacian normalization alters geometry substantially.
2.  **Integrating richer metadata:** To what extent does incorporating genre, meter, or chronology refine the uncertainty-aware psychological profiling and reveal finer-grained patterns?
3.  **Cross-corpus transfer:** Does the pipeline generalize to other literary traditions, and what adaptations are needed for different metaphorical densities, ontologies, and data availability?
4.  **Ontology revision:** How should the psychological ontology be revised to address extreme sparsity (e.g., `idealization` occurs only 3 times) and improve construct validity for classical Persian poetry?

## Limitations
*   Abstention strongly correlates with poet identity (e.g., Khayyam 38.5% abstention), introducing potential selection bias into divergence measurements.
*   Concept graph filtering (baseline share ≥10⁻³) removes rare constructs; eigenvectors may be sensitive to this arbitrary threshold, risking exclusion of meaningful but infrequent psychological dimensions.
*   The unnormalized Laplacian geometry is not invariant—alternative normalizations yield correlations as low as ~0.5, questioning the stability of the Eigenmood space.

## Confidence
*   **Uncertainty-aware aggregation:** **High**. Strong inter-annotator agreement (κ=0.818), low calibration error (ECE=0.0346), and explicit abstention handling provide robust poet–concept distributions.
*   **JS divergence individuality:** **Medium-High**. Rankings are robust to confidence thresholds (ρ≥0.964) and bootstrap, but differing abstention rates across poets may bias the baseline comparison.
*   **Eigenmood embedding:** **Medium**. Axes are interpretable and enable retrieval, yet high sensitivity to Laplacian normalization and concept-filtering thresholds limits generalizability.
