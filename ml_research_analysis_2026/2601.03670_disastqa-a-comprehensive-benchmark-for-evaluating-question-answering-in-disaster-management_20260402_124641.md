# DisastQA: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Evaluating Question Answering in Disaster Management

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03670
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03670
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** Introduces a 3,000-question disaster QA benchmark with tri-level evaluation (Base/Mix/Golden), revealing that all tested models suffer significant factual completeness drops under noisy retrieval, with performance diverging sharply from general-domain leaderboards.

## Executive Summary
DisastQA presents a novel 3,000-question benchmark (2,000 multiple-choice, 1,000 open-ended) spanning eight disaster types, constructed via a human-LLM collaboration pipeline to ensure factual rigor. It evaluates models under three evidence conditions—closed-book (Base), noisy retrieval (Mix), and perfect context (Golden)—to separate internal knowledge from evidence-reasoning robustness. Testing 20 models shows that while open-weight models approach proprietary systems in the Golden setting, all exhibit sharp accuracy and coverage declines under Mix noise. Critically, performance on DisastQA poorly correlates with general QA benchmarks like MMLU-Pro, highlighting that high general capability does not transfer to safety-critical disaster contexts.

## Method Summary
The benchmark is built from DisastIR passages through stratified sampling across disaster types. An LLM first drafts questions and answers; human experts then rewrite questions for clarity, verify gold answers, author/improvise distractors (MCQ) or final answers (OE), and decompose OE gold answers into atomic keypoints (for a 200-question subset). Models are evaluated in three settings: Base (closed-book), Mix (1 gold + 4 distractor passages simulating Top-5 retrieval), and Golden (gold passage only). Primary metrics are MCQ accuracy and human-verified Keypoint Coverage (factual completeness of OE responses), with ROUGE/BLEU/BERTScore as secondary fluency metrics.

## Key Results
- Benchmark comprises 3,000 questions (2,000 MCQ, 1,000 OE) covering 8 disaster types, with human-verified keypoints for 200 OE items.
- Models like Qwen-3-8B approach GPT-5.2 and Gemini-3 Pro in Golden OE (keypoint coverage), but all show 5–10% absolute drops in Mix, especially on complex multi-fact questions.
- DisastQA performance diverges significantly from MMLU-Pro rankings, indicating poor transfer of general QA skills to disaster response contexts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The tri-level evaluation framework (Base, Mix, Golden) systematically disentangles parametric knowledge from evidence-reasoning robustness under realistic noise.
- **Mechanism:** Base tests internal knowledge; Mix (1 gold + 4 distractors) measures discriminative ability to ignore noise; Golden provides an upper bound with perfect evidence. The Golden→Mix performance gap isolates noise-handling capability.
- **Core assumption:** Errors in Mix stem primarily from inability to disregard plausible distractors rather than knowledge gaps (since Golden supplies correct evidence).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "separate internal knowledge from evidence-reasoning robustness"; [section 5.1] Mix simulates Top-5 retrieval to "quantify the LLM’s discriminative ability."
- **Break condition:** If models show no meaningful Golden→Mix drop, the noise simulation fails to create a meaningful robustness test.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Keypoint Coverage—a human-verified atomic fact recall metric—provides a reliable measure of factual completeness in open-ended disaster QA, avoiding over-reward of fluency.
- **Mechanism:** Gold answers are manually decomposed into minimal, independently verifiable facts (keypoints). Model outputs are scored on the proportion of keypoints they semantically entail, regardless of phrasing.
- **Core assumption:** Lexical overlap metrics (ROUGE, BLEU) fail to penalize answers that are fluent but omit critical quantitative or qualitative details essential for disaster decision-making.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "human-verified keypoint coverage metric measures factual completeness"; [section 6.2] shows Gemma-7B high ROUGE but lags in Keypoint Coverage.
- **Break condition:** If human-annotated keypoints have low inter-annotator agreement or miss critical non-atomic facts required by decision-makers.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A human-LLM collaboration pipeline with stratified sampling produces a large-scale, factually rigorous benchmark by combining LLM scalability with domain-expert oversight for plausibility and grounding.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs generate initial QA pairs from DisastIR passages; human experts rewrite questions, verify/author gold answers, craft discriminative distractors (MCQ), or decompose answers (OE). Stratified sampling balances disaster types.
- **Core assumption:** Raw LLM outputs contain systematic failures (implausible distractors, vague questions, incomplete answers) that render them unsuitable for safety-critical benchmarking without human correction.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3] pipeline details; Table 5 shows high rewrite rates (35–100%); [section B.1] lists common LLM draft issues.
- **Break condition:** If post-human verification consistency is low (e.g., <90% on solve-check), the process lacks reliability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) under noise**  
  *Why needed:* DisastQA's Mix setting tests RAG robustness where retrieved contexts contain both gold evidence and plausible distractors, a core real-world failure mode.  
  *Quick check:* Why would a model that performs perfectly with a gold passage fail when provided with the same gold passage plus four irrelevant but topic-related distractors?

- **Concept: Factual Completeness Metrics vs. Fluency Metrics**  
  *Why needed:* The paper shows surface-level metrics (ROUGE, BLEU) mask reliability gaps in disaster QA; understanding their limitations is key to interpreting results.  
  *Quick check:* Give an example of a fluent, high-ROUGE answer that would still score poorly on Keypoint Coverage in a disaster QA context.

- **Concept: Human-in-the-Loop Data Construction for Safety-Critical Domains**  
  *Why needed:* Benchmark validity hinges on human correction of LLM drafts; knowing specific interventions (rewriting, distractor crafting, keypoint decomposition) is crucial.  
  *Quick check:* What are two specific ways human experts improve MCQ distractors beyond what an LLM generates in a single prompt?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** DisastIR queries -> Stratified sampler -> LLM drafter -> Human refiner -> Keypoint annotator (subset) -> Mix context builder -> Evaluator -> Metric calculator
- **Critical path:** DisastIR queries are sampled across disaster types; an LLM drafts questions/answers; humans rewrite questions, verify/author gold answers, and create distractors or keypoints; the Mix context builder combines 1 gold + 4 stratified distractors; models are run under Base/Mix/Golden prompts; outputs are scored via accuracy or keypoint coverage.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Scalability vs. Factual Rigor:* LLM drafts enable 3k questions but require expensive human rewrites (35–100% rewrite rates); higher automation risks domain-specific errors.  
  - *Automatic vs. Human OE Evaluation:* Lexical metrics are cheap but unreliable; human keypoint coverage is gold-standard but only annotates 200 items (20% of OE set).  
  - *Noise Realism:* Mix uses 4 fixed distractor relevance strata (0–3), a simplified proxy for real retrieval; actual failures may have different distributions.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - MCQ outputs in wrong format (e.g., text instead of A/B/C/D).  
  - Mix context construction errors (e.g., gold passage missing, wrong distractor tiers).  
  - High ROUGE/BLEU but low Keypoint Coverage, especially for quantitative details.  
  - Larger performance gaps for "Biological" and "Extraterrestrial" events.  
  - Smaller models degrade more severely on "Hard"/"Extremely Complex" OE questions.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate Base vs. Mix accuracy gap using Llama-3-8B on 100 sampled MCQ items; expect ~5–10% absolute drop.  
  2. On the 200-item OE subset, compute correlation between ROUGE-L and Keypoint Coverage; expect low/negative correlation in Mix.  
  3. Evaluate Qwen-3-8B on MCQ Golden for "Biological" vs. "Geohazard" events; expect >5% gap.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What specific architectural or training modifications enable models like Gemini-3 Pro to demonstrate greater relative robustness to noisy retrieval contexts (Mix setting) compared to other frontier models, despite similar performance in the Golden setting?
2. To what extent can the human-verified keypoint coverage metric be reliably automated or approximated by LLM-based evaluation without significant loss of correlation with human judgment, especially for complex, multi-fact disaster QA responses?
3. Do the observed performance divergences between DisastQA and general-domain benchmarks like MMLU-Pro stem primarily from (a) a lack of domain-specific knowledge in model pre-training corpora, (b) impaired reasoning capabilities under uncertainty and noise, or (c) a combination of both, and how can these factors be disentangled?
4. How does the complexity of disaster information needs, as quantified by the number of required atomic facts (keypoints), interact with the fidelity of retrieved evidence to determine the threshold at which current LLMs fail to produce factually complete open-ended answers?
5. Can the human-LLM collaboration pipeline for constructing high-quality, evidence-grounded QA pairs be scaled to cover a broader range of disaster types and multilingual contexts while maintaining the same level of factual rigor and distractor plausibility?

## Limitations
- Keypoint annotations exist for only 200 of 1,000 open-ended questions; coverage metric lacks full coverage.
- Distractor sampling procedure and relevance stratification details are unspecified; Mix noise realism may vary.
- No reported inter-annotator agreement for keypoint decomposition; gold standard reliability uncertain.

## Confidence
- Tri-level evaluation disentangles knowledge vs. reasoning: Medium
- Keypoint Coverage as factual completeness metric: Medium
- Human-LLM collaboration pipeline ensures benchmark rigor: Medium
- Model robustness degradation under noisy context: High
