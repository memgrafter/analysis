# Dual-Metric Evaluation of Social Bias in Large Language Models: Evidence from an Underrepresented Nepali Cultural Context

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07792
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07792
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Implicit completion bias rates (0.740–0.755) exceeded explicit agreement bias rates (0.36–0.43), with weak or negative correlations between the two measures across seven LLMs.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the gap in evaluating social bias within underrepresented cultural contexts, specifically Nepal. The authors introduce the EquiText-Nepali dataset and a Dual-Metric Bias Assessment (DMBA) framework to measure both explicit model agreement with stereotypical statements and implicit stereotypical tendencies in generative completions. Testing seven state-of-the-art LLMs, they found that implicit bias was substantially higher and more consistent across models than explicit bias. Decoding parameters like temperature amplified explicit bias but left implicit generative bias largely stable. The weak or negative correlation between explicit and implicit measures indicates they capture distinct bias phenomena, underscoring the need for culturally specific benchmarks and debiasing strategies that address both expressed beliefs and generative behavior.

## Method Summary
The evaluation utilizes the EquiText-Nepali dataset, a Croissant-compliant collection of over 2400 English sentence pairs (stereotypical vs. anti-stereotypical) stratified by gender, race, and sociocultural domains relevant to Nepal. For each of seven LLMs accessed via OpenRouter API, the DMBA framework computes two metrics: (1) **Explicit agreement bias** (`Bias_agreement`), derived from model-scored agreement with full sentences using a standardized prompt template; (2) **Implicit completion bias** (`Bias_completion` rate), where models generate continuations from 6-token truncated prompts, and completions are classified as stereotypical if their TF-IDF cosine similarity to a stereotypical reference exceeds a threshold (τ=0.7) and is greater than similarity to an anti-stereotypical reference. The study also analyzes domain-specific bias and sensitivity to decoding parameters (temperature, top-p). Critical unknowns include the exact prompt template wording for explicit scoring and the TF-IDF vectorization hyperparameters, which pose reproducibility challenges.

## Key Results
- Implicit generative bias rates (0.740–0.755) were consistently higher than explicit agreement bias rates (0.36–0.43) across all models.
- Explicit and implicit bias measures showed weak or negative correlations, suggesting they capture fundamentally different bias dimensions.
- Decoding parameters (temperature, top-p) amplified explicit bias but had minimal effect on implicit generative bias, which remained stable across sampling settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not theorize specific cognitive or architectural mechanisms behind the dual-metric divergence. Operationally, the approach works by decoupling **stated agreement** (influenced by instruction tuning and prompt phrasing) from **generative tendencies** (shaped by next-token prediction and latent representations). The stability of implicit bias across decoding parameters suggests it reflects deeper, representation-level associations, whereas explicit bias is more susceptible to output-level sampling strategies and prompt formulation.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: EquiText-Nepali dataset -> Prompt Templates & Truncated Inputs -> LLM Inference (via OpenRouter) -> Explicit Scoring & Completion Generation -> TF-IDF Vectorization -> Similarity Computation -> Bias Metrics (Bias_agreement, Bias_completion)

Critical path: Data loading → Per-model evaluation (explicit & implicit) → Metric aggregation → Correlation & domain analysis.

Design tradeoffs: TF-IDF chosen over embeddings for simplicity and interpretability, but may miss nuanced semantics; fixed 6-token truncation limits context but isolates stereotypical associations; exact prompt template unreported, trading reproducibility for methodological clarity.

Failure signatures: High variance in explicit bias scores across prompt variants; API rate limits or errors causing incomplete model coverage; similarity scores near τ=0.7 leading to unstable classification flips.

First three experiments: (1) Pilot explicit bias with 3 prompt variants on 50 sentence pairs to quantify template sensitivity. (2) Re-run implicit bias with standardized TF-IDF settings (e.g., ngram_range=(1,2), max_features=5000) and compare rates to original. (3) Compute per-model Spearman correlation between explicit and implicit scores; test significance and stability under vectorization audits.

## Limitations
- The exact prompt template for explicit agreement scoring is unreported, preventing direct replication of `Bias_agreement`.
- TF-IDF vectorization hyperparameters (n-grams, feature limits, stop-words) are unspecified, potentially altering `Bias_completion` classification.
- Findings are confined to the Nepali cultural context; generalizability to other underrepresented regions remains unvalidated.

## Confidence
- Implicit bias robustness across models: **High** (consistent rates observed).
- Explicit bias sensitivity to decoding parameters: **High** (clearly documented amplification).
- Weak/negative explicit-implicit correlation: **Medium** (plausible but may be confounded by prompt/vectorization noise).
- Generalizability beyond Nepal: **Low** (single-context evaluation).
