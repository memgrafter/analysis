# Scaled Gradient Descent for Ill-Conditioned Low-Rank Matrix Recovery with Optimal Sampling Complexity
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00060
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00060
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Scaled Gradient Descent (ScaledGD) with spectral initialization achieves linear convergence with iteration complexity independent of the condition number κ and sample complexity optimal up to a κ² factor, m = Θ((n₁+n₂)rκ²), for Gaussian measurements.

## Executive Summary
This paper resolves a key trade-off in non-convex low-rank matrix recovery. Prior ScaledGD methods either suffered from iteration complexity dependent on the condition number κ (like vanilla gradient descent) or required a suboptimal number of samples. The authors prove that under Gaussian measurements, ScaledGD with a spectral initializer converges linearly to the true matrix using an optimal number of samples (Θ((n₁+n₂)r) up to κ²) and with iteration complexity O(log(1/ε)) that is completely independent of κ. The analysis introduces a refined decoupling technique using virtual sequences to tighten operator norm bounds, eliminating a problematic √r factor present in earlier work. This unifies optimal sample complexity and condition-number-free convergence for general (non-PSD) low-rank matrices.

## Method Summary
The algorithm is Scaled Gradient Descent (ScaledGD) applied to a factorization X = LRᵀ. Starting from a spectral initializer (top-r SVD of A*(y)), each iteration updates the factors L and R with gradients preconditioned by the inverses of the local Gram matrices RᵀR and LᵀL, respectively. This adaptive scaling normalizes the local curvature. The step size μ is a small constant (μ ≤ 1/32 in theory). The theoretical guarantee requires the linear measurement operator A to satisfy a rank-2r Restricted Isometry Property (RIP) with a small constant, which holds with high probability for Gaussian measurements when m ≳ (n₁+n₂)rκ².

## Key Results
- **Convergence:** For m ≥ C(n₁+n₂)rκ² Gaussian measurements, ScaledGD with spectral initialization converges linearly: after T = O(log(√r)/μ) iterations, dist(X_T, X*) ≤ σ_min(X*)/√r, and subsequently dist(X_t, X*) ≤ (1−0.6μ)^{t−T} dist(X_T, X*). This implies ‖X_t − X*‖_F = O(√r (1−µ/10)^t σ_min(X*)).
- **Sample Complexity:** The required number of samples is optimal up to a κ² factor, matching information-theoretic lower bounds for general low-rank matrices.
- **Conditioning:** The iteration complexity and convergence rate are independent of the condition number κ, a significant improvement over convex or non-preconditioned non-convex methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** ScaledGD's preconditioned updates eliminate condition number dependence in iteration complexity.
- **Mechanism:** Updates for factor matrices L and R are scaled by (RᵀR)⁻¹ and (LᵀL)⁻¹. This pre-conditioning adapts to the local geometry of the factorization, effectively normalizing the curvature so that the Lipschitz constant of the gradient becomes independent of κ.
- **Core assumption:** A satisfies rank-2r RIP with constant δ₂r ≤ 0.02.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states ScaledGD reduces iteration complexity to O(log(1/ε)); [section 4.1] cites prior local convergence theory showing linear convergence once dist(X_T, X*) ≤ 0.1σ_min(X*).
- **Break condition:** δ₂r > 0.02 or step size μ > 1/32 invalidates the linear convergence guarantee.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Spectral initialization under Gaussian measurements yields an initial point within O(σ_min(X*)) distance using m = Θ((n₁+n₂)rκ²) samples.
- **Mechanism:** The top-r SVD of A*(y) concentrates around X* when the number of measurements is large enough. RIP is used to bound ‖X₀ − X*‖₂.
- **Core assumption:** A consists of i.i.d. Gaussian matrices; X* has condition number κ.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] gives the sample bound and initialization distance; [section 4.1] and Lemma B.1 formalize ‖X₀ − X*‖₂ ≤ c₀σ_min(X*) under m = O((n₁+n₂)rκ²).
- **Break condition:** Insufficient samples (m below a constant×(n₁+n₂)rκ²) or extremely large κ can cause the spectral error bound to fail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The virtual sequence decoupling argument avoids a √r factor in the operator norm contraction, enabling optimal sample complexity.
- **Mechanism:** For each pair (w,v) in an ε-net of the product of unit spheres, a virtual iterate X_t^{(w,v)} is constructed via a modified operator A^{(w,v)} that makes the sequence independent of the inner products ⟨A_i, wvᵀ⟩. This decoupling allows bounding ‖(A*A − I)(X* − X_t)‖₂ directly in terms of ‖X* − X_t‖₂, bypassing the weaker Frobenius-norm bound that incurs a √r loss.
- **Core assumption:** The ε-net has size exp(O(n₁+n₂)); A satisfies RIP of order 4r+1 with a small constant.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.2] states the goal is to show ‖(A*A − I)(X* − X_t)‖₂ ≲ ‖X* − X_t‖₂ and that the decoupling technique from [29] is employed.
- **Break condition:** Virtual sequences must stay close to true iterates; large step sizes or large RIP constants cause the decoupling bounds to fail.

## Foundational Learning
### Restricted Isometry Property (RIP)
- **Why needed:** Ensures the measurement operator acts nearly isometrically on low-rank matrices, controlling gradient errors and initialization bounds.
- **Quick check:** If A satisfies rank-r RIP with constant δ_r, then (1−δ_r)‖Z‖_F² ≤ ‖A(Z)‖_F² ≤ (1+δ_r)‖Z‖_F² for all rank-r matrices Z.

### Scaled Gradient Descent (ScaledGD)
- **Why needed:** The core algorithm; its preconditioned updates neutralize ill-conditioning by adapting to local geometry.
- **Quick check:** For X = LRᵀ, the ScaledGD updates are: L ← L − μ (∇_L f(L,R)) (RᵀR)⁻¹ and R ← R − μ (∇_R f(L,R)) (LᵀL)⁻¹. How do the inverses affect the effective step size along directions with small/large singular values?

### Virtual Sequence Decoupling
- **Why needed:** The key analytical innovation that tightens the operator norm bound from O(√r) to O(1), enabling optimal sample complexity.
- **Quick check:** Why is the virtual operator A^{(w,v)} constructed with an extra measurement in the wvᵀ direction? To make the virtual sequence's error independent of the inner product ⟨A_i, wvᵀ⟩, enabling a uniform net argument.

### Spectral Initialization
- **Why needed:** Provides a starting point inside the local convergence region of ScaledGD using only a near-optimal number of samples.
- **Quick check:** How is the spectral initializer X₀ formed from the measurements y = A(X*)? X₀ is the top-r SVD of the "dual" matrix A*(y).

### Low-Rank Factorization
- **Why needed:** Parameterizes the low-rank matrix X as LRᵀ, transforming the original, non-convex problem into an optimization over factors.
- **Quick check:** What are the degrees of freedom in an n₁×n₂ rank-r factorization? They are r(n₁+n₂−r), which is sublinear in n₁n₂ for r ≪ min(n₁,n₂).

### Gaussian Measurements
- **Why needed:** The measurement model under which RIP holds with high probability with m = O((n₁+n₂)r) samples (up to κ²), enabling the sample complexity claim.
- **Quick check:** What does "i.i.d. Gaussian measurements" mean? Each measurement is y_i = ⟨A_i, X*⟩ where A_i has i.i.d. N(0,1) entries.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Spectral Initialization -> ScaledGD (L,R updates with preconditioning) -> Virtual Sequence Construction (for analysis) -> RIP Verification (Gaussian concentration) -> Local Convergence Lemma (from prior work) -> Final Error Bound.

### Critical path
The proof of Theorem 3.1 proceeds as: 1) Under m = C(n₁+n₂)rκ², A satisfies rank-2r RIP with δ₂r ≤ 0.02 w.h.p. (Lemma 4.2). 2) Under the same sample complexity, spectral initialization gives ‖X₀ − X*‖₂ ≤ c₀σ_min(X*) (Lemma B.1). 3) Using virtual sequences, prove after T = Θ(log(√r)/μ) iterations: ‖X_T − X*‖₂ ≤ σ_min(X*)/√r (Lemma 4.7). 4) Apply local convergence (Lemma 4.3): for t ≥ T, dist(X_t, X*) ≤ (1−0.6μ)^{t−T} dist(X_T, X*). 5) Combine to obtain final bounds (6) and (7).

### Design tradeoffs
- **Sample complexity vs. iteration complexity:** Achieves information-theoretic optimal sample complexity Θ((n₁+n₂)r) (up to κ²) with iteration complexity O(log(1/ε)) independent of κ, contrasting with some convex methods.
- **Measurement model:** Analysis requires Gaussian measurements to ensure RIP with a sufficiently small constant. For other models (e.g., subsampled Fourier), RIP may hold but with different, possibly larger constants.
- **Initialization:** Theory requires spectral initialization; random initialization is not covered and is an open problem.
- **Step size:** Theory requires μ ≤ 1/32; empirically larger steps (e.g., 0.5) work, indicating a gap between analysis and practice.

### Failure signatures
- **Insufficient samples:** If m < c(n₁+n₂)rκ², RIP may fail, causing large initial error or divergence.
- **Extreme conditioning:** If κ is huge, the required m scales with κ²; with fixed m, the algorithm may fail or converge slower than predicted.
- **Poor initialization:** If ‖X₀ − X*‖₂ > c₀σ_min(X*), the local convergence region is not entered; may require many iterations or a different initializer.
- **Non-Gaussian measurements:** If A does not satisfy RIP with small enough constants, the error bounds break down.
- **Overparameterization:** Using factorization rank k > r is not covered by the theory; could lead to rank overfitting or altered convergence.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Convergence rate validation:** Generate random ill-conditioned X* (n₁=n₂=100, r=30, κ=100) with Gaussian measurements m=4n₁r. Run ScaledGD (μ=0.5) and vanilla GD (step size ∝ 1/σ_max). Plot relative error vs. iterations and time. Expect ScaledGD to show rapid linear decay, while GD is slower.
2. **Phase transition in sample complexity:** Fix n₁=70, n₂=80, κ=5. Vary r ∈ {1,5,10,15,20} and m from 1000 to 14000. For each (r,m), run 10 trials; record success rate (relative error < 1e-8 after 100 iterations). Plot heatmap; expect a sharp transition line m ∝ r, confirming optimal sample complexity scaling.
3. **Condition number robustness:** For n₁=n₂=100, r=30, set m=5n₁r. Vary κ from 1 to 50. For each κ, run ScaledGD, GD, and RGD until relative error ≤ 1e-6 and record time. Plot time vs. κ. Expect ScaledGD and RGD times to be nearly constant, while GD time grows linearly with κ.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can the results be extended to random initialization instead of spectral initialization?
- Can the κ² dependence in the sample complexity be removed for general matrices, matching the information-theoretic lower bound?
- Does the analysis extend to other measurement models (e.g., subsampled Fourier, Rademacher) without a significant loss in the RIP constant?
- What is the behaviour for over-parameterized factorizations (k > r)?
- Can the theoretical step size bound (μ ≤ 1/32) be relaxed to match practical observations?

## Limitations
- The κ² factor in the sample complexity requirement may be significant for extremely ill-conditioned matrices.
- The analysis is confined to Gaussian measurements; extension to other ensembles requires new RIP arguments and may not preserve the κ² factor.
- The theory does not cover random initialization or overparameterization (k > r).
- The step size constraint μ ≤ 1/32 is stricter than necessary in practice, suggesting room for sharper analysis.

## Confidence
- **Optimal sample complexity (Θ((n₁+n₂)r) up to κ²):** High. Proof under Gaussian RIP and virtual sequence technique is sound.
- **Condition-number-free iteration complexity (O(log(1/ε))):** High. Preconditioning neutralizes curvature; local convergence lemma is established.
- **Spectral initialization within O(σ_min(X*)) distance:** Medium. Bound follows from RIP but tightness for very large κ or fewer samples is uncertain.
- **Generalization beyond Gaussian measurements or to random initialization:** Low. Paper does not address these.

## Next Checks
1. **Test alternative measurement models** (e.g., subsampled discrete Fourier transform, Rademacher) to verify if the same sample complexity and κ-free iteration rate hold, and to quantify any degradation in the required RIP constant.
2. **Empirically calibrate the κ² factor** in the sample complexity by running ScaledGD with varying κ and m, checking if the phase transition scales as κ² or if a linear dependence suffices under different measurement ensembles.
3. **Explore random initialization** (e.g., small random factors) followed by a modest number of ScaledGD steps to assess whether the local convergence region can be enlarged without sacrificing linear convergence, potentially reducing reliance on spectral initialization.
