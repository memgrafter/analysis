# Building AI Agents to Improve Job Referral Requests to Strangers
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.10726
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10726
- **Reference count**: 13
- **Primary result**: AI agents using LLM revisions and RAG improve predicted referral success for weak requests by 14% without harming strong ones.

## Executive Summary
Job seekers often write ineffective referral requests to strangers on professional platforms, with 54% failing to attract any referrals. This paper introduces an AI agent system to automatically improve such requests. The system includes an LLM-based improver agent that rewrites the request, and an evaluator agent that predicts the likelihood of a successful referral. To enhance quality, the system employs Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): a retriever supplies relevant high-quality examples, and an explainer provides sentence-level feedback. The key finding is that LLM revisions alone boost weaker requests but degrade stronger ones; however, with RAG the degradation is avoided and gains for weak requests are amplified, increasing their predicted success from 39.2% to 44.7% (14% relative improvement). Model-predicted improvements offer a low-cost signal for validating features before costly real-world deployment.

## Method Summary
The core method consists of two cooperating agents. The improver agent uses a large language model (LLM) to rewrite a referral request, optionally conditioned on retrieved examples from a corpus of high-quality requests (RAG). The evaluator agent, a fine-tuned sentence transformer (AUROC 0.681), predicts the probability that a given request will yield a referral. An explainer component extends the evaluator to output sentence-level ratings, guiding the improver toward specific improvements. The workflow begins with the user’s initial request, the retriever fetches similar high-quality examples, and the improver generates a revised draft. The revised request is evaluated and its sentences highlighted for issues; the cycle can repeat until a satisfactory score is achieved. This setup provides an automated, data-driven approach to enhance request quality while avoiding the need for human labeling.

## Key Results
- LLM-only revisions improve the predicted success of weaker initial requests by +3.4 percentage points but harm stronger ones by -3.4 points.
- Adding RAG prevents degradation for strong requests and amplifies gains for weak ones, raising their average predicted success from 39.2% to 44.7% (14% relative improvement).
- Model-predicted improvements serve as a low-cost validation signal to prioritize feature development before field testing.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system leverages the language generation capability of LLMs to rephrase requests in a more effective style. The evaluator model provides an objective quality signal trained on historical success data. RAG enriches the rewriting process by supplying concrete, contextually relevant examples, preventing the LLM from drifting toward generic or mismatched language. The explainer’s sentence-level feedback pinpoints exact weaknesses, enabling targeted edits. Together, these components form a closed loop where the improver iteratively refines the request until the evaluator’s score improves. The mechanism works because it combines the generative power of LLMs with data-driven assessment and retrieval of proven templates, steering the output toward patterns historically associated with higher referral rates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**: Why needed: To ground the LLM’s rewriting in real, high-quality examples that are semantically similar to the input, avoiding hallucination and ensuring stylistic relevance. Quick check: Retrieve the top-5 similar requests from the corpus and verify that they share key attributes (e.g., industry, connection type).
- **Sentence Transformers**: Why needed: To produce dense embeddings of referral requests for similarity search and as features for the evaluator model. Quick check: Encode a set of requests and compute pairwise cosine similarities; confirm that high similarities correspond to human-judged topical similarity.
- **Evaluator Model (Success Predictor)**: Why needed: To provide an automated, scalable proxy for the likelihood of a referral, guiding the improvement process without manual labeling. Quick check: Run the model on a sample of known successful/unsuccessful requests and check if the predicted scores separate the two groups.
- **LLM-based Improver Agent**: Why needed: To leverage the expressive power of large language models to generate fluent, context-appropriate revisions. Quick check: Feed the original request and retrieved examples into the LLM with a prompt; inspect that the output maintains the core intent while adopting stylistic improvements.
- **Sentence-Level Explanation**: Why needed: To deliver fine-grained feedback that highlights problematic sentences, allowing for targeted refinements. Quick check: Input a request into the explainer and verify that low-rated sentences align with common pitfalls (e.g., vagueness, overbearing tone).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
User -> Improver Agent -> Evaluator Agent -> Explainer -> (optional loop back to Improver).  
Retriever supplies context to Improver.  
Corpus of high-quality examples feeds Retriever.

**Critical path:**  
1. User submits initial referral request.  
2. Retriever fetches relevant high-quality examples from the corpus.  
3. Improver agent rewrites the request using the original and examples (RAG).  
4. Evaluator agent predicts success probability.  
5. Explainer produces sentence-level ratings.  
6. If score is unsatisfactory or feedback indicates issues, the system may iterate (back to step 3 with revised request); otherwise returns final output.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Retrieval corpus diversity vs. relevance: A larger, diverse corpus improves coverage but may return less relevant examples; a focused corpus yields precise examples but risks overfitting to a narrow style.  
- Evaluator complexity vs. latency: A more accurate model (e.g., larger transformer) improves prediction quality but adds computational cost.  
- LLM rewriting temperature: Higher randomness increases creativity but may deviate from proven patterns; low temperature ensures consistency but may produce generic text.  
- Iteration depth: More loops can refine the request but increase response time and risk of over-editing.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Without RAG, the improver may degrade strong requests, indicating a tendency to over-generalize.  
- Low AUROC of the evaluator suggests wide prediction errors; monitor frequent large residuals on unseen data.  
- Retriever returns examples that are topically unrelated (e.g., different industry), signaling embedding mismatch or corpus bias.  
- Explainer highlights sentences arbitrarily, implying poor model calibration or misalignment with human judgment.  
- System exhibits high latency (>10 seconds) for a single request, pointing to inefficient retrieval or LLM inference.

**First experiments:**  
1. **End-to-end integration test**: Run a batch of 50 mixed initial requests through the full pipeline, confirming successful execution and basic output quality (no crashes, non-empty responses, evaluator scores within expected range).  
2. **RAG benefit quantification**: For a balanced sample of weak and strong initial requests (by evaluator score), compare the predicted success after LLM-only rewriting vs. RAG-enabled rewriting. Expect improved scores for weak requests and no decline for strong ones.  
3. **Explaination utility**: Recruit a small set of users (e.g., 10 job seekers) to revise their original requests using the sentence-level feedback. Measure whether the final evaluator scores improve compared to a control group who revise without feedback, and collect subjective ratings on feedback usefulness.

## Limitations
- The evaluator model’s moderate AUROC (0.681) may lead to noisy predictions of referral success.
- Findings are based on model-predicted outcomes; real-world response rates remain unmeasured.
- The retrieval corpus may lack diversity, potentially biasing revisions toward certain styles or contexts.

## Confidence
- LLM revisions improve weak but harm strong requests: **Medium**
- RAG prevents harm to strong requests and amplifies gains for weak ones: **Medium**
- Model-predicted improvements as a low-cost validation signal: **Low**

## Next Checks
- Conduct a field experiment on LinkedIn (or a similar platform) measuring actual response rates to AI-rewritten vs. original referral requests.

- Perform an ablation study varying the retrieval corpus size and diversity to assess sensitivity of the RAG component to corpus coverage.
