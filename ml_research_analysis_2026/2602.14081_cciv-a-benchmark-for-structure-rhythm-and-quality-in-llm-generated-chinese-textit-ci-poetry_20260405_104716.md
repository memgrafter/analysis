# CCiV: A Benchmark for Structure, Rhythm and Quality in LLM-Generated Chinese \textit{Ci} Poetry

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14081
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14081
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** LLMs often generate historically valid variants of Ci forms; form‑aware prompting helps strong models but harms weak ones; formal correctness correlates weakly with literary quality.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces CCiV, a benchmark for evaluating LLM‑generated Chinese Ci poetry across structure, rhythm (tonal patterns), and literary quality. Covering 30 Cipai forms and accounting for historical variants, the study tests 17 LLMs under direct and form‑aware prompting. Key findings include that models frequently produce valid but non‑standard historical variants, boosting variant‑aware structural accuracy by up to 18.66 points. Tonal accuracy lags structural accuracy by 3–6 points across all models. Form‑aware prompting substantially improves structural compliance for capable models (e.g., Qwen‑max +39.67) but degrades performance for smaller ones (e.g., Qwen2.5‑7b −16.67). LLM‑as‑a‑judge assessments reveal a weak correlation between formal metrics and perceived informativeness or aesthetics, with Deepseek‑R1 achieving the highest quality scores despite moderate formal accuracy. Overall quality remains low (<3.5/5), highlighting the challenge of balancing formal constraints with creative expression.

## Method Summary
The CCiV dataset comprises 30 traditional Cipai forms, each with 10 poem titles (300 prompts). For each Cipai, the authors collected both standard and historically documented variant templates, specifying line counts, character counts per line, and tonal patterns (level/oblique). The study compares two prompting strategies: direct prompt (form name + title) and form‑aware prompt (which appends the full template specification). Seventeen LLMs (7B–70B parameters and closed‑source APIs) generated three poems per prompt with temperature 0.7 and top‑p 0.95. Evaluation was automatic: structural accuracy matched any valid variant’s pattern; tonal accuracy computed the fraction of characters whose tone (via pypinyin) aligned with the required pattern, maximized over variants. Literary quality was judged by three top‑tier LLMs (Kimi‑K2, Doubao‑seed‑1.6, Gemini‑2.5‑pro) using standardized prompts rating informativeness and aesthetics on a 1–5 scale for four representative models. Results were aggregated to produce structural, tonal, and quality metrics.

## Key Results
- Variant‑aware structural accuracy exceeds standard accuracy for all models; Qwen2.5‑72b improves by 18.66 points.
- Tonal accuracy consistently trails structural accuracy by 3–6 points across models.
- Form‑aware prompting yields large gains for strong models (e.g., Qwen‑max structural +39.67) but performance drops for weaker ones (e.g., Qwen2.5‑7b −16.67).
- LLM‑judge scores show weak correlation with formal metrics; Deepseek‑R1 leads in informativeness (3.28) and aesthetic (3.37) despite moderate formal accuracy.
- Overall literary quality remains below 3.5 out of 5 for all models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: LLMs generate historical variants
- **Claim:** LLMs frequently generate historically valid variants of a Cipai instead of the standard form when given a direct prompt.
- **Mechanism:** Pre‑training data contains multiple documented historical variants for many Cipai. Without explicit guidance, the model samples from the learned distribution of these forms rather than a single canonical template.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s internal representations encode several structurally distinct patterns for the same Cipai name.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: “models frequently generate valid but unexpected historical variants of a poetic form”.
  - Section 3.1: “many apparent errors are actually correct generations of alternative forms”.
  - Table 2: variant‑aware structural accuracy exceeds standard accuracy for all models (e.g., Qwen2.5‑72b +18.66).

### Mechanism 2: Form‑aware prompting has differential effects
- **Claim:** Form‑aware prompting improves structural and tonal accuracy for strong models but degrades performance for weaker ones.
- **Mechanism:** Capable models can integrate detailed template constraints without overload, using them to guide generation precisely. Smaller models may be overwhelmed by the added complexity, leading to confusion or failure to maintain content quality.
- **Core assumption:** There is a capability threshold; above it, explicit guidance acts as a useful scaffold, below it becomes a source of interference.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: “form‑aware prompting improves strong models (e.g., Qwen‑max structural +39.67 points) but degrades weaker ones (e.g., Qwen2.5‑7b −16.67)”.
  - Section 3.3: “For models with strong instruction‑following, the improvements are large… For smaller models, form‑aware prompting hurts performance.”
  - Table 2: large gains for Qwen‑max and GLM‑4‑plus, but losses for Qwen2.5‑7b and Qwen2.5‑3b.

### Mechanism 3: Formal accuracy does not predict literary quality
- **Claim:** Higher formal accuracy (structural/tonal) does not reliably predict higher literary quality (informativeness, aesthetic) in LLM‑generated Ci.
- **Mechanism:** Formal metrics measure only compliance with rigid templates, while literary quality depends on imagery, emotional resonance, and originality, which can be constrained by strict adherence or achieved through creative deviation.
- **Core assumption:** Quality in poetry is multidimensional and not fully captured by rule‑based metrics.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: “LLM‑as‑a‑judge scores show weak correlation between formal correctness and literary quality; Deepseek‑R1 ranks highest in informativeness (3.28) and aesthetic (3.37) despite moderate formal metrics.”
  - Section 3.4: “formal accuracy does not reliably predict artistic quality in our sample.”
  - Table 3: Deepseek‑R1 leads in quality while ranking third in both structural and tonal accuracy.

## Foundational Learning

### Variant‑aware evaluation
- **Why needed:** To avoid underestimating model capability when they produce historically valid but non‑standard forms; ensures fair assessment across the diversity of Ci templates.
- **Quick check:** What is the numeric improvement in structural accuracy when evaluating against all variants for Qwen2.5‑72b, and why does this occur?

### Tonal patterns in Chinese Ci
- **Why needed:** Tonal adherence is a core rhythmic requirement; the paper shows it is harder for LLMs than structural compliance. Understanding tonal categories (level/oblique) is essential to interpret results.
- **Quick check:** Why might character‑level tonal constraints be more difficult for LLMs than sentence‑level structural patterns?

### Form‑aware prompting
- **Why needed:** It is the key experimental manipulation testing whether explicit constraints improve control; its differential effects reveal model capabilities.
- **Quick check:** Under what model capability conditions does form‑aware prompting help versus degrade performance?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Data collector -> Prompt generator -> Inference runner -> Metrics engine -> Quality judge

**Critical path:**  
1. Assemble CCiV: select 30 Cipai, extract standard/variant patterns, sample 10 titles per Cipai (300 prompts).  
2. For each model, generate poems under both direct and form‑aware prompt conditions.  
3. Automatically evaluate structural and tonal accuracy.  
4. Select four representative models for LLM‑as‑a‑judge quality evaluation.  
5. Aggregate and analyze results, including variant‑aware improvements and prompting effects.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Variant‑aware vs. standard evaluation: captures true capability but may inflate scores; standard is stricter but penalizes historically valid outputs.  
- Judge selection: using multiple top‑tier judges improves reliability but increases cost; a single judge is faster but less robust.  
- Tonal labeling: automated pypinyin mapping is scalable but suffers from polyphonic ambiguities; manual correction is accurate but not feasible at scale.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Sudden drop in structural accuracy with form‑aware prompting indicates the model cannot handle explicit constraints.  
- Tonal accuracy consistently 3–6 points below structural accuracy across models signals a fundamental difficulty with phonetic control.  
- High formal scores paired with low quality scores suggest over‑fitting to templates at the expense of creativity.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate the variant generation phenomenon: run direct prompts on a short (e.g., “望江南”) and a long (e.g., “金缕曲”) Cipai and compare standard vs. variant‑aware scores.  
2. Identify the capability threshold: test at least three model sizes (e.g., 7B, 14B, 70B) with both prompt types, measure Δstructural accuracy.  
3. Validate the quality‑formal disconnect: compute Spearman correlation between structural/tonal accuracy and LLM‑judged aesthetic scores across models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do LLMs frequently generate historically valid variants instead of the requested standard Cipai form under direct prompting? The paper documents the phenomenon but does not explain its underlying cause.  
2. What specific linguistic or architectural factors cause the persistent performance gap between structural and tonal accuracy across all LLMs? The gap is empirically established but not dissected.  
3. Why does form‑aware prompting significantly improve formal adherence in strong models but degrade it in weaker ones? The paper observes the dichotomy but does not test specific hypotheses.  
4. What explains the weak and inconsistent alignment between formal correctness and human‑judged literary quality in LLM‑generated Ci poetry? The misalignment is documented but its source is unclear.

## Limitations
- The origin of the variant‑generation behavior remains speculative without training‑data analysis or probing.
- The reported capability threshold for prompting effects is based on limited model families and may not generalize.
- LLM‑as‑a‑judge quality scores lack human validation, raising concerns about reliability of the observed weak correlation.
- Access to all original models and exact judge LLMs may be restricted; tonal mapping ambiguity introduces noise.

## Next Checks
1. Probe variant internalization: Fine‑tune a small model on only standard‑form Ci data, then retest variant generation under direct prompts.
2. Cross‑family prompting threshold: Test form‑aware prompting on at least three additional model families (e.g., Llama, Mistral, Yi) across 7B–70B scales, re‑evaluate structural accuracy breakpoint.
3. Human validation of quality rankings: Commission human experts to rate poems from top/bottom formal‑scoring models for informativeness and aesthetics, compute correlation with LLM‑judge scores.
