# Geometry-Preserving Aggregation for Mixture-of-Experts Embedding Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14039  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14039  
- Reference count: 25  
- Primary result: Spherical Barycentric Aggregation (SBA) consistently improves MTEB task performance (e.g., SprintDuplicateQuestions: 83.66→89.06) by preventing inward norm collapse from linear aggregation, with no extra computational cost.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a geometric collapse issue in Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) embedding models caused by standard linear aggregation of expert outputs. The authors demonstrate that expert outputs reside on a hyperspherical manifold with consistent norms and large angular separation, and linear summation distorts both magnitude and direction. To preserve this structure, they propose Spherical Barycentric Aggregation (SBA), which separately aggregates radial and angular components. Evaluated on MTEB tasks using the nomic-embed-text-v2-moe model, SBA yields consistent improvements (e.g., SprintDuplicateQuestions AP from 83.66 to 89.06) while maintaining geometric integrity without additional computational overhead.

## Method Summary
The study focuses on top-K MoE embedding models where experts produce outputs on a shared hypersphere due to contrastive training and normalization. The baseline linear aggregation (weighted sum) causes inward collapse because vector addition reduces norm when directions are non-aligned. SBA addresses this by decomposing each expert output into norm and unit direction, aggregating norms via weighted average, and computing a norm-aware weighted barycenter for directions before recombination. The method is applied as a drop-in replacement for linear aggregation in all MoE layers, requiring no retraining of experts. In experiments, the authors fine-tune a pretrained nomic-embed-text-v2-moe model (top-2 routing) on 30K NLI triplets using MultipleNegativesRankingLoss, comparing linear aggregation against SBA and ablations (norm-free angle, unit-normalized). Evaluation on selected MTEB tasks measures downstream performance and geometric consistency via relative output norm ratio.

## Key Results
- SBA improves performance across multiple MTEB tasks; e.g., SprintDuplicateQuestions AP increases from 83.66 (linear) to 89.06 (SBA).
- Linear aggregation induces inward norm collapse, resulting in output norms significantly below average expert norm; SBA preserves radial consistency (norm ratio ≈1).
- Ablations show that decoupling radial and angular components is beneficial overall, though a norm-free angle variant sometimes excels on duplicate detection tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Hyperspherical Manifold of Expert Outputs**
- **Claim:** MoE embedding expert outputs occupy a shared hyperspherical manifold characterized by tightly clustered norms and substantial pairwise angular separation.
- **Mechanism:** Training objectives like contrastive loss with cosine similarity and normalization practices encourage representations to reside on a sphere. This leads to radial consistency (similar norms across experts) and directional specialization (large angles between expert outputs).
- **Core assumption:** Semantic information is primarily encoded in angular relationships rather than magnitude, aligning with cosine-similarity-based downstream tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper's abstract and Section III (Figures 1, 2) empirically validate norm concentration and angular separation in the nomic-embed-text-v2-moe model across layers, tokens, and MMLU tasks.
- **Break condition:** If expert norms exhibit high variance or angular separations cluster near 0°, the hyperspherical manifold assumption fails, negating the need for SBA.

**Mechanism 2: Inward Collapse from Linear Aggregation**
- **Claim:** Standard linear aggregation (weighted sum) induces inward collapse when applied to hyperspherically distributed expert outputs, distorting both magnitude and direction.
- **Mechanism:** Given expert norms *r* ≈ constant and non-aligned directions (angular separation >40°), the vector sum's norm ||**y**<sub>linear</sub>|| = *r* · ||Σ*w*<sub>i</sub> **ê**<sub>i</sub>|| is strictly less than *r* due to trigonometric cancellation. This shifts the aggregated embedding toward the manifold's interior.
- **Core assumption:** Expert directions are not perfectly collinear (subspace orthogonality or large angles).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section IV.B (Equation 3, Figures 3, 5) illustrate the geometric failure and empirical norm reduction.
- **Break condition:** If all expert directions are perfectly collinear (θ ≈0°), linear aggregation would not cause inward collapse, making SBA unnecessary.

**Mechanism 3: Spherical Barycentric Aggregation Preserves Geometry**
- **Claim:** SBA preserves hyperspherical structure by separately aggregating radial (weighted average of norms) and angular (norm-aware weighted barycenter) components.
- **Mechanism:** SBA decomposes each expert output *e*<sub>i</sub> into norm *r*<sub>i</sub> and unit direction **ê**<sub>i</sub>. The aggregated radius *r* = Σ*w*<sub>i</sub>*r*<sub>i</sub> and direction **ê**(θ) with θ = Σ(*w*<sub>i</sub>*r*<sub>i</sub>θ<sub>i</sub>)/Σ(*w*<sub>i</sub>*r*<sub>i</sub>) are recombined as *y* = *r* · **ê**(θ). This avoids trigonometric cancellation by not summing raw vectors.
- **Core assumption:** The hyperspherical manifold structure is intrinsic and must be maintained for cosine-based similarity tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Sections IV.C–E (Equations 4–11, Figure 4, Table II) define SBA and confirm the contribution of radial-angular coupling.
- **Break condition:** If expert norms are not positively correlated with semantic importance, weighting angles by *r*<sub>i</sub> could degrade performance; indeed, the norm-free angle ablation sometimes outperforms full SBA on specific tasks.

## Foundational Learning

**Hyperspherical geometry in embedding spaces**
- *Why needed:* Downstream tasks like STS and clustering rely on cosine similarity, which is invariant to norm but sensitive to angular distortion. Inward collapse from linear aggregation changes both norm and direction, harming comparability.
- *Quick check:* If two vectors have identical direction but different norms, their cosine similarity is 1. Does preserving direction alone suffice for all embedding tasks? (No—Table II shows unit-normalized SBA degrades clustering, implying norm carries signal.)

**Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing and aggregation**
- *Why needed:* Understanding MoE sparsely activates experts (top-K) and combines outputs via router weights is essential to see where linear aggregation occurs and how SBA replaces it without altering routing.
- *Quick check:* In top-K routing with K=2 and router weights [0.6, 0.4], how does linear aggregation differ from SBA in terms of output norm? (Linear: ||**y**|| ≤ 0.6*r1 + 0.4*r2 with equality only if directions align. SBA: ||**y**|| = 0.6*r1 + 0.4*r2 exactly.)

**Norm and angular decomposition of vectors**
- *Why needed:* SBA's core operation decomposes each expert output into magnitude and direction, aggregates each separately, then recombines, requiring comfort with spherical coordinates in high dimensions.
- *Quick check:* Given two 2D vectors **v**1=(1,0) and **v**2=(0,1) with equal norms, what is their linear sum's norm vs. SBA's output norm with equal weights? (Linear: ||**v**1+**v**2||=√2≈1.414; SBA: radius=1, direction=45°, norm=1. Linear collapses inward.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Experts -> Router -> Aggregation Operator (Linear or SBA)

**Critical path:**  
1. Extract expert outputs **e**<sub>i</sub> from MoE layer.  
2. Compute norms *r*<sub>i</sub> = ||**e**<sub>i</sub>|| and unit directions **ê**<sub>i</sub> = **e**<sub>i</sub>/*r*<sub>i</sub>.  
3. Using router weights *w*<sub>i</sub>, compute aggregated radius *r* = Σ*w*<sub>i</sub>*r*<sub>i</sub>.  
4. Compute weighted angular barycenter: θ = arg(Σ*w*<sub>i</sub>*r*<sub>i</sub>**ê**<sub>i</sub>) (or equivalent in chosen coordinate system).  
5. Reconstruct **y** = *r* · **ê**(θ).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- SBA adds negligible compute (norms, normalization, weighted direction sum) but preserves geometry; linear is simpler but causes collapse.  
- Full SBA vs. norm-free angle: Incorporating *r*<sub>i</sub> into angular aggregation generally helps, but task-specific tuning may be needed (e.g., duplicate detection favors pure angular).  
- Unit-normalized variant forces ||**y**||=1, losing radial information; harms clustering but may simplify certain similarity tasks.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Geometric: Post-aggregation norms ||**y**|| significantly below average expert norm (measure ||**y**||/̄*r*); indicates linear collapse.  
- Performance: Drop in clustering (V-measure) or semantic similarity (Spearman) compared to SBA; unit-normalized variant may show catastrophic failure on clustering (e.g., StackExchange: 68.35 vs. 85.66).  
- Numerical: Directional averaging without norm-weighting may produce unstable θ if *r*<sub>i</sub> vary widely (rare given hyperspherical consistency).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Geometry validation:** Compute histogram of relative norm ratios *r*<sub>1</sub>/*r*<sub>2</sub> and pairwise angular distances across MoE layers on a held-out corpus (e.g., MMLU). Expect tight norm concentration (mean ≈1) and mean angle >40° if hyperspherical.  
2. **Aggregation comparison:** Replace linear aggregation with SBA in a pretrained MoE embedding model (e.g., nomic-embed-text-v2-moe), freeze experts, fine-tune on NLI data, evaluate on MTEB tasks (STSBenchmark, SprintDuplicate). Expect consistent gains.  
3. **Ablation study:** Implement norm-free angle and unit-normalized variants. Compare full SBA against these on diverse MTEB tasks to isolate contribution of radial-angular coupling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does SBA's performance gain generalize to other MoE embedding architectures? The study tested only nomic-embed-text-v2-moe; other designs may have different geometry or routing.  
2. Under what conditions does the hyperspherical geometry assumption for expert outputs hold? Only one model with specific normalization and contrastive loss was analyzed; the boundary of the assumption is unknown.  
3. How does SBA scale with the number of activated experts K>2? Only top-2 evaluated; larger K may cause increased angular diversity or norm variance affecting aggregation.

## Limitations
- The hyperspherical manifold claim is based on a single MoE model; its prevalence across other architectures or routing schemes remains unproven.
- Reproducibility is hindered by missing details: exact MoE submodule injection point, triplet negative-sampling strategy, and evaluation subset definitions.
- The norm-free angle ablation sometimes surpasses full SBA on SprintDuplicate, indicating the radial-angular coupling may not be universally optimal and could be task-dependent.

## Confidence
- Hyperspherical geometry (Mechanism 1): Medium (evidence persuasive but lack cross-model validation).  
- Inward collapse (Mechanism 2): High (mathematically sound, empirical Figure 5 shows norm reduction).  
- SBA effectiveness (Mechanism 3): Medium-High (consistent MTEB improvements, but limited task diversity and mixed ablation results).

## Next Checks
1. Cross-model geometry verification: Compute norm concentration and angular separation for multiple MoE models (e.g., Switch Transformer, Google’s GMoE) to test generality.  
2. Broad task evaluation: Apply SBA to diverse downstream tasks beyond MTEB (clustering, classification, retrieval) and to non-embedding MoE tasks (e.g., language modeling) to assess generality.  
3. Radial-angular coupling ablation: Systematically vary weighting of norms in angular aggregation (e.g., raw, squared, learned scalars) and analyze per-task performance to determine when decoupling is beneficial.
