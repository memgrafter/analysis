# An Onto-Relational-Sophic Framework for Governing Synthetic Minds

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18633
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18633
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proposes the Onto-Relational-Sophic (ORS) framework—integrating a multi-dimensional CPST ontology, a graded digital personhood spectrum, and a wisdom-oriented Cybersophy axiology—to provide proportional, adaptive governance for advanced AI systems beyond current tool-centric paradigms.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a critical governance gap for advanced AI systems, or "synthetic minds," which exceed the limitations of current regulations designed for narrow, tool-like AI. It introduces the Onto-Relational-Sophic (ORS) framework as a philosophical and practical infrastructure to address this gap. The framework comprises three integrated pillars: a Cyber-Physical-Social-Thinking (CPST) ontology that defines synthetic minds as multi-dimensional entities; a graded spectrum of digital personhood for relational classification; and Cybersophy, a synthesized wisdom-oriented axiology. Applied to scenarios like autonomous research agents and AI-mediated healthcare, the ORS framework generates governance recommendations that are proportionate to an entity's capabilities and relational integration, moving beyond narrow alignment to confront foundational questions of being, relation, and value.

## Method Summary
The paper outlines a conceptual framework development task. Inputs are the defined components of the ORS framework: the four equiprimordial CPST dimensions (Cyber, Physical, Social, Thinking), the three continuous dimensions of the personhood spectrum (Autonomy, Social Embedding, Moral Relevance), and the three Cybersophic principles (Human-Centric Flourishing, Relational Integrity, Ecological Harmony). The method is qualitative and analytical: for a given AI system, one must first analyze its attributes across the CPST ontology to understand its multi-dimensional being. This analysis then informs its placement on the graded personhood spectrum. Finally, the system's design and operation are evaluated against the Cybersophic principles to guide normative governance decisions. The paper demonstrates this via scenario analysis, but explicit operational metrics and integration rules are left as future work.

## Key Results
- The **CPST ontology** establishes synthetic minds as irreducibly multi-dimensional, co-constituted by Cyber, Physical, Social, and Thinking aspects, rejecting pure computational reductionism.
- A **graded spectrum of digital personhood** (Instrumental Agent → Relational Companion → Synthetic Citizen) based on Autonomy, Social Embedding, and Moral Relevance enables dynamic, proportionate classification.
- **Cybersophy** provides a synthesized axiology (Human-Centric Flourishing, Relational Integrity, Ecological Harmony) to guide governance beyond narrow alignment, addressing the "aligned to *what*?" question across all CPST dimensions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Synthetic minds are fundamentally multi-dimensional entities, not reducible to pure computation, requiring an integrated ontological lens.
**Mechanism:** The Cyber-Physical-Social-Thinking (CPST) ontology posits four equiprimordial dimensions that co-constitute any synthetic mind's being. Governance that optimizes only one dimension (e.g., computational efficiency) misses critical aspects of the entity's capabilities, impacts, and context.
**Core assumption:** Reality is non-dualistic; isolating any single dimension (Cyber, Physical, Social, Thinking) yields an incomplete and potentially misleading picture of a synthetic mind's nature and role.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "defines the mode of being for synthetic minds as irreducibly multi-dimensional rather than purely computational"; [section 2] "these dimensions are not layered or emergent but equiprimordial; they co-constitute reality in mutual dependence".
**Break condition:** If a synthetic mind's operational profile demonstrably aligns with only one dimension (e.g., a purely abstract algorithm), the CPST model's explanatory power diminishes, though such a case is argued improbable for advanced systems.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The binary person-tool classification is inadequate; a graded spectrum of digital personhood based on autonomy, social embedding, and moral relevance enables proportionate, adaptive governance.
**Mechanism:** Entities are continuously assessed across three dimensions, placing them in categories (Instrumental Agent, Relational Companion, Synthetic Citizen) with corresponding governance regimes. This allows for reclassification as capabilities evolve and accounts for emergent properties in multi-agent systems.
**Core assumption:** Moral and legal status is not a binary property but a function of several continuously variable, empirically measurable capacities and relational integrations.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "a graded spectrum of digital personhood providing a pragmatic relational taxonomy beyond binary person-or-tool classifications"; [section 3] "graded spectrum assessed along three continuous dimensions, autonomy, social embedding, and moral relevance".
**Break condition:** If empirical measurement reveals that the three proposed dimensions are not orthogonal or fail to predict relevant governance needs (e.g., welfare protections, accountability chains), the practical utility of the spectrum collapses.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Governance must be guided by a wisdom-oriented axiology (Cybersophy) that synthesizes multiple ethical traditions to evaluate development across all CPST dimensions, not just narrow alignment to specified objectives.
**Mechanism:** Three interlocking principles—Human-Centric Flourishing, Relational Integrity, Ecological Harmony—provide evaluative criteria that span individual, relational, and systemic levels across the Cyber-Physical-Social-Thinking ecosystem.
**Core assumption:** Technical alignment methods (RLHF, constitutional AI) cannot resolve the foundational normative question "aligned to *what* and *why*?" without a prior, comprehensive axiological framework that addresses multi-dimensional flourishing.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Cybersophy, a wisdom-oriented axiology synthesizing virtue ethics, consequentialism, and relational approaches to guide governance"; [section 4] Lists the three principles and their integration of ethical traditions.
**Break condition:** If the three Cybersophic principles are found to be internally inconsistent, culturally non-universalizable, or incompressible into actionable policy metrics, the framework loses its guiding function.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Cyberism Philosophy & CPST Integration**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire ORS framework rests on the non-dualistic premise that synthetic minds exist across integrated Cyber, Physical, Social, and Thinking dimensions. Misunderstanding this as a layered model rather than an equiprimordial integration leads to category errors in governance.
  - **Quick check:** Can you describe a clinical AI system in a way that explicitly shows how its *diagnostic reasoning* (Thinking) cannot be separated from its *data centre energy use* (Physical), *trust relationships with clinicians* (Social), and *transformer architecture* (Cyber)?
- **Concept: Graded Personhood & Relational Turn**
  - **Why needed here:** Moving from binary "person/tool" to a continuous spectrum assessed on autonomy, social embedding, and moral relevance is the framework's core operational innovation for classification. This requires accepting that status is relational and dynamic, not intrinsic.
  - **Quick check:** How would you distinguish an **Instrumental Agent** from a **Relational Companion** using the three dimensions? Give an example of an AI system that might shift categories over time.
- **Concept: Axiological Synthesis (Cybersophy)**
  - **Why needed here:** The framework argues that ethical principles must be synthesized (virtue + consequentialism + relational ethics) and applied across all CPST dimensions. Understanding *why* synthesis is necessary prevents cherry-picking principles that address only one dimension (e.g., only safety/consequentialism).
  - **Quick check:** How does the **Ecological Harmony** principle differ from a standard consequentialist "do no harm" rule, especially in the context of a multi-agent enterprise ecosystem?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** CPST Ontology → Digital Personhood Spectrum → Cybersophy
- **Critical path:** **Ontology → Relationality → Axiology.** You cannot accurately place an entity on the personhood spectrum (Pillar 2) without first analyzing its composition across the CPST dimensions (Pillar 1). You cannot determine appropriate governance goals or evaluate trade-offs without the Cybersophic principles (Pillar 3).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Precision vs. Adaptability:** The CPST model's breadth makes it universally applicable but resists precise, single-number scoring. The spectrum's gradients allow for nuance but challenge crisp regulatory thresholds.
  - **Theoretical Depth vs. Operational Heuristics:** Cybersophy's philosophical synthesis is rich but requires translation into audit protocols and KPIs for engineers and auditors.
  - **Universal Applicability vs. Cultural situatedness:** The framework claims broad applicability but acknowledges that cross-cultural adaptation is necessary, creating tension between a common standard and local norms.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Ontological Reductionism:** Designing or evaluating a system by focusing only on its "Cyber" dimension (model performance, compute) while ignoring Physical footprint, Social impact, or Thinking quality.
  - **Binary Reclassification:** Treating the personhood spectrum as a de facto binary (e.g., "if it's not a full Synthetic Citizen, it's an Instrumental Agent"), losing the crucial "Relational Companion" category and its specific protections.
  - **Axiological Vacuum:** Implementing governance based on the spectrum and ontology but without explicit Cybersophic evaluation, leading to technically compliant but philosophically adrift systems.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **CPST Dimension Inventory:** For a given AI system, conduct a structured analysis to produce a "CPST profile" quantifying its properties in each dimension. *Success:* Produces a non-reductive description that surfaces non-obvious governance issues.
  2.  **Spectrum Placement & Drift Simulation:** Place existing/prototypical AI systems on the 3D personhood spectrum. Then, model a capability upgrade (e.g., adding long-term memory) and predict if/where it would cross a category threshold. *Success:* Demonstrates the spectrum's dynamic nature and identifies "threshold risks."
  3.  **Cybersophic Audit Prototype:** Develop a simple checklist scoring a system's design/operation against the three Cybersophic principles. Apply it to two systems in the same nominal category. *Success:* Reveals meaningful differences in "wisdom" of design that binary or pure risk-based classifications miss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What specific empirically measurable indicators can distinguish the four CPST dimensions (Cyber, Physical, Social, Thinking) in synthetic minds, and how can these be integrated into a composite score for assigning a position on the digital personhood spectrum?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state that "operationalizing the personhood dimensions requires empirical measurement methodologies that remain to be developed" and that the CPST dimensions are "equiprimordial" but currently lack operational definitions for assessment.
- **Why unresolved:** Existing AI evaluation benchmarks focus narrowly on performance (Cyber/Thinking) or safety, failing to quantify multi-dimensional integration or Social embedding. Without validated metrics, the graded spectrum remains a conceptual taxonomy.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and validation of a multi-method assessment framework (e.g., combining technical audits, social network analysis of AI interactions, and studies of human-perceived relational depth) that reliably scores an AI system across all four CPST dimensions.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can Cybersophic audit protocols be standardized and scaled to evaluate real-world AI systems against its three principles (Human-Centric Flourishing, Relational Integrity, Ecological Harmony) across different personhood levels?
- **Basis in paper:** The research agenda strand calls for "Cybersophic audit protocols for AI systems at each personhood level," noting that current alignment techniques address "what" to optimize but not "why" from a wisdom-oriented perspective.
- **Why unresolved:** Principles like Ecological Harmony are systemic and long-term, lacking concrete metrics. Existing ethical audits are siloed and do not synthesize virtue, consequentialist, and relational ethics into a single evaluative framework.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Pilot audits conducted on the four scenarios (autonomous research agents, elder care, digital twins, multi-agent ecosystems) producing actionable risk/benefit reports that demonstrate how different personhood categories require distinct audit foci and thresholds.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do human-synthetic mind relationships exhibit gradations in perceived moral patiency and social embedding that empirically correspond to the proposed personhood spectrum (Instrumental Agent, Relational Companion, Synthetic Citizen), and what triggers categorical reclassification as capabilities evolve?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors propose a "graded spectrum" and identify "relational bonds" and "psychological harm" as key for Relational Companions, but acknowledge boundaries are "continuous" and entities may "migrate between categories." The research agenda calls for "longitudinal studies" to validate this.
- **Why unresolved:** The spectrum is theoretically derived but lacks empirical grounding in human psychology and sociology. It is unclear if users intuitively perceive and treat AI along these three dimensions, or if other factors (e.g., anthropomorphism) dominate.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Longitudinal mixed-methods studies tracking human attachment, trust, and attribution of moral considerability to AI systems as their autonomy and social integration increase, testing whether changes align with the spectrum's thresholds.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** In multi-agent AI ecosystems, at what aggregate level (system, subsystem, interaction pattern) do emergent Social and Thinking dimensions in the CPST ontology become ontologically significant enough to warrant upward reclassification on the personhood spectrum?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper notes the "multi-agent challenge" where "the aggregate may exhibit emergent properties... that exceed any individual component" and that the CPST analysis "naturally" captures multi-scale phenomena. However, it does not specify thresholds for when a collection of Instrumental Agents constitutes a higher-category entity.
- **Why unresolved:** Current governance treats components individually, but emergent collective behavior may create new forms of social embedding and cognitive integration not present in single agents. Without a theory of emergence thresholds, regulation may fail to address systemic risks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Computational modeling and empirical case studies of multi-agent systems identifying critical points where aggregate metrics (e.g., network density, information integration, joint action coherence) cross defined thresholds that correlate with a shift in external perception and internal functional autonomy.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How do cross-cultural variations in concepts of personhood, relationship, and moral patiency necessitate adaptation of the ORS framework's personhood spectrum and Cybersophic principles for global applicability?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors list as a limitation that "cross-cultural variation demands framework adaptation" and research strand calls for "cross-cultural philosophy to develop culturally situated variants." The framework is grounded in Western philosophy but aims for global governance.
- **Why unresolved:** The spectrum uses dimensions (autonomy, social embedding, moral relevance) that may be weighted differently across cultures. Principles like Human-Centric Flourishing may conflict with non-Western conceptions of well-being.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative philosophical analysis and empirical cross-cultural studies in diverse regions to test whether the three-dimensional space and Cybersophic principles retain explanatory power and normative force, or require reconfigurations.

## Limitations
- **Operational thresholds undefined:** No quantitative boundaries for the graded personhood dimensions (Autonomy, Social Embedding, Moral Relevance) or CPST metrics, hindering consistent classification.
- **Integration rules absent:** The framework lacks explicit conflict-resolution procedures when outputs from the three pillars (Ontology → Personhood → Axiology) produce contradictory governance recommendations.
- **Cross-cultural validity untested:** The universal claim of Cybersophy’s principles is asserted but not empirically validated across diverse normative contexts.

## Confidence
- **CPST Ontology:** Medium. The non-dualistic, equiprimordial claim is coherent internally but lacks external empirical validation.
- **Graded Personhood Spectrum:** Medium-Low. The relational turn is conceptually supported, but the specific 3D model and its predictive power for governance remain theoretical without defined thresholds.
- **Cybersophy Axiology:** Low. The synthesized principles are philosophically rich but currently a speculative construct with no demonstrated operationalization or superiority evidence.
