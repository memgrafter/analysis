# On the Non-Identifiability of Steering Vectors in Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06801
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06801
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Steering vectors in LLMs are non-identifiable due to large Jacobian null space (86–94%), leading to infinitely many behaviorally equivalent directions.

## Executive Summary
Activation steering aims to modify model behavior by adding a vector to internal activations, assuming a unique direction corresponds to a desired trait. This paper challenges that assumption, showing theoretically and empirically that steering vectors are non-identifiable: the Jacobian of the output with respect to activations has a large null space (86–94% of the activation dimension), meaning infinitely many vectors produce identical output changes. The authors prove that any vector in the null space can be added to a steering vector without affecting behavior (up to scaling). Empirically, across Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-3B on five traits, orthogonal perturbations to steering vectors yield negligible semantic shifts (mean Cohen’s d < 0.2), and vectors extracted via contrastive mean versus PCA are geometrically distinct (cosine similarity −0.54 to +0.32) yet produce equivalent outputs. Non-identifiability holds across steering strengths (α ≤ 3) and prompt distributions. The conclusion is that behavioral testing alone cannot pinpoint a unique steering vector; additional structural constraints are needed.

## Method Summary
The study combines theoretical analysis with empirical validation. Theoretically, under a local linear approximation, the output change is αJv, where J is the Jacobian and v the steering vector. If v’ is in the null space of J, then v+v’ yields the same output change, establishing non-identifiability. Empirically, the authors: (1) estimate the null-space dimension via SVD of activation covariances over many prompts; (2) test whether orthogonal perturbations to a steering vector affect behavior by comparing outputs with Cohen’s d; (3) compare steering vectors extracted by two common methods (contrastive mean and PCA) in terms of geometric similarity and behavioral equivalence. Experiments used two open-weight models, five linguistic traits (formality, politeness, sentiment, truthfulness, agreeableness), and multiple evaluation environments (in-distribution, topic-shift, genre-shift). Primary metrics include null-space fraction, Cohen’s d for semantic changes, and cosine similarity between extraction-method vectors.

## Key Results
- The Jacobian null space occupies 86–94% of the activation space across tested models and layers.
- Orthogonal perturbations to a steering vector produce negligible semantic effects (mean Cohen’s d < 0.2), confirming that most perturbation energy lies in the null space.
- Extraction methods yield geometrically distinct vectors (cosine similarity ranging from −0.54 to +0.32) but behaviorally equivalent outputs (Cohen’s d < 0.27), illustrating large equivalence classes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Null-Space-Induced Non-Identifiability
- **Claim:** Steering vectors are non-identifiable because the Jacobian has a large null space, allowing infinitely many behaviorally equivalent directions.
- **Mechanism:** Under local linear approximation, the output change is αJv. For any v0 ∈ ker(J), the vector v+v0 produces J(v+v0)=Jv, identical output change (up to scaling). Thus all vectors differing by a null-space component are equivalent.
- **Core assumptions:** Linear approximation holds (smooth activations, small α) and rank(J) is much smaller than the ambient dimension d.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statement about large null space; Proposition 1 constructs equivalence; Appendix A SVD shows null-space fraction 86–94%; corpus lacks direct prior work on identifiability.
- **Break condition:** If rank(J) approaches d (unlikely in overparameterized models) or nonlinear effects dominate at very high α (>3), though tests show robustness up to α=3.

### Mechanism 2: Orthogonal Perturbations Reside Largely in Null Space
- **Claim:** Random orthogonal perturbations to a steering vector have negligible semantic effect because most of the perturbation lies in the null space.
- **Mechanism:** With null space occupying 86–94% of the activation space, a random unit vector orthogonal to v has high probability of a large null-space component that contributes zero to Jv. Hence adding such a perturbation does not change the output.
- **Core assumptions:** Null space is high-dimensional and perturbations are isotropic.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports mean Cohen’s d < 0.2; Section 6.2 Table 1 confirms; scale invariance for α ≤ 3 (Section 6.4); corpus does not directly address this.
- **Break condition:** If the null-space dimension is drastically reduced or perturbations are intentionally aligned with row(J), but random orthogonality suffices for equivalence.

### Mechanism 3: Extraction Methods Sample Different Null-Space Components
- **Claim:** Contrastive mean and PCA extraction produce geometrically distinct vectors that are semantically equivalent, confirming large equivalence classes.
- **Mechanism:** Both methods produce vectors that affect the same observable dimensions (row space of J), but their null-space components differ, causing geometric diversity without behavioral change.
- **Core assumptions:** Output depends only on Jv; extraction methods do not constrain the null-space part.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: cosine similarity −0.54 to +0.32 with equivalent outputs; Section 6.3 Figure 2 and Table 2; Section 6.5 null-space spanning test with 50 directions; corpus papers use various methods without analyzing decoupling.
- **Break condition:** If extraction methods explicitly constrain to row space (e.g., regularization), but standard methods do not.

## Foundational Learning
- **Jacobian and Rank-Nullity Theorem**: The dimension of the null space of a linear map J is d - rank(J). This theorem is needed to quantify the size of the equivalence class of steering vectors. Quick check: For a linear map J: ℝᵈ → ℝᵛ with rank r, dim(ker(J)) = d - r.
- **Local Linear Approximation**: Steering effect is analyzed via first-order Taylor expansion: output ≈ output₀ + αJv. This approximation holds for small α and smooth activations. Quick check: Under what conditions does the first-order approximation accurately represent f(x+αv) for small α? (Answer: When f is differentiable and α is small enough that higher-order terms are negligible.)
- **Identifiability in Statistics**: A parameter θ is identifiable if different values of θ produce distinct observable distributions. Here, v is identifiable only if no other v' yields the same output distribution under steering. Quick check: Define parameter identifiability in a statistical model p(y|x;θ).

## Architecture Onboarding
Transformer LLM (L layers, hidden dim d) -> Activation Extractor -> Steering Vector Module -> Interceptor -> Output Evaluator.

Critical path:
1. Choose intervention layer ℓ (typically middle layer).
2. Gather activation pairs (hℓ(x⁺), hℓ(x⁻)) from contrastive prompts.
3. Compute steering vector v via chosen extraction method.
4. For evaluation, compute modified activation ˜hℓ = hℓ(x) + αv.
5. Forward pass from ˜hℓ to generate outputs.
6. Compare outputs between v and perturbed vectors (e.g., v+v⊥) using Cohen’s d or logit distances.

Design tradeoffs:
- Layer selection: Mid-layers balance effect strength and generalizability; earlier layers may cause broader disruptions.
- Extraction method: Contrastive mean is simple but may miss subtle variance; PCA captures dominant variance but not necessarily semantic direction.
- Steering strength α: Small α ensures linear regime; paper tests α ≤ 3, but larger α may introduce nonlinearities.
- Evaluation metric: Semantic classifiers are accessible but noisy; logit-based measures are model-internal but less interpretable.

Failure signatures:
- High null-space fraction: Steering vectors are non-unique; avoid overinterpreting any single vector as the "true" direction.
- Nonlinear effects: If equivalence breaks at high α, monitor output coherence and classifier stability.
- Classifier unreliability: For abstract traits, classifiers may have high variance; corroborate with multiple traits or logit analysis.
- Architecture sensitivity: Null-space dimensionality may vary across models; validate per target architecture.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Null-space estimation**: Collect activations over 200 prompts, compute activation covariance, perform SVD, measure effective rank (singular values > ε=0.01) and null-space fraction (d - rank)/d.
2. **Orthogonal perturbation test**: For a steering vector v, generate 10 random unit vectors v⊥ orthogonal to v (Gram-Schmidt). Steer evaluation prompts with v and v+v⊥ (α=1.0). Get classifier scores, compute Cohen’s d per prompt, average.
3. **Extraction method equivalence**: Compute v₁ via contrastive mean and v₂ via PCA for the same trait. Report cosine(v₁, v₂) and Cohen’s d between steered outputs from each.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can structural interventions (independence constraints, sparsity regularization, invariance objectives) recover uniquely identifiable steering vectors from behavioral equivalence classes?
2. Does the semantic null space estimated via activation SVD coincide with the Jacobian null space (ker(Jℓ)) that theoretically causes non-identifiability?
3. Can prompt design strategies reduce the common null space intersection across diverse prompts, thereby improving identifiability?
4. Does the non-identifiability result extend to the exact nonlinear regime for larger steering strengths (α > 3)?
5. What specific structural constraints (architectural modifications, training objectives, additional supervision) are necessary and sufficient to ensure identifiability of steering vectors?

## Limitations
- Exact contrastive prompt sets and zero-shot classifier implementations are underspecified, risking replication deviations.
- Generalizability limited to two models (Llama, Qwen) and five linguistic traits; other architectures or abstract traits may differ.
- Analysis assumes single-layer, white-box access; black-box or multi-layer steering may have different null-space properties.

## Confidence
- **High**: Theoretical mechanism (Jacobian null-space causes non-identifiability) is strongly supported by SVD analysis and algebraic proof.
- **Medium**: Empirical equivalence claims (orthogonal perturbations, extraction-method decoupling) rely on specific classifiers and prompt distributions; minor changes could affect Cohen’s d thresholds.
- **Low**: Broader applicability beyond tested models/traits; corpus shows no direct prior work on identifiability, limiting external validation.

## Next Checks
1. Cross-layer null-space profiling: Repeat SVD across all layers for Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-3B to confirm if mid-layers uniquely exhibit high null space or if this is universal.
2. Alternative extraction methods with regularization: Test whether sparse PCA or CCA produce vectors with reduced null-space components and whether behavioral equivalence still holds.
3. Model-agnostic evaluation: Replace human-defined trait classifiers with linear probes on hidden states or downstream task performance (e.g., truthfulness in QA) to verify non-identifiability independent of classifier noise.
