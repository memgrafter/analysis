# VideoSeek: Long-Horizon Video Agent with Tool-Guided Seeking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20185  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20185  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: On LVBench, improves accuracy by 8.3-10.2 points over GPT-5 while reducing frame usage by 76-93%; achieves state-of-the-art on Video-MME, LongVideoBench, and Video-Holmes with often <30 frames versus hundreds/thousands.

## Executive Summary
Video agents that exhaustively parse every frame incur high computational cost, especially for long videos. VideoSeek addresses this by introducing a think-act-observe loop coupled with a multi-granular toolkit—overview, skim, and focus—that actively seeks answer-critical evidence guided by the video's temporal and causal structure (video logic flow). This approach replaces uniform dense sampling with adaptive, reasoning-based selection of video segments, drastically reducing the number of frames processed. On LVBench, VideoSeek outperforms GPT-5 by 8.3-10.2 accuracy points while using only 68.4% of the frames (92.3 vs 384 on average). It also sets new state-of-the-art results on Video-MME, LongVideoBench, and Video-Holmes, often consuming fewer than 30 frames compared to hundreds or thousands used by prior methods. The system is training-free, relying on pre-trained large multimodal models and a reasoning LLM.

## Method Summary
VideoSeek formulates video question answering as a long-horizon decision-making problem. The agent maintains a trajectory τ of past thoughts, actions, and observations. At each step, a thinking LLM processes the entire τ together with the user query to decide the next thought and action. The action is either to call one of three tools—`overview`, `skim`, or `focus`—or to produce a final `answer`. The tools operate at different granularities: `overview` samples the entire video sparsely to produce a coarse global storyline; `skim` samples a specified time interval with moderate density to quickly scan for relevant content; `focus` densely samples a short clip (≤4α seconds) for fine-grained analysis. A tool executor extracts the requested video segment, constructs the tool-specific prompt (including frames and optional subtitles), and invokes a pre-trained LMM to generate an observation. The observation is appended to τ, and the loop continues. Hyperparameters include a frame budget α (e.g., α=4 for LVBench, α=2 for other benchmarks) and a maximum number of turns (typically 20). No model training is required; the agent leverages off-the-shelf LMMs (e.g., GPT-5) for both visual understanding and reasoning.

## Key Results
- On LVBench, VideoSeek improves accuracy by 8.3-10.2 points over GPT-5 while reducing frame usage by 76-93% (e.g., 68.4% vs 60.1% with 92.3 vs 384 frames).
- It achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on Video-MME, LongVideoBench, and Video-Holmes, processing often less than 30 frames versus hundreds or thousands used by prior agents.
- The multi-granular toolkit and active seeking reduce computational cost dramatically without sacrificing performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Active Evidence Seeking via Video Logic Flow
VideoSeek reduces computational cost by exploiting the video’s temporal and causal structure to localize where answer-critical evidence is likely to appear, rather than parsing every frame. The reasoning LLM infers relevant segments from the query, the initial overview, and subsequent observations, then invokes tools to collect evidence only from those intervals. This active seeking contrasts with greedy dense sampling. Core assumption: videos have a logical flow that can be leveraged, and the LLM can correctly infer relevant intervals from coarse information. Evidence includes the paper’s abstract and Section 2, which describe the human-like strategy. A break condition occurs when the video lacks coherent logic (e.g., random clips) or the LLM’s inference fails, causing mislocalization.

### Multi-Granular Toolkit for Adaptive Navigation
The three-toolkit (overview, skim, focus) enables efficient allocation of visual budget by operating at complementary temporal granularities, from global to local. The agent starts with `overview` to obtain a sparse global storyline; then uses `skim` to quickly scan candidate segments; finally employs `focus` for dense inspection of short clips. Each tool is invoked on demand, allowing dynamic adjustment of observation granularity. Core assumption: different understanding tasks require different granularities—global context suffices to identify promising regions, and dense inspection is needed only for specific short intervals. Evidence: Section 3.2 defines each tool’s role, and Section 4.3 ablations show that removing any tool hurts performance, with `overview` removal causing the largest drop (13.3 points on LVBench), confirming their complementary roles. Break condition: Questions requiring simultaneous global and local information at multiple locations may force frequent tool switching, potentially increasing frame usage; a failed initial overview may cause the agent to miss relevant segments entirely.

### Long-Horizon Reasoning over Accumulated Observations
VideoSeek’s think-act-observe loop reasons over the full trajectory of past actions and observations, enabling adaptive exploration and evidence refinement over time. The trajectory τ = ⟨z₁, a₁, o₁⟩, …, ⟨zₙ, aₙ, oₙ⟩ serves as dynamic memory, allowing the LLM to incorporate new evidence, correct earlier mistakes, and plan subsequent steps. Core assumption: the LLM can effectively utilize the growing history to make increasingly informed decisions about where to look next. Evidence: Section 3.1 factorizes the problem into trajectory generation and answer prediction; Algorithm 1 shows the loop that appends new triplets; Section 4.3 demonstrates that replacing GPT-5 with weaker GPT-4.1 reduces accuracy and also decreases number of turns and frames, indicating stronger reasoning leads to better long-horizon planning. Break condition: Context window limits may truncate the trajectory, losing critical history; early reasoning errors may lead to convergence on suboptimal answers if the agent cannot recover.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input Module (video, query, subtitles) -> Trajectory Memory & Thinking LLM -> Action (tool call or answer) -> [if tool] Tool Executor -> Toolkit (overview/skim/focus) -> LMM -> Observation -> back to Trajectory Memory -> loop -> Answer -> Output Module  

- **Critical path** (one iteration):
  1. Thinking LLM reads current trajectory (system instruction, query, past tuples).
  2. LLM outputs a thought and an action (tool call with parameters or `answer`).
  3. If action is a tool, Tool Executor extracts the requested video segment, constructs the tool prompt (frames, subtitles, instructions), and invokes the LMM to produce an observation.
  4. The new (thought, action, observation) is appended to the trajectory.
  5. Repeat from step 1 until `answer` or max turns reached.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Frame budget (α)**: Larger α means more frames per tool call, potentially more information but higher cost. Paper sets α per benchmark based on video length.
  - **Tool selection flexibility**: Agent can choose any tool at any time, but misuse (e.g., repeated `overview`) may waste frames; system instructions discourage it but enforcement relies on LLM compliance.
  - **Reasoning vs. efficiency**: Stronger LLMs (e.g., GPT-5) lead to better decisions and fewer total frames but cost more per call; weaker models may use more frames or fail.
  - **Subtitles**: With subtitles, performance improves and frame usage drops dramatically because textual logic flow reduces need for visual seeking.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Early answering**: Agent calls `answer` with insufficient evidence, often due to over-confidence; monitor thoughts for unsupported confidence claims.
  - **Tool misuse**: Repeated calls to `overview` or `skim` on same/overlapping intervals without progress; indicates the agent is stuck.
  - **Excessive frame usage**: Frames approach dense sampling levels (e.g., >100 on a long video); suggests ineffective toolkit use or poor reasoning.
  - **No answer after max steps**: Agent fails to converge, possibly because the video logic is too complex or LLM planning is inadequate.
  - **Incorrect localization**: Consistently looks at wrong timestamps; could stem from poor initial overview or reasoning errors.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Tool ablation**: Remove each tool (overview, skim, focus) one at a time and evaluate on a validation set (e.g., LVBench without subtitles); measure accuracy and average frames. Expect removal of `overview` to cause the largest drop.
  2. **Reasoning model ablation**: Replace the thinking LLM with models of varying strength (GPT-5, o4-mini, GPT-4.1) and compare performance and efficiency. Expect stronger reasoning to yield higher accuracy with fewer frames and more turns.
  3. **Subtitles vs. no subtitles**: Run identical queries with and without subtitles; measure accuracy and frame usage. Expect subtitles to significantly reduce frame usage and often improve accuracy.

## Limitations
- The core claim of "video logic flow" guidance lacks robust validation on non-causal or chaotic videos; the break condition is noted but not systematically tested.
- The multi-granular toolkit assumes questions can be solved with mostly global-then-local search; it may fail for multi-local detail queries requiring frequent tool switching.
- The long-horizon reasoning benefit is conflated with overall system performance; the trajectory’s utility has not been isolated (e.g., ablating memory while keeping tools).

## Confidence
- Multi-granular toolkit effectiveness: High
- Active evidence seeking via video logic flow: Medium
- Long-horizon reasoning benefit: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Logic flow stress test**: Run VideoSeek on videos with scrambled temporal order or non-causal edits; measure accuracy drop compared to coherent videos to validate the logic flow assumption.
2. **Toolkit necessity ablation**: Provide oracle segment localizations (bypassing logic flow) but still require toolkit usage; compare against the full system to isolate the benefit of the hierarchical granularity.
3. **Trajectory utility isolation**: Replace the full trajectory history with only the last *k* steps (e.g., *k*=3) while keeping the toolkit; evaluate accuracy and frame usage to quantify how much the agent depends on long-horizon memory.
