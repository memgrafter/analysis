# Diffusion-MPC in Discrete Domains: Feasibility Constraints, Horizon Effects, and Critic Alignment: Case study with Tetris

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.02348
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02348
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Primary result**: Feasibility masking via logit masking removes 46% invalid placements and yields a 6.8× mean score improvement; naive DQN reranking causes high decision regret; shorter planning horizons (H=4) outperform longer ones (H=8) in score and latency.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates diffusion-based model predictive control (Diffusion-MPC) in discrete combinatorial domains, using Tetris as a case study with hard placement feasibility constraints. The core method employs a MaskGIT-style discrete denoiser (PlanDenoiser) to generate candidate action sequences, augmented with feasibility-constrained sampling that masks invalid placements during generation. Candidates are reranked using a heuristic, a pretrained DQN critic, or a hybrid. Key findings demonstrate that feasibility masking is essential for performance, DQN reranking is systematically misaligned leading to high decision regret, and shorter planning horizons mitigate compounding uncertainty and critic mismatch, achieving higher scores and lower latency than longer horizons.

## Method Summary
The approach follows a sampling-based Model Predictive Control loop. A PlanDenoiser—a CNN encoder plus Transformer decoder trained via MaskGIT on expert trajectories—generates candidate action sequences of length *H*. During sampling, at each step, a feasibility mask derived from the current simulated board excludes invalid placements, ensuring all candidates are executable. For each of *K* candidates, the full *H*-step trajectory is simulated in a cloned environment. A reranker then assigns a score to each candidate: either a heuristic board evaluation (lines, holes, height, etc.), a pretrained DQN value (max<sub>*a*</sub> *Q*(*s*′,*a*)), or a hybrid combining both. The first action of the highest-scoring candidate is executed in the real environment. The denoiser is trained via behavior cloning on expert data; the DQN is trained separately with standard Q-learning.

## Key Results
- Feasibility masking removes ~46% of invalid placements, increasing mean score 6.8× (0.13→0.89) and survival rate 5.6×.
- Naive DQN reranking yields high decision regret (mean 17.6, p90 36.6) and low mean score (0.14); a hybrid reranker (α=0.05) matches heuristic performance with near-zero regret.
- Shorter horizon *H*=4 outperforms *H*=8 in mean score (1.48 vs 0.89) and reduces DQN regret (11.47 vs 17.59), with lower latency due to reduced simulation cost and compounding errors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Feasibility-Constrained Sampling Increases Valid Candidate Diversity
**Claim**: Logit masking improves planning performance by eliminating invalid action mass and increasing the proportion of executable candidate trajectories.  
**Mechanism**: At each denoising step, the sampling distribution is masked to only geometrically feasible placements for the current simulated board. This ensures all sampled trajectories are executable, converting previously wasted invalid samples into valid search candidates.  
**Evidence**: The paper reports ~46% of placements are masked, yielding a 6.8× score increase (0.13→0.89) and a 5.6× survival improvement. Without masking, 91% of episodes terminate within 20 steps versus 23% with masked heuristic sampling.  
**Break condition**: If the environment has no hard feasibility constraints, masking has no effect. If the denoiser already samples only valid actions, masking may be redundant or over-restrictive.

### Mechanism 2: DQN Reranking Misalignment Causes High Decision Regret
**Claim**: Using a pretrained DQN as a reranking critic leads to systematically poor candidate selection, quantified by high decision regret.  
**Mechanism**: The DQN’s Q-values are learned under its own behavior policy, which differs from the diffusion proposal distribution. When evaluating out-of-distribution candidates, these Q-values become misleading, causing the planner to select suboptimal trajectories. Decision regret (gap between selected and best candidate under rollout heuristic) directly measures this misalignment.  
**Evidence**: Naive DQN reranking produces mean regret 17.6 at *H*=8 (p90 36.6), with regret>10 in 63% of decisions. Heuristic and hybrid reranking keep regret near zero. DQN regret decreases from 17.59 to 11.47 when shortening horizon from 8 to 4.  
**Break condition**: If the critic is trained on data from the same proposal distribution or with a return-aware objective matching the planning objective, misalignment may reduce. Hybrid approaches (bounded critic influence) also mitigate.

### Mechanism 3: Shorter Horizons Mitigate Uncertainty Compounding and Critic Mismatch
**Claim**: In this sparse-reward, behavior-cloned setting, shorter planning horizons (*H*=4) yield higher score and lower latency than longer horizons (*H*=8).  
**Mechanism**: The denoiser is trained via behavior cloning on expert trajectories, optimizing short-horizon imitation. Longer horizons require predicting farther into imagined futures (beyond the next piece), where the denoiser is out-of-distribution, leading to compounding errors in rollouts. Longer horizons also amplify critic misalignment. Shorter horizons keep rollouts closer to the training distribution, reducing both denoising and evaluation errors.  
**Evidence**: *H*=4 achieves mean score 1.48 vs 0.89 for *H*=8. Under DQN reranking, shortening horizon reduces misranking severity (mean regret 17.59→11.47). Latency is lower with shorter horizons due to fewer simulation steps and less denoising complexity.  
**Break condition**: If the denoiser were trained with a long-horizon objective (e.g., RL with dense rewards), longer horizons might become beneficial. Dense intermediate rewards could also reduce compounding errors.

## Foundational Learning
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC)**: Why needed: Outer loop re-planning at each timestep by generating *K* candidates, simulating, selecting best, executing first action. **Quick check**: "Why does MPC re-plan at each timestep instead of executing a full open-loop plan? What are the tradeoffs?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input state (board, current piece, next piece) -> PlanDenoiser (CNN encoder + Transformer decoder) -> Feasibility masking (logit masking per step) -> Candidate simulation (*K* trajectories of *H* steps) -> Reranking (heuristic/DQN/hybrid) -> Action selection (first action of best candidate)
- **Critical path**: Current state is fed to PlanDenoiser; with feasibility masking, it autoregressively samples *K* action sequences; each sequence is simulated forward *H* steps; reranker scores each full trajectory; top candidate's first action is executed in environment.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Masking vs parallelism: Masking ensures validity but forces sequential sampling (each step depends on previous simulation), reducing batch efficiency; unmasked parallel sampling is faster but yields many invalid trajectories.
  - Candidate count *K*: Higher *K* improves candidate quality but increases latency linearly; diminishing returns after *K*=64.
  - Horizon *H*: Longer *H* may capture long-term effects but increases simulation cost, compounds denoiser errors, and exacerbates critic misalignment; shorter *H* is faster and more reliable but shortsighted.
  - Reranker: Heuristic reliable but hand-crafted; DQN learned but misaligned; hybrid (α=0.05) bounds DQN influence to act as tiebreaker.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High decision regret (>10) when using DQN reranker (especially *H*=8).
  - Low survival rate (<10%) and high early termination without feasibility masking.
  - Performance drop when *H*=8 vs *H*=4 with DQN; DQN regret decreases with shorter horizon.
  - Linear latency increase with *K*, but quality plateaus after *K*=64.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Masking ablation**: Run with/without feasibility masking (*K*=64, *H*=8, heuristic rerank). Expect: masked version much higher mean score (0.89 vs 0.13) and survival (28% vs 5%). Measure masked fraction (~46%) and confirm zero regret for heuristic.
  2. **Reranker comparison**: With masking fixed, compare heuristic, DQN, hybrid (*K*=64, *H*=8). Expect: DQN low score (0.14) and high regret (mean ~17.6); hybrid matches heuristic (0.89) with near-zero regret.
  3. **Horizon scaling**: For heuristic and DQN, compare *H*=4 vs *H*=8 (masking, *K*=64). Expect: heuristic *H*=4 outperforms *H*=8 (1.48 vs 0.89); DQN both poor but *H*=4 less bad (regret 11.47 vs 17.59). Note latency reduction with shorter *H*.

## Limitations
- Domain limitation: Results only on Tetris; transfer to other combinatorial problems unknown.
- Expert policy details vague; undefined heuristic may affect denoiser training distribution.
- Ground-truth for reranking (heuristic rollout) may not reflect optimal performance.

## Confidence
- Feasibility masking: High – strong ablation gains (6.8× score, 46% invalid removed).
- DQN misalignment: Medium – high regret observed, but causal hypotheses not fully isolated.
- Horizon effect: Medium – *H*=4 consistently better, but could be artifact of behavior cloning training.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate findings in at least two new discrete combinatorial domains (e.g., packing, scheduling).
2. Train DQN on data from the diffusion proposal distribution; measure change in decision regret.
3. Benchmark Diffusion-MPC against model-based RL methods (e.g., MCTS) for performance-cost tradeoffs.
