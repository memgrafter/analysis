# Evaluating LLMs in Finance Requires Explicit Bias Consideration
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14233
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14233
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Financial LLM evaluations systematically suffer from five biases (look-ahead, survivorship, narrative, objective, cost) that inflate performance and invalidate deployment claims; a proposed Structural Validity Framework with a pass/fail checklist aims to ensure reports reflect deployable net utility.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies five pervasive biases that systematically inflate performance metrics in financial LLM evaluations, rendering reported results invalid for real-world deployment. A review of 164 papers (2023–2025) reveals these biases are rarely acknowledged. The authors introduce a Structural Validity Framework with a concrete checklist to enforce temporal sanitation, dynamic universe construction, rationale robustness, epistemic calibration, and realistic cost/latency accounting. A practitioner survey highlights a severe lack of evaluation tools, with 74% reporting inadequate resources. The framework provides minimal, explicit requirements to bridge the gap between academic evaluation and deployable financial decision-making.

## Method Summary
The core methodology involves a systematic literature review of 164 financial LLM papers to document the prevalence of five specific biases. Based on this analysis, the authors derive a Structural Validity Framework comprising a pass/fail checklist. Each checklist item corresponds to one bias and defines a necessary condition for a valid evaluation (e.g., non-anticipativity for look-ahead bias). The framework's practicality is informed by a user study of 112 practitioners, which exposes a critical tool gap. Reproduction involves applying the checklist to existing studies or new evaluations, though the paper acknowledges a significant implementation gap: the checklist is currently a manual audit without automated tooling, and precise operational definitions for some checks (e.g., narrative robustness) remain vague.

## Key Results
- Financial LLM evaluations are universally affected by five biases: look-ahead (temporal leakage), survivorship (truncated observation windows), narrative (ungrounded explanations), objective (overconfidence), and cost (ignored deployment expenses).
- These biases are severely under-discussed in literature (look-ahead: 26.8%, survivorship: 1.2%, narrative: 11.6%, objective: 12.2%, cost: 28.0% of 164 papers).
- The proposed Structural Validity Framework provides a pass/fail checklist to enforce evaluation validity; a user study shows 74% of practitioners lack adequate tools, with 50% citing this as the primary bottleneck.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Temporal and Universe Contamination
- **Claim:** Invalid performance estimates arise because evaluation design introduces contamination via temporal leakage (look-ahead bias) and truncated observation windows (survivorship bias).
- **Mechanism:** The evaluation protocol fails to enforce *non-anticipativity* and *dynamic universes*. Look-ahead occurs when model weights or retrieval access information unavailable at decision time *t*. Survivorship bias occurs when the evaluation universe is defined by entities that survive the sample period, conditioning on a non-random subset. These flaws change the estimand: metrics measure performance under contaminated data, not deployable policy returns.
- **Core assumption:** Evaluation validity is determined by data provenance and universe construction, not just downstream task metrics.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "financial tasks ... systematically suffer from five recurring biases—look-ahead, survivorship ... which inflate performance and invalidate deployment claims."; [section 2.1] Parametric knowledge leakage; [section 2.2] "Conditioning on survival ... removes regimes that dominate risk"; corpus shows low discussion rates.
- **Break condition:** Any evaluation that does not (a) verify model weights and retrieval are point-in-time consistent for every decision, and (b) explicitly includes delisted/merged entities in the tradable universe at their historical observation times.

### Mechanism 2: Illusion of Validity from LLM Alignment
- **Claim:** LLM-generated narratives and objectives create an *illusion of validity* by rewarding fluency and confidence over factual grounding and calibrated uncertainty.
- **Mechanism:** The model's training objective (next-token prediction) and alignment (RLHF) optimize for linguistic coherence and preference satisfaction, not evidential support or honest uncertainty expression. This induces *narrative bias* (plausible but ungrounded causal stories) and *objective bias* (prioritizing confident guesses over abstention). In finance, this substitutes persuasive explanation for evidence-based reasoning, miscalibrates risk, and hides ignorance.
- **Core assumption:** Language model alignment objectives are mis-specified for high-stakes financial decision support.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Narrative bias ... model tells a convincing story that sounds reasonable but is not fully supported by the data."; [section 2.3] CoT rationales are post-hoc; [section 2.4] "Standard LLMs are primarily optimized for likelihood maximization ... creates a structural tendency to favor plausible-sounding text over reliable uncertainty quantification"; corpus shows low discussion rates for narrative and objective biases.
- **Break condition:** Any evaluation that does not (a) require audit trails for factual claims in rationales and measure violation rates, and (b) treat abstention ("Do Not Know") as a valid output with appropriate scoring.

### Mechanism 3: Decoupling of Gross and Net Utility
- **Claim:** Reported gross performance metrics are decoupled from net economic utility by ignoring deployment constraints, leading to *cost bias*.
- **Mechanism:** Evaluations assume zero transaction costs, zero inference/operational costs, and instantaneous execution. The reported objective *R_gross* diverges from the economically relevant *R_net = R_gross - C_trans - C_M - slippage(Δgen)*. This favors complex, expensive pipelines that appear superior in backtests but would be unprofitable or slow in production.
- **Core assumption:** A system's evaluation metric must reflect its total cost of operation, not just its signal precision.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Cost bias ... performance looks good on paper because real-world costs and delays are ignored."; [section 2.5] Defines monetary and latency cost bias; [section 3.5] Requires "net metrics should serve as primary outcomes"; cost bias discussed in only 28.0% of papers.
- **Break condition:** Any deployment claim based on backtests that do not incorporate realistic transaction costs, measured inference latency under expected load, and operational expenses into the primary performance metric.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Temporal Non-Anticipativity**
  - *Why needed:* The core validity condition for any historical backtest. Without it, the model uses future information, making the backtest simulate an impossible policy.
  - *Quick check:* For each decision timestamp *t*, can you prove that every input (model state, retrieved document, tool output) was *immutable* and *publicly available* at or before *t*?
- **Concept: Dynamic Tradable Universe**
  - *Why needed:* Survivorship bias distorts risk and return distributions. A valid evaluation must reflect the actual set of assets that were tradable at each point in time, including those that later delist.
  - *Quick check:* Does your backtest universe for time *t* include all assets that were listed and trading on that date, even if they delist later?
- **Concept: Epistemic Calibration Interface**
  - *Why needed:* Generic LLMs are overconfident. A financial system must communicate uncertainty and refuse to act when evidence is weak. The evaluation must score abstention correctly.
  - *Quick check:* Does your model's action space include a formal "No Trade / Insufficient Evidence" option, and is this option scored as a valid (not failed) outcome?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Structural Validity Checklist -> Temporal Sanitation (data pipeline guard) -> Dynamic Universe (data universe constructor) -> Rationale Robustness (explanation auditor) -> Epistemic Calibration (output interface) -> Realistic Constraints (cost-latency simulator)
- **Critical path:** Implement Temporal Sanitation first. Temporal contamination invalidates all subsequent results. Start by instrumenting a trace logger (prompts, retrieved doc IDs, timestamps) and enforcing point-in-time data access. Then build Dynamic Universe to feed correct asset lists into the simulation. The other blocks depend on these foundations.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Temporal Sanitation: Point-in-time archived data (e.g., Wayback Machine) is costly/limited vs. using cleaned historical datasets with unknown revisions.
  - Epistemic Calibration: Adding a calibrated abstention option may reduce apparent "hit rate" on binary accuracy metrics, requiring new scoring rules (e.g., Brier score with abstention).
  - Realistic Constraints: High-fidelity cost-latency modeling increases experimental complexity; may require shadow trading simulations.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Temporal: Model generates specific post-cutoff details; retrieval returns documents with future edits.
  - survivorship: Backtest universe consists only of current S&P 500 constituents; delisted firms < 1% of sample.
  - Narrative: Rationales contain unsupported causal claims ("X caused Y") not traceable to contemporaneous sources.
  - Objective: Model never outputs "Do Not Know" or uncertainty estimates; confidence correlates with output length.
  - Cost: Paper reports only Sharpe ratio or accuracy; no mention of token counts, latency, or transaction cost assumptions.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Temporal Sanitation Audit:** Run a single backtest with full trace logging. Manually inspect 100 random decision traces for references to post-*t* events or documents. Goal: 0% violation rate.
  2. **Survivorship Rate Diagnostic:** For a given backtest period, compute: (Number of distinct firms that ever trade) / (Number of firms trading on final date). Report separately for surviving vs. delisted cohorts. Goal: Delisted cohort >5% of total firm-time observations.
  3. **Cost-Latency Stress Test:** Re-run a promising backtest with *realistic* latency (e.g., +5 minutes for RAG+generation) and *conservative* transaction costs (e.g., 10bps per trade + 0.1% market impact). Compare net Sharpe to original gross Sharpe. Goal: Net Sharpe > 0 and within 20% of gross (if gross was strong).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What concrete mitigation techniques can effectively address survivorship, narrative, objective, and cost biases in financial LLM evaluations?** The field lacks systematic solutions for these four biases, leaving evaluations vulnerable.
- **How can we build and widely adopt accessible evaluation tools that embed the Structural Validity Checklist?** 74% of practitioners report scarce or non-existent tools; 50% cite this as the biggest bottleneck.
- **How can we democratize access to point-in-time financial data and other resources required for rigorous, bias-free evaluation?** The framework's data requirements may create access barriers for smaller academic groups.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gap:** The checklist is a manual audit; no standardized tools exist for automated enforcement, especially for temporal sanitation and narrative robustness, creating high implementation barriers.
- **Generalizability:** Findings are based on a 2023–2025 corpus and a 112-practitioner survey; rapid LLM evolution may already be shifting evaluation practices.
- **Adoption unknowns:** Real-world constraints (proprietary data, latency requirements, cost structures) may prevent full framework compliance, but the paper does not quantify these feasibility trade-offs.

## Confidence
- **Bias prevalence in literature:** High (direct corpus analysis of 164 papers shows low discussion rates for each bias).
- **Framework theoretical validity:** High (mechanisms logically link design flaws to invalid estimands; break conditions are well-defined).
- **Framework practical efficacy:** Low (no empirical validation showing that applying the checklist materially improves deployment outcomes or net utility).
- **Practitioner tool gap:** Medium (user study size is decent but may not represent all financial sectors or roles).

## Next Checks
1. **Compliance audit:** Apply the checklist to 10 recent (2024–2025) financial LLM papers claiming deployment readiness; document pass/fail rates per bias and correlate with reported performance tiers.
2. **Net utility replication:** For one selected paper with available code/data, re-run its backtest under the framework’s constraints (temporal sanitation, dynamic universe, cost/latency adjustments); quantify the delta between original gross metrics and net metrics.
3. **Tooling feasibility test:** Implement a minimal automated detector for look-ahead bias by tracing data versioning and retrieval timestamps in a sample LLM evaluation pipeline; measure false-positive/negative rates on known contaminated vs. clean setups.
