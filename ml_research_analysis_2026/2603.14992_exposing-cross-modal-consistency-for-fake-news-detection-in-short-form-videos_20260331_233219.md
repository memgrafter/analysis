# Exposing Cross-Modal Consistency for Fake News Detection in Short-Form Videos

**Quick Facts**
- arXiv ID: 2603.14992
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14992
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: MAGIC3 detects multimodal inconsistencies in short-form videos, outperforming non-VLM baselines and matching VLM-only accuracy with 18–27× higher throughput and 93% VRAM reduction via selective VLM routing.

**Executive Summary**
The paper introduces MAGIC3, a detector for fake news in short-form videos that explicitly models cross-modal consistency across text, visuals, and audio. It addresses the challenge where each modality appears individually plausible but subtle inconsistencies reveal manipulation. The system integrates multiple granularity signals—pairwise and global consistency scores, token/frame-level fields, and temporal audio-visual mismatches—and incorporates LLM-generated rewrites for style robustness. A two-stage pipeline routes ambiguous samples to a heavyweight VLM, achieving comparable or better accuracy than VLM-only while drastically improving efficiency. Evaluations on FakeSV and FakeTT show significant gains over prior non-VLM methods and superior cost-performance trade-offs.

**Method Summary**
MAGIC3 processes pre-extracted frozen features: BERT text embeddings, Swin Transformer visual features from 5 keyframes, and ExHuBERT audio embeddings. It generates three stylistic rewrites of the original text via DeepSeek-V3.2. The core architecture comprises: 1) Cross-Modal Consistency Gate (CMCG) producing pairwise and global consistency scalars; 2) Consistency Field Estimator (CFE) converting cross-attention matrices into fine-grained token/frame-level fields; 3) Adversarial-Aware Rewrite Fusion (AARF) gating the original text with rewrites; 4) Hierarchical Multimodal Transformer (HMT) with intra-modal refinement, consistency-weighted cross-attention, and global fusion; 5) Temporal Cross-Modal Inconsistency (TCMI) module scoring audio-visual temporal mismatches. The final classifier outputs fake probability, confidence, and uncertainty (entropy plus learned scalar). Training uses Contrastive-Adversarial Joint Learning (CAJL), combining binary cross-entropy, InfoNCE contrastive losses (intra- and cross-modal), adversarial consistency regularization, style invariance, and field alignment. Hyperparameters include AdamW (lr=5e-5), batch 32, and loss weights λintra=λcross=0.1, λadv=0.5, λsem=0.1, λstyle=0.1, λreg=0.05, αmin=0.5. The uncertainty estimate drives a router that sends ~25% of samples with high entropy/low confidence to a heavyweight VLM in a two-stage pipeline.

**Key Results**
- On FakeSV, MAGIC3 baseline (no VLM) achieves +1.98% accuracy over prior state-of-the-art KDSGAT-FNVD.
- In the two-stage pipeline, MAGIC3+VLM reaches 90.93% accuracy on FakeSV and 89.52% on FakeTT, matching or exceeding VLM-only (89.60%, 88.90%) while improving throughput 18–27× and reducing VRAM by 93%.
- The system routes ~25% of ambiguous samples to the VLM, demonstrating a superior cost-performance trade-off for scalable fake news detection.

**Why This Works (Mechanism)**
The paper's core insight is that fake short-form videos often contain subtle inconsistencies across modalities that are not apparent when examining each modality alone. By explicitly modeling cross-modal consistency at multiple levels—global pairwise scores, fine-grained token/frame alignment, and temporal audio-visual coherence—MAGIC3 captures these hidden cues. The consistency signals are integrated throughout the multimodal transformer, guiding attention to discordant regions. Additionally, LLM rewrites make the text representation robust to stylistic variations, and an uncertainty head enables selective invocation of a powerful but expensive VLM only for ambiguous cases, preserving efficiency without sacrificing accuracy.

**Foundational Learning**
- **Cross-modal consistency**: Measures alignment between text, visual, and audio streams; needed because fakes may have individually plausible modalities but mismatched content. Quick check: Compute similarity between modality features (e.g., CLIPScore) and verify lower for known fakes.
- **Contrastive adversarial joint learning (CAJL)**: Training objective that aligns intra- and cross-modal representations while regularizing consistency fields; needed to learn discriminative features without supervision on inconsistency. Quick check: Monitor contrastive loss values; ensure they decrease and embeddings cluster by modality alignment.
- **Adversarial-aware rewrite fusion (AARF)**: Dynamically weights original text and LLM-generated stylistic rewrites; needed to maintain semantic content invariant to expression style. Quick check: Inspect fusion weights α; original text should retain at least αmin=0.5.
- **Hierarchical multimodal transformer (HMT)**: Performs intra-modal refinement followed by consistency-weighted cross-attention and global fusion; needed to hierarchically integrate local and global cues. Quick check: Verify cross-attention weights are modulated by CMCG scalars (e.g., via attention visualization).
- **Temporal cross-modal inconsistency (TCMI)**: Quantifies mismatches between audio and visual streams over time; needed to catch edits where audio-visual sync is broken. Quick check: Compute TCMI scores for videos with known sync issues; expect higher inconsistency.
- **Uncertainty-based routing**: Estimates sample difficulty via entropy and learned scalar; needed to decide when to invoke expensive VLM. Quick check: Compute correlation between uncertainty and error on validation; higher uncertainty should correspond to misclassifications.

**Architecture Onboarding**
Component map: AARF (text rewrite fusion) -> HMT (intra-modal + cross-attention with CMCG modulation + global fusion) + TCMI (audio-visual temporal) -> Classifier (with uncertainty head). CMCG acts on raw modality features to produce scalars; CFE operates on HMT cross-attention matrices to produce fine fields; both feed into HMT's cross-attention weighting.
Critical path: Input features -> AARF (text) -> HMT processing (intra-refinement, cross-attention modulated by CMCG/CFE, global fusion) -> combine with TCMI score -> classifier -> output and uncertainty.
Design tradeoffs: Multi-granularity consistency (rich signal vs. computational overhead), frozen encoders (stability vs. domain adaptation), LLM rewrites (style robustness vs. preprocessing cost), selective VLM routing (efficiency vs. potential coverage gap), CAJL loss complexity (training stability vs. hyperparameter sensitivity).
Failure signatures:
- Lreg (field alignment loss) remains high, indicating misalignment between global and local consistency signals.
- AARF original text weight α0 collapses to αmin, losing the contribution of the original caption.
- Uncertainty scores uncorrelated with error, causing routing to select easy or random samples, degrading two-stage accuracy.
- Poor convergence when λadv or λreg are too high, causing training instability.
First experiments:
1) Sanity check: Train on a tiny subset (e.g., 50 videos) and confirm >95% training accuracy to verify gradient flow.
2) Component ablation: Remove each of CMCG, CFE, AARF, TCMI one at a time and measure expected performance drop (>2% on validation).
3) Routing sanity: Compute validation accuracy vs. uncertainty threshold; expect monotonic decrease in accuracy for high-uncertainty samples.

**Limitations**
- Architectural details (MLP dimensions, projection sizes in AARF) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- Contrastive loss positive/negative pair definitions are ambiguous, risking implementation drift.
- Performance depends on quality of LLM rewrites; prompt engineering or model version mismatches may degrade style robustness.

**Confidence**
- Performance improvements over baselines: Medium confidence
- Efficiency gains (throughput, VRAM reduction): Medium-High confidence
- Generalizability to other datasets: Low confidence
- Correctness of two-stage routing trade-off: Medium confidence

**Next Checks**
1) Inspect released code for exact MLP dimensions in CMCG, AARF, and TCMI, and projection layer sizes; if missing, implement with widths proportional to 256-d hidden state and validate convergence.
2) Ablate contrastive pairing strategies (e.g., treat all text rewrites as positives vs. only style-invariant pairs; cross-modal pairs with/without global fusion) to identify which matches reported loss impacts.
3) Re-evaluate routing calibration: Bin test samples by final uncertainty score and compute per-bin accuracy; confirm strong positive correlation (Pearson’s r > 0.8) to ensure router selects harder samples.
