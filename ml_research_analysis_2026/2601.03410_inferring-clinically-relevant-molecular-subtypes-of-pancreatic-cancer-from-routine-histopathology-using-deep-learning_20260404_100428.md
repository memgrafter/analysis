# Inferring Clinically Relevant Molecular Subtypes of Pancreatic Cancer from Routine Histopathology Using Deep Learning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03410
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03410
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: PanSubNet predicts basal-like and classical PDAC subtypes from H&E whole-slide images with AUC 90.3% (internal CV) and 84.0% (external TCGA) and preserves prognostic stratification.

## Executive Summary
Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) exhibits distinct molecular subtypes (basal-like and classical) that guide prognosis and therapy, but current subtyping relies on RNA-sequencing, which is costly and not routine. PanSubNet is a deep learning framework that predicts these subtypes directly from H&E whole-slide images. It employs a dual-scale architecture: cellular morphology is captured by CellViT++ (40×), and tissue architecture by UNI2-h (20×). A spatially-biased attention mechanism fuses cell-level and patch-level features, and an attention-based multiple instance learning (AttMIL) aggregates patches for slide-level prediction. Trained on 171 high-confidence cases, PanSubNet achieved a mean AUC of 90.3% in five-fold cross-validation and 84.0% on the external TCGA cohort without fine-tuning. The model’s predictions maintained, and for metastatic disease even strengthened, the prognostic separation of transcriptomic labels, demonstrating the clinical potential of histopathology-based subtyping.

## Method Summary
PanSubNet processes whole-slide images in two magnification streams. At 40×, CellViT++ segments nuclei, classifies cell types, and extracts embeddings for each cell, along with spatial centroids. At 20×, the WSI is tiled into patches (256×256), each encoded by the foundation model UNI2-h into a 768-dimensional vector. For each patch, the associated cells are identified by centroid location. A spatially-biased self-attention layer aggregates the cell embeddings, with attention modulated by Euclidean distance to encode cellular neighborhoods. The resulting cell representation is combined with the patch embedding via an outer product, then projected to a 768-dimensional fused patch embedding. All fused patch embeddings are fed into a 2D attention-based MIL (AttMIL) that learns to weight diagnostically relevant patches, culminating in a slide-level score for the basal-like vs. classical subtype. The model is trained using a binary label derived from the difference of ssGSEA scores (Classical – Basal) with a threshold of |z| > 1, ensuring high-confidence molecular assignments. This design leverages routine histology to replicate transcriptomic subtyping at scale.

## Key Results
- Internal five-fold cross-validation mean AUC: 90.3% (n=171 high-confidence cases).
- External validation on TCGA cohort (unseen during training) AUC: 84.0% without fine-tuning.
- Prognostic stratification by model predictions mirrored that of RNA-seq labels and showed enhanced separation in metastatic patients.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** PanSubNet integrates complementary morphological signals by fusing cellular-level features (from CellViT++) with tissue-level architectural features (from UNI2-h) before slide-level prediction.
- **Mechanism:** Cellular embeddings capture fine-grained nuclear morphology and phenotypic properties of individual cells (e.g., size, shape, texture). Tissue patch embeddings from UNI2-h capture broader architectural patterns (gland formation, stromal composition, spatial organization). An outer product operation creates a fused representation encoding pairwise interactions between cellular and tissue features, which is then projected to a fixed dimension.
- **Core assumption:** The morphological correlates of molecular subtypes are encoded in both individual cell phenotypes *and* their spatial arrangement within tissue architecture; neither scale alone is sufficient.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "dual-scale deep learning framework integrates cellular morphology (via CellViT++) and tissue architecture (via UNI2-h) with attention-based fusion."
  - [section]: "Our strategy implements a dual-scale approach, where cells at 40× magnification serve as fundamental 'words'... and tissue patches at 20× magnification represent 'sentences'... To integrate cellular information with tissue architecture, we employed an outer product operation..."
  - [corpus]: Corpus evidence is weak; neighboring papers focus on alternative modalities (CECT, IHC) or different architectures (contrastive learning, matrix factorization). No direct validation of the dual-scale fusion mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If cellular-level features are uninformative (e.g., nuclei segmentation fails on low-quality slides) or if architectural patterns are noise-dominated (e.g., severe staining artifacts), the fused representation may degrade performance relative to a single-scale model.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The model uses attention mechanisms at multiple levels (cell-to-patch within patches, and patch-to-slide for whole-slide aggregation) to weight biologically relevant regions and instances, improving robustness and interpretability.
- **Mechanism:** Within each tissue patch, a spatially biased self-attention layer aggregates cell embeddings, where attention scores are modulated by Euclidean distance between cell centroids, encoding proximity as a proxy for functional cellular neighborhoods. At the slide level, 2D attention-based multiple instance learning (AttMIL) aggregates patch embeddings, learning to focus on diagnostically relevant regions (shown in Figures 4 & 5).
- **Core assumption:** Not all cells or tissue regions contribute equally to determining molecular subtype; attention allows the model to dynamically select informative morphologic patterns while suppressing irrelevant background or artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "attention-based fusion" and "transparent feature attribution."
  - [section]: "we established a spatial correspondence between cells and patches... using a spatially biased self-attention mechanism... The fused embeddings... were aggregated using 2D AttMIL for slide-level prediction."
  - [corpus]: Weak indirect support; attention is standard in MIL and transformer architectures, but no corpus paper directly tests its role in PDAC subtyping from H&E.
- **Break condition:** If attention weights become diffuse (entropy too high) or collapse to a few patches/cells, the model may overfit to spurious correlations (e.g., tissue processing artifacts) or fail to capture distributed histologic patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Training exclusively on high-confidence transcriptomic labels (|z-score| > 1) teaches the model the clearest morphological endpoints of the subtype spectrum, enabling it to interpolate across intermediate states at inference.
- **Mechanism:** The supervised target is a binary label derived from a continuous molecular score (ssGSEA Classical – Basal) with a strict threshold. This filters out transcriptionally ambiguous tumors. The learned feature space is then evaluated on the full cohort, where prediction confidence (decision margin) correlates with molecular certainty, and an auxiliary model can predict label confidence from embeddings.
- **Core assumption:** High-confidence tumors represent "pure" morphological expressions of each subtype; intermediate cases are biologically real but noisy for training. A model trained on extremes can generalize across the continuum if its representations encode the underlying axis.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Trained on 171 high-confidence cases... PanSubNet preserved, and in metastatic disease strengthened, prognostic stratification compared to transcriptomic labels."
  - [section]: "High-confidence cases were used for all supervised training... intermediate cases were excluded from model training but retained as a biological reference group... PanSubNet representations capture meaningful biological signals associated with subtype certainty (AUC 74.9% for confidence prediction)."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; neighboring works on molecular subtyping typically use all available labels or different thresholding strategies.
- **Break condition:** If the morphological continuum does not align with the transcriptomic continuum (e.g., due to lineage plasticity or sampling bias), the model trained on extremes may misclassify intermediate tumors or produce confidence scores that do not reflect biological ambiguity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multiple Instance Learning (MIL)**: Why needed here: Whole-slide images are too large to process at high resolution; they must be broken into patches. MIL treats the WSI as a "bag" of patches (or cells+patches), where slide-level label is known but patch-level labels are not. PanSubNet uses AttMIL to aggregate patch embeddings. Quick check: If you have a WSI divided into 10,000 patches and only one slide-level label (basal/classical), what learning paradigm lets you train a classifier without labeling each patch?
- **Vision Transformer (ViT) and Foundation Models**: Why needed here: CellViT++ and UNI2-h are pre-trained transformers that provide powerful, general-purpose embeddings for cells and tissue patches. PanSubNet relies on these off-the-shelf feature extractors rather than training from scratch. Quick check: Why might using a pre-trained model like UNI2-h (trained on diverse histology) be better than training a CNN from scratch on only PDAC slides?
- **Self-Supervised Pre-training**: Why needed here: Both CellViT++ and UNI2-h are likely pre-trained via self-supervision (e.g., MAE, contrastive learning) on large, unlabeled histopathology datasets. This gives them rich representations of cellular and tissue morphology before any subtyping task. Quick check: How can a model learn useful features without any class labels? What objective might it use (e.g., predicting missing image parts, matching augmented views)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** WSI → CellViT++ (40× cell segmentation & embeddings + centroids) + UNI2-h (20× patch embeddings) → For each patch, gather associated cells → spatially-biased self-attention → cell CLS token → outer product with patch embedding → projection to fused patch embedding → bag of fused embeddings → 2D AttMIL → slide-level prediction (basal/classical). Interpretability via attention maps.
- **Critical path:** Input WSI → CellViT++ (cells) + UNI2-h (patches) → Fusion per patch → AttMIL over patches → Output score. Spatial correspondence (cell centroid → patch ID) must be maintained throughout.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Dual-scale vs. single-scale: Dual-scale captures both cell morphology and architecture; single-scale may miss one signal. Evidence: PanSubNet outperforms AttMIL (patch-only) on external TCGA (AUC 84.0% vs. 82.8%).
  - Outer product fusion vs. concatenation: Outer product captures all pairwise interactions but expands dimensions; concatenation is simpler but may miss multiplicative synergies. No ablation reported.
  - High-confidence training vs. full distribution: Training on clean labels improves pure signal learning but discards common intermediate cases. Evidence: AUC drops from 90.3% to 71.3% on full cohort.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Shattered attention: Attention maps are noisy, scattered, or uniform → model not focusing on meaningful regions.
  - Confidence collapse: Decision margins cluster near 0.5 → model fails to distinguish subtypes.
  - Cohort shift: Large performance drop on external cohort (AUC <75%) → overfitting to dataset-specific artifacts.
  - Cell segmentation failure: CellViT++ fails (e.g., poor contrast) → fallback to patch-only or NaNs.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablate cellular branch: Replace fused patch embeddings with UNI2-h-only patch embeddings (AttMIL baseline). Predict: drop in external TCGA AUC due to loss of cellular signal.
  2. Remove spatial bias: Eliminate Euclidean distance subtraction in cell-to-patch attention. Test if performance degrades, indicating spatial priors are important.
  3. Evaluate on intermediate cases: Apply final model to low-confidence (|z-score| ≤1) PANCAN cases. Check if prediction confidence correlates with |ΔZ|; expect lower confidence for true intermediates. Compute auxiliary confidence classifier AUC (should be ~75%).

## Limitations
- The benefit of outer product fusion versus simpler alternatives (e.g., concatenation) is not ablated; its specific contribution remains uncertain.
- Attention mechanisms' robustness to artifacts and their causal role in performance are inferred but not directly validated.
- Training exclusively on high-confidence cases yields a sharp AUC drop (71.3%) on the full cohort, suggesting limited generalizability to the clinically prevalent intermediate subtype spectrum.

## Confidence
- **Dual-scale integration (CellViT++ + UNI2-h):** Medium
- **Attention-based fusion & aggregation:** Medium-Low
- **High-confidence training strategy:** Medium

## Next Checks
1. Ablate Fusion Mechanistically: Train models with patch-only (UNI2-h), concatenated cell+patch, and outer product fusion. Compare performance on an internal test set and an additional external histopathology cohort to isolate fusion contribution.
2. Stress-Test Attention Robustness: Quantify attention entropy and spatial concentration on held-out slides with known artifacts (staining variation, folds). Correlate high-attention regions with artifact maps to detect spurious focusing.
3. Prospective Intermediate Case Evaluation: Apply the model to a large independent cohort with full RNA-seq data, stratifying by molecular score certainty (|z-score| bins). Primary endpoint: calibration of prediction confidence against true biological ambiguity and overall survival in the intermediate bin.
