# Latent Reasoning with Supervised Thinking States

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08332
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08332
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** Thinking States improves accuracy on complex reasoning tasks while reducing inference cost via recurrent state compression and teacher-forced training.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Thinking States, a method to improve the efficiency of chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in large language models. By generating intermediate reasoning tokens during input processing and compressing them into fixed-size states, the model can recurrently condition future computations without increasing context length. This design enables parallel training and faster inference, achieving significant speedups over standard CoT while maintaining or improving accuracy on benchmarks like GSM8K, 2-Hop QA, and state-tracking tasks. The approach also demonstrates strong length generalization, handling sequences longer than those seen during training.

## Method Summary
Thinking States processes input in fixed-size chunks. For each chunk, a deep-layer representation of the existing tokens is used to generate a natural-language thought sequence via a lightweight Thinking Block. This sequence is compressed by a Compression Block into a fixed-size state, which is added to the embeddings of the next chunk at a shallow layer. This recurrent conditioning allows the model to incorporate intermediate reasoning without growing the context window. Training uses teacher-forcing: gold CoT reasoning sequences are provided for each chunk, enabling all chunks to be processed in parallel and avoiding backpropagation through time. The backbone LLM's weights remain frozen; only the Thinking and Compression Blocks are trained.

## Key Results
- GSM8K: Achieves 42.22% accuracy with a 2.66× speedup over standard CoT.
- 2-Hop QA: Matches CoT accuracy (54.91% vs. 54.79%) with a 1.19× speedup.
- State-tracking: Surpasses CoT in length generalization, reaching up to 100% accuracy on sequences longer than training lengths.
- Training time remains constant with increasing recurrence depth, unlike BPTT-based methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper explains three core mechanisms:

**Mechanism 1: Thoughts from deep representations**  
Thoughts are generated not from newly generated tokens but from deep hidden states (`Hout_i`) of the current input chunk. This leverages the backbone LLM's pretrained knowledge to extract a reasoning signal. The Thinking Block, a lightweight decoder, predicts a natural-language reasoning sequence conditioned on `Hout_i`. If the deep representation lacks a coherent signal (e.g., for ambiguous chunks), the Thoughts may become trivial or nonsensical, harming the reasoning chain. Evidence: [section 3.1] describes `Hout_i` as input to T; the abstract notes thoughts are computed from representations of existing tokens.

**Mechanism 2: Recurrent conditioning via fixed-size states**  
The variable-length thought sequence is compressed by the Compression Block into a fixed-size state (`S_i`). This state is added to the next chunk's token embeddings (`tilde{X}_{i+1} = X_{i+1} + S_i`) at a shallow layer (`L_in`). This residual injection influences all subsequent deep-layer computations without increasing context length. Effective compression and appropriate injection layer are critical; loss of information or semantic disruption can derail reasoning. Evidence: [abstract] and [section 3.1] equations (1) and (4); length generalization results in [section 4.2] demonstrate effective state use.

**Mechanism 3: Teacher-forcing for parallel training**  
During training, gold CoT reasoning sequences (`Z*`) are aligned to chunks. The corresponding gold states (`S*`) are injected, allowing all chunk representations to be computed in a single parallel forward pass. The Thinking Block is trained with next-token prediction on `Z*` in parallel over all chunks. This avoids backpropagation through time and keeps training time constant regardless of recurrence depth. However, at inference, the model uses its own (potentially imperfect) states, which may diverge from the gold-state distribution and cause error accumulation. Evidence: [abstract] and [section 3.2] describe parallel computation; [section 4.1] shows constant training time.

## Foundational Learning
- **Teacher-forcing:**  
  *Why needed:* It decouples the recurrent dependency from the training graph, enabling parallel computation across chunks and constant training time.  
  *Quick check:* How does using gold states during training (instead of model-generated states) change the computational graph and allow parallel processing of all chunks?

- **Chunk-based processing & KV cache:**  
  *Why needed:* The architecture divides input into fixed-size chunks and uses the KV cache to maintain autoregressive dependencies across chunks. This is key to understanding inference scheduling and the speculative prefill algorithm.  
  *Quick check:* Why is the KV cache essential for processing chunks sequentially without reprocessing prior tokens, and how does speculative prefill exploit state sparsity?

- **Deep-to-shallow residual connection:**  
  *Why needed:* Thoughts are extracted from a deep layer (`L_out`) and injected at a shallow layer (`L_in`). This leverages deep computation for thought generation while ensuring thoughts influence nearly all subsequent layers.  
  *Quick check:* What would be the consequence of extracting thoughts from a shallower layer or injecting them at a deeper layer?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Backbone LLM (`M_θ`) processes chunks sequentially with KV cache. At layer `L_out` (second-to-last), it outputs `Hout_i` to the Thinking Block (`T_φ`), which generates thought tokens `Z_{i+1}`. The Compression Block (`C_φ`) compresses `Z_{i+1}` into state `S_{i+1}`. For the next chunk, `S_{i+1}` is added to the token embeddings `X_{i+1}` before they enter the backbone at layer `L_in` (first layer). The process repeats.

**Critical path (inference loop for chunk *i*):**  
1. Prepare input: `tilde{X}_i = X_i + S_i` (with `S_1=0` for the first chunk).  
2. Forward pass through `M_θ` using KV cache to get `Hout_i`.  
3. Autoregressively decode `Z_{i+1}` from `T_φ(Hout_i)` until `<EOS>`.  
4. Compress `Z_{i+1}` via `C_φ` to obtain `S_{i+1}`.  
5. Update KV cache and proceed to chunk *i+1*.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Chunk size (c):** Larger chunks reduce iteration count and improve latency but risk compressing multiple reasoning steps into one state. Smaller chunks increase state update frequency but raise overhead. Performance peaks at medium sizes (e.g., c=8 on GSM8K).  
- **Recurrence depth (`L_out - L_in`):** A deeper `L_out` gives the Thinking Block more computational power, improving accuracy but requiring more layers to be re-executed per chunk, reducing speedup.  
- **State sparsity:** The speculative prefill speedup relies on many chunks producing trivial (`<EOS>`) states. The distribution of non-trivial states varies by task and affects actual speedup.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **State ambiguity:** When the final question arrives late, early states may encode reasoning about the wrong intermediate quantity. Prepending the final question (out-of-distribution) mitigates this.  
- **Trivial state overload:** If `T` frequently outputs `<EOS>`, the model loses recurrent advantages, effectively becoming feedforward.  
- **Teacher-forcing mismatch:** High training accuracy but low inference accuracy suggests the model's generated states diverge from gold states during inference, causing error accumulation.

**First 3 experiments (reproducibility checks):**  
1. **Single-chunk sanity check:** Feed a single chunk with `S_1=0` and verify `T` produces the expected gold thought sequence `Z*` for that chunk (given perfect `Hout_1`). Isolates `T` and `C` functionality.  
2. **Multi-chunk recurrence validation:** Process a 2-chunk sequence. Compare predictions with and without `S_2` injection to confirm that `S_2` correctly influences `Hout_2`.  
3. **Speedup measurement (prefill):** Run a long query with varying chunk sizes. Measure wall-clock time for sequential vs. speculative prefill. Confirm the |R|+1 round behavior and quantify speedup versus CoT baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Dynamic compute during decoding:** Can Thinking States be extended to the decoding phase to allocate compute dynamically during token generation?  
2. **RL optimization:** Can reinforcement learning optimize the internal thinking process beyond human-generated CoT traces?  
3. **Bidirectional query processing:** Does bidirectional processing mitigate the "state ambiguity" error where the final question specifies the target too late?  
4. **Optimal chunk size:** What is the optimal chunk size trade-off between reasoning frequency and per-state capacity across diverse task types?

## Limitations
- **Teacher-forcing gap:** Inference uses model-generated states, unlike training's gold states. Error accumulation in early chunks could degrade thought quality, but the paper lacks analysis of divergence between gold and generated thought sequences.
- **State sparsity dependency:** The reported speedups rely on frequent trivial (`<EOS>`) thoughts. The distribution of non-trivial states across diverse tasks is unreported, making speedup claims potentially brittle.
- **Thought coherence verification:** Claims that the Thinking Block extracts "meaningful" thoughts are not substantiated by human evaluation or automatic thought validity metrics; nonsensical thoughts might still yield correct answers via shortcuts.

## Confidence
- **Efficiency claims (speedup):** *Medium* (depends on unmeasured state sparsity variance).
- **Accuracy claims on reported benchmarks (GSM8K, 2-Hop, state-tracking):** *High* (results are clear on these benchmarks).
- **Length generalization:** *High* (robust extrapolation beyond training lengths).
- **Teacher-forcing stability (generalization from gold to model states):** *Low* (no evidence that state distributions stay close during inference).

## Next Checks
1. **Measure state divergence:** Compare the distribution of generated thought sequences (using model states) versus gold thought sequences (teacher-forced) on a validation set. Quantify divergence (e.g., KL, BLEU) and correlate with accuracy drop.
2. **Profile state sparsity:** For each benchmark, compute the percentage of chunks where `T` outputs `<EOS>` immediately. Report mean/variance and analyze correlation with actual wall-clock speedup.
3. **Evaluate thought utility:** Conduct human evaluation or use an LLM judge to score coherence and relevance of generated thought sequences. Correlate scores with downstream task accuracy.
