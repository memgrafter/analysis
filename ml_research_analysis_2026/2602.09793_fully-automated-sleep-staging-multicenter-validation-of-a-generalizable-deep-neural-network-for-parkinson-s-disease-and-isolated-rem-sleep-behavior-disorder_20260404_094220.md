# Fully-automated sleep staging: multicenter validation of a generalizable deep neural network for Parkinson's disease and isolated REM sleep behavior disorder

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09793
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09793
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuned U-Sleep achieves human-level sleep staging agreement in PD/iRBD (Cohen's κ up to 0.74) and enables high-confidence REM detection.

## Executive Summary
Manual sleep staging in Parkinson's disease (PD) and isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD) is hindered by EEG abnormalities and fragmented sleep. This study fine-tuned the U-Sleep deep neural network on multicenter neurodegenerative datasets (112 PD, 138 iRBD, 89 controls) from two centers. The resulting generalized model significantly improved agreement with human scorers on both fine-tuning data (Cohen's κ from 0.66 to 0.74, *p*<0.001) and an independent clinical hold-out set (mean κ 0.60→0.64, median 0.64→0.69, both *p*<0.001), reaching human interrater reliability. Model confidence emerged as the sole pre-polysomnography predictor of agreement (*p*<0.001), and applying a confidence threshold boosted correct REM epoch identification from 85% to 95.5% while retaining >5 minutes of REM sleep for 95% of subjects. The publicly available model promises scalable, reliable sleep analysis in neurodegenerative disorders.

## Method Summary
The work employs transfer learning: a U-Sleep model initially pretrained on ~19,000 non-neurodegenerative polysomnograms (PUB) is fine-tuned on combined research datasets (PACE and CBC) comprising PD, iRBD, and control subjects. This adaptation targets pathological EEG patterns and sleep fragmentation characteristic of neurodegeneration. Performance is evaluated via 10-fold subject-level cross-validation on the fine-tuning data and on an independent hold-out set from a third center (DCSM). Primary outcome is per-night Cohen's κ agreement with human scorers. Secondary analyses examine whether model confidence (mean per-epoch maximum probability) predicts κ and whether confidence thresholding can optimize REM staging precision while preserving clinically meaningful REM duration.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuning increased Cohen's κ on the neurodegenerative fine-tuning data from 0.66 (pretrained) to 0.74 (*p*<0.001).
- On an independent clinical hold-out set, the generalized model improved mean κ from 0.60 to 0.64 and median κ from 0.64 to 0.69 (both *p*<0.001), matching typical human interrater reliability.
- Model confidence was the only significant pre-PSG predictor of agreement (*p*<0.001); applying a confidence threshold (e.g., 0.8) raised correct REM epoch identification from 85% to 95.5% while retaining >5 minutes of REM sleep in 95% of subjects.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Performance gains arise from three interconnected mechanisms. First, **transfer learning adaptation**: fine-tuning adjusts the pretrained U-Sleep's weights on neurodegenerative data, capturing disease-specific EEG slowing, spindle loss, and atypical REM while retaining robust feature extractors from multisite healthy data. This reduces the domain shift, evidenced by a κ rise from 0.66 to 0.74 on fine-tuning data. Second, **confidence-agreement correlation**: the model's per-night mean confidence (average max softmax output across epochs) predicts Cohen's κ because higher certainty corresponds to clear sleep stages where humans also agree; lower confidence marks ambiguous epochs (e.g., during arousals) prone to disagreement. This is supported by a linear mixed-effects model where confidence was the sole significant predictor (*p*<0.001). Third, **confidence-thresholded REM optimization**: low-confidence REM epochs often represent false positives during wake/arousal transitions. Filtering them via a threshold (e.g., 0.8) discards these errors, lifting REM precision from 85% to 95.5% while preserving >5 minutes of REM in 95% of subjects—a clinically viable trade-off.

## Foundational Learning
- **Domain adaptation & transfer learning**: Needed to bridge the large distribution shift from healthy (PUB) to neurodegenerative sleep (PD/iRBD) without discarding general features learned from thousands of PSGs. *Quick check:* Why is joint fine-tuning on multiple sites preferable to separate site-specific models for generalization to a new center? (Answer: It exposes the model to multi-scorer variability, reducing overfit to local quirks and yielding a single model applicable across sites.)
- **Sleep staging standards (AASM) and neurodegenerative deviations**: U-Sleep is trained on AASM rules, but PD/iRBD exhibit EEG slowing, reduced spindles/K-complexes, and ambiguous REM features, making strict human scoring inconsistent. The model implicitly learns to handle these deviations. *Quick check:* How might the model's tendency to produce longer REM and N2 periods reflect adaptation to AASM rules amid ambiguous features? (Answer: It suggests "backward scoring" and reduced sensitivity to short epoch transitions, mirroring human inconsistency.)
- **Confidence calibration in neural networks**: U-Sleep's per-epoch confidence (max softmax) is used to predict reliability and filter REM. This is a heuristic, not a rigorous uncertainty measure, and its validity hinges on calibration to true error probability. *Quick check:* Why do arousal-affected epochs show significantly lower confidence across all stages? (Answer: Arousals create mixed-frequency EEG and artifacts that blur stage boundaries, increasing model uncertainty.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Preprocessing pipeline (resample to 128 Hz, scale/clip, high-pass filter at 0.1 Hz, reject records with <3h sleep, >20% unscored, or >10% flatline) → U-Sleep core (12-layer encoder-decoder fully convolutional network: encoder uses 1D conv + batch norm + max pooling; decoder up-samples with skip connections; segment classifier averages over 30-s epochs and applies dense layer with softmax over 5 stages) → Inference (aggregate predictions across all EEG+EOG channel pairs, majority-vote epoch stage, compute mean max(softmax) as confidence).

**Critical path:** 1) Load preprocessed PSG with at least one EEG and one EOG channel. 2) During training, randomly sample channel pairs per minibatch; at inference, use all pairs. 3) Forward pass through encoder → decoder → segment classifier. 4) Output per-epoch stage = argmax(summed class logits); confidence = mean max(softmax) across epochs. 5) For REM optimization, apply confidence threshold to REM class probabilities.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- Multi-site pretraining vs. site-specific fine-tuning: Pretraining provides generic features but suffers domain shift; joint fine-tuning yields a generalizable model without per-site retraining, though it may miss rare local pathologies.
- Channel selection: Using 2 channels (EEG+EOG) keeps complexity low; semi-random pairing during training forces channel-agnostic learning, enhancing robustness to montage variations.
- Epoch resolution vs. rule compliance: Sub-epoch predictions support "backward scoring" but may not perfectly align with AASM epoch-boundary rules (e.g., K-complex placement).

**Failure signatures:**
- Global low confidence (<0.7 mean) indicates poor signal quality or extremely fragmented sleep.
- Stage-specific low confidence, especially in N1, reflects inherent human scoring difficulty; expect higher N1 errors.
- Demographic/clinical correlates: higher age and apnea-hypopnea index predict lower κ.
- Longer REM periods than human scoring; if precise REM boundaries are needed (e.g., for RSWA quantification), confidence thresholding is essential.
- Performance drop >0.1 κ on a new site suggests distribution shift (e.g., different EEG montage or scoring nuances).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Reproduce the pretrained model: Run inference on a few PUB PSGs to verify expected κ ~0.81 on non-neurodegenerative data.
2. Fine-tune on PACE+CBC: Perform 10-fold subject-level cross-validation as in the paper; confirm κ improves from ~0.66 (no fine-tuning) to ~0.74 (fine-tuned) on in-distribution data.
3. Test on DCSM hold-out: Load the provided generalized model checkpoint, run on DCSM PSGs, compute per-night κ and REM precision/recall. Then apply a confidence threshold (e.g., 0.8) and verify precision >95% with >5 min REM retained in >90% of subjects.

## Limitations
- Generalization validated only on three centers (two training, one hold-out); performance on vastly different hardware, montages, or non-AASM scoring conventions is unknown.
- Fine-tuning benefits may not be uniform across PD and iRBD subgroups; disaggregated performance by diagnosis is not reported.
- Model confidence calibration is not rigorously tested against human disagreement; miscalibration could undermine threshold reliability, especially in severe pathology.
- The >5 minute REM retention criterion is pragmatic but not linked to specific clinical endpoints; impact of thresholding on downstream RSWA analysis is unstudied.

## Confidence
- Fine-tuning efficacy: High (repeated significant κ improvements on multiple datasets)
- Confidence as predictor of κ: Medium (strong association shown, but calibration to true uncertainty not validated)
- Confidence-thresholded REM optimization: Medium-High (empirical precision/recall trade-off demonstrated, but clinical actionability unestablished)

## Next Checks
1. External site validation: Deploy the public model on a completely independent PSG dataset from a fourth center (different hardware/scoring lab) to measure κ drop and identify site-specific failure modes.
2. Disaggregated performance analysis: Re-analyze DCSM hold-out results separately for PD, iRBD, and control groups to test for differences in κ gain and confidence-κ correlation across diagnostic groups.
3. Confidence vs. human uncertainty calibration: On a subset of DCSM data with double human scoring, compare model confidence distributions for epochs with human agreement vs. disagreement to assess calibration.
