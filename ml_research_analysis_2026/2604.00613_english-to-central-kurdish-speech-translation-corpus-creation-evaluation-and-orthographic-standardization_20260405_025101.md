# English to Central Kurdish Speech Translation: Corpus Creation, Evaluation, and Orthographic Standardization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00613
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00613
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Orthographic standardization of the 170‑hour KUTED corpus improves fine‑tuned Seamless model BLEU from 11.34 to 15.18 on the in‑domain test set; the cascaded system achieves 15.57 BLEU; out‑of‑domain FLEURS gains 3.0 BLEU.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles data scarcity and orthographic variation in Central Kurdish speech‑to‑text translation. The authors construct KUTED, a 170‑hour parallel corpus from TED/TEDx talks paired with human‑produced Central Kurdish translations. They introduce a three‑step orthographic standardization pipeline (Unicode/punctuation normalization, a general correction table, and a corpus‑specific correction table), which halves the target vocabulary. Fine‑tuning the pretrained Seamless model on the standardized data yields a 15.18 BLEU score on the KUTED test set and a 3‑point gain on the out‑of‑domain FLEURS benchmark. A cascaded system (Seamless ASR + NLLB MT) slightly outperforms the end‑to‑end model (15.57 BLEU). The work demonstrates that orthographic consistency is critical for low‑resource speech translation.

## Method Summary
KUTED is built by extracting English audio and Central Kurdish translations from TED talks, aligning them at the sub‑sentence level using caption timestamps, and filtering misaligned segments via a Seamless ASR model with a normalized Levenshtein distance threshold (D > 0.3). The standardization pipeline applies: N1 – AsoSoft Unicode/punctuation/number normalization; N2 – a general correction table derived from a large Kurdish corpus (19,700 pairs); N3 – a corpus‑specific correction table based on KUTED’s token list (11,860 pairs). For modeling, the authors fine‑tune Seamless v2 Large end‑to‑end on the standardized data; alternatively, they finetune Seamless for ASR and NLLB for MT in a cascade. A Fairseq Transformer trained from scratch serves as a lower bound. Evaluation uses BLEU on KUTED test and FLEURS, with WER for the ASR component and human evaluation for quality.

## Key Results
- Standardization increased fine‑tuned Seamless BLEU from 11.34 (with N1 only) to 15.18 on the KUTED test set.
- The cascaded system (Seamless ASR + NLLB MT) achieved 15.57 BLEU, marginally surpassing the end‑to‑end model.
- On the out‑of‑domain FLEURS benchmark, fine‑tuning improved BLEU from 9.36 to 12.50, a gain of ~3.0 points.
- Human evaluation of the KUTED translations yields an average rating of >4/5, confirming high linguistic quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Orthographic standardization of Central Kurdish text substantially improves speech‑to‑text translation (S2TT) performance by reducing token sparsity and inconsistency.
- **Mechanism:** The pipeline applies Unicode/punctuation/number normalization (N1), then uses a general correction table from a large Kurdish corpus (N2), and finally a corpus‑specific correction table derived from KUTED's token list (N3). Each step collapses multiple surface forms into standardized variants, shrinking the target vocabulary and making patterns more learnable.
- **Core assumption:** The model's ability to map acoustic features to correct Kurdish strings is hindered when semantically equivalent expressions are spelled differently; standardization provides consistent training signals.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Standardization increased the fine‑tuned Seamless model's BLEU from 11.34 to 15.18"
  - [section] Section 3.7 details the three‑step standardization and Table 5 shows unique tokens halved from 235,674 to 118,643.
  - [corpus] Table 5 provides quantitative evidence of token consolidation after each normalization step.
- **Break condition:** If standardization rules incorrectly merge distinct lexical items or fail to cover all variant forms present in the corpus, the model may learn incorrect mappings or suffer from over‑normalization, harming performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Creating a domain‑specific, high‑quality speech‑translation corpus (KUTED) mitigates data scarcity for Central Kurdish S2TT by providing aligned English audio with human‑translated Kurdish text.
- **Mechanism:** Leveraging an existing community of Kurdish TED translators yields linguistically valid translations. Rigorous audio‑text alignment (sub‑sentence and sentence‑level) and ASR‑based misalignment detection ensure the integrity of the audio‑transcript pairs, making the corpus suitable for supervised training.
- **Core assumption:** Human‑translated, domain‑matched (TED talks) parallel data is more effective for S2TT than pseudo‑labeled or out‑of‑domain data, even at moderate scale (170 hours).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "created KUTED, a 170‑hour corpus... fine‑tuning... yielded a 15.18 BLEU score"
  - [section] Section 3 describes the community workflow, alignment steps, and ASR‑based filtering (Table 4).
  - [corpus] Table 2 specifies corpus size (91k pairs, 170h) and human evaluation scores (Table 3, avg >4/5).
- **Break condition:** If the audio segmentation or translation quality degrades significantly beyond the sampled 0.7% misalignments, or if the domain (TED) is too narrow, generalization to other speech (e.g., FLEURS) will remain limited without further data augmentation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Fine‑tuning a large, multilingual, pretrained speech‑translation model (Seamless) on a small, standardized in‑domain corpus effectively adapts it to Central Kurdish S2TT.
- **Mechanism:** The Seamless model's W2V‑BERT 2.0 speech encoder and NLLB‑based decoder provide strong multilingual representations. End‑to‑end fine‑tuning on KUTED adjusts these representations to the specific acoustic‑prosodic patterns of TED talks and the standardized Kurdish orthography, requiring far less data than training from scratch.
- **Core assumption:** The pretrained Seamless model contains transferable knowledge for Central Kurdish (at least via its NLLB decoder), and the KUTED corpus is large enough to steer this knowledge toward the target task.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Fine‑tuning the Seamless model on standardized KUTED data yielded a 15.18 BLEU score"
  - [section] Section 4.1 details Seamless architecture; Section 6.1 shows baseline (5.04 BLEU) vs. fine‑tuned (13.51 BLEU with N2).
  - [corpus] The corpus provides the in‑domain training data; however, direct evidence that Seamless's pretraining included Central Kurdish is not stated (Assumption: it is covered in NLLB's 200‑language set).
- **Break condition:** If Central Kurdish was absent from the pretrained modules' language coverage, or if the 170‑hour corpus is below the threshold for effective adaptation, fine‑tuning may yield minimal gains (as seen in the small baseline improvement from N1 to N3 in Table 9).

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Orthographic variation in low‑resource languages**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper identifies non‑standard spelling (joined/separate words, loanword variants, allomorphs) as a primary cause of poor Kurdish MT/S2TT performance. Understanding this variation is key to appreciating why the standardized pipeline is necessary.
  - **Quick check question:** How might writing the verb "use" as one word vs. three separate words impact the vocabulary size and model training for Kurdish?

- **Concept: Cascaded vs. End‑to‑End S2TT architectures**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper evaluates both approaches (Seamless E2E vs. Seamless ASR + NLLB MT). Knowing the trade‑offs—error propagation in cascades vs. data efficiency in E2E—is essential to interpret the results (e.g., cascade achieving 15.57 BLEU vs. E2E 15.18).
  - **Quick check question:** What are the potential failure modes unique to a cascaded S2TT system that an E2E system might avoid?

- **Concept: Tokenization and evaluation metrics (BLEU) for morphologically rich languages**
  - **Why needed here:** Kurdish is agglutinative, leading to fewer but longer tokens than English. BLEU, which operates on tokens, may not fully capture semantic equivalence if standardization changes token boundaries. The paper's token count analysis (Section 3.4) and standardization impact (Table 9) hinge on this.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might a lower token count in Kurdish not imply less information content, and how could this affect BLEU comparisons with English?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - **Data layer:** Raw TED/TEDx audio + CaptionHub subtitles → alignment pipeline → KUTED segments (audio + EN transcript + CKB translation).
  - **Preprocessing layer:** AsoSoft normalizer → general correction table → KUTED‑specific correction table → standardized text.
  - **Model layer (E2E):** W2V‑BERT 2.0 speech encoder → length adapter → NLLB‑based decoder (Seamless). Fine‑tuned end‑to‑end on KUTED.
  - **Model layer (Cascade):** Fine‑tuned Seamless (ASR only) → transcripts → fine‑tuned NLLB 1.3B (MT).
  - **Model layer (Scratch):** Fairseq Transformer (speech encoder + text decoder) trained only on KUTED.
  - **Evaluation layer:** BLEU on KUTED test set (in‑domain) and FLEURS (out‑of‑domain); WER for ASR component.

- **Critical path:**
  1. **Collect & align:** Obtain TED talks, extract audio, manual Kurdish translations, perform multi‑level alignment.
  2. **Filter misalignments:** Run ASR (Seamless baseline) on all segments, compute normalized Levenshtein distance, filter D > 0.3.
  3. **Standardize text:** Apply N1 (normalizer), N2 (general corrections), N3 (corpus‑specific corrections).
  4. **Train models:** Fine‑tune pretrained Seamless (E2E) or NLLB (MT) on standardized KUTED train split; or train Fairseq from scratch.
  5. **Evaluate:** Test on held‑out TED talks (KUTED test) and FLEURS.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Standardization depth (N1 vs. N2 vs. N3):** Deeper standardization (N3) yields larger gains for trained models (Table 9) but may overfit to corpus‑specific choices; N1/N2 are more generalizable.
  - **E2E vs. Cascade:** Cascade uses separate ASR and MT components, allowing independent optimization and leveraging stronger MT (NLLB). E2E is a single model but may suffer from limited speech‑text data. Here, cascade slightly outperformed E2E (15.57 vs. 15.18 BLEU).
  - **Pretrained vs. Scratch:** Using Seamless/NLLB yields strong results with 170h; training from scratch (Fairseq) gives only 7.90 BLEU, indicating KUTED alone is insufficient without transfer learning.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Low BLEU on in‑domain test (<10):** Could indicate poor audio‑transcript alignment, insufficient standardization, or model architecture mismatch.
  - **Large gap between in‑domain (KUTED) and out‑of‑domain (FLEURS) scores:** Suggests overfitting to TED domain or that orthographic standardization didn't cover FLEURS variants.
  - **High WER in cascade ASR (>20):** Points to audio quality issues or mismatch between ASR training data and test acoustics.
  - **Minimal improvement from N1→N3 for baseline (non‑fine‑tuned) Seamless (Table 9):** Expected, as baseline wasn't trained on Kurdish; if fine‑tuned model also shows minimal gain, standardization pipeline may be flawed or not applied consistently.

- **first 3 experiments:**
  - Validate the audio‑text alignment pipeline by manually inspecting a sample of aligned segments to ensure timestamps and transcriptions match the audio content.
  - Quantify token reduction after each normalization step (N1, N2, N3) on a held‑out portion of the corpus to replicate the reported halving of the target vocabulary.
  - Evaluate the pretrained Seamless ASR model on a small subset of KUTED to establish baseline WER and confirm that the normalized Levenshtein distance threshold (D > 0.3) effectively filters misaligned segments.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open research questions. However, its Limitations section highlights challenges that suggest future directions, such as: making the KUTED corpus and correction tables publicly available; improving the cross‑domain generalization of orthographic standardization; and clarifying whether Central Kurdish is covered in the pretraining of the Seamless model.

## Limitations
- Data accessibility: KUTED corpus and correction tables (N2, N3) are not publicly released, hindering exact replication and requiring access to restricted CaptionHub translations.
- Generalization of standardization: Corpus‑specific corrections (N3) may overfit to the TED domain; the modest out‑of‑domain gain (3 BLEU) indicates limited transfer.
- Pretraining coverage: Unclear whether Seamless pretraining included Central Kurdish; if absent, fine‑tuning gains might reflect orthographic adaptation rather than linguistic transfer.
