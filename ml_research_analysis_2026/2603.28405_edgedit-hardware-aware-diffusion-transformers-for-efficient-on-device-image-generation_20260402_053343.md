# EdgeDiT: Hardware-Aware Diffusion Transformers for Efficient On-Device Image Generation

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28405  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28405  
- **Reference Count:** 14  
- **Primary Result:** 20-30% fewer parameters, 36-46% lower FLOPs, 1.65× latency reduction on Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra, and lower FID (12.3 vs 16.23) on ImageNet 256×256 compared to DiT-XL/2.

## Executive Summary

EdgeDiT tackles the challenge of deploying powerful Diffusion Transformers, which are computationally intensive, on resource-constrained edge devices. The authors present a hardware-aware optimization framework that prunes redundant DiT blocks and replaces them with lightweight surrogate modules. These surrogates are trained via feature-wise knowledge distillation to mimic the teacher model's intermediate features, and a multi-objective Bayesian optimizer selects architectures that balance generation quality and on-device latency.

The method yields models with 20-30% fewer parameters and 36-46% less compute than DiT-XL/2 while reducing on-device inference time by factors of 1.65 (Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra) and 1.45 (iPhone 16 Pro Max). Surprisingly, the optimized models also achieve better sample quality, attaining an FID of 12.3 on ImageNet 256×256 versus 16.23 for the original DiT-XL/2, thereby establishing a superior Pareto front between efficiency and quality.

## Method Summary

EdgeDiT's pipeline consists of three stages. First, a search space is defined where each DiT block in the backbone may be replaced by one of several lightweight surrogate designs (e.g., identity, linear projection, or a shallow transformer) or kept unchanged. Second, for each surrogate design, a feature-wise knowledge distillation process trains it to match the intermediate feature maps of the corresponding teacher block, minimizing feature reconstruction errors across multiple layers. Third, full candidate architectures are assembled by selecting a surrogate for each stage, their quality estimated by a predictor trained on a small set of fully fine-tuned models, and their latency measured on the target hardware. A multi-objective Bayesian optimization loop then navigates this discrete space to propose new architectures that improve the quality–latency trade-off, with the best candidates undergoing end-to-end fine-tuning before deployment.

## Key Results

- Parameter count reduced by 20-30% and FLOPs by 36-46% relative to DiT-XL/2.
- On-device inference latency improved by 1.65× on Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra and 1.45× on iPhone 16 Pro Max.
- Achieved lower Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of 12.3 vs. 16.23 for DiT-XL/2 on ImageNet 256×256.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

EdgeDiT achieves efficiency without sacrificing quality by (1) removing structural redundancies through learned surrogate blocks, and (2) directly optimizing for hardware-specific latency alongside generative quality. The feature-wise knowledge distillation ensures that the surrogates preserve the teacher's intermediate representations, which is critical for maintaining diffusion dynamics. Meanwhile, the multi-objective Bayesian optimizer efficiently explores the massive combinatorial space of block replacements, finding architectures that lie on the Pareto frontier. The decoupling of full training from the search—using a quality predictor trained on a small subset—keeps the overall search cost manageable, while the final end-to-end fine-tuning of the selected architecture recovers any minor quality losses.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Surrogate Block Definition → Feature-wise Distillation Training → Architecture Assembly → Latency Measurement & FID Prediction → Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization → Selected Architecture → End-to-End Fine-tuning → Deployment

**Critical path**  
1. Define surrogate block search space (types of replacements).  
2. Train surrogate blocks with FwKD to match teacher features.  
3. For each candidate architecture (a combination of surrogates per stage), compute approximated quality via predictor and measure actual hardware latency.  
4. Feed these two objectives into MOBO to generate new candidates.  
5. Iterate steps 3–4 until convergence or budget exhausted.  
6. Fully train the top candidate(s) end-to-end.  
7. Deploy the best model on the target device.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Quality vs. Latency**: Removing more blocks greatly reduces latency but risks quality degradation; the Pareto frontier balances these.  
- **Search Space Granularity**: Finer-grained (per-block) replacement offers better optimization but increases combinatorial complexity and search time.  
- **Surrogate Complexity**: Simpler surrogates (e.g., identity) are more efficient but may lose more information; larger surrogates preserve quality at higher cost.  
- **Predictor Accuracy vs. Training Cost**: A more accurate FID predictor requires more fully-trained models, raising upfront compute; a less accurate one may mislead the optimizer.  
- **Hardware Specificity**: Models tuned for one NPU may not transfer to another due to different microarchitectural properties.

**Failure signatures**  
- Candidate FID exceeding a threshold (e.g., > 20 on ImageNet 256×256) indicates quality collapse; search should penalize.  
- Latency not decreasing despite block removal might reveal memory bandwidth bottlenecks or overhead from irregular memory access patterns.  
- MOBO fails to improve the Pareto front after several iterations, suggesting stagnation.  
- Surrogate training diverges or yields NaNs, indicating unstable feature alignment.  
- End-to-end fine-tuning of selected architectures does not recover expected quality, pointing to predictor inaccuracies.

**First experiments**  
1. **Baseline Establishment**: Run DiT-XL/2 on the target device (Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra) to measure reference latency and compute FID on a held-out set.  
2. **Single Surrogate Validation**: Train and evaluate a candidate where two consecutive DiT blocks are replaced by identity surrogates in one stage; verify that FwKD yields comparable features and that the resulting model's FID remains close to baseline.  
3. **Mini-MOBO Exploration**: Execute a small Bayesian optimization loop with 10 candidates and 2 MOBO iterations to observe convergence behavior, estimate search cost, and validate the latency/FID predictor's accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does EdgeDiT's hardware-aware optimization framework generalize to other diffusion transformer architectures beyond DiT-XL/2, such as SiT or MDT?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The Limitations section states: "extending it to other diffusion transformer families such as SiT[7] or MDT[3] remains future work."
- Why unresolved: The method was validated only using DiT-XL/2 as the teacher model; architectural differences in SiT/MDT could affect surrogate efficacy and Pareto trade-offs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply the same surrogate training and MOBO search pipeline to SiT/MTD backbones, evaluate FID vs. latency on edge hardware, and compare resulting Pareto frontiers against native optimized variants.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can EdgeDiT's search space be specialized for hardware accelerators beyond mobile NPUs (e.g., TPUs, AR/VR devices) to improve deployment efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Limitations note: "the search space can be further specialized for different hardware accelerators such as TPUs and other edge devices such as AR/VR/XR."
- Why unresolved: The current surrogate design and latency measurements are tailored to Qualcomm Hexagon and Apple ANE; different accelerator architectures (e.g., systolic arrays, memory hierarchies) may favor alternative structural reductions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Re-define surrogate blocks and cost metrics (e.g., memory bandwidth, tensor core utilization) for target hardware, re-run the MOBO search, and benchmark on actual devices to quantify latency gains.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would a larger training budget for end-to-end fine-tuning of more candidate architectures reveal a superior Pareto front than currently discovered?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Limitations state: "due to the high cost of diffusion training, only a subset of candidate architectures is trained end-to-end. Larger compute budgets could enable broader exploration of the architecture space."
- Why unresolved: The MOBO search relies on surrogate-predicted FID; some promising architectures may have been dropped without full training, possibly missing better quality-efficiency trade-offs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Increase the number of candidates fully trained from the search space (e.g., 50+ instead of 2), reconstruct the empirical Pareto front from actual FID/latency, and compare with the current MOBO-selected set.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can the feature-wise knowledge distillation (FwKD) process be enhanced to support more aggressive structural pruning (e.g., removing >2 consecutive blocks) without quality degradation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Sensitivity analysis (Fig 3) showed image quality drops when replacing 3 DiT blocks with a removal surrogate, limiting Stage 1 search space to 2^14 instead of 3^14.
- Why unresolved: The current FwKD may not fully transfer higher-order representations across larger architectural gaps; improved distillation objectives or multi-stage alignment could preserve performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Design enhanced FwKD losses (e.g., attention map matching, intermediate feature regularization), train surrogates for 3+ block removal, and evaluate FID change in assembled candidates against the 2-block limit.

## Limitations

- **Evaluation confined to ImageNet 256×256**; unknown performance on other datasets or resolutions.
- **Hardware experiments limited to two flagship devices** (Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra, iPhone 16 Pro Max); generalizability to other NPUs or mid-range hardware not tested.
- **No comparisons with recent efficient diffusion models** (e.g., LCM, Turbo-Diffusion, compact DiT variants), limiting the strength of the claimed Pareto optimality.
