# SEED-SET: Scalable Evolving Experimental Design for System-level Ethical Testing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01630
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01630
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SEED-SET generates up to 2× more optimal test candidates than baselines and achieves 1.25× improvement in search space coverage.

## Executive Summary
SEED-SET introduces a Bayesian experimental design framework to automate ethical benchmarking of autonomous systems. It separates objective system metrics (e.g., cost, resilience) from subjective ethical preferences using hierarchical variational Gaussian processes. A novel acquisition function balances exploration of uncertain objective regions with exploitation of learned preferences, while LLMs serve as proxy evaluators for pairwise preference elicitation. Validated on power grid and fire rescue tasks, the method significantly outperforms baselines in candidate optimality and coverage.

## Method Summary
The method employs a two-layer hierarchical model: an objective GP maps scenario parameters (x) to performance metrics (y), and a subjective GP maps these metrics to a latent utility (z) inferred from pairwise preferences. An acquisition function V(x) combines mutual information in objective space (MI₁), mutual information in subjective space (MI₂), and expected subjective value. Starting with an initial dataset, the loop proposes candidate scenario pairs that maximize V(x), simulates to obtain y, queries an LLM for pairwise preferences over the outcomes, updates both GPs, and repeats.

## Key Results
- Up to 2× more optimal test candidates generated compared to baselines (Single GP, VS-AL).
- 1.25× improvement in coverage of high-dimensional search spaces.
- Robustness to LLM prompt variations and temperature settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SEED-SET works because its acquisition function explicitly balances two complementary goals: (1) reducing uncertainty in the objective GP (exploration of poorly understood regions of the design space) and (2) reducing uncertainty in the subjective GP while maximizing expected utility (exploitation of preferred regions). By modeling objectives and preferences separately, it avoids conflating measurable outcomes with stakeholder values. The hierarchical structure allows the method to adapt to complex, high-dimensional trade-offs where ethical preferences are derived from multiple conflicting metrics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bayesian experimental design**: Core paradigm for efficient data collection; needed to formalize the trade-off between exploration and exploitation. Quick check: Can derive acquisition functions from expected information gain.
- **Gaussian processes (GPs)**: Non-parametric models for continuous functions; needed to model smooth mappings from x→y and y→z. Quick check: GP posterior predictive distribution gives mean and variance.
- **Variational inference for GPs**: Enables scalability; needed to approximate intractable posteriors with inducing points. Quick check: ELBO optimization with variational distributions.
- **Mutual information (MI)**: Quantifies expected reduction in uncertainty; needed for acquisition components MI₁ and MI₂. Quick check: MI between data and model parameters.
- **Pairwise preference learning**: Probabilistic model (e.g., Bradley-Terry) for ranking; needed to infer latent utility from LLM comparisons. Quick check: Probit likelihood for binary comparisons.
- **LLM-as-judge**: Proxy for human ethical judgment; needed to scalably elicit preferences. Quick check: Prompt engineering for consistent binary responses.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**
Random Initialization -> ObjectiveGP & SubjectiveGP -> Acquisition Optimizer -> Scenario Simulator -> LLM Preference Query -> GP Updates -> Repeat

**Critical path**
1. Initialize with random (x,y) pairs and pairwise preferences.
2. Optimize acquisition V(x) to propose candidate scenario pairs.
3. Simulate scenarios to obtain objective metrics y.
4. Query LLM for pairwise preference over the two y outcomes.
5. Update ObjectiveGP with new (x,y) and SubjectiveGP with new (y, preference).
6. Return to step 2 until budget exhausted.

**Design tradeoffs**
- Hierarchical modeling adds complexity but cleanly separates objective and subjective layers.
- Variational GPs trade exact inference for scalability; inducing point count affects accuracy.
- LLM proxy reduces human cost but introduces alignment risk and prompt sensitivity.
- Acquisition function balances competing objectives (exploration vs exploitation) via weighted MI terms.

**Failure signatures**
- Acquisition stagnates: posterior variances vanish → candidate diversity drops.
- LLM inconsistency: repeated queries yield different preferences >10% of time.
- Simulator mismatch: reported trends (e.g., high spread factor preferred) not observed in collected y.
- Coverage score low: search space remains under-explored in high dimensions.

**First three experiments**
1. Sanity check GP posteriors: after 10 initial points, plot mean/variance of ObjectiveGP; ensure variance non-zero everywhere.
2. Validate LLM prompt: send 10 identical pairwise comparison queries at temperature=0; expect 100% consistency.
3. Test acquisition behavior: run 5 iterations with random candidates; verify V(x) values differ and selection is not trivial.

## Limitations
- Missing simulation implementation details (Webots asset models, exact damage formulas).
- Undisclosed GP hyperparameters (kernel lengths, noise, inducing points) hinder exact replication.
- LLM prompt design and parsing specifics not fully specified, causing potential variance.
- Validation limited to two synthetic tasks; real-world generalizability untested.
- Handcrafted preference score h(y) may not capture true stakeholder utility.
- LLM-as-proxy alignment with human ethical judgments remains unverified.

## Confidence
- **Effectiveness (2× optimal, 1.25× coverage)**: Medium (depends on unreported hyperparameters and simulation fidelity).
- **LLM robustness**: Medium (limited prompt variation testing; no full sensitivity analysis).
- **Generalization**: Low (only two synthetic domains; real-world complexity unexplored).

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep**: Vary inducing points (50-200), learning rates (0.001-0.05), and kernel options; measure stability of 2× improvement claim.
2. **Cross-simulator replication**: Reimplement fire rescue task in Gazebo using paper's parameters; verify coverage and optimality gains.
3. **Human-LLM alignment study**: Collect expert pairwise preferences on same scenarios; compare SEED-SET selections using LLM proxy vs. human preferences.
