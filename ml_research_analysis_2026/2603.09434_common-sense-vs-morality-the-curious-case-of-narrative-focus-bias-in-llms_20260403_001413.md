# Common Sense vs. Morality: The Curious Case of Narrative Focus Bias in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09434
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09434
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** LLMs show a pervasive "narrative focus bias," detecting commonsense contradictions more readily in statements by secondary characters than by the primary narrator, and perform poorly on implicit contradiction detection without explicit prompting.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CoMoral, a benchmark of 802 scenarios embedding commonsense contradictions within moral dilemmas. Evaluating ten instruction-tuned LLMs (0.5B–8B parameters), the authors find that models perform poorly at identifying contradictions without explicit instruction to check for them (e.g., LLaMA 8B: 0.261 implicit vs. 0.845 explicit accuracy). A consistent "narrative focus bias" is observed: models are significantly more accurate at detecting contradictions when they are attributed to a secondary character rather than the primary narrator. This suggests that alignment training may override basic commonsense reasoning and that models treat narrator statements as authoritative, applying less scrutiny. The findings highlight a tension between moral alignment and robust commonsense understanding.

## Method Summary
The study uses the CoMoral dataset, generated by prompting LLaMA 70B with 88 seed commonsense contradictions to create moral dilemma scenarios. Each contradiction is embedded in two narrative frames: one where the narrator makes the contradictory claim (primary) and one where a secondary character does (secondary). Ten smaller instruction-tuned models are evaluated on all 802 scenarios under two conditions: an implicit prompt that asks for a moral decision without mentioning contradictions, and an explicit prompt that instructs the model to point out any logical inconsistencies. Model responses are scored by an LLM-as-a-judge (GPT-OSS-120B) against the ground-truth contradiction. Accuracy is computed overall and separately for primary/secondary character types and contradiction categories.

## Key Results
- LLMs exhibit a large performance gap between implicit and explicit prompting; accuracy improves substantially (up to 60 percentage points for LLaMA 8B) when explicitly instructed to check for contradictions.
- A robust "narrative focus bias" is observed across all models: detection accuracy is consistently higher for contradictions involving secondary characters than for those involving the primary narrator.
- Even with explicit prompting, certain contradiction types (Temporal, Unreal) remain difficult, and the primary-secondary gap persists, indicating a deeper bias in how models process narrative roles.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Alignment Override
- **Claim:** LLMs prioritize moral framing over commonsense due to alignment training that suppresses challenges to the narrator.
- **Mechanism:** Post-training objectives (e.g., RLHF, SFT) emphasize safety and social acceptability, causing models to avoid questioning the narrator's statements as potentially unsafe or impolite, thereby overriding basic contradiction detection.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes "tendency to prioritize moral reasoning"; Section 5.1 links poor implicit performance to alignment guardrails overriding commonsense.

### Mechanism 2: Narrative Authority Bias
- **Claim:** The bias arises from pretraining data where narrator text is typically coherent and contradiction-free.
- **Mechanism:** Models learn from statistical patterns that treat narrators as authoritative sources, applying less scrutiny to their statements compared to those of secondary characters.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes "narrative focus bias"; Section 5.2 argues models treat narrator statements as factual due to training data distribution.

### Mechanism 3: Explicit Prompt Activation
- **Claim:** Explicit prompting triggers a self-consistency checking mode not spontaneously engaged.
- **Mechanism:** The instruction to find contradictions accesses stored contradiction-detection knowledge; without it, models focus on surface-level question-answering and miss inconsistencies.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes marked improvement with explicit prompts; Figure 6 shows consistent implicit/explicit gaps.

## Foundational Learning
- **Commonsense Reasoning:** Recognizing widely shared factual knowledge (e.g., "new moon has no moonlight") embedded in scenarios. *Quick check:* Is "A kindergarten student has a naturally grown beard" a contradiction? Yes, because kindergartners are young children who do not naturally grow beards.
- **Value Alignment:** Understanding how techniques like RLHF instill safety/ethical objectives. *Quick check:* How could RLHF lead a model to ignore factual inconsistencies? By rewarding outputs that are safe and socially acceptable, the model may learn to avoid challenging user statements or narratives, even when they contain factual errors.
- **Narrative Focus Bias:** The systematic difference in detecting contradictions based on whether they are uttered by the primary narrator or a secondary character. *Quick check:* In CoMoral, what makes a contradiction "primary" vs. "secondary"? Primary: the narrator makes the contradictory claim. Secondary: a character within the story (other than the narrator) makes it.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Data Generation (LLaMA 70B + prompt) → Evaluation Harness (load models, format prompts) → Judge Module (GPT-OSS-120B scoring) → Analysis Pipeline (aggregate accuracy, compare groups)

### Critical Path
1. Download/regenerate CoMoral dataset.
2. Run inference for each target model on implicit and explicit prompts.
3. Evaluate all responses with the LLM-as-a-judge.
4. Compute accuracy metrics (overall, by character type, by contradiction category).

### Design Tradeoffs
- LLM-as-a-judge vs. human evaluation: scalable but may inherit same biases; human evaluation more reliable but costly.
- Synthetic data: cheap and large but risks generator artifacts and limited diversity.
- Model selection: small models (≤8B) used due to compute; findings may not generalize to larger or base models.
- Binary evaluation: simplifies analysis but may mask partial understanding; judge prompt allows some flexibility.

### Failure Signatures
- Near-random accuracy (<0.1) across models: likely dataset generation error or judge malfunction.
- No primary-secondary gap (within 0.05): dataset imbalance or unclear prompt definitions.
- Judge scores correlate with response length: check judge prompt for adherence to "brief explanation."
- High variance across random seeds: insufficient data points; consider increasing sample size.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Data generation sanity check:** Run the generation prompt for one seed contradiction (e.g., "Moonlight during new moon") and verify produced scenarios are coherent and embed the contradiction implicitly.
2. **Pilot evaluation:** Test LLaMA 8B on 5 primary and 5 secondary instances with both prompts; manually inspect responses to confirm expected pattern (implicit low, explicit high; secondary > primary).
3. **Judge consistency:** For 20 responses, run two separate judge evaluations (or different judge model) and compute agreement; low agreement indicates judge instability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Mechanistic causes of narrative focus bias:** Why do models prioritize moral framing and treat narrator statements as authoritative? Is it due to attention mechanisms, training data distribution, or alignment objectives?
2. **Scalability across model scales:** Do the findings (implicit/explicit gap, primary-secondary bias) hold for larger models (>70B), base (non-instruction-tuned) models, or with advanced reasoning (e.g., chain-of-thought)?
3. **Generalization to other tasks:** Does the narrative focus bias appear in other narrative reasoning tasks or ethical dilemmas not specifically constructed with embedded contradictions?
4. **Difficulty by contradiction type:** Why are Temporal and Unreal contradictions consistently harder even under explicit prompting? Is it due to abstraction, counterfactual reasoning, or knowledge gaps?
5. **Mitigation interventions:** Can training or inference-time techniques (e.g., balanced narrative fine-tuning, multi-agent RLHF) reduce the bias without harming aligned capabilities?

## Limitations
- Dataset schema and exact file format are unspecified, hindering scripted loading and filtering.
- Reproducibility parameters (sampling seeds, decoding settings) are undisclosed, risking output variability.
- LLM-as-a-judge may inherit the same narrative bias it is meant to measure.
- Synthetic data generation may introduce artifacts and limited diversity.
- Evaluation restricted to small (0.5B–8B) instruction-tuned models; results may not generalize to frontier or base models.
