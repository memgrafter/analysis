# TAG: Thinking with Action Unit Grounding for Facial Expression Recognition

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18763
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18763
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** TAG (7B) achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy 83.78% (RAF-DB 92.80%, FERPlus 91.50%, AffectNet 67.03%) with AU IoU 60.24.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language models (VLMs) often produce plausible but unverified rationales for facial expression recognition (FER), limiting reliability. TAG (Thinking with Action Unit Grounding) addresses this by anchoring intermediate reasoning steps to facial Action Units (AUs)—physiologically meaningful muscle activations. The authors first construct TAG-310k, a large dataset of AU-grounded reasoning traces derived from RAF-DB, FERPlus, and AffectNet. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning teaches the model to produce structured traces with AU references; reinforcement learning (GRPO) then refines the policy using a reward that combines format compliance, answer correctness, and spatial alignment of predicted AU bounding boxes with external detector outputs. TAG attains SOTA accuracy on the three benchmarks while maintaining high AU IoU, demonstrating both performance and interpretable, verifiable reasoning.

## Method Summary
**Dataset construction:** TAG-310k is built by processing images from the three FER datasets. For each image, the GraphAU detector identifies activated AUs and associates them with landmark-based bounding boxes. The top three AUs by confidence are selected. A VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) is prompted to generate a reasoning trace that references these AUs and predicts the expression label. Iterative label elimination ensures label correctness, and a quality filter discards unsuitable samples, yielding ~318k examples.

**Training pipeline:** A Qwen2.5-VL-7B model is first fine-tuned on TAG-310k using LoRA adapters, learning to output structured traces with `<think>` blocks, AU-boundary pairs (format `<AUx> [x1,y1,x2,y2]`), and a final `<answer>` label. Then, for each target dataset, reinforcement learning via GRPO is applied. The reward function enforces valid output format (`R_fmt`), correct label (`R_ans`), and spatial grounding measured by AU IoU between predicted boxes and GraphAU’s boxes (`R_AU`). Only the SFT policy is used as reference, and KL regularization prevents deviation. At inference, the model generates up to three bounding boxes and an answer without external tools.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy:** Average 83.78% across RAF-DB (92.80%), FERPlus (91.50%), and AffectNet (67.03%), outperforming larger VLMs and specialized FER methods.
- **Grounding quality:** AU IoU reaches 60.24 on RAF-DB, indicating faithful visual grounding.
- **Human preference:** Evaluators prefer TAG’s explanations over baselines in 66% of cases; an LLM-as-judge gives 72% preference.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** AU-grounded reasoning prevents hallucination by forcing intermediate steps to be verifiable.  
**Mechanism:** The model generates structured traces that explicitly reference facial Action Units via bounding boxes. These boxes must spatially align with regions identified by an external AU detector (measured by IoU). This perceptual constraint prevents the model from producing plausible but unverifiable explanations; it must point to real facial features.  
**Core assumption:** Physiologically meaningful AUs provide a stable, interpretable intermediate representation that correlates with expression categories and is robust to dataset shifts.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “explicitly constrains multimodal reasoning to be supported by facial Action Units (AUs)”; Section 3.1 requiring verifiable visual evidence; neighbor literature emphasizes AU-based interpretability.  
**Break condition:** If the external AU detector is unreliable (low accuracy or high false negatives) on the target domain, the grounding signal becomes noisy, potentially leading to misaligned boxes that still achieve high AU-IoU by exploiting detector biases.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Two-stage training (SFT then RL) decouples grounding acquisition from performance optimization.  
**Mechanism:** SFT on TAG-310k instills the basic capability to produce AU-grounded traces in the correct format. RL with GRPO then fine-tunes this policy using a composite reward (format + answer + AU-IoU). This separation prevents RL from completely overriding the grounding behavior learned in SFT, which can happen if RL optimizes only for answer correctness.  
**Core assumption:** The SFT model provides a sufficiently strong prior that AU-grounded reasoning is the optimal policy; RL adjustments are best treated as refinements rather than wholesale relearning.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5.3 shows RL with only format and answer rewards improves accuracy but degrades AU IoU; adding AU reward improves both; Section C.3 shows AU IoU continues increasing during RL even after answer accuracy plateaus.  
**Break condition:** If the SFT stage underfits (e.g., insufficient dataset quality), the RL stage may struggle to recover meaningful grounding because the policy starts too far from the desired structured reasoning manifold, leading to instability or mode collapse.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Composite reward design (format + answer + AU-IoU) mitigates reward hacking while aligning multiple objectives.  
**Mechanism:** `R_fmt` enforces structural compliance; `R_ans` provides the primary task signal; `R_AU` offers a dense, continuous signal for visual grounding by matching predicted boxes to external AU detector outputs. Averaging only the top-min(n,k) IoU prevents reward hacking via over-prediction of boxes.  
**Core assumption:** The external AU detector’s output is sufficiently accurate and unbiased to serve as a proxy for “correct” grounding; the format constraint prevents degenerative strategies like predicting many overlapping boxes.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 defines rewards; Section 5.4 shows IoU-based reward outperforms AU-label-only validation; Section 5.3 ablation confirms removing `R_AU` degrades IoU while preserving accuracy.  
**Break condition:** If the external AU detector suffers from domain shift, the `R_AU` signal may become misaligned, guiding the model toward detector-specific artifacts rather than generalizable facial cues.

## Foundational Learning
- **Facial Action Coding System (FACS):** Why needed: TAG’s grounding relies on AUs as intermediate representation; understanding FACS explains why AUs are chosen (physiologically meaningful, anatomically localized).  
  *Quick check:* Given AU1 (inner brow raiser) and AU4 (brow lowerer), what emotional contrast might their combination signal, and how would that manifest visually?

- **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) / Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Why needed: TAG’s RL stage optimizes a multi-component reward; understanding RLHF principles is key to diagnosing training stability and interpreting per-dataset RL gains.  
  *Quick check:* Why does GRPO use a KL penalty term relative to a reference policy, and what would happen if omitted?

- **Faithfulness vs. Plausibility in Interpretability:** Why needed: TAG distinguishes “ungrounded” (plausible but unverifiable) from “grounded” (verifiable) reasoning; this split underpins reward design and evaluation.  
  *Quick check:* If a model’s explanation perfectly describes a facial feature that is *not* actually present in the image but is commonly associated with the predicted emotion, is that a faithfulness failure or a plausibility success?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Input pipeline: face image → resize (224×224) → visual encoder (frozen) → projector → visual tokens. Generation head: LLM decoder (Qwen2.5-VL-7B base, LoRA-adapted) → autoregressive token generation, including special `<bbox>` tokens with normalized coordinates on a 512×512 canvas. Training modules: SFT dataset (TAG-310k) with structured traces; RL reward function (R_fmt + R_ans + R_AU) using external AU detector (GraphAU); external detector used only during training. Inference: same decoder, no external detector, generates `<think>` with ≤3 `<bbox>` and `<answer>`.

### Critical path
1. Construct TAG-310k: Filter images → detect AUs (GraphAU + landmarks) → select top‑K activated AUs → prompt Qwen2.5‑VL‑32B to generate AU‑grounded traces → iterative label elimination for mismatches → store (image, label, trace, AU boxes).  
2. SFT: Train LoRA adapters on TAG-310k to produce correct format and approximate AU grounding.  
3. RL: For each dataset, sample rollouts from SFT policy → compute reward (R_fmt + R_ans + R_AU using GraphAU boxes) → GRPO update → repeat until convergence.  
4. Inference: Pass image, decode structured output, evaluate accuracy against test labels.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Pseudo-label vs. human annotation:** Using GraphAU outputs scales dataset creation but introduces detector bias; human AU annotations could improve grounding fidelity.  
- **Per-dataset RL vs. unified policy:** Cross‑dataset RL generalizes somewhat but per‑dataset RL yields best performance; trade‑off between engineering cost and marginal accuracy gain.  
- **Bounding box format:** Normalized on virtual 512×512 canvas simplifies token prediction but decouples from actual image resolution; uniform resizing to 224×224 may lose fine details critical for subtle AU detection.

### Failure signatures
- **High answer accuracy + low AU IoU:** Model shortcutting; RL without R_AU causes this.  
- **Excessive boxes (>3):** Model ignoring format reward or exploiting min(n,k) averaging.  
- **Cross‑dataset degradation:** SFT‑only model shows large gaps (e.g., AffectNet 50% vs 67% after per‑dataset RL), indicating need for domain‑specific adaptation.  
- **Reward hacking:** Using AU‑label (F1) instead of spatial IoU degrades grounding, confirming spatial alignment matters.

### First experiments
1. **Zero‑shot baseline evaluation:** Compare Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B, InternVL3‑38B, GPT‑5, etc. on RAF‑DB/FERPlus/AffectNet with unified CoT prompt; establishes generic VLMs are ungrounded and inaccurate.  
2. **SFT ablation with/without `<bbox>`:** Remove bounding box content from traces while keeping structure; shows grounding itself provides ~2% gain beyond structured reasoning.  
3. **RL ablation with/without AU reward:** Add RL with only R_fmt+R_ans vs. full R_fmt+R_ans+R_AU; reveals unconstrained RL degrades grounding and AU reward is necessary to improve both accuracy and IoU.

## Limitations
- Sole reliance on GraphAU for AU boxes introduces detector biases.
- Absence of human AU validation means the AU‑IoU metric may not reflect true physiological correspondence.
- Per‑dataset RL required for optimal performance increases computational cost and limits generality.

## Next Checks

3. **Unified RL experiment:** Train a single RL model on the entire TAG‑310k and evaluate on all three datasets to test whether a unified policy can match per‑dataset RL.
