# CPMobius: Iterative Coach-Player Reasoning for Data-Free Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.02979
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02979
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Without external training data during co-evolution, CPMobius raises overall accuracy by +4.9 points and out-of-distribution accuracy by +5.4 points on Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, exceeding RENT by +1.5 overall and R-zero by +4.2 OOD.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) typically rely on large-scale human-curated data to improve reasoning abilities. CPMobius introduces a collaborative Coach-Player framework that eliminates this dependence during the main training phase. A Coach model generates math problems tailored to the Player’s current skill level, while the Player solves these tasks and learns from verifiable rewards derived from majority voting. The Coach is incentivized based on the Player’s improvement on a fixed validation set, creating a closed-loop co-evolutionary system. Experiments on multiple base models show substantial gains in mathematical reasoning, especially on out-of-distribution benchmarks, demonstrating the effectiveness of data-free reinforcement learning.

## Method Summary
CPMobius trains two models iteratively: a Coach that proposes tasks and a Player that solves them. The Coach first generates candidate problems; each is evaluated by having the Player produce multiple solutions (n=16). The instruction-level accuracy determines difficulty—tasks with 0.2–0.8 accuracy are kept. The Player then updates its policy via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), using pseudo-labels obtained by majority voting over its own solutions to generate binary rewards. After each Player update, the Player’s accuracy on a held-out validation set (AMC) is measured; the improvement Δt is used to compute the Coach’s reward (R_Coach_i = R_Player_i · Δt). The Coach is updated via REINFORCE. The process repeats, with both models co-evolving without external data after an optional warm-up phase for the Coach on a small set of human-curated math problems.

## Key Results
- On Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, CPMobius increases overall accuracy by +4.9 points and out-of-distribution accuracy by +5.4 points.
- It outperforms RENT by +1.5 points overall and R-zero by +4.2 points on OOD benchmarks.
- Similar improvements are observed across other base models, including Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B, and OctoThinker-3B-Hybrid-Zero.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The Coach generates tasks at an optimally challenging difficulty zone (0.2–0.8 Player accuracy) to maximize learning efficiency.
- **Mechanism**: For each candidate task, the Player produces n samples; majority voting yields a pseudo-label, and the instruction-level accuracy acc_i is computed. Tasks with 0.2 ≤ acc_i ≤ 0.8 are kept; others are discarded and resampled.
- **Core assumption**: The Player’s immediate performance on a task is a reliable proxy for its difficulty relative to the Player’s current capability frontier.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract]: “The Coach proposes instructions targeted at the Player’s current capability”
  - [section 4.1]: “retaining only problems whose accuracy scores fall within the pedagogically optimal zone of 0.2≤acc_i≤0.8”
  - [corpus]: Evidence is weak; this mechanism is specific to CPMobius and not reflected in neighboring corpus papers.
- **Break condition**: If the Player’s ability shifts rapidly, the fixed [0.2, 0.8] band may become mismatched, causing either saturation (too many easy tasks) or starvation (too few tasks pass filter).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The Coach is rewarded based on the Player’s *validation-set improvement* (Δt), not just on the Player’s performance on the Coach’s tasks, aligning the Coach’s objective with long-term learning.
- **Mechanism**: After each Player update, accuracy on a fixed validation set D_val is measured. The Coach’s reward for task x_i is R_Coach_i = R_Player_i · Δt, where R_Player_i is the Player’s average reward on x_i and Δt = Acc(πP_ϕt+1; D_val) – Acc(πP_ϕt; D_val).
- **Core assumption**: The Player’s improvement on D_val correlates with genuine skill acquisition and is meaningfully influenced by the Coach’s tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract]: “the Coach receives rewards based on changes in the Player’s performance”
  - [section 4.1]: “R_Coach_i = R_Player_i · Δt … directly incentivizing it to generate instructions that lead to tangible learning progress”
  - [corpus]: Weak; neighboring papers on self-play (e.g., R-Zero) use adversarial rewards, not validation-based improvement.
- **Break condition**: If D_val is unrepresentative or the Player improves due to factors orthogonal to the Coach’s tasks (e.g., random exploration), Δt becomes a noisy or misleading reward signal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The Player learns from *pseudo-labels* generated via majority voting across multiple rollouts, enabling stable policy updates without ground-truth labels.
- **Mechanism**: For each task x_i, the Player produces n solutions {y_i,j}. The majority-voted answer becomes the pseudo-label y_i*. Each solution receives a binary reward r_i,j = 1[y_i,j = y_i*]. GRPO then updates the Player policy using these rewards and group-normalized advantages.
- **Core assumption**: Majority voting over n i.i.d. samples from the same policy yields a consistent and correct pseudo-label for the task, despite the absence of external supervision.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract]: “the Player learns by solving these tasks and receiving verifiable rewards”
  - [section 4.2]: “The Player employs multi-sample reasoning … majority voting over the n answers yields a pseudo-label y_i*”
  - [section 3.2]: RLVR can use self-consistency (majority voting) as an unsupervised reward source.
- **Break condition**: If n is too small or the policy is highly stochastic, majority voting may produce incorrect pseudo-labels, leading to reward hacking or divergence. If tasks have multiple valid solutions, majority voting may penalize correct but minority answers.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**: Why needed: The Player’s training uses binary correctness rewards from rule-based verifiers (or self-consistency) instead of learned reward models. Understanding RLVR clarifies why CPMobius can operate without human feedback. Quick check: How does using a verifiable reward (e.g., `1[y = y*]`) avoid reward hacking compared to a learned reward model?
- **Curriculum Learning & Zone of Proximal Development**: Why needed: The Coach’s difficulty filter (0.2–0.8 accuracy) embodies an adaptive curriculum. This concept explains why tasks should be neither trivial nor impossible. Quick check: Why is a task difficulty band (e.g., 20–80% accuracy) preferable to a fixed difficulty threshold for optimizing learning progress?
- **Policy Optimization (GRPO & REINFORCE)**: Why needed: The Player uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a critic-free RL algorithm; the Coach uses REINFORCE. Understanding these updates is key to grasping how both agents are optimized. Quick check: What is the difference between GRPO’s group-normalized advantage and REINFORCE’s baseline-free update, and why might GRPO be more stable for the Player?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Coach model (πC_θ) → generates candidate tasks → Difficulty filter (0.2–0.8 accuracy) → keeps tasks → Player model (πP_ϕ) → produces n rollouts → Pseudo-label generator (majority vote) → yields binary rewards → GRPO update → Validation set (D_val) → computes Δt → Coach REINFORCE update (R_Coach_i = R_Player_i · Δt)
- **Critical path**:
  1. Coach generates m candidate tasks.
  2. For each candidate, Player rolls out n solutions → compute acc_i → keep if in [0.2,0.8].
  3. For each kept task, Player produces n solutions → majority vote → compute R_Player_i → GRPO update for Player.
  4. Evaluate updated Player on D_val → compute Δt.
  5. For each kept task, compute R_Coach_i = R_Player_i · Δt → REINFORCE update for Coach.
  6. Repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Validation-set dependency: Δt relies on a fixed D_val. If D_val is too narrow or biased, the Coach may overfit to that distribution.
  - Warm-up requirement: The Coach is warmed up on 4K external math problems to generate constructive tasks initially. This uses external data once but enables subsequent data-free co-evolution.
  - Computational cost: Two active models (Coach+Player) double forward passes vs. single-agent self-play. The n-sample rollouts per task further multiply cost.
  - Difficulty band heuristic: The [0.2,0.8] band is a fixed heuristic; it may not be optimal for all models or stages (e.g., a highly proficient Player might need a narrower band).
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Coach collapse: Coach generates tasks with acc_i consistently outside [0.2,0.8] → filter discards most candidates → training stalls.
  - Player degradation: Player’s validation accuracy (Acc(πP_ϕt; D_val)) declines over time → Δt negative → Coach gets penalized for all tasks → curriculum may degrade.
  - Reward hacking: Coach learns to generate tasks that inflate R_Player_i without contributing to Δt (e.g., trivial tasks that Player always solves) → R_Coach_i remains high but Player doesn’t improve on D_val.
  - Length divergence: If Coach’s output length grows indefinitely while Player’s shrinks excessively (beyond normal efficiency gains), it may indicate tasks becoming unnecessarily verbose or Player overfitting to short, incorrect answers.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Task difficulty distribution**: After one training step, log acc_i for all kept tasks. Verify most lie within [0.2,0.8]. If not, adjust n or the band.
  2. **Ablation: no Coach update**: Freeze Coach parameters. Compare Player’s Δt progression vs. full CPMobius. Expect slower/no improvement if adaptive coaching is critical.
  3. **Validation set sensitivity**: Train with different D_val choices (e.g., AMC vs. MATH subset). Compare final OOD performance to assess whether Δt signals generalize or overfit to D_val.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to other domains**: How can the CPMobius framework be extended to complex reasoning domains beyond mathematics, such as code generation or scientific question answering? The paper only evaluates on mathematical reasoning benchmarks; it is unclear whether the same cooperative dynamic translates to other types of reasoning tasks with different structure and evaluation metrics.
2. **Long-term stability**: What are the long-term stability and emergent behaviors of the Coach-Player co-evolution when trained for many iterations, and do they exhibit patterns such as convergence, cycling, or collapse? The training runs in the paper are limited to up to 1000 steps; stability over longer horizons or with different hyperparameters is not explored.
3. **Warm-up initialization**: To what extent does the warm-up initialization of the Coach influence the overall performance of CPMobius, and can a high-quality curriculum be generated from a randomly initialized or weakly supervised Coach without external warm-up data? The warm-up data is still external supervision; the claim of "data-free" applies only after warm-up. It remains open whether a fully data-free initialization of the Coach is possible.
4. **Validation set sensitivity**: How sensitive is CPMobius to the choice of the validation set used for computing the environment feedback signal Δt, and would alternative moderate-difficulty datasets yield similar improvements? The paper uses AMC as the fixed held-out validation set, motivated by its moderate difficulty. No other validation sets are compared, and the robustness to this choice is not analyzed.

## Limitations
- The warm-up of the Coach on 4K external math problems introduces curated data use, partially contradicting the "data-free" claim for the main co-evolution phase.
- The fixed difficulty band [0.2,0.8] is a heuristic not validated across models or training stages; it may become mismatched as the Player's ability shifts, leading to inefficient curricula.
- The validation set (AMC) is fixed and may be narrow; the Coach's reward signal (Δt) could overfit to that distribution, though the paper shows some OOD generalization.

## Confidence
- Overall gains without external training data during co-evolution: **Medium**
- Adaptive difficulty filtering (0.2–0.8 band): **Medium**
- Validation-based Coach reward (Δt alignment): **Medium**
- Pseudo-label learning via majority voting: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Validation set ablation**: Train CPMobius with different validation sets (e.g., MATH-500 subset, Olympiad) and compare OOD performance on AIME, Minerva, etc. to assess generalization of Δt.
2. **Difficulty band sensitivity**: Experiment with alternative accuracy bands (e.g., [0.1,0.9], [0.3,0.7]) and measure final accuracy and training stability.
3. **Warm-up removal**: Initialize the Coach randomly (no warm-up) and run full co-evolution; compare performance to warm-up version to determine necessity of external data for Coach initialization.
