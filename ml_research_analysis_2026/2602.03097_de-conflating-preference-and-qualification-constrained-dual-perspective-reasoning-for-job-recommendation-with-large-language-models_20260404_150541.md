# De-conflating Preference and Qualification: Constrained Dual-Perspective Reasoning for Job Recommendation with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03097
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03097
- **Reference Count:** 38
- **Primary Result:** JobRec improves Preference Recall@5 from 0.3352 to 0.7520 and Qualification Recall@5 from 0.3778 to 0.7670 over LLM+ICL baselines by de-conflating preference and qualification.

## Executive Summary
Job recommendation systems often conflate candidate preference (would the candidate apply?) and employer qualification (is the candidate eligible?) into a single success signal. This conflation, combined with recruitment funnel censoring where negative outcomes are ambiguous, leads to biased models that fail to respect explicit eligibility constraints. The proposed JobRec framework addresses this by de-conflating the two decision dimensions. It uses a Unified Semantic Alignment Schema (USAS) to structure candidate and job attributes into four aligned semantic layers, enabling separate reasoning. A two-stage training process first learns independent preference and qualification experts, then applies a Lagrangian-based policy alignment to optimize ranking under user-specified eligibility thresholds, providing controllable trade-offs without retraining.

## Method Summary
JobRec introduces a two-stage cooperative training paradigm. First, a shared large language model (Llama-3-8B) fine-tuned with LoRA produces two separate scores: `s_pref` (preference) from semantic/constraint layers and `s_qual` (qualification) from capability/semantic layers, guided by a multi-task loss with adaptive weighting. Second, a policy alignment stage freezes the encoder and learns a final ranking score `s_final = s_pref + λ*(s_qual - ε)`, where λ is a Lagrange multiplier optimized via dual ascent to satisfy the qualification constraint `s_qual ≥ ε`. The system relies on a synthetic dataset generated via an expert-in-the-loop process, where USAS guides the creation of structured candidate-job pairs with clean, disentangled labels for both preference and qualification.

## Key Results
- Against LLM+ICL, JobRec improves Preference Recall@5 from 0.3352 to 0.7520 and Qualification Recall@5 from 0.3778 to 0.7670.
- Ablations confirm the necessity of both de-conflation (separate heads) and Stage-II Lagrangian alignment.
- Varying the eligibility threshold `ε` enables controllable trade-offs: qualification ranking improves monotonically while preference performance remains stable.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Conflating preference and qualification into a single success signal leads to biased learning under funnel censoring because negative outcomes are counterfactually ambiguous.
- **Mechanism:** Observed success `Y_succ` is the latent conjunction `Y_pref ∧ Y_qual`. A `Y_succ=0` could mean failure in preference, qualification, or both. Training on `Y_succ` forces the model to attribute failure to a single, often preference-related, cause, causing it to neglect true eligibility boundaries.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes collapse into a single signal yields confounded supervision; [section 3.2] formalizes the conjunction and counterfactual ambiguity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Unified Semantic Alignment Schema (USAS) provides a structured, symmetric representation that separates eligibility-relevant (`Φ_cap`) and interest-relevant (`Φ_sem`) attributes across four layers, enabling disentangled expert learning.
- **Mechanism:** USAS maps candidates and jobs to aligned layers: (I) Fundamental Metadata, (II) Competency & Qualification, (III) Operational Constraint & Commitment, (IV) Deep Semantic & Experience. This explicit separation ensures each expert attends to its relevant attribute subset.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes USAS aligning attributes into structured layers; [section 3.1] defines the four paired layers and their roles.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Lagrangian-based policy alignment optimizes ranking under explicit eligibility constraints, allowing smooth, post-hoc control over the preference-qualification trade-off.
- **Mechanism:** The constrained objective `max s_pref s.t. s_qual ≥ ε` is solved via a saddle-point problem `min_λ≥0 max_θ L`. At inference, `s_final = s_pref + λ*(s_qual - ε)`. The learned dual variable `λ` adapts the penalty, and varying `ε` shifts the feasible region, enabling controllable strictness.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions optimizing under explicit constraints; [section 3.3] derives the Lagrangian; results show monotonic qualification gain with stable preference as `ε` increases.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Recruitment funnel censoring
  - *Why needed:* Explains why negative outcomes are uninformative for disentangling preference vs. qualification, which is the core motivation for de-conflation.
  - *Quick check:* "If a candidate is not hired, what are the possible reasons, and why does this make a binary 'hire' label problematic for training?"
- **Concept:** Constrained optimization via Lagrangian duality
  - *Why needed:* JobRec's Stage II transforms a constrained ranking problem into an unconstrained objective using a Lagrange multiplier; understanding how `λ` and `ε` interact is key to its controllable behavior.
  - *Quick check:* "How does increasing the eligibility threshold `ε` affect the final ranking score `s_final` if the Lagrange multiplier `λ` is held constant versus if it adapts?"
- **Concept:** Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)
  - *Why needed:* JobRec uses LoRA to adapt the large LLM backbone efficiently during Stage I; knowing rank (`r`) and scaling (`α`) parameters is necessary for implementation.
  - *Quick check:* "What does setting LoRA rank `r=16` imply about the rank decomposition applied to the LLM's weight updates?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Raw Input -> USAS Encoder -> Shared LLM Backbone (with LoRA) -> Separate Linear Heads (pref/qual) -> [Stage I: Multi-task Training] -> Frozen Encoder + Policy Layer (λ, ε) -> [Stage II: Lagrangian Alignment] -> s_final -> Ranking
- **Critical path:** 1) Parse input into USAS layers. 2) Encode and compute `s_pref` and `s_qual`. 3) Apply policy layer: `s_final = s_pref + λ*(s_qual - ε)`. 4) Rank candidates/jobs by `s_final`.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Schema rigidity vs. coverage: USAS's manual, fixed taxonomy is interpretable but may miss implicit signals (e.g., cultural fit) present in raw text.
  - Global vs. per-context constraints: A single `λ` and `ε` is simple and controllable but cannot represent employer-specific or role-specific eligibility rules.
  - Synthetic vs. real data: Synthetic data provides clean dual labels but may not capture real-world noise, distribution shifts, or diverse demographic patterns.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Stage I heads collapse: Validation `s_pref` and `s_qual` scores become highly correlated or one task significantly underperforms.
  - Stage II misalignment: Qualification recall does not increase monotonically with `ε`, or `λ` saturates at boundary values (0 or max) too quickly.
  - Distribution shift: Significant performance drop on real-world, messy data compared to the synthetic evaluation set.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline comparison (RQ1):** Evaluate JobRec against LLM+ICL and deep recommendation baselines on Preference and Qualification Recall@K, expecting significant improvements on both.
  2. **De-conflation necessity (RQ3):** Train an ablation that shares a single head for both tasks. Expect severe degradation on Qualification Recall, proving separate heads are necessary.
  3. **Controllability (RQ5):** Vary `ε` from low to high and plot Qualification Recall@5 and Preference Recall@5. Expect a monotonic increase in qualification with stable preference, confirming Lagrangian control.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Automatic Schema Induction:** How can USAS be automatically induced from noisy, real-world recruitment data to reduce manual design and improve robustness to attribute extraction errors?
- **Personalized Constraints:** Can the Lagrangian alignment be extended to handle employer-specific or role-specific eligibility constraints rather than a global dual variable and threshold?
- **Generalization to Real Logs:** Does JobRec generalize to non-CS domains and real recruitment logs with inherent exposure and censoring biases, or does performance degrade under distribution shift?
- **Bias Mitigation:** How can demographic biases from LLM pretraining or schema design be mitigated in dual-perspective reasoning while preserving decision accuracy?

## Limitations
- **Real-world validation gap:** Evaluation uses a synthetic, CS-domain dataset; performance on real, censored recruitment logs with messy attribute extraction and diverse job markets remains unproven.
- **Schema rigidity:** USAS is a manually defined taxonomy that may not capture all critical signals (e.g., implicit cultural fit) and is sensitive to attribute extraction quality.
- **Constraint simplicity:** The use of a single global eligibility threshold `ε` and Lagrange multiplier `λ` assumes uniform constraints, whereas real-world eligibility rules are often heterogeneous across employers and roles.

## Confidence
- **De-conflation necessity (Mechanism 1):** **Medium**. Strong formal reasoning and synthetic evidence, but limited real-world validation on truly ambiguous negative outcomes.
- **USAS representation (Mechanism 2):** **Low-Medium**. The schema is well-specified internally, but lacks external corpus evidence for its completeness or superiority over other representation choices (e.g., heterogeneous graphs).
- **Lagrangian controllability (Mechanism 3):** **Medium**. The derivation is sound and results show the intended monotonic behavior, but the assumption of a simple, convex constraint is not validated against complex, non-monotonic real eligibility rules.

## Next Checks
1. **Schema Coverage Stress Test:** Run the USAS extraction pipeline on a raw sample of real job descriptions and candidate profiles. Quantify the fraction of attributes that fall outside the four defined layers or require heuristic mapping, measuring potential signal loss.
2. **Synthetic-Real Data Ablation:** Train JobRec on a hybrid dataset (e.g., 50% synthetic dual-labeled, 50% real logs with only `Y_succ`). Measure performance degradation to test robustness to the synthetic distribution bias.
3. **Per-Employer Constraint Extension:** Modify Stage II to support per-employer or per-role `ε` values (e.g., estimated from historical qualification rates). Compare the resulting preference-qualification trade-off curves against the global `ε` approach to test the "single constraint" assumption.
