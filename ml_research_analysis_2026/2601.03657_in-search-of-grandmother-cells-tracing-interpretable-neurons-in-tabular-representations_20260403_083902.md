# In Search of Grandmother Cells: Tracing Interpretable Neurons in Tabular Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03657
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03657
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Significant knee points for ICU transfer and hospitalization tasks with selectivity up to 0.73, but pure concept selectivity rare; sparse probe baselines are Pareto-dominated.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether neurons in tabular foundation models can act as "grandmother cells"—individual neurons that respond exclusively to a single high-level concept. Using TabPFN, a transformer-based model pretrained on synthetic tabular data, the authors analyze neuron activations on the MIMIC-IV-ED dataset with medical concepts defined by ICD codes. They introduce two information-theoretic metrics: neuronal saliency (surprisal derived from mutual information between neuron and concept) and selectivity (normalized surprisal across concepts). A simple search across all 2,304 neurons identifies Pareto-optimal neuron-concept pairs that balance high saliency and high selectivity. Statistically significant knee points are found for ICU transfer and hospitalization tasks, with selectivities up to 0.73, but no significant results for mortality or critical outcome. Baselines using sparse linear probes are Pareto-dominated, suggesting that interpretable neurons emerge naturally in the pretrained model, though pure selectivity remains elusive.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds in three main steps. First, the MIMIC-IV-ED dataset is preprocessed: ICD codes are grouped into 24 high-level chapters serving as binary concepts, and training sets are undersampled to address class imbalance while keeping the test set intact. Second, the pretrained TabPFN 2.0.9 model is forward-passed on all test samples, and the 192-dimensional output of each of its 12 encoder layers is extracted and concatenated, yielding an activation matrix of size M×2304. Third, for every neuron i and concept j, empirical mutual information is computed between the neuron’s activations and the binary concept labels. This yields a tail probability p_tail(i,j), from which surprisal = −log(p_tail) is derived. Selectivity is defined as surprisal(i,j) divided by the sum of surprisal across all concepts for that neuron. Neuron-concept pairs are then plotted in the (surprisal, selectivity) plane; the Pareto front is constructed, and the knee point—maximizing the sum of min‑max scaled surprisal and selectivity—is selected. Significance is assessed via a combined p‑value that accounts for multiple comparisons using a Bonferroni correction. The same data and train/test splits are used to train two sparse probe baselines (multivariate SHAP‑based logistic regressions and univariate optimal linear probes), and their suprisal/selectivity are compared to the Pareto front.

## Key Results
- Significant knee points (p<0.05) exist for ICU transfer (surprisal=6.13, selectivity=0.73, p=2.1×10⁻⁶) and hospitalization (selectivity=0.62).
- Neuron 49 in layer 8 exhibits high selectivity for trauma‑related ICD features.
- Sparse probe baselines are Pareto‑dominated, indicating that the naturally occurring neurons are more efficient concept encoders.
- Selectivity never exceeds 0.73 and no significant knee points are found for inhospital mortality or critical outcome tasks, suggesting that pure grandmother‑cell behavior is rare.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Grandmother cells are defined by two properties: strong association with a single concept (saliency) and exclusivity to that concept (selectivity). The proposed metrics capture these directly. Surprisal, the negative log tail probability of mutual information, quantifies how unlikely the observed dependence is under independence—high values indicate strong saliency. Selectivity normalizes surprisal across all concepts for a neuron, penalizing broad tuning; a value near 1 means the neuron’s information is almost entirely about one concept. By searching the Pareto front, the method selects pairs that cannot be improved in one metric without sacrificing the other, thus balancing saliency and exclusivity. The knee point heuristic chooses a pair with a good trade‑off, avoiding extremes (e.g., a neuron that is highly selective but only weakly associated). Statistical testing with Bonferroni correction controls false discoveries across the large number of neuron‑concept pairs. This combination enables identification of candidate grandmother cells that are both interpretable and statistically reliable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mutual Information**  
  *Why needed*: Measures dependence between continuous neuron activations and binary concept labels; the basis for surprisal.  
  *Quick check*: For independent variables, MI ≈ 0; does it increase when a synthetic neuron is correlated with the label?  
- **Selectivity**  
  *Why needed*: Normalizes saliency across concepts to quantify concentration; high selectivity indicates concept specificity.  
  *Quick check*: Selectivity lies in [0,1] and sums to 1 across concepts for a given neuron by definition.  
- **Pareto Optimality**  
  *Why needed*: Filters out dominated neuron‑concept pairs that are inferior in both saliency and selectivity; crucial for candidate selection.  
  *Quick check*: Plot surprisal vs selectivity; any pair that lies to the right‑and‑up of another is non‑dominated.  
- **Bonferroni Correction**  
  *Why needed*: Adjusts p‑values for multiple testing across 2,304 neurons × 24 concepts to control family‑wise error.  
  *Quick check*: Multiply raw p‑value by number of tests; if result >1 set to 1—does the combined p‑value become conservative?  

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
MIMIC-IV-ED raw data → Preprocessing (ICD chapter binarization, undersampling) → TabPFN 2.0.9 (12 encoder layers × 192 hidden units) → Layer‑wise activation extraction → Activation concatenation (M×2304) → Mutual information estimator → Surprisal & selectivity calculator → Pareto front builder → Knee point selector → Statistical testing.

**Critical path**:  
1. Prepare balanced train/test splits with binary concept labels.  
2. Forward pass through TabPFN to collect activations from each encoder layer.  
3. Compute empirical MI for every neuron‑concept pair.  
4. Derive surprisal and selectivity, construct Pareto front, and pick the knee point.  
5. Evaluate significance with Bonferroni‑adjusted combined p‑value.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Histogram MI binning vs k‑NN: histogram is fast but sensitive to bin count; k‑NN is parameter‑free but slower.  
- Pareto knee vs top‑N selection: knee provides a balanced trade‑off; top‑N could favor one metric arbitrarily.  
- Bonferroni correction strongly controls false positives but reduces power, especially with thousands of tests.  
- Using all layers increases coverage but multiplies tests; restricting to a few layers might miss relevant neurons.  
- Undersampling balances classes but discards data and introduces randomness; class weighting might be an alternative.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Inflated type I error: many pairs with p≈0 under null; suggests MI estimator or null distribution mis‑specified.  
- Knee point changes drastically with small changes in bin count or random seed; indicates instability.  
- Extracted activation dimensionality ≠ 2,304; suggests wrong extraction point (e.g., including classification head).  
- Selectivity values clustered below 0.5; might indicate neurons are broadly tuned or metric mis‑definition.  
- Baselines outperform reported Pareto dominance; points to baseline implementation differences.

**First three experiments**:  
1. Verify activation extraction: Run a few samples through TabPFN and confirm that concatenated layer outputs yield exactly 2,304 features per sample.  
2. Validate MI estimator: Compute MI between an independent Gaussian neuron and a random binary concept; ensure p‑tail is uniform and mean surprisal ≈ 1. Also test on a perfectly correlated synthetic neuron.  
3. Check Pareto stability: For one prediction task, compute knee point using different histogram bin counts (5, 10, 20) and multiple undersampling seeds; report variation in selected neuron‑concept pair and metrics.

## Limitations
- Mutual information estimation details (binning vs k‑NN, number of bins) are unspecified, affecting reproducibility of surprisal and selectivity.
- The exact set and hierarchical level of ICD concepts (likely 24 chapters) are not confirmed, leading to ambiguity in concept definition.
- Undersampling random seeds, precise training set sizes for several tasks, and TabPFN’s internal preprocessing (scaling, missing‑value handling) are not described, introducing variability.
- The activation extraction point within each encoder layer is unclear, which may alter neuron count and rankings.
- The knee‑point selection heuristic is ad hoc and may not generalize across datasets or tasks.
- Baseline implementation details (SHAP algorithm, regularization, background) are lacking, making the claim of Pareto domination fragile.
- The study uses a single model and a single dataset, limiting the generalizability of the findings.
- The observed selectivity ceiling of 0.73 could be an artifact of the metrics or the discretization scheme.

## Confidence

- Significant knee points for ICU transfer and hospitalization tasks: **Medium**
- Sparse probe baselines are Pareto‑dominated: **Low**

## Next Checks
1. **MI estimator robustness**: Repeat the entire analysis using multiple mutual information estimators (histogram bins 5/10/20; k‑NN with k=3,5,10) and assess stability of top knee points and their selectivity values.
2. **Undersampling variability**: Execute the pipeline with five different random seeds for undersampling each task. Compute mean and variance of knee‑point surprisal and selectivity; verify that significance (p<0.05 after Bonferroni) persists across seeds.
3. **Baseline reproducibility**: Implement the SHAP probe with the `shap` library using a fixed background set and L2 regularization. Train on the same splits as the original paper and evaluate whether the baseline neurons remain on the Pareto front or are dominated under consistent conditions.
