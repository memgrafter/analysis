# PEAfowl: Perception-Enhanced Multi-View Vision-Language-Action for Bimanual Manipulation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.17885
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17885
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** 47.1% average success on RoboTwin 2.0 with domain randomization (23.0 percentage point improvement over strongest baseline SEM), with reliable sim-to-real transfer and consistent gains from depth distillation.

## Executive Summary
PEAfowl addresses poor generalization in bimanual vision-language-action models by enhancing 3D spatial understanding and instruction grounding. It introduces geometry-guided multi-view fusion that predicts per-token depth distributions, performs differentiable 3D lifting, and aggregates cross-view neighbors to form spatially consistent representations. Language grounding is improved via a Perceiver-style text-as-query readout over frozen CLIP features, enabling iterative evidence accumulation. A training-only depth distillation from a pretrained teacher injects geometry-aware priors without inference overhead. On RoboTwin 2.0, PEAfowl achieves 47.1% success (+23.0 pp over SEM) and demonstrates reliable sim-to-real transfer with consistent depth distillation benefits.

## Method Summary
PEAfowl processes multi-view RGB-D inputs through parallel RGB (Swin-T) and depth (ResNet-34) encoders. Co-located RGB-D tokens are fused and a depth distribution head predicts discrete depth bins. Using expected depth, tokens are back-projected into a shared robot base frame; nearby cross-view tokens are aggregated based on 3D proximity to create geometry-enhanced representations. Language instruction is grounded by feeding text tokens as queries into a Perceiver that iteratively cross-attends to frozen CLIP patch tokens, producing context tokens. A diffusion decoder, conditioned on fused geometry, context, and proprioceptive state, denoises to predict action chunks. Depth distillation during training uses a pretrained depth teacher (CDM) to supervise the depth distribution, improving robustness to noisy commodity sensors.

## Key Results
- 47.1% average success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 (23.0 pp improvement over strongest baseline SEM).
- Reliable sim-to-real transfer validated on a physical robot.
- Depth distillation provides consistent performance gains in real-world settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Geometry-guided multi-view fusion creates spatially consistent representations by leveraging depth distributions and cross-view neighbor aggregation, improving robustness to occlusions and viewpoint changes.
- Mechanism: For each view, predict a discrete depth distribution per token; back-project token centers into a shared base frame using expected depth; aggregate RGB tokens from nearby cross-view tokens based on 3D distance; produce geometry-enhanced tokens that align across cameras.
- Core assumption: Even noisy depth provides approximate 3D correspondences; local neighborhoods in 3D space represent the same physical region.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "predicts per-token depth distributions, performs differentiable 3D lifting, and aggregates local cross-view neighbors to form geometrically grounded, cross-view consistent representations."
  - [section 3.2] Details of pairwise fusion, depth distribution head, and cross-view aggregation.
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related multi-view perception work (e.g., GP3, StereoVLA) but citation counts are zero, indicating preliminary evidence.
- Break condition: Extreme depth noise or textureless surfaces yield uniform distributions, causing poor 3D lifting; insufficient camera overlap leaves many tokens without cross-view neighbors, failing aggregation.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Perceiver-style text-as-query readout over frozen CLIP features yields language-grounded visual tokens via iterative evidence accumulation, sharpening attention on task-relevant objects and relations in clutter.
- Mechanism: Text tokens act as latent queries that repeatedly cross-attend to CLIP patch tokens per view; iterative refinement aggregates visual evidence relevant to the instruction; pooled context tokens condition the policy.
- Core assumption: Frozen CLIP retains sufficient vision-language alignment; iterative steps allow progressive focus despite clutter.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "replaces global conditioning with a Perceiver-style text-aware readout over frozen CLIP features, enabling iterative evidence accumulation."
  - [section 3.3] Describes Perceiver latent blocks and readout pooling.
  - [corpus] Corpus includes OTTER and SG-VLA exploring text-aware extraction, but citations are zero.
- Break condition: Highly ambiguous instructions or fine-grained spatial needs exceed CLIP's pretraining; too few latent iterations limit evidence accumulation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Training-only depth distillation from a pretrained teacher injects geometry-aware priors into the depth head without inference overhead, stabilizing predictions under noisy commodity sensors.
- Mechanism: During training, a pretrained Camera Depth Model (CDM) provides soft depth-bin targets; the policy's depth distribution is supervised by a validity-weighted BCE loss; at test, only raw depth is used.
- Core assumption: CDM depth is more reliable than commodity depth; distilling it teaches the head to output plausible distributions even with noisy inputs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "apply training-only depth distillation from a pretrained depth teacher to supervise the depth-distribution head, providing perception front-end with geometry-aware priors."
  - [section 3.5] Specifies depth distillation loss and pipeline.
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence weak or missing: no directly related papers on depth distillation for VLAs.
- Break condition: CDM's data distribution mismatches robot scenes (e.g., different objects/lighting); head capacity insufficient to capture teacher knowledge.

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map:
  - Inputs: multi-view RGB-D (4 cameras), proprioception (joint angles/poses), instruction text.
  - Modules: RGB encoder (Swin-T), depth encoder (ResNet-34), pairwise RGB-D fusion, depth distribution head, 3D lifting, cross-view aggregation, fusion MLP, frozen CLIP, Perceiver latent blocks, readout pooling, joint-centric state encoder, diffusion action decoder.
  - Losses: diffusion L2, FK consistency, depth distillation (BCE).
  - Output: denoised joint angles → closed-loop execution.
- Critical path (forward pass):
  1. Extract multi-scale RGB and depth features per view.
  2. Tokenize and fuse co-located RGB-D pairs.
  3. Predict depth distribution; compute expected 3D anchor.
  4. Find top-K cross-view neighbors by 3D distance; aggregate RGB tokens.
  5. Gated residual update; fuse with depth tokens and 3D point embeddings.
  6. Extract frozen CLIP patch tokens and text tokens.
  7. Run Perceiver latent blocks (M=3) per view: text-as-query cross-attention to patches.
  8. Pool view-specific and text tokens into context sequence.
  9. Encode joint state into state tokens.
  10. Condition diffusion decoder on geometry tokens, context, state; denoise to predict action chunk.
  11. Execute first H_exec actions; repeat.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Local pairwise fusion vs. global: reduces noise propagation but may miss long-range dependencies.
  - Frozen CLIP vs. fine-tuned: preserves pretrained alignment but limits adaptation; Perceiver mitigates.
  - Depth distribution (B bins) vs. single depth: models uncertainty but increases complexity; distillation stabilizes.
  - Cross-view top-K vs. all: efficient and noise-robust but may drop distant context.
  - Diffusion decoder vs. deterministic: handles multi-modality but slower; upsampling reduces steps.
- Failure signatures:
  - Depth distribution: near-uniform or overly confident (low entropy).
  - Cross-view aggregation: t-SNE shows no view clustering post-aggregation; performance drops when removing a camera.
  - Language readout: vision-grounded text tokens insensitive to instruction changes (e.g., color swaps).
  - Generalization: large clean vs. DR gap; failure on occlusion-heavy or bimanual coordination tasks.
  - Sim-to-real: low real-robot success despite fine-tuning, possibly due to depth noise/lighting.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablate geometry-guided module: remove pairwise fusion and cross-view aggregation; expect depth loss divergence and DR performance drop.
  2. Ablate Perceiver readout: replace with global text conditioning (e.g., add text token); expect lower success on attribute-sensitive tasks.
  3. Ablate depth distillation: train without CDM supervision; evaluate on real robot with raw depth; expect degradation on depth-sensitive tasks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How sensitive is PEAfowl's geometry-guided multi-view fusion to inaccuracies in camera calibration (intrinsics and extrinsics) during real-world deployment?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The fusion process (Sec 3.2) relies on known camera parameters for differentiable 3D lifting and cross-view aggregation, but the paper does not evaluate robustness to calibration errors.
- Why unresolved: Real-world calibration can be noisy or drift over time; performance degradation under such perturbations remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically perturb calibrated parameters in simulation (RoboTwin 2.0) and real-robot experiments, then measure success rate changes across tasks to quantify sensitivity and identify critical parameters.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact on policy performance when the depth distillation teacher (CDM) produces erroneous depth estimates in out-of-distribution scenes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Sec 3.5 uses a pretrained Camera Depth Model (CDM) as a training-only teacher to supervise the depth-distribution head.
- Why unresolved: The teacher's domain is fixed; if deployment scenes involve materials or conditions not seen by CDM (e.g., transparent objects, extreme lighting), its supervision may mislead the policy, but this failure mode is not analyzed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluate PEAfowl on scenes where CDM's depth errors are high; correlate task success with teacher confidence scores or error magnitudes; alternatively, replace CDM with weaker or alternative teachers and measure performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does freezing CLIP features limit PEAfowl's ability to adapt to task-specific language instructions or novel object categories beyond CLIP's pretraining?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Sec 3.3 uses frozen CLIP text and vision encoders without fine-tuning to produce language-guided context tokens.
- Why unresolved: CLIP's web-pretrained representations may not capture fine-grained distinctions for bimanual manipulation (e.g., subtle spatial relations, rare objects); freezing prevents task-specific adaptation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Compare full PEAfowl against variants with fine-tuned CLIP on the same tasks; test with instructions containing novel attribute combinations or objects not well-represented in CLIP's dataset.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can PEAfowl generalize to arbitrary numbers or placements of cameras at inference without retraining?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The architecture and hyperparameters (Tab 5) assume a fixed multi-view setup (V=4 specific views); cross-view aggregation is designed for this fixed topology.
- Why unresolved: Real-world deployments may use different camera rigs (e.g., more views, different positions); the paper does not explore zero-shot generalization to varying view counts or placements.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train models with varying subsets or numbers of cameras; at test time, feed inputs with additional views or from novel camera positions to see if performance degrades or if features can be integrated seamlessly.

## Limitations
- Depth distillation may fail on new depth sensors or lighting conditions, as teacher model bias is untested.
- The Perceiver's reliance on frozen CLIP may limit fine-grained spatial and relational reasoning.
- Cross-view aggregation assumes sufficient overlap; sparse views or occlusions could break it.
- Generalization beyond RoboTwin 2.0 is unproven; real-world diversity may expose weaknesses.

## Confidence
- Spatial Fusion (geometry-guided multi-view): **Medium** (well-specified but break conditions noted)
- Language Grounding (Perceiver readout): **Medium** (relies on frozen CLIP; limited citations)
- Depth Distillation: **Low** (no prior work; susceptible to teacher distribution mismatch)
- Overall Performance: **High** (rigorous experiments, real-robot validation)

3. Camera overlap: Vary number of views and introduce synthetic occlusions to evaluate robustness of cross-view aggregation.
