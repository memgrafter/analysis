# Experimental Comparison of Light-Weight and Deep CNN Models Across Diverse Datasets
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03463
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03463
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: A compact CNN (~1.9M parameters) achieved 72–94% test accuracy across five Bangladeshi datasets, while deeper models reached up to ~97% with 6–20× more parameters and longer training.

## Executive Summary
This study benchmarks a custom lightweight CNN (CustomCNN) against deep architectures (EfficientNetB0, ResNet18) on five Bangladeshi vision datasets covering smart‑city and agricultural domains. With only ~1.9 million parameters, CustomCNN attained competitive test accuracies between 72.00% and 94.12% and required minimal computational resources. In contrast, deep models—especially with ImageNet transfer learning—achieved higher peak accuracies (up to ~97%) but demanded 6–20× more parameters and substantially longer training times. The results demonstrate that well‑regularized shallow networks are highly efficient for resource‑constrained deployment, while deeper models are preferable when maximizing accuracy is critical and resources are abundant. The paper also highlights that transfer learning can misalign with fine‑grained tasks, where training from scratch may yield better performance, and that systematic regularization stabilizes learning even for compact architectures.

## Method Summary
The experiments used five Bangladeshi datasets: RoadDamageBD, PaddyVarietyBD, MangoImageBD, FootpathVision, and Auto‑RickshawImageBD. Each dataset was split stratified by class into 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% test sets. Models included a custom three‑block CNN (CustomCNN) and two deep architectures: EfficientNetB0 and ResNet18. The deep models were evaluated both from scratch and with ImageNet pretraining. Training employed Adam (learning rate 1e‑3, weight decay 1e‑4) and a weighted cross‑entropy loss to handle class imbalance. Regularization comprised batch normalization, dropout (rate not specified), He initialization for convolutions, and early stopping (delta 0.001, patience 10). A ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler (factor 0.5, patience 5) adjusted the learning rate. Data augmentation included random horizontal flips, rotations within ±10°, and color jitter; all images were resized to 224×224 and normalized with ImageNet statistics. Evaluation metrics were test accuracy, precision, recall, macro F1, parameter count, model size, and training time. Some details—such as exact augmentation probabilities, dropout rate, random seeds, and hardware configuration—are not fully specified, which may affect reproducibility.

## Key Results
- CustomCNN (1.9M parameters) achieved test accuracies between 72.00% and 94.12% across the five datasets.
- Deep models (EfficientNetB0, ResNet18) with transfer learning attained higher peak accuracies up to ~97%, but required 6–20 times more parameters and significantly longer training times.
- When trained from scratch, deep models exhibited unstable convergence and much lower accuracy (e.g., EfficientNetB0: 72.1% on RoadDamageBD), underscoring the importance of pretraining for large architectures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Architectural Compactness
- **Claim**: Architectural compactness (shallow depth, controlled channel growth) reduces computational cost while maintaining sufficient representational capacity for heterogeneous tasks.
- **Mechanism**: Limiting depth to three convolutional blocks with channel progression 3→64→128→256 constrains parameter count (~1.9M) yet enables hierarchical feature extraction; fewer layers reduce optimization difficulty and memory footprint.
- **Core assumption**: Task complexity in the studied Bangladeshi datasets is adequately captured by mid‑level semantic features without requiring very deep hierarchies.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract]: "...well‑regularized shallow architecture can serve as a highly competitive baseline across heterogeneous domains...without requiring large GPUs or specialized pre‑trained models."
  - [PAGE 4, Section 2.2.1]: Explicit block definitions with n_k={2,2,3} and channel progression.
  - [corpus]: Weak/limited (avg FMR 0.499); no direct citations on lightweight CNN causal mechanisms.
- **Break condition**: If tasks require highly abstract, multi‑scale features (e.g., fine‑grained paddy varieties), shallow capacity may be insufficient, as suggested by lower accuracy on PaddyVarietyBD (78.76% vs. deeper models).

### Mechanism 2: Transfer Learning Benefits and Risks
- **Claim**: Transfer learning improves convergence stability and generalization when source‑domain pretraining (ImageNet) aligns with target visual patterns, but can hurt performance on fine‑grained, domain‑specific datasets due to feature misalignment.
- **Mechanism**: Pretrained weights provide optimized initialization, avoiding poor local minima; however, features from natural images may not capture subtle intra‑class variations in specialized agricultural or urban‑scene datasets.
- **Core assumption**: Pretrained feature extractors generalize to new domains only if low/mid‑level cues (edges, textures) are shared, and high‑level semantics transfer.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [PAGE 9, Table 2]: EfficientNetB0 (Transfer) = 0.941 vs. Scratch = 0.721 on RoadDamageBD; but on PaddyVarietyBD [PAGE 18, Table 5], Transfer = 0.664 vs. Scratch = 0.917.
  - [PAGE 12, Section 3.1]: "transfer learning‑based models demonstrate the fastest convergence and most stable learning behavior" on FootpathVision, but on PaddyVarietyBD "transfer learning...performance is lower" due to "subtle visual differences."
  - [corpus]: Limited; neighboring papers on lightweight CNNs focus on efficiency, not transfer learning domain shift.
- **Break condition**: When target data distribution diverges significantly from ImageNet (e.g., Bangladeshi crop varieties), scratch training on domain data may learn more relevant features, despite slower convergence.

### Mechanism 3: Systematic Regularization Stabilizes Training
- **Claim**: Systematic regularization (batch normalization, dropout, He initialization, early stopping) stabilizes training of lightweight models, preventing overfitting and reducing train‑validation gaps.
- **Mechanism**: Batch norm mitigates internal covariate shift; dropout prevents co‑adaptation; He init preserves gradient variance in ReLU networks; early stopping halts upon validation plateau, avoiding overfit.
- **Core assumption**: Even shallow networks benefit from explicit regularization to handle class imbalance and data variability without excessive capacity.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [PAGE 5, Section 2.2.3]: Weight initialization details; [PAGE 6, Section 2.3]: Adam + weight decay + ReduceLROnPlateau; [PAGE 6, Section 2.4]: Early stopping criterion.
  - [PAGE 8, Section 3]: Learning curves show CustomCNN with "smooth loss decay" and "minor divergence" vs. scratch deep models with "spikes" and "gaps."
  - [corpus]: Weak; corpus includes papers on activation functions and compression, but not specific to this regularization suite.
- **Break condition**: If dropout rate or weight decay is too high, underfitting may occur; paper does not ablation‑test, so optimal thresholds remain dataset‑dependent.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Stratified Splitting and Weighted Loss for Class Imbalance
- **Why needed here**: Datasets like Auto‑RickshawImageBD have uneven class distributions; naive splitting could bias splits, and standard cross‑entropy would overfit majority classes.
- **Quick check question**: If you omit class weighting during training, what metric would be most artificially inflated, and why?
- **Answer**: Overall accuracy, because majority class dominates loss minimization.

### Concept: Batch Normalization and He Initialization for Gradient Stability
- **Why needed here**: Prevents vanishing/exploding gradients during backpropagation, especially critical when training from scratch without pretrained scales.
- **Quick check question**: How does batch norm interact with dropout during training?
- **Answer**: Batch norm normalizes pre‑activation statistics; dropout randomly zeros activations—they are typically applied in sequence, with dropout after batch norm and ReLU, as in this architecture.

### Concept: Global Average Pooling (GAP) as a Flattening Alternative
- **Why needed here**: Replaces flattening + large FC layers, reducing parameters and spatial bias; aligns with convolutional philosophy by preserving spatial hierarchy until final collapse.
- **Quick check question**: What is a key disadvantage of GAP compared to a flattening layer followed by FC?
- **Answer**: GAP loses precise spatial location information, which may matter for tasks requiring spatial reasoning.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input (224x224x3) → Conv Block 1 (Conv3x3→BN→ReLU×2, 64 channels, /2 pooling) → Conv Block 2 (Conv3x3→BN→ReLU×2, 128 channels, /2 pooling) → Conv Block 3 (Conv3x3→BN→ReLU×3, 256 channels, /2 pooling) → Global Average Pooling (1x1x256) → FC1 (256→512, BN, ReLU, Dropout) → FC2 (512→C classes, no activation) → Softmax

### Critical Path
1. Load dataset with recursive scan → stratified 70/15/15 split.
2. Apply augmentation (flip, rotation ±10°, color jitter) + ImageNet normalization.
3. Forward through CNN blocks → GAP → classifier.
4. Compute weighted cross-entropy loss (if class_weight=True).
5. Backpropagate; Adam (lr=1e-3, wd=1e-4).
6. After each epoch: evaluate validation loss; ReduceLROnPlateau (factor=0.5, patience=5).
7. Check early stopping: if validation loss improvement <0.001 for 10 epochs → terminate.
8. Load best checkpoint → final test evaluation.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Depth vs. efficiency**: 3 blocks vs. 18+ layers in ResNet; accuracy tradeoff evident in PaddyVarietyBD (78.76% vs. 91.7%).
- **Scratch vs. transfer**: Transfer gives stability but may underfit fine‑grained tasks; scratch gives peak accuracy but with volatile validation curves.
- **Augmentation strength**: Mild rotations/flips chosen to preserve semantics; aggressive augmentation might harm subtle feature learning in agricultural tasks.

### Failure Signatures
- **High validation loss spikes** early in training: indicates scratch deep model struggling with optimization (seen in EfficientNetB0 scratch on PaddyVarietyBD).
- **Large train‑val accuracy gap (>10%)**: potential overfitting, may need more dropout or augmentation.
- **Convergence to low accuracy (<70%)**: model capacity mismatch; consider deeper architecture or transfer learning.
- **Early stopping triggered too soon**: check delta (0.001) relative to loss scale; may need smaller delta or higher patience.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline CustomCNN train/validation**: Verify stable loss decay and reasonable accuracy (~72-94%) across all datasets; confirm early stopping activates around epoch 50-150 depending on dataset.
2. **Scratch deep model comparison**: Train EfficientNetB0 and ResNet18 from scratch with same protocol; expect poor performance on small datasets (e.g., EfficientNetB0 scratch on RoadDamageBD = 72.1%) and unstable curves.
3. **Transfer learning comparison**: Initialize EfficientNetB0/ResNet18 with ImageNet weights; freeze early layers optionally; expect higher accuracy (up to 97%) but longer training time (5-10x) and larger model size. Compare learning curves: smoother validation but possible performance drop on fine‑grained tasks (PaddyVarietyBD).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: What specific visual and statistical characteristics of domain‑specific datasets determine the suitability of lightweight versus deep CNN architectures?
- **Basis in paper**: (inferred) The paper shows CustomCNN performs well on RoadDamageBD (94.12% accuracy) but poorly on PaddyVarietyBD (78.76%) and Auto‑RickshawImageBD (72.00%), suggesting dataset‑dependent factors. However, the authors do not analyze dataset characteristics (e.g., inter‑class similarity, background clutter) to explain these variations.
- **Why unresolved**: Without identifying key dataset traits, model selection remains empirical rather than principled for new domains.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A systematic correlation analysis between dataset metrics (e.g., class separability, image complexity) and performance gaps across multiple datasets, including non‑Bangladeshi ones.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can transfer learning be adapted to improve performance on fine‑grained classification tasks where ImageNet pretraining provides limited transferable features?
- **Basis in paper**: (inferred) On PaddyVarietyBD, transfer learning models (EfficientNetB0: 66.4%, ResNet18: 63.7% accuracy) underperform scratch‑trained deep models (EfficientNetB0: 86.6%, ResNet18: 91.7%), indicating misalignment between ImageNet features and fine‑grained agricultural patterns.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper notes this phenomenon but does not investigate why or propose solutions, leaving a gap for domain‑specific transfer learning strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments with alternative pretraining (e.g., on large agricultural datasets) or fine‑tuning techniques (e.g., attention mechanisms) on fine‑grained tasks, measuring performance improvements.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Do the computational efficiency advantages of lightweight CNNs persist when measured by inference latency and energy consumption on resource‑constrained edge devices, rather than parameter count and training time alone?
- **Basis in paper**: (inferred) The paper compares models based on parameters, size, and training time, but does not evaluate inference speed or power usage on actual hardware, which are critical for deployment.
- **Why unresolved**: Real‑world deployment constraints depend on inference efficiency, which may differ from training metrics due to memory bandwidth, quantization effects, etc.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Benchmarking all models on edge devices (e.g., Raspberry Pi, smartphone) for latency, throughput, and energy per inference, across different optimization techniques (e.g., pruning, quantization).

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: To what extent do the findings generalize to vision tasks beyond classification (e.g., detection, segmentation) and to non‑Bangladeshi cultural contexts?
- **Basis in paper**: (inferred) All datasets are Bangladeshi and focus on classification; the paper does not test other vision tasks or datasets from other regions, limiting generalizability.
- **Why unresolved**: Applications in other regions or tasks may have different data distributions and requirements, so the reported trade‑offs might not hold universally.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Replicating the study on diverse international datasets and for other vision tasks, comparing the same models under similar protocols.
