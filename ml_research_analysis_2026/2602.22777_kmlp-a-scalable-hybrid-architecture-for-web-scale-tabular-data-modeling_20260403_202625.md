# KMLP: A Scalable Hybrid Architecture for Web-Scale Tabular Data Modeling
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22777
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22777
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: KMLP outperformed LightGBM by 1.76 KS points on a billion-sample industrial dataset, with advantage growing with data scale, leading to $46M reduction in overdue loans and $670M increased credit scale in six months.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces KMLP, a hybrid deep learning architecture designed for web-scale tabular data with billions of samples. It addresses key limitations of Gradient Boosting Decision Trees (GBDTs)—such as sequential training bottlenecks and reliance on manual feature engineering—by combining a Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) front-end for automated per-feature non-linear transformation with a Gated MLP (gMLP) backbone for high-order interactions. A novel Quantile Linear Transformation (QTL) preprocessing step stabilizes training by mapping numerical features to uniform distributions while preserving intra-bin magnitude order. Evaluated on a billion-sample industrial credit scoring dataset, KMLP demonstrated superior scalability and business impact, reducing overdue loans by $46 million and increasing credit volume by $670 million within six months of deployment.

## Method Summary
KMLP is a hybrid pipeline: (1) **Quantile Linear Transformation (QTL)** preprocesses each numerical feature by binning into quantiles and linearly interpolating within bins, enforcing uniform marginal distributions while preserving local order. (2) A shallow **KAN front-end** applies a learnable univariate spline-based function per feature, then sums across features through outer functions, automating non-linear feature transformation. (3) A **gMLP backbone** of SwiGLU blocks captures high-order interactions from the KAN output. Training uses Adam with learning rate decay and early stopping on validation KS. The design targets web-scale data where GBDTs become inefficient and manual feature engineering is prohibitive.

## Key Results
- On a 1B-sample industrial credit scoring dataset, KMLP improved the KS metric by 1.76 points over LightGBM.
- The performance gap between KMLP and GBDT baselines widened as training data scale increased from 200K to 2B samples.
- Real-world deployment yielded a $46 million reduction in overdue loans and a $670 million increase in credit scale within six months.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The shallow KAN front-end automates per-feature non-linear transformation via learnable B-spline functions, reducing manual feature engineering and adapting to non-stationary distributions. It operationalizes the Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem by decomposing a multivariate function into sums of univariate functions. Ablating KAN causes significant performance drops (e.g., -1.04 KS on industrial data). However, on small datasets (<2M samples), KMLP underperforms LightGBM, indicating that KAN's capacity may be excessive for limited data and that the shallow design’s sufficiency depends on data scale.

### Mechanism 2
The gMLP backbone efficiently models high-order interactions among KAN-transformed features using a SwiGLU gating mechanism (SiLU(xV+b1) ⊗ (xU+b2)). This parallelizable structure avoids the sequential training bottleneck of GBDTs and provides dynamic, input-dependent feature selection. Removing gMLP leads to measurable performance degradation (e.g., -1.04 KS), but its necessity is contingent on KAN first producing expressive univariate representations.

### Mechanism 3
Quantile Linear Transformation (QTL) stabilizes neural network training by mapping each numerical feature to a uniform distribution via quantile binning and linear interpolation within bins. This preserves intra-bin magnitude relationships (e.g., spending $100 vs. $200) while eliminating scale disparities and skew. QTL’s ablation causes the largest performance drop (e.g., -12.93 average KS on public datasets), confirming its critical role. Without QTL, training becomes unstable on heterogeneous numerical features.

## Foundational Learning
- **Kolmogorov-Arnold Representation Theorem**: Justifies KMLP’s core assumption that any multivariate continuous function can be represented as a finite superposition of univariate continuous functions. *Why needed*: It theoretically motivates learning complex tabular relationships via sum of spline-based univariate transforms. *Quick check*: Does the theorem guarantee a shallow network with 2d+1 summands suffices for practical d-dimensional tabular data?
- **Gated Linear Unit (GLU) / SwiGLU**: The gMLP backbone uses SwiGLU, which gates one linear projection by a sigmoid-weighted second projection. *Why needed*: Provides dynamic, input-dependent feature selection more expressive than static activations like ReLU for heterogeneous tabular features. *Quick check*: How does element-wise multiplication (⊗) in SwiGLU differ from pointwise addition in standard MLPs, and why might this be better for capturing interactions?
- **Quantile Normalization vs. Standard Normalization**: QTL forces a uniform marginal distribution, unlike z-score normalization’s Gaussian target. *Why needed*: Uniformization removes skew and heavy tails, benefiting neural optimization, while linear interpolation within bins preserves semantic magnitude order vital for tasks like credit scoring. *Quick check*: What is the key difference between a pure quantile transform (output i/n) and QTL’s linear interpolation, and why does preserving intra-bin linearity matter?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Raw Numerical Features -> QTL Preprocessor -> KAN Front-end (1-2 layers) -> gMLP Backbone (stacked blocks) -> Output Head (linear layer)
- **Critical path**: QTL distribution stabilization -> KAN per-feature nonlinear transform -> gMLP interaction modeling. Flow is strictly sequential; no residual shortcuts bypass KAN.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - KAN grid size vs. computation: Larger B-spline grids increase univariate modeling capacity but linearly increase memory.
  - gMLP depth vs. interaction order: More blocks capture higher-order effects but risk overfitting on smaller data.
  - QTL bin count (n) vs. information loss: More bins give finer uniformization but may distort extreme tails if interpolation fails.
- **Failure signatures**:
  1. On small datasets (<2M), KMLP underperforms LightGBM due to excessive capacity.
  2. QTL may produce NaNs if quantile bins have zero width; constant or sparse features require special handling.
  3. Training instability without QTL on features with disparate raw scales.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Ablate QTL: Train `KMLP w.o. QTL` on a medium-scale financial dataset; expect significant KS/AUC drop.
  2. Scaling study: Replicate Figure 3 by training KMLP and LightGBM on 200K, 2M, 20M, 200M, 2B samples; validate growing advantage.
  3. Component isolation: Train `KMLP w.o. KAN` (replace KAN with linear/MLP) and `KMLP w.o. gMLP`; expect `w.o. KAN` to drop more, confirming KAN’s primary role in automated feature engineering.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What underlying factors drive KMLP's increasing performance advantage over GBDTs as data scale grows? The paper observes the trend but provides no mechanistic explanation.
- To what extent does KMLP actually reduce reliance on manual feature engineering and frequent retraining in real-world, non-stationary web environments? The claim is qualitative; no quantitative comparison of engineering effort or retraining cycles is offered.
- How does the architectural design (shallow KAN, gMLP backbone) affect scalability and performance limits on ultra-large-scale tabular data? No systematic architectural search is conducted across scales.

## Limitations
- Architectural fidelity may vary due to unspecified `efficient-kan` implementation details (e.g., knot placement, B-spline degree).
- The scale advantage is validated on a single proprietary credit scoring dataset; generalizability to other web-scale domains is unknown.
- Neighboring literature provides weak explicit validation for the specific KAN+gMLP hybrid; evidence is largely confined to this paper’s internal experiments.

## Confidence
- **QTL Preprocessing (Mechanism 3)**: High. Strong ablation evidence (-12.93 KS drop) and clear theoretical rationale.
- **KAN Front-end Automation (Mechanism 1)**: Medium. Contribution confirmed by ablation, but reliance on Kolmogorov-Arnold sufficiency for shallow spline networks is a theoretical leap; poor performance on small datasets.
- **gMLP Interaction Capture (Mechanism 2)**: Medium. Ablation supports role, but gain over standard MLP not fully isolated; benefit contingent on KAN transforms.
- **Web-Scale Scalability Claim**: Medium. Compelling billion-sample result but single case study; scaling curve lacks statistical variance.

## Next Checks

2. **KAN Grid Sensitivity**: Systematically vary KAN `grid_size` (e.g., {3,5,10,20}) on a fixed medium dataset to establish performance/memory trade-offs.
