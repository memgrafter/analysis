## From Fluent to Verifiable: Claim-Level Auditability for Deep Research Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13855
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13855
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Semantic provenance graphs enable perfect claim-level auditability (PCov=1.0, PSnd=1.0, CTran=1.0) compared to black-box aggregation failures.

## Executive Summary
Deep research agents produce fluent outputs but suffer from weak or missing links between claims and evidence, undermining trust. This paper introduces the Auditable Autonomous Research (AAR) standard, defining four metrics (Provenance Coverage, Provenance Soundness, Contradiction Transparency, Audit Effort) to measure claim-level auditability. It advocates a shift from action logs to persistent semantic provenance graphs that encode claim–evidence relations with explicit reasoning steps and continuous validation. Case studies demonstrate that transparent provenance achieves perfect scores while black-box aggregation fails, proving the framework’s ability to measure and improve verifiability.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes constructing a persistent, queryable semantic provenance graph \( G_q = (V, E) \) during agent execution. Nodes represent sources \( S_q \), reasoning steps \( R_q \) (typed as deduction/induction/synthesis), and final claims \( C_q \). Edges encode typed relations (supports, contradicts, refines, prerequisite) with entailment strength \( \nu \) for “supports” edges via Natural Language Inference. The graph is built incrementally as the agent plans, retrieves, reasons, and synthesizes, and is continuously validated against constraints and contradictions. The AAR metrics then evaluate the graph: Provenance Coverage (fraction of claims with complete backward paths), Provenance Soundness (fraction of claim-source pairs with \( \nu > \tau \)), Contradiction Transparency (fraction of detected source conflicts surfaced), and Audit Effort (human verification time).

## Key Results
- Perfect claim-level auditability scores (PCov=1.0, PSnd=1.0, CTran=1.0) achieved with semantic provenance graphs in a case study.
- Black-box aggregation yields poor auditability (PCov=0.33, PSnd=0.25, CTran=0.0).
- The four AAR metrics successfully differentiate verifiable from non-verifiable agent outputs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Claim-Level Traceability
Auditability requires a backward-traceable path from each claim to its sources via explicit reasoning steps. The semantic provenance graph replaces generic execution logs with nodes for sources, intermediate reasoning, and claims, connected by typed edges. This persistent structure allows reconstruction of evidential lineage without re-execution. Break condition: incomplete graph construction leaves claims orphaned (low PCov).

### Mechanism 2: Constraint Persistence
Transient constraints (e.g., experimental specifications) are lost in long-horizon tasks due to stateless context windows. A persistent constraint graph stores initial specifications as structured, queryable nodes and verifies continuously that code and results satisfy them. Break condition: if constraint nodes are not updated or checked during revisions, drift persists undetected.

### Mechanism 3: Contradiction Surfacing
Vector-based retrieval and aggregation cause citation decorrelation: sources appear relevant but do not logically entail claims. Semantic provenance uses typed edges (supports/contradicts) with directional entailment strength \( \nu \) from NLI, explicitly representing logical relationships and surfacing conflicts. Break condition: poorly calibrated \( \nu \) or ambiguous sources can mislead PSnd; confidence intervals and source reliability weights are needed.

## Foundational Learning
- **W3C PROV Data Model:** Extends PROV’s entities/activities/agents to claim-level semantics; PROV lacks native claim-evidence edges. Quick check: Which three PROV relation types (wasGeneratedBy, wasDerivedFrom, wasAssociatedWith) map to reasoning nodes \( R_q \)?
- **Natural Language Inference (NLI):** PSnd relies on measuring \( \nu(c,s) \) via entailment; distinguishing “supports” from “contradicts” is core to CTran. Quick check: Given claim “Treatment X reduces symptom Y” and source abstract “Treatment X had no significant effect on Y,” assign NLI label and \( \nu \).
- **Knowledge Graph-based Memory:** Persistent constraint graphs and reasoning traces are temporal knowledge graphs; architectures like HippoRAG and Knowledge Graph of Thoughts provide patterns. Quick check: How does a graph node for “constraint \( k \in \{2,3,4,5\} \)” differ from a vector memory of the same string in queryability for verification?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
User query q -> Planner (outputs task DAG with objective node) -> Executor (updates persistent constraint graph, links code to constraints) -> Synthesizer (creates claim nodes, links to reasoning nodes) -> Validator (continuous entailment checks, contradiction detection) -> Provenance Store (graph DB: V = S_q ∪ R_q ∪ C_q, E = typed edges) -> Final report + provenance graph G_q.

**Critical path:** Query → Planning (store objective) → Execution loop (update constraint graph) → Synthesis (create claim→reasoning→source paths) → Continuous validation (compute ν, detect conflicts) → Final report with integrated provenance. Any broken link reduces PCov/PSnd.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Graph overhead vs. audit cost: storage/compute for graphs vs. manual audit time savings; assumption: graphs cheaper.  
- Validation latency: per-claim checks add delay; trade-off: prevention cheaper than correction.  
- Open vs. closed models: open weights allow entailment fine-tuning; closed models may have better reasoning but unverifiable activations.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low PCov (~0.33): orphaned claims, missing reasoning nodes.  
- Low PSnd (~0.25): high invalid citation rate, ν(c,s) below τ.  
- Low CTran (=0): conflicts in sources not surfaced due to aggregation.  
- High AEFF: human verification time ~ generation time; graph not queryable/comprehensible.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate Figure 4 contrast: implement RAG with black-box aggregation vs. explicit reasoning nodes; compute AAR metrics; expect PCov=1/3, PSnd=0.25, CTran=0 vs. perfect scores.  
2. Constraint persistence test: inject multi-constraint plan (e.g., “evaluate k=2,3,4,5”); run long execution (100+ steps) with/without persistent constraint graph; measure PCov for constraint-adherent claims; expect drift in stateless condition.  
3. Contradiction injection benchmark: provide sources with opposing claims; measure CTran and AEFF; expect CTran=0 and high AEFF without explicit contradicts edges.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Universal provenance schema standardization:** How to standardize a schema for semantic provenance graphs to enable cross-system auditing? Currently, PROV lacks claim-level structure; community-driven extension needed.
2. **Domain-specific entailment thresholds:** What are appropriate, calibrated thresholds \( \tau \) for PSnd? Entailment is context-sensitive; requires human annotation across domains to map NLI scores to soundness.
3. **Scalability of provenance graphs:** Can graphs be constructed and queried at scale (thousands of claims) without prohibitive overhead? The paper asserts cost control but provides no empirical scalability analysis.
4. **Real-time continuous validation:** How to architect validation to provide real-time feedback without unacceptable latency? The paper claims millisecond per claim but offers no implementation details or timing benchmarks.
5. **AAR benchmark design:** What benchmark effectively measures PCov, PSnd, CTran, AEFF? Existing benchmarks focus on fluency/completion; a proper benchmark requires tasks with ground-truth evidence links, contradictory sources, and human audit studies.

## Limitations
- Exact NLI model, prompt format, and calibrated threshold \( \tau \) for Provenance Soundness are unspecified; assumptions (e.g., RoBERTa-MNLI, \( \tau=0.5 \)) are untested.
- Concrete algorithmic rules for continuous validation (gating, re-retrieval triggers) are underspecified; implementation details are inferred.
- Scalability of graph construction and continuous NLI checks for long-horizon tasks lacks empirical latency data.
- Validation relies on a single curated case study; performance on diverse open-domain queries is unproven.

## Confidence
- **Claim-Level Traceability Mechanism:** High confidence (directly supported by formalism and case study contrast).
- **Constraint Persistence Mechanism:** Medium confidence (supported by bug example and architecture contrast, but validation protocol vague).
- **Contradiction Transparency Mechanism:** High confidence (clearly defined via NLI edges, empirically shown).
- **AAR Metrics Validation:** Medium confidence (internally validated but lack external benchmarking).
- **Architecture Trade-offs (Overhead/Latency):** Low confidence (asserted without empirical measurement).
