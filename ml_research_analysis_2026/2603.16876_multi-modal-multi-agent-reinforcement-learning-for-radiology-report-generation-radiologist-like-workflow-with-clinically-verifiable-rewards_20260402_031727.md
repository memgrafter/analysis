# Multi-Modal Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Radiology Report Generation: Radiologist-Like Workflow with Clinically Verifiable Rewards

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16876
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16876
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieved state-of-the-art clinical efficacy with RadGraph F1 scores of 0.294 on MIMIC-CXR and 0.337 on IU X-ray, and CheXbert F1 scores of 0.544 and 0.501, respectively, outperforming all compared methods.

## Executive Summary
Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for radiology report generation optimize a single monolithic model, which does not reflect a radiologist's sequential region-by-region examination and often suffers from laterality errors. This paper introduces MARL-Rad, a multi-modal multi-agent RL framework that decomposes chest X-ray interpretation into three region-specific agents (left, right, central) and a global integrating agent. The agents are jointly trained on-policy using clinically verifiable rewards (RadGraph F1, CheXbert F1, GREEN) to mimic the radiologist workflow. MARL-Rad achieves state-of-the-art clinical efficacy on both MIMIC-CXR and IU X-ray datasets, with significant improvements in laterality consistency and factual accuracy.

## Method Summary
MARL-Rad builds upon the MedGemma-4B vision-language model. The chest X-ray is partitioned into three anatomical zones; each region-specific agent processes its assigned crop to generate a draft findings and impression section. The global integrating agent then synthesizes these drafts into a final coherent report. The entire multi-agent system is optimized end-to-end using the MA-GSPO algorithm, an on-policy RL method. For each training image, multiple joint rollouts are sampled, and the resulting full reports are scored by a reward function that combines RadGraph F1 (entity/relation extraction), CheXbert F1 (disease classification), and GREEN (LLM-based clinical correctness). The shared group-relative advantage computed from this reward updates all agents' policies simultaneously, fostering cooperative specialization toward clinically accurate outputs.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art clinical efficacy: RadGraph F1 0.294 (MIMIC-CXR) and 0.337 (IU X-ray); CheXbert F1 0.544 and 0.501.
- Ablation study confirms joint multi-agent RL is essential; the training-free multi-agent workflow ("Multi-Agent w/o RL") degrades performance (RadGraph F1 0.106 vs. 0.299).
- Laterality consistency dramatically improved (e.g., right-lung RadGraph F1 rose from 0.142 to 0.277 on MIMIC-CXR).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decomposing the chest X-ray into left, right, and central regions and assigning dedicated agents reduces spatial reasoning interference and improves laterality consistency.
- **Mechanism:** Each region-specific agent processes visual features from its assigned anatomical zone, generating a localized diagnosis. This forces the model to focus on spatially coherent findings rather than treating the image as a global whole. The global integrating agent then synthesizes these region-specific drafts.
- **Core assumption:** Radiologists examine anatomical regions sequentially and discretely; mimicking this region-by-region workflow reduces left-right confusion errors common in global vision-language models.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "region-specific agents... to mimic a radiologist's region-by-region examination workflow"
  - [section 4.4] Laterality-specific RadGraph F1 improved from 0.142 (single-agent RL) to 0.277 (right) on MIMIC-CXR.
  - [section 4.5] Example shows left-agent outputs only left-lung findings, right-agent only right-lung findings.
  - [corpus] Weak: Neighboring papers (e.g., RadAgents [139], CXRAgent [79]) also use region decomposition but lack joint RL, making the causal link to laterality gains indirect.
- **Break condition:** If clinical findings inherently span multiple regions (e.g., bilateral pneumonia), rigid regional boundaries may fragment critical information, harming completeness.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Joint on-policy multi-agent reinforcement learning (MA-GSPO) coordinates agents better than training them independently or using a single monolithic model.
- **Mechanism:** All agents' policies are updated simultaneously using shared group-relative advantages computed from the final report's clinically verifiable reward. This joint optimization allows agents to implicitly learn complementary roles (e.g., central agent focusing on heart/mediastinum, left/right on lung fields) through credit assignment across the entire rollout.
- **Core assumption:** A multi-agent system achieves superior collaboration only when the entire system is optimized end-to-end with a shared objective, rather than relying on post-hoc composition of pretrained specialists.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "jointly trains multiple agents and optimizes the entire agentic system through reinforcement learning"
  - [section 4.3] Ablation: "Multi-Agent w/o RL" (Fig. 2 workflow without RL) performs worse than even single-agent RL on CE metrics (e.g., RadGraph F1: 0.106 vs. 0.299 on MIMIC-CXR).
  - [section 3.2] MA-GSPO objective (Eq. 5) shares advantage `Âi` across all agents, enabling cooperative policy updates.
- **Break condition:** Poor reward shaping or sparse signals could lead to lazy agent equilibria where some agents default to minimal outputs, relying on others.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Using clinically verifiable rewards (RadGraph, CheXbert, GREEN) instead of NLG metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) directly optimizes for factual and relational clinical correctness.
- **Mechanism:** The reward function `r(x_i, a)` combines:
  - RadGraph F1 (entity/relation extraction correctness),
  - CheXbert F1 (disease classification accuracy),
  - GREEN (LLM-based clinical correctness).
  This anchors policy gradients to outcomes that correlate with radiologist judgment, not just lexical similarity.
- **Core assumption:** Surface-level text similarity (BLEU/ROUGE) is a poor proxy for clinical utility; outcome-based rewards grounded in medical knowledge are necessary for high-stakes domains.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "optimized via clinically verifiable rewards"
  - [section 4.2] "Our method does not align with some NLG metrics, while the strong performance on CE metrics demonstrates its clinical effectiveness."
  - [section 1] Cites prior work [9, 70, 108] showing NLG metrics fail to reflect clinician assessments.
  - [corpus] Corpus shows other RL-RRG works (LM-RRG [145], BoxMed-RL [47]) also use clinical rewards, but MARL-Rad uniquely applies them to multi-agent optimization.
- **Break condition:** If clinical reward functions are noisy or biased (e.g., CheXbert misses rare findings), they may misguide the multi-agent system, especially if one agent's output disproportionately affects the reward.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**  
  *Why needed here:* MARL-Rad extends GSPO (a sequence-level RLVR method) to multi-agent settings. Understanding group-relative advantage and importance ratios is key to interpreting why joint policy updates work.  
  *Quick check question:* How does sharing the same advantage `Âi` across all agents in Eq. (5) differ from giving each agent its own per-step reward? What cooperative behavior might this encourage/discourage?

- **Concept: Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) Credit Assignment**  
  *Why needed here:* The framework assumes a shared team reward but distributed policies. The engineer must grasp why independent learning fails here (the "lazy agent" problem) and how joint on-policy updates mitigate it.  
  *Quick check question:* In the ablation, why did "Multi-Agent w/o RL" (same workflow, no RL) underperform single-agent RL? What does this imply about the role of RL in coordination versus the static workflow itself?

- **Concept: Clinical Efficacy Metrics vs. NLG Metrics**  
  *Why needed here:* The entire reward design prioritizes RadGraph/CheXbert/GREEN over BLEU/ROUGE. Engineers must understand why these clinical metrics are considered more valid for deployment.  
  *Quick check question:* If a generated report has high BLEU but low RadGraph F1, what clinical failure mode does that represent? (Answer: Lexical similarity to reference but incorrect factual/relational content.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Base model:** MedGemma-4B (vision-language model) initialized for each agent.
  - **Region-specific agents (3×):** Each receives image crops/features for its anatomical zone (left, right, central) and drafts a `### Findings` / `### Impression` for that region.
  - **Global integrating agent:** Takes the three regional drafts as context, synthesizes a final full report.
  - **RL loop:** For each query (CXR image + ground truth report), sample G joint rollouts from all agents. Compute reward `r(x_i, a)` using clinical metrics. Compute group-relative advantage `Âi`. Update all agents' policies via MA-GSPO (Eq. 5) using shared advantage.
  - **Reward calculator:** Runs RadGraph extraction, CheXbert classifier, and GREEN scorer on generated vs. reference reports.

- **Critical path:**  
  `Input CXR image` → `Region agents generate drafts in parallel` → `Global agent integrates drafts` → `Full report` → `Reward calculation (clinical metrics)` → `Policy gradient update for ALL agents simultaneously`.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *More agents (e.g., per-organ vs. per-hemithorax):* Finer decomposition may improve specialization but increases credit assignment difficulty and rollout cost.
  - *Reward weighting:* Unweighted sum (as used) assumes equal importance of RadGraph, CheXbert, GREEN; tuned weights could prioritize certain clinical aspects.
  - *Shared vs. separate base models:* Here agents are separate parameters from same MedGemma checkpoint; parameter sharing could reduce compute but risk losing specialization.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Laterality regression:** Drop in laterality-specific RadGraph F1 (Tab. 4) indicates agents are ignoring spatial boundaries.
  - **Reward hacking:** High GREEN score but low RadGraph/CheXbert suggests the model learns to generate superficially plausible but factually incorrect reports.
  - **Integration failure:** Global agent merely concatenates regional outputs instead of synthesizing (e.g., repeating "left lung clear, right lung clear" instead of "lungs clear").
  - **Training instability:** MA-GSPO clipping thresholds (`ε_high`, `ε_low`) too tight/lazy leads to no learning; monitor per-agent importance ratios `s_i(k)(θ_k)`.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablate joint RL:** Run "Multi-Agent w/o RL" as in Tab. 3 to confirm necessity of end-to-end optimization. Expect drop in CE metrics.
  2. **Region boundary ablation:** Change region definitions (e.g., split central into heart vs. mediastinum agents) to test sensitivity to decomposition granularity.
  3. **Reward component ablation:** Remove one reward component (e.g., only RadGraph + CheXbert) to measure each's contribution to final performance and failure modes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does MARL-Rad's multi-agent RL framework generalize effectively to other medical imaging modalities (e.g., ECG, echocardiography) or non-imaging clinical tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state in the conclusion: "Although this work focuses on radiology report generation, our MARL framework itself is not limited to chest X-rays and can be applied to other workflow-structured real-world tasks. Future work includes applying this framework to other modalities in medical AI, such as electrocardiogram (ECG) and echocardiography."
- **Why unresolved:** The study only evaluates MARL-Rad on chest X-ray datasets. The region-specific agent design assumes spatial anatomical divisions that may not exist in other modalities, and the clinical workflow alignment might not transfer.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement MARL-Rad for at least two additional modalities (e.g., ECG interpretation and echocardiography reporting), define appropriate decomposition strategies, and evaluate using clinically relevant metrics and clinician assessments to confirm generalizability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What causes the performance degradation observed when the multi-agent workflow is used without reinforcement learning ("Multi-Agent w/o RL") compared to both the vanilla model and full MARL-Rad?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Ablation studies (Table 3) show "Multi-Agent w/o RL" scores lower than "MedGemma (vanilla)" on several metrics. The authors note that "training-free agentification alone is insufficient," but do not investigate the underlying mechanism.
- **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether the drop stems from misalignment between agents, inadequate prompting for the base model, or the absence of reward signals needed to learn effective collaboration. The paper does not analyze agent outputs or training dynamics to pinpoint the cause.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct comparative analysis of agent communication, inter-agent agreement, and output consistency between the multi-agent systems with and without RL. Probe the base model's ability to follow the structured workflow via zero-shot prompting experiments, and examine gradient updates during RL to see how coordination emerges.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is the global integrating agent a necessary component, or could a deterministic aggregation of regional agents' outputs (e.g., concatenation, template-based merging) achieve similar clinical efficacy?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The architecture includes a global agent that synthesizes regional drafts (Fig. 2, Sec. 3.3). The authors claim it "adds relevant global findings" but provide no ablation or comparison to simpler merging strategies.
- **Why unresolved:** The contribution of the learned integration versus a rule-based merger is unknown. Removing it could simplify the system if unnecessary, or reveal that the synthesized output is critical for coherence and global consistency.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace the global agent with deterministic methods (e.g., direct concatenation, instruction-based merging without RL) and evaluate the resulting reports on clinical efficacy metrics (RadGraph, CheXbert) and linguistic quality. Human evaluation could assess whether the integration step improves readability and clinical soundness.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How do the individual components of the clinically verifiable reward (RadGraph F1, CheXbert F1, ROUGE-L) influence the balance between factual/clinical accuracy and linguistic naturalness?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The reward is defined as the unweighted sum of these three metrics (Sec. 3.3). The authors report improvements on all three in the main results but do not analyze their individual contributions or potential conflicts, despite noting that MARL-Rad does not align with some NLG metrics.
- **Why unresolved:** Without component-wise ablation, it is unclear whether one metric dominates the optimization or whether there are synergistic or antagonistic effects. The choice of equal weighting may not be optimal, and the observed trade-off with NLG metrics might be reward-ratio dependent.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train MARL-Rad with different subsets and weightings of the reward components (e.g., only RadGraph, only CheXbert, various linear combinations) and evaluate the full metric suite. This would map how each component shapes the generated reports and identify weightings that better balance clinical accuracy and linguistic naturalness.

## Limitations
- **Generalization:** SOTA on MIMIC-CXR/IU X-ray may not transfer to other institutions or modalities due to data distribution shifts and reward function biases (e.g., RadGraph trained on MIMIC).
- **Reward reliability:** Clinical metrics (RadGraph, CheXbert) are noisy proxies; their errors may compound in multi-agent credit assignment, especially for rare findings.
- **Clinical workflow alignment:** Mimicking radiologist "region-by-region" examination is assumed beneficial but not validated with real radiologists—could introduce unnatural fragmentation.
- **Computational cost:** Training 4 agents (3 regional + 1 global) via on-policy RL is resource-intensive; not justified against simpler ensembling or single-agent fine-tuning.

## Confidence
- **Region decomposition improves laterality:** Medium. Strong ablation metrics (Tab. 4) but no evidence that rigid boundaries match true radiologist cognitive patterns; bilateral findings may be harmed.
- **Joint RL necessity over static multi-agent:** Medium. "Multi-Agent w/o RL" ablation (Fig. 2) underperforms single-agent RL, implying RL coordination is key, but alternative explanations (e.g., pretraining gaps) not fully ruled out.
- **Clinical rewards > NLG metrics:** High. Consistent with broader clinical AI literature; paper shows clear metric divergence (Sec. 4.2), though GREEN’s LLM basis introduces its own biases.

## Next Checks
1. **External hospital validation:** Test on a held-out institutional dataset (e.g., Stanford ChestX-ray) to measure performance drop and reward metric correlation with radiologist adjudication.
2. **Reward component ablation:** Isolate RadGraph, CheXbert, and GREEN contributions in the reward sum; measure conflicts (e.g., does GREEN "forgive" factual errors that RadGraph penalizes?).
3. **Bilateral findings stress test:** Curate a subset of reports with truly bilateral pathologies (e.g., "bilateral effusions"); compare region-agent outputs for coherence and global agent integration quality vs. single-agent baseline.
