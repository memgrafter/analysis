# On the Emotion Understanding of Synthesized Speech

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16483
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16483
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** SER models experience a severe performance drop on synthesized speech (15–50% accuracy) compared to human speech (~80–100%), primarily due to a representation mismatch introduced during speech token prediction.

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically evaluates the ability of Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) models to generalize from human to machine-synthesized speech. Across both discriminative (e.g., Emotion2vec) and generative (e.g., GPT-4o Audio) models, a consistent and substantial performance gap is observed. The root cause is identified as a representation mismatch originating in the discrete speech token prediction stage of modern TTS/S2S pipelines. Subsequent stages (flow matching, vocoder) contribute minimally. Furthermore, generative Speech LLMs exhibit "text dominance," relying on textual semantics rather than paralinguistic acoustic cues, a behavior resistant to prompt engineering. Attempts to improve generalization via domain-adversarial fine-tuning fail, suggesting current SER models exploit non-robust, synthesis-specific shortcuts rather than invariant emotional features.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates SER models on human datasets (TESS, CREMA-D, IEMOCAP, RAVDESS, MELD) and synthesized speech generated by TTS (CosyVoice2, IndexTTS2) and S2S LLMs (Kimi-Audio, GLM4-Voice). Synthesized samples were manually filtered to ensure clear emotional expression. Primary evaluations used the open-source discriminative model Emotion2vec-base and commercial/generative models (Qwen3-Omni, GPT-4o Audio, Gemini 2.5 Pro). Key analyses included: 1) comparing zero-shot SER accuracy between human and synthetic speech; 2) isolating the contribution of synthesis stages (token prediction, flow matching, vocoder) via controlled ablation; 3) probing the SER model's embedding space (t-SNE, linear classification) to diagnose encoded information; and 4) testing domain-adversarial fine-tuning and prompt variations for Speech LLMs.

## Key Results
- SER models (Emotion2vec) show a 30–85 percentage point accuracy drop on synthesized speech versus human speech across multiple datasets and synthesis methods.
- The speech token prediction stage is the dominant source of the performance gap; errors in flow matching and vocoding have negligible impact.
- Generative Speech LLMs display "text dominance," accurately inferring emotion from lexical content but failing on same-text/different-prosody datasets (e.g., TESS), unaffected by acoustic-focused prompts.
- Linear probes on frozen SER embeddings can classify the synthesis model/vocoder with >96% accuracy but struggle to classify emotion (~60%), indicating encoding of domain-specific shortcuts.
- Both standard and domain-adversarial fine-tuning fail to produce models that generalize to unseen synthesis models or human speech.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The speech token prediction stage in TTS/S2S synthesis introduces a representation mismatch that is the primary cause of SER performance degradation on synthesized speech.
- **Mechanism:** Autoregressive language models generate discrete speech tokens that systematically differ from the token distributions produced by human speech tokenizers. These differences shift the representation space of downstream SER models (trained on human tokens), making emotional features unrecoverable.
- **Core assumption:** SER models rely on representations derived from human speech token distributions; disruption at the token level propagates through flow matching and vocoder stages with minimal additional degradation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...largely because speech token prediction during synthesis induces a representation mismatch between synthesized and human speech."
  - [section 5.3] Ablation study isolating synthesis stages shows token generation errors cause a >50% accuracy drop, while vocoder and flow matching errors are negligible.
  - [section 5.1] t-SNE visualizations show synthesized speech embeddings cluster separately from human speech, correlated with synthesis model/vocoder, not emotion.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Generative Speech LLMs exhibit "text dominance," inferring emotion from textual semantics while ignoring paralinguistic cues, and this is resilient to prompt engineering.
- **Mechanism:** SLMs are trained on massive text-speech paired data where textual semantics strongly correlate with emotion. They learn a shortcut: predicting emotion from the ASR transcript rather than acoustic-prosodic features.
- **Core assumption:** SLMs' internal architectures and objectives prioritize linguistic over paralinguistic processing; emotion labels co-occur with semantic content in training data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] SLMs achieve high SER accuracy on datasets where text semantics determine emotion (StepEval, Multi-speaker SE) but fail on TESS/CREMA-D where same text expresses different emotions via prosody.
  - [appendix B] Explicit prompts instructing models to ignore text and use only acoustic features do not improve performance.
  - [section 4.3] Performance drop coincides with datasets requiring prosodic inference, not lexical.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Current SER models exploit non-robust synthesis-specific shortcuts rather than fundamental emotional features, making them inherently vulnerable to domain shifts.
- **Mechanism:** Models learn high-frequency or model-specific artifacts (e.g., vocoder noise, tokenization patterns) that correlate with emotion labels in human training data but are absent or altered in synthetic speech.
- **Core assumption:** Supervision from human-labeled datasets leads to learning of spurious correlations that do not reflect invariant emotional correlates in the acoustic signal.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.1] Linear probes on frozen Emotion2vec embeddings easily classify synthesis model/vocoder (96%+ accuracy) but poorly classify emotion (~60%).
  - [section 5.2] Domain-adversarial fine-tuning fails to improve out-of-distribution generalization; models still encode domain information that hurts SER on unseen synthesis models.
  - [table 1] Models trained on human speech fail on synthetic and vice-versa, confirming distinct learned features.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Speech Synthesis Pipeline (Token → Mel → Waveform)**
  - Why needed here: The paper's core diagnosis isolates the token prediction stage. Understanding the three-stage flow (language model → flow matching → vocoder) is essential to interpret the ablation experiments and why token errors dominate.
  - Quick check: If you replace ground-truth mel-spectrograms with synthesized ones (using ground-truth tokens), does SER accuracy drop significantly? What does that imply about the flow matching stage?

- **Concept: Representation Space & Linear Probing**
  - Why needed here: The authors visualize and probe the embedding space of Emotion2vec to test what information is encoded (synthesis artifacts vs. emotion). This is key evidence for shortcut learning.
  - Quick check: What does it mean if a linear classifier on frozen embeddings can perfectly distinguish "synthesized vs. human" but struggles with the emotion task?

- **Concept: Domain Generalization & Domain-Adversarial Training (DANN)**
  - Why needed here: The paper evaluates cross-domain SER (human → synthetic) and attempts DANN to force domain-invariant features. Understanding the goal and mechanics of DANN (gradient reversal) is crucial to interpret Table 4.
  - Quick check: If DANN increases domain classifier loss but does not improve OOD SER accuracy, what does that suggest about the learned representations?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - SER Model: Emotion2vec -> log-Mel spectrogram -> emotion logits
  - Synthesis Pipeline: Text -> Speech Tokens (AR LM) -> Mel (Flow Matching + Ref Audio) -> Waveform (Vocoder)
  - Evaluation Engine: Audio (human/synthetic) -> SER Model or SLM -> Predicted Emotion -> Compare to Ground Truth
  - Analysis Tools: t-SNE; Linear Probes on frozen embeddings; Domain-Adversarial setup with Gradient Reversal Layer

- **Critical path:**
  1. Establish baseline: Run Emotion2vec on human vs. filtered synthetic speech → large gap.
  2. Isolate cause: Ablate synthesis stages (use ground-truth tokens/mels) → token prediction stage accounts for most error.
  3. Diagnose representation: Visualize/t-SNE embeddings → synthetic samples form separate clusters; probe shows synthesis model easily classified, emotion poorly.
  4. Attempt fix: Fine-tune with mixed data + DANN → fails to generalize to OOD synthesis models.
  5. Test SLMs: Evaluate Qwen3-Omni/GPT-4o Audio on text-emotion mismatched datasets → text dominance.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Manual filtering ensures test set quality but limits scale and introduces subjectivity.
  - Frozen vs. fine-tuned backbone: Freezing avoids catastrophic forgetting but may limit adaptation; fine-tuning on synthetic data improves in-domain but not OOD performance.
  - Synthetic data source: Using strong TTS and S2S LLMs provides relevant artifacts; weaker models may not expose the same gap.
  - Evaluation metric: Accuracy assumes single dominant emotion; confusion matrices reveal systematic confusions.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - SER discriminative model: Non-diagonal confusion matrix on filtered synthesized speech; large accuracy drop (>30 pp) from human to synthetic; embeddings clustered by synthesis model in t-SNE.
  - SLM: High accuracy on datasets where text determines emotion, low accuracy on same-text/different-emotion datasets; no improvement with "ignore text" prompts.
  - DANN: Domain classifier loss decreases (adversarial success) but OOD SER accuracy does not improve.
  - Generalization: Model trained on synthetic data performs poorly on human speech and vice-versa; poor OOD accuracy on unseen synthesis models/datasets.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline human vs. synthetic SER:** For a given SER model (Emotion2vec), compute accuracy on human test set (TESS/CREMA-D) and on synthetic set (same text, synthesized by CosyVoice2). Expect ~80% vs. ~30% gap, confirming domain shift.
  2. **Stage-wise ablation:** For TESS, compute SER accuracy on: (a) real human; (b) vocoder-reconstructed from ground-truth mel; (c) flow-matched from ground-truth tokens; (d) fully synthesized from text tokens. Expect major drop at (d), minimal at (b) and (c).
  3. **SLM text dominance test:** Evaluate SLM on (a) StepEval (text determines emotion) and (b) TESS (same text, different prosodic emotion). Expect high accuracy on (a), low on (b). Then run with prompts forcing acoustic-only focus; expect negligible change.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Why do generative Speech Language Models (SLMs) exhibit persistent "text dominance" for emotion inference, ignoring paralinguistic cues even with explicit prompt engineering?
- How can the representation mismatch introduced by discrete speech token prediction in TTS/S2S models be mitigated to enable robust SER on synthesized speech?
- Why does standard and domain-adversarial fine-tuning fail to produce SER models that generalize to unseen synthesis models or datasets, despite the objective to learn domain-invariant emotion features?
- Can large-scale synthetic speech data with reliable pseudo-emotion labels (from human verification or synth-conditioned SER) improve intrinsic emotion representation learning, or does it entrench the synthetic artifact problem?

## Limitations
- Unspecified TTS/S2S prompts and manual filtering protocol hinders reproducibility.
- Missing dataset splits and DANN hyperparameters prevent exact replication.
- Results from commercial models (GPT-4o, Gemini) depend on unspecified API versions.
- Potential confounders (speaker identity, recording environment) are not fully controlled.
- Conclusions based on limited set of synthesis models; future architectures (e.g., diffusion-based) may differ.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (token prediction mismatch):** High confidence. Stage-wise ablation shows >50% accuracy drop at token generation; t-SNE visualizations reveal domain separation; linear probes indicate domain information dominates emotion.
- **Mechanism 2 (text dominance in Speech LLMs):** High confidence. Performance strongly correlates with text-emotion association; explicit acoustic-only prompts yield negligible improvement.
- **Mechanism 3 (shortcut learning):** Medium confidence. Linear probes successfully classify synthesis model/vocoder but not emotion; DANN fails to improve OOD generalization, although alternative explanations (e.g., limited feature capacity) are possible.
- **Overall finding (SER models fail on synthesized speech):** High confidence. Consistent across discriminative and generative models, multiple datasets, and varied synthesis methods.

## Next Checks
1. **Token distribution comparison:** Extract discrete speech tokens from human and synthetic utterances of identical text. Compute distribution similarity metrics (FID, token precision/recall) to directly quantify the mismatch.
2. **Prompt robustness study:** For each TTS model, generate speech using a range of emotion prompts. Annotate emotion intensity and evaluate SER performance to assess gap persistence and correlation with perceived emotional strength.
3. **Domain generalization method sweep:** Systematically vary DANN hyperparameters and compare with alternative methods (CDAN, MMD). Test on held-out synthesis models to determine whether failure is methodological or fundamental.
