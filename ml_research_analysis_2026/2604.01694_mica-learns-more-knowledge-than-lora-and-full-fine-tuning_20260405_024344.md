# MiCA Learns More Knowledge Than LoRA and Full Fine-Tuning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01694
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01694
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** MiCA achieves up to 5.9x improvement in knowledge acquisition while using only 6-60% of LoRA's trainable parameters, outperforming both on domain-specific benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
MiCA (Minor Component Adaptation) is a novel parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for large language models that directs weight updates toward the minor (least salient) singular subspaces of pre-trained weight matrices, in contrast to LoRA's focus on dominant subspaces. By freezing the minor subspace basis vectors and training only a projection matrix, MiCA integrates new factual knowledge more effectively and with far fewer trainable parameters. Evaluations on two domain-specific knowledge benchmarks (BLOGS-MC and HISTORY-MC) show consistent gains over LoRA and full fine-tuning, with superior parameter efficiency and stability in continued pre-training scenarios.

## Method Summary
MiCA leverages singular value decomposition (SVD) on a pre-trained weight matrix \( W = U\Sigma V^\top \). It initializes a frozen adapter matrix \( B \) with the last \( r \) columns of the left singular vectors \( U \) (corresponding to the smallest singular values), and a trainable matrix \( A \) initialized to zero. During fine-tuning, only \( A \) is updated, and the adapted output is \( Wx + BAx \). This constrains updates to the minor subspaces of \( W \). The method is applied to the `q_proj` and `v_proj` layers. After continued pre-training, the adapter weights are merged into the base model before applying a separate instruction-tuning delta. This contrasts with LoRA, which uses randomly initialized \( A \) and \( B \) (both trainable) spanning no prescribed subspace.

## Key Results
- **Knowledge acquisition:** On Llama-2-7B, MiCA achieves 61.33% accuracy on BLOGS-MC vs. LoRA's 58.28% and full FT's 56.18%. On a German history task, MiCA scores 39.2% vs. LoRA's 29.4% and full FT's 30.4%.
- **Parameter efficiency:** MiCA uses only 6-60% of LoRA's trainable parameters while delivering up to 5.9x more knowledge gain per parameter.
- **Subspace superiority:** Ablation studies confirm that adapting minor subspaces outperforms adapting major or random subspaces, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed constrained update direction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism is that new factual knowledge is often encoded in the minor singular subspaces of a model's weights, which are less occupied by general, high-variance features. By freezing the minor subspace basis (\( B \)) and learning only the projection coefficients (\( A \)), MiCA performs a low-rank update precisely in this underutilized subspace. This minimizes interference with the model's existing major capabilities (which reside in the dominant subspaces) and reduces the risk of catastrophic forgetting. LoRA, by contrast, adapts a random low-rank subspace that may intersect with major components, leading to less efficient knowledge integration and greater forgetting. The direct initialization of \( B \) from the actual minor components of the pre-trained weights ensures the update direction is aligned with the model's existing latent structure, promoting stability and faster convergence.

## Foundational Learning
- **SVD and singular subspaces**  
  *Why needed:* Decomposes weight matrices into orthogonal directions of varying importance; minor subspaces contain low-variance features suitable for new knowledge injection.  
  *Quick check:* Confirm `torch.linalg.svd(W)` returns matrices where singular values in `Σ` are sorted descending; MiCA uses columns of `U` corresponding to smallest values.
  
- **Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PeFT)**  
  *Why needed:* Enables adaptation of massive LLMs with limited compute and memory by updating only a small subset of parameters.  
  *Quick check:* Verify that total trainable parameters for MiCA are \( r \times d \) (only \( A \)), while LoRA is \( 2 \times r \times d \) (both \( A \) and \( B \)).
  
- **Catastrophic forgetting**  
  *Why needed:* Continual learning on new domains can degrade performance on previously learned tasks; constraining updates to minor subspaces mitigates this.  
  *Quick check:* Evaluate on general benchmarks (e.g., TruthfulQA, HellaSwag) before and after fine-tuning to ensure no significant drop.
  
- **Knowledge acquisition in LLMs**  
  *Why needed:* The paper's goal is to integrate new factual knowledge efficiently, not just improve task performance.  
  *Quick check:* Use domain-specific multiple-choice benchmarks (BLOGS-MC, HISTORY-MC) that test recall of facts from the training corpus.
  
- **Singular value ordering**  
  *Why needed:* The distinction between "major" (largest singular values) and "minor" (smallest) subspaces is fundamental to MiCA's design.  
  *Quick check:* After SVD, check that `U[:, -r:]` indeed corresponds to the smallest singular values by inspecting the diagonal of `Σ`.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Pre-trained Weights -> SVD Decomposition -> B (frozen, minor subspace basis) + A (trainable) -> Fine-tuning -> Merge Adapter -> Instruct-Tuned Model -> Evaluation

**Critical path:**  
1. Load pre-trained weight matrix \( W \).  
2. Compute full SVD: \( W = U\Sigma V^\top \).  
3. Initialize \( B = U[:, -r:] \) (frozen), \( A = 0 \) (trainable).  
4. For each forward pass: \( h = Wx + BAx \).  
5. Train only \( A \) on domain corpus.  
6. Merge \( BA \) into \( W \), then add instruction-tuning delta.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Minor subspace rank \( r \):* Larger \( r \) increases capacity but risks moving into more dominant subspaces; paper finds small \( r \) (16-32) optimal.  
- *Frozen vs. trainable:* Freezing \( B \) drastically reduces parameters but forfeits adaptation of the basis itself; this tradeoff yields efficiency gains.  
- *Stability vs. flexibility:* Constraining to minor subspaces enhances stability (less forgetting) but may limit the complexity of knowledge that can be acquired.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Wrong subspace selection:** Using top-\( r \) singular vectors (major) instead of bottom-\( r \) (minor) will likely underperform and defeat the method's purpose.  
- **Evaluation misalignment:** Non-greedy decoding or incorrect prompt templates will corrupt accuracy measurements, as the benchmark expects a single letter token.  
- **Hyperparameter mismatch:** Using LoRA-optimized hyperparameters (e.g., LR, epochs) for MiCA may lead to suboptimal results due to different optimization dynamics.

**First three experiments:**  
1. **SVD initialization check:** For a linear layer, compute SVD and verify that `B`'s columns are orthogonal and correspond to the smallest `r` singular values. Confirm `B` remains frozen after training.  
2. **One-token evaluation check:** With a known training sample, run evaluation using `do_sample=False, max_new_tokens=1` and confirm the generated token matches the expected answer letter deterministically.  
3. **Hyperparameter replication:** Train MiCA and LoRA on the BLOGS corpus using the exact ranks, learning rates, and epochs from Table 1 (e.g., Llama: MiCA r=16, LR=5e-4, epochs=4; LoRA r=128, LR=1e-4, epochs=8) and verify relative parameter counts.

## Limitations
- **Data unavailable:** The BLOGS and HISTORY corpora and their associated MCQ evaluations are not publicly released, preventing direct independent replication.
- **Implementation ambiguity:** SVD computation details (full vs. truncated, numerical precision, GPU acceleration) are unspecified, risking incorrect adapter initialization.
- **Evaluation sensitivity:** Results depend on precise prompt templates and greedy one-token decoding; minor deviations could significantly alter reported accuracy.
- **Statistical reporting:** Ablation studies lack means and standard deviations across random seeds, limiting assessment of result robustness.

## Confidence
- **MiCA method correctness:** Medium – description is clear but SVD implementation nuances introduce uncertainty.
- **Knowledge acquisition gains:** Low – performance numbers rely on unavailable data and sensitive hyperparameters.
- **Parameter efficiency claim:** High – trainable parameter counts follow directly from stated rank choices.
- **Ablation superiority of minor subspaces:** Low – no access to raw results or variance estimates prevents full validation.
