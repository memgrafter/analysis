# Communication-Efficient and Robust Multi-Modal Federated Learning via Latent-Space Consensus

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.19067
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19067
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Primary result**: CoMFed reduces uplink communication to hundreds of kilobytes while achieving comparable or better multi-modal federated learning accuracy and robustness versus state-of-the-art baselines.

## Executive Summary
Multi-modal federated learning (FL) with heterogeneous clients poses challenges: clients possess different sensor modalities and model architectures, making traditional parameter sharing difficult, while communication costs can explode if full models or logits are exchanged. CoMFed addresses this by learning a projection matrix on each client that compresses intermediate features into a shared low-dimensional latent space. Clients periodically exchange per-class mean latent representations, whose size is linear in the number of classes and far smaller than model parameters. A latent-space regularizer aligns local representations to a globally agreed consensus, and geometric median aggregation is used to robustify against Byzantine clients. Evaluated on inertial-sensor (USC-HAD) and mmWave/LiDAR (DeepSense) datasets, CoMFed matches or exceeds the test accuracy of Harmony, FedMD, and FedIoT while consuming only hundreds of kilobytes of uplink traffic compared to gigabytes for Harmony. The approach also converges faster and demonstrates resilience to simple Byzantine attacks.

## Method Summary
CoMFed operates in two intertwined phases. Locally, each client processes mini-batches through its task-specific feature extractor and then applies a learned projection matrix to obtain d-dimensional latent vectors. These latents are accumulated separately for each class, yielding per-class mean vectors. These means are uploaded to the central server. The server aggregates the received per-class vectors across clients using the geometric median, producing a robust global consensus for each class. The consensus is broadcast back to all clients. Each client then computes a regularization loss that minimizes the divergence (e.g., KL) between its own per-class latent distributions and the global consensus. This loss, combined with the standard task loss, guides updates to both the model parameters and the projection matrix. By sharing only compact per-class statistics rather than full logits or weights, communication is drastically reduced, while the latent alignment ensures that heterogeneous models learn compatible representations. The geometric median step provides Byzantine robustness without increasing payload size.

## Key Results
- **Communication reduction**: Uplink traffic is reduced to hundreds of kilobytes, whereas Harmony requires gigabytes to reach comparable accuracy.
- **Accuracy**: CoMFed attains higher or comparable test accuracy to Harmony, FedMD, and FedIoT on both USC-HAD and DeepSense.
- **Efficiency and robustness**: Faster convergence than baselines and resilience to Byzantine clients with minimal accuracy degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CoMFed succeeds because it replaces heavyweight model or logit exchanges with lightweight per-class latent summaries. The projection matrices compress high-dimensional features into a common space that retains class-discriminative information; the small size (O(|M|·d)) makes communication feasible. The latent-space regularizer forces local models to align their internal representations, effectively performing a form of distributed distillation that harmonizes heterogeneous architectures without requiring parameter matching. Using geometric median for aggregating the per-class statistics mitigates the influence of malicious or corrupted updates, offering robustness at no extra communication cost. This combination yields both efficiency and security in multi-modal FL.

## Foundational Learning
- **Federated Learning (FL)**: A distributed training paradigm where clients keep data locally and only exchange model updates.
  - *Why needed*: CoMFed builds on FL to preserve privacy and decentralization.
  - *Quick check*: Verify that no raw data is transmitted; only compact statistics are transmitted.
- **Multi-modal Learning**: Integrating information from diverse data types (e.g., inertial, mmWave, LiDAR).
  - *Why needed*: The target scenario involves clients with heterogeneous modalities; CoMFed must handle modality-specific feature extractors.
  - *Quick check*: Ensure each client's local model accepts its native modality and that the projection maps to a shared space.
- **Latent Space Alignment**: Mapping local features to a shared low-dimensional space via learned projections.
  - *Why needed*: Enables compression and semantic interoperability across heterogeneous models.
  - *Quick check*: Evaluate the discriminative power of the projected latents (e.g., via linear probe accuracy).
- **Geometric Median**: A robust aggregation operator that minimizes the sum of distances to points, resistant to outliers.
  - *Why needed*: Protects the consensus from Byzantine clients that may send corrupted per-class means.
  - *Quick check*: Simulate outlier vectors and compare stability of geometric median vs. arithmetic mean.
- **Per-class Statistics**: Maintaining separate aggregation for each class label.
  - *Why needed*: Preserves class-specific information while keeping the message size small (linear in number of classes, not model size).
  - *Quick check*: Compare alignment quality when using per-class means versus a single global mean.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Local Feature Extractor -> Projection Matrix -> d-dimensional Latent -> Per-Class Mean Calculator -> Uplink -> Server (Geometric Median Aggregator) -> Downlink -> Client (Global Per-Class Means) -> Regularization Loss -> Local Optimizer -> Model Update.

**Critical Path**: For each communication round: (1) clients compute per-class latent means on local data; (2) clients upload means to server; (3) server aggregates per-class means via geometric median; (4) server broadcasts aggregated means; (5) clients compute regularization loss aligning their per-class distributions to the consensus; (6) clients update model and projection matrix. This chain must succeed to maintain consensus.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- *Communication vs. Accuracy*: Smaller projection dimension d reduces bandwidth but may harm alignment quality and final accuracy.
- *Robustness vs. Computation*: Geometric median offers strong Byzantine resistance but is computationally heavier than averaging; approximations might be needed for large client sets.
- *Heterogeneity vs. Convergence*: Latent alignment mitigates heterogeneity but can slow convergence if client data distributions differ significantly across classes.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Diverging or exploding regularization loss, indicating misalignment.
- Sudden drop in accuracy on certain client modalities.
- High variance in per-class mean vectors across consecutive rounds.
- Server aggregation fails to converge within a reasonable number of iterations, often due to many outliers.

**First Experiments**:
1. *Replication*: Reproduce the original experiments on USC-HAD and DeepSense with identical splits and hyperparameters to confirm reported accuracy (±2%) and communication volume (±10%).
2. *Communication-Accuracy Tradeoff*: Vary the latent dimension d (e.g., 4, 8, 16, 32) and measure test accuracy and uplink payload per round to characterize the tradeoff curve.
3. *Robustness Stress Test*: Introduce 20% Byzantine clients performing label-flipping, sign-flipping, and targeted attacks; compare CoMFed with geometric median against mean aggregation and Krum in terms of final accuracy and resilience.

## Limitations
- Validation is limited to two datasets (inertial and mmWave/LiDAR); generalization to other modalities or larger client populations remains uncertain.
- The computational overhead of learning projection matrices and computing geometric median per class may offset communication savings, especially with many classes or clients.
- The method assumes each client possesses samples from all classes, which may not hold under severe class imbalance or few-shot scenarios, potentially weakening alignment.
