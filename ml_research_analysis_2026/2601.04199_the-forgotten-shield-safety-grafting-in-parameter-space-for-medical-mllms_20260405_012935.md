# The Forgotten Shield: Safety Grafting in Parameter-Space for Medical MLLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04199
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04199
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: PSI restores safety dramatically (e.g., HarmBench-FigStep from 0.2375 to 0.9675, MedSafetyBench-QR from 0.6000 to 0.9422) with minimal medical performance loss (MedQA USMLE drop 2.0%).

## Executive Summary
Medical large language models fine-tuned for medical tasks suffer from catastrophic forgetting of safety alignment, becoming vulnerable to jailbreak attacks. This paper introduces Parameter-Space Intervention (PSI), a framework that disentangles intrinsic safety representations from medical capability vectors and merges them via layer-wise coefficients optimized by CMA-ES. Evaluated on Lingshu-7B and HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B, PSI achieves near-perfect safety on cross-modality jailbreaks while preserving core medical accuracy (MedQA USMLE drop ~2%). PSI outperforms baselines like ModelMerge and RESTA, demonstrating a viable path to re-align safety in domain-adapted MLLMs.

## Method Summary
PSI operates in three stages. First, it extracts a safety vector by un-aligning the base model on a small harmful dataset to create an unsafe variant, computing `v_raw^s = θ_base − θ_unsafe`. The medical capability vector is obtained from the fine-tuned medical model as `v_raw^m = θ_med − θ_base`. Second, these raw vectors are orthogonalized using Gram-Schmidt to produce a safety direction that does not interfere with medical knowledge: `v_⊥^s = v_raw^s − proj_{v_raw^m}(v_raw^s)`, followed by L2-normalization per layer. Third, a layer-wise merging procedure adds scaled copies of the unit vectors to the base model: `θ_target^(l) = θ_base^(l) + α^(l)·ˆv_s^(l) + β^(l)·ˆv_m^(l)`. The coefficients `[α^(l), β^(l)]` for each layer are optimized with CMA-ES to maximize a reward that balances safety (1−ASR on benchmarks) and medical performance (accuracy on MedQA, VQA-RAD, etc.). This black-box search navigates the trade-off space without requiring differentiable metrics, yielding a model that recovers safety while retaining most medical capability.

## Key Results
- Dramatic safety restoration: HarmBench-FigStep improves from 0.2375 to 0.9675; MedSafetyBench-QR from 0.6000 to 0.9422.
- Minimal medical performance loss: MedQA USMLE score drops only about 2.0% after PSI re-alignment.
- Superior to baselines: PSI outperforms ModelMerge and RESTA, achieving near-perfect safety on cross-modality jailbreaks while preserving core medical accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Orthogonal Disentanglement
- **Claim**: Orthogonal disentanglement of safety and medical parameter vectors prevents destructive interference during merging.
- **Mechanism**: Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization projects the raw safety vector onto a subspace orthogonal to the medical performance vector, ensuring that adjusting safety coefficients does not shift parameters along the medical capability axis.
- **Core assumption**: The intrinsic safety knowledge (`v_s`) and medical fine-tuning knowledge (`v_m`) are linearly separable directions in the parameter space.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section 3.2 detail the derivation; orthogonalization eliminates interference, while neighbor works do not address this.
- **Break condition**: If `v_raw^s` and `v_raw^m` are perfectly collinear, orthogonalization fails to produce a meaningful safety component.

### Mechanism 2: Layer-Wise Coefficient Optimization
- **Claim**: Layer-wise coefficients (via CMA-ES) are necessary because safety and medical capabilities are non-uniformly distributed across transformer layers.
- **Mechanism**: Independent α^(l) and β^(l) per layer allow the optimizer to modulate injection intensity at different depths, addressing layer-specific vulnerabilities that global scaling misses.
- **Core assumption**: Different layers specialize in distinct functions (e.g., syntax vs. reasoning), requiring fine-grained intervention.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions layer-wise coefficients; Section 4.3 shows layer-wise achieving near-perfect safety (e.g., HarmBench-FigStep 1.0000) vs. model-wise (0.9675).
- **Break condition**: If layers encode identical information, layer-wise offers no benefit over global coefficients but adds cost.

### Mechanism 3: CMA-ES Black-Box Optimization
- **Claim**: CMA-ES effectively navigates the high-dimensional trade-off space where gradient-based methods fail.
- **Mechanism**: CMA-ES iteratively samples and evaluates candidate coefficient vectors `x ∈ R^{2L}` on a validation set, updating its distribution to converge to a Pareto-optimal solution for reward `R = λ₁S_med + λ₂S_safe`.
- **Core assumption**: The safety-performance trade-off can be formulated as a differentiable reward over discrete metrics, and a satisfactory solution exists within the searched subspace.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section 3.3 justify black-box use; Section 4.4 ablation shows random coefficients cause collapse, while CMA-ES finds stable solutions.
- **Break condition**: If validation metrics are extremely noisy or search space is too large (L large), CMA-ES may fail to converge within reasonable compute.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Task Vectors in Parameter Space  
  *Why needed here*: PSI represents capabilities as displacement vectors (`v_raw^s`, `v_raw^m`), viewing knowledge as directions in weight space to enable grafting.  
  *Quick check*: If `θ_med` is fine-tuned from `θ_base`, what does `v_raw^m` intuitively represent about the model's new capability?

- **Concept**: Gram-Schmidt Orthogonalization  
  *Why needed here*: Core disentanglement step uses this to ensure safety updates do not alter medical capability; engineers must understand projection to diagnose interference.  
  *Quick check*: Why is `v_⊥^s` guaranteed to have zero dot product with `v_raw^m` after orthogonalization?

- **Concept**: Black-Box Optimization with CMA-ES  
  *Why needed here*: Safety evaluation uses non-differentiable LLM-as-a-Judge; CMA-ES is the chosen gradient-free optimizer for the 2L-dimensional search.  
  *Quick check*: Why can't you use SGD here, and what property of CMA-ES makes it suitable?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:

1. Base Model (θ_base): Pre-trained MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B).
2. Unsafe Model (θ_unsafe): Obtained by reverse gradient updates on θ_base using a small harmful dataset.
3. Vector Extractors: Compute raw safety and medical vectors via subtraction.
4. Orthogonalization Module: Applies Gram-Schmidt to produce orthogonal vectors, then normalizes to unit directions.
5. CMA-ES Optimizer: Searches over layer-wise coefficients [α^(1..L), β^(1..L)].
6. Parameter Grafting Layer-wise: For each layer l, θ_target^(l) = θ_base^(l) + α^(l)·ˆv_s^(l) + β^(l)·ˆv_m^(l).
7. Validation Suite: Medical datasets (MedQA, VQA-RAD) and safety benchmarks (HarmBench, MedSafetyBench) with LLM-as-a-Judge.

**Critical path**: θ_base → extract vectors → orthogonalize → initialize CMA-ES → sample coefficients → graft parameters → evaluate on safety/medical metrics → compute reward → update CMA-ES distribution → repeat until convergence → return θ_target.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Granularity*: Layer-wise vs. model-wise. Layer-wise improves safety robustness but increases search cost (2L vs. 2 dimensions).
- *Safety-Performance Balance*: Controlled by λ₁, λ₂ in reward; tuning shifts the Pareto frontier.
- *Vector Derivation*: Using θ_unsafe requires a harmful dataset and un-alignment steps; alternative safety representations could reduce overhead.

**Failure signatures**:
- Random coefficient merge: Severe medical performance collapse (e.g., MedQA MCMLE ∼0.44 vs. 0.7456).
- Poor orthogonalization: High cosine similarity between v_⊥^s and v_raw^m indicates residual coupling; safety injection still affects medical capability.
- CMA-ES stagnation: No reward improvement over many iterations; may indicate inadequate population size or too narrow initialization.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Compute cosine similarity between `v_raw^s` and `v_raw^m` before and after orthogonalization; expect post-ortho similarity ≈ 0.
2. Apply global α=β=1 (naive sum) to `θ_base`; measure safety and performance; expect catastrophic forgetting reversal but performance drop.
3. Run CMA-ES with L=2 (simplified) to visualize reward landscape; verify it finds a peak distinct from random sampling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scalability**: Can PSI scale to 70B+ models, and is CMA-ES feasible at that scale?  
2. **Orthogonalization Validity**: Do safety and medical capabilities truly occupy orthogonal subspaces across architectures? Does the Gram-Schmidt step sometimes discard useful, domain-specific safety knowledge?  
3. **Broader Model Properties**: Does PSI preserve fairness across demographics, calibration, and general-purpose reasoning outside medicine?  
4. **Safety Vector Derivation**: Is the un-alignment approach optimal? Could more efficient or robust methods identify safety-critical subspaces without full un-alignment?  
5. **Multimodal Vulnerabilities**: Does PSI specifically address cross-modality jailbreak weaknesses, or does it mainly boost textual safety? Which layers are responsible for resisting visual triggers?

## Limitations
- The unalignment procedure for creating `θ_unsafe` is underspecified (dataset, steps, learning rate), hindering replication.
- Safety and medical evaluation rely on LLM-as-a-Judge; prompt templates and judge consistency are not fully detailed, risking metric noise.
- Results are limited to two 7B-parameter medical MLLMs on Qwen-VL; generality to other architectures and scales remains unproven.

## Confidence
- **High**: Empirical outcome (PSI restores safety with minimal performance drop on tested models).
- **Medium**: Layer-wise coefficient necessity (supported by ablation, but scalability uncertain).
- **Low**: Core assumption of linear separability (`v_s` ⊥ `v_m`) as a universal property; perfect collinearity break condition not empirically ruled out.

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate Unalignment**: Obtain the harmful dataset; run reverse gradient updates on `θ_base` to derive `θ_unsafe`; compute cosine similarity between `v_raw_s` and `v_raw_m`.
