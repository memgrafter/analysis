# Decoder-based Sense Knowledge Distillation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22351  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22351  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** DSKD integrates a sense dictionary and lexical relations into decoder training via a semantic consistency loss, improving accuracy on ARC, CSQA, MMLU, PIQA, and SQuAD while keeping inference unchanged and reducing training cost.

## Executive Summary
Decoder-based large language models (LLMs) typically lack explicit word sense knowledge, which can be crucial for language understanding. The authors propose Decoder-based Sense Knowledge Distillation (DSKD), a method that enriches decoder training by aligning student hidden states with sense embeddings derived from a teacher model, using a semantic consistency loss and incorporating lexical relations (synonyms, antonyms). DSKD does not modify inference, and it reduces training cost relative to standard knowledge distillation. Evaluations on LLaMA-3-8B and Mistral-7B show consistent gains on ARC, MMLU, and other benchmarks (e.g., MMLU +3.12% for LLaMA-3-8B). The approach is general and compatible with any decoder-only architecture.

## Method Summary
DSKD constructs a sense dictionary from the teacher model's contextual embeddings, capturing distinct word senses. During training, the student model processes the same input; a semantic consistency loss aligns the student's hidden states for each token with the corresponding sense embedding from the dictionary. Additionally, the loss encourages consistency across lexical neighbors (synonyms and antonyms) based on predefined lexical relations. The overall objective combines this semantic consistency loss with the standard knowledge distillation loss (e.g., KL divergence between teacher and student logits). The sense dictionary and lexical relations are integrated only during training; inference proceeds as usual, without any dictionary lookup or additional parameters.

## Key Results
- **ARC accuracy** for LLaMA-3-8B improves from 77.89% (KD) to 79.66% (DSKD); Mistral-7B from 61.11% to 64.78%.  
- **MMLU accuracy** for LLaMA-3-8B rises from 61.26% to 64.38%; for Mistral-7B from 52.04% to 54.44%.  
- Training overhead is minor (~5%) and inference is unchanged; effectively, training cost is halved while preserving performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DSKD works by directly modeling word senses during training. The sense dictionary provides sense-specific target embeddings derived from the teacher's contextual representations, which already capture fine-grained semantic distinctions. The semantic consistency loss forces the student to produce hidden states that are close to these sense embeddings, thereby internalizing the teacher's sense knowledge. Incorporating lexical neighbors (synonyms, antonyms) regularizes the student to maintain semantic consistency across related words, enhancing generalization and robustness of sense discrimination. Because the teacher's embeddings are used as a static guide, no additional computation is needed at inference time; the student simply uses its own parameters.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sense dictionary:** A mapping from tokens to multiple sense embeddings, built by clustering teacher contextual embeddings. *Why needed:* Provides explicit sense targets for the student. *Quick check:* Verify that a word like "bank" has distinct vectors for financial vs. river senses.  
- **Semantic consistency loss:** A distance-based loss (e.g., MSE) between student hidden states and sense embeddings. *Why needed:* Aligns student representations with teacher's sense knowledge. *Quick check:* Ensure loss decreases during training and correlates with downstream accuracy.  
- **Lexical relations (synonyms, antonyms):** Predefined connections between words from a resource like WordNet. *Why needed:* Encourages the student to treat related words similarly locally, improving sense awareness. *Quick check:* Check that loss contributions from neighbor pairs are balanced.  
- **Knowledge distillation (KD):** Standard technique where the student mimics the teacher's output distribution. *Why needed:* Preserves general language modeling capabilities. *Quick check:* Compare student logits to teacher logits via KL divergence.  
- **Contextual embeddings:** Token representations conditioned on surrounding context. *Why needed:* Underlie the construction of sense-specific vectors. *Quick check:* Ensure teacher model is capable of producing diverse embeddings for polysemous words.  
- **Decoder-only architecture:** Autoregressive models that generate text left-to-right. *Why needed:* Target platform for DSKD; inference remains decoder-only without extra modules. *Quick check:* Confirm that no dictionary lookup occurs during generation.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Input text -> Tokenizer -> Student model (decoder) -> Hidden states  
Teacher model (frozen) -> Contextual embeddings -> Offline sense dictionary construction  
Lexical relations database -> Sense neighbor pairs  
Training loop: For each batch, fetch sense vectors for input tokens and their neighbors; compute semantic consistency loss; combine with KD loss; backpropagate to update student.

Critical path: 1) Tokenize input. 2) Forward pass through teacher (to generate sense dictionary offline; but during training, teacher forward may also be used for KD). 3) Look up sense embeddings for each token and its lexical neighbors. 4) Compute student hidden states. 5) Calculate semantic consistency loss between student states and sense vectors. 6) Calculate KD loss (teacher vs. student logits). 7) Sum losses and update student.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Sense dictionary granularity: More senses improve specificity but increase dictionary size and potential noise.  
- Lexical relation coverage: Including many neighbors may over-regularize; too few may miss useful signals.  
- Loss weighting: Balancing semantic consistency vs. KD is crucial; too much emphasis on senses can degrade general LM performance.  
- Overhead: Sense dictionary lookups add ~5% training time but no inference cost.

Failure signatures:  
- Performance drop on benchmarks if sense dictionary is misaligned with student capacity (e.g., too many senses causing underfitting).  
- Overfitting to sense dictionary if the student memorizes sense vectors without generalization, detectable by validation accuracy divergence.  
- Increased training instability if loss weights are not tuned, leading to oscillating losses.

First experiments (three to start):  
1. Train student with DSKD and standard KD as baseline; compare on validation sets of ARC, MMLU, CSQA.  
2. Ablate lexical neighbor component: Run DSKD without neighbor constraints to isolate effect of sense alignment alone.  
3. Vary sense dictionary size (e.g., different clustering thresholds) to study impact on performance and overhead.

## Limitations
- Sense dictionary construction details (lexical resource, aggregation method) are not described, hindering reproducibility.  
- Benchmarks used (ARC, CSQA, MMLU, PIQA, SQuAD) test general reasoning, not explicit word sense disambiguation; thus claims about sense awareness are indirect.  
- Results lack variance estimates and statistical significance tests, so the magnitude of gains is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Method description** (sense dictionary integration, semantic consistency loss): Medium – conceptually clear but implementation details sparse.  
- **Performance improvements** on listed benchmarks: Medium – positive numbers reported but no error bars or significance tests.  
- **Efficiency claims** (training overhead ~5%, inference unchanged, cost halved): Low – no timing data or clear baseline definitions provided.

## Next Checks
- **Replication with variance:** Train student models (LLaMA-3-8B, Mistral-7B) using DSKD and standard KD; evaluate on all reported benchmarks across ≥3 random seeds; report mean ± standard deviation and perform paired significance tests.  
- **Ablation study:** Systematically remove lexical neighbor constraints and the semantic consistency loss individually; additionally vary sense dictionary sources to assess robustness of the performance gains.  
- **Cost measurement:** Under identical hardware, log wall-clock time and memory consumption for DSKD vs. KD during training; compute actual overhead and verify the “cost halved” claim relative to a fair baseline (e.g., same student capacity and number of training steps).
