# SFedHIFI: Fire Rate-Based Heterogeneous Information Fusion for Spiking Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14956
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14956
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** Enables heterogeneous Spiking FL, achieving up to 30% higher accuracy on CIFAR-10 under non-IID Dir(0.3) and 43.74% energy savings vs ANN-FL with <1% accuracy drop.

## Executive Summary
SFedHIFI is a novel framework that brings federated learning to spiking neural networks (SNNs) while accommodating clients with diverse computational resources. It leverages channel-wise matrix decomposition to allow each client to train a locally pruned SNN model of adaptable width, and introduces a firing rate-based heterogeneous information fusion (HIFI) module that identifies and merges the most information-rich layers across different model scales. This effectively counters the severe information loss typically caused by SNN sparsity. Coupled with an orthogonal regularizer that enriches the shared knowledge basis, SFedHIFI demonstrates consistent superiority over homogeneous FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova) on Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, with notable gains in non-IID settings and substantial energy efficiency compared to ANN-based FL.

## Method Summary
At its core, SFedHIFI factorizes each client's local convolution weight tensor \(W_p\) (for scale factor \(p\)) into a shared basis \(B\) and a scale-specific factor \(M_p\): \(W_p \approx B \cdot M_p\). The server aggregates all \(B\) matrices globally via FedAvg to capture universal channel patterns, while aggregating \(M_p\) separately within each scale group to preserve scale-specific adaptations. To mitigate information loss from pruning, the HIFI module computes the average firing rate per layer across all scales; the layer with the highest mode is selected as the most informative. For that layer, the corresponding \(M_p\) tensors from all scales undergo Tucker decomposition into a shared core \(G\) and scale-specific factor matrices. The core \(G\) is aggregated across scales, and each factor reconstructs its \(M_p\). During local training, the loss combines the standard temporal error tolerance (TET) loss with an orthogonal regularizer \(\lambda \|B B^T - I\|^2\) to keep the shared basis expressive. The updated \(B\) and \(\{M_p\}\) are then used to reconstruct each client's model \(W_p = B \cdot M_p\).

## Key Results
- **Accuracy gains:** Up to **30% higher** than homogeneous FL baselines on CIFAR-10 under non-IID Dirichlet α=0.3; improved average accuracy across all model scales.
- **Energy efficiency:** Reduces **energy consumption by 43.74%** relative to ANN-based FL while sacrificing less than 1% accuracy.
- **Robustness:** Consistent performance across IID and non-IID settings on Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SFedHIFI's success relies on three synergistic mechanisms:

### Mechanism 1: Heterogeneous Decomposition and Aggregation
Clients factorize their SNN convolution weights into a shared global basis \(B\) and a client-scale-specific factor \(M_p\). The server aggregates all \(B\) matrices via FedAvg, capturing common channel patterns across all clients, while \(M_p\) matrices are aggregated only within the same scale group. This decoupling permits cross-scale knowledge sharing through \(B\) while maintaining scale-specific diversity in \(M_p\).  
**Break condition:** If local data distributions are extremely skewed (high non-IID) or the chosen decomposition ranks are too low, the shared basis \(B\) may fail to encode meaningful global patterns, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 2: Firing Rate-Based Information Fusion
The HIFI module computes the average spike firing rate \(r_L\) for each layer across all scales. The layer \(\hat{L}\) that appears as the mode of the highest rates is considered the most informative. For \(\hat{L}\), the \(M_p^{\hat{L}}\) tensors are decomposed via Tucker into a shared core \(G\) and scale-specific factors \(\{A_p\}\). Aggregating \(G\) across scales fuses the high-information-density parameters while preserving scale-specific transformations through \(\{A_p\}\).  
**Break condition:** If firing rates do not correlate well with actual information content (e.g., on datasets with inherently sparse activations or poorly trained SNNs), the selected \(\hat{L}\) may be suboptimal or harmful.

### Mechanism 3: Orthogonal Regularizer
An orthogonal penalty \(\lambda \|B_L B_L^T - I\|^2\) is added to the local TET loss, encouraging the rows of each \(B_L\) to form an orthogonal set. This prevents \(B\) from collapsing to a narrow subspace and enriches its knowledge representation capacity, improving generalization across heterogeneous clients.  
**Break condition:** An excessively large \(\lambda\) over-constrains \(B\) and slows convergence; too small a \(\lambda\) fails to prevent basis collapse.

## Foundational Learning
Understanding SFedHIFI requires familiarity with:

- **Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) and LIF dynamics:** SNNs process information via binary spikes and membrane potential dynamics (leaky integrate-and-fire). Firing rates encode information density, contrasting with continuous activations in ANNs.

- **Federated Learning (FL) and Model Heterogeneity:** FL coordinates distributed training without sharing raw data, aggregating client updates centrally. System heterogeneity (clients with different compute/memory budgets) breaks the homogeneous-model assumption of algorithms like FedAvg.  
  *Quick check:* Why do standard FL algorithms like FedAvg fail or exclude clients when client devices have significantly different memory/compute budgets?

- **Tensor Decomposition (CP/Tucker):** These methods factor high-dimensional tensors into smaller basis and coefficient tensors, enabling compression and alignment. Channel-wise matrix decomposition resembles CP factorization, while HIFI employs Tucker decomposition.

## Architecture Onboarding
The system operates as follows:

**Component map:**  
Clients: hold local data and SNN models of width \(p \times\) base. They decompose local weights into \(B^n\) and \(M_p^n\), train using TET loss + orthogonal regularizer, then upload \((B^n, M_p^n)\).  
Server: maintains global \(B\) and per-scale \(\{M_p\}\). It aggregates \(B\) across all clients (FedAvg) and aggregates each \(M_p\) within its scale group. The HIFI module processes \(\{M_p\}\) to identify the informative layer \(\hat{L}\) and fuse its tensors via Tucker. The server then reconstructs \(W_p = B \cdot M_p\) and sends them back to clients of each scale.  
HIFI Module: computes per-layer firing rates (from client statistics), selects \(\hat{L}\) as the mode of best layers, applies Tucker decomposition to \(\{M_p^{\hat{L}}\}\), aggregates the core \(G\), and reconstructs each \(M_p^{\hat{L}}\).

**Critical path:**  
Client initialization (weight decomposition) → Local training (update \(B^n, M_p^n\)) → Upload → Server aggregates \(B\) and \(\{M_p\}\) → HIFI fusion on \(\{M_p\}\) → Distribution of \(\{W_p\}\) → Client reconstruction \(W_p = B \cdot M_p\).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Decomposition ranks* (\(a_1, a_2\) for matrix, Tucker ranks): Higher ranks preserve information but increase communication; lower ranks risk information loss.  
- *HIFI frequency*: Running HIFI every round incurs server overhead (Tucker decomposition) but may be necessary under high non-IID; less frequent reduces overhead but slows cross-scale transfer.  
- *Orthogonal regularization weight* \(\lambda\): Balances basis expressiveness vs. training stability; requires per-dataset tuning.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Disproportionate accuracy drop for small-scale clients (\(p=0.25\)) under non-IID, indicating ineffective fusion.  
- Degradation in global \(B\) quality (noisy aggregation) due to extreme data heterogeneity, hurting all scales.  
- Unexpectedly large uploads/downloads, suggesting rank hyperparameters are too large or decomposition not compressing.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Decomposition validity:* Train a single client with full model, decompose its weights, reconstruct \(W_p' = B \cdot M_p\), and compute \(\|W_p - W_p'\|_F^2\); should be small for chosen ranks.  
2. *HIFI isolation:* Run full SFedHIFI but disable HIFI (only FedAvg on \(M_p\)); compare accuracy on CIFAR-10 Dir(0.3). Expect significant drop, especially for small scales, confirming HIFI's role.  
3. *Firing rate correlation:* For a fixed heterogeneous model set, compute per-layer firing rates on a large validation batch; manually select the layer with highest rate as \(\hat{L}\) and fuse only that layer. Compare against random layer selection and a fixed (e.g., last conv) baseline; expect rate-based selection to perform at least as well.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multi-layer or weighted fusion:** Can HIFI be extended to fuse multiple high-rate layers with learned weights, rather than a single selected layer, to capture more information?  
2. **Firing rate sufficiency in advanced SNNs:** Is firing rate a reliable proxy for information density in larger architectures (spiking transformers) and datasets like ImageNet, or are other signals (e.g., spike timing) needed?  
3. **Sensitivity and auto-tuning:** How sensitive are the decomposition hyperparameters (ranks, Tucker dimensions) to performance, and can they be automatically tuned per client or round?

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter ambiguity:** The paper does not disclose critical hyperparameters (decomposition ranks, Tucker ranks, orthogonal regularization strength, local training epochs/batch size/learning rate, model scaling details for ResNet), hindering exact replication.
- **Firing rate computation details:** Unclear whether firing rates are computed on local training or validation data, how many samples are used, and whether rates are averaged across clients before or after selecting \(\hat{L}\), potentially affecting selection reproducibility.
- **Baseline and loss implementation gaps:** Missing hyperparameters for FedProx (μ) and FedNova (τ) and specifics of the TET loss (surrogate gradient, loss weighting) may compromise fairness of comparisons.

## Confidence
- **Heterogeneous FL mechanism (decomposition + aggregation):** Medium
- **HIFI (firing-rate fusion):** Medium
- **Energy savings claim:** High
