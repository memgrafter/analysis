# Incorporating contextual information into KGWAS for interpretable GWAS discovery
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25855
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25855
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** A context-aware knowledge graph built from Perturb-seq data reduces graph size 19-fold while improving recall of independent GWAS loci by over 20% in small cohorts (10,000 samples), yielding more consistent and biologically robust disease networks.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the limited interpretability and power of the general-purpose knowledge graph in KGWAS, which introduces spurious correlations. The proposed solution replaces the generic graph with a sparse, context-specific one constructed from Perturb-seq data in a disease-relevant cell type (K562 for hematopoiesis). By pruning low-confidence edges and incorporating only functionally validated gene-gene interactions from the Perturb-seq screen, the method dramatically reduces noise. Evaluated on UK Biobank blood traits, the approach significantly improves the recovery of known GWAS loci in small cohorts and produces disease-critical networks that are more consistent across random seeds and enriched for biologically plausible genes.

## Method Summary
The core method involves sparsifying the original KGWAS knowledge graph. First, all gene-program (G2P) edges and program nodes are removed. Variant-gene (V2G) edges are restricted to local, cis-regulatory types. General gene-gene (G2G) edges are pruned to only "major" types (e.g., from BioGRID/STRING with >10k connections). The set of gene nodes is restricted to those included in the Perturb-seq screen (~9,831 genes). Finally, the remaining general G2G edges are entirely replaced with edges derived from the Perturb-seq data: genes are linked if their cosine similarity in transcriptional response to CRISPR perturbation exceeds a threshold (τ=0.5). This context-aware graph is fed into a two-layer heterogeneous Graph Attention Network (GAT) trained with an LD-aware loss to predict GWAS χ² statistics.

## Key Results
- Pruning and replacing knowledge graph edges yields a 19-fold reduction in total graph size.
- The context-aware model improves recall of independent GWAS loci by over 20% in small cohorts (e.g., 10,000 samples) compared to the original KGWAS.
- Resulting disease-critical networks are more consistent across training runs and implicate genes relevant to the cellular context (e.g., chronic myeloid leukemia for K562).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Sparsifying the general-purpose knowledge graph by removing low-confidence and redundant edges reduces spurious correlations without degrading statistical power.
- **Mechanism:** The original KG contains many broad, low-specificity connections (e.g., V2G edges to the 20 closest genes to a TSS) and redundant self-loops. These introduce noise by creating multiple plausible but biologically irrelevant paths for message passing in the graph neural network (GAT). Pruning removes these diluting signals, allowing the model to focus on higher-confidence, functionally informative edges.
- **Core assumption:** The functional signal for disease-relevant pathways is concentrated in a subset of high-confidence interactions; the rest are noise or context-irrelevant redundancy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "pruning low-confidence edges... yields a 19-fold reduction in graph size while improving the recall..."
  - [section 5.1.1] Ablation shows removing all G2P edges and many minor G2G edge types ("self-edges") does not harm performance, and restricting V2G to local, cis-regulatory types slightly improves it.
  - [corpus] Weak: neighboring papers discuss feature selection and heterogeneity in GWAS but do not directly address KG sparsification in KGWAS.
- **Break condition:** If the retained high-confidence edge types are insufficient to capture essential biological pathways for the trait, or if the pruning thresholds are set too aggressively, important signal paths could be removed, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Replacing general gene-gene (G2G) interactions with context-specific edges derived from Perturb-seq data in a disease-relevant cell type improves the biological relevance and consistency of inferred disease-critical networks.
- **Mechanism:** Perturb-seq measures the transcriptional response to gene perturbation in a specific cellular context (K562). Genes with similar perturbation responses (high cosine similarity) are inferred to be functionally linked *in that context*. Integrating these edges as the sole G2G layer focuses the GAT's attention on interactions active in the relevant cellular state, rather than generic, cell-type-agnostic interactions from databases like STRING.
- **Core assumption:** Similar transcriptional response to perturbation indicates a functional relationship relevant to the cellular context of the disease.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "incorporating gene-gene relationships derived from Perturb-seq data... improving the recall of independent GWAS loci by over 20%... more consistent and biologically robust."
  - [section 4.1] Validates the Perturb-seq similarity signal by showing gene pairs annotated in STRING have significantly higher cosine similarity than random pairs (Fig 2).
  - [section 5.1.3] Replacing the original G2G edges with Perturb-seq edges (the "replace" strategy) performs as well as adding/merging while achieving the largest edge count reduction (19-fold).
  - [section 5.3] Disease-critical networks become more consistent across random seeds and implicate genes relevant to K562's biological context (CML, erythropoiesis).
- **Break condition:** If the Perturb-seq data is from a cell type that is not truly disease-relevant for the analyzed trait, the context-specific edges will misalign the model, reducing performance and interpretability.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Prioritizing gene nodes to those expressed/essential in the disease-relevant cell type (from Perturb-seq) further sparsifies the graph and improves predictive power by removing noise from irrelevant genes.
- **Mechanism:** Most genes in the human genome are not expressed or are non-essential in any given cell type. Including them as nodes creates many potential but irrelevant V2G and G2G connections. Restricting the node set to genes perturbed in the K562 screen (~9,831 genes instead of ~23,746) eliminates these irrelevant subgraphs, focusing the model's inductive bias.
- **Core assumption:** Genes not measurably expressed or non-essential in the disease-relevant cell type are unlikely to be causal drivers of a trait manifesting in that cellular context.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "pruning... and replacing... yields a 19-fold reduction... improving recall."
  - [section 5.1.3] "restricting KGWAS gene nodes to the set of genes perturbed in Replogle et al. (2022)... yields a performance gain of over 10%."
  - [table 1] The step "+ Prioritize genes" shows a clear jump in loci recalled (e.g., from 67.33 to 78.00 at 10k samples) with a reduction in edges from 2.3M to 1.3M.
- **Break condition:** If the disease mechanism involves genes not captured in the specific Perturb-seq screen (e.g., genes with low expression in that cell line but critical in a *different* relevant cell type), true signal could be lost.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) & Heterogeneous Graph Attention Networks (GATs)**
  - *Why needed here:* KGWAS is built on a heterogeneous GAT that processes multi-type nodes (variants, genes, programs) and edges (V2G, G2G, G2P). Understanding message passing, node/edge type aggregation, and attention weights (for interpretability) is essential to grasp how the model integrates the KG.
  - *Quick check question:* How does a heterogeneous GAT layer handle different edge types (e.g., eQTL vs. protein interaction) during neighborhood aggregation?

- **Concept: GWAS Summary Statistics & LD-Aware Training**
  - *Why needed here:* The model is trained to predict χ² association statistics from GWAS. The loss function accounts for linkage disequilibrium (LD) between variants to avoid doubly counting correlated signals. This is core to the model's objective.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is an LD-aware loss function necessary when training on GWAS summary statistics, and how might it differ from a standard regression loss?

- **Concept: Perturb-seq Experimental Design & Analysis**
  - *Why needed here:* The context-specific G2G edges are derived from Perturb-seq data. Understanding pooled CRISPR screens, single-cell RNA-seq readouts, log-fold change calculation, and the use of cosine similarity on reduced (ICA) embeddings is key to evaluating the validity of the inferred edges.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the biological rationale for using cosine similarity between perturbation-induced transcriptional response profiles to infer gene-gene functional relationships?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  KG Inputs (V2G, G2G, G2P, Perturb-seq matrix) -> Sparsification Module (prune edges, filter genes, replace G2G) -> Final Sparse Heterogeneous Graph (variants, genes) -> Two-Layer Heterogeneous GAT (node features, type-specific attention) -> LD-Aware Loss Training (Adam, early stop) -> Trained Model -> Interpretability (attention aggregation, disease-critical network extraction)

- **Critical path:**
  1. Process Perturb-seq data: compute z-scored LFC, select 5k feature genes, run ICA (60 components), compute pairwise cosine similarity, binarize at τ=0.5 to create `E_Perturb_G2G`.
  2. Load original KG edge lists; apply sparsification: remove G2P, restrict V2G to local cis types, retain only major G2G types, collapse G2G to single type, filter gene nodes to Perturb-seq set.
  3. Integrate context-specific G2G edges by *replacing* the pruned general G2G edges.
  4. Initialize node features (variants: 70 functional annotations; genes: PoPS), build final sparse heterogeneous graph with self-loops.
  5. Train 2-layer GAT on trait-specific GWAS χ² (sub-sampled cohorts) with LD-aware MSE loss; 5% validation split for early stopping.
  6. Evaluate: (a) Prediction: Top-100 independent loci recovery vs. full-cohort GWAS. (b) Interpretability: Network consistency across seeds, biological plausibility of top-attended genes.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Sparsity vs. Generality:** Extreme sparsification (19x fewer edges) risks losing rare pathways but shows net gain for specific blood traits. A more general KG would be less interpretable and noisier.
  - **Context-specificity vs. Transferability:** Using K562 Perturb-seq edges makes the model specific to blood/erythroid traits. Applying it to a neurological trait would require a different Perturb-seq atlas.
  - **Edge Replacement Strategy:** "Replace" yields smallest graph and best performance but discards all general G2G knowledge. "Add" or "Merge" retains some generality but adds complexity. Success with "Replace" suggests context-specific edges are sufficient and superior for matched traits.
  - **Model Depth:** Two-layer GAT is used. A three-layer model can recover performance when G2G is removed, implying depth can compensate via longer routes, but two layers are simpler and sufficient with context-specific edges.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Mismatched cell type:** If Perturb-seq cell line is not disease-relevant (e.g., liver data for brain disorder), context-specific edges misalign the model, likely causing performance to drop below baseline and networks to lack coherence.
  - **Over-pruning:** If τ is too high or gene filters too strict, the graph becomes disconnected or loses hub genes. Symptoms: very low locus recall, networks with isolated nodes.
  - **Under-pruning:** Retaining low-confidence edges (e.g., "20 closest TSS", minor G2G self-loops) may prevent improvement over original KGWAS, and disease networks remain inconsistent and cluttered.
  - **Train/test contamination:** Using full-cohort GWAS to define "ground truth" loci could bias evaluation if the same data informed KG construction (unlikely but a risk).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation of Relation Types:** Train models with G2G edges removed, G2P edges removed, and both removed, at cohort size 10,000. **Goal:** Confirm redundancy of G2P and that G2G can be removed with increased depth or replaced by context-specific genes. **Success metric:** Performance (loci recalled) of "remove G2G & G2P" should be non-inferior to full KGWAS in a 3-layer GAN, or superior when using context-specific genes.
  2. **G2G Edge Replacement Strategy Test:** For a trait (e.g., MCH), train three models with Perturb-seq edges: (a) Add (new edge type), (b) Merge (combined with original G2G), (c) Replace (remove original G2G, use only Perturb-seq). **Goal:** Verify that the sparsest option (Replace) does not sacrifice and likely improves performance. **Success metric:** Replace model should have equal or higher loci recall than Add/Merge, with far fewer total edges.
  3. **Cohort Size Sensitivity Analysis:** Train the final context-aware KGWAS model and the original KGWAS baseline across subsampled cohorts (1k–50k). **Goal:** Demonstrate that the benefit of context-specific KG is most pronounced in small, data-scarce regimes. **Success metric:** The performance gap (context-aware minus baseline) should be largest at smallest sample sizes (e.g., 1k–10k) and narrow at 50k.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does the performance gain extend to non-hematopoietic diseases and cell types beyond K562?
- **Open Question 2:** Which specific retained G2G edge types are essential for the observed improvement, and which are redundant?
- **Open Question 3:** How sensitive are the results to the cosine similarity threshold (τ=0.5) used to binarize Perturb-seq G2G edges?
- **Open Question 4:** Are disease-critical networks from context-aware KGWAS quantitatively more reproducible across random seeds and traits than those from the original KGWAS?

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to three blood traits and K562 Perturb-seq; broader disease relevance untested.
- Thresholds (τ=0.5, major G2G) lack sensitivity analysis.
- Training hyperparameters and GWAS clumping details are underspecified, risking irreproducibility.
- Possible circularity if full-cohort GWAS informed KG construction.

## Confidence
- **Sparsification:** Medium – solid ablations but limited to one biological system.
- **Perturb-seq G2G:** Medium – replacement beats add/merge; depends on correct cell-type match.
- **Gene prioritization:** High – consistent >10% recall boost across cohorts.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-cell-type test:** Use Perturb-seq from neurons for brain traits; expect benefit only for aligned contexts.
2. **Threshold robustness:** Grid search τ and G2G edge count thresholds; report performance stability.
3. **Hyperparameter audit:** Run full evaluation with 5 random seeds for key hyperparameters; confirm gain exceeds variance.
