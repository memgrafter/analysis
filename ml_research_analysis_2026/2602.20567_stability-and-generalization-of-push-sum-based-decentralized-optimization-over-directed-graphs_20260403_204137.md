# Stability and Generalization of Push-Sum Based Decentralized Optimization over Directed Graphs
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.20567
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20567
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: SGP achieves excess generalization error $\tilde{O}\bigl(1/\sqrt{mn} + \gamma/(\delta (1-\lambda)) + \gamma\bigr)$ for convex losses and $\tilde{O}\bigl(\kappa \cdot (1 + 1/(\delta (1-\lambda)))\bigr)$ under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, with explicit optimal early stopping, where $\delta$ is the stationary distribution imbalance and $1-\lambda$ the spectral gap of the mixing matrix.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the open problem of finite-iteration stability and generalization of Stochastic Gradient Push (SGP) over directed communication networks, where asymmetry induces structural bias. It introduces a unified uniform-stability framework that hinges on an imbalance-aware consistency bound for Push-Sum, cleanly separating the influence of topology (imbalance $\delta$, spectral gap $1-\lambda$) from statistical factors. For convex objectives, SGP attains an excess generalization error of $\tilde{O}(1/\sqrt{mn} + \gamma/(\delta(1-\lambda)) + \gamma)$ under step-size schedules, with an explicit optimal early stopping time. Under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, the bound becomes multiplicative: $\tilde{O}(\kappa \cdot (1 + 1/(\delta(1-\lambda))))$. Theoretical predictions are validated on logistic regression and CIFAR-10/LeNet across directed topologies, clarifying when Push-Sum correction is necessary compared to standard decentralized SGD.

## Method Summary
Stochastic Gradient Push (SGP) combines stochastic gradient descent with the Push-Sum consensus protocol for directed graphs. Each node $i$ maintains a local model $w_i(t)$ and a weight $u_i(t)$. In every iteration, the node samples a mini-batch, computes the gradient of its loss evaluated on the debiased parameter $z_i(t) = w_i(t)/u_i(t)$, and takes a gradient step to obtain a temporary update. The temporary $w$ and current $u$ are then mixed with neighbors according to a column-stochastic matrix $P$ encoding the communication topology. After aggregation, the node updates $w_i(t+1)$ and $u_i(t+1)$, and normalizes to $z_i(t+1)$. Push-Sum ensures that the debiased parameters converge to the global average of the local models despite directed and possibly imbalanced communication.

The analysis establishes uniform stability, which upperbounds generalization error. A key technical contribution is an imbalance-aware bound on the consensus error between debiased iterates and the global average, expressed in terms of the minimum entry $\delta$ of the stationary distribution of the random walk on the graph and the spectral gap $1-\lambda$ of $P$. The stability recursion decomposes into three terms reflecting initialization/mixing, topology-induced bias, and stochastic sampling. Optimizing the bound yields an explicit early stopping rule that balances optimization error and stability growth. For smooth convex losses the bound attains the claimed rate; for non-convex losses satisfying Polyak-Łojasiewicz, the topology dependence becomes multiplicative.

## Key Results
- **Convex excess error**: $\tilde{O}\bigl(1/\sqrt{mn} + \gamma/(\delta(1-\lambda)) + \gamma\bigr)$ with step-size schedules; optimal early stopping time $T^*$ explicitly characterized.
- **Polyak-Łojasiewicz excess error**: $\tilde{O}\bigl(\kappa \cdot (1 + 1/(\delta(1-\lambda)))\bigr)$, where $\kappa$ is the condition number.
- **Topology separation**: The bounds isolate the impact of directed topology ($\delta$, $1-\lambda$) from statistical limits ($1/\sqrt{mn}$); experiments confirm the predicted scalings on logistic regression and CIFAR-10/LeNet.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Push-Sum correction yields an unbiased approximation of the global average for distributed SGD over directed graphs. Each node maintains an auxiliary weight $u_i(t)$ that sums to 1 under mixing; the debiased model $z_i(t) = w_i(t)/u_i(t)$ tracks the average $w(t)$. Lemma 9 bounds the consensus error between $z_i(t)$ and $w(t)$ by a factor involving the imbalance parameter $\delta$ and the spectral gap $1-\lambda$, assuming $P$ is column-stochastic, primitive, and that stochastic gradients and initial proxies are bounded.

Uniform stability of SGP is shown by analyzing how a single-data change propagates through iterations. Under convexity, the stability bound decomposes into three terms: (i) a transient from initialization decaying like $\lambda^t$, (ii) a topology-dominated term scaling as $1/(\delta(1-\lambda))$, and (iii) a stochastic sampling term scaling as $1/(mn)$. The decomposition relies on nonincreasing step sizes and the consistency bound. For non-convex losses satisfying Polyak-Łojasiewicz, the topology term enters multiplicatively, yielding the stated $\tilde{O}\bigl(\kappa\cdot(1+1/(\delta(1-\lambda)))\bigr)$ bound.

Optimal early stopping emerges from balancing the decreasing optimization error (e.g., $O(1/T)$ for constant step size) against the increasing stability error (which grows with $T$). Minimizing the bound gives $T^* = \Theta\bigl(\sqrt{mn/\gamma}/\sqrt{\delta(1-\lambda)}\bigr)$ and final excess risk $\tilde{O}\bigl(1/\sqrt{mn} + \sqrt{\gamma/(\delta(1-\lambda))} + \gamma\bigr)$. This quantifies precisely how directed topology degrades learning beyond statistical limits.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
A directed strongly connected communication graph $G$; each node knows its in- and out-neighbors. A column-stochastic mixing matrix $P$ (e.g., $P_{ij}=1/(d_j+1)$ for $j \in \mathcal{N}_{\text{in}}(i)$). Local state per node $i$: $(w_i(t), u_i(t), z_i(t)=w_i(t)/u_i(t))$. Iteration: (1) sample mini-batch from local data; (2) compute gradient $g_i(t)=\nabla f(z_i(t);\xi_i(t))$; (3) temporary update $w_i^{\text{temp}} = w_i(t) - \gamma_t g_i(t)$; (4) communicate $P_{ij}\cdot w_i^{\text{temp}}$ and $P_{ij}\cdot u_i(t)$ to each out-neighbor $j$; (5) aggregate: $w_i(t+1)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{in}}(i)} P_{ij} w_j^{\text{temp}}$, $u_i(t+1)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{in}}(i)} P_{ij} u_j(t)$; (6) set $z_i(t+1)=w_i(t+1)/u_i(t+1)$. Output is typically $z_i(T)$ or a global average after additional Push-Sum rounds.

**Critical Path**:  
Gradient step on $z_i(t)$ → compute $g_i(t)$; Push-Sum mixing (communicate weighted $w_i^{\text{temp}}$ and $u_i(t)$, aggregate) → produce $w_i(t+1), u_i(t+1)$; normalization yields $z_i(t+1)$ that tracks the global average $w(t)$. The stability proof traces the divergence between two runs differing in one sample, using the consistency bound (Lemma 9) to control consensus error and a martingale expansion (Lemma 16) to bound stochastic gradient effects over time.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Step size: Larger constant $\gamma$ speeds optimization but amplifies stability error; diminishing $\gamma_t$ (e.g., $\propto 1/t$) mitigates stability growth at the cost of slower convergence.
- Graph topology: Larger spectral gap ($1-\lambda$) and larger $\delta$ (closer to uniform) reduce both convergence rate and generalization error. Fully connected graphs are ideal but communication-heavy; sparse directed graphs (e.g., rings) have small $1-\lambda$ and thus higher error.
- Push-Sum overhead: Requires communication of both $w$ and $u$ (two scalars per dimension), necessary for unbiased averaging on directed graphs; overhead is modest compared to the benefit.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Topology: Extremely small $\delta$ (high imbalance) or $\lambda$ near $1$ (slow mixing) cause $1/(\delta(1-\lambda))$ to blow up, leading to large consensus errors.
- Learning rate: Constant step size in non-convex settings can cause exponential stability growth; diminishing schedule is required.
- Boundedness: Unbounded gradients or excessively large initial iterates violate assumptions, inflating constants and degrading the bound.

**First Three Experiments**:  
1. **Logistic regression on a9a** (convex). Vary $\gamma$, $m$, topology (directed ring, expander, fully connected). Measure stability $\|w(t)_{\text{avg}} - w'(t)_{\text{avg}}\|$ and training loss. Larger $\gamma$ speeds optimization but worsens stability; larger $m$ improves both; better topology (higher $\delta$, larger spectral gap) yields lower errors.
2. **LeNet-5 on CIFAR-10** (non-convex). Vary $\gamma$, $m$, and directed topologies (Full, Exp, Star, B-tree, Ring, Sub-Ring, D-Ring). Confirm stability growth patterns (exponential for constant $\gamma$, polynomial for diminishing) and corresponding loss curves.
3. **Topology sweep**: Systematically vary $\lambda$ and $\delta$ (by modifying edge directions) and plot excess generalization error. Verify scaling $\propto 1/(\delta(1-\lambda))$ as predicted.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Push-Sum necessity**: Under what conditions is Push-Sum correction required compared with standard decentralized SGD? The paper identifies $\delta < 1/m$ as a theoretical threshold, but its practical tightness and whether lower bounds exist for non- Push-Sum algorithms remain open.
2. **Topology–step size interplay**: How exactly do $\delta$ and $1-\lambda$ interact to determine the optimal learning rate schedule and early-stopping time in convex optimization? The current early-stopping formulas involve complicated dependence; a minimax treatment with explicit $\gamma^*(\delta,\lambda)$ is missing.
3. **Non-convex beyond PL**: Can uniform stability and generalization be established for non-convex objectives that do not satisfy the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition? For generic non-convex losses the stability bound may become vacuous; identifying broader structural conditions that yield meaningful bounds (and how they interact with $\delta$, $\lambda$) is an open direction.
4. **Time-varying graphs**: How do time-varying directed communication graphs (e.g., random edge failures) impact SGP’s stability and generalization? Extending the static analysis to settings where $\delta$ and $\lambda$ change over time, possibly via time-averaged quantities, is unexplored.

## Limitations
- The precise construction of mixing matrices $P$ and the resulting imbalance $\delta$ for some directed topologies (e.g., B-tree, Sub-Ring) are not fully detailed, making verification of theoretical constants difficult.
- Experiments likely use i.i.d. data partitions, while the theory allows heterogeneous local distributions; robustness of the trends under severe non-i.i.d. settings is unclear.
- Assumptions of bounded gradients and initial iterates, required for the analysis, may not hold for deep networks (e.g., LeNet), potentially affecting bound tightness.

## Next Checks
1. **Mixing matrix validation**: For each directed topology used in the experiments, explicitly construct the column-stochastic matrix $P$ (e.g., based on uniform out-degree weighting), compute its second-largest eigenvalue $\lambda$ and stationary distribution $\pi$, and verify that $\delta = \min_i \pi_i$ aligns with the values used in the theoretical bounds.
2. **Two-run stability measurement**: Implement the standard stability protocol—run SGP twice on the same dataset with exactly one sample swapped, using synchronized random seeds—and track the average distance between debiased iterates $z_i(t)$ across nodes. Check that the observed scaling matches the predicted dependence on $1/\sqrt{mn}$ and $\gamma/(\delta(1-\lambda))$.
3. **Effect of data heterogeneity**: Partition CIFAR-10 with deliberate label skew (e.g., each node receives only a subset of classes) and run the same experiments. Compare the excess generalization error against the bound to see whether an additional heterogeneity penalty appears beyond the topology term.
