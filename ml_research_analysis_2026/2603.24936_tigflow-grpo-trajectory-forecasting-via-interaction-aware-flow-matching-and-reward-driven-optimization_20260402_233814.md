# TIGFlow-GRPO: Trajectory Forecasting via Interaction-Aware Flow Matching and Reward-Driven Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24936  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24936  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Best-in-class performance on ETH/UCY (ADE 0.20, FDE 0.31) and SDD (ADE 7.37, FDE 11.67), with collision rate on SDD at 4.8s horizon reduced from 19.74% to 12.29%.

## Executive Summary
Human trajectory forecasting often misaligns with social norms and physical constraints. TIGFlow‑GRPO introduces a two‑stage framework: first, a Conditional Flow Matching predictor with a Trajectory‑Interaction‑Graph Attention (TIG‑GAT) module enhances fine‑grained social‑spatial modeling; second, Flow‑GRPO post‑training converts deterministic ODE rollout into stochastic SDE sampling and optimizes a composite reward combining view‑aware social compliance and map‑aware physical feasibility. The approach achieves state‑of‑the‑art accuracy and significantly lower collision rates, demonstrating improved long‑horizon stability and behavioral plausibility.

## Method Summary
The framework comprises two stages.  
1. **Supervised pretraining:** A dual‑branch Spatio‑Temporal Encoder processes historical trajectories. The explicit TIG‑GAT branch constructs a target‑centric dynamic graph via an accumulated visibility mask and edge‑aware gated attention to filter irrelevant neighbors and refine interaction features. These context tokens condition a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) predictor that learns a velocity field to map noise to future trajectories via ODE rollout, trained with the flow‑matching loss.  
2. **Reward‑driven post‑training (Flow‑GRPO):** The deterministic ODE rollout is recast as stochastic SDE sampling by recovering the score function from the pretrained velocity field and injecting noise with a controlled diffusion coefficient. For each observed condition, multiple trajectories (G=4) are sampled. A composite reward—comprising view‑aware social penalties (front, peripheral, rear fields), map‑aware Signed Distance Field clearance, plus accuracy and smoothness regularizers—is computed. Advantages are estimated within each sample group using group‑relative normalization, and the policy is updated with a clipped PPO‑style objective plus a KL penalty towards the frozen reference model, thereby aligning predictions with behavioral and physical norms without sacrificing generative quality.

## Key Results
- State‑of‑the‑art average ADE/FDE on ETH/UCY (0.20 / 0.31) and SDD (7.37 / 11.67).  
- Collision rate on SDD at 4.8s horizon reduced from 19.74% to 12.29%.  
- Improved long‑horizon stability evidenced by lower error accumulation over time.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: TIG‑GAT filters social context via visual field
The Trajectory‑Interaction‑Graph Attention (TIG‑GAT) constructs a dynamic graph where neighbors are selected based on an accumulated visibility mask over history, considering only those within the target agent’s field of view (e.g., 120°) and top‑K proximity. This reduces noisy social context. Edge‑aware gated attention then passes messages with spatial gates, avoiding Softmax competition dilution. Temporal gated pooling summarizes frame‑wise features into a physical representation, and strength‑modulated fusion combines it with implicit social context. *Core assumption:* relevant interactions are visible and directionally pertinent; suppressing irrelevant neighbors enhances conditional representations. *Evidence:* Section 3.3 details the mask and gated attention; Table 5 ablation shows benefit. *Potential breakage:* Under heavy occlusions or non‑visual social cues, the selection may discard behaviorally relevant agents.

### Mechanism 2: Stochastic SDE rollout from learned flow prior
The deterministic ODE rollout of CFM is recast as stochastic SDE sampling to enable exploration. Using the pretrained velocity field \(v_\theta\), the score \(s_\theta\) is derived (Eq. 5), and a Gaussian transition is defined: \(y_{t+\Delta t} = \mu_t + \sigma_t \epsilon_t\), with diffusion coefficient \(g(\bar{t}) = \eta/\sqrt{(1-\bar{t})/\bar{t}}\). Controlled noise is injected at sampling only, preserving the flow prior’s manifold. *Core assumption:* the flow’s continuous path is compatible with SDE transitions, and stochasticity can be added without retraining. *Evidence:* Abstract and Section 3.4 describe the conversion; Flow‑GRPO is implemented. *Potential breakage:* If the flow prior is poorly calibrated or variance too low, SDE noise may produce out‑of‑manifold trajectories, harming feasibility.

### Mechanism 3: Composite reward aligns predictions with norms
A group‑relative RL framework optimizes a composite reward. The view‑aware social reward (Eq. 11) penalizes unsafe proximity in the agent’s front field (strong‑view), balances contact in peripheral view (weak‑view), and mildly penalizes rear separation. The map‑aware reward (Eq. 13) uses Signed Distance Fields to penalize clearance deficits relative to historical baseline. These are weighted with accuracy and smoothness terms, then normalized via group advantages (Eq. 7). *Core assumption:* social and physical constraints can be encoded as differentiable or piecewise penalties; group‑relative advantages stabilize updates without a separate value network. *Evidence:* Abstract and Section 3.5 define masks and SDF penalty; collision reduction (Table 4) validates effectiveness. *Potential breakage:* Poor weight tuning or inaccurate SDFs may cause reward hacking (e.g., excessive risk aversion or goal neglect).

## Foundational Learning
**Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)**  
*Why needed:* Provides a stable, one‑step generative process that maps a Gaussian prior to trajectory distributions, forming the pretrained backbone.

**Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) with Attention**  
*Why needed:* Models multi‑agent interactions; TIG‑GAT extends this with dynamic graph construction and gated message passing for target‑centric reasoning.

**Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**  
*Why needed:* Enables critic‑free RL alignment with non‑differentiable rewards by estimating advantages within a sampled group, reducing memory overhead for continuous trajectory optimization.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Spatio‑Temporal Encoder (dual‑branch: Social Transformer + TIG‑GAT) → Flow Matching Backbone (CFM predictor with ODE rollout) → Flow‑GRPO Module (ODE‑to‑SDE transition, reward models, GRPO objective)  
Composite Reward (view‑aware social + map‑aware SDF + accuracy + smoothness) feeds into the GRPO objective.

**Critical path:**  
Pretraining: Historical trajectories → TIG‑GAT context tokens → CFM ODE rollout → flow‑matching loss.  
Post‑training: Same context → stochastic SDE rollout (G=4 samples) → composite reward → group‑relative advantage → GRPO update with KL regularization to frozen reference.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- TIG‑GAT’s dynamic graph adds overhead but reduces noise; fixed Top‑K trades coverage for efficiency.  
- ODE‑to‑SDE stochasticity balances exploration vs. deviation from flow prior; η in diffusion coefficient controls noise scale.  
- Composite reward weights (ω_sv, ω_map, etc.) require dataset‑specific tuning; misweights may cause reward hacking.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High collision rates (>19% on SDD) indicate insufficient social reward or poor SDFs.  
- Rapid error increase over horizon (ADE/FDE drift) suggests unstable SDE sampling or inadequate long‑horizon rewards.  
- Mode collapse (low multimodal diversity) may result from excessive KL regularization (β) or narrow σ_t schedule.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Ablation study: Remove TIG‑GAT, social reward, map reward, or GRPO individually; measure impact on ADE/FDE and collision rates (Table 5).  
2. Collision rate analysis: Compare collision rates across horizons (1.2s to 4.8s) on all datasets (Table 4).  
3. Extended horizon robustness: Plot ADE/FDE per time step (e.g., ETH results in Table 3) to assess error accumulation and stability from Flow‑GRPO.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How does the diffusion coefficient scale η in Flow‑GRPO’s SDE affect diversity vs. constraint adherence?  
*Basis:* The SDE uses \(g(t)=\eta/\sqrt{(1-t)/t}\) but η is not studied.  
*Why unresolved:* Optimal η may vary with scene complexity; no analysis exists.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Ablate η, measure diversity (entropy) and violations (collision/off‑map).

**Open Question 2:** How sensitive is TIG‑GAT to field‑of‑view angle θ_FoV and neighbor selection criteria?  
*Basis:* The paper fixes θ_FoV=120° and Top‑K but provides no sensitivity analysis.  
*Why unresolved:* Interaction relevance depends on density; fixed parameters may not generalize.  
*What evidence:* Vary θ_FoV/K across densities, evaluate ADE/FDE/Col.

**Open Question 3:** Do the composite reward components conflict, and how should their weights be tuned?  
*Basis:* The reward combines social, map, accuracy, smoothness with dataset‑specific weights, but interactions are not studied.  
*Why unresolved:* Conflicting terms could produce hack behaviors; weight selection is ad hoc.  
*What evidence:* Analyze per‑term gradients and weight sensitivity across metrics.

**Open Question 4:** How can VLM‑based scene reasoning be integrated into TIGFlow‑GRPO to enhance generalization?  
*Basis:* The conclusion states future work includes VLM‑based reasoning.  
*Why unresolved:* Benefits, challenges, and architectural changes are unexplored.  
*What evidence:* Implement VLM module, test on diverse scenes, measure improvements in accuracy, constraint adherence, and interpretability.
