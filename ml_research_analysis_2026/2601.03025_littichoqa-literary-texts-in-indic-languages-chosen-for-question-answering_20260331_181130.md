# LittiChoQA: Literary Texts in Indic Languages Chosen for Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03025
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03025
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** Introduces a dataset of 270K+ automatically generated QA pairs from 17 Indic languages; full-context fine-tuning yields best semantic score (76.1 for Krutrim-2), while context-shortening improves throughput at a performance cost.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of long-context literary QA resources for low-resource Indic languages by introducing **LittiChoQA**, a dataset of over 270K automatically generated question-answer pairs derived from 3,824 naturally sourced literary stories across 17 languages. The authors fine-tune six multilingual LLMs on this non-factoid QA task and evaluate a trade-off between full-context training and two context-shortening strategies (answer paragraph selection, vector retrieval). Results show full-context fine-tuning achieves the highest semantic scores, with Krutrim-2 reaching 76.1, while shortening methods substantially improve computational throughput but reduce scores to 74.9 and 71.4 respectively. Qualitative evaluations support these quantitative findings.

## Method Summary
Stories collected from open web sources are processed (OCR where needed) and split into non-overlapping chunks to fit GPT-4.1’s token limits. A structured prompt instructs GPT-4.1 to generate both factoid and non-factoid question-answer pairs in the source language. The resulting dataset is curated with metadata and split 70:10:20 into train, dev, test. Six multilingual LLMs (e.g., Llama 3.1, Krutrim-2) are fine-tuned using LoRA for 1 epoch. Full-context training uses the entire story; shortening methods either select a relevant paragraph via a fine-tuned Answer Paragraph Selection (APS) model or retrieve top‑k chunks using vector similarity (`paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2`). Models are evaluated on ROUGE and semantic textual similarity (STS MuTe) and measured for throughput (questions per second).

## Key Results
- Full-context fine-tuning attains the highest semantic score: Krutrim‑2 = 76.1.
- Answer paragraph selection yields 74.9; vector retrieval yields 71.4 on the same metric.
- Context‑shortening increases throughput by roughly 20× compared to full‑context processing.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Automatic QA Generation with GPT‑4.1
- **Claim:** GPT‑4.1 can effectively generate a large, diverse QA dataset for low‑resource Indic literary texts without human annotation.
- **Mechanism:** Raw story chunks are fed to GPT‑4.1 with a structured prompt that instructs the model to produce factoid and non‑factoid pairs in the source language, leveraging its multilingual and instruction‑following capabilities.
- **Core assumption:** GPT‑4.1 possesses sufficient linguistic and cultural knowledge to create contextually relevant, grammatically sound questions from raw prose.
- **Evidence anchors:** The dataset contains 274K+ pairs; manual annotation (Table 3) shows average scores of ≥3.5 on readability, relevance, and non‑factoidness for three languages.
- **Break condition:** Systematic quality degradation for certain languages (e.g., complex scripts) or literary styles (e.g., poetry) would undermine dataset consistency.

### Mechanism 2: Performance‑Efficiency Trade‑off in Long‑Context QA
- **Claim:** Full‑context fine‑-tuning maximizes semantic understanding for non‑factoid literary QA, while context‑shortening methods dramatically improve throughput at a cost to answer quality.
- **Mechanism:** Transformers’ quadratic attention handles long narratives holistically, preserving cross‑passage dependencies. Shortening by pre‑filtering reduces compute but risks losing subtle, narrative‑wide information essential for abstractive answers.
- **Core assumption:** Non‑factoid literary QA fundamentally requires reasoning over the entire story; aggressive truncation disrupts this.
- **Evidence anchors:** Across all six models, full‑context STS scores exceed shortened‑context scores (Table 4), while throughput increases ~20×. Krutrim‑2’s full‑context score is 76.1 vs. 74.9 (APS) and 71.4 (vector).
- **Break condition:** Architectural innovations eliminating quadratic complexity (e.g., linear attention) could close the performance gap, rendering the trade‑off unnecessary.

### Mechanism 3: Krutrim‑2’s Inherent Suitability
- **Claim:** Krutrim‑2’s architecture and pretraining make it particularly well‑suited for Indic literary non‑factoid QA, leading to consistently superior performance.
- **Mechanism:** Krutrim‑2 likely benefits from a higher proportion or quality of Indic text in pretraining, and/or from architectural choices that better handle multilingual semantics and long‑range dependencies.
- **Core assumption:** Base model capabilities in a language family are a primary determinant of downstream task performance, especially under low‑resource fine‑tuning.
- **Evidence anchors:** Krutrim‑2 achieves the highest STS score in all three settings (full, APS, vector) in Table 4 and ranks best in qualitative evaluations.
- **Break condition:** If future evaluations on other long‑context Indic benchmarks show Krutrim‑2’s advantage diminishes, or if newer models surpass it, this mechanism weakens.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Data Pipeline: Web Scraping -> OCR Processing -> Story Chunking (non‑overlapping) -> GPT‑4.1 QA Generation -> Metadata Storage.  
Model Fine‑tuning: Base Multilingual LLM -> LoRA Adapter (PEFT) -> Full/Shortened Context Training (using APS or retriever outputs).  
Evaluation Engine: Test Set (held‑out stories) -> Model Inference -> Metric Calculation (ROUGE, STS) -> Qualitative Comparison (human/GPT‑4.1 judge).

**Critical path**  
Raw story collection → Chunking → GPT‑4.1 generation → Dataset curation → Train/Dev/Test split → Model fine‑tuning (full or shortened context) → Evaluation on common test set. A break in QA generation quality or chunk‑reanswer traceability breaks the entire pipeline.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Generation:* Cost/API limits (TPM) vs. avoiding redundant QA from overlapping chunks (chose non‑overlapping).  
- *Evaluation:* Throughput (questions/sec) vs. semantic fidelity (STS score); full‑context is gold standard but slow.  
- *Coverage:* Number of languages (17) vs. depth per language (annotation done for 3, single annotator).

**Failure signatures**  
- Data Quality: Low annotation scores (<3) on readability, relevance, or non‑factoidness in Table 3.  
- Model Collapse: Krutrim‑2 not ranking best in qualitative evaluation for multiple languages.  
- Context Shortening Inefficacy: Vector retrieval performing *better* than APS, contradicting Table 4 (APS > Vector).  
- Throughput Metric: Shortened‑context models not showing ~20× throughput increase over full‑context.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Annotation Replication:** Manually annotate 20 random QA pairs from the released dataset for one language (e.g., Hindi) using the provided Likert scales. Verify scores align with Table 3 averages.  
2. **Trade‑off Verification:** Fine‑tune Llama 3.1 8B on a small subset under three conditions: (a) full story context, (b) APS‑selected context, (c) vector‑retrieved context. Measure STS and throughput; expect STS: (a) > (b) > (c), throughput: (c) > (b) > (a).  
3. **Krutrim‑2 Benchmark:** Load provided Krutrim‑2 full‑context and APS‑fine‑tuned checkpoints. Run inference on 50 held‑out non‑factoid questions from 3 languages (e.g., Hindi, Bhojpuri, Telugu). Expect full‑context answers to be qualitatively richer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Dataset extension:** How can LittiChoQA be extended to additional Indic languages, literary genres, and historical periods? (Current focus limited to 17 languages and Gangetic plains prose; expansion requires new sources.)
- **Dynamic context filtering:** Can integrated long‑context models with dynamic context filtering effectively mitigate “lost‑in‑the‑middle” issues for literary QA? (Not tested; remains unproven.)
- **New long‑context LLMs:** How do state‑of‑the‑art long‑context LLMs perform on LittiChoQA compared to the evaluated models? (Newer models with extended contexts were not assessed.)
- **Multilingual transfer:** What are the effects of multilingual transfer and cross‑lingual generalization on literary QA performance for low‑resource Indic languages? (Not systematically probed.)

## Limitations
- **Limited manual validation:** Quality assessment was restricted to 3 languages with a single annotator, risking bias and incomplete coverage.
- **Undefined metric:** “STS MuTe” is not defined, hindering precise replication of semantic scores.
- **Unspecified shortening parameters:** Details for vector retrieval (k, chunking post‑generation) and APS training data are missing, affecting reproducibility of the trade‑off.

## Confidence
- **Dataset Scale & Generation:** *Medium* (scale is solid, but quality evidence is constrained by narrow validation).
- **Full‑vs‑Shortened Context Trade‑off:** *High* (consistent across 6 models, aligns with known transformer limitations).
- **Krutrim‑2 Superiority:** *Medium* (strong in‑paper results but lacking external benchmark corroboration).

## Next Checks
1. **Annotation Replication:** Manually evaluate 50 random QA pairs per language (minimum 5 languages) using the paper’s 4‑point Likert scales; verify average scores (readability, relevance, non‑factoidness) match Table 3 ranges.
2. **Trade‑off Mechanics Test:** Fine‑tune Llama 3.1 8B on a 10% LittiChoQA subset under three conditions; confirm STS ranking (full > APS > vector) and that vector retrieval yields ≥15× throughput gain over full‑context.
3. **Krutrim‑2 Cross‑Benchmark:** Run the provided Krutrim‑2 full‑context fine‑tuned checkpoint on a held‑out subset of an external long‑context Indic QA benchmark (e.g., from “Long‑context Non‑factoid QA in Indic Languages”); compare performance to other models to test transferability of its advantage.
