# Semantically Conditioned Diffusion Models for Cerebral DSA Synthesis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11703  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11703  
- **Reference count:** 29  
- **Primary result:** Synthesized cerebral DSA frames achieve overall Likert scores of 3.1–3.3/5 in a reader study, with an FID of 15.27 and high inter-rater reliability (ICC(2,k)=0.80–0.87).

## Executive Summary
Cerebral digital subtraction angiography (DSA) is limited by data scarcity due to its invasive nature and cost, hindering large-scale research. This paper introduces a semantically conditioned latent diffusion model (LDM) to generate synthetic arterial-phase DSA frames. The model uses BERT text embeddings to control anatomical circulation (anterior vs. posterior) and C-arm geometry (Plane A/B with canonical angles). A large dataset of 99,349 arterial-phase frames was curated from 22,064 DSA series using an automated classifier. In a reader study with four experts, 400 synthetic images received moderate overall quality ratings (Likert 3.1–3.3/5) and demonstrated distributional similarity to real DSA (FID 15.27). Inter-rater reliability was high, though quality varied by condition, with underrepresented combinations yielding lower scores.

## Method Summary
The approach centers on a conditional latent diffusion model. First, a ResNet-18 classifier automatically extracts arterial-phase frames from a multi-center DSA repository, yielding 99,349 images. These frames are paired with structured text prompts describing circulation (anterior/posterior) and C-arm plane/angle. A VAE compresses 256×256 grayscale images into 64×64×3 latents. A conditional UNet denoising network operates in this latent space, with BERT-encoded text embeddings injected via cross-attention to guide reverse diffusion. The model learns the conditional distribution p(x|c) and synthesizes images by denoising random latent vectors under the specified condition.

## Key Results
- Reader study: overall Likert scores 3.1–3.3/5; inter-rater reliability ICC(2,k)=0.80–0.87.  
- Distributional similarity: Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) = 15.27.  
- Dataset scale: 99,349 arterial-phase DSA frames (78.77% anterior circulation, 21.23% posterior).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Semantic conditioning via BERT text embeddings enables controllable generation of DSA frames matching specified anatomical circulation (anterior/posterior) and C-arm geometry (Plane A/B with canonical angles).  
**Mechanism:** Text prompts are encoded by BERT into fixed-length vectors. These vectors are injected into the UNet denoising network via cross-attention layers, biasing the reverse diffusion process in latent space toward features associated with the specified condition. The model learns a conditional distribution p(x|c).  
**Core assumption:** Textual descriptions capture sufficient visual variability to distinguish circulation-plane-angle combinations, and training data adequately covers these conditions.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states control over circulation and geometry; Section 2.3 defines four conditioning prompts; Section 2.4 shows per-condition Likert variation (e.g., PC A lower at 2.73), indicating condition-specific generation.  
**Break condition:** Underrepresented conditions (e.g., PC A, 0.9% of data) yield lower-quality outputs (Likert 2.73) and more "external circulation" flags, indicating failure to learn the conditional distribution.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Latent-space diffusion reduces computational burden while preserving high-resolution details necessary for DSA vascular structures.  
**Mechanism:** A VAE maps high-resolution frames (256×256) to a lower-dimensional latent (64×64×3). Diffusion operates on these latents; the UNet predicts noise in latent space. The VAE decoder reconstructs the final image. This reduces memory and compute vs. pixel-space diffusion.  
**Core assumption:** The VAE latent space is expressive enough to capture clinically relevant vascular details (e.g., small peripheral vessels) without significant information loss, and reconstruction loss (LPIPS + KL) preserves perceptual fidelity.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "latent diffusion model" and "high-resolution image synthesis"; Section 4.3 details VAE architecture and latent shape; Section 2.4 shows lower peripheral segment scores (e.g., 3.07 in PC B), suggesting some detail loss.  
**Break condition:** VAE fails to reconstruct small vessels or subtle contrast dynamics, leading to blurry or missing peripheral vasculature in generated images.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Large-scale curation of arterial-phase DSA frames via automated classification enables effective training by providing a substantial and clinically relevant dataset.  
**Mechanism:** A ResNet-18 classifier, trained on 953 manually annotated series, identifies arterial-phase frames from 22,064 series with high precision (0.92) and recall (0.89). This yields 99,349 arterial-phase frames for training.  
**Core assumption:** Classifier accuracy is high enough to avoid significant label noise corrupting the training distribution; the arterial phase is the most informative for diagnosis and provides a coherent visual target.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 2.2 details the classifier's performance (precision 0.92, recall 0.89, accuracy 0.95) and final frame count; Section 2.4 shows 239/400 generated images passed arterial screening, indicating多数 are plausible.  
**Break condition:** Classifier errors introduce non-arterial frames or exclude arterial ones, distorting the training distribution and causing inappropriate contrast dynamics or opacification in outputs.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Text Prompt -> BERT Encoder -> Condition Embedding -> Cross-Attention in UNet; Input Image -> VAE Encoder -> Latent z_t -> Denoising UNet (with condition) -> Denoised Latent z_0 -> VAE Decoder -> Synthetic Image  
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Preprocess: Extract arterial-phase frames via ResNet-18 classifier; assign text conditions based on circulation/plane/angle.  
  2. Train VAE on all arterial frames (256×256 grayscale input/output).  
  3. Train conditional LDM on VAE latents with noise prediction; condition via BERT embeddings.  
  4. Generate: Sample noise, denoise iteratively with condition, decode to image.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Latent resolution (64×64) vs. detail preservation: Lower resolution reduces compute but may lose small vessels; perceptual loss helps but peripheral scores remain lower.  
  - Conditioning granularity: Only four conditions evaluated; finer control (e.g., exact angles) needs more data and complexity.  
  - Dataset diversity vs. quality: Single-centre data ensures consistency but limits generalizability; multi-centre variation could improve robustness.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Low Likert scores (<3) for peripheral vessels.  
  - Poor quality for underrepresented conditions (e.g., PC A).  
  - Generated images flagged as non-arterial phase.  
- **First experiments:**  
  1. Reproduce arterial-phase classification performance on held-out data to verify dataset curation.  
  2. Evaluate VAE reconstruction quality on DSA frames using LPIPS and SSIM to assess detail retention.  
  3. Measure per-condition FID scores to quantify conditioning effectiveness and identify weak conditions.

## Limitations
- Single-center dataset (UMM) limits generalizability to other imaging protocols and populations.  
- Underrepresented conditions (e.g., posterior circulation Plane A, 0.9% of data) produce lower-quality outputs.  
- VAE latent resolution (64×64) risks loss of fine peripheral vessel detail, reflected in lower peripheral segment scores.
