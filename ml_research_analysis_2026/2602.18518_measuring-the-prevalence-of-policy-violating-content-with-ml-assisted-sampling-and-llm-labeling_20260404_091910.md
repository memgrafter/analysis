# Measuring the Prevalence of Policy Violating Content with ML Assisted Sampling and LLM Labeling
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18518
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18518
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: ML-assisted sampling + LLM labeling achieves ~15× reduction in labeling latency and >10× cost reduction while enabling daily operational monitoring of policy-violating content prevalence with unbiased estimates and drill-down capabilities.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a system for measuring the prevalence of policy-violating content on large-scale platforms. The core challenge is that violations are rare and human labeling is expensive. The proposed solution combines ML-assisted probability sampling with LLM-based labeling. The sampling uses auxiliary safety model scores to concentrate labeling effort on high-exposure and high-risk content while preserving statistical unbiasedness. LLMs label sampled items using policy prompts validated against gold standards. This approach yields daily prevalence estimates with confidence intervals and supports granular drill-downs across segments from a single global sample, achieving significant cost and latency reductions compared to human-only workflows.

## Method Summary
The system implements a design-based probability sampling framework. Each content unit receives a weight `w_jd = C_jd^ν (s_jd^γ + ε)` based on its impressions `C_jd` and auxiliary safety model score `s_jd`. A weighted reservoir sample of size `m` is drawn with probabilities proportional to these weights. For sampled items, a multimodal LLM applies a structured policy prompt to produce binary violation labels. Prevalence is estimated using the Hansen-Hurwitz ratio estimator with inverse-probability weighting to correct the biased sample. The same sample supports drill-downs by storing segment impression counts `C_jdg` per item and applying post-stratification. Label quality is monitored via gold-set validation and random human review, with optional Rogan-Gladen adjustments for estimated error rates.

## Key Results
- ~15× reduction in labeling latency and >10× cost reduction versus human-only workflows.
- ML-assisted sampling increased sample positive fraction by 6–11× over impression-only sampling at fixed budgets.
- System enables daily operational monitoring with configurable sensitivity and statistical confidence intervals.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: ML-assisted probability sampling
- **Claim:** Concentrates labeling budget on high-exposure and high-risk content while preserving unbiasedness.
- **Mechanism:** Sampling weight formula `w_jd = C_jd^ν (s_jd^γ + ε)` combines impressions and safety model scores. Sample drawn with probabilities `p_jd ∝ w_jd` (e.g., via weighted reservoir). Estimate prevalence via Hansen–Hurwitz: `θ̂ = Σ (C_jd * Y_jd / p_jd) / Σ (C_jd / p_jd)`.
- **Core assumption:** Auxiliary scores are correlated with true violations; sampling probabilities are stable and known.
- **Break condition:** Auxiliary scores independent of `Y_jd` yields no efficiency; extreme weight variance reduces effective sample size.

### Mechanism 2: Multimodal LLM labeling with validation
- **Claim:** Scalable labeling with quality approaching human review.
- **Mechanism:** LLM receives content and SMEs' structured policy prompt; outputs label, rationale, confidence. Quality gated and monitored via gold sets and random human validation. Optional Rogan–Gladen adjustment for label error rates.
- **Core assumption:** LLM accurately interprets policy given prompt; quality stable with monitoring.
- **Break condition:** Policy drift, content shifts, or unchecked model updates degrade labeling quality.

### Mechanism 3: Single-sample drill-down estimation
- **Claim:** One global sample supports prevalence estimates across many segments without resampling.
- **Mechanism:** During sampling, persist per-item segment impressions `C_jdg`. For any segment `g`, compute `N̂_dg = (1/m) Σ (C_jdg * Y_jd / p_jd)` and use known denominator `D_dg` from logs or ratio estimator. Uses same inverse-probability weights.
- **Core assumption:** Segment breakdowns are additive and accurately attributed; global sample representative after reweighting.
- **Break condition:** Overlapping/erroneous segment definitions or too few sample items in a segment cause bias or high variance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Design-based probability sampling & Hansen–Hurwitz estimation.
  - *Why needed:* Unbiasedness hinges on correct inverse-probability weighting.
  - *Quick check:* "Why does `θ̂ = Σ (x_j y_j / p_j) / Σ (x_j / p_j)` remain unbiased for the population ratio `Σ x_j y_j / Σ x_j` under PPSWR?"
- **Concept:** Model-assisted (ML-assisted) sampling for rare events.
  - *Why needed:* Auxiliary scores improve efficiency while preserving unbiasedness.
  - *Quick check:* "How does increasing exponent γ in `p_j ∝ s_j^γ` affect expected positives and estimator variance?"
- **Concept:** Post-stratification and single-sample pivotability.
  - *Why needed:* Architecture's value proposition includes multiple drill-downs from one sample.
  - *Quick check:* "Given a global probability sample with stored `C_jdg`, how to estimate segment prevalence without a new sample?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Impression Logs → Auxiliary Safety Models → Sampling Engine (draws sample, stores IDs/weights/C_jdg) → Content Store → LLM Labeler → Gold Sets & Validation (quality monitoring) → Estimator Service (computes θ̂) → Dashboard & Storage.
  Feedback loops: Validation results → LLM Labeler (prompt/model iteration); Sample metadata → Estimator Service directly.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Ingest day d logs & scores.
  2. Compute weights w_jd, draw sample size m via PPSWOR, store IDs, weights, τ_d, and C_jdg.
  3. Fetch content, invoke LLM labeler; persist Y_jd with prompt/model version.
  4. Estimate θ̂_d (and θ̂_dg) using HH or HT formulas; compute variance, CI.
  5. Publish results, trigger alerts if MDE threshold exceeded.
  6. Monitor quality: compare LLM labels to gold set/random validation; log metrics.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Sample size m vs. cost/latency.
  - ML-assist strength γ: higher increases positive rate but raises weight variance → lower ESS.
  - PPSWR vs. PPSWOR: simplicity vs. variance.
  - LLM vs. human: throughput vs. monitoring overhead.
  - Gold set size vs. monitoring sensitivity.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Excessive CI widths → check ESS; weight variance may be too high.
  - Sudden jump in positive rate → drill down: gold set performance, score distribution, segments.
  - Degrading LLM quality → accuracy/F1 below threshold; iterate prompt/model.
  - Segment estimates with NaN/huge CIs → denominator too small or no sample items.
  - Cost overruns → rising token usage; check prompt inflation.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Simulate Appendix A: compare bias/CI coverage of PPS vs. ML-PPS on synthetic heavy-tailed population.
  2. Hyperparameter tuning: sweep (ν,γ); maximize sample positive rate while keeping ESS > threshold.
  3. LLM quality assessment: label 500 gold-set items blind; compute accuracy/F1; if below gate, revise prompt.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **User reach estimation:** How to efficiently estimate the fraction of daily active users exposed to at least one policy-violating impression? The current system measures impression-weighted prevalence, not user-level reach. Requires different sampling design (two-stage user-then-impression). (§10)
- **Ultra-rare violations:** What techniques optimally balance precision and cost when base rates ≪0.1% yield few positives even with ML-assist? Current adaptations (tuning, pooling, weekly windows) are heuristic; theoretical guidance missing. (§13)
- **Policy change comparability:** How to maintain auditability across policy definition updates and prompt/model drift? Backfilling is costly; statistical properties of combined pre/post-change estimates not analyzed; no bridging framework. (§13)
- **Variance decomposition:** How to decompose day-to-day fluctuations into sampling variance, labeler quality variation, and genuine content shifts? Current diagnostics are separate; integrated attribution model absent. (§11.4)

## Limitations
- Undisclosed prompt/gold-set details limit reproducibility and monitoring robustness.
- Efficiency depends on auxiliary model quality; degradation could erase gains and increase weight variance.
- Segment drill-downs susceptible to attribution errors; heavy-tailed impressions with high γ may lower effective sample size.

## Confidence
- Unbiased prevalence estimation (Hansen–Hurwitz + ML-PPS): **High**
- LLM labeling quality near human: **Medium**
- Multi-segment estimation from one sample: **High**
- Operational improvements (>10× cost reduction): **High**

## Next Checks
1. **Auxiliary correlation stress test:** In simulation, vary auxiliary model AUC and measure CI width/ESS to find efficiency breakpoints.
2. **Prompt robustness ablation:** Reword policy definitions, remove few-shots on gold set; record LLM accuracy/F1 changes.
3. **Segment estimate cross-check:** For high-volume segments, draw independent PPS samples and compare drill-down estimates to detect attribution bias.
