# Recovering Performance in Speech Emotion Recognition from Discrete Tokens via Multi-Layer Fusion and Paralinguistic Feature Integration

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17085  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17085  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Discrete WavLM tokens lose 6–14% Macro F1 versus continuous features; attention-based multi-layer fusion recovers 62.1% of this loss, and adding discretized openSMILE features recovers 75%, achieving Macro F1 0.3534.

## Executive Summary
Discrete speech tokens, used for efficient storage and processing, suffer significant performance degradation in Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) due to the loss of fine-grained paralinguistic and prosodic cues during quantization. This paper demonstrates that this gap can be largely closed by strategically combining information across multiple layers of a self-supervised learning (SSL) model via attention-based fusion and augmenting with hand-crafted paralinguistic features (openSMILE) that are also discretized. While neural audio codecs perform poorly due to reconstruction-oriented objectives, the proposed method with WavLM tokens recovers most of the lost performance, highlighting the importance of preserving and explicitly reintegrating acoustic details.

## Method Summary
The approach uses a frozen, fine-tuned WavLM-Large encoder to extract continuous hidden states from all 24 layers. Each layer’s features are independently quantized via k-means into discrete tokens (codebook sizes K∈{256,512,1000,2000,4000}), forming per-layer discrete streams. An attention-based fusion module then dynamically weights and combines these streams, allowing the model to leverage complementary information across layers (early layers for acoustic details, later layers for semantics). To recover further lost paralinguistic cues, the system concurrently extracts 74 hand-crafted openSMILE features (e.g., spectral bands, formants), which are separately quantized into tokens per feature category. These discrete paralinguistic tokens are aligned temporally with the SSL tokens, normalized, and concatenated before attentive statistical pooling and a downstream MLP classifier. Only the fusion, pooling, and classifier are trained; all encoders and codebooks remain frozen.

## Key Results
- Discrete tokens from a single WavLM layer cause a 6–14% Macro F1 drop versus continuous features, with the last layer (L23) being particularly affected.
- Attention-based fusion across all layers (L0–23) recovers approximately 62.1% of the performance gap, yielding a 0.02–0.03 Macro F1 gain over the best single-layer discrete configuration.
- Augmenting fused tokens with discretized openSMILE features recovers up to 75% of the gap, reaching a Macro F1 of 0.3534; formant and spectral features yield the largest individual gains, especially for sparse layer selections.
- Neural audio codecs (SpeechTokenizer, DAC, EnCodec) exhibit over 50% performance degradation relative to WavLM tokens and show inconsistent or minimal improvements from openSMILE augmentation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Information Loss via Quantization**  
Discretizing continuous SSL representations via k-means or neural codecs discards subtle spectral and prosodic variations critical for emotion perception (e.g., pitch tremor, spectral tilt). Neural codecs, optimized for reconstruction, further distort task-relevant signals. This loss manifests as a 6–14% Macro F1 drop for WavLM tokens and >50% for codecs. The core assumption is that emotional cues reside in continuous acoustic patterns misaligned with codebook boundaries.

**Mechanism 2: Complementary Information in Multi-Layer Fusion**  
SSL layers encode different aspects: earlier layers preserve low-level acoustic details, while later layers capture high-level semantics. Attention-based fusion dynamically weights contributions from multiple layers, avoiding destructive averaging and recovering information irretrievably lost in any single quantized layer. This explains the 62.1% gap recovery, with attention weights showing non-trivial contributions from early layers (e.g., L0–L5) despite dominance by final layers (L22–L23). Sparse layer selections sometimes outperform dense configurations, indicating strategic combination matters.

**Mechanism 3: Explicit Paralinguistic Feature Reintegration**  
Hand-crafted openSMILE features directly measure paralinguistic properties (formants, spectral energy, voice quality) that are quantized away in SSL tokens. Augmenting fused SSL tokens with these discrete features reinjects such cues, recovering up to 75% of the gap. Gains are largest for sparse layer configs, which lack intermediate acoustic information, and for specific categories like formants (F1–F3). This confirms that the residual loss after fusion overlaps with information captured by traditional feature sets.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Vector Quantization (k-means & RVQ)**  
  *Why needed:* Explains how continuous embeddings are mapped to discrete indices, a process that inherently loses information due to centroid approximation and codebook size limits. The paper uses per-layer k-means for WavLM and residual vector quantization (RVQ) for neural codecs.  
  *Quick check:* Doubling codebook size (K) does not guarantee performance increases due to diminishing returns and potential overfitting (see Figure 2’s marginal gains beyond K=1000).

- **Concept: Self-Supervised Learning Layer Dynamics**  
  *Why needed:* The fusion hypothesis relies on different SSL layers encoding complementary information (acoustic vs. semantic). Knowing that earlier layers are speaker/acoustic-sensitive and later layers are content-focused is key.  
  *Quick check:* The last layer of a fine-tuned WavLM may be less stable for SER when discretized because it is optimized for content understanding, not fine-grained emotional nuances, leading to sharper decision boundaries that are more vulnerable to quantization.

- **Concept: Paralinguistic Feature Sets (eGeMAPS / openSMILE)**  
  *Why needed:* These hand-crafted features (prosody, spectral, formants, etc.) serve as a proxy for lost emotional cues. Their dimensions and categories explain why certain augmentations help more (e.g., formants for capturing vocal tract shape).  
  *Quick check:* If formant features yield the largest gain for a dense layer configuration, it suggests that quantization particularly degraded information about vowel quality and articulation, which formants measure directly.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Frozen SSL Extractor (fine-tuned WavLM) -> Per-Layer Codebooks (k-means centroids) -> Reconstruction Lookup (centroid vectors) -> Layer Attention Module (temperature-scaled softmax) -> Modality Normalizer (LayerNorm + γ) -> Concatenation with openSMILE tokens -> Attentive Statistics Pooling -> MLP Classifier

**Critical path:** Multi-layer continuous features are extracted, quantized per layer, reconstructed to continuous approximations, fused via attention, aligned and normalized with discretized openSMILE features, pooled, and classified. Only modules post-reconstruction are trained.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Codebook size (K):* Larger K preserves more information but increases memory and overfitting risk; optimal K varies with layer configuration (sparse layers often prefer smaller K).  
- *Layer selection:* Dense (all layers) provides more raw material but may include redundant/noisy layers; sparse configurations are computationally cheaper and benefit more from augmentation.  
- *openSMILE granularity:* Full 74-feature set is safest, but category-specific ablation (e.g., formants alone) can match full-set gains, suggesting feature redundancy.  
- *Neural codec choice:* SpeechTokenizer, DAC, and EnCodec are frozen; their reconstruction-focused objectives mismatch SER, making them poorly suited even with augmentation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Single-layer discrete tokens (especially L23) plateau near Macro F1 0.32, far below continuous ~0.36.  
- Neural codecs remain below 0.18 even with openSMILE, while discrete WavLM reaches 0.35+.  
- Adding openSMILE to dense L0–23 yields minimal gain (<1%), indicating saturation; effort wasted if fusion already captures most paralinguistic info.  
- Layer attention weights collapsing to a single layer (e.g., only L23) suggests fusion failed—check normalization or temperature parameter.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Establish continuous WavLM baseline (~0.362 Macro F1) and sweep single-layer discrete WavLM (6 layer configs × 5 K values) to map performance gap.  
2. Implement attention-based multi-layer fusion; compare all-layer, last-8, and sparse-6 selections to confirm >0.015 Macro F1 gain and non-uniform attention weights.  
3. Augment best fused config (e.g., L0–23, K=1000) and a sparse config (e.g., L1,3,7,12,18,23) with each openSMILE category; expect sparse config to show >2% gain with formants/spectral, dense config <1%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Which paralinguistic features are most degraded by quantization in SSL tokens, and how does this vary with layer and codebook size? The paper lacks direct measurement of degradation per feature.
2. What additional strategies are needed to fully close the remaining ~25% Macro F1 gap between discrete and continuous SER models? The residual loss may be irrecoverable or require richer features.
3. Why do neural audio codecs underperform (>50% Macro F1 drop) on SER and show inconsistent gains from acoustic augmentation? Their reconstruction-focused objectives may fundamentally misalign with emotional salience.

## Limitations
- Missing key hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, optimizer, MLP architecture, attention temperature schedule) hinders exact replication.
- No details on k-means training (sample size, seed, split) for codebooks, risking token variability.
- “Adaptive resampling” for openSMILE alignment is undefined; frame-rate mismatches could distort augmentation.
- Statistical reporting limited to means over three runs; no variance or significance tests provided.
- Single corpus (MSP-Podcast v1.12) limits generalizability.

## Confidence
- Quantization information loss: **High** – Consistent 6–14% drop across WavLM variants, >50% for neural codecs.  
- Multi-layer fusion recovery: **High** – Attention fusion yields significant gains; weights spread non-uniformly.  
- OpenSMILE augmentation benefit: **Medium** – Gains strong for sparse layers but negligible for dense configs; 75% recovery is best-case.  
- Neural codec underperformance: **High** – Severe and consistent degradation across all tested codecs.
