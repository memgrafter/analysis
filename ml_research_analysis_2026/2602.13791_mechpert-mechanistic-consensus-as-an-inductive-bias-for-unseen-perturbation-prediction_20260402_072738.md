# MechPert: Mechanistic Consensus as an Inductive Bias for Unseen Perturbation Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.13791
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13791
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Improves prediction of unseen perturbation effects (Pearson correlation) by up to 10.5% in low‑data regimes and selects experimental anchor genes outperforming centrality heuristics by up to 46%.

## Executive Summary
Predicting transcriptomic responses to novel genetic perturbations is a fundamental challenge in functional genomics, with applications in CRISPR screening and drug target discovery. Existing language model‑based approaches rely on symmetric co‑occurrence in text, missing directed regulatory logic. MechPert introduces a mechanistic consensus framework: multiple LLM agents independently propose two hypothesis sets for a target gene—a Semantic set (functionally similar genes) and a Causal set (directed regulators, e.g., upstream TFs) with confidence scores. These are aggregated via a consensus mechanism that filters spurious associations, yielding a weighted gene neighborhood. The final prediction is a weighted average of the observed perturbation profiles of selected neighbors. Evaluated on Perturb‑seq data across four human cell lines, MechPert improves Pearson correlation by up to 10.5% over similarity baselines with only 50 observed perturbations. For active experimental design, it selects anchor genes that yield up to 46% better prediction than network centrality heuristics in K562. Ablations confirm the value of directed causal hypotheses and consensus, though binary consensus often outperforms confidence‑weighted aggregation due to LLM miscalibration.

## Method Summary
MechPert is a zero‑shot, inference‑only method. Given observed perturbation profiles for N genes, for a query gene it retrieves two gene sets from a candidate pool using a large language model: a Semantic set (functionally similar genes) and a Causal set (directed regulators) with confidence scores. Retrieval is repeated across K=3 independent reasoning chains to reduce randomness. A Consensus Aggregator merges outputs: each gene receives a binary weight (1 if present in any chain) or a confidence‑weighted score (causal genes scaled by normalized confidence, semantic genes always weight 1). The Neighborhood Builder selects the top‑k genes by aggregated weight, and the Predictor outputs the weighted average of their observed expression profiles. For experimental design (Task II), an iterative Anchor Selector employs the same dual‑generation/consensus logic to choose diverse master regulator genes that maximize predictive coverage under a diversity constraint.

## Key Results
- In low‑data settings (N=50), MechPert achieves up to 10.5% higher Pearson correlation than similarity‑based baselines across four human cell lines.
- For active perturbation selection, MechPert‑chosen anchors improve prediction accuracy by up to 46% compared to standard network‑centrality heuristics (e.g., in K562).
- Ablation studies show that removing the causal set reduces performance, and binary consensus often outperforms confidence‑weighted aggregation, indicating LLM confidence is poorly calibrated.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Independent multi‑agent consensus filters stochastic LLM hallucinations by treating consistent hypotheses across reasoning chains as more biologically plausible.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple LLM agents (K=3) independently generate candidate regulators for a target gene. The frequency of a gene's appearance across these independent chains (binary consensus) acts as a proxy for regulatory reliability, reducing the impact of any single agent's confabulation.
- **Core assumption:** Biologically correct regulatory relationships are more likely to be recovered consistently across diverse reasoning traces than spurious associations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Multiple agents independently propose candidate regulators with associated confidence scores; these are aggregated through a consensus mechanism that filters spurious associations."
  - [section 3.1] "To reduce sensitivity to any single LLM call, we execute K independent reasoning chains (we use K=3) and retain genes that appear across multiple chains."
  - [corpus] Neighbor paper "Progressive Multi‑Agent Reasoning for Biological Perturbation Prediction" (arxiv:2602.07408) explores similar multi‑agent frameworks for disentangling biological causality, providing indirect support for the consensus approach.
- **Break condition:** If the underlying pretraining distribution systematically lacks directional regulatory knowledge (e.g., for understudied genes in sparse literature), consensus frequency may converge on a consistent but incorrect hub due to publication bias, as suggested by poor performance in HepG2/Jurkat [Table 2].

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Incorporating the LLM's self‑reported confidence scores for causal hypotheses (confidence‑weighted aggregation) provides a second‑order refinement over simple frequency counting, stabilizing predictions by down‑weighting low‑certainty suggestions.
- **Mechanism:** For genes identified in the causal set, their contribution to the final predicted expression profile is scaled by the agent's confidence score `c_i ∈ [0,1]`, while semantic neighbors receive unit weight. This allows the model to modulate the influence of its own uncertainty.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM's internal calibration for biological certainty, while imperfect, contains a signal that distinguishes high‑plausibility regulators from speculative ones.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1] "Confidence‑Weighted: Semantic neighbors receive unit weight (w_j = 1), while causal hypotheses are scaled by the agent’s reported confidence c_j (w_j = c_j)."
  - [section 4.3] Ablation study shows confidence weighting adds a +4.9% gain over binary consensus in Jurkat (N=50), and provides lower variance (±0.031 vs ±0.035).
  - [section 6] "The framework relies on consensus across multiple LLM agents rather than calibrated confidence estimates. We observe that majority voting performs comparably to or better than confidence‑weighted aggregation, indicating that model‑reported confidence is not reliably aligned with biological plausibility." **Assumption:** The observed benefit in some regimes suggests partial alignment, but the limitation notes this calibration is weak.
- **Break condition:** If the model's confidence scores are systematically misaligned with biological truth (e.g., overconfident on popular but incorrect pathways), weighted aggregation could amplify errors relative to simple voting.

### Mechanism 3
- **Mechanism:** By explicitly prompting the LLM to generate *two distinct sets*—(1) a "Semantic Set" of functionally similar genes and (2) a "Causal Set" of directed regulators (e.g., upstream TFs)—the framework disentangles symmetric co‑occurrence from asymmetric regulatory logic. The final prediction is a weighted combination of both sets.
- **Core assumption:** Functional similarity (co‑mention) and directed regulatory relationships capture complementary, non‑redundant aspects of a perturbation's effect; their combination yields a more robust neighborhood for kNN‑style prediction than either alone.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "MechPert encourages LLM agents to generate directed regulatory hypotheses rather than relying solely on functional similarity."
  - [section 3.1] "The causal set is the key addition: by explicitly prompting for directed relationships, we encourage the model to reason about regulatory logic rather than symmetric similarity."
  - [section 4.4] Case study on *NVL* shows the semantic baseline conflates *NVL* with paralog *VCP* due to lexical co‑occurrence, predicting an Unfolded Protein Response. The causal consensus correctly identifies the distinct ribosomal stress pathway via mechanistic reasoning, demonstrating the value of the directed hypothesis set.
- **Break condition:** In contexts where the gene's function is defined entirely by its membership in a complex or pathway (symmetric similarity is highly predictive) and its directed regulators are poorly characterized, the causal set may add noise or be sparse, potentially degrading performance if given equal weight.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Gene Regulatory Networks (GRNs)**
  - **Why needed here:** MechPert's core innovation is shifting from retrieving *functionally similar* genes to retrieving *directed regulators/targets*. Understanding GRNs—asymmetric, signed (activation/repression), context‑specific relationships—is essential to grasp why co‑occurrence (symmetrical) is insufficient and what "causal hypothesis" means in this biological context.
  - **Quick check question:** *If Gene A is a transcription factor that activates Gene B, what is the expected directional effect of knocking out Gene A on Gene B's expression? How would this relationship appear in a scientific text corpus?*

- **Concept: Perturbation‑Seq Experimental Design**
  - **Why needed here:** The framework is evaluated on and designed for predicting the outcome of *unseen genetic perturbations* (e.g., CRISPRi knockdown). Knowing that Perturb‑seq measures transcriptome‑wide effects of such interventions, and that screening all 20k genes is infeasible, explains the need for few‑shot prediction and active anchor selection.
  - **Quick check question:** *In a Perturb‑seq experiment with 50 observed perturbations, what is the computational task when asked to predict the effect of a 51st, unobserved perturbation?*

- **Concept: LLM Agent Ensembles & Consensus Mechanisms**
  - **Why needed here:** MechPert's aggregation logic (binary/confidence‑weighted) is not a standard model ensemble but a hypothesis aggregation over *independent reasoning chains* from a single model. Understanding how this differs from traditional ML ensembles (different models) and why it's used to mitigate LLM‑specific failure modes (hallucination, lack of consistency) is key.
  - **Quick check question:** *Why might running the same LLM prompt multiple times with temperature >0 yield different candidate regulator lists? How does taking the intersection (consensus) of these lists act as a filter?*

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Dual Prompt Generator (issues Semantic and Causal prompts) -> Consensus Aggregator (merges K independent outputs via binary or confidence‑weighted scoring) -> Neighborhood Builder (selects top‑k genes) -> Predictor (fixed weighted average of observed profiles). For Task II (experimental design), an Anchor Selector iterates the same generation/consensus loop with diversity constraints.
- **Critical path:** Query Gene -> Dual Hypothesis Generation (K chains) -> Consensus Scoring per Gene -> Top‑k Selection -> Weighted Averaging of Training Profiles -> Predicted Response.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Simplicity vs. Expressiveness: Uses a simple weighted average, ignoring sign (activation/repression) and non‑linear combinatorial effects; reduces complexity but discards known regulatory mechanics.
  - Generality vs. Context‑Specificity: Relies on LLM's latent knowledge, which may be generic; does not fine‑tune or ground in the specific cell line’s data beyond observed perturbation profiles.
  - Computational Cost: Requires K LLM inference calls per query; lightweight compared to retraining a GNN but non‑trivial for large‑scale screening.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Performance collapse in sparse‑knowledge contexts: Significant drop in correlation for HepG2/Jurkat in active design [Table 2], where PPI/literature may be less comprehensive.
  - Over‑reliance on lexical similarity: Semantic baseline failure on *NVL* vs. *VCP* [4.4] indicates the model can conflate paralogs; the causal set disambiguates.
  - Confidence miscalibration: Ablation [4.3] shows confidence weighting helps but is not always superior; [section 6] notes model confidence is not reliably aligned with biological plausibility.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablate the Causal Set:** Run the pipeline with *only* the Semantic Set (LangPert baseline) across all cell lines and N. Quantify the performance delta attributable solely to adding directed regulatory hypotheses.
  2. **Test Consensus Depth:** Vary K (number of independent chains) from 1 to 5. Measure how prediction stability (variance) and accuracy (mean C20) change. Expect diminishing returns; check if K=3 is sufficient.
  3. **Sign‑Agnostic vs. Sign‑Aware Aggregation:** Implement a simple heuristic to assign sign (±1) to causal hypotheses based on known TF→target polarity (e.g., from a small knowledge base). Compare against MechPert’s sign‑agnostic additive model to measure the cost of ignoring directionality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does gene- and cell line-specific knowledge density (e.g., PubMed publication counts, experimental coverage) affect MechPert's robustness, given the noted performance drop in HepG2/Jurkat cells?
- Can LLM confidence scores be calibrated or post-processed to better reflect biological plausibility, thereby improving the reliability of confidence‑weighted aggregation?
- What is the potential performance gain from incorporating directional regulatory signs (activation vs. repression) into the neighborhood prediction, and what practical knowledge sources (e.g., ENCODE) could enable this?
- How can the reproducibility of LLM‑based perturbation prediction be standardized across different models, prompts, and data splits to facilitate faithful benchmarking?

## Limitations
- Limited reproducibility: exact LLM model, parameters, and confidence calibration not fully specified.
- Data preprocessing ambiguities (gene filtering, splits, candidate pool) hinder faithful replication.
- Performance degrades in HepG2/Jurkat cells, indicating limited robustness in sparse‑knowledge regimes.

## Confidence
- Consensus reduces hallucination: Medium
- Confidence‑weighted aggregation: Low
- Directed regulatory hypotheses add signal: High
- Overall predictive improvement: Medium
