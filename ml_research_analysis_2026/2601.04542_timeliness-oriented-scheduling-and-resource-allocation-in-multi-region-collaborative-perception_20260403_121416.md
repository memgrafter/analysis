# Timeliness-Oriented Scheduling and Resource Allocation in Multi-Region Collaborative Perception

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.04542
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04542
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposes TAMP, a Lyapunov-based online algorithm that jointly schedules regions and allocates communication volume to minimize long-term penalty from Age of Information and bandwidth use, achieving up to 27% higher Average Precision than four baselines in multi-region collaborative perception simulations.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the core challenge of dynamic scheduling and resource allocation in multi-region collaborative perception (CP). The goal is to maintain high perception accuracy under limited communication bandwidth and computational resources, while respecting stringent information timeliness constraints. It introduces the Timeliness-Aware Multi-region Prioritized (TAMP) algorithm, which uses a Lyapunov optimization framework to convert a long-term stochastic problem into a per-slot decision. A key innovation is an empirical penalty function, fitted from real-world RCooper data, that models the non-linear degradation of detection Average Precision (AP) with Age of Information (AoI) and transmitted communication volume. Extensive simulations on intersection and corridor scenarios show TAMP consistently outperforms heuristics like Age-Prio and Max-Weight, demonstrating the effectiveness of its co-designed scheduling and volume allocation strategy.

## Method Summary
TAMP formulates the problem as minimizing a long-term average penalty `f(h,b)`, which increases with a region's AoI `h` (slots since last update) and decreases with the communication volume `b` (megabits) allocated to it. The penalty function parameters are empirically fitted from the RCooper dataset. The intractable problem with average communication budget constraints `Γ_a` is solved via Lyapunov optimization. A virtual queue `Q_a` tracks budget excess. Per slot, for each idle region, TAMP first computes the slot-optimal volume `b*` by minimizing a derived term involving the penalty's partial derivative. It then computes a priority index `Π_a = U_a(h_a, b*) - V_a * Q_a * b*`, where `U` approximates the marginal penalty reduction and `V_a` controls the budget trade-off. The scheduler selects the top `M` idle regions by `Π`, grants them volume `b*`, and splits it among sensors using a spatial confidence mask. After task completion, AoI and `Q` are updated.

## Key Results
- TAMP achieves up to **27% higher AP** compared to the best baseline (Max-Weight) across varied transmission rates, communication budgets, and processing delays.
- It demonstrates **robust adaptation** to channel conditions and computational latency by jointly optimizing scheduling (`u_a,k`) and volume (`b_a,k`), whereas fixed-volume baselines falter.
- The Lyapunov-based virtual queue effectively enforces long-term communication budgets (`Γ_a`) with low violation, confirming the theoretical `O(1/V)` utility-`O(V)` violation trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: A non-linear penalty function `f(h,b)` maps the joint impact of Age of Information (AoI) and communication volume on perception accuracy (AP), enabling a quantitative trade-off.
- **Mechanism**: The function is fitted from real-world RCooper dataset experiments where AP is measured under controlled AoI and communication volume. The penalty is defined as `˜f(h, b) = ρ_max - ρ_fitted(h, b)`. This creates a continuous, monotonic surface: penalty increases with AoI (stale data) and decreases with communication volume (richer features).
- **Core assumption**: The empirical relationship between AP, AoI, and communication volume generalizes across similar dynamic environments and is stable enough for scheduling decisions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "proposes an empirical penalty function that models the non-linear degradation of Average Precision (AP) with Age of Information (AoI) and communication volume, derived from the real-world RCooper dataset."
  - [section IV] Details the fitting process using exponential and sigmoid-exponential decay models for intersection and corridor scenarios, respectively.
- **Break condition**: If the underlying relationship between AoI, data volume, and AP changes significantly (e.g., new sensor modalities, drastic environmental shifts), the fitted penalty function becomes misaligned, causing suboptimal scheduling.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: A Lyapunov optimization framework transforms the intractable long-term stochastic scheduling problem with average constraints into a per-slot deterministic maximization of a priority index.
- **Mechanism**: A virtual queue `Q_a,k` tracks deviation from the long-term communication budget `Γ_a`. The algorithm minimizes an upper bound on the "drift-plus-penalty" expression `∆(Q_a,k) - V_a * U_a(h_a,k, b_a,k) * u_a,k` each slot. This decomposes the problem: (1) compute optimal volume `b*` by minimizing per-slot penalty term from Lemma 1, (2) decide scheduling by maximizing `U_a(h_a,k, b*_a,k) - V_a * Q_a,k * b*_a,k`.
- **Core assumption**: The system is stabilizable; there exists a stationary policy that satisfies the long-term average constraints. The Lyapunov drift bound provides a valid performance proxy.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section III.B] "We introduce a virtual queue, Q_a,k for each region... A stable virtual queue enforces the long-term communication budget Γ_a."
  - [appendix C] Provides the mathematical drift bound derivation.
- **Break condition**: If the system dynamics are non-ergodic, or if the penalty function `f(h,b)` is highly discontinuous/non-convex, the Lyapunov drift bound may be too loose, and the per-slot greedy decision based on the index could lead to instability or constraint violation over the long term.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The scheduling priority index `Π_a,k` effectively balances immediate scheduling worth (reducing cumulative penalty) against immediate resource cost (communication volume), enabling adaptive region selection.
- **Mechanism**: The worth term `U_a(ha,b)` (derived from KKT conditions of a quasi-convex approximation of the long-term penalty) increases with AoI `ha` (urgency) but decreases with chosen volume `b` (due to added delay). The cost term `V_a * Q_a,k * b` penalizes allocating volume to regions that have already exceeded their communication budget. The index is computed for each idle region using its state (`ha,k`) and its slot-optimal `b*_a,k`, and the top regions are scheduled.
- **Core assumption**: The quasi-convex approximation and use of average delay `¯d` in the utility index `U(h,b)` accurately characterize the optimal long-term scheduling threshold for the stochastic system.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section III.A] "By analyzing the KKT condition of P2, we derive a metric that captures the scheduling urgency... U(h,b) = ..."
  - [section V.C.2] Demonstrates via fixed-volume baselines that a static `b` is suboptimal across channel conditions, implying the adaptive `b*` in `Π` is beneficial.
- **Break condition**: If the penalty function's shape changes such that `U(h,b)` is no longer monotonic in `h` or if the correlation between `b` and instantaneous delay `d` is highly non-linear/variable, the index `Π` may misrank regions, selecting low-value updates or starving high-value ones.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Age of Information (AoI)**
  - **Why needed here**: AoI (`h_a,k`) is the core state variable quantifying information staleness. The entire scheduling objective is built on reducing the long-term penalty, which is a function of AoI. Understanding its discrete evolution (staircase increase, reset on task completion) is essential to grasp the penalty accumulation.
  - **Quick check question**: How does the system's slot duration `τ` affect the relationship between physical task delay (in seconds) and the AoI measured in slots?
- **Concept: Lyapunov Optimization for Queueing Systems**
  - **Why needed here**: The method is the theoretical engine that converts the average-cost, average-constraint stochastic problem into the per-slot index `Π_a,k`. It provides a provable performance bound (O(1/V) utility vs O(V) constraint violation) for the online algorithm. Without it, solving P1 directly is intractable.
  - **Quick check question**: In the drift-plus-penalty expression, what is the role of the control parameter `V_a`? What happens to the trade-off between maximizing utility and enforcing the communication budget as `V_a` is increased?
- **Concept: Feature-Level Fusion in Collaborative Perception**
  - **Why needed here**: This is the CP paradigm used. It explains *why* communication volume (`b`) matters: transmitting richer features (higher `b`) preserves more spatial/semantic detail for fusion, improving detection AP, but increases transmission and processing delay. This links `b` to the penalty function and to the physical delay `d_sec`.
  - **Quick check question**: In feature-level fusion, what is the key difference in information content and bandwidth requirement compared to raw data (point cloud) sharing or object-level (bounding box) sharing?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  Central Scheduler (BS) -> Per-Region Agents (Sensors) -> Channel -> Edge Server (Co-located with BS)
- **Critical path**:
  1. **Begin Slot k**: BS has state `{h_a,k-1, Q_a,k-1, A_idle,k, A_k-1}`.
  2. **Candidate Evaluation**: For each `a ∈ A_idle,k`, solve (18) for `b*_a,k` (requires `d_a,k` estimate) and compute `Π_a,k`.
  3. **Competitive Selection**: Sort candidates by `Π`, select top up to `M_rem`. Sets `A_selected,k`.
  4. **Update Active Set**: `A_k = (A \ A_idle,k) ∪ A_selected,k`.
  5. **Resource Allocation**: For `a ∈ A_selected,k`, set `u_a,k=1`, `b_a,k=b*_a,k`, call `Split(b_a,k)` to get `{b_n,k}`. For all `a ∈ A_k`, allocate equal bandwidth `B_total / |A_k|`.
  6. **System Update**: Sensors execute extraction/compression/transmission. BS waits for completion. For completed regions, `A_k` shrinks. Then update `Q_a,k+1` (22) and `h_a,k+1` (9) for all `a`. Refresh `A_idle,k+1`.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **`V_a` Parameter**: Larger `V_a` increases penalty for using communication budget, leading to more conservative volume allocation and faster convergence to `Γ_a` but potentially higher penalty from stale data. Smaller `V_a` prioritizes AP but risks budget violation.
  - **`M` (Capacity)**: Larger `M` allows more concurrent CP tasks, reducing average AoI, but increases interference/contention for bandwidth `B_total` and may increase per-region `d_tr` if bandwidth is divided equally. TAMP's competitive selection aims to pick the most urgent `M` regions.
  - **`Split(b_a,k)` Algorithm**: The binary search for confidence threshold `θ` (Fig. 3) is a heuristic. It assumes spatial confidence maps from Where2Comm are good proxies for feature importance. If this assumption fails (e.g., in novel object types), the allocated `b_n,k` may not align with actual perceptual value.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Persistent High `Q_a,k`**: A region's virtual queue consistently at high value indicates its communication budget `Γ_a` is too low for its required `b*` to achieve reasonable AP, or its `U_a` is perpetually low (low urgency). Action: Increase `Γ_a` or reduce `M`.
  - **Low `Π_a,k` for all idle regions**: Indicates system is overscheduled (high `|A_k|` causing high delays `d_sec`, or all `h_a` are low). TAMP will idle many slots. Action: Increase `M` or `B_total`.
  - **AP Degradation with High `M`**: Performance plateaus or drops when `M` is large (Fig. 10). Signifies bandwidth `B_total` is the bottleneck; equal allocation `B_total/M` per region becomes too small, making `d_tr` dominate. Action: Increase `B_total` or implement smarter, unequal bandwidth allocation.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Vary Transmission Rate (Fig. 7a/8a)**: Tests adaptability to channel conditions. TAMP's advantage is largest in low-to-mid rate regimes where baselines' fixed-volume strategies fail. Verifies the co-design of `u` and `b`.
  2. **Vary Communication Bound `Γ_a` (Fig. 7b/8b)**: Tests long-term budget management. TAMP's Lyapunov-based control (`Π` with `Q`) handles tight budgets (`Γ_a=0.75`) gracefully, while myopic baselines struggle.
  3. **Vary Feature Extraction Delay (Fig. 7c/8c)**: Tests robustness to sensor-side compute variability. All degrade, but TAMP degrades less because its `b*` and scheduling adapt to the resulting `d_sec` via `U(h,b)`, while baselines cannot.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Does the empirically fitted penalty function generalize to collaborative perception scenarios beyond the RCooper dataset's intersection and corridor environments? The paper evaluates TAMP using the same fitted penalty functions across simulations but does not validate them on out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Open Question 2**: Can the TAMP algorithm adapt its penalty function parameters online to handle environmental distribution shifts without retraining? The current approach uses fixed parameters derived from offline fitting and lacks a mechanism for updates.
- **Open Question 3**: How does TAMP compare against modern lightweight reinforcement learning (RL) based scheduling policies for multi-region CP under the same constraints? The authors contrast their approach with DRL but only compare against simple heuristics, not RL baselines.
- **Open Question 4**: Does TAMP support dynamic changes in the set of regions or sensors, such as sudden sensor failures or the addition of new roadside units? The system model assumes a fixed set of regions and sensors; no mechanism for dynamic topology changes is provided.

## Limitations
- The exact numerical values of the fitted penalty function parameters are not provided, forcing approximate re-fitting that may alter absolute AP baselines.
- The penalty function is derived solely from the RCooper dataset; its transferability to other datasets, sensor configurations, or adverse conditions (weather, lighting) is unknown.
- Theoretical assumptions (e.g., system stabilizability, quasi-convex `U(h,b)` with constant `\bar{d}`) may not hold in highly variable real-world delay distributions, potentially loosening the Lyapunov bound.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (empirical penalty function)**: *Medium*. The empirical fit is plausible and well-motivated but lacks uncertainty quantification and out-of-distribution validation.
- **Mechanism 2 (Lyapunov optimization)**: *High*. The framework is standard, rigorously derived, and provides theoretical performance guarantees.
- **Mechanism 3 (priority index `Π`)**: *Medium*. Its design is logical and simulation-validated but relies on the accuracy of `U(h,b)` and the constant `\bar{d}` assumption.
- **Overall reported AP improvement (up to 27%)**: *Medium*. Results are strong and consistent across simulation scenarios but are not backed by real-world deployment.

## Next Checks
1. **Re-fit penalty with confidence intervals**: Obtain RCooper data, perform cross-validation, and report parameter stability and prediction intervals for `f(h,b)`.
2. **Cross-dataset evaluation**: Run TAMP on an entirely different CP dataset (e.g., OPV2V) using its own fitted penalty and compare performance trends against RCooper-based `f`.
3. **Empirical drift measurement**: Track virtual queue `Q` over a long simulation horizon (e.g., 10k slots) and measure the observed utility-constraint violation trade-off, comparing it to the theoretical `O(1/V)` bound.
