# Variational Autoencoders for P-wave Detection on Strong Motion Earthquake Spectrograms

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05759
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05759
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Architectural constraints favoring global context over pixel-perfect reconstruction are essential for robust, self-supervised P-wave detection; attention mechanisms achieved an overall AUC of 0.875 and 0.91 in the near-source range.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of robust P-wave detection for earthquake early warning in noisy strong-motion records with limited labeled data. The authors reframe the task as a self-supervised anomaly detection problem, training Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) exclusively on normal P-wave spectrograms. Four VAE architectures were evaluated to investigate the trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and detection performance. Results demonstrate that skip connections, while minimizing reconstruction error, cause overgeneralization and degrade detection. In contrast, architectures with self-attention bottlenecks prioritize global context, yielding the highest detection performance. The findings establish that architectural biases toward abstract, global representations are critical for effective anomaly-based P-wave detection under real-world noise.

## Method Summary
The method uses 648 records from the Turkish National Strong Motion Network (AFAD). Preprocessing involves adaptive sliding window artifact removal, synthetic fractional Brownian motion noise augmentation, and short-time Fourier transform to generate [32×92] spectrograms (<30 Hz). Fixed-duration (2.44 s) windows centered on P-wave onsets are extracted for training. Four VAE variants are trained: Basic-VAE (baseline), Skip-VAE (U-Net style skip connections), Attention-VAE (Transformer self-attention bottleneck), and Hybrid-VAE (both). The encoder downsamples to a [48,8,23] latent tensor; the loss combines mean squared reconstruction error and KL divergence. For detection, a sliding window scans test spectrograms; per-window Normalized Cross-Correlation (NCC) between input and reconstruction serves as an anomaly score, thresholded to produce P-wave predictions. Performance is primarily evaluated by AUC-ROC on NCC scores.

## Key Results
- A fundamental trade-off exists: architectures achieving lower reconstruction error (MAE) do not necessarily yield better detection (AUC). Skip-VAE attained the lowest MAE (~0.0012) but an AUC of only ~0.82 due to overgeneralization.
- Attention-VAE achieved the highest overall AUC (0.875) and excelled in the critical near-source range (0–40 km, AUC 0.91) by enforcing global context.
- Architectural design is decisive: skip connections act as identity shortcuts that bypass the latent bottleneck, enabling noise reconstruction; attention bottlenecks force reliance on compressed global representations, widening the reconstruction error gap between normal and anomalous inputs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Architectural constraints that limit reconstruction fidelity can enhance anomaly detection performance**
- **Claim:** Forcing the decoder to rely on a generalized, context-aware latent representation widens the reconstruction error gap between normal and anomalous inputs, improving separability.
- **Mechanism:** The VAE learns a compressed manifold of normal P-wave patterns from training data. Overly powerful decoders (e.g., with skip connections) can reconstruct anomalies directly, collapsing the gap. Constrained architectures (e.g., bottlenecks) yield higher error for anomalies while maintaining manageable reconstruction for normals.
- **Evidence:** Abstract states constraints favoring global context are essential. Section 4.2 links Attention-VAE's high AUC to its forced learning of global context that suppresses local noise. Visual comparisons (Section 4.3) show Skip-VAE's near-identical reconstructions (low MAE) versus Attention-VAE's blurrier, generalized outputs (higher MAE).

**Mechanism 2: U-Net style skip connections cause "overgeneralization"**
- **Claim:** Skip connections allow anomalous noise patterns to bypass the variational bottleneck, reducing detection performance.
- **Mechanism:** Skip connections concatenate high-resolution encoder features directly into the decoder, providing pathways for local details to be reproduced without full reliance on the latent code. This enables the model to reconstruct noise patterns as effectively as P-waves, minimizing the NCC gap.
- **Evidence:** Abstract explicitly notes skip connections induce overgeneralization. Section 4.2 describes skips as detrimental "information leaks" that create near-identity mappings. Skip-VAE's low MAE (~0.00119) but lower AUC (~0.821) compared to Attention-VAE empirically supports this.

**Mechanism 3: Self-attention bottlenecks improve detection by enforcing global context**
- **Claim:** Attention mechanisms compel the model to capture global spectro-temporal dependencies, suppressing local noise and emphasizing discriminative P-wave structure.
- **Mechanism:** The attention bottleneck tokenizes the latent feature map, allowing each token to attend to all others. This forces reconstruction based on global relationships rather than copying local pixels. Consequently, reconstruction error increases for inputs with anomalous global context (noise-dominated windows) but remains lower for normal P-waves.
- **Evidence:** Abstract highlights attention's priority on global context and high AUC. Section 4.2 explicitly states Attention-VAE's architecture forces global context learning. Optimal performance with 16-24 attention heads (Section 4.4.2) suggests multi-scale global dependencies are key. Temporal sensitivity analysis (Section 4.5) indicates reliance on specific global spectro-temporal patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Variational Autoencoder probabilistic latent space**
  - *Why needed:* The VAE's KL divergence regularization creates a smooth, continuous latent manifold. This manifold should encompass normal P-wave variations but exclude anomalous patterns, forming the basis for anomaly detection via reconstruction error.
  - *Quick check:* How does the KL term in a VAE loss function shape the latent space geometry, and why might that help distinguish unseen anomalies?

- **Concept: Self-supervised anomaly detection via reconstruction**
  - *Why needed:* The entire method is one-class; the model must internalize that only normal data is seen during training. Failure to reconstruct a new input indicates it lies outside the learned manifold, i.e., an anomaly.
  - *Quick check:* If a VAE trained only on cat images is given a dog image, what reconstruction error would you expect and why?

- **Concept: Architectural inductive bias (skip connections vs. attention)**
  - *Why needed:* The core finding is that architectural choices control the reconstruction-detection trade-off. Understanding that skip connections bias toward local pixel fidelity while attention biases toward global abstract structure is key to interpreting the results.
  - *Quick check:* In a convolutional network, what is the primary functional effect of a U-Net skip connection compared to a self-attention layer?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Preprocessing -> Core VAE Backbone -> [Variant 1: Skip-VAE | Variant 2: Attention-VAE | Variant 3: Hybrid-VAE] -> Detection Head (NCC scoring) -> Evaluation (AUC)
- **Critical path:** 1. Preprocess strong-motion records (ASW artifact removal, FBM noise augmentation, STFT to [32×92] spectrograms). 2. Slice into 2.44s windows with 100ms shift; use only P-wave-containing windows for VAE training. 3. Train 492 VAE configurations (vary architecture, latent dimension, attention depth/heads). 4. Select best model per architecture family based on highest AUC. 5. Analyze trade-off: compare best models' AUC vs. MAE. 6. Conduct sensitivity analyses: latent capacity impact (Table 2), attention hyperparameters (Table 3), temporal shift (Fig 7), distance performance (Fig 8).
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Reconstruction Fidelity vs. Detection: Skip connections minimize MAE but harm AUC; attention maximizes AUC but increases MAE.
  - Architectural Complexity vs. Stability: Pure Attention-VAE achieves peak AUC but is high-variance across hyperparameters; Hybrid-VAE is more stable.
  - Temporal Precision vs. Robustness: Attention-VAE is highly sensitive to P-wave alignment (Fig 7), requiring high-overlap windows; 1D convolutional VAEs (prior work) are more position-robust.
  - Near-source Performance: Attention-VAE excels at 0-40 km (AUC 0.91) but degrades with distance due to attenuation/scattering (Fig 8).
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - Overgeneralization (Skip-VAE): Very low MAE (~0.0012) and low AUC (~0.82). Reconstructions visually nearly identical to input, including noise.
  - Over-smoothing (Attention/Basic-VAE): Higher MAE, blurry reconstructions that miss sharp P-wave onsets. This is desirable for detection.
  - Capacity Collapse (Basic-VAE): At high latent dimension (256), detection fails (AUC ~0.5), indicating identity function learning.
  - Training Instability (Attention-VAE): High variance in AUC across attention hyperparameters (Table 3); some configurations yield poor performance.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Establish the reconstruction-detection trade-off with Basic-VAE across latent dimensions: confirm that as capacity increases, MAE decreases but AUC may drop (overgeneralization).
  2. Assess Skip-VAE impact: hypothesize it achieves lowest MAE but not highest AUC, confirming overgeneralization; compare reconstructions visually (Fig 6).
  3. Assess Attention-VAE impact: hypothesize it achieves highest AUC despite higher MAE; identify optimal head/depth ranges (e.g., 16-24 heads); compare its temporal sensitivity to Basic-VAE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What techniques can improve the training stability of Transformer-based VAEs for P-wave detection under the low-data conditions typical of strong-motion records? The authors note instability in pure attention models and plan to address this in future work.
2. How does the size and diversity of the training dataset influence the detection performance, hyperparameter sensitivity, and generalization of Attention-VAE to unseen seismic environments? Expanding the dataset is identified as a key future direction.
3. What effective methods exist to reduce the computational overhead of the Transformer bottleneck in the Attention-VAE while preserving its high detection capability? The authors plan to explore distillation or quantization.
4. What are the real-time processing latency and robustness of the Attention-VAE under realistic early warning conditions, including variable noise, missing data, and integration with a seismic network? The authors intend to evaluate these factors in operational settings.

## Limitations
- **Dataset scope:** Only 648 Turkish strong-motion records used; limited noise and site diversity threatens generalizability to other regions.
- **Reproducibility:** Key training hyperparameters (optimizer, learning rate, batch size, β coefficient) and data split seeds are not explicitly specified; details of ASW artifact removal and FBM noise synthesis are ambiguous.
- **Mechanism proof:** Proposed causal links (e.g., skip connections → overgeneralization) rely on indirect metrics and lack controlled ablations to isolate effects.
- **External evidence:** The neighboring corpus does not directly investigate the reconstruction-detection trade-off via architectural biases in VAEs for strong-motion P-wave detection, leaving the paper's specific hypotheses unsupported by prior work.

## Confidence
- Architectural constraints (global context over fidelity) aid detection: **Medium** (trade-off observed, but causality not proven via ablation).
- Skip connections harm detection via overgeneralization: **Medium** (strong correlation in results, but potential confounders exist).
- Attention mechanisms improve detection: **High** (consistent high AUC across tests, sensitivity analyses support the mechanism).

## Next Checks
1. Ablate skip connections: Train Skip-VAE variants with stochastic depth or bottlenecked skip pathways; if AUC improves, it directly supports the overgeneralization hypothesis.
2. Cross-region validation: Evaluate the best Attention-VAE on an independent strong-motion dataset (e.g., KiK-net). Successful transfer would increase confidence in generalizability.
3. Attention attribution: Use visualization techniques (e.g., attention rollouts) to inspect learned attention weights; if they highlight global P-wave patterns (e.g., energy ramp-up), it validates the global-context mechanism.
