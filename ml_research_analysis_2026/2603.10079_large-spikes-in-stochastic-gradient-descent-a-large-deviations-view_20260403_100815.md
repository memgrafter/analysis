# Large Spikes in Stochastic Gradient Descent: A Large-Deviations View

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10079
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10079
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: The sign of the drift function G(λ) determines whether large loss spikes in SGD occur almost surely (inflationary) or with polynomially decaying probability (deflationary), and escaping lazy training requires a spike.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates large transient spikes in the loss during stochastic gradient descent (SGD) training of neural networks, termed the "catapult phase". Using large-deviation theory in the neural tangent kernel (NTK) scaling limit for a shallow network, the authors derive an explicit drift function G(λ) that depends on the kernel, learning rate, and data statistics. They show that G(λ₀)>0 leads to almost certain spikes of size ~n/η within O(log(n/η)/G(λ₀)) steps, while G(λ₀)<0 yields spikes with probability ~(n/η)^{-ϑ(λ₀)/2}. Moreover, slow reduction of the kernel without a spike is exponentially unlikely, establishing spikes as the primary mechanism to exit the lazy regime. The analysis extends to ReLU networks under a w-biased initialization, revealing a decoupled two-component dynamics.

## Method Summary
The study models SGD on a shallow network with a single scalar feature, reducing the dynamics to two macroscopic order parameters: the prediction µ(t) and the curvature λ(t). In the NTK limit (width n→∞), these evolve as a discrete-time Markov chain with updates driven by i.i.d. sampled data points. The core approximation is that λ remains nearly constant while |µ| is small, allowing µ to be approximated as a multiplicative random walk: µ(t) ≈ µ₀ ∏_{u=1}^t |1−ηλ(s_{u})²|. This leads to a log-drift G(λ)=∑ pᵢ log|1−ηλsᵢ²|. Large-deviation theory then characterizes the hitting probability of large thresholds for |µ|, distinguishing two regimes based on the sign of G(λ₀). The analysis for ReLU activations leverages a w-biased initialization to decouple positive and negative input regions, applying the same framework per component.

## Key Results
- **Dichotomy**: The sign of G(λ₀) determines the spike behavior: G(λ₀)>0 (inflationary) → almost sure spike; G(λ₀)<0 (deflationary) → spike probability decays polynomially as (|µ₀|²/L)^{ϑ(λ₀)/2}.
- **Escape from lazy training**: Reducing the kernel without a spike is exponentially unlikely; spikes are essentially the only way to achieve significant flattening.
- **ReLU extension**: Under w-biased initialization, the dynamics split into two independent linear-like components, each following the same drift criterion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The SGD noise induces a multiplicative random walk on the prediction µ(t) whose logarithmic drift G(λ) (an explicit data-dependent function) determines whether large loss spikes occur almost surely or only with polynomially decaying probability.
- **Mechanism:** In the NTK scaling limit for a shallow network, the prediction evolves approximately as µ(t) ≈ µ₀ ∏_{u=1}^t |1−ηλ(sᵤ)²| where samples sᵤ are drawn i.i.d. (Eq. 1.15). The log-drift G(λ) = Σ pᵢ log|1−ηλsᵢ²| is the mean of this product. If G(λ₀)>0 (inflationary), the law of large numbers forces |µ(t)| to grow exponentially, guaranteeing a spike to ~√(n/η). If G(λ₀)<0 (deflationary), spikes require large deviations, with probability ~(|µ₀|/M)^{ϑ(λ₀)} where ϑ(λ₀) is the Cramér exponent (Eq. 1.8).
- **Core assumption:** The kernel λ(t) remains approximately constant λ₀ until |µ(t)| reaches ~√(n/η). This holds while |µ(t)| ≪ √(n/η) because λ(t) decreases only by O(η|µ|²/n) per step (Eq. 1.13).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "We identify an explicit criterion separating two behaviours: When ... G is positive, SGD produces large NTK-flattening spikes with high probability; when G<0, their probability decays like (n/η)^{-ϑ/2}."
  - Section 1.2: "As a result, the classical convergence theorems of probability produce a trichotomy ... a). If G(λ₀) > 0, then ... a spike is guaranteed; b). If G(λ)< 0 ... spikes of size M ≲ √(n/η) occur with probabilities ∼ M^{-ϑ} ..."
  - Corpus: Neighbor "Catapults in SGD" (fmr 0.549) also ties spikes to the multiplicative random walk perspective.
- **Break condition:** If λ(t) begins to change significantly before |µ(t)| reaches the threshold, the approximation fails. This is addressed by showing that slow kernel reduction without a spike is exponentially improbable (Proposition 4.2).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Even in the deflationary case (G<0), spikes are not exponentially rare; their probability decays only polynomially in n/η, meaning spikes can retain appreciable probability for realistic network sizes (n~10¹²).
- **Mechanism:** Large deviations theory (Cramér's theorem) gives the exact decay exponent ϑ(λ₀) as the unique positive root of Σ pᵢ |1−ηλsᵢ²|^θ = 1. The tilted measure (change of probability) under which the drift becomes positive yields matching upper and lower bounds (Lemma 4.1).
- **Core assumption:** The scaling regime is such that n/η is large but not so large that polynomial decay becomes negligible; ϑ(λ₀) may be small (e.g., 0.1) leading to probability ~(n/η)^{-0.05} ≈ 0.25 for n=10¹² (Section 1.3.5).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 1.1: "In contrast, the polynomial decay of probabilities discussed in (2) means that such events may retain appreciable probability in practice when the exponent ϑ is not too large."
  - Section 4: The entire section derives the two-sided bound (Theorem 4) and defines ϑ(λ) via Eq. (4.1).
  - Corpus: Neighbor "What is the long-run distribution..." (fmr 0.553) uses large deviations for SGD but focuses on stationary distribution, not spike hitting times.
- **Break condition:** The analysis requires the initial λ₀ to satisfy λ_{MB}^{crit} < λ₀ < λ_{MB}^{max}. If λ₀ ≤ λ_{MB}^{crit}, all factors |1−ηλsᵢ²| ≤ 1, making |µ(t)| monotonically decreasing, precluding spikes.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Escaping the lazy training regime (small λ) without a large spike is exponentially unlikely; spikes are the dominant mechanism for significant kernel reduction (flattening).
- **Mechanism:** While the prediction is sub-threshold (|µ| ≪ √(n/η)), the kernel decreases by at most O(η|µ|²/n) per step. The total decrease over any time T is O(ηT|µ|²/n). To achieve a fixed reduction Δλ, this requires T = O(nΔλ/(η|µ|²)). For small |µ|, this T becomes enormous, and the probability of such a slow accumulation is bounded via exponential moments (Khas’minskii lemma), yielding exponential decay in (n/(ηM²))^{β/2} (Proposition 4.2).
- **Core assumption:** The dynamics are Markov and the per-step kernel decrease is bounded by a₂|µ|²η/n (Eq. 1.14). The analysis relies on moment bounds and optional stopping.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "large spikes are, up to exponentially unlikely events, the only way in which the lazy training regime can be escaped and the curvature reduced."
  - Section 1.2, Proposition 4.2: "Proposition 4.2 shows that the probability of the kernel changing by some threshold ϵ while the prediction remains below M decays like ≲ exp(−ϵγ (n/(ηM²))^{β})."
  - Section 4, Step 2 of Theorem 4 proof: Uses scale decomposition to show error probabilities from slow escape are summable and dominated by polynomial spike probabilities.
- **Break condition:** If the data has very small sᵢ or very large ηλ₀sᵢ² close to 1, the constants a± in the kernel decrease bounds become poorly conditioned, weakening the exponential bound.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Large Deviations Theory (Cramér's theorem, tilting/change of measure)
- **Why needed here:** Provides the asymptotic probability of rare events (large deviations of the random walk log|µ(t)|). The exponent ϑ(λ) is derived via the tilted measure under which the log-drift becomes positive, enabling matching lower bounds.
- **Quick check question:** Given i.i.d. increments Xᵢ with mean μ<0, how does the probability that Σ₁ᵗ Xᵢ > a decay for large a? What role does the Cramér function play?
### Concept: Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) and Lazy Training
- **Why needed here:** The entire analysis is performed in the NTK scaling limit (width n→∞, parameters initialized so that NTK is constant). The prediction µ(t) and kernel λ(t) become macroscopic order parameters. The "lazy training" regime (λ small) assumes features are nearly constant, making the dynamics linear in the parameters.
- **Quick check question:** In the NTK limit, what does it mean for a network to be "lazy"? How does the learning rate η interact with the initial kernel λ₀ to determine the regime (Eq. 1.6)?
### Concept: Martingale Techniques and Optional Stopping
- **Why needed here:** To obtain hitting time bounds, the proof constructs explicit (sub/super)martingales: e.g., |µ(t)|^{ϑ} is a martingale when ϑ = ϑ(λ) (Eq. 4.3), and modified processes for the inflationary case. Optional stopping translates martingale properties into probability estimates for hitting thresholds.
- **Quick check question:** Why does the process Z_t = |µ(t∧τ)|^{ϑ} become a martingale for a specific ϑ? How is optional stopping used to bound P(τ_M < τ_λ)?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Data -> Network -> Macroscopic state -> SGD -> Key derived quantities
Data: finite set {(s_i, p_i)} where s_i are scalar features (e.g., ⟨x, e⟩ for a single channel), p_i sampling probabilities.
Network: Shallow fully connected with width n, activation φ (linear or ReLU). Parameters Θ = {(w_r, a_r)}.
Macroscopic state: prediction µ(t) = n^{-1/2} Σ a_r w_r (linear) or components µ± for ReLU; kernel λ(t) = n^{-1} Σ (w_r² + a_r²) (or λ± with ReLU).
SGD: minibatch size 1, update rule (SGD); drives discrete-time Markov chain (µ(t), λ(t)).
Key derived quantities: G(λ) = Σ p_i log|1−ηλ s_i²|, ϑ(λ) = sup{θ≥0: Σ p_i |1−ηλ s_i²|^θ ≤ 1}.

### Critical path
1. Initialize (µ₀, λ₀) with λ₀ in the range λ_{MB}^{crit} < λ₀ < λ_{MB}^{max} (Eqs. 1.6, 1.10).
2. At each step, sample i(t) ~ p_i, update (µ, λ) via (1.12) for linear or (6.5) for ReLU.
3. Track hitting times τ_{Pred}^M (|µ|≥M) and τ_{Ker}^λ (λ<λ_ref).
4. Analyze via large deviations: compute G(λ₀), ϑ(λ₀). If G>0 → spike guaranteed (Theorem 3/5). If G<0 → spike probability ~(|µ₀|/M)^{ϑ} (Theorem 4/6).
5. For ReLU, ensure w-biased initialization (Definition 6.1) so dynamics decouple into two independent linear-like components until first spike.

### Design tradeoffs
- NTK scaling (n→∞, λ stays O(1)) vs. finite-width effects. This work analyzes the limit exactly but ignores finite-n corrections and feature learning.
- Shallow vs. deep networks: The analysis relies on complete decoupling of neurons; deep networks have more complex NTK structure.
- Batch size b=1 assumed for simplicity; larger b reduces noise and changes the criterion (G becomes Σ p_i log|1−ηλb s_i²|).
- Quadratic loss assumed; other losses would modify the update rules and drift.

### Failure signatures
- If initialization violates w-biased condition for ReLU, neurons may flip sign prematurely, coupling the (µ+, λ+) and (µ−, λ−) dynamics and breaking the decoupled analysis.
- If λ₀ ≤ λ_{MB}^{crit} (i.e., ηλ₀ s_*² ≤ 2 where s_* = max |s_i|), then all factors |1−ηλ₀ s_i²| ≤ 1, causing monotonic decrease of |µ(t)|, no spikes possible.
- If during simulation the kernel λ(t) drifts quickly without a corresponding increase in |µ(t)|, the approximations (1.15) fail; this signals that the "slow escape" exponential bound (Proposition 4.2) might be violated, meaning the dynamics are not following the predicted large-deviation path.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the large-deviation framework and the resulting phase diagram (inflationary/deflationary regimes) be extended from shallow to deep neural networks?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors analyze a shallow network with scalar curvature; they do not address depth or full NTK matrices.
- **Why unresolved:** Deep networks exhibit coupled dynamics across layers and a full NTK matrix, making the scalar reduction invalid; extending requires handling high-dimensional stochastic processes and possibly matrix-valued large deviations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A rigorous large-deviation analysis for deep networks in the NTK scaling, deriving an analogous criterion (e.g., based on NTK eigenvalues) and verifying that spikes persist with polynomial probabilities.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the mini-batch size b>1 affect the occurrence and characteristics of spikes in SGD?
- **Basis in paper:** The analysis restricts to b=1, noted in Assumption 1.1 and the introduction. They state "We consider... mini-batch size b = 1."
- **Why unresolved:** The dynamics with b>1 involve sums of independent gradients per step, altering the distribution of increments and the log-drift G(λ); the martingale and tilting arguments may need modification.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derivation of the effective drift function for general b and a large-deviation theory showing whether the polynomial decay survives, possibly via a central limit scaling or direct computation of the large-deviation rate function.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Under standard random initializations (e.g., LeCun), does the asymmetric initialization condition required for ReLU decoupling (Definition 6.1) hold with high probability for finite widths?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors impose a w-biased initialization (|w_r|≥|a_r|) to ensure no sign flips before spikes. They reference similar assumptions in prior works but do not verify it for common initializations.
- **Why unresolved:** This condition is not automatically satisfied by isotropic Gaussian initializations; its probability depends on the variance scaling and width. Without it, the decoupling may fail, affecting the analysis of ReLU networks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A probabilistic analysis of standard weight initializations showing that the condition holds with probability tending to 1 in the NTK limit, or counterexamples demonstrating failure.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Do the theoretically predicted polynomial hitting probabilities for spikes match empirical measurements in practical deep learning systems?
- **Basis in paper:** They provide example calculations (e.g., Section 1.3.5) showing decay rates, but no empirical validation on real networks beyond synthetic two-point datasets.
- **Why unresolved:** Real-world training involves additional optimizers (e.g., momentum, Adam), varying batch sizes, and finite-width effects; these could alter the observed spike frequencies relative to the NTK-limit predictions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Large-scale experiments measuring spike frequencies across a range of widths, learning rates, and architectures, comparing against the theoretical decay curve (n/η)^(-θ/2) with θ computed from data statistics.

## Limitations
- The analysis assumes the NTK limit (n→∞) with λ=O(1); finite-width effects are not quantified.
- For ReLU, the decoupling requires a strict w-biased initialization; common schemes (e.g., LeCun) may violate it, coupling the two components.
- Data are reduced to scalar projections s_i; real high-dimensional data may not be well captured.
- Focus is on shallow networks; deep architectures introduce kernel interactions that could break the drift picture.
- Batch size 1 and quadratic loss are assumed; other settings may change the criterion G(λ).

## Confidence
- **Spike dichotomy (G(λ₀) sign determines behavior):** High – proven via large-deviation theory under NTK scaling.
- **Spikes as dominant escape from lazy regime:** High – Proposition 4.2 gives exponential bounds on slow kernel reduction.
- **ReLU decoupled dynamics:** Medium – hinges on w-biased initialization and absence of sign flips; rigorous but may not cover all realistic initializations.
- **Implications for practical deep learning:** Low – the model is highly idealized; feature learning and deeper NTK behavior are not addressed.
