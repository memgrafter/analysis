# ABCD: All Biases Come Disguised

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17445  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17445  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** The Matched-and-Dashed (M&D) evaluation protocol reduces mean accuracy variance by 3× with only minimal performance drop.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) often exploit superficial artifacts in multiple-choice question (MCQ) evaluation—such as label position, few-shot prompt distributions, and prediction modes—rather than engaging in genuine reasoning. This leads to overestimated capabilities and poor robustness. The authors introduce Matched-and-Dashed (M&D), a bias‑reduced evaluation protocol that replaces standard letter labels with uniform dashes, requires models to generate full‑text answers, and uses semantic similarity matching to select the correct option. Across 13 LLMs and five diverse benchmarks (MMLU‑Pro, GPQA, CommonsenseQA, ARC, INCLUDE), M&D cuts the mean accuracy variance by a factor of three (variance ratio geometric mean = 0.33) while incurring only a small drop in absolute performance. On the synthetic NonsenseQA diagnostic, most models fall to near chance‑level accuracy (25%) under M&D, compared to >95% with standard evaluation, confirming effective bias suppression.

## Method Summary
The Matched-and-Dashed protocol consists of three core modifications to standard MCQ evaluation. First, all option labels are replaced with identical dashes (e.g., “–”) to eliminate any positional or alphabetical bias. Second, instead of forcing the model to choose a letter, the model is prompted to generate a free‑text answer that ideally corresponds to the correct option. Third, a semantic similarity model (e.g., based on sentence embeddings) computes the similarity between the generated answer and each of the original option texts; the option with the highest similarity is selected as the model’s prediction. This approach prevents the model from relying on shortcut cues and ensures that the evaluation is based on the content of the answer rather than on superficial patterns.

## Key Results
- Mean accuracy variance reduced by 3× (variance ratio geometric mean = 0.33).  
- On NonsenseQA, model accuracy drops to ~25% (chance) under M&D vs. >95% under standard evaluation.  
- Computational overhead increases by only ~3%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
M&D attacks known bias artifacts at their root. Uniform dashes remove label‑position bias, preventing models from associating correctness with a specific letter. Full‑text generation forces the model to produce an answer rather than merely outputting a label, eliminating the prediction‑mode shortcut where models repeat the most frequent label in the prompt. Semantic matching aligns the generated answer with the correct option based on meaning, allowing for paraphrases while still requiring the model to demonstrate understanding. Together, these changes ensure that high performance reflects true reasoning ability and not exploitation of test‑format quirks.

## Foundational Learning
- **MCQ bias:** Models exploit superficial cues (position, distribution) instead of reasoning. *Why needed:* Identifies the core problem the paper addresses. *Quick check:* Models often score >90% on nonsensical questions with standard MC format.
- **Uniform labeling:** Replaces A/B/C/D with identical symbols to erase positional bias. *Why needed:* Prevents models from using label position as a heuristic. *Quick check:* Permuting option letters significantly changes model accuracy in biased settings.
- **Generative evaluation:** Requires free‑text output rather than label selection. *Why needed:* Stops models from scanning options and picking a label without comprehension. *Quick check:* In few‑shot settings, models frequently output the most common label from demonstrations.
- **Semantic textual similarity:** Uses embeddings to match generated answers to options. *Why needed:* Provides a flexible, meaning‑based selection that tolerates paraphrasing. *Quick check:* Sentences with similar embeddings convey the same meaning (e.g., “dog” vs. “canine”).
- **Variance reduction metric:** Geometric mean of variance ratios across tasks. *Why needed:* Quantifies stability of performance against bias artifacts. *Quick check:* Lower variance indicates consistent scores across label permutations.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Question + Options → Label Normalization → Model Generation → Semantic Matching → Selection  

**Critical path:**  
1. Receive question and original options.  
2. Replace option labels with dashes to produce normalized prompt.  
3. Query LLM to generate a full‑text answer.  
4. Compute semantic similarity between the generated answer and each original option text.  
5. Select the option with highest similarity as the final prediction.  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Bias suppression vs. computational cost:* Adding generation and semantic matching increases latency (~3% overhead).  
- *Dependence on semantic model:* Matching quality relies on the embedding model’s ability to capture meaning; failures occur with nuanced paraphrases.  
- *Loss of directness:* Free‑text generation may produce off‑topic or empty responses, requiring robust parsing.  

**Failure signatures:**  
1. All similarity scores are low, indicating poor alignment (possible generation failure).  
2. Generated answer is nonsensical, empty, or diverges from the question.  
3. Significant slowdown or resource exhaustion due to additional steps.  

**Exactly 3 first experiments:**  
1. Run M&D on NonsenseQA to confirm bias suppression.  
2. Compare accuracy variance across standard and M&D evaluations on multiple benchmarks.  
3. Measure absolute performance drop and computational overhead relative to standard MCQ evaluation.

## Limitations
- Synthetic NonsenseQA may not capture all real‑world bias artifacts; ecological validity uncertain.  
- Variance reduction metric (geometric mean) can be sensitive to outlier models/tasks; per‑model breakdown not provided.  
- “Minimal performance drop” lacks precise quantification across fine‑grained skills and tasks.

## Confidence
- M&D reduces accuracy variance: **High**  
- NonsenseQA results near chance: **High**  
- Minimal performance drop: **Medium**  
- Improved cross‑benchmark agreement: **Low**  
- ~3% computational overhead: **Medium**
