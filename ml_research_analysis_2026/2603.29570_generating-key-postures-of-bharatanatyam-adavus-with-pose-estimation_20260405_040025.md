# Generating Key Postures of Bharatanatyam Adavus with Pose Estimation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29570
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29570
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The Conditional Diffusion with Pose Estimation (CDMPE) model achieves FID 19.32 and MS-SSIM 0.78, with pose supervision consistently improving anatomical and stylistic fidelity.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of generating anatomically and culturally accurate key postures for Bharatanatyam dance to support digital preservation. The authors frame the task as conditional image generation, training generative models (cGAN and diffusion) to produce images of specific codified dance poses (adavus). To ensure structural correctness, they integrate a pose estimation module that provides supervisory signals via keypoint alignment and pose consistency losses. Their best model, Conditional Diffusion with Pose Estimation (CDMPE), outperforms GAN-based alternatives, demonstrating that pose-aware supervision is critical for capturing the precise geometric constraints of this classical dance form.

## Method Summary
The authors use a dataset of 1,276 videos depicting 15 adavus (58 variants) from 10 dancers, extracting 128×128 image frames labeled by posture class. They implement four configurations: cGAN, cGAN with pose supervision (CGPE), conditional diffusion, and conditional diffusion with pose supervision (CDMPE). A pre-trained pose estimator (e.g., MediaPipe) extracts 2D keypoints from both real and generated images. Two auxiliary losses are added to the primary generation loss: keypoint loss (L_kp) minimizes absolute coordinate differences, and pose consistency loss (L_pose) enforces relative joint geometry (distances/angles). The total loss combines these with weighting (λ_pose=1). The diffusion model uses a U-Net with attention and time embeddings, while the GAN uses a fully connected generator. Models are trained and evaluated using FID (realism) and MS-SSIM (structural similarity).

## Key Results
- CDMPE achieves the best quantitative results: FID 19.32 and MS-SSIM 0.78, outperforming CGPE (FID 24.28, MS-SSIM 0.73).
- Incorporating pose supervision (L_kp and L_pose) consistently improves synthesis quality across both cGAN and diffusion architectures.
- The approach addresses digital preservation of codified cultural dance forms by generating anatomically precise postures aligned with Bharatanatyam’s stylistic rules.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's success rests on three interconnected mechanisms. First, **conditional generative models** (cGAN and diffusion) can be directed to synthesize specific Bharatanatyam postures by conditioning on discrete class labels, partitioning the data distribution by pose category. This is supported by the problem formulation in section 3.1 and conditioning on posture labels in the abstract. Second, **integrating a pose estimation module** with keypoint and pose consistency losses supervises the generator to respect anatomical and cultural geometric constraints. The losses penalize absolute joint coordinate errors (L_kp) and relative joint geometry errors (L_pose), pulling generated poses toward ground-truth templates. Ablation studies in Tables 1 and 2 confirm that adding these losses improves metrics. Third, the **diffusion-based generator** (CDMPE) outperforms the GAN-based one (CGPE) likely because diffusion’s iterative denoising objective is less prone to mode collapse and better handles the narrow, high-precision data manifold of codified postures. This is evidenced by CDMPE’s superior FID/MS-SSIM in Table 1 and section 4.3.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input (posture class label y, random noise z) → Generator G (cGAN or diffusion U-Net) → Generated image Î → Pose Estimator E (pre-trained MediaPipe) → Extracted keypoints P̂ → Loss Calculator (computes L_gen, L_kp, L_pose) → L_total → Optimizer updates G (and Discriminator D for GAN). E remains frozen.

Critical path: The conditioning signal (y) must be correctly embedded into G; the pose supervision branch (E and losses) must be properly connected and weighted to guide G without overwhelming texture generation.

Design tradeoffs: cGAN is faster to train/sample but less stable; diffusion is more stable but computationally heavy. Loss weighting (λ_pose=1) balances structure vs. texture; misweighting causes overfitting to pose or blurriness. Pose estimator choice: generic MediaPipe may lack dance-specific keypoints (e.g., for mudras); a custom detector would be better but requires new data.

Failure signatures: Poor structural fidelity (correct colors but wrong limbs) indicates pose losses too low or pose estimator failures. Mode collapse (identical outputs per class) suggests GAN issues; switch to diffusion. High FID with decent pose implies texture degradation; increase L_gen weight. Training instability common with diffusion on small data; adjust timesteps or use gradient clipping.

First 3 experiments:
1. Train a standard cGAN (no pose losses) on a simple posture class to verify basic human figure generation; expect poor pose consistency.
2. Ablate pose components: train cGAN with only L_kp, only L_pose, and both on a small subset; visually inspect which errors each corrects.
3. Reproduce CDMPE on full dataset; confirm FID ~19.3 and MS-SSIM ~0.78. If metrics diverge, debug data loading, pose estimator outputs, and λ scheduling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Integrating facial expressions**: How can facial expressions (abhinaya) and nuanced emotional cues be integrated to achieve full cultural fidelity? The current framework focuses solely on body pose, omitting facial dynamics essential for Bharatanatyam storytelling. Resolution requires facial landmark loss, abhinaya-specific datasets, and user studies with cultural experts.
2. **Scalable dataset construction**: What scalable methods exist for building larger, diverse, expertly annotated datasets of Bharatanatyam key postures to overcome current scarcity? The curated dataset (1,276 videos) is limited in dancer diversity and stylistic variations. Resolution involves collaborative annotation with gurus, standardized labeling for mudras and symmetry, and public releases with motion capture validation.
3. **Pose estimator suitability**: Are standard pose estimation models (like MediaPipe) sufficient to capture culturally specific joint configurations and symbolic gestures (mudras), or are dance-specific keypoint detectors needed? The paper uses MediaPipe’s generic model without testing alignment with intricate hand/finger positions. Resolution requires comparative accuracy analysis on Bharatanatyam-specific gestures and possibly custom landmark addition.
4. **Better evaluation metrics**: What metrics best capture structural and cultural fidelity given that standard metrics like FID are biased toward high intra-class diversity? Existing metrics may not measure adherence to Bharatanatyam’s precise geometric rules (symmetry, alignment). Resolution involves developing pose-consistency metrics (joint angle errors, symmetry scores) validated against expert judgments.
5. **Generalizability to other dances**: How well does the framework generalize to other codified dance forms (e.g., Kathak, Ballet) with different alignment rules? Claims of generalizability lack cross-dance validation; losses may need adjustment for style-specific constraints (e.g., ballet turnout). Resolution requires testing on datasets for other dances and analyzing necessary modifications.

## Limitations
- **Pose estimator ambiguity**: The exact pose estimation model and keypoint set are unspecified (“e.g., MediaPipe”); generic models may fail on Bharatanatyam-specific configurations like Araimandi or complex mudras.
- **Missing hyperparameters**: Diffusion noise schedule, timesteps, and the λ_kp value are not detailed, hindering faithful reproduction; the default λ_pose=1 may not be optimal.
- **Metric limitations**: FID may saturate due to low intra-class diversity, and MS-SSIM can be insensitive to cultural pose nuances, potentially masking quality issues.

## Confidence
- Conditional generation yields target postures: **Medium** (plausible but low intra-class diversity may challenge conditioning effectiveness).
- Pose-supervision losses improve fidelity: **High** (ablation studies in Tables 1–2 show consistent quantitative gains).
- Diffusion superiority over GAN: **Medium-High** (CDMPE’s metrics are better, but dataset size may affect generalizability and training stability).

## Next Checks
1. **Metric reproducibility**: Train CDMPE using official or clean-room implementation and confirm reported FID (~19.3) and MS-SSIM (~0.78) within ±5% margin.
2. **Pose estimator robustness audit**: Run the chosen pose estimator (e.g., MediaPipe) on a random sample of real Bharatanatyam frames; report per-joint detection accuracy and visualize failures, especially on mudras.
3. **Ablation on loss weighting**: Systematically vary λ_kp on a small subset; measure impact on pose accuracy (keypoint consistency metric) and image realism (FID) to identify stable weighting.
