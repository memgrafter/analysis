# CausalEvolve: Towards Open-Ended Discovery with Causal Scratchpad

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14575
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14575
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Outperforms ShinkaEvolve across four tasks; on AIME, achieves 38.89% accuracy vs. 34.44%.

## Executive Summary
CausalEvolve addresses the inefficiency and oscillatory behavior of LLM-based evolutionary agents in open-ended scientific discovery. It introduces a causal scratchpad that maintains two types of causal factors: outcome-level factors (numerical descriptors of program outputs) and procedure-level factors (code design elements). The system uses a multi-armed bandit to select outcome-factor-guided interventions for parent selection and estimates average treatment effects for procedure factors to guide mutations. Abductive reasoning on surprising shifts in these estimates generates new factor hypotheses, enabling the agent to break out of plateaus. Evaluated on Hadamard Matrix, Second Autocorrelation Inequality, Circle Packing, and AIME, CausalEvolve consistently improves both average performance and best-found results over the ShinkaEvolve baseline.

## Method Summary
CausalEvolve implements an evolutionary coding agent augmented with a causal scratchpad. Before evolution, an LLM identifies outcome-level factors (task-specific metrics computed from program outputs) and initial procedure-level factors (code motifs). During each generation, a causal planner (multi-armed bandit) selects an outcome-level factor and direction (+1/-1) to sort the population and choose elite parents for mutation. After evaluation, a procedure factor evaluator estimates the average treatment effect (ATE) of each active procedure factor on the objective. A surprise detector monitors ATE histories for significant shifts (e.g., sign inversions). When surprises occur, an abductive hypothesis generator (LLM) proposes new factors to explain the pattern, which are added to the scratchpad. The evolution loop then proceeds with the updated factor set. Key unknowns include exact LLM prompts, hyperparameters (e.g., bandit discount τ, exploration steps K, surprise thresholds), and precise baseline implementation details.

## Key Results
- CausalEvolve exceeds ShinkaEvolve in both mean performance and best-found solutions on all four tasks.
- On the AIME exam, the agent achieves 38.89% accuracy versus the baseline's 34.44%.
- Ablation studies confirm contributions from both outcome-level and procedure-level factors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Outcome-Level Factors for Sample Efficiency
- **Claim:** Outcome-level factors improve sample efficiency by focusing evolution on promising program-space regions.
- **Mechanism:** Precomputed task-specific numerical descriptors (e.g., row orthogonality for Hadamard matrices) guide parent selection. A multi-armed bandit (MAB) dynamically chooses which factor to intervene on, balancing exploration of factor directions and exploitation of the best-performing factor. Parents are sorted by factor×direction; children are generated via mutation. Reward for an action is defined as Ra := (yc − τ·vt)⁺, where yc is the child’s objective, vt is the parent’s objective, and τ∈(0,1) is a discount.
- **Core assumption:** Outcome factors meaningfully correlate with underlying causal drivers of the objective, allowing the MAB to identify beneficial interventions.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions outcome-level factors and CausalPlanner; [section 4.1] details factor construction and MAB reward; corpus evidence for related causal-LLM work is indirect.
- **Break condition:** If factors are orthogonal or negatively correlated with true drivers, the MAB fails to find beneficial interventions, degrading to random-walk evolution.

### Mechanism 2: Procedure-Level Factors for Escaping Local Optima
- **Claim:** Procedure-level factors (code design elements) explain performance differences, enabling discovery of structurally superior solutions and preventing oscillation.
- **Mechanism:** An LLM analyzes past program histories to propose candidate procedure-level factors (e.g., specific algorithmic motifs). CausalEvolve estimates a (biased) average treatment effect (ATE) for each factor to rank general utility. This knowledge guides mutations toward incorporating beneficial code structures.
- **Core assumption:** Performance differences between programs can be attributed to identifiable, intervenable code features, and approximate ATE preserves the ranking of factor usefulness despite confounding.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] references procedure-level factors; [section 4.2] describes the COAT framework and ATE estimation, noting order-preservation suffices; corpus evidence for LLM-based factor extraction exists but not for evolutionary ATE ranking.
- **Break condition:** Severe, unaddressed confounding (e.g., a hidden variable making a beneficial factor appear detrimental) inverts the ATE ordering, leading the agent away from truly beneficial designs.

### Mechanism 3: Abductive Reasoning on Surprises for Novel Directions
- **Claim:** Abductive reasoning on "surprise patterns" (e.g., a positively correlated factor suddenly producing negative outcomes) reveals hidden confounders or new factors, generating novel search directions and breaking plateaus.
- **Mechanism:** The system monitors estimated ATEs over time. Significant shifts (sign inversions or >2σ jumps) are flagged as surprises. An LLM performs abductive reasoning, prompted with the factor’s history and the surprising outcome, to hypothesize the missing causal factor (e.g., a hidden interaction). The new factor is added to the set for future interventions.
- **Core assumption:** Surprise patterns in evolving ATE estimates are systematic indicators of an incomplete causal model; a capable LLM can generate plausible explanatory hypotheses.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] inspects surprise patterns; [section 4.2] details surprise detection and abductive generation; corpus work on LLM abduction is limited.
- **Break condition:** If surprise patterns are predominantly stochastic (especially with small samples), abductive reasoning generates spurious hypotheses, wasting search effort and destabilizing evolution.

## Foundational Learning
- **Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP):** Reformulates scientific discovery as a POMDP where true scientific knowledge (θ_sci) is hidden. The agent acts (proposes programs) to gather observations and update beliefs (memory). This framing justifies using causal knowledge to improve sample efficiency and explains why unguided evolution oscillates: the agent cannot reliably infer long-term value from immediate noisy rewards.
  - Quick check: How does the POMDP formulation explain oscillation near performance boundaries without causal guidance?
- **Average Treatment Effect (ATE) Estimation:** CausalEvolve estimates the effect of procedure-level factors on the objective to rank utility. It uses a simple, biased estimate (e.g., mean difference) relying on order-preservation despite hidden confounders.
  - Quick check: Why might a naive mean difference not reflect the true causal effect, and how does this impact guidance reliability in small-data regimes?
- **Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB):** Used by the causal planner to adaptively select which outcome-level factor to intervene on. Arms correspond to (factor, direction) pairs; reward is the normalized improvement of a child over its parent.
  - Quick check: In this context, what are the "arms," and what does the reward signal represent?
- **Abductive Reasoning (Inference to the Best Explanation):** Generates new factor hypotheses from observed surprise patterns (e.g., a factor’s effect suddenly reverses). The goal is to hypothesize the hidden confounder or interaction that explains the pattern.
  - Quick check: How is abductive reasoning here different from simple inductive generalization from past successful programs?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Factor Identifier (LLM) -> Causal Planner (MAB) -> Evolution Loop (generate-evaluate-select) -> Procedure Factor Evaluator -> Surprise Detector -> Abductive Hypothesis Generator (LLM) -> Scratchpad/Memory (stores factors, ATE histories, hypotheses)

**Critical path:** Initialization: Factor Identifier precomputes outcome factors and proposes initial procedure factors. For each generation: Causal Planner selects (factor, direction) -> parent selection guided by factor -> propose children -> evaluate -> update population. Post-evaluation: Procedure Factor Evaluator updates ATEs -> Surprise Detector checks for shifts -> if surprise, Abductive Generator hypothesizes new factor -> Scratchpad updated -> next generation.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Fixed vs. Dynamic Outcome Factors:* Predefining factors is cheap but may miss emergent task-specific nuances; dynamically generating them adds complexity and instability.
- *ATE Estimation Reliability:* Simple biased estimates (raw mean difference) are sample-efficient but brittle under confounding; sophisticated methods (propensity scoring) are too sample-hungry for evolutionary small-N regimes.
- *Surprise Threshold Sensitivity:* Tight thresholds yield false positives from noise; loose thresholds miss subtle pattern shifts. Heuristic thresholds (sign inversion, >2σ) are used without calibration.

**Failure signatures:**
- *Oscillation without progress:* Best score repeatedly increases then decreases; indicates misaligned guidance (irrelevant outcome factors or inverted procedure ATEs).
- *Premature plateau:* Stagnation while population diversity remains high; suggests exhausted factor set or failed surprise detection.
- *Factor instability:* ATE estimates for a procedure factor flip signs erratically between generations; indicates severe confounding or insufficient samples.
- *Abductive hypothesis spam:* Continuous generation of trivial/nonsensical new factors after surprises; points to poor prompt design or mismatch between surprises and explainable patterns.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Validate Outcome Factor Utility:** Run CausalEvolve with only CausalPlanner (no procedure factors/abduction) on a simple task (e.g., Hadamard Matrix). Confirm early-step performance surpasses ShinkaEvolve.
2. **Test Procedure Factor Contribution:** Run with CausalPlanner + COAT (procedure factors) but disable abduction. Compare best-found results after fixed budget against CausalPlanner-only; expect improvement in final optimality.
3. **Stress-Test Abduction:** On a task with known deceptive patterns (e.g., a factor beneficial only up to a threshold), engineer a "surprise" (e.g., high-ATE factor suddenly fails) and verify abductive module proposes a hypothesis capturing the interaction (e.g., "factor X beneficial only when factor Y < value").

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can evolution-based agents perform guided scientific discovery akin to human scientists?
- **Basis:** The introduction explicitly poses this as the central research challenge.
- **Why unresolved:** CausalEvolve improves guidance but does not fully capture human-like hypothesis generation, experimental design, and theory formation across diverse open-ended domains.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluation on long-horizon, multi-cycle discovery tasks (e.g., autonomous theory formulation) with domain expert assessments, demonstrating adaptability beyond benchmark tasks.

### Open Question 2
How can outcome-level factors be dynamically discovered and updated during evolution to capture emergent properties not anticipated initially?
- **Basis:** CausalEvolve predefines outcome-level factors before evolution (Section 4.1) with no later adjustment.
- **Why unresolved:** Fixed factors may miss important solution-space dimensions that only emerge after initial exploration, limiting responsiveness to novel patterns.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A module that monitors surprises/plateaus to propose new outcome-level factors, integrating them into the scratchpad and showing improved convergence on tasks where optimal solutions have unforeseen characteristics.

### Open Question 3
How can we reliably estimate causal effects of procedure-level factors in the low-data, high-variance regime of evolutionary search?
- **Basis:** The paper acknowledges biased ATE estimates due to hidden confounders and small samples (Section 4.2), relying on order-preservation.
- **Why unresolved:** Biased estimates could misguide bandit selection and abductive reasoning, leading to suboptimal trajectories or incorrect hypotheses.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Robust causal inference techniques (e.g., Bayesian regularization, sensitivity analysis) tailored to evolution, showing that more accurate estimates correlate with better solutions and fewer spurious factor selections.

### Open Question 4
How can the hypotheses generated by abductive reasoning on surprise patterns be validated to ensure they reflect true causal mechanisms rather than spurious correlations?
- **Basis:** CausalEvolve uses LLMs to abductively explain surprises and propose new factors but lacks built-in validation (Section 4.2).
- **Why unresolved:** LLM-generated explanations may be plausible yet incorrect, risking misleading factors that degrade progress or lead the agent astray.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A validation loop where hypotheses are tested via targeted interventions before integration, using multiple LLM calls for consensus or formal verification; measuring validation's impact on solution quality and stability.

## Limitations
- **Unknown 1:** Exact LLM prompts for factor identification, program generation, and abductive reasoning are not provided.
- **Unknown 2:** Critical hyperparameters (e.g., discount τ, exploration steps K, surprise thresholds, ATE estimation method, LLM API parameters) are unspecified.
- **Unknown 3:** Implementation details of the ShinkaEvolve baseline and its meta summary module are insufficient for exact replication.

## Confidence
- **Outcome-level factors improve sample efficiency:** Medium. Mechanism is clear and ablated, but causal assumption (factor-objective correlation) lacks independent validation.
- **Procedure-level ATE estimates guide superior designs:** Low. Relies on brittle order-preservation under confounding; corpus evidence for LLM factor extraction exists but not for reliable ATE ranking in evolution.
- **Abductive reasoning discovers hidden factors:** Low. Evidence is conceptual and internal; no ablation isolates abduction’s contribution or validates hypothesis correctness.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt Sensitivity Analysis:** Systematically vary LLM prompts for factor identification and abductive reasoning; measure performance stability. A robust system should show less variance than the reported improvement over baseline.
2. **ATE Ordering Robustness Test:** Introduce synthetic, known confounders into procedure factor data (e.g., a hidden variable that inverts a truly beneficial factor’s apparent effect). Check if the agent’s factor ranking inverts, confirming reliance on order-preservation.
3. **Surprise Detection Calibration:** Run evolution with randomized labels or extreme noise injections to quantify the false positive rate of the surprise detector. Validate whether detected "surprises" are signal or statistical artifacts given the small-N regime.
