# Balancing Fidelity, Utility, and Privacy in Synthetic Cardiac MRI Generation: A Comparative Study

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.04340
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04340
- **Reference Count**: 24
- **Primary Result**: Under limited-data conditions, DDPM provides the optimal balance of fidelity (lowest FID), utility (high segmentation Dice), and privacy (MIA AUC ~0.60), outperforming LDM and Flow Matching for safe synthetic data augmentation.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates generative models for creating synthetic cardiac MRI (CMR) to overcome data scarcity and privacy barriers in deep learning research. It introduces and evaluates a two-stage pipeline: first generating plausible segmentation masks via diffusion, then synthesizing images conditioned on these masks. Three state-of-the-art models—DDPM, LDM, and Flow Matching—are compared using a comprehensive framework assessing fidelity (distribution and pixel-level similarity), utility (performance on a downstream segmentation task), and privacy (resistance to membership inference and memorization). Findings indicate DDPM achieves the best overall trade-off under limited training data, while Flow Matching offers enhanced privacy at a minor cost to utility. The work establishes a benchmark for quantifying and balancing these critical dimensions in medical synthetic data.

## Method Summary
The authors employ a two-stage generative pipeline. First, a segmentation mask generator (a 2D U-Net DDPM) is trained on combined ACDC and M&Ms dataset masks. Second, three image synthesis models—DDPM, Latent Diffusion Model (LDM), and Flow Matching (FM)—are trained to generate 128x128 CMR images conditioned on the masks. All models are trained on the same limited set of real CMR scans. Evaluation is multi-faceted: fidelity metrics (FID, SSIM, LPIPS) compare generated to real images; utility is measured by training a downstream segmentation network (DynUNet) on synthetic data and testing Dice scores on real held-out datasets; privacy employs nearest-neighbor analysis and membership inference attacks (MIA) to detect memorization.

## Key Results
- **Fidelity**: DDPM achieves the lowest FID (72.52), indicating the best distribution match; LDM is computationally more efficient; Flow Matching shows highest MS-SSIM/LPIPS.
- **Utility**: All models yield competitive cardiac segmentation Dice scores (~0.87-0.90). DDPM and LDM slightly outperform Flow Matching in downstream task performance.
- **Privacy**: All models show MIA AUC near 0.60 (chance=0.5), with Flow Matching and LDM showing slightly better scores and higher nearest-neighbor distances, suggesting marginally stronger privacy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
A two-stage pipeline (mask generation → mask-conditioned image synthesis) enables anatomically plausible synthetic CMR while decoupling structure from texture. The first stage generates a segmentation mask scaffold. The second stage conditions image synthesis on this mask, enforcing spatial correspondence between anatomy and intensity patterns. This separation allows explicit control over cardiac morphology.
*Evidence*: [abstract] mentions the two-stage design; [section III.B] explains mask conditioning enforces anatomical structure. The core assumption is that generated masks are anatomically valid (Table III). If mask quality degrades (e.g., disconnected ventricles), the entire pipeline fails regardless of image model capability.

### Mechanism 2
Diffusion-based models (DDPM, LDM) achieve a better utility-fidelity balance under limited data, while Flow Matching prioritizes privacy via deterministic sampling. DDPM/LDM's stochastic reverse diffusion may better capture data distribution diversity with small datasets. Flow Matching learns a deterministic ODE path, potentially reducing memorization risk but at a cost to distribution coverage when data is scarce.
*Evidence*: [abstract] and [section V.B-C] show DDPM's lowest FID and high Dice, FM's slightly lower utility. [section V.D] shows similar MIA AUC for all (~0.60), with LDM/FM having slightly better privacy metrics. The trade-off is data-dependent; with extremely small datasets, FM's determinism may lead to mode collapse.

### Mechanism 3
Privacy preservation is achieved through model architecture and training dynamics that prevent exact memorization, measured via MIA and nearest-neighbor analysis. The training objective (noise/velocity prediction) combined with limited data prevents memorizing solutions. Privacy metrics detect residual memorization.
*Evidence*: [section V.D, Table VI] shows MIA AUC ~0.6 and L2 distances >10, suggesting no direct copying. [section III.C.4] explains the complementary metrics. A break condition is MIA AUC >0.65 or nearest-neighbor L2 <5.0, indicating unacceptable memorization.

## Architecture Onboarding
1. **Mask Generator**: 2D U-Net DDPM → outputs 4-class segmentation masks.
2. **Image Generator** (choose one): DDPM: 2D U-Net (mask concatenated as channels); LDM: VQ-VAE + conditional U-Net in latent space; FM: U-Net velocity network with ControlNet for mask conditioning.
3. **Evaluation Suite**: Fidelity (FID, SSIM, LPIPS); Utility (DynUNet segmentation Dice, HD95); Privacy (Nearest Neighbor L2/NNDR, MIA AUC).
**Critical Path**: Mask Generator → (per mask) → Image Generator (with conditioning) → Generated image → (batch) → Utility/Privacy evaluation. Never train image generator without mask generator first.
**Design Tradeoffs**: Fidelity vs. Utility (DDPM optimized for FID, may not max Dice); Speed vs. Privacy (LDM fastest, may leak more; FM slowest, best privacy); Mask fidelity impact (poor masks corrupt entire pipeline).
**Failure Signatures**: Mask stage: generated masks failing KS test for area/eccentricity (Table III). Image stage: FID >120 or SSIM <0.15 (unrealistic); Dice <0.80 on real test (useless augmentation). Privacy stage: MIA AUC >0.65 or NNDR <0.80 (memorization).
**First 3 Experiments**:
1. **Mask plausibility check**: Generate 1000 masks, compute area/eccentricity distributions, run KS test vs. real. Must have non-significant p-values (>0.05) for area/eccentricity; roundness/solidity deviations acceptable.
2. **Fidelity benchmark**: Generate 500 synthetic images per model, compute FID/SSIM against real test. Target: FID <100, MS-SSIM >0.35. DDPM should lead on FID; FM on MS-SSIM/LPIPS.
3. **Utility smoke test**: Train DynUNet on 10% synthetic + 90% real, evaluate on real test. Dice improvement must be >1% over real-only baseline; prioritize models with smaller HD95 reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How do fidelity-utility-privacy trade-offs among DDPM, LDM, and Flow Matching change with larger CMR training sets? *Basis*: Study limited to "limited-data conditions" (Abstract). *Why unresolved*: Scaling behavior unknown; DDPM's optimal balance may not hold with more data.
- **Open Question 2**: Does the two-stage mask→image pipeline limit utility compared to an end-to-end generative approach? *Basis*: Table V shows synthetic masks lower utility; end-to-end not evaluated. *Why unresolved*: Unclear if two-stage structure is suboptimal or if better mask generation could close the gap.
- **Open Question 3**: Are the achieved MIA scores (~0.58–0.60) sufficient for regulatory privacy compliance in synthetic CMR? *Basis*: Paper notes 0.5 is ideal but calls 0.60 "safe" without quantitative threshold. *Why unresolved*: No established threshold; risk level at ~0.60 ambiguous.
- **Open Question 4**: Does synthetic CMR utility for segmentation transfer to other clinical tasks (e.g., functional quantification, disease classification)? *Basis*: Utility evaluation restricted to segmentation only (Section IV.C.3). *Why unresolved*: Different tasks rely on distinct features; segmentation performance may not predict other task utility.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps**: Exact formulas for shape analysis (roundness/solidity), MIA attack protocol (shadow model architecture/training), Flow Matching ODE solver details, and DynUNet hyperparameters/splits are undisclosed.
- **Mask quality concerns**: Deviations in roundness/solidity (p < 1e-40) hint at potential long-tail anatomical failures not quantified; mask generator is a critical bottleneck.
- **Privacy evidence lacks rigor**: No statistical distribution over random seeds; standardized attack setup not used; threshold for "safe" MIA AUC is not established.

## Confidence
- **Two-stage pipeline anatomical plausibility**: *Medium* – Depends on mask generator quality; basic shape metrics pass but complex morphology not guaranteed.
- **DDPM provides best fidelity/utility balance**: *High* – Consistently lowest FID (72.52) and competitive Dice (0.87–0.90) across datasets.
- **Flow Matching offers strongest privacy**: *Medium* – Similar MIA AUC to DDPM but higher neighbor distances; privacy gains are marginal.
- **Framework successfully quantifies trade-offs**: *Medium-High* – Comparative results are clear, but limited to three models and specific hyperparameters; generalizability uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. **Mask quality stress test**: Generate 10,000 synthetic masks and compute failure rate of anatomical validity (e.g., disconnected ventricles, non-clinical shapes). Must be <0.1% for pipeline reliability.
2. **Ablative privacy audit**: Implement MIA with a standardized shadow-model protocol (same architecture, independent data split) and report AUC distribution over 5 seeds; all models should remain within 0.58–0.62.
3. **Cross-dataset utility check**: Train DynUNet on synthetic data from ACDC and test on M&Ms (and vice versa). Dice drop relative to in-dataset training must be ≤5% to demonstrate portable synthetic data.
