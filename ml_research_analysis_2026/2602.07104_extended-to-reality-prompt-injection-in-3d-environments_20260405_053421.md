# Extended to Reality: Prompt Injection in 3D Environments
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07104
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07104
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Introduces PI3D, a physical-world prompt injection attack in 3D environments using an experience-guided planner to place adversarial text, achieving up to 87.5% attack success rate and rendering current defenses ineffective.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents PI3D, the first systematic attack that places physical objects bearing adversarial text in 3D environments to hijack Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The core innovation is an experience-guided planner that optimizes the 6-DoF (position and orientation) pose of the text-bearing object to maximize the attack's success while maintaining physical plausibility. The planner reuses past evaluations to reduce the number of costly victim MLLM queries. Evaluations in virtual and real-world settings demonstrate high attack success rates and physical plausibility, and show that two common prompt-injection defenses fail against this physical threat vector.

## Method Summary
PI3D formulates the attack as a constrained optimization problem: maximize an objective `J = Y - λV`, where `Y` is binary attack success (victim MLLM follows injected instructions) and `V` is a physical plausibility penalty derived from a critic MLLM. An experience-guided planner proposes candidate 6-DoF poses `Θ` and injected text `ϕ`. For each candidate, it computes a similarity mass `W(Θ)` against a memory `D` of past evaluations using a kernel over pose space. If `W(Θ)` is below a threshold `τ`, the system renders an image, queries the critic for `V` and the victim for `Y`, and stores the result. If `W(Θ)` is high, it estimates `Y` and `V` via similarity-weighted aggregation from memory, avoiding expensive queries. The candidate with the highest `J` is selected for deployment.

## Key Results
- PI3D achieves high Attack Success Rates (ASR) across different MLLMs and environments in simulation (e.g., up to 87.5% on GPT-4o-mini in Office scenes).
- It produces placements with high physical plausibility, outperforming baselines (Single-Placement, Iterative-Plausibility) in human evaluations.
- The experience-guided mechanism reduces MLLM query calls by ~3% compared to a full-evaluation variant while maintaining competitive ASR.
- Two existing defenses—instructional prevention and known-answer detection—prove insufficient, with ASR remaining high (e.g., >50% under instructional prevention).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The experience-guided planner efficiently identifies effective object poses by reusing past evaluations via a pose-space similarity kernel, reducing computational cost.
- **Mechanism:** A kernel computes similarity between a novel pose and stored poses using Euclidean distance (position) and angular difference (rotation). For novel poses (low total similarity), full MLLM evaluation occurs. For familiar poses, attack success `ˆY` and plausibility penalty `ˆV` are estimated via similarity-weighted aggregation of stored values.
- **Core assumption:** Physically similar poses yield similar MLLM responses and plausibility scores.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "uses an experience-guided planner to optimize the 6-DoF pose... reuses past evaluations to reduce computational cost."
  - [section 3.2] "We formulate a similarity kernel over pose space... estimate the attack success and physical plausibility penalty by similarity-weighted aggregation."
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers on adaptive attacks provide context but weak direct linkage.
- **Break condition:** If pose similarity does not correlate with MLLM response similarity (e.g., due to viewpoint-dependent text legibility), estimates become unreliable.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A critic MLLM enforces physical plausibility constraints (gravity, lighting, collisions), increasing stealth and real-world transferability.
- **Mechanism:** The critic evaluates rendered images of candidate placements, outputting a score `S_phys(Θ) ∈ [0,100]` which is converted to a penalty `V(Θ) = 1 - S_phys/100`. This penalty is minimized alongside attack success.
- **Core assumption:** MLLMs can reliably assess physical realism in 3D scenes, aligning with human judgment.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1.2] "We adopt an MLLM-based evaluator to obtain the physical plausibility... analyzing key physical constraints, including gravity compliance, rotation realism, lighting consistency."
  - [section 4.4] "Human-rated scores... participants rated experience-guided planning as having the highest physical plausibility."
  - [corpus] Weak; corpus mentions physical-world attacks but not MLLM-based plausibility evaluation.
- **Break condition:** Systematic critic biases (e.g., over-penalizing certain orientations) could lead to implausible-but-effective placements that fail in deployment.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Context-ignoring injected text (e.g., "Ignore previous instructions") exploits MLLMs' instruction-following vulnerabilities, overriding their intended task.
- **Mechanism:** The attacker crafts text `ϕ` that explicitly commands the MLLM to disregard its original prompt and produce a specific output. The MLLM processes the camera-captured image containing the text and executes the injected instruction.
- **Core assumption:** MLLMs prioritize literal text instructions in visual inputs over higher-level prompts or context.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "induce the MLLM to perform the injected task... override MLLMs’ intended task."
  - [section 3.1.1] "injected text encoding an injected prompt... induce the model to follow the injected instructions."
  - [section 4.1] "We adopt injected text ϕ for the context-ignoring attack... this attack achieves a high success rate."
- **Break condition:** If MLLMs improve at distinguishing injected from legitimate scene content (e.g., via better alignment), efficacy drops.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal LLM (MLLM) Vulnerabilities**
  - *Why needed here:* PI3D exploits that MLLMs process visual-textual inputs in a unified stream, making them susceptible to injected instructions appearing as scene content.
  - *Quick check question:* How might an MLLM’s architecture (e.g., visual encoder + LLM) contribute to prompt injection susceptibility compared to text-only LLMs?

- **Concept: 6-DoF Pose Optimization**
  - *Why needed here:* Attack effectiveness depends on precise object placement (position + orientation) to ensure text legibility across camera views while maintaining realism.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is optimizing full 6-DoF (not just 2D screen position) critical for physical-world attacks in 3D environments?

- **Concept: Experience Replay in Black-Box Optimization**
  - *Why needed here:* PI3D reduces costly MLLM queries by reusing past evaluations; understanding the trade-off between exploration and exploitation is key.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the risks of over-relying on experience estimates when the environment or camera viewpoint changes significantly?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Planner MLLM -> Candidate Poses (Θ, ϕ) -> Experience Memory (D) <- Similarity Kernel (W(Θ))
  Candidate -> (If W<τ) Render Image -> Critic MLLM -> V(Θ) & Victim MLLM -> Y(Θ,ϕ) -> Store in D
  Candidate -> (If W≥τ) Estimate ˆY, ˆV from D -> Selection Module (max J) -> Optimal Pose Θ⋆

- **Critical path:**
  1. Input: scene image, camera trajectory, target output `y`.
  2. Planner proposes `N` candidate poses in camera coordinates (transformed to global).
  3. For each candidate `Θ`: compute total similarity mass `W(Θ)` from memory.
     - If `W(Θ) < τ`: render image, query critic for `S_phys → V`, query victim for `y_out → Y`, store `(Θ, V, Y)` in `D`.
     - Else: estimate `ˆY`, `ˆV` via similarity-weighted aggregation from `D`.
  4. Select `Θ⋆` maximizing `J = Y - λ·V` (or estimate).
  5. Deploy physical object with text `ϕ` at `Θ⋆`.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - `λ` (plausibility weight): higher values favor realism but may reduce ASR; paper uses `λ=0.3`.
  - `τ` (novelty threshold): lower increases exploration (more queries) but may find better poses; higher risks missing effective novel placements.
  - `N` (candidates per trial): too small under-explores; too large introduces noise; paper finds `N=4` optimal.
  - Pose tolerances `σ_p`, `σ_θ`: wider tolerances increase reuse but risk aggregating dissimilar placements (`σ_p=0.3m`, `σθ=30°`).

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low ASR in cluttered scenes (e.g., Home) due to limited valid placement surfaces.
  - High false negatives in experience estimation: `W(Θ)` just below `τ` triggers full evaluation, increasing cost.
  - Defense bypass failure: if target MLLM uses robust input parsing (e.g., separates instructions from captions), attack success drops (~50% ASR under instructional prevention).
  - Real-world degradation: outdoor variable lighting reduces text legibility, lowering ASR (50–58%).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline comparison:** Run PI3D vs. Single-Placement and Iterative-Plausibility in virtual environments (Office, Home, Outdoor) using robot/human routes. Measure ASR and plausibility (Table 1).
  2. **Efficiency test:** Compare token usage and MLLM calls of PI3D with its variant without experience guidance (Table 2, Appendix B).
  3. **Defense evaluation:** Apply instructional prevention and known-answer detection to PI3D attacks, compute ASR (Table 5, Section 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we design effective defenses specifically against PI3D-style physical prompt injection attacks in 3D environments?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors explicitly evaluate two existing defenses and conclude they are "insufficient to defend against PI3D" (Abstract, Section 5, Section 7). This directly identifies a gap in viable defensive strategies.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates the failure of current text-domain and image-domain defenses but does not propose, implement, or evaluate any novel defense mechanisms tailored to the 3D physical realism constraints of PI3D.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A rigorous evaluation of proposed defense mechanisms (e.g., 3D-aware input validation, multi-view consistency checks, physical plausibility filters) against PI3D in both virtual and real-world settings, measuring reduction in ASR while maintaining normal functionality.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent does the experience-guided planner's similarity kernel (using only 6-DoF pose) capture the true decision boundary for "similar" placements, and could incorporating additional environmental features improve estimation accuracy and attack efficiency?
- **Basis in paper:** The planner's kernel is defined solely on Euclidean position distance and angular orientation difference (Section 3.2). The paper acknowledges that physical plausibility depends on factors like "gravity compliance, rotation realism, lighting consistency" (Section 3.1.2), but the kernel does not model these. The conservative bias in estimates (Table 8) suggests the kernel may be overly simplistic.
- **Why unresolved:** The kernel's design prioritizes computational simplicity but may misjudge similarity. For example, two poses with similar coordinates might be on different surfaces (wall vs. floor) with drastically different plausibility and attack success. The impact of this simplification on planner performance is not ablated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study expanding the similarity metric to include features like surface type, local illumination variance, or semantic segmentation maps. Comparing estimation error and final attack objective against the baseline kernel would quantify the benefit.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the effectiveness and generalizability of PI3D scale with environmental complexity and diversity (e.g., cluttered vs. sparse scenes, dynamic elements, varied architectural styles), and are there fundamental limits to attack success in highly constrained or atypical 3D layouts?
- **Basis in paper:** The evaluation uses three scene types and notes that "plausibility is higher in open spaces (e.g., Outdoor) and lower in cluttered settings (e.g., Home)" (Section 4.4). However, the scenes are still relatively simple and static. The threat model assumes static environments.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not systematically vary environmental complexity (object density, occlusions, non-Manhattan geometries). It is unclear if PI3D's success generalizes to, say, a busy factory floor or a dense forest. There may be scene configurations where no physically plausible placement yields high ASR.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A larger-scale evaluation across a diverse dataset of 3D scenes (e.g., Matterport3D, ScanNet) with quantified complexity metrics. Correlating PI3D's ASR and required plausibility penalty with these metrics would reveal scaling laws and failure modes.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are the underlying model-specific factors (architecture, training data, instruction tuning) that cause significant variance in PI3D's attack success rate across different MLLMs, and can we predict or transfer attack success between models?
- **Basis in paper:** Table 1 shows large ASR variations between evaluator MLLMs for the same scene. The paper notes differences but does not analyze their root causes.
- **Why unresolved:** The work treats MLLMs as black-box evaluators. It is unknown whether vulnerability stems from visual grounding capabilities, instruction-following robustness, or internal representation of "scene context." The transferability of a successful placement from one model to another is not studied.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A failure-mode analysis: (1) Controlled experiments with frozen model components to isolate the vulnerable component. (2) Measuring similarity in internal representations (attention, hidden states) for successful vs. failed attacks across models. (3) Testing if placements optimized for one model transfer to others, measuring transfer success rate.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How does the long-term operation of the experience-guided planner affect its performance, particularly regarding memory saturation, catastrophic forgetting, and the trade-off between memory size and computational savings?
- **Basis in paper:** The planner uses a growing memory `D` with a fixed novelty threshold `τ`. The paper reports efficiency gains over a "without experience-guided" variant but only for a limited number of trials. There is no discussion of memory management policies, memory aging, or performance degradation as memory accumulates.
- **Why unresolved:** In real-world deployment (e.g., a robot operating for months), memory would grow indefinitely. The similarity kernel may become less discriminative with more data, or old experiences might become irrelevant due to environmental changes (e.g., seasonal lighting, moved furniture). The fixed `τ` may need dynamic adjustment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A simulation study where the planner runs over thousands of sequential placement queries in a dynamic environment (with occasional scene changes). Metrics: (1) Attack success and plausibility over time, (2) Average MLLM calls per query as memory grows, (3) Impact of memory pruning strategies on maintaining performance and efficiency.

## Limitations
- The similarity kernel's assumption that physically similar poses yield similar MLLM outputs is unverified; pose-output correlation is not measured.
- The plausibility critic MLLM may have systematic biases not accounted for; its reliability is only partially validated by human study.
- Reproduction is hindered by proprietary 3D assets, closed camera trajectories, and reliance on commercial MLLM APIs with cost/rate limits.
- Real-world ASR drops to ~50% reveal environmental sensitivity to lighting and occlusions not fully captured in simulation.
- Evidence linking findings to broader corpus is weak; the work stands relatively isolated in the physical prompt injection space.

## Confidence
- **Experience-guided planning efficiency:** Medium (efficiency shown, but kernel assumption lacks broader proof).
- **MLLM plausibility critic validity:** Medium (human evaluations partially support, but systematic errors possible).
- **Injection override success in 3D:** High (strong empirical results virtual/real).
- **Defense insufficiency claim:** High (direct experimental evaluation of two defenses).
- **Real-world transfer gap:** High (sim-to-real drop documented, ~50% ASR).

## Next Checks
1. **Test pose–output correlation:** Sample a dense grid of 6-DoF poses in test scenes, query the victim MLLM for each rendered view, and measure output similarity (e.g., via embedding cosine) against pose distances to validate the kernel's core assumption.
2. **Benchmark critic MLLM:** Collect a diverse dataset of placements (both plausible and implausible), obtain human plausibility ratings, and compute correlation/error with the critic's scores to identify systematic biases.
3. **Real-world transfer trial:** Implement the top-performing poses from simulation in a controlled lab with identical camera paths. Compare ASR and plausibility to simulation results to isolate rendering/lighting gaps and quantify the sim-to-real transfer gap.
