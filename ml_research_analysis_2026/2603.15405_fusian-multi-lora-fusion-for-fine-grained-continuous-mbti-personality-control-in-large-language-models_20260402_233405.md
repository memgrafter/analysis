# Fusian: Multi-LoRA Fusion for Fine-Grained Continuous MBTI Personality Control in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15405
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15405
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 6.79 and a Pearson correlation of 0.88 for continuous control of MBTI trait intensities in Qwen3-14B.

## Executive Summary
Fusian proposes a novel framework to achieve fine-grained, continuous control over personality traits in large language models. While existing methods are limited to discrete category switches, Fusian captures the fine-tuning trajectory of a specific MBTI dimension by frequently saving LoRA checkpoints during supervised fine-tuning (SFT). These checkpoints are filtered to a stable basis set. A reinforcement learning policy then learns to fuse these adapters dynamically to match a target trait intensity percentage. Evaluated on Qwen3-14B across all eight MBTI dimensions, Fusian significantly outperforms prompting, individual LoRA selection, and model merging baselines, demonstrating precise continuous control. Ablations confirm the necessity of the RL-based fusion, stable basis selection, and the aggressive reward design.

## Method Summary
Fusian operates in two stages. Stage 1 (Trajectory Collection): Perform trait-specific SFT on Qwen3-14B using LoRA (r=8, α=32, dropout=0.05 on q_proj/v_proj). After *every* optimizer step, save the LoRA adapter and immediately evaluate the frozen model on the validation MBTI questionnaire for the target trait. Compute the Trait Percentage (Pt) from the responses and record the pair (adapter, Pt). Continue until the final adapter reaches near-complete trait expression. Stage 1 yields a trajectory library T of adapters spanning a range of Pt values.

Stage 2 (RL Fusion): Apply stable basis selection to T. Sort adapters by Pt; for each sliding window of size 3, compute Pt variance. Discard adapters where the window variance exceeds 10.0. From the remaining stable set, uniformly sample N=10 adapters to cover the intensity range, forming basis set B. Then train an MLP policy: input is normalized target intensity (ˆp ∈ [-1,1]), output is α ∈ ℝ^N (Dirichlet parameters via Softplus+0.01). For each sampled target, draw mixing weights w ∼ Dirichlet(α). Fuse the adapters in B: ∆θ_fused = Σ w_i ∆θ_i. Apply the fused LoRA to the base model, generate responses to a fixed batch of diagnostic prompts, compute actual Pt, and calculate reward R = exp(-λ|diff|) + I(|diff|<δ)·B, where diff = Ptarget−Pactual, λ=20.0, δ=0.01, B=0.5. Optimize the policy with REINFORCE, using an exponential moving average baseline (decay=0.95) and an entropy coefficient β_t that decays from 0.1 to 0.01. Training uses AdamW (lr=1e-3), gradient accumulation (4 steps), and gradient clipping (0.5).

## Key Results
- MAE = 6.79 and Pearson r = 0.88 on Qwen3-14B across MBTI dimensions, indicating strong continuous control.
- Outperforms prompting, standard LoRA selection, and model merging baselines by a significant margin.
- Ablations demonstrate that the RL fusion, stable basis selection, and aggressive reward are essential for high precision.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Fusian works because it constructs a continuous manifold over trait intensities from a discrete set of LoRA adapters extracted along the SFT trajectory. Frequent checkpointing provides dense coverage of the intensity space, while variance filtering ensures each adapter represents a stable, meaningful point. The RL policy learns a non-linear interpolation strategy: given a target intensity, it outputs Dirichlet parameters that bias the mixture toward adapters whose Pt bracket the target, enabling precise adjustment. The reward’s exponential penalty amplifies small errors, forcing the policy to fine-tune its mixing weights. The entropy term prevents premature convergence, encouraging exploration of diverse combinations. Together, these elements allow the model to synthesize personalities at arbitrary intensities without additional fine-tuning.

## Foundational Learning
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**  
  *Why needed:* Efficiently injects trainable updates into a frozen model; enables saving many checkpoints without full model storage.  
  *Quick check:* Verify adapter rank (r=8) and alpha (32) yield correct weight update shapes for target model layers.
- **Reinforcement Learning Policy Gradient (REINFORCE)**  
  *Why needed:* Optimizes the non-differentiable composition of adapters via reward from the evaluation metric.  
  *Quick check:* Implement a simple REINFORCE loop on a toy environment to ensure gradient computation and baseline update are correct.
- **Dirichlet Distribution**  
  *Why needed:* Provides a distribution over the mixing weight simplex; supports both sparse and uniform mixtures and integrates naturally with the policy’s softplus activation.  
  *Quick check:* Sample from Dirichlet(α) for various α and confirm weights sum to 1 and concentration matches expectations.
- **Trait Percentage (Pt) Metric**  
  *Why needed:* A differentiable (via RL) proxy for the model’s expressed personality intensity, derived from a psychometric questionnaire.  
  *Quick check:* Compute Pt from sample responses using the described scoring (1/2/4/5 → points) and compare with expected trait percentages.
- **High-Frequency Checkpointing**  
  *Why needed:* Captures the continuous trajectory of trait change during SFT, forming the pool of adapters.  
  *Quick check:* Plot Pt vs. training steps; ensure relatively smooth progression and adequate coverage.
- **Stable Basis Selection (Variance Filter)**  
  *Why needed:* Eliminates noisy or unstable adapters that could degrade fusion quality.  
  *Quick check:* Apply sliding window variance threshold to Pt series; verify retained adapters have variance below threshold.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Base Model (Qwen3-14B) → Trajectory Collection (SFT + LoRA) → Adapter Library T → Stable Basis Filter → Basis Set B → RL Policy (MLP) [takes target p] → Dirichlet(str. α) → Mixing weights w → Fused Adapter (Σ w_i·∆θ_i) → Apply to Base Model → MBTI Test (questionnaire) → Pactual → Reward → Policy gradient update.

**Critical path**  
Target intensity → Policy output (α) → Sample w → Fuse adapters → Model inference → Questionnaire → Compute Pactual → Reward → REINFORCE loss → Policy update.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Checkpoint frequency vs. storage*: High frequency yields dense trajectory but huge I/O and storage; mitigated by filtering down to N=10 adapters.
- *RL policy complexity vs. sample efficiency*: The policy introduces many hyperparameters (lr, entropy decay) but enables non-linear interpolation; an alternative would be linear interpolation which is simpler but less expressive.
- *Reward shaping*: Exponential penalty (λ=20) amplifies small errors, improving precision but risking gradient explosion if |diff| large; clipping and baseline help.
- *Basis size (N=10)*: Larger N gives richer mixing space but increases policy output dimension and computational cost; N=10 balances expressiveness and efficiency.

**Failure signatures**  
- Trajectory adapters non-monotonic or variance filter removes >70% → insufficient basis coverage.
- RL training: reward plateaus near minimum, entropy collapses → policy not learning meaningful mixtures.
- Poor performance on specific trait (e.g., 'N') → likely noisy Pt measurement or unsuitable basis; validate questionnaire parser and adapter Pt spread.

**First experiments**  
1. *Trajectory density pilot*: Run Stage 1 on a 1-2B model; record number of gradient steps, checkpoint size, and Pt progression smoothness. Confirm that the variance filter retains at least 10 adapters.
2. *Parser validation*: Using any MBTI questionnaire, implement the scoring rule and parse 50+ model responses per trait. Report parsing accuracy and inter-item consistency.
3. *RL sanity check*: With a fixed basis of three adapters (low, mid, high Pt), train policy on a single target (e.g., 50%). Verify that Pactual converges to within ±5% and that mixing weights are sensible (e.g., higher weight on brackets).

## Limitations
- Dataset accessibility: The Personality Control Datasets (Chen et al., 2024) are not publicly released, preventing independent verification and reproduction.
- Evaluation parser underspecification: The exact prompts, pole mappings, and parsing logic are not detailed, leading to potential inconsistencies in computing Pt.
- Storage and I/O demands: Saving a LoRA checkpoint after every gradient update on a 14B model may be prohibitively expensive in terms of storage and I/O throughput.

## Confidence
- **RL-based fusion mechanism & ablations**: High
- **Continuous control performance (MAE=6.79, r=0.88)**: Medium
- **Broad applicability beyond MBTI/Qwen3-14B**: Low
