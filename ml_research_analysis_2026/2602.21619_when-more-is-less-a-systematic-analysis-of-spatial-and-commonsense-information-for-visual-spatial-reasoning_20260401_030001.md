# When More Is Less: A Systematic Analysis of Spatial and Commonsense Information for Visual Spatial Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.21619
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21619
- **Reference count**: 16
- **Primary result**: Single task-relevant spatial cues improve accuracy by up to +9.6%, but multi-context aggregation, irrelevant commonsense, or combined interventions degrade performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the effect of injecting external spatial and commonsense information into vision-language models (VLMs) for visual spatial reasoning (VSR). The authors treat three VLMs (Qwen-2-VL-7B, LLaVA-Next-1.6-7B, BLIP-3-5B) as black boxes and systematically vary the injected content: nine spatial contexts derived from perception modules, filtered commonsense from ATOMIC, and chain-of-thought prompting. Evaluations are conducted on VSR and EmbSpatial benchmarks.

The key finding is that more information is not necessarily better. While individual, task-relevant spatial cues yield significant gains (up to 9.6%), combining multiple contexts or adding weakly relevant commonsense often leads to performance drops, sometimes falling below baseline. CoT reasoning helps on EmbSpatial (up to +12.2%) but harms VSR (-2.0%), indicating that its effectiveness depends on the quality of underlying spatial grounding. Combined interventions exacerbate overload, underscoring the importance of information quality and integration capacity.

## Method Summary
The study employs a black-box evaluation framework without fine-tuning. For each input image and caption, the authors extract nine spatial context types using off-the-shelf perception tools: bounding boxes and segmentation (Grounding DINO, SAM 2), depth (Depth Anything v2), and orientation (Orient Anything). Commonsense knowledge is retrieved from the ATOMIC dataset and filtered by semantic similarity to the caption or detected entities, using a tunable threshold. Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting instructs the model to reason step-by-step. The VLM is then queried with a prompt that optionally includes the spatial contexts, commonsense statements, and/or CoT instruction, followed by the original question. Accuracy on the two benchmarks is measured across a grid of conditions: single context types, cumulative context counts, varying similarity thresholds, and combinations of all three interventions. The design enables systematic isolation of each factor’s contribution.

## Key Results
- Single, highly relevant spatial cues improve accuracy by up to 9.6 percentage points over zero-shot baseline.
- Aggregating multiple spatial contexts, injecting low-relevance commonsense, or adding CoT generally harms performance; combined spatial+commonsense+CoT interventions produce the largest accuracy drops.
- CoT yields a gain on EmbSpatial (+12.2%) but a loss on VSR (-2.0%), revealing that stepwise reasoning only helps when spatial grounding is precise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The observed patterns can be explained by the VLM’s limited capacity to integrate heterogeneous information and its susceptibility to distraction. Spatial contexts provide explicit, deterministic constraints (e.g., bounding box coordinates) that the model can directly leverage without ambiguity. In contrast, commonsense statements are natural language and may be only loosely related; when relevance is low, they act as noise and cause cognitive overload. CoT reasoning is effective only if the underlying visual grounding is accurate; on VSR, where spatial frames can be ambiguous, CoT may amplify errors. The consistent degradation with combined interventions suggests that the model’s context window and reasoning bandwidth are exceeded, leading to interference among signals. Thus, information quality and careful alignment with the task matter far more than sheer quantity.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:
Image -> Perception Modules (Grounding DINO, SAM 2, Depth Anything v2, Orient Anything) -> Spatial Contexts
Caption/Entities -> ATOMIC -> Filtered Commonsense (by semantic similarity)
Spatial Contexts + Filtered Commonsense + Question + (optional CoT) -> Assembled Prompt -> VLM (Qwen-2-VL-7B / LLaVA-Next-1.6-7B / BLIP-3-5B) -> Parsed Answer

**Critical path**:
1. Receive image and caption.
2. Run perception modules to extract nine spatial context types.
3. Query ATOMIC for commonsense; filter by similarity to caption/entities.
4. Construct prompt by concatenating injected information (if any), the original question, and CoT instruction (if used).
5. Feed prompt to frozen VLM; generate response.
6. Parse response to extract final answer (e.g., “Yes”, “No”, or option letter).
7. Compare answer to ground truth to compute accuracy.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Black‑box vs. fine‑tuning: Simpler setup but limited control; results reflect model’s existing capabilities rather than optimized integration.
- Fixed similarity threshold for commonsense: Easy to implement but may not balance relevance and coverage optimally; a sweep is required.
- Off‑the‑shelf perception modules: Avoid custom training but may produce noisy outputs that propagate errors.
- Prompt length: Adding many contexts increases token count and can dilute the signal or exceed context limits.
- Model selection: Only three VLMs were tested due to resource constraints, restricting generalizability.

**Failure signatures**:
- Accuracy decreases when adding a second or third spatial context beyond the most effective one.
- Sharp drop in accuracy at low commonsense similarity thresholds (e.g., <0.3), indicating injection of irrelevant statements.
- CoT improves on EmbSpatial but degrades on VSR, suggesting failure when spatial frames are ambiguous.
- Combined interventions (SC+CK+SR) produce the lowest scores, a clear overload indicator.
- Discrepancies between models may reveal architecture-specific sensitivities.

**First experiments** (to validate reproduction):
1. **Baseline replication**: Run zero‑shot evaluations on VSR and EmbSpatial for each model to confirm reported baseline numbers.
2. **Single spatial context gain**: Inject each of the nine spatial contexts individually and measure accuracy; identify which contexts yield the largest improvements.
3. **Commonsense threshold sweep**: Filter ATOMIC at three thresholds (e.g., 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) and evaluate their impact on accuracy; observe the threshold at which gains peak.

## Limitations
- The ordering of cumulative spatial context experiments is unspecified; reproduction must infer an order from single-context effectiveness.
- Details of the ATOMIC retrieval pipeline (embedding model, similarity computation) are missing, potentially affecting results.
- Exact prompt templates for zero‑shot, spatial reasoning, and combined conditions are not provided, introducing ambiguity in replication.
- The study is limited to three VLMs and two benchmarks, limiting broader conclusions about VLM spatial reasoning.

## Confidence
- Spatial context benefits: **Medium**
- Multi‑context/weak‑commonsense harms: **Medium**
- CoT benefit on EmbSpatial vs. harm on VSR: **Medium**
- Combined interventions degrade accuracy: **Medium**
