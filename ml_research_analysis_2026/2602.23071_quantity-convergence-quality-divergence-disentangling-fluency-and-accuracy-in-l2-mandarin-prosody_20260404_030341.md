# Quantity Convergence, Quality Divergence: Disentangling Fluency and Accuracy in L2 Mandarin Prosody

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.23071
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23071
*   **Reference Count:** 0
*   **Primary Result:** High-proficiency L2 Mandarin learners achieve native-like *quantity* of major prosodic boundaries (B3) but show significant *quality* divergence in how those boundaries map onto core syntactic structures, revealing an "illusion of fluency."

## Executive Summary
This study tests whether advanced L2 learners produce nativelike prosody or an "illusion of fluency" where surface fluency masks deeper structural errors. Analyzing speech from 67 native Mandarin speakers and 67 Vietnamese learners (stratified by HSK proficiency) in the BLCU-SAIT corpus, researchers mapped C-ToBI prosodic boundaries (B1-B3) onto dependency syntax. While high-proficiency learners (VNH) converged with natives on the total number of major phrase (B3) boundaries, their assignment of these boundaries to specific syntactic positions diverged sharply. VNH speakers under-segmented the native-canonical Subject-Verb (SBV) interface at the Major Phrase level while over-segmenting the Verb-Object (VOB) interface, concurrently promoting weak boundaries at SBV. This indicates a compensatory "prosodic demotion" strategy that maintains long, fluent speech streams at the cost of fossilized, inverted prosodic-syntactic alignment.

## Method Summary
The research employed a corpus-based comparative analysis. Speech from 134 speakers (67 natives, 67 Vietnamese learners) was drawn from the BLCU-SAIT corpus, containing 13,802 utterances from 103 sentences. Prosodic boundaries were annotated using the Mandarin adaptation of ToBI (C-ToBI), identifying three levels: B1 (Prosodic Word), B2 (Intermediate Phrase), B3 (Major Phrase). Syntactic structure was parsed using the CIR-CTB dependency grammar guidelines via the HIT-LTP platform, followed by manual correction. The primary analysis involved two steps: 1) Fitting negative binomial regression models to boundary count data to assess effects of speaker group (native, VNH, VNL) and boundary type (B1, B2, B3). 2) Conducting chi-squared tests of independence on contingency tables cross-tabulating syntactic relation types (e.g., SBV, VOB) with prosodic boundary levels, followed by calculation of standardized residuals to pinpoint mapping divergence.

## Key Results
*   **Quantity Convergence:** High-proficiency learners (VNH) produced a nativelike total quantity of Major Phrase (B3) boundaries (p=1.00).
*   **SBV Divergence:** VNH speakers significantly under-segmented the Subject-Verb (SBV) syntactic relation at the Major Phrase (B3) level (StdRes = -3.28) and simultaneously over-promoted weak Prosodic Word (B1) boundaries at SBV (StdRes = +6.02).
*   **VOB Divergence:** VNH speakers significantly over-segmented the Verb-Object (VOB) relation at the Major Phrase (B3) level (StdRes = +1.99).
*   **Fossilized Pattern:** This results in an inverted, fossilized prosodic hierarchy [(Subject-Verb)-(Object)] replacing the native [(Subject)-(Verb-Object)] template.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The "prosodic demotion" strategy allows high-fluency learners to maintain the *quantity* of major phrasing (thus sounding fluent) while fundamentally altering its *quality*—its syntactic alignment. By demoting essential structural breaks at the SBV interface (reducing B3, increasing B1), learners create longer, uninterrupted Subject-Verb chunks. This compensatory mechanism prioritizes the rhythmic continuity of the core predicate-argument frame (VO) at the expense of marking the subject's nominal boundary. The fossilization occurs because this inverted mapping successfully supports intelligible, fluent output, removing the performance pressure to correct it despite its non-nativelike syntactic representation.

## Foundational Learning
*   **C-ToBI (Mandarin ToBI):** A prosodic annotation system labeling boundary tones and break indices. **Why needed:** Provides the discrete, comparable units (B1-B3) for measuring phrasal breaks. **Quick check:** Can annotators reliably distinguish a B1 (word boundary) from a B3 (major phrase break) using acoustic cues like pitch reset and pre-boundary lengthening?
*   **Dependency Grammar:** A syntactic formalism representing relations between words (e.g., SBV for subject-predicate, VOB for verb-object). **Why needed:** Offers a fine-grained, head-dependent structure to map prosodic breaks onto specific syntactic positions. **Quick check:** Does the parser correctly identify SBV in "他 跑步" (he run) and VOB in "吃 苹果" (eat apple)?
*   **Negative Binomial Regression:** A generalized linear model for overdispersed count data. **Why needed:** Boundary counts are non-normal integers with variance exceeding the mean; Poisson regression would be insufficient. **Quick check:** Is the residual deviance/df ratio substantially >1, confirming overdispersion?
*   **Chi-Squared Test of Independence & Standardized Residuals:** Tests association between two categorical variables (syntax relation x boundary level); residuals identify which cells drive the association. **Why needed:** Moves beyond "is there a difference?" to "which specific syntax-prosody alignments are unusual?" **Quick check:** Are absolute standardized residuals >|1.96| considered statistically significant indicators of divergence?
*   **Interlanguage Fossilization:** The stabilization of a non-target-like linguistic system in advanced L2 speakers. **Why needed:** Frames the core phenomenon—why a non-native pattern persists despite high fluency and exposure. **Quick check:** Does the error pattern persist or intensify at the highest proficiency level, rather than diminishing?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    BLCU-SAIT Corpus -> C-ToBI & Dependency Annotation -> Contingency Tables (Syntax x Boundary) -> Statistical Modeling (Regression, Chi-Sq) -> Divergence Metrics (StdResiduals)
*   **Critical Path:** Access Corpus -> Achieve High Annotation Agreement (κ≥0.8) -> Correctly Specify Overdispersed Count Model -> Compute & Interpret StdResiduals.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** The study trades broader syntactic coverage (only SBV/VOB analyzed) for depth on core SVO structure. It relies on automated parsing (HIT-LTP) for scalability but requires manual correction for accuracy, creating a labor-intensity tradeoff.
*   **Failure Signatures:** 1) **Annotation Drift:** Low inter-annotator agreement (κ<0.8) on B1/B3 labels or SBV/VOB relations. 2) **Model Misspecification:** Using Poisson regression despite overdispersion (deviance/df >>1). 3) **Mapping Misalignment:** Parser errors causing incorrect SBV/VOB tags, especially in the learner group, contaminating the contingency tables.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** To what extent does L1 Vietnamese prosodic phonology directly cause or reinforce the observed "prosodic demotion" strategy and inverted (Subject-Verb)-(Object) template in L2 Mandarin?
    *   **Basis:** The authors state this pattern is "potentially reinforced by L1 Vietnamese prosodic structures which warrants further cross-linguistic investigation."
    *   **Why Unresolved:** The study only examined Vietnamese learners. Without comparative data from learners with different L1 prosodic systems, L1 transfer cannot be isolated from universal L2 strategies.
    *   **Resolving Evidence:** A cross-linguistic replication comparing Vietnamese learners with learners from stress-timed (e.g., English) or mora-timed (e.g., Japanese) L1s on identical tasks. A correlation between specific L1 phrasing patterns and the L2 error pattern would support transfer.
*   **Open Question 2:** Is the "prosodic demotion" strategy a universal compensatory mechanism for high-proficiency fluency, or is it specific to the L1 Vietnamese-L2 Mandarin pairing?
    *   **Basis:** The conclusion about fossilization is based solely on the Vietnamese learner data.
    *   **Why Unresolved:** With data from only one L1, it's unclear if this is a unique fossilized interlanguage grammar or a common endpoint for many L2 learners. It could be a performance optimization.
    *   **Resolving Evidence:** Comparative analysis of the same boundary quantity/quality dissociation in other large learner corpora (e.g., Korean, Japanese learners of Mandarin) using identical C-ToBI and dependency mapping. Replication suggests universality; divergence suggests L1-specificity.
*   **Open Question 3:** What is the temporal acquisition trajectory of this fossilized pattern? Does the inverted mapping appear early and become entrenched, or emerge as a later compensatory strategy?
    *   **Basis:** The authors note the stronger deviation in VNH than VNL "points toward fossilization rather than gradual acquisition," but lack longitudinal data.
    *   **Why Unresolved:** The cross-sectional design cannot determine the developmental path. The stronger VNH deviation could mean the error crystallized late or that VNH suppressed other errors while cementing this one.
    *   **Resolving Evidence:** A longitudinal study tracking a cohort of Vietnamese learners from beginner to advanced, with regular speech sampling and identical annotation. This would reveal the emergence and stabilization stage of the demotion strategy.
*   **Open Question 4:** Does the "illusion of fluency" phenomenon generalize beyond simple SVO constructions to other Mandarin syntactic structures (e.g., serial verbs, adjuncts, ditransitives)?
    *   **Basis:** The analysis focused exclusively on simple SVO sentences (SBV and VOB relations).
    *   **Why Unresolved:** Mandarin prosodic phrasing is influenced by constituent length and complexity. The demotion strategy might be specific to the canonical, short SVO frame.
    *   **Resolving Evidence:** Extending the C-ToBI/dependency mapping analysis to a wider variety of syntactic structures within the corpus (e.g., sentences with ATT, ADV, multiple clauses). Persistence of the SBV demotion/VOB promotion pattern across structures indicates a deep interlanguage rule; isolation to simple SVO suggests a limited coping mechanism.

## Limitations
*   **Corpus Accessibility:** The access procedure and licensing terms for the BLCU-SAIT corpus are unspecified, blocking direct replication.
*   **Annotation Specificity:** Subtle implementation details for C-ToBI boundary thresholds and CIR-CTB dependency edge cases are not fully documented, risking annotation drift that could alter mapping results.
*   **L1 Generalizability:** Findings are restricted to Vietnamese learners; conclusions about universal L2 prosodic strategies or fossilization cannot be drawn without comparative L1 data.

## Confidence
*   **Quantity Convergence (B3 boundary density):** **High.** Relies on robust count data from a large sample (N=134) and clear statistical significance (p=1.00), with low sensitivity to minor annotation variations.
*   **Syntactic Mapping Divergence (SBV/VOB residuals):** **Medium.** Depends on correct dependency annotation and chi-squared residual calculation; vulnerable to parser discrepancies and guideline interpretation.
*   **"Prosodic Demotion" Strategy Interpretation:** **Low.** This is a theoretical synthesis of the quantitative patterns, not a directly measured variable. It assumes a causal link between boundary promotion/demotion and fluency, hinging on the accuracy of the preceding mapping analysis.
