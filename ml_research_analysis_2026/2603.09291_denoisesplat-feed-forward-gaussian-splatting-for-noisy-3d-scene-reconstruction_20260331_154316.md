# DenoiseSplat: Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting for Noisy 3D Scene Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09291
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09291
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** DenoiseSplat achieves higher PSNR and SSIM and lower LPIPS compared to both the noisy-input MVSplat baseline and a two-stage pipeline (IDF denoiser + MVSplat) across Gaussian, Poisson, speckle, and salt-and-pepper noise at various intensities.

## Executive Summary
DenoiseSplat addresses the challenge of reconstructing 3D scenes from noisy multi-view images, where standard Gaussian splatting methods degrade. It introduces a feed-forward network built on MVSplat that directly predicts clean 3D Gaussian primitives from noisy inputs. The core innovation is a dual-branch head that decouples geometry and appearance estimation, combined with a Cross-Branch Boundary-Guided (CBC) module to refine appearance using geometry-derived cues. The model is trained end-to-end using only clean 2D renderings as supervision on a newly introduced noisy-clean RE10K benchmark, enabling it to learn noise-robust representations without 3D ground truth. Across various synthetic noise types and intensities, DenoiseSplat consistently outperforms baselines in novel-view synthesis quality.

## Method Summary
DenoiseSplat takes noisy multi-view images and camera parameters as input. It uses a shared 2D CNN to extract multi-scale features per view, then lifts these into a 3D cost volume via differentiable homography-based plane sweeps. A dual-branch Gaussian head predicts scene parameters: one branch estimates geometry (Gaussian centers, rotations, scales, opacities), while the other estimates appearance (spherical-harmonics coefficients, base color). The CBC module uses disparity and confidence maps from the geometry branch (with gradients stopped) to conditionally refine appearance. The resulting 3D Gaussian scene is rendered via differentiable splatting; the loss between rendered images and clean targets (L1 + SSIM) drives end-to-end training. This design isolates stable geometric cues from noisy appearance signals and encourages cross-view geometric consensus for denoising.

## Key Results
- DenoiseSplat achieves higher PSNR and SSIM and lower LPIPS than both the noisy-input MVSplat baseline and a two-stage pipeline (IDF denoiser + MVSplat).
- Improvements hold across Gaussian, Poisson, speckle, and salt-and-pepper noise at various intensities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Decoupling geometry and appearance
- **Claim:** Decoupling geometry and appearance estimation via a dual-branch Gaussian head improves noise robustness.
- **Mechanism:** Geometry parameters (centers, rotation, scale, opacity) are predicted from one branch, while appearance parameters (SH coefficients, color) come from a separate branch. This isolates the estimation of stable structural cues from noisy color/texture signals, preventing cross-contamination during training.
- **Core assumption:** Multi-view geometric consistency cues (e.g., photometric alignment across views) are more robust to sensor noise than fine texture/appearance details.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "dual-branch head to decouple geometry and appearance estimation"; [section 3.3] "geometry branch predicts centers, rotations, scales, and opacities... appearance branch predicts spherical-harmonics coefficients and colors."

### Mechanism 2: Scene-level noise-consistent training
- **Claim:** Training on a scene-level noise-consistent benchmark enables the network to learn noise-robust multi-view representations.
- **Mechanism:** For each scene, a single noise type and intensity is applied to all views, mimicking real acquisition conditions. This encourages the network to exploit cross-view geometric consensus to suppress noise rather than overfit to per-view artifacts.
- **Core assumption:** Shared noise statistics across views allow cross-view validation to distinguish signal from noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] "scene-level noise configuration... all views of a scene share the same noise type and level... images from the same scene are typically captured with the same device"; [section 4.2] "DenoiseSplat maintains more stable performance across seen and novel views... indicative of improved cross-view coherence."

### Mechanism 3: End-to-end 2D supervision for internal 3D denoising
- **Claim:** End-to-end training with only 2D clean rendering supervision forces the 3D Gaussian representation to internally denoise.
- **Mechanism:** The network receives noisy images and must output a Gaussian scene whose renderings match clean targets. The reconstruction loss implicitly drives the network to predict Gaussians that explain clean views, effectively filtering noise during 3D aggregation and parameter prediction without explicit 2D denoising.
- **Core assumption:** The feed-forward architecture with differentiable rendering provides a sufficient prior for mapping noisy multi-view features to a clean 3D representation, even without 3D supervision.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "train end-to-end using only clean 2D renderings as supervision and no 3D ground truth"; [section 3.3] "we supervise reconstruction only in the 2D image domain... After end-to-end training... the model learns to predict a Gaussian scene representation whose renderings match the clean targets."

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Noisy multi-view images + camera parameters -> Shared 2D CNN (per-view) -> Homography-based plane-sweep 3D volume -> Dual-branch Gaussian head (geometry branch: xyz, rotation, scale, opacity; appearance branch: SH coefficients, color) -> CBC appearance refinement (uses geometry-derived boundary and confidence) -> Differentiable Gaussian splatting -> Rendered images -> Loss (L1+SSIM) against clean images.

**Critical path**:  
Noisy images → per-view features → 3D feature volume → dual-branch Gaussian prediction → rendered images → loss vs. clean targets. The geometry branch feeds disparity/confidence into CBC but gradients are stopped, so appearance branch updates independently with gated correction.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Dual-branch vs. single head*: Adds slight parameters but may improve robustness by preventing noisy appearance from corrupting geometry estimates. Risk: if geometry is wrong, CBC may amplify artifacts at boundaries.  
- *Scene-level noise training*: Better simulates realistic conditions but may not generalize to per-view varying noise.  
- *End-to-end 2D supervision*: No need for 3D ground truth, but relies on rendering loss to properly credit 3D structure; may struggle with ambiguity.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Over-smoothing of textures (similar to two-stage denoiser failures) if network becomes too conservative.  
- Halo/ringing near depth discontinuities if CBC misgates refinement.  
- Geometric collapse or floating artifacts if geometry branch fails under heavy noise.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How does DenoiseSplat perform on real-world noisy data involving camera sensor noise, motion blur, and compression artifacts, beyond synthetic noise types?  
  *Basis*: "our current noise modeling is based mainly on synthetic noise and does not yet capture more complex degradations such as real camera noise, motion blur, and compression artifacts."  
  *Why unresolved*: The paper only evaluates on synthetic noise injected into RE10K; real degradations involve correlated, non-stationary noise patterns and multi-factor artifacts that may affect multi-view consistency differently.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Benchmarking on datasets with authentic noisy captures or integrating physical camera noise models into training; quantitative PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS comparisons and qualitative analysis of geometric coherence.

- **Open Question 2**: Does DenoiseSplat generalize across diverse 3D scene datasets (e.g., indoor scenes, forward-facing captures) and maintain noise robustness without dataset-specific tuning?  
  *Basis*: "our experiments are primarily conducted on RE10K, and the cross-dataset and real-world generalization of the model remains to be systematically evaluated."  
  *Why unresolved*: RE10K consists of outdoor, trajectory-based scenes; other datasets have different baselines, sparsity, and scene structures that may challenge the feed-forward architecture trained on one domain.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Cross-dataset evaluation on benchmarks like LLFF, NeRF Synthetic, or TanksAndTemples with injected noise; measuring performance drop and analyzing failure modes to assess transferability.

- **Open Question 3**: Can DenoiseSplat be extended to dynamic scenes and non-noise degradations (e.g., blur, low-light) while preserving feed-forward efficiency and reconstruction quality?  
  *Basis*: "extend the framework to broader degradation types and dynamic scenes."  
  *Why unresolved*: The current method assumes static scenes and focuses on additive noise; dynamics introduce temporal inconsistencies and motion, while other degradations require different priors (e.g., deblurring).  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Developing temporal extensions (e.g., incorporating flow or time-aware features) and multi-degradation training; evaluating on dynamic datasets like D-NeRF or KITTI with synthetic and real degradations.

- **Open Question 4**: How can semantic understanding be integrated into DenoiseSplat to produce 3D representations that are both noise-robust and semantically meaningful for downstream tasks?  
  *Basis*: "integrate our approach with higher-level semantic cues, aiming for feed-forward 3D representations that are not only noise-robust but also better aligned with downstream 3D understanding tasks."  
  *Why unresolved*: The work focuses on pixel-level reconstruction; semantic segmentation, object detection, or scene parsing in Gaussian splatting remain underexplored, especially under noise.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Joint training with semantic segmentation networks or incorporating language-guided features; evaluating on noisy scenes with instance-level annotations for tasks like 3D object detection or editable scene modeling.

## Limitations
- Implementation details missing: RE10K splits, hyperparameters (e.g., ResNet depth, plane sweeps), training schedule (batch, epochs, LR, loss weights), CBC architecture, noise distribution specifics.
- Synthetic only: Noise evaluated only on synthetic RE10K; no real noisy data.
- No 3D GT: Supervision solely 2D; geometric accuracy not directly validated.

## Confidence
- Mech1 (dual-branch decoupling): Medium – Plausible but indirect evidence; break condition plausible.
- Mech2 (scene-level noise training): Medium – Consensus assumption may not hold under per-view noise variation.
- Mech3 (2D supervision): Medium – Rendering loss may not fully constrain 3D under ambiguity.
- Overall performance: High – Reported metrics show consistent gains.
