# FUTON: Fourier Tensor Network for Implicit Neural Representations
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13414
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13414
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Fourier bases fused with low-rank CP decomposition yield fast convergence, better generalization, and linear‑time inference; achieves 37.12 dB PSNR on Kodak images and outperforms WIRE in multiple inverse problems.

## Executive Summary
FUTON reparameterizes implicit neural representations as generalized Fourier series with coefficients given by a low‑rank Canonical Polyadic tensor decomposition. This design leverages the spectral sparsity of natural signals: separable orthonormal cosine bases capture smoothness and periodicity, while the CP factorization imposes a low‑dimensional structure on Fourier coefficients. Theoretical analysis proves universal approximation and linear‑time inference. Experiments on image and volume representation, as well as inverse problems (super‑resolution, denoising, CT), show that FUTON trains 2–5× faster than the recent state‑of‑the‑art WIRE and consistently achieves higher fidelity, especially on natural signals.

## Method Summary
FUTON models a signal \(s(\mathbf{x})\) via a truncated generalized Fourier series:
\[
\hat{s}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k_1=1}^K \cdots \sum_{k_C=1}^K W_{k_1\ldots k_C} \prod_{c=1}^C \phi_{k_c}(x_c),
\]
where \(\phi_k\) are orthonormal 1D basis functions (cosine) and \(W\) is a \(K^C \times D\) coefficient tensor. Instead of learning \(W\) directly, FUTON imposes a CP decomposition: \(W = \sum_{r=1}^R \mathbf{u}_r^{(1)} \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathbf{u}_r^{(C)} \otimes \mathbf{v}_r\), with factor matrices \(\mathbf{U}^{(c)} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times R}\) and \(\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times R}\). The forward pass evaluates each basis \(\phi_c(x_c)\), projects onto \(\mathbf{U}^{(c)}\), takes the Hadamard product across dimensions, and finally multiplies by \(\mathbf{V}\). This rearrangement reduces inference complexity from exponential \(O(K^C D)\) to linear \(O(CKR + CR + DR)\). Training minimizes MSE (with optional regularization) using Adam. Implementation details: coordinates normalized to \([0,1]\), values to \([-1,1]\) with tanh output, typical hyperparameters \(K=R=512\), and cosine annealing.

## Key Results
- **Image representation (Kodak):** 37.12 dB PSNR, 96.54% SSIM, 2–5× faster training than WIRE.
- **Volume representation (Thai Statue):** 99.67% IoU.
- **Inverse problems:** Outperforms WIRE in super‑resolution (30.74 vs 30.54 dB), denoising (27.04 vs 26.51 dB), and CT reconstruction (29.18 vs 27.80 dB) with faster convergence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Fourier basis functions capture smoothness and periodicity in natural signals.
- **Mechanism:** FUTON uses separable, orthonormal cosine basis functions (e.g., \(\phi_k(x) = \sqrt{2}\cos(k\pi x)\) for \(k \geq 1\)) from generalized Fourier series. These form a complete set in \(L^2([0,1])\), allowing approximation of any square‑integrable signal by linear combinations. The cosine basis aligns with the compressible frequency‑domain structure of natural images, akin to the discrete cosine transform in JPEG.
- **Core assumption:** Natural signals are sparse or low‑dimensional in the frequency domain, with most energy in low‑frequency components.
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract: “Fourier bases capture smoothness and periodicity”; Section 3.2 Theorem 2 proves convergence; neighbor works (FLAIR, NSTR) identify frequency‑awareness as key.
- **Break condition:** If the target lacks spectral sparsity (e.g., pure noise or highly irregular textures), many high‑frequency components are needed, reducing efficiency.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Low‑rank CP decomposition of Fourier coefficients enforces low‑dimensional spectral structure, improving generalization and preventing overfitting.
- **Mechanism:** The coefficient tensor \(\mathbf{W}\) is factorized as \(\mathbf{W} = \sum_{r=1}^R \mathbf{u}_r^{(1)} \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathbf{u}_r^{(C)} \otimes \mathbf{v}_r\). This reduces effective degrees of freedom, acting as an implicit regularizer that captures dominant spectral correlations across dimensions. Rank \(R\) controls capacity to represent multi‑way frequency interactions.
- **Core assumption:** The Fourier coefficient tensor of natural signals has low effective rank.
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract: “low‑rank parameterization enforces low‑dimensional spectral structure”; Section 4.1 Equation (8) and Figure 1; related works (F‑INR, TensoRF) use tensor decompositions.
- **Break condition:** For intrinsically high‑rank spectra (e.g., white noise, fine uncorrelated textures), the low‑rank constraint may underfit unless \(R\) is increased, sacrificing efficiency.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Efficient tensor contraction order yields linear‑time inference, enabling scalability.
- **Mechanism:** The computation \(\hat{\mathbf{s}}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{x}) \bullet \mathbf{W}\) is rearranged into three stages: (1) project each basis \(\phi_c(x_c)\) onto factor matrix \(\mathbf{U}^{(c)}\) (cost \(O(CKR)\)), (2) Hadamard product of projected features (cost \(O(CR)\)), (3) multiply by \(\mathbf{V}\) (cost \(O(DR)\)). This avoids exponential \(O(K^C D)\) cost.
- **Core assumption:** The contraction order can be optimized due to separability of \(\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{x})\) and CP structure of \(\mathbf{W}\).
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract: “derive an inference algorithm with complexity linear in the spectral resolution and the input dimension”; Section 4.2 complexity analysis and Figure 3; tensor network literature (Kolda & Bader) supports order optimization.
- **Break condition:** If bases are non‑separable or decomposition is not CP, efficient contraction may not be possible.

## Foundational Learning
- **Generalized Fourier Series**  
  *Why needed:* FUTON represents signals as truncated generalized Fourier series using separable orthonormal bases. Understanding convergence in \(L^2\) and basis completeness explains why any square‑integrable function can be approximated.  
  *Quick check:* Why does convergence of \(\hat{s}_K\) to \(s\) in \(L^2\) require the basis to be complete and orthonormal?

- **Canonical Polyadic (CP) Decomposition**  
  *Why needed:* CP factorizes the weight tensor \(\mathbf{W}\) into rank‑1 components, reducing parameters and enabling efficient computation. Distinguishing CP rank from exact tensor rank clarifies capacity control.  
  *Quick check:* How does CP rank \(R\) relate to the number of parameters, and why might \(R \leq K^{C-1}\min\{K, D\}\) suffice for universal approximation?

- **Tensor Contraction and Order Optimization**  
  *Why needed:* The forward pass’s linear complexity depends on contracting tensors in an order that avoids large intermediates. Understanding mode‑\(m\) products, Hadamard products, and tensor diagrams guides optimal sequencing.  
  *Quick check:* In the contraction \(\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{x}) \bullet \mathbf{W}\), why is contracting each mode of \(\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{x})\) with \(\mathbf{U}^{(c)}\) first more efficient than forming the full \(\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{x})\) tensor?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input \(\mathbf{x} \in [0,1]^C\) → evaluate 1D cosine bases \(\phi_c(x_c)\) → compute \(\mathbf{h}_c = \mathbf{U}^{(c)\top} \phi_c(x_c)\) for all \(c\) → form \(\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}) = \odot_{c=1}^C \mathbf{h}_c(x_c)\) → output \(\hat{\mathbf{s}}(\mathbf{x}) = \tanh(\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}))\). Parameters: \(\{\mathbf{U}^{(c)}\}_{c=1}^C, \mathbf{V}\).

- **Critical path:** Coordinate → basis evaluation → factor projections → Hadamard product → output. The factorization and Hadamard product replace MLP nonlinearities.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Rank \(R\)*: larger improves quality but slows inference; optimal \(R=512\) for natural images.  
  *Basis choice*: Cosine basis yields best speed/quality; polynomial bases (Legendre, Chebyshev) are slower and less accurate.  
  *Spectral resolution \(K\)*: larger captures higher frequencies but increases cost; \(K=512\) sufficient for natural images.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  *Overfitting*: too high \(R\) relative to data; may overfit noise but low‑rank prior offers some resistance.  
  *Underfitting*: too low \(R\) or \(K\) yields blurry reconstructions.  
  *Numerical instability*: using non‑orthonormal bases breaks theoretical guarantees.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Image representation (Kodak):** Train on all pixel coordinates, report PSNR/SSIM; verify FUTON outperforms WIRE and is faster.  
  2. **Volume representation (Thai Statue):** Train on 3D coordinates for occupancy, report IoU; check better surface reconstruction and faster convergence than MLP baselines.  
  3. **Inverse problem (super‑resolution):** Train on downsampled image, evaluate at full resolution; confirm superior generalization over bilinear interpolation and WIRE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptive bases:** Can learned, adaptive orthonormal bases improve FUTON on diverse signal classes beyond natural images? (Conclusion suggestion; untested.)
- **Other tensor decompositions:** Do Tucker or Tensor Train formats offer better expressiveness–efficiency trade‑offs for specific signals? (FUTON uses only CP; comparative analysis missing.)
- **Scalability to high dimensions:** Can FUTON effectively scale to 4D spatio‑temporal data or 5D radiance fields while maintaining linear complexity? (Experiments only up to 3D.)
- **Sparsity‑rank relationship:** What is the theoretical link between signal sparsity in the Fourier domain and the required CP rank that explains the cosine basis advantage? (Empirical only; no formal bound.)
- **Implicit regularization:** To what extent does the low‑rank factorization itself provide implicit regularization compared to explicit task‑specific regularizers in inverse problems? (Regularizer contributions not isolated.)
