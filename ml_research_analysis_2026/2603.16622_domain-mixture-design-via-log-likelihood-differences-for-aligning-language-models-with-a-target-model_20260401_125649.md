# Domain Mixture Design via Log-Likelihood Differences for Aligning Language Models with a Target Model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16622
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16622
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** A two-pass domain mixture recipe (aggregated-LLD) reduces KL divergence to target models (Gemma-2B, CodeGemma-2B) by 0.3–0.6 bits/byte versus uniform weighting, without requiring target access during training.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of aligning a base language model with a target model's behavior using only continued pretraining, avoiding knowledge distillation. The core insight represents models as vectors of mean log-likelihoods over fixed evaluation domains. It derives domain-specific training proportions that steer the base model's expected update direction in this space toward the target's position. A practical two-pass method first estimates time-varying weights based on log-likelihood differences, aggregates them via geometric mean into a fixed recipe, and then trains the base model with that recipe. Experiments with NanoGPT show consistent KL reductions to Gemma-2B and CodeGemma-2B compared to uniform domain mixing, though distillation still performs better. The approach offers a feasible alternative when the target model is inaccessible during training.

## Method Summary
The method aligns a base model to a frozen target by designing a fixed domain mixture for continued pretraining. First, both models are evaluated on a fixed, small evaluation corpus *D* to compute per-domain mean log-likelihood vectors. During a first training pass, at sparse steps *T*, the base model's current log-likelihood vector is compared to the target's; domain weights are heuristically estimated as a softmax over their differences (scaled by temperature *τ*). After the pass, these temporal weight estimates are aggregated via geometric mean to produce a single fixed recipe *π*⁎. In a second pass, the base model is retrained from the same initialization using *π*⁎—no target access is needed. The theoretical derivation posits that under SGD, the expected update in log-likelihood space is proportional to *J*⊤*Jπ*, where *J* is the domain-wise gradient sensitivity matrix; setting this proportional to the target difference yields *π* ∝ (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹(ℓᵗᵍᵗ − ℓ). In practice, (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹ is omitted due to cost, relying on the heuristic.

## Key Results
- For random-init NanoGPT aligned with CodeNanoGPT, KL dropped from 4.39 to 4.07 bits/byte using aggregated-LLD.
- For pretrained NanoGPT aligned with CodeGemma-2B, KL dropped from 2.59 to 1.96 bits/byte.
- Knowledge distillation achieved lower KL (2.13 and 0.99 bits/byte), but aggregated-LLD improved downstream task performance correlation toward the target relative to uniform weighting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Aligning the expected update direction in log-likelihood space toward the target model’s position guides the base model’s distributional shift.
- **Mechanism:** Models are represented as vectors of mean log-likelihoods over fixed evaluation domains. The expected change in this vector under SGD with domain mixture *π* is approximately *ηJ*⊤*Jπ*. Setting this proportional to the target difference ℓᵗᵍᵗ − ℓ yields *π* ∝ (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹(ℓᵗᵍᵗ − ℓ). This geometric alignment is the theoretical criterion for domain weights.
- **Core assumption:** The first-order SGD approximation holds, and moving in log-likelihood space correlates with minimizing KL divergence to the target.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "viewing models as points in log-likelihood space and aligning the training update direction with the direction toward the target model." Section 3.3 derives *J*⊤*Jπ* ≈ ℓᵗᵍᵗ − ℓ and the unconstrained solution *π̃* = (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹(ℓᵗᵍᵗ − ℓ). Neighbor papers (DoGE, GRAPE) use domain reweighting for alignment but differ in objective; the paper’s geometric formulation is distinct.
- **Break condition:** If the log-likelihood vector space poorly approximates model distributions (e.g., due to architectural mismatches or non-smooth optimization), the derived *π* may not yield KL reduction.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Aggregating dynamically estimated domain weights via geometric mean produces a stable fixed training recipe that approximates the time-varying optimal trajectory.
- **Mechanism:** In a first pass, domain weights *π*(*t*) are estimated at steps *T* using the heuristic *π*(*t*) ∝ exp((ℓᵗᵍᵗ − ℓ)/*τ*). These are aggregated as *π*⁎ ∝ (∏ₜ *π*(*t*))¹/|*T*|, which solves min_*π*∈∆ ∑ₜ KL(*π*, *π*(*t*)). This yields a single recipe for second-pass training.
- **Core assumption:** The aggregated mixture *π*⁎ effectively summarizes the sequence of optimal local directions over training, and a fixed recipe suffices for global alignment.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.4 defines geometric-mean aggregation (8) and its equivalence to minimizing summed KL divergences (9). Section 4.3 shows aggregated-LLD achieves KL close to iterative-LLD (which updates every step), confirming the aggregation’s validity. Neighbors like "Scaling Laws for Optimal Data Mixtures" optimize mixtures but not via temporal aggregation; this method’s two-pass design is novel.
- **Break condition:** If the optimal domain weights change drastically non-smoothly during training, a single aggregated recipe may inadequately capture the trajectory, leading to suboptimal alignment.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Using the softmax of domain-level log-likelihood differences (ℓᵗᵍᵗ − ℓ)/*τ* as a heuristic for *π*, without explicit sensitivity adjustment (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹, remains effective and computationally feasible.
- **Mechanism:** The full theoretical solution requires computing *J*⊤*J* (O(*K*²*P*) cost), which is prohibitive. The practical approximation drops (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹, relying on the observation that diagonal dominance in (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹ (Fig. 6) makes per-domain scaling less critical. The temperature *τ* controls weight sharpness.
- **Core assumption:** Inter-domain correlations captured by (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹ are secondary to the raw log-likelihood differences for alignment; the heuristic captures the primary signal.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3: "we omit (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹... and instead use a heuristic approximation given by the softmax of (ℓᵗᵍᵗ − ℓ)/*τ*." Section 4.3: iterative-LLD (heuristic) and adjusted-LLD (full) both outperform uniform, with adjusted-LLD slightly better; the gap is small, supporting the heuristic’s sufficiency. No direct corpus anchor; neighbor papers (DoGE) use likelihood ratios but not this specific geometric approximation.
- **Break condition:** If sensitivity adjustments are crucial (e.g., for models with highly correlated domain gradients), the heuristic may underperform relative to adjusted-LLD, especially in Pretrained settings where off-diagonals are non-negligible (Fig. 6).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  Base model (θ) + Target model (frozen) + Evaluation corpus *D* + Domain corpora {*C*ₖ} + Steps *T* + Temp *τ* -> First-pass training with checkpoint evaluations at *T* -> Compute π(*t*) = softmax((ℓᵗᵍᵗ − ℓ(θ))/τ) -> Geometric mean aggregation -> π* -> Second-pass training with fixed π* -> Aligned model + π* + trajectory.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Precompute ℓᵗᵍᵗ on *D*.
  2. First pass: train base model for 10k steps; at each *t*∈*T*, evaluate ℓ(θ) on *D*, compute π(*t*).
  3. Aggregate π(*t*) to π* via geometric mean.
  4. Second pass: reinitialize base model, train 10k steps with fixed π*.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Fixed recipe vs. dynamic: aggregated-LLD simplifies deployment but may miss early/late phase nuances (Appendix C suggests possible two-stage mixtures).
  - Heuristic vs. full: Omitting (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹ reduces O(*K*²*P*) cost but may lose sensitivity correction; pretrained models show some off-diagonal structure (Fig. 6).
  - Evaluation corpus *D*: Must be fixed and representative; token-length normalization is a practical hack (limitation 4).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - π(*t*) trajectories show high variance or drift far from aggregated mean (Appendix C: Jensen–Shannon divergence blocks indicate stabilization only late).
  - KL reduction minimal compared to uniform; downstream task correlation not improving (Fig. 8).
  - (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹ entries highly non-diagonal and heuristic performs much worse than adjusted-LLD.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validation against ground truth:** Use CodeNanoGPT (known weights) as target; compare KL from estimated π* to KL from true weights vs. uniform (Section 4.2, Fig. 4). Confirms π* captures target’s domain preference.
  2. **KL divergence to public targets:** Train random-init/pretrained NanoGPT toward Gemma-2B/CodeGemma-2B; compare uniform, iterative-LLD, aggregated-LLD, adjusted-LLD (Section 4.3, Fig. 5, Table 1). Shows consistent improvement over uniform.
  3. **Temperature ablation:** Vary *τ*; observe KL to CodeNanoGPT (Section 4.5, Table 2). *τ*=1.0 optimal; extremes harm alignment, validating softmax scaling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does the domain mixture design method scale effectively to large language models with billions of parameters?
2. How does the method generalize to corpora with different domain structures, ambiguous boundaries, or finer granularity (e.g., sentence-level or clustered pseudo-domains)?
3. What is the impact of the discrepancy between the SGD-based theoretical derivation and the use of AdamW in experiments on the validity of the log-likelihood space geometric interpretation?
4. Can the computational and memory costs of the full adjustment matrix (*J*⊤*J*)⁻¹ be reduced without sacrificing the alignment performance benefits observed for adjusted-LLD?
5. Does dynamically scheduling the temperature *τ* or using weighted aggregation in (8) improve alignment compared to fixed *τ*=1 and unweighted geometric mean?

## Limitations
- Evaluation corpus details (text selection, length normalization) are underspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Theoretical derivation relies on first-order SGD approximation and Euclidean–KL correspondence in log-likelihood space; validity beyond small updates or deep architectures is unclear.
- Heuristic ignores full second-order sensitivity (*J*⊤*J*); while experiments show minor gap, its necessity under stronger domain correlations remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Empirical KL reduction (random/pretrained NanoGPT → Gemma/CodeGemma):** High (consistent across multiple experiments; large effect sizes despite lacking p-values).
- **Downstream task correlation improvement:** Medium (trend positive but metrics show variance; Pearson correlation only).
- **Theoretical alignment criterion (*J*⊤*Jπ* ≈ target difference):** Medium (derivation sound under assumptions; limited direct validation).
- **Aggregation via geometric mean approximates iterative updates:** Medium to High (iterative vs. aggregated results close, yet limited to 10k steps).
- **Heuristic (softmax of ℓᵗᵍᵗ−ℓ) adequacy:** Medium (adjusted-LLD marginally better; gap small in tested settings).

## Next Checks
1. **Log-likelihood vs. KL correlation:** Throughout first-pass training, compute both Euclidean distance in log-likelihood space ℓ(θ) and true model KL(θ‖target) on held-out domain samples. Assess linearity and *R*² to validate the core proxy.
2. **Sensitivity matrix impact:** For a range of model scales (e.g., 124M, 355M, 1B) and varied domain sets (including correlated corpora), explicitly compute *J*⊤*J* and compare heuristic π vs. adjusted-LLD π in terms of KL reduction. Identify regimes where off-diagonals materially affect performance.
3. **Aggregation robustness:** Vary the estimation step set *T* (e.g., uniform, early-heavy, late-heavy) and number of checkpoints. Measure resulting π* variance and final KL to determine sensitivity to temporal sampling and whether multi-stage mixtures are needed.
