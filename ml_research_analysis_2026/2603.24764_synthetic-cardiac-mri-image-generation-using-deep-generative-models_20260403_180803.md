# Synthetic Cardiac MRI Image Generation using Deep Generative Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24764
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24764
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Mask-conditioned diffusion models achieve high anatomical fidelity and superior boundary preservation, improving downstream segmentation by up to 4% Dice and reducing Hausdorff distance by 40% in multi-vendor settings.

## Executive Summary
Synthetic generation of cardiac MRI (CMRI) using deep generative models addresses data scarcity and vendor variability. This paper evaluates mask-conditioned diffusion models, which condition image synthesis on segmentation masks to enforce anatomical correctness. The models demonstrate lower FID scores and better boundary preservation compared to GANs and VAEs. Moreover, synthetic data augments training for segmentation networks, yielding up to a 4% increase in Dice score and a 40% reduction in Hausdorff distance across different scanner vendors. Vendor-style conditioning further enhances cross-domain generalization. While privacy assessments indicate potential leakage risks, radiologists can only distinguish synthetic from real images at 60% accuracy, indicating high realism. The study also notes that flow-matching models offer faster inference but tend to overfit on small datasets.

## Method Summary
The goal is to generate synthetic cardiac MRI (CMRI) images conditioned on segmentation masks to augment training data and mitigate vendor domain shift. Inputs are short-axis CMRI slices (end-diastolic and end-systolic phases) with multi-class masks (LV, RV, MYO). Preprocessing includes center-cropping to 256×256, Z-score intensity normalization over the foreground, and one-hot mask encoding. The core method is a mask-conditioned Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) with a U-Net backbone. The model concatenates the noisy image with the mask at each denoising step and is trained to predict noise via MSE loss under a linear noise schedule (β from 1e-4 to 0.02). Optional conditioning on vendor labels enables style adaptation. Evaluation uses FID, SSIM, PSNR for fidelity, and Dice/Hausdorff distance for downstream segmentation utility.

## Key Results
- Mask-conditioned diffusion models outperform GANs and VAEs, achieving lower FID scores and superior boundary preservation.
- Synthetic data boosts segmentation performance: Dice score improves by up to 4% and Hausdorff distance is reduced by 40% in multi-vendor settings.
- Vendor-style conditioning enhances cross-domain generalization, mitigating domain shift between scanner vendors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Mask Conditioning for Anatomical Fidelity
- **Claim:** Mask-conditioned diffusion models achieve high anatomical fidelity and superior boundary preservation.
- **Mechanism:** The segmentation mask acts as a spatial constraint during the iterative denoising process of diffusion (e.g., DDPM). At each step, the model is conditioned on the binary mask, guiding noise prediction to reconstruct image content only within masked regions. This enforces structural consistency for cardiac chambers, preventing implausible anatomies.
- **Core assumption:** Explicit spatial conditioning on high-quality masks provides a stronger signal for anatomy than latent-space or unconditional generation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract], [section III.B.8], and neighbor paper benchmarks.
- **Break condition:** Performance degrades if input masks contain significant errors or if training data lacks anatomical diversity within masked regions.

### Mechanism 2: Vendor-Style Conditioning for Cross-Domain Generalization
- **Claim:** Vendor-style conditioning (or domain factorization) improves cross-vendor generalization of synthetic CMRI.
- **Mechanism:** The model is conditioned on a discrete vendor label or continuous domain latent vector. During training, it learns to map the same anatomical mask to images mimicking each vendor's intensity, contrast, and texture patterns, decoupling anatomy from vendor-specific appearance.
- **Core assumption:** Major cross-center segmentation drops are driven by superficial vendor characteristics rather than fundamental anatomical differences, modelable as a stylistic domain.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract], [section III.B.2], [section III.B.7] (FFD-VAE).
- **Break condition:** Fails if vendors use fundamentally different acquisition protocols creating irreducible contrast differences, or if conditioning signal is noisy/unavailable.

### Mechanism 3: Privacy Auditing via Membership Inference Attacks
- **Claim:** Privacy risks (memorization) in synthetic CMRI can be audited using membership inference attacks (MIAs) and nearest-neighbor analyses, though consistent evaluation standards are lacking.
- **Mechanism:** An MIA treats the generative model as a black box; an adversary attempts to distinguish whether a real image was in the training set based on metrics like reconstruction error (from inverting diffusion) or model confidence. Lower attack accuracy suggests less memorization. Nearest-neighbor searches check for inadvertent replication.
- **Core assumption:** If a model memorized a training sample, it will reconstruct it with systematically higher fidelity (lower error) than unseen samples, creating a detectable signal.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract], [section V.B]; evidence is weak; neighbor papers focus on fidelity/utility.
- **Break condition:** Attack signals are weak if the model is properly regularized (e.g., differential privacy) or if the dataset is very large and diverse.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Preprocessor -> Mask Encoder/Embedder -> Diffusion U-Net Backbone -> Scheduler -> (Optional) Vendor Encoder -> Post-processor

**Critical path**  
Raw DICOM -> Preprocessor -> Cleaned Image/Mask -> [For Training: Add noise to image] -> U-Net (conditioned on mask + vendor) -> Predict noise -> Update image -> Repeat for T steps -> Final Cleaned Synthetic Image

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Speed vs. Quality:** More denoising steps (e.g., 50 vs. 10) improve fidelity but increase inference time (~2.3s vs. faster for flow-matching). Tradeoff dictates deployment feasibility.
- **Mask Fidelity vs. Image Realism:** Strong mask conditioning ensures anatomical correctness but can lead to "copy-paste" texture artifacts if the model doesn't learn enough variability within mask bounds. Weakening conditioning (e.g., lower classifier-free guidance scale) increases diversity but risks boundary breaches.
- **Vendor Conditioning Granularity:** Using a discrete vendor label is simple but may not capture continuous style variations. A latent domain vector (like in FFD-VAE) is more flexible but harder to train and requires a domain encoder.

**Failure signatures**  
1. **Blurred or "washed-out" anatomy:** Indicates insufficient denoising steps, overly aggressive noise schedule, or excessive regularization (e.g., strong DP noise).
2. **Anatomical breaches (m leaked tissue):** Synthetic image shows structures outside the mask boundaries. Sign of too weak conditioning, poor mask quality, or mode collapse.
3. **Vendor artifacts persisting:** Synthetic images from a "Source Vendor A" model applied to a "Target Vendor B" mask still show Vendor A's intensity profile. Indicates ineffective domain disentanglement.
4. **High MIA success rate (>50%):** Privacy leakage sign; model likely memorizing high-frequency, patient-specific details.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Fidelity-Sanity Check:** Train mask-conditioned diffusion on a single vendor's healthy subjects. Generate 500 samples. Compute FID and mean SSIM between generated images and corresponding real images. Expected: FID < 50, SSIM > 0.85. If not, training is unstable.
2. **Mask Conditioning Ablation:** Repeat Experiment 1 but corrupt 20% of training masks (randomly erode/dilate boundaries). Evaluate downstream segmentation Dice on a clean test set. Expected: >5% Dice drop versus uncorrupted mask training, proving mask fidelity is critical.
3. **Vendor Generalization Probe:** Train vendor-conditioned model on 3/4 vendors from M&Ms. Generate 100 samples per held-out vendor (using that vendor's mask & label). Train a segmentation network only on these synthetic samples. Evaluate on real held-out vendor data. Report Dice. Success: Dice within 2-3% of a model trained on 100 real images from that vendor. Failure: >10% gap, indicating insufficient cross-vendor transfer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can mask-conditioned generative models be extended to synthesize specific cardiac pathologies (e.g., hypertrophy, DCM) with controllable severity?  
*Basis in paper:* Explicitly identified as a key gap; existing models focus on healthy anatomy and lack controlled synthesis of diseases.  
*Why unresolved:* Latent spaces do not encode pathological variations; clinical validation for realism and utility is absent.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* A latent-pathology model generating 3D-consistent pathological variations, validated by clinician review and showing improved segmentation on rare-disease datasets.

**Open Question 2:** What standardized privacy evaluation framework (e.g., membership inference attacks with calibrated thresholds, differential privacy budgets) is necessary to quantify leakage risks without excessively degrading anatomical fidelity?  
*Basis in paper:* The paper states privacy evaluation is "largely neglected" and calls for integrated audits like MIAs and DP mechanisms.  
*Why unresolved:* Assessment methods are inconsistent; DP noise blurs structures; trade-offs between privacy and fidelity are poorly quantified.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* A benchmark suite measuring MIA success rates and segmentation utility across varying DP budgets on multi-vendor cardiac datasets, establishing minimum reporting standards.

**Open Question 3:** Can flow-matching models be adapted to prevent overfitting on small cardiac MRI datasets while preserving their fast inference advantage?  
*Basis in paper:* The abstract and Section VIII.B.2 note that "flow-matching offers faster inference but overfits on small datasets," highlighting an underexplored limitation.  
*Why unresolved:* Systematic studies under limited-data conditions are lacking; acceleration techniques may exacerbate memorization.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments training flow-matching models on subsets of M&Ms/ACDC, comparing fidelity (FID), utility (Dice), and training/validation gaps, with regularization or data-augmentation strategies.

**Open Question 4:** What cross-dataset validation protocols (e.g., standardized vendor-to-vendor transfers with pathology stratification) are needed to reliably assess generalization of synthetic cardiac MRI?  
*Basis in paper:* "Insufficient cross-dataset utility evaluation" is highlighted; most studies use single-dataset validation, and performance drops on multi-vendor tests are reported but not standardized.  
*Why unresolved:* Lack of fixed benchmarks and pathology-aware reporting prevents fair comparison of models' domain generalization.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* A community challenge using multi-vendor datasets (M&Ms, ACDC) with fixed segmentation backbones, measuring synthetic augmentation's impact on cross-vendor Dice scores across disease subtypes.

## Limitations
- Reliance on the M&Ms dataset (n=350) may limit generalizability; vendor diversity is constrained.
- Exact diffusion architecture (U-Net details) and hyperparameters are unspecified, risking irreproducibility.
- Privacy evaluation lacks standardized benchmarks; evidence for MIA leakage is weak and inconsistent.

## Next Checks
1. **Boundary adherence stress test:** Generate images using corrupted masks (simulated segmentation errors). Quantify Hausdorff distance between generated image edges and original (uncorrupted) mask; expect >5% degradation if conditioning is robust.
2. **Protocol shift generalization:** Train vendor-conditioned model on Siemens/GE data from M&Ms. Test on masks from a third vendor with a different sequence (e.g., different TE/TR). Measure Dice drop in downstream segmentation; >10% gap confirms insufficient disentanglement.
3. **Standardized MIA benchmark:** Implement a published MIA (e.g., using reconstruction error thresholds) on the released synthetic dataset. Report attack accuracy (AUC) and compare against a non-memorizing baseline (DP-trained model). Consistency requires AUC < 0.55.
