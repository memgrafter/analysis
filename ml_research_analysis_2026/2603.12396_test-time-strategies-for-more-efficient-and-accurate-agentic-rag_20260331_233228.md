# Test-Time Strategies for More Efficient and Accurate Agentic RAG

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12396
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12396
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: The best contextualization variant with GPT-4.1-mini improved exact match by 5.6% and reduced retrieval turns by 10.5% on HotpotQA/NQ validation sets versus baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles inefficiencies in the Search-R1 agentic RAG framework, specifically repetitive retrieval and poor contextualization of information across reasoning steps. The authors propose two test-time modules—contextualization and de-duplication—without retraining the base model. The contextualization module uses an external LLM to extract and cache relevant facts from retrieved documents, enabling the base model to build on prior information. The de-duplication module filters out previously seen documents to encourage retrieval of novel content. Evaluated on HotpotQA and Natural Questions, the contextualization module alone yields a 5.6% gain in exact match and a 10.5% reduction in retrieval turns compared to baseline. De-duplication alone increases turns and provides only marginal accuracy improvement. Combining both (hybrid) gives benefits smaller than contextualization alone, suggesting that information forgetting—not document insufficiency—is the primary bottleneck.

## Method Summary
The authors modify the Search-R1 inference loop, which uses an RL-trained LLM (Qwen2.5-7b) that alternately reasons and retrieves. At each retrieval step, they optionally apply two test-time modules. First, de-duplication: retrieved documents are filtered by ID to exclude those already seen in prior turns; duplicates are replaced by the next-ranked unseen passages from the retriever. Second, contextualization: an external LLM (GPT-4.1-mini) processes the (possibly deduped) documents together with the user query and a persistent memory cache, extracting only relevant facts into a concise summary that updates the cache. The base LLM then receives this summary and the full cache for its next reasoning step. No model retraining is performed. The system is evaluated on a 500-question sample from HotpotQA and Natural Questions validation sets, with metrics including exact match (EM), LLM-judged semantic match, and average retrieval turns.

## Key Results
- Contextualization (GPT-4.1-mini) increased exact match by 5.6% and reduced retrieval turns by 10.5% over baseline Search-R1.
- De-duplication alone increased average retrieval turns by ~4.4% and only marginally improved EM (+1.4%).
- Hybrid (both modules) improved EM to 0.480 and reduced turns to 2.154 vs. baseline 0.464 and 2.392, but gains were smaller than contextualization alone (EM 0.490, turns 2.142).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Contextualization Module
- **Claim**: Adding an LLM-powered contextualization module that extracts and caches relevant information across retrieval steps reduces information forgetting and improves answer accuracy and efficiency.
- **Mechanism**: After each retrieval, an external LLM processes the new documents (`D_i`) and the user prompt (`p`), extracting only relevant facts into a concise summary (`D_i*`). This summary is appended to a persistent memory cache. At the next reasoning step, the base LLM receives both the newly retrieved documents and the full accumulated cache, allowing it to build on previously extracted knowledge without reprocessing raw documents.
- **Core assumption**: The base LLM (Qwen2.5-7b) struggles to identify and retain key information from raw retrieved passages during its reasoning chain, leading to redundant queries and incomplete answer synthesis. A separate, more capable extractor (GPT-4.1-mini) can perform this extraction more reliably.
- **Evidence**: The abstract reports the module and its gains (+5.6% EM, -10.5% turns). Section 3.1 describes the module's function. Neighboring papers (Search-o1, A Memory Knowledge Reservoir) also explore external modules for information extraction/retention, providing indirect support.
- **Break condition**: If the base model's internal reasoning already effectively summarizes and uses retrieved passages, then adding an external extraction step would not yield gains and could introduce latency or errors.

### Mechanism 2: De-duplication Module
- **Claim**: Implementing a de-duplication module that filters out previously seen documents and forces retrieval of novel passages increases contextual diversity but, in this setup, did not improve efficiency and only marginally improved accuracy.
- **Mechanism**: The system maintains a set of seen document IDs. When `k` documents are retrieved in a turn, any duplicates are discarded and replaced by the next-highest-ranked, unseen document from the retriever's full list. This ensures the base LLM is exposed to a broader set of documents over its trajectory.
- **Core assumption**: The base model generates repeated search queries because it perceives the initial top-`k` results as insufficient, not because it has forgotten their content. Providing completely new documents should therefore lead to better answers or, at minimum, not harm performance.
- **Evidence**: The abstract mentions the module. Section 3.2 explains the hypothesis and implementation. Section 4.2 reports the outcome: de-duplication is less efficient than baseline (2.498 vs. 2.392 average retrievals) and only a small EM gain (1.4%).
- **Break condition**: If repeated queries are primarily caused by the model *forgetting* information it has already seen, then de-duplication would force the model to chase new, potentially irrelevant information, increasing turns without accuracy gains—which is exactly what occurred.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid (Contextualization + De-duplication)
- **Claim**: Combining contextualization and de-duplication yields benefits in both accuracy and efficiency, but the gains are dominated by the contextualization component alone.
- **Mechanism**: The hybrid pipeline first applies de-duplication to the retrieved set, then passes the novel documents to the contextualization module for extraction and cache update. The base model then reasons with the new contextualized summary plus the full historical cache.
- **Core assumption**: The two modules address orthogonal failure modes: de-duplication addresses retrieval redundancy/diversity, while contextualization addresses information extraction/retention. Their combination should therefore be synergistic.
- **Evidence**: The abstract describes the hybrid approach and notes that it achieves gains, although not as strongly as contextualization alone. Section 3.3 discusses the integration. Table 1 shows hybrid EM 0.480 vs. contextualization 0.490; turns 2.154 vs. 2.142, indicating contextualization drives most of the improvement.
- **Break condition**: If the primary bottleneck for accuracy is *only* information extraction/retention (as contextualization's strong performance suggests), then adding de-duplication provides little marginal benefit.

## Foundational Learning
### Agentic RAG / Search-R1
- **Why needed**: The paper modifies the Search-R1 framework, which uses an RL-trained LLM that interleaves reasoning and retrieval calls. Understanding this baseline loop is essential to see where the test-time modules plug in.
- **Quick check**: In Search-R1's inference loop, what triggers a retrieval action, and how are retrieved documents incorporated into the next reasoning step?

### Test-Time Strategies / Inference-Time Modifications
- **Why needed**: The paper's contributions are *not* architectural changes or retraining; they are post-training, runtime modifications. Distinguishing this from training-time methods is key.
- **Quick check**: How does a "test-time" module differ from a module that would be integrated during the RL training of Search-R1?

### Information Forgetting & Contextualization in Multi-Hop QA
- **Why needed**: The core problem identified is that the base agent fails to synthesize information across multiple retrieval steps. The proposed solution is an external memory and extractor. This requires understanding the difference between raw document storage and distilled context.
- **Quick check**: Why might passing raw retrieved documents (`D_i`) to the base LLM be less effective than passing a concise, extracted summary (`D_i*`) plus a cache?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Baseline: Base LLM -> Thought/Query -> Retriever (E5) -> Raw Docs (D_i) -> Base LLM (next step)
With modules: Retriever -> De-duplication (optional) -> Contextualization (External LLM) -> Cache update -> Base LLM (receives new summary D_i* and full accumulated cache)

### Critical path
1. User question `p` enters.
2. Base LLM generates first thought/query `q1`.
3. Retriever returns top-3 docs `D1`.
4. If hybrid/de-dup enabled, filter `D1` for unseen IDs; replace duplicates with next-unseen docs to get `D1'`. Else `D1' = D1`.
5. Contextualization module: External LLM takes `p`, `D1'`, and current cache (empty initially) → extracts summary `D1*`, updates cache.
6. Base LLM receives `p`, previous thought, and `D1*` + full cache → generates next thought/query or answer.
7. Repeat from step 3 for subsequent retrievals, using updated cache.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Latency vs. Gain**: Contextualization adds an external LLM call per retrieval turn, increasing cost and time.
- **Cache Management**: The persistent cache grows unbounded; the paper does not describe truncation/compression, which could become a bottleneck for long queries.
- **Extractor Quality**: The module's performance depends on the extractor LLM's (GPT-4.1-mini) ability to condense relevant info without losing facts.
- **De-duplication's Cost/Benefit**: Forcing retrieval of lower-ranked documents may introduce noise, possibly explaining its increased turn count.

### Failure signatures
- **Contextualization failure**: Cache becomes irrelevant or contradictory, causing incoherent reasoning. Monitor cache growth and inspect content on failures.
- **De-duplication failure**: Average turns increase significantly without EM improvement (as observed), indicating the model is "spinning" on novel but unhelpful documents.
- **Hybrid failure**: De-duplication's noise could overwhelm contextualization's benefit, causing performance below pure contextualization.
- **API dependency/outage**: External LLM unavailability causes failure; a fallback to raw document mode is needed.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablation - Contextualization Only**: Run the pipeline with only contextualization enabled (no de-dup). Confirm the reported EM gain (~+5.6%) and turn reduction (~-10.5%) on the 500-question validation set.
2. **Ablation - De-duplication Only**: Run with only de-duplication enabled (no contextualization, so base LLM gets raw, novel documents). Expect a small EM gain (~+1.4%) but **increase** in average turns (~+4.4% vs baseline), validating the "information forgetting vs. insufficiency" hypothesis.
3. **Cache Behavior Analysis**: For successful Contextualization runs, log the size (token count) of the accumulated cache per turn. Plot cache size vs. turn number. Hypothesis: Cache grows sublinearly as the module learns to stop adding new info once the core context is captured. Linear growth would indicate a future scaling issue.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Does extracting and caching relevant information from retrieved documents improve multi-hop QA accuracy and reduce retrieval steps?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors explicitly ask: "Will a concise representation of relevant information help an LLM become more efficient and accurate in question-answering tasks?" and evaluate a contextualization module that uses GPT-4.1-mini to extract and cache key content.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper shows gains with GPT-4.1-mini, but it is unclear whether the improvement stems from conciseness, the external model's capability, or the persistent cache. The approach also adds cost and latency.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablating extraction granularity, comparing different extraction models (including the agent itself), and testing alternative cache mechanisms (e.g., summarization, sliding window) would isolate factors and assess feasibility without external LLMs.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Does filtering out previously retrieved documents (de-duplication) improve efficiency and accuracy in agentic RAG?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors ask: "Can preventing redundant document retrieval encourage greater contextual diversity, thereby improving efficiency and answer accuracy?" and implement a de-duplication module.
- **Why unresolved**: De-duplication increased turns and only marginally improved EM, contrary to efficiency goals. Observations suggest repeated queries stem from forgetting, not insufficiency. The root cause remains unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Detailed analysis of reasoning traces to understand why duplicate queries are generated, and experiments with adaptive de-duplication (e.g., based on semantic similarity) could identify conditions where de-duplication reduces turns while maintaining or improving accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can the benefits of contextualization be achieved without relying on an external LLM at test time?
- **Basis in paper**: The contextualization module depends on GPT-4.1-mini, but the paper does not investigate whether the agent itself or a lightweight model could perform extraction or if this could be integrated into RL training.
- **Why unresolved**: Using a state-of-the-art external LLM adds cost and latency. It is unknown if the agent can learn extraction during training or if a smaller model can match GPT-4.1-mini. The trade-off remains unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Training the Search-R1 agent with an extraction head or auxiliary loss to produce its own contextualized summaries, and substituting GPT-4.1-mini with a smaller model (e.g., T5-base) while measuring EM and turn counts, would determine feasibility.

## Limitations
- **External LLM reliance**: The contextualization module depends on GPT-4.1-mini, introducing latency, cost, and potential model bias.
- **De-duplication inefficiency**: De-duplication increased average retrieval turns without significant accuracy gains, indicating the approach may not address the core issue of information forgetting.
- **Evaluation scope**: Results are based on only 500 questions from two datasets; generalizability to other domains and larger-scale settings is uncertain.

## Confidence
- Contextualization improves EM (+5.6%) and reduces turns (−10.5%): Medium confidence.
- De-duplication increases turns (+4.4%) with small EM gain (+1.4%): Medium confidence.
- Hybrid gains dominated by contextualization: Medium confidence.
- Mechanistic explanation (forgetting vs. insufficiency): Low–Medium confidence.
