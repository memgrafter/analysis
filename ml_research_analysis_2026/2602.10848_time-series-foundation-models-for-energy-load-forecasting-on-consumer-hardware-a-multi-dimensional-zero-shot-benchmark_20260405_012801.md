# Time Series Foundation Models for Energy Load Forecasting on Consumer Hardware: A Multi-Dimensional Zero-Shot Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10848  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10848  
- **Reference count:** 4  
- **Primary result:** TSFMs achieve up to 47% lower MASE versus Seasonal Naive at long contexts (C=2048h) and remain stable with minimal context (24h), while Prophet catastrophically fails (MASE >74). Chronos-2 produces well-calibrated probabilistic intervals (95% coverage at 90% nominal), whereas Moirai-2 and Prophet are substantially overconfident (~70% coverage).

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive zero-shot benchmark of four time series foundation models (TSFMs) for electricity demand forecasting on consumer hardware. Using ERCOT hourly load data across multiple test periods—including extreme events like Winter Storm Uri and the COVID-19 pandemic—the study evaluates TSFMs against classical baselines (Prophet, SARIMA) and Seasonal Naive. The key finding is that TSFMs maintain stable accuracy even with very short context windows (as little as 24 hours), whereas Prophet requires at least one full seasonal cycle to avoid degenerate parameter estimation. Moreover, probabilistic calibration varies significantly: Chronos-2 yields well-calibrated prediction intervals, while Moirai-2 and Prophet suffer from overconfidence. The results highlight the practical robustness and amortized inference cost of TSFMs for energy forecasting without task-specific training.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses ERCOT hourly system-wide electricity demand from 2020–2024, obtained via the EIA API. Three test periods are defined: Summer 2023, Winter 2022–2023, and the COVID-19 peak (Mar–Apr 2020). For each period, rolling forecast origins are generated with context lengths \(C \in \{24,48,96,168,336,512,1024,2048\}\) hours and horizons \(H \in \{24,168\}\) hours. Four TSFMs (Chronos-Bolt-small, Chronos-2, Moirai-2-small, TTM-r2) run in zero-shot mode on a CPU-only machine (AMD Ryzen 7 8845HS, 16GB RAM). Prophet and SARIMA are refitted on every context window, and Seasonal Naive serves as a reference. Performance is assessed via point accuracy (MASE, scaled by 168‑hour seasonal naive) and probabilistic metrics (CRPS, empirical coverage at nominal 90%, Winkler Score). Inference time is also recorded. The benchmark harness loops over all combinations, aggregates results across seven rolling windows per period, and produces per‑model comparisons.

## Key Results
- **Context stability:** TSFMs maintain low, stable MASE (~0.31–0.55) across all context lengths, while Prophet collapses with short context (MASE >74 at 24h) and only recovers after observing a full weekly cycle (C ≥ 336h).
- **Calibration:** Chronos-2 achieves 95% empirical coverage at 90% nominal, indicating well‑calibrated uncertainty. Moirai-2 and Prophet are overconfident, with coverage around 70%, risking under‑preparedness in grid operations.
- **Robustness:** TSFMs perform consistently across diverse test periods, including during extreme events (Winter Storm Uri, COVID‑19), whereas baselines show larger performance swings, suggesting better generalization from pre‑training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) maintain stable accuracy with minimal context because they recognize temporal patterns (e.g., daily, weekly cycles) learned during pre‑training, rather than estimating them from scratch.
- **Mechanism:** Pre‑training on large, diverse time series corpora imbues TSFMs with internal representations of common periodicities. When presented with a short context window, the model matches the observed pattern to its pre‑trained knowledge and extrapolates accordingly, bypassing the need for sufficient data to estimate seasonal parameters.
- **Core assumption:** The target time series (ERCOT load) exhibits seasonal structures (daily, weekly) that are statistically similar to patterns in the pre‑training corpora of models like Chronos and Moirai.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “TSFMs maintain stable accuracy even with minimal context because they recognise temporal patterns learned during pre‑training rather than estimating them from scratch.”  
  - Section 5.1: “Foundation models, by contrast, use the context window not for parameter estimation but for pattern recognition. During pre‑training, these models have processed millions of time series exhibiting daily and weekly seasonality.”  
  - Corpus: Neighboring papers discuss zero‑shot capabilities but do not directly evidence the pattern‑recognition vs. estimation distinction; evidence is weak.
- **Break condition:** If the target series contains novel, unseen temporal structures not represented in the pre‑training data (e.g., a new type of cycle), the model’s pattern recognition may fail, requiring longer context or fine‑tuning.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Prophet catastrophically fails with context shorter than the seasonality period because it must estimate Fourier coefficients for seasonality from insufficient data, leading to degenerate parameter fits.
- **Mechanism:** Prophet decomposes a series using Fourier series for daily and weekly seasonality. Estimating these coefficients requires observing multiple full cycles; with only 24 hours of context (less than one weekly cycle), the Fourier parameters become underdetermined, causing numerically unstable forecasts.
- **Core assumption:** Prophet’s default configuration (e.g., number of Fourier harmonics, changepoints) assumes a minimum context length proportional to the seasonal period.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “Prophet fails when the fitting window is shorter than its seasonality period (MASE > 74 at 24‑hour context).”  
  - Section 4.1: “With 24 hours of data—one‑seventh of a weekly cycle—the model cannot observe a single complete weekly period, making the Fourier coefficient estimation degenerate.”  
  - Corpus: No direct evidence; neighboring papers focus on TSFMs, not Prophet’s failure modes.
- **Break condition:** When the context window spans at least one full seasonal cycle (e.g., ≥168 hours for weekly seasonality), Prophet can estimate stable Fourier coefficients and recover accuracy (as shown by MASE dropping below 1.0 at C≥336h).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Calibration differences across probabilistic models stem from training objectives and post‑hoc sample generation procedures that affect tail representation in predictive distributions.
- **Mechanism:** Models like Moirai‑2 are trained with quantile losses and generate samples by inverse CDF interpolation restricted to a central probability range (e.g., [0.05, 0.95]), systematically excluding tail quantiles and producing overconfident intervals. Chronos‑2’s architecture and training yield better‑calibrated tails without such restrictions.
- **Core assumption:** The predictive distribution’s tail behavior is determined by both the loss function during training and any post‑processing applied when converting quantile forecasts to samples.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “Calibration varies substantially across models—Chronos‑2 produces well‑calibrated prediction intervals (95% empirical coverage at 90% nominal) while both Moirai‑2 and Prophet exhibit overconfidence (~70% coverage).”  
  - Section 4.2: “Moirai‑2’s overconfidence likely stems from a combination of its quantile loss training objective… and the post‑hoc sample generation procedure that restricts sampling to the [0.05, 0.95] probability range.”  
  - Corpus: Neighboring papers on TSFMs do not discuss calibration mechanisms in this depth; evidence is limited to the paper’s own analysis.
- **Break condition:** If Moirai‑2’s sample generation were modified to include full tail quantiles (e.g., by not restricting the range) or if post‑hoc recalibration (e.g., conformal prediction) were applied, the overconfidence could be mitigated.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Probabilistic forecasting and calibration (empirical coverage vs. nominal level).  
  *Why needed here:* The paper’s key findings depend on understanding why miscalibrated intervals (e.g., 70% coverage for a 90% nominal interval) are operationally risky for grid reserve planning. Without this, the significance of Chronos‑2’s 95% coverage cannot be appreciated.  
  *Quick check:* If a model’s 90% prediction intervals contain only 70% of observed values, what does that imply about its uncertainty estimates?

- **Concept:** Zero‑shot learning in foundation models.  
  *Why needed here:* The core comparison is between zero‑shot TSFMs (no task‑specific fitting) and fitted models (Prophet, SARIMA). Understanding zero‑shot—amortizing pre‑training cost across tasks—explains why TSFMs work with minimal context.  
  *Quick check:* How does a zero‑shot TSFM differ from Prophet in terms of what it does with the provided context window?

- **Concept:** Evaluation metrics for time series (MASE, CRPS).  
  *Why needed here:* The results are reported in MASE (relative to Seasonal Naive) and CRPS (for full distributions). These metrics quantify accuracy and distributional quality; misinterpretation would lead to incorrect conclusions about model performance.  
  *Quick check:* What does a MASE value of 0.31 indicate about a model’s forecast error relative to simply repeating last week’s load?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
ERCOT hourly load (Data layer) -> Model layer (TSFMs zero‑shot inference or baselines fitted) -> Evaluation layer (compute MASE, CRPS, coverage, Winkler Score) -> Benchmark harness (loop over contexts, horizons, rolling windows) -> Results aggregation.

### Critical path
1. Fetch and preprocess ERCOT data (fill missing values? The paper uses linear interpolation for robustness tests but main evaluation likely assumes complete data from EIA).  
2. For each context length C and horizon H, generate rolling forecast origins within each test period.  
3. For each model: if TSFM, run zero‑shot inference on the context window; if Prophet/SARIMA, fit on context and forecast.  
4. Collect forecasts and compute point errors (for MASE) and distributional metrics (CRPS, coverage, Winkler) against actuals.  
5. Repeat for all rolling windows (7 per period) and aggregate means/variances.  
6. Validate: Compare Chronos‑Bolt at C=512, H=24 to Table 1 (MASE ~0.315) and Table 2 (coverage ~86%).

### Design tradeoffs
- Hardware vs. coverage: CPU‑only evaluation excluded GPU‑required models (TimesFM, Lag‑Llama), favoring accessibility but potentially missing higher‑accuracy large models.
- Model variants: Used small variants (Chronos‑Bolt‑small, Moirai‑2‑small) to fit consumer RAM; larger variants might improve accuracy but increase memory/time.
- Univariate vs. multivariate: Excluded weather/calendar features for a clean benchmark, but operational forecasts typically use such covariates; this may understate fitted models’ potential.
- Zero‑shot only: Did not fine‑tune TSFMs on ERCOT data, preserving the zero‑shot premise but possibly missing domain adaptation gains (especially for TTM).

### Failure signatures
- Prophet MASE > 10 at short contexts (C≤48h) indicates expected failure due to insufficient seasonal cycles.
- TTM coverage = 100% with extremely wide intervals signals vacuous uncertainty (synthetic noise approximation).
- SARIMA inference time > 10s at C=2048h indicates computational scaling issue on consumer hardware.
- Moirai‑2 coverage ~70% at 90% nominal reveals overconfidence requiring recalibration before operational use.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Single‑window sanity check:** Run Chronos‑Bolt on C=512, H=24 for one Summer 2023 window; verify MASE ≈0.33 and CRPS ≈1000 MW. Confirms model loading and metric pipeline.
2. **Context sensitivity smoke test:** For Prophet and Chronos‑Bolt, run C=24, 168, 512 on the same window; expect Prophet’s MASE to drop dramatically from >10 to ~0.5, while Chronos‑Bolt remains stable (~0.5→0.33). Validates the core mechanism contrast.
3. **Calibration check:** For Chronos‑2 and Moirai‑2, generate 1000 probabilistic forecasts on a held‑out set and compute empirical coverage at 90% nominal; expect Chronos‑2 ~95% and Moirai‑2 ~70%. Ensures distributional output handling is correct.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What is the minimum context length required for TSFMs to match or exceed the accuracy of fitted baselines (e.g., Prophet) in energy load forecasting?**  
  *Basis:* Posed as RQ1 in Section 1.  
  *Why unresolved:* The benchmark evaluated context lengths 24–2048 hours on ERCOT data, showing TSFMs maintain stability at short contexts while Prophet fails. However, the optimal context may vary with grid characteristics (e.g., renewable penetration, climate), and the study lacks multi‑grid validation.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Comparative experiments across diverse energy systems (e.g., Nordic heating grids, solar‑dominated regions) to determine if context requirements generalize beyond ERCOT’s patterns.

- **Are probabilistic forecasts from TSFMs reliably calibrated for risk‑sensitive energy operations, and what causes miscalibration in models like Moirai‑2 and Prophet?**  
  *Basis:* Posed as RQ2 in Section 1.  
  *Why unresolved:* The study finds Chronos‑2 well‑calibrated (95% coverage at 90% nominal) but Moirai‑2 and Prophet overconfident (~70% coverage). Mechanisms are hypothesized (e.g., domain shift, post‑hoc sampling) but not rigorously tested, and calibration under extreme events or different contexts remains unclear.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled ablation studies isolating factors like training data distribution, quantile loss formulations, and sampling procedures, coupled with calibration tests on held‑out extreme‑event data from multiple grids.

- **How robust are TSFMs to distribution shifts beyond those tested (e.g., long‑term electrification trends, policy‑driven load changes), and why do models like Moirai‑2 show lower relative degradation?**  
  *Basis:* Posed as RQ3 in Section 1.  
  *Why unresolved:* Robustness was assessed only for COVID‑19 and Winter Storm Uri on ERCOT data. The paper speculates Moirai‑2’s diverse pre‑training corpus aids tail handling, but this is not causally established, and shifts like gradual renewable adoption or behavioral changes are unexamined.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Longitudinal evaluations spanning decades with synthetic or real shifts (e.g., simulated electrification scenarios), and architectural analysis linking pre‑training data diversity to out‑of‑distribution resilience.

- **Can TSFM probabilistic forecasts directly inform operational decisions (e.g., demand response, storage dispatch) with measurable cost savings, and how do they compare to domain‑specific heuristics?**  
  *Basis:* Posed as RQ4 in Section 1.  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper illustrates prescriptive use cases but uses synthetic price data for storage optimization and lacks comparisons to simpler heuristics (e.g., historical quantile thresholds for peak detection). Real‑world operational validation and economic impact quantification are absent.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Pilot deployments with grid operators, backtesting against actual demand response events and market prices, and rigorous cost‑benefit analyses versus traditional operational rules.

## Limitations
- **External validity:** Findings are based on a single dataset (ERCOT load). Performance may not generalize to other energy grids, regions, or frequency (e.g., 15‑minute data). The exclusion of covariates (weather, calendar) limits operational relevance.
- **Mechanism evidence:** The proposed “pattern recognition vs. estimation” mechanism for TSFMs and the specific sampling‑restriction cause for Moirai‑2’s overconfidence rely primarily on the paper’s internal analysis; corroborating evidence from the broader corpus is weak (low FMR, zero citations).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Exact model versions (HuggingFace revisions) and Prophet/SARIMA hyperparameters (e.g., Fourier orders, changepoint prior scale) are not fully specified, creating risk of configuration drift.

## Confidence
- **TSFM Stability vs. Context Length:** Medium‑High (strong empirical results, but the inferred “pattern recognition” mechanism is plausible yet not directly proven).  
- **Prophet Failure with Short Contexts:** High (directly observed, aligns with known Prophet requirements for seasonal cycle observation).  
- **Calibration Differences (Chronos‑2 vs. Moirai‑2):** Medium (paper provides specific technical rationale, but this analysis is not independently validated in the corpus).
