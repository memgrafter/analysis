# SOMtime the World Ain$'$t Fair: Violating Fairness Using Self-Organizing Maps

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18201
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18201
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Unsupervised Self-Organizing Maps can encode sensitive ordinal attributes (age, income) as a dominant geometric axis with Spearman correlations up to 0.85, far exceeding common dimensionality reduction techniques.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals that purely unsupervised representation learning can violate fairness-through-unawareness by embedding sensitive attributes like age and income as dominant latent structure. The authors introduce SOMtime, a high-capacity Self-Organizing Map that produces topology-preserving embeddings. Experiments on World Values Survey and Census-Income datasets show that SOMtime recovers monotonic orderings aligned with withheld sensitive attributes, achieving correlations up to 0.85, while PCA, UMAP, t-SNE, and autoencoders reach at most 0.34. Furthermore, clustering these embeddings yields demographically skewed clusters. The work demonstrates that sensitive information can emerge geometrically in unsupervised settings, mandating fairness audits that extend to representation components themselves.

## Method Summary
SOMtime trains a high-capacity 2D Self-Organizing Map (SOM) with lattice size \( K = 5 \cdot N^{0.54} \) on tabular data without sensitive attributes. Each observation is mapped to its Best Matching Unit (BMU); the 3D embedding uses the BMU's (x,y) grid coordinates and the quantization error \( z = \|x - w_{BMU}\| \) as the third coordinate. A post-processing trajectory algorithm (Algorithm 1) constructs a graph over SOM centroids (with z scaled by K) to extract a 1D path of monotonically changing activation, which is then correlated with the withheld sensitive attribute. Performance is audited by the maximum absolute Spearman/Pearson correlation across embedding axes and the accuracy of recovered monotonic group orderings, and compared against PCA, UMAP, t-SNE, and autoencoders of varying capacities.

## Key Results
- SOMtime achieves Spearman correlations up to 0.85 with age/income on WVS and Census datasets; all baselines (PCA, UMAP, t-SNE, autoencoders) reach at most 0.34.
- The trajectory recovery algorithm perfectly (accuracy 1.0) orders age groups on the WVS Canada subset, visually forming a clear ramp.
- Unsupervised clustering of SOMtime's embeddings produces clusters with significant demographic skew, evidencing latent sensitive-attribute encoding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SOMtime's competitive learning on a high-capacity fixed lattice forces globally ordered representations aligned with ordinal sensitive attributes.
- **Mechanism:** Each observation maps to a Best Matching Unit (BMU); the neighborhood function updates adjacent prototypes. With high lattice capacity (\( K = 5 \cdot N^{0.54} \)), many weak, individually noisy features correlated with the sensitive attribute collectively form a smooth gradient across neighboring neurons, manifesting as a monotonic axis in the 3D embedding.
- **Core assumption:** The data contains many weakly correlated but locally consistent features that jointly relate to the sensitive attribute.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports up to 0.85 Spearman; Section 3.6 describes distributed signal amplification; Appendix B shows capacity dependence.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The SOM's absence of variance-based filtering preserves low-variance sensitive-signal directions that PCA discards.
- **Mechanism:** PCA prioritizes components with highest global variance, potentially discarding subtle but consistent signals. SOMs treat all dimensions equally in distance calculations; no components are dropped based on marginal variance, allowing low-variance but locally consistent sensitive-attribute signals to survive and organize.
- **Core assumption:** The sensitive attribute signal has lower global variance than other factors.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.6 contrasts PCA's limitations; Section 4.3 shows PCA correlations typically below 0.23 versus SOMtime's 0.85.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The fixed topological structure of the SOM lattice converts distributed latent information into a geometric axis, unlike autoencoders that encode diffusely.
- **Mechanism:** Autoencoders minimize reconstruction without constraining latent geometry; sensitive information may be scattered across dimensions. The SOM's competitive learning and fixed grid impose a spatial organization: similar inputs activate neighboring units, forcing any consistent signal to become a smooth spatial gradient.
- **Core assumption:** The sensitive attribute correlates with nonlinear, locally-consistent data manifolds.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.6 contrasts autoencoder's unconstrained latent space; Section 5.2 and Appendix B show autoencoders fail to improve leakage with increased capacity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Topology Preservation**
  - *Why needed:* SOMtime's core innovation is mapping input similarities to spatial proximity on a fixed lattice; this forces any globally consistent signal to appear as a geometric gradient. Without it (e.g., PCA), global order may be lost.
  - *Quick check:* Why might a method preserving local neighborhoods (t-SNE) still fail to reveal a global monotonic axis for a sensitive attribute?
- **Concept: Competitive Learning**
  - *Why needed:* The winner-takes-most update (BMU and neighbors adapt) creates localized, smooth specialization on the lattice, amplifying weak, distributed signals into coherent spatial patterns.
  - *Quick check:* How would the representation differ if every data point updated all weights (e.g., linear autoencoder) instead of just the BMU's neighborhood?
- **Concept: Quantization Error as a Coordinate**
  - *Why needed:* The `z`-coordinate (distance to BMU) is explicitly used in the 3D embedding and trajectory algorithm, helping distinguish dense vs. sparse regions often correlated with sensitive distributions.
  - *Quick check:* What might uniformly low quantization error across a sensitive-attribute gradient imply about data density?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data -> SOM training (withhold sensitive attributes) -> BMU mapping (x,y) + quantization error (z) -> 3D embedding -> Trajectory extraction (Algorithm 1) -> Correlation/ordering audit.
- **Critical path:** The audit is strictly post-hoc: sensitive attributes are never used during SOM training or embedding construction. Leakage is discovered only by correlating the final geometric coordinates with the withheld attribute.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *High lattice capacity* vs. *computational/memory cost*: Larger `K` reveals finer structure but increases `O(K²)` storage and training time.
  - *Fixed lattice topology* vs. *flexibility*: The grid forces a discrete structure that amplifies gradients but may poorly fit highly irregular manifolds.
  - *Post-hoc audit* vs. *in-training mitigation*: Current design is diagnostic; fairness would require modifying the SOM objective, which is future work.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Max Spearman correlation across all axes < 0.3: sensitive attribute not encoded as dominant structure.
  - Trajectory algorithm fails to connect multiple centroids or produces non-monotonic path: no global ordering exists.
  - High reconstruction error from prototypes: underfitting due to insufficient lattice capacity (`K` too small).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate SOMtime on WVS Canada subset; expect max |Spearman| ~0.85.
  2. Compare against capacity-matched PCA/UMAP/t-SNE/AE; SOMtime correlation should exceed baselines by >3×.
  3. Run Algorithm 1 on SOMtime's 3D centroids; trajectory accuracy should be 1.0, and age groups should form a clear ramp in visualization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What auditing metrics are appropriate for categorical sensitive attributes (e.g., race, gender) in unsupervised representations? The paper's monotonic correlation metric only applies to ordinal attributes.
- **Open Question 2:** Does the emergence of sensitive-attribute structure in topology-preserving embeddings generalize to non-tabular data (images, text, graphs)? Experiments were restricted to tabular surveys and census.
- **Open Question 3:** Does SOMtime preferentially amplify sensitive-attribute information beyond proportional preservation of overall data structure? The study only measures sensitive-attribute leakage, not how well non-sensitive attributes are preserved.
- **Open Question 4:** How can we design effective fairness regularizations for Self-Organizing Maps that mitigate sensitive-attribute encoding without severely harming representation utility? No concrete methods exist for imposing fairness constraints during SOM training.

## Limitations
- **Implementation ambiguity:** Exact SOM training epochs and convergence criterion are unspecified; autoencoder architectures are inferred only from parameter counts.
- **Algorithm fragility:** The trajectory algorithm's scaling (multiplying z by K) is ad hoc and may be sensitive to this choice.
- **Dataset scope:** Evaluation uses only WVS (5 countries) and Census-Income; no evidence of generalizability to other domains or sensitive attributes (e.g., race).
- **Mechanistic evidence:** Proposed mechanisms (distributed amplification, variance filtering, fixed topology) lack direct experimental validation via ablation or counterfactuals.
- **Baseline capacity matching:** Autoencoder capacities are claimed but not verified; potential mismatch could invalidate performance comparisons.

## Confidence
- **Performance superiority (SOMtime vs. baselines):** Medium. Strong empirical results but dependent on unreported hyperparameters and limited datasets.
- **Mechanistic hypotheses (why SOMtime leaks):** Low. Mechanisms are plausible interpretations without direct experimental isolation or causal validation.
- **Clustering bias manifestation:** Medium. Skewed clusters are reported but statistical robustness and effect size are not characterized.

## Next Checks
1. **Capacity ablation:** Train SOMtime with systematically varied lattice size \( K \) (e.g., \( K = 3 \cdot N^{0.54}, 5 \cdot N^{0.54}, 7 \cdot N^{0.54} \)) to verify that high capacity amplifies weak signals.
2. **Synthetic control:** Generate synthetic data with identical feature distributions but independent sensitive attribute; confirm SOMtime embedding shows near-zero correlation (|ρ| < 0.05), ruling out artifact.
3. **Trajectory scaling sensitivity:** Re-run Algorithm 1 with alternative scaling factors for z (e.g., z, z·√K, z·K²) and report ordering accuracy stability; if accuracy collapses, the current scaling is critical and needs justification.
