# SQuTR: A Robustness Benchmark for Spoken Query to Text Retrieval under Acoustic Noise

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12783
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12783
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SQuTR shows consistent performance degradation with noise; even large models (e.g., Qwen3-Embedding-8B) drop significantly (nDCG@10 from 0.8033 on text to 0.7302 at 0 dB), and end-to-end systems lag behind cascaded ones.

## Executive Summary
SQuTR is a novel benchmark designed to evaluate the robustness of spoken query to text retrieval systems under realistic acoustic noise. It addresses a critical gap in existing evaluation by providing a large-scale, systematically degraded dataset. The benchmark consists of 37,317 spoken queries derived from six prominent text retrieval datasets, synthesized from 200 distinct speaker voices using CosyVoice-3. Environmental noise from 17 categories (DEMAND and NOISEX-92) is injected at controlled signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) levels ranging from clean to 0 dB, yielding 149,268 evaluation instances. SQuTR supports both cascaded (ASR followed by text-based retriever) and end-to-end (direct audio-to-embedding) evaluation paradigms.

Results across a diverse set of models reveal a consistent decline in retrieval performance as acoustic noise increases. Even state-of-the-art dense retrieval models, such as Qwen3-Embedding-8B, exhibit a substantial relative drop in nDCG@10 (from 0.803 on clean text to 0.730 at 0 dB SNR). End-to-end spoken retrieval systems, represented by Omni-Embed-Nemotron-3B, consistently underperform the best cascaded configurations. The findings underscore that noise robustness remains a major bottleneck for real-world deployment of voice-based search interfaces.

## Method Summary
The SQuTR dataset is built from text queries in six IR benchmarks (FiQA, HotpotQA, NQ, MedicalRetrieval, DuRetrieval, T2Retrieval) covering English and Chinese. Each query is converted to a clean audio utterance using CosyVoice-3 with 200 different speaker profiles; candidates are generated and filtered to ensure low word error rate (WER ≤ 0.30) and high linguistic quality (LLM score ≥ 4). Real-world environmental noise from DEMAND and NOISEX-92 (17 categories) is then mixed into the speech at four SNR levels: Clean, 20 dB, 10 dB, and 0 dB. The mixing uses an RMS-based approach (Eq. 1) to achieve precise SNR control, resulting in 149,268 audio-query pairs.

For evaluation, cascaded systems employ an ASR front-end (Whisper-Large-v3 for English, Paraformer-Large for Chinese, and others) to transcribe the noisy audio; the transcript is fed to a retrieval backend. Retrieval models include the lexical BM25 and dense models like BGE series, Qwen3-Embedding variants, with language-specific query prefixes. End-to-end evaluation uses Omni-Embed-Nemotron-3B, which directly embeds the audio. Performance is measured using nDCG@10, Recall@10, and MRR@10 against the original relevance judgments, allowing analysis of both accuracy and stability (variance across noise conditions).

## Key Results
- All models exhibit systematic performance degradation as SNR decreases; even the most robust models (Qwen3-Embedding-8B) show a ~9% relative drop in nDCG@10 (from 0.8033 on clean text to 0.7302 at 0 dB).
- End-to-end spoken retrieval (Omni-Embed-Nemotron-3B) consistently underperforms cascaded systems across all noise levels, indicating a gap in direct audio-to-text retrieval robustness.
- Model scale and architecture matter: larger dense retrievers demonstrate higher absolute performance and lower variance across noisy conditions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Noise degrades retrieval via ASR error propagation
- **Claim:** Acoustic noise degrades retrieval performance primarily by introducing ASR transcription errors, which then propagate to the retrieval stage.
- **Mechanism:** 噪声污染语音信号 → ASR模型输出错误转录 → 文本查询失真 → 检索模型匹配文档能力下降。
- **Core assumption:** The dominant failure mode in spoken query retrieval under noise is error propagation from the ASR front-end to the text-based retriever.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...retrieval quality is inherently affected by transcription errors." and "Experimental results show that retrieval performance decreases as noise increases."
  - [section 4.2]: "We observe a consistent decline in retrieval effectiveness as acoustic noise increases... Even large-scale retrieval models struggle under extreme noise."
  - [corpus]: Weak direct corpus evidence; neighboring papers focus on hallucinations, not ASR-error propagation. The mechanism is inferred from the paper's experimental results and stated motivation.
- **Break condition:** If an end-to-end system shows significantly better robustness than cascaded systems at high noise, it would challenge the assumption. The paper's results (Table 5) show end-to-end lagging, supporting the assumption.

### Mechanism 2: Controlled SNR synthesis creates a reproducible testbed
- **Claim:** The benchmark's controlled, multi‑SNR noise synthesis creates a reproducible testbed that systematically isolates acoustic noise as the variable of interest.
- **Mechanism:** 干净语音 + 标准化的文本查询 + 17类真实环境噪声 + 精确SNR控制 = 可重复的、梯度化的声学扰动数据集。
- **Core assumption:** Additive noise with controlled SNR from real‑world environments realistically simulates the acoustic variability encountered in deployed voice interfaces.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...mix 17 categories of real‑world environmental noise under controlled SNR levels, enabling reproducible robustness evaluation from quiet to highly noisy conditions."
  - [section 3.3 & A]: Detailed mathematical formulation of the SNR‑based mixing process (Eq. 1–5) using DEMAND/NOISEX-92 databases.
  - [corpus]: Neighboring papers ("Salmon", "RARE") also focus on robustness evaluation but under different contexts; SQuTR's unique value is the combination of retrieval task + controlled acoustic SNR.
- **Break condition:** If real‑world deployment noise characteristics (e.g., non‑additive, reverberant, non‑stationary) differ substantially from the DEMAND/NOISEX-92 corpus used, the benchmark's ecological validity would break. The paper assumes these databases are sufficiently representative.

### Mechanism 3: Model architecture and scale determine robustness
- **Claim:** Model architecture and scale are primary determinants of robustness, with larger, more integrated models (e.g., Qwen3‑8B) showing greater stability (lower variance) across noise conditions.
- **Mechanism:** 模型容量 + 训练目标 (e.g. dense retrieval vs. lexical) + 模态融合方式 (end‑to‑end audio→embedding) → 对转录错误或声学扰动的敏感度差异。
- **Core assumption:** Retrieval models that learn more robust, semantic representations (often via larger scale) are less sensitive to surface‑form errors caused by noise.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3]: Figure 3 and analysis showing "model scaling and direct modality modeling improve robustness... Within the Qwen3 series, increasing model size... reduces variance."
  - [section 4.4]: "the choice of retrieval backend proves more critical than ASR size... lexical and dense retrieval exhibit highly consistent degradation trends."
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "GOAT‑SLM" and "Omni‑Embed‑Nemotron" explore audio‑to‑embedding models, indirectly supporting that architecture choice matters. However, SQuTR's comparative results on retrieval backends are primary evidence.
- **Break condition:** If a simple lexical retriever (BM25) paired with a perfect ASR (0% WER) matched a dense retriever's performance under all noise levels, it would challenge the claim. The persistent gap even with strong ASR (Table 7) supports the mechanism.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Source Text Queries (FiQA, HotpotQA, NQ, MedicalRetrieval, DuRetrieval, T2Retrieval) → Speech Synthesis (CosyVoice‑3, 200 speakers) → Acoustic Degradation (DEMAND/NOISEX‑92 noise, SNR mixing) → [ASR Front‑end: Whisper/Paraformer/Qwen3‑ASR] → Retrieval Back‑end (BM25, BGE, Qwen3‑Embedding) → Evaluation (nDCG@10, MRR@10).  
End‑to‑end path: Audio → Omni‑Embed‑Nemotron‑3B → Embedding → same evaluation.

**Critical path:**  
(Text Query) → Synthesis → [Noise Injection] → [ASR (optional for cascaded)] → Retrieval Model → Ranked List → Metrics. Noise injection is the independent variable; metrics are the dependent variables.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Synthetic vs. Real Speech:* Synthesis enables control and scale but may lack spontaneous speech traits.  
- *Controlled SNR vs. Real Environments:* SNR control isolates noise intensity but may miss complex real‑world acoustic interactions (reverberation, overlapping speakers).  
- *Cascaded vs. End‑to‑End Evaluation:* Cascaded mirrors current deployments but conflates ASR & retrieval errors; end‑to‑end tests direct robustness but has fewer comparable systems.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *ASR‑level:* Sharp WER/CER increase at 0 dB SNR (Table 4).  
- *Retrieval‑level:* Consistent nDCG drop from Clean→High Noise for all models (Table 5). Larger variance (σ) for smaller dense models (Figure 3).  
- *System‑level:* End‑to‑end (Omni‑Embed) significantly underperforms the best cascaded systems (Qwen3‑8B) across all noisy conditions.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Sanity Check:** Run the full SQuTR evaluation with a single cascaded system (e.g., Whisper‑Large‑v3 + BM25) on just the "Clean" and "High Noise (0 dB)" conditions. Verify the expected ~15–20% relative nDCG drop reported in Table 5.  
2. **ASR Impact Isolation:** For a fixed retriever (e.g., Qwen3‑Embedding‑8B), evaluate performance using 3 different ASR outputs (e.g., Whisper‑Large‑v3, SenseVoice‑Small, Qwen3‑ASR‑1.7B) under all 4 noise conditions. Confirm that the relative ranking of ASR models (by WER) does not perfectly predict retrieval performance drops (cf. Table 16–21), demonstrating the "retrieval backend > ASR size" finding.  
3. **Stability‑Accuracy Trade‑off:** For a set of dense retrievers (e.g., BGE‑Small, BGE‑Large, Qwen3‑0.6B, Qwen3‑8B), compute the mean nDCG@10 across all 2 languages × 4 noise conditions and the standard deviation. Plot as in Figure 3. Verify that larger models move toward the lower‑right (higher mean, lower σ).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do end‑to‑end spoken retrieval systems underperform cascaded ones under high noise?  
2. Can retrieval models be made robust to ASR errors, closing the cascade gap?  
3. How does query complexity (multi‑hop vs. factoid) interact with noise to affect retrieval?  
4. Do different noise categories yield systematically different performance drops?

## Limitations
- Synthetic speech (CosyVoice‑3) may lack characteristics of real spontaneous speech, potentially affecting ASR and retrieval behavior.  
- The noise corpus (DEMAND/NOISEX‑92) may not fully capture complex real‑world acoustics such as reverberation and overlapping sources.  
- Only one end‑to‑end system was evaluated, limiting the generalizability of the conclusion that end‑to‑end approaches underperform cascaded ones.
