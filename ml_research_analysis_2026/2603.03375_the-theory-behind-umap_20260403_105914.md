# The Theory behind UMAP?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03375  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03375  
- **Reference count:** 16  
- **Primary result:** The paper corrects foundational errors in UMAP's theoretical basis, specifically in Spivak's metric realization and McInnes et al.'s finite variant, providing a rigorous framework for topological preservation claims.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses critical mathematical flaws in the theoretical foundation of UMAP, a popular dimensionality reduction algorithm. The authors identify errors in Spivak's metric realization (including undefined log(0), division by zero, and failure to prove non‑expansiveness) and in McInnes et al.'s finite metric realization. By redefining metric simplices with fixed underlying sets and ℓ₁ metrics, establishing an equivalence between classical and sheaf‑theoretic fuzzy sets, and constructing finite variants via left Kan extensions with precise finiteness conditions, they provide a corrected categorical framework. Although this repairs the theoretical justification for UMAP's topological preservation claims, empirical validation of the algorithm's performance with these corrections remains an open question.

## Method Summary
The paper employs categorical constructions from algebraic topology, focusing on simplicial fuzzy sets and Kan extensions. It analyzes Spivak's metric realization functor and identifies three technical errors: (1) log(0) is undefined because parameters include 0, (2) division by zero in scaling factor log(b)/log(a) when a=1, and (3) missing proof that degeneracy maps are non‑expansive. The repair redefines metric simplices using a common underlying set and scales the ℓ₁ metric. Additionally, the authors establish an equivalence between classical valued sets and sheaf‑theoretic valued sets, enabling flexible formulations. The finite metric realization of McInnes et al. is constructed as a left Kan extension along the Yoneda embedding, with explicit interpretation of "finite" (finite underlying sets) and "bounded" (sheaves with finite support). The work is purely theoretical; no reproduction notes or empirical validation are provided.

## Key Results
- Corrects mathematical errors in the metric realization underlying UMAP, providing a well‑defined functorial framework with ℓ₁ metrics and non‑expansive degeneracies.
- Establishes equivalence between classical and sheaf‑theoretic fuzzy sets and constructs both infinite and finite metric realizations via left Kan extensions.
- Offers a rigorous foundation for UMAP's topological preservation claims, though empirical validation of the algorithm's performance under the corrected theory remains open.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Spivak's metric realization contains mathematical errors that invalidate UMAP's theoretical claims about topological preservation.  
- **Mechanism:** The paper identifies three specific technical errors: (1) log(0) undefined and missing empty set in topology, (2) division by zero in scaling factor log(b)/log(a), (3) failure to prove non‑expansiveness of degeneracy maps. Each is repaired by redefining metric simplices with fixed underlying sets and ℓ₁ metrics.  
- **Core assumption:** A dimensionality reduction algorithm claiming topological preservation must be grounded in mathematically sound categorical constructions.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]  
  - [Section 1.1, Item 3] "They involve logarithms of parameters a and b where 0 ≤ a ≤ b ≤ 1 but including 0 breaks this definition as log(0) is undefined... Independent of issue 3(a), the scaling factor log(b)/log(a) may result in division by 0, because a can take the value 1. We repair this by defining all metric simplices with the same underlying set and instead scaling the metric by a factor in Definition 4.2.1."  
  - [corpus] Limited direct corpus evidence; related work like "ActUp: Analyzing and Consolidating tSNE and UMAP" (fmr 0.525) discusses UMAP parameters but does not address these foundational errors.  
- **Break condition:** If these corrections were not implemented, the metric realization functor would not be well‑defined, making any topological preservation claims mathematically meaningless.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Core theoretical layers: (1) Valued sets framework (CFuz/Fuz equivalence via M/C functors), (2) Uncurried simplicial valued sets (USFuzz with fixed underlying sets), (3) EPMet structure (coproducts, quotients), (4) Kan extension machinery (Yoneda, colimits), (5) Finite variant interpretation.  
  Key components needing implementation: functors M, C, SM, SC; metric realization MetRe (Definition 4.2.6); finite metric realization Fin‑MetRe‑log (Proposition 4.5.9).

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Establish categorical equivalence between classical and sheaf‑theoretic valued sets (Theorem 3.2.11).  
  2. Define metric simplices with ℓ₁ metric ensuring non‑expansiveness (Definition 4.2.1).  
  3. Construct T functor with face/degeneracy maps (Lemma 4.2.4).  
  4. Verify T properties for Kan extension existence (Lemma 4.2.5).  
  5. Define finite variants with precise finiteness/boundedness (Definitions 4.5.1, 4.5.3).  
  6. Prove finite colimit object has finitely many equivalence classes (Proposition 4.5.9).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Classical vs sheaf‑theoretic: Sheaf formulation simplifies categorical properties but obscures concrete computations; classical formulation is computationally clearer but requires translating injectivity conditions. The paper uses both: proofs in sheaf‑theoretic, computations in classical via equivalences.  
  - ℓ₁ metric choice: Only ℓ₁ makes degeneracies non‑expansive (Remark 4.2.2); other ℓ_p metrics fail.  
  - Fixed underlying sets: Avoids scaling factor division‑by‑zero; all metric simplices share underlying set Δ^n, scaling via metric factor alone.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Non‑well‑defined functors: maps not preserving morphism types, or restriction maps not injective in valued sets.  
  - Non‑expansiveness violation: any construction where degeneracy maps increase distances (test Example: σ^n_i on specific points in Δ^{n+1}).  
  - Infinite colimit in finite metric realization: quotient space M/∼ from Proposition 4.5.9 having infinitely many equivalence classes, indicating failure to exploit finiteness property (must show identification with finite U subset).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Validate ℓ₁ metric non‑expansiveness: For Δ^2, verify d₁(s₂(x)) ≤ d₂(x) for sample x, where s₂ is degeneracy.  
  2. Test equivalence M‑C: Construct small classical fuzzy set (X={a,b}, μ(a)=0.5, μ(b)=1), compute M(X) and C(M(X)), verify isomorphism.  
  3. Finite metric realization sanity check: For finite EP‑space with 3 points, compute Fin‑Sing‑log manually, verify membership strengths match expectations from Lipschitz maps.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does the corrected theoretical framework for UMAP (using ℓ1 metrics and proper sheaf‑theoretic foundations) lead to empirical performance that matches or improves upon the original algorithm's success?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Abstract: "These repairs yield a rigorous framework for UMAP's claims about topological preservation, though empirical validation of the algorithm's performance remains open."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper mathematically corrects the theory but does not provide any experimental validation that this corrected theory better explains or predicts UMAP's performance on real datasets. The claims about topological preservation remain untested.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic benchmark comparisons of UMAP implementations based on the corrected ℓ1 metric versus the original ℓ2‑like metric, measuring topological preservation metrics such as persistent homology, to determine whether the repaired theory yields measurable improvements or aligns with empirical results.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the binary union operation for classical fuzzy sets be consistently extended to simplicial classical fuzzy sets, thereby establishing a rigorous correspondence between the graph G constructed in UMAP and the 1‑skeleton of the union of finite singular nerves?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.4: "Further work is necessary to extend the union of classical fuzzy sets to simplicial classical fuzzy sets and verify the correspondence between G and ⋃ FinSing(X,di)."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper verifies the correspondence for 1‑skeletons (edges) but not for higher‑dimensional simplices, which are needed to interpret the full simplicial structure of the finite metric realization; the union operation for simplicial fuzzy sets remains undefined.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A definition of union for simplicial fuzzy sets together with a proof that the resulting 1‑skeleton equals the graph G from UMAP's construction, and that higher‑dimensional simplices correspond to higher‑order topological features.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the stochastic gradient descent optimization step in UMAP, when combined with the corrected metric realization, actually produce embeddings that preserve the topological features of the original dataset as claimed?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.4: "McInnes et al. claim that ... the iterative procedure from step4 produces lower‑dimensional output coordinates Y that match the topology of M as close as possible. Further work is necessary to verify this claim."  
- **Why unresolved:** The claim is central to UMAP's justification but lacks mathematical proof; the optimization objective (cross entropy) may not directly correlate with topological preservation metrics, and no analysis connects the loss minimization to topological invariants.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A mathematical proof showing that minimizing the cross‑entropy loss between graph weights in high and low dimensions guarantees preservation of specific topological features (e.g., via the nerve lemma or persistent homology) under appropriate conditions.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the functor m : Shv(I) → Fuz, which Spivak attempted to construct in his draft, actually exist? If so, what is its correct construction?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 1.1 (last paragraph): "When trying to prove (c), one should run into an issue: ... It then seems impossible to lift this element up to the desired level mS(a). This is, of course, no counterexample. However, it is a gap in the argument, and Spivak does not offer a proof in [9]."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies a gap in Spivak's proof regarding the sheaf condition for mS; it is unclear whether such a functor exists or if the attempted construction can be repaired.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Either a concrete counterexample proving that no such functor exists or an explicit construction of m with a rigorous proof that it preserves the sheaf condition and functoriality.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Do the edge weights in the UMAP graph G have a valid probabilistic interpretation, as claimed by McInnes et al., and if so, is this interpretation compatible with the corrected metric realization framework?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.4: "The article [5] does not provide a probability theoretic argument for this claim. ... We are thus not yet convinced that the weights of edges of the graph G should be thought of as probabilities. Further work is necessary to formally capture this claim and verify it."  
- **Why unresolved:** The claim that edge weights represent probabilities is central to UMAP's use of cross entropy and the probabilistic T‑conorm, but no probability‑theoretic justification is given; it is unclear whether the weights satisfy probability axioms or correspond to meaningful events.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A formal derivation of the edge weights as conditional probabilities of connectivity based on a statistical model of the data manifold, together with a proof that this interpretation aligns with the finite metric realization (e.g., that probabilities match membership strengths in fuzzy sets).

## Limitations
- The paper fixes theoretical errors but does not empirically confirm that the corrected constructions align with the actual UMAP algorithm's behavior on real datasets.
- The abstract categorical framework may hinder practical adoption; its impact on computational efficiency and result quality remains to be demonstrated.
- Assumptions like fixed underlying sets and ℓ₁ metrics could restrict applicability, especially regarding UMAP's heuristic parameters (`a`, `b`) and scalability.
