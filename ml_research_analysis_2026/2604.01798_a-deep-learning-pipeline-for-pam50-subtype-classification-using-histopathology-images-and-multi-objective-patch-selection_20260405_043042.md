# A deep learning pipeline for PAM50 subtype classification using histopathology images and multi-objective patch selection
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01798  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01798  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** On internal TCGA-BRCA (627 WSIs) the model achieved a macro F1-score of 0.8812 and an AUC of 0.9841; on external CPTAC-BRCA (122 WSIs) it achieved an F1-score of 0.7952 and an AUC of 0.9512.

## Executive Summary
Accurate molecular subtyping of breast cancer using the PAM50 assay is critical for treatment decisions but relies on expensive molecular profiling. This work introduces a deep learning pipeline that predicts PAM50 subtypes directly from H&E-stained whole-slide images (WSIs), thereby reducing dependence on molecular assays. The method employs a frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 to extract patch embeddings, estimates epistemic uncertainty per patch via Monte Carlo dropout, and applies a multi-objective NSGA-II algorithm to select a compact, diverse, and reliable subset of patches. A lightweight classification head trained on these embeddings aggregates patch-level probabilities to produce a slide-level prediction. Evaluated on TCGA-BRCA (627 WSIs) and externally on CPTAC-BRCA (122 WSIs), the pipeline achieves strong performance (F1 up to 0.8812, AUC up to 0.9841) while significantly lowering computational cost compared to existing approaches.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with WSI preprocessing: non-overlapping 512×512 patches are extracted, filtered for tissue content (M≥0.2) and blur (Laplacian variance≥100), and stain-normalized using Macenko’s method; patches are resized to 224×224. A frozen ResNet-18 (ImageNet-pretrained) yields 512-dimensional embeddings for each patch. To quantify epistemic uncertainty, a separate classification head (with dropout) runs T=20 stochastic forward passes per patch, and the mean class-wise variance of softmax outputs serves as the uncertainty score. Patch selection proceeds in two stages: first, patches with uncertainty above a threshold τ are discarded; second, NSGA-II optimizes four objectives on the remaining patches—maximizing diversity (cosine dissimilarity of embeddings), maximizing informativeness (L2 norm), minimizing patch count, and minimizing mean uncertainty—producing a Pareto front of candidate subsets. Typically the first solution meeting a target count (e.g., ~500 patches) is chosen. The final classifier consists of global average pooling, a fully connected layer (256 units), ReLU, batch normalization, dropout (0.5), and a 4-class softmax. It is trained from scratch on the selected patch embeddings using weighted cross-entropy loss (Adam, lr=1e-4, batch size 64, early stopping). Slide-level labels are obtained by averaging the softmax probabilities of the selected patches. The study uses a patient-level split of TCGA-BRCA (80% train, 20% validation) and evaluates on the independent CPTAC-BRCA cohort.

## Key Results
- **Internal validation (TCGA-BRCA):** Macro F1-score = 0.8812, AUC = 0.9841.  
- **External validation (CPTAC-BRCA):** Macro F1-score = 0.7952, AUC = 0.9512.  
- The multi-objective patch selection reduces computational load while maintaining or improving accuracy relative to prior MIL methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
#### Mechanism 1: Joint multi‑objective patch selection
- **Claim:** NSGA‑II evolves small, diverse, and reliable patch subsets, boosting classification accuracy while cutting computational cost.  
- **Mechanism:** After uncertainty filtering, NSGA‑II evolves binary selection vectors over patches, evaluating each subset on four objectives: diversity (cosine dissimilarity of ResNet embeddings), informativeness (L2 norm), compactness (negative size), and reliability (negative mean uncertainty). Non‑dominated sorting yields a Pareto front; a solution is selected from the front for training.  
- **Core assumption:** A carefully chosen small patch set can capture the slide’s discriminative morphology, and the four objectives adequately capture informativeness, representativeness, and reliability.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Sec. 3.6 describe the framework; corpus shows prior optimization‑based methods typically use a single reward, making this multi‑objective approach novel.  
- **Break condition:** If the Pareto front fails to converge or objectives conflict heavily, the selected subset may be suboptimal, harming accuracy.

#### Mechanism 2: Uncertainty‑guided filtering
- **Claim:** Monte Carlo dropout estimates epistemic uncertainty to filter out ambiguous patches and steer NSGA‑II toward reliable regions.  
- **Mechanism:** For each patch, the classification head (with dropout enabled) performs T=20 stochastic forward passes; the mean class‑wise variance of softmax outputs yields an uncertainty score. Patches above threshold τ are discarded; the remaining scores also inform the reliability objective.  
- **Core assumption:** High predictive variance indicates patches that are out‑of‑distribution, artifact‑affected, or histologically ambiguous, and their inclusion degrades slide‑level classification.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Sec. 3.5 (MC dropout procedure), Sec. 3.6.1 (filtering step); uncertainty quantification is highlighted as critical in medical AI but rarely applied to PAM50 histopathology.  
- **Break condition:** Poor MC dropout calibration (e.g., over‑confidence on OOD patches or high variance on clear tissue) leads to removal of informative patches or retention of noise.

#### Mechanism 3: Frozen ResNet‑18 backbone
- **Claim:** An ImageNet‑pretrained ResNet‑18 kept frozen provides robust, generalizable patch embeddings while minimizing trainable parameters to avoid overfitting.  
- **Mechanism:** The ResNet‑18 is used as a fixed feature extractor; only the lightweight classification head is trained on the selected patch embeddings.  
- **Core assumption:** Features learned on natural images transfer sufficiently to H&E histopathology, and freezing the backbone prevents overfitting given limited labeled patches and high dimensionality.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Sec. 3.4 and 3.7; this is a standard practice in histogramical MIL studies.  
- **Break condition:** If domain shift from ImageNet to histopathology is too large, frozen embeddings may lack discriminant power, capping performance.

## Foundational Learning
#### Multiple Instance Learning (MIL)
- **Why needed:** The WSI‑to‑subtype task is a MIL problem: a slide (bag) contains many unlabeled patches (instances); the slide label is known but patch labels are absent. The proposed patch selection and average‑pooling aggregation are standard MIL strategies.  
- **Quick check:** *What is the key difference between instance‑level and bag‑level labels in MIL, and why does it require a pooling operation?*

#### Multi‑objective optimization (Pareto Frontier)
- **Why needed:** Patch selection must balance competing goals (e.g., informativeness vs. compactness). NSGA‑II computes a Pareto front of non‑dominated trade‑offs, allowing selection of a suitable subset under constraints.  
- **Quick check:** *Explain non‑dominated sorting and crowding distance in NSGA‑II. How do they together maintain a diverse Pareto front?*

#### Uncertainty quantification (epistemic)
- **Why needed:** To identify patches where the model’s knowledge is limited (epistemic uncertainty), enabling the system to avoid ambiguous regions and improve reliability.  
- **Quick check:** *How does Monte Carlo dropout approximate Bayesian inference, and what type of uncertainty does it estimate?*

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component Map
Preprocessing (tissue detection, blur filtering, stain normalization, resize) → Feature extraction (frozen ResNet-18, 512‑D embeddings) → Uncertainty estimation (MC dropout, T=20) → Patch selection (uncertainty filtering τ, NSGA‑II) → Classification head (GAP, FC‑256, ReLU, BN, Dropout 0.5, Softmax) → Slide aggregation (average softmax probabilities).

#### Critical Path
1. Preprocess all WSIs: extract tissue patches, filter, normalize stains, resize.  
2. Train a provisional classifier on all (or a random subset of) patches from the training cohort to obtain f_θ (needed for MC dropout).  
3. For each WSI (train/val/test): compute patch embeddings with frozen ResNet‑18; run f_θ with MC dropout to get uncertainties; filter patches (uᵢ ≤ τ); execute NSGA‑II to select a subset.  
4. Train the final classification head from scratch on the selected patches from the training WSIs.  
5. For test WSIs: apply the same selection pipeline with the provisional f_θ to get patches; apply the trained final head; average patch probabilities for the slide label.

#### Design Tradeoffs
- **NSGA‑II complexity:** Provides multi‑objective trade‑offs but costs ~3 min/WSI on GPU; simpler heuristics (e.g., top‑k by a single score) are faster but less balanced.  
- **Uncertainty threshold τ:** Lower τ yields fewer, higher‑confidence patches but risks under‑coverage; higher τ includes more patches but may admit noise. Requires validation tuning.  
- **Frozen backbone:** Reduces parameters and overfitting risk; fine‑tuning could improve domain adaptation but needs more data and careful regularization.  
- **Patch size 224×224:** Matches ResNet pretraining; smaller patches lose context, larger patches increase memory and may dilute local features.

#### Failure Signatures
- **Calibration collapse:** MC dropout uncertainty poorly correlates with true error (e.g., all patches have similar low variance); diagnose by comparing uncertainty distributions on a validation set with known misclassifications.  
- **NSGA‑II stagnation:** Pareto front does not improve over generations; monitor hypervolume indicator or crowding distance decay.  
- **Class imbalance persists:** Despite weighted loss, rare subtypes show low recall; consider oversampling selected patches or focal loss.  
- **Stain normalization artifacts:** Macenko fails on heavily stained or decalcified slides; monitor tissue detection rate and visually inspect normalized patches.

#### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline replication:** Train the full pipeline on TCGA‑BRCA training set, evaluate on internal validation; confirm F1 ≥ 0.88 and AUC ≥ 0.98.  
2. **Ablation study:** Systematically remove uncertainty modeling, patch selection, and stain normalization (as in Table 2) to quantify each component’s contribution.  
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Vary NSGA‑II population (50→100) and generations (50→100), and uncertainty threshold τ, measuring impact on patch count reduction and external CPTAC performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do IHC surrogate‑based labels, compared to transcriptomic PAM50 annotations, impact the accuracy and reliability of histopathology‑based breast cancer subtype classification, especially for morphologically similar subtypes like Luminal A and Luminal B?  
- To what extent does the NSGA‑II‑based patch selection framework generalize across diverse medical centers with varying staining protocols and scanner technologies, and can it maintain performance without heavy reliance on stain normalization?  
- Do the patches selected by the multi‑objective optimization align with histologically relevant regions for PAM50 subtype diagnosis as identified by expert pathologists, and can this selection be interpreted to enhance model explainability?

## Limitations
- Missing critical hyperparameters: the uncertainty threshold τ and the exact rule for selecting a subset from the NSGA‑II Pareto front are not specified.  
- Ambiguous training paradigm: it is unclear whether the classification head is trained per WSI on its selected patches or globally on all selected patches from the training cohort.  
- Domain shift risk: the performance drop from TCGA to CPTAC (F1: 0.8812 → 0.7952) suggests overfitting to TCGA’s staining/scanner characteristics or insufficient robustness of stain normalization.

## Confidence
- Multi‑objective patch selection (Mechanism 1): Medium (due to missing τ and Pareto selection details).  
- MC dropout uncertainty (Mechanism 2): Medium (no calibration analysis provided).  
- Frozen ResNet‑18 backbone (Mechanism 3): High (standard practice).  
- Reported metrics (F1/AUC): Medium (replication hindered by missing details; external drop limits generalizability).
