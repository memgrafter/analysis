# PMIScore: An Unsupervised Approach to Quantify Dialogue Engagement

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13796
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13796
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: PMIScore achieves ROC-AUC >0.93 on English response ranking and Spearman >0.47 with human relevance scores, and outperforms baselines on synthetic distributions.

## Executive Summary
Dialogue engagement is subjective and lacks a gold standard, making evaluation difficult. PMIScore introduces an unsupervised, interpretable metric based on pointwise mutual information (PMI) between dialogue context and response. By leveraging the dual form of KL-divergence, it trains a lightweight neural network on top of frozen LLM embeddings using positive (context-response) and negative (mismatched) pairs. Theoretical analysis shows convergence to true PMI under semantic embedding and i.i.d. assumptions. Experiments on synthetic distributions and the DSTC-11 Track 4 multilingual dataset demonstrate state-of-the-art performance: PMIScore yields the lowest mean squared error and highest Pearson correlation against ground-truth PMI on synthetic data, and attains ROC-AUC above 0.93 on English response ranking while correlating strongly with human relevance scores (Spearman >0.47).

## Method Summary
PMIScore estimates the pointwise mutual information (PMI) between a dialogue context \(c\) and a response \(r\) by modeling the density ratio \(p(c,r) / p(c)p(r)\). The method uses frozen embeddings from a large language model (LLM) for each (context, response) pair, obtained by feeding a fixed prompt into the LLM and extracting the last-token embedding. A small multi-layer perceptron (MLP) is trained with a dual-form mutual information objective: for each positive pair, a few negative responses are sampled (either random or from the same dialogue). The loss encourages the MLP output to approximate the logarithm of the density ratio, i.e., the PMI. After training, the final score is \(\text{PMIScore}(c,r) = \log(\text{softcap}(\text{NN}_\theta(\phi(c,r))))\), where softcap is a bounded nonlinearity (e.g., \(20\tanh(x/20)\)). The approach is unsupervised, requiring only dialogue pairs without human annotations.

## Key Results
- On synthetic distributions (Diagonal, Block, Independent), PMIScore achieves the lowest mean squared error and highest Pearson correlation with true PMI compared to baselines MINE, InfoNCE, KDE, and MEEP.
- On DSTC-11 Track 4 (English and Chinese), PMIScore attains ROC-AUC over 0.93 for distinguishing relevant from non-relevant responses in English and over 0.89 in Chinese.
- PMIScore yields the highest Spearman correlation with human-annotated relevance scores among tested metrics, exceeding 0.47 on English and 0.40 on Chinese.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PMIScore works by explicitly optimizing an estimator of the density ratio between the joint distribution of context-response pairs and the product of their marginals, which equals the PMI. The dual-form of KL-divergence provides a tractable loss that can be minimized using samples from the true joint (positive pairs) and a proposal distribution (negative samples). Under the assumptions that LLM embeddings preserve semantic similarity (i.e., similar contexts/responses have similar embeddings) and that data are i.i.d., the trained neural network converges to the true PMI. The contrastive setup using negatives approximates the expectation under the marginal distribution, while the positive pairs represent the joint. This theoretical grounding, combined with the expressive power of LLM embeddings, yields an accurate and interpretable engagement metric.

## Foundational Learning
- **Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI)**: Measures the discrepancy between the joint probability of two events and their product of marginals; it quantifies how much more likely the pair is than under independence. *Why needed*: It is the core quantity PMIScore estimates. *Quick check*: Verify that \( \text{PMI}(c,r) = \log \frac{p(c,r)}{p(c)p(r)} \).
- **Dual Form of KL-divergence**: The optimization dual of KL divergence yields a loss involving an expectation under the joint minus the log of an expectation under a proposal; this leads to a computable objective for density ratio estimation. *Why needed*: Provides the theoretical loss that drives training without explicit density models. *Quick check*: Derive the dual representation: \( \sup_f \mathbb{E}_{P}[f] - \log \mathbb{E}_{Q}[e^f] \).
- **Contrastive Learning**: Learning by comparing positive pairs against negative samples, which approximates the denominator expectation in the dual loss. *Why needed*: Supplies samples from the marginal distribution without requiring explicit density estimation. *Quick check*: Ensure negative pairs are drawn from a distribution approximating \(p(c)p(r)\).
- **LLM Embeddings as Semantic Representations**: Frozen embeddings from large language models capture contextual meaning, enabling meaningful similarity measures in embedding space. *Why needed*: They serve as the feature space where the density ratio is modeled, avoiding training from scratch. *Quick check*: Compute cosine similarities; positive pairs should be more similar than random pairs on average.
- **Neural Network as Density Ratio Estimator**: A parameterized function (MLP) maps embeddings to a scalar score that approximates the log density ratio. *Why needed*: Provides flexibility to learn complex relationships. *Quick check*: Outputs should be unbounded before softcap; training should reduce the dual loss.
- **Softcap Operation**: Applies \(x \mapsto \alpha\tanh(x/\alpha)\) to bound the output, preventing gradient explosion. *Why needed*: Stabilizes training by keeping the scale of scores finite. *Quick check*: Monitor that pre-softcap values occasionally exceed the cap (e.g., >15) but gradients remain controlled.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Dialogue pairs -> Embedding Extraction (LLM + fixed prompt) -> Fixed embedding φ(c,r) -> MLP Scorer (Linear→PReLU→Linear→PReLU→Linear->softcap) -> log(softcap output) -> PMIScore(c,r)  

Critical path: Collect dialogue corpus -> format prompts -> compute embeddings with frozen LLM -> initialize MLP -> sample negatives -> train using dual-form loss -> validate on held-out set -> output final PMIScore weights.

Design tradeoffs:
- LLM model size: Larger models yield better semantic embeddings but increase compute and memory; smaller models are faster but may compromise accuracy.
- Negative sampling: More negatives improve density ratio estimation but increase batch complexity; mix of in-dialogue and random negatives balances difficulty.
- Softcap constant: Larger values (e.g., 50) reduce bias but risk instability; smaller values (e.g., 10) stabilize but may cap large PMI values.

Failure signatures:
- Diverging loss or NaNs: indicates unbounded gradients; check softcap application and learning rate.
- Low validation AUC: embeddings may not separate positives/negatives; inspect embedding similarity distributions.
- Training too slow: consider reducing MLP depth or increasing learning rate with caution.

First experiments:
1. Train on synthetic Diagonal distribution with known PMI to verify convergence (Pearson >0.9).
2. Ablate negative sampling ratio (1 vs 3 vs 5 negatives per positive) and measure AUC on DSTC-11 validation.
3. Compare last-token pooling vs. mean pooling of LLM embeddings on correlation with human scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can PMIScore be integrated into dialogue generation systems, e.g., as a reward in RL or fine-tuning objective, to improve engagement?
2. Can PMIScore be extended to an online learning setting to adapt in real-time to new conversational styles and domain-specific dynamics?
3. How can we obtain more robust LLM embeddings to mitigate potential errors in PMIScore and ensure reliability across linguistic variations and model architectures?
4. What are theoretically grounded methods to bound gradients or adaptively set the softcap parameter in the PMIScore dual-form objective to improve stability?

## Limitations
- Theoretical assumptions (semantic embedding, i.i.d. data) may not fully hold in real dialogues, limiting convergence guarantees.
- Underspecified implementation details (embedding extraction API, negative sampling protocol, synthetic data generation) create reproducibility risks.
- Human alignment scores (Spearman ~0.47) are modest, indicating PMIScore captures only part of subjective engagement.

## Confidence
- Theoretical soundness: **High** (derivation follows dual-form MI literature, assumptions clearly stated).
- Synthetic distribution results: **Medium** (depends on faithful reproduction of synthetic data; potential variation in paraphrase generation).
- Multilingual & human alignment: **Low** (highly contingent on embedding extraction, LLM choice, negative sampling; no ablations reported).
