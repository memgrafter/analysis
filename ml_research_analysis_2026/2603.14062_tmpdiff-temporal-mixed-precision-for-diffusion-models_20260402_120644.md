# TMPDiff: Temporal Mixed-Precision for Diffusion Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14062
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14062
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves ~90% SSIM relative to full precision on FLUX.1-dev at a 2.5× speedup over 16-bit inference via temporally-aware mixed-precision quantization.

## Executive Summary
TMPDiff addresses the high inference latency of diffusion models by introducing temporal mixed-precision quantization. Instead of applying a uniform bitwidth across all denoising timesteps, it strategically assigns higher precision (e.g., full or 8-bit) to critical timesteps and lower precision (e.g., 4-bit) to others. The method is built on a validated additive error model that predicts how quantization error from individual timesteps accumulates. Using an efficient adaptive bisectioning algorithm, it identifies the most impactful timesteps to preserve in higher precision, constructing a precision schedule that maximizes perceptual quality for a given target speedup. Across multiple models and datasets, TMPDiff consistently outperforms uniform quantization at matched speedups, delivering 10–20% gains in perceptual metrics.

## Method Summary
The core idea is to allocate numeric precision per denoising timestep based on each step's contribution to final output quality. First, the paper validates an additive error model: the final latent error from quantizing all timesteps can be approximated as the sum of individual timestep errors. This is confirmed by measuring "upcasting" gains (improvement from restoring one step to full precision) and "downcasting" losses (degradation from quantizing one step) across models, showing strong linear correlation. Second, an adaptive bisectioning algorithm efficiently approximates the upcasting gain (Δ↑_t) for all timesteps with a linear budget B, avoiding a full O(T) sweep. It focuses sampling on promising intervals, interpolating missing values. Third, given a target speedup *r*, Amdahl's law relates the fraction of high-precision steps *K/T* to the speedup, using profiled latency ratios (λ). The top-K timesteps by Δ↑_t are selected for full precision, forming the schedule Z*. During inference, the model switches between a quantized variant (e.g., W4A4R32 via SVDQuant) and full precision according to Z*.

## Key Results
- TMPDiff achieves 10–20% better perceptual quality (SSIM, LPIPS, PSNR) than uniform-precision quantization at comparable speedups.
- On FLUX.1-dev, it retains ~90% SSIM relative to full precision while accelerating inference by 2.5× over standard 16-bit execution.
- The additive error model holds robustly across four diffusion models (FLUX, PixArt-Σ, Sana, SDXL), enabling effective greedy scheduling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The additive error model is the foundational insight: it assumes the final error is a sum of independent per-timestep errors (E(Z) ≈ Σ_{t∈Z} ε_t). This transforms the combinatorial problem of selecting K timesteps into a simple greedy selection of the K largest Δ↑_t values. The paper empirically validates this by measuring single-timestep upcasting gains and downcasting losses; their strong linear correlation (R² > 0.9) justifies the additive assumption. The adaptive bisectioning algorithm then efficiently estimates Δ↑_t for all timesteps with only O(B) full evaluations, by iteratively refining intervals that show high variance in endpoint gains. This makes the calibration cost linear in the budget B rather than the total timesteps T. Finally, linking K to speedup via Amdahl's law (with λ from profiling) allows precise control over the performance-quality tradeoff.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion Denoising Steps:** Iterative generative process where noise is gradually removed over T steps. Early steps refine coarse structure; later steps add fine details. *Why needed:* Precision needs vary across the timeline; TMPDiff exploits this. *Quick check:* Visualize full-precision outputs at different steps to see quality sensitivity.
- **Quantization Basics:** Lowering numeric precision (bits) of weights/activations to reduce compute and memory. Uniform quantization applies one bitwidth everywhere. *Why needed:* Core accelerator for latency reduction. *Quick check:* Run a full-precision model vs. a W4A4 quantized version and compare FID/SSIM.
- **Additive Error Model:** Hypothesis that total error from quantizing a set of timesteps is the sum of individual timesteps' error contributions. *Why needed:* Enables greedy selection of critical timesteps without exhaustive search. *Quick check:* For a subset of timesteps, compare measured combined error vs. sum of individual errors (hold-one-out).
- **Adaptive Bisectioning:** Budgeted algorithm to estimate a function (Δ↑_t) over an ordered index set by iteratively sampling midpoints of high-variance intervals. *Why needed:* Avoids O(T) calibration while prioritizing informative samples. *Quick check:* Implement on a synthetic 1D function; confirm error reduction with budget.
- **Amdahl's Law for Speedup:** Speedup = 1 / [(1 - f) + f/s], where f is parallelizable fraction. Here, f = K/T is the high-precision portion, s is speedup of quantized step vs. full-precision. *Why needed:* Converts desired speedup into required K. *Quick check:* Profile latencies of full-prec vs. quantized step; compute predicted speedup for given K and compare to measured.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Calibration Module (samples, single-step toggles) -> Additive Error Validator (correlation check) -> Adaptive Bisectioning (estimates Δ↑_t) -> Schedule Optimizer (selects Z* via Amdahl) -> Inference Engine (precision toggle per step)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Calibrate: For budget B, run single-toggle experiments to measure Δ↑_t at sampled timesteps.  
2. Validate: Check linear correlation between upcasting/downcasting; if low, adjust quantization or data.  
3. Optimize: Interpolate all Δ↑_t, compute K from target speedup r and λ, pick top-K.  
4. Deploy: Load full-prec and quantized model weights; at each step t, select model variant per Z*.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Memory vs. Speed: Requires storing both full-prec and quantized models (≈2× memory).  
- Calibration Cost vs. Accuracy: Higher budget B improves Δ↑_t estimates but increases upfront cost.  
- Additive Assumption vs. Reality: Greedy selection is optimal only if errors are independent; non-linear interactions could degrade schedule quality.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Low Spearman ρ (<0.8) between upcasting and downcasting → additive model invalid; revisit quantization scheme.  
- Measured speedup significantly below predicted → precision-switching overhead dominates; optimize model loading.  
- Uniform quantization outperforms TMPDiff at same speedup → calibration set too narrow or Δ↑_t ranking unstable.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Replicate additive error validation: compute Δ↑_t and Δ↓_t for all T steps on a small calibration set; plot correlation, report R².  
2. Profile per-step latency: measure full-prec and quantized step times on target hardware (e.g., RTX 4090); compute λ and predicted speedup for K=10.  
3. Calibration sensitivity: vary calibration set size (32, 128, 512 images) and bisection budget (B=5,10,20); track stability of top-K selected timesteps and final SSIM.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can TMPDiff's memory overhead from storing both full-precision and quantized models be eliminated for memory-constrained deployment?  
  *Basis:* Conclusion identifies memory overhead as a key limitation, suggesting weight offloading or nested quantization. *Why unresolved:* No implementation exists; speculative approaches may not preserve quality gains. *Resolution evidence:* Implementation of offloading/nested quantization that matches TMPDiff's improvements within single-model footprint.
- **Open Question 2:** What kernel-level optimizations can achieve near-zero precision switching overhead to realize theoretical speedups?  
  *Basis:* Paper states efficient kernel-level integration is future work; Supplementary D documents switching overhead. *Why unresolved:* Current speedup measurements assume idealized switching; actual deployment may suffer latency. *Resolution evidence:* Custom GPU kernels/runtime with switching latency <1% per step, validated by end-to-end speedups matching λ-predictions.
- **Open Question 3:** Does the additive error model remain valid under extreme low-bit quantization (≤2-bit) or alternative quantization schemes beyond SVDQuant?  
  *Basis:* Additive model validated only for 4- and 8-bit quantization; linear assumption may break with large errors. *Why unresolved:* Non-linear interactions could invalidate greedy scheduling optimality. *Resolution evidence:* Evaluation across 1-bit, 2-bit, and diverse quantization schemes showing predicted vs. actual error correlation (Pearson r > 0.9) and greedy schedules near-optimal.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gap:** No official code; reimplementation may deviate from algorithm, especially adaptive bisectioning and model toggling.
- **Hardware/calibration specificity:** Latency profiles (λ) and calibration set choice (128 images) are model- and hardware-dependent; generalizability unproven.
- **Model and evaluation scope:** Results on only four models and three datasets; no comparison to other mixed-precision diffusion methods (e.g., DiffPro, TMPQ-DM).
