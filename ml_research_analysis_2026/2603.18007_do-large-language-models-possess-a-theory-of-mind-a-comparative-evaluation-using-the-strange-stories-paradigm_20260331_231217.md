# Do Large Language Models Possess a Theory of Mind? A Comparative Evaluation Using the Strange Stories Paradigm

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.18007
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18007
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** Only GPT-4o achieved human-level accuracy on Strange Stories and showed no performance decline when inferential cues were reduced or distracting information was added.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluated whether large language models (LLMs) possess a robust Theory of Mind (ToM) using the Strange Stories paradigm. Five LLMs—GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4o, Gemma 2, LLaMA 3.1, and Phi 3—were compared to human controls across baseline stories and two modified versions that either reduced inferential cues or added distracting information. GPT-4o matched human overall accuracy and remained unaffected by either manipulation, while all smaller/earlier models were significantly impaired by reduced cues and all non-GPT-4o models struggled with distractions. This indicates that human-like ToM performance requires both substantial model scale and robustness to inferential complexity, with only GPT-4o demonstrating stable mental-state attribution across conditions.

## Method Summary
The study used the Strange Stories task, comprising 34 short narratives across four categories (Mental State, Animal, Fictitious Animal, Physical State). Each story had seven versions: baseline, three intermediate reductions, a reduced-information version, and a distraction version with irrelevant added sentences. Participants (30 humans) and five LLMs were prompted with a story and an open-ended "why/how" question, instructed to include reasoning. Models were run five times per trial at temperature 0.5; humans saw one version each. Responses were scored 0 (incorrect), 1 (partially correct), or 2 (fully correct) based on published guidelines. Mean scores per category and modification were compared using ANOVA and cumulative link mixed-effects models.

## Key Results
- Only GPT-4o achieved human-level overall accuracy and showed no significant performance decline under reduced inferential cues or added distractions.
- Smaller/earlier models (GPT-3.5, Gemma 2, LLaMA 3.1, Phi 3) were significantly impaired by reduced cues, indicating reliance on explicit mental-state keywords.
- All non-GPT-4o models were significantly affected by distracting information, though the effect size was similar to humans for distraction, suggesting a general challenge with in-context noise filtering.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Scale enables stable representations**
- **Claim:** Model scale (parameters, compute) allows development of stable, generalizable internal representations of mental states and causal relations, making performance robust to superficial text manipulations.
- **Mechanism:** Larger models trained on more data with greater capacity learn to extract and abstract underlying relational patterns (e.g., belief-desire-action links) rather than relying on surface lexical cues.
- **Core assumption:** ToM-like competence can emerge from purely statistical language learning if model capacity and data are sufficient to capture the deep structure of social narratives.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] only GPT-4o demonstrated stable mental-state attribution; [section 3.2] GPT-4o showed no decline under manipulations while smaller models were impaired by reduced cues; corpus from "Re-evaluating Theory of Mind" shows larger models have more consistent ToM performance.
- **Break condition:** If a significantly smaller model with specialized architecture/training matches GPT-4o's robustness without scale increase, then scale alone is insufficient and specific inductive biases are necessary.

**Mechanism 2: Cue-dependent heuristic in smaller models**
- **Claim:** Smaller models succeed on baseline via cue-dependent heuristic (e.g., matching keywords like "lie" to memorized templates), while GPT-4o uses flexible, context-sensitive inference.
- **Mechanism:** Original stories contain reiterated mental-state terms and causal connectors; smaller models latch onto these high-frequency signals, which fail when cues are reduced or distractors trigger irrelevant matches.
- **Core assumption:** Standard ToM vignettes can be solved via shallow pattern completion without building a coherent character model.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] Gemma 2 and Phi 3 especially vulnerable to cue reduction; [abstract] earlier/smaller models strongly affected by number of relevant inferential cues; [section 1.3.2] cites Ullman (2023) and Shapira et al. (2024) showing brittleness under minor manipulations.
- **Break condition:** If Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting eliminates the performance gap for smaller models under reduced cues, it suggests representations were present but not activated; if gap persists, it indicates representational limits.

**Mechanism 3: Contextual integration capacity**
- **Claim:** Robustness is facilitated by effective use of the model's full context window to integrate disparate information over the narrative, resisting loss of key phrases and noise from distractors.
- **Mechanism:** Strange Stories are ~80–120 words. Reducing cues disperses triggers; distractors increase competing info. Superior contextual attention allows discriminative weighing of evidence, maintaining coherent representation of causal/mental architecture.
- **Core assumption:** The primary challenge is information integration over fixed context length, not just background knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] all non-GPT-4o models affected by distraction; [abstract] robustness to inferential complexity is the differentiator; corpus from "Through the Theory of Mind's Eye" on multimodal models hints that contextual integration across time/space is key.
- **Break condition:** If artificially extending context window of smaller model (e.g., efficient attention) does not improve robustness, then context capacity is not the primary limiting factor.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Theory of Mind (ToM) constructs (belief, intention, emotion, double bluff)**
  - Why needed: Model must distinguish physical causality from mental-state attribution and understand complex social manipulations like false beliefs and double bluffs.
  - Quick check: In the prisoner story, what is the prisoner's belief about what the captors believe about the tank location?
- **Concept: Strange Stories task structure and scoring**
  - Why needed: Evaluation relies on specific format (short narrative + open-ended question) and nuanced 0–2 rubric distinguishing correct outcome prediction from genuine mental-state understanding.
  - Quick check: What differentiates a score of "1" (partial) from "2" (full) for the Mental State story example?
- **Concept: Inferential cue dependency vs. abstraction**
  - Why needed: Core experimental manipulation tests whether reasoning depends on redundant explicit mental-state keywords (heuristic) or can abstract core logic from minimal cues.
  - Quick check: Does removing the phrase "he will want to save his army, and so he will certainly lie" fundamentally alter the logical structure of the prisoner's dilemma?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input Encoder -> Contextual Transformer Core -> Reasoning/Generation Head

Critical path: Story input → positionally encoded tokens → multi-layer self-attention (building entity/event representations) → final layer representation → linear projection to vocabulary → token-by-token generation of answer text. Robustness emerges from self-attention's ability to form stable, persistent representations of character beliefs that survive input perturbations.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Scale vs. Efficiency:** Larger models show robustness but are computationally expensive; tradeoff between single capable model vs. pipeline of smaller specialized modules.
- **Prompt Engineering vs. Intrinsic Robustness:** Relying on CoT prompts shifts burden to user and doesn't test innate capability; target architecture should produce robust answers under neutral prompting.
- **Generalization vs. Memorization:** Training data includes many social vignettes; system must not overfit to specific templates but learn underlying "grammar" of mental-state attribution.

Failure signatures:
1. **Sensitivity to Cue Reduction:** Accuracy drops significantly (>15–20%) on Reduced Information stories compared to Baseline, especially for Mental State and Fictitious Animal.
2. **Distractor Influence:** Performance decline on Distraction stories correlates with salience (e.g., proper nouns, concrete objects) of added irrelevant phrases.
3. **Template Matching:** Answers contain verbatim phrases or reasoning chains from training data but fail to adapt when story elements are superficially altered.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Baseline Replication:** Run all 34 baseline stories for the target model, establish performance benchmark, compare to human mean scores (~1.8–2.0 on mental state), confirm general capability.
2. **Controlled Cue Reduction:** Automate removal of reiterated mental-state terms and causal connectors from Mental State and Physical State stories. Measure mean score delta vs. baseline. Robust system shows non-significant or small delta (<0.2 points).
3. **Distractor Injection:** Automate insertion of two irrelevant sentences per story (one about agent/object, one about prominent concept). Measure performance delta. Compare delta to human delta (~ -1.12 for distraction). System with human-like robustness shows comparable delta magnitude.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Are LLMs' ToM-like outputs the result of genuine, context-sensitive mental-state reasoning or sophisticated but cue-dependent pattern completion?**
   - **Basis in paper:** The core debate is framed in the abstract and introduction: "Are they capable of robust mental state attribution indistinguishable from human ability in its output, or do their outputs merely reflect superficial pattern completion?" The study's manipulation tests robustness as a key discriminant.
   - **Why unresolved:** The study shows GPT-4o matches human accuracy and is robust to manipulations, but this behavioral equivalence does not prove identity of underlying mechanisms. The "cautious interpretation" in the conclusion notes that stable performance "is consistent with the possibility" of ToM but does not establish it.
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Direct analysis of internal model representations during ToM tasks (e.g., probing for belief-state representations), comparison to neural data from humans performing the same tasks, or testing on a wider battery of manipulations designed to cleanly separate heuristic-based from generative inference processes.

2. **What specific methodological criteria define a sufficiently "robust" and "theory-driven" evaluation for LLM Theory of Mind, beyond simple canonical task accuracy?**
   - **Basis in paper:** The conclusion explicitly states the methodological takeaway: "evaluating LLM mentalising requires advanced ToM tasks with systematic, theory-driven modifications that go beyond surface familiarity reductions and directly manipulate inferential demands."
   - **Why unresolved:** The paper implements two manipulations (cue reduction, distraction) but does not provide a general framework for designing future tasks. It is unclear which manipulations are most diagnostic, how many and what type are needed to distinguish robust ToM from advanced mimicry, and how these should be balanced against general comprehension load.
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and validation of a standardized benchmark suite with multiple, orthogonal manipulations (e.g., varying abstraction, narrative coherence, perspective-switching demands) whose combined failure patterns reliably predict lack of robust ToM in ablation studies or in models known to lack specific capacities.

3. **Is the observed performance gap between GPT-4o and smaller/earlier models primarily a function of scale (parameter count/training data) or of specific architectural or training innovations that emerged in more recent models?**
   - **Basis in paper:** The results show a stark performance difference aligned with model generation/size. The paper attributes this to "both scale and robustness" but does not test this causal claim. The models compared differ on multiple axes (size, architecture details, training methods, instruction tuning), making the source of the robustness gap unclear.
   - **Why unresolved:** The study is a comparative evaluation, not an ablation. It cannot isolate whether scale alone is sufficient for robust ToM, or whether specific techniques (e.g., instruction tuning, RLHF, architectural tweaks) in GPT-4o are necessary. The performance of similarly-sized local models (Gemma 2, LLaMA 3.1, Phi 3) was poor, suggesting scale is not sufficient.
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled study holding training data and objective constant while systematically varying model scale, or fine-tuning smaller models with targeted objectives to enhance inferential robustness, to see if human-level stability on modified ToM tasks can be induced without a full-scale GPT-4o-class model.

4. **To what extent does performance on complex verbal vignette tasks like Strange Stories reflect a unified "Theory of Mind" capacity versus a cluster of distinct skills (e.g., pragmatic inference, narrative comprehension, real-world knowledge integration)?**
   - **Basis in paper:** The authors cite Nawaz et al. (2023): "Recent psychometric work suggests that Strange Stories performance may not reflect a single unified ToM factor, but instead may draw on a cluster of advanced social-cognitive and pragmatic competencies." They acknowledge this is "important to acknowledge when interpreting scores as 'pure' ToM."
   - **Why unresolved:** The study uses Strange Stories as the primary assay but does not decompose performance to see which sub-skills (e.g., detecting irony, understanding faux pas, physical causality) are most predictive of robustness to manipulation. It remains unclear whether the GPT-4o advantage stems from a specific mentalizing module or from superior general narrative inference.
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Administering a large battery of tasks that cleanly isolate sub-components (e.g., pure belief reasoning without narrative, pragmatic inference without mental states, physical causality without social context) to the same models, followed by factor analysis to see if a common factor emerges and how it correlates with robustness on the modified Strange Stories.

5. **Can models trained solely on linguistic data develop a robust Theory of Mind, or is some form of embodied or social grounding a necessary precondition for human-like mental-state attribution?**
   - **Basis in paper:** The paper's entire motivation contrasts LLMs' "language-only training" with the "socially embodied" nature of human ToM. The finding that GPT-4o achieves human-level robustness is presented as evidence that "language-only training can, in some cases, provide sufficiently rich statistical and relational structure." This is a direct challenge to the grounding hypothesis.
   - **Why unresolved:** The study demonstrates behavioral parity but cannot rule out that the model's internal solution is fundamentally different from a grounded one. It does not test whether grounding (e.g., multimodal training, interactive reinforcement) would yield qualitatively different robustness profiles or generalize better to novel social scenarios. The conclusion calls for "cautious interpretation."
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Directly comparing models with identical architectures and language training data, but differing in exposure to multimodal/interactive data, on the same systematically modified ToM battery. Alternatively, testing whether "grounded" models show different patterns of error or generalization on out-of-distribution social scenarios compared to purely language-trained models.

## Limitations
- Full story texts for all 34 stories across all 7 versions are not provided in the paper; only examples are given, so reconstruction from descriptions may introduce variation.
- Exact model versions/checkpoints used (e.g., specific Gemma 2 or LLaMA 3.1 variants) and the precise statistical code are not uniquely identified, requiring reconstruction from methodological descriptions.
- Scoring rubrics for non–Mental State stories are inferred from guidelines rather than explicitly provided, risking rater drift and low inter-rater reliability.

## Confidence
- **GPT-4o’s robust ToM-like performance:** High (consistent pattern across manipulations in reported results).
- **Scale as primary differentiator:** Medium (correlational; alternative architectures could break condition).
- **Heuristic vs. abstraction mechanism:** Medium (plausible but untested without CoT ablation).
- **Context integration as key factor:** Low (speculative; not directly isolated).
- **Findings generalize beyond Strange Stories:** Low (corpus signals active debate, e.g., "GPT-4o Lacks Core Features of Theory of Mind").

## Next Checks
1. **Stimulus reconstruction audit:** Create full story set from paper examples; validate against authors or use open-source Strange Stories corpora to ensure fidelity.
2. **Baseline replication with specified versions:** Test all models using exact version identifiers (e.g., `gpt-4o-2024-05-13`) and report variance across 5 runs.
3. **CoT ablation test:** Prompt smaller models with explicit chain-of-thought instructions in Reduced-cue condition; if performance improves to baseline, supports heuristic claim; if not, indicates representational limits.
