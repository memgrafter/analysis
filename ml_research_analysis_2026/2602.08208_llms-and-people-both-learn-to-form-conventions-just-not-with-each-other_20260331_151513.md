# LLMs and people both learn to form conventions -- just not with each other

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08208
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08208
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** Human-human and AI-AI dyads develop effective conventions (increasing accuracy, decreasing message length, increasing lexical overlap) across 50 turns, while human-AI pairs show minimal improvement.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) form conversational conventions similarly to humans, using a tangram reference game. In same-type dyads (human-human or AI-AI), participants improved across 50 turns: accuracy rose, messages shortened, and lexical overlap increased. Mixed human-AI pairs, however, exhibited little improvement despite both agents learning when interacting with same-type partners. A follow-up experiment prompting the LLM to adopt humanlike brevity and analogical style aligned message length but failed to raise accuracy or lexical convergence. The findings suggest that convention formation requires shared interpretative biases beyond simple mimicry, and that human-AI communication faces a fundamental alignment gap.

## Method Summary
The study used a repeat-reference tangram game where a director described a cued figure from a 10-image grid to a matcher, who selected the target. Dyads alternated roles over 50 turns (5 blocks of 10). Three dyad types were tested: Human-Human, Human-AI, and AI-AI. The AI agent was GPT-5 via API, with two prompt conditions: Default (matching human instructions) and Humanlike (added brevity and analogical style guidance). Primary metrics were accuracy, message length (word count), and lexical overlap (Jaccard similarity between consecutive descriptions of the same tangram). Exit surveys gauged perceived collaboration and partner type detection. Experiment 1 compared the three dyad types; Experiment 2 focused on Human-AI pairs comparing Default vs. Humanlike prompts.

## Key Results
- Same-type dyads (H-H and AI-AI) showed clear conventionalization: accuracy improved, message length decreased, and lexical overlap increased across blocks.
- Mixed human-AI pairs (H-AI, Default prompt) showed minimal improvement on all metrics and ended with significantly lower accuracy (~70%) than H-H (~93%).
- Prompting the AI to mimic human brevity (Humanlike) reduced message length to match H-H levels, but accuracy and lexical overlap remained low, indicating that form mimicry alone cannot bridge the convention gap.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Shared Interpretative Biases Enable Convention Formation
- **Claim:** Convention formation requires shared interpretative biases (common ground) to map linguistic forms to referents consistently.
- **Mechanism:** Agents co-ordinate by aligning on meaning representations; when their initial biases align (e.g., both humans or both LLMs), they can bootstrap from ambiguous signals to stable conventions. When biases differ (human vs LLM), even if they repeat each other's forms, the mapping fails to converge.
- **Assumption:** Representational tendencies (e.g., analogical vs geometric description styles) differ systematically between humans and LLMs and persist across interactions.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "conversational alignment requires more than just the ability to mimic previous interactions, but also shared interpretative biases toward the meanings that are conveyed."
  - Experiment 1: H-H used 68% analogical descriptions vs. AI-AI 5%; H-AI pairs showed low lexical overlap and accuracy.
  - Neighbor corpus (*Diverse Conventions for Human-AI Collaboration*): self-play conventions often fail to transfer to human partners.
- **Break condition:** Mismatched representational biases prevent convention formation, as seen in H-AI dyads where both partners learn but cannot align on a common mapping.

### Mechanism 2: In-Context Learning Drives AI-AI Convergence
- **Claim:** LLMs can form conventions through in-context learning (ICL) by reusing and adapting expressions from the dialogue history.
- **Mechanism:** The transformer architecture attends over the entire context, allowing the model to detect and replicate successful referring expressions from earlier turns, leading to increased lexical overlap and accuracy over repeated interactions.
- **Assumption:** The model's context window is sufficient to retain relevant previous descriptions and the model is trained to prioritize successful patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "LLMs are especially effective at In-Context Learning (ICL): adapting to patterns that appear in their immediate context during inference without the opportunity to encode these patterns into their weights."
  - Experiment 1: AI-AI accuracy rose from 56% to 99% by Block 4, and lexical overlap increased steadily.
  - ICL literature (e.g., Olsson et al., 2022) on induction heads.
- **Break condition:** If the model fails to recognize which prior patterns led to success (e.g., due to noisy context or lack of explicit feedback), ICL will not produce convergence.

### Mechanism 3: Resource Sensitivity Drives Human Efficiency but Is Insufficient Alone
- **Claim:** Humans' drive to minimize effort shortens messages, reinforcing convention stability; however, this alone does not yield effective conventions without shared biases.
- **Mechanism:** Humans incur cognitive and production costs, leading them to adopt shorter, more efficient expressions as conventions form. LLMs lack intrinsic cost pressure, so they do not shorten messages unless explicitly prompted.
- **Assumption:** Communicative efficiency is a primary motivator for human convention formation (Gricean maxims, Zipf's principle).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: Humans align to be "informative, concise, and on-topic."
  - Experiment 1: H-H message length dropped from 20 to 6 words, while AI-AI remained ~58–56; Experiment 2: Humanlike prompt reduced AI length but not accuracy/overlap.
  - Neighbor paper (*Success and Cost Elicit Convention Formation for Efficient Communication*): explicit length penalties induce efficiency.
- **Break condition:** Artificially imposing cost minimization without aligning interpretative biases (as in Humanlike-prompted H-AI) may shorten messages but does not improve convention quality.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
- Human Interface ↔ Role Manager ↔ Prompt Manager → Context Buffer → Language Model (GPT-5) → Feedback Processor → Evaluation Module
- Feedback Processor → Context Buffer (update loop)
- Evaluation Module receives metrics from Feedback Processor
- Role Manager controls turn order and role assignment

### Critical path
For a director turn: (1) Receive current grid and target cue; (2) Retrieve previous turns from context buffer; (3) Prompt manager assembles a prompt containing instructions, history, and current task; (4) LM generates description; (5) Description sent to matcher. For a matcher turn: (1) Receive description and grid; (2) Prompt LM to select an image; (3) Record selection; (4) Game provides feedback; (5) Feedback processor updates context. Context buffer is updated after each turn.

### Design tradeoffs
- Model size vs. cost: Larger models exhibit stronger ICL but increase latency and expense.
- Context window management: Longer histories improve convention tracking but risk exceeding limits; strategies like summarization or recent-turn truncation must be chosen.
- Prompting strategy: Simple role instructions vs. detailed style guidelines affect behavior; over-constraining may hinder natural adaptation.
- Feedback incorporation: Explicitly including success/failure signals may boost adaptation but consumes context slots.
- Humanlike prompting: Adding brevity and analogical constraints can align AI messages with human style but may not improve coordination if biases remain mismatched.

### Failure signatures
- Message length remains constant or increases across blocks.
- Lexical overlap (Jaccard similarity) between descriptions of the same item in consecutive blocks stays below 0.2.
- Accuracy plateaus below 80% for H-AI pairs, while H-H or AI-AI reach >90%.
- Human partners rate AI as unhelpful, non-adaptive, or clearly AI in exit surveys.
- Conditional analysis shows no increase in lexical similarity after successful turns.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline replication:** Run AI-AI, H-H, and H-AI pairs for 50 turns on the tangram game. Verify that H-H and AI-AI show improving accuracy/decreasing length/increasing overlap, while H-AI does not.
2. **Humanlike prompting test:** Use the refined prompt from Experiment 2 (instructing brevity and analogical style) for AI in H-AI pairs. Check if message length matches H-H and whether accuracy/overlap still lag.
3. **Diagnostic style analysis:** Tag descriptions as analogical/geometric/mixed. Correlate style congruence (e.g., proportion of analogical descriptions in both partners) with convention formation metrics to confirm the role of representational bias alignment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do human-LLM pairs fail to develop effective conventions despite both humans and LLMs forming conventions in same-type interactions?
- **Basis in paper:** "The fact that both AI-AI pairs and H-H pairs are able to achieve high accuracy rates raises the question of why H-AI pairs perform so much more poorly."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates that both agent types can learn conventions alone, but mixed dyads show minimal improvement, indicating a dyad-level incompatibility not explained by individual learning capacity.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Directly comparing how humans and LLMs interpret and describe tangram images (e.g., via representational similarity analysis of neural activations or embeddings) and experimentally manipulating alignment of their conceptual spaces (e.g., fine-tuning LLMs on human-generated descriptions) to test if convergence improves.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific shared interpretative biases are necessary for convention formation, and why are they absent in human-LLM interactions?
- **Basis in paper:** "conversational alignment requires more than just the ability to mimic previous interactions, but also shared interpretative biases toward the meanings that are conveyed."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the lack of shared biases as central to the H-AI failure but does not define these biases—whether they involve perceptual categorization, analogical reasoning, or theory of mind—leaving a theoretical gap.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical studies probing the conceptual categories humans and LLMs use for ambiguous referents (e.g., via feature listing, similarity judgments) and testing whether interventions that induce shared biases (e.g., training LLMs on human-centric visual data) enable H-AI pairs to match H-H conventionalization.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do humans and LLMs employ similar underlying mechanisms (e.g., statistical learning vs. recursive mental modeling) to form conventions, or are their processes fundamentally different?
- **Basis in paper:** "Future work using mechanistic approaches may inform whether humans and LLMs form conventions in similar ways."
- **Why unresolved:** Both agent types exhibit convention-like improvements, but disparities (e.g., AI-AI’s persistent verbosity) suggest divergent processes; however, the cognitive or architectural bases remain unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Process-tracing studies comparing human and LLM dynamics during the game (e.g., eye-tracking for humans, attention/activation analysis for LLMs) to assess sensitivity to partner feedback, memory for prior turns, and adaptation strategies; computational modeling to test if LLM in-context learning recapitulates human interactive alignment.

## Limitations
- Exact experimental materials (human instructions, AI response formatting, tangram set, target sequence) are unreported, blocking faithful replication.
- The claim of "shared interpretative biases" is based on post-hoc style correlations, not causal manipulation; thus the mechanism remains speculative.
- Results are tied to GPT-5 and a specific visual reference task; generalizability to other LLMs and domains is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Main outcome** (H-H/AI-AI improve, H-AI does not): High
- **Mechanism 1** (shared biases needed): Medium
- **Mechanism 2** (ICL drives AI-AI convergence): High
- **Mechanism 3** (resource sensitivity insufficient alone): Medium-High
