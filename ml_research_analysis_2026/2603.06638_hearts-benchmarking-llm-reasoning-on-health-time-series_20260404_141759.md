# HEARTS: Benchmarking LLM Reasoning on Health Time Series

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.06638
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06638
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** LLMs achieve a top score of 0.66 on the benchmark, only marginally exceeding a naive baseline of 0.61 and lagging far behind specialized models (SOTA ML average >0.84 on a 32-task subset).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces HEARTS, a comprehensive, living benchmark designed to evaluate large language models (LLMs) on real-world health time series reasoning. HEARTS unifies 16 datasets spanning 12 health domains and 20 signal modalities, organized into a hierarchical taxonomy of 110 tasks across Perception, Inference, Generation, and Deduction. Evaluating 14 state-of-the-art LLMs on over 20,000 test samples reveals a stark performance gap: LLMs barely surpass simple heuristics and perform significantly worse than domain-specialized machine learning models. Performance degrades with longer sequences and higher frequencies, and all model families exhibit a consistent ordering of task difficulty, suggesting fundamental limitations in current LLM architectures for processing dense physiological signals. The HEARTS benchmark is released as a community-driven resource with standardized APIs to catalyze future development.

## Method Summary
The evaluation employs a **CodeAct agent** framework, where LLMs act as agents that read data files (`.npy`, `.csv`) and generate executable Python code to perform analysis. No model training occurs; it is a zero-shot assessment. An `ExperimentBase` orchestrator manages data loading, iteration over fixed test samples (200 per task), agent invocation, output parsing, and task-specific metric calculation (e.g., Accuracy, IoU, 1−½sMAPE). The overall score is a macro-average across the four reasoning categories. A living ecosystem is established via standardized APIs (`AgentBase`, `ExperimentBase`) and contribution protocols, allowing the community to add new datasets, tasks, and models.

## Key Results
*   LLMs achieve a top macro-average score of 0.66, only 0.05 above the naive baseline (0.61), indicating reliance on simple strategies.
*   Specialized ML models achieve an average score >0.84 on a 32-task subset, revealing a substantial capability gap.
*   Performance strongly declines with increased sequence length (Spearman ρ = -0.55) and sampling frequency (ρ = -0.53), highlighting architectural bottlenecks.
*   All three major model families (GPT, Claude, Gemini) show a high rank correlation (r > 0.84) in task scores, pointing to a universal, data-driven difficulty hierarchy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Heuristic Reliance Over True Reasoning
*   **Claim:** LLMs rely on low-complexity heuristics and surface statistics rather than multi-step temporal reasoning.
*   **Mechanism:** Faced with high-dimensional continuous signals, LLMs default to learnable, simple patterns (e.g., linear interpolation, copy-pasting, constant forecasting) that yield moderate scores but fail to model causality, periodicity, or cross-channel dependencies.
*   **Core assumption:** LLM internal representations are optimized for token prediction on language, not for continuous numerical dynamics with rich temporal structure.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "LLMs often rely on simple heuristics and struggle with multi-step temporal reasoning." [section 4.2] Generation outputs dominated by heuristics; Inference failures on tasks without explicit thresholds.
*   **Break condition:** An LLM consistently matches specialized model performance on long-horizon, cross-modal deduction tasks without tool augmentation.

### Mechanism 2: Architectural Bottleneck for Dense Temporal Data
*   **Claim:** Performance degradation with longer sequences and higher frequencies stems from attention and positional encoding limitations, not just a lack of domain knowledge.
*   **Mechanism:** The volume of numerical tokens in long, high-frequency signals (up to 1M+ steps, 48 kHz) overwhelms context windows and dilutes positional signals, causing information loss.
*   **Core assumption:** Standard LLM tokenizers and attention are inefficient for representing continuous, high-frequency streams versus specialized encoders.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.3] Correlations ρ = -0.55 (length), -0.53 (frequency), p < 1e-6. [section 3.1] HEARTS captures this scale variation (60 to 1M+ steps, daily to 48kHz).
*   **Break condition:** A model maintains performance on >100k-step, >1kHz inputs without external compression or tool use.

### Mechanism 3: Universal Data-Bound Difficulty Ceiling
*   **Claim:** Consistent, family-wide difficulty ordering indicates performance is bounded by intrinsic data complexity and a universal representation gap, not scale or architectural tweaks within LLM families.
*   **Mechanism:** Tasks and modalities impose a fixed "difficulty profile" based on signal noisiness, semantic accessibility, and temporal sparsity. Scaling improves absolute scores marginally but does not change relative rankings.
*   **Core assumption:** LLM pretraining (next-token prediction on language) does not naturally imbue the ability to interpret raw biomedical waveforms.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.4] Within-family task-score correlations >0.84. [section 4.3] Universal poor performance on ECG/CGM vs. good on HR/aggregated data.
*   **Break condition:** A scaled model dramatically reorders the modality/difficulty hierarchy (e.g., excels at ECG while failing on HR).

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Temporal Hierarchy in Physiological Signals**
    *   *Why needed:* Tasks span milliseconds to years (e.g., ECG R-peaks vs. diabetes progression). Understanding nested timescales (e.g., respiration within sleep stages) is essential for task design and interpretation.
    *   *Quick check:* For a 30-second PPG segment, what distinct physiological processes span seconds vs. sub-seconds, and how would their feature extraction differ?

*   **Concept: Tokenization of Continuous Data**
    *   *Why needed:* HEARTS exposes failure of naive digit-to-token conversion for waveforms. Discretization and binning cause loss of fine-grained information, crucial for diagnosing LLM limits.
    *   *Quick check:* Converting a 100 Hz, 10-second ECG (1000 samples) to a space-separated number string—what are three information-theoretic consequences for an LLM's attention?

*   **Concept: Hierarchical Reasoning Taxonomy (Perception → Inference → Generation → Deduction)**
    *   *Why needed:* This is the core evaluation framework. Distinguishing "feature extraction" (Perception) from "cross-modal translation" (Generation) is key to setting correct metrics and expectations.
    *   *Quick check:* For "EEG to HR cross-modal translation," which cognitive layer does it test, and why is it harder than "HR calculation from PPG" (Perception)?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** `ExperimentBase` (orchestration) -> `AgentBase` (LLM interface via CodeAct) -> File System (`.npy`/`.csv` I/O)
*   **Critical path:** 1) Task definition (difficulty from data: length/frequency/modality). 2) Agent deployment (CodeAct reads files, writes outputs). 3) Metric calculation (task-specific: Accuracy, IoU, 1−½sMAPE). 4) Leaderboard macro-average aggregation.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** Standardization (`ExperimentBase`) vs. flexibility for novel agents; open submissions vs. integrity (metric gaming, privacy); file-based access vs. I/O overhead and fidelity loss.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   *Agent-level:* Invalid JSON, runtime errors, truncated code, defaulting to naive strategies.
    *   *Task-level:* Low inter-annotator agreement, ceiling effects from trivial baselines, collapse on long sequences.
    *   *System-level:* Toolset sparsity (e.g., Biomni lacking time-series tools), inability to parse `.npy`.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline sanity check:** Run the naive baseline (constant/random) on all 110 tasks. Verify score ordering matches expected difficulty.
    2.  **Single-model smoke test:** Evaluate a small model (e.g., Nemotron Nano) on 5 tasks spanning the 4 categories. Check parsing pipeline for bugs.
    3.  **Modality isolation test:** On a high-frequency (ECG) vs. low-frequency (daily aggregates) task pair, confirm the same model shows the expected performance gap.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** Do LLMs perform genuine multi-step temporal reasoning on health time series, or do they rely primarily on surface-level heuristics and pre-trained priors?
    *   *Basis:* The paper explicitly asks this and finds LLMs "rely on simple heuristics."
    *   *Why unresolved:* Evaluation measures final accuracy only; the validity of intermediate reasoning steps is unverified.
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* A task-agnostic framework scoring faithfulness and validity of reasoning trajectories, possibly via automated checks and human expert validation.

*   **Open Question 2:** What principled methods can effectively incorporate domain-specific physiological priors and constraints into LLM agents?
    *   *Basis:* Paper notes "many failures suggest... lack of domain prior knowledge" and calls for "principled ways to incorporate such priors."
    *   *Why unresolved:* No concrete framework or evaluation of different approaches (tool integration, constraints, rule injection) exists.
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic experiments comparing prior-incorporation methods (e.g., RAG with medical literature, physiological modeling tools) on the same tasks, measuring performance and clinical plausibility.

*   **Open Question 3:** What architectural modifications or training paradigms can enable LLMs to handle extreme temporal scales (sequence lengths up to 1M+, frequencies up to 48kHz) without degradation?
    *   *Basis:* Paper documents performance declines and states "scaling alone is insufficient," implying fundamental architectural limits.
    *   *Why unresolved:* Paper does not investigate specific solutions (e.g., specialized tokenization, hierarchical attention, multi-resolution processing).
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablation studies comparing signal encoding strategies (raw digits vs. binned embeddings vs. learned patches) and attention mechanisms on high-scale scenarios.

*   **Open Question 4:** Why do domain-specialist biomedical agents (e.g., Biomni) fail to outperform generalist agents (e.g., CodeAct)?
    *   *Basis:* Paper finds Biomni's "toolset sparsity regarding health time-series analysis" causes it to "rarely invokes its specialized library."
    *   *Why unresolved:* Unclear if this is due to absent tools, agent's failure to recognize need, or incompatibility between biomedical tool design and time-series reasoning.
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Failure mode analysis of Biomni trajectories to determine why specialized tools aren't invoked, plus development/evaluation of a dedicated time-series toolset.

*   **Open Question 5:** What is the fundamental "universal hierarchy of difficulty" across health domains and modalities causing consistent performance ordering?
    *   *Basis:* Paper observes "domains accessible to one model are generally tractable for the entire cohort," suggesting a data-intrinsic hierarchy.
    *   *Why unresolved:* Underlying factors (signal-to-noise ratio, predictability, feature separability) remain unidentified.
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Comprehensive analysis correlating task performance across all models with intrinsic signal properties (stationarity, autocorrelation, entropy) to identify latent difficulty determinants.

## Limitations
*   **Replication barriers:** Unreported CodeAct agent setups (prompts, sandbox rules), undisclosed LLM configurations (exact model versions, API parameters, generation settings), and underspecified naive baseline implementations hinder faithful reproduction.
*   **Benchmark scope:** 16 datasets, while diverse, may not cover the full spectrum of health time series scenarios; conclusions rely on macro-average scores that may mask critical per-task failures.
*   **Evaluation depth:** Assessment captures end-task accuracy but does not probe the reasoning process itself, leaving the "how" of LLM decision-making unverified.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (Heuristic Reliance):** **Medium**. Evidence includes narrow score gaps and qualitative output analysis, but lacks systematic probing of reasoning chains.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Architectural Limits):** **Medium**. Strong negative correlations (length ρ=-0.55, frequency ρ=-0.53, p<1e-6) suggest a bottleneck, but causality is not proven.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Universal Difficulty):** **High**. High within-family task-score correlations (>0.84) across three model families indicate a stable, data-driven difficulty profile resistant to scaling.

## Next Checks
1.  **Heuristic identification audit:** Manually annotate a stratified sample of LLM Generation outputs to quantify heuristic patterns (copy-paste, linear interpolation, constant). Correlate heuristic frequency with performance gaps over baselines.
2.  **Tokenization ablation study:** For a mid-sized LLM (e.g., Llama 4 Maverick), replace digit tokenization with binned (256-bin) or learned patch embeddings on long-sequence (>100k steps) tasks. Compare performance against reported digit-based results.
3.  **Intra-family scaling analysis:** Evaluate three size variants (small, medium, large) from a single family (e.g., Gemma 2) on the full 110-task benchmark. Compute Spearman rank correlation of task scores across sizes; a high correlation (ρ>0.8) would confirm a fixed difficulty hierarchy.
