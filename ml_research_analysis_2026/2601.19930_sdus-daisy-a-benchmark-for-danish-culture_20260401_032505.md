# SDUs DAISY: A Benchmark for Danish Culture
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.19930
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19930
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** A new Danish cultural benchmark (Daisy) reveals large performance gaps in five contemporary LLMs, with the best model (Llama 3.3 70B) scoring only BLEU 0.166 and F1 0.268.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Daisy, the first benchmark designed to evaluate large language models' knowledge of Danish cultural heritage. The dataset consists of 741 validated, closed-ended question-answer pairs automatically generated from the official Danish Culture Canon (Kulturkanon). Baseline evaluations on five state-of-the-art models show uniformly poor performance, indicating a significant deficiency in specific Danish cultural literacy despite general Danish language capabilities. The work highlights the challenge of preserving and assessing cultural knowledge in machine learning systems for low-resource languages.

## Method Summary
Daisy was constructed by first selecting 108 artifacts from the state-endorsed Danish Culture Canon. For each artifact, its Wikipedia page was retrieved, and the Gemma3 27B model was prompted to generate five random closed-ended QA pairs. These auto-generated pairs underwent multi-annotator human validation to ensure out-of-context validity, correctness, and relevance; invalid or incorrect pairs were removed or rewritten. The final dataset of 741 pairs was used to evaluate five models (GPT-OSS series, Gemma3, Llama 3.3, Mistral Small) via a fixed prompt template. Performance was measured using BLEU (with smoothing) and token-level F1, comparing normalized predictions against normalized gold answers.

## Key Results
- All evaluated models performed poorly on Daisy, with top scores below 0.27 F1.
- Llama 3.3 70B-Instruct was the best performer (BLEU: 0.166, F1: 0.268).
- Performance gaps suggest that general Danish language proficiency does not transfer to specific cultural heritage knowledge.
- The dataset reveals a lack of Danish cultural knowledge in current LLMs, motivating further research.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Random sampling within canonical artifacts yields a difficulty spectrum
- **Claim**: Generating questions randomly from each Wikipedia page produces a mix of central (obvious) and peripheral (detailed) factual questions, increasing benchmark difficulty and ecological validity.
- **Mechanism**: For each Kulturkanon artifact, the system retrieves its Wikipedia page and prompts Gemma3 27B to generate five questions using a template emphasizing diversity and fundamental aspects. This within-artifact sampling aims to cover both mainstream and niche aspects without explicit stratification.
- **Core assumption**: A random sampling approach from a comprehensive source will naturally cover a knowledge spectrum.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract]: mix of central and peripheral questions; 
  - [section 3.1]: sampling strategy within each work to generate 5 random questions focusing on fundamental TEXT.
- **Break condition**: If the generation prompt consistently yields only high-level facts (e.g., creator, date) and never addresses detailed attributes (e.g., specific dimensions, lesser-known features), the intended difficulty mix is not achieved.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-annotator human validation ensures dataset quality
- **Claim**: Having several human annotators review each auto-generated QA pair for validity, correctness, and relevance filters noise and elevates dataset reliability.
- **Mechanism**: Annotators assess whether a question is understandable out-of-context, whether the answer matches the source, and whether the pair is relevant. Pairs failing any criterion are discarded; others may be rewritten for conciseness.
- **Core assumption**: Human experts with Danish cultural knowledge can reliably apply these criteria, and consensus reduces individual bias.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract]: curated for relevance and correctness; 
  - [section 3.1]: validated by multiple human annotators; some pairs removed or rewritten.
- **Break condition**: Low inter-annotator agreement (e.g., Cohen's κ < 0.6) indicates subjective criteria; or annotators consistently miss subtle errors, compromising reliability.

### Mechanism 3: Grounding in the official Kulturkanon provides authoritative, educationally relevant content
- **Claim**: Basing the benchmark on the state-endorsed Danish Culture Canon ensures the content reflects a nationally recognized core of cultural heritage, giving the benchmark legitimacy and educational relevance.
- **Mechanism**: The Kulturkanon is a curated list of 108 works selected by expert committees across eight domains, explicitly intended for cultural education. All Daisy questions derive from Wikipedia pages tied to these artifacts.
- **Core assumption**: The Kulturkanon's rigorous selection represents genuine Danish cultural competence, not just trivia.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract]: derived from the official Danish Culture Canon; 
  - [section 2]: Kulturkanon is an authoritative, curated compilation with explicit pedagogical purpose.
- **Break condition**: If the Kulturkanon is contested as unrepresentative of Denmark's diverse cultures or excludes significant cultural domains (e.g., immigrant cultures), the benchmark's scope becomes narrow and potentially biased.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Closed-ended QA benchmark design
  - **Why needed here**: Daisy uses single-correct-answer questions to enable automated evaluation via F1/BLEU. Understanding this trade-off is key: it allows scalable scoring but cannot reward nuanced, differently phrased correct answers.
  - **Quick check**: What are the trade-offs between using closed-ended QA versus open-ended generation for evaluating cultural knowledge?

- **Concept**: Cultural canon as a curated knowledge base
  - **Why needed here**: The entire benchmark derives from the Kulturkanon, so understanding how a canon shapes the definition of 'cultural knowledge' is crucial to interpreting what Daisy measures (and what it omits).
  - **Quick check**: How might using the Kulturkanon instead of a crowd-sourced list affect the types of cultural knowledge covered and the benchmark's perceived legitimacy?

- **Concept**: Low-resource language evaluation challenges
  - **Why needed here**: Danish is labeled low-resource; this context explains the poor baseline scores and motivates specialized benchmarks like Daisy. Recognizing LLM limitations in such languages is key.
  - **Quick check**: Why might a model that achieves high scores on general Danish NLP tasks still perform poorly on Daisy, despite the benchmark being in Danish?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  - KulturkanonRegistry -> WikipediaRetriever -> QuestionGenerator (Gemma3 27B) -> ValidationDashboard -> DatasetCompiler -> Daisy dataset -> EvaluationHarness -> MetricsEngine

- **Critical path**:
  1. Populate KulturkanonRegistry from official list (108 artifacts across 8 domains).
  2. For each artifact, run WikipediaRetriever; skip if page missing/incomplete (6 omitted).
  3. Execute QuestionGenerator on each retrieved page → 5 raw QA pairs (up to 540 initial pairs).
  4. Annotators use ValidationDashboard to review all pairs; export accepted set (final 741 pairs include some multi-work groupings).
  5. DatasetCompiler merges accepted pairs → Daisy v1.0.
  6. EvaluationHarness iterates questions, calls target models with PROMPT_TEMPLATE, collects answers.
  7. MetricsEngine normalizes predictions and gold answers, computes BLEU/F1, aggregates scores.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Scale vs quality: Auto-generation covers many artifacts quickly but yields noisy questions needing manual cleanup; manual creation would be smaller but cleaner.
  - Evaluation simplicity vs richness: Closed-ended answers allow automatic F1/BLEU but cannot reward nuanced correct responses differing from the gold string.
  - Canonical narrowness vs cultural breadth: Using the Kulturkanon ensures authoritative coverage of official heritage but misses popular or contemporary cultural knowledge outside the canon.
  - Quantized generator: 4-bit Gemma3 reduces hardware needs but may generate lower-quality questions, increasing validation burden.
  - Normalization heuristics: Stripping articles/punctuation increases match tolerance but risks false positives if gold expects an article.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - >30% of generated pairs rejected during validation → generation prompt or Wikipedia content inadequate.
  - Model outputs include explanatory text despite strict prompt → prompt template ineffective for that model; need prompt engineering.
  - Inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's κ) < 0.6 → validation criteria ambiguous, guidelines need refinement.
  - Significant number of Wikipedia redirects/disambiguation pages → artifact list outdated; mapping errors.
  - F1 scores near zero while BLEU is non-zero → answers share some words but not correct content; models generate plausible but wrong answers.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Baseline replication**: Use provided evaluation code to score all five models. Verify Llama 3.3 70B achieves BLEU ≥0.16 and F1 ≥0.26; if not, check prompt compatibility and normalization.
  2. **Validation impact study**: Sample 100 auto-generated QA pairs before human review. Annotate with same criteria, compare to final dataset. Quantify proportion removed vs edited; assess if edits changed answer correctness.
  3. **Prompt ablation**: For a random 50-question subset, test variations of PROMPT_TEMPLATE (e.g., adding "Answer only the exact phrase", including examples). Measure changes in answer conformity (e.g., extra text presence) and F1 scores. Identify if any model is particularly prompt-sensitive.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the Daisy benchmark be expanded beyond the Danish Culture Canon to capture a more comprehensive and diverse representation of Danish cultural heritage?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors state in Section 7 (Limitations) that Daisy is centered around the Danish Culture Canon and "does not argue that the Canon contains nor represents the whole Danish culture heritage," noting that culture is more diverse and always developing. They invite contributions to extend the dataset.
- **Why unresolved**: The benchmark's scope is inherently limited by its reliance on a single, state-defined canonical list, which may exclude marginalized, contemporary, or everyday cultural expressions.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A systematic methodology for incorporating additional cultural sources (e.g., oral histories, regional traditions, digital culture) with validation by diverse cultural stakeholders.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: To what extent do training data composition and preference alignment processes contribute to the observed deficits in LLMs' Danish cultural knowledge, as measured by Daisy?
- **Basis in paper**: In Section 5 (Discussion), the authors speculate poor performance may stem from limited representation of Danish-language and Denmark-specific content in training data, and that preference alignment and safety tuning may bias models toward cautious, generalized responses. This remains a speculative explanation without empirical isolation of these factors.
- **Why unresolved**: Baseline results show low scores even for models likely trained on Danish Wikipedia, but the relative contribution of data scarcity versus alignment effects is unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled experiments fine-tuning models on curated Danish cultural corpora with and without alignment tuning, followed by Daisy evaluation to measure separate impacts.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What evaluation methodologies (e.g., prompt design, answer normalization) are most effective for assessing cultural knowledge in LLMs, and how can they be standardized across different models and cultures?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors acknowledge in Section 7 that their "single prompt evaluation" uses a template that "is not necessarily optimal for all models" and they plan to iterate on it, calling for community engagement.
- **Why unresolved**: The current evaluation relies on a single prompt template and a specific normalization strategy (case/punctuation/article removal). Their effectiveness for cultural QA—which may involve nuanced phrasing, naming conventions, or temporal expressions—has not been systematically validated.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A meta-evaluation studying multiple prompt formulations and normalization rules across a Daisy subset, measuring consistency in model scores and correlating with human judgments of answer correctness.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How can culturally-grounded benchmarks like Daisy avoid conflating factual, stereotypical, and genuinely interpretive cultural knowledge while maintaining operational validity?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors critique the DaKultur benchmark for including questions that elicit "stereotypical behavioral patterns" or "broad generalizations" rather than deeper cultural competence. They position Daisy as focusing on "factual knowledge regarding cultural heritage," but the line between factual recall and cultural interpretation remains blurry for canonized works.
- **Why unresolved**: Daisy’s questions are derived from Wikipedia summaries of canonical artifacts, which may emphasize objective facts over cultural context, values, or evolving interpretations. Without clarity on what dimension of "cultural knowledge" is measured, results risk misrepresenting competence.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A qualitative analysis of Daisy items coded for knowledge type (e.g., factual, conceptual, evaluative) and an assessment of how different item types correlate with model performance, alongside expert review to ensure items reflect culturally meaningful understanding rather than trivia.

## Limitations
- Dataset size is relatively small (741 pairs), which may limit statistical power and generalizability.
- Closed-ended metrics (F1/BLEU) penalize valid paraphrases, potentially under-scoring nuanced cultural understanding.
- Reproduction is hindered by missing details: exact normalization rules (which articles/punctuation removed), generation hyperparameters, and prompt formatting specifics.
- The Kulturkanon's curated selection may not reflect Denmark's diverse, contemporary culture and could overlook marginalized perspectives.

## Confidence
- **Auto-generation mix produces central/peripheral questions**: Medium (no evidence on actual peripheral question proportion in final set).
- **Multi-annotator validation ensures dataset quality**: Low (no inter-annotator agreement metrics reported; process underspecified).
- **Canonical grounding provides authoritative content**: High (Kulturkanon is an official state-endorsed source with clear pedagogical intent).
- **Reported baseline scores are reproducible**: Low (critical hyperparameters and normalization details are missing).
- **LLM deficits stem from data scarcity and alignment**: Medium (hypothesized in discussion but not empirically isolated).

## Next Checks
1. Analyze the pre-validation auto-generated QA set to quantify the distribution of question difficulty and verify the presence of both central and peripheral knowledge items.
2. Compute inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's κ) on a held-out validation subset; if κ < 0.6, the dataset's reliability is questionable.
3. Perform a normalization ablation study: re-evaluate a model subset with alternative preprocessing (e.g., no article removal, different punctuation handling) to measure score volatility and identify the most stable metric configuration.
