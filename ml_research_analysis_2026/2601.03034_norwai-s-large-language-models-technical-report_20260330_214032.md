# NorwAI's Large Language Models: Technical Report

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.03034  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03034  
- **Reference Count:** 20  
- **Primary Result:** Developed open-source Norwegian/Scandinavian LLMs (350M–45B params) that achieve SOTA on NLEBench, top 7B results in NorEval, and instruction-tuned Mixtral outperforms GPT-4 in human-evaluated Norwegian news summarization.

## Executive Summary
The NorwAI project tackles the severe underrepresentation of Norwegian in large language models by building a suite of open-source models specifically for Norwegian and Scandinavian languages. Their approach centers on a scaled Norwegian corpus (25B–88.45B tokens), a Norwegian-extended SentencePiece tokenizer, and a rigorous post-training pipeline including instruction tuning and RLHF. The resulting models, spanning from 350 million to 45 billion parameters, demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on the newly introduced NLEBench benchmark. Notably, their instruction-tuned NorwAI-Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct was judged superior to both GPT-4 and professional journalist summaries in a human evaluation of news summarization, validating the quality of Norwegian generation. All models are publicly released to empower Nordic research and practical applications.

## Method Summary
The team employed a multi-stage training strategy. First, models were either pretrained from scratch or continually pretrained on the evolving NorLLM_Corpus (V1–V3), which grew from 25B to 88.45B tokens through the inclusion of high-quality Norwegian sources like news archives (NRK, Schibsted) and cultural repositories (CulturaX, HPLT), with increasing emphasis on Nynorsk and Sámi. A Norwegian-extended tokenizer (vocabulary 64k–158k) was trained on this corpus to efficiently represent Norwegian morphology. Post-training involved supervised instruction tuning on a curated 5k-sample Norwegian dataset, followed by task-specific LoRA adapters. For summarization, an RLHF stage used a NorBERT-based semantic similarity reward model to align outputs with human-preferred news summary style.

## Key Results
- NorwAI-Mistral-7B achieved top-ranked performance among 7B-parameter models on the NorEval benchmark.
- Instruction-tuned NorwAI-Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct surpassed GPT-4 and professional journalist summaries in a human evaluation of Norwegian news summarization.
- The suite of models (dense and MoE) is openly released under permissive licenses for the Nordic research community.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Norwegian-extended subword tokenization
- **Claim:** A Norwegian-optimized tokenizer improves linguistic coverage and modeling efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** Training a SentencePiece unigram model with a large vocabulary (64k–158k) on the Norwegian corpus captures rich morphology and dialectal variation, reducing OOV rates and token sequence length compared to English-centric tokenizers.
- **Core assumption:** Language-specific vocabulary optimization yields more efficient text representation and downstream task performance.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "...using a Norwegian-extended tokenizer..."  
  [section 3.1] "Tokenizers are trained using SentencePiece with vocabulary sizes scaling from 64k to 158k tokens, allowing efficient representation of Norwegian morphology and dialectal variation."  
  [corpus] Tokenizer design is explicitly tied to the curated corpus in Section 3.2; the assumption is that optimized Norwegian tokens drive gains over off-the-shelf tokenizers.
- **Break condition:** OOV rates remain high (>5%) or common Norwegian compounds are excessively fragmented, nullifying efficiency gains.

### Mechanism 2: Staged training pipeline
- **Claim:** Sequential pretraining → instruction tuning → RLHF builds general competence, task alignment, and human preference alignment.
- **Mechanism:** Massive pretraining learns general Norwegian statistics; supervised instruction tuning on 5k samples teaches task formats; RLHF with a NorBERT-based reward fine-tunes stylistic preferences (e.g., for news summaries).
- **Core assumption:** Each stage addresses a distinct capability gap, and the sequence is critical—alignment without a strong base model is ineffective.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "...pretrained or continually pretrained... instruction-tuned variants... RLHF."  
  [section 3.3] Details the three-stage process, including the 5k instruction set and NorBERT reward for summarization.  
  [section 4.2.2] MAUVE scores improve after RLHF, indicating better alignment to human writing distribution.
- **Break condition:** Catastrophic forgetting of general Norwegian knowledge during post-training, or reward model misalignment (semantic similarity ≠ human preference for style/coherence).

### Mechanism 3: Corpus curation and evolution
- **Claim:** Systematic improvement of corpus quality and linguistic diversity (especially Nynorsk/Sámi) directly enhances task performance and reduces language bias.
- **Mechanism:** Replacing noisy web-crawl with curated news/transcriptions (Schibsted, NRK) and adding structured repositories (CulturaX, HPLT) increases domain relevance. Explicitly expanding minority language text (Nynorsk: 1.1%→5.2%) boosts competence on those variants.
- **Core assumption:** Data quality and balanced representation are primary determinants of full-spectrum Norwegian language capability.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.2] Corpus evolution (V1→V2→V3) with token counts and source shifts; Figure 1 shows declining "Others" and rising industry/Nynorsk/Sámi proportions.  
  [section 4.2.5] Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct (trained on V3) outperforms GPT-4 on Schibsted's news summarization, suggesting effective integration of partner domain data.  
  [corpus] Lists specific high-quality sources (Mímir, NCC, NRK). *Assumption:* Performance leap on partner tasks is causally linked to inclusion of their data.
- **Break condition:** High-quality data volume is insufficient to offset removal of other sources, or minority language data remains too sparse relative to Bokmål.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Subword Tokenization (e.g., SentencePiece)**  
  *Why needed:* The entire training efficiency and morphological representation hinges on the Norwegian-extended tokenizer. Understanding BPE/Unigram algorithms and vocabulary training explains why a custom tokenizer was created.  
  *Quick check:* How does vocabulary size (e.g., 64k vs 158k) trade off between representing rare compounds efficiently and maintaining manageable sequence length?

- **Concept: Multi-stage LLM Training Pipeline (Pretraining → SFT → RLHF)**  
  *Why needed:* The NorwAI results hinge on this sequence; understanding each stage's distinct goal (statistical learning, instruction following, preference alignment) is key to interpreting evaluations.  
  *Quick check:* What is a key risk of applying RLHF to a model that has undergone minimal supervised instruction tuning (SFT)?

- **Concept: Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Architecture**  
  *Why needed:* NorwAI-Mixtral uses MoE for scaling (47B params) with efficient inference (2 experts/token). Grasping sparsity is essential for predicting resource needs and suitability for long-context tasks.  
  *Quick check:* For a fixed compute budget, what is the primary advantage and primary challenge of an MoE model (e.g., 8x7B) versus a dense model with similar activated parameter count?

## Architecture Onboarding
Tokenizer -> Base Model -> Adapters (LoRA) -> Inference Backend

**Critical path:** For a new Norwegian task, load the appropriate base model (e.g., `NorwAI-Mistral-7B-base`). Then decide: use an existing instruct-tuned variant (e.g., `-instruct`) for general tasks, or apply a task-specific LoRA adapter on the base model for a specialized domain.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Model Size vs. Resources:** 3B runs on consumer GPUs (<16GB); 7B dense needs ~24GB; MoE/Magistral require multi-GPU or high-memory (64GB+). Quantization (GGUF Q4–Q8) trades minor precision for major memory savings.
- **Corpus Version vs. Task:** V2 (Mistral/Llama2/Mixtral) excels in general/Bokmål; V3 (Magistral) has better Nynorsk/Sámi coverage. Choose based on target variant.
- **Dense vs. MoE:** Dense (Mistral) has lower latency per token. MoE (Mixtral) offers higher quality/parameter efficiency for complex generation but has higher memory bandwidth and potential load imbalance.

**Failure signatures:**
- High OOV/poor generation: Default English tokenizer used instead of Norwegian-extended.
- Poor Nynorsk/Sámi performance: Using V2-based model (Mistral/Llama2) instead of V3 Magistral.
- Low throughput serving: Using naive `transformers.generate()` for MoE/Magistral instead of `vLLM` or omitting quantization for edge deployment.
- Inappropriate output style: Using base (`-base`) model for conversational tasks instead of instruct-tuned variant.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Load `NorwAI-Mistral-7B-Instruct` with `device_map="auto"` and generate a response to `"Hva er Norges største fjell?"`. Verify coherent Norwegian output.
2. Load `NorwAI-Mistral-7B-base`, then apply a publicly available LoRA adapter (e.g., from `NorGLM` collection) for a specific task like medical QA. Compare style/accuracy to base model.
3. Run a subset of NLEBench's NO-QNLI or NO-BoolQ tasks for two variants (e.g., `NorwAI-Mistral-7B-instruct` vs `NorGPT-3B`). Observe performance delta and compute cost difference.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How effectively does the Norwegian-extended tokenizer (vocabulary sizes 64k–158k) handle morphological and dialectal variations across Bokmål, Nynorsk, and regional dialects in terms of tokenization efficiency and downstream task performance? *Basis:* Tokenizer is designed for morphology/dialects (Section 3.2) but no empirical dialect-specific evaluation is provided.
- **Open Question 2:** What is the optimal strategy for merging multiple LoRA adapters (e.g., instruction-tuning, summarization, cultural QA) to maximize multi-task performance without catastrophic forgetting? *Basis:* Paper describes adapters as modular (Section 3.3.1) but does not investigate merging methods.
- **Open Question 3:** Does reward modeling based on semantic similarity (using NorBERT) to gold summaries produce outputs more human-preferred for Norwegian news summarization compared to traditional RLHF with explicit human preference rankings? *Basis:* RLHF pipeline uses NorBERT similarity (Section 3.3.2) but no comparative study against standard RLHF is conducted.
- **Open Question 4:** Given extremely low Sámi text representation (0.155% in V3), what targeted data augmentation or transfer learning strategies can improve Sámi performance without degrading Norwegian (Bokmål/Nynorsk) capabilities? *Basis:* Corpus expands Sámi marginally (Figure 1B) but no Sámi-specific evaluation is reported.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter opacity:** Exact pretraining configurations (learning rates, batch sizes, data mixing ratios) are undisclosed, hindering precise replication; assumptions of standard settings may not hold.
- **RLHF reward validity:** Using NorBERT cosine similarity to reference summaries may not capture human preferences for style, coherence, or factuality, risking reward hacking.
- **Causality uncertainty:** Performance gains are correlated with corpus/tokenizer improvements but confounded by architecture/scale changes; lack of ablation studies prevents isolating primary drivers.

## Confidence
- **Tokenizer efficacy (Mechanism 1):** High. Direct evidence via tokenizer design and corpus analysis; OOV rates are a standard diagnostic.
- **Staged pipeline necessity (Mechanism 2):** Medium. Evidence from MAUVE scores and standard practice, but lacks ablations showing failure when stages are omitted.
- **Corpus evolution causality (Mechanism 3):** Low. Performance correlates with corpus version, but confounding factors (architecture, scale) prevent causal attribution.

## Next Checks
1. **Tokenizer efficiency audit:** Compute OOV rates and average tokens/word for the Norwegian-extended tokenizer versus a standard multilingual tokenizer (e.g., Llama2) on a held-out, dialectally diverse corpus. A sub-5% OOV and <10% longer sequences for common compounds would validate the claim.
2. **RLHF reward alignment test:** Run a paired comparison with human annotators on summaries from the RLHF-tuned model versus the SFT-only model. Measure Spearman correlation between NorBERT reward scores and human preferences; correlation >0.7 would support reward validity.
3. **Minority language performance tracking:** Evaluate the V3 Magistral model on dedicated Nynorsk and Sámi test suites (e.g., from Norwegian Language Bank). Track performance per variant against training data proportion; disproportionate Bokmål gains would indicate persistent bias.
