# From Intent to Evidence: A Categorical Approach for Structural Evaluation of Deep Research Agents

## Quick Facts
*   arXiv ID: 2603.25342
*   Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25342
*   Reference count: 31
*   Primary result: A new theoretically-grounded benchmark reveals top deep research agents achieve only ~20% average accuracy, with universal failure on multi-hop structural synthesis tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of theoretical foundation for evaluating deep research agents (DRAs). It introduces a formal model using category theory, representing the research workflow as functors between categories of intent, web knowledge, retrieval, and reasoning. Based on this model, the authors construct a mechanism-aware benchmark with 296 questions targeting four specific structural reasoning axes. Systematic evaluation of 11 state-of-the-art models shows a persistent low baseline; even the best model, Grok Deep Research, scores only 19.9% on average. Results indicate a critical gap: advanced DRAs handle topological re-ordering and ontological verification but universally fail on tasks requiring compositional multi-hop synthesis, suggesting reliance on brittle heuristics rather than robust structural reasoning.

## Method Summary
The paper formalizes a DRA's operation as a composition of functors mapping between categories that represent successive stages of research: from user **Intent** to retrieved **Web Knowledge**, through **Retrieval** actions, to final **Reasoning** and **Evidence**. This categorical blueprint directly informs a benchmark designed to stress-test specific structural properties. The benchmark's 296 questions are engineered to probe four axes: sequential connectivity chains (long-hop synthesis), V-structure pullbacks (evidence convergence), substructure re-ordering (topological flexibility), and Yoneda ontological probes (conceptual consistency). Model outputs are evaluated against ground-truth structural requirements, yielding scores for Search (S) and Reasoning (R) components.

## Key Results
*   **Persistently low baseline:** Average accuracy across 11 models is below 20%, with Grok Deep Research the top performer at 19.9%.
*   ** stark performance dichotomy:** Models match pure reasoning systems on re-ordering and ontological tasks but collapse completely on multi-hop synthesis (e.g., Task 13).
*   **Component-specific failure:** Most models score near zero on both S and R scores; Grok Deep Research is a significant outlier with S-score 11.8 and R-score 21.6.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The categorical formalism works by decoupling *what* a DRA must achieve (the structural composition of evidence) from *how* it is implemented. By modeling the research pipeline as a functorial composition, the framework makes explicit the necessary transformations and invariants between abstract stages (intent, knowledge, retrieval, reasoning). This allows for the design of evaluation tasks that directly test whether those transformations preserve required structural properties, such as connectivity or pullback consistency, independent of surface-level content. The benchmark’s targeted axes are direct instantiations of these categorical constraints.

## Foundational Learning
1.  **Category & Functor:** A category is a collection of objects and morphisms; a functor maps between categories, preserving composition. *Why needed:* To formally model the transformation from one stage of research (e.g., intent) to the next (e.g., retrieved snippets). *Quick check:* Can you define a functor from a "Query" category to a "Snippet" category?
2.  **Natural Transformation:** A mapping between functors that commutes with the functorial structure. *Why needed:* To model the coherent refinement or adaptation of a retrieval strategy as intent evolves. *Quick check:* What would a natural transformation between two different retrieval functors look like?
3.  **Pullback (V-structure):** A limit construction capturing the "inverse image" of two morphisms with a common codomain. *Why needed:* To formalize tasks requiring evidence from two independent paths that must converge on a shared fact. *Quick check:* Design a research question where two distinct searches must yield a common supporting document.
4.  **Yoneda Lemma:** A fundamental result embedding a category into a functor category, relating objects to their relationships with all others. *Why needed:* To probe ontological consistency—whether an agent's representation of a concept is stable across different retrieval contexts. *Quick check:* How would you test if an agent's understanding of "mitochondria" is consistent across biology and history queries?
5.  **Compositionality:** The principle that complex structures are built from simpler, composable parts. *Why needed:* The entire benchmark tests whether DRAs can build long, correct evidence chains from atomic retrieval steps. *Quick check:* Is a 5-hop chain simply 5 independent 1-hop tasks, or does it introduce new combinatorial failure modes?
6.  **Functorial Composition:** Sequencing functors end-to-end (F ∘ G ∘ H). *Why needed:* The research workflow is a pipeline; evaluating the composed functor tests end-to-end integrity. *Quick check:* If functor G fails on a particular input, what does that imply for the composite F ∘ G ∘ H's output?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** User Intent -> Retrieval Functor -> Web Knowledge Category -> Reasoning Functor -> Evidence Output
*   **Critical Path:** The core evaluation follows the functorial composition: a query (Intent) triggers a retrieval process (Functor) that populates a knowledge space (Category), which is then processed by a reasoning engine (Functor) to produce a structured answer (Evidence). Failure at any functor corrupts the final composite.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** The formalism prioritizes **structural clarity** over implementation fidelity. It abstracts away neural architectures, prompting strategies, and tool use details to isolate logical composition failures. This tradeoff risks missing nuances of real-world, stochastic agent behaviors that don't fit a deterministic categorical mold.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    1.  **Multi-hop collapse:** Accuracy plummets on chains requiring >2 compositional steps (e.g., Task 13), indicating broken functorial composition.
    2.  **Task-specific brittleness:** Absolute failure on isolated tasks (e.g., Task 10) suggests overfitting to superficial heuristics rather than learning the target structure.
    3.  **Search-Reasoning decoupling:** Near-zero S and R scores for most models show they cannot coordinate retrieval and reasoning as a unified functorial process.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Isolate the Retrieval Functor:** Disable the reasoning stage; evaluate if retrieved snippets alone satisfy the structural constraints (e.g., does the set of snippets contain all nodes for a required 4-chain?).
    2.  **Swap Knowledge Categories:** Replace the "Web Knowledge" category's objects with a noisy, synthetic counterpart. Measure degradation in final accuracy to quantify robustness of the reasoning functor.
    3.  **Force a Non-Commutative Diagram:** Design a task where two different paths (A->B->C vs A->D->C) must yield consistent intermediate evidence. Test if the agent's composed functor respects this commutativity.

## Limitations
*   **Generality of formalism:** The functorial model may not capture non-compositional, stochastic, or highly interactive research behaviors.
*   **Benchmark scope:** 296 structurally-targeted questions may not represent the full diversity and open-endedness of real-world research tasks.
*   **Model coverage:** Evaluation is limited to 11 primarily commercial/API models; performance of open-source or specialized architectures is unknown.

## Confidence
*   **High:** Empirical finding of low average accuracy (<20%) and large performance variance across structural axes, based on systematic evaluation.
*   **Medium:** The categorical framework is a compelling *theoretical foundation* for structural evaluation, but its adequacy for all DRA behaviors remains untested.
*   **Low:** The claim that "all DRAs" collapse on multi-hop synthesis, given limited model diversity and benchmark scope.
*   **Medium:** Attribution of failures to "brittle heuristics" is consistent with observed patterns but lacks mechanistic ablation studies for proof.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-domain validation:** Apply the categorical benchmark to non-web agents (e.g., scientific literature synthesis, code repair) to test generalizability of the formalism and failure modes.
2.  **Mechanistic ablation:** For catastrophically failing tasks (e.g., Task 13), run controlled interventions: freeze search, constrain reasoning steps, or provide oracle snippets to isolate whether failure stems from retrieval, reasoning, or their composition.
3.  **Scalability stress-test:** Extend benchmark to longer chains (6+ hops) and environments with deliberate retrieval noise or distractors to map the degradation trajectory of each model's composed functor.
