# The ARC of Progress towards AGI: A Living Survey of Abstraction and Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13372
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13372
- **Reference Count:** 10
- **Primary Result:** All 82 surveyed approaches show a consistent 2-3x performance drop from ARC-AGI-1 to ARC-AGI-2, indicating a fundamental unsolved challenge in compositional generalization despite refinement loops and test-time adaptation.

## Executive Summary
This living survey analyzes 82 AI systems on the ARC-AGI benchmark for fluid intelligence. Top systems achieve near-human performance on ARC-AGI-1 (93.0%) but collapse on deeper compositions in ARC-AGI-2 (68.8%) and ARC-AGI-3 (13%), while humans remain near-perfect. Success correlates strongly with iterative refinement loops and test-time adaptation, which provide ~20-30 point gains. However, a critical gap remains: no architecture implements robust hierarchical decomposition for systematic compositional generalization, causing exponential search space growth and catastrophic failure as reasoning depth increases. One-year cost-per-task fell 390x, but this reflects reduced parallelism rather than improved algorithmic efficiency.

## Method Summary
The survey audits performance across 82 published approaches (program synthesis, neuro-symbolic, neural) on ARC-AGI-1, -2, and -3. It correlates accuracy with architectural features (e.g., refinement loops, test-time adaptation), computational cost, and error patterns. A minimum viable reproduction involves: (1) setting up the public ARC evaluation, (2) implementing a baseline solver (e.g., program enumeration or LLM-based), and (3) adding iterative refinement with verification against demonstrations. Key unknowns blocking exact replication include proprietary model details, hyperparameters for refinement, and inconsistent cost calculation methodologies.

## Key Results
- **Consistent Performance Cliff:** All paradigms exhibit a 2-3x drop from ARC-AGI-1 to ARC-AGI-2, highlighting a systematic failure in compositional depth generalization.
- **Refinement Loops Are Critical:** Every system scoring >70% on ARC-AGI-1 uses iterative candidate generation/verification/correction, providing ~20-30pt gains over frozen models.
- **Efficiency Gap Persists:** Humans solve tasks with ~20W in seconds; top AI requires massive test-time compute (e.g., parallel sampling of thousands of candidates), and cost reductions stem from scaling down parallelism, not algorithmic compression.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Iterative Refinement Loops
- **Claim:** Systems that employ iterative cycles of candidate generation, verification against task examples, and hypothesis correction achieve significantly higher performance.
- **Mechanism:** The system maintains a hypothesis space, proposes candidate solutions (programs, transformations), evaluates each against demonstration pairs, and uses feedback to guide the next generation iteration. This turns the task into a guided search problem.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] identifies refinement loops as critical; [section 4.5] shows all 2025 winners used refinement; [corpus] includes implementations like TRM's recursive latent refinement and LLaDA's iterative denoising.
- **Break Condition:** Effectiveness degrades catastrophically when required compositional depth exceeds the loop's ability to prune the hypothesis space.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical Compositional Decomposition (Absent)
- **Claim:** Current systems lack a robust mechanism for hierarchical decomposition of multi-step transformations, leading to exponential search growth and failure on deeper compositions.
- **Mechanism:** Human reasoning breaks complex transformations into a hierarchy of sub-problems, solves each with appropriate abstractions, and composes solutions via structured interfaces. Current systems treat composition as linear concatenation (O(n^d) complexity).
- **Evidence:** [section 3.3.2] states no current architecture implements explicit hierarchical reasoning; [section 5.3.1] identifies catastrophic failure beyond shallow depth and insufficiency of brute-force search.
- **Break Condition:** The observed performance cliff itself—collapse rather than graceful degradation with increased depth.

### Mechanism 3: Test-Time Adaptation / Fine-Tuning
- **Claim:** Adapting parameters or search strategy during inference on the specific task's few examples unlocks latent reasoning capabilities in pretrained models.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of a frozen model, the system performs gradient updates, prompt optimization, or dynamically adjusts its search heuristic based on task demonstrations, specializing broad pretrained knowledge to the current rule.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] highlights test-time adaptation; [section 3.2] notes it's present in every >70% system with ~20-30pt gains; [section 4.4] example: MindsAI's jump from 5% zero-shot to 58% with test-time fine-tuning.
- **Break Condition:** Diminishing or negative returns when distribution shift is too great (ARC-AGI-1→2) or adaptation cannot learn correct abstraction from minimal examples.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fluid vs. Crystallized Intelligence:** ARC-AGI measures *fluid* intelligence (novel problem-solving with minimal prior knowledge), not *crystallized* (applying memorized knowledge). This explains why knowledge-heavy benchmark gains don't transfer.
  - *Quick Check:* Does the system solve by inferring a novel rule from 3-5 examples, or by applying a stored pattern?
- **Core Knowledge Priors:** Humans solve ARC tasks using innate, domain-general abilities (object permanence, basic geometry). A system must have equivalents to even frame the problem correctly.
  - *Quick Check:* Does the solution process rely on flexible understanding of "object," "connectedness," or "symmetry" independent of visual textures?
- **Efficiency as Diagnostic:** Human-level intelligence implies *compression* and *understanding*, not brute-force. High-cost correct outputs may not demonstrate fluid intelligence.
  - *Quick Check:* Is success due to discovering a compact abstraction or executing vast task-specific search?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** Input Encoder -> Hypothesis Generator -> Verification/Evaluator <-> Refinement Controller -> Output Decoder
- **Critical Path:**
  1. Encode demonstration grids into state S.
  2. Generate initial hypothesis H₀ from S.
  3. Loop: Verify Hᵢ on demos -> get feedback Fᵢ.
  4. If Fᵢ is perfect (exact match), break and apply Hᵢ to test input.
  5. Else, Hᵢ₊₁ = Refine(Hᵢ, Fᵢ, S) and repeat.
  6. Decode final Hₖ to output grid.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - **Search Breadth vs. Depth:** Many diverse candidates vs. deep refinement of few. Breadth risks blowup; depth risks local optima.
  - **Representation Flexibility vs. Grounding:** Free-form code/LLM outputs are expressive but hard to verify; constrained primitive sets enable efficient search but may lack expressivity.
  - **Test-Time Compute vs. Prior Knowledge:** Heavy search can compensate weak priors but hits efficiency walls; strong priors may reduce test-time needs but risk superficial pattern matching.
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - **Catastrophic composition failure:** >50% drop from ARC-AGI-1 to ARC-AGI-2.
  - **High-cost brittle success:** >80% on ARC-AGI-1 only with >$100/task, sharply dropping with budget cuts.
  - **Error inconsistency:** Failures not clustered by task family or reasoning type, indicating incoherent abstraction strategy.
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. **Ablate refinement loop:** Compare accuracy with and without iterative correction on ARC-AGI-1; expect >20pt gain if loop functional.
  2. **Cost-performance profiling:** For fixed accuracy (e.g., 50%), vary refinement budget (iterations, samples). Plot accuracy vs. cost to find diminishing returns knee.
  3. **Compositional transfer stress test:** For a >70% ARC-AGI-1 system, evaluate on ARC-AGI-2 tasks that are exact compositions of ARC-1 primitives. Sharp drop confirms missing hierarchical decomposition.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **OQ1:** How can ARC-AGI scores be mechanistically interpreted? Scores lack linkage to underlying computational mechanisms; similar accuracy may arise from brute-force search or true abstraction. Resolution requires correlating performance with efficiency, adaptability, and error patterns across ARC versions.
- **OQ2:** Why does current AI rely on scaling rather than compression for reasoning? Objectives optimize for accuracy, not discovery of minimal abstractions; no architectures explicitly reward compression. Resolution: human-level accuracy with near-human computational efficiency (<100× human energy cost).
- **OQ3:** Do iterative refinement loops constitute genuine intelligence or brute-force approximation? Refinement improves scores but often with high compute; unclear if it captures human reasoning fluidity and efficiency. Resolution: refinement solving ARC-AGI-2/3 with human-like efficiency (seconds, watts) and robustness to deeper composition.
- **OQ4:** What architectural mechanisms enable hierarchical decomposition to avoid exponential search? Systems treat composition as sequential concatenation, causing blowup; humans use hierarchical decomposition. Resolution: a system maintaining stable performance on ARC-AGI-2 without catastrophic degradation using explicit hierarchical planning with sublinear compute growth relative to depth.

## Limitations
- Proprietary details of top systems (training data, hyperparameters, search budgets) prevent exact replication and cross-system comparison.
- Inconsistent cost calculation methodology (API pricing vs. full overhead) invalidates direct efficiency claims.
- Risk of overfitting to public ARC-AGI-1 via cherry-picking <100 tasks inflates reported accuracy by 20-30%.

## Confidence
- **High:** Refinement loops/test-time adaptation are critical; humans remain near-perfect; compositional reasoning unsolved.
- **Medium:** Consistent 2-3x drop across all 82 paradigms; 390x cost reduction in one year (methodology unclear).
- **Low:** None explicitly; all major claims have at minimum medium confidence.
