# Unbiased Multimodal Reranking for Long-Tail Short-Video Search
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24975
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24975
- **Reference Count:** 35
- **Primary Result:** Offline NDCG@1 0.849, human preference +11.7%; online long-tail CTR +1.24%, LVR +1.67%.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles bias in short-video search for long-tail queries, where sparse user interactions amplify clickbait and shallow content. The authors propose an LLM-driven multimodal reranking framework that uses two-stage training: supervised fine-tuning on multimodal annotations (titles, ASR, OCR, visuals) to assess quality, followed by pairwise preference optimization to learn comparable experience scores. These scores promote high-quality underexposed videos and guide page-level reinforcement learning. Offline, the model achieves high NDCG and human preference gains; online A/B testing shows improved engagement metrics for long-tail and all queries.

## Method Summary
The method involves a two-stage training pipeline. First, a quality assessment model is fine-tuned using supervised annotations derived from multiple modalities—textual (title, ASR, OCR), and visual features—to predict video quality. Second, pairwise preference optimization is applied, where the model learns to rank videos based on comparable experience scores, leveraging human or LLM-generated preferences. These scores are then used in a page-level reinforcement learning setup to optimize the overall ranking of video lists, balancing click-through with long-term user satisfaction and diversity.

## Key Results
- ExpModel achieves NDCG@1 0.849 offline, with a human preference advantage of +11.7% over baselines.
- Online A/B test (15% traffic): long-tail queries show IQRR -1.28%, CTR +1.24%, LVR +1.67%.
- All queries also exhibit improvement in the same online metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach reduces long-tail bias by decoupling quality assessment from sparse click data. Multimodal signals provide a richer, more objective representation of video content, allowing the model to identify high-quality videos that lack engagement history. Pairwise preference optimization directly learns from comparative judgments, which better capture nuanced user preferences than pointwise relevance labels. Page-level reinforcement learning optimizes for holistic page experience (e.g., diversity, depth) rather than per-video clicks, mitigating the amplification of clickbait. Together, these elements promote underexposed but valuable content.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Fusion**  
  *Why needed:* Combines complementary signals (text, audio, visual) for robust quality assessment when any single modality may be noisy or incomplete.  
  *Quick check:* Ablate each modality and measure NDCG drop; fuse late vs. early.

- **Pairwise Preference Optimization**  
  *Why needed:* Models relative quality without requiring absolute scores, aligning better with human judgment and handling ambiguous relevance.  
  *Quick check:* Evaluate held-out preference pairs; compare to pointwise regression.

- **Reinforcement Learning for Ranking**  
  *Why needed:* Optimizes long-term user engagement and page-level diversity beyond immediate clicks.  
  *Quick check:* Simulate user sessions; compare cumulative reward vs. greedy baseline.

- **Long-tail Bias Mitigation**  
  *Why needed:* Addresses sparsity in user behavior data that leads to popular/clickbait bias.  
  *Quick check:* Segment queries by frequency; measure performance disparity.

- **LLM-driven Annotation**  
  *Why needed:* Scales quality assessment labels when human annotation is costly.  
  *Quick check:* Sample LLM vs. human labels; compute agreement.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Multimodal Encoders (Text/Visual/Audio) -> Quality Assessment Model (SFT) -> Preference Scoring -> RL Reranker -> Ranked Video List

**Critical Path:**  
Raw video input → encode title, ASR, OCR, frames → fuse multimodal features → predict quality score → adjust via pairwise preferences → apply RL policy to order whole page → output final ranking.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- LLM annotation scalability vs. label noise/consistency.  
- Multimodal fusion complexity vs. inference latency.  
- Offline metric optimization vs. online engagement signals.  
- RL sample efficiency vs. stability in large action spaces.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Degraded performance on videos with erroneous ASR/OCR.  
- Overfitting to training distribution (e.g., specific content genres).  
- RL policy collapse to short-term click optimization.  
- Modality imbalance (e.g., visual overfitting at text's expense).

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Modality ablation: Remove each modality (text, visual, audio) to measure impact on NDCG@1.  
2. Preference loss sensitivity: Vary pairwise margin in loss function and track human preference accuracy.  
3. RL reward shaping: Compare reward functions weighting CTR, dwell time, and diversity.

## Limitations
- Human evaluation lacks details on rater pool size, expertise, and inter-annotator agreement.
- "Long-tail" query threshold and sparsity metric are undefined, hindering reproducibility.
- Multimodal annotation process and quality control consistency are unspecified.

## Confidence
- **Offline NDCG@1 (0.849):** High (standard metric, but dataset undisclosed).  
- **Online CTR/LVR gains:** Medium (A/B size adequate, but long-tail definition opacity).  
- **Multimodal preference optimization:** Medium (method plausible, human eval grounding weak).

## Next Checks

2. Replicate long-tail definition: Request precise query-frequency cutoff and sparsity measures.
3. Validate annotation quality: Sample original multimodal labels to assess cross-modal alignment errors.
