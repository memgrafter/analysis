# From Chaos to Clarity: Schema-Constrained AI for Auditable Biomedical Evidence Extraction from Full-Text PDFs
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.14267  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14267  
- **Reference Count:** 14  
- **Primary Result:** A schema-constrained AI pipeline achieves stable, fully automated extraction of auditable evidence from heterogeneous biomedical PDFs, with expert-verified correctness gains of 62 percentage points after schema refinement.

## Executive Summary
Biomedical evidence synthesis is bottlenecked by the manual effort required to extract structured data from full-text PDFs, which suffer from layout inconsistencies, OCR errors, and a lack of traceable provenance. This paper presents an auditable, schema-constrained pipeline that transforms entire corpora of PDFs into analysis-ready datasets. The system uses deterministic chunking, asynchronous OCR under strict rate limits, and domain-specific typed schemas with controlled vocabularies. Every extraction is bound to sentence-level evidence, enabling efficient expert verification. Evaluated on 734 DOAC measurement studies, the pipeline processed all documents without manual intervention, achieved high proxy transcription quality (median 98.6%), and demonstrated that iterative schema refinement substantially improves extraction fidelity, making large-scale evidence synthesis more reliable and efficient.

## Method Summary
The pipeline ingests PDFs, assigns canonical IDs via SHA-1 hashing, and splits documents into non-overlapping page chunks (default 8 pages). Each chunk and caption text unit is sent asynchronously to a Mistral OCR service, respecting configurable concurrency (default 3) and rate limits (default 5 RPS). Extractions are guided by multiple domain-specific Pydantic schemas that enforce field types, controlled enumerations, and mandatory evidence subfields (verbatim justifying sentences). Chunk-level results for the same document are merged: scalar fields require agreement across all non-null values (conflicts are flagged), while list-valued fields are aggregated via set operations. The consolidated records, along with caption-aware markdown reconstructions, are exported to CSV/Parquet. A persistent state index allows resumable processing by skipping already-completed source keys.

## Key Results
- Fully automated processing of 734 DOAC measurement studies without manual intervention.
- Stable throughput of 3.1 completed requests/sec under a 5 RPS constraint.
- High proxy transcription quality (median 98.6%) and >99% population of evidence fields.
- After iterative schema refinement, expert evaluation on 50 studies showed clinical outcome definition correctness rise from 33% to 95% and assay classification from 70% to 95%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Typed schemas with closed vocabularies prevent over-inference and reduce hallucination.
- **Mechanism:** By restricting model outputs to predefined field types and enumerations, the system eliminates plausible but unsupported values.
- **Core Assumption:** Domain experts can define comprehensive schemas for the synthesis task.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "extraction decisions are bound to sentence-level evidence"; [section 3.3] "schema description[s] encode explicit hierarchies" and "explicit negative rules"; iterative refinement improved fidelity, confirming initial schema gaps are closable.
- **Break Condition:** Novel information outside schema enumerations (e.g., an unlisted assay type) leads to null fields or misclassification unless "other/specify" options exist.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Sentence-level evidence binding creates auditability by citing explicit source text for each classification.
- **Mechanism:** Models must return verbatim sentence(s) justifying each extracted categorical/complex value, enabling verification without full document re-read.
- **Core Assumption:** Relevant evidence for synthesis-critical constructs appears in isolatable narrative sentences; tables/figures without captions are secondary.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "sentence-level evidence for traceability"; [section 3.3.2] "For each high-level classification... there is an associated 'sentence from text' field"; evidence populated for nearly all decisions, but missing evidence linked to dense tables.
- **Break Condition:** Critical information exists solely in table cells or figure graphics without narrative text, causing missing evidence and null fields despite information presence.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Deterministic page-chunking with conflict-aware merging prevents cascading errors.
- **Mechanism:** PDFs split into independent chunks; scalar fields must agree across all non-null chunk outputs (disagreements flagged), list fields aggregated via set operations.
- **Core Assumption:** True study-level constructs (e.g., primary design) are stated consistently; discrepancies reflect extraction noise.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [section 3.4.1] "non-overlapping chunks of at most k pages"; [section 3.6.1] scalar fields require identical non-null values; low conflict rates indicate schema stability.
- **Break Condition:** Legitimate, non-contradictory variations (e.g., multiple designs mentioned) trigger flags requiring manual review instead of automated resolution.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Schema-First Design for High-Stakes Extraction
- **Why needed:** Prevents hallucination by constraining outputs, making extraction more reliable than unconstrained generation.
- **Quick check:** How would you design a "study design" schema field to balance coverage (capture all relevant designs) with precision (avoid misclassifying observational as randomized)?

### Concept: Evidence-Gated Conservative Inference
- **Why needed:** In evidence synthesis, omission with traceability is preferable to incorrect completeness; prioritizes values with explicit sentence support.
- **Quick check:** What are the trade-offs between requiring narrative sentence evidence versus allowing extraction from table cells and figure captions for key constructs like assay thresholds?

### Concept: Deterministic Document Chunking and Merge Logic
- **Why needed:** Long PDFs exceed model context limits; merge strategy must reconcile inconsistent chunk outputs into one coherent record.
- **Quick check:** When do you use set-based aggregation (lists) versus conflict-sensitive merging (scalars), and why is this distinction appropriate for biomedical articles?

### Concept: Async Orchestration with Resume-Aware State
- **Why needed:** Processing many PDFs with external OCR requires managing rate limits, transient failures, and incremental updates without redoing work.
- **Quick check:** How does resume-aware hashing (SHA1 of canonical file ID) enable idempotent processing, and what edge cases could break this assumption?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Ingestion module -> Chunking module -> OCR orchestrator -> Schema engine -> Consolidation layer -> Export module; State manager indexes source keys at ingestion to gate entry.

### Critical path
For a new PDF: canonical ID -> page count -> chunk creation -> for each chunk & payload: OCR call -> collect chunk results -> scalar conflict check & list aggregation -> cross-payload integration -> export. Resume check (is source key in index?) occurs before OCR.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Chunk size (k=8):** Smaller chunks reduce failure impact but increase merge complexity and call volume; larger chunks risk losing whole pages on failure.
- **Concurrency (3) vs RPS (5):** Parallelism improves throughput but must respect upstream limits; 3.1 RPS achieved shows backpressure from retries/captions.
- **Conservative extraction:** Null over speculation improves auditability but reduces completeness; schema refinement initially improved outcomes.
- **Evidence requirement:** Sentence-level provenance enables verification but misses table-only data; captions are treated as first-class text to partially address this.

### Failure signatures
- **High scalar conflict rate (>5%):** Schema too broad or documents legitimately inconsistent; check schema descriptions and examples.
- **Low evidence population (<90% for eligible fields):** Caption extraction failing or model not quoting sentences; inspect OCR output and prompts.
- **Many retries per document (>3):** Problematic scans or OCR service issues; check proxy transcription quality scores.
- **Missing payloads across corpus:** Schema definition gap or systematic reporting difference (e.g., all studies omit follow-up duration).

### First 3 experiments
1. **Schema validation smoke test:** Process 5 diverse PDFs in debug mode. Verify each payload returns schema-conformant JSON, evidence fields populate when expected, and no unexpected nulls in required fields.
2. **Resume functionality test:** Run pipeline on test corpus, note count. Re-run with same corpus + one new PDF. Verify original count unchanged, new PDF processed, index updated without re-OCR'ing old files.
3. **Conflict detection sanity check:** Create synthetic documents with deliberate contradictory scalar statements (e.g., Methods: "randomized trial"; Results: "observational cohort"). Confirm conflict flags appear, not silent selection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How well does the schema-constrained extraction generalize to other biomedical domains with different reporting standards and variable structures?
- **Basis:** The architecture is general but only evaluated on DOAC measurements; transfer depends on schema quality and expert input.
- **Why unresolved:** Only tested on one specialized domain; performance on other tasks unevaluated.
- **Resolution evidence:** Systematic evaluation across multiple biomedical domains using domain-specific schemas, measuring fidelity, completeness, and audit burden vs. baselines.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the actual character-level OCR accuracy, and how does it correlate with the proxy transcription quality scores?
- **Basis:** OCR quality estimated via downstream heuristics and schema sanity checks, not character-level ground truth; operational signals are appropriate for deployment but may miss subtle errors.
- **Why unresolved:** Indirect heuristics used; no CER/WER measured against gold transcriptions.
- **Resolution evidence:** Character-level transcriptions for a representative subset to compute CER/WER and correlate with proxy scores.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does schema-constrained extraction compare to unconstrained generative approaches in over-inference, silent errors, and audit burden?
- **Basis:** Future work should include systematic head-to-head comparisons against alternative paradigms, including unconstrained generative approaches.
- **Why unresolved:** No controlled comparisons; benefits of constraints inferred from stability and audit features.
- **Resolution evidence:** Direct comparative study on same corpus using matched OCR inputs, measuring hallucination rates, audit time, and expert-adjusted correctness.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can field-level confidence calibration improve efficiency and reliability of expert review?
- **Basis:** Attaching calibrated uncertainty estimates would allow workflows to weight variables, triage records, or defer aggregation when confidence is low.
- **Why unresolved:** Confidence estimates not produced or evaluated; impact on review efficiency hypothetical.
- **Resolution evidence:** Implement confidence scoring (e.g., model probabilities, consistency checks) and conduct user studies measuring review time, error detection, and adjudication with vs. without confidence-guided triage.

## Limitations
- Generalizability limited to DOAC measurement domain; schema and corpus not publicly released, hindering replication.
- Conservative reliance on narrative text (vs. tables/figures) may miss evidence in data-dense articles; caption extraction does not fully resolve this.
- OCR quality assessment uses indirect proxies, not character-level ground truth; subtle errors may go undetected.
- Break conditions exist for novel schema items and table-only information, requiring manual intervention or schema updates.

## Confidence
- **Typed schemas prevent hallucination:** High confidence. Supported by 62-percentage-point gain in clinical outcome definition correctness after schema refinement.
- **Sentence-level evidence enables auditability:** Medium confidence. Evidence binding is implemented and traces most decisions, but >99% population metric likely excludes table-only data, limiting full auditability.
- **Deterministic chunking/merge ensures stability:** High confidence. Low scalar conflict rates and stable throughput (3.1 RPS under 5 RPS limit) empirically validate the design.

## Next Checks
1. **Schema Gap Stress Test:** Process 50 highly heterogeneous biomedical PDFs (different journals, assay types). Flag records with systematic nulls or values outside controlled vocabularies to reveal schema coverage holes.
2. **Caption-Robustness Audit:** On 20 table/figure-heavy articles, manually verify whether all critical assay thresholds/outcome definitions are in extracted captions vs. embedded tables. Quantify proportion of key constructs captured only in tables.
3. **Conflict Logic Sanity Check:** Create 10 synthetic documents with deliberate, valid scalar conflicts (e.g., Methods: "prospective cohort"; Results: "retrospective"). Confirm pipeline flags these, not silently selecting a value.
