# Deep TPC: Temporal-Prior Conditioning for Time Series Forecasting
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.16188
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16188
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: TPC achieves state-of-the-art long-term forecasting performance, consistently outperforming full fine-tuning and shallow conditioning (e.g., positional embeddings) across multiple datasets in MSE and MAE, while using fewer trainable parameters.

## Executive Summary
Deep TPC introduces Temporal-Prior Conditioning (TPC), a novel approach to enhance large language models (LLMs) for time series forecasting. Existing methods inject temporal cues only shallowly at the input, causing the information to degrade through the model's layers. TPC treats time as a first-class modality by introducing learnable time-series tokens that cross-attend to frozen-LLM-encoded temporal embeddings at multiple decoder layers, then propagate this context via self-attention. Only the TPC modules and a patch encoder are trained, keeping the LLM frozen. This design yields state-of-the-art long-term forecasting results, consistently beating both full fine-tuning and shallow conditioning baselines, while using a fraction of the trainable parameters.

## Method Summary
TPC processes a raw time series by first normalizing it with RevIN and splitting it into patches of fixed length. These patches are projected into the LLM's embedding space via a linear layer. A small bank of learnable time-series (TS) tokens is initialized and concatenated with the patch embeddings to form the initial sequence fed into the frozen LLM decoder (e.g., GPT-2 small). The decoder contains TPC modules inserted at selected layers. Each TPC module performs a gated cross-attention where only the TS-tokens attend to a precomputed bank of temporal embeddings (E_temp). These embeddings are generated by feeding textual prompts describing the time span (e.g., "from 2017-01-01 to 2017-01-02") through the same frozen LLM and mean-pooling its final hidden states. The cross-attention allows TS-tokens to gather temporal context, which is then disseminated to the entire sequence via subsequent self-attention layers of the decoder. After the final decoder layer, only the patch tokens are extracted and passed through a linear head to predict the next patch. During training, only the patch encoder, the TS-tokens, and the parameters of the TPC modules (cross-attention, feed-forward network, and gating scalars) are updated; the LLM backbone remains frozen. This parameter-efficient adaptation maintains the LLM's pre-trained knowledge while learning to fuse temporal priors with the time-series signal.

## Key Results
- TPC achieves state-of-the-art long-term forecasting performance on multiple benchmark datasets (e.g., ETTh1, ETTm1), surpassing both full fine-tuning and shallow conditioning methods like positional embeddings.
- It consistently outperforms baselines in both MSE and MAE metrics while training only a small fraction of parameters (often <50% of full fine-tuning).
- The improvements hold across various prediction horizons, demonstrating effective capture of long-range temporal dependencies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Deep, multi-layer temporal conditioning**  
Shallow injection of temporal cues (e.g., at the input only) leads to degradation as the signal propagates through deep layers. TPC injects temporal context repeatedly: learnable TS-tokens are introduced into the patch stream and persist throughout the decoder. At designated layers, these tokens perform cross-attention to the precomputed temporal embeddings E_temp, gathering time-related information. This enriched context is then spread to all tokens via the decoder's self-attention. By refreshing the temporal signal at multiple depths, TPC prevents dilution and maintains strong conditioning.

**Disentangling temporal and signal modalities**  
TPC enforces a strict separation between the time-series signal (patch embeddings) and temporal priors (E_temp). Only the TS-tokens are allowed to query E_temp via a masked cross-attention, acting as a dedicated routing layer. This disentanglement lets the frozen LLM focus on its core language modeling while a lightweight adapter handles the novel fusion. The result is high parameter efficiency and effective learning without interfering with the LLM's pre-trained representations.

**LLM-native temporal representations**  
Temporal context is expressed within the LLM's own representational space. Textual prompts describing calendar properties are processed by the frozen LLM to produce E_temp. This aligns temporal information with the model's internal embeddings, enabling semantically meaningful interactions. Empirically, this textual encoding outperforms numeric positional encodings or simple prefix prompts, confirming the advantage of leveraging the LLM's built-in temporal knowledge.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input Encoder (RevIN + linear patch projector) → TS-Tokens Bank (learnable vectors) → Temporal Embedding Generator (prompt → frozen LLM → mean-pool → E_temp) → Frozen Decoder Backbone (e.g., GPT-2 small) with TPC Modules inserted at selected layers → Output Head (linear layer).

**Critical path:**  
Raw time series → RevIN normalization → Patch segmentation → Linear projection to embeddings → Concatenation with TS-tokens → Sequence fed into frozen decoder. At specific decoder layers, the TPC Module activates: TS-tokens perform masked cross-attention to E_temp, gathering temporal context. This context is then distributed to all tokens via subsequent self-attention layers. After the final decoder layer, patch tokens are extracted and passed through the output linear head to predict the next patch. The process repeats autoregressively during inference.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Depth vs. Parameters*: More TPC modules (L) increase interaction depth but add linear parameters (cross-attention, FFN, gates). The paper finds an optimal spacing (e.g., every 4th layer) that balances gain and overhead.  
- *TS-Token count (n_f)*: More tokens increase channel capacity for temporal context but also increase sequence length and TPC module parameters. A fixed small number (e.g., 10) is used.  
- *Gating*: Gate scalars are initialized to 0.5 to stabilize early training, preventing abrupt updates from the new cross-attention.

**Failure signatures:**  
- No improvement over shallow baselines (e.g., positional embeddings) may indicate poor TPC placement, insufficient n_f, or uninformative E_temp.  
- Performance degradation when adding more TPC modules suggests over-parameterization of the adapter or disruption of frozen LLM representations.  
- TS-tokens collapsing to near-identical vectors (high pairwise cosine similarity) indicates ineffective training dynamics.  
- Inference slowdown disproportionate to the number of trained parameters could arise from mismatched patching strategies between training and autoregressive rollout.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Incorporating word embeddings with numeric patches** – Can we augment the input representation with word embeddings alongside patch embeddings for richer multimodal alignment? The authors plan to explore this.  
- **Generalization across LLM backbones** – Does TPC work with alternative LLMs (e.g., LLaMA, BERT)? The authors intend to test model-agnostic generalization.  
- **Isolating the key factor** – Is TPC's superiority due to deep temporal conditioning, the disentangled token design, or their combination? Ablations in the paper do not separate these contributions.  
- **Replacing textual prompts with numeric encodings** – Could simpler numeric temporal encodings (e.g., Fourier features) replace textual prompts without performance loss, thereby reducing computation? This remains untested.

## Limitations
- The proposed degradation mechanism (shallow injection loses temporal info) is largely asserted without direct experimental proof; the neighbor corpus lacks independent evidence.  
- Community validation is weak: the average neighbor FMR is 0.477 with zero citations, indicating limited independent scrutiny.  
- The design relies on specific choices (GPT-2 small, fixed patch configuration); generalizability to other LLM architectures or patch sizes is not established.

## Confidence
- Deep, multi-layer temporal conditioning prevents degradation: Medium  
- Disentangling temporal modality improves parameter efficiency: Medium-High  
- LLM-encoded prompts outperform numeric encodings: Medium
