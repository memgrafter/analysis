# Structural Sensitivity in Compressed Transformers: Error Propagation, Lyapunov Stability, and Formally Verified Bounds
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20991
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20991
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Early-layer MLP up-projections are catastrophically sensitive to compression (20,000× PPL increase in GPT-2 Small); residual connections induce error contraction (ρmax=0.96); formal verification guarantees zero violations across 14,040+ configurations; robustness combines stability and architectural redundancy (CFI).

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals that transformer compression sensitivity spans five orders of magnitude, dominated by a few critical components—especially the MLP up-projection in the first hidden layer. Compressing just this single matrix in GPT-2 Small by 5% raises perplexity by 20,000×, while value projections compress almost for free. Using Lyapunov stability theory, the authors show that residual connections contract errors by growing the hidden state faster than the error, yielding ρ<1 across layers. Nevertheless, error contraction is insufficient; architectural redundancy (e.g., hybrid designs) also matters for robust compression. The work presents ten formal Lean 4 theorems that rigorously bound per-matrix approximation errors, verified with zero violations over 14,040+ configurations. A Compression Fragility Index (CFI) combines instability and width penalties to rank model robustness, explaining why Qwen3-8B collapses under 50% Wanda (42,922× degradation) while Mistral-7B (11×) and LFM2-2.6B (7×) remain relatively robust. The findings underscore the need for non-uniform, principled compression strategies.

## Method Summary
The study combines systematic empirical ablations with formal verification. Compression techniques include simplified Wanda (weight × column-norm scoring without calibration) and activation‑aware Wanda using calibration data, as well as SVD low‑rank truncation. For each model, every linear layer (e.g., 468 matrices in GPT‑2 Small) is wrapped to track per‑matrix error ∥Wx−Ŵx∥ against a theoretical bound ∥W_residual∥·∥x∥. Experiments systematically compress individual layers or component types to map sensitivity, inject small perturbations (ϵ∈{0.001,0.01,0.05,0.1}) at the embedding to measure Lyapunov contraction factors ρ_t, and verify formal bounds across a large configuration space. Evaluation uses WikiText‑103 and C4 with 4,096 tokens to match paper baselines. A greedy bandit allocation is used for FLOP‑optimal compression, though hyperparameters are unspecified. The minimum viable reproduction involves implementing the compression pipeline, matching baseline perplexities (Table 1), and confirming the sensitivity hierarchy and ρ<1 pattern across at least two architectures.

## Key Results
- Compression sensitivity varies by five orders; a single early‑layer MLP up‑projection in GPT‑2 Small (5% sparsity) causes a 20,000× perplexity increase, while value projections compress nearly for free.
- Residual connections contract errors via hidden state growth (ρ<1, ρmax=0.96 for GPT‑2 Small), but contraction alone is insufficient—architectural redundancy (e.g., LFM2 hybrid) can sustain robustness even with some ρ>1.
- Formal verification via ten Lean 4 theorems guarantees per‑matrix error bounds with zero violations across 14,040+ configurations; the Compression Fragility Index combines instability and width penalties to explain observed robustness differences (Qwen3‑8B: 42,922×, Mistral‑7B: 11×, LFM2‑2.6B: 7×).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper explains the observed sensitivity and robustness through three interacting mechanisms:

1. **Early-layer MLP up‑projections as a feature bottleneck.** The first hidden layer synthesizes nonlinear features from token embeddings. Its MLP up‑projection operates at full rank (low output covariance rank, heavy‑tailed activations, low GELU sparsity), making it a critical, functionally irreducible feature‑creation step. Any compression error here propagates to all subsequent layers. Catastrophe is avoided if this matrix is excluded from compression or if its output covariance rank becomes high (indicating redundancy).

2. **Residual‑driven error contraction.** In Pre‑LN transformers, the relative error V(t) evolves as V(t+1)=ρ_t V(t)+c_t with ρ_t=(β_t/α_t)², where α_t is the hidden state growth factor (norm ratio) and β_t the error growth factor. Residual addition causes the hidden state norm to increase monotonically, so typically α_t>β_t and ρ_t<1, yielding geometric error contraction. If in some layers α_t<β_t (e.g., disproportionate attention outputs), ρ_t>1 and errors amplify.

3. **Robustness requires both stability and redundancy.** Error contraction (Lyapunov stability) is necessary but not sufficient. Narrow representations (small d_model) have less margin per singular direction, making them vulnerable even when ρ<1. Architectural redundancy—such as hybrid convolution‑attention mixes in LFM2—provides alternative pathways that absorb perturbations. The Compression Fragility Index (CFI) quantifies robustness by combining the fraction of amplifying layers (ρ>1) and a width penalty. Thus, a model with high ρ_max and narrow width collapses (Qwen3‑8B: 42,922×), while one with ρ_max>1 but hybrid design remains robust (LFM2: 7×).

## Foundational Learning

### Lyapunov stability for dynamical systems
*Why needed*: The paper models layer‑to‑layer error propagation as a dynamical system and uses Lyapunov stability to derive contraction conditions (ρ<1) that bound error growth.  
*Quick check*: Given V(t+1)=ρ_t V(t)+c_t with ρ_t<1, what does Lyapunov stability imply about the long‑term error? It converges to a finite bound, not diverging.

### Transformer Pre‑LN architecture
*Why needed*: The analysis relies on the Pre‑LN ordering (LayerNorm before sublayer) and residual connections; the hidden state growth mechanism depends on residual addition increasing norm.  
*Quick check*: In a Pre‑LN transformer, what operations are applied at each layer? How does the residual connection affect the norm of the hidden state? LayerNorm normalizes input, then attention and MLP sublayers are applied; the input is added to each sublayer’s output, typically increasing the norm unless the sublayer output is near zero.

### Compression‑induced error propagation
*Why needed*: Compressing weight matrices introduces approximation errors that propagate through the network; understanding linear maps and error accumulation is key.  
*Quick check*: If a weight matrix W is approximated by Ŵ, the error ∥Wx−Ŵx∥ can be bounded by ∥W−Ŵ∥∥x∥. How does Theorem 2 formalize this bound across layers? It recursively bounds the per‑matrix error by the operator norm of the residual matrix and the input vector norm, enabling a per‑layer guarantee.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Token embedding -> (LayerNorm -> Attention (Q/K/V linear -> attention -> output linear) -> MLP (up projection -> GELU -> down projection) -> Residual addition) repeated per layer.

Critical path: The MLP up‑projection in the first layer (layer 0) is the most compression‑sensitive component; protecting it avoids catastrophic degradation. The safest components to compress are value (V) projections and attention output projections.

Design tradeoffs:
- Uniform compression fails; non‑uniform allocation is mandatory for acceptable quality.
- Greedy sequential compression achieves ~24% FLOPs reduction at +27% perplexity, but beyond ~32% savings errors compound super‑linearly.
- Activation‑aware pruning (Wanda) outperforms naive weight pruning by an order of magnitude.
- Formal per‑matrix bounds are conservative but guarantee zero violations, enabling principled allocation.

Failure signatures:
- **Catastrophic failure**: Compressing layer 0’s MLP up‑projection leads to perplexity increases of 20,000×.
- **Error accumulation**: Without residual contraction, errors would grow linearly; observed geometric contraction indicates residual‑driven dilution.
- **Bound violation**: None observed in 14,040+ configurations; formal per‑matrix bounds hold exactly.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Single‑layer ablation**: Compress all matrices within a single layer (e.g., layer 0) using a fixed sparsity (5%) and rank (16), then measure perplexity.
2. **Lyapunov stability measurement**: Inject a small perturbation (ϵ=0.01) at the embedding layer, record hidden state and error norms after each layer, and compute ρ_t = (β_t/α_t)².
3. **Cross‑architecture replication**: Repeat experiments 1 and 2 on a different architecture (e.g., Mistral‑7B) to compare sensitivity hierarchies and ρ_t patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to larger models**: Does the catastrophic sensitivity of early‑layer MLP up‑projections persist beyond 8B parameters? Future work includes scaling to 13B+ models. Evidence: replicate per‑matrix compression sweeps on larger models.
2. **Analytical derivation of ρ**: Can the Lyapunov contraction factor ρ be derived analytically from model weights and LayerNorm statistics instead of via perturbation? The paper states it was measured empirically. Evidence: derive closed‑form ρ in terms of weight norms, LayerNorm variance, and activation distributions; validate against current measurements.
3. **Quantifying architectural redundancy**: Beyond Lyapunov stability and width, what architectural properties (e.g., hybrid designs, tied embeddings, attention head count) confer robustness? The CFI currently uses a simplistic width penalty and fails to rank some models correctly. Evidence: extract candidate redundancy metrics from diverse architectures, correlate with degradation under uniform compression, and refine the CFI.

## Limitations
- Formal verification details (Lean 4 version, tactics) are not provided; independent confirmation of the zero‑violation claim is blocked.
- Baseline perplexity is highly sensitive to tokenization and chunking; the paper reports a 3.5× gap between NumPy and HuggingFace pipelines, so exact settings must be matched.
- Cross‑architecture findings rely on one model per family, limiting generalizability; differences may be due to training data, size, or objectives rather than architecture alone.
