# Progressive Learning with Anatomical Priors for Reliable Left Atrial Scar Segmentation from Late Gadolinium Enhancement MRI

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.26186  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26186  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A three-stage progressive learning framework with anatomical priors achieved scar segmentation Dice of 0.50, HD 11.84 mm on LASCARQS, outperforming two-stage and one-stage baselines.

## Executive Summary
Automatic segmentation of left atrial scar from late gadolinium enhancement MRI is hindered by low contrast, annotation variability, and a lack of anatomical constraints. This paper proposes a three-stage progressive learning framework using a SwinUNETR backbone. The approach first pre-trains on left atrial cavity segmentation, then jointly learns atrial and scar segmentation with an anatomy-aware spatially weighted loss that restricts scar predictions to wall regions, and finally fine-tunes on scar segmentation alone. Evaluated on the LASCARQS dataset with 5-fold cross-validation, the full model improves boundary accuracy over baseline methods, demonstrating the value of integrating anatomical priors through progressive training.

## Method Summary
The method employs a SwinUNETR architecture in three sequential stages. Stage 1 pre-trains the network on binary left atrial cavity segmentation. Stage 2 performs dual-task learning for both atrial and scar segmentation, incorporating an anatomy-aware spatially weighted loss function that assigns higher weights to scar pixels within the atrial wall, thereby constraining predictions to anatomically plausible regions. Stage 3 fine-tunes the network using only scar segmentation data. This progressive integration transfers robust anatomical features from the easier cavity segmentation task to the more challenging scar segmentation task, aiming to mitigate issues of low contrast and annotation noise.

## Key Results
- Scar segmentation on LASCARQS: Dice 0.50, Hausdorff Distance 11.84 mm, Average Surface Distance 1.80 mm (5-fold CV).
- Outperforms a two-stage variant (Dice 0.49, HD 13.02 mm, ASD 1.96 mm).
- Shows improved boundary accuracy compared to a one-stage baseline and nnU-Net.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The progressive learning framework allows the model to first capture coarse anatomical structures (atrial cavity) before focusing on finer, low-contrast scar details. The anatomy-aware loss explicitly penalizes scar predictions outside the atrial wall, reducing false positives in non-wall tissues and leveraging the known spatial pattern of fibrosis. This combination addresses the core challenges: the pre-trained features provide a strong initialization, while the spatial constraints guide the model toward anatomically plausible solutions despite noisy or ambiguous annotations.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Progressive Learning** – Sequential training on related tasks of increasing difficulty. *Why needed:* Builds general features on easier tasks (LA segmentation) before specializing to harder ones (scar segmentation). *Quick check:* Ablate by training scar-only from scratch; expect performance drop.
2. **Anatomical Priors** – Incorporating known spatial constraints (scar occurs only on atrial wall). *Why needed:* Reduces hypothesis space and combats low contrast/annotation noise. *Quick check:* Visualize test predictions; false positives should rarely appear outside wall regions.
3. **SwinUNETR** – Transformer-based segmentation network with hierarchical shifted windows. *Why needed:* Captures multiscale context efficiently for small, diffuse scar structures. *Quick check:* Compare against a CNN backbone (e.g., ResUNet) within the same progressive framework.
4. **Multi-task Learning** – Joint optimization of LA and scar segmentation in Stage 2. *Why needed:* Encourages shared representations that benefit both tasks. *Quick check:* Monitor LA segmentation Dice in Stage 2; should remain high.
5. **Spatially Weighted Loss** – Loss function that upweights scar pixels within the wall. *Why needed:* Focuses learning on anatomically valid regions, mitigating class imbalance and annotation variability. *Quick check:* Inspect weight map; highest values should overlap wall mask.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
LA Cavity Pre-training -> Dual-task (LA + Scar) with Anatomy-Aware Loss -> Scar Fine-tuning

**Critical path:** The three stages must be executed sequentially; weights from Stage 1 initialize Stage 2, and Stage 2 weights initialize Stage 3. The anatomy-aware loss is only active in Stage 2.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Accuracy vs. complexity:* More stages increase training time and pipeline complexity but yield modest accuracy gains.  
- *Dependency risk:* Performance hinges on Stage 1 LA segmentation quality; errors may propagate.  
- *Loss sensitivity:* Spatially weighted loss requires careful tuning of weight parameters to avoid over-constraining.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Poor LA segmentation in Stage 1 leads to misplaced scar predictions.  
- Over-constrained loss may suppress true scar in distal/patchy regions.  
- Model may still produce false positives in ambiguous low-contrast areas despite priors.

**First experiments:**  
1. Ablate Stage 1: Train scar segmentation from random initialization; compare Dice/HD.  
2. Architecture swap: Replace SwinUNETR with standard nnU-Net in the three-stage pipeline.  
3. Loss perturbation: Vary the spatial weighting parameters (e.g., wall margin width, weight magnitude) and measure impact on false-positive rate outside the wall.

## Limitations
- Dataset scale & diversity: Evaluation limited to LASCARQS; generalizability across centers, protocols, and scar patterns is unknown.
- Annotation variability: Inter-observer agreement not quantified; model sensitivity to annotation style not assessed.
- Clinical utility gap: Dice 0.50 is low for clinical use; the practical impact of ~1.2 mm HD reduction is unclear.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** Progressive learning with anatomical priors improves scar segmentation over one-stage and two-stage baselines. **Confidence:** Medium. Supported by cross-validation results, but gains are small and statistical significance not reported.
- **Claim:** Anatomy-aware loss constrains scar predictions to plausible wall regions. **Confidence:** Medium. Logical design, but no direct ablation showing reduced false positives in non-wall tissues.
- **Claim:** SwinUNETR backbone effectively leverages progressive learning. **Confidence:** Low-Medium. No architecture comparisons within the progressive framework; gains may be backbone-dependent.

## Next Checks
1. **External validation:** Test the full pipeline on an independent multi-center LGE-MRI dataset (e.g., from an LGE-MRI challenge) to assess generalization.
2. **Component ablation:** Systematically remove stages (train scar-only), remove anatomy-aware loss (use standard Dice), and remove LA pre-training; measure impact on Dice, HD, and false-positive rate in non-wall regions.
3. **Annotation robustness:** Obtain multiple independent scar annotations for a test subset; compute performance per annotator and against consensus to quantify sensitivity to annotation variability.
