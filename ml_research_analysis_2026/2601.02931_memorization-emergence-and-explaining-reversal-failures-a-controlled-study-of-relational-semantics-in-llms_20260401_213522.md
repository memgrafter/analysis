# Memorization, Emergence, and Explaining Reversal Failures: A Controlled Study of Relational Semantics in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.02931
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02931
- Reference count: 21
- Primary result: Relational semantics (inversion, symmetry) emerge in autoregressive LLMs via a sharp phase transition when trained on sufficient logic-bearing data; reversal failures are primarily caused by left-to-right decoding bias, not missing semantics.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether autoregressive large language models (LLMs) internalize relational semantics such as inversion and symmetry, and why they fail on reversed queries (e.g., “B is son of A” after training on “A is father of B”). Using a controlled synthetic framework based on knowledge graphs, the authors train GPT-style models from scratch and evaluate memorization, logical inference, and in-context generalization. They find that relational semantics emerge abruptly after a threshold of logic-bearing training samples, even in shallow networks, and that successful generalization correlates with stable intermediate-layer representations. Crucially, reversal failures are shown to stem from the autoregressive training objective’s left-to-right bias, not a deficiency in learned relational logic, as evidenced by forward/reverse tests and a diffusion model baseline that exhibits no such gap.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed a synthetic text corpus from knowledge graphs containing three relation types: inversion pairs (father/son), symmetric relations (friend), and person–job assignments. Each graph was verbalized into paragraphs using multiple sentence templates and shuffled. Models were pre-trained on a mixture of train graphs (all triples) and evaluation graphs (missing reverse facts), then supervised fine-tuned (SFT) on QA pairs derived only from the train content. Evaluation comprised three query sets: memorization (observed facts), logic (implied facts), and in-context learning (ICL) with unseen entities. Key manipulated variables included training sample count (N), template diversity (K), model depth (L), and a diffusion model baseline with bidirectional attention.

## Key Results
- Relational semantics (inversion/symmetry) emerge via a sharp phase transition in in-context learning accuracy when training data exceeds a logic-bearing threshold (~2000 samples), even in 2–3 layer models.
- Successful generalization correlates with stable, logic-relevant signals forming in intermediate layers (≈layers 5–8); failure shows late-layer degradation.
- Reversal-type failures (e.g., poor reverse query performance) are primarily driven by left-to-right autoregressive decoding bias; a diffusion model with bidirectional attention shows no forward/reverse gap.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Claim: Relational semantics emerge through a sharp phase transition when models receive sufficient logic-bearing supervision, even in shallow architectures.  
Mechanism: Data scale (number of logic-bearing samples) acts as a trigger. Below a threshold, models rely on local co-occurrence heuristics; above it, a reusable rule mechanism forms, enabling generalization to unseen entities. This manifests as abrupt jumps in in-context learning (ICL) performance.  
Core assumption: Relational logic can be induced from repeated exposure to structurally consistent examples within a controlled distribution, without requiring massive model scale.  
Evidence anchors: [abstract] "relational semantics emerge with sufficient logic-bearing supervision... even in shallow (2–3 layer) models... sharp phase transition"; [section 4.2] "QICL-C exhibits a clear phase transition when N exceeds 2,000... QLogic decreases as N increases but QICL increases".  
Break condition: Insufficient number of logic-bearing training samples (N below threshold) or excessive data scale causing memorization interference (QLogic declines as N grows large).

### Mechanism 2
Claim: Successful relational generalization correlates with stable, logic-relevant signals forming in intermediate layers (≈layers 5–8), while failure shows late-layer degradation.  
Mechanism: In successful models, relational mechanisms are composed early in the residual stream and persist through to output readout. In failing models, weak or unstable mid-layer signals degrade in final layers due to distributional alignment or feature competition.  
Core assumption: Intermediate layers are the primary locus for feature composition; final layers primarily read out and project to vocabulary space.  
Evidence anchors: [section 4.3] "high QICL-C accuracy coincides with an early improvement... around layers 5–8, whereas low-performing models show a delayed onset (around layers 9–11) followed by a sharp final-layer degradation"; [section 4.4] links to residual-stream/circuits view.  
Break condition: Model fails to develop stable mid-layer representations (e.g., due to shallow depth L=1 or insufficient training), leading to collapse at final layer.

### Mechanism 3
Claim: Reversal-type failures are primarily driven by left-to-right autoregressive order bias, not deficient inversion semantics.  
Mechanism: Autoregressive decoding exposes the model only to prefix contexts during training, creating a directional bias. The model learns to predict suffixes conditioned on prefixes, making reverse-ordered queries challenging even when inversion semantics are internally represented.  
Core assumption: The reversal curse reflects a training-time distributional bias (causal masking) rather than an absence of logical relation knowledge.  
Evidence anchors: [abstract] "order-matched forward/reverse tests and a diffusion baseline indicate that reversal failures are primarily driven by autoregressive order bias"; [section 5.2] Forward/Reverse gaps vanish for job facts when both orders are present, and for diffusion models.  
Break condition: Strict left-to-right training without any bidirectional exposure or alternative conditioning patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Controlled Synthetic Data Generation from Knowledge Graph Schemas.  
  *Why needed*: Isolates relational semantics from confounding factors like polysemy and data contamination. Enables precise manipulation of logic-bearing supervision.  
  *Quick check*: Can you design a KG triple set where every inversion pair (father/son) and symmetric pair (friend) is explicitly represented, and verbalize it with multiple templates while controlling for sentence order?

- **Concept**: Phase Transitions and Grokking Dynamics.  
  *Why needed*: Explains abrupt emergence of generalization (QICL) after prolonged training, distinct from standard overfitting curves. Critical for interpreting when and why relational logic appears.  
  *Quick check*: Given accuracy curves over training steps that show memorization (QLogic) peaking early then declining, while ICL (QICL-C) jumps later, does this suggest the model first memorizes then groks the rule, or the opposite?

- **Concept**: Autoregressive vs. Non-Autoregressive Decoding Inductive Biases.  
  *Why needed*: Diagnosing reversal failures requires contrasting architectures. AR models suffer order bias; diffusion (bidirectional) models do not. Understanding this distinction is key to root-cause analysis.  
  *Quick check*: If a model trained on “A is B” fails “B is A”, but a diffusion model trained on the same data succeeds, what does that imply about the failure mode?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Synthetic KG Generator → Verbalized Corpus (train + eval paragraphs) → GPT-style AR LM (pre-training) → SFT on QA pairs → Evaluation Suite (QMem, QLogic, QICL forward/reverse).  
  Diffusion baseline: Same corpus → Bidirectional Transformer → Evaluation.

- **Critical path**:  
  1. Generate KG triples with disjoint entity pools.  
  2. Verbalize into paragraphs using templates; shuffle sentences.  
  3. Pre-train AR LM on combined corpus.  
  4. SFT on QA pairs from train-only triples.  
  5. Evaluate on memorization, logic, and ICL tasks with forward/reverse queries.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Template diversity (K) vs. overfitting: More templates reduces superficial memorization but may require more samples (N) for logic to emerge.  
  - Model depth (L) vs. data scale (N): Deeper models help at large N; shallow models can grok logic at moderate N but may degrade at final layer.  
  - AR vs. bidirectional training: AR is standard but induces order bias; bidirectional (or mixed-order) training mitigates reversal but changes objective.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - High QMem but low QLogic: Memorization without rule induction.  
  - High forward QLogic but low reverse QLogic: Classic reversal curse (order bias).  
  - Layer-wise: Early signal improvement followed by late-layer rank drop → unstable mechanism.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Phase transition sweep**: Fix L=12, K=10, vary N (500→100k). Plot QICL-C accuracy vs. N. Expect sharp rise after N≈2000–10k. Verify QLogic declines at large N.  
  2. **Forward/Reverse contrast**: Train AR model with one-directional people facts. Evaluate QInv/QSym in forward (A→query→B) and reverse (B→query→A) formats. Expect large gap.  
  3. **Diffusion comparison**: Train diffusion model on same corpus. Evaluate forward/reverse Memorize-people QA. Expect near-identical accuracy, confirming order bias as root cause.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Question: How do other relational properties (e.g., transitivity, antisymmetry) affect emergence and reversal behavior?  
Basis in paper: [explicit] Limitation: "we do not test properties such as transitivity, antisymmetry, hierarchical relations, multi-hop compositional rules" (Page 8).  
Why unresolved: These properties may exhibit different learning thresholds and mechanisms, which is crucial for generalizing the study's conclusions beyond symmetric/inverse relations.  
What evidence would resolve it: Extend the synthetic framework to include additional relation types, measure phase transitions and reversal sensitivity across them, and compare to the baseline.

### Open Question 2
Question: Do the findings generalize to natural language text rather than synthetic templates?  
Basis in paper: [explicit] Limitation: "Template-based language generation... may not fully reflect the breadth of paraphrastic variation and contextual nuance in natural text" (Page 8).  
Why unresolved: Real-world text contains noise, ambiguity, and diverse linguistic structures that could alter emergence dynamics and reversal failures.  
What evidence would resolve it: Evaluate pretrained LLMs on relational facts extracted from natural corpora, or train models on more diverse synthetic paraphrases and compare results.

### Open Question 3
Question: Why do logic QA (memorization-based) and in-context learning (ICL) show opposite scaling trends with training data size?  
Basis in paper: [inferred] Figure 4 shows QLogic decreases while QICL increases as N grows; the authors speculate "entity-memorization interference" but do not explain the mechanism.  
Why unresolved: Understanding this trade‑off is key for training models that both retain facts and generalize rules.  
What evidence would resolve it: Track per‑instance learning curves, probe representations for rule‑like vs. entity‑specific features, and control entity frequency distributions.

### Open Question 4
Question: What specific transformer circuits or computations implement relational reasoning (e.g., inversion, symmetry)?  
Basis in paper: [inferred] The layer‑wise analysis (Result 2, Figure 6) correlates stable intermediate‑layer signals with success but does not identify the responsible heads, layers, or pathways.  
Why unresolved: A mechanistic account is necessary for true interpretability and targeted improvements.  
What evidence would resolve it: Apply activation patching, head ablation, and circuit‑tracing on models that exhibit relational reasoning to map the causal subnetwork.

## Limitations
- The synthetic template-based data may not capture natural language variation and noise, limiting external validity.
- Exact details of template selection probabilities, SFT subset sampling, and diffusion inference parameters are unspecified, affecting reproducibility thresholds.
- The study focuses on limited relation types (inversion, symmetry); generalizability to other logical properties (transitivity, antisymmetry) is unknown.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (phase transition via logic-bearing supervision): **High**
- Mechanism 2 (mid-layer representation stability): **Medium**
- Mechanism 3 (reversal failures driven by order bias): **High**
- Overall reproducibility of core dynamics: **Medium** (exact thresholds may shift with unpublished pipeline choices)
