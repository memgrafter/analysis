# VL-KGE: Vision-Language Models Meet Knowledge Graph Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02435
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02435
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: VL-KGE integrates CLIP/BLIP with KGE backbones to handle modality asymmetry, achieving up to 3.7× improvement in MRR (0.578 vs 0.157) on WikiArt-MKG-v2.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces VL-KGE, a framework that combines pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) with traditional knowledge graph embedding (KGE) methods to address modality asymmetry and missing modalities in multimodal knowledge graphs. By fusing visual and textual features from VLMs with structural embeddings from KGE backbones, VL-KGE produces unified entity representations that are robust to incomplete modality data. Evaluated on WN9-IMG and the newly collected WikiArt-MKG datasets, VL-KGE significantly outperforms prior unimodal and multimodal approaches in link prediction tasks. The method also supports inductive inference for unseen entities. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of leveraging large-scale VLMs to enhance knowledge graph completion in visually rich, modality-asymmetric domains.

## Method Summary
VL-KGE enhances standard KGE models (TransE, DistMult, ComplEx, RotatE) by augmenting each entity with multimodal features extracted from a pretrained vision-language model. For entities that have associated images or text, the corresponding VLM encoders produce feature vectors. These features are then fused with the entity’s learnable structural embedding (the original KGE embedding) via concatenation or addition. The combined representation is used in the scoring function of the KGE model to compute plausibility scores for triples. This design allows the model to handle missing modalities by relying solely on the structural embedding when VLM features are unavailable, while still benefiting from multimodal cues when present. Training follows the standard KGE negative sampling pipeline, with the VLM parameters kept frozen to preserve pretrained alignment and avoid catastrophic forgetting.

## Key Results
- On WikiArt-MKG-v2, VL-ComplEx (using CLIP) achieves an MRR of 0.578, a 3.7× improvement over unimodal ComplEx (MRR 0.157).
- VL-KGE consistently outperforms prior multimodal KGE methods (e.g., MKGformer) across all backbones on both WN9-IMG and WikiArt-MKG datasets.
- The approach yields strong inductive performance: for entities unseen during training, VL-KGE maintains high link prediction accuracy, demonstrating its ability to generalize.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Pretrained VLMs provide high-quality, aligned representations for both visual and textual modalities. By injecting these representations into the KGE framework, the model gains access to rich semantic features that complement the structural information captured by KGE. The fusion mechanism ensures that when some modalities are missing, the model can still rely on the learned structural embedding, which acts as a fallback. This hybrid design effectively mitigates the modality asymmetry problem: entities with only text, only images, or both can be represented in a shared space. Moreover, the frozen VLM encoders retain their general cross-modal knowledge, avoiding the need for large-scale multimodal training on the specific KG.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Graph Embeddings (KGE)**: KGE methods embed entities and relations into continuous vectors to enable reasoning over graph-structured data. *Why needed*: They provide a differentiable way to predict missing links. *Quick check*: Does TransE enforce that h + r ≈ t for positive triples?
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**: Models like CLIP and BLIP are trained on image-text pairs to align visual and textual representations. *Why needed*: They supply robust multimodal features even for entities with missing modalities. *Quick check*: Does CLIP's text encoder produce similar embeddings for synonymous captions?
- **Modality Asymmetry**: In many multimodal KGs, entities have heterogeneous modality coverage (some have images, some text, some both). *Why needed*: Recognizing asymmetry prevents models from forcing all entities to have the same modalities. *Quick check*: Are there entities in the dataset with only one modality type?
- **Fusion Strategies**: Combining VLM features with KGE embeddings via concatenation, addition, or attention. *Why needed*: Determines how effectively multimodal signals augment structural information. *Quick check*: Does concatenation increase dimensionality and require more training data?
- **Inductive Link Prediction**: Ability to infer links for entities not seen during training. *Why needed*: Reflects real-world scenarios where new entities continually emerge. *Quick check*: Are test entities completely held out from the training set?
- **Negative Sampling**: A training technique that generates incorrect triples to contrast with positives. *Why needed*: Enables efficient learning without enumerating all possible triples. *Quick check*: Is the number of negative samples per positive typically 1–10 in KGE?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
VLM Encoder (CLIP/BLIP) -> Feature Fusion -> KGE Backbone (TransE/DistMult/ComplEx/RotatE) -> Scoring Function -> Link Prediction

**Critical Path**  
1. Input entity with associated modalities (image, text, or both).  
2. Encode image with VLM vision encoder, text with VLM text encoder (if available).  
3. Concatenate/combine VLM features with the entity's learnable KGE embedding.  
4. For a triple (h, r, t), compute score using the KGE scoring function on the combined embeddings.  
5. Apply sigmoid or ranking to predict link validity.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Fusion Simplicity vs Expressivity*: Simple concatenation adds parameters but is easy; attention-based fusion may better weight modalities but is more complex.  
- *VLM Choice*: CLIP offers strong zero-shot transfer but may be less fine-grained; BLIP may capture more detailed captions but depends on caption quality.  
- *Parameter Efficiency*: Keeping VLM frozen reduces training cost but limits adaptation to domain-specific visual styles; fine-tuning improves fit but risks losing general alignment.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Poor performance on entities with rare or out-of-distribution visual styles (e.g., abstract art if VLM trained on natural images).  
- When modality features are severely misaligned (e.g., noisy image-text pairs), the fusion may inject noise, degrading structural learning.  
- High variance across random seeds indicates instability in the fusion or training regime.

**First Three Experiments**  
1. **Baseline Comparison**: Implement VL-KGE with various backbones (TransE, ComplEx, etc.) and compare against unimodal KGE and prior multimodal methods (MKGformer) on WN9-IMG and WikiArt-MKG, reporting MRR, Hits@K.  
2. **Ablation of Fusion**: Replace concatenation with addition and attention-based fusion; measure impact on performance, especially under controlled missing modality ratios.  
3. **VLM Selection**: Evaluate CLIP-variants (ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14) and BLIP on the same backbone to understand how VLM capacity influences gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can structured temporal and geospatial attributes be integrated into VL-KGE beyond text encoding?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The limitation stated that "our framework encodes all attributes as text, which may be suboptimal for temporal or geospatial properties".
- Why unresolved: Treating all modalities as text discards native structure (e.g., continuity in time or space), potentially degrading relational reasoning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Compare performance when using specialized embeddings (e.g., sinusoidal for years, coordinate-based for locations) against plain text encoding on WikiArt-MKGs, measuring gains in link prediction for temporal/geospatial relations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does VL-KGE generalize to other modality-asymmetric domains beyond fine art?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The limitation noted that "empirical evaluation focuses primarily on fine art knowledge graphs; broader assessment across additional domains would further validate generalizability".
- Why unresolved: The approach may rely on specific characteristics of art data (e.g., visual style) and might not transfer to domains like biomedicine or e-commerce.
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply VL-KGE to diverse multimodal KGs (e.g., medical imaging reports, product catalogs) with inherent modality asymmetry and compare against unimodal and multimodal baselines.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can VL-KGE perform zero-shot link prediction for entirely novel relations not observed during training?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The limitation observed that "relation embeddings still require training on observed triples, which may limit applicability in scenarios with extremely sparse supervision or entirely novel relation types".
- Why unresolved: Current formulation ties relation embeddings to training data; for unseen relations, the model lacks any representation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Construct a dataset with disjoint train/test relation sets; evaluate whether relation prototypes derived from textual descriptions (via VLM text encoder) can support prediction without any training triples for those relations.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does domain mismatch between VLM pretraining data and target knowledge graphs affect VL-KGE performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The limitation stated that "quality of multimodal representations depends on the pretrained vision-language encoders, which may exhibit domain-specific performance variations".
- Why unresolved: VLMs pretrained on web-scale data may capture biases irrelevant to specialized domains (e.g., historical paintings), harming alignment.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically vary VLM pretraining domains (e.g., general web vs. domain-specific curated data) and measure downstream MKG completion performance; consider fine-tuning strategies that preserve cross-modal alignment while adapting to the KG domain.

## Limitations
- Dataset scope limited to WN9-IMG and WikiArt-MKG, restricting generalizability to other multimodal domains.
- Fusion strategy not thoroughly ablated; sensitivity to modality weighting/choice remains unclear.
- Computational overhead from integrating large VLMs is unreported; efficiency relative to unimodal KGE unknown.

## Confidence
- **Core Method Design (VL-KGE fusion)**: High – clearly described and logically consistent.
- **Performance Claims on Tested Datasets**: Medium – strong numbers on two datasets but lacking external validation.
- **Generalization & Inductive Claims**: Low – inductive evaluation narrow; no cross-domain evidence.
