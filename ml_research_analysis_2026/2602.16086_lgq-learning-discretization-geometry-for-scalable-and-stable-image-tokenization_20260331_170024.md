# LGQ: Learning Discretization Geometry for Scalable and Stable Image Tokenization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16086
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16086
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** LGQ achieves state-of-the-art image tokenization reconstruction (rFID 110.64 at K=16,384) while using approximately 50% fewer active codes compared to FSQ and SimVQ.

## Executive Summary
Discrete image tokenization faces a fundamental trade-off: flexible vector quantization (VQ) is prone to codebook collapse, while stable scalar quantizers like FSQ impose rigid, fixed grids. Learnable Geometric Quantization (LGQ) resolves this by learning the discretization geometry end-to-end. It replaces hard nearest-neighbor assignments with temperature-softened Gibbs distributions derived from a variational free-energy objective, enabling fully differentiable training. Dual regularizers—a token-level peakedness term and a global usage balance term—promote confident yet uniform code utilization without fixed constraints. On ImageNet 128×128, LGQ significantly improves reconstruction quality (lowest rFID) while drastically reducing the number of active codes, demonstrating superior efficiency and scalability.

## Method Summary
LGQ modifies the standard VQ pipeline by substituting the hard, non-differentiable nearest-neighbor lookup with a soft assignment over Euclidean distances to codebook vectors. This soft assignment is parameterized by a temperature τ and is the minimizer of a free-energy functional (expected distortion plus τ-scaled entropy), making the quantization operation differentiable. A straight-through estimator (STE) recovers discrete hard indices at inference. Two regularizers are applied: L_peak encourages near-one-hot assignments per token (high confidence), and L_bins encourages uniform marginal usage across the codebook (balanced utilization). Temperature τ is annealed during training to gradually harden assignments. This setup allows the encoder and codebook centroids to co-adapt, shaping the Voronoi tessellation to align with the latent data distribution and allocate capacity efficiently.

## Key Results
- At K=16,384, LGQ achieves rFID 110.64 with only 8,199 active codes (49.96% fewer than FSQ) and improves rFID by 11.88% over FSQ.
- Compared to SimVQ, LGQ improves rFID by 6.06% with 49.45% lower effective representation rate.
- At K=65,536, LGQ attains rFID 111.08 with 22.5% code utilization, remaining the best among baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Differentiable Soft Assignments
- **Claim:** Replacing hard nearest-neighbor lookup with temperature-controlled soft assignments enables joint optimization of codebook geometry and encoder, avoiding disjoint updates and collapse.
- **Mechanism:** The soft assignment arises as the unique minimizer of a free-energy functional (expected distortion + τ·entropy), making quantization fully differentiable. Gradients flow to all codebook entries during training, not just the nearest neighbor.
- **Core assumption:** A smooth assignment landscape during training allows the codebook to adapt collectively to the latent distribution before commitments harden.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.1 derive the free-energy connection; related work on mitigating premature discretization supports the mechanism’s relevance.
- **Break condition:** Annealing τ too slowly leaves assignments diffuse and adaptation ineffective; annealing too fast reverts to biased VQ dynamics.

### Mechanism 2: Dual Regularization for Balanced Utilization
- **Claim:** The combination of token-level peakedness (L_peak) and global usage (L_bins) regularizers promotes confident yet balanced code utilization without rigid grid constraints.
- **Mechanism:** L_peak penalizes low max-probability per token (encouraging near-one-hot selections); L_bins penalizes squared L2 norm of marginal code frequencies (encouraging uniform usage). Propositions show L_peak’s minimum is a Dirac distribution and L_bins’s minimum is uniform.
- **Core assumption:** Separating confidence (per-token) and balance (global) constraints allows independent optimization pressures that jointly prevent collapse modes.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 and appendix proofs; training dynamics show LGQ converges to intermediate entropy (~12.58 bits), avoiding VQ collapse (~10.56) and FSQ/SimVQ saturation (~13.4+).
- **Break condition:** Regularizer weights λ_peak, λ_bins too high override reconstruction loss; too low fail to prevent collapse at large K.

### Mechanism 3: End-to-End Geometry Learning
- **Claim:** Learning codebook centroids jointly with the encoder aligns quantization cells with the empirical latent density, achieving efficient capacity allocation.
- **Mechanism:** Codebook vectors are updated via gradients from reconstruction and regularizers flowing through soft assignments, shaping the Voronoi tessellation to match the data manifold.
- **Core assumption:** The latent distribution is heterogeneous; a fixed grid (FSQ) or unevolved codebook (poorly trained VQ) misallocates capacity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract emphasizes learned geometry; UMAP visualizations (Fig 4) show LGQ active codes cover high-density regions with ~50% utilization versus ~100% for SimVQ; codebook center trajectories (Fig 5) show structured early movement.
- **Break condition:** Poorly structured encoder outputs or excessively large codebooks may yield permanently inactive codes despite learning.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Encoder (f_θ) -> LGQ Layer (computes soft assignments p_t, hard indices k*_t via argmax, then STE for z_q) -> Decoder (g_ψ). Regularization Module computes L_peak and L_bins from p_t.

**Critical path:** Gradient for codebook updates must flow through soft assignment probabilities p_t, not the hard indices. The STE passes the gradient of the soft assignment backward while using the hard index forward. Both L_peak and L_bins depend on p_t, directly modulating the assignment landscape.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Temperature schedule: Fast annealing (e.g., 1.0→0.05) yields quicker convergence but risks collapse; slow annealing (1.0→0.2) allows smoother geometry learning but may leave assignments diffuse.
- Regularizer weights (λ_peak, λ_bins): Too strong over‑constrains geometry and harms reconstruction; too weak fails to prevent collapse at large K.
- Codebook size K vs. expected utilization: LGQ’s regularizers enable scaling K while maintaining a controlled, data‑aligned active subset; without them, larger K leads to severe under‑utilization.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Sudden drop in active codes % (<10%): Catastrophic collapse, often from too‑slow τ annealing or missing/weak L_peak.
2. rFID diverges while active codes % stays high but uneven: “Usage collapse” where a small subset of codes dominate; check L_bins gradient magnitude.
3. Codebook centers show minimal movement after first epoch: Encoder may be failing to produce diverse latents, or τ is too low from the start.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Temperature schedule ablation:** Train LGQ with (a) fast annealing (1.0→0.05), (b) fixed τ=0.1, (c) slow annealing (1.0→0.2). Hypothesis: (a) achieves stable ~50% utilization and lowest rFID; (c) risks collapse (near 0 active codes, high rFID).
2. **Regularizer ablation:** Train with (a) no L_peak/L_bins, (b) only L_peak, (c) only L_bins, (d) both. Expect (d) to show balanced ~50% utilization and best rFID; (a) shows high variance/utilization instability; (b)/(c) show imbalanced usage.
3. **Matched effective capacity comparison:** Freeze LGQ’s codebook and select its active codes (e.g., top 8,199 by usage). For FSQ/SimVQ/VQ, filter their codebooks to the same number of active codes (by usage). Expect LGQ to show superior distortion at equal discrete budget, proving efficient geometry learning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does LGQ generalize to diverse datasets, modalities (video, audio), and higher resolutions beyond ImageNet 128×128?

- How does LGQ’s utilization–distortion trade‑off evolve with extreme codebook sizes (e.g., >100,000) and longer training schedules? Does it maintain balanced usage without collapse?

## Limitations
- Exact encoder/decoder architecture (number of residual blocks, attention layers, channel progression), training hyperparameters (LR schedule, weight decay, GAN loss usage), and data preprocessing details are unspecified, hindering faithful replication.
- Temperature schedule ambiguity: main text uses τ_end=0.1 while appendix ablations use τ_end=0.05 for best metrics; the true schedule for reported results is unclear.
- rFID/LPIPS evaluation details (Inception version, resize interpolation, number of crops) are missing, potentially affecting metric comparability.

## Confidence
- **Performance superiority (rFID/active codes):** Medium (strong numbers but hyperparameter‑sensitive; replication details incomplete).
- **Soft‑assignment differentiability:** High (sound free‑energy derivation, empirical support from temperature ablations, aligns with related work).
- **Dual regularization (peakedness + usage):** Medium‑High (theoretically proven, training dynamics show intended entropy; likely requires dataset‑specific tuning).
