# Scalable Adaptation of 3D Geometric Foundation Models via Weak Supervision from Internet Video

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.07891
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07891
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** SAGE reduces Chamfer Distance by 20–42% on zero-shot benchmarks (7Scenes, TUM-RGBD, Matterport3D) and shows log-linear improvement as training scenes scale from 100 to 10K.

## Executive Summary
SAGE tackles the data scarcity bottleneck in 3D geometric foundation models by adapting them with weak supervision from internet videos. It introduces a hierarchical mining pipeline that samples informative video trajectories, anchors geometry using sparse Structure-from-Motion (SfM) point clouds for global coherence, and enforces dense multi-view consistency through differentiable 3D Gaussian rendering. Regularization with a small fraction of anchor data prevents catastrophic forgetting. By fine-tuning MV-DUSt3R on over 10,000 video clips, SAGE achieves substantial gains on zero-shot 3D reconstruction benchmarks and demonstrates promising scalability, paving the way for learning from vast unlabeled video corpora.

## Method Summary
The method begins with a curated set of internet videos (RealEstate10K and DL3DV) filtered for duration and resolution. For each video, SfM (via COLMAP) produces sparse 3D points and camera poses. Training trajectories are constructed by sampling 8 context frames and 2–8 target frames with temporal jitter. The pretrained MV-DUSt3R model processes the context views to predict a point cloud and Gaussian parameters. Two supervision signals are used: (1) Sparse anchor loss regresses predicted 3D points onto the SfM points, weighted by confidence; (2) Dense rendering loss compares photometrically rendered target views (using 3D Gaussian splatting) against ground truth images via a combination of LPIPS and L2. A small portion (~3%) of samples from the original anchor dataset is mixed in to regularize the model and avoid forgetting. The Gaussian head is frozen, and only the reconstruction backbone is trained for 5 epochs with 15k trajectories per epoch at 224×224 resolution. This hybrid approach leverages the strengths of sparse geometry and dense appearance without requiring full 3D ground truth.

## Key Results
- Chamfer Distance reduced by 20–42% on zero-shot benchmarks (7Scenes, TUM-RGBD, Matterport3D) compared to the pretrained foundation model.
- Performance improves log-linearly as the number of training video scenes increases from 100 to 10,000.
- The method retains in-distribution performance on ScanNet while generalizing to novel scenes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SAGE’s effectiveness stems from its hybrid supervision: sparse SfM anchors provide globally consistent geometric constraints that prevent drift, while dense differentiable rendering captures fine-grained multi-view appearance and enforces local consistency. The anchor data regularizer acts as an “anchor” to the pretrained knowledge, mitigating catastrophic forgetting. The hierarchical sampling efficiently extracts informative video trajectories, and the frozen Gaussian head preserves the rendering quality of the foundation model during adaptation. Together, these components enable scalable learning from weak video data without requiring costly 3D annotations.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Video clip sampling → COLMAP SfM → Sparse anchors/poses + context frames → MV-DUSt3R backbone (point cloud + Gaussian params) → Frozen Gaussian head → Rendering of target views → Loss computation (Anchor loss + Rendering loss + Anchor regularization) → Backbone weight update

**Critical path:** The training loop must: (1) obtain a video clip with valid SfM anchors, (2) forward the context views through MV-DUSt3R, (3) render target views, (4) compute all losses, and (5) backpropagate to update the backbone. Failure at any step (e.g., no SfM points) aborts the iteration.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Sparse vs. dense supervision*: Relying only on sparse anchors may harm multi-view consistency; only dense rendering may drift globally. The hybrid balances both.  
- *Anchor mixing ratio*: Too low risks forgetting; too high reduces adaptation. The paper found ~3% optimal.  
- *Number of target views*: More views increase consistency but also computation and risk of extrapolation.  
- *Frozen vs. fine-tuned Gaussian head*: Freezing preserves rendering quality; fine-tuning might adapt appearance but risks overfitting.

**Failure signatures:**  
- NaNs in loss due to extreme photometric values from out-of-frustum target views.  
- Sudden rise in ScanNet CD indicates catastrophic forgetting.  
- COLMAP fails to reconstruct (fewer than 500 points) leads to insufficient anchor supervision.  
- Out-of-memory errors if the number of Gaussians per scene exceeds rasterization limits.

**First experiments:**  
1. Ablate supervision signals: train with anchor loss only, rendering loss only, and both; compare zero-shot CD.  
2. Vary anchor mixing ratio (1%, 3%, 5%, 10%) and measure zero-shot improvement vs. in-distribution forgetting.  
3. Change the number of target views (2, 4, 8) and monitor training stability and final CD.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can SAGE be extended to handle dynamic video scenes with moving objects and ego-motion?  
**Basis in paper:** The limitation section states that the method leverages largely static environments and cannot fully exploit dynamic content.  
**Why unresolved:** The pipeline assumes static scenes for reliable SfM and consistency; dynamic elements violate this assumption.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Integrate motion segmentation or optical flow to isolate dynamic regions; evaluate on dynamic datasets (DAVIS, KITTI) and compare reconstruction metrics.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** What is the minimal density of SfM anchor points required to prevent catastrophic forgetting without overly constraining adaptation?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred; anchor density is not analyzed.  
**Why unresolved:** Anchor density varies widely across videos; the trade-off between regularization strength and bias is unknown.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically downsample SfM points per scene (e.g., 10%, 30%, 50%) while keeping training samples constant; measure forgetting (ScanNet CD) and zero-shot CD.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Does the log-linear scaling law of performance with video scene count hold beyond 10K scenes?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred; scaling only tested up to 10K.  
**Why unresolved:** Larger datasets may introduce lower-quality videos or exceed model capacity, causing saturation.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Train on progressively larger video sets (e.g., 20K, 50K, 100K scenes) and plot zero-shot CD against log(scene count) to check linearity continuation.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Can the hierarchical supervision (sparse anchors + dense consistency) be simplified or replaced with a single, more robust weak supervision signal?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred; ablations show both signals are necessary but their interaction is unclear.  
**Why unresolved:** It is unknown whether one signal could substitute the other under certain conditions, or if a unified loss could suffice.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Degrade one signal (add noise to SfM points, weaken rendering loss) and see if the other compensates; or design a combined loss and compare performance.

## Limitations
- Missing critical hyperparameters: exact loss weights, optimizer settings, and precise data mixture proportions hinder exact reproduction.
- Unclear Gaussian rendering implementation details (number of Gaussians, covariance parameterization) and potential mismatch with the frozen pretrained head.
- No systematic ablations on anchor density, regularization strength, or scaling beyond 10K scenes, leaving key trade-offs unexplored.
