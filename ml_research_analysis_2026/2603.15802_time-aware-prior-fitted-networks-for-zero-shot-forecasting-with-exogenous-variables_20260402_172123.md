# Time-Aware Prior Fitted Networks for Zero-Shot Forecasting with Exogenous Variables

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15802  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15802  
- **Reference count:** 21  
- **Primary result:** ApolloPFN achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot forecasting with exogenous variables, reducing sCRPS by 12% over TabPFN-TS on electricity price forecasting and outperforming on M5 competition, with only 11M parameters.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces ApolloPFN, a time-aware prior-data fitted network for zero-shot forecasting with exogenous variables. Current foundation models either ignore exogenous covariates or require fine-tuning. ApolloPFN addresses this by innovating in synthetic data generation (using a Single Root Node Growing Network and time-dependent root nodes) and architecture (Rotary and absolute positional encodings plus full attention). In evaluations on electricity price forecasting and the M5 competition, ApolloPFN outperforms larger models (30–70× parameters) in zero-shot settings. Ablations confirm the necessity of both the SRNGN generator and the temporal inductive biases.

## Method Summary
ApolloPFN builds on the Prior-Data Fitted Network (PFN) paradigm: it trains on synthetic datasets generated from structural causal models (SCMs) to approximate the Bayesian posterior predictive distribution, enabling zero-shot inference on real data. The synthetic data generator uses a Single Root Node Growing Network (SRNGN) to create directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) with a single root and longer path lengths, and samples root node values as time-dependent stochastic processes (sine/cosine with random frequencies/amplitudes) to embed temporal structure. The architecture modifies TabPFN's sample-feature separable transformer: it adds Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) and absolute positional encodings to the sample attention (AttnSamp), and uses full attention (test points attend to all history and future exogenous data) to leverage future covariates and enforce temporal order. Inputs are normalized per dataset; the model outputs quantiles for a Riemann approximation of the predictive distribution.

## Key Results
- On electricity price forecasting (5 markets, 24/48h horizons), ApolloPFN reduces sCRPS by 12% relative to TabPFN-TS.
- On the M5 competition (daily, weekly, monthly aggregations), ApolloPFN achieves superior RMSSE and sCRPS compared to TabPFN-TS, Moirai, Chronos, and Sundial.
- Despite having only 11M parameters, ApolloPFN outperforms models 30–70× larger in zero-shot settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: SRNGN Graph Generation
- **Claim:** SRNGN generates DAGs with a single root and longer average path lengths, accelerating learning of temporal dependencies versus the Random Growing Network (RGN) used in TabPFN.
- **Mechanism:** SRNGN forces each new node to connect to at least one prior node and, with probability ρ, to a popular node, creating a connected graph with one root and extended causal chains. Longer paths encourage learning multi-step temporal patterns rather than i.i.d. feature-target associations.
- **Core assumption:** Real exogenous drivers propagate through a system from latent temporal sources; a single-root graph mimics this hierarchical propagation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions SRNGN; [section 4.1] Algorithm 1 and Figure 6 show graph properties; [section 5.4] Figure 3 shows faster convergence with SRNGN on M1 Yearly and M4 Daily.
- **Break condition:** If the true data generating process has multiple independent temporal sources, forcing a single root may create spurious dependencies.

### Mechanism 2: Time-Dependent Root Node Excitation
- **Claim:** Sampling root node values as stochastic processes (e.g., v_{t,r} = α₁ sin(ϕ₁t) + α₂ cos(ϕ₂t)) injects structured temporal autocorrelation, enabling the model to learn time-dependent patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Time-varying roots propagate through the SCM graph, inducing correlations across features and target that reflect seasonal, trend, or cycle patterns. Random frequencies/amplitudes provide diversity.
- **Core assumption:** Many real time series arise from latent temporal processes; i.i.d. roots (as in TabPFN) fail to generate ordered patterns like ramp-ups before spikes.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions time-dependent root nodes; [section 4.1] Equation for v_{t,r}; [section 3] describes TabPFN-TS failures on ordered patterns.
- **Break condition:** If the series is purely stochastic (white noise), time-dependent roots may introduce false periodicities and overfit.

### Mechanism 3: Temporal Inductive Biases via Positional Encodings and Full Attention
- **Claim:** Adding Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) and absolute positional encodings to sample attention, and using full attention (test points attend to all history and future exogenous), counters TabPFN-TS's permutation invariance and leverages future covariates.
- **Mechanism:** RoPE biases attention toward temporally proximate observations; absolute PE encodes absolute time step. Full attention removes test-test masking, allowing future exogenous variables (e.g., known promotions) to inform predictions.
- **Core assumption:** Time series forecasting requires exploiting temporal order and exogenous future context; permutation-invariant attention is inadequate.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions RoPE, absolute PE, full attention; [section 4.2] details; [section 5.4] Figure 4 ablations show contributions; [section 3] describes TabPFN-TS failures addressed by these changes.
- **Break condition:** If future exogenous variables are unreliable, full attention may propagate errors backward. Positional encodings may overfit to synthetic periodicities if not diverse enough.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Prior-Data Fitted Networks (PFNs)**  
  Why needed: PFNs approximate the posterior predictive distribution by training on synthetic datasets, enabling zero-shot inference without fine-tuning.  
  Quick check: How does training on many synthetic datasets allow a neural network to perform Bayesian prediction on real data without explicit posterior sampling?
- **Concept: Sample-Feature Separable Transformer**  
  Why needed: TabPFN's architecture processes features and samples independently via AttnFeat and AttnSamp; this is efficient and suitable for tabular i.i.d. data, but needs modification for time series.  
  Quick check: Why is AttnSamp's permutation invariance suitable for tabular i.i.d. data but problematic for time series, and how do the modifications change this?
- **Concept: Synthetic Data Generation via SCMs and DAGs**  
  Why needed: The synthetic prior defines the model's inductive biases; graph structure (SRNGN) and root excitation shape what temporal patterns ApolloPFN can learn.  
  Quick check: How does the choice of DAG generator (SRNGN vs RGN) and root node sampling (time-dependent vs i.i.d.) affect the synthetic data's suitability for time series forecasting?
- **Concept: Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE)**  
  Why needed: RoPE injects relative position information into attention, biasing toward recent observations and addressing permutation invariance.  
  Quick check: How does RoPE modify query and key projections to encode temporal distance, and why is this important for time series?
- **Concept: Full Attention for Exogenous Variables**  
  Why needed: Future exogenous variables are known at inference; allowing all tokens to attend enables the model to use this information.  
  Quick check: Why does the standard TabPFN-TS mask test-test attention, and how does removing that mask help with exogenous-driven spikes?
- **Concept: Structural Causal Models (SCMs)**  
  Why needed: SCMs with DAGs provide a generative framework for synthetic data that respects causal relationships, mimicking real-world time series generation.  
  Quick check: How do SCMs with time-dependent roots generate autocorrelated series, and how does the graph structure influence temporal dependencies?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input normalization (z-score target history per dataset; group and z-score features) → Embedding (linear projection + fixed random feature positional encodings) → Transformer blocks (L layers: AttnFeat → AttnSamp with RoPE + absolute PE on temporal axis → MLP, with layer norms) → Output (linear layer maps to quantiles for Riemann approximation).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Normalize input series and covariates.
  2. Embed into Z with shared feature positional encodings.
  3. Process through L layers: AttnFeat → AttnSamp (with RoPE/absPE, full attention) → MLP.
  4. Final linear layer outputs quantile predictions.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Quadratic attention limits maximum sequence length; long or high-frequency series may need approximations.
  - SRNGN graphs have longer paths, improving temporal modeling but potentially increasing training complexity.
  - Full attention requires future exogenous data at inference; if unavailable, the model cannot be used as intended.
  - Positional encodings impose periodic biases; synthetic diversity in frequencies/amplitudes is needed to capture non-standard patterns.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Training with RGN instead of SRNGN: slower convergence, poor trend extrapolation (Figure 3).
  - Removing RoPE: loss of recency bias; predictions revert to historical averages (Figure 2c).
  - Removing full attention: cannot leverage future exogenous variables; misses spikes driven by known events (Figure 1a).
  - Inadequate synthetic frequency diversity: model fails on unseen periodicities or complex seasonal patterns.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Graph generation ablation: Train two ApolloPFN models with SRNGN and RGN (all else equal). Evaluate sCRPS on M1 Yearly and M4 Daily every 10K steps (Figure 3). Expect SRNGN to reach lower error faster.
  2. Architecture ablation: Fix synthetic data from ApolloPFN. Train variants: (a) TabPFN-TS architecture (no RoPE, test-isolated attention), (b) +RoPE, (c) +RoPE+Full Attention. Evaluate on M3(M/O), Tourism(M/Y), M4(D/M) (Figure 4). Expect incremental improvement with each addition.
  3. Exogenous benchmark test: Zero-shot evaluate on electricity price forecasting (5 markets, 24/48h horizons) and M5 aggregations (state/store, daily/weekly/monthly). Compute sCRPS and RMSSE against TabPFN-TS, Moirai, Chronos, Sundial (Tables 1,2). Expect ApolloPFN to outperform, especially on series with sharp exogenous-driven changes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does the scale, diversity, and structure of synthetic training data influence ApolloPFN's generalization to real-world time series? The synthetic data generation process is heuristic and lacks theoretical analysis.
- **Open Question 2:** How can hierarchical mechanisms be integrated into ApolloPFN to pool information across related time series while preserving series-specific dynamics? Currently, each series is treated independently.
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent can ApolloPFN be compressed (e.g., via quantization) to reduce GPU memory requirements without degrading forecasting performance? The sensitivity of PFN's training dynamics and probabilistic outputs to compression is unexplored.

## Limitations
- Major uncertainties block full replication: exact SRNGN hyperparameters (log V range, ρ distribution) and SCM function probabilities are unreported, limiting synthetic prior fidelity assessment.
- The reliance on future exogenous data at inference is a practical constraint; performance may degrade if such data is missing or erroneous.
- Break conditions for the mechanisms (e.g., multiple independent temporal sources for SRNGN, pure noise for time-dependent roots) are unvalidated on real-world data.

## Confidence
- SRNGN & Time-Dependent Roots: Medium confidence. Ablations show faster convergence, but break conditions untested.
- Positional Encodings & Full Attention: High confidence. Ablations and alignment with neighboring work strongly support their necessity.
- Zero-Shot Exogenous Forecasting Claims: Medium confidence. Strong benchmark results demonstrated, but comparative baselines may evolve; no statistical significance tests reported.
