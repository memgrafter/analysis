# LegalMALR:Multi-Agent Query Understanding and LLM-Based Reranking for Chinese Statute Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17692  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17692  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** LegalMALR achieves Recall@10 of 0.8195 and MRR@10 of 0.7367 on STARD, outperforming the strongest Qwen3-based RAG baseline by over 6 percentage points, and also shows significant gains on the cross-domain CSAID dataset.

## Executive Summary
Chinese legal statute retrieval faces unique challenges due to users' colloquial, implicit, and multi-issue queries that conventional dense retrievers cannot handle through surface-level matching. LegalMALR introduces a Multi-Agent Query Understanding System (MAS) that generates diverse, legally grounded reformulations via specialized agents and iterative retrieval, combined with a GRPO-optimized policy to stabilize the stochastic process. A zero-shot large language model (Qwen-Max) then performs substantive statutory reasoning to rerank the accumulated candidates. On the STARD benchmark, LegalMALR substantially outperforms state-of-the-art RAG baselines in both recall and ranking quality, with further gains confirmed on the challenging cross-domain CSAID dataset.

## Method Summary
LegalMALR operates in two stages. First, a Multi-Agent System (MAS) controlled by a Planner agent iteratively selects from specialized rewrite agents (e.g., Single-Element, Decomposition) to reformulate the user query. Each reformulation triggers independent dense retrieval (Qwen3-Embedding-4B) followed by lightweight reranking (Qwen3-Reranker-4B); the resulting statutes are merged into a candidate pool. The Planner dynamically terminates based on coverage improvement. The MAS policy (a shared Qwen3-4B-Instruct backbone) is refined via Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) using a recall-based reward on the STARD training set. Second, the merged candidate set (average ~13.6 statutes) is reranked by a zero-shot Qwen-Max model, which evaluates doctrinal applicability and factual alignment to produce the final top-10 ranking.

## Key Results
- On STARD, LegalMALR achieves **Recall@10 0.8195** and **MRR@10 0.7367**, improving over the strongest Qwen3-RAG baseline by **+6.16** and **+6.31** percentage points respectively.
- On the cross-domain CSAID dataset, it reaches **Recall@10 0.6841 (+8.09)** and **MRR@10 0.9161 (+4.41)**.
- Ablations confirm that both the MAS (vs. single-query) and the LLM reranker (vs. lightweight cross-encoder) contribute significantly to the observed gains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Claim 1:** The Multi-Agent System (MAS) improves recall by generating diverse, legally grounded query reformulations that expose implicit statutory elements. Specialized agents reinterpret the query from complementary doctrinal angles; each reformulation triggers independent retrieval, and the merged candidate set broadens coverage beyond the original query's surface semantics. The core assumption is that different agent perspectives surface complementary provisions missed by a single-query retriever. Evidence includes the abstract's description of MAS, Section 3.3 on agent roles, and Section 4.3 ablation showing zero-shot MAS already outperforms single-query RAG.

**Claim 2:** Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) stabilizes the stochastic MAS policy, leading to more reliable high-coverage trajectories. GRPO optimizes the Planner's agent-selection actions using a unified Qwen-3-4B-Instruct backbone. Trajectories are rewarded based on final recall, and group-normalized gradients encourage consistent coverage-maximizing behavior. The core assumption is that a trajectory-level recall reward can shape sequential decisions for stable exploration. Evidence includes Section 3.5.1's quantification of zero-shot instability (min recall 0.7511 vs. mean 0.8098), reward design in 3.5.2, and Section 4.3's improvement from zero-shot (Recall@14 0.8012) to GRPO-trained MAS (0.8396).

**Claim 3:** The zero-shot LLM Reranker (Qwen-Max) improves ranking by performing substantive statutory reasoning that aligns query facts with conditional statutory structures, beyond token-level similarity. The reranker evaluates doctrinal applicability, factual alignment, and conditional satisfaction without chain-of-thought, leveraging pretrained legal knowledge. The core assumption is a capable general-purpose LLM can perform such zero-shot relevance judgment. Evidence includes the abstract's claim, Section 3.4's prompt design, and Section 4.4's consistent MRR gains over a lightweight reranker for both dense and MAS candidate pools.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dense Retrieval & Embedding Similarity:** Why needed: Provides initial candidate gathering by mapping queries and statutes to a shared semantic space. Its limitation (surface matching) motivates MAS. Quick check: Why might a dense retriever fail for a query like "Will I be criminally punished if I ate a protected animal under extreme hunger?"  
- **Query Reformulation & Legal Element Extraction:** Why needed: MAS agents rewrite queries to make implicit legal elements (e.g., necessity defense) explicit, bridging colloquial language to statutory terminology. Quick check: What legal element might the Supplementary-Element agent add to a query about a "back-to-back clause" in engineering contracts?  
- **Reinforcement Learning for Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Why needed: Stabilizes MAS's stochastic outputs by optimizing the Planner's sequential decisions with a recall reward, avoiding full supervision. Quick check: What is the trade-off of using a trajectory-level recall reward versus per-step rewards in this legal retrieval task?  
- **Zero-Shot Reranking with Large Language Models:** Why needed: Lightweight cross-encoders lack capacity for multi-element legal reasoning; the LLM compensates by assessing doctrinal coherence. Quick check: How does the LLM reranker's "no-CoT" configuration balance reasoning depth with output determinism and cost?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** User Query -> Planner Agent (Qwen3-4B-Instruct) -> Rewrite Agents (6 types) -> Dense Retriever (Qwen3-Embedding-4B) + Light Reranker (Qwen3-Reranker-4B) -> Candidate Pool Merge -> LLM Reranker (Qwen-Max) -> Final Top-10 Ranking  
- **Critical path:** 1) Query enters MAS. 2) Planner selects an agent → reformulation. 3) Retrieval per reformulation → merge candidates. 4) Planner assesses coverage → repeat or terminate. 5) Final candidate set → Qwen-Max reranking → output.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Recall vs. Latency: Adaptive iteration (avg 2.01 calls) helps but complex queries are slower.  
  - Diversity vs. Noise: Multiple agents increase coverage but may yield irrelevant rewrites; GRPO mitigates by rewarding coverage.  
  - Capability vs. Cost: Qwen-Max is powerful but proprietary and expensive; trade-off with transparency.  
  - Training Data Dependence: GRPO trained on STARD; performance on CSAID shows generalization but may degrade elsewhere.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - MAS stagnation: Planner selects same agent repeatedly, coverage plateaus.  
  - GRPO collapse: Policy converges to a single agent, reducing diversity.  
  - LLM reranker misranking: High MRR drop despite good MAS recall; check prompt/specification.  
  - Budget overrun: Embedding calls per query >> 60, indicating faulty termination logic.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Ablate MAS: Run LegalMALR with MAS disabled (single rewrite). Expect significant Recall@10 drop, especially on CSAID.  
  2. GRPO training effect: Compare zero-shot vs. GRPO-trained MAS (same architecture). Expect improved Recall@14 and reduced rollout variance.  
  3. LLM reranker impact: Fix candidate set, swap reranker (Qwen3-Reranker vs. Qwen-Max). Expect MRR@10 gains with Qwen-Max.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Cross-lingual generalizability:** How does LegalMALR perform on non-Chinese legal systems (e.g., common law) with different statutory structures? The paper explicitly states this remains unexplored.  
2. **Reward design:** Can the MAS policy be optimized with richer rewards (e.g., ranking quality, legal validity) beyond trajectory-level recall? The current reward focuses solely on coverage.  
3. **Open-source reranker substitution:** Can open-source LLMs replace the commercial Qwen-Max reranker without performance loss, enabling reproducibility?  
4. **Process compression:** Can the iterative multi-agent retrieval be compressed into a single-step reformulation without significant recall degradation?

## Limitations
- **GRPO training details** (learning rate, LoRA configuration, reward shaping coefficients) are unreported, hindering precise replication.  
- **LLM reranker specifics** (verbatim prompt, JSON schema, Qwen-Max version, decoding parameters) are not provided, making reranking performance sensitive to minor variations.  
- **External validity** is limited to two Chinese legal datasets; generalization to other legal systems or domains is unknown.

## Confidence
- **MAS diversity → recall:** Medium confidence. Ablation supports the claim, but direct evidence of complementary statute coverage across agents is inferred.  
- **GRPO stabilization:** High confidence. Clear empirical improvement from zero-shot to GRPO-trained MAS in recall and variance reduction.  
- **LLM reasoning → ranking:** Medium confidence. Consistent MRR gains over lightweight reranker suggest value, but zero-shot no-CoT setup is highly prompt- and model-dependent.

## Next Checks
1. **GRPO policy analysis:** During training, log Planner's action distribution per epoch. Check for healthy entropy (exploration) vs. premature collapse to a single agent, indicating reward mis-specification.  
2. **Reranker prompt fidelity test:** Run Qwen-Max on 50 held-out queries with the exact reported prompt and a slightly altered prompt (e.g., removing "doctrinal applicability"). Check for significant MRR@10 degradation (>2%) with the altered prompt.  
3. **Retrieval call budget audit:** Implement the described Planner termination logic and log dense retrieval calls per query on STARD test. Check that mean calls ≈ 60.3 and variance is reasonable; higher mean indicates termination heuristic mis-implementation.
