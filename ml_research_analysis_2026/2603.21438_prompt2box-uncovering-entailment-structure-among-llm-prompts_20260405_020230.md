# PROMPT2BOX: Uncovering Entailment Structure among LLM Prompts
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.21438
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21438
- **Reference count**: 29
- **Primary result**: Box embeddings achieve 33% greater depth-specificity alignment, 0.738 FollowBench accuracy (vs 0.640), and 12.6% improved local score consistency on UltraFeedback.

## Executive Summary
PROMPT2BOX introduces box embeddings—prompt representations parameterized by a center and width—to disentangle semantic similarity from specificity in LLM prompt clustering. By encoding entailment as geometric containment (a more specific prompt’s box lies within a general prompt’s box), the method reveals fine‑grained weaknesses in model performance that vector embeddings obscure. Trained on 203K synthesized and existing prompt pairs, PROMPT2BOX preserves both topical relevance and asymmetric specificity. On UltraFeedback with 17 LLMs, its hierarchical clustering uncovers 8.9% more weakness clusters and aligns cluster depth with specificity 33% better than vector baselines. It also sets a new FollowBench accuracy of 0.738 (vs 0.640) and improves local score consistency by 12.6%, demonstrating robust specificity modeling without sacrificing semantic quality.

## Method Summary
PROMPT2BOX maps each prompt to a high‑dimensional box defined by a center vector and a width vector using a MPNet‑base encoder with two MLP heads. Training employs a contrastive loss across multiple datasets: prompt–response pairs (Infinity Instruct) for relevance, sentence entailment triples (MultiNLI), synthesized hierarchical instructions (WildChat), and constraint trees (SURI). The loss maximizes intersection volume for related pairs and encourages containment probability for entailment pairs. A linkage dataset aligns the relevance and entailment spaces by linking Infinity Instruct prompts to the most specific WildChat instructions they entail (determined by GPT‑4.1). After training, boxes are projected to two dimensions via BOX‑SNE, which preserves both intersection and containment relationships. For weakness analysis, prompts are clustered agglomeratively using a volume‑based join distance that favors merges with minimal incremental volume expansion, producing hierarchies where depth correlates with specificity. Evaluation on UltraFeedback and FollowBench confirms significant gains over vector‑based approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Box embeddings (center+size vectors) encode asymmetric entailment relations through geometric containment, separating semantic similarity from specificity.
- **Mechanism**: A prompt’s box size represents its constraint space: larger boxes = more general prompts, smaller boxes = more specific prompts. Entailment (a|=b) is modeled as Box(a) ⊆ Box(b), where the intersection volume ratio p(b|a)=VolInt(a,b)/Vol(Box(a)) measures containment probability.
- **Core assumption**: Constraint inclusion (C(a)⊇C(b)) is equivalent to semantic entailment between prompts, and solution space shrinks with added constraints (S(a)⊆S(b)).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "box embeddings capture both semantic proximity and asymmetric specificity."
  - [section 3.2] Formal definition: Box(a) parameterized by center and width; entailment score defined in Eq. (7).
  - [corpus] Weak: Neighbors discuss box embeddings generally but not this specific entailment encoding for prompts.
- **Break condition**: If constraint sets cannot be reliably approximated by box geometry (e.g., multimodal constraint distributions), containment no longer cleanly maps to entailment.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Training on synthesized hierarchical instruction pairs and existing entailment data teaches the encoder to output boxes where size correlates with specificity and center captures topical similarity.
- **Mechanism**: Contrastive loss maximizes VolInt(a,b) for semantically related pairs (prompt‑response, entailment pairs) while encouraging p(b|a)≈1 for parent‑child entailment pairs. The "linkage" dataset (Infinity Instruct↔WildChat) aligns representation spaces to preserve semantic relevance.
- **Core assumption**: Synthesized hierarchies (WildChat via GPT‑4.1) and SURI constraint combinations provide valid entailment/sibling relationships approximating human‑annotated structure.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.4] Data sources: Infinity Instruct (relevance), WildChat (hierarchy), MNLI (sentence entailment), SURI (siblings), linkage (space alignment).
  - [section 3.3] Contrastive objectives with Gumbel Box for differentiable intersection/containment.
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct validation that synthesized hierarchies match ground‑truth human specificity judgments.
- **Break condition**: If LLM‑generated hierarchies systematically misrepresent real‑world prompt specificity (e.g., over‑generalizing), learned box sizes will not align with actual difficulty.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Volume‑based join distance for box clustering d_join(A,B)=Vol(A∨B)−Vol(A∪B) produces hierarchies where depth correlates with prompt specificity, improving weakness cluster detection.
- **Mechanism**: Merging clusters increases bounding box volume; the incremental volume beyond existing union measures dissimilarity. More specific prompts (small boxes) merge deeper in the tree, aligning cluster depth with specificity.
- **Core assumption**: Hierarchical tree structure of prompt specificity exists and can be recovered by minimizing volume expansion during agglomerative clustering.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 7] Definition of d_join in Eq. (9) and evaluation metrics: local score consistency, specificity‑depth agreement, weakness cluster counts.
  - [abstract] "achieves 33% greater depth‑specificity alignment" and "identifies 8.9% more weakness clusters."
  - [corpus] Weak: No external corpus validates this volume heuristic against alternative hierarchical structures.
- **Break condition**: If specificity does not follow a clean tree‑like structure (e.g., multiple independent specificity axes), volume‑based merging may group unrelated prompts, diluting weakness signals.

## Foundational Learning
- **Box Embeddings**
  - *Why needed*: Vectors lack asymmetry; boxes naturally model entailment via containment and specificity via size.
  - *Quick check*: How would you represent "write a poem" vs "write a sonnet" using boxes? Which box should be larger and why?
- **Entailment as Constraint Inclusion**
  - *Why needed*: Foundation for mapping prompt specificity to geometric containment; if "write a sonnet" entails "write a poem", its constraints are stricter.
  - *Quick check*: If prompt A has constraints {c1, c2} and prompt B has {c1}, does A entail B? Does B entail A?
- **Gumbel Box Approximation**
  - *Why needed*: Hard min/max in intersection/containment are non‑differentiable; Gumbel noise enables end‑to‑end training.
  - *Quick check*: Why can't we directly differentiate through max(a_upper, b_upper)? What does the LSEβ operator approximate?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Encoder (MPNet‑base + two MLP heads) → high‑dimensional box parameters; Training objectives (Multiple Negatives Loss on VolInt and containment) → shape embeddings; Dimension reducer (BOX‑SNE) → 2D boxes preserving relational structure; Clustering (Agglomerative with d_join) → hierarchical weakness analysis.
- **Critical path**: Data curation → Encoder training (with linkage) → BOX‑SNE → Hierarchical clustering → Weakness analysis.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Similarity vs Specificity: Removing linkage dataset boosts FollowBench (specificity) but hurts STS‑B (relevance). Weight α=0.8, β=0.2 in BOX‑SNE balances both.
  - High‑dim vs Low‑dim: High‑dim boxes capture complex entailment; 2D boxes require delta‑constraint to avoid collapse, potentially losing nuance.
  - Data scale vs Quality: Synthetic hierarchies (WildChat) provide volume but may contain invalid entailments; MNLI/SURI offer quality but limited scope.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor STS‑B score (<0.70) → semantic relevance degraded (likely overfitting to entailment).
  - Low specificity‑depth agreement (~50%) → boxes not capturing size‑specificity relationship.
  - Degenerate 2D boxes (near‑zero width) → BOX‑SNE collapsed without delta constraint.
  - Weakness cluster count drop → volume‑based merge distance not separating low‑score prompts.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Intrinsic validation: Compute Spearman correlation between box volumes and known specificity (e.g., WildChat vs WildBench) to verify size encoding.
  2. Entailment probing: Use FollowBench retrieval accuracy to test if box containment recovers specificity gradients.
  3. Downstream clustering: Compare #weakness clusters (size≥2) and local score consistency against vector baseline on UltraFeedback subset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Can PROMPT2BOX's prompt specificity/difficulty estimation be effectively used for LLM routing?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] In the "Future Work" section (Sec. 9), the authors state that "our prompt specificity/difficulty estimation might help LLM routing (Guha et al., 2024; Kashani et al., 2025)."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper did not explore or evaluate the application of PROMPT2BOX to the LLM routing problem, leaving its practical utility untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An empirical study integrating PROMPT2BOX into an LLM routing framework, measuring improvements in routing accuracy, computational efficiency, or model performance on downstream tasks compared to vector‑based routing methods.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Does PROMPT2BOX improve evaluation data selection by better covering the space of prompt specificity?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Future Work mentions that specificity estimation "might help ... evaluation data selection (Zouhar et al., 2025)."
- **Why unresolved**: No experiments were conducted to assess whether using box embeddings leads to more comprehensive or balanced evaluation datasets.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An experiment where evaluation datasets are constructed by sampling prompts guided by box‑derived specificity distributions, then measuring diversity, coverage, and correlation with model performance against standard selection strategies.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can PROMPT2BOX's representation of prompt constraints aid in automated creativity evaluation of LLM outputs?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors note potential applications in "creativity evaluation (Atmakuru et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2025)."
- **Why unresolved**: The relationship between box containment metrics and creativity criteria (e.g., originality, diversity) was not investigated.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A study correlating box‑derived specificity scores or containment patterns with human ratings of creative outputs, or using the representation to control creative constraint levels in generation tasks.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Is the box embedding infrastructure suitable for knowledge editing tasks, such as precise localization and modification of LLM knowledge?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Future Work cites "knowledge editing (Ge et al., 2024)" as a potential application.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper did not test whether the geometric entailment structure corresponds to knowledge subspaces that can be edited with minimal collateral.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Applying PROMPT2BOX to locate knowledge‑related prompts, performing targeted edits (e.g., using a model editor), and evaluating edit success, locality, and re‑application of related prompts.

## Limitations
- Synthetic hierarchy validity: Training relies on LLM‑generated prompt trees (WildChat) and constraint combinations (SURI); no human validation confirms these match true specificity relations.
- Tree‑structure assumption: Volume‑based clustering assumes prompt specificity follows a clean hierarchy; real prompts may have multiple independent specificity axes, limiting d_join effectiveness.
- Proprietary evaluation: Key results use non‑public datasets (UltraFeedback, FollowBench), hindering external replication.

## Confidence
- **High**: Box geometry cleanly encodes asymmetric entailment (containment ⊆).
- **Medium**: Volume‑based clustering aligns depth with specificity (shown internally).
- **Low**: Synthesized training data approximates human specificity judgments (no ground‑truth validation).
