# AutoViVQA: A Large-Scale Automatically Constructed Dataset for Vietnamese Visual Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09689  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09689  
- **Reference Count:** 19  
- **Primary Result:** An LLM-driven Vietnamese VQA dataset (19,411 images, 37,077 questions) that yields a 3× F1 improvement and 8× CIDEr increase when used to fine-tune a Vintern model.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality Vietnamese visual question answering (VQA) datasets with diverse reasoning. It introduces AutoViVQA, an automated framework using a large language model (Gemini 2.5 Flash) to generate questions and answers from Vietnamese image captions, controlled by a five-level reasoning taxonomy. An ensemble-based validation protocol filters weakly grounded samples, resulting in a balanced dataset of 19,411 images and 37,077 questions across nine categories. Fine-tuning a Vintern model on this data dramatically improves performance over the base model, and human validation confirms reasonable quality.

## Method Summary
The construction pipeline starts with MS COCO images paired with Vietnamese captions from VISTA. Gemini 2.5 Flash generates question-answer pairs guided by prompts that enforce specific reasoning levels (e.g., object recognition, spatial reasoning) and categories. To ensure quality, an ensemble of models evaluates each generated sample against 18 criteria (e.g., visual grounding, answer plausibility); samples passing a majority vote are retained. The final dataset is balanced across reasoning levels. For model training, a Vintern-base model is fine-tuned on the filtered training split (80%) using standard optimization, then evaluated on a validation split (20%) with a suite of metrics (F1, CIDEr, etc.).

## Key Results
- Produced AutoViVQA with 19,411 images, 37,077 questions, and 185,385 answers (5 per question), showing better balance and reasoning coverage than prior Vietnamese VQA benchmarks.
- Fine-tuning Vintern-base on AutoViVQA yielded a 3× F1 improvement (0.5376 vs. 0.1755) and an 8× CIDEr increase (0.7284 vs. 0.0854) over the base model.
- Human validation achieved substantial inter-annotator agreement (Krippendorff’s α=0.72), indicating high perceived quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by combining controlled LLM generation with rigorous automated filtering. The reasoning-level constraints force diversity in question types, while the ensemble validation—though details are undisclosed—acts as a quality sieve to remove噪声 (noisy, weakly grounded samples). This yields a dataset that is both large and reasonably clean, providing a strong training signal that addresses the domain-specific scarcity of Vietnamese VQA data. The resulting diversity and scale enable a base multilingual model (Vintern) to learn robust multimodal representations for Vietnamese.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
MS COCO images + VISTA Vietnamese captions -> Gemini 2.5 Flash (reasoning-constrained generation) -> Raw Q-A pairs -> Ensemble validation filter (18 criteria, majority vote) -> Filtered AutoViVQA dataset -> Vintern-base fine-tuning -> Trained Vietnamese VQA model

**Critical Path:** The generation step (Gemini prompting) and the ensemble filter are the core innovative components; their design directly determines dataset diversity and quality.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Automation vs. Control:* Full automation enables scale but risks subtle errors; reasoning-level prompts trade strict control for flexibility.  
- *Diversity vs. Noise:* Encouraging varied reasoning increases complexity but may produce less coherent Q-A; filtering balances this.  
- *Ensemble Cost vs. Reliability:* More models in the ensemble improve robustness but increase computational overhead during construction.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Low human agreement (α < 0.6) on a validation sample indicates persistent quality issues.  
- Fine-tuned model shows minimal gain over base model, suggesting dataset flaws or training misconfiguration.  
- Model performance drops sharply on higher reasoning levels (e.g., “strategic” questions), revealing unbalanced data.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Filtering Impact Ablation:** Train Vintern on raw (unfiltered) data vs. filtered data; compare F1/CIDEr to quantify filter contribution.  
2. **Reasoning Level Analysis:** Evaluate the fine-tuned model separately on each of the five reasoning levels; identify any level where performance lags.  
3. **Sample Efficiency Curve:** Fine-tune Vintern on increasing subsets (e.g., 10%, 30%, 50%, 80%) of AutoViVQA; plot metric improvements to assess data efficiency.

## Limitations
- The ensemble validation protocol (exact model composition, 18 criteria, thresholds) is not described, hindering full reproduction.
- Fine-tuning hyperparameters for Vintern-base (learning rate schedule, warmup steps, augmentation) are unspecified.
- Dataset relies solely on COCO images and Vietnamese captions; generalizability to other domains or languages is unclear.
- Human validation showed moderate agreement (α=0.72); details on annotator number and guidelines are absent.

## Confidence
- **Dataset diversity & balance:** Medium – summary stats are provided, but no per-level breakdowns or generation logs.  
- **Ensemble filtering effectiveness:** Low – protocol entirely undisclosed; cannot assess reliability.  
- **Performance gains on Vintern:** Medium – metrics are strong but depend on unreproducible training setup.  
- **Human validation quality:** Medium – α=0.72 indicates reasonable but not high reliability.
