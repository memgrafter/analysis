# AMALIA Technical Report: A Fully Open Source Large Language Model for European Portuguese

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26511
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26511
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: AMALIA improves European Portuguese linguistic competence and safety by using high-quality pt-PT data and novel native benchmarks, matching strong baselines on general tasks and exceeding them on pt-PT-specific evaluations.

## Executive Summary
AMALIA is a fully open-source large language model designed to address the underrepresentation of European Portuguese (pt-PT) in existing LLMs. The model integrates a carefully curated pt-PT corpus (Arquivo.pt, 5.8B tokens) into a three-stage training pipeline: continued pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and preference optimization. To properly evaluate its capabilities, the authors also release four new native pt-PT benchmarks (PT-Exams, ALBA, P3B3, PT-C) that capture linguistic and cultural nuances missing from machine-translated datasets. AMALIA demonstrates strong performance on general reasoning tasks while achieving significant gains on pt-PT-specific metrics and safety, illustrating the effectiveness of targeted data and culturally-aware evaluation.

## Method Summary
The training process begins with a modified EuroLLM-9B decoder-only transformer, extended to a 32K token context via RoPE scaling. The continued pretraining stage enriches the base multilingual mixture with the high-quality Arquivo.pt pt-PT corpus, along with additional code and synthetic long-context data. Next, supervised fine-tuning applies LoRA or full parameter updates on a diverse set of Portuguese-focused instruction data, including pedagogical dialogues and linguistic examples. Finally, the preference optimization (DPO) stage refines the model using 478K carefully constructed preference pairs, emphasizing pt-PT persona consistency, mathematical reasoning, and safety. This sequential approach ensures a strong pt-PT foundation while aligning the model to user preferences and harm reduction.

## Key Results
- Surpasses baseline models on native pt-PT benchmarks: PT-Exams (68.4), ALBA (43.6), and P3B3 (95.9).
- Achieves very low attack success rate (0.8%) on ADV-Bench, outperforming the SFT variant (1.9%).
- Maintains competitive performance on general reasoning (e.g., BBH, GSM8K) while delivering improved pt-PT generation quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### 1. High-Quality pt-PT Data Integration
- **Claim**: Using more high-quality pt-PT data during both mid- and post-training stages significantly improves the model's linguistic competence and generation for European Portuguese.
- **Mechanism**: A dedicated, quality-filtered pt-PT corpus (Arquivo.pt, 5.8B tokens) is curated and injected into both pretraining and SFT mixtures. This shifts the model's internal distribution toward pt-PT syntactic, lexical, and semantic patterns, overriding biases toward pt-BR or English.
- **Core assumption**: Variant-specific competence can be boosted by targeted exposure to native data, even with smaller absolute token counts relative to dominant languages.
- **Evidence anchors**: Detailed data pipeline (Section 3.1), inclusion of 5.8B tokens in pretraining (Section 4.1), and corroborating neighboring work (CLARIN-PT-LDB, GlórIA).
- **Break condition**: If increasing pt-PT data proportion or quality fails to yield significant gains on native pt-PT benchmarks compared to models with generic or pt-BR-weighted data.

### 2. Native Culturally-Aware Benchmarks
- **Claim**: Purpose-built pt-PT benchmarks are necessary to accurately measure variant competence because machine-translated benchmarks miss linguistic and cultural nuances.
- **Mechanism**: Four new datasets (PT-C, PT-Exams, ALBA, P3B3) target specific pt-PT features—lexical selection, national curriculum knowledge, morphological categories, and dialectal bias—that are diluted or misrepresented in translations, revealing hidden strengths.
- **Core assumption**: Machine-translated tasks are a poor proxy for true capability in a specific language variant due to loss of culturally-bound semantics and lexical discrepancies.
- **Evidence anchors**: Creation of native benchmarks (Section 5.3), large performance gaps on them (e.g., P3B3 95.9) versus smaller margins on translated tasks (Section 6.3/6.4), and related work on ALBA and P3B3.
- **Break condition**: If models that perform poorly on native benchmarks nonetheless score highly after machine translation, it indicates the benchmark design—not model capability—is the issue.

### 3. Sequential Training Pipeline
- **Claim**: A sequential pipeline (continued pretraining → SFT → DPO) is more effective for aligning a base model to pt-PT preferences and safety than any single stage.
- **Mechanism**: Continued pretraining establishes a robust pt-PT linguistic base. SFT introduces task adherence and conversational norms using diverse Portuguese instructions. DPO then refines outputs with pt-PT and safety preference pairs, improving helpfulness, accuracy, and harm reduction while preserving earlier skills.
- **Core assumption**: Skills from pretraining and SFT can be retained and enhanced by preference learning if the preference data mix is curated to avoid reward hacking or catastrophic forgetting.
- **Evidence anchors**: Three-stage process (Section 4), DPO gains over SFT on PT-Exams (68.4 vs 63.1) and lower ASR on ADV-Bench (0.8% vs 1.9%) (Section 6.1/6.5), and initial DPO degradation leading to data adjustments (Section 4.3).
- **Break condition**: If DPO causes significant regression on core pt-PT benchmarks or safety metrics despite improvements on generic preference benchmarks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Data Quality Filtering for Low-Resource Languages**: Why needed: AMALIA’s success hinges on extracting a high-signal pt-PT corpus from noisy web archives (Arquivo.pt). Quick check: Why is a language identifier calibrated for European Portuguese (not just “Portuguese”) critical when processing web data from a .pt domain?
- **Language Variant Bias (pt-PT vs pt-BR)**: Why needed: The core problem is dialectal under-representation; understanding lexical, grammatical, and cultural differences is necessary to interpret benchmark results and why pt-PT models can be outperformed by pt-BR-biased ones. Quick check: What are two concrete linguistic features (morphological or syntactic) that distinguish European Portuguese from Brazilian Portuguese and would be probed by the ALBA benchmark?
- **LLM-as-a-Judge for Variant-Sensitive Evaluation**: Why needed: Native benchmarks use LLM-based evaluation (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro); understanding the risk of judge bias (e.g., favoring pt-BR/English patterns) is crucial for score interpretation. Quick check: Why might using a general-purpose multilingual judge instead of a pt-PT-specialized one introduce measurement error in evaluating pt-PT generation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Pretraining Core (modified EuroLLM-9B, 32K context) -> SFT Stage (LoRA/Full FT on PT-focused mix) -> DPO Stage (478K preference pairs including pt-PT persona, math contrasts, safety, UltraFeedback)

**Critical path**: Data Curation (Arquivo.pt pipeline) → Pretraining (compute-heavy, establishes pt-PT distribution) → SFT (teaches task formats, adds pt-PT instructions) → DPO (aligns preferences, boosts safety). Skipping or reducing the Arquivo.pt segment risks weak foundational pt-PT.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Quality vs. Quantity: Opted for 5.8B high-quality pt-PT tokens over larger, noisier crawls.
- Synthetic Data Risk: Used Gemma 3-27B to generate synthetic pt-PT data; mitigated potential bias with large, varied models and DEITA filtering.
- DPO Data Mix: Added extra pt-PT preference data after observing initial DPO harmed Portuguese-specific capabilities, trading some general capability for variant fidelity.

**Failure signatures**:
- Poor P3B3 scores indicate failure in pretraining/SFT to capture pt-PT distinctives.
- High ASR on SST/XSTest after DPO suggests insufficient or flawed safety preference data.
- Degradation on BBH/GSM8K post-DPO points to overfitting to conversational fluency at the expense of reasoning.
- Low ALBA Culture-bound Semantics scores imply inadequate cultural knowledge sourcing or retention.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Pretraining Ablation**: Train a model from EuroLLM-9B with and without the 5.8B Arquivo.pt segment; measure KLD between pt-PT and pt-BR token distributions on held-out web text. Expect lower KLD with the segment.
2. **SFT Data Mix Analysis**: Remove the “Portuguese Linguistic Instructions” (200 entries) and “PTradutor” datasets from the SFT mixture; evaluate the drop on PT-C (lexical choice) and FRMT (translation) to quantify their contributions.
3. **DPO Pair Construction Test**: Compare DPO models using (a) generic preference pairs (UltraFeedback only) vs (b) the full pt-PT-enriched mix; measure changes on PT-Exams and P3B3 to verify that pt-PT-focused pairs prevent forgetting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: What specific flaws in the DPO process cause degradation in mathematical reasoning and instruction following, and how can these be mitigated?
- **Basis in paper**: Standard DPO led to declines; authors hypothesized the reward model favored responses not adhering to task constraints (Section 4.3). The hypothesis lacks direct validation; mitigation was ad-hoc.
- **Why unresolved**: No controlled experiments isolating reward model design, response sampling temperature, or constraint adherence metrics.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled studies on reward distribution learning across tasks; analysis of how preference pairs affect reasoning vs. conversational skills.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Which training data characteristics or fine‑tuning choices enable some models (e.g., AMALIA, EuroLLM, Gemma) to consistently generate pt‑PT, while most others default to pt‑BR when instructed?
- **Basis in paper**: Evaluation results show most models exhibit strong bias toward pt‑BR; only a few produce pt‑PT consistently (Section 6.4, Table 4).
- **Why unresolved**: The paper does not analyze differences in training corpora or fine‑tuning objectives that might explain this disparity.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Comparative analysis of pretraining data pt‑PT/pt‑BR ratios; ablation studies varying fine‑tuning data composition; probing internal representations for dialect‑specific features.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Do the newly introduced pt‑PT benchmarks sufficiently cover the full range of linguistic and cultural nuances of European Portuguese, or are critical gaps remaining?
- **Basis in paper**: Native benchmarks were created because machine‑translated ones miss nuances (Abstract, Section 5.3); Future Work states intention to expand the suite.
- **Why unresolved**: The current suite is limited in size and scope, and its coverage of diverse registers, regionalisms, and cultural knowledge has not been extensively validated.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Detailed error analysis on diverse pt‑PT corpora; expert linguistic annotation to identify uncovered phenomena; development of additional benchmarks targeting slang, dialects, and cultural references.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Under constraints of limited pt‑PT data and computational resources, what is the optimal strategy (data selection, quantity, training duration) to maximize model performance?
- **Basis in paper**: Future Work highlights scarcity of pt‑PT datasets and limited compute; pretraining used only 5.8B pt‑PT tokens (Section 3.1).
- **Why unresolved**: No systematic study of data efficiency or scaling laws specific to pt‑PT has been conducted.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic experiments varying data size, quality filters, and training steps; learning curve analysis; cost‑benefit evaluation of synthetic vs. curated data sources.

## Limitations
- **Data pipeline reliance**: Results depend heavily on the proprietary Arquivo.pt crawl and synthetic data from Gemma 3-27B, which may introduce unmeasured biases.
- **Benchmark validation**: The newly introduced native benchmarks lack independent validation and may be affected by judge model bias (e.g., using Gemini).
- **Training stability**: The DPO stage required manual correction after initial performance regression, indicating low robustness of the preference optimization process.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (high-quality pt-PT data integration): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (native culturally-aware benchmarks): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (sequential training pipeline): Low
- Overall claims (considering novelty and limited external vetting): Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Independent Data Pipeline Replication**: Reimplement the Arquivo.pt filtering pipeline on a different European Portuguese web corpus (e.g., Common Crawl pt-PT subset) and measure the KLD shift between pt-PT and pt-BR token distributions. Does a similar distribution shift occur?
2. **Cross-Benchmark Concordance**: Evaluate AMALIA-DPO on established Portuguese NLP benchmarks (e.g., ASSIN 2, FLUPT) that were not created in this work. Do the large gains on native benchmarks correlate with performance on these independent, variant-sensitive tasks?
3. **DPO Pair Ablation Analysis**: Systematically ablate the different preference pair sources (pt-PT persona, math contrasts, safety, UltraFeedback) from the final 478K mix. Quantify the specific contribution of the pt-PT-focused pairs to the gains on PT-Exams and the reduction in ASR, isolating them from general preference data.
