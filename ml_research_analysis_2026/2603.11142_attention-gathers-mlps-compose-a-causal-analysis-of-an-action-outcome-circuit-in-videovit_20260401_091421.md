# Attention Gathers, MLPs Compose: A Causal Analysis of an Action-Outcome Circuit in VideoViT
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.11142
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11142
- **Reference count:** 2
- **Primary result:** Delta analysis identifies a success/failure signal amplified over 300% from layer 5 onward; activation patching shows MLP blocks are primary outcome composers (42-60% recovery) and attention heads gather evidence (37-54%), with the circuit being distributed and resilient to ablations.

## Executive Summary
This study reverse-engineers an internal circuit in a pre-trained VideoViT model that represents the outcome of bowling actions (strike versus gutter). Using contrastive videos and a suite of interpretability methods—including delta analysis, activation patching, and ablation—the authors discover that the model develops an abstract semantic representation of the outcome that begins to amplify from layer 5 and grows stronger through the final layer. Activation patching reveals a division of labor: multi-layer perceptron (MLP) blocks act as the primary composers of this outcome signal, recovering 42-60% of the final layer’s signal when patched, while attention heads function as evidence gatherers, recovering 37-54%. This distributed circuit explains why the model remains robust when the most visually salient patches are ablated, demonstrating that abstract outcome concepts are computed internally even when final classifications are identical.

## Method Summary
The study analyzes a pre-trained VideoViT (vivit-b-16x2-kinetics400) using two 10-second contrasting videos from the Kinetics-400 bowling class, one showing a strike and the other a gutter ball. Videos are preprocessed identically, with frames uniformly sampled and a fixed temporal jitter seed. The authors extract [CLS] token activations after each layer for both videos and compute the L2 norm of the activation delta to track the outcome signal's amplification. They then perform component-wise activation patching: for layers 4–10, they replace the output of either the attention or MLP subcomponent in the gutter run with that from the strike run, continue the forward pass, and measure the percentage of the layer-11 delta signal recovered. Additional analyses include direct logit attribution, attention visualization, linear probing, and top-K token ablation to assess the circuit's distribution and robustness.

## Key Results
- The outcome signal (delta between strike and gutter) amplifies from layer 5 onward, with L2 norm rising over 300% (from ~75 to >250).
- Activation patching shows MLP blocks are primary composers, recovering 42-60% of the final layer’s signal, while attention heads act as evidence gatherers, recovering 37-54%.
- The circuit is distributed and redundant, as ablating the top 10% of saliency-contributing patches yields negligible changes to classification logits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The model computes a progressively amplified, abstract "Success vs. Failure" signal starting from mid-layers (layer 5 onward).
- **Mechanism:** A cascade where low-level differences are transformed into a high-level semantic representation, quantified by a rising L2 norm of activation deltas between contrastive videos.
- **Core assumption:** The L2 norm of the activation delta between a 'strike' and 'gutter' video (`act_strike - act_gutter`) tracks a meaningful, outcome-specific signal rather than low-level noise.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...abstract and semantic representation of the outcome is progressively amplified from layers 5 through 11."
  - [section 4.2] "The average layer L2 norm shows a consistent increase of over 300%, rising from approximately 75 at layer 5 to over 250 in the final layer."
  - [corpus] Neighbor corpus shows weak thematic overlap (general video transformers, mechanistic circuits in QA), but no direct anchor for this specific delta amplification pattern.
- **Break condition:** If the delta L2 norm decrease or plateau before final layers, it would challenge the amplification cascade hypothesis. Also, if the signal is not linearly *probeable* by mid-layers (though probe failure here was attributed to superficial learning).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** There is a functional division of labor: Attention heads act as "evidence gatherers" and MLP blocks as robust "concept composers" for the outcome signal.
- **Mechanism:** Activation patching reveals that patching single MLP blocks recovers a larger percentage (42-60%) of the final layer's outcome signal than patching attention heads (37-54%), indicating MLPs are primary drivers. Attention provides necessary but partial signal.
- **Core assumption:** The percentage of signal recovery from patching a component (`||Δ_patch|| / ||Δ_strike||`) causally indicates that component's role in *generating* the final outcome representation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Attention Heads act as 'evidence gatherers'... while MLP Blocks function as robust 'concept composers', each of which is the primary driver..."
  - [section 4.4] Table 3 shows MLP recovery percentages consistently higher than attention for layers 4-9. Figure 7 illustrates MLPs as causally sufficient.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "On Mechanistic Circuits for Extractive Question-Answering" suggest circuit analysis in other domains, but no direct evidence for *this* attention/MLP split in video.
- **Break condition:** If patching an MLP block yields lower recovery than a specific attention head in the same layer, it would contradict the primary-composer role. Also, if the recovery percentages were near-zero for all but one component, it would indicate a bottleneck, not a distributed pattern.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The outcome signal circuit is distributed and redundant across layers/components, explaining model resilience to ablating salient visual features.
- **Mechanism:** Top-K ablation of tokens with highest contribution to the "bowling" class had negligible effect on final classification logits, while activation patching showed signal is cumulatively built by many components. No single component is necessary.
- **Core assumption:** Negligible logit change after ablating the top 10% of contributing tokens implies the classification decision does not critically depend on those specific spatio-temporal features. The "hidden" outcome signal operates independently.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "This distributed and redundant circuit... explains its resilience to simple ablations."
  - [section 4.3] Tables 1 & 2 show minimal logit changes (-0.34, -0.02) after ablating top-contributing patches.
  - [corpus] No direct anchor; this is a novel finding for this specific study.
- **Break condition:** If the same ablation caused a catastrophic drop in the target class logit, it would mean the circuit is fragile and concentrated, not distributed. The existence of a circuit resilient to component removal but still computing a signal is the key.

## Foundational Learning
### Activation Patching
- **Why needed here:** It is the primary causal intervention technique used to assign functional roles (gatherer vs. composer) to model components by measuring signal recovery.
- **Quick check question:** If patching MLP layer 5 recovers 60% of the final signal, what does that percentage represent and what is the null hypothesis?

### Delta Analysis (L2 Norm of Activation Difference)
- **Why needed here:** It locates the internal signal by filtering out noise, showing where the 'strike' and 'gutter' representations diverge meaningfully.
- **Quick check question:** Why might a linear probe show 100% accuracy at layer 0, while the delta L2 norm only rises sharply from layer 5?

### Spatio-Temporal Tokenization in VideoViT
- **Why needed here:** Understanding the model's input representation (tubelets) is crucial to interpret attention maps and ablation targets (patches).
- **Quick check question:** How does a 16x2 tubelet embedding differ from standard image patch embedding, and what new dimension does it introduce?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
VideoViT (base) with 12 layers. Each layer: Multi-head Self-Attention (over spatio-temporal tokens) -> MLP (channel mixer) -> Residual connections. Special [CLS] token aggregates info for classification.

### Critical path
Input video -> Tubelet projection -> Position embeddings -> 12x (Attention + MLP) blocks -> [CLS] token -> Classification head. The "outcome signal" path is parallel within residual streams, amplified from L5 to L11.

### Design tradeoffs
- Spatio-temporal tubelet size (16x16x2) trades fine-grained motion detail for computational cost.
- Pure transformer (vs. CNN hybrids) prioritizes long-range spatio-temporal mixing but may require more data/layers to learn temporal dynamics.

### Failure signatures
1. Linear probe failing to find semantic separation (detecting fingerprints, not concepts).
2. Ablation of "important" patches not affecting final logits (indicating distributed computation).
3. Delta L2 not amplifying (no clear signal cascade).

### First 3 experiments
1. Direct Logit Attribution & Attention Visualization: Identify *where* the model looks (e.g., ball-pin interaction) but not *how* it represents outcome.
2. Delta Analysis (L2 norm): Pinpoint *when* in the layer stack the outcome signal emerges and amplifies.
3. Activation Patching (component-wise): Causal test to assign functional roles (Attention vs. MLP) to the circuit at each layer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** Does the "Attention Gathers, MLPs Compose" circuit generalize across diverse action outcomes in Kinetics-400, or is it an artifact of the specific strike/gutter contrast?  
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors acknowledge the study's scope is limited to a single minimal pair, and they "cannot strictly rule out the possibility that the identified circuit relies on features specific to this pair" rather than a generalized semantic concept (Section 5.2).  
**Why unresolved:** The controlled case study prioritized granular causal analysis over statistical generalizability; the circuit's invariance to different video contexts remains untested.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Applying Automated Circuit Discovery (ACDC) across a large, diverse set of contrastive video pairs from Kinetics-400 to quantify consistency of the MLP-driven amplification pattern.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How do architectural choices, such as spatio-temporal patch size or attention pattern, influence the distribution of labor between attention and MLP blocks in outcome representation?  
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that investigating similar mechanisms in different architectures (e.g., TimeSformer) "will shed light on how model architecture and patch size influence the distribution of labor" (Section 5.3).  
**Why unresolved:** The analysis is confined to one pre-trained VideoViT (vivit-b-16x2) with fixed tubelet embeddings; cross-architectural comparisons are pending.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Replicating activation patching on models with varied patch sizes (e.g., 8x8 spatial, different temporal strides) and alternative video transformers to test if the MLP-composer role persists or shifts.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Do mechanistic interpretability techniques like activation patching offer superior fidelity in isolating semantic circuits compared to gradient-based attribution methods in the video domain?  
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors propose to "benchmark these mechanistic findings against standard interpretability baselines, such as Integrated Gradients, to empirically demonstrate that mechanistic techniques offer superior fidelity" (Section 5.3).  
**Why unresolved:** The study solely employs causal interventions; no direct comparison to gradient-based feature attribution (e.g., Integrated Gradients) is performed.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Applying Integrated Gradients to the same strike/gutter videos and quantitatively comparing saliency maps against the components identified by patching; assessing which method better isolates the outcome signal with lower spurious correlation.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Are MLP blocks causally necessary for the outcome representation, or could attention heads alone achieve similar signal amplification under different training or architectural conditions?  
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] While MLPs show higher recovery (42-60%) than attention (37-54%), the circuit is described as "distributed and redundant." The paper does not test whether MLPs are indispensable or if attention could compensate.  
**Why unresolved:** Activation patching tests individual components in isolation; it does not ablate entire component types (all MLPs or all attention) to assess necessity versus sufficiency.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Performing systematic component-type ablations (e.g., zeroing all MLP outputs or all attention outputs at key layers) and measuring the resulting change in the layer-11 outcome delta signal.

## Limitations
- The analysis is confined to one pre-trained VideoViT and a pair of bowling videos, restricting statistical power.
- Patching details are underspecified (e.g., exact intervention point in residual stream), risking inconsistent implementations.
- The delta L2 metric may capture non-semantic differences, and the findings may only apply to this specific outcome discrimination task.

## Confidence
- **Amplification of outcome signal (rising L2):** Medium
- **Functional split (attention gatherers, MLP composers):** Medium
- **Distributed redundancy explaining resilience:** Medium
