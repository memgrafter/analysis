# Dual-Stage LLM Framework for Scenario-Centric Semantic Interpretation in Driving Assistance

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.27536
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27536
*   **Reference Count:** 24
*   **Primary Result:** A scenario-centric framework with deterministic windows and fixed prompt constraints reveals systematic, multi-dimensional divergence in risk interpretation among LLMs, quantifying semantic ambiguity as a graded property of driving scenarios.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses safety-critical failures in Advanced Driving Assistance Systems (ADAS) arising not from sensor errors but from semantic ambiguity in risk interpretation. It introduces a two-stage, scenario-centric framework that constructs deterministic, temporally bounded windows from multimodal driving data and evaluates Large Language Models (LLMs) under strict prompt constraints and a closed numeric risk schema. Experiments on 16 curated near-people scenarios show that different models (text-only and multimodal) systematically disagree on risk severity, escalation, evidence use, and causal attribution. The multimodal model tends to assign higher risks, while text-only models cite more evidence. This divergence extends to interpreting vulnerable road users, indicating that variability reflects inherent scenario ambiguity rather than isolated model failure. The framework operationalizes a composite uncertainty metric to grade this ambiguity, arguing for its explicit management in safety-aligned ADAS validation per SOTIF principles.

## Method Summary
The core method involves preprocessing raw multimodal sensor streams (camera, LiDAR, telemetry) into a normalized 1 Hz semantic substrate. A deterministic query operator extracts fixed-length temporal windows (e.g., ±3 seconds) around user-selected anchor events, creating reproducible scenario contexts. These contexts are transformed into structured JSON inputs for LLMs via a rigid prompt that enforces a closed numeric taxonomy for outputs: risk level (0–6), evidence signals, and risk factor categories. Three models (GPT-4o-vision, GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek-Chat) are evaluated on the same 16 scenarios. Divergence is measured across four dimensions: mean risk level, high-risk escalation rate (≥4), average evidence signals referenced, and dominant risk factor attribution. A composite uncertainty score aggregates per-scenario, per-model deviations across these dimensions to quantify graded semantic ambiguity.

## Key Results
*   Models exhibit systematic inter-model divergence across all measured dimensions (severity, escalation, evidence, attribution), not random noise.
*   The multimodal model (GPT-4o-vision) assigned significantly higher mean risk levels and escalated more scenarios to high risk compared to text-only models, which referenced more distinct evidence signals.
*   Disagreement persisted in the interpretation of vulnerable road user presence, and composite uncertainty scores successfully tiered scenarios into low, medium, and high ambiguity groups.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Deterministic, temporally bounded scenario windows enable reproducible LLM auditing by eliminating input variability.
*   **Mechanism:** Raw multimodal sensor data is preprocessed into a normalized 1 Hz semantic substrate. A deterministic query operator (`B(·)`) extracts temporally bounded windows around user-selected anchor moments, ensuring identical scenario representations across all evaluations.
*   **Evidence:** [abstract], [section III.B.1], [section IV.A].

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** A fixed prompt constraint layer with a closed numeric risk schema forces models into a comparable semantic space, exposing divergence in risk framing rather than free-text variation.
*   **Mechanism:** All LLMs receive the same structured scenario JSON and a prompt prefix that strictly enforces output as a JSON object using only predefined numeric codes for risk severity, evidence, and categories.
*   **Evidence:** [abstract], [section IV.C].

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Composite uncertainty quantification across multiple dimensions (severity, escalation, evidence, attribution) reveals graded, scenario-specific ambiguity intrinsic to the evidence, not just model noise.
*   **Mechanism:** For each scenario, divergence is flagged if any model deviates from consensus on any dimension. A continuous "composite uncertainty score" aggregates these deviations, normalized to [0,1], allowing scenarios to be tiered by inherent ambiguity.
*   **Evidence:** [abstract], [section VI.D].

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Scenario-Centric Abstraction**
    *   **Why needed:** Replaces continuous sensor streams with discrete, auditable scenario units to isolate semantic interpretation from data variance.
    *   **Quick check:** If you change the anchor time `t₀` or the pre/post seconds (`k`, `m`), does the scenario's semantic content change systematically? Why or why not?
*   **Concept: Closed Semantic Schema for Risk**
    *   **Why needed:** The comparative analysis depends on models reasoning within the same bounded meaning-space (e.g., risk levels 0-6).
    *   **Quick check:** What would happen to inter-model comparison if the prompt allowed free-text risk descriptions instead of numeric codes?
*   **Concept: SOTIF-Inspired Ambiguity Management**
    *   **Why needed:** The paper frames findings within ISO 21448 (SOTIF), where hazards arise from intended functionality limitations under ambiguity. The composite uncertainty metric operationalizes "performance limitation" due to semantic indeterminacy.
    *   **Quick check:** How does a high composite uncertainty score for a scenario relate to a SOTIF 'triggering condition' for a hazardous event?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Multimodal Data Platform -> Normalized Semantic Layer `U` -> Backend Query Service `B(·)` -> Web Authoring Interface (selects `θ`, `t₀`) -> Canonical Scenario `S` -> Temporal Expansion `∆T` -> Prompt Construction -> LLM Inference -> Structured Log `R` -> Analysis Pipeline (metrics, ambiguity).
*   **Critical path:** Data Ingestion (`D_t`) -> Normalized Layer (`U`) -> Scenario Query (`B(U, θ)` -> `S`) -> Temporal Expansion (`∆T`) -> Prompt Construction -> LLM Inference -> Structured Log (`R`) -> Metric Calculation -> Ambiguity Analysis.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Determinism vs. Expressiveness:** The 1 Hz abstraction and fixed prompts sacrifice high-frequency dynamics and rich language for reproducibility and comparability.
    *   **Multimodal Grounding vs. Auditability:** The multimodal model's visual input may yield higher risk sensitivity but produces shorter, less-externalized evidence (fewer `evidence_signals`), trading transparency for perceptual richness.
    *   **Fixed Schema vs. Intrinsic Ambiguity:** The closed numeric taxonomy forces interpretation into predefined buckets, which may itself constrain or distort the model's representation of a genuinely ambiguous scene.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Reproducibility Failure:** Same `θ` and `t₀` yield different `S` across runs (bug in `B(·)` or unstable `U`).
    *   **Constraint Violation:** LLM output is not valid JSON or uses non-permitted numeric codes (prompt engineering failure).
    *   **Ambiguity Collapse:** All scenarios receive near-zero composite uncertainty, suggesting the evaluation setup or metrics fail to capture meaningful divergence.
    *   **Temporal Misalignment:** Expansion `∆T` includes irrelevant context or cuts off critical pre-event cues due to poor anchor selection.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Reproduce Baseline Metrics:** Run the 16 curated near-people scenarios through the full pipeline (GPT-4o-vision, GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek-Chat). Verify replication of key plots: mean risk levels, high-risk escalation, evidence signal counts, dominant risk factor distributions.
    2.  **Validate Composite Uncertainty:** Implement the composite uncertainty calculation as described. Confirm the three-tier split (`S_low`, `S_med`, `S_high`) and generate the heatmap. Check that scenario ordering matches the paper's trend.
    3.  **Probe Prompt Sensitivity:** For a single high-ambiguity scenario, run it with slight prompt variations (e.g., reword instruction, change evidence list order). Observe if the model's core risk level and attribution are stable or if they flip.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question:** How can structured ambiguity metrics derived from LLM-based scenario evaluations be operationalized within adaptive alert policies and SOTIF validation workflows?
    *   **Basis:** [explicit] The conclusion states future work will investigate integration into alert policies and safety assurance.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The paper proposes composite uncertainty scores but does not implement or evaluate their integration into actual alert mechanisms or compliance evidence.
    *   **Resolution evidence:** Empirical deployments measuring how ambiguity-driven thresholds affect alert timing, false alarm rates, and alignment with SOTIF performance evidence requirements.
*   **Question:** To what extent do the observed inter-model divergences generalize beyond the 16 curated near-people scenarios to larger and more diverse driving scenario corpora?
    *   **Basis:** [inferred] The paper acknowledges a small-scale evaluation; larger-scale evaluations are feasible but were limited.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The limited scenario set may not capture full urban driving variability, so robustness of divergence patterns is unknown.
    *   **Resolution evidence:** Systematic large-scale evaluations across hundreds/thousands of diverse scenarios from multiple datasets.
*   **Question:** What prompt engineering or output structuring strategies can mitigate the trade-off between response conciseness and audit traceability observed in multimodal versus text-only LLMs?
    *   **Basis:** [inferred] Results show multimodal models reference fewer evidence signals, suggesting visual grounding reduces textual justification.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the trade-off but does not explore modifications to balance conciseness with justifications.
    *   **Resolution evidence:** Controlled experiments varying prompt constraints (e.g., mandatory minimum evidence listing) and measuring model compliance, response length, and auditor accuracy in reconstructing rationales.
*   **Question:** Which specific semantic or contextual factors within near-people scenarios drive the divergent interpretations of vulnerable road user presence across different LLM architectures?
    *   **Basis:** [inferred] The paper reports disagreement in VRU risk attribution but does not analyze responsible scenario features.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Without isolating contributing factors, it is unclear whether ambiguity stems from model biases, prompt limits, or inherent scenario characteristics.
    *   **Resolution evidence:** Feature attribution studies or systematic ablation experiments that selectively vary scenario elements (e.g., pedestrian trajectory clarity, lighting) and measure changes in VRU classification.

## Limitations
*   **Deterministic scenario windows:** High confidence in concept, but Low confidence in exact implementation due to proprietary fusion/query operator `B(·)` details being omitted.
*   **Fixed prompt constraints & closed schema:** Medium confidence. Taxonomies are defined, but full prompt templates and few-shot examples are not provided, requiring inference.
*   **Composite uncertainty as graded ambiguity:** Medium confidence. The aggregation logic is specified, but weighting rationale and tier split criteria are not fully detailed.
