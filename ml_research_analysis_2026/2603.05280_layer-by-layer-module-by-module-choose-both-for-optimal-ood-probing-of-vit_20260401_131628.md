# Layer by layer, module by module: Choose both for optimal OOD probing of ViT
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05280  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05280  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** For out-of-distribution (OOD) probing of a ViT pretrained on ImageNet-21k, the feedforward activation (Act) yields the best accuracy under strong distribution shift, while the second LayerNorm (LN2) is optimal and stable under weak shift; the standard block output (RC2) is suboptimal except near negligible shift.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates where to probe inside Vision Transformer (ViT) blocks for out-of-distribution (OOD) image classification. By linearly probing every internal module across layers on 11 benchmarks with varying distribution shifts, the authors find that final-layer representations degrade primarily due to shift, making intermediate layers more robust. Probing the standard block output (RC2) is suboptimal under shift. Instead, probing the feedforward activation (Act) excels under strong shift (e.g., image corruptions), while probing the preceding LayerNorm (LN2) provides stable performance under weak shift. The second feedforward layer (FC2) consistently performs worst, likely due to compression.

## Method Summary
The study uses a ViT-Base model pretrained on ImageNet-21k. For each of 11 downstream datasets (e.g., Cifar10, Cifar10-C variants, DomainNet), features are extracted from eight specific operations within every transformer block: LN1, MHA, RC1, LN2, FC1, Act, FC2, RC2. A linear classifier (logistic regression with L-BFGS) is trained on each (layer, module) feature set. Best test accuracies across layers are compared per module. A full finetuning baseline is also reported. The experimental pipeline involves registering forward hooks to capture the CLS token after each operation, preprocessing all images to 224×224 with ImageNet normalization, and using deterministic train/test splits where needed.

## Key Results
- Under **strong distribution shift** (e.g., Cifar10-C Contrast, Motion Blur, Snow), probing **Act** yields the highest accuracy, outperforming RC2 by up to ~2 percentage points.
- Under **weak shift** (e.g., Flowers102, Cifar10), probing **LN2** is optimal and most stable across layers.
- Probing **FC2** consistently produces the worst performance across all datasets and shifts.
- Probing **RC2** (the standard block output) is rarely best, only matching top performers when shift is negligible.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Distribution shift drives final-layer degradation
The drop in linear probing accuracy for deeper layers is primarily caused by the distribution shift between pretraining (ImageNet-21k) and downstream data. Deeper layers specialize to features of the pretraining distribution; under OOD conditions, these specialized features become less discriminative, while intermediate layers retain more general, robust representations. This is evidenced by consistent divergence between ID (Cifar10) and OOD (Cifar10-C) accuracy curves across layers.

### Mechanism 2: Standard probing (RC2) is suboptimal under shift
RC2 combines attention and feedforward outputs with residual addition, creating a composite representation that may dilute discriminative signals preserved more clearly in specific intermediate modules. RC2 is suboptimal on all datasets but one (Flowers102), where shift is minimal.

### Mechanism 3: Module-specific optimality
- **Act** (post-GeLU) operates in a higher-dimensional space (4d), promoting feature disentanglement and filtering noise, which benefits linear separation under strong covariate shift.
- **LN2** (pre-ffn normalized residual) provides a stable, whitened representation that generalizes well under weak shift without the compression of FC2.
- **FC2** consistently worst due to compression into the original hidden dimension, harming linear separability.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input → LN1 → MHA → RC1 → LN2 → FC1 → Act → FC2 → RC2  
(Each block processes the sequence; CLS token extracted after each operation)

**Critical path:**  
1. Estimate shift severity between pretraining (ImageNet-21k) and downstream task.  
2. If shift is **strong** (corruptions, sketch/clipart), probe **Act** in intermediate layers (typically 30–80% depth).  
3. If shift is **weak or unknown**, probe **LN2** for stable performance; avoid **FC2**.  
4. Do not default to **RC2** for OOD without assessing shift.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Act vs LN2:** Act gives higher peaks under strong shift but may degrade in very deep layers; LN2 is more consistent but may cap lower under extreme shift.  
- **Depth:** Intermediate layers often outperform both very shallow and very deep layers under shift.  
- **Computational cost:** All probing points are equally cheap (feature extraction only); selection is purely efficacy-based.

**Failure signatures:**  
- RC2 accuracy >> fine-tuning accuracy gap indicates severe shift degradation.  
- FC2 consistently lowest across datasets confirms it is a poor probing point.  
- If Act performance collapses beyond a certain depth, switch to shallower layers or use LN2.  
- If all modules perform similarly (e.g., Flowers102), the task may be near-ID and final layers suffice.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Shift validation:** Replicate Fig. 1 by linearly probing RC2 across layers for Cifar10 (ID) and Cifar10-C Contrast (OOD); verify final-layer degradation under shift.  
2. **Module sweep:** For a strong-shift dataset (e.g., Motion Blur), plot accuracy vs. layer for all 8 modules (Fig. 6); confirm Act’s mid-layer advantage and FC2’s underperformance.  
3. **Stability check:** Across all 11 datasets, compute mean and variance of best-layer accuracy per module; verify LN2 has lowest variance while Act has highest mean on the four most shifted datasets.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
What information-theoretic properties (e.g., entropy, mutual information) of different transformer modules can explain their varying robustness to distribution shift, and can these properties be used to predict optimal probing points?  
*Basis:* Authors propose extending analysis with information-theoretic, geometric, and invariance measures at the module level.  
*Why unresolved:* No quantitative analysis linking module representations to robustness metrics is provided.

### Open Question 2
Can we automatically detect the degree and nature of distribution shift between pretraining and downstream data and use this to dynamically select the optimal layer and module for probing without labeled examples?  
*Basis:* Identifying whether a setting is ID or OOD is crucial for selecting probe points when finetuning is prohibitive.  
*Why unresolved:* Paper assumes known benchmark identities; no unsupervised shift quantification method is offered.

### Open Question 3
Do the observed module-level robustness patterns (Act excels under strong shift, LN2 stable under weak shift, FC2 consistently poor) hold for other vision transformer architectures (e.g., Swin, Twins), pretraining objectives (MAE, DINO), and model scales?  
*Basis:* Study restricted to a single ViT-Base/ImageNet-21k model.  
*Why unresolved:* Different architectures, pretraining methods, and scales may alter internal representation dynamics; generalizability is unknown.

### Open Question 4
What is the causal mechanism behind the superior robustness of feedforward activation (Act) under strong shift and the consistent failure of FC2? Is it due to higher dimensionality, non-linearity, or position in the residual stream?  
*Basis:* Authors speculatively attribute Act’s performance to feature disentanglement in higher dimension and FC2’s failure to compression.  
*Why unresolved:* The relative contributions of dimensionality, activation type, and sequence context are not isolated experimentally.

## Limitations
- **Model coverage:** Only ViT-Base/ImageNet-21k is studied; other architectures, pretraining objectives, and scales are untested.
- **Dataset scope:** Focuses on synthetic corruptions and two domain shifts; real-world shifts (temporal, geographic) not evaluated.
- **Evaluation scope:** Relies on linear probing; fine-tuning performance and practical deployment impact remain open.

## Confidence
| Claim Cluster | Confidence |
|----------------|------------|
| Shift drives final-layer degradation | High |
| RC2 is suboptimal under OOD | High |
| Act optimal under strong shift; LN2 optimal/stable under weak shift | Medium |

**Rationale:** High confidence for first two stems from consistent evidence across 11 datasets. Medium for third due to approximate shift categorization and nuanced depth interactions.

## Next Checks
1. **Replication with controlled hyperparameters:** Extract features for all 8 modules on Cifar10-C Contrast and Flowers102 using the same pretrained ViT-Base; train logistic regression with `solver='lbfgs'`, `C=1.0`, `max_iter=5000`, standardized inputs; verify per-layer and best-layer accuracies match reported trends.
2. **Cross-model generalization:** Apply the probing pipeline to ViT-Large/16 pretrained on ImageNet-1k on a subset of benchmarks (Contrast, Sketch, Flowers); confirm whether the Act/LN2 performance ordering persists for a larger model with different pretraining data.
3. **Activation function ablation:** For a strong-shift dataset (e.g., Motion Blur), replace GeLU with ReLU in the FFN via model surgery; re-probe Act and FC2 across depths to test whether Act’s advantage relies on GeLU’s disentanglement properties.
