# Flow caching for autoregressive video generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10825
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10825
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: FlowCache achieves speedups of 2.38× on MAGI-1 and 6.7× on SkyReels-V2 with minimal quality degradation (VBench changes: +0.87% and -0.79%).

## Executive Summary
Autoregressive video generation processes long videos by iteratively denoising sequential chunks, incurring high latency due to repeated computation. Existing caching strategies assume uniform denoising across chunks, but video chunks exhibit varying similarity at the same timestep. FlowCache introduces two innovations: (1) a chunkwise adaptive caching policy where each chunk independently decides whether to reuse cached features based on its cumulative denoising state, and (2) a joint importance‑redundancy KV cache compression that bounds memory while preserving context. Evaluated on MAGI‑1 and SkyReels‑V2, FlowCache delivers up to 6.7× acceleration with negligible loss in video quality.

## Method Summary
FlowCache is a training‑free inference accelerator for autoregressive video diffusion models. Its first component computes the relative L1 distance between a chunk’s consecutive denoising states; if the value is below a threshold ϵ and the timestep is not among the final m steps, cached activations are reused, otherwise recomputation occurs. The second component maintains a fixed‑size KV cache split into a compressed region (historical clean chunks) and an active region. When the cache exceeds its budget, a compression engine scores each token per head using a linear combination of importance (average attention over the last 50 queries) and redundancy (average cosine similarity among keys), retaining only the top B tokens. This joint strategy limits memory growth while keeping critical context.

## Key Results
- Speedups: 2.38× on MAGI‑1 and 6.7× on SkyReels‑V2.
- Quality: VBench changes of +0.87% (MAGI‑1) and -0.79% (SkyReels‑V2), indicating minimal degradation.
- Memory: KV cache compression successfully bounds peak memory usage for long sequences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Heterogeneous Chunk Denoising States
Different video chunks at the same denoising timestep exhibit different similarity to their previous states. Theorem 1 proves that the relative L1 distance between consecutive states increases monotonically as denoising progresses; Corollary 1 shows that chunks with distinct content norms yield different distances at identical timesteps, invalidating a single global caching threshold. This heterogeneity necessitates per‑chunk policies. Break condition: If all chunks are nearly identical (e.g., static scenes), uniform caching may suffice; non‑optimal training could also break monotonicity.

### Chunkwise Adaptive Caching
Each chunk maintains a cumulative reuse budget f(X,t,i) based on relative L1 history. At timestep t, if f exceeds ϵ or t lies in the final m steps, the chunk recomputes; otherwise it reuses cached features (Equation 8). The early‑timestep exclusion m protects critical early denoising steps. Proper tuning of ϵ and m is essential; thresholds set too high give no speedup, too low cause quality loss.

### Joint Importance‑Redundancy KV Cache Compression
The KV cache is compressed by scoring tokens per attention head: importance gImp derives from average attention over the last 50 queries; redundancy Red comes from average cosine similarity among keys. A linear combination Score = λ·gImp - (1‑λ)·Red (λ=0.07) ranks tokens; the top B are kept. This approach balances preserving query‑relevant context and eliminating redundant entries, bounding memory without sacrificing quality. Break conditions: λ near 1 retains redundant tokens, near 0 discards important context; budget B too small harms long‑range coherence.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Chunk Manager -> Reuse Policy Module -> Base Generator -> KV Cache Manager -> Compression Engine

**Critical path**:
1. For each active chunk, Reuse Policy computes relative L1 and decides to fetch cached activations or run the Base Generator.
2. After a chunk finishes denoising, its KV cache is moved to the compressed region via the KV Cache Manager.
3. When the total KV budget would be exceeded, the Compression Engine is invoked: it concatenates the compressed region with new entries, computes importance‑redundancy scores, and retains the top B tokens per head.
4. The next chunk starts denoising with the compressed KV as context.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Speed vs. Quality: Higher reuse threshold ϵ increases speed but risks quality; m must exclude critical early steps.
- Memory vs. Quality: Smaller cache budget reduces memory but may drop essential context; λ balances importance and redundancy.
- Granularity: Token‑level selection is accurate but adds overhead; frame‑level reduces cost but may lose detail.

**Failure signatures**:
- Quality drop: Significant VBench decrease, visible artifacts, chunk inconsistencies.
- Memory overflow: OOM errors when KV cache grows without bound.
- No speedup: Most chunks trigger recompute, often due to overly strict ϵ.
- Baseline mismatch: Vanilla results differ, indicating checkpoint/parameter errors.

**First 3 experiments**:

2. Ablation study: Disable chunkwise reuse and KV compression separately to measure individual contributions.
3. KV compression analysis: Ablate λ, query/key granularity, and cache budgets on Physics‑IQ to explore the importance‑redundancy tradeoff and memory‑quality frontier.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the threshold parameters (ϵ and m) be automatically adapted to different models or content without manual tuning? (Values are manually set per model.)
2. Can the fixed λ parameter be made dynamic (e.g., varying across layers or timesteps) to further optimize the quality‑memory trade‑off? (Currently fixed at 0.07.)
3. Does the chunkwise caching principle apply to non‑autoregressive or diffusion‑based video generation models that lack explicit chunking but still exhibit spatial‑temporal redundancy variations?
4. What theoretical limits exist on acceleration ratios for chunkwise caching, and how does FlowCache compare? (No formal analysis provided.)
5. How does KV cache compression interact with different chunkwise policies, and can those components be jointly optimized? (Evaluated as separate modules.)

## Limitations
- Parameter ambiguity: Missing KV cache budget, active region size, compression granularity, and batch size details hinder exact replication.
- Theoretical assumptions: Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 rely on optimally trained flow‑matching models and a power‑law noise schedule; real‑world deviations could break the monotonicity claim.
- Limited generalization: Evaluation spans only two models (MAGI‑1, SkyReels‑V2), offering weak evidence for broader applicability.

## Confidence
- Heterogeneous chunk denoising (Theorem 1, Corollary 1): Medium.
- Chunkwise adaptive caching (Equation 8): Medium.
- Joint importance‑redundancy KV compression (Equations 9‑12): Low.
- Overall speedup and quality results: Medium.
