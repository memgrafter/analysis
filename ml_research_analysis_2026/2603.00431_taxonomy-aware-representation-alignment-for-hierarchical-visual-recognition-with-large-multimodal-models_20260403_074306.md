# Taxonomy-Aware Representation Alignment for Hierarchical Visual Recognition with Large Multimodal Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00431
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00431
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** TARA improves Hierarchical Consistent Accuracy by 5.86% and leaf accuracy by 1.76% on iNaturalist-Plant/Animal, and raises Order F1 by 18.26% and Family F1 by 14.00% on TerraIncognita for known species.

## Executive Summary
Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle with hierarchical visual recognition, especially for novel taxonomic categories, due to inconsistent predictions across granularity levels. This paper introduces Taxonomy-Aware Representation Alignment (TARA), which injects hierarchical structure by aligning LMM internal representations—visual features and first answer token embeddings—with those from a frozen Biology Foundation Model (BFM) via cosine-similarity losses. This forces the LMM to extract taxonomy-structured visual cues and flexibly map them to any queried level. Experiments on iNaturalist and TerraIncognita show significant gains in both known and novel hierarchical accuracy, demonstrating the effectiveness of representation alignment for consistent, fine-grained recognition.

## Method Summary
TARA adds two lightweight MLP projectors to a trainable LMM (e.g., Qwen3-VL-2B), mapping its intermediate visual features (from a chosen layer ℓ) and first answer token embedding (from layer m) to the BFM’s (BioCLIP2) visual and text embedding spaces, respectively. Two cosine-similarity losses minimize the distance between these projections and BFM embeddings for the same image (L_V) and ground-truth label text at the queried granularity (L_C). Training alternates between: (1) updating the LMM and projectors with L_align = (L_V + L_C)/2, and (2) updating only the LMM with a reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) accuracy reward (L_RFT), freezing projectors. This schedule injects taxonomic structure without overwriting it during accuracy optimization.

## Key Results
- On iNaturalist-Plant and iNaturalist-Animal, TARA with Qwen3-VL-2B improves Hierarchical Consistent Accuracy by 5.86% and leaf accuracy by 1.76% over base RFT training.
- On TerraIncognita, TARA raises Order F1 by 18.26% and Family F1 by 14.00% for known species, with smaller but positive gains on novel species (Order F1 +10.15%, Family F1 +1.20%).
- Ablations confirm both alignment losses are complementary, and first-token alignment with all visual tokens yields the best hierarchical consistency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Visual Representation Alignment Structures Taxonomy-Aware Features**
Aligning LMM visual features with BFM embeddings via L_V forces the LMM to reorganize its visual hierarchy to match the BFM’s taxonomy-structured space. The BFM, trained with hierarchical contrastive learning, places taxonomically similar species in proximity. This supervision encourages the LMM to extract discriminative cues consistent with that structure. Evidence: The paper’s formulation of L_V and rationale in Section 4.1; neighbor works confirm BFMs encode taxonomy. Break condition: If the BFM’s taxonomy mismatches the evaluation hierarchy, the signal misleads.

**Mechanism 2: First-Token Alignment Enables Flexible Granularity Mapping**
Aligning the LMM’s first answer token embedding with the BFM’s text embedding for the ground-truth label (L_C) teaches the LMM that a single visual representation can map to any taxonomic level. The BFM’s text encoder produces embeddings consistent with its visual space, allowing the LMM to condition its initial generation state on the queried granularity. Evidence: Abstract and Section 4.2 define L_C using first-token embeddings; rationale for flexibility. Break condition: If the first token is a special start token or the LMM’s generation decouples early states from final output, the signal is ineffective.

**Mechanism 3: Alternating Training Stabilizes Knowledge Absorption**
The alternating schedule updates projectors and LMM with alignment losses, then updates only LMM with RFT. This prevents the alignment-induced feature distribution from being overwritten by the accuracy-only reward, allowing the LMM to absorb taxonomy structure while optimizing task performance. Projectors act as adapters bridging modality gaps without full-model competition. Evidence: Section 4.3 and Algorithm 1 detail the two-step update. Break condition: If projectors lack capacity or RFT steps dominate, the injected structure washes out.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hierarchical Contrastive Learning:** BFM embeddings encode taxonomy because training uses contrastive losses where positives share taxonomic ancestors. Why needed: TARA’s supervision relies on BFM’s pre-structured space. Quick check: If BFM used standard image-text contrastive learning, its embeddings would lack explicit hierarchy, weakening L_V and L_C signals.
- **Intermediate Representation Supervision:** TARA aligns LMM internal layers with BFM layers, not final outputs. Why needed: This knowledge distillation shapes feature geometries without copying logits. Quick check: Mismatched architectures/layer counts could cause misalignment if mappings are nonlinear.
- **No-Thinking Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT):** RFT uses a strict accuracy reward with "output answer only" prompts. Why needed: Ensures clean output space; reasoning traces might confound first-token alignment. Quick check: A thinking prompt would extend the answer sequence, likely decoupling the first token from the final label.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (image + question) → Trainable LMM (extract visual layer ℓ, first answer token layer m) → Projectors (P_V, P_T) → Cosine loss vs. BFM (frozen E_V, E_T). Also: LMM output → RFT reward (accuracy). During training: LMM+projectors updated with L_align; then LMM only with L_RFT (projectors frozen). Inference: discard BFM and projectors; use prompted LMM directly.

**Critical path:**  
1. Select alignment layers (ℓ for vision, m for text) based on model depth and ablation results.  
2. Design features: use all visual tokens for L_V; use first answer token for L_C.  
3. Set alternating schedule (e.g., 1 alignment step, 1 RFT step).  
4. At inference, use standard prompting without projectors.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Layer selection: Earlier layers lack semantics; later layers may lose fine-grained cues. Evidence: Table 3 shows 14th/28th layers best for 28-layer Qwen.  
- L_V pooling: All tokens vs. last token. All tokens preserve spatial structure. Evidence: Table 4.  
- L_C token position: First token vs. last/mean. First token aligns with label generation start. Evidence: Table 4.  
- Projector capacity: Simple MLP (3-layer) vs. complex. Balance between bridging gap and overfitting.  
- Alternating ratio: More alignment steps inject structure faster but may hurt accuracy if overdone.

**Failure signatures:**  
- HCA/leaf accuracy drops: Alignment signal noisy or misaligned (check BFM taxonomy match, layer choice, projector convergence).  
- No gain on novel categories: Representations not generalizing; probe analysis would show no improvement.  
- Training instability (loss spikes): Gradient scale mismatch between L_align and L_RFT; may need weighting.  
- First-token alignment ineffective: LMM uses special tokens for first position; inspect tokenizer and generation behavior.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Train base LMM with only No-Thinking RFT on iNat-Plant; record HCA, leaf accuracy.  
2. **Ablation of alignment components:** Train with only L_V, only L_C, and both; quantify additive contributions.  
3. **Probing analysis:** Freeze trained models (RL vs. RL+TARA); extract final image token embeddings; train linear probe on species classification; compare accuracy to test feature quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can TARA generalize to non-biological hierarchical tasks (e.g., product taxonomies, knowledge graphs)? The paper suggests future work but only tests biological domains.
2. What are optimal alignment layers across LMM architectures and sizes? The study identifies best layers for one model (Qwen3-VL-2B) but no general principle.
3. Why are gains smaller on novel species in TerraIncognita? The paper does not analyze whether BFM embeddings capture enough visual variability for unseen taxa or if alignment overfits known species.
4. Is aligning the first answer token optimal for all hierarchy levels? The ablation aggregates across levels; per-level optimality (coarse vs. fine) is unexplored.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter dependency:** Critical training details (G value, baseline scope, exact alternating ratio, learning rates) are unreported; reproduction relies on assumptions.
- **Distractor construction mismatch:** Paper uses SigLIP for distractor selection; reproduction substitutes BioCLIP2, altering task difficulty.
- **BFM and layer choices:** Optimal alignment layers are model-specific; BioCLIP2 variant used by authors may differ from public checkpoint; results may not transfer across BFMs.

## Confidence
- **TARA effectiveness:** Medium (empirical gains but depend on unreported settings and BFM quality)
- **Visual alignment (L_V):** Medium (plausible mechanism assuming BFM taxonomy matches, but cross-dataset transfer untested)
- **First-token alignment (L_C):** Low (weak evidence that first token links to label semantics; may fail with special tokens or different generation patterns)
- **Alternating training scheme:** Low (novel schedule with no ablation; projector capacity or step ratio could be brittle)

## Next Checks
1. **BFM taxonomy sanity check:** For held-out species, compute nearest neighbors in BFM’s visual space; verify taxonomic neighbors (same genus) are consistently closer than non-neighbors (different family). If violated, L_V’s signal is misaligned.
2. **First-token alignment ablation:** Train TARA variants replacing first-token embedding with last-token or mean-pooled in L_C; measure hierarchical consistency. A significant drop would confirm the mechanism’s necessity.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Systematically vary G (1–8), alignment/RFT step ratio (1:1, 2:1, etc.), and vision layer ℓ; report HCA changes to identify robust configurations vs. overfit settings.
