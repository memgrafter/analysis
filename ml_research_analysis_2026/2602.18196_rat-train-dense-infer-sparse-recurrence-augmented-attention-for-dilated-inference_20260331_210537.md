# RAT+: Train Dense, Infer Sparse -- Recurrence Augmented Attention for Dilated Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18196
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18196
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** A single pretrained model adapts to any dilated attention pattern (e.g., D=16, D=64) with just 1B tokens of fine-tuning, matching dense accuracy at D=16 and dropping only 2–3 points at D=64 on commonsense and long-context tasks.

## Executive Summary
Dilated attention reduces computational cost and KV cache memory by a factor of the dilation size \(D\), but applying it to a dense-pretrained model causes severe accuracy loss due to disconnected attention graphs. RAT+ solves this by incorporating full-sequence recurrence during pretraining and using active recurrence learning (joint training with dense and sparse configurations per batch). After pretraining, a single model can be adapted to any dilation size or hybrid pattern with minimal additional tokens (1B), no full retraining. At 1.5B/100B and 2.6B/200B tokens, it achieves near-dense accuracy at D=16 and small drops at D=64, while delivering up to 20× higher decoding speed for long contexts.

## Method Summary
RAT+ augments standard attention with a recurrence module applied to keys and values. The recurrence uses a forget-gate-based update over chunks, bridging gaps created by dilated patterns. Crucially, it employs active recurrence learning (ARL): during pretraining, each batch randomly mixes dense (\(D=1\)) and sparse (\(D>1\)) configurations (e.g., 50% \(D=1\), remainder distributed across \(D\in\{2,4,8,16,32,64\}\)). This forces the recurrence to acquire sufficient length capability for sparse settings while preserving dense attention performance. Training uses full-sequence recurrence (enforced length \(L^*\) held fixed regardless of dilation) to ensure distributional stability across configurations. After pretraining, adaptation to a target dilation requires only 1B tokens with a small learning rate, without altering the recurrence mechanism.

## Key Results
- Matches dense accuracy at dilation \(D=16\) on commonsense reasoning and LongBench.
- Drops only 2–3 points at \(D=64\) on the same benchmarks.
- Outperforms standard attention on top-k block attention tasks (e.g., 93.8 vs 63.2 on NIAH-MK-2).
- Delivers up to 10× (4K context) and 20× (16K context) higher decoding speed vs. dense attention at \(D=16\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Recurrence bridges disconnected graphs
- **Claim:** Simple recurrence provides explicit connectivity that bridges the disconnected attention graph created by dilated patterns.
- **Mechanism:** The recurrence equation applies a forget-gate-based update over keys and values within each token's effective window, creating temporal dependencies that connect tokens across dilation gaps. This constructs a complete receptive field that dilated attention alone cannot achieve.
- **Core assumption:** Dilated attention with chunk size equal to dilation size creates an attention graph where tokens in different chunks have no direct or indirect connections.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes accuracy degradation from sparsifying dense models; [section 3] Table 1 shows dilated attention without recurrence converges to poor perplexity (43.89 at D=8 vs 7.52 with recurrence).
- **Break condition:** If recurrence is not properly trained (lazy recurrence), it fails to bridge connections effectively, leaving disconnected receptive fields.

### Mechanism 2: Active recurrence learning forces useful recurrence
- **Claim:** Joint training with mixed dense/sparse batches forces the recurrence to acquire the necessary length capability rather than relying on dense attention.
- **Mechanism:** The sparse configurations create pressure for the recurrence to learn long-range connections; the dense configurations prevent degradation of base attention capability. Without sparse pressure, recurrence learns only minimal capacity (lazy recurrence).
- **Core assumption:** Dense attention already provides full connectivity, so recurrence has little incentive to learn length without explicit sparse-task pressure.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4] states direct training leads to lazy recurrence; Table 10 row "L=T D=1" shows high perplexity at D=64 (9.96) without ARL.
- **Break condition:** If joint training ratio is too sparse-heavy, dense performance suffers; if too dense-heavy, recurrence doesn't learn required length.

### Mechanism 3: Fixed enforced length \(L^*\) stabilizes adaptation
- **Claim:** Full-sequence recurrence with fixed enforced length \(L^*\) maintains consistent recurrence output distributions across different dilation settings, enabling flexible adaptation.
- **Mechanism:** Using overlapped chunks with fixed \(L\) during training (instead of setting chunk size = dilation) avoids distribution shift in recurrence outputs at early time steps when dilation changes at inference.
- **Core assumption:** Distribution shift in early-step recurrence outputs hinders dilated attention's ability to adapt after pretraining.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4] notes recurrence outputs at early steps differ when L=D; Table 10 shows higher perplexity for L=D adaptation (8.39 at D=1) vs fixed L (7.40).
- **Break condition:** If \(L^*\) is too small for desired dilation, receptive field is incomplete; if too large, may add unnecessary computation.

  Why needed: RAT+ works even without positional encoding (NoPE); understand why joint training enables this.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input → RoPE → Q/K/V projections → Recurrence (on K/V only) → Dilated attention mask (D) + optional local mask (W) → Output gating → Feed-forward

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Implement parallel associative scan for recurrence (training) and single-step update (decoding).  
  2. Build data sampler assigning each batch a random dilation \(D \in \{1,2,4,8,16,32,64\}\) with 50% probability \(D=1\) (dense) and 50% distributed across others.  
  3. During sparse batches (\(D>1\)), ensure recurrence capacity is challenged by requiring effective context ≥64 tokens.  
  4. After pretraining, adaptation: 1B tokens with LR=2e-5 on target \(D\), no warmup.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **\(L^*\) size**: Larger (e.g., 128) supports bigger dilation but may harm short-context performance; paper finds \(L^*=64\) optimal for 4K context.  
  - **Joint training ratio**: More frequent sparse batches improve recurrence learning but risk underfitting dense attention; paper uses 50% dense.  
  - **Local window addition**: \(W\) preserves local information but adds \(O(W)\) cache; must balance based on task.  
  - **Full-sequence vs overlapped**: Full-sequence (\(L=T\)) simplifies implementation but may learn longer-than-needed recurrence; overlapped with fixed \(L\) avoids this but requires careful masking.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Lazy recurrence**: Good \(D=1\) perplexity but >10× worse at \(D=16\) (e.g., Table 10: \(D=1\) PPL 7.39 vs \(D=16\) 8.66).  
  - **Distribution shift adaptation failure**: Adapting from \(L=D\) training shows high initial loss and unstable curves.  
  - **NoPE collapse**: Without joint training, NoPE models degrade significantly on longer contexts (Table 7 shows RoPE vs NoPE gap <0.03 only with ARL).  
  - **KV cache mismatch**: If recurrence implementation differs between training (parallel scan) and inference (step update), numerical drift accumulates.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Lazy recurrence validation**: Train three 1.5B models: (a) \(D=1\) only, (b) ARL (50% \(D=1\), 50% \(D=64\)), (c) \(D=64\) only. Adapt each to \(D=64\) with 100M tokens. Expect (a) large PPL gap (>10×), (b) minimal gap, (c) poor \(D=1\) performance.  
  2. **\(L^*\) sensitivity**: Pretrain with ARL but vary enforced \(L^* \in \{16,32,64,128\}\). Evaluate \(D=64\) after adaptation. Expect U-curve: \(L^*<64\) hurts, \(L^*=64\) optimal, \(L^*=128\) marginal/no gain.  
  3. **Hybrid pattern flexibility**: After ARL pretraining (\(L^*=64\)), create layer-wise hybrids (e.g., alternating \(D=1\) and \(D=16\) layers). Test on LongBench subsets. Expect intermediate performance between pure \(D=1\) and \(D=16\), confirming pattern-agnostic adaptation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the optimal hybrid sparse configuration (layer-wise or head-wise combinations of dilation and local window sizes) for different tasks and model scales?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state in Section 5.2: "We emphasize that our goal is to demonstrate the feasibility, rather than to identify optimal hybrid designs... We leave the search of optimal hybrid sparse patterns to future work."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper presents only a few hand-picked hybrid examples without systematic exploration. The optimal configuration likely depends on task-specific requirements and model size, but this trade-off space remains unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation across a wide range of hybrid configurations—varying number/placement of dense vs. sparse layers, per-head dilation ratios, and local window sizes—across multiple benchmarks (LongBench, commonsense reasoning, retrieval tasks) and model scales (1.5B, 2.6B, larger). Results would map which patterns maximize accuracy for each task category and scale.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the precise mechanism by which recurrence in RAT+ improves token representations for block-based selection in top-k block attention, and does this generalize across different block scoring algorithms?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper hypothesizes in Section 5.2 that "recurrence encourages token representations to better reflect the content of their block, so that block selection algorithms... can capture this information more effectively." This is supported by large accuracy gaps (e.g., 93.8 vs 63.2 on NIAH-MK-2) but not validated.
- **Why unresolved:** The hypothesis remains speculative; it is unclear whether recurrence improves block coherence, changes attention entropy, or affects other properties. Additionally, the benefit may vary between block scoring methods (Quest’s min/max vs. MoBA’s mean pooling), but this comparison is incomplete.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis comparing token and block representations (e.g., via probing, PCA, attention distribution statistics) between RAT+ and attention-only models under top-k sparsification. Ablation studies with modified recurrence (e.g., removing input-dependent gates) would identify which component aids block scoring. Evaluating across diverse block selection algorithms would test generalizability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the required recurrence capacity (\(L^*\)) scale with model size, and is there a predictable relationship that guides \(L^*\) selection for arbitrary model scales?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 5 shows that scaling from 1.5B to 2.6B reduces the performance gap between \(D=64\) and \(D=1\). The authors attribute this to "increased capacity of recurrence with a larger hidden dimension" but used the same \(L=64\) for both. They chose \(L=64\) empirically without a scaling law.
- **Why unresolved:** Only two model scales were tested. It is unknown whether \(L=64\) suffices for much larger models (e.g., 10B+) or if larger models require proportionally larger \(L^*\). The minimal recurrence length needed for stable dilated performance remains unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Training models at multiple scales (e.g., 0.2B, 0.5B, 1B, 2B, 5B, 10B) with fixed \(L=64\), measuring the performance drop between \(D=1\) and \(D=64\). Repeating with varying \(L^*\) would find the minimal required recurrence length per scale. Correlating these with architectural dimensions (hidden size, layers) could yield a predictive formula.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the NoPE configuration in RAT+ truly eliminate the need for positional information across all context lengths and dilation sizes, or are there breakdown points at extreme lengths?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 5.4 reports that RAT+ with NoPE achieves comparable perplexity to RoPE at 4096 context, hypothesizing that joint training with sparse configurations provides effective positional cues through shorter effective contexts. However, tests beyond 4096 are limited (NTK extended to 16384 in some tables).
- **Why unresolved:** The claim is based on a narrow context range. For very long contexts (e.g., 100K+ tokens) even with large dilation, the effective receptive field of recurrence may become large enough that NoPE fails. Additionally, the interaction between NoPE and extreme dilation (e.g., \(D=256\)) at long contexts is unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation of RAT+ with NoPE vs. RoPE across an extensive range of context lengths (4K to 1M tokens) and dilation sizes (1 to 256), measuring perplexity and downstream task accuracy. Identifying specific length/dilation combinations where NoPE degrades would reveal its limits and refine the hypothesis about implicit positional encoding.

## Limitations
- **Scope of validation:** Results are shown on commonsense (1.5B/100B) and LongBench (2.6B/200B) with 4K/16K context. Unknown if benefits hold for larger models (>10B), ultra-long contexts (>100K), or non-English/multimodal tasks.
- **Computational cost:** Joint training mixes dense/sparse batches, potentially increasing overhead vs. standard dense-only pretraining. Exact trade-off not quantified.
- **Recurrence stability:** Analysis assumes recurrence outputs are distributionally stable across dilation (\(L^*\) fixed). Numerical drift between parallel-scan training and sequential inference could accumulate, especially at high \(D\).

## Confidence
- **High:** Joint training (ARL) prevents lazy recurrence (Table 10 evidence). \(D=16\) matches dense accuracy (consistent across scales).
- **Medium:** Generalization to \(D=64\) (2–3 point drop may vary by task). Mechanistic claim that recurrence bridges disconnected graphs (plausible but not exhaustively probed).
- **Low:** Guaranteed adaptation with 1B tokens across *all* hybrid patterns (paper tests limited hybrids).
