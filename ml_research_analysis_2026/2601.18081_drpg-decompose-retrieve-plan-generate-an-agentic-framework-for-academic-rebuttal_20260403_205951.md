# DRPG (Decompose, Retrieve, Plan, Generate): An Agentic Framework for Academic Rebuttal

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.18081
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18081
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** DRPG outperforms prior rebuttal agents by ~40 Elo points and surpasses average human rebuttal quality using only an 8B model, with a Planner accuracy >98%.

## Executive Summary
Academic rebuttal generation is a complex, long-context task where existing LLM pipelines often produce generic responses. DRPG introduces a four-agent framework—Decomposer, Retriever, Planner, Executor—that systematically breaks down reviews, retrieves relevant paper excerpts, plans a persuasive strategy, and generates targeted replies. Evaluated on a large corpus of computer science reviews, DRPG achieves a substantial gain of approximately 40 Elo points over prior state-of-the-art agents and even exceeds the quality of average human rebuttals, all while using an 8B parameter model. The core innovation lies in the Planner module, which selects from multiple candidate rebuttal perspectives with over 98% accuracy, and a fallback mechanism that maintains robustness when planning uncertainty is high.

## Method Summary
DRPG processes each reviewer comment through four stages. First, a Decomposer LLM splits the full review into atomic points. For each point, a Retriever based on the BGE-M3 dense encoder identifies the top-15 most relevant paragraphs from the paper, reducing the context length by over 75%. An internal Idea Proposer generates five candidate rebuttal perspectives. A trained Planner (an MLP atop a frozen BGE-M3 encoder) scores each perspective against the retrieved paragraphs and selects the most supportive one if its confidence exceeds 0.8; otherwise it falls back to a null perspective. Finally, an Executor LLM generates the rebuttal paragraph conditioned on the atomic review point, the retrieved evidence, and the selected perspective (or without a specific plan). The final rebuttal concatenates the responses for all atomic points. The Planner is trained on 50k examples to rank the human-written perspective highest among candidates.

## Key Results
- DRPG achieves ~40 Elo point improvement over prior rebuttal agents.
- Surpasses average human rebuttal quality using only an 8B model.
- Planner module attains >98% accuracy in selecting the most feasible rebuttal perspective.
- Maintains performance in multi-round discussion settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Reducing Search Space via Decomposition & Targeted Retrieval
- **Claim:** Breaking a long review into atomic points and retrieving only relevant paper paragraphs mitigates the "Lost in the Middle" problem, allowing an LLM to focus on specific evidence without being overwhelmed by irrelevant context.
- **Mechanism:** The Decomposer isolates distinct reviewer concerns. The Retriever (BGE-M3 dense retrieval) then finds the top-K (~15) most relevant paragraphs for each point, reducing the input length to the Executor by ~75%. This creates focused, evidence-dense contexts for response generation.
- **Core assumption:** The most relevant evidence for addressing a specific atomic reviewer point can be found in a small, concentrated subset of the full paper, and retrieval can identify this subset accurately.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "To mitigate long-context challenges, DRPG first employs a Decomposer to break a review into several 'points'... For each point, a Retriever then selects the most relevant paragraphs... reducing the input length by over 75% while preserving critical evidence."
  - [section 3.2]: "Due to the substantial length of academic papers, the performance... may be adversely affected by the 'Lost in the Middle' phenomenon... we divide the paper into paragraphs and employ a Retriever to identify the most relevant paragraphs."
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers discuss retrieval and decomposition (e.g., `Plan Then Retrieve`, `Comprehensive Survey on Agentic Search`), indicating this is a recognized architectural pattern for long-context tasks.
- **Break condition:** If the Decomposer fails to produce truly atomic points or the Retriever fails to rank the actually relevant paragraphs within the top-K, the Executor receives a noisy or incomplete context, leading to generic or off-point responses.

### Mechanism 2: Explicit Strategy Selection via a High-Accuracy Planner
- **Claim:** Training a dedicated Planner module to select from multiple candidate rebuttal perspectives (clarification vs. justification) based on their supportability in the paper's text leads to more persuasive, tailored, and higher-quality rebuttals than letting the LLM generate perspectives implicitly.
- **Mechanism:** An idea proposer (LLM) generates candidate perspectives for a review point. The Planner (trained MLP+encoder) scores each perspective by averaging its learned compatibility with the set of retrieved relevant paragraphs. The perspective with the highest supportive score is selected if confidence (softmax of scores) exceeds a threshold (T=0.8); otherwise, it falls back. This forces a deliberative "planning" step before generation.
- **Core assumption:** The best rebuttal direction for a given reviewer point can be identified by measuring the alignment between a candidate perspective's claim and the factual/argumentative content in the paper's relevant sections. This alignment signal can be learned from human rebuttal examples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "the Planner in DRPG reaches over 98% accuracy in identifying the most feasible rebuttal direction... its value in providing multi-perspective and explainable suggestions."
  - [section 3.3]: Details the scoring function (Eq. 1) and training loss (Eq. 2). The Planner is trained to rank the human-selected perspective highest among 6 candidates (5 synthetic + 1 ground-truth).
  - [section 5.1]: Ablation shows the full Planner (98.64% test acc) vastly outperforms no-paper (61.55%), full-paper (86.41%), and encoder-only (45.44%) variants, proving learned scoring over retrieved paragraphs is critical.
  - [corpus]: Neighbor `Dancing in Chains` also explores strategic persuasion in academic rebuttal, suggesting the community sees planning as key.
- **Break condition:** If the Planner's confidence is low for all perspectives, the fallback occurs, and the system defaults to a simpler generate-only mode, likely reducing the quality boost from planning.

### Mechanism 3: Fallback Mechanism Ensures Robustness to Uncertainty
- **Claim:** The Planner's self-confidence threshold provides a safety net, preventing the system from generating a poorly supported or irrelevant perspective when no strong candidate exists, thereby maintaining baseline performance instead of causing a catastrophic failure.
- **Mechanism:** During inference, after scoring all candidate perspectives for a review point, the system computes the softmax-normalized score (conf) for the top perspective. If conf < T (0.8), the system sets the selected perspective to a null token (ϵ), signaling the Executor to proceed without a specific planned direction (relying on its own capabilities with just the review point and retrieved paragraphs).
- **Core assumption:** A low maximum confidence score among candidates reliably indicates that none of the proposed perspectives are strongly supported by the paper's content for this specific point, and forcing one would degrade quality. The Executor can handle such cases reasonably well with its pre-trained knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3]: "During inference, we design a self-confidence mechanism... a perspective is passed to the Executor only if its confidence score exceeds a predefined threshold T; otherwise, DRPG falls back to the setting without the Planner."
  - [section 4.1]: "Under this setting, approximately 62% of review points are assigned a valid perspective. The remaining cases... it's heavily dependent on specific paper content, making it difficult to propose a valid perspective."
  - [corpus]: The concept of confidence-based fallback is common in retrieval-augmented systems (seen in neighbor papers like `CiteLLM` for trustworthy discovery) but is specifically adapted here for planning.
- **Break condition:** The fallback itself is a designed break. The mechanism fails if the fallback rate is excessively high (>~40%), indicating the Planner is too conservative or the candidate generation is inadequate, causing the system to lose its planned-strategy advantage too often.

## Foundational Learning
#### Decomposition of Complex Tasks
**Why needed:** Academic reviews are multifaceted; decomposing them into atomic concerns prevents context overload and enables parallel, focused processing downstream.  
**Quick check:** Given a sample review comment, can you split it into 2-3 distinct, self-contained questions or criticisms that could be answered independently?

#### Dense Retrieval for Evidence Grounding
**Why needed:** The rebuttal must be factually grounded in the specific paper; retrieval maps abstract review points to concrete paragraphs, creating a short, relevant context for the Executor and Planner.  
**Quick check:** For a given atomic review point and a list of paper paragraphs (with embeddings), how would you compute a relevance score? What is the key difference between this and keyword matching?

#### Implicit vs. Explicit Planning in LLMs
**Why needed:** Standard LLM prompting asks the model to "think" but doesn't enforce a discrete planning step with candidate evaluation. DRPG's Planner externalizes this, training a model to explicitly choose a response direction from a set, which is shown to be more effective than implicit internal planning.  
**Quick check:** What is the fundamental difference between an LLM generating a rebuttal in one pass vs. first generating several candidate "perspectives" and then selecting the best one based on paper content?

#### Confidence-Triggered Fallback in Hybrid Pipelines
**Why needed:** The Planner is a learned module and will be uncertain on some inputs. A hard fallback rule prevents the system from committing to a low-confidence, potentially harmful plan, trading off the planning benefit for robustness when planning is unlikely to help.  
**Quick check:** If the Planner outputs confidence scores of [0.3, 0.25, 0.2] for its top three candidates, what should the system do given T=0.8? What is the risk of not falling back in this case?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Review → Decomposer → Atomic Points → Retriever → Relevant Paragraphs → Planner → Selected Perspective → Executor → Rebuttal Paragraph

**Critical Path:** For each atomic review point *i*: Review → Decomposer → Point_i → Retriever(Point_i) → Paragraphs_i → Planner(Point_i, Paragraphs_i, [Perspectives]) → Selected_Perspective_i → Executor(Point_i, Paragraphs_i, Selected_Perspective_i) → Rebuttal_Point_i. Final rebuttal = concatenation of all Rebuttal_Point_i.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Planner Accuracy vs. Complexity: The trained MLP achieves high accuracy (98%) but requires supervised training data; a simpler similarity scorer (encoder-only) is less effective.
- Retrieval Recall vs. Noise: Using only top-K paragraphs reduces noise and fits context limits but risks missing crucial evidence if K is too small or retrieval fails.
- Confidence Threshold (T): A high T (0.8) ensures only high-confidence plans are used but increases fallback rate; a lower T risks poor perspectives being applied.

**Failure Signatures:**
- High fallback rate (>40%): Indicates Planner is overly conservative, candidate generation is poor, or training data distribution mismatch.
- Lower Elo vs. DRG (no Planner): If DRPG performs worse than the Decompose-Retrieve-Generate baseline, the Planner is actively harmful.
- Poor Judge Score despite high Elo: Suggests pairwise comparisons (Elo) might be gamed by superficial fluency; judge model evaluates actual convincingness.

**First 3 Experiments:**

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Integration with Automated Experimentation Systems
**Question:** How can DRPG be integrated with automated experimentation systems (e.g., AI Scientist) to generate new experimental results during rebuttal?  
**Basis in paper:** The limitation section states: "An interesting future direction is to integrate DRPG with AI Scientist systems to support experimental supplementation and achieve complete automation of the rebuttal process."  
**Why unresolved:** The current DRPG only leverages existing paper content and cannot produce new experimental evidence to address reviewer requests for additional validation or data.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a closed-loop pipeline where DRPG identifies missing experiments, triggers an AI Scientist-like system to execute them, and incorporates the results into the rebuttal. Evaluate on a benchmark where rebuttals traditionally require new experiments, measuring improvements in reviewer scores.

### Open Question 2: Generalization of Planner to Other Domains
**Question:** Does the Planner's high accuracy (98.6%) in selecting rebuttal perspectives generalize to academic domains beyond computer science or to reviews with non-standard phrasing?  
**Basis in paper:** The Planner is trained and evaluated solely on the Re2 dataset from CS conferences; no cross-domain or robustness tests are reported.  
**Why unresolved:** Performance may rely on patterns specific to CS papers/reviews; other fields may have different argument structures, evidence types, or linguistic styles.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Test the trained Planner on datasets from other disciplines (e.g., biology, social sciences) or on synthetically perturbed reviews (e.g., with ambiguity, sarcasm, or informal language). Report accuracy drop and analyze failure modes.

### Open Question 3: Completeness of Rebuttal Strategy Taxonomy
**Question:** Are "Clarification" and "Justification" sufficient as a complete taxonomy for rebuttal strategies, or do effective human rebuttals also employ other high-level strategies (e.g., negotiation, partial concession, redirection)?  
**Basis in paper:** Section 3.3 defines only two types; Section 5.2 shows that restricting to one type degrades performance. The perspective generator is prompted within this binary framework without exploring a broader space.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper does not analyze whether human rebuttals use additional strategic moves not captured by these categories. The Planner's training data combines synthetic perspectives with ground-truth extracted from rebuttals, but the extraction may implicitly assume this binary.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a qualitative study coding a large corpus of human rebuttals for strategy types beyond the two defined. Extend DRPG's Planner to generate and select from an expanded taxonomy, then compare rebuttal quality via human or LLM evaluation.

### Open Question 4: Sensitivity to Confidence Threshold
**Question:** How sensitive is DRPG's overall performance to the self-confidence threshold T in the Planner, and what is the optimal trade-off between using planner-selected perspectives and falling back to a non-planning baseline?  
**Basis in paper:** Section 4.1 sets T=0.8 arbitrarily, noting that 38% of review points fall back to non-planner settings. No ablation or sensitivity analysis on T is provided.  
**Why unresolved:** The threshold critically determines when the Planner’s perspective is trusted. An inappropriate T might cause unnecessary fallbacks (missing good strategies) or force weak perspectives (hurting quality). This trade-off may vary by review type.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically vary T (e.g., 0.5 to 0.95) and measure Elo scores, fallback rate, and breakdown by review characteristics. Identify the T that maximizes performance and characterize when fallbacks are most beneficial.

## Limitations
- **Planner checkpoint availability:** The exact pretrained weights for the Planner MLP are not confirmed present in the public repository, blocking exact replication of its high-accuracy performance.
- **Executor prompt & merging ambiguity:** The paper's provided prompt is for a "Whole Review," but the system processes atomic points. The precise logic for merging point-level responses into a coherent final rebuttal is unspecified.
- **Retriever specifics:** It is unclear if the BGE-M3 encoder used is the base model or a fine-tuned variant on scientific text, which could significantly impact retrieval quality for this domain.
