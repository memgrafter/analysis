# Improving Generalizability of Hip Fracture Risk Prediction via Domain Adaptation Across Multiple Cohorts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.17962
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17962
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Combining MMD, CORAL, and DANN domain adaptation improved hip fracture risk prediction AUC from 0.81 to 0.95 for females (SOF→UKB) and from 0.71 to 0.88 for males (MrOS→UKB) on held-out UKB test cohorts (p<0.001).

## Executive Summary
Hip fracture risk prediction models often fail when applied to new cohorts due to covariate shift in clinical and DXA features. This study addresses the problem using unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) to align feature distributions between source (SOF/MrOS) and target (UKB) cohorts without requiring target outcomes. The authors combined three alignment methods—Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD), Correlation Alignment (CORAL), and Domain-Adversarial Neural Networks (DANN)—and selected hyperparameters by minimizing MMD between source and target embeddings (an outcome-free criterion). On held-out UKB test sets, the combined approach significantly boosted discrimination: AUC increased from 0.81 to 0.95 for female-sourced models and from 0.71 to 0.88 for male-sourced models. These gains demonstrate that multi-method domain adaptation can yield robust generalization across diverse populations even with limited target data and no target labels.

## Method Summary
The task is to predict 10-year incident hip fracture using harmonized clinical and DXA features (12 total) from three cohorts: SOF (females), MrOS (males), and UKB (target). Models are trained on a source cohort and adapted to UKB using only unlabeled UKB data. The architecture comprises a two-layer feature extractor (MLP with ReLU and normalization) producing a 256-dimensional embedding, a linear classifier with sigmoid, and three alignment modules: MMD (kernel-based mean alignment), CORAL (covariance alignment), and a domain discriminator with gradient reversal (DANN) to encourage domain-invariant features. Hyperparameters (learning rates, λ weights) are chosen via an outcome-free grid search that minimizes MMD between source and target embeddings on a pseudo-training split of UKB. The final model is then trained on the full source + pseudo-target data and evaluated on a held-out UKB test set. This protocol avoids using UKB fracture outcomes for tuning, mimicking real-world deployment where target labels are unavailable.

## Key Results
- Combined MMD+CORAL+DANN achieved the highest AUC: 0.95 (female SOF→UKB) and 0.88 (male MrOS→UKB), up from baseline 0.81 and 0.71 respectively (p<0.001).
- Outcome-free hyperparameter selection (minimizing MMD) identified configurations that maximized downstream performance.
- Single-method adaptations provided smaller gains; the full combination was synergistic, indicating complementary alignment effects.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)** reduces the difference in mean embeddings between source and target cohorts in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS). By minimizing MMD loss during training, the feature extractor is encouraged to produce embeddings whose distributions have matching first-order statistics (means) in the RKHS, mitigating mean shifts characteristic of covariate shift. The method uses a multi-scale Gaussian RBF kernel with bandwidth set by the median heuristic. Evidence: MMD is used for outcome-free hyperparameter selection and is a component of the best combination. Break condition: poor kernel bandwidth or insufficient feature extractor capacity may distort predictive structure without achieving true alignment.

**Correlation Alignment (CORAL)** aligns second-order statistics (covariance matrices) of source and target embeddings, addressing domain shift arising from differing feature variances or correlation patterns. CORAL minimizes the squared Frobenius norm between empirical covariance matrices, forcing embeddings to have similar variability structure. It complements mean alignment (e.g., after standardization) and handles moderate differences in measurement scales. Evidence: CORAL is part of the top-performing combination and evaluated in ablations. Break condition: high embedding dimensions relative to target sample size can make covariance estimates noisy and destabilize training.

**Domain-Adversarial Neural Networks (DANN)** employ a gradient reversal layer (GRL) to train a domain discriminator that distinguishes source from target embeddings while simultaneously updating the feature extractor to fool the discriminator. This adversarial game drives the extractor to remove domain-specific information while retaining fracture-predictive signals. The overall objective combines task loss, domain loss (reversed for the extractor), and alignment weights. Evidence: DANN is critical in the best combination and its objective is derived formally. Break condition: an overly strong discriminator yields no useful gradient; class imbalance between source and target can also destabilize the game.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Input (12 harmonized clinical/DXA features) -> FeatureExtractor (2-layer MLP, ReLU, LayerNorm/BatchNorm, 256-dim embedding) -> Parallel alignment: (1) MMD loss computed on embeddings, (2) CORAL loss computed on embeddings, (3) DomainDiscriminator with GradientReversalLayer -> Classifier (linear layer + sigmoid) -> Output probability. Source embeddings also feed the classifier; target embeddings are used only for alignment.

**Critical path:**
1. Split UKB target cohort into pseudo-training (for alignment) and held-out test (evaluation), stratified by outcome.
2. For each hyperparameter configuration (learning rate, weight decay, λ weights), train the full model on source data (with labels) plus pseudo-target data (without labels) to convergence, optimizing the combined loss.
3. After training, compute MMD between source and target embeddings on the pseudo-training set. Select the configuration that minimizes this MMD (outcome-free criterion).
4. Retrain the model with the selected hyperparameters on the full source + pseudo-target dataset.
5. Evaluate AUC (and other metrics) only on the held-out UKB test set; repeat over multiple seeds.

**Design tradeoffs:** Combining all three alignment methods (MMD+CORAL+DANN) yields the highest AUC (0.95/0.88) but increases computational cost and hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., λ weights). The outcome-free selection avoids target-label leakage but relies on MMD as a proxy for downstream performance, which may not always correlate. Splitting the target cohort ensures unbiased evaluation but reduces the number of samples available for alignment, potentially limiting alignment strength, especially for the small male UKB cohort.

**Failure signatures:**
- MMD or CORAL loss does not decrease during training → feature extractor capacity insufficient, λ too low, or implementation errors.
- Domain discriminator accuracy remains near 0.5 → GRL λ mis-set, discriminator too weak/strong, or features already domain-invariant.
- Source task loss diverges or plateaus at a high value → alignment losses dominate and corrupt supervised signal; reduce λ weights.
- Held-out target AUC is worse than baseline → alignment overfits to the small pseudo-target set or distorts predictive features.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline replication: Train a source-only model (no adaptation) on SOF/MrOS and evaluate on UKB to confirm domain shift (AUC ~0.81 female, ~0.71 male).
2. Single-method ablation: Apply each adaptation method (MMD, CORAL, DANN) separately with outcome-free selection, measure ΔAUC versus baseline to quantify individual contributions.
3. Combination evaluation: Train with all dual and triple combinations (MMD+CORAL, MMD+DANN, CORAL+DANN, all three) to assess complementarity and identify the best-performing setup.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the size of the target cohort pseudo-training split affect the stability and performance of outcome-free hyperparameter selection via MMD minimization?
- Do alternative unsupervised domain adaptation methods (e.g., conditional alignment, optimal transport, contrastive learning) outperform the combined MMD-CORAL-DANN approach for hip fracture risk generalization?
- Can the proposed domain adaptation framework be extended to multimodal inputs (e.g., raw DXA images plus clinical data) while maintaining or improving generalization?

## Limitations
- Small target UKB male cohort (n=210) may lead to unstable alignment and high variance in performance estimates.
- Outcome-free hyperparameter selection via MMD minimization lacks direct validation that MMD correlates with downstream AUC across the hyperparameter space.
- Incomplete reporting of architectural details (e.g., normalization layer placement) and full hyperparameter grids hinders exact replication.
- Harmonization to 12 features may discard predictive information; results may not generalize to raw or richer data.
- Evaluation restricted to UKB; no external validation on other populations.

## Confidence
- Combined domain adaptation (MMD+CORAL+DANN) improves AUC: **Medium**
- Outcome-free hyperparameter selection via MMD: **Low-Medium**
- Mechanistic explanations (MMD aligns means, CORAL aligns covariances, DANN enforces domain invariance): **Medium**
