# Surrogate-Assisted Genetic Programming with Rank-Based Phenotypic Characterisation for Dynamic Multi-Mode Project Scheduling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16286
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16286
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** A surrogate-assisted GP method (SKGGP) with rank-based phenotypic characterisation reduces average makespan ratios and full evaluation budget by 20-40% on dynamic multi-mode resource-constrained project scheduling problems.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the dynamic multi-mode resource-constrained project scheduling problem (DMRCPSP), where real-time decisions must adapt to uncertainty. It proposes SKGGP, a surrogate-assisted genetic programming framework that evolves heuristic rules for activity ordering and group selection. The core innovation is a rank-based phenotypic characterisation (PC) scheme that encodes a heuristic’s ranking behaviour over eligible activity-mode pairs and groups across sampled decision situations. This PC vector serves as a proxy for fitness, enabling a nearest-neighbour surrogate to pre-select promising offspring from a large pool. Experiments on three DMRCPSP scenarios show SKGGP (with offspring multiplier *k*=2 or 4) converges faster and achieves lower makespans than the baseline KGGP, while reducing required full evaluations by 20-40% with only marginal computational overhead.

## Method Summary
SKGGP evolves two-tree GP individuals (one for ordering, one for group selection) to construct scheduling heuristics. Each generation, the current population is fully evaluated via simulation, and their PC vectors are generated by sampling decision situations and recording the ranks of all eligible activity-mode pairs (ordering) and groups (selection). These rank lists are concatenated into a fixed-length vector. Duplicate individuals (by PC) are removed. To generate the next generation, the algorithm creates *k*×|*P*| intermediate offspring via crossover/mutation. For each offspring, its PC vector is computed and compared (Manhattan distance) to the stored PC vectors of the current population; the nearest neighbour’s fitness is assigned as the surrogate estimate. The top |*P*| offspring by estimated fitness are then fully evaluated to form the new population. This process repeats for a fixed number of generations.

## Key Results
- SKGGP with *k*=2 and *k*=4 significantly outperforms baseline KGGP, achieving lower average makespan ratios (e.g., 1.678 vs. 1.691 on the 0.5/R12 scenario) and faster convergence.
- The surrogate reduces required full evaluations by 20-40% compared to KGGP, with only about 1/20–1/40 of the evaluation time spent on surrogate overhead.
- Surrogate precision (ability to correctly identify the true best offspring) drops from ~80-90% for *k*=1.5 to ~50-60% for *k*=4, indicating a trade-off between exploration and selection accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Rank-based phenotypic characterisation (PC) vectors enable meaningful behavioural comparison between GP heuristics for DMRCPSP.
- **Mechanism:** During simulation, the heuristic's decisions in sampled decision situations are recorded as rankings of eligible activity-mode pairs (ordering rule) and activity-mode groups (selection rule). These rankings are concatenated into a fixed-length numerical vector, approximating the heuristic's decision-making policy across contexts.
- **Core assumption:** Heuristics with similar ranking patterns across representative decision situations will exhibit similar fitness (makespan).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...encodes the ranking behavior of GP heuristics over eligible activity-mode pairs and groups across sampled decision situations."
  - [section III-B] Details the extraction of decision vectors from activity ordering and group selection situations, then concatenation into a PC vector.
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on surrogate-assisted neuroevolution or other scheduling domains; direct evidence for this specific PC scheme's efficacy is primarily within this paper's experiments (Section IV).
- **Break condition:** If the sampled decision situations fail to capture critical behavioural nuances (e.g., rare resource conflict patterns), or if two heuristics produce identical rankings in sampled situations but diverge in unsampled ones, the PC vector becomes an inadequate proxy for fitness.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A nearest-neighbour surrogate model, using PC vectors, can effectively pre-select promising offspring from a large pool of intermediate candidates, improving evolutionary efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** The surrogate stores the PC vectors and true fitnesses of the current population. For a new offspring, its PC vector is compared (Manhattan distance) to all stored vectors; the fitness of the nearest neighbour is assigned as the estimate. The top-|*P*| offspring by estimated fitness are selected for full evaluation.
- **Core assumption:** The behavioural proximity in PC space (low distance) is a reliable indicator of fitness proximity in the true simulation environment.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section III-C] Describes the nearest-neighbour estimation procedure and the use of only current-generation data due to stochastic simulation.
  - [section IV-D] Shows precision drops from ~80-90% (*k*=1.5) to ~50-60% (*k*=4), indicating the assumption weakens as candidate pool size (*k*) grows beyond the representational capacity of the population-sized surrogate database.
  - [corpus] Reference [4] introduced PC-based surrogates for single-choice scheduling; this paper adapts the concept to subset/ranking decisions, but generalisation across problem domains is not evidenced.
- **Break condition:** When the offspring multiplier *k* is large, the surrogate database (size = |*P*|) becomes too small to accurately cover the expanded PC space of *k*×|*P*| candidates, leading to high false-positive selection rates (Fig. 7).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Generating a multiple (*k*) of the population size in intermediate offspring, then using the surrogate to select the best, introduces beneficial genetic material and accelerates convergence.
- **Mechanism:** Standard GP generates |*P*| offspring. SKGGP generates *k*×|*P*| intermediate offspring via the same variation operators. The surrogate filters this larger pool, selecting the top |*P*| for full evaluation. This increases the chance that high-quality, novel individuals survive to the next generation.
- **Core assumption:** The additional intermediate offspring contain a higher concentration of high-fitness individuals (relative to standard generation) that the surrogate can identify with sufficient precision.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...generates k× population size offspring, uses the surrogate to select the top |P| for full evaluation."
  - [section IV-D] For *k*=2, ~350 correctly added individuals per generation come from the extra offspring pool, demonstrating net quality improvement. For *k*=4, correctly added individuals increase slightly but incorrectly added rise sharply (~300), netting similar overall performance to *k*=2.
  - [section IV-C] Shows 20-40% budget savings versus KGGP for *k*=2 and 4.
- **Break condition:** If the increase in incorrectly selected individuals from the extra offspring pool outweighs the gain from correctly selected ones, the net effect on population quality diminishes or becomes negative. This is suggested by the performance plateau between *k*=2 and *k*=4.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Genetic Programming (Tree-based Representation)
  - *Why needed here:* The system evolves heuristic rules (programs) for dynamic scheduling decisions. Understanding how trees map to functions (ordering/selection rules) and how variation (crossover/mutation) creates new heuristics is fundamental.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the multi-tree GP representation separate the activity ordering rule from the activity group selection rule, and how are they combined during simulation?

- **Concept:** Phenotypic Characterisation (PC) in Surrogate Modelling
  - *Why needed here:* PC is the bridge between a GP individual's genotype (tree structure) and its behavioural phenotype (decision rankings). A valid PC scheme must be computationally cheap, discriminative, and correlated with fitness. This paper's core innovation is a rank-based PC for a dual-decision context.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a rank-based PC (recording full orderings) proposed over the earlier index-based scheme (recording only the selected candidate's rank), and what specific decision complexity does it address?

- **Concept:** Nearest-Neighbour Surrogate & Distance Metrics
  - *Why needed here:* The surrogate's accuracy hinges on the distance metric (Manhattan) in PC space measuring functional similarity. Understanding the curse of dimensionality and the "no free lunch" nature of distance metrics is key to diagnosing precision drops.
  - *Quick check question:* Given the Manhattan distance on concatenated rank vectors, what type of behavioural difference between two heuristics would yield a small distance, and what type would yield a large distance?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Initialise Population → Full Evaluator (Simulation) → PC Vector Generator → Surrogate Database → Offspring Generator → PC Vector Generator (for offspring) → Surrogate Estimator (Nearest-Neighbour Search) → Selector (Top-|P| by estimate) → Full Evaluator (Re-evaluate selected) → Population Update → Repeat

- **Critical path:** After each full population evaluation, PC vectors are built and stored. Each generation, intermediate offspring are generated, their PC vectors computed, nearest-neighbour fitness estimated, and the top subset selected for full re-evaluation. The PC computation for thousands of offspring lies on the critical path.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Offspring multiplier (*k*):** Higher *k* explores more candidates but requires a larger surrogate database (fixed at |*P*|) for accurate estimation, leading to precision loss. *k*=2 offers a practical balance.
  - **Number of decision situations:** More situations yield a longer, more descriptive PC vector but increase computational overhead for PC generation. The paper uses 10 per type.
  - **Surrogate simplicity:** Nearest-neighbour is simple and training-free but scales linearly with database size and uses a fixed distance metric. More complex surrogates could improve accuracy but add training overhead and risk overfitting with small |*P*|.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Plummeting surrogate precision:** Indicates the PC space is too sparse relative to candidate offspring, or the distance metric is failing. Check: Are decision situations diverse enough? Is *k* too high?
  - **No convergence speedup:** If the PC-based duplicate removal alone doesn't help, the primary value is in the *k*>1 offspring generation, not just diversity management.
  - **High surrogate overhead:** PC computation becoming a bottleneck. Profile: Is decision situation sampling or heuristic evaluation dominating? Might need to reduce the number of situations or optimise PC caching.
  - **Degraded final solution quality:** If incorrectly added individuals from extra offspring contaminate the population over generations. Monitor the ratio of correctly:incorrectly added individuals; if incorrectly added consistently exceed ~30% of the extra pool, *k* is likely too large.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline replication & PC vector sanity check:** Run KGGP to confirm baseline results. Then, for a few known high/low fitness heuristics from the final population, manually inspect their PC vectors. Do the rank sequences in ordering/selection situations reflect expected strategies (e.g., shortest processing time first)? This validates the PC scheme's face validity.
  2.  **Ablation on offspring multiplier:** Fix other parameters, systematically vary *k* (e.g., 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4). Track: (a) convergence curve (avg best fitness vs. evaluations), (b) per-generation precision, (c) ratio of correctly/incorrectly added extra offspring. Goal: Identify the *k* where the marginal gain in correctly added individuals plateaus or the incorrectly added curve rises steeply.
  3.  **PC vector dimensionality sensitivity:** Vary the number of sampled decision situations (e.g., 5, 10, 20, 50). Measure impact on: (a) surrogate precision, (b) per-generation CPU time for PC generation, (c) final solution quality. Goal: Find the minimum number of situations that maintains precision while minimising overhead. Watch for overfitting: very high dimensions might capture noise from specific simulation runs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the surrogate model's accuracy be improved to maintain high precision when selecting from a large intermediate offspring set (e.g., with offspring multiplier *k*=4)? *Basis in paper:* The conclusion identifies improving surrogate accuracy as future work, and Section IV.D reports a precision drop to 50–60% when *k*=4. *Why unresolved:* The current nearest-neighbour surrogate, trained only on the current population, suffers from limited representativeness when the offspring pool grows. *What evidence would resolve it:* Comparative experiments evaluating different surrogate model variants (e.g., using larger or multi-generational training data, different distance metrics, or ensemble methods) on precision and resulting algorithm performance.
- **Open Question 2:** Would expanding the surrogate database beyond the current generation (e.g., by accumulating individuals from previous generations or multiple independent runs) improve fitness estimation and evolutionary outcomes? *Basis in paper:* Future work mentions expanding the surrogate database; the current approach restricts the database to the current population due to non-comparable fitness values across generations. *Why unresolved:* The paper acknowledges the database limitation but does not investigate whether incorporating historical data could maintain comparability and enhance surrogate accuracy. *What evidence would resolve it:* An ablation study comparing the standard SKGGP with variants that incorporate past generations' individuals (with appropriate fitness value adjustments) to measure changes in surrogate precision, saved budget, and final solution quality.
- **Open Question 3:** Can more expressive phenotypic characterisation (PC) schemes, beyond rank-based encoding (e.g., incorporating priority values or decision context features), better capture heuristic behaviour and improve surrogate model performance? *Basis in paper:* The conclusion proposes exploring more expressive PC schemes; the paper also notes that the rank-based scheme may lose semantic information compared to using raw priority values. *Why unresolved:* It is unknown whether richer feature representations would yield more accurate distance measurements and fitness estimates. *What evidence would resolve it:* Design alternative PC schemes (e.g., using raw priority vectors, adding project state attributes) and compare their effectiveness via surrogate precision and overall SKGGP performance.
- **Open Question 4:** Would alternative machine learning models (e.g., kernel-based regression, random forests, or neural networks) provide more accurate fitness estimates than the nearest-neighbour surrogate in this context? *Basis in paper:* Future work suggests investigating alternative machine learning models; the current implementation uses a simple nearest-neighbour approach. *Why unresolved:* The suitability of more complex models for the high-dimensional, possibly noisy PC space is unknown; they may overfit or fail to generalize without sufficient training data. *What evidence would resolve it:* Train various surrogate models on the same PC-fitness dataset and evaluate their prediction accuracy (e.g., mean squared error) and the resulting impact on the evolutionary process.

## Limitations
- The PC scheme's effectiveness depends critically on the unspecified protocol for sampling decision situations, which affects the descriptive power and generalisability of the behavioural proxy.
- The nearest-neighbour surrogate's precision degrades significantly when the offspring multiplier *k* increases beyond ~2, limiting the extent of beneficial exploration.
- The paper provides no comparative evidence that the proposed rank-based PC scheme generalises to other scheduling domains beyond the specific DMRCPSP context studied.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (PC vectors as behavioral proxies):** *Medium confidence.* Evidence is primarily internal; the core assumption of ranking similarity correlating with fitness similarity is plausible but not stress-tested on diverse, unseen decision contexts.
- **Mechanism 2 (Nearest-neighbor surrogate accuracy):** *High confidence.* Supported by explicit precision metrics showing predictable degradation with *k*, aligning with the stated break condition of database size limits.
- **Mechanism 3 (k-offspring benefit):** *Medium confidence.* Results show net gain for *k*=2 but plateau for *k*=4, with precision drop and high false positives. The benefit is real but fragile, dependent on *k* and surrogate precision.

## Next Checks
1.  **PC sampling ablation:** Replicate SKGGP with modified PC generation—use fixed vs. per-individual random decision situations, and vary the number of situations (5, 10, 20). Measure impact on surrogate precision (top-|*P*| selection accuracy) and final makespan. This tests the robustness of the PC scheme's descriptive power.
2.  **Surrogate precision vs. dimensionality profiling:** For a fixed *k*=2, systematically increase PC vector length (by increasing sampled situations) and monitor per-generation surrogate precision and CPU overhead. Identify the "sweet spot" where precision is stable but computational cost is minimal.
3.  **Isolate the *k* effect via controlled offspring pools:** Generate *k*×|*P*| offspring, but evaluate *all* of them fully (no surrogate). Compare the true quality distribution of the extra (*k*-1)×|*P*| individuals against those *selected* by the surrogate. This quantifies the exact loss/gain from surrogate filtering and isolates the value of generating more offspring from the value of accurate selection.
