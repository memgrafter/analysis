# From Data Statistics to Feature Geometry: How Correlations Shape Superposition

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.09972
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09972
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Correlated features can be superposed with constructive interference, leading to efficient reconstruction, lower weight norms, and emergent geometric structures (e.g., clusters, cycles); value-coding features produce ordered geometries without correlations.

## Executive Summary
This paper re-examines superposition in neural networks, arguing that interference from feature correlations is not merely harmful noise but can be harnessed constructively. The authors introduce Bag-of-Words Superposition (BOWS), a controlled autoencoder framework that encodes binary bag-of-words representations from internet text, capturing realistic co-activation patterns with known ground truth. They show that when data covariance is low-rank, the autoencoder weights align with principal components, allowing interference to reinforce the reconstruction of active features. This reduces reliance on non-linear filtering and yields weight savings, while ReLU non-linearities still suppress residual false positives. The work distinguishes presence-coding features (which benefit from correlations) from value-coding features (which encode continuous quantities linearly), explaining why circles appear in modular arithmetic tasks without correlations. Empirical validation across synthetic and real text data confirms linear decodability for many features, alignment with PCA, and the emergence of semantic clusters and cyclical arrangements under bottlenecks or weight decay.

## Method Summary
The authors propose Bag-of-Words Superposition (BOWS), an autoencoder-based framework. Inputs are binary bag-of-words vectors constructed from internet text: a corpus is tokenized, stop-words removed, and each sample aggregates multiple context windows to capture co-occurrence statistics. The autoencoder uses a linear encoder with weight matrix \( W \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times V} \) and a ReLU decoder with tied weights \( W^T \) and bias. Training employs mean squared error loss, often with weight decay and a bottleneck dimension \( m < V \). Analysis involves computing the Gram matrix \( W^T W \), performing PCA on weight columns, and training linear probes to measure per-feature \( R^2 \) without non-linearities. This setup allows controlled study of how data covariance shapes superposition geometry.

## Key Results
- Correlated (presence-coding) features can be arranged so that interference aligns with shared variance, enabling efficient reconstruction with lower weight norms, while ReLU filters residual harmful interference.
- Semantic clusters (e.g., proper names, verbs) and cyclical structures (e.g., months, Roman numerals) emerge in weight space, especially under tight bottlenecks or weight decay.
- Value-coding features (e.g., coordinates, sine/cosine) produce ordered geometric manifolds (e.g., circles) via linear superposition without relying on feature correlations.
- Linear probes achieve high \( R^2 \) for many features, and learned weights align with the principal subspace of the data covariance; ablation studies confirm complementary roles of constructive interference and ReLU-based filtering.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Constructive Interference via Covariance Alignment
- **Claim:** When features are correlated, interference in superposition can be self-reinforcing rather than purely harmful, allowing efficient reconstruction with lower weight norms.
- **Mechanism:** In low-rank data (e.g., text co-occurrences), the principal subspace captures shared variance. The autoencoder weights \( W \) become an approximate projector onto this subspace, so the interference term \( \sum_{j \neq i} \langle w_i, w_j \rangle f_j \) aligns with the signal \( \|w_i\|^2 f_i \) because \( \langle w_i, w_j \rangle \) encodes covariance between features \( i \) and \( j \). This reduces reliance on non-linear filtering.
- **Core assumption:** Data covariance is approximately low-rank; features exhibit meaningful co-activation patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "interference can be constructive rather than just noise to be filtered out... arranged according to their co-activation patterns." [section] 3.1: Derives \( I_i = (1-\|w_i\|^2) f_i - \varepsilon_i \) and shows \( \varepsilon_i \to 0 \) when \( \operatorname{rank}(\Sigma) \le m \). [corpus] Papers on spectral superposition (84506) and superposition as lossy compression (4840) explore geometric structures from covariance.
- **Break condition:** Features become orthogonal or uncorrelated; \( \operatorname{rank}(\Sigma) > m \) significantly.

### Mechanism 2: Coexistence with ReLU-Based Filtering
- **Claim:** Constructive interference does not eliminate the need for non-linear filtering; ReLU + negative bias suppress residual harmful interference (false positives) from variance outside the principal subspace.
- **Mechanism:** The autoencoder geometry exploits shared variance for reconstruction (linear superposition subspace), while the ReLU non-linearity zeros out pre-activations when inactive features are present due to residual interference \( \varepsilon_i \).
- **Core assumption:** Data is only approximately low-rank; \( \varepsilon_i \) is non-zero but manageable.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "arranging features according to their co-activation patterns, making interference between active features constructive, while still using ReLUs to avoid false positives." [section] 4: Shows 'Beatles' and 'Christmas' examples where contextual interference improves reconstruction for active samples but ReLU suppresses false positives in inactive contexts. [corpus] Paper on adversarial attacks (66350) shows interference can be leveraged destructively if not filtered.
- **Break condition:** Residual \( \varepsilon_i \) is too large; ReLU threshold/bias cannot compensate, leading to many false negatives/positives.

### Mechanism 3: Value-Coding Features Yield Geometry Without Correlation
- **Claim:** Ordered geometric manifolds (e.g., circles) can arise from linearly represented trigonometric/coordinate value-coding features, even when inputs are uncorrelated.
- **Mechanism:** For tasks like modular addition or coordinate prediction, the network learns linear decoders for sine/cosine or \( (x,y) \) components. Plotting embeddings along these value-coding directions reveals structures (circles, maps) that are *not* due to superposition of correlated features but to the functional form of the target values.
- **Core assumption:** Task requires linear representation of continuous values; features are task-relevant variables.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "distinguish between presence-coding features... and value-coding features... explaining structures like circles in modular arithmetic without correlations." [section] 5: Shows linear probes recover coordinates/sine-cosine with high \( R^2 \); ablation removes orthogonal subspace with minimal accuracy loss. [corpus] Work on representation of space and time (Gurnee & Tegmark, 2024) and modular addition geometry (Li et al., 2025).
- **Break condition:** Task does not require linear value readout; features are purely presence-coding.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Projection Matrices**
  - *Why needed here:* The mechanism relies on \( W \) approximating the PCA subspace; \( W^T W \) is the orthogonal projector onto the span of top-\( m \) eigenvectors of \( \Sigma \). Understanding PCA explains why circular data yields circular weight geometry.
  - *Quick check question:* Given a 2D dataset with cyclic covariance (e.g., points on a circle), what do the first two principal components look like, and why would a linear autoencoder’s weight columns align with them?
- **Concept: Superposition Definitions (Linear vs. Non-linear)**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper redefines superposition based on recoverability with linear vs. non-linear decoders. This distinction determines whether interference is purely filtered (non-linear) or constructively used (linear).
  - *Quick check question:* For a feature with \( R^2_i(W, \psi_{\text{lin}}) < 1-\varepsilon \) but \( R^2_i(W, \psi) \ge 1-\varepsilon \) with a non-linear \( \psi \), why is it in non-linear superposition? What property of the covariance makes linear recovery possible?
- **Concept: Weight Norm as Regularization Signal**
  - *Why needed here:* Weight decay biases toward solutions with smaller \( \|W\|_F^2 \). Linear superposition (rank-\( m \) projector) has \( \operatorname{tr}(W^T W) = m \), while interference filtering scales with number of features \( d \). This explains why weight decay favors constructive interference.
  - *Quick check question:* If you have \( d=1000 \) features in \( m=50 \) dimensions, why would a ReLU-AE with weight decay prefer a rank-50 projection over 500 antipodal pairs? Compare \( \|W\|_F^2 \) in each case.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  BOWS Data Generator -> Autoencoder -> Analysis Toolkit
- **Critical path:**  
  Data preprocessing (tokenization, stop-word removal, context aggregation) -> train AE with varying \( m \)/weight decay -> extract \( W \) -> compute Gram matrices, PCA, linear probes -> validate with examples (months, Beatles).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Tied vs. untied weights: Paper uses tied for theoretical alignment; untied may break clean PCA link.  
  - Bottleneck size \( m \): Controls capacity; small \( m \) forces low-rank structure, large \( m \) allows near-orthogonal features.  
  - Weight decay: Encourages norm minimization → favors constructive interference; must tune vs. sparsity.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. No semantic clusters or cyclical structures at any \( m \) → covariance too weak or AE failing to learn low-rank subspace.  
  2. Months/roman numerals become orthogonal prematurely for small \( m \) → interference filtering dominant, constructive underutilized.  
  3. Linear probe \( \text{FEV} \approx 0 \) → \( W \) representations not linearly decodable; ReLU is essential for reconstruction.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Synthetic cyclic covariance test:** Train on the "months" synthetic dataset (Section A) with \( m=2,3,\dots,12 \). Verify \( W^T W \) matches PCA circle for \( m<6 \) and antipodal for \( m\ge6 \).  
  2. **WikiText-BOWS structure validation:** Train ReLU-AE on WikiText-BOWS (\( V=10000 \), \( c=20 \)). Extract weights for months/verbs/proper names; compute PCA/UMAP to confirm clusters/cycles. Compare with/without weight decay.  
  3. **Linear superposition test:** For a group (e.g., months), train a *linear* decoder \( \psi_{\text{lin}} \) on frozen \( W \). Compute \( R^2_i(W, \psi_{\text{lin}}) \); if >0.95 for all, they are in linear superposition. Repeat for random rare words to find differences.

## Limitations
- Empirical scope limited to BOWS autoencoders; unclear if findings generalize to deeper networks or non-tied weight settings.
- Synthetic cyclic data may oversimplify real-world language correlations; natural corpora likely exhibit heterogeneous low-rank structures that challenge theoretical assumptions.
- Reproducibility details scarce (e.g., exact hyperparameters, training stability); no independent replications reported.

## Confidence
- **Constructive Interference (Mechanism 1):** High
- **ReLU Coexistence (Mechanism 2):** Medium
- **Value-Coding Geometry (Mechanism 3):** Medium
