# Comparative Analysis of Deep Learning Architectures for Multi-Disease Classification of Single-Label Chest X-rays

## Executive Summary
This study addresses chest X-ray diagnostic challenges from radiologist shortages by systematically comparing seven deep learning architectures on a balanced dataset of 18,080 images across five diseases. Using patient-level splitting to prevent data leakage, all models were trained under identical conditions and achieved over 90% test accuracy. ConvNeXt-Tiny performed best overall (92.31% accuracy, 95.70% AUROC), and MobileNetV2 offered superior efficiency (3.5M parameters, 48-minute training). Tuberculosis and COVID-19 classification was near-perfect (AUROC ≥99.97%), but Normal, Cardiomegaly, and Pneumonia proved more difficult, likely due to overlapping radiographic features. Results demonstrate strong potential but highlight areas for improvement in challenging classes.

## Method Summary
The study used a balanced dataset of 18,070 chest X-ray images, each labeled for a single disease among Normal, Cardiomegaly, Pneumonia, Tuberculosis, and COVID-19. Patient-level splitting was implemented to prevent data leakage. Seven deep learning architectures (including ConvNeXt-Tiny and MobileNetV2) were trained from scratch or with fine-tuning under identical hyperparameters, augmentation, and optimization settings. Performance was evaluated on a held-out test set using accuracy and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC).

## Key Results
- All seven models achieved >90% test accuracy; ConvNeXt-Tiny led with 92.31% accuracy and 95.70% AUROC.
- MobileNetV2 was most parameter-efficient (3.5M parameters) with 90.42% accuracy and 94.10% AUROC, requiring only 48 minutes of training.
- Tuberculosis and COVID-19 classification reached near-perfect AUROC (≥99.97%), while Normal, Cardiomegaly, and Pneumonia showed lower performance, attributed to overlapping radiographic features.

- **Patient-Level Data Splitting**: Ensuring all images from a single patient reside exclusively in either train, validation, or test sets. *Why needed*: Prevents data leakage and overfitting to patient-specific anatomy, ensuring generalization to unseen patients. *Quick check*: Confirm that splitting is based on patient IDs, not individual image filenames or random assignment.
- **Evaluation Metrics (Accuracy, AUROC)**: Accuracy measures exact class prediction rate; AUROC assesses ranking ability across classification thresholds. *Why needed*: Provides complementary views—accuracy for overall correctness, AUROC for separability across decision thresholds. *Quick check*: Derive both from a confusion matrix and discuss their interpretational differences.
- **Model Efficiency Trade-offs**: Balancing parameters, inference speed, and accuracy. *Why needed*: Critical for deployment in resource-limited clinical settings (e.g., edge devices). *Quick check*: Compare parameter counts and training times across models to identify efficiency frontiers.

Near-perfect TB/COVID-19 AUROCs may reflect dataset artifacts (e.g., distinct imaging protocols or patient populations).
