# Ex-Omni: Enabling 3D Facial Animation Generation for Omni-modal Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.07106  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07106  
- **Reference Count**: 36  
- **Primary Result**: Ex-Omni achieves state-of-the-art Lip Vertex Error (3.866) on A2F-Bench for speech-driven 3D facial animation within a unified omni-modal LLM framework, with human A/B preference of 55–80% for audio-visual consistency.

## Executive Summary
Ex-Omni extends open-source omni-modal large language models (OLLMs) to natively generate speech-accompanied 3D facial animation. It addresses the mismatch between discrete, token-level LLM reasoning and dense, fine-grained temporal motion by decoupling high-level semantic reasoning from low-level temporal synthesis. The framework uses discrete speech units as temporal scaffolding and a token-as-query gated fusion (TQGF) mechanism for controlled semantic injection. Trained on the constructed InstructEx dataset, Ex-Omni outperforms cascaded baselines on facial animation benchmarks while maintaining competitive speech and language capabilities.

## Method Summary
Ex-Omni employs a four-stage training pipeline on a unified architecture. A frozen speech encoder (Whisper) and a trainable speech projector align audio with the LLM (Qwen3-8B). The LLM performs semantic reasoning and either generates text or, via a trainable speech unit generator (Qwen3-0.6B), predicts discrete speech units autoregressively. These units are resampled to frame-level queries for a facial decoder (Transformer) that predicts ARKit-52 blendshape coefficients. Two TQGF modules selectively fuse LLM semantics into the speech generator and facial decoder, enabling controlled conditioning. All components are jointly fine-tuned on mixed instruction data (InstructEx) comprising ASR, TTS, S2S QA, T2T, and synthetic S2F pairs.

## Key Results
- Lowers Lip Vertex Error (LVE) to **3.866** on A2F-Bench, significantly below cascaded OLLM+task-specific S2F models (~6.5–7.6).
- Achieves **55–80%** human A/B preference for audio-visual consistency against baselines.
- Remains competitive on speech benchmarks: VoiceBench (S2T) and Seed-TTS-Eval (TTS) among open-source models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decoupling Semantic Reasoning from Temporal Generation
Ex-Omni simplifies learning temporally coherent 3D facial animation by separating the LLM’s high-level instruction understanding from the fine-grained temporal synthesis handled by dedicated modules. The LLM produces semantically coherent outputs (text or speech unit sequences), while the speech unit generator and facial decoder manage dense temporal generation. This reduces optimization difficulty under limited data, as the LLM is not forced to directly model frame-level dynamics.  
*Break condition*: If speech units are low-quality or misaligned, or if LLM semantics are incorrect, downstream generation degrades.

### Mechanism 2: Discrete Speech Units as Temporal Scaffolding
Discrete speech units (from GLM4-Voice) provide a structured, quantized temporal representation at a fixed rate (~50–100 Hz). These units bridge the gap between token-level LLM outputs and per-frame facial motion: the speech generator predicts unit sequences, which are resampled to create frame-level queries for the facial decoder. This scaffolding imposes temporal coherence and aligns speech semantics with facial dynamics.  
*Break condition*: If units fail to encode necessary prosodic/articulation cues or resampling introduces misalignment, facial animation quality drops.

### Mechanism 3: Token-as-Query Gated Fusion (TQGF)
TQGF enables selective injection of LLM semantic context into temporal generators. In each cross-attention layer, the token sequence (speech units or frame queries) serves as query, while LLM hidden states act as key/value. A sigmoid gate modulates the attention output, allowing adaptive control over semantic conditioning at each timestep. This prevents interference and stabilizes training compared to naive concatenation.  
*Break condition*: If gating values degenerate (near 0 or 1), semantic injection fails; increased computational overhead may strain memory for long sequences.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept 1: Omni-modal Large Language Models (OLLMs)
**Why needed**: Ex-Omni extends an OLLM (Qwen3-8B) to handle multiple modalities (text, speech, 3D face) within one framework. Understanding OLLM tokenization, output spaces, and training objectives is key.  
**Quick check**: How do OLLMs differ from traditional LLMs in input tokenization, output space, and training objectives?

### Concept 2: Discrete Speech Units
**Why needed**: Ex-Omni uses discrete speech units (e.g., from GLM4-Voice) as an intermediate representation for speech and facial generation. Their quantized nature and fixed temporal resolution differ from continuous features like mel-spectrograms.  
**Quick check**: How do discrete speech units (50–100 Hz token rate) affect prosody modeling and synchronization with facial motion versus continuous representations?

### Concept 3: ARKit Blendshape Coefficients
**Why needed**: Facial animation is represented as ARKit-52 blendshape coefficients—a low-dimensional, identity-agnostic parameterization. This choice influences loss design (LVE), evaluation, and renderability.  
**Quick check**: What are the advantages and limitations of blendshape coefficients versus 3D mesh vertices for speech-driven facial animation?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Speech Encoder (Whisper-Large-V3, frozen) -> Speech Projector (MLP) -> LLM (Qwen3-8B) -> Speech Unit Generator (Qwen3-0.6B, w/ TQGF) -> Facial Decoder (Transformer w/ TQGF) -> Speech Decoder (GLM4-Voice-Decoder, frozen)

### Critical Path
**Training**:  
- Stage I: Train speech projector on ASR (freeze others).  
- Stage II: Train speech generator on TTS units (LLM frozen).  
- Stage III: Add facial decoder with L_face (L2 + velocity loss) on TTS+blendshape data.  
- Stage IV: Joint fine-tuning on all mixed data (unfreeze all).  

**Inference**: Input (text/speech) -> LLM generates response tokens. If speech units are generated, decode to audio via speech decoder; simultaneously, facial decoder produces ARKit coefficients from units and LLM semantics.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Decoupling vs. End-to-End**: Decoupling eases optimization but may lose synergistic effects between speech and face; Ex-Omni uses separate generators.
- **Discrete Units vs. Continuous**: Units provide temporal scaffolding for LLMs but may lose fine acoustic details.
- **TQGF vs. Simple Fusion**: TQGF allows selective conditioning but adds parameters and slight latency; ablations show it stabilizes cross-lingual performance.

### Failure Signatures
- **High Lip Vertex Error (LVE)**: Poor lip-speech sync; check facial decoder training, TQGF gating, resampling alignment.
- **High WER/CER in Speech**: Speech generator failures; verify unit vocabulary coverage and TQGF conditioning.
- **Training Instability**: Loss spikes in Stage I/IV, especially with larger LLMs; monitor gradient norms.
- **Mode Collapse in Facial Animation**: Conservative mouth movements; increase λ_vel or verify TQGF usage.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Stage I Validation**: Train speech projector on a small ASR subset (e.g., 1000 samples); evaluate WER on held-out ASR to confirm speech-LLM alignment.
2. **Stage II Validation**: Train speech generator on TTS data (e.g., 10K samples); generate units and use ASR to check intelligibility; compare unit sequence length to text tokens.
3. **S2F Sanity Check**: After Stage III, feed ground-truth speech units to facial decoder; compute LVE on a few samples to verify smooth, lip-synced motion before end-to-end testing.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Emotional expressiveness**: How to generate emotionally expressive 3D facial animation capturing higher-level expressions beyond lip sync? Current framework focuses on mouth articulation without emotional modeling.
2. **Real-time latency**: What architectural/algorithmic improvements can reduce generation latency (current RTF ~2.158) for interactive applications?
3. **Speech controllability**: How to support variable speaker identities and vocal timbres in speech generation while maintaining synchronized facial animation? Currently fixed to one speaker.
4. **Synthetic data dependency**: Does reliance on synthetic facial motion (from Audio2Face-3D) limit naturalness and diversity? How to reduce the domain gap to real human motions?

## Limitations
- **InstructEx dataset** is not public; reconstructed versions may differ in language balance or sampling, harming reproducibility.
- **GLM4-Voice speech unit tokenizer** details (vocabulary, temporal resolution) are unavailable, risking misalignment with substitutes.
- **Fixed speaker identity** in TTS synthesis limits speech diversity and may bias facial animation style.
- **Evaluation constraints**: Benchmarks (A2F-Bench, VoiceBench) are single-dataset; cross-dataset generalization is untested.

## Confidence
- **Decoupling semantic reasoning from temporal generation** (Mechanism 1): Medium. Supported by comparison to cascaded baselines and theoretical motivation, but no direct ablation of the decoupling structure.
- **Discrete speech units as temporal scaffolding** (Mechanism 2): Medium. Relies on external tokenizer quality; alternative representations not tested.
- **Token‑as‑Query Gated Fusion (TQGF)** (Mechanism 3): High. Ablation shows significant LVE increase (3.667→3.682) and training slowdown when removed.
- **Reported benchmark results** (LVE 3.866, human preference 55–80%): High for exact numbers under the described setup, but Medium for broader generalizability due to dataset and code availability constraints.

## Next Checks

2. **Ablation on speech representation**: Compare discrete units against continuous features (e.g., HuBERT embeddings) to quantify impact on temporal alignment and LVE.
