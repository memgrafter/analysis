# RelaxFlow: Text-Driven Amodal 3D Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.05425
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05425
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Training-free dual-branch framework RelaxFlow achieves text-driven amodal 3D generation, preserving visible evidence while steering occluded regions, with significant improvements on proposed benchmarks (e.g., CLIP-txt 24.08→27.26, Point-FID 100.38→81.11 on ExtremeOcc-3D).

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces the task of text-driven amodal 3D generation, where the goal is to complete the 3D shape of an object from an occluded image while strictly preserving the visible parts and following a textual description for the unseen part. The proposed method, RelaxFlow, is a training-free dual-branch ODE-based framework. It decouples control by using two parallel branches: an Observation Branch that rigidly preserves visible evidence and a Semantic-Prior Branch that provides loose structural guidance from text-derived priors. A Multi-Prior Consensus Module builds a robust conditioning from multiple prior images, and a low-pass Relaxation Mechanism smooths cross-attention logits to suppress high-frequency noise. A visibility-aware fusion steers only occluded regions. The authors also propose two benchmarks: ExtremeOcc-3D (extreme occlusion) and AmbiSem-3D (semantic branching). On SAM3D backbone, RelaxFlow improves CLIP-txt and Point-FID on ExtremeOcc-3D, and a user study on AmbiSem-3D shows strong preference, demonstrating effective disambiguation without fidelity loss.

## Method Summary
RelaxFlow operates as a training-free extension to an existing rectified-flow 3D generative backbone (e.g., SAM3D or TRELLIS). The system takes a single masked image and a text prompt. From the prompt, it generates or retrieves multiple prior images, encodes them into tokens, and concatenates them to form a consensus conditioning. During ODE sampling, two velocity fields are computed in parallel: one conditioned strictly on the observed image tokens (Observation Branch) and one conditioned on the multi-prior tokens with a novel cross-attention logit relaxation (Semantic-Prior Branch). The relaxation applies a separable Gaussian blur to the attention logits, theoretically equivalent to low-pass filtering the generative vector field. A visibility mask derived from projected voxel depths determines which voxels are occluded. The two velocities are blended using a time-dependent schedule that fully respects the observation in early steps and gradually incorporates semantic guidance for occluded regions only. After the ODE, the latent is decoded to a 3D mesh or Gaussian splats. The entire process requires no retraining and can be applied to any rectified-flow backbone that supports cross-attention conditioning.

## Key Results
- On ExtremeOcc-3D, RelaxFlow (with SAM3D) improves CLIP-txt similarity from 24.08 to 27.26 and reduces Point-FID from 100.38 to 81.11.
- On AmbiSem-3D, a user study reports 68.52% overall preference for RelaxFlow over baselines.
- The method preserves visible evidence (high LPIPS/CLIP-img) while successfully steering occluded regions according to text.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decoupled Control Granularities
- **Claim:** Decoupling control granularities (rigid for observation, relaxed for semantic prior) resolves the tension between preserving visible evidence and following textual intent.
- **Mechanism:** The framework runs two parallel ODE branches: an *Observation Branch* conditioned strictly on the input image tokens to preserve high‑frequency details, and a *Semantic‑Prior Branch* conditioned on text‑derived visual tokens with low‑pass relaxation to provide loose structural guidance. The two velocity fields are fused via a time‑ and visibility‑dependent schedule.
- **Core assumption:** The observation condition encodes pixel‑level details that must be preserved without alteration, while the prompt condition encodes only coarse semantic structure that can tolerate high‑frequency suppression.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states “decouples control granularities: rigid control for the observation versus relaxed structural control for the prompt.” Section 3.1 defines the dual‑branch ODE interpolation: \(d\mathbf{x}_t/dt = (1\!-\!\alpha_t)\,\mathbf{v}_\text{obs} + \alpha_t\,\mathbf{v}_\text{prior}\).
- **Break condition:** If the fusion schedule or the separation between branches breaks down (e.g., \(\alpha_t\) not decreasing), the semantic branch may overwrite visible evidence, causing fidelity loss; conversely, an observation branch that is not truly rigid leads to drift from the input.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-Prior Consensus
- **Claim:** Conditioning on multiple prior images derived from the text prompt builds a consensus that reduces the gap between free‑form language and the visual token space, yielding a conditioning that better reflects user intent.
- **Mechanism:** For a given prompt, \(N\) prior images are generated (or retrieved), each encoded into token sequences. These sequences are concatenated into a long sequence and fed to cross‑attention. Consistent semantic features across priors receive amplified attention, while inconsistent instance‑specific details are averaged out.
- **Core assumption:** The intersection of features across diverse priors corresponds to the core semantic concept intended by the prompt, and the attention mechanism performs a soft majority vote.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “Multi‑Prior Consensus Module for semantic guidance.” Section 4.2: “concatenate the per‑prior token sequences … induces a simple consensus effect: attributes that are consistent across priors appear repeatedly … while idiosyncratic or conflicting details tend to be diluted.”
- **Break condition:** If priors are too heterogeneous, consensus may wash out discriminative features; with only one prior (\(N=1\)), the method loses the disambiguation benefit and becomes a single noisy proxy.

### Mechanism 3: Low-Pass Relaxation of Attention Logits
- **Claim:** Smoothing the cross‑attention logits in the Semantic‑Prior Branch is theoretically equivalent to applying a low‑pass filter to the generative vector field, which suppresses high‑frequency instance‑specific noise while preserving low‑frequency semantic structure, thereby stabilizing generation.
- **Mechanism:** Before softmax in cross‑attention, a separable Gaussian blur (\(G_\sigma\)) is applied to the logit matrix \(L\). Implementation uses two 1D convolutions along query and key dimensions. This relaxation reduces the velocity estimate’s sensitivity to fine‑grained prior details.
- **Core assumption:** The ideal semantic transport field is band‑limited (low‑frequency), while errors that cause instability are dominated by high‑frequency components. Gaussian blur selectively attenuates the error.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “low‑pass Relaxation Mechanism applied to cross‑attention logits, theoretically equivalent to filtering high‑frequency details from the generative vector field.” Section 3.2 provides a theoretical proof and Theorem A.9 links the relaxed error to a tighter Wasserstein bound.
- **Break condition:** Excessive smoothing (large \(\sigma\)) may remove essential semantic details (e.g., shape boundaries), causing under‑generation; insufficient smoothing fails to suppress high‑frequency conflicts, leading to observation‑drifting artifacts.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input Encoder → Prior Generator → Multi-Prior Consensus → [Observation Branch, Semantic‑Prior Branch] → Visibility Mask → Fusion Controller → ODE Solver → Decoder.  
  Descriptions:  
  *Input Encoder*: Encodes the occluded image and mask into visual tokens \(c_\text{obs}\).  
  *Prior Generator*: Text-to-image model or retrieval pool produces \(N\) prior images; each is encoded to tokens \(c_\text{prior}^n\).  
  *Multi‑Prior Consensus*: Concatenates \(\{c_\text{prior}^n\}\) into a single sequence.  
  *Observation Branch*: Takes \(c_\text{obs}\), computes velocity \(\mathbf{v}_\text{obs}\) via the backbone.  
  *Semantic‑Prior Branch*: Takes consensus tokens, applies Gaussian blur to cross‑attention logits (\(\sigma\)), outputs relaxed velocity \(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_\text{prior}\).  
  *Visibility Mask*: Projects voxels to image plane using backbone pose, builds soft visibility weights \(m_i\) from depth comparison.  
  *Fusion Controller*: Schedules \(\alpha_k\) (linear cutoff) and blends velocities: \(\mathbf{v}_i = \mathbf{v}_{\text{obs},i} + (1\!-\!m_i)\alpha_k (\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{prior},i} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{obs},i})\).  
  *ODE Solver*: Euler steps update latent \(x_{k+1} = x_k + \Delta t\,\mathbf{v}_k\).  
  *Decoder*: SS stage → occupancy volume; SLAT stage → textured mesh/Gaussian splats.

- **Critical path:**  
  Input image → \(c_\text{obs}\); text → priors → \(c_\text{prior}\); initialize \(x_0\); for each solver step: compute \(\mathbf{v}_\text{obs}(x_k, t_k, c_\text{obs})\); compute \(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_\text{prior}(x_k, t_k, c_\text{prior})\) with smoothed attention; compute \(m\) from pose & depth; blend; update \(x_k\); after SS decode to mesh; SLAT applies same dual‑branch logic (with spatial mask); final 3D asset.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Training‑free*: No backbone retraining, but relies on pretrained visual‑language alignment; may limit adaptation to novel domains.  
  - *Dual‑branch parallelization*: Adds compute roughly double the backbone cost, but can be fused; overhead is acceptable on single GPU.  
  - *Relaxation strength \(\sigma\)*: Larger \(\sigma\) suppresses more high‑frequency noise but risks oversmoothing semantic structure.  
  - *Number of priors \(N\)*: Larger \(N\) improves consensus at cost of memory and potential conflict; empirical sweet spot \(N=3\).  
  - *Visibility mask parameters* (\(\beta, \gamma, \lambda\)): Control the spatial separation; must scale with object size and depth to avoid bleeding of prior into visible regions.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. *Observation drift*: Output view deviates from input, often due to \(\alpha_t\) plateauing or mask mis‑registration.  
  2. *Semantic mismatch*: Shape does not respect prompt, caused by low‑quality priors or insufficient consensus.  
  3. *Visible/occluded misalignment*: Hard edges at visibility boundaries, indicating incorrect depth or pose estimation.  
  4. *Over‑smoothing*: Shape appears bloated or detail‑free, typical of excessive \(\sigma\) or aggressive \(\alpha_t\) decay.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. *Single‑case sanity check*: Run one extreme occlusion example (SAM3D backbone, “bed” prompt). Visually confirm that the visible backboard is preserved and the unseen region forms a bed‑like shape.  
  2. *Ablation of relaxation*: Set \(\sigma=0\) (no smoothing). Measure Point‑FID and CLIP‑txt; expect degradation, verifying the role of low‑pass filtering.  
  3. *Prior count sensitivity*: Vary \(N \in \{1,3,5\}\) on ExtremeOcc‑3D. Record CLIP‑txt and Point‑FID; expect improvement up to \(N=3\) then plateau or slight drop due to conflicting priors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can we detect and resolve conflicts between the observation and prior when they are mutually incompatible?  
*Basis:* Appendix G: "mutually conflicting evidence ... the model has no consistent solution and may fail."  
*Why unresolved:* Current soft blending interpolates without satisfying either condition. No conflict detection or resolution.  
*What evidence:* Conflict detection metric or adaptive fusion, validated on synthetic conflict sets.

**Open Question 2:** Can the dual-branch design be unified into a single-branch framework without losing the decoupling of control granularities?  
*Basis:* "To resolve this tension, our core insight ... we adopt a dual-branch strategy."  
*Why unresolved:* Dual-branch doubles computation; a single-branch would be more efficient and integrable.  
*What evidence:* A single-branch model with internal separation (e.g., attention heads) matching dual-branch performance.

**Open Question 3:** How can we automatically adapt the relaxation strength (σ) and fusion schedule (ρ) to the specific input?  
*Basis:* Fixed σ=1.0, ρ=0.2 for all experiments.  
*Why unresolved:* Inputs vary (occlusion level, ambiguity); fixed settings may be suboptimal.  
*What evidence:* Learn mapping from input features (e.g., occlusion ratio) to σ/ρ, or RL-based adjustment during sampling.

**Open Question 4:** How does RelaxFlow generalize to real-world images with noise, clutter, and imperfect pose estimation?  
*Basis:* Benchmarks use synthetic rendered images; assumes clean masks and accurate poses.  
*Why unresolved:* Real images violate assumptions; observation branch and visibility mask may fail.  
*What evidence:* Evaluation on a real-world dataset with photogrammetry ground truth and user studies on in-the-wild images.

## Limitations
- Reproduction depends on exact backbone implementation (SAM3D/TRELLIS) and preprocessing details, which are not fully disclosed; pose/depth estimation for the visibility mask is a hidden dependency.
- Benchmark novelty: ExtremeOcc-3D and AmbiSem-3D lack external validation; results may not generalize beyond controlled synthetic settings.
- Prior quality dependence: Multi-prior consensus assumes retrieved/generated priors are semantically aligned but stylistically neutral, which may not hold for diverse prompts.

## Confidence
- Decoupled Control Mechanism: Medium – Theoretical ODE interpolation is sound, but external validation of the “rigid vs. relaxed” assumption is absent.
- Multi-Prior Consensus: Medium – Internal ablations support it, but sensitivity to prior heterogeneity is not fully explored.
- Low-Pass Relaxation: Medium – Theorem provides a bound, but empirical optimality of σ=1.0 may be dataset-specific.
- Overall empirical gains: Medium-Low – Improvements on novel benchmarks are promising but unreplicated; user study size/methodology unclear.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate relaxation (σ) systematically**: Run ExtremeOcc-3D with σ ∈ {0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0}. Expect CLIP-txt/Point-FID peak near σ=1.0; σ=0 should degrade metrics, verifying the low-pass hypothesis.
2. **Test prior count and diversity**: Vary N ∈ {1,3,5} using retrieved priors (same-category renders) vs. generated priors (Z-Image). Track CLIP-txt and failure cases where consensus washes out discriminative features.
3. **Probe fusion schedule (ρ cutoff)**: Sweep ρ ∈ {0.05, 0.2, 0.5} on AmbiSem-3D. Monitor LPIPS (observation fidelity) and CLIP-txt (semantic alignment). An optimal ρ should balance both; extreme values should cause either observation drift (ρ high) or semantic neglect (ρ low).
