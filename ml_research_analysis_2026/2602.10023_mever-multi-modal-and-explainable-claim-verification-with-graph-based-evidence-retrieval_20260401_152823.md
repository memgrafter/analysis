# MEVER: Multi-Modal and Explainable Claim Verification with Graph-based Evidence Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10023
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10023
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MEVER achieves state-of-the-art on four multi-modal claim verification benchmarks, with retrieval MAP 71.4%, verification Macro F1 71.6%, and explanation ROUGE-L 34.5% on the new AIChartClaim dataset.

## Executive Summary
MEVER introduces a unified framework for multi-modal claim verification that jointly handles evidence retrieval, claim verification, and natural language explanation generation. It addresses a critical gap in prior work, which either focused on text-only verification or produced black-box decisions without faithful explanations. The core innovations include a two-layer multi-modal graph for cross-modal evidence retrieval, a dual-granularity fusion mechanism for interactive claim-evidence verification, and a multi-modal Fusion-in-Decoder with consistency regularization to generate explanations aligned with verification outcomes. The authors also release AIChartClaim, a scientifically grounded dataset of 1,200 claims, 300 chart images, captions, and human-written explanations. Extensive experiments across four benchmarks consistently show MEVER outperforming existing methods, with ablations confirming the necessity of each architectural component.

## Method Summary
MEVER operates in two stages. First, a multi-modal retriever encodes claims and evidence (texts and associated images) using a two-layer graph: a textual layer and a visual layer with cross-layer and intra-layer edges. Graph Neural Networks aggregate variable-sized image sets into virtual tokens, which are combined with text embeddings and processed iteratively via Transformers/ViTs to produce unified evidence embeddings. This retriever is trained with contrastive loss. Second, a verification module fuses the claim and retrieved evidence at two granularities: token-level (Multi-Head Attention between unified claim and evidence matrices) and evidence-level (GNN aggregation over evidence pieces). The fused representations are classified via an MLP. An explanation generator uses a Fusion-in-Decoder architecture: for each claim-evidence pair, text tokens and image embeddings are combined, encoded, and mean-pooled into a memory for a T5 decoder. A consistency regularizer (KL divergence) ensures the explanation’s implied label matches the verification label. The full system is trained with a joint loss combining verification, explanation, and regularization losses.

## Key Results
- On the new AIChartClaim dataset, MEVER achieves retrieval MAP 71.4%, verification Macro F1 71.6%, and explanation ROUGE-L 34.5%.
- Across three existing datasets (ChartCheck, Mocheg, MR2) and AIChartClaim, MEVER consistently surpasses prior state-of-the-art methods in retrieval, verification, and explanation metrics.
- Ablations demonstrate that removing the two-layer graph, either fusion mechanism, or the consistency regularizer leads to significant performance drops, validating each component’s contribution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Multi-Modal Evidence Retrieval via Two-Layer Graph
MEVER constructs a two-layer multi-modal graph: a bottom textual layer (evidence texts) and a top visual layer (associated images). Cross-layer edges link each text to its images; intra-layer edges fully connect all images and self-loop texts. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) aggregate each set of images into a virtual token `[z_t]`, which is concatenated with the text embedding `[h_t]`. This combined representation is fed back into a Transformer/ViT for cross-modal attention. This nested process (image-to-text → Transformer → text-to-image → ViT) repeats for `L` steps, yielding unified evidence embeddings that integrate both modalities. The core assumption is that iterative cross-modal exchange within a graph structure yields embeddings more discriminative for retrieval than separate or simple concatenation approaches. Evidence: [abstract], Section 3.2 (Eqs. 1–6, Fig. 2). A break condition: if no images exist, the graph collapses to a single textual layer, degrading performance to a text-only retriever (as shown by the `MEVER w/o images` ablation).

### Mechanism 2: Joint Claim-Evidence Verification via Token- and Evidence-Level Fusion
MEVER fuses claim and evidence at two granularities. **Token-level fusion** applies Multi-Head Attention between the claim’s unified matrix `U_c` (from text and image tokens) and each evidence’s unified matrix `U_{t_k}`, producing `U_{t_k,c}` for fine-grained token-wise interaction. Mean pooling across all retrieved evidence yields the final claim embedding. **Evidence-level fusion** uses GNNs: first, each evidence’s images are aggregated into `hat{z}_{t_k}` and combined with the text `[CLS]` to form evidence embedding `tau_k`; second, a GNN aggregates all `{tau_k}` using the claim as a query to produce a global evidence embedding `tau`. The concatenation `[c || tau]` is fed to an MLP for label prediction. The core assumption is that both granularities are necessary: token-level captures fine-grained alignments, while evidence-level hierarchically consolidates multi-evidence support. Evidence: [abstract], Section 3.3 (Eqs. 8–13, Fig. 2). Ablations (Fig. 3b) show that removing either fusion stage significantly hurts Macro F1.

### Mechanism 3: Faithful Explanation Generation via Multi-Modal Fusion-in-Decoder and Consistency Regularization
For explanation generation, MEVER employs a **multi-modal Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD)**: for each claim `c` and evidence `t_k`, raw text tokens `[c][SEP][t_k]` are embedded, and associated image embeddings (`z_c`, `z_{t_k}`) are projected and prepended, forming a multi-modal sequence `widehat{E}_{c,t_k}`. This is encoded by a Transformer; mean pooling across `K` evidence yields a fused memory `bar{E}_c` for the T5 decoder to generate `hat{e}`. A **consistency regularizer** takes mean-pooled decoder logits `L`, passes them through an MLP to predict an implicit label `hat{y}_e`, and computes `L_Reg = KL(hat{y}||hat{y}_e) + KL(hat{y}_e||hat{y}) - CE(hat{y}_e)`, where `hat{y}` is the verification output. This enforces label consistency between the verifier and the explanation. The core assumption is that multi-modal grounding (via FiD) and explicit label alignment yield explanations that are both content-rich and faithful. Evidence: [abstract], Section 3.4 (Eqs. 15–19, Fig. 2). Ablations (Fig. 3d) show that removing FiD or the regularizer lowers explanation quality (ROUGE-L, VLM scores).

## Foundational Learning
- **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs):** Used to aggregate variable-sized sets (e.g., images per evidence) into fixed-dimensional representations. *Why needed:* The two-layer graph contains nodes with varying degrees; GNNs with attention-based aggregators (Eqs. 2, 5, 12, 13) handle this heterogeneity. *Quick check:* How does Eq. 2 differ from standard Graph Attention Networks? It uses a sigmoid-scored attention between a central text `[CLS]` and each image `[CLS]`, rather than pairwise token attention.
- **Multi-Head Attention (MHA):** The core mechanism in Transformers for dynamic token interaction. *Why needed:* For token-level fusion (Eqs. 4, 8, 11) and the nested graph encoder, enabling cross-attention between claim and evidence tokens augmented with virtual tokens. *Quick check:* In Eq. 4, why are `W_Q`, `W_K`, `W_V` separate from the Transformer’s inherent parameters? They are additional projections to handle the augmented key/value matrices that include virtual tokens, while the query remains the original layer’s hidden state.
- **Contrastive Learning:** Trains the retriever with an InfoNCE loss to pull matching claim-evidence pairs together and push non-matching pairs apart. *Why needed:* To learn a multi-modal embedding space where semantically related claim and evidence are close, using `[CLS]` embeddings from the nested encoder (Eq. 7). *Quick check:* What constitutes a negative evidence `t'` in Eq. 7? Any evidence text from a different claim within the same mini-batch, assumed non-matching.
- **Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD):** Extends text generation to multiple document contexts by encoding each separately and mean-pooling the outputs. *Why needed:* To condition explanation generation on multiple retrieved evidence pieces, each with associated images, without exceeding the decoder’s maximum input length. *Quick check:* Why mean pool the encoded evidence matrices instead of concatenating? Concatenation would create a prohibitively long sequence for the decoder, especially with many evidence pieces; mean pooling compresses the information into a fixed-size context.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (claim `c`, images `I(c)`; evidence corpus: texts `T`, images `I(t)`) → Retriever Encoder (two-layer multi-modal graph) → Verification Module (token- & evidence-level fusion) → Explanation Module (multi-modal FiD + consistency regularizer) → Joint Training Loss.

**Critical path:**  
1. Train retrierver with contrastive loss until convergence (Algorithm 1).  
2. Freeze retriever, retrieve top-`K` evidence for all training claims.  
3. Jointly train verification and explanation modules using retrieved evidence (Algorithm 2).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Graph complexity vs. scalability:** The two-layer graph with fully connected image intra-edges works for small `|I(t)|` (≤5). For larger sets, sampling or sparsification is needed.  
- **Modality dependency:** Model assumes paired text-image evidence. Missing images force a text-only path, harming performance (see `MEVER w/o images` ablation).  
- **Length limits:** Underlying SciBERT/T5 have maximum sequence lengths (e.g., 512). Long evidence or explanations must be truncated, risking information loss.  
- **Consistency regularization weight `λ`:** Set to 0.5 empirically. Too high may force explanations to conform even when the verifier is wrong; too low reduces alignment.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Retrieval failure:** MAP/Prec@K significantly below text-only baselines (e.g., BM25) on datasets with noisy/uninformative images.  
- **Inconsistent explanation:** High verification loss but low ROUGE-L/METEOR, or VLM-as-a-Judge scores indicate label-explanation mismatch (detected via `λ=0` ablation).  
- **Chart-type sensitivity:** Sharp performance drop on scatterplots/pie/heat maps (Table 7) due to limited training examples and different visual reasoning patterns.  
- **GNN aggregation failure:** If evidence images are highly dissimilar, the attention-based GNN (Eq. 2) may produce a noisy virtual token, hurting downstream tasks.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Isolate the retriever:** Evaluate MEVER retriever against BM25, RA_V, JustiLM. Expect MEVER > MEVER w/o images > text-only.  
2. **Ablate fusion mechanisms:** For verification, remove token-level fusion (Eq. 11) or evidence-level fusion (Eq. 13). Expect significant Macro F1 drops (Fig. 3b).  
3. **Test consistency regularizer:** Train with `λ=0` vs. `λ=0.5`. Expect lower explanation quality (ROUGE-L, VLM scores) when `λ=0` (Fig. 3d).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Adapting to text-only evidence:** How can multi-modal claim verification models be used when only textual evidence is available, without associated images? The paper notes that obtaining relevant images for text-only data is non-trivial and leaves it as future work.
2. **Building large-scale multi-domain datasets:** How can large-scale, multi-domain scientific datasets be constructed for multi-modal claim verification to improve generalization? AIChartClaim is small (1,200 claims) and restricted to AI; creating larger, cross-domain benchmarks is needed.
3. **Integrating multi-modal knowledge graphs:** How can multi-modal knowledge graphs be effectively integrated into claim verification systems to enhance structured reasoning and explainability? The paper suggests exploring knowledge graph integration as future work.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (learning rates, batch sizes, epochs) are not fully reported, hindering exact replication.
- Dataset splits for existing benchmarks are ad-hoc and may differ from prior works, affecting comparability.
- The model requires paired text-image evidence; performance degrades when images are missing or noisy.
- Model length constraints may truncate evidence, losing information.
- Chart-type sensitivity indicates limited generalization across visual encodings.

## Confidence
- **Graph-based retrieval design:** High confidence. Supported by detailed equations (Sec 3.2), architecture figure (Fig. 2), and an ablation showing a significant MAP drop when the graph is removed (Table 3).
- **Dual-granularity verification fusion:** High confidence. Backed by explicit equations (Sec 3.3) and ablations where removing token- or evidence-level fusion hurts Macro F1 (Fig. 3b).
- **Explainable FiD with consistency regularization:** High confidence. Has a dedicated section (3.4), an ablation (Fig. 3d) demonstrating ROUGE-L drop without the regularizer, and a figure illustrating the FiD construction.

## Next Checks
1. **Full hyperparameter sweep:** Re-run training with learning rates {1e‑5, 2e‑5, 5e‑5}, batch sizes {8, 16}, and for {3, 5} epochs; report mean and variance of MAP, F1, and ROUGE‑L to establish robustness and optimal settings.
2. **Cross-dataset generalization:** Evaluate the pre-trained MEVER (trained on AIChartClaim) on out-of-domain chart datasets (e.g., ChartQA, PlotQA) without fine-tuning; measure retrieval and verification performance to test modality reasoning transfer.
3. **Component isolation study:** Build reduced baselines: (a) replace the two-layer GNN with simple concatenation; (b) remove consistency regularization (`λ=0`); (c) use text-only FiD. Run on a held-out split of AIChartClaim to quantify each component’s contribution under identical compute and data.
