# Building Autonomous GUI Navigation via Agentic-Q Estimation and Step-Wise Policy Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13653
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13653
- **Reference Count:** 15
- **Primary Result:** A framework using agentic-Q estimation and step-wise policy optimization enables a 9B model (Ovis2.5-9B) to achieve SOTA success rates (~78.8% on WebVoyager, ~64.0% on Online-Mind2Web, ~92.4% on ScreenSpot v2), outperforming larger models like GPT-4o and Claude with ~10 percentage point gains over its SFT baseline.

## Executive Summary
Training GUI agents is costly and unstable due to non‑stationary environments and sparse supervision. This paper proposes a two‑component framework to decouple policy learning from the dynamic environment. First, **agentic‑Q estimation** trains a model to assign step‑wise values using self‑generated trajectories. Second, **step‑wise policy optimization** uses these values for critic‑free reinforcement learning, updating the policy entirely on self‑collected data without further environment interaction. Applied to Ovis2.5‑9B, the method sets new state‑of‑the‑art results on three key GUI navigation benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The framework begins by training a supervised‑fine‑tuned (SFT) policy. This SFT model then generates trajectories, which are used to train an **agentic‑Q model** via binary cross‑entropy loss, predicting whether a state‑action step leads to eventual success. The Q model is fixed thereafter. For policy optimization, the SFT policy is further trained using a step‑wise policy gradient objective where the reward signal is provided by the fixed agentic‑Q model, eliminating the need for an online environment critic. This decoupling allows policy updates using only the static pool of self‑generated trajectories, reducing environmental interactions and stabilizing training.

## Key Results
- Achieves 78.8% average success on WebVoyager, 64.0% on Online‑Mind2Web, and 92.4% on ScreenSpot v2.
- Surpasses larger models (GPT‑4o, Claude) by significant margins.
- Improves upon its own SFT baseline by approximately 10 percentage points across benchmarks.
- Demonstrates stable training with reduced entropy variance using a sliding‑window size of 1 for Q estimation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by **decoupling value estimation from policy improvement**. Agentic‑Q estimation converts the sparse, delayed reward problem into a dense, step‑wise binary classification task using offline, self‑generated data. This provides a stable learning signal that does not depend on a non‑stationary environment during policy updates. Step‑wise policy optimization then uses this fixed critic to perform policy gradient updates on the same static dataset, avoiding distribution shifts caused by interacting with a changing environment. The use of binary, rather than continuous, Q targets may further stabilize learning by simplifying the estimation task and reducing noise from inaccurate long‑horizon credit assignment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Step‑wise Value Estimation:** Assigns a value to each individual action in a trajectory, predicting eventual success. *Why needed:* Converts sparse terminal rewards into dense supervision for learning. *Quick check:* Can the model predict success/failure from a single action in a mid‑trajectory state?
- **Critic‑Free Reinforcement Learning:** Performs policy updates without a separate, learned value function (critic) that interacts with the environment. *Why needed:* Avoids instability from fitting a critic to a non‑stationary data distribution. *Quick check:* Does removing the online critic reduce variance in policy gradient estimates?
- **Self‑Generated Trajectory Mining:** Uses the current policy to collect data for training the Q estimator. *Why needed:* Provides task‑relevant, on‑policy data without external environment cost. *Quick check:* Are trajectories from the SFT policy sufficiently diverse to cover common failure modes?
- **Binary Cross‑Entropy for Q:** Trains Q to classify a step as leading to success (1) or failure (0). *Why needed:* Simpler than regression, potentially more robust to noise in binary outcome labels. *Quick check:* Does a binary target yield more stable Q training than a continuous return prediction?
- **Sliding‑Window Value Assignment:** Labels a step based on the outcome within a fixed future window. *Why needed:* Provides a finite‑horizon approximation to the episodic return. *Quick check:* How does window size affect the bias‑variance trade‑off in Q estimates?
- **Static Dataset Policy Optimization:** Updates policy on a fixed replay buffer of trajectories. *Why needed:* Decouples policy updates from environmental non‑stationarity. *Quick check:* Does performance degrade if the policy diverges significantly from the data‑generating policy?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
SFT Policy -> Trajectory Generation -> Agentic‑Q Training (Binary Classifier) -> Fixed Q Model -> Step‑wise Policy Gradient Updates on same Trajectories -> Optimized Policy

**Critical Path:**  
1. Train initial SFT policy via demonstration imitation.  
2. Generate trajectories from SFT policy.  
3. Train agentic‑Q model on these trajectories using binary labels derived from terminal outcomes within a sliding window.  
4. Freeze Q model. Perform policy optimization on the SFT policy using the fixed Q model to provide step‑wise advantages, updating only on the stored trajectories.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Binary vs. Continuous Q:* Binary classification simplifies the learning task and may be more noise‑tolerant but discards information about the magnitude of future returns.  
- *Fixed vs. Iterative Q:* Using a Q model trained on initial SFT rollouts is computationally efficient but risks distribution shift as the policy improves; iterative retraining could maintain accuracy at the cost of complexity.  
- *Window Size 1:* Simplicity and stability as shown in ablation, but may be suboptimal for tasks requiring longer‑term credit assignment.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Policy divergence: Entropy drops sharply if policy overfits to the static dataset, indicating lack of exploration.  
- Q misestimation: Low correlation between predicted binary Q and actual trajectory outcomes, especially on states far from the SFT policy’s visitation distribution.  
- Compounding errors: High rate of invalid actions or dead‑end loops in generated trajectories that were not present in the SFT data.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Ablation - Q Type:** Train a regression‑based Q head predicting continuous discounted returns and compare performance, training stability, and entropy against the binary version on WebVoyager.  
2. **Ablation - Window Size:** Systematically vary the sliding‑window size (e.g., 1, 3, 5, 10) across all three benchmarks, measuring success rate and policy entropy to test generalizability.  
3. **Iterative Q Refresh:** Implement a scheme where the Q model is periodically retrained on trajectories from the latest policy (every K updates) and compare final performance and Q‑error against the fixed‑Q baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Is binary agentic‑Q optimal, or would continuous‑valued Q improve step‑wise policy learning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section 3.3 uses binary cross‑entropy on terminal reward; no regression baseline is tested.
- Why unresolved: Continuous returns may provide richer supervision but could introduce noise; the design choice lacks comparative justification.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train a regression‑based Q head (predicting a continuous value) and compare performance, stability, and entropy against the binary version on the same benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How generally optimal is the sliding‑window size = 1, and does it hold for tasks with longer‑term dependencies?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section 3.3 introduces sliding window; ablation on WebVoyager shows size 1 yields best results and entropy stability (Figure 4), but only one benchmark is evaluated.
- Why unresolved: GUI tasks vary in horizon and context needs; a single window size may not be universally optimal.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically vary window sizes across a diverse set of GUI tasks with different horizon lengths, measuring the impact on success rate and entropy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does using a fixed Q model trained on initial SFT rollouts cause distribution shift as the policy evolves, necessitating periodic Q refresh?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section 3.3 collects trajectories from the SFT model to train Q, and Section 3.4 then uses that fixed Q for all policy updates; iterative retraining is not discussed.
- Why unresolved: As the policy diverges from SFT, Q estimates may become biased, potentially harming optimization; the assumption of a stationary Q is unverified.
- What evidence would resolve it: Run experiments with iterative schemes where Q is periodically retrained on recent policy rollouts, and compare performance, Q‑error, and stability against the fixed‑Q approach.

## Limitations
- Reliance on self‑generated trajectories risks compounding hallucinations and distributional bias not captured by static benchmarks.
- The "critic‑free" assumption of accurate agentic‑Q estimation is untested in highly non‑stationary real‑world environments with user drift.
- Reported gains are confined to three specific benchmarks; generalizability to other GUI domains (mobile, desktop) or unseen layouts is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Framework Design (Agentic‑Q + Step‑wise Policy):** High – Clear methodology with theoretical decoupling rationale.
- **Reported Benchmark Results:** Medium – Results are specific and SOTA but lack independent replication; possible overfitting to benchmark quirks.
- **Generalization Claims:** Low – No evidence beyond the three test sets; real‑world deployment viability is speculative.
