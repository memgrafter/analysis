# Semi-Supervised Cross-Domain Imitation Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10793  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10793  
- **Reference count**: 0  
- **Primary result**: AdaptDICE, the first algorithm for semi-supervised cross-domain imitation learning, transfers policies using only offline data and achieves superior performance with as few as one target expert trajectory.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the problem of Semi-Supervised Cross-Domain Imitation Learning (SS-CDIL), where an agent must learn a policy in a target domain by leveraging offline data from a source domain and only a small number of target expert demonstrations together with unlabeled imperfect trajectories. The proposed solution, AdaptDICE, is the first algorithm for SS-CDIL with a theoretical convergence guarantee. AdaptDICE (1) learns cross-domain state-action mappings that preserve Bellman consistency, (2) combines source and target density ratios into a hybrid ratio, and (3) adaptively balances the two components using a weight that depends on their relative estimation errors. Experiments on MuJoCo locomotion and Robosuite manipulation tasks show that AdaptDICE consistently outperforms prior methods (SMODICE, GWIL, IGDF+IQ-Learn), often with dramatic margins, and remains effective even when only a single target expert trajectory is available. The approach thus offers a practical and data-efficient solution for cross-domain policy transfer without requiring online interaction or paired demonstrations.

## Method Summary
AdaptDICE operates in two phases: source pre‑training and target adaptation. First, a source policy is obtained by running DemoDICE on the source dataset (400 expert + 2000 suboptimal trajectories), yielding a source Q‑function \(Q_s\) and density ratio \(w_s\). A discriminator is then trained on the target data to produce a pseudo‑reward \(r_t\). 

During target adaptation, three networks are trained: (i) mapping functions \(G:S_t\rightarrow S_s\) and \(H:S_t\times A_t\rightarrow A_s\) (implemented as MLPs followed by normalizing flows), (ii) a target critic \(\nu_t\), and (iii) a policy \(\pi\). In each iteration, an adaptive mixing weight \(\beta(t)\) is computed from the current estimation errors of \(w_s\) (after mapping) and \(w_t\). The mappings are updated by minimizing a Bellman‑consistency loss \(L_{\text{MAP}}\) that aligns target transitions with the source Q‑function, the critic is updated via the DICE loss \(L_{\text{DICE}}\), and the policy is improved by weighted behavioral cloning using the hybrid density ratio \(w_{\text{cross}} = \beta w_s(G,H) + (1-\beta) w_t\). The loop runs for 500k steps, after which the final policy is evaluated.

## Key Results
- AdaptDICE significantly outperforms all baselines on every environment; e.g., on Hopper it achieves \(2289.9 \pm 328.7\) vs. SMODICE’s \(2046.6 \pm 53.3\), and on Ant it reaches \(3672.6 \pm 450.8\) vs. SMODICE’s \(86.3 \pm 30.6\).
- The algorithm remains robust with extremely limited target supervision, performing well even with a single expert trajectory.
- The adaptive weighting \(\beta(t)\) successfully shifts reliance between source and target components based on their relative reliability, as supported by theoretical error bounds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Cross‑Domain Mapping via Bellman Consistency
AdaptDICE learns deterministic/probabilistic mappings \(G:S_t\rightarrow S_s\) and \(H:S_t\times A_t\rightarrow A_s\) that minimize the Bellman residual in the source space:  
\(L_{\text{MAP}} = \mathbb{E}_{(s,a,s')\sim D_t}\big| r_t(s,a) + \gamma \mathbb{E}_{a'\sim\pi(s')}[Q_s(G(s'), H(s',a'))] - Q_s(G(s), H(s,a)) \big|\).  
By matching the Bellman equation after mapping, the source Q‑function becomes valid for target trajectories, enabling value transfer across domains.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid Density Ratio
The target policy is derived from a hybrid density ratio \(w_{\text{cross}}(s,a) = \beta\, w_s(G(s),H(s,a)) + (1-\beta)\, w_t(s,a)\), which combines the source ratio (obtained after mapping) and the target ratio (estimated from target data). This convex combination allows the policy to benefit from robust source knowledge while adapting to target‑specific dynamics.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Balancing via \(\beta(t)\)
The mixing weight \(\beta(t)\) adapts continuously based on the relative estimation errors \(\Delta_{\text{src}}\) and \(\Delta_{\text{tar}}\) of the two density ratios:  
\(\beta(t) = \frac{1/\Delta_{\text{src}}(t)}{1/\Delta_{\text{src}}(t) + 1/\Delta_{\text{tar}}(t)}\).  
Theoretically, this yields an expected error no worse than the better of the two estimators. Practically, it avoids manual tuning and stabilizes learning by automatically favoring the more reliable component.

## Foundational Learning

- **Markov Decision Process (MDP) and Occupancy Measure**  
  An MDP models decision‑making via states, actions, transitions, and rewards. The occupancy measure \(d^\pi(s,a) = (1-\gamma) \sum_{t=0}^\infty \gamma^t P(s_t=s, a_t=a|\pi)\) captures the long‑term state‑action visitation frequency of a policy \(\pi\). It is fundamental to density‑ratio‑based imitation learning (e.g., DemoDICE).  
  *Quick check*: For an infinite‑horizon discounted MDP with discount \(\gamma\) and initial state distribution \(\rho_0\), express \(d^\pi(s,a)\) in terms of \(\pi\), transition dynamics, and \(\rho_0\).

- **Density Ratio in Imitation Learning (DemoDICE)**  
  DemoDICE formulates imitation learning as minimizing the convex \(f\)-divergence between the learned policy’s occupancy and the expert’s occupancy. This yields a dual objective involving a state‑value function \(\nu\), and the optimal density ratio is \(w^*(s,a) = \exp\left((r(s,a)+\gamma \mathbb{E}_{s'}[\nu(s')]-\nu(s))/(1+\alpha)\right)\). This ratio provides a reward shaping signal and enables policy extraction.  
  *Quick check*: Write the DemoDICE primal objective (Eq. 1) and its dual loss \(L(\nu; r)\) (Eq. 2). What does \(w^*(s,a)\) represent intuitively?

- **Bellman Equation and Advantage Functions**  
  The Bellman equation states \(V^\pi(s) = \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}[r(s,a) + \gamma V^\pi(s')]\). The advantage function \(A^\nu(s,a) = r(s,a) + \gamma \mathbb{E}_{s'}[\nu(s')] - \nu(s)\) generalizes this concept for an arbitrary value function \(\nu\). Advantages appear in the derivation of density ratios and in the cross‑domain mapping loss, where Bellman consistency is enforced.  
  *Quick check*: Starting from the definition of \(V^\pi\), derive \(Q^\pi(s,a)\) and the advantage \(A^\pi(s,a)\). How does the Bellman error reflect optimality?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Pretrained source module (Q_src, w_src) → Mapping networks (G, H) → Target critic (ν_tar) → Adaptive weight (β(t)) → Hybrid ratio (w_cross) → Policy (π). Additionally, a target discriminator (c_tar) produces the pseudo‑reward r_tar.

### Critical path
1. Train c_tar on target expert vs. union data to obtain r_tar.  
2. Pre‑train source DemoDICE to compute Q_src and w_src.  
3. Inside the main loop (500 k iterations):  
   a. Compute β(t) from current Δ_src and Δ_tar.  
   b. Update G and H by minimizing L_MAP using sampled target batches and current π.  
   c. Update ν_tar by minimizing L_DICE.  
   d. Update π via weighted behavioral cloning using w_cross.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Normalizing flows for G/H**: ensure mapped outputs lie within the source support without projection, but add pretraining cost and limit flexibility if capacity is insufficient.  
- **Adaptive vs. fixed β**: adaptive β removes manual tuning and responds to error dynamics, yet its estimates can be noisy with very few target trajectories; a moving average (ψ=0.9) trades responsiveness for stability.  
- **Imperfect target trajectories**: including suboptimal data improves coverage but introduces noise; the regularization parameter α in DemoDICE mitigates this.  
- **Source data scale**: performance is robust to the amount of source data due to adaptive weighting, but extremely poor source data still harms transfer.

### Failure signatures
- **L_MAP not decreasing**: mapping fails; check source Q_src quality or flow capacity.  
- **w_tar diverges**: ν_tar unstable; adjust learning rate, batch size, or α.  
- **β(t) stuck at 0 or 1**: error estimates saturated; inspect Δ components, increase moving average ψ, or collect more target data.  
- **Policy collapse**: vanishing action entropy; monitor variance and consider an entropy bonus.  
- **Training instability**: oscillating losses; lower learning rates or increase gradient‑penalty coefficients.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Mapping sanity check** on a synthetic domain with a known linear mapping; verify that L_MAP decreases and that G(H) align target samples with the source manifold.  
2. **Baseline comparison on default Hopper**: run AdaptDICE, SMODICE, GWIL, IGDF+IQ-Learn; expect AdaptDICE to achieve >2000 return while GWIL remains near random.  
3. **Adaptive β ablation**: fix β to 0 (target‑only), 0.5, 1 (source‑only) and compare to adaptive β, especially under large domain gaps (e.g., Ant); also visualize β(t) to confirm it interpolates based on dataset conditions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. *Can AdaptDICE eliminate the need for any labeled target‑domain demonstrations?* The paper acknowledges reliance on a small amount of labeled target data as a limitation; fully unsupervised transfer would broaden applicability.  
2. *Does the theoretical error bound hold under neural network function approximation?* The convergence analysis assumes tabular MDPs and smoothness; practical use of deep networks lacks guarantees.  
3. *How does continuous adaptive weighting β(t) affect convergence compared to the discrete theoretical rule?* The paper claims the continuous version does not alter the convergence rate but provides no proof.  
4. *Can AdaptDICE generalize to cross‑domain transfer with different task objectives?* All experiments use identical goals; robustness to reward or task shifts remains unexplored.

## Limitations
- **Implementation details missing**: Hyperparameters (gradient penalties, flow pretraining, policy distribution), initial‑state sampling for LDICE, and source Q construction for stochastic dynamics are not fully specified, hindering faithful reproduction.  
- **Dependence on pretrained source knowledge**: The method assumes transferable source Q and overlapping supports; performance may degrade under large morphological gaps or poor source data.  
- **Adaptive β sensitivity**: Error estimates can be noisy with few target trajectories, leading to erratic weighting; theoretical assumptions (e.g., mapping preserves transitions, approximator capacity) may not hold in practice.

2. **Adaptive β robustness ablation**: Vary the number of target expert trajectories (1–60); compare adaptive β to fixed baselines (0, 0.5, 1) and an oracle using true errors; measure performance stability and β(t) trajectories.
