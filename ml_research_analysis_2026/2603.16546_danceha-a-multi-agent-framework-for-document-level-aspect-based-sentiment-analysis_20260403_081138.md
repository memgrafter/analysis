# DanceHA: A Multi-Agent Framework for Document-Level Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.16546
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16546
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** DanceHA achieves up to 63.18% F1 on document-level ABSIA with informal styles using a multi-agent framework with Manager Agent, and distills knowledge to smaller models (e.g., Qwen-14B) that match larger LLMs.

## Executive Summary
DanceHA introduces a multi-agent divide-and-conquer framework for document-level Aspect-Based Sentiment Intensity Analysis (ABSIA) focusing on informal styles like word lengthening. The paper presents the Inf-ABSIA dataset, containing 2,714 documents and 23,024 fine-grained ACOSI (Aspect Category, Opinion, Sentiment Intensity) tuples, curated via Human-AI collaboration. The framework employs a Divider agent to segment documents into thought groups, specialized Dance agents (Category, Opinion, Sentiment) to extract ACOSI components per group, and a Manager agent to resolve inconsistencies across multiple Dance teams. Experiments show that Dance with GPT-4o achieves up to 47.80% F1, while the Manager Agent boosts performance to 63.18% F1 in the Restaurant domain. Knowledge distillation transfers insights to smaller models, with fine-tuned Qwen-14B outperforming few-shot CoT GPT-4o and matching Dance with Qwen2.5-72B. The study also finds that informal styles like lengthening words significantly amplify sentiment intensity across domains.

## Method Summary
DanceHA’s method centers on a hierarchical multi-agent architecture. First, a Divider agent partitions a document into coherent thought groups, each potentially discussing distinct aspects. Then, for each thought group, a Dance team of three specialized agents (Category, Opinion, Sentiment) extracts the corresponding ACOSI tuples. Multiple Dance teams with different backbone LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3, Qwen2.5-72B) run in parallel. Finally, a Manager agent aggregates the outputs from all teams, resolving conflicts via reasoning to produce a consensus set of ACOSI tuples. The framework is evaluated on the newly created Inf-ABSIA dataset, which focuses on restaurant reviews containing informal linguistic styles. Additionally, knowledge distillation is applied: the outputs from large LLM-based Dance teams are used to train smaller models (e.g., Qwen-14B) that can perform the task more efficiently.

## Key Results
- Inf-ABSIA dataset: 2,714 documents, 23,024 ACOSI tuples, with annotation via Human-AI collaboration.
- Dance with GPT-4o: up to 47.80% F1; Manager Agent (DeepSeek-V3) boosts to 63.18% F1 in Restaurant domain.
- Knowledge distillation: fine-tuned Qwen-14B outperforms few-shot CoT GPT-4o and matches Dance with Qwen2.5-72B.
- Informal styles (e.g., lengthening words) are shown to significantly amplify sentiment intensity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The divide-and-conquer approach addresses the complexity of document-level ABSIA by breaking down the task into manageable sub-tasks: segmentation, aspect categorization, opinion extraction, and sentiment intensity scoring. Specialized agents focus on specific components, reducing interference and improving accuracy. The Manager agent leverages multiple diverse LLM teams to correct individual errors through consensus, mimicking a human committee discussion. The use of thought groups helps maintain contextual coherence within each segment, mitigating issues like coreference across sentences. Additionally, the framework’s adaptability allows integration of various LLMs and supports knowledge distillation to smaller models, preserving performance while reducing computational cost.

## Foundational Learning
**Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA)**  
*Why needed:* To move beyond document-level sentiment and provide fine-grained opinions on specific aspects.  
*Quick check:* Can you identify the aspect terms (e.g., "battery life") and their polarities in a product review?  

**Document-Level ABSIA**  
*Why needed:* Extends ABSA to multi-sentence documents and introduces continuous sentiment intensity scores.  
*Quick check:* Can you handle cross-sentence dependencies and assign intensity scores (e.g., 1-5) to each aspect?  

**ACOSI Tuples**  
*Why needed:* Unified representation capturing Aspect Category, Opinion, and Sentiment Intensity for comprehensive analysis.  
*Quick check:* For the sentence "The camera is amazing but the battery drains quickly," can you produce tuples: (camera, positive, 4.5), (battery, negative, 2.0)?  

**Informal Styles in Text**  
*Why needed:* User-generated content often contains informal expressions (e.g., lengthening, emojis) that modulate sentiment intensity.  
*Quick check:* Does "sooo delicious" imply stronger positivity than "very delicious"?  

**Multi-Agent Systems**  
*Why needed:* Complex NLP tasks can be decomposed into specialized sub-tasks executed by different agents, improving robustness.  
*Quick check:* Can a team of classifiers (each handling a sub-task) outperform a single end-to-end model?  

**Knowledge Distillation**  
*Why needed:* To transfer capabilities from large, expensive LLMs to smaller, deployable models.  
*Quick check:* Can a small model achieve comparable performance to a large model when trained on the large model’s outputs?  

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Divider Agent -> Dance Teams (Category, Opinion, Sentiment Agents) -> Manager Agent -> Final ACOSI Tuples  

**Critical path:**  
1. Divider splits document into thought groups.  
2. For each group, each Dance team (parallel) runs Category, Opinion, Sentiment agents to extract ACOSI candidates.  
3. Manager aggregates all candidates, resolves conflicts, and outputs final tuples.  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *LLM vs. smaller models:* Using LLMs ensures high reasoning ability but incurs high cost and latency; smaller models are efficient but may sacrifice accuracy.  
- *Divide-and-conquer vs. end-to-end:* Decomposition improves handling of long documents and specialized extraction, but errors in early stages (e.g., Divider) propagate.  
- *Manager agent complexity:* Adding a Manager improves performance by consensus but adds another layer of inference and prompt engineering overhead.  

**Failure signatures:**  
- Divider produces incoherent or incomplete thought groups, causing missing aspects or incorrect segmentation.  
- Dance agents hallucinate aspect categories or opinions, especially with informal styles.  
- Manager fails to resolve conflicts when teams disagree, leading to noisy or inconsistent tuples.  
- Informal style handling (e.g., lengthening) may be over- or under-amplified, distorting intensity scores.  

**First three experiments:**  
1. **Single-document walkthrough:** Process a short document through the entire pipeline, inspecting outputs at each stage (Divider groups, Dance team tuples, Manager consensus) to understand data flow.  
2. **Ablation of Manager:** Run the pipeline with a single Dance team (e.g., GPT-4o) and compare performance to the full multi-team Manager setup to quantify the Manager’s contribution.  
3. **Agent substitution:** Replace one Dance agent (e.g., Sentiment) with a fine-tuned smaller model (e.g., RoBERTa) and measure the drop in F1/MAE to evaluate trade-offs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does varying the number of specialized agents (team size) within the Dance component affect the trade-off between annotation quality (e.g., F1-score) and computational cost (e.g., inference time, API calls) in the DanceHA framework?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The "Limitations" section states: "First, the influence of team size on annotation quality remains an open question."
- Why unresolved: The paper evaluates Dance with a fixed configuration of three specialized agents (Category, Opinion, Sentiment) but does not experiment with scaling the number of agents or teams within the divide-and-conquer structure.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the number of specialized agents per thought group or the number of parallel Dance teams, measuring performance metrics (F1, MAE) and computational overhead (token usage, latency) across multiple domains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what extent can smaller, specialized language models (e.g., T5, RoBERTa) replace the LLM-based expert agents (Divider, Category, Opinion, Sentiment) in the Dance pipeline without a significant drop in ACOSI extraction performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The "Limitations" section notes: "some sub-tasks might be replaced with small language models such as T5 and RoBERTa to improve efficiency."
- Why unresolved: The framework exclusively uses large LLMs (GPT-4, DeepSeek, Qwen) for all agents. The potential efficiency gains from using smaller models and the performance degradation (if any) from replacing LLM reasoning with specialized classifiers are not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replacing each LLM-based agent (or combinations) with fine-tuned smaller models on the Inf-ABSIA dataset, comparing F1-score and MAE against the full LLM-based Dance baseline, and measuring speed/cost improvements.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do other informal linguistic phenomena (e.g., emojis, hashtags, slang, code-switching) beyond word lengthening and punctuation influence the granularity and accuracy of sentiment intensity prediction in document-level ABSIA?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses exclusively on "lengthening words" as the informal style of interest (Abstract, Introduction, Dataset Construction). While it mentions emojis in passing, it does not analyze them. The Inf-ABSIA dataset was filtered only for lengthening words.
- Why unresolved: The claim that "informal styles... often intensify opinions" is based solely on lengthening words. The generality of this finding to other informal features and their interaction with aspect categories is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: An annotation or analysis study on a diverse set of informal expressions within document reviews, measuring their correlation with SIS scores across aspects. Extension of Inf-ABSIA or creation of a new dataset with rich informal style annotations.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What specific characteristics of the "Divider" agent's thought grouping (e.g., segmentation coherency, aspect term coverage, handling of coreference) most strongly predict the overall ACOSI tuple extraction performance of the downstream Dance pipeline?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Table 3 shows the Divider agent (F_D) has the lowest accuracy across all models and domains (e.g., ~54% for GPT-4o on Rest). The paper notes "Divider agent has potential for further improvement" but does not analyze error types or their downstream impact.
- Why unresolved: The paper evaluates Divider via aspect and thought group accuracy but does not conduct an error analysis linking specific grouping failures (e.g., missed aspects, over-segmentation, incorrect aspect spans) to failures in the subsequent category, opinion, or sentiment stages.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed error taxonomy for Divider outputs, followed by correlation analysis between specific error types and the final F1-score of the complete ACOSI tuples. Intervention studies where ground-truth thought groupings are provided to measure the upper bound on performance from improved segmentation.

### Open Question 5
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for fusing outputs from multiple heterogeneous Dance teams (with different backbone LLMs) within the Manager Agent (MA) to maximize performance and robustness? Should fusion be based on model confidence, agreement, task-specific strengths, or a learned combination?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The MA uses three Dance teams (GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3, Qwen2.5-72B) and resolves inconsistencies to produce a consensus, leading to significant gains (e.g., MA DeepSeek-V3 F1 63.18% vs. Dance GPT-4o 47.80% in Rest). However, the fusion heuristic is rule-based and not analyzed.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates the benefit of multi-team aggregation but does not investigate *how* to aggregate optimally. Factors like model diversity, individual agent strengths per sub-task (from Table 3), and the nature of conflicts (e.g., which agent's predictions are more reliable for SIS) are not studied.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing different fusion mechanisms (e.g., voting, weighted by per-agent historical accuracy on validation sets, learned meta-model) and analyzing which Dance teams contribute most to correcting specific error types (e.g., intensity scoring vs. aspect extraction).

## Limitations
- **Dataset generalizability:** Inf-ABSIA is limited to restaurant reviews and lacks inter-annotator agreement metrics; its applicability to other informal domains is uncertain.
- **Computational overhead:** The multi-agent framework relies on large LLMs and multiple parallel calls, resulting in high inference costs and latency.
- **Causal inference gap:** The observed amplification of sentiment intensity by informal styles is correlational; controlled studies are needed to establish causality.
