# Joint Embedding Variational Bayes
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05639
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05639
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves 68.2% top-1 linear accuracy on ImageNet-1K, matching SimSiam/VICReg, and 92.4% average AUROC for OOD detection on OpenOOD CIFAR-10.

## Executive Summary
Joint Embedding Variational Bayes (VJE) introduces a variational formulation for non-contrastive self-supervised learning, synthesizing joint embedding with variational inference. It maximizes a symmetric conditional evidence lower bound (ELBO) using a heavy-tailed Student-t likelihood applied to a polar decomposition of embeddings, explicitly decoupling directional and radial factors. This design prevents norm-induced instabilities during training. An amortized inference network parameterizes a diagonal Gaussian posterior with feature-wise variances tied to the likelihood scale, enabling anisotropic uncertainty without auxiliary projection heads. On ImageNet-1K, VJE reaches 68.2% top-1 linear accuracy, competitive with SimSiam (68.1%) and VICReg (68.6%). Under k-NN evaluation, it attains 91.8% on CIFAR-10, and for out-of-distribution detection, it achieves 92.4% average AUROC on the OpenOOD CIFAR-10 benchmark, surpassing comparable variational baselines.

## Method Summary
VJE formulates non-contrastive learning as maximizing a symmetric conditional ELBO. Given a batch, two augmented views are processed by online and target encoders (target via stop-gradient or EMA). The online embedding \(z\) is fed to an inference network \(g_\phi\) producing a diagonal Gaussian posterior \(q(s|z) = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \text{diag}(\sigma^2))\) over a latent \(s\). The target provides a fixed observation \(y = (\hat{z}, \|\hat{z}\|)\). The conditional likelihood \(p(y|s)\) factorizes into a directional Student-t on the unit sphere (using a Schur complement Mahalanobis distance in the tangent space) and a radial Student-t on the norm residual \(\Delta r = \|\hat{z}\| - \|s\|\). The ELBO sums the directional and radial log-likelihoods and a KL divergence \(\text{KL}(q(s|z) \| \mathcal{N}(0,I))\). The feature-wise variance \(\sigma^2\) is shared between the posterior and the directional likelihood scale. Gradients flow only through the online branch (encoder and inference network).

## Key Results
- ImageNet-1K linear evaluation: 68.2%±0.3 top-1 accuracy.
- CIFAR-10 k-NN evaluation: 91.8%±0.1 (with EMA target encoder, no projection head).
- OpenOOD CIFAR-10 AUROC: 92.4%±0.2 average, outperforming VI-SimSiam (83.0%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decoupling directional and radial factors prevents norm-induced instability during non-contrastive training.
- **Mechanism:** The model reparameterizes embeddings into unit directions and norms using a polar decomposition. The conditional likelihood factorizes into a Student-t term on the unit sphere (directional) and a 1D Student-t on the norm residual (radial). This removes the coupling between angular alignment and magnitude that exists in a standard Mahalanobis distance under a full-covariance likelihood, where large norms amplify angular errors.
- **Core assumption:** The geometric separation of concerns (direction vs. scale) allows independent optimization and avoids degenerate solutions where models exploit norm shortcuts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...explicitly decoupling directional and radial factors to prevent norm-induced instabilities during training."
  - [section 4.1] "To address this coupling, we reparameterize the radial channel as the difference of norms: Δr:=||z||−||s||. This parameterization measures magnitude discrepancy independently of angular alignment."
  - [corpus] The Var-JEPA paper also discusses separating geometric factors but does not implement the radial reparameterization as Δr; VJE's ablation in Table 10 shows failure without the directional term, but the radial term alone does not cause collapse.
- **Break condition:** Removing the radial term does not degrade performance (Table 10, Ldir+LKL matches full objective), indicating the *critical* mechanism is the directional-factor independence. However, the complete factorization (both terms) is the proposed stable solution; removing either term leads to catastrophic failure in the other's absence under KL regularization.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Heavy-tailed Student-t likelihoods provide robustness to large residuals by bounding gradient influence, stabilizing training without heuristic clipping.
- **Mechanism:** The Student-t negative log-likelihood grows only logarithmically for large residuals, and its gradient magnitude decays with the Mahalanobis distance, unlike the unbounded linear growth of a Gaussian. This bounded influence prevents a few outlier pairs from dominating the update.
- **Core assumption:** High-dimensional embedding spaces naturally contain large-distortion pairs due to augmentations; a heavy-tailed model is more appropriate than Gaussian for such residuals.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...heavy-tailed Student-t likelihood... explicitly decoupling directional and radial factors to prevent norm-induced instabilities... gradients saturate and then decay so that outliers contribute only a bounded amount of signal."
  - [section 4] Figure 2 illustrates the bounded gradient property of Student-t vs. Gaussian.
  - [section 5.5] Table 9 shows catastrophic collapse when ν→∞ (Gaussian limit) across all datasets, while ν∈[1,20] works. ν=50 shows partial collapse.
- **Break condition:** The heavy tail is necessary but not sufficient; the Gaussian limit (ν→∞) fails catastrophically. However, for fixed ν>1, the model is robust; the ablation shows ν=1.0 and 3.0 perform best, but ν=20 still works, indicating a range of acceptable tail heaviness. The break is specifically at the Gaussian tail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Tying feature-wise variance between the variational posterior and the directional likelihood enables structured, anisotropic uncertainty without auxiliary projection heads.
- **Mechanism:** The inference network outputs a diagonal Gaussian posterior q(s|z)=N(μ, diag(σ²)). The same σ² vector is used as the scale matrix Σ in the directional Student-t term. This sharing means each feature's uncertainty (variance) controls both how we sample latent s and how we score the angular match between s and the target direction on the sphere. This yields per-feature "confidence" in the representation space itself.
- **Core assumption:** Uncertainty should be represented directly in the encoder's embedding space, not in a separate projected space, and should be feature-dependent rather than isotropic.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...feature-wise variances tied to the likelihood scale... to capture anisotropic uncertainty without auxiliary projection heads."
  - [section 4.2] "We tie the variance vector σ² between the two distributions... This choice makes each σ²ₖ a shared per-feature scale parameter."
  - [section 5.3] Table 3 shows that mean variance and NLL correlate with inter-class margin, while anisotropy (CoV(σ²)) correlates with within-class radius. The posterior is not isotropic; different features have different learned scales.
  - [section C.3] Isotropic scalar variance (σ² = αI) fails completely (Table 13), proving that feature-wise variance is necessary.
- **Break condition:** Removing tying (e.g., using separate variances for posterior and likelihood) would likely break the semantic link between uncertainty and directional scoring, but this specific ablation is not run. The isotropic ablation shows that without feature-wise degrees of freedom, the model cannot learn meaningful structure.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Encoder (fθ) -> Embedding z -> Inference Network (gϕ) -> (μ, σ²) -> Sample s ~ q(s|z).  
Target Encoder -> Target y = (ẑ, ||ẑ||).  
Likelihood: p(y|s) = p_dir(unit(s), unit(ẑ); σ²) * p_rad(||ẑ|| - ||s||).  
Symmetric application (i↔j). KL regularizer added.

**Critical Path:**  
Forward: x -> fθ -> z_i -> gϕ -> (μ_i, σ²_i). Sample s_i ~ q. Evaluate L_dir(target_j, ŝ_i, σ²_i) + L_rad(||target_j|| - ||s_i||). Symmetrize i↔j. Add β*KL. Backward only through online branch (fθ, gϕ); target branch detached.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Stop-gradient vs EMA: Both provide fixed-observation semantics; EMA yields smoother targets but adds a momentum hyperparameter; paper uses both.
- ν (Student-t DoF): Lower ν = heavier tails = more robustness to outliers but potentially underfitting. ν=1.0 default; ν>20 leads to collapse.
- No projection head: VJE defines likelihood directly on encoder features, saving parameters; ablations show headless VJE matches or beats head-equipped versions in k-NN.

**Failure Signatures:**  
1. **Gaussian collapse:** ν→∞. Posterior variance collapses, KL inflates, representation effective rank plummets (Table 11, Figure 7).
2. **Isotropic posterior failure:** Using scalar variance (σ² = αI) for posterior and likelihood. Posterior mean becomes uninformatic (chance accuracy), α global constant (Table 13).
3. **Standard (unfactorized) likelihood failure:** Using full Student-t on z without polar decoupling. Norm-induced pathology: posterior variance contracts, KL inflates, directional performance suffers compared to factorized (Table 11, Figure 8).

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Train VJE (ν=1.0) on CIFAR-10 for 200 epochs. Verify k-NN ~87-88% (Table 9) and stable training (no KL explosion). If collapse occurs, check ν and detach target correctly.
2. **ν sweep:** Train with ν ∈ {1, 3, 7, 20, 50, ∞}. Expect collapse at ν=50 or ∞ (Table 9). This confirms heavy-tail necessity.
3. **Factorized vs. Standard likelihood ablate:** Implement the standard multivariate Student-t on z (no polar split). Compare directional NLL (post-hoc factorized eval), posterior variance, and k-NN to factorized version. Expect standard to show higher directional NLL, lower variance, and worse k-NN on larger datasets (Table 11, Figure 8).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can VJE's representation-space posterior uncertainty be converted into calibrated predictive uncertainty for downstream tasks? (Projecting uncertainty into downstream task spaces is a separate modelling problem.)
- How can VJE’s directional and radial likelihoods be adapted to token-level representations in Vision Transformers? (Current framework operates on a single global embedding; patch-based/hierarchical architectures require new formulations over token collections.)
- Do alternative heavy-tailed likelihoods (e.g., hyperbolic distribution, normal-inverse-gamma) improve robustness and uncertainty calibration in VJE beyond Student-t? (Framework is not restricted to Student-t; exploring other kernels may refine robustness and geometric expressiveness.)

## Limitations
- External validity: Evaluation limited to vision benchmarks (ImageNet, CIFAR, STL); applicability to other modalities or large-scale pretraining unknown.
- Missing ablation: Tying variance between posterior and likelihood is advocated but not directly ablated (untied-variance case not tested).
- ν selection: No systematic guideline for choosing ν across architectures/augmentations; default ν=1.0 may not generalize.

## Confidence
- **Decoupling directional/radial factors**: High (theoretical derivation and ablation support; radial component's precise role ambiguous but directional factor independence validated).
- **Heavy-tailed Student-t**: High (catastrophic collapse under Gaussian limit consistently validates robustness).
- **Feature-wise tied variance**: Medium (anisotropy correlations presented; untied-variance ablation missing).
- **Performance parity with SimSiam/VICReg**: High (reported numbers precise and align with known baselines, though no independent replication).
- **OOD AUROC 92.4%**: Medium (comparison only to VI-SimSiam; broader baselines absent).
