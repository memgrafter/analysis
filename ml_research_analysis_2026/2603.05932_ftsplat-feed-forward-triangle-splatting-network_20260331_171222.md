# FTSplat: Feed-forward Triangle Splatting Network

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.05932
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05932
*   **Reference Count:** 29
*   **Primary Result:** Achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction among mesh-based methods (PSNR 20.39, SSIM 0.707, LPIPS 0.257 on RealEstate10K) with fast feed-forward inference (0.17s), producing connected meshes directly importable into simulators.

## Executive Summary
FTSplat addresses a critical gap in 3D reconstruction: the inefficiency of per-scene optimization in methods like NeRF and 3DGS, and the lack of explicit, simulation-ready geometry in feed-forward Gaussian approaches. It proposes a feed-forward network that directly predicts continuous triangular surfaces from multi-view images. The core innovation lies in a pixel-aligned triangle generation module that guarantees a connected mesh topology without post-processing, and a relative 3D point cloud supervision signal from a pretrained depth model to stabilize geometric learning. This yields a favorable trade-off: slightly lower photorealistic fidelity than state-of-the-art feed-forward Gaussian methods but superior geometric consistency and explicit mesh outputs suitable for simulation, all in a single forward pass.

## Method Summary
The method takes calibrated multi-view images as input. A shared ResNet backbone and multi-view Swin Transformer extract features, which are fused with monocular depth priors from a pretrained Depth Anything V2 model. A multi-view cost volume built from these features estimates depth maps, whose values are back-projected using camera poses to form an initial 3D point cloud. A 2D U-Net decoder processes the fused features, and a lightweight MLP ("triangle head") predicts per-vertex attributes (opacity, spherical harmonics color) for these points. Crucially, each vertex is mapped back to its source image pixel coordinate, and a fixed adjacency rule (connecting neighbors `(u+1,v)` and `(u,v+1)`) generates triangular faces, ensuring a globally connected mesh. This mesh is rendered via a differentiable triangle rasterizer to novel views. The training loss combines photometric terms (L1, LPIPS, depth smoothness) with a point cloud alignment loss (`L_points`). A curriculum strategy uses a high weight for `L_points` early on to lock in coarse geometry before gradually reducing it to refine appearance.

## Key Results
*   **SOTA among mesh-based methods:** On RealEstate10K, achieves PSNR 20.39, SSIM 0.707, LPIPS 0.257, outperforming prior optimization-based triangle splatting (PSNR 19.78) while being **5700x faster** (0.17s vs. ~17 mins).
*   **Explicit, simulation-ready output:** Produces a single, connected triangular mesh per scene directly, avoiding the need for time-consuming mesh extraction or surface reconstruction post-processing.
*   **Critical role of point cloud supervision:** Ablation study shows removing the relative 3D point cloud supervision reduces PSNR dramatically from 20.39 to 13.06 and causes catastrophic planar collapse, proving its necessity for stable geometry learning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Pixel-aligned triangle generation provides a stable, connected surface topology directly from image features without explicit surface reconstruction post-processing.
*   **Mechanism:** After decoding per-pixel vertex attributes from a 2D U-Net, each 3D vertex is mapped back to its source image pixel `(u, v)`. For each pixel, two triangular faces are created by connecting vertices corresponding to neighbors `(u+1, v)` and `(u, v+1)` (and similarly for the other diagonal). This pixel-level adjacency rule ensures the output mesh is globally connected and covers visible surfaces.
*   **Core assumption:** Surface continuity in 3D can be adequately approximated by preserving 2D pixel adjacency in the reference view(s), leveraging the structured nature of image grids.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section III-B] describes the "simple yet effective pixel-level face generation strategy." The [abstract] states the method "directly predicts continuous triangular surface representations... without requiring additional mesh reconstruction or post-processing."
*   **Break condition:** Likely fails on surfaces with significant perspective distortion or occlusions where a single view's pixel grid does not correspond to a coherent 3D manifold, leading to non-manifold or incorrectly connected geometry.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Relative 3D point cloud supervision from pretrained depth models stabilizes geometric learning in the absence of explicit 3D ground truth, preventing degenerate surface collapse.
*   **Mechanism:** During training, an external depth model (e.g., Depth Anything V3) predicts a sparse 3D point cloud from the input views. The network's predicted vertices are normalized (removing translation/scale ambiguity) and penalized against these supervisory points via an L2 loss (`L_points`). The loss weight `λ_points` is high initially then annealed.
*   **Core assumption:** Relative geometry (shape, up to scale) from a pretrained model is a sufficiently strong and consistent supervisory signal to guide a feed-forward network toward valid 3D surfaces.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section III-B] defines `L_points` and normalization `N(·)` explicitly. [section IV-C] ablation shows removing point cloud supervision drops PSNR from 20.39 to 13.06 and causes planar collapse (Fig. 6).
*   **Break condition:** If the external depth model produces highly inconsistent or degenerate point clouds (e.g., for textureless regions), the supervision could misguide the network, propagating errors into the surface.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** A geometry-to-appearance curriculum (high `λ_points` early, low late) enables stable convergence by first learning coarse geometry before refining texture.
*   **Mechanism:** The total loss `L = L_photo + λ_points * L_points`. In early training, `λ_points` is large, forcing the network to match the coarse shape of the supervisory point cloud. This establishes a geometrically plausible surface. Later, `λ_points` is reduced, letting photometric losses dominate to capture high-frequency appearance details without destabilizing the learned geometry.
*   **Core assumption:** Geometry is a harder constraint to satisfy from sparse views than appearance; locking in a reasonable shape first prevents the network from fitting image space at the expense of 3D coherence.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section III-B] "assign a larger weight λ_points to the geometry loss during the early training stage... gradually reduced." The [abstract] mentions the approach "emphasizes geometric consistency in the early training stage."
*   **Break condition:** If `λ_points` is annealed too quickly, geometry may degrade and floating artifacts may reappear; if kept high too long, the network may overfit to noisy supervisory shape, limiting fine appearance.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Differentiable Triangle Rasterization**
    *   **Why needed here:** This is the rendering engine connecting the predicted triangular mesh to image-space photometric supervision. It projects triangles, computes coverage, and composites color/opacity, enabling gradient flow from the rendered image loss back to vertex positions and connectivity.
    *   **Quick check:** How does the rasterizer handle transparency and sorting for correct blending when multiple triangles project to the same pixel? (Likely follows a depth-sorted, alpha-compositing approach similar to 3DGS).
*   **Concept: Multi-view Cost Volume Depth Estimation**
    *   **Why needed here:** Provides per-pixel depth hypotheses and a dense initial point cloud via back-projection. This is the primary source of 3D structure *within* the network before external point cloud supervision, leveraging known camera poses for cross-view feature matching.
    *   **Quick check:** Why use a cost volume instead of directly regressing depth? (Cost volumes explicitly model multi-view photometric consistency, more robust for sparse view inputs than monocular regression).
*   **Concept: Spherical Harmonics (SH) for Vertex Color**
    *   **Why needed here:** Allows a compact, view-dependent color representation at each vertex. Unlike a single RGB triplet, SH coefficients enable color to vary with viewing direction, necessary for realistic rendering of glossy/reflective surfaces from a static mesh.
    *   **Quick check:** How does the rendering equation incorporate SH? (SH coefficients are evaluated for the *view direction* from the camera to the vertex, producing an RGB color `c_i` used in the splatting equation).

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input Images -> Shared ResNet Backbone + Multi-view Swin Transformer -> Fused Features (with Depth Anything V2) -> Multi-view Cost Volume -> Depth Maps -> Back-projection -> Initial 3D Point Cloud -> 2D U-Net Decoder -> Triangle Head (MLP) -> Per-vertex Attributes (opacity, SH color) -> Pixel-Aligned Face Connectivity -> Mesh -> Differentiable Rasterizer -> Rendered Images. Parallel path: External Depth Model -> Point Cloud -> Normalization -> L_points Loss.
*   **Critical path:** The primary data flow from images to rendered output traverses feature extraction, cost volume depth estimation, back-projection to vertices, attribute decoding, fixed face generation, and rasterization. The point cloud supervision path runs in parallel to stabilize vertex positions.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Pixel-level connectivity vs. learned/3D connectivity:** The fixed pixel rule (connecting grid neighbors) sacrifices potential geometric accuracy on complex surfaces for guaranteed manifoldness and training stability. The authors found learned (e.g., 3D KNN) or depth-edge pruning methods caused holes and instability.
    *   **External depth supervision vs. pure photometric:** Using a pretrained depth model (Depth Anything) adds a dependency but drastically improves geometric stability, as shown by the severe ablation failure. The tradeoff is between robustness with a prior and purity of learned representation.
    *   **Feed-forward speed vs. peak photorealism:** The method accepts a PSNR/SSIM gap compared to feed-forward Gaussian splatting (e.g., Mvsplat) to gain explicit, connected geometry and simulation readiness.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Without point cloud supervision:** Severe degradation (PSNR drop to ~13.06), visible blurring/artifacts, and most critically, **planar collapse** where surfaces lose 3D structure and appear stitched together (Fig. 6b).
    *   **Incorrect `λ_points` schedule:** If annealed too quickly, the network may revert to photo-fitting, reintroducing floating artifacts (like in feed-forward GS) but now on a connected mesh. If kept too high, the network overfits to noisy supervisory shape.
    *   **On highly occluded regions:** The pixel-rule may generate triangles in empty space where no true surface exists, leading to holes or incorrect geometry.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Comparison with optimization-based triangle methods (Table I, Fig. 3):** Validates the core claim: higher quality (PSNR 20.39 vs. 19.78) and connectivity in a *single forward pass* (0.17s) vs. minutes of optimization.
    2.  **Comparison with feed-forward Gaussian methods (Table II, Fig. 4):** Quantifies the cost of explicit geometry: lower photometric scores than Mvsplat/Depthsplat but demonstrates superior **3D spatial consistency** (no fog/floats), critical for robotics/simulation.
    3.  **Ablation on relative point cloud supervision (Table III, Figs. 5 & 6):** The *critical* experiment. Shows the absolute necessity of this signal for both rendering quality and fundamental 3D structure recovery. Also compares different depth model sources (Depth Anything V3 > VGGT).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   What alternative face connectivity strategies can improve geometric reconstruction in occluded regions compared to the current pixel-aligned approach?
*   Do the generated triangular meshes from FTSplat satisfy watertight and manifold properties, and how does this impact their direct use in physics-based simulators?
*   How does FTSplat's reconstruction quality and geometric consistency generalize to indoor scenes with complex geometries compared to the outdoor-focused RealEstate10K dataset?
*   How sensitive is FTSplat's reconstruction quality to the accuracy of external depth models used for point cloud supervision, and can robustness be improved?

## Limitations
*   Hyperparameters (channel sizes, network depths) and exact training schedule (λ_points decay, optimizer) are unspecified, requiring tuning for reproduction.
*   The pixel-aligned connectivity heuristic may fail on surfaces with extreme perspective distortion or in heavily occluded regions, leading to non-manifold edges or holes.
*   The method has a dependency on an external pretrained depth model (Depth Anything), whose errors can propagate and degrade geometry.

## Confidence
*   **High:** Point cloud supervision is essential (ablation shows catastrophic failure without it). SOTA among mesh-based methods with claimed speed (numbers align with reported comparisons).
*   **Medium:** Pixel-aligned mesh connectivity stabilizes training (implied by ablation success) but may break on complex topologies. Geometry-to-appearance curriculum is plausible but not directly ablated.
*   **Low:** Generalization to indoor scenes or sensitivity to depth model errors (no evidence provided beyond one model comparison).
