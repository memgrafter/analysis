# Rethinking Code Similarity for Automated Algorithm Design with LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.02787
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02787
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Integrating BehaveSim into LLM-AAD frameworks (FunSearch, EoH) improves performance on three tasks and successfully clusters AI-generated algorithms by behavioral logic where code similarity metrics (e.g., CodeBLEU) fail.

## Executive Summary
Existing code similarity metrics focus on surface-level syntax or output equivalence, but fail to capture algorithmic similarity in large language model-based automated algorithm design (LLM-AAD). This paper introduces BehaveSim, a method that measures similarity via problem-solving trajectories (PSTrajs)—sequences of intermediate solutions—using dynamic time warping (DTW). BehaveSim is integrated into LLM-AAD frameworks to promote behavioral diversity, leading to reduced gaps to best-known optima on three tasks: the Admissible Set Problem, Traveling Salesman Problem, and Circle Packing. Furthermore, BehaveSim clusters algorithms by underlying logical strategies, revealing distinctions missed by code-based metrics.

## Method Summary
BehaveSim represents an algorithm’s behavior as PSTraj, a sequence of intermediate solutions produced during iterative execution. For a given problem, a pairwise distance between intermediate solutions is defined (e.g., edit distance for permutations, Euclidean for vectors). DTW aligns two PSTrajs of possibly different lengths, and a normalized similarity score is computed. The final BehaveSim score averages this similarity over multiple problem instances and starting solutions. When integrated into LLM-AAD frameworks: in FunSearch+, new algorithms are assigned to islands with the most similar PSTrajs; in EoH+, BehaveSim acts as a second objective alongside fitness, encouraging diversity. This approach prevents premature convergence to syntactic variations of the same logic and enables discovery of novel algorithmic strategies.

## Key Results
- Performance gains: BehaveSim-integrated FunSearch and EoH consistently reduce relative gaps to best-known optima on the Admissible Set Problem, Traveling Salesman Problem, and Circle Packing.
- Behavioral clustering: BehaveSim successfully groups algorithms by logical behavior (e.g., nearest-neighbor vs. farthest-neighbor heuristics), while code similarity metrics like CodeBLEU fail to distinguish them.
- General utility: The method enhances search diversity and improves analysis of algorithmic strategies across multiple LLM-AAD frameworks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Mechanism 1**: Algorithms solving the same problem may produce syntactically similar code or final outputs but follow distinct computational paths, reflected in their PSTrajs. BehaveSim uses DTW to align these trajectories, capturing behavioral differences even when code or final solutions are similar. Break condition: the algorithm is non‑iterative or intermediate states are undefined/noisy.
- **Mechanism 2**: Static code metrics (token, AST, CodeBLEU) cannot detect divergence in problem‑solving logic. Because PSTrajs encode the progression of partial solutions, BehaveSim yields low similarity for algorithms that take different approaches (e.g., argmin vs. argmax selections) despite high code similarity. Break condition: intermediate solutions are not informative or converge trivially.
- **Mechanism 3**: Incorporating BehaveSim into LLM-AAD injects a behavioral diversity pressure. In FunSearch+, island assignment based on PSTraj similarity maintains coherent behavioral clusters; in EoH+, it serves as a dissimilarity objective. This prevents the population from collapsing into syntactic variants of a single strategy, leading to higher‑quality discoveries. Break condition: PSTraj computation is too costly or diversity pressure overwhelms fitness optimization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Problem‑Solving Trajectories (PSTrajs)**: PSTrajs are sequences of intermediate solutions that capture an algorithm’s progress. They are needed because behavioral similarity depends on the order and nature of partial states (e.g., a partial tour in TSP). Quick check: For Circle Packing, what constitutes a meaningful intermediate state—e.g., current circle placements or a partial packing score?
- **Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)**: DTW aligns two sequences of possibly different lengths by finding an optimal non‑linear mapping, handling temporal shifts and variable trajectory lengths. It is needed because mean pairwise distance would be biased by length differences. Quick check: Why would averaging pairwise edit distances fail for algorithms that take different numbers of steps to converge?
- **Iterative Algorithm Design in LLM‑AAD**: LLM‑AAD loops involve LLMs proposing code, a search routine evaluating it, and population management. BehaveSim fits as a diversity signal in this loop. Quick check: In FunSearch’s multi‑island database, how does using PSTraj similarity instead of syntactic similarity change the meaning of an “island”?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: PSTraj Extractor → Solution Pairwise Distance Module → Trajectory Similarity Engine (DTW) → BehaveSim Aggregator. Integration Adapter (FunSearch+ / EoH+) consumes the aggregated similarity to modify island assignment or selection.

Critical path: (1) Define PSTraj for the problem (what counts as an intermediate solution). (2) Implement PSTraj extractor and problem‑specific distance function. (3) Integrate DTW and aggregation (averaging over instances and starts). (4) Hook BehaveSim into the LLM‑AAD framework’s population manager. (5) Tune parameters (truncation `k`, sampling `n`).

Design tradeoffs: (i) Granularity vs. compute: longer PSTrajs are more accurate but costlier; truncation/sampling trade signal for speed. (ii) DTW vs. alternatives: DTW handles warping but is O(mn); mean pairwise is faster but less robust. (iii) Integration depth: FunSearch+ changes diversity maintenance; EoH+ changes diversity pressure. (iv) Stochasticity: sampling multiple trajectories is robust but costly; fixing seeds is cheap but possibly biased.

Failure signatures: (i) Invalid PSTraj: all extracted trajectories are identical or trivial (e.g., only final state). Check: variance in trajectories should be non‑zero. (ii) Shallow diversity: intra‑island distance remains high and inter‑island low; PSTraj definition likely not discriminative. (iii) Search collapse: top population BehaveSim values converge to ~1.0, indicating no exploration. (iv) Metric inversion: CodeBLEU clusters similarly but BehaveSim differs (expected) – the opposite (BehaveSim similar but clearly different) indicates a flawed pairwise distance.

First 3 experiments: (1) Replicate the discriminativity test on the Type 1‑4 dataset; verify Type‑3 pairs score >0.95 and Type‑4 <0.6. (2) Ablate inter‑island selection probability `ps1` in FunSearch+BehaveSim on ASP; expect `ps1=0.5` to outperform. (3) Perform hierarchical clustering on TSP final populations using BehaveSim vs. CodeBLEU; replicate Figure 5 to show behavioral separation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can BehaveSim be extended to incorporate other dimensions of algorithmic similarity, such as time and space complexity? The current formulation focuses solely on behavioral similarity via PSTrajs.
2. For stochastic algorithms, what is the most reliable way to compute pairwise distances between PSTrajs: sampling multiple solutions per step to approximate distributions, or fixing random seeds? The paper discusses both but provides no empirical comparison.
3. Does BehaveSim scale effectively to larger problem instances with higher‑dimensional solution spaces or longer trajectories? Its DTW‑based computation may become costly, and no scaling analysis is given.
4. What are the most effective strategies for integrating BehaveSim into LLM‑AAD frameworks beyond the niche‑based and multi‑objective approaches proposed? Alternative mechanisms (e.g., as a novelty score) remain unexplored.

## Limitations
- PSTraj definition and the distributions over problem instances and starting solutions are not fully specified, which may affect metric fidelity and reproducibility.
- Hyperparameters for PSTraj processing (truncation proportion `k`, sampling interval `n`) and the specific DTW variant used in main experiments are undisclosed.
- The protocol for handling stochastic algorithms (fixed seeds vs. sampling) is not empirically validated, leaving uncertainty about robustness.

## Confidence
- BehaveSim captures behavioral similarity (high evidence from curated dataset): **High**.
- Integration of BehaveSim improves LLM‑AAD performance (promising but sensitive to unspecified choices): **Medium**.
- Clustering with BehaveSim reveals logical strategies (compelling figures but depends on correct PSTraj extraction): **Medium**.
