# VFIG: Vectorizing Complex Figures in SVG with Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24575
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24575
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: VFIG achieves state-of-the-art among open-source models for SVG vectorization, matching GPT-5.2 (VLM-Judge score 0.829).

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of converting rasterized figures into editable, high-fidelity SVG code. They propose VFIG, a vision-language model trained via a two-stage curriculum: first supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a coarse-to-fine schedule to learn primitives and composition, then reinforcement learning (RL) with rendering-aware rewards to optimize global layout, connectivity, and detail fidelity. The authors also introduce VFIG-DATA (66K figure-SVG pairs) and VFIG-BENCH with novel structure-aware metrics. VFIG outperforms existing open-source baselines and matches GPT-5.2 according to a VLM-Judge, with significant gains in perceptual similarity (LPIPS, VisualSim), SVG cleanliness, and rendering success. RL consistently improves structural and geometric quality over SFT alone.

## Method Summary
VFIG employs a vision-language model that takes a raster figure as input and generates SVG code as a token sequence. Training follows a curriculum: initially, SFT on a coarse-to-fine schedule teaches the model to generate basic primitives (lines, shapes) and then combine them into coherent structures. After SFT, RL fine-tuning is applied using rewards from a rendering-aware evaluator (Gemini-based) that scores the rendered SVG against the input image across four equally weighted components: global layout, connectivity, detail fidelity, and overall quality. The reward signal guides the model to adjust its outputs for better visual and structural alignment. Additionally, the authors curate VFIG-DATA with 66K figure-SVG pairs covering scientific diagrams and propose VFIG-BENCH, which includes structure-aware metrics for evaluation.

## Key Results
- VFIG achieves SOTA among open-source models, matching GPT-5.2 (VLM-Judge score 0.829).
- Shows significant improvements in LPIPS, VisualSim, SVG cleanliness, and rendering success over baselines.
- RL phase consistently enhances structural and geometric quality compared to SFT alone.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The two-stage training regimen is key. SFT on a coarse-to-fine schedule establishes a strong prior for generating syntactically correct SVG by first mastering individual primitives and then their composition, preventing the model from being overwhelmed by complex structures early on. RL then optimizes higher-level, non-differentiable properties using a rendering-aware reward computed by a powerful vision-language model (Gemini). This reward evaluates the final rendered image, focusing on global layout, connectivity, and fine details, thereby aligning the output with human perceptual expectations. The large-scale VFIG-DATA provides diverse examples of scientific diagrams, enabling the model to generalize across various structural patterns. The VLM-Judge, being correlated with human judgment, supplies a nuanced and robust learning signal that bridges the gap between token-level accuracy and visual fidelity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**: Needed to jointly understand the input raster image and generate structured SVG as text. Quick check: Can the VLM accurately parse image content and produce hierarchical descriptions?
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Needed to teach the fundamental mapping from images to SVG primitives and their composition in a controlled, stepwise manner. Quick check: Does SFT on a coarse-to-fine schedule improve primitive generation accuracy?
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Custom Rewards**: Needed to optimize non-differentiable, global properties (layout, connectivity, detail) that are hard to capture with standard losses. Quick check: Does RL improve metrics like LPIPS and rendering success over SFT alone?
- **Rendering-Aware Evaluation**: Needed because the ultimate goal is a visually faithful SVG; evaluating via rendering closes the loop between token-level generation and visual quality. Quick check: Does the VLM-Judge correlate with human evaluations of SVG quality?
- **Curriculum Learning**: Needed to gradually increase complexity, allowing the model to build skills without being overwhelmed. Quick check: Does a coarse-to-fine curriculum outperform a flat training schedule?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Input Raster Image -> Vision Encoder (e.g., CLIP) -> Language Decoder (Transformer) -> SVG Token Sequence -> Rendered Output (for RL)  
Reward Branch: Rendered Output -> VLM-Judge (Gemini) -> Reward Scores

**Critical Path**  
During inference, the image is encoded and the decoder generates SVG tokens autoregressively. During training, SFT uses standard cross-entropy loss on token sequences; RL uses policy gradient (e.g., PPO) where the reward is computed from the VLM-Judge after rendering the generated SVG.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- VLM-based reward is computationally heavy and may introduce judge bias.  
- RL with non-differentiable rewards can be unstable and requires careful hyperparameter tuning.  
- Coarse-to-fine SFT might limit early exposure to full composites, potentially slowing convergence on complex structures.  
- Model capacity vs. dataset size: 66K pairs may be modest for a large VLM, risking underfitting or limited diversity.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Inaccurate local details: thin lines, arrowheads, small text.  
- Incorrect global layout: misaligned components, broken connectivity.  
- Over-generation of paths or unnecessary elements.  
- Poor generalization to non-diagram graphics (icons, sketches).  
- RL sometimes degrades performance if reward components are misaligned.

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. Ablate the coarse-to-fine SFT schedule by training a model with a flat SFT curriculum; compare primitive accuracy and composite structure metrics.  
2. Replace the VLM-Judge with a pixel-level loss (e.g., MSE between rendered and input images); assess impact on structural quality and detail fidelity.  
3. Evaluate VFIG on an external dataset of logos and hand-drawn sketches; measure performance drop to identify distribution shift.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization Beyond Diagrams**: How can VFIG generalize to vector graphics beyond complex scientific diagrams (e.g., icons, logos, sketches), and what data or architectural modifications are needed?
- **RL Reward Design**: Can alternative RL reward designs—such as different VLM judges, adjusted component weights, or differentiable rendering losses—improve fine-grained structural fidelity without harming global layout?
- **Local Detail Errors**: What architectural or training changes can address VFIG's persistent errors in fine-grained local details (e.g., thin lines, arrowheads, small text) while maintaining global structure?
- **Scaling to Proprietary VLMs**: What are the main bottlenecks in scaling VFIG to match proprietary VLMs like GPT-5.2, and how do model size, data diversity, and reward design interact to limit performance?

## Limitations
- Dataset diversity and real-world complexity of VFIG-DATA (66K pairs) are uncertain; no detailed breakdown of figure types is provided.
- Evaluation relies on an automatic VLM-Judge without human validation, leaving its correlation with true human preference unverified.
- The RL ablation study shows improvement but does not fully explain *why* RL enhances layout/connectivity; failure modes of SFT alone are not deeply analyzed.

## Confidence
- **SOTA claim (open-source)**: Medium  
- **RL efficacy over SFT**: Medium  
- **Dataset/Benchmark utility**: Low
