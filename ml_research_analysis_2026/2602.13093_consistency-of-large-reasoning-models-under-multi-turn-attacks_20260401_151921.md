# Consistency of Large Reasoning Models Under Multi-Turn Attacks
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13093  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13093  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Extended reasoning traces provide meaningful but incomplete robustness to multi-turn adversarial attacks; however, reasoning-induced overconfidence breaks standard confidence-based defenses like CARG.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates nine frontier large reasoning models against a structured 8-turn adversarial attack protocol. It finds that reasoning models significantly outperform a non-reasoning baseline (GPT-4o) in maintaining consistent correct answers across attacks, as measured by Position-Weighted Consistency (PWC). However, all models exhibit distinct vulnerability profiles: misleading suggestions are universally effective, while social pressure shows model-specific efficacy. Trajectory analysis identifies five failure modes, with Self-Doubt and Social Conformity accounting for half of all failures. Crucially, the Confidence-Aware Response Generation (CARG) defense, effective for standard LLMs, fails for reasoning models because extended reasoning traces induce systematic overconfidence, destroying the correlation between confidence scores and answer correctness. Counterintuitively, random confidence embedding outperforms targeted extraction, indicating confidence-based defenses require fundamental redesign for this model class.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses the MT-Consistency dataset of 700 factual multiple-choice questions. For each model, initially correct responses are subjected to a randomized sequence of eight attack types over eight follow-up turns. Model responses are parsed to track answer correctness per round. Primary robustness is measured by Position-Weighted Consistency (PWC), which discounts correctness by turn order. For CARG defense experiments, a confidence score is extracted from each response (via the `answer_only` method) and embedded into the conversation history for the next turn. Trajectory analysis classifies flip patterns and attributes failures to cognitive modes based on response content.

## Key Results
- Reasoning models (8 of 9) significantly outperform GPT-4o in PWC, with effect sizes d=0.12–0.40.
- All models exhibit distinct vulnerability profiles; misleading suggestions (A3) are universally effective, while social pressure attacks show model-specific efficacy.
- The CARG defense fails for reasoning models; confidence scores show poor calibration (point-biserial r=0.07, ROC-AUC=0.57) and a compressed distribution (mean ~93.5%, SD 4.4%). Random confidence embedding outperforms targeted extraction.
- Five failure modes are identified: Self-Doubt (16.7%), Social Conformity (33.3%), Suggestion Hijacking, Emotional Susceptibility, and Reasoning Fatigue, with the first two accounting for 50% of failures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Reasoning Trace Anchoring:** Extended chain-of-thought generation creates a coherent internal reference that resists arbitrary answer revision under social pressure. The stable scratchpad effect is supported by 8/9 models showing higher PWC vs. baseline, but fails when attacks directly target reasoning premises (e.g., Suggestion Hijacking).
- **Reasoning-Induced Overconfidence:** Fluent, lengthy reasoning traces inflate subjective confidence independently of accuracy, compressing the confidence distribution and breaking the confidence-correctness correlation required by defenses like CARG. Evidence: r=0.07, ROC-AUC=0.57.
- **Attack-Type-Specific Vulnerability Profiles:** Different attacks exploit distinct cognitive shortcuts (e.g., low-effort bypass for Misleading Suggestions, heuristic deference for Social Pressure). Models show heterogeneous weakness polygons, indicating robustness is multidimensional and requires attack-specific mitigation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Position-Weighted Consistency (PWC)**  
  *Why needed:* Primary metric for multi-turn robustness; penalizes early flips more severely than average accuracy, distinguishing catastrophic early failure from transient wavering.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a model with high average accuracy still be considered fragile under multi-turn attack? (Answer: If it flips early and often, PWC will be low despite occasional recoveries.)
- **Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) & Inference-Time Scaling**  
  *Why needed:* Defines the key architectural difference between reasoning models and the baseline; the source of hypothesized anchoring (Mechanism 1) and overconfidence (Mechanism 2).  
  *Quick check:* What is a potential downside of verbose CoT in adversarial multi-turn settings? (Answer: It can induce systematic overconfidence that breaks confidence-based defenses.)
- **Concept: Confidence Calibration & CARG**  
  *Why needed:* Central to the paper’s third contribution—the failure of a state-of-the-art defense. Understanding that CARG relies on well-calibrated confidence (high confidence → high accuracy) is essential.  
  *Quick check:* Why does the `answer_only` confidence extraction method still fail for reasoning models? (Answer: Overconfidence is inherent to the reasoning process itself, contaminating even the final answer token’s signal.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Dataset Loader -> Initial Inference Filter -> Attack Permuter -> Model Inference Pipeline (with History) -> Response Evaluator (Correctness) -> Confidence Extractor (for CARG) -> Trajectory Analyzer -> Metrics Aggregator -> Statistical Test Suite

**Critical path**  
1. Input 700 factual MC questions.  
2. Run initial inference; filter to keep only initially correct responses.  
3. For each retained question, generate random attack sequence (A1–A8). Sequentially append each attack, call model, parse response, evaluate correctness.  
4. After 8 rounds, compute PWC (with discount γ), classify flip pattern/failure mode. For CARG, re-run with confidence scores embedded in history.  
5. Aggregate metrics; compare reasoning models to GPT-4o baseline via Welch’s t-test on PWC.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Attack diversity vs. runtime: 8 random attacks per question increases validity but multiplies API calls (~14–55 hours).  
- Confidence extraction fidelity vs. reasoning interference: `answer_only` aims to isolate signal but reasoning process itself contaminates confidence; more invasive extraction might alter behavior.  
- Trajectory classification granularity: Manual failure mode coding provides nuance but introduces subjectivity vs. automated clustering.

**Failure signatures**  
- High Oscillation rate → Reasoning Fatigue.  
- Low PWC despite high initial accuracy → Catastrophic early failure (e.g., to Misleading Suggestion).  
- Poor confidence-correctness correlation (r≈0) → Reasoning-induced overconfidence; CARG will fail.  
- Spiky vulnerability polygon (Figure 1) → Attack-specific weaknesses requiring targeted mitigation.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Reproduce primary robustness result: Run full protocol on GPT-5.1 vs. GPT-4o; confirm significant PWC advantage (d>0.1).  
2. Attack-specific profiling: Isolate flip rates per attack type (A1–A8); verify A3 (Misleading Suggestion) is universally high and identify the model’s weakest attack.  
3. CARG failure reproduction: Compare `answer_only`, `overall`, and `random` CARG conditions; verify `random` outperforms `answer_only` and that confidence correlation is low (r≈0.07).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Do architectural or training differences (e.g., RLHF vs. SFT) explain distinct vulnerability profiles (e.g., Claude-4.5’s instability, Grok-4.1’s suggestion hijacking)? The study compares models as black boxes without ablating training factors.
- **Open Question 2:** Can targeted interventions (adversarial fine-tuning, prompt engineering) mitigate dominant failure modes (Self-Doubt, Social Conformity) without degrading general reasoning? The paper diagnoses but does not test interventions.
- **Open Question 3:** Why does extended reasoning induce overconfidence, and how can confidence be reliably elicited? The paper rules out `answer_only` extraction but does not probe the mechanistic cause or alternative estimators (e.g., self-consistency).
- **Open Question 4:** Do vulnerabilities generalize beyond factual multiple-choice to open-ended generation, tool-augmented reasoning, or ambiguous domains? The evaluation is limited to objectively verifiable answers.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Exact MT-Consistency dataset split (700 questions) and precise model API configurations (version, sampling parameters) are unspecified, blocking exact replication.
- **Mechanistic evidence:** Proposed cognitive mechanisms (anchoring, heuristic triggers) are inferred from aggregate patterns and qualitative coding, not directly tested; corpus support is weak.
- **Generalizability:** Findings are restricted to 9 frontier models on factual MC QA; transfer to open-ended, interactive, or ambiguous tasks is unknown.

## Next Checks
1. **Controlled replication of primary robustness:** Run full protocol on a newly constructed 700-question subset from MMLU/CommonsenseQA/TruthfulQA; confirm PWC advantage for reasoning models survives dataset reconstruction.
2. **Isolate reasoning process from answer confidence:** Compare calibration (r, AUC) across conditions: (a) standard CoT, (b) forced "answer only" (no reasoning), (c) "reasoning then forced confidence statement." Test if overconfidence is inherent to reasoning vs. extraction artifact.
3. **Automate failure mode taxonomy:** Implement an automatic classifier for the 5 failure modes using response text embeddings; measure agreement with human annotations on held-out set to assess taxonomy objectivity.
