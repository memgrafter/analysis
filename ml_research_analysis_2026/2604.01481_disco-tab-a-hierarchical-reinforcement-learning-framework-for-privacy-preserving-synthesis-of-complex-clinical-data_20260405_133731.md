# DISCO-TAB: A Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning Framework for Privacy-Preserving Synthesis of Complex Clinical Data

## Quick Facts
*   **ArXiv ID:** 2604.01481
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01481
*   **Reference Count:** 32
*   **Primary Result:** Achieves up to 38.2% improvement in clinical classifier performance (TSTR F1) over GAN/Diffusion baselines while maintaining statistical fidelity (JSD < 0.01) and strong privacy (low DCR).

## Executive Summary
DISCO-TAB is a framework for generating high-fidelity, privacy-preserving synthetic Electronic Health Record (EHR) data. It addresses the dual challenges of modeling complex, non-linear feature dependencies and severe class imbalances common in clinical datasets. The method fine-tunes an LLM (GPT-2) as a generator and employs a hierarchical reinforcement learning (RL) loop with a multi-granular discriminator ensemble. This ensemble evaluates synthetic data at token, sentence, feature-pair, and entire-row levels. An automated constraint discovery process identifies critical feature correlations to preserve, while an inverse-frequency reward shaping (IFRS) mechanism specifically combats minority-class collapse. The result is synthetic data that maintains exceptional statistical and clinical validity, demonstrably improving downstream task performance compared to prior state-of-the-art approaches.

## Method Summary
The training procedure begins by linearizing tabular EHR records into fixed-order token sequences (e.g., `"[Feature] is [Value]"`). A GPT-2 backbone is fine-tuned on these sequences. Concurrently, automated constraint discovery computes global feature correlations (using Pearson, Cramér’s V, etc.) to identify a critical set `P_crit` of feature pairs whose relationships must be preserved. A four-level discriminator ensemble is constructed: a BiLSTM (`D_token`) for token syntax, a BERT-base model (`D_sent`) for feature-value clause semantics, an MLP (`D_feat`) on pooled embeddings for pairs in `P_crit`, and an MLP (`D_row`) on mean-pooled sequence embeddings for global coherence. The generator is then optimized via PPO using a composite reward that aggregates scores from all discriminators. This raw reward is scaled by an IFRS weight `W_y` derived from the inverse frequency of the record's class label `y` to boost learning on rare classes. Discriminators and generator are trained in alternating epochs with KL regularization against the reference policy to prevent mode collapse.

## Key Results
*   **Utility:** Achieves state-of-the-art downstream task performance (TSTR F1-score), with up to a 38.2% relative improvement over GAN and Diffusion baselines on clinical classifiers.
*   **Fidelity:** Maintains exceptionally low statistical divergence from real data, with a Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) below 0.01 on benchmark datasets.
*   **Privacy & Imbalance:** Demonstrates robust resistance to membership inference attacks (low DCR) and successfully mitigates minority-class mode collapse, preserving rare clinical events.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework succeeds by combining two synergistic innovations. First, the **hierarchical multi-granular discriminator ensemble** provides dense, multi-level feedback that guides the generator toward producing syntactically valid tokens, semantically plausible clauses, statistically correlated feature pairs, and globally coherent records—preventing the "local validity but global nonsense" failure mode common in text-based generation. Second, the **Inverse-Frequency Reward Shaping (IFRS)** directly intervenes in the RL objective to up-weight the reward for generating records belonging to rare classes. This counteracts the natural tendency of likelihood-based models to ignore minority modes, ensuring the synthetic data retains the full spectrum of clinical phenomenon, including low-prevalence diagnoses and outcomes.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Reinforcement Learning (PPO) for Text Generation
    *   **Why needed:** Provides a stable policy gradient method to optimize a non-differentiable composite reward from multiple discriminators, avoiding the mode collapse of standard maximum likelihood estimation.
    *   **Quick check:** Verify PPO clip parameter (`ϵ`) and KL penalty weight (`β`) are tuned to balance reward improvement with policy stability; monitor the KL divergence between current and reference policy during training.
*   **Concept:** Multi-Granular Discriminator Design
    *   **Why needed:** Single-discriminator approaches provide coarse feedback that cannot diagnose specific failure modes (e.g., a valid sentence that violates a critical feature correlation). Granular evaluation enforces validity at every level of abstraction.
    *   **Quick check:** Ablate each discriminator level and measure the drop in specific fidelity metrics (e.g., remove `D_feat` and observe increased JSD on correlated feature pairs).
*   **Concept:** Inverse-Frequency Reward Shaping (IFRS)
    *   **Why needed:** Directly combats the optimizer's bias toward majority classes in imbalanced data by scaling the final reward based on class rarity, without altering the data distribution.
    *   **Quick check:** Without IFRS (`α=0`), evaluate per-class F1-scores in TSTR; minority-class performance should significantly drop compared to the full model.
*   **Concept:** Automated Constraint Discovery
    *   **Why needed:** Manually specifying all critical clinical correlations (e.g., between lab values and diagnoses) is infeasible. Automated discovery from real data `D_real` initializes the `D_feat` discriminator with domain-specific logic.
    *   **Quick check:** Inspect the top-*k* discovered pairs `P_crit` for known clinical relationships (e.g., `creatinine` and `kidney disease`). A sensitive `δ_thresh` should yield plausible medical correlations.
*   **Concept:** FAITH Framework for Hallucination Detection
    *   **Why needed:** LLMs are prone to generating factually incorrect or logically inconsistent values ("ghost features"). FAITH provides a multi-component metric (Factuality, Alignment, Integrity, Tracking) to quantify and mitigate this.
    *   **Quick check:** Compute FAITH scores on a held-out real dataset. Synthetic data should have high Alignment (value distribution match) and Integrity (no impossible values), and low Tracking (low temporal drift if data is sequential).

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Real EHR Data -> Schema-Aware Linearization -> GPT-2 Generator (π_θ) & Constraint Discovery (P_crit) -> [Token Sequence] -> D_token, D_sent, D_feat (on P_crit), D_row -> Aggregated Score R_raw -> * IFRS Weight W_y -> R_total -> PPO Update -> π_θ. Discriminators are trained in parallel on real/synthetic batches at their respective granularities.

**Critical Path:**
1.  **Preprocessing:** Linearization must be deterministic and schema-consistent. This is the foundational data transformation; any error here propagates irrecoverably.
2.  **Constraint Discovery:** The selection of `P_crit` directs `D_feat`'s focus. The threshold `δ_thresh` and top-*k* logic are critical hyperparameters that determine which clinical logic is explicitly enforced.
3.  **RL Loop:** The alternating update cycle between discriminators (to improve discrimination) and generator (via PPO to maximize composite reward) is the core optimization engine. IFRS is applied at reward computation, immediately before PPO.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Discriminator Strength vs. Generator Collapse:** Overly powerful discriminators can provide sparse, near-binary rewards, making PPO unstable. The use of sigmoid outputs and aggregate weighting (`λ_k`) attempts to smooth this.
*   **Constraint Coverage vs. Overfitting:** `P_crit` must capture essential correlations without being so restrictive that the generator cannot learn novel, valid combinations. The `k` parameter controls this coverage.
*   **Privacy (KL) vs. Utility:** The KL regularization penalty (`β`) prevents the generator from straying too far from the real data manifold (protecting privacy) but if too high, it can also stifle learning of novel valid records (hurting utility).

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **Local Validity, Global Incoherence:** `D_token` and `D_sent` scores are high early in training, but `D_row` score remains low. Generated records consist of plausible sentences that are nonsensical when combined.
*   **Persistent Minority-Class Collapse:** Despite IFRS, the synthetic data's class distribution remains skewed. Check if `α` is too low or if `W_y` calculation is incorrect (e.g., wrong `N_y` or `|Y|`).
*   **Numerical Degradation:** Categorical feature fidelity (JSD) is excellent, but continuous feature JSD is high (>0.09). Suggests `D_feat` is not effectively penalizing inaccurate continuous correlations or the linearization format blurs numerical precision.
*   **Excessive DCR Spike:** DCR increases sharply while F1 plateaus or drops. Indicates over-regularization (high `β`) or discriminator overfitting, pushing synthetic records too far from real ones.

**First Experiments:**
1.  **Linearization Sanity Check:** Apply the schema-aware linearization to a small sample of real records. Visually and programmatically verify that each row produces a consistent, canonical token sequence with correct handling of missing values (`[MISSING]`) and categorical IDs.
2.  **Constraint Discovery Probe:** Run the discovery process on a benchmark dataset (e.g., Heart Failure). Manually inspect the top-10 pairs in `P_crit`. Do they correspond to known, clinically meaningful correlations? Vary `δ_thresh` and `k` to assess sensitivity and stability of the discovered set.
3.  **IFRS Ablation Study:** Train a full DISCO-TAB model and an ablated version (`α=0`) on a dataset with a known, severe class imbalance (e.g., rare diagnosis). Compare the synthetic data's class distribution and, crucially, the TSTR F1-score *per class*. The ablated model should show a significant drop in minority-class F1.

## Limitations
*   **Specification Gaps:** The exact GPT-2 variant (base vs. medium), complete PPO hyperparameters (KL weight `β`, value network learning rate, number of PPO epochs per update), and the MLP architectures for `D_feat`/`D_row` are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
*   **Implementation Risk:** No official code is released. A full reimplementation from the methodological description carries a high risk of subtle divergence in the data pipelines (e.g., discriminator input construction) or reward aggregation logic.
*   **Parameter Sensitivity:** The performance is likely sensitive to the constraint discovery parameters (`δ_thresh`, `k`) and the IFRS scaling factor `α`. Their optimal values may be dataset-dependent and were not extensively studied in the provided summary.

## Confidence
*   **High Confidence:** The conceptual design of a hierarchical multi-granular discriminator ensemble and the use of inverse-frequency reward shaping for class imbalance are logically sound and well-described.
*   **Medium Confidence:** The reported state-of-the-art utility gains (38.2% improvement) and ultra-low statistical fidelity (JSD < 0.01) are plausible given the framework's intended effects but depend on correct tuning of the unspecified hyperparameters and faithful implementation.
*   **Low Confidence:** The exact numerical values for privacy metrics (DCR), detailed breakdowns of fidelity per feature type, and the generalizability of the automated constraint discovery process cannot be verified without the missing implementation specifics or code.
