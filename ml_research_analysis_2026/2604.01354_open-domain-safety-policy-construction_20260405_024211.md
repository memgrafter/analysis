# Open-Domain Safety Policy Construction

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2604.01354
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01354
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** DPR automates policy drafting from a one-sentence spec, improving moderation F1 by up to 4 percentage points across multiple domains and recovering most human policy benefit on an in-house ad benchmark.

## Executive Summary
DPR (Deep Policy Research) is a minimal agent that constructs domain-specific safety policies by iteratively searching the web and distilling results into structured rule predicates, organized via keyphrase clustering into an indexed document. Starting from a single-sentence domain specification, DPR generates targeted queries, retrieves web snippets, extracts and consolidates rules through self-critique, and clusters them into thematic sections. On the OpenAI undesired content benchmark across five domains, DPR boosts moderation F1 significantly over definition-only, in-context learning, and a general deep research baseline when using Llama 3.1 8B and Qwen2.5 7B as reader models. On an internal multimodal advertisement benchmark, DPR-generated sections achieve near-parity with human-crafted policies under majority voting. Human evaluations confirm the generated rules are clear, actionable, and relevant to the domain.

## Method Summary
DPR operates in iterative cycles. Given a seed specification, a research LLM generates queries aimed at definitional gaps and high-risk subtypes. A search tool retrieves top results, from which the LLM extracts candidate rules in a predicate style. A self-critique step removes irrelevant/generic rules, merges redundancies, and resolves conflicts. The accumulating rule set is indexed via keyphrase clustering, producing sections with titles and summaries. After a fixed number of iterations (e.g., three), the final indexed document is supplied in-context to a fixed reader LLM, which performs binary moderation classification. The entire system uses a single LLM for generation and a single search tool, minimizing complexity while harnessing web-scale knowledge.

## Key Results
- DPR improves moderation F1 on OpenAI domains: +0.040 (Llama 3.1 8B) and +0.021 (Qwen2.5 7B) over strong baselines.
- On in-house ad benchmark, DPR achieves average F1 0.75 (vs. 0.68 no policy) and 98% of human policy performance on the Misrepresentative category under majority voting.
- Human evaluation: DPR-generated rules score highly on clarity, actionability, and domain relevance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** DPR likely improves moderation by harvesting diverse, real-world examples and edge cases from the web that are absent in a seed specification or few-shot examples.
- **Mechanism:** The iterative query generation step targets definitional gaps and high-risk subtypes. The retrieved web snippets provide concrete instances of policy violations, which the rule extraction step abstracts into predicate-style statements with qualifiers.
- **Core assumption:** Publicly available web content contains a sufficiently dense and relevant signal of normative content boundaries for the target moderation domain.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "...iteratively searches the web and distills results into structured policy rules..."
  - Section 3.2: "Step 1: Query generation... targets definitional boundaries, common edge cases, high-risk subtypes..."
- **Break condition:** If the target domain's boundary conventions are not publicly documented or are heavily proprietary (e.g., Finance Claims), this mechanism yields limited returns.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The self-critique and consolidation step likely reduces noise and redundancy, producing a cleaner, more consistent rule set than raw extraction.
- **Mechanism:** After initial extraction, DPR prompts the LLM to remove irrelevant/generic rules, merge redundant ones, and resolve conflicts by preferring multi-source support. This filters out spurious correlations and synthesizes overlapping evidence.
- **Core assumption:** An LLM can effectively judge the relevance, specificity, and consistency of policy rules relative to the domain goal, and can merge them without losing critical nuance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 3.2: "Step 2: Rule extraction and consolidation... self-critique pass to improve precision and reduce noise... removes irrelevant or overly generic rules, merges redundant rules..."
  - Section B.3: Qualitative examples show DPR performs "significant rephrasing and abstraction operations over the raw webpage information."
- **Break condition:** If retrieval yields predominantly low-quality or contradictory sources, consolidation may amplify noise or create over-generalized rules, especially in subjective domains (e.g., Harassment, Self-Harm).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Keyphrase-based clustering and indexing likely improves downstream utility by presenting rules in a structured, thematic format that is easier for a reader LLM to parse and apply than a flat list.
- **Mechanism:** Clustering rules by keyphrases creates coherent sections with summaries and titles. This organizes the policy document, potentially reducing cognitive load and improving the reader model's ability to locate relevant decision boundaries.
- **Core assumption:** Reader LLMs benefit from hierarchical, semantically grouped policy documents when performing in-context classification, especially for longer policies.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 3.2: "Step 3: Indexing... uses keyphrase-based clustering... improve readability and help reader models consume long policies."
  - Section B.1: "Indexing Strategy... Compare three ways of presenting... Summaries cut prompt length but come with a small accuracy drop. The indexed rules... achieves the best performance on Llama 3.1 8B Instruct."
- **Break condition:** If the reader model has strong long-context capabilities, the benefit of indexing may diminish, as a flat list performs similarly.

  Why needed here: The paper's core evaluation is the F1 score of a fixed reader LLM using the generated policy. This requires understanding that policy value is instrumental and model-dependent.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Seed specification → Research LLM (M) generates queries → Search Tool (G) retrieves results → M extracts/consolidates rules → Updates Policy Draft (P_i) → M indexes into (I_i) → (I_i) used by Reader LLM for moderation. Loop repeats for k iterations; final I_k is output.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Initialize P_0 = I_0 = seed specification s.  
  2. For i in 1..k:  
     a. Analyze I_{i-1} → generate queries Q_i.  
     b. For each q in Q_i: G(q) → retrieve → extract candidate rules from chunks.  
     c. Self-critique & merge candidates → consolidated rule set R_i.  
     d. P_i ← P_{i-1} ∪ R_i.  
     e. Index P_i into I_i via keyphrase clustering, naming, summarization, and title merging.  
  3. Output final I_k as policy P.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Simplicity vs. Expressiveness: Single tool (search) and lightweight scaffolding vs. potential limitations in multi-hop reasoning about policy.  
  - Structured Output vs. Natural Language: Enforcing JSON schemas and clustering ensures consistency but may constrain complex conditional policies.  
  - Clustering Granularity: Fixed n=20 clusters is heuristic; too broad or fragmented sections can hurt usability; authors merge overlapping semantics to mitigate.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Early convergence plateau: >74% of rules found in iteration 1; diminishing returns after iteration 2; running k=3 justified.  
  - Domain-specific failure: Finance Claims shows poor recovery; signature: domain relies on proprietary standards/legal disclaimers not well-represented on open web.  
  - Human evaluation variance: Lower Fleiss' κ for "Internal Usability" and "Relevance" in some domains indicates rules are clear in isolation but ambiguous in organizational context.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Reproduce main result: Run DPR on Violence domain with Llama 3.3 70B researcher and Llama 3.1 8B reader; verify final F1 surpasses Seed Information and In-Context baselines.  
  2. Test indexing impact: For a fixed P_k, generate three presentations (full indexed document, flat list, summary-only); evaluate each with reader models of varying context lengths to replicate indexing benefit findings.  
  3. Analyze source diversity: Log all retrieved URLs; categorize source domains (.gov, .edu, news, forums); verify pattern of formal sources for OpenAI domains vs. marketing sources for ad domains; check correlation with rule quality in human eval.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How should policy documents be formatted and structured to maximize moderation accuracy across different downstream LLM reader architectures, and does the current indexing strategy generalize beyond the tested models?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state that DPR "provides a reproducible environment for future work on policy drafting agents, including reader-model-specific policy presentation."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper evaluated only three presentation formats and only two reader models; optimal format likely depends on reader-specific factors like context length, attention patterns, and reasoning capabilities, which were not systematically varied.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct controlled experiments using a diverse set of reader models (different sizes, architectures, context windows) while varying policy formatting dimensions (summarization level, hierarchical depth, example inclusion). Measure moderation accuracy and interpretability to identify format-reader interactions.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can generating illustrative examples alongside rule predicates clarify decision boundaries and significantly improve the downstream utility of automatically created policies?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors list "generating illustrative examples alongside rule predicates to clarify decision boundaries" as a specific direction for future work.
- **Why unresolved:** DPR currently produces only textual rule predicates without accompanying examples. The paper does not evaluate whether adding examples would enhance policy clarity for human reviewers or moderation accuracy for LLM readers.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend DPR to produce both rules and a set of positive/negative examples per rule during the distillation phase. Compare downstream moderation performance and human assessments between predicate-only and predicate+example versions across multiple domains.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does DPR underperform in domains that rely on proprietary or organization-specific standards (e.g., Finance Claims), and how can the system be adapted to incorporate non-public knowledge without sacrificing its open-domain, web-based approach?
- **Basis in paper:** Results show Finance Claims remains an outlier, with DPR failing to reach human policy performance. The domain "relies heavily on organization-specific compliance language and fine-grained disclaimer patterns, which are difficult to recover from open web sources."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not explore whether hybrid approaches (e.g., limited internal document access, expert feedback loops) or targeted query strategies could bridge the gap while maintaining the minimal-tool philosophy.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run DPR with controlled access to synthetic or real proprietary policy fragments, or with iterative expert validation steps. Measure improvements in Finance Claims and similar domains to determine necessary conditions for matching human expertise.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How well does DPR handle policy evolution over time, and can the research loop autonomously detect and incorporate updates as content trends and moderation guidelines change?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper frames policy construction as a one-shot generation from a static seed specification. No evaluation considers temporal dynamics, yet real-world policies require frequent updates.
- **Why unresolved:** Unknown whether DPR’s iterative search-and-distill naturally discovers emerging topics or produces redundant rules when applied to an existing draft. Stability across time and sensitivity to evolving web content remain unstudied.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Simulate policy aging by providing DPR with an outdated initial draft and a stream of newer web data, then assess whether generated updates align with ground-truth policy revisions from experts. Alternatively, run DPR periodically on the same domain over real time and measure coverage drift.

## Limitations
- Generalizability to proprietary/less-documented domains (e.g., Finance Claims underperformance) due to reliance on publicly available normative documentation.
- Dependence on public web content quality and availability; low-quality or biased sources can introduce noise.
- Reliability of LLM self-critique in subjective domains (Harassment, Self-Harm) may be limited, risking over-generalization or conflict resolution errors.
- Human evaluation variance indicates contextual usability gaps; rules may be clear in isolation but ambiguous for specific organizational application.
- Limited external validation (low neighbor citations) suggests the findings need broader community verification.

## Confidence
- Main F1 improvement claim: **Medium** (strong in 5/6 domains, fails in Finance Claims).
- Web diversity mechanism: **Medium** (effective only when public normative documentation exists).
- Self-critique consolidation: **Medium** (LLM-dependent, risks noise amplification in subjective domains).
- Indexing structure: **Medium** (benefit model-dependent; diminishes for long-context readers).
- Foundational concepts (policy-as-KB, downstream eval): **High** conceptually, **Medium** in practical application risks.
