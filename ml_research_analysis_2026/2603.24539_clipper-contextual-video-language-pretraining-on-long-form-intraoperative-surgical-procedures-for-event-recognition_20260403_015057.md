# CliPPER: Contextual Video-Language Pretraining on Long-form Intraoperative Surgical Procedures for Event Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24539
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24539
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art zero-shot surgical event recognition, e.g., phase recognition F1 36.8% (+14.4% abs) over prior work, step F1 14.0% (+5.3%), triplet mAP 6.9% (+60.5% rel).

## Executive Summary
CliPPER is a video-language pretraining framework designed to address the challenges of zero-shot surgical event recognition in long-form intraoperative procedures. The scarcity of labeled data and the need for fine-grained temporal understanding motivate a pretraining approach that leverages large-scale, weakly-supervised surgical lecture videos and online videos. CliPPER introduces a dual-encoder architecture with a temporal video encoder and separate context encoders to model cross-clip dependencies across hour-long surgeries. Four novel pretraining objectives are proposed: Contextual Video-Text Contrastive Learning (aligns clips with text conditioned on context), Cycle-Consistency Alignment (reduces many-to-one ambiguities), Clip Order Prediction (captures procedural ordering), and Frame-Text Matching (fine-grained alignment). Pretrained on 422 hours of data, CliPPER achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on multiple surgical benchmarks, significantly outperforming prior work in phase, step, triplet, and instrument recognition.

## Method Summary
CliPPER processes long surgical videos by splitting them into 45-second clips, each annotated with an ASR- or GPT-generated caption. From every clip, four frames are uniformly sampled and resized to 224×224. The visual backbone is a BEiT model augmented with temporal attention to capture short-term dynamics; the text backbone is BERT base. For each video, two 4-layer Transformer context encoders (2 heads each) independently aggregate information from all clip-level visual and textual embeddings. A Multi-Modal Encoder then fuses frame-level visual tokens and text tokens using alternating self- and cross-attention layers. Pretraining employs five losses: standard video-text contrastive (VTC), contextual VTC (VTC_CTX) which conditions alignment on the video context, cycle-consistency (reconstructing a clip from its text embedding), clip order prediction (COP) that predicts the order of shuffled clips, and frame-text matching (FTM) for fine-grained alignment. Training uses AdamW (lr=1e-4, cosine decay) for 20 epochs, batch size 32 videos (8 clips each), equal loss weighting, and progressive context sampling: the allowed temporal distance between context clips grows linearly from ~15 minutes to unlimited. For downstream zero-shot evaluation, non-overlapping 4-frame clips are extracted at 1 FPS, and their embeddings (with and without context) are compared to label prompts (four per label, generated by various LLMs) via cosine similarity; clip scores are aggregated (max or average) to produce video-level predictions.

## Key Results
- Phase recognition: 36.8% average F1-score (vs. 21.8% prior SOTA, +14.4% absolute).
- Step recognition: 14.0% F1-score (vs. 5.7%, +5.3% absolute).
- Triplet recognition: 6.9% mAP (60.5% relative improvement).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CliPPER’s effectiveness stems from its explicit modeling of long-range temporal context and its suite of pretraining objectives that target different aspects of surgical procedure understanding. The Contextual Video-Text Contrastive Learning aligns a clip with its caption while conditioning on the entire video context, reducing false matches that would occur using isolated clips. Cycle-Consistency Alignment creates a bidirectional mapping between clip and text embeddings, mitigating the inherent many-to-one ambiguity between visual events and language descriptions. Clip Order Prediction forces the model to learn the canonical temporal ordering of surgical steps, embedding procedural knowledge into the representations. Finally, Frame-Text Matching refines alignment at the frame level, capturing subtle visual details such as instrument usage. Together, these mechanisms yield robust, fine-grained, and temporally coherent embeddings that generalize zero-shot to downstream surgical event recognition tasks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Video-Language Contrastive Pretraining**  
  Why needed: Establishes a shared embedding space for cross-modal retrieval, enabling zero-shot classification without task-specific labels.  
  Quick check: For a batch of clips and captions, compute cosine similarities; the correct clip-caption pairs should rank significantly higher than random pairs (e.g., recall@K well above chance).

- **Temporal Context Modeling**  
  Why needed: Surgical events span minutes; single-clip representations lack the broader procedural context needed for accurate recognition.  
  Quick check: Ablate the context encoders and evaluate on long-video benchmarks; performance should drop, especially on tasks requiring understanding of step/phase dependencies.

- **Cycle-Consistency Regularization**  
  Why needed: Reduces many-to-one mapping ambiguities where multiple visual patterns could correspond to the same text description, improving alignment precision.  
  Quick check: Train with and without the cycle loss; the version with cycle consistency should show improved retrieval precision and downstream zero-shot scores.

- **Clip Order Prediction (COP)**  
  Why needed: Encodes the inherent temporal grammar of surgeries, teaching the model the canonical sequence of steps.  
  Quick check: Shuffle a set of clips from the same video and ask the model to recover the correct order; accuracy should be far above random (e.g., >80% for 5 clips).

- **Frame-Text Matching (FTM)**  
  Why needed: Captures fine-grained correlations between individual frames and textual subsegments, crucial for recognizing instrument usage and fine actions.  
  Quick check: For a given frame, retrieve the most relevant sentence from its caption; high accuracy indicates the model learns pixel-level correspondences.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map:
Raw video -> Clip segmentation -> Frame sampling (4 frames/clip) -> Video Encoder (BEiT + Temporal Attention) -> Clip visual embeddings
Caption -> Text Encoder (BERT) -> Text embeddings
(Per video: all clip visual embeddings) -> Video Context Encoder -> Contextual video embeddings
(Per video: all text embeddings) -> Text Context Encoder -> Contextual text embeddings
Contextual video embeddings + Contextual text embeddings -> Multi-Modal Encoder -> Fused representations
Fused representations -> Pretraining Objectives (Contextual VTC, Cycle Consistency, COP, FTM)

Critical Path:
The most critical flow is: Video Encoding → Context Aggregation → Multi-Modal Fusion → Contrastive Alignment. Errors in the context encoders or fusion stage most directly degrade the quality of the final embeddings.

Design Tradeoffs:
- Sparse frame sampling (4 frames/clip) vs. dense frames: reduces memory/compute but risks losing fine details; partially compensated by FTM objective.
- Separate visual and textual context encoders: adds parameters but allows independent long-range reasoning; a shared encoder might force premature modality fusion and lose strength.
- Progressive context sampling: stabilizes early training by limiting context size, but introduces an extra schedule hyperparameter; a fixed large window may cause divergence or OOM.
- Multi-source captions (Whisper + GPT): increases linguistic diversity and coverage, yet introduces noise and domain shift that the model must learn to ignore.

Failure Signatures:
- Pretraining losses diverge or become NaN: often due to incorrect contrastive loss implementation (missing temperature scaling or normalization) or aggressive context sampling leading to empty context sets.
- Zero-shot performance near random: likely the context encoders are not being applied during inference, or the prompts/aggregation mismatch the training objective.
- Out-of-memory on long videos: check if the context window includes too many clips; ensure progressive sampling limits early epochs; consider gradient accumulation or mixed precision.

Three First Experiments:
1. Single-batch overfit: Train on one video (8 clips) for 200 steps; all pretraining losses should decrease and the model should retrieve the correct caption with >90% accuracy within that video.
2. Context ablation: After a few epochs, evaluate zero-shot phase recognition on Cholec80 with context encoders disabled; expect a significant drop (e.g., >10% F1) confirming the necessity of long-range context.
3. Progressive schedule validation: Compare two models – one with fixed 15‑minute context window and one with full context from epoch 1 – on AutoLaparo; the latter should underperform or fail to converge, illustrating the need for gradual context expansion.

## Limitations
- Exact architectural details (temporal attention placement, Multi-Modal Encoder layer configuration) are not fully specified in the paper and require access to the codebase for faithful reproduction.
- The progressive context sampling schedule lacks numeric specifications (e.g., maximum clip distance per epoch), hindering precise replication of the training dynamics.
- Zero-shot results depend on unreleased, LLM-generated prompts; any deviation in prompt wording or selection may significantly affect reported metrics.

## Next Checks
