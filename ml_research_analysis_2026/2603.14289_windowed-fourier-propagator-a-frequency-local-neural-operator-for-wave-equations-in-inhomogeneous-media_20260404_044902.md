# Windowed Fourier Propagator: A Frequency-Local Neural Operator for Wave Equations in Inhomogeneous Media

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14289
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14289
- **Reference Count:** 38
- **Primary Result:** A neural operator that learns frequency-local propagators, achieving O(N·r^d) complexity and a test MSE of 8×10⁻⁶ on 2D wave tasks, with robust generalization from plane-wave training to complex states.

## Executive Summary
Simulating wave propagation in inhomogeneous media is computationally expensive due to all-to-all frequency coupling in the spectral domain. The Windowed Fourier Propagator (WFP) addresses this by learning a frequency-local neural operator. It models wave energy scattering within small, learnable windows around dominant input frequencies, explicitly preserving the superposition principle to generalize from simple plane-wave training data to arbitrary complex initial conditions. Operating resolution-invariantly in the frequency domain, WFP achieves superior accuracy and efficiency compared to standard Fourier Neural Operators.

## Method Summary
The WFP processes inputs in the frequency domain. First, a d-dimensional DFT extracts a sparse set of dominant frequencies and amplitudes from the initial condition, and a compact low-frequency representation of the velocity field. A neural network then predicts, for each driving frequency, a small complex-valued "evolution dictionary" within a local frequency window, conditioned on the medium's spectral features. The final Fourier spectrum at the target time is synthesized by linearly scaling each dictionary by its initial amplitude and aggregating contributions via scatter-add. An inverse DFT yields the spatial solution. This design replaces dense quadratic-frequency interactions with a linear-in-N process.

## Key Results
- Achieves computational complexity **O(N·r^d)**, where N is the number of dominant frequencies and r the window radius.
- Reports a test mean squared error of **8×10⁻⁶** on 2D wave propagation, outperforming Fourier Neural Operator variants (e.g., 7×10⁻⁵).
- Demonstrates robust **generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios** (discontinuous media, stronger velocity perturbations), with predictable error increase as measured by L1 loss.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Frequency-Local Scattering
**Claim:** WFP achieves efficiency and accuracy by restricting frequency interactions to small, learnable windows around each dominant input frequency.
**Mechanism:** The architecture extracts a sparse set of dominant initial frequencies and their amplitudes. A neural network predicts a small "evolution dictionary" (a window of complex amplitudes) for each driving frequency, conditioned on a compact representation of the medium's low-frequency coefficients. The final Fourier spectrum is synthesized by aggregating these windowed contributions. This replaces a dense, quadratic-frequency interaction model with a linear-in-N model.
**Core Assumption:** Wave energy transfer in inhomogeneous media is primarily confined to a narrow spectral neighborhood around the initial driving frequency (frequency locality), as formalized in Theorem 1.
**Evidence Anchors:**
- Abstract: "...models wave energy scattering within small, learnable frequency windows..."
- Section 3: Theorem 1 bounds energy transfer to modes near the initial frequency 𝐤₀, with error scaling with 𝜂 = 𝜖𝑇|𝐤₀| ≪ 1.
- Section 4.1: Defines the frequency window (𝐤c, 𝑟) and extraction function .
**Break Condition:** The locality assumption breaks down for media with large, sharp discontinuities (∇𝑐 large) or very long propagation times 𝑇, where the spectral neighborhood width grows, requiring larger windows 𝑟.

### Mechanism 2: Explicit Superposition Preservation
**Claim:** Explicit preservation of the superposition principle enables generalization from simple plane-wave training data to arbitrary complex initial conditions.
**Mechanism:** The neural network learns a mapping D(𝐤ᵢ, ) from a frequency index 𝐤ᵢ and medium features  to a propagator dictionary, independent of the initial amplitude ̂𝑢𝐤ᵢ(0). The final evolved coefficient is a linear sum: ̂𝑢𝐤′(Δ𝑡) = Σᵢ ̂𝑢𝐤ᵢ(0) · (D(𝐤ᵢ, )𝐤′). This linear scaling step ensures the overall operator is linear in the input function.
**Core Assumption:** The underlying wave equation is linear; therefore, a neural operator that correctly learns the response to a basis (plane waves) can reconstruct the response to any superposition via linear combination.
**Evidence Anchors:**
- Abstract: "...explicitly preserving superposition...enables remarkable generalization from simple training data (e.g., plane waves) to arbitrary, complex wave states."
- Section 4.2: "...the linearity with respect to initial conditions is preserved...the network’s prediction D(𝐤ᵢ, 𝑐) is independent of the initial amplitude..."
- Section 5.2: Demonstrates generalization to Gaussian beam initial conditions despite training only on single-frequency plane waves.
**Break Condition:** The linearity assumption fails for nonlinear wave equations or media; the architecture would require fundamental redesign.

### Mechanism 3: Resolution-Invariant Frequency-Domain Processing
**Claim:** Operating in the frequency domain with fixed-size windowed representations makes WFP's core computational cost resolution-invariant.
**Mechanism:** After the initial DFT, the network processes fixed-length vectors: a frequency index and a compact, low-frequency medium descriptor  (length ~ (2𝑟𝑐+1)ᵈ). The number of dominant frequencies 𝑁 and the window size 𝑟 are properties of the *input signal* and physical problem (via Theorem 1), not the spatial grid size. The main cost is O(𝑁·𝑟ᵈ) for aggregating window contributions, independent of spatial grid points.
**Core Assumption:** The essential dynamical information is captured by the evolution of a sparse set of dominant Fourier modes and their immediate neighbors; highest spatial frequencies on fine grids are either unexcited or governed by the same local interactions learned on coarse grids.
**Evidence Anchors:**
- Abstract: "...operating resolution-invariantly in the frequency domain."
- Section 4.2: Highlights that the core network operates on fixed-size representations, enabling deployment on high-resolution domains without retraining.
**Break Condition:** Fails if the input on a high-resolution grid has a significantly different spectral sparsity pattern (many more frequencies exceed threshold 𝜏), increasing 𝑁 and cost. Also, if required window 𝑟 grows with resolution, the O(𝑁·𝑟ᵈ) cost may implicitly depend on resolution.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Fourier Analysis & Spectral Representation**
  - **Why needed:** WFP's entire operation—extracting dominant frequencies, representing the medium in the frequency domain, defining local windows—relies on understanding functions as sums of sinusoids. Theorem 1 proving frequency locality is a Fourier-domain analysis.
  - **Quick check:** If a wave initial condition is a single Fourier mode eⁱᵏ⁰·ˣ, what does Theorem 1 say about the magnitude of its Fourier coefficient ̂𝑢ₙ(𝑡) for |𝐧| >> |𝐤⁰| after a finite time T?
- **Concept: Linear Operators & Superposition Principle**
  - **Why needed:** WFP's generalization power stems from learning a linear operator. Understanding that a linear operator is fully defined by its action on a basis (plane waves) is key to why training on simple data suffices.
  - **Quick check:** Why is the step "̂𝑈contrib(𝐤ᵢ) = ̂𝑢𝐤ᵢ(0) · D(𝐤ᵢ, 𝑐)" critical for ensuring the learned operator is linear?
- **Concept: Spectral Methods & Pseudospectral Discretization**
  - **Why needed:** The paper contrasts with pseudospectral methods and builds on the idea that global spectral representations are efficient for certain PDEs. The DFT implementation and threshold choice are practical discretizations of spectral concepts.
  - **Quick check:** What is the computational complexity trade-off between a standard pseudospectral method for the wave equation and WFP's O(N·r^d) cost, and what physical assumption allows WFP to avoid the full spectral cost?
- **Concept: Neural Operators**
  - **Why needed:** WFP is a specific instantiation of a neural operator, which learns mappings between function spaces. The design choices (frequency locality, superposition) are informed by operator-learning paradigms.
  - **Quick check:** How does WFP differ from the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) in its treatment of frequency interactions?
- **Concept: Inductive Biases for Oscillatory PDEs**
  - **Why needed:** The custom activation σ_custom(x) = A e^{-B x²} sin(C x) injects an inductive bias for handling oscillatory dynamics, which standard activations like tanh lack.
  - **Quick check:** Why might a Gaussian-modulated sine activation be better suited for wave propagation than a ReLU or tanh?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Input Extraction Module -> Localized Evolution Network -> Amplitude Scaling & Aggregation Module -> Output Reconstruction Module

**Critical path:** DFT initial condition & velocity field -> Extract dominant frequencies 𝜏 and low-freq medium descriptor  -> Parallel loop over each 𝐤ᵢ: NN([𝐤ᵢ; ]) outputs dictionary D(𝐤ᵢ, ) -> Scale by ̂𝑢𝐤ᵢ(0) -> Scatter-add into global spectrum -> IFFT.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Window radius r (r_c, r_w):** Smaller r reduces cost O(N·r^d) but risks missing relevant scattering; larger r increases capacity and accuracy but also cost and overfitting risk.
- **Amplitude threshold τ:** Higher τ reduces N (fewer frequencies) but may lose energetic components; lower τ increases fidelity but cost.
- **Network capacity vs. custom activation:** The gated network with σ_custom trades simpler MLPs for stronger inductive bias, requiring careful hyperparameter tuning (A, B, C).

**Failure signatures:**
- **Spatially localized high error at discontinuities:** Errors spike at sharp velocity boundaries, violating the smoothness assumption (low ∇𝑐).
- **Error growth proportional to OOD perturbation strength:** Predictable L1 error increase as velocity perturbation magnitude exceeds training range.
- **Poor generalization to very high-frequency initial conditions:** If test |𝐤| vastly exceeds training cap, required r may effectively need to be larger than learned, causing delocalized error.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Validate Frequency Locality (Theorem 1 numerically): Generate single-frequency initial condition in smooth medium, solve with high-fidelity solver, plot |̂𝑢ₙ(𝑡)| vs. |𝐧 - 𝐤⁰|, confirm rapid decay.
2. Test Superposition Generalization: Train WFP only on single-frequency plane waves; test on superpositions of multiple plane waves and on Gaussian beams. Expected: matches or outperforms model trained directly on complex states.
3. Verify Resolution Invariance: Train WFP on coarse-grid data (e.g., 257×257); evaluate *same model* on fine-grid test cases (e.g., 2049×2049). Expected: error does not catastrophically increase.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization boundaries for OOD media:** What precise conditions allow WFP to generalize to media with high discontinuity or strong nonlinearities? (Basis: "not unconditionally generalizable to arbitrary velocity fields"; OOD tests limited to weak discontinuities, α≤0.2.)
2. **Adaptive window radius selection:** How does r trade off efficiency vs. accuracy for diverse media/times, and can it be adaptively determined? (Basis: r set via threshold τ, but no sensitivity analysis or adaptive strategy proposed.)
3. **Extension to nonlinear PDEs:** Can WFP be extended to nonlinear wave equations while maintaining frequency-local efficiency and superposition-based generalization? (Basis: WFP explicitly preserves superposition, valid only for linear equations; paper focuses solely on linear wave problems.)
4. **Design principles for oscillatory activations:** What governs activation function design for neural operators handling oscillatory PDEs, and can the custom trigonometric activation be automated? (Basis: Custom activation exp(-20x²) sin(10πx) reduces error vs. tanh, but parameters found via manual hyperparameter search.)

## Limitations
- **Critical hyperparameters undisclosed:** τ, r_c, r_w, and activation parameters (A,B,C) are missing, blocking faithful replication.
- **Locality requires smoothness:** Theorem 1 assumes small ∇𝑐 and η=εT|k₀|; sharp boundaries or long T demand larger r_w, increasing cost/error.
- **Linearity and resolution assumptions:** Only applies to linear waves. Resolution invariance assumes N,r independent of grid size; may fail for fine grids with many high-frequency modes.

## Confidence
- **Frequency-local scattering:** High (Theorem 1 provides theoretical bound; O(N·r^d) complexity follows.)
- **Superposition generalization:** High (Linear scaling enforces linearity; experiments on superpositions confirm.)
- **Resolution invariance & accuracy:** Medium (Frequency tokens suggest invariance, but cross-resolution evidence is thin; reported MSE 8×10⁻⁶ unverified.)
- **OOD robustness:** Medium (Shows predictable error increase for moderate OOD cases, but extrapolation limits unquantified.)

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sweep:** Grid search τ, r_c, r_w, and (A,B,C) on a validation set to achieve MSE ≤1×10⁻⁵; track resultant N and computational cost.
2. **Test Theorem 1 empirically:** For varied ε, k₀, compute solution spectra; measure 99% energy radius vs. time; compare to theoretical bound to infer required r_w.
3. **Cross-resolution test:** Train on low-resolution data (e.g., 129×129); evaluate same model on high-resolution test (e.g., 2049×2049). Compare MSE, N, and runtime against a model trained at high resolution.
