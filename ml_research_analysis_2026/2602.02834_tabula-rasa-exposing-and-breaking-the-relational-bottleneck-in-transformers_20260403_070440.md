# Tabula RASA: Exposing and Breaking the Relational Bottleneck in Transformers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02834
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02834
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** RASA achieves 97.7% accuracy on 3-hop MetaQA questions, surpassing EmbedKGQA by 2.9 percentage points while reducing attention entropy by 3×.

## Executive Summary
Standard transformers struggle with multi-hop relational reasoning because their self-attention mechanism lacks an inductive bias for structured data. Each layer can only propagate information one hop along graph edges, requiring a number of layers linear in the reasoning depth. This paper introduces RASA, a minimal architectural change that injects graph structure directly into the attention mechanism via sparse adjacency masking and learnable edge-type biases. On the MetaQA benchmark, RASA significantly outperforms baselines on 3-hop questions, demonstrating that constraining the attention pattern search space enables deeper reasoning with fewer layers.

## Method Summary
RASA modifies the transformer attention block in two ways. First, it applies a hard, input-dependent sparse mask based on the knowledge graph adjacency matrix, allowing attention only between nodes connected by an edge (or to themselves). Second, it adds a learnable scalar bias per relation type to the attention scores, which is added whenever two tokens are connected by that relation. These changes are applied per layer without altering other components. The input is a subgraph extracted for a query, encoded into token sequences by a text encoder (DistilBERT). The model is trained end-to-end for entity prediction.

## Key Results
- RASA reaches 97.7% accuracy on 3-hop MetaQA questions, a 2.9-point gain over the EmbedKGQA baseline.
- Performance advantage grows with reasoning depth; gains are smallest on 1-hop.
- Attention entropy decreases by approximately 3× compared to a standard transformer, confirming that structural constraints focus the attention distribution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Depth Bottleneck Mitigation via Structural Inductive Bias
**Claim:** Standard transformers require Ω(k) layers for k-hop relational reasoning because each layer extends the "known neighborhood" by at most one hop.  
**Mechanism:** Without architectural priors, a transformer must learn sequential composition of hops solely through attention weight updates. RASA's adjacency mask provides an explicit prior that enforces hop-by-hop propagation along graph edges, allowing the model to focus learning on *how* to aggregate information rather than *where* to look.  
**Evidence anchors:** Theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.4) shows the Ω(k) lower bound; attention entropy reduction (Table 2) indicates less distributed, more structured attention.  
**Break condition:** Tasks with fewer than 3 hops or non-relational patterns may not engage this bottleneck; missing/incorrect graph edges cripple the mechanism.

### Mechanism 2: Attention Pattern Search Space Reduction
**Claim:** Sparse adjacency masking reduces the learned attention pattern hypothesis space from O(2^{n²}) to O(2^m) for a graph with n nodes and m edges.  
**Mechanism:** By hard-masking to only graph-connected positions, the model no longer needs to learn *which* positions are semantically related. It instead learns *how* to weight known connections, with edge-type biases guiding the aggregation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Proposition 3.6 formalizes the combinatorial reduction; Table 2 shows entropy drop from ~0.9 to ~0.3, confirming a much narrower distribution.  
**Break condition:** High-density graphs (m ≈ n²) yield diminishing returns; tasks requiring latent/implicit relations not in the adjacency matrix will fail.

### Mechanism 3: Relation-Specific Routing via Edge-Type Bias
**Claim:** Learnable scalar biases per relation type (b_r) enable specialization for different semantic relationships (e.g., "born_in" vs. "directed_by").  
**Mechanism:** During attention score computation, b_r is added to all pairs connected by relation r. This direct signal helps the model distinguish how different relation types should influence multi-hop composition, reducing the burden on node embeddings alone.  
**Evidence anchors:** Implementation shows `scores += edge_bias[edge_types]`; largest gains on 3-hop (where correct relation sequencing matters) suggest the bias aids path formation.  
**Break condition:** Numerous, noisy, or ambiguous relation types may exceed the expressive capacity of a single scalar; path-specific (not type-specific) routing needs remain unmet.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Circuit Complexity (TC⁰)**
  - *Why needed here:* To understand the theoretical impossibility claim: constant-depth transformers cannot solve graph connectivity. This frames the depth bottleneck as fundamental.
  - *Quick check question:* What does TC⁰-completeness imply about a model's ability to solve "Is there a path of length k between nodes s and t?" with only L layers where L is a small constant independent of k?

- **Concept: Sparse Attention Masking**
  - *Why needed here:* RASA's core operational change. Distinguishes *learned* sparsity (e.g., Longformer) from *hard, input-dependent* sparsity (structural prior).
  - *Quick check question:* How would you implement the adjacency mask `A` for a KG query subgraph? What are the dimensions, and what values indicate "allowed to attend"?

- **Concept: Knowledge Graph (KG) Question Answering (QA)**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire evaluation is on KGQA. Understanding multi-hop traversal (e.g., (subject)-[relation]->(object)-[relation]->...) is essential. RASA operates on graph-structured tokens, not raw text.
  - *Quick check question:* For "Who directed films starring actors born in London?", what are the entities and relations in the 3-hop chain? How is this represented as a subgraph?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Encoder (DistilBERT) -> Graph Structure Constructor (subgraph + adjacency matrix `A` + edge-type matrix `E`) -> RASA Attention Layer (Q·Kᵀ/√d + edge_bias[E] then mask with `A`) -> Stack (3 layers) -> Output Head (MLP on [CLS] for answer prediction).
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Extract KG subgraph containing all relevant nodes/edges for the query path.  
  2. Build binary adjacency matrix `A` (n×n) where `A_ij=1` iff nodes i,j are connected by any edge (include self-loops).  
  3. Build edge-type matrix `E` (n×n) storing relation IDs for each pair.  
  4. Ensure `edge_bias` table is learnable and sized for the relation vocabulary.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Rigidity vs. Flexibility: Hard masking provides a strong structural prior but cannot learn latent connections missing from the input graph.  
  - Scalability: Sparse attention reduces memory, but constructing `A` for large subgraphs (paper uses ≤750 nodes) still bottlenecks; use sparse tensors.  
  - Pre-training: Using DistilBERT (no KG pre-training) hurts 1-hop but isolates structural effects; production may need KG-pretrained encoders.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Poor 1-hop accuracy: Text encoder lacks entity knowledge; check encoder pre-training or add KG embeddings.  
  - No 3-hop gain: Verify `A` construction (directional edges? self-loops?); monitor `edge_bias` gradients.  
  - High attention entropy: Mask is ineffective (e.g., all-ones); visualize attention matrices.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline sanity check: Standard transformer (no mask/bias) on 1-hop MetaQA should reach ~97% accuracy, confirming learnability without structure.  
  2. Ablation 1 (Mask only): Enable adjacency masking without edge-type bias; expect intermediate 2/3-hop gains but less than full RASA.  
  3. Ablation 2 (Bias only): Keep global attention but add edge-type bias; expect minimal gains, confirming mask necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does RASA perform on larger, noisier, or more diverse knowledge graphs beyond MetaQA?
- What is the impact of imperfect or incomplete graph extraction on RASA's robustness?
- Is a single scalar bias per relation type sufficient for complex relation compositions or ambiguous semantics?
- Can the approach be extended to text-only multi-hop reasoning without an explicit graph structure?

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to MetaQA, a small academic KGQA benchmark; generalizability is unproven.
- Mechanism critically depends on correctness and completeness of the input subgraph; robustness to noise or missing edges is unexplored.
- The learnable scalar edge-type bias may be overly simplistic for complex or ambiguous relational patterns.

## Confidence
- **Depth bottleneck claim (Ω(k) layers):** High. Supported by formal theorem (TC⁰-completeness) and aligns with known circuit complexity results.
- **Search space reduction (O(2^m) vs O(2^{n²})):** High. Direct combinatorial consequence of hard masking; mathematically sound.
- **Empirical gain on multi-hop QA:** Medium. Strong results on MetaQA, but lack of external validation on other benchmarks limits broader confidence.
- **Edge-type bias mechanism:** Low-Medium. Ablation suggests contribution, but isolated effect not rigorously quantified across diverse relation schemas.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-benchmark replication:** Replicate MetaQA results on at least two other multi-hop KGQA benchmarks (e.g., WebQSP, QALD) with identical subgraph extraction and model configuration.
2. **Robustness to graph noise:** Systematically corrupt input subgraphs (random edge drops/additions) and measure degradation in 3-hop accuracy relative to a standard transformer baseline.
3. **Attention path analysis:** For correctly answered 3-hop questions, extract and visualize the attended edge sequence across RASA layers; verify alignment with ground-tr reasoning chains compared to a standard transformer's fragmented attention.
