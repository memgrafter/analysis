# Synthetic Rewriting as a Quality Multiplier: Evidence from Portuguese Continued Pretraining

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.24826
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24826
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Primary result**: Synthetic rewriting amplifies gains from continued pretraining in low-resource languages but depends critically on source data quality; the effect is scale-dependent, significant at 7B parameters but weak at 1.1B.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether synthetic rewriting of training data can enhance continued pretraining for low-resource languages, using Portuguese as a case study.

The authors take two subsets of the ClassiCC-PT corpus—one high-quality and one low-quality—and rewrite each document into four distinct styles via a 7B model. After mixing originals with rewrites (or repeating originals for baselines), they continue pretraining 1.1B and 7B English-centric models. Evaluation on the PoETa V2 benchmark reveals a striking asymmetry: at the 7B scale, rewriting high-quality data yields a +3.4 NPM improvement over using the same data unmodified, while rewriting low-quality data provides only a +0.5 gain. The smaller model shows no clear benefit. The study concludes that synthetic rewriting acts as a quality multiplier—it amplifies the value of already sound data but cannot compensate for poor sources—and that this effect depends on model scale.

## Method Summary
The experiment uses continued pretraining of two English-pretrained models (TinyLLaMA-derived 1.1B and LLaMA-2-derived 7B) on Portuguese data derived from the ClassiCC-PT corpus, which provides per-document quality scores (STEM and Educational) on a 0-5 scale. Two 10B-token subsets are extracted: high-quality (score >2.5) and low-quality (0.5-2.0). For the rewritten conditions, a Qwen-2.5-7B model generates four style variants (Easy, Medium, Hard, QA) per document, producing ~30B synthetic tokens; these are mixed with the original 10B tokens to form ~40B-token training corpora. Unrewritten baselines repeat the original 10B tokens four times to match the token count. All models are trained with AdamW (learning rate 3e-4, cosine decay, 5% warmup), sequence length 4096, until processing 40B tokens. Performance is measured on the PoETa V2 benchmark (44 tasks) using Normalized Performance Metric (NPM). The methodology thus isolates the interaction between source data quality, synthetic rewriting, and model scale within a fixed computational budget.

## Key Results
- At 7B scale: Rewriting high-quality data outperforms unrewritten high-quality data by +3.4 NPM; rewriting low-quality data yields only +0.5 NPM over unrewritten low-quality.
- At 1.1B scale: Benefits of rewriting are weak and inconsistent; unmodified low-quality data performs comparably to rewritten high-quality data.
- Overall, synthetic rewriting is a quality multiplier, not a substitute for curation, and its impact is scale-dependent.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Synthetic document rewriting acts primarily as a *quality multiplier* for high-quality source data, substantially amplifying downstream performance.
- **Mechanism:** The rewriting model (Qwen-2.5-7B) restructures and clarifies text into predefined styles (Easy, Medium, Hard, QA), increasing structural and lexical diversity while preserving semantic content. When applied to already coherent, informative source documents (ClassiCC-PT STEM/Educational score > 2.5), this transformation produces synthetic samples that present the same knowledge in more varied, potentially clearer, or better-structured forms. This variety likely improves the model's ability to generalize across linguistic realizations of the same facts, thereby amplifying the learning signal from the high-quality source corpus.
- **Core assumption:** The rewriting model introduces *beneficial* transformations (e.g., improved clarity, consistent style) that do not distort or omit core information, and that these transformations are more valuable when the source information is already sound.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States: "rewriting high-quality data yields a +3.4 NPM gain over the same data unmodified, while rewriting low-quality data provides only +0.5 NPM."
  - [section 4.1] Reports the persistent performance hierarchy: "edu + rewrites" consistently outperforms "edu" by ~+3.4 NPM at 7B scale.
  - [corpus] Weak: Neighboring papers (e.g., Curió-Edu 7B) confirm the importance of data quality for Portuguese continued pretraining but do not directly evidence the *interaction* with rewriting.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The amplification effect of rewriting is *scale-dependent*, becoming significant only at larger model sizes (7B parameters).
- **Mechanism:** Larger models possess greater capacity to ingest, process, and benefit from the more structured, diverse, and potentially complex linguistic patterns introduced by the rewritten styles. At 7B scale, the model can leverage these enhanced presentations to build more robust internal representations. Smaller models (1.1B) may lack the capacity to differentiate between the nuanced variations in the rewritten data versus the raw data, or they may saturate on the surface-level patterns present even in unrewritten data, making the additional complexity from rewriting superfluous or even noisy.
- **Core assumption:** There exists a minimum model capacity threshold necessary to extract additional utility from style-conditioned synthetic transformations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Explicitly notes: "At the 1.1B scale, this interaction is weaker and less consistent, with unmodified low-quality data performing comparably to rewritten high-quality data."
  - [section 4.3] Shows that at 1.1B, the performance curves for all conditions converge and cross, with "non-edu" matching "edu + rewrites".
  - [section 4.4] Category-level noise at 1.1B supports reduced sensitivity.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Synthetic rewriting injects *content diversity* (more unique tokens), but this diversity alone is *not* the primary driver of the observed gains; the *quality* of the rewritten content is paramount.
- **Mechanism:** The rewritten conditions contain ~40B unique tokens (30B synthetic + 10B original) versus the 10B unique tokens (repeated) in non-rewritten conditions. This increased diversity could theoretically improve learning by reducing memorization and exposing the model to more contexts. However, if diversity were the dominant factor, the performance boost should be similar for both high-quality and low-quality source subsets. The stark asymmetry in gains (+3.4 vs. +0.5 NPM) indicates that the *information value* and *clarity* of the rewritten content—properties derived from the source quality—are the key multipliers.
- **Core assumption:** The rewriting model's output quality is strongly conditioned on the input quality; high-quality sources yield high-quality rewrites, while low-quality sources yield rewrites that remain limited.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1] Acknowledges the unique token confound but argues: "if unique token count were the dominant factor, the gain should be comparable across quality tiers, whereas we observe a much larger gain for high-quality data."
  - [section 4.2] Notes that "General Knowledge" tasks show *hurt* performance from rewriting, suggesting quality of content (factual accuracy) matters more than mere token diversity.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Continued Pretraining
- **Why needed here:** The paper's entire experiment is built on continued pretraining—the process of taking an already-pretrained (English-centric) model and further training it on a target-language corpus (Portuguese). This is the core methodology for adapting models without full retraining.
- **Quick check question:** What is the key practical difference between pretraining from scratch and continued pretraining regarding data requirements and model initialization?

### Concept: Data Quality Quantification
- **Why needed here:** The study's independent variable is source data quality, operationalized via ClassiCC-PT's continuous STEM and Educational scores. Understanding how continuous quality scores are derived (GPT-4o annotations on 120K documents) and used to create binary high/low subsets is essential to interpreting the manipulation.
- **Quick check question:** How might a continuous quality score be thresholded to create distinct data subsets, and what are the risks of such thresholding?

### Concept: Synthetic Data Generation via Style-Conditioned Rewriting
- **Why needed here:** The key intervention is rewriting documents into four specific styles (Easy, Medium, Hard, QA). This requires understanding that the same semantic content can be expressed in multiple linguistic forms, and that controlling this form can affect learning efficiency.
- **Quick check question:** What is the hypothesized difference between a "Medium" (encyclopedic) rewrite and an "QA" rewrite in terms of structure and potential learning signal?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map:
Data Source -> Subset Constructor -> Rewriter -> Data Merger -> Base Models -> Trainer -> Evaluator

### Critical path:
ClassiCC-PT → Filter (High/Low) → Rewrite (Qwen-2.5-7B) → Merge (Orig + Synthetic) → Train (Base Model) → Evaluate (PoETa V2). The path splits for High/Low and Rewritten/Unrewritten conditions.

### Design tradeoffs:
- **Rewriter Model Size:** Using a 7B model to rewrite data for 1.1B and 7B models creates a capacity mismatch. A smaller rewriter might be more coherent for the 1.1B model's capacity.
- **Token Budget vs. Unique Tokens:** Rewritten conditions have 4x more *unique* tokens (40B) than unrewritten (10B repeated). This confounds "quality" with "diversity." The design tries to isolate quality via the asymmetric gain pattern.
- **Tokenizer:** No Portuguese tokenizer adaptation. This may limit efficiency but ensures differences come from data, not vocabulary.

### Failure signatures:
- **Hypothesis 1 Failure (Quality Multiplier):** Rewriting low-quality data shows a gain *comparable* to rewriting high-quality data (>+2 NPM).
- **Hypothesis 2 Failure (Scale Dependence):** The 1.1B model shows a *clear and consistent* benefit from rewriting high-quality data over unrewritten.
- **Generalization Failure:** The pattern holds for PoETa V2 but fails on a different Portuguese benchmark (e.g., a factual recall or code generation task).

### First 3 experiments:
1. **Matched Diversity Control:** For the "Low-Quality" condition, generate a control dataset by sampling and mixing diverse *unrewritten* documents (from a broader pool) to match the ~40B unique token count of the rewritten condition. Does it still lag far behind "High-Quality + Rewrites"?
2. **Ablate Rewriting Styles:** Retrain the 7B model using only one rewrite style at a time (e.g., only "QA" or only "Medium"). Which style(s) drive the +3.4 NPM gain? Is there an optimal mix?
3. **Scale the Rewriter:** Repeat the high-quality rewriting pipeline with a larger rewriter (e.g., 70B model) and a smaller one (e.g., 1B model). Does rewriter capability correlate with final model performance, especially at the 1.1B scale?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does synthetic rewriting fail to compensate for low-quality source data, acting only as a minor improvement instead of a substitute for curation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The results show rewriting high-quality data yields +3.4 NPM, while rewriting low-quality data yields only +0.5 NPM at 7B scale. The authors conclude rewriting is a "quality multiplier, not a substitute for curation."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates the asymmetry but does not analyze the linguistic or statistical properties (e.g., noise propagation, semantic drift) that cause rewriting to amplify quality rather than rectify deficiency.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablating rewrite outputs (e.g., measuring semantic preservation, factual accuracy, or perplexity of rewrites vs. originals) and correlating these metrics with downstream performance on low- vs. high-quality source data.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the mechanistic cause of the scale-dependent interaction between rewriting and model size, where the benefit is strong at 7B but weak/inconsistent at 1.1B?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The 7B model shows clear quality-rewriting interaction; the 1.1B model does not, with unmodified low-quality data matching rewritten high-quality performance.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper speculates on capacity limits or raw diversity benefits but offers no controlled analysis (e.g., probing model representations, memorization vs. generalization) to isolate whether smaller models cannot leverage rewrites' structural improvements or simply benefit more from repeated exposure to diverse surface forms.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative probing (e.g., knowledge retention, syntactic generalization) or training dynamics analysis (e.g., loss curves, gradient norms) for both scales on matched data diversity/quantity conditions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the optimal mixing ratio between rewritten synthetic data and original (unrewritten) data within a fixed token budget?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper concatenates all rewrites with originals (30B synthetic + 10B original) without exploring alternative mixtures. The future work section explicitly lists "varying the ratio of rewritten to original data" as an open direction.
- **Why unresolved:** The design confounds total unique tokens (40B in rewrite conditions vs. 10B repeated in baseline). The observed gains could stem from either the rewrite quality or the increased unique content; the optimal trade-off remains unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments varying the proportion of synthetic vs. original tokens (e.g., 0%, 25%, 50%, 75% synthetic) while strictly controlling for total unique tokens (e.g., by adding non-rewritten diverse data to baselines).

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Which of the four applied rewriting styles (Easy, Medium, Hard, QA) contributes most to the quality-multiplier effect, and are certain styles more effective for specific downstream task categories?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The four styles are defined, but results are aggregated across all styles. The future work section calls for "ablating individual rewriting styles."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper reports only the combined effect of all four styles. It is unknown if one style drives most gains, if styles are additive, or if certain tasks (e.g., Exams vs. Social Media) benefit differentially from specific transformations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Training and evaluating separate models on data rewritten in each single style (and their combinations) and analyzing per-category performance differences on PoETa V2.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Does the documented quality-rewriting interaction generalize to other low-resource languages beyond Portuguese, and how does it interact with language-specific characteristics (e.g., morphological complexity, script, available pretraining corpora)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The study is confined to Portuguese. The future work section lists "extending the study to other low-resource languages" as a key direction.
- **Why unresolved:** The findings are based on a single language family (Romanic) with one quality annotation scheme (ClassiCC-PT's STEM/Educational scores). The interaction may not hold for languages with different data ecosystems, writing systems, or quality definitions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicating the experimental design (controlled source quality subsets, multi-style rewriting, dual-scale models) for at least 2–3 diverse low-resource languages (e.g., a highly inflected language like Czech, a non-Latin script language like Swahili) using language-appropriate quality filters and benchmarks.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps**: Exact rewrite prompts, decoding parameters, mixing strategy, and full training hyperparameters are unreported, leading to high replication uncertainty.
- **Token budget confound**: Rewritten conditions have 4× unique tokens compared to repeated baselines; the isolated effect of quality versus diversity lacks a matched control.
- **Corpus availability**: ClassiCC-PT may not be publicly accessible, requiring substantial effort to reconstruct the quality annotation pipeline.

## Confidence
| Claim | Confidence |
|-------|------------|
| Quality multiplier effect (asymmetric gains) | High |
| Scale dependence (benefit at 7B but not 1.1B) | Medium |
| Diversity is not the primary driver | Low |

2. **Matched diversity control**: For low-quality data, sample a diverse set of *unrewritten* documents (from a broader pool) to match the ~40B unique token count of the rewritten condition; compare performance to rewritten high-quality.
3. **Style ablation at 7B**: Train with only one rewrite style (e.g., only "QA") to isolate which style(s) contribute most to the +3.4 NPM gain.
