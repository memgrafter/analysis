# Breaking Contextual Inertia: Reinforcement Learning with Single-Turn Anchors for Stable Multi-Turn Interaction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04783
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04783
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** RLSTA mitigates contextual inertia by anchoring multi-turn reasoning to the model's own correct single-turn policy, yielding large multi-turn accuracy gains (e.g., +11.3% avg for Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct) across math and code tasks.

## Executive Summary
Multi-turn interactions with large language models (LLMs) suffer from **contextual inertia**: models rigidly adhere to earlier reasoning traces even after user corrections, causing significant performance drops compared to single-turn settings. This paper identifies the problem and introduces **Reinforcement Learning with Single-Turn Anchors (RLSTA)**, a method that combats inertia by using the model’s own single-turn capabilities as stable behavioral anchors. RLSTA first filters conversation histories where single-turn accuracy exceeds multi-turn accuracy, isolating cases where the model possesses latent knowledge but is derailed by context. During RL training (via GRPO), it adds a **single-turn anchor reward**—the length-normalized likelihood of the multi-turn response under the single-turn policy conditioned on the full instruction—to the standard outcome verification reward. Experiments on multi-turn math (MT-Add, MT-Refine) across Qwen and Llama models show RLSTA substantially outperforms SFT, DPO, and vanilla GRPO, with gains generalizing to code and persisting even without external verifiers.

## Method Summary
RLSTA addresses contextual inertia through a two-stage process. First, it **filters** raw multi-turn histories to retain only those where the model’s accuracy on the full problem in a single-turn setting is higher than in the multi-turn setting, ensuring a reliable “anchor” where the model’s latent capability is correct. Second, during **RL training**, it modifies the GRPO objective by adding an **anchor reward** \( R_s \). For a sampled multi-turn response \( m_n \), \( R_s \) is the length-normalized log-likelihood of generating \( m_n \) under the reference model’s single-turn policy given the fully merged instruction \( i_{\text{full}} \). This reward pulls the multi-turn generation toward the correct single-turn distribution. The total reward \( R = R_v + \alpha R_s \) (where \( R_v \) is a sparse outcome verification reward) guides policy updates, with a KL penalty preventing excessive deviation from the reference policy. The filtering ensures \( R_s \) is only introduced where the single-turn anchor is demonstrably more reliable than the multi-turn history.

## Key Results
- RLSTA yields substantial multi-turn accuracy improvements: for Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, average accuracy rises from 43.9% (base) to 57.2% (RLSTA); MT-Add improves from 49.3% to 71.5%.
- Gains are consistent across model families (Qwen2.5, Qwen3, Llama) and generalize to code tasks (MBPP in MT-Add).
- Ablations confirm the anchor reward’s efficacy: RLSTA outperforms GRPO (with \( R_v \) only) and other baselines (SFT, DPO).
- RLSTA reduces contextual inertia intensity on low-quality histories and remains effective even when external verifiers are removed, demonstrating robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Latent Capability Filtering isolates conversation histories where the model possesses the correct answer in a single-turn setting but fails in the multi-turn setting due to contextual inertia, providing a reliable “anchor” for training.
- **Mechanism:** For each raw multi-turn history \( H \), compute the expected verification score for the single-turn response under the full merged instruction \( i_{\text{full}} \) and the multi-turn final response \( m_n \). Retain only histories where \( \mathbb{E}_{m \sim \pi(\cdot|i_{\text{full}})}[\text{Ver}(m)] > \mathbb{E}_{m_n \sim \pi(\cdot|H)}[\text{Ver}(m_n)] \).
- **Core assumption:** The model’s single-turn performance on the full problem is a stable, superior indicator of the correct reasoning path compared to its multi-turn generation under a potentially misleading history.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “RLSTA first filters conversation histories where the model’s single‑turn accuracy exceeds its multi‑turn accuracy, ensuring a reliable ‘anchor’.” [section 5.1] Formal definition (Equation 2) and rationale. [corpus] Neighbors (ACR, VISTA) address context drift but not this filtering criterion.
- **Break condition:** If the model’s single-turn accuracy is not meaningfully higher than its multi-turn accuracy on a given problem (i.e., the model lacks latent capability), the filter yields no training signal.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Single‑Turn Anchor Reward \( R_s \) aligns the multi‑turn response toward the correct reasoning path by rewarding its likelihood under the model’s own single‑turn policy.
- **Mechanism:** For a sampled multi-turn response \( m_n^{(i)} \), compute the length-normalized log-likelihood of generating that token sequence when conditioned on the full instruction \( i_{\text{full}} \) using the reference model \( \pi_{\theta_{\text{ref}}} \). Add this to the outcome verification reward \( R_v \).
- **Core assumption:** The single‑turn policy \( \pi_{\theta_{\text{ref}}}(\cdot|i_{\text{full}}) \) represents a correct, inertia‑free reasoning trajectory; pulling the multi‑turn generation toward this distribution mitigates bias from misleading history.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “adds a single‑turn anchor reward: the length‑normalized likelihood … pulls the generation toward the correct single‑turn path.” [section 5.2] Equation (4) defines \( R_s \). [corpus] Neighbors (ResT, DART) modify RL objectives but not via an internal single‑turn distribution.
- **Break condition:** If the single‑turn policy itself is erroneous or \( i_{\text{full}} \) does not reflect ground truth (e.g., in MT‑Refine’s corrupted prompt), the anchor reward may misguide.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Integrating \( R_s \) with \( R_v \) within GRPO stabilizes multi‑turn reasoning without requiring external verifiers for every turn.
- **Mechanism:** Total reward \( R = R_v + \alpha R_s \) computes advantages in GRPO. KL‑penalty prevents excessive deviation from reference policy. Filtering ensures \( R_s \) is only applied where the anchor is reliable.
- **Core assumption:** Combining a sparse final‑answer verification signal with a dense process‑level anchor provides sufficient gradient to overcome inertia while maintaining answer correctness.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “RLSTA leverages the model’s superior single‑turn capabilities as stable internal anchors.” [section 5.2] Equation (5) and reward combination; α set so a correct answer keeps advantage positive. [section 6.3] “Even in the absence of external verifiable rewards … performance gap narrows.”
- **Break condition:** Improper α may over‑regularize (ignoring multi‑turn coherence) or let \( R_v \) be too sparse to correct misguidance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) / Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**
  - *Why needed here:* RLSTA builds on GRPO, a PPO variant, to optimize policy using rewards. Understanding on‑policy RL, advantage estimation, and KL constraints is essential.
  - *Quick check:* What is the purpose of the clipping operation and KL penalty in the PPO/GRPO objective?
- **Concept: Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT) reasoning and token‑level generation**
  - *Why needed here:* Contextual inertia manifests in CoT traces across turns. The anchor reward operates at token level, requiring understanding of sequential generation and likelihood computation.
  - *Quick check:* How does length‑normalization of the log‑likelihood in \( R_s \) affect its scale relative to the binary verification reward?
- **Concept: Verification‑based rewards**
  - *Why needed here:* The outcome reward \( R_v \) relies on a verifier (rule‑based or LLM‑based) to assess final answer correctness, common in math/coding RL setups.
  - *Quick check:* In a math problem, how might a verifier extract and compare the final numerical answer from a free‑form response?

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Filtering Module -> Single‑Turn Anchor Calculator -> Reward Combiner -> GRPO Trainer.

**Critical path:** Raw conversation collection → \( H \) generation → filtering (single‑turn vs. multi‑turn score computation) → for each retained \( H \), generate \( m_n \) samples → compute \( R_v \) (verifier) and \( R_s \) (anchor) → GRPO update with advantage clipping and KL penalty.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Filtering strictness:* Higher threshold (single‑turn > multi‑turn) yields purer anchor signals but less training data.
- *α scaling:* Balances process (anchor) vs. outcome (verifier) guidance; too high may ignore final correctness.
- *Reference model choice:* Using base model (\( \pi_{\theta_{\text{ref}}} \)) avoids reward hacking but may have weaker single‑turn capability than a fine‑tuned variant.

**Failure signatures:**
- Filtering returns zero or very few histories → model lacks baseline single‑turn competence.
- Multi‑turn performance degrades after training → \( R_s \) over‑regularized or single‑turn anchor incorrect.
- Single‑turn performance drops → KL penalty too low or anchor reward overwriting single‑turn knowledge.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. *Validation of filtering:* Sample 100 random raw histories; compute proportion where single‑turn accuracy > multi‑turn accuracy. Should be high enough (>50%) to build a dataset.
2. *Ablation of \( R_s \):* Train with \( R_v \) only (GRPO) vs. \( R_v + R_s \) (RLSTA) on a small subset; compare multi‑turn vs. single‑turn gaps.
3. *Cross‑domain generalization check:* Train on math (GSM8K) only; evaluate on code (MBPP) in MT‑Add. Expect gains if inertia‑breaking transfers.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does RLSTA perform on open-ended dialogue tasks (e.g., Multi‑WOZ) versus structured math/code?
- What is the impact of using a stronger reference model (e.g., GPT‑4o) as the single‑turn anchor?
- Can the anchor reward be extended to provide turn‑level guidance without a verifier?

## Limitations
- RLSTA assumes the single‑turn policy provides a correct anchor; if the model’s single‑turn answer is erroneous, training may reinforce incorrect reasoning.
- Evaluation is limited to structured math/code tasks with few model families; generalizability to free‑form dialogue or knowledge‑intensive domains is unclear.
- The balance coefficient α and KL penalty are set heuristically; improper tuning could cause over‑regularization or loss of multi‑turn adaptability.
