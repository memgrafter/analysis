# Information-Guided Noise Allocation for Efficient Diffusion Training
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18647
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18647
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: InfoNoise dynamically re-allocates training compute to the most informative noise levels, achieving up to 1.4× faster training on continuous images and up to 3× fewer steps on discrete datasets compared to fixed schedules.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models typically use fixed noise schedules, which can be inefficient as the optimal allocation of compute varies across datasets and representations. This paper proposes InfoNoise, an information-theoretic method that adjusts the training noise distribution based on the conditional entropy rate, estimated online from denoising losses. By sampling noise levels in proportion to this rate, training updates focus on the region where uncertainty about clean data decreases fastest. Experiments on CIFAR-10, FFHQ, DNA, and binarized MNIST show significant speedups and quality improvements, and the same entropy profile can guide inference-time discretization for better samples at fixed compute.

## Method Summary
InfoNoise modifies the noise-level sampler π(σ) used during training while keeping the denoising objective, weighting w(σ), and architecture constant. It estimates the conditional entropy rate r(σ) ∝ MSE(σ)/σ³ from recent denoising losses over a logarithmic grid of σ values. The empirical per-bin MSE is smoothed with a FIFO buffer and exponential moving average. After every M training steps (once sufficient samples per bin are collected), the schedule is refreshed: a gate function suppresses the low-σ tail (σⁿ/(σⁿ+cⁿ), n=3) to avoid degenerate updates, and the pivot c is calibrated either by a threshold on normalized r (continuous data) or by detecting a stable power‑law segment on a log‑log plot (discrete data). The unnormalized probabilities are qk ∝ gated(rk)/w(σk), then a continuous cumulative distribution function is built via trapezoidal integration over logσ. During training, σ is sampled by inverse-CDF interpolation. A warm‑up phase uses a fixed base distribution (e.g., EDM’s log‑normal). The method can also be applied at inference time (InfoGrid) by sampling σ uniformly according to the final entropy profile’s CDF, yielding improved sample quality for a given number of function evaluations.

## Key Results
- On continuous image benchmarks (CIFAR-10, FFHQ), InfoNoise matches carefully tuned EDM schedules with up to 1.4× faster training.
- On discrete datasets (DNA, binarized MNIST) where standard image schedules perform poorly, InfoNoise achieves superior quality using up to 3× fewer training steps.
- The same entropy profile can guide inference-time discretization (InfoGrid), improving sample quality at fixed compute budgets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core insight is that the conditional entropy H[x₀|xσ] quantifies the remaining uncertainty about the clean data given the noisy observation. Its rate of change with respect to σ indicates which noise levels are most informative for learning. In diffusion training, the denoising objective can be related to an estimate of the I‑MMSE function, whose derivative (in σ) is proportional to the conditional entropy rate. By sampling σ in proportion to r(σ), InfoNoise allocates more compute to noise levels where a small reduction in σ yields a large reduction in uncertainty, i.e., where the model learns most efficiently. This adaptive allocation automatically shifts focus to the appropriate region for a given dataset or representation, eliminating the need for manually tuned fixed schedules.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Data loader -> Noise sampler (π(σ)) -> Corruption (xσ = x0 + σϵ) -> Denoiser (U‑Net) -> Loss computation (MSE weighted by w(σ)) -> Loss aggregation per σ bin -> Entropy‑rate estimator (smoothed MSE/σ³) -> Schedule refresher (gate, calibrate c, form qk, build CDF) -> Updates to π(σ). Warm‑up uses fixed πbase.

**Critical path**: At each training step, sample σ from current π(σ). Compute loss for that σ. Accumulate loss into per‑bin statistics (FIFO buffer, EMA). Every M steps (after minimum samples per bin), recompute r(σ) over grid, calibrate pivot c, gate low‑σ tail, compute qk ∝ gated(rk)/w(σk), integrate to get CDF, and update the continuous sampler.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Buffer size B and EMA rate β: Larger yields smoother r(σ) but slower adaptation; too small causes noisy schedule updates.
- Refresh period M: Frequent updates react quickly but increase overhead; infrequent updates save compute but may lag behind data distribution shifts.
- Log‑grid granularity K: Finer grid captures sharper peaks in r(σ) but increases computation and may reduce samples per bin.
- Gating parameters n and c: Controls suppression of low‑σ tail; c determines cutoff, n shapes smoothness.

**Failure signatures**:
- Oscillating or diverging FID: likely r(σ) too noisy → increase B, β, or M.
- Over‑emphasis on extreme tails: inspect gated r(σ) plot; tail should be near zero. Adjust c or use automatic calibration thresholds.
- Poor speedup or degraded quality: verify that π(σ)w(σ) aligns with r(σ); misalignment indicates missing scaling or bug in CDF construction.
- For discrete datasets, a flat r(σ) suggests the breakpoint detector failed; check log‑log slope stability.

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameter values (refresh cadence M, buffer size B, EMA rate β, minimum samples per bin, warm‑up steps) may require empirical tuning, affecting reproducibility.
- The pivot calibration for discrete data (“maximal stable log‑log segment”) is vaguely defined and lacks a concrete algorithm, hindering faithful replication on DNA and binarized MNIST.
- The theoretical justification linking Gaussian corruption, MSE, and conditional entropy is weak for discrete data, raising questions about the validity of the entropy‑rate proxy.

## Confidence
- Continuous image speedups: **Medium** (core method clear but hyperparameter sensitivity unknown).
- Discrete dataset gains: **Low** (ambiguous calibration and questionable assumptions for discrete data).
- Inference‑time InfoGrid: **Medium** (procedure described but depends on accurate offline estimation).
