# TaigiSpeech: A Low-Resource Real-World Speech Intent Dataset and Preliminary Results with Scalable Data Mining In-the-Wild

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TaigiSpeech, the first real-world spoken intent dataset for Taiwanese Hokkien, a low-resource, unwritten language. It comprises 3,079 utterances from 21 elderly speakers across 8 healthcare and smart-home intents. To address data scarcity, the authors explore two scalable, in-the-wild data mining strategies using large-scale Taiwanese drama videos: (1) keyword match mining with Mandarin subtitles and LLM pseudo-labeling, and (2) audio-visual mining with a pretrained cross-modal encoder. A key finding is a substantial domain mismatch: classifiers trained on the mined drama data perform well on a drama test set (70–92%) but degrade to near-random performance (~50%) on the real-world TaigiSpeech test set. However, fine-tuning self-supervised learning (SSL) speech models (e.g., WavLM-base-plus) on just 1,600 in-domain TaigiSpeech utterances achieves 90.2% accuracy on the full 8-intent test set. Furthermore, end-to-end speech classifiers significantly outperform cascaded ASR+LLM baselines, which peak at 74.5% accuracy.

## Method Summary
The core methodology involves two data mining pipelines to generate large-scale, weakly-labeled training data from ~7,000 hours of Taiwanese drama videos. **Keyword match mining** filters subtitle sentences containing Mandarin keywords for target intents, uses an LLM (Gemini) to pseudo-label the context as positive/negative for the corresponding Taiwanese Hokkien intent, and extracts aligned audio segments. **Audio-visual mining** uses a pretrained cross-modal encoder (PE-AV) to embed video clips (audio+visual) and English intent descriptions, retrieving top-matching clips via cosine similarity; for fine-grained emergencies, scores are aggregated. For the primary result, SSL models (HuBERT, WavLM) are first pre-trained (optional) on the mined data and then **fine-tuned** on the small, labeled TaigiSpeech training split (10 speakers, 1,600 utterances) for the final 8-intent classification task. A cascade baseline transcribes audio with Whisper or Qwen3-ASR and classifies intent with a Qwen3 LLM.

## Key Results
*   Pre-trained classifiers on mined drama data suffer a severe performance drop from the drama test set (70–92%) to the real-world TaigiSpeech test set (~50% for audio-visual, 70% for keyword mining).
*   After fine-tuning on 1,600 in-domain TaigiSpeech samples, WavLM-base-plus achieves **90.2% accuracy** on the full 8-intent TaigiSpeech test set.
*   Cascaded ASR+LLM systems achieve at most **74.5% accuracy**, underperforming end-to-end SSL classifiers by a large margin (>15 percentage points).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Keyword match mining using Mandarin subtitles and LLM pseudo‑labeling produces a large pseudo‑labeled dataset that can pre‑train intent classifiers, but models suffer a large performance drop when transferred to real‑world elderly speech.
*   **Mechanism:** From Taiwanese drama videos, extract subtitle sentences containing intent‑specific Mandarin keywords (plus context). Use an LLM to judge whether the context corresponds to the target intent in Taiwanese Hokkien. Keep audio segments aligned with positive pseudo‑labels; collect negatives with daily‑life keywords.
*   **Core assumption:** The LLM’s semantic judgment based on Mandarin subtitles correctly transfers to Taiwanese Hokkien intents, and the drama’s conversational style approximates target home‑assistant scenarios.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Abstract: models trained on mined drama data achieved 70–92% accuracy on drama test but dropped to ≈50% on TaigiSpeech.
    *   Section 4.1: LLM pseudo‑labeling determines if contextualized segment semantically corresponds to intent.
    *   Corpus/Table 8: Mined training set size 11.3k for 5 classes; accuracy drop from drama to Taigi (C=5) is ~20–25 pp for top SSL models (e.g., WavLM‑large 92.36% → 70.00%).
*   **Break condition:** Fails if drama speech’s acoustic characteristics, speaking style, or stress levels differ too much from real elderly speech, or if Mandarin subtitles misrepresent Taiwanese Hokkien content.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Audio‑visual mining with a pretrained cross‑modal encoder yields weakly labeled data without text, but the resulting classifiers perform near chance on the real‑world intent detection task beyond coarse emergency detection.
*   **Mechanism:** Use PE‑AV to embed drama video clips (audio + visual) and English intent descriptions. Retrieve top‑M clips per intent by cosine similarity. For fine‑grained emergency intents, aggregate scores across all four emergencies to retrieve coarse‑level clips.
*   **Core assumption:** The shared embedding space aligns English intent semantics with Taiwanese Hokkien audio‑visual content, and visual context provides language‑agnostic grounding.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Abstract: audio‑visual mining models dropped to near‑random (≈50%) on TaigiSpeech.
    *   Section 4.2.1: adopts pretrained cross‑modal encoder PE‑AV for retrieval.
    *   Section 4.2: fine‑grained emergency labeling remains challenging; scores are aggregated for coarse retrieval.
    *   Corpus: For binary (C=2) mining, drama accuracy peaks at 81.75% (WavLM‑large) but Taigi accuracy peaks at 54.79% (WavLM‑base) – both ≈50 % random baseline.
*   **Break condition:** Fails for fine‑grained intent discrimination because the embedding space does not capture subtle lexical/semantic differences in the target language, and drama visual/acoustic domain differs from real elderly contexts.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Fine‑tuning on a small in‑domain labeled subset (1,600 utterances) recovers high accuracy (>90%) for SSL models, and end‑to‑end speech classifiers substantially outperform cascaded ASR + LLM systems.
*   **Mechanism:** Initialize a speech encoder (HuBERT, WavLM) with weights from either scratch or pre‑training on mined data. Then fine‑tune the model on TaigiSpeech training data. SSL features transfer to the target domain even with limited labels.
*   **Core assumption:** SSL models learn universal speech representations that can be efficiently adapted to new languages and domains with relatively few samples.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Abstract: After fine‑tuning on 1,600 utterances, SSL models like WavLM‑base‑plus reached 90.2% accuracy; cascaded systems achieved at most 74.5%.
    *   Section 5.1: fine‑tune models pretrained on mined data using TaigiSpeech data of 10 speakers (1,600 utterances).
    *   Section 6.1: under Taigi‑adapt setting, HuBERT‑base and WavLM‑base‑plus achieve 90.10% and 90.21% accuracy.
    *   Section 6.4 & Table 9: Best cascaded (Qwen3‑ASR 1.7B + Qwen3‑8B) yields 74.48% vs end‑to‑end >88%.
    *   Corpus: TaigiSpeech total 3,079; adaptation uses 1,600 train, 960 test.
*   **Break condition:** If in‑domain data is extremely scarce (< few hundred) or severely biased, fine‑tuning may not reach high accuracy. Heavy pre‑training on mismatched data could hurt convergence if fine‑tuning regime (LR, layer unfreezing) is poor.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Self‑supervised learning (SSL) for speech.
    *   *Why needed:* All top models (HuBERT, WavLM) rely on SSL pre‑training; understanding masked prediction and contrastive objectives explains robust feature learning.
    *   *Quick check:* What is the difference between HuBERT’s k‑means cluster prediction and WavLM’s masked prediction with contextualized targets? How does this affect low‑resource transfer?
*   **Concept:** Cross‑lingual transfer via pivot languages.
    *   *Why needed:* Keyword mining uses Mandarin as a pivot; audio‑visual mining uses English intents. This bootstraps data without target‑language text.
    *   *Quick check:* What risks arise from using a pivot language (e.g., lexical mismatch, translation errors) and how did they manifest here?
*   **Concept:** Domain mismatch and adaptation.
    *   *Why needed:* The paper’s main finding is the drastic drama→Taigi performance drop; recognizing and addressing domain shift is essential for real‑world deployment.
    *   *Quick check:* Which acoustic or linguistic factors likely cause the mismatch between scripted drama and spontaneous elderly emergency speech?
*   **Concept:** End‑to‑end vs. cascaded SLU.
    *   *Why needed:* The comparison shows end‑to‑end speech classifiers beat ASR + LLM cascades in low‑resource settings; this guides system design.
    *   *Quick check:* Why does the cascade suffer more in low‑resource scenarios? Consider error propagation and data requirements.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    TaigiSpeech dataset (real audio) -> SSL model (HuBERT/WavLM) + classifier head -> fine-tuning -> evaluation
    Mined drama data (subtitles/video) -> Keyword mining (LLM pseudo-label) / Audio-visual mining (PE-AV retrieval) -> pre-training data -> (optional) pre-training -> fine-tuning
    Cascade baseline: TaigiSpeech audio -> ASR (Whisper/Qwen3-ASR) -> transcript -> LLM (Qwen3-8B) -> intent label
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Obtain drama dataset with Mandarin subtitles (~7,000 h).
    2.  Implement chosen mining pipeline; generate pseudo‑labeled audio segments.
    3.  Train intent classifiers on mined data; validate on drama test split.
    4.  Acquire TaigiSpeech; split into speaker-independent train (1,600), val (119), test (960).
    5.  Fine‑tune pre‑trained models on TaigiSpeech train; tune hyperparams on val.
    6.  Evaluate on TaigiSpeech test; compare with scratch baseline and cascades.
    7.  Ablate: mined data size, fine‑tuning steps, model capacity.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Keyword vs. audio‑visual mining:* Keyword gives finer labels but depends on subtitles and LLM; audio‑visual avoids text but only coarse labels and may need more data.
    *   *Model size:* SSL (94 M+) vs. MatchboxNet (<150 k). Heavy models give ~20 % higher accuracy but are less deployable on edge devices.
    *   *Pre‑training scale vs. domain similarity:* Mined data is large but mismatched; filtering by acoustic similarity or using domain‑adversarial training could improve transfer.
    *   *Cascaded vs. end‑to‑end:* Cascade leverages powerful ASR/LLM but adds latency, privacy issues, and error propagation; end‑to‑end is more efficient and robust to ASR errors in low‑resource regimes.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Drama→Taigi accuracy gap > 30 pp: severe domain mismatch; consider collecting more in‑domain data or stronger adaptation.
    *   Scratch performance already high (> 85 %) with 1,600 samples: SSL models are strong without any pre‑training; if pre‑training does not improve, it may be harmful.
    *   Cascaded accuracy > 10 pp below end‑to‑end: ASR errors are dominant; could fine‑tune ASR on Taigi data.
    *   Audio‑visual Taigi‑adapt ≈ scratch: retrieval data not helpful; need better ranking or larger candidate pool.
    *   Validation loss increases during fine‑tuning: learning rate too high or catastrophic forgetting; try gradual unfreezing or smaller LR.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  *Scratch baseline:* Train HuBERT‑base and WavLM‑base‑plus from scratch on TaigiSpeech train (1,600). Expected accuracy ~85‑90% on test.
    2.  *Domain mismatch quantification:* Train same models on full keyword‑mined set (11,300). Evaluate on drama test (70‑92%) and Taigi test (~50‑73%). Confirms gap.
    3.  *Adaptation benefit:* Pre‑train on keyword‑mined data, fine‑tune on TaigiSpeech train. Compare to scratch benchmark. Expect modest gain (e.g., WavLM‑base‑plus from 86.98% → 90.21%). Also run audio‑visual pre‑training + fine‑tune for comparison.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** Why does audio-visual mining fail to produce fine-grained emergency intent labels in low-resource languages?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that "under the audio‑visual mining setting, obtaining fine‑grained emergency labels (e.g., distinguishing breath‑related emergencies from fall events) remains challenging, possibly due to the cross‑lingual setting and weak supervision," leading them to aggregate emergency intents.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper does not investigate solutions such as fine‑tuning the multimodal encoder or exploring alternative training objectives to improve fine‑grained discrimination.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirically evaluate a fine‑tuned PE‑AV model on TaigiSpeech or design a multi‑task retrieval objective that explicitly distinguishes between emergency subtypes.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** What is the root cause of the severe domain mismatch between drama‑sourced training data and real‑world elderly speech in TaigiSpeech?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Models trained on mined drama data achieve 70–92% accuracy on the drama test set but drop to near‑random (~50%) on TaigiSpeech, indicating a "substantial performance degradation" and "domain mismatch."
*   **Why unresolved:** The authors hypothesize that models may rely on "superficial patterns" from drama data that do not generalize, but this is not empirically validated.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze feature representations (e.g., via t‑SNE or domain discrepancy metrics) or apply domain adaptation techniques (e.g., adversarial training) to measure and reduce the gap.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** How can cascaded ASR+LLM systems be improved to match the performance of end‑to‑end speech classifiers on low‑resource intent recognition?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Cascaded systems (e.g., Whisper + Qwen3) attain at most 74.5% accuracy, while end‑to‑end SSL models exceed 90% after fine‑tuning; the authors note that fine‑tuning ASR and LLM remains future work.
*   **Why unresolved:** No experiments were conducted with fine‑tuned ASR or LLM on TaigiSpeech, leaving the potential for improvement unknown.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine‑tune foundation ASR models (Whisper, Qwen3‑ASR) and the LLM (Qwen3) on the TaigiSpeech training set and re‑evaluate the cascaded pipeline.

## Limitations
*   **Domain mismatch causes:** The paper identifies a large drama→Taigi performance gap but does not disentangle whether the primary cause is acoustic (e.g., elderly speech, background noise) or linguistic (e.g., language shift from Mandarin subtitles to Taiwanese Hokkien speech).
*   **Contribution of pre‑training:** It is unclear how much of the final 90.2% accuracy is due to the specific mined pre‑training data versus the generic SSL pre‑training (HuBERT/WavLM). The benefit of domain‑specific pre‑training vs. just fine‑tuning a strong off‑the‑shelf SSL model is not isolated.
*   **Cascade underperformance baseline:** The cascaded ASR+LLM baseline uses off‑the‑shelf models without any Taigi‑specific fine‑tuning, so the reported 74.5% ceiling may be lower than what a fully adapted cascade could achieve.

## Confidence
*   **Domain mismatch existence:** **High** – Strong, consistent evidence across mining strategies and models (70–92% → ~50% drop).
*   **SSL fine‑tuning efficiency:** **Medium** – Robust result (90.2%) but confidence reduced by unreported hyperparameters and ambiguous incremental value of mismatched pre‑training.
*   **End‑to‑end vs. cascade superiority:** **High/Medium** – Large gap (>15 pp) is convincing, but cascade uses unadapted ASR; gap may narrow with fine‑tuning.
*   **Audio‑visual mining utility:** **Low** – Near‑random performance on real‑world test (~50%) suggests the approach is not effective for fine‑grained intent detection in this setting.

## Next Checks
1.  **Hyperparameter robustness for SSL fine‑tuning:** Perform a sweep over learning rates (1e‑5 to 1e‑3), batch sizes, and layer‑unfreezing strategies on the TaigiSpeech train/val splits to verify the 90.2% result is stable and not hyperparameter‑specific.
2.  **Acoustic‑linguistic mismatch analysis:** Extract features (speaking rate, pitch, SNR, spectral characteristics) from the drama audio and TaigiSpeech audio. Correlate these feature shifts with the model accuracy drop to identify dominant mismatch sources.
3.  **Improved cascade baseline:** Fine‑tune the ASR component (e.g., Whisper or Qwen3‑ASR) on the TaigiSpeech training transcripts, and then fine‑tune the LLM (Qwen3) on the resulting ASR hypotheses and true labels. Re‑evaluate the cascade to determine if the end‑to‑end gap persists.
