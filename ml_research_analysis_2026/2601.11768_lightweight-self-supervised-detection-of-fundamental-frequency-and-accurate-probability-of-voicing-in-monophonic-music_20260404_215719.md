# Lightweight Self-Supervised Detection of Fundamental Frequency and Accurate Probability of Voicing in Monophonic Music

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.11768
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11768
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** A fully self-supervised, lightweight F0 and voicing estimator achieves RPA 95.84% and RCA 96.24% on clean held-out data after training only on noisy real recordings, with a small gap (0.70 RPA, 1.31 RCA) to in-domain supervised training.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a self-supervised framework for fundamental frequency (F0) and voicing estimation in monophonic music that does not require manual labels. The core idea is to train a neural network on real, acoustically challenging recordings (MedleyDB) by enforcing **transposition-equivariance** on Constant-Q Transform (CQT) features: shifted views of the same audio must produce consistent pitch distributions. An **EM-style iterative reweighting** scheme uses the **Shift Cross-Entropy (SCE)** loss, which measures this consistency, to down-weight frames where the constraint fails (typically noisy or unvoiced regions). The converged sample weights then serve as pseudo-labels to train a separate, lightweight binary voicing classifier. The resulting system is robust to bleed, reverb, and noise, and generalizes reasonably across instruments, with errors dominated by octave confusions rather than arbitrary drift.

## Method Summary
The method has three stages: (1) **Self-supervised F0 representation learning** using a ResNeXt1D encoder and a Toeplitz-structured pitch head. Training pairs consist of a CQT frame and a pitch-shifted copy (via circular bin shift). The model is trained to satisfy equivariance (shifted output matches shifted input) and invariance (timbre representation stable) losses, weighted by sample-wise reliability scores. (2) **EM reweighting**: Every few epochs, the SCE loss—measuring mismatch between the two views—is computed for all frames. Frame weights are reduced proportionally to this loss, progressively suppressing updates from high-variance, likely unvoiced/noisy frames. (3) **Voicing classifier**: After F0 training, the final weights are thresholded into binary pseudo-labels. A single linear layer is then trained on raw CQT frames to predict voicing using these labels, leveraging the F0 model's implicit knowledge without joint optimization.

## Key Results
- Competitive cross-corpus performance on clean MDB-stem-synth: **RPA 95.84%**, **RCA 96.24%**, only 0.70 RPA and 1.31 RCA below in-domain supervised training.
- Strong cross-instrument generalization within families (e.g., violin→cello), but significantly lower performance for bass instruments (RPA ~68–77%), indicating family-specific timbre limitations.
- Error analysis shows octave confusions are the primary failure mode, suggesting the learned pitch representation is generally stable but has log-frequency biases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Transposition-Equivariant Learning on CQT** forces the network to encode pitch structure directly without supervision. Given a CQT frame `x` and its pitch-shifted version `x'` (by bin shift `δ`), the model `fθ` is trained so that the predicted pitch distributions satisfy `p' ≈ roll(p, δ)`. This is enforced via equivariance and invariance losses. The core assumption is that pitch transposition in the signal maps to a fixed, linear bin shift in the log-frequency CQT. Evidence anchors include the abstract’s statement that the objective “forces the network to encode pitch structure directly” and section 3.1.3’s formalization of the shift. The mechanism breaks if the CQT’s discretization misaligns with true pitch (e.g., due to inharmonicity, very low fundamentals, or heavy reverberation that smears harmonic structure).

**EM-style Iterative Reweighting** uses the SCE loss—computed from the equivariant constraint—as a heuristic to down-weight unreliable frames. Frames with consistent harmonic content (voiced) yield low SCE because their pitch distributions are sharp and shift predictably. Frames dominated by noise, bleed, or reverb tails yield high, unstable SCE because predictions fluctuate randomly. In the E-step, weights `w_i` are reduced by `Δw_i = λ(e) * normalized_Lsce,i * w_i`. In the M-step, the total loss is weighted by `w_i`. This progressively annihilates gradients from high-variance frames. The core assumption is that SCE reliably correlates with frame-level “voiced-ness” and stability. Evidence includes section 3.2.2’s weight update rule, section 3.2.3’s gradient-variance argument, and Figure 4’s bimodal weight histogram. The mechanism fails if SCE does not separate voiced/unvoiced (e.g., for poor initializations or unconventional playing techniques) or if the annealing schedule `λ(e)` is mis-tuned.

**Pseudo-Labeling for Voicing Classifier** leverages the converged EM weights as confidence scores to train a separate binary voicing classifier without manual annotations. After self-supervised F0 training, the final weights `w_i` are thresholded at `θ` to produce pseudo-labels `v*i`. A single-layer linear classifier is then trained on CQT frames `X` using binary cross-entropy against `v*i`. The core assumption is that EM weights not only aid F0 learning but also indicate the presence of a stable, transposition-consistent harmonic structure—a reasonable proxy for voicing in monophonic music. Evidence includes the abstract’s description, section 3.3’s procedure, and the appendix’s justification for using a low-capacity model. The approach breaks if the EM weights are poorly calibrated (not bimodal) or if the threshold `θ` is chosen badly, leading to biased pseudo-labels that the linear classifier cannot correct.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Constant-Q Transform (CQT)**  
  *Why needed:* Provides a log-frequency representation where pitch transposition corresponds to a fixed bin shift, enabling the transposition-equivariance pretext task.  
  *Quick check:* If you shift a note up by 12 semitones (one octave), how many CQT bins does its harmonic ridge move, assuming 36 bins per octave?  
  **Answer:** 36 bins.

- **Concept: Transposition-Equivariance**  
  *Why needed:* The core self-supervision signal. It forces the network’s pitch representation to shift predictably under pitch augmentation, disentangling pitch from timbre without labels.  
  *Quick check:* For a model output `p` over pitch bins, what operation should you apply to `p` to get the target for a pitch-shifted input that was shifted up by 5 bins?  
  **Answer:** Circularly roll `p` by 5 bins.

- **Concept: Shift Cross-Entropy (SCE) Loss**  
  *Why needed:* Enforces equivariance on the full probability distribution and, critically, its per-frame value serves as a heuristic for reliability: low SCE indicates a sharp, consistent distribution (voiced); high SCE indicates a flat, inconsistent one (unvoiced/noisy).  
  *Quick check:* In `Lsce = 1/2 [CE(y', roll(p, δ)) + CE(y, roll(p', -δ))]`, what do `y` and `y'` represent, and what is the purpose of `roll(p, δ)`?  
  **Answer:** `y` and `y'` are the pitch logits for the original and shifted views. `roll(p, δ)` shifts the probability distribution to align with the transposed input.

- **Concept: Expectation-Maximization (EM) for Sample Reweighting**  
  *Why needed:* Implements a robust training loop that gradually suppresses gradients from frames where the equivariance constraint fails (noisy/unvoiced), preventing them from dominating learning.  
  *Quick check:* In the EM loop, what happens in the E-step and what happens in the M-step? What triggers a weight update?  
  **Answer:** E-step: compute normalized SCE for all frames and reduce weights `w_i` based on SCE. M-step: train for `K` epochs using weighted loss. Trigger: every `K` epochs.

- **Concept: Pseudo-Labeling**  
  *Why needed:* To obtain a voicing classifier without manual labels, by converting the EM weights (which embody the self-supervised model’s belief about voicing) into binary targets.  
  *Quick check:* Why train a separate, linear voicing classifier instead of just thresholding the F0 model's confidence (e.g., max probability)? What risk does the separate classifier mitigate?  
  **Answer:** Thresholding the F0 model’s confidence risks overfitting to its specific probabilistic quirks. A separate linear classifier, trained on pseudo-labels, can learn a more robust decision boundary and is regularized by its limited capacity.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Raw Audio → CQT → Augment & Shift → ResNeXt1D Encoder → Toeplitz Pitch Head → Pitch Logits → SCE Loss → EM Weight Update → (After convergence) → Freeze F0 Model → Threshold `w_i` → Train Linear Voicing Classifier on CQT

- **Critical path:** The pipeline depends on the EM reweighting producing a meaningful bimodal weight distribution. The voicing classifier’s quality cascades from this: poor weights yield noisy pseudo-labels, leading to a biased linear classifier.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Encoder capacity:* A deeper/wider ResNeXt captures more timbre detail but risks overfitting in low-data regimes and increases inference cost; the paper opts for lightweight for rapid single-instrument training.  
  - *Toeplitz vs. FC head:* Toeplitz reduces parameters drastically (~1000× vs. full FC for 269 bins) and enables efficient post-hoc calibration via circular shift. A FC head might fit training data better but is inefficient and less interpretable.  
  - *Separate voicing classifier:* Pros: avoids forcing the pitch-equivariant network to also perfect voicing; uses a simple model that generalizes from pseudo-labels. Cons: introduces an extra component; voicing decision not jointly optimized with pitch.  
  - *EM frequency (`K=5` epochs):* More frequent updates adapt faster but increase overhead; less frequent may allow poor frames to damage learning before correction.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Poor cross-instrument transfer on bass instruments (RPA ~68–77%) suggests low-frequency or timbre biases.  
  - Weight histogram not bimodal indicates EM failed to separate voiced/unvoiced; check SCE distribution, annealing `λ(e)`, or augmentation strength.  
  - Voicing classifier overfits pseudo-labels if it performs poorly on real noise vs. MedleyDB; reduce capacity or increase regularization.  
  - Large synthetic clean vs. real performance gap may indicate overfitting to MedleyDB artifacts; adjust augmentation SNR range.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Validate SCE as a voicing proxy on synthetic data: train on clean notes, plot `Lsce` vs. ground-truth voicing; then add controlled noise and observe `Lsce` increase on unvoiced frames.  
  2. EM reweighting ablation: train with uniform weights (`w_i=1`) vs. full EM on MedleyDB; compare F0 loss curves and voicing accuracy on a small manually labeled validation set.  
  3. Cross-instrument matrix replication: pick two instruments from the same family (e.g., violin & cello) and two from different families (e.g., clarinet & electric bass); train on one, test on the other, and replicate the pattern from Table 2 (high within-family, lower across families).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do bass instruments exhibit significantly lower cross-instrument RPA compared to other instrument families?
2. How does the proposed monophonic method extend to polyphonic music, where multiple F0s coexist?
3. Does the SCE-based EM reweighting remain a reliable voicing proxy under acoustic conditions not seen during MedleyDB training?
4. How sensitive is the voicing classifier's performance to the threshold `θ` used for pseudo-label generation, and what is an optimal selection strategy?
5. Would a more expressive voicing classifier architecture improve performance, or does the single linear layer inherently regularize against pseudo-label noise?

## Limitations
- **Architecture gaps:** ResNeXt1D layer dimensions, cardinality, and downsampling are not fully specified, risking replication errors.
- **SCE as voicing proxy:** The assumption that high SCE indicates unreliable frames may fail for instruments with weak harmonic structure or inharmonicity (e.g., double bass, bells).
- **Instrument transfer:** Bass-class instruments show a large performance drop (RPA ~68%), indicating potential low-frequency or timbre biases not addressed.

## Confidence
- **Transposition-equivariance mechanism:** Medium – Theoretically sound and supported by prior self-supervised pitch works, but empirical validation across diverse acoustics is limited.
- **EM reweighting separation:** Medium – Figure 4 shows bimodal weights, but no ground-truth verification; reliance on SCE consistency remains an assumption.
- **Reported metrics (RPA 95.84%, RCA 96.24%):** High – Directly measured on MDB-stem-synth, though cross-corpus generalization beyond this set is untested.
- **Cross-instrument generalization within families:** Medium – Evidence from a small instrument set; bass failure suggests boundary conditions not fully understood.

## Next Checks
1. **SCE–voicing correlation:** Manually annotate a small MedleyDB validation subset. Compute per-frame SCE loss and correlate with ground-truth voicing to confirm the reliability signal.
2. **EM reweighting ablation:** Train with uniform weights (`w_i=1`) versus the full EM scheme. Compare convergence behavior, weight histograms, and final RPA/RCA to quantify EM’s contribution.
3. **External clean corpus test:** Evaluate the trained model on a separate monophonic dataset (e.g., MIR‑1K) to verify that the small gap to supervised training holds beyond MDB‑stem‑synth.
