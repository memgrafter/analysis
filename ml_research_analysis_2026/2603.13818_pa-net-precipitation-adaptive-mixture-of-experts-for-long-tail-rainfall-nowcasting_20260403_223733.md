# PA-Net: Precipitation-Adaptive Mixture-of-Experts for Long-Tail Rainfall Nowcasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13818
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13818
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art category-averaged IoU (45.38%) and TS (24.62) on ERA5 with only 18.90M parameters, improving heavy rain IoU by 3.35 points to 24.37% and rainstorm IoU by 9.37 points to 23.41%.

## Executive Summary
PA-Net addresses two fundamental challenges in precipitation nowcasting: the computational burden of modeling large spatiotemporal fields and the extreme long-tailed distribution of rainfall where high-impact heavy events are rare. It introduces a Transformer framework with three synergistic components: Precipitation-Adaptive Mixture-of-Experts (PA-MoE) that dynamically scales model capacity based on local rainfall intensity; Dual-Axis Compressed Latent Attention (DACLA) that factorizes spatiotemporal attention with convolutional reduction to manage million-token sequences; and an intensity-aware training protocol that progressively emphasizes extreme-rainfall samples. On the ERA5 benchmark, PA-Net achieves state-of-the-art performance with 45.38% IoU and 24.62 TS (category-averaged) using only 18.90M parameters, with particularly large gains in the tail: heavy rain IoU improves by 3.35 points to 24.37% and rainstorm IoU by 9.37 points to 23.41%.

## Method Summary
PA-Net is built on a Transformer backbone and combines three innovations. (1) **PA-MoE** dynamically assigns per-token expert count (k_token) based on precipitation intensity thresholds derived from the training CDF, allocating more experts to heavy-rain tokens and fewer to light-rain tokens. (2) **DACLA** reduces the quadratic complexity of full spatiotemporal attention by sequentially applying spatial attention (after 2D strided convolution down-projection) and temporal attention (after 1D causal convolution down-projection), each followed by upsampling. (3) **Intensity-aware training** employs a soft Dice loss, a PA-MoE regularizer to balance expert utilization and encourage sharp routing, and a curriculum that linearly increases a sample re-weighting factor w(r) proportional to intensity. This combination effectively combats the long-tail distribution while maintaining efficiency.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art category-averaged IoU (45.38%) and TS (24.62) with only 18.90M parameters on ERA5.
- Substantial tail improvements: heavy rain IoU +3.35 to 24.37%; rainstorm IoU +9.37 to 23.41%.
- Ablation confirms each component’s contribution; PA-MoE alone provides +2.15 IoU.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Precipitation-Adaptive MoE (PA-MoE)**  
PA-MoE routes tokens to a variable number of experts based on local rainfall intensity r, using thresholds λ_wk and λ_str from the training CDF. Tokens with r < λ_wk (light) activate k_min experts; λ_wk ≤ r < λ_str (medium) activate k_med; r ≥ λ_str (heavy/rainstorm) activate k_max. The router selects top-k_j experts by affinity and renormalizes their weighted outputs. This concentrates representational capacity on rare, high-complexity events. Evidence: abstract and Section V.A.4. Break condition: mis-calibrated thresholds misallocate capacity, either over-provisioning light rain or under-provisioning heavy rain.

**Mechanism 2: Dual-Axis Compressed Latent Attention (DACLA)**  
DACLA factorizes full spatiotemporal self-attention into sequential spatial and temporal stages, each preceded by convolutional down-projection (2D strided for spatial, 1D causal for temporal). This reduces the token count before attention, lowering complexity from O(n²) to O(n_spatial² + n_temporal²). The upsampling restores resolution. Evidence: abstract and Section V.A.3. Break condition: aggressive compression discards high‑frequency spatial details (small convective cells) or precise temporal dynamics, causing oversmoothing.

**Mechanism 3: Intensity‑Aware Training Protocol (IATP)**  
IATP mitigates optimizer bias toward abundant light‑rain samples via: (i) soft Dice loss (down‑weights majority classes), (ii) PA‑MoE regularizer (L_equity for load balancing, L_decisive for sharp routing), and (iii) curriculum re‑weighting w(r)=1+η*(r/r_ref)^α_w, where η ramps up during training to amplify gradients from high‑intensity tokens. Evidence: abstract and Section V.B.3. Break condition: too aggressive re‑weighting causes gradient explosion/overfitting on the sparse tail; too weak a ramp fails to overcome bias.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
1. **Input Tokenizer**: Patchifies multivariate atmospheric fields and adds solar‑geometric positional encoding.
2. **DACLA Block**: Sequential Intra‑Frame (spatial) and Cross‑Frame (temporal) attention modules with convolutional down/up‑projection.
3. **PA‑MoE Layer**: Router takes token features and an external intensity map Ω, computes per‑token k_token, selects top‑k experts from shared/routed pools, and fuses outputs with renormalized weights.
4. **Intensity‑Aware Training Loop**: Computes sample weighting w(r), applies to loss, includes PA‑MoE auxiliary loss (L_equity + L_decisive), and ramps η over epochs.
5. **Categorical Decoder**: Convolutions mapping final states to rainfall category probabilities.

### Critical Path
Data flow: `[Multivariate Input] -> Tokenization + Positional Encoding -> [PA‑Transformer Block: DACLA -> PA‑MoE -> Residual/Normalization] * N -> Decoder -> [Categorical Output]`. The intensity map Ω (derived from precipitation prior R_prior) is fed to the PA‑MoE router at every block to control routing dynamically.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Expert Allocation Granularity vs. Routing Overhead**: Finer‑grained k tiers (more than 3) could allocate capacity more precisely but increase router complexity and instability; coarser tiers are simpler but may not capture continuous difficulty.
- **Compression Aggressiveness vs. Detail Preservation**: Stronger spatial/temporal strides yield greater compute savings but risk losing high‑frequency spatial structures (small storms) or precise temporal cues critical for heavy rain.
- **Reweighting Aggressiveness vs. Training Stability**: A steeper η ramp or larger α_w focuses faster on the tail but risks gradient explosion on extremely sparse rainstorm samples; a conservative ramp may be too slow to overcome bias.

### Failure Signatures
- **Uniform Expert Activation**: Monitor per‑expert token fractions f_i and mean gate ¯g_i; if f_i is nearly uniform, the router ignores intensity signals—check λ_str/λ_wk or router gradients.
- **Spatial Oversmoothing**: Forecasts show diffuse, blob‑like rain areas; likely cause: excessive DACLA spatial compression or k_min too large for dry tokens.
- **Heavy Rain Skill Stagnation**: If IoU/TS for heavy rain/rainstorm does not improve, verify Ω is correctly derived and fed, and k_max sufficiently larger than k_min.
- **Training Instability**: Loss spikes or divergence after curriculum ramp‑up; may indicate too aggressive re‑weighting or poor load balancing.

### First Three Experiments
1. **Ablation – PA‑MoE in Isolation**: Train a model with only the PA‑MoE component (fixed k elsewhere) on a baseline Transformer+DACLA. Expect single‑component gain ~+2.15 IoU.
2. **Token Budget Analysis**: For a fixed validation batch, plot distribution of assigned k_token across all tokens; should be tri‑modal and correlate with binned rainfall intensity histogram.
3. **DACLA Compression Sensitivity Sweep**: Vary spatial/temporal downsampling factors (e.g., stride 2,4,8). Measure MACs/throughput, light‑rain IoU (robustness), heavy‑rain IoU (sensitivity) to identify Pareto frontier.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can PA‑Net reduce spatial oversmoothing of isolated convective cells? Basis: explicit mention. Why unresolved: current DACLA and MoE may lack resolution for sharp boundaries. Evidence needed: object‑based verification (SAL, ETS) on high‑resolution data; test multi‑scale features or edge‑aware losses.
- **Open Question 2**: Can predefined intensity thresholds in PA‑MoE be replaced with a continuous, learnable mapping? Basis: paper states future work. Why unresolved: fixed thresholds are static and may not generalize; a differentiable mapping could optimize allocation dynamically. Evidence needed: implement neural router outputting real‑valued expert count; compare heavy‑rain IoU and efficiency.
- **Open Question 3**: How can neural precipitation forecasts better match the discrete, patchy nature of real rainfall? Basis: gap between continuous predictions and discrete observations. Why unresolved: regression outputs smooth sparse rainfall, missing stochastic patterns. Evidence needed: incorporate discrete latent variables or GAN training; evaluate with spatial point metrics and expert assessment.

## Limitations
- Architecture hyperparameters (including d_model, layers, expert sizes, DACLA strides, and intensity thresholds λ_wk, λ_str) are undisclosed, hindering exact reproduction.
- Training details (learning rate, batch size, curriculum schedule, loss weights) are missing, requiring extensive tuning.
- Inference‑time routing (source of intensity for k_token) is unspecified, and solar‑geometric positional encoding integration is unclear.

## Confidence
- PA‑MoE adaptive routing: **Medium** (ablation shows gain, but thresholds and expert pool sizes unknown; risk of misallocation if thresholds shift).
- DACLA factorization: **Medium** (efficiency claim plausible but no direct memory/speed benchmarks; aggressive compression could lose fine details).
- Intensity‑aware training: **Medium** (curriculum appears effective in ablations, but precise schedule parameters and stability on diverse datasets are unclear).
- Overall benchmark results: **Medium** (ERA5 is standard, but results depend on many undisclosed hyperparameters; external validation needed).
