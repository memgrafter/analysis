# Enhancing Bandit Algorithms with LLMs for Time-varying User Preferences in Streaming Recommendations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08067  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08067  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** HyperBandit+ achieves state‑of‑the‑art normalized accumulated reward (e.g., 5.08 ± 0.11 on KuaiRec) by combining a time‑aware hypernetwork, LLM‑based warm‑start, and low‑rank factorization.

## Executive Summary
Streaming recommendation systems must cope with time‑varying user preferences and sparse early interactions. Existing contextual bandit algorithms often assume stationarity and suffer from inefficient exploration. HyperBandit+ addresses these challenges by introducing a time‑aware hypernetwork that generates dynamic user preference matrices conditioned on time embeddings, supplemented by an LLM Start mechanism that synthesizes offline data for warm‑starting. Low‑rank factorization reduces computational costs while maintaining accuracy. Theoretical analysis yields a sublinear regret bound. Empirical evaluations on three real‑world datasets (KuaiRec, Foursquare‑NYC, Foursquare‑TKY) show consistent and significant improvements over seven strong baselines.

## Method Summary
HyperBandit+ is a contextual bandit algorithm that models time‑varying user preferences through a hypernetwork. The hypernetwork receives a time‑period embedding and outputs low‑rank factor matrices whose product approximates the preference matrix Θₚ. This matrix maps user and item contexts to expected rewards, enabling UCB‑based arm selection. To mitigate cold‑start inefficiency, LLM Start enriches side information (user profiles, item attributes) via large language models and simulates multi‑step interactions to produce synthetic training data that pretrains the hypernetwork and latent item features. Online, the algorithm updates per‑arm ridge regression statistics and periodically retrains the hypernetwork on buffered data. Low‑rank factorization (rank τ) reduces the hypernetwork’s output dimensionality from dₐ·dᵤ to τ(dₐ+dᵤ), cutting training and inference time without sacrificing accuracy. Theoretical analysis shows that HyperBandit+ enjoys a sublinear regret bound, which adapts to the time‑varying nature of the problem.

## Key Results
- HyperBandit+ outperforms all baseline algorithms (LinUCB, HybridLinUCB, DLinUCB, ADTS, FactorUCB, HyperBandit) in normalized accumulated reward across all three datasets.  
- On KuaiRec, HyperBandit+ achieves 5.08 ± 0.11 versus HyperBandit’s 4.55 ± 0.08 and LinUCB’s 3.02 ± 0.03.  
- LLM Start provides a substantial boost in early‑stage performance (first thousands of steps).  
- Low‑rank factorization attains near‑full‑rank accuracy while significantly reducing training time.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hypernetwork‑driven Time‑Aware Preference Modeling
HyperBandit+ employs a hypernetwork (an MLP) to generate user preference matrices conditioned on time period embeddings (via Euler encoding or one‑hot). This allows the bandit policy to adapt to periodic shifts in user preferences without storing a separate model for each period. The hypernetwork outputs low‑rank factors Aₚ and Bₚ whose product gives Θₚ, which maps user context vectors to item preference scores. The UCB scores are then computed using this time‑specific matrix. The core assumption is that user preferences follow a periodic pattern (e.g., weekly cycles) that can be captured by conditioning Θₚ on a discrete time period. Evidence includes the abstract’s explicit mention of the time‑aware hypernetwork, Section 4.3.2 detailing the architecture, and the related HyperBandit work that validates hypernetworks for time‑varying bandits. If preferences are aperiodic or the hypernetwork fails to learn the mapping (e.g., due to training instability), this mechanism may degrade to a stationary model and lose its advantage.

### Mechanism 2: LLM Start for Offline Warm‑Start
LLM Start uses large language models to enrich side information and simulate user–item interactions across time periods via step‑by‑step prompting. The resulting synthetic dataset is used to pretrain the bandit’s latent item features and hypernetwork before online deployment. This provides an informative warm‑start that approximates a good policy from the first online step, bridging the gap caused by sparse early interactions. The mechanism assumes that LLM‑generated data faithfully approximates real user preferences and can serve as a proxy for initial exploration. The abstract describes LLM Start as a multi‑step data augmentation technique; Section 4.2 explains the two‑step embedding enhancement and simulation process; neighboring work on combining LLMs with bandits supports the warm‑start idea. Poor quality or biased synthetic data (e.g., LLM hallucinations, distribution shift) could mislead the bandit and harm early performance instead.

### Mechanism 3: Low‑Rank Factorization for Efficient Hypernetwork
Low‑rank factorization reduces the hypernetwork’s output dimensionality from dₐ·dᵤ to τ(dₐ+dᵤ), where τ is the chosen rank, while preserving most of the expressive power of the full preference matrix. The hypernetwork produces two matrices Aₚₜ ∈ ℝ^{dₐ×τ} and Bₚₜ ∈ ℝ^{dᵤ×τ} whose product approximates Θₚₜ ≈ AₚₜBₚₜᵀ. This design exploits the empirically observed low‑rank structure of Θₚ, as shown by singular value decomposition in Section 4.4.1. The factorization reduces both training and inference time. The core assumption is that the true time‑varying preference matrices have low effective rank, i.e., most singular values concentrate in the first few dimensions. Evidence is internal: Section 4.4.1 presents the SVD analysis, and Section 4.4.2 derives the training process with factorization. Underestimation of τ causes underfitting, while overestimation erodes efficiency gains.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Time period embedding → Hypernetwork (MLP) → Low‑rank factor matrices (Aₚ, Bₚ) → Preference matrix Θₚ → Bandit policy (UCB) → Item selection.  
Additional components: LLM‑Enhanced Embedding supplies context vectors (𝒄ᵤ, 𝒔ₐ); LLM Start module provides pretraining data for latent features and hypernetwork; Online update loop periodically retrains the hypernetwork using buffered interactions.

### Critical Path
1. At timestep *t*, receive user *uₜ* and time period *pₜ*.  
2. Compute time embedding *sₚₜ*; feed to hypernetwork to obtain *Aₚₜ*, *Bₚₜ*; multiply to get Θₚₜ.  
3. For each candidate item *a*, compute latent feature *𝑥ₐ(t)* by solving ridge regression (Eq. 6) and obtain observed feature vector *𝒔ₐ*.  
4. Compute UCB score *fₜ(𝒄ᵤ,𝒄ₐ)* and select arm *aᵢₜ*.  
5. Observe reward *rₜ*; update per‑arm sufficient statistics Ψₐᵢₜ and *𝒃ₐᵢₜ* (Eqs. 23‑24).  
6. After every *Tₙ* steps, gather data buffer *Dₙ* and update hypernetwork parameters via gradient descent on loss *L* (Eq. 28).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Low‑rank τ:** Higher τ improves approximation but raises computational cost; lower τ saves time but risks underfitting.  
- **LLM data quality:** Enriched side information and synthetic interactions aid warm‑start, but LLM hallucinations or distribution shift can bias the model; filtering/calibration may be required.  
- **Update frequency:** More frequent hypernetwork updates enhance adaptation to time shifts but increase training overhead; the paper uses mini‑batches every few thousand steps.  
- **Exploration parameter α:** Larger α encourages exploration at the expense of immediate reward; tuned empirically.

### Failure Signatures
- Normalized accumulated reward plateaus or declines relative to baselines.  
- Regret curve shows a persistent gap that does not close over time.  
- LLM Start yields no improvement in the first few thousand steps (as in Figure 10).  
- Hypernetwork training loss oscillates or diverges; singular values of Θₚ remain flat (no low‑rank concentration).

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Main result replication:** Run HyperBandit+ on KuaiRec, Foursquare‑NYC, and Foursquare‑TKY; compare normalized accumulated reward against LinUCB, HybridLinUCB, DLinUCB, ADTS, FactorUCB, and HyperBandit; verify reported numbers (e.g., ~5.08 on KuaiRec).  
2. **LLM Start ablation:** Disable LLM Start warm‑start (use random initialization) for the first 10 000 timesteps; the performance gap should match Figure 10, demonstrating the early‑stage benefit of LLM Start.  
3. **Low‑rank factorization sensitivity:** Vary the estimated rank τ (e.g., 1, 3, 5, full) and measure both normalized reward and hypernetwork training time; expect diminishing returns beyond a certain τ and clear time savings, as shown in Figure 9.

## Limitations
- LLM‑generated synthetic data may contain hallucinations or distribution shift, risking poor warm‑start.  
- Low‑rank factorization assumes a low effective rank; a suboptimal choice of τ leads to underfitting or wasted computation.  
- The periodic time embedding may not capture aperiodic preference drifts; evaluation is limited to three datasets, which may not generalize.

## Confidence
- Time‑aware hypernetwork: **Medium** (Assumption: periodic user preferences; may fail on aperiodic drift)
- LLM Start: **Medium** (Risk: synthetic data quality may be poor)
- Low‑rank factorization: **Medium** (Trade‑off: rank τ choice critical)
- Overall superiority: **Medium** (Combined uncertainties from all components)

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate LLM Start:** Replace LLM synthetic data with small real early interactions or random initialization to isolate the effect and correlate synthetic realism with performance.  
2. **Test on non‑periodic data:** Use datasets with irregular concept drift (e.g., click‑fraud patterns) to evaluate robustness of the time embeddings.  
3. **τ‑sensitivity study:** Sweep τ from 1 to full rank across at least two datasets, measuring regret and training time to identify the optimal trade‑off.
