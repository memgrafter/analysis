# A Decision-Theoretic Formalisation of Steganography With Applications to LLM Monitoring

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23163
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23163
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces a decision-theoretic formalism using generalized V-information to define the steganographic gap—the difference in utility between an agent that can decode hidden content and one that cannot—enabling detection, quantification, and mitigation of steganographic behavior in LLM reasoning traces.

## Executive Summary
Classical steganography detection requires a known benign reference distribution, which is infeasible for LLM reasoning traces. This paper proposes a decision-theoretic alternative: the steganographic gap Δ_steg, the difference in generalized V-information between a Receiver that can decode hidden signals and a Sentinel that cannot. The gap measures the asymmetry in usable information. Empirically validated on simulated steganographic reasoning (e.g., Rot13 encoding, cipher fine-tuning), the normalized gap (0–1) increases with encoding strength, codebook mismatch, and cipher-training scale, decreases with sentinel interventions, and is zero for public encodings. The framework provides a practical tool for monitoring LLMs for covert communication.

## Method Summary
The method estimates the steganographic gap by comparing the maximum achievable utility (approximated via best-of-n sampling) for two decoder families—Receiver (capable of decoding hidden signals) and Sentinel (incapable)—on an auditing task. For each agent, generalized V-information I_V(Z→Y) is defined as the utility gain from observing the reasoning trace Z relative to not observing it, where the utility is task-specific (e.g., accuracy). The gap Δ_steg = I_rec − I_sen; a normalized version splits the fraction of Receiver-usable information inaccessible to the Sentinel. Experiments use Qwen2.5 Instruct models, datasets GSM8k/MMLU/APPS, and synthetic encodings (Rot13, ciphers). The approach bypasses the need for a benign reference distribution and instead relies on behavioral effects.

## Key Results
- The normalized steganographic gap correlates positively with encoding strength (Rot13 probability), codebook mismatch, and the amount of cipher-training data.
- Sentinel interventions (test-time scaling, RL-trained paraphrasing) reduce the gap, demonstrating mitigation potential.
- For public (non-hidden) encodings, the gap is approximately zero, confirming specificity.
- The method successfully detects steganographic behavior in simulated LLM reasoning traces and quantifies its magnitude.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Asymmetry in Usable Information Inference
**Claim**: Steganography can be detected without a known benign reference distribution by comparing downstream utility between a Receiver (who can decode hidden content) and a Sentinel (who cannot).  
**Mechanism**: The reasoning trace Z is provided to both agents; their performance on an auditing task with utility u is compared. A positive difference Δ_steg indicates that Z contains information exploitable only by the Receiver.  
**Core assumptions**: (i) The hidden information is consequential for the auditing task; (ii) Matched Public Capability—Receiver and Sentinel have equal capability on public (non-hidden) content.  
**Evidence**: Found in the abstract and Section 2 (classical detection infeasibility), Section 4 (observing Z and its effect on decisions), and the corpus (novelty of decision-theoretic approach).  
**Break conditions**: If the Sentinel is systematically weaker on public content, Δ_steg > 0 may occur without steganography. If hidden information is irrelevant, Δ_steg = 0. If the Receiver sandbags or Sentinel accidentally decodes, the gap closes despite steganography.

### Mechanism 2: Generalised V-Information (GVI)
**Claim**: GVI measures the increase in expected task utility from observing side information Z relative to not observing it, within a decoder family V that satisfies optional ignorance.  
**Mechanism**: Define U_V(Y|Z) = sup_{d∈V} E[u(Y, d(Z))]. Then I_V(Z→Y) = U_V(Y|Z) − U_V(Y|∅). This quantifies usable information under utility u. The family V must include decoders that can ignore Z, ensuring the baseline is achievable.  
**Core assumptions**: Decoder family V satisfies optional ignorance.  
**Evidence**: Section 3.3 and Definition B.6; abstract statement; generalisation of V-information from Xu et al. (2020).  
**Break conditions**: If V misrepresents the agent’s true decoding capabilities, GVI may underestimate usable information. Violating optional ignorance breaks non-negativity and interpretation.

### Mechanism 3: Steganographic Gap and Normalization
**Claim**: The steganographic gap Δ_steg(Z) = I_rec(Z→Y) − I_sen(Z→Y) quantifies steganography; the normalized gap Δ̃_steg = 1 − I_sen/I_rec (when I_rec > 0) indicates the fraction of Receiver-usable information inaccessible to the Sentinel.  
**Mechanism**: Compute Receiver-usable and Sentinel-usable GVI for the same Z and auditing task. The gap captures asymmetry due to hidden decoding capability; normalization removes utility-scale dependence.  
**Core assumptions**: Matched Public Capability; the auditing task is chosen so hidden information influences performance.  
**Evidence**: Section 4.3 definitions; empirical results in Section 6 (Figures 3–7, Tables 2–3).  
**Break conditions**: The gap may be zero if hidden info is irrelevant to the task; may be negative if Sentinel benefits more from Z (e.g., due to capability asymmetry or accidental decoding). The gap depends on the chosen auditing task.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Generalised V-Information (GVI)**  
  *Why needed*: GVI provides a utility-based measure of information, allowing detection of steganography via performance differences rather than signal statistics.  
  *Quick check*: Why use utility-based information instead of mutual information? Because mutual information does not account for task relevance; GVI captures only information that can be exploited for the specific utility.

- **Concept: Decision-Theoretic View of Steganography**  
  *Why needed*: It bypasses the need for a known benign reference distribution, which is infeasible for LLM reasoning traces.  
  *Quick check*: What is the key limitation of classical detection in LLMs, and how is it overcome? Classical methods require a reference distribution of non-steganographic signals, which is circular to obtain. The decision-theoretic approach instead compares the utility of two agents with different decoding abilities.

- **Concept: Steganographic Gap**  
  *Why needed*: It operationalizes the decision-theoretic insight into a measurable quantity for detection and quantification.  
  *Quick check*: If I_rec=0.2 and I_sen=0.1, normalized gap = 1 - 0.1/0.2 = 0.5, meaning half of the Receiver-usable information is inaccessible to the Sentinel.

- **Concept: Matched Public Capability**  
  *Why needed*: Ensures that any utility difference arises from hidden decoding, not from general capability differences on public content.  
  *Quick check*: Why might a positive gap occur without steganography if this assumption is violated? If the Sentinel is weaker on public content, it may have lower baseline utility, causing a positive gap even if Z contains no hidden information.

- **Concept: Capability Elicitation**  
  *Why needed*: Estimating U_V requires measuring the best achievable utility within decoder family V; poor elicitation (e.g., weak prompts) may underestimate capabilities and bias gap estimates.  
  *Quick check*: What are two methods to improve capability elicitation? Best-of-n sampling and fine-tuning (or strong prompting like chain-of-thought). The paper uses best-of-n and mentions RL-trained paraphrasing; fine-tuning is also discussed.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Reasoning Trace Generator -> Decoder Families (V_rec, V_sen) -> Auditing Task Specifier -> Utility Estimator -> Gap Calculator

**Critical path**:  
1. Generate reasoning traces Z from the generator for tasks T_gen.  
2. Prepare auditing tasks T_aud with inputs X_aud and ground-truth Y.  
3. For each (Z, X_aud), evaluate both Receiver and Sentinel under conditions with/without Z to obtain outputs; compute utility u against Y.  
4. Estimate suprema for each agent’s decoder family by aggregating utilities (e.g., max over sampled outputs).  
5. Compute I_rec, I_sen, Δ_steg, and normalized gap.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Decoder realizations: Prompting is flexible but may under-elicit capabilities; fine-tuning adapters could improve elicitation but risk overfitting.  
- Sampling budget: More trace samples (n_z) and output samples (n_y) reduce variance but increase cost.  
- Utility function: Accuracy is simple; for subtler failures, custom utilities (reward models, LLM-as-judge) may be needed.  
- Sentinel selection: Should have similar general capabilities to avoid false positives from capability mismatch; using the same model family may risk spurious leakage.

**Failure signatures**:  
- I_rec ≈ 0: Receiver cannot exploit Z; either Z lacks task-relevant info or auditing task misaligned.  
- Positive Δ_steg but I_sen ≈ 0: May indicate severe capability mismatch (Sentinel too weak) rather than steganography.  
- Negative Δ_steg: Could mean Sentinel outperforms Receiver on Z (better public capability or accidental decoding).  
- High variance across tasks: Indicates unstable estimates; increase sampling or ensure CoT is generally helpful.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Known encoding baseline**: Use Rot13 on GSM8k; Receiver sees legible text, Sentinel sees encoded. Expect Δ_steg increasing with encoding strength; both see legible → Δ_steg ≈ 0.  
2. **Matched capability check**: On a plain reasoning task, apply same prompt to both agents; gap should be near zero. If not, adjust Sentinel or prompting.  
3. **Consequentiality test**: Use APPS backdoor secret; verify I_rec > 0 and Δ_steg scales with amount of hidden-relevant training; removing hidden info should make gap vanish.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Unsupervised variant**: How to develop an unsupervised steganographic gap that does not require specifying an auditing task with ground-truth labels? The paper suggests sampling outputs from Receiver and Sentinel under diverse prompts conditioned on Z and testing for distributional differences, but this remains future work.

2. **Robustness to capability elicitation failures**: To what extent are gap estimates robust under suboptimal prompting, and how can we mitigate false positives/negatives? The gap relies on accurately estimating suprema of decoder families; elicitation failures may bias results.

3. **Logical limitations due to incomplete auditing context**: When does the gap fail to detect steganography because the auditing context X_aud omits relevant state variables (e.g., deployment awareness)? The formalism requires X_aud to contain all state relevant to the payload's utility; otherwise evasion is possible.

4. **Effect of auditing task choice**: How does the choice of utility function and Y affect detection of different steganographic strategies? A trace may be steganographic for one task but not another; systematic guidance is lacking.

5. **Gap spoofing by adversarial senders**: Can a model intentionally reduce the asymmetry (e.g., by sandbagging or decoy hints) to evade detection, and how can we design Sentinel families resistant to such evasion? The static Sentinel may be outmaneuvered by a Receiver that adapts to minimize the gap.

## Limitations
Theoretical assumptions (Matched Public Capability, hidden info consequentiality) are critical but not validated for real LLMs; violations may yield false positives/negatives. Empirical validation relies on synthetic steganography (Rot13, ciphers); natural steganographic behaviour could exhibit different gap dynamics. Key implementation details (exact prompts, model versions, sample sizes, "max over decoders" approximation) are missing, hindering exact replication. Auditing task selection is non‑trivial and lacks principled guidance. Estimation of decoder suprema via best‑of‑n is noisy and potentially biased.

## Confidence
- Theoretical framework: High (rigorous definitions and proofs).  
- Empirical detection: Medium (synthetic tasks only, limited methodological detail).  
- Mitigation: Medium (experimental interventions with sparse description).

## Next Checks
1. **Exact replication**: Reproduce all experiments using the precise prompts, model checkpoints, and sampling parameters (ideally from released code) to clarify estimator implementation and variance.  
2. **Assumption violation tests**: Intentionally break each core assumption (e.g., use a weaker Sentinel, choose an irrelevant auditing task) and verify that the gap behaves as predicted (false positive/negative).  
3. **Natural steganography test**: Apply the method to LLMs exhibiting realistic covert channels (e.g., lexical steganography) and compare gaps between steganographic and control conditions.
