# Rejection Mixing: Fast Semantic Propagation of Mask Tokens for Efficient DLLM Inference

## Quick Facts

- arXiv ID: 2602.22868  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22868  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: ReMix introduces a continuous mixing state to resolve combinatorial contradictions in DLLM parallel decoding, achieving 2.5‑5× step reduction and up to 8.5× speedup while improving accuracy (e.g., +2.65% on GSM8K).

## Executive Summary

Diffusion large language models (DLLMs) suffer from **combinatorial contradiction** when tokens are decoded in parallel, degrading output quality and limiting speed gains. This paper presents **Rejection Mixing (ReMix)**, a training‑free inference modification that introduces an intermediate **continuous mixing state (C)** between the masked (M) and final token (T) states. Positions not yet decoded are continuously refined via a **mixing rule** that incorporates the model’s output distribution, while a **rejection rule** resets unstable positions back to [MASK] to prevent error propagation. Evaluated on LLaDA‑8B and MMaDA‑8B across 8 language and 6 multimodal benchmarks, ReMix reduces decoding steps by 2.5‑5× and latency by 2.4‑7.5× (up to 8.5× speedup), while simultaneously improving accuracy (e.g., +2.65% on GSM8K, +14.05% on ARC‑C). The method adds only ~9% overhead, demonstrating a highly favorable quality‑speed trade‑off for DLLM inference.

## Method Summary

ReMix alters the standard masked diffusion decoding process used in DLLMs. Each sequence position maintains a state: **M** (masked), **C** (continuous), or **T** (final token). At every step, the model’s forward pass produces a probability distribution over the vocabulary for every position given the current continuous embeddings. Positions with confidence above a threshold $\tau_{\text{conf}}$ transition directly to **T**. For remaining positions, two rules apply:

1. **Mixing rule** ($\text{M} \rightarrow \text{C} \circlearrowleft$ or $\text{C} \rightarrow \text{C} \circlearrowleft$): The embedding is updated to a convex combination of the projected expected token embedding $W^\top p_\theta(\cdot)$ (using an adaptive top‑p sample) and the fixed [MASK] embedding, controlled by a mixing ratio $\beta$.
2. **Rejection rule** ($\text{C} \rightarrow \text{M}$): If the Jensen‑Shannon divergence between the current and previous output distributions exceeds $\tau_{\text{rej}}$, the position is reset to **M** and its embedding reverted to [MASK].

This loop repeats until all positions are **T**, preserving inter‑position dependencies through continuous refinement and discarding unstable intermediates. The approach requires no model retraining and fits within existing DLLM codebases.

## Key Results

- **Speed**: Reduces decoding steps by 2.5‑5× and latency by 2.4‑7.5×, achieving up to 8.5× acceleration on LLaDA‑8B and MMaDA‑8B.
- **Accuracy**: Improves performance across benchmarks; e.g., +2.65% on GSM8K, +14.05% on ARC‑C, with gains on most language and multimodal tasks.
- **Efficiency**: Adds only ~9% runtime overhead relative to the baseline, yet delivers net speedups.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

- **Continuous State Preserves Dependencies**: Instead of immediately remasking or sampling discrete tokens, ReMix maintains unfilled positions in a continuous embedding space. This allows information to flow between positions through the transformer’s shared context across steps, enabling mutual coordination before final discretization. Evidence includes ablations showing that removing continuous mixing degrades performance, and the theoretical link to continuous‑space diffusion methods like CRoCoDiL.
- **Soft Look‑Ahead via Mixing**: The mixing rule injects a gradient‑like signal by blending the current output distribution (the model’s belief) into the embedding. Even low‑confidence distributions carry semantic cues about a position’s eventual role; continuous updates let positions adjust softly in response to changes elsewhere, reducing the risk of contradictory combinations.
- **Stability through Rejection**: Abrupt changes in a position’s output distribution indicate instability. By computing Jensen‑Shannon divergence between consecutive distributions, ReMix identifies such positions and resets them to [MASK] before they can propagate errors. This acts as a backtracking mechanism that maintains convergence reliability.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
State tracker $S$ (per‑position state), embedding storage $Y_{\text{emb}}$ → transformer forward pass $p_\theta(\cdot|X,Y_{\text{emb}})$. The output distribution feeds three rules: (1) Decoding rule (M→T if $\max p > \tau_{\text{conf}}$), (2) Rejection rule (C→M if $D_{\text{JS}}$ > $\tau_{\text{rej}}$), (3) Mixing rule (update $Y_{\text{emb}}$ for undecoded positions). Updated states and embeddings are fed back in the next step.

**Critical path** (per decoding step):  
1. Perform forward pass: compute $P = p_\theta(\hat{Y}|X,Y_{\text{emb}})$.  
2. For each position $i$ with $S_i \neq T$:  
   - If $\max_v P_{i,v} > \tau_{\text{conf}}$: set $S_i = T$, freeze $Y_{\text{emb},i}$ to argmax token embedding.  
   - Else if $S_i = C$ and $D_{\text{JS}}(P_i \| P_i^{\text{old}}) > \tau_{\text{rej}}$: set $S_i = M$, reset $Y_{\text{emb},i} = \text{Emb}[M]$.  
   - Else: set $S_i = C$ and update $Y_{\text{emb},i}$ via mixing rule.  
3. Store $P$ as $P^{\text{old}}$ and repeat until all $S_i = T$.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Mixing ratio $\beta$: higher strengthens refinement but risks noise; lower is safer but provides less coordination.  
- Confidence threshold $\tau_{\text{conf}}$: higher delays decoding (more mixing steps, potential speedup) but may cause over‑refinement; lower reduces steps but increases contradiction risk.  
- Rejection threshold $\tau_{\text{rej}}$: lower increases resets (more robust, more steps); higher allows longer evolution (fewer steps) but may admit instability.  
- Overhead vs. speedup: mixing and rejection add ~9% runtime, offset by fewer decoding steps.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Accuracy drop: mis‑tuned $\beta$ (too high) or $\tau_{\text{rej}}$ (too low/high) causing under‑refinement or instability.  
- No speedup: $\tau_{\text{conf}}$ set too high, yielding few tokens per step.  
- Oscillation: insufficient rejection (high $\tau_{\text{rej}}$) letting unstable positions persist.  
- Excessive resets: $\tau_{\text{rej}}$ too low, increasing steps and eroding speedup.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Baseline comparison** – Run vanilla LLaDA/MMaDA and ReMix on a small benchmark (e.g., GSM8K). Expect ReMix to achieve fewer steps, lower latency, and equal or higher accuracy as reported in the paper.
2. **Ablation of mixing** – Set $\beta = 0$ (no continuous mixing). Performance should degrade to that of a confidence‑aware parallel decoder, confirming the mixing rule’s contribution.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity** – Sweep $\beta \in \{0.3,0.4,0.5,0.6\}$ and $\tau_{\text{rej}} \in \{0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4\}$ on GSM8K. Plot accuracy vs. step reduction to locate a favorable trade‑off region, reproducing the sensitivity patterns shown in the paper.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Adaptive hyperparameters** – Can $\beta$ and $\tau_{\text{rej}}$ be tuned dynamically during decoding based on token confidence or sequence statistics, rather than fixed globally? The paper manually selects values from small candidate sets, leaving room for more adaptive schedules.
2. **Generality beyond masked diffusion** – Does ReMix’s continuous mixing concept generalize to other discrete diffusion processes (e.g., uniform transition matrices) that lack an explicit [MASK] state? The current evaluation is limited to masked diffusion models.
3. **Theoretical foundation** – What formal property of continuous representations enables resolution of combinatorial contradictions? Could an information‑theoretic measure (e.g., reduced joint entropy) explain the benefit?
4. **Scaling overhead** – How does the computational cost of mixing and rejection scale with vocabulary size, model dimension, and sequence length? The reported ~9% overhead is on an 8B model; larger models or vocabularies may incur higher relative costs.

## Limitations

- Exact hyperparameter values ($\beta$, $\tau_{\text{rej}}$) per benchmark are not provided, requiring additional tuning to replicate results.
- Multimodal evaluation relies on GPT‑4o mini as a judge; prompts and API details are absent, limiting full reproducibility.
- Implementation depends on the WINO codebase, making an independent re‑implementation non‑trivial.
- The adaptive top‑p sampling procedure (residual mass reassignment to [MASK]) is described ambiguously and may affect outcomes if implemented differently.
