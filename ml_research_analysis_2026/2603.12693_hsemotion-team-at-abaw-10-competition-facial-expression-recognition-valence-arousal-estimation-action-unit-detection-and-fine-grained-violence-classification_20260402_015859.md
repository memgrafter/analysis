# HSEmotion Team at ABAW-10 Competition: Facial Expression Recognition, Valence-Arousal Estimation, Action Unit Detection and Fine-Grained Violence Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.12693  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12693  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Achieved large gains over baselines on all four ABAW-10 tasks: EXPR macro F1=47.40 (↑22.4), VA mean CCC=0.562 (↑0.342), AU macro F1=54.7% (↑15.7), VD macro F1=0.783 (↑0.143).

## Executive Summary
The HSEmotion team participated in the ABAW-10 competition, tackling four tasks: facial expression recognition (EXPR), valence-arousal estimation (VA), action unit detection (AU), and fine-grained violence detection (VD). For the facial tasks, they employed a pipeline that extracts fast embeddings from pre‑trained EfficientNet‑based emotion models. High‑confidence predictions are used directly; lower‑confidence ones are processed by an MLP with Generalized Logit Adjustment (GLA) to handle class imbalance. Predictions are refined with temporal smoothing and audio fusion. For VD, they evaluated several visual encoders and identified ConvNeXt‑T combined with a temporal convolutional network (TCN) as the best performer. On the validation set, the approach substantially outperformed the competition baselines across all tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness of their modular design and pre‑trained representations.

## Method Summary
The facial analysis pipeline begins by extracting frame‑level embeddings from a pre‑trained EfficientNet model trained on emotion recognition. Each frame receives a confidence score derived from the model’s softmax output. If confidence exceeds a threshold, the prediction is accepted; low‑confidence frames are processed by a separate MLP classifier that incorporates Generalized Logit Adjustment to mitigate long‑tail label distribution. After frame‑level decisions, a temporal smoothing filter enforces consistency across adjacent frames. Finally, audio features are fused (e.g., via late fusion) to leverage complementary modality cues. For violence detection, the team compared several 2D and 3D video backbones. The top configuration used a ConvNeXt‑T feature extractor followed by a TCN to model short‑term temporal dynamics, yielding higher macro F1 than 3D CNN alternatives.

## Key Results
- **Facial tasks**: EXPR macro F1=47.40 (baseline 25.0), VA mean CCC=0.562 (baseline 0.22), AU macro F1=54.7% (baseline 39.0%).  
- **Violence detection**: VD macro F1=0.783 (baseline 0.640).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Pre‑trained embeddings**: Provide strong, generalizable visual features for facial states without expensive training from scratch.  
- **Confidence‑based routing**: Efficiently combines reliable predictions from the pre‑trained model with a specialized MLP for ambiguous frames, optimizing compute.  
- **Generalized Logit Adjustment**: Explicitly corrects for imbalanced label distributions, preventing majority‑class bias.  
- **Temporal smoothing**: Reduces frame‑wise noise and ensures smooth transitions, crucial for continuous affect recognition.  
- **Audio fusion**: Adds complementary affective cues (e.g., vocal tone) that disambiguate visual signals.  
- **2D encoder + TCN for VD**: ConvNeXt‑T extracts high‑quality spatial features; TCN captures local temporal patterns in short clips more effectively than 3D CNNs, which may be mismatched to the data.

## Foundational Learning

- **Generalized Logit Adjustment (GLA)**  
  *Why needed*: Mitigate long‑tail class imbalance by adjusting classifier logits based on empirical frequencies.  
  *Quick check*: Compare per‑class recall before/after GLA; minority classes should show improved recall.

- **Temporal Smoothing**  
  *Why needed*: Reduce flickering and enforce temporal coherence in frame‑level predictions.

- **Audio‑Visual Fusion**  
  *Why needed*: Audio (e.g., voice tone) provides complementary affective information, especially when visual cues are ambiguous.  
  *Quick check*: Remove audio from the pipeline and measure performance drop, particularly on low‑visibility segments.

- **2D Encoder + TCN for Video**  
  *Why needed*: 2D backbone efficiently extracts spatial features; TCN models short‑term temporal dependencies without heavy 3D parameters.  
  *Quick check*: Compare speed and accuracy of 2D+TCN against a 3D CNN on fixed‑length clips.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Facial pipeline: Input Frame → EfficientNet Embedding → Confidence Estimator → {High → Direct Prediction; Low → MLP (with GLA)} → Temporal Smoothing → Audio Fusion → Final Prediction  
VD pipeline: Input Clip → ConvNeXt‑T Encoder → TCN → Violence Classifier

### Critical Path
A frame proceeds through embedding, confidence evaluation, either direct output or MLP classification, then temporal smoothing and audio fusion. The confidence check and fallback MLP are central to handling uncertainty.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Speed vs Accuracy**: High‑confidence frames bypass the MLP, saving compute; but a too‑high threshold may misroute borderline cases.  
- **Simplicity vs Calibration**: GLA improves class balance but requires a validation set for bias tuning.  
- **Modality Dependence**: Audio fusion boosts performance but depends on audio availability and quality; missing audio needs a fallback.

### Failure Signatures
- Large proportion of low‑confidence frames overloading the MLP, indicating distribution shift.  
- Excessive temporal smoothing causing lag in expression updates.  
- Audio noise or desynchronization degrading fusion outputs.  
- For VD, poor performance on clips with motion patterns unseen during training, suggesting over‑reliance on short‑term cues.

### First Three Experiments
1. **Component Isolation**: Evaluate using only the pre‑trained model, only the MLP, and the combined confidence‑routed pipeline to measure synergy.  
2. **GLA Ablation**: Train the MLP without GLA and compare per‑class metrics to confirm its role in balancing performance.  
3. **Audio Fusion Robustness**: Test the system with audio removed, with clean audio, and with corrupted audio to quantify the contribution and sensitivity of the audio branch.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Do self‑supervised pretrained facial embeddings improve EXPR/VA/AU performance over current supervised ones?  
- **Basis**: [explicit] Conclusion identifies “stronger self‑supervised pretraining” as a key future direction.  
- **Why unresolved**: The pipeline relies on supervised AffectNet‑pretrained models; the potential gains from self‑supervised pretraining on large‑scale in‑the‑wild video remain unexplored.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Pre‑train a self‑supervised encoder (e.g., MAE) on unlabeled frames from AffWild2, extract embeddings, train the MLP and apply smoothing/GLA. Compare macro F1 for EXPR/AU and CCC for VA against the supervised baseline on the validation set.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can calibration for label bias be made adaptive to new domains without requiring labeled validation data?  
- **Basis**: [inferred] The Generalized Logit Adjustment (GLA) method requires a validation set to tune biases; the paper’s conclusion calls for “domain‑adaptive calibration”.  
- **Why unresolved**: GLA biases optimized on the AffWild2 validation set may not generalize to other datasets or deployment scenarios with different label distributions, limiting practicality.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Design a method to estimate target label distribution from unlabeled data (e.g., via pseudo‑labeling or clustering) and adjust biases accordingly. Validate by simulating domain shift (e.g., testing on a different affect dataset) and measuring performance changes.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Why do 3D video backbones underperform compared to 2D+TCN on the DVD violence detection dataset, and how can this gap be closed?  
- **Basis**: [inferred] Experimental results show 3D models pretrained on Kinetics achieve lower macro F1 than the ConvNeXt‑T+TCN baseline; the paper does not provide an explanation or investigation.  
- **Why unresolved**: DVD clips are short (32 frames) and the task may demand different temporal modeling than standard 3D architectures pretrained on action recognition, creating a mismatch.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct ablations varying clip length, pretrain 3D models on violence‑specific video data, or adjust temporal receptive fields (e.g., dilation). Compare with the 2D+TCN approach under identical training protocols to isolate factors causing underperformance.

## Limitations
- Validation‑only results; no test‑set evaluation limits claims about generalization.  
- No ablation studies to isolate contributions of key components (GLA, temporal smoothing, audio fusion).  
- Absence of statistical analysis (confidence intervals, significance tests) for reported improvements.

## Confidence

- **VA mean CCC (0.562)**: **Medium** – similar concerns; unclear how CCC is aggregated across frames/subjects.  
- **AU macro F1 (54.7%)**: **Medium** – no per‑AU breakdown or significance testing.
