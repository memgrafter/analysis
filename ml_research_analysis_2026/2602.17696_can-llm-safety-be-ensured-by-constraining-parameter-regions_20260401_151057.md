# Can LLM Safety Be Ensured by Constraining Parameter Regions?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17696
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17696
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Current methods for identifying "safety regions" in LLM parameter space show low to moderate overlap across diverse safety datasets, and this overlap deteriorates further when isolating safety from utility functionality, challenging the feasibility of stable parameter-based safety constraints.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether well-defined, dataset-agnostic "safety regions" exist within large language model (LLM) parameters. Evaluating four representative identification methods (SNIP & Wanda, SafeNeuron, SafeLayer, NLSR) across multiple model families and ten safety datasets, the core metric is *convergent identifiability*—the degree of overlap (IoU) in regions identified from different safety datasets. Findings reveal only low to moderate overlap (IoU 0.28–0.72). Furthermore, when attempting to isolate safety-specific parameters by subtracting regions identified from utility data, overlap decreases substantially, indicating deep entanglement between safety and general capabilities. The results suggest that current parameter-constraint approaches for ensuring LLM safety lack a stable foundation, as the identified regions are highly sensitive to the choice of identification data.

## Method Summary
The study systematically evaluates the convergent identifiability of safety regions identified by four distinct methods operating at different granularities: parameter-level (SNIP & Wanda), neuron-level (SafeNeuron), layer-level (SafeLayer), and LoRA-level (NLSR). For each method and model (six instruct-tuned LLMs), safety regions are derived from ten diverse multi-category safety identification datasets. Overlap between regions from all dataset pairs is quantified using Intersection over Union (IoU). A second analysis computes "Iso-Utility IoU" by first subtracting a utility region (identified from non-harmful queries) from each safety region. Additionally, for single-category datasets, the correlation between dataset semantic similarity and pairwise Iso-Utility IoU is measured. SafeLayer could not be reliably reproduced and is excluded from main results.

## Key Results
- Safety regions identified from different datasets exhibit low to moderate overlap (multi-dataset IoU range: 0.28–0.72), indicating poor convergent identifiability.
- Overlap drops substantially after utility isolation; Iso-Utility IoU decreases significantly (e.g., ~38% reduction for SNIP), demonstrating strong safety-utility entanglement.
- Method-specific patterns emerge: SNIP & Wanda's IoU correlates with dataset semantic similarity (Pearson *r* up to 0.77), while NLSR shows extreme dataset dependence (IoU near 0.01 on Mistral).
- SafeLayer could not be reliably reproduced, highlighting implementation fragility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Identified safety regions are not dataset-agnostic; they depend on the specific harmful queries used for identification.
- **Mechanism:** The paper evaluates multiple identification datasets across four methods. Overlap between regions derived from different datasets is low to moderate (IoU 0.28–0.72), indicating that the parameter subsets flagged as "safety-critical" shift with the input data distribution.
- **Core assumption:** A true, intrinsic safety region would be consistently identified regardless of the safety dataset used.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "identified safety regions exhibit only low to moderate overlap"
  - [section 5.1] Table 2 shows IoU values for 10 datasets; overlap drops further after utility isolation.
  - [corpus] Nearby work on "safety regions" (e.g., SafeNeuron, SNIP & Wanda) assumes a stable locus, but this study’s multi-dataset tests contradict that assumption.
- **Break condition:** If future work finds a method where IoU converges to >0.9 across semantically diverse safety datasets, this mechanism would be weakened.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Safety regions are entangled with utility functionality; isolating purely safety-specific parameters reduces overlap even more.
- **Mechanism:** For each safety region, the authors subtract a utility region (identified via non-harmful queries). The resulting utility-isolated safety regions show poorer convergent identifiability (IoU drops substantially, e.g., 38% reduction for SNIP).
- **Core assumption:** Safety and utility computations share parameter substrate; thus, regions identified via safety data also capture utility-related parameters.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "safety regions have a non-negligible overlap with utility regions"
  - [section 5.1] "IoU decreases substantially for all methods" after utility isolation.
  - [corpus] Consistent with prior observations of safety-utility entanglement (Wei et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025).
- **Break condition:** Discovery of a utility-isolation method that yields high IoU (e.g., >0.8) across datasets would challenge this entanglement claim.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Method-specific sensitivities exist: parameter-level methods (SNIP/Wanda) show correlation with dataset semantics, while neuron-level (SafeNeuron) and LoRA-level (NLSR) show extreme dataset dependence or weak correlation.
- **Mechanism:** With single-category datasets, SNIP/Wanda’s IoU correlates with semantic similarity (Pearson *r* up to 0.77). SafeNeuron shows weak correlation (*r* 0.12–0.28). NLSR yields near-zero IoU (0.01) on Mistral, suggesting high dataset sensitivity.
- **Core assumption:** Different granularities capture different aspects of model computation; some may be more data-dependent.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.2] Fig. 3 shows correlation analysis; SNIP/Wanda show stronger link between cosine similarity and Iso-Utility IoU.
  - [section 5.1] "NLSR shows extreme dataset dependence (IoU near 0.01)"
  - [corpus] SafeLayer could not be reproduced, indicating method-specific reproducibility issues.
- **Break condition:** If neuron-level or LoRA-level methods demonstrate high IoU and strong semantic correlation across models, this granularity-dependent pattern would need revision.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Convergent Identifiability**
  - *Why needed here:* Central criterion for a meaningful safety region—it must be identifiable across diverse safety datasets, not just one.
  - *Quick check question:* If you identify a safety region using Dataset A and Dataset B, what metric would you compute to test convergence? (Answer: IoU across regions.)
- **Concept: IoU (Intersection over Union)**
  - *Why needed here:* Quantifies overlap between sets of identified safety components (parameters, neurons, etc.). Higher IoU suggests more consistent regions.
  - *Quick check question:* What does an IoU of 0.4 imply about the consistency of two safety regions? (Answer: Only 40% of the union of the two sets overlap.)
- **Concept: Safety-Utility Entanglement**
  - *Why needed here:* Safety behaviors rely on both recognizing harm (utility) and refusing (safety). Overlap in parameter use complicates isolating purely safety-critical parameters.
  - *Quick check question:* How might you operationally define a "utility-isolated safety region"? (Answer: Safety region minus a region identified from utility data.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input (Safety Identification Datasets) -> Method (SNIP/Wanda, SafeNeuron, NLSR, SafeLayer) -> Scoring & Selection -> Safety Region (Set of indices) -> Evaluation (Compute IoU & Iso-Utility IoU) -> Results.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Choose target LLM and method.
  2. Prepare *n* identification datasets (ensure variety in harm categories or semantic similarity).
  3. For each dataset, run method to extract safety region.
  4. Compute pairwise IoU across all regions; optionally compute utility-isolated IoU.
  5. Repeat for different model families, thresholds.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Granularity:* Parameter-level offers fine control but may be noisy; layer-level is coarse but potentially more stable (though SafeLayer was irreproducible here).
  - *Dataset diversity:* More diverse datasets test convergent identifiability rigorously but may yield lower IoU.
  - *Threshold selection:* Fixed *q%* (SNIP/Wanda) or *m* (SafeNeuron) affects region size; thresholds from original papers used, but sensitivity not fully explored.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low IoU (<0.5) across datasets → no convergent identifiability.
  - Large drop in IoU after utility isolation → strong safety-utility entanglement.
  - Inconsistent results across model families → method not robust.
  - Reproduction failure (SafeLayer) → implementation or evaluation fragility.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Multi-category IoU:** Run all four methods on 10 diverse safety datasets; report IoU range (Table 2). Expected: low-moderate overlap.
  2. **Utility-isolation:** Subtract utility region from each safety region; recompute IoU. Expected: substantial IoU drop.
  3. **Single-category correlation:** For SNIP/Wanda and SafeNeuron, use per-category datasets; compute Pearson *r* between dataset semantic cosine similarity and pairwise Iso-Utility IoU (Fig. 3). Expected: SNIP/Wanda show stronger correlation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Do stable, dataset-agnostic safety regions exist in LLM parameter space, or are safety behaviors distributed across entangled representations? *Basis:* Paper directly questions existence; found low IoU (0.28–0.72). *Why unresolved:* Current methods fail tests, but could reflect methodological limits. *Resolving evidence:* Method with IoU >0.9 across diverse datasets and causal validation.
- **Open Question 2:** What alternative theoretical frameworks can characterize LLM safety if parameter-level safety regions do not exist? *Basis:* Paper notes weight-space approaches fail. *Why unresolved:* No exploration of alternatives. *Resolving evidence:* Consistent safety manipulation via representational/activation/circuit interventions.
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent do identified safety regions correspond to actual causal mechanisms for safety behavior, versus statistical correlations with specific dataset artifacts? *Basis:* Inferred from dataset-semantic correlation (SNIP/Wanda *r* up to 0.77). *Why unresolved:* No causality tests. *Resolving evidence:* Causal interventions on regions from one dataset tested on held-out harm categories showing predictable effects.
- **Open Question 4:** Can safety constraints be effectively applied in practice if safety regions are unstable across datasets, or must safety preservation rely on entirely different mechanisms? *Basis:* Paper notes unstable regions pose challenges for reliable constraints. *Why unresolved:* No tests on downstream fine-tuning or jailbreaking. *Resolving evidence:* Large-scale fine-tuning experiments using constraints from various datasets, measuring utility retention and robustness.
- **Open Question 5:** Do compensatory mechanisms exist that maintain safety when identified safety regions are modified, and can they be leveraged to design more robust safety systems? *Basis:* Inferred suggestion of functional compensation. *Why unresolved:* No tests for redundancy/adaptability. *Resolving evidence:* Progressive damage to identified regions while monitoring activation changes and safety maintenance.

## Limitations
- Dataset composition unknown: Exact safety identification datasets (including D₀) are not released; sampling variance may alter IoU results.
- NLSR hyperparameters underspecified: LoRA fine-tuning details (rank, learning rate, epochs) are inferred from related work, risking implementation drift.
- SafeLayer irreproducible: Authors themselves failed to replicate; suggests fragility or missing details.

## Confidence
- Dataset-agnostic instability (low-moderate IoU): **Medium‑High** – supported by multi-dataset experiments but limited by non-public data.
- Safety-utility entanglement (Iso-Utility IoU drop): **High** – consistent, substantial reduction observed for all methods.
- Granularity-specific sensitivities (SNIP/Wanda correlation vs. NLSR extremes): **Medium** – trends present but could be confounded by implementation choices.
- SafeLayer reproducibility failure: **High** – explicitly reported; no code released.

## Next Checks
1. **Full dataset disclosure:** Authors should publish the exact safety splits, random seeds, and embedding similarity matrices to enable exact IoU replication.
2. **Threshold sweep study:** For each method, recompute IoU and Iso-Utility IoU across a wide range of region sizes (e.g., top 0.5%–10% parameters, 500–5000 neurons, LoRA magnitude percentiles) to assess robustness to threshold selection.
3. **Cross-architecture generalization:** Repeat the evaluation on larger models (e.g., Llama-2-70B, GPT-3.5) and diverse architectures (encoder‑decoder, MoE) to test whether findings hold beyond the current model set.
