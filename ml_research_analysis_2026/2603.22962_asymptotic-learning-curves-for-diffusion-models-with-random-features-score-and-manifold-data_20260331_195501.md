# Asymptotic Learning Curves for Diffusion Models with Random Features Score and Manifold Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22962
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22962
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** For linear manifolds, the sample complexity to learn the diffusion score scales linearly with intrinsic dimension D, not ambient dimension d; a memorization phase transition occurs at p/d = n/d.

## Executive Summary
This work provides an asymptotic analysis of denoising score matching for diffusion models when data lies on a low-dimensional manifold and the score is parameterized by a random feature neural network (RFNN). In a high-dimensional limit where ambient dimension d, manifold dimension D, sample size n, and RFNN width p grow proportionally, the authors derive exact expressions for test, train, and score errors. For linear manifolds, the sample complexity scales with the intrinsic dimension D. A phase transition at p/d = n/d separates a generalization regime from memorization. The score error is connected to the free energy of a generalized linear model, linking it to the minimum mean squared error of an associated channel. For nonlinear manifolds, the intrinsic dimension benefit diminishes.

## Method Summary
The analysis uses a high-dimensional asymptotic framework (d, D, n, p → ∞ with fixed ratios ψ_D = D/d, ψ_n = n/d, ψ_p = p/d). Data is generated from a hidden manifold model: x = σ(M/√D ξ) with ξ ~ N(0,I_D), where σ controls manifold nonlinearity and M is a random projection. The score is modeled as an RFNN: s_A(x) = A√p ϱ(W/√d x) with fixed random first-layer weights W. The denoising score matching objective reduces to a ridge regression problem (closed-form optimal A). Errors are expressed via deterministic equivalents of random matrix transforms, leading to self-consistent equations for auxiliary variables (ζ₁…ζ₄). The score error decomposes into a term proportional to the test error and an exact test error term derived from the GLM free energy (Barbier et al., 2019), which for linear manifolds simplifies via the Marčenko–Pastur distribution.

## Key Results
- For linear manifolds (σ = identity), the sample complexity to achieve a target score error scales linearly with the intrinsic dimension D, not the ambient dimension d.
- A memorization phase transition occurs when the ratio ψ_p (model width relative to dimension) equals ψ_n (sample size relative to dimension); above this, the RFNN fits the empirical optimal score including noise.
- For nonlinear manifolds (σ nonlinear), the advantage of low intrinsic dimension is reduced; error curves for different ψ_D collapse toward the isotropic (full-rank) case.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
For linear manifolds, the sample complexity to learn the diffusion score scales with intrinsic dimension D, not ambient dimension d. The RFNN parameterization and denoising score matching objective effectively reduce the problem's dimensionality because the exact score has a sparse structure along orthogonal manifold directions. The self-consistent equations (Theorem 2) show that test/train errors depend on ψ_D = D/d, not d directly. When σ is linear, the data covariance along the manifold is low-rank, and error terms involve traces that scale with D. This relies on the high-dimensional limit and linear manifold structure (σ(x)=x). Evidence: Abstract states the intrinsic dimension scaling; Section 3.5 notes sample complexity grows approximately linearly with ψ_D. The benefit vanishes for nonlinear σ because the data covariance becomes full-rank (Lemma 1).

### Mechanism 2
A phase transition occurs at ψ_p = ψ_n (p/d = n/d), separating generalization (low score error) from memorization (high score error). The ratio ψ_p/ψ_n controls RFNN capacity relative to data. Below the threshold, the RFNN can represent the exact score's orthogonal components; above it, overparameterization permits fitting the empirical optimal score (Eq. 13), including noise. This is visible in test/train error curves (Fig. 1) and the qualitative change in solutions for K(q,z). The transition smooths for larger t and with nonzero regularization λ. Core assumption: independent RFNN per diffusion time step. Evidence: Section 3.5 describes the threshold; Fig. 1 shows divergence of test/train curves near ψ_p = ψ_n.

### Mechanism 3
The score error decomposes as E_test = h_t E_score + E_test^*, with E_test^* linked to the MMSE of a Gaussian channel and computable via the free energy of a generalized linear model (GLM). Lemma 4 gives the bias-variance split; E_test^* depends on the manifold's effective signal-to-noise ratio (a_t²/h_t). For linear σ, it connects to the Marčenko–Pastur transform (Eq. 16). For nonlinear σ, one must solve the replica-symmetric GLM free energy (Lemma 3), capturing the folded manifold geometry via ν₁ = E[gσ(g)]. This GLM equivalence holds specifically for the random feature model. Assumptions: valid asymptotic limits for GLM and Nishimori symmetry. Evidence: Section 3.3 and Appendix A.2 derive the connection explicitly.

## Foundational Learning
- **Denoising Score Matching (DSM):** Needed because the learning objective optimizes the DSM loss (Eq. 5), a tractable surrogate for score matching that unbiasedly estimates the score by denoising. *Quick check:* How does the weighting w(t)=h_t/d in Eq. 4 arise from the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, and why is it critical for unbiased estimation?
- **Random Feature Neural Network (RFNN):** Needed because the score is parameterized as a fixed random feature map (first layer frozen), simplifying analysis via random matrix theory and controlling capacity via ψ_p. It implicitly approximates a kernel. *Quick check:* Why does freezing the first layer make analysis amenable to deterministic equivalents, and what kernel does the RFNN correspond to?
- **Hidden Manifold Model (HMM):** Needed to generate data on a D-dimensional manifold in ℝ^d. The smoothness of σ and the random linear projection M define the manifold's geometry, directly entering error formulas through ν₁ and ψ_D. *Quick check:* Which parameters control linearity vs. nonlinearity, and how do they appear in Theorem 2's equations?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data generator (HMM: ξ ~ N(0,I_D) → x = σ(M/√D ξ)) → Score model (RFNN: s_A(x)=A√p ϱ(W/√d x)) → Loss function (DSM loss L(At) with λ, h_t) → Solver (closed-form optimal At via ridge regression, Eq. 15) → Evaluators (Test/Train/Score errors via deterministic equivalents and GLM free energy).
- **Critical path:** 1) Define high-dimensional limit (fixed ψ_D, ψ_n, ψ_p). 2) Express errors as rational functions of random matrices. 3) Compute deterministic equivalents using feature moments (Mehler kernel, CLT). 4) Solve self-consistent equations for ζ₁…ζ₄ (Section 3.2). 5) Compute K and its derivatives to get asymptotic E[E_test], E[E_train]. 6) Use Lemma 4 and GLM free energy for E_score. 7) Analyze dependence on ψ_D, ψ_p, ψ_n, and ν₁.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Linear vs. nonlinear manifold: linear σ gives clean E_score ~ ψ_D scaling; nonlinear (ν₁<1) degrades benefit. Width vs. samples: ψ_p < ψ_n favors generalization; ψ_p > ψ_n risks memorization. Regularization λ increases effective noise in resolvent (s²), delaying memorization. Activation ϱ affects c(γ) and μ₁; theory holds for Lipschitz ϱ with zero mean.
- **Failure signatures:** High ψ_D: score error plateaus, insensitive to n. ψ_p >> ψ_n: score error rises with ψ_p. λ → 0: memorization transition sharp. Nonlinear σ with high power: all ψ_D curves converge to isotropic behavior.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Reproduce Fig 1: Train RFNNs on linear HMM (σ(x)=x) with ψ_D∈{0.01,0.1,0.99}, ψ_n=10, sweep ψ_p across ψ_n; verify test/train errors match Theorem 2 and diverge at ψ_p≈ψ_n. 2) Test nonlinear manifold: use σ(x)=0.975x+0.223(x²−1)/√2; repeat experiment 1 and show collapse of ψ_D curves relative to linear case (Fig. 2b). 3) Vary λ: fix ψ_D=0.1, ψ_n=10, sweep ψ_p for λ∈{0,0.01,0.1}; measure h_t²·score error and verify λ smooths/shifts memorization threshold.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can the asymptotic learning curves be extended to fully trained neural networks (beyond random features) to capture feature learning and potentially lower sample complexity?
- How do different noise schedules (e.g., non-isotropic or data-dependent perturbations) affect sample complexity and generalization for manifold data?
- Can the asymptotic results be turned into non-asymptotic finite-sample guarantees for diffusion models on manifold data?
- Does the phase transition at p/d = n/d persist when using a single time-dependent score network (shared parameters across time) rather than independent RFNNs per time step?

## Limitations
- The high-dimensional asymptotic may not hold for moderate ambient dimension d, affecting finite-sample sample complexity estimates.
- The random feature assumption limits direct applicability to trained diffusion U-nets where features are learned.
- Analysis of nonlinear manifolds relies on the replica symmetric GLM free energy, whose validity beyond the asymptotic limit is not proven.
- The memorization phase transition appears sharp only as λ→0 and may be smoothed by implicit regularization in practice.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1 (Linear manifold sample complexity scales with intrinsic dimension D):** High (explicitly proven via Theorem 2 and deterministic equivalents).
- **Claim 2 (Phase transition at ψ_p = ψ_n):** Medium (supported by self-consistent equations and Fig 1, but transition smooths for larger t and finite λ).
- **Claim 3 (Score error decomposition linking to GLM MMSE):** High (underpinned by Lemma 4 and established GLM free energy results).
