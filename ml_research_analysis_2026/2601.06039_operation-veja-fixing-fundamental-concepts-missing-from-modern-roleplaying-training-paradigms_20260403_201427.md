# Operation Veja: Fixing Fundamental Concepts Missing from Modern Roleplaying Training Paradigms

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06039
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06039
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** In a pilot study, VEJA-grounded dialogues for Makise Kurisu were significantly preferred over a state‑of‑the‑art synthetic baseline (43 wins vs. 28 losses, 29 ties) in an LLM‑as‑judge pairwise comparison.

## Executive Summary
Modern roleplaying models often fail to produce believable characters because they lack explicit modeling of internal value systems and deliberative reasoning. This paper introduces the VEJA framework (Values, Experiences, Judgments, Abilities) as a new data curation paradigm designed to ground character authenticity. In a pilot study, manually curated dialogues for Makise Kurisu using a VEJA profile were pitted against synthetic baselines; the VEJA version was consistently preferred by an LLM judge, with qualitative improvements in narrative continuity, nuanced reactions, and coherent value‑conflict resolution. The findings highlight a qualitative gap in current synthetic approaches and suggest a path toward more psychologically plausible roleplaying agents.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a controlled comparison between two datasets for the character Makise Kurisu. The synthetic baseline used Gemini Pro 2.5 to generate dialogues based on a predefined 10‑day story timeline and 20 user interaction scenarios. The VEJA dataset was created by human writers guided by a structured profile containing Values, Experiences, Judgments, and Abilities, using the same timeline and scenarios. Both datasets were evaluated through 100 blind pairwise comparisons by an LLM‑as‑judge (Gemini 2.5 Flash) using a prompt from Appendix A.1, with the primary metric being preference for more in‑character, consistent, and believable responses.

## Key Results
- VEJA‑grounded dialogues achieved a higher win rate (43 wins vs. 28 losses, 29 ties) over the synthetic baseline.
- The LLM judge frequently cited superior narrative continuity, nuanced reactions, and coherent value‑conflict resolution as reasons for preferring the VEJA responses.
- In particular, the baseline showed failures such as “overly mean” or “over‑sharing” behavior, indicating poor handling of value conflicts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper proposes three mechanisms linking the VEJA framework to improved character authenticity:

1. **Explicit Values and Experiences prevent over‑indexing on isolated traits.** Past Experiences shape a character’s core Values; together they inform situation‑specific Judgments, which are then expressed through Abilities during dialogue. This causal chain grounds responses in an internal deliberative process rather than reactive pattern‑matching. *Core assumption:* Human writers can reliably map a predefined VEJA profile to appropriate Judgments across varied contexts. *Evidence:* [abstract] VEJA addresses systemic failures; [section 2.2] describes the decontextualization problem; [corpus] notes grounding in verifiable context. *Break condition:* Inconsistent or sparse Values/Experiences lead to shallow or inconsistent dialogue.

2. **Judgments enable nuanced value conflict resolution.** When facing competing values (e.g., curiosity vs guardedness), the character consults pre‑formed Judgments—heuristics derived from past Experiences—allowing a pre‑negotiated balance rather than an extreme or ad‑hoc reaction. *Core assumption:* Relevant Judgments can be retrieved and applied to novel situations if properly grounded. *Evidence:* [section 1] frames human interaction as constant negotiation of conflicting values; [section 4.3] the judge cited “coherent value‑conflict resolution”; [section A.2] baseline flagged as “overly mean” or “over‑sharing”. *Break condition:* Novel value conflicts not reflected in existing Judgments may cause reversion to a single dominant value.

3. **Abilities as a filter ensure dialogue respects the character’s skill set.** Abilities act as a content filter, limiting expressed topics to the defined competency and influencing which Experiences and Judgments become relevant. *Core assumption:* Characters are more believable when operating within a well‑defined scope of competence. *Evidence:* [section 3.4] defines Abilities; [section 4.3] judge noted alignment with Kurisu’s research interests and scientific language; [section A.2] baseline introduced irrelevant personal topics. *Break condition:* Abilities too broad lead to unrealistic omniscience; too narrow cause monotonous dialogue.

## Foundational Learning
- **Internal State Modeling (Beliefs, Desires, Intentions).** Understanding VEJA requires distinguishing internal drivers from observable behavior. *Quick check:* What is the difference between a character’s Judgment and the Value that underlies it?

- **Value Conflict and Deliberation.** The thesis hinges on characters negotiating competing values; without this, the importance of VEJA’s structure is lost. *Quick check:* Give an example where honesty clashes with kindness, and describe a possible Judgment that resolves it.

- **Data Curation Paradigms for Roleplaying.** Familiarity with how datasets are generated (manual vs synthetic) is necessary to evaluate the paper’s claims. *Quick check:* Why might large‑scale synthetic data generation hit a quality ceiling for character depth?

## Architecture Onboarding
The VEJA framework comprises four interconnected nodes: Values, Experiences, Judgments, Abilities, forming a directed cycle: Experiences → Values → Judgments → Abilities → dialogue. New Experiences from interactions can refine the cycle.

**Critical Path:** 1. Define a coherent VEJA profile (specify V, E, J, A). 2. Develop writer guidelines mapping the profile to dialogue generation. 3. Generate initial dialogue corpus. 4. Evaluate with pairwise LLM‑as‑judge; analyze failures. 5. Iterate on profile and guidelines. (Future work aims to automate step 2.)

**Design Tradeoffs:** Manual curation yields high quality but does not scale; semi‑automated generation risks re‑introducing synthetic biases. Depth of Experiences versus writer cognitive load; profile size versus maintainability. Automated evaluation is cheap but inherits LLM biases; human evaluation is costly but more reliable.

**Failure Signatures:** (a) Character repeatedly over‑indexes on a single value; (b) Dialogue references Experiences not present in the profile or contradicts stated Values; (c) Character displays knowledge outside defined Abilities; (d) Absence of value trade‑offs (black‑and‑white choices); (e) Generic, context‑insensitive responses that do not reflect the VEJA profile.

**First Experiments:** 1. **Cross‑character generalisation:** Apply the VEJA curation process to a character from a different genre (e.g., a fantasy rogue) and compare against a synthetic baseline. 2. **Component ablation:** Remove one VEJA component (e.g., no Experiences) from writer instructions; measure impact on judge preferences and specific quality metrics. 3. **Evaluation calibration:** Compare LLM‑as‑judge preferences with human expert ratings on a shared subset to quantify alignment and identify systematic biases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can the VEJA data curation process be automated or scaled without prohibitive human labor?**  
   *Basis:* The paper acknowledges manual curation is labor‑intensive; future work mentions semi‑automated methods. *Why unresolved:* Nuanced human judgment is hard to replicate. *Needed evidence:* Benchmarked semi‑automated pipelines that approach human‑curated quality.

2. **What objective metrics can measure character authenticity (e.g., value‑conflict resolution, experiential recall) beyond LLM‑based preference?**  
   *Basis:* The conclusion calls for metrics beyond preference scores. *Why unresolved:* Current evaluation does not isolate specific VEJA‑driven traits. *Needed evidence:* Benchmark tasks with known ground truth and automated scorers correlating with human authenticity judgments.

3. **What are the individual and combinatorial contributions of each VEJA component?**  
   *Basis:* The paper notes ablation studies were not conducted. *Why unresolved:* Components are interconnected but relative importance unknown. *Needed evidence:* Controlled experiments removing specific components and measuring impact on authenticity scores.

4. **Does the VEJA framework generalize to diverse characters (different personalities, genres, cultures)?**  
   *Basis:* Pilot focused on a single archetype (Makise Kurisu). *Why unresolved:* Success may be specific to her traits; other characters may have different value conflicts. *Needed evidence:* Replication with multiple, diverse characters from various sources.

5. **Can synthetic generation ever match human‑curated VEJA data in value‑conflict resolution and experiential grounding?**  
   *Basis:* The paper suggests a quality gap and implies human writers may be necessary for the next leap. *Why unresolved:* It is unclear if the gap is temporary or inherent. *Needed evidence:* Iterative experiments with increasingly capable LLMs guided by detailed VEJA profiles, evaluated against the human gold standard.

## Limitations
- The exact VEJA profile for Makise Kurisu and the synthetic baseline generation prompts are not disclosed, hindering precise replication.
- The pilot study involved a single character and only 100 pairwise comparisons, limiting statistical power and generalizability.
- Evaluation relied solely on an LLM‑as‑judge without calibration against human experts, introducing potential biases.

## Confidence
- **VEJA improves authenticity over baseline:** Medium confidence (small‑scale but significant result, single character).
- **Mechanistic claims (causal chain of VEJA components):** Low‑Medium confidence (qualitative support, no ablation or process tracing).
- **Baseline limitation (synthetic data omits deliberative modeling):** Medium confidence (aligns with known data quality issues but not directly proven).

## Next Checks
1. **Replication via reconstruction** – Re‑create Makise Kurisu’s VEJA profile and baseline prompts from canonical sources; run the full pilot to recover the reported win rate (43–28).
2. **Component ablation study** – Remove one VEJA component from writer guidelines (e.g., no Experiences) and measure degradation in judge preferences and specific quality metrics (e.g., conflict resolution mentions, continuity errors).
3. **Judge calibration** – Compute agreement between the LLM‑as‑judge and human experts (n≥3) on a shared subset (e.g., 50 dialogues); identify systematic LLM biases.
