# Project and Generate: Divergence-Free Neural Operators for Incompressible Flows
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24500
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24500
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A hard-constraint framework using differentiable spectral Leray projection and divergence-free Gaussian reference measure achieves near-zero divergence error (~10⁻⁷) and significantly improved long-term rollout stability on 2D Navier-Stokes (Re=1000).

## Executive Summary
Learning-based fluid dynamics models often violate the incompressible continuity equation (∇·u = 0), leading to unstable simulations. This paper introduces a hard-constraint framework that enforces incompressibility both for deterministic predictions and generative modeling. The core is a differentiable spectral Leray projection, derived from the Helmholtz–Hodge decomposition, which maps any velocity field onto the divergence-free subspace. For generation, the authors construct a divergence-free Gaussian reference measure by pushing forward a smooth stream function via the curl operator, ensuring that the entire probability path remains physically admissible. On 2D Navier-Stokes at Re=1000, the method achieves near-zero divergence error (∼10⁻⁷), reduces long-term rollout mean squared error compared to unconstrained baselines, and correctly preserves turbulent energy spectra.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework combines three technical ingredients: (1) a spectral Leray projection applied to the output of any neural operator to guarantee divergence-free predictions; (2) a generative prior defined by sampling a smooth stream function from a Gaussian measure and applying the curl to obtain a divergence-free velocity field; and (3) a flow matching objective that learns a deterministic velocity field transporting the prior to the data distribution while respecting the divergence-free constraint. The projection is differentiable and exact for periodic domains, and the generative path is constructed so that every intermediate velocity field lies in the divergence-free subspace. This yields models that are both accurate and stable for long-term simulations.

## Key Results
- Near-zero divergence error (∼10⁻⁷) on held-out test data.
- Significantly lower long-term rollout mean squared error than unconstrained baselines.
- Preservation of the correct enstrophy (energy) spectrum in generated trajectories.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The differentiable spectral Leray projection enforces exact incompressibility (∇·u=0) for all deterministic model outputs by construction, mapping any intermediate prediction onto the divergence-free subspace V.
- **Mechanism:** The Leray projection P is the L²-orthogonal projection onto V, derived from the Helmholtz–Hodge decomposition. For any unconstrained velocity field w, Pw removes the irrotational (gradient) component, yielding the unique divergence-free field closest in L² norm. Its spectral implementation uses Fourier transforms: for wavevector k≠0, it applies the matrix I - (k⊗k)/||k||² to the Fourier coefficients of w, zeroing the zero mode.
- **Core assumption:** The periodic domain and Fourier basis correctly represent the Leray projection operator; the FFT implementation is numerically stable and differentiable.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "integrate a differentiable spectral Leray projection grounded in the Helmholtz–Hodge decomposition"
  - [section 4.1]: Detailed spectral formula (Eq. 6) and Algorithm 3.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "Divergence-Free Diffusion Models" and "HDNet" also use Helmholtz decomposition, indicating community recognition of this mechanism for constraint enforcement.
- **Break condition:** Numerical drift in FFT (e.g., due to finite-precision errors) or mishandling of the zero mode could introduce small divergence; incorrect handling of boundary conditions (non-periodic) would invalidate the spectral approach.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Constructing the reference Gaussian measure via a curl (∇⊥) pushforward from a stream function space ensures the generative prior and all conditional samples lie exactly in V, maintaining subspace consistency throughout the probability flow.
- **Mechanism:** A scalar stream function ψ is sampled from a Gaussian measure on the space of zero-mean H¹ functions. The velocity is defined as u = ∇⊥ψ. By Lemma 4.1, this mapping is linear, bounded, and has image exactly V. Thus, any Gaussian pushforward (∇⊥)#γ_ψ is a Gaussian measure supported on V.
- **Core assumption:** The stream function Gaussian measure is correctly defined (e.g., covariance operator yields sufficiently smooth samples); the differentiation for ∇⊥ is accurate on the discrete grid.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3]: "construct a divergence-free Gaussian reference measure via a curl-based pushforward" and Lemma 4.1.
  - [corpus]: "HelmFluid" uses Helmholtz decomposition for prediction, showing related use of stream-function formulations.
  - [corpus evidence weakness]: No direct neighbor validates the *Gaussian measure construction* via curl pushforward specifically; this is a novel contribution of the paper.
- **Break condition:** If the stream function sampling produces non-smooth ψ (e.g., insufficient smoothing), the resulting u may have high-frequency artifacts; discretization errors in spatial gradients could introduce tiny divergence.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** With a divergence-free reference measure and projected vector field, the functional flow matching objective yields a well-defined probability path entirely within V, ensuring that the generative model's learned dynamics respect incompressibility at every intermediate state.
- **Mechanism:** In standard flow matching, the target velocity field along the interpolation path must be divergence-free when both endpoints are in V. Composing the unconstrained neural vector field vθ with P ensures v_τ(x) ∈ V. The reference measure being on V means conditional paths (μ^y_τ) are supported on V. Under absolute continuity (data distribution on Cameron–Martin space), Theorem 4.2 guarantees existence of a generating vector field v_τ that is the ν-average of conditional velocities, hence also in V.
- **Core assumption:** The data distribution ν is supported on the Cameron–Martin space H_μ0 of the reference Gaussian; all conditional measures μ^y_τ are mutually absolutely continuous with the marginal μ_τ.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3]: "ensuring the entire probability flow remains subspace-consistent by construction" and Theorem 4.2.
  - [abstract]: "ensuring the entire probability path remains physically admissible."
  - [corpus]: "Learning Flow Distributions via Projection-Constrained Diffusion" also addresses manifold-constrained generative flows, indicating the importance of consistent subspaces.
- **Break condition:** If ν has mass outside H_μ0 (e.g., contains very rough fields), absolute continuity may fail, potentially making the marginal path ill-defined or causing the learned v_τ to not generate the intended path. In practice, finite resolution mitigates this.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Helmholtz–Hodge Decomposition  
  - **Why needed here:** It mathematically justifies the Leray projection as the unique way to split a vector field into divergence-free and gradient components, providing the geometric foundation for the hard constraint.  
  - **Quick check question:** Given a periodic 2D vector field, what are the necessary and sufficient conditions on its Fourier coefficients for it to be divergence-free?
- **Concept:** Gaussian Measures on Hilbert Spaces  
  - **Why needed here:** The generative framework requires defining a noise prior that is a probability measure on the infinite-dimensional space V; Gaussian measures are the canonical choice with well-defined Cameron–Martin spaces.  
  - **Quick check question:** If γ is a Gaussian measure on a Hilbert space H with covariance C, what is the Cameron–Martin space H_γ and why does it determine the regularity of sample paths?
- **Concept:** Flow Matching (Continuous Normalizing Flows)  
  - **Why needed here:** The paper uses flow matching to learn a deterministic velocity field that transports a simple reference distribution to the data distribution; here it must be adapted to a constrained subspace.  
  - **Quick check question:** In standard flow matching, how is the target vector field along a linear interpolation path defined, and why is it critical that this field matches the model's output for training?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Base Neural Operator (fθ) → Spectral Leray Projector (P) → Divergence-free velocity (regression). For generation: Stream Function Sampler → ∇⊥ to obtain u⁰ → Interpolation with target y → fθ → P → integrated ODE.
- **Critical path (generation):**  
  1. Sample ψ ~ N(0, C_ψ) (with optional blur).  
  2. Compute u⁰ = ∇⊥ψ (divergence-free).  
  3. Interpolate u_τ = (1-τ)u⁰ + τ y (y from data).  
  4. Compute model velocity v_θ(u_τ, τ).  
  5. Project: v = P(v_θ).  
  6. Integrate ODE using v to generate final sample.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - FFT vs. other bases: FFT is O(N log N) and exact for periodic domains but does not generalize to complex boundaries; alternatives (e.g., finite-element solvers) trade speed for flexibility.  
  - Stream function covariance: Choice of C_ψ controls roughness of u⁰; must be aligned with dataset's energy spectrum to avoid poor alignment.  
  - Projection placement: Output projection is simplest; integrating projection internally (e.g., per layer) might improve trainability but increases complexity.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Divergence spikes: Even after projection, numerical errors (FFT, finite differences) may cause ∇·u > 1e-6. Check if P is applied correctly and if interpolation paths are truly in V.  
  - Spectral distortion: Enstrophy spectrum deviates from k⁻³ scaling, indicating loss of turbulent cascade; likely due to overly smooth stream function prior or aggressive projection dampening high frequencies.  
  - Rollout divergence: During long-term autoregressive prediction, errors accumulate despite projection. Might indicate the base operator fθ is not learning correct divergence-free dynamics; projection is only a corrective layer.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Divergence error benchmark:** Compute mean ||∇·u||² on a held-out set for both base and projected models; expect ~1e-7 for projected model (Table 1).  
  2. **Spectral fidelity:** Compare enstrophy spectra of generated rollouts against ground truth (Figure 4); projected models should match inertial range.  
  3. **Long-term rollout stability:** Run autoregressive simulation for T=300 steps; unconstrained models blow up (MSE → ∞), constrained models maintain bounded error (Table 1, Gen. Ours).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the proposed framework be effectively extended to three-dimensional turbulent flows and domains with complex (non-periodic) boundary conditions?
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion states: "While focusing on periodic domains, the principles established here provide a foundation for future extensions to complex boundary conditions and 3D fluid systems."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only evaluates the method on 2D periodic Navier-Stokes at Re=1000. Extensions to 3D and non-periodic geometries are not implemented or tested, leaving open whether the spectral Leray projection and curl-based Gaussian construction generalize without modification.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Applying the framework to 3D Navier-Stokes (e.g., using spectral methods on a 3D torus) and to domains with walls (requiring alternative projection operators, e.g., via finite elements) while measuring divergence error, stability, and spectral fidelity compared to baselines.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Under what conditions does the absolute continuity assumption (µᵧ_τ ≪ µ_τ) required for the existence of a divergence-free flow matching vector field hold in the infinite-dimensional limit for realistic fluid data distributions?
- **Basis in paper:** In Theorem D.1's proof discussion: "Verifying this condition directly is generally intractable, particularly in infinite-dimensional settings... A rigorous characterization of when such conditions hold in the infinite-dimensional limit is left for future work."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper assumes this condition for theoretical soundness but does not verify it for the actual data distribution ν. In practice, they operate on finite grids where absolute continuity automatically holds, but the infinite-dimensional justification remains open.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis linking the data covariance structure to the reference Gaussian's Cameron-Martin space, or empirical convergence studies showing that as grid resolution increases, the finite-dimensional approximations satisfy analogous conditions uniformly.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the method's performance in maintaining divergence-free constraints and physical fidelity scale with increasing Reynolds number, particularly in highly turbulent regimes where small-scale dynamics are more vigorous?
- **Basis in paper:** The experiments are conducted solely at Re=1000. While the method achieves near-zero divergence at this Re, higher Reynolds numbers involve more intense vorticity and finer scales, which may challenge the spectral projection's ability to eliminate divergence without over-damping or instability.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not test beyond Re=1000, so it's unclear whether the divergence errors remain as low (∼10⁻⁷) or if the energy spectrum preservation holds for higher Re where forward energy cascade is more pronounced.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation across a range of Reynolds numbers (e.g., Re=5000, 10000) on the same 2D setup, monitoring divergence norms, enstrophy spectra, and long-term rollout MSE to see if the hard constraint continues to provide stability advantages.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the spectral Leray projection be replaced with a differentiable, geometry-agnostic projection operator (e.g., based on finite elements) to handle arbitrary computational domains while preserving the hard incompressibility guarantee?
- **Basis in paper:** The method relies on Fourier-space implementation which inherently assumes periodic boundaries. The paper notes this as a limitation in the conclusion and Appendix A, suggesting generalizability but without implementation.
- **Why unresolved:** For real-world applications with complex boundaries (e.g., flow around an airfoil), periodic BCs are inappropriate. A different projection method is needed that is both differentiable and compatible with neural operator training, but the paper does not explore this.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Developing and integrating a finite-element-based Leray projection (or similar) into the neural operator framework, testing on a domain with non-periodic boundaries (e.g., lid-driven cavity), and comparing divergence errors and accuracy against the spectral version on periodic domains.

## Limitations
- Spectral Leray projection is limited to periodic domains; extension to complex geometries requires non-spectral, potentially less efficient projection operators.
- The stream function prior's covariance must be carefully tuned to match the data's energy spectrum; mismatched roughness may degrade high-frequency turbulent content.
- The absolute continuity condition underpinning the flow matching existence is not rigorously verified for realistic fluid data distributions, leaving theoretical gaps.

## Confidence
- Differentiable spectral Leray projection enforces exact incompressibility: **Medium** (validated on periodic 2D domains, but may not generalize to complex boundaries).
- Divergence-free Gaussian prior via curl pushforward: **Medium** (theoretically sound but novel and sensitive to covariance choice).
- Absolute continuity condition for flow matching: **Low-to-medium** (assumed but not proven for infinite-dimensional turbulent flows).
- Empirical success on 2D Navier-Stokes at Re=1000: **High** (near-zero divergence, stable rollouts, correct spectra).
