# A Function-Space Stability Boundary for Generalization in Interpolating Learning Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03514
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03514
- **Reference count:** 33
- **Primary result:** A practical contractive propagation certificate that, when small, guarantees stability-based generalization for interpolating learners; but stability is shown to be insufficient in some interpolating regimes with small risk.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the long-standing puzzle of why overparameterized interpolating models (like deep neural networks) generalize well despite conventional wisdom suggesting they should overfit. It proposes to analyze training as a trajectory in function space and define a stability certificate by unrolling a contractive propagation recursion. A small certificate implies uniform stability and thus a generalization bound. Conversely, the authors prove that there exist interpolating regimes (e.g., benign overfitting) where any contractively stable explanation would require a large certificate, even though the achieved risk is small. Hence, stability cannot universally explain generalization. Empirical experiments on overparameterized linear regression validate the theory: the certificate correlates strongly with generalization gaps across step sizes, optimizers (GD, SGD, Adam), and neighbor perturbations. For instance, Adam produces a certificate about 28.3 and test error 0.22, while GD gives 0.022 and 0.060. High-leverage sample replacement reduces the certificate compared to random replacement, and label permutation leaves both certificate and test error unchanged. The work thus establishes a function-space stability boundary that separates when stability explains generalization and when alternative mechanisms must be invoked.

## Method Summary
The method operationalizes stability in function space. Given neighboring datasets S and S′ that differ in a single example and shared randomness (initialization, minibatch ordering), the algorithm’s update map G_t transforms predictors. The one-step sensitivity to historical discrepancies is captured by a_t (a Lipschitz constant) and to the novel sample by b_t. The certificate C_t is computed recursively: C_{t+1} = a_t C_t + b_t, with C_0 = 0. After T steps, the terminal C_T multiplied by a constant L_d (which dominates loss differences via a discrepancy d) yields a stability parameter β_T(S). The expectation of β_T over dataset draws upper bounds the expected generalization gap. For linear regression with gradient descent, a_t = ||I - η X_{S′}^T X_{S′}||_2 and b_t = ||G(w_t; S) - G(w_t; S′)||_2. The certificate is computed online during training and tracked. Experiments vary hyperparameters and neighbor types to assess correlation between certificate magnitude and generalization gap.

## Key Results
- **Sufficiency:** If the contractive certificate is small, the learner is uniformly stable in expectation and thus enjoys a generalization bound.
- **Necessity:** There exist overparameterized interpolating regimes (e.g., ridgeless least squares with high-leverage points) where any contractive stability bound must be large despite the model having small risk, proving stability is not a universal explanation.
- **Empirical correlation:** In overparameterized linear regression, the certificate predicts generalization gaps across step sizes, optimizers (GD/SGD/Adam), and neighbor perturbation type; e.g., Adam yields a certificate ~28.3 and test error 0.22, whereas GD yields 0.022 and 0.060. High-leverage replacement reduces the certificate relative to random; label permutation does not affect it.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper’s core technical contribution is a contractive propagation condition and an unrolled certificate that provides a tractable stability diagnostic. Three mechanisms underpin its validity:

1. **Bounding trajectory discrepancy.** The recursion C_{t+1} = a_t C_t + b_t upper bounds the actual discrepancy between predictors trained on neighboring datasets, provided the one-step Lipschitz constant a_t and injection b_t are valid. This bound is tight if a_t and b_t are themselves tight. If C_T is small, then the expected difference in loss between training and test is also small (via L_d), implying uniform stability.

2. **Practical approximation.** For differentiable update maps G_t, a_t can be bounded by the operator norm of the Jacobian ∂G_t along the trajectory, and b_t can be bounded by the norm of the difference between updates using the differing sample. These estimates allow computation of the certificate during training without needing explicit neighboring runs, making it a practical diagnostic.

3. **Limitation of stability.** The certificate can become large even when the learned predictor has small risk, as demonstrated in benign overfitting scenarios. In such regimes, the data distribution contains high-leverage points that strongly influence the solution; replacing one such point causes a large change in the predictor but only a small increase in population risk. Hence, stability-based generalization analysis fails to explain the success, and alternative theories (e.g., implicit bias, benign overfitting) are needed.

Break conditions: if a_t ≥ 1 or b_t grows, the certificate may diverge, indicating violation of contractivity; if the certificate does not correlate with generalization gaps, either the mechanism is not operative or the estimates are too loose.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Algorithmic Stability (uniform stability)**  
- *Why needed:* Provides the standard bridge from sensitivity to dataset perturbations to generalization bounds; the certificate serves as a stability parameter.  
- *Quick check:* If a learning algorithm is uniformly stable with parameter β, what is the bound on the expected generalization gap?  

**Concept: Function-Space Trajectory Modeling**  
- *Why needed:* Avoids parameter-dependent assumptions and expresses stability directly in predictor space, making the analysis compatible with any optimizer or architecture.  
- *Quick check:* Why is it important that the stability definition uses the predictor f_t(S) rather than the parameter vector?  

**Concept: Contractive Recursion and Certificate Unrolling**  
- *Why needed:* Converts local one-step sensitivity into a single scalar that upper bounds the entire trajectory discrepancy and thus controls generalization.  
- *Quick check:* How does the unrolling process (4.2) account for both propagated and injected instability?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Discrepancy d -> Contractivity profile (a_t, b_t) -> Certificate (Cert_T) -> Stability level (β_T = L_d * Cert_T) -> Generalization bound (E[gen gap] ≤ E[β_T])

**Critical path:**
1. Define neighboring datasets S ≃ S′ and couple algorithmic randomness (shared initialization, minibatch order).
2. For each training step t, compute or bound a_t and b_t for the pair (S,S′) under shared randomness.
3. Unroll the recursion to obtain Cert_T(S,S′;U), then Cert_T(S) = sup_{S′} E_U[Cert_T(S,S′;U)].
4. Multiply by L_d to obtain β_T(S).
5. Report β_T(S) as the stability‑based generalization diagnostic; optionally track prefix Cert_t for early detection.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Choice of d:* Should dominate loss differences; common choices include L2 prediction norm for regression or L1/L2 for bounded losses.
- *Bounding a_t:* Jacobian norm is precise but costly; smoothness‑based bounds (e.g., ∥I−ηH∥) are cheaper but may be loose; constant a_t≡1 is simplest but overestimates.
- *Bounding b_t:* Exact per‑sample gradient difference requires extra computation; conservative bound (∥η ∇ℓ(z_t)∥) is cheap but loose.
- *Computational cost:* Jacobian‑vector products via autodiff scale with model size; random probing can estimate spectral norms more cheaply.
- *Certificate tightness vs. generality:* Tighter bounds yield more credible diagnostics but risk optimism; looser bounds are safer but may always be large.

**Failure signatures:**
- Certificate grows explosively (e.g., > orders of magnitude) even for small step sizes → likely violation of contractivity (a_t>1).
- Probe discrepancy d_t measured on a held‑out set diverges from Cert_t prediction → underestimation or invalid a_t/b_t bounds.
- Certificate does not correlate with test error across random seeds → either stability mechanism is not operative (e.g., benign overfitting) or the certificate estimate is inaccurate.
- High variance in Cert_T across seeds → unstable estimate, possibly due to high variance in Jacobian or b_t estimation.
- Interpolation is achieved but β_T is large despite good test error → classic sign of a non‑stability explanation.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Step‑size sweep in Gradient Descent** – Vary η and log Cert_t and test MSE; expect monotonic increase in certificate with η and only mild change in test MSE (Section 8.2, Table 1).
2. **Neighbor‑selection ablation** – Replace a random index vs a high‑leverage index; expect smaller certificate and smaller probe discrepancy for high‑leverage replacement (Section 8.3, Figure 2, Table 2).
3. **Optimizer comparison** – Train with GD, SGD, Adam under fixed step sizes; expect large differences in certificate growth and test error, with Adam showing dramatically larger values (Section 8.4, Figure 3, Table 3).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can the stability certificate be effectively computed and validated for deep neural networks?**  
   - *Basis:* The authors state that "extending diagnostics to deep architectures remains future work" (Section 9.2).  
   - *Why unresolved:* Current analysis and experiments are limited to linear regression; deep networks introduce nonlinearities and high-dimensional parameter spaces that may challenge the contractivity assumption and Jacobian estimation.  
   - *What evidence:* Apply the diagnostic (using Jacobian norm proxies) to standard deep architectures across various datasets and optimizers; test correlation with generalization gaps and whether the contractivity condition holds.

2. **How tight is the proposed stability certificate relative to the true trajectory sensitivity?**  
   - *Basis:* The paper notes the certificate "is not known to be tight" and "practical estimators can be conservative" (Section 9.2).  
   - *Why unresolved:* The certificate is an upper bound; its conservatism could limit diagnostic precision.  
   - *What evidence:* In analytically tractable settings (e.g., overparameterized linear regression), derive exact expressions for both the certificate and the actual discrepancy, then compare them quantitatively; also develop lower bounds to match the upper bound in certain regimes.

3. **What are the necessary and sufficient conditions for contractive trajectory stability (and thus stability‑based generalization) in interpolating learning systems?**  
   - *Basis:* Theorem 5.2 shows existence of interpolating regimes with small risk where any contractivity‑based stability bound is large, but a complete characterization is lacking.  
   - *Why unresolved:* The boundary between stability‑explained and non‑stability‑explained generalization remains abstract; understanding the role of data geometry (e.g., effective rank, leverage), model architecture, and optimizer would clarify when the certificate is informative.  
   - *What evidence:* Extend the necessity construction to more model classes (e.g., shallow neural networks, kernel methods); empirically survey the certificate across a diverse set of problems and correlate its magnitude with data/optimizer properties; develop theoretical criteria that predict when contractivity must fail.

## Limitations
- Certificate validity depends on contractive propagation; it fails in benign overfitting regimes, so stability is not a universal explanation for generalization.
- For non‑linear models, Jacobian‑based bounds may be loose and computationally costly; their tightness remains unknown.
- Assumptions (Lipschitz loss, data‑independent initialization) may not cover all modern practices (e.g., data‑dependent initialization, non‑Lipschitz losses).
- Experiments are restricted to linear regression; extension to deep networks is an open challenge.

## Confidence
- **Sufficiency (small cert ⇒ stability):** High (theoretical proof)
- **Empirical correlation (cert ↔ gen. gap) in linear settings:** Medium (limited to synthetic linear regression; needs broader validation)
- **Necessity (interpolating regimes with large cert & small risk):** High (theorem plus supporting experiments)
