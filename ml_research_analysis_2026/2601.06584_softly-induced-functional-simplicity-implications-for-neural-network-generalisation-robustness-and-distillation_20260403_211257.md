# Softly Induced Functional Simplicity: Implications for Neural Network Generalisation, Robustness, and Distillation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.06584  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06584  
- Reference count: 19  
- Primary result: Soft symmetry-constraining SEAL regularization reduces leading Hessian eigenvalue by 92.1% and trace by 88.1%, improves OOD robustness (ROC-AUC +0.114) and distillation efficiency without sacrificing in-distribution performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the effect of a soft symmetry-constraining inductive bias, implemented via SEAL regularization, on a transformer model for high-energy physics jet classification. By penalizing output discrepancies between original and Lorentz-boosted inputs, the method induces low-curvature pseudo-Goldstone modes aligned with approximate Lorentz symmetry. Quantitative analysis shows dramatic reductions in Hessian curvature (leading eigenvalue ratio 0.079, trace ratio 0.119) compared to an unconstrained baseline. Functionally, this induced simplicity yields enhanced robustness to momentum perturbations, superior out-of-distribution generalisation (e.g., top quark ROC-AUC +0.114), and more efficient knowledge distillation into a smaller student network, while maintaining comparable in-distribution accuracy.

## Method Summary
The authors train a transformer encoder (3 layers, 4 heads, 256-dim embeddings) on the hls4ml jet dataset (200k train, 50k val, 105k test) for binary classification (gluon vs W). Two variants are compared: an unconstrained model trained with binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss, and a constrained model trained with BCE plus a SEAL regularization term (λ=0.1) that computes the mean squared error between the model's output for an input and its Lorentz-boosted counterpart. Both models are optimised with Adam (lr=0.005, weight_decay=0.0005) for 100 epochs, selecting the checkpoint with lowest validation loss. Key analyses include: (1) Hessian geometry estimation via stochastic Lanczos (leading eigenvalue) and Hutchinson trace estimator; (2) Robustness tests by applying increasing levels of constituent transverse momentum smearing; (3) Out-of-distribution evaluation on near-OOD (gluon vs Z) and far-OOD (gluon vs top) tasks; (4) Distillation compressibility by training a fixed smaller student (487k parameters) to mimic teacher logits via MSE.

## Key Results
- SEAL reduces leading Hessian eigenvalue by 92.1% (λ₁ ratio = 0.079) and trace by 88.1% relative to unconstrained baseline.
- Constrained model exhibits higher robustness to pₜ perturbations, with ROC-AUC degrading more slowly as smearing increases.
- On far-OOD (top quark) classification, constrained model achieves ROC-AUC 0.532 vs 0.418 for unconstrained (+0.114).
- Distillation: The constrained teacher yields lower final MSE when distilled into the smaller student, indicating higher compressibility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Soft symmetry constraints reshape loss landscape
The SEAL loss penalises discrepancies between an input and its transformed (Lorentz-boosted) view, creating approximate degeneracies along directions corresponding to the symmetry group. Optimising this composite loss steers the solution toward a region where the loss gradient is small along these symmetry directions, i.e., low‑curvature pseudo‑Goldstone modes. Evidence: λ₁ reduced to 0.079 of baseline; visualisation of loss along the top eigenvector shows a shallow valley for the constrained model versus a steep curvature for the unconstrained one. Break condition: if the assumed symmetry is not approximately present in the data, the soft constraint may conflict with the learning task and harm performance.

### Mechanism 2: Reduced Hessian curvature and compressibility signal lower functional complexity
A flatter loss landscape (smaller λ₁ and trace) indicates the solution is less sensitive to parameter perturbations, implying a smoother, less “wiggly” input‑output mapping. This lower effective dimensionality means the function can be represented with fewer independent degrees of freedom. The constrained model distills more efficiently into a smaller student, directly testing and confirming this compressibility. Break condition: Hessian flatness may not capture true functional complexity if the target function is inherently high‑frequency; flatness alone does not guarantee simplicity.

### Mechanism 3: Lower‑complexity solutions improve robustness, OOD generalisation, and distillation
Smoother, lower‑complexity decision boundaries are less sensitive to small input perturbations (robustness) and capture more abstract, invariant features that transfer to shifted distributions (OOD). Their compressibility implies the essential “signal” is low‑dimensional, enabling a small student to replicate them effectively. Evidence includes improved ROC‑AUC under pₜ smearing, higher far‑OOD performance, and lower student MSE. Break condition: if an OOD distribution relies on high‑frequency features, a low‑complexity solution might underfit.

## Foundational Learning
- **Loss Landscape Geometry (Hessian)**  
  *Why needed:* Quantifies local curvature and effective dimensionality of a learned solution; hypothesised to relate to generalisation and robustness.  
  *Quick check:* If λ₁ is large but trace is small, the loss valley is narrow in one direction but otherwise flat, indicating a sharp, potentially brittle minimum.

- **Inductive Bias (Hard vs. Soft)**  
  *Why needed:* SEAL is a soft bias; understanding the distinction clarifies why it preserves expressivity while guiding optimisation.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a hard Lorentz‑equivariant architecture be too restrictive for realistic HEP jet classification? (e.g., it cannot model detector effects or subtle symmetry breaking.)

- **Functional Complexity vs. Model Capacity**  
  *Why needed:* The paper argues that parameter count alone is a poor proxy for the complexity of the learned function; SEAL aims to reduce the function’s effective complexity without changing capacity.  
  *Quick check:* How can a larger model learn a simpler function than a smaller one? (If guided by inductive biases that favour smoother solutions.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input Processor → Transformer Encoder → Pooling & Classifier → (Original Output) and Boosted Output → SEAL loss added to BCE loss → Total loss.  
  Diagnostics (pyhessian) and Distillation (Student) are external.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Preprocess hls4ml jet dataset into fixed-size constituent features.  
  2. Train two teacher variants (unconstrained and SEAL-constrained) for 100 epochs with Adam; select best checkpoint by validation loss.  
  3. For selected checkpoints: compute Hessian metrics (λ₁, Tr(H)); evaluate robustness (pₜ smearing); test on OOD sets; train fixed student via distillation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Soft vs. Hard bias: SEAL retains model expressivity but adds computational overhead and requires tuning λ; a hard equivariant architecture would be more efficient but possibly unable to capture broken symmetries.  
  - Hessian estimation: Full Hessian is O(N²) in parameters; using stochastic Lanczos and Hutchinson is tractable but introduces estimator variance and requires careful configuration.  
  - Distillation probe: Student architecture is fixed; if it is too small, both teachers may fail, masking complexity differences; the chosen size (487k vs 1.26M) is critical.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - SEAL failure: Constrained model shows no λ₁ or trace reduction ⇒ λ may be mis‑set or boost generation incorrect.  
  - OOD failure: Constrained model does not outperform on far‑OOD ⇒ bias may be too strong or not aligned with useful invariants.  
  - Distillation failure: Student MSE curves converge for both teachers ⇒ student either too small or teachers’ complexity differences vanish.  
  - In‑distribution degradation: Constrained model accuracy drops significantly ⇒ λ too high, over‑regularisation.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline replication: train unconstrained and constrained (λ=0.1) for three seeds; confirm comparable in‑distribution Gluon vs W performance.  
  2. Hessian metric extraction: for best checkpoints, compute λ₁ and Tr(H); report mean ratios (expected ≈0.079 and 0.119).  
  3. Robustness sweep: apply pₜ smearing (σ=0.0–0.25) to test set, evaluate ROC‑AUC, and plot curves showing slower degradation for constrained model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the SEAL‑induced simplicity bias be effectively leveraged in resource‑ and latency‑constrained HEP trigger and reconstruction systems? The paper suggests future work but provides no concrete deployment benchmarks.
- **Open Question 2:** To what extent do pseudo‑Goldstone modes extend into the full Hessian spectrum, and how does this full spectrum correlate with robustness and generalisation? The analysis focuses on the top eigenpair; alignment of subleading modes is unverified.
- **Open Question 3:** Is the benefit specific to jet classification on hls4ml, or does it generalise to other HEP tasks (e.g., particle ID, event‑level anomaly detection) and datasets with different systematics? Experiments are limited to one binary classification task.
- **Open Question 4:** How do the strength of the soft constraint (λ) and model capacity interact to shape the loss geometry and trade‑offs between in‑distribution performance, robustness, and generalisation? Only one λ and architecture are studied; the optimal λ for a given capacity and saturation point remain unknown.

## Limitations
- Student architecture unspecified → exact distillation replication impossible.
- Hessian hyperparameters missing (e.g., Lanczos iterations, Hutchinson samples) → curvature ratio stability uncertain.
- Evaluation confined to one HEP dataset; generalizability untested.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1:** SEAL induces low‑curvature pseudo‑Goldstone modes. *Medium* – Hessian reductions are clear, but alignment with symmetry is indirect.
- **Claim 2:** Hessian curvature + compressibility define functional simplicity. *Medium* – Metrics are plausible but not universally validated.
- **Claim 3:** Simpler functions yield robustness, OOD, and distillation gains. *Medium* – Empirical support exists but confounders limit causal certainty.
