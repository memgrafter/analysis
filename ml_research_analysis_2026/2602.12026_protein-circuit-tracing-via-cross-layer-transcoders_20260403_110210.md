# Protein Circuit Tracing via Cross-layer Transcoders
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12026  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12026  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** ProtoMech uses cross-layer transcoders to identify interpretable circuits in protein language models, enabling high-fidelity protein steering and recovering near-original performance with extreme sparsity.

## Executive Summary
Protein language models (pLMs) like ESM2 are powerful but lack interpretable explanations of their internal computations. Existing methods (e.g., sparse autoencoders) analyze layers in isolation, missing cross-layer interactions. This paper introduces ProtoMech, a framework based on cross-layer transcoders (CLTs) that learns sparse latent representations jointly across all transformer layers. Applied to ESM2, ProtoMech recovers 82–89% of original performance on classification and function tasks while using <1% of the latent space. Identified circuits retain up to 79% accuracy under extreme compression and steer protein generation to high-fitness variants, aligning with known biological motifs. This enables circuit-based interpretation and controlled protein design.

## Method Summary
ProtoMech replaces each pLM layer’s activations with a learned sparse representation via a cross-layer transcoder (CLT). A CLT consists of an encoder (mapping activations from all layers to a shared latent space) and per-layer decoders (reconstructing each layer’s activations from the latents). The model is trained to minimize reconstruction error while enforcing sparsity in the latents (via L1 regularization). Once trained, latents that are highly active across many inputs are candidate "circuit" features. Steering is achieved by clamping these latents to extreme values and observing output changes. The approach moves beyond per-layer analysis to capture computational pathways that span layers.

## Key Results
- ProtoMech recovers 82–89% of ESM2’s performance on protein family classification and function prediction while using <1% of latent dimensions.
- Sparse circuits (as few as 0.2% of latents) retain up to 79% accuracy on downstream tasks, demonstrating extreme compressibility.
- Steering along identified circuits generates high-fitness protein variants, outperforming baselines in 71% of 7 DMS assays.
- Recovered latents align with known biological motifs (e.g., kinase catalytic loops, binding sites), validating biological interpretability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Cross-layer transcoders (CLTs) learn a shared sparse representation across all layers, capturing features that persist or transform through the network. This joint optimization forces latents to represent distributed, multi-layer computational motifs rather than layer-specific noise. Sparsity过滤 out task-irrelevant variance, effectively denoising the representation. The decoder matrices map latents back to each layer, allowing precise reconstruction and identification of which latents influence which layers. Steering by clamping active latents exploits their causal role in the forward pass, perturbing the computational pathway to generate desired outputs. The extreme sparsity (<<1% active) indicates that pLMs rely on a small set of reusable circuit-like features.

## Foundational Learning
- **Protein Language Models (pLMs)**  
  *Why needed:* pLMs (e.g., ESM2) are pre-trained on massive protein sequence corpora to predict structure/function; understanding their internal representations is key to bio-interpretability.  
  *Quick check:* Can a pLM predict a protein’s secondary structure or family from sequence?  

- **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**  
  *Why needed:* SAEs learn interpretable latent features by enforcing sparsity; they are the per-layer building block for many circuit-tracing methods.  
  *Quick check:* Apply an SAE to a single pLM layer: are recovered latents monosemantic (represent one biological concept)?  

- **Cross-layer Transcoders (CLTs)**  
  *Why needed:* CLTs extend SAEs to multiple layers, capturing computational pathways that span layers, which is critical for complete circuit tracing.  
  *Quick check:* Does a CLT reconstruct layer activations better than independent per-layer SAEs?  

- **Circuit Discovery**  
  *Why needed:* Circuits are minimal subnetets that implement specific functions; tracing them reveals how pLMs solve tasks.  
  *Quick check:* Do identified latents consistently activate for inputs sharing a known motif (e.g., ATP-binding)?  

- **Steering via Latent Manipulation**  
  *Why needed:* Steering tests causality: if clamping a latent changes output in a predictable way, it is part of a functional circuit.  
  *Quick check:* Clamp a latent associated with “membrane binding”; does output sequence gain hydrophobic regions?  

- **Direct Mutational Scanning (DMS)**  
  *Why needed:* DMS provides experimental fitness measures; used here to evaluate steering-generated variants.  
  *Quick check:* Are DMS fitness scores correlated with model-predicted fitness for wild-type sequences?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
ESM2 -> CLT Encoder -> Sparse Latents (shared) -> CLT Decoders (per layer) -> Reconstructed Activations -> pLM Outputs  
Circuit ID: Active Latents -> Steering via Clamping -> Modified pLM Outputs

**Critical path:**  
Input sequence -> pLM forward pass (store activations per layer) -> CLT encodes all-layer activations to sparse latents -> latents decoded to each layer -> reconstruction loss trains CLT -> high-activation latents extracted -> steering by clamping latents during forward pass -> output sequence/fitness evaluated.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Sparsity vs. reconstruction:* Higher sparsity yields more interpretable circuits but risks losing information.  
- *CLT scope:* Including more layers improves cross-layer tracing but increases parameters (O(L²) scaling).  
- *Latent dimensionality:* Too small underfits; too large reduces sparsity and interpretability.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Poor reconstruction (low accuracy recovery) indicates CLT capacity insufficient or sparsity too high.  
- Circuits that do not align with known biology suggest overfitting or nonsensical latents.  
- Steering produces sequences with low DMS fitness or unnatural properties (e.g., charge imbalance).

**Three first experiments:**  
1. **Reconstruction fidelity check:** Train CLT on small subset; measure per-layer reconstruction MSE and downstream task accuracy vs. original pLM.  
2. **Sparsity sweep:** Vary L1 penalty; plot accuracy recovery and number of active latents to find elbow point.  
3. **Single-layer ablation:** Train CLTs that exclude one layer at a time; measure drop in circuit identification quality to quantify cross-layer importance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the parameter overhead of cross-layer transcoders (CLTs) be reduced to enable scaling of ProtoMech to larger protein language models?
- Basis in paper: explicit (the Limitations section states the CLT's parameter count scales as O(L^2) and that scaling to larger ESM2 variants is an interesting future direction).
- Why unresolved: Currently, training a CLT for ESM2-8M requires ~28M parameters, a 3.5× increase over the original model. This becomes prohibitive for larger models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Develop and evaluate methods to reduce the number of decoder matrices (e.g., low-rank approximations, shared decoders, or learned sparsity patterns) while maintaining reconstruction fidelity and circuit discovery accuracy.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can we automate the annotation of ProtoMech circuit latents to avoid reliance on manual interpretation and existing biological knowledge?
- Basis in paper: explicit (Limitations: "Developing automated annotation pipelines remains a critical next step to accelerate the discovery of novel biological insights.").
- Why unresolved: Current analysis is labor-intensive and limited to cases where known motifs exist; many latents may correspond to poorly characterized mechanisms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Build a pipeline that links latents to biological databases via motif scanning, structural mapping, and functional enrichment, and validate by discovering previously unknown associations that are experimentally testable.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What causes ProtoMech to sometimes outperform the original pLM in regimes of low model performance, and can this denoising effect be systematically controlled?
- Basis in paper: inferred (Results/Discussion note that ProtoMech circuits achieve higher scores when ESM2 F1 < 0.5, attributed to a denoising effect of sparse latent representation, but not fully investigated).
- Why unresolved: The hypothesis that sparsity filters out task-irrelevant noise is plausible but lacks direct evidence; the relationship between sparsity level, circuit size, and denoising is unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying sparsity and measuring performance on synthetic or benchmark tasks designed to isolate noise, along with analysis of which features are discarded vs retained.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Are the steering strategies derived from ProtoMech circuits transferable across diverse protein families and fitness landscapes, or are they tightly coupled to the specific DMS assay used for circuit discovery?
- Basis in paper: inferred (Steering was evaluated on seven DMS assays; no cross-assay transfer experiments were reported).
- Why unresolved: It is unknown whether the same latents or clamping multipliers effective for one protein (e.g., GB1) would work for another; this limits practical application.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments where circuits discovered for one DMS assay are used to steer sequences for a different but related protein function, measuring fitness improvement relative to assay-specific baselines.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to ESM2; unknown if results generalize to other pLMs (e.g., ProtT5, ESM-1b) or architectures.
- Performance metrics based on limited tasks (family classification, function prediction); broader applicability (e.g., stability, binding) untested.
- Biological motif alignment may be selective; systematic validation with domain experts lacking.
- Sparse circuit extraction sensitive to hyperparameters (sparsity level, cross-layer scope); robustness not thoroughly explored.
- Reproducibility details sparse; no official code release mentioned.

## Confidence
- Performance recovery (82–89%): **Medium** – plausible high numbers but no independent verification.
- Circuit accuracy (up to 79% under compression): **Medium** – depends on circuit selection thresholds.
- Steering for fitness (71% outperformance): **Low–Medium** – limited evaluation set, potential overfitting.
- Motif alignment with known biology: **Medium** – consistent but requires expert curation.

## Next Checks
1. **Generalization:** Apply ProtoMech to at least two other pLMs (e.g., ProtT5, ESM-1b) and compare circuit recovery and performance retention.
2. **Task diversity:** Evaluate steering and circuit accuracy on additional protein tasks (e.g., stability prediction, protein-protein interaction) to assess breadth.
3. **Ablation study:** Systematically vary sparsity constraints and number of layers included in transcoders; report trade-offs in compression, accuracy, and interpretability.
