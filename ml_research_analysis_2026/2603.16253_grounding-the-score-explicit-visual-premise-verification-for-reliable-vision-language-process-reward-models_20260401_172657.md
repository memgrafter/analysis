# Grounding the Score: Explicit Visual Premise Verification for Reliable Vision-Language Process Reward Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.16253
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16253
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: EVPV-PRM achieves 67.46% Macro-F1 on VisualProcessBench, surpassing prior PRMs, and yields +8.83 to +9.78 Best-of-8 reranking gains over Pass@1.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language process reward models (VL-PRMs) evaluate step-wise correctness in multimodal reasoning but suffer from entanglement: a low score may reflect a visual misperception rather than a logical error. This paper introduces Explicit Visual Premise Verification (EVPV) to disentangle perception from reasoning. EVPV prompts the policy to output a step-dependent visual checklist of explicit premises. In parallel, a constraint extractor independently produces a structured set of visual facts (numeric, relational, structural) from the image. The checklist claims are matched against the constraints to compute a visual reliability score, which gates the step rewards—attenuating them when visual reliability is low and preserving them when high. This approach avoids per-step tool calls while improving step-level Macro-F1 and Best-of-N reranking across multiple vision-language benchmarks.

## Method Summary
EVPV introduces a two-stream verification process. A policy model (e.g., InternVL2.5) generates a solution trajectory, and for each step it declares a `visualdependency`—a natural-language claim about visible facts needed for that step. Separately, a trained constraint extractor $E_\phi$ converts the image and question into a canonical JSON set $C$ of structured visual constraints (e.g., numeric values, spatial relations, object attributes). A verifier $V_\theta$ scores each step's logical correctness as a base reward $R^{\text{base}}_t$. A reliability calibrator then: (1) aligns each checklist claim to constraints via type-aware matching (numeric tolerance, relation Jaccard, structural similarity) to produce support scores $p_j$; (2) aggregates these into an instance-level reliability $r$; (3) applies a sigmoid gating function $\alpha(r) = \sigma(10(r-0.5))$ to multiply $R^{\text{base}}_t$ for steps with non-null `visualdependency`. The aggregated trajectory score (e.g., fraction of gated steps with positive reward) is used for Best-of-N selection. The constraint extractor is trained via supervised fine-tuning on gold constraints followed by DPO to improve fidelity using a schema-aware distance metric.

## Key Results
- EVPV-PRM attains 67.46% Macro-F1 on VisualProcessBench, exceeding VisualPRM (62.0%) and TIM-PRM (61.7%).
- In Best-of-8 reranking with InternVL2.5 policies, EVPV yields consistent gains: Δ8 = +8.83 (8B), +9.52 (26B), +9.78 (38B) points over Pass@1.
- Constraint corruption experiments show monotonic performance drops, confirming the causal role of reliable premise verification.
- Ablations demonstrate that removing structured facts (-5.35 Macro-F1) or shuffling structure (-7.64) severely harms performance, proving the necessity of structured constraints.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: EVPV reduces false positives/negatives in VL-PRM step scoring by decoupling visual premise reliability from logical correctness.
- **Mechanism**: The policy outputs a step-wise *visual checklist* of explicit premises. An independent constraint extractor produces *structured visual constraints* from the image. A consistency match between checklist claims and constraints yields a scalar *visual reliability score* $r$. Step rewards are gated by $\alpha(r)$: visually dependent steps are attenuated when $r$ is low, preserved when $r$ is high, all without per-step tool calls.
- **Core assumption**: A step built on an incorrect visual premise is inherently flawed, regardless of algebraic correctness; perceptual certainty must be assessed before logical judgment.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] conditions step scoring on visual premise reliability; [section 3.2] formalizes checklist, constraints, matching, and gating; corpus work on visual grounding faithfulness supports explicit premise verification.
- **Break condition**: If the constraint extractor lacks coverage or the policy produces vague checklists, $r$ becomes uninformative, reverting to entangled scoring.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Structured visual constraints (vs. raw captions) are necessary for reliable premise verification.
- **Mechanism**: Constraints are schema-defined JSON objects (numeric, relational, structural) that enable type-aware matching against checklist claims. Matching uses entity/attribute alignment, numeric tolerance, Jaccard similarity for relations/structures, and confidence weighting, producing sensitive support scores.
- **Core assumption**: Free-form captions lack discrete, comparable units; structured facts provide a canonical reference.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 3.2.2 & Appendix B] details JSON schema and type-specific matching; [section 4.5, Table 4] large ablation drops when structured facts are removed or shuffled.
- **Break condition**: If the problem requires visual facts outside the constraint schema (e.g., qualitative textures) or entity labeling is inconsistent, matching fails.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Training the constraint extractor with distillation + DPO improves constraint fidelity, which causally drives EVPV's gains.
- **Mechanism**: The extractor $E_\phi$ is first SFT on gold constraints, then DPO-refined using preference pairs based on a schema-aware distance (category recall, numeric deviation, relation precision). Higher fidelity $C$ yields more accurate reliability $r$.
- **Core assumption**: A more faithful constraint set better discriminates between supported and unsupported checklist claims.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 3.4 & Appendix C] describes SFT and DPO losses with distance metric; [section 4.4, Figure 4] monotonic Macro-F1 drop under constraint corruption.
- **Break condition**: If the teacher's constraints are noisy or preference pairs are poorly formed, distillation propagates errors and DPO may not recover.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Process Reward Modeling (PRM)**  
  *Why needed*: EVPV is a PRM variant; understanding standard PRMs (step-wise binary correctness, trajectory aggregation for Best-of-N) is prerequisite to seeing entanglement failure and gating's modification of rewards.  
  *Quick check*: Given a step $s_t$ and context, what does a PRM's output $u_t$ represent, and how is it used in Best-of-N reranking?

- **Concept: Multimodal Perception–Reasoning Entanglement**  
  *Why needed*: The paper's core diagnosis is that VL-PRMs cannot separate "did the model see correctly?" from "did it reason correctly?". This entanglement causes systematic false positives/negatives.  
  *Quick check*: Provide an example where a logically sound step is incorrectly penalized by a VL-PRM due to a misperceived visual premise.

- **Concept: Structured Constraint Representation**  
  *Why needed*: EVPV's verification relies on extracting and matching against a canonical set of visual facts (numeric, relational, structural). This is not free-form description but a schema-enforced JSON.  
  *Quick check*: Why might a caption describing "the triangle is large" be insufficient as a constraint for verifying a step claiming "the base length is 5cm"?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Policy Model -> Constraint Extractor $E_\phi$ -> Step Verifier $V_\theta$ -> Reliability Calibrator -> Aggregator. The policy generates steps and checklist; the extractor produces constraints $C$; the verifier scores step correctness; the calibrator matches checklist vs. $C$ to compute $r$ and apply gating $\alpha(r)$; the aggregator combines gated rewards into a trajectory score.
- **Critical path**: Inference flow: (Policy + Extract Constraints) -> Match checklist vs. $C$ -> get $r$ -> (Verifier scores steps) -> Gate visually-dependent steps -> Aggregate trajectory scores -> Rerank candidates. The extractor runs once per instance; matching and gating are per-step.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  *Gating threshold $\tau$ & sharpness $\beta$*: Sensitive thresholds may over-/under-attenuate; paper uses $\tau=0.5, \beta=10$ with stable performance.  
  *Constraint schema coverage*: Must enumerate all fact types the policy might claim; missing categories cause unmatched claims and low $r$.  
  *Checklist quality*: Policy must output explicit, atomic visual claims; vague or missing `visualdependency` nulls disable verification.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  Macro-F1 drops sharply when constraints are dropped or shuffled (Table 4).  
  Monotonic degradation under constraint corruption (Figure 4) indicates dependency on constraint fidelity.  
  If $r$ is near 0.5 for most traces, either checklist or constraints are too noisy/skewed.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Component sanity check**: Feed a known image/question, manually inspect $C$ and policy checklist—do they cover gold visual facts? Can matching find alignments?  
  2. **Gating ablation**: Compare gated vs. ungated rewards on a subset with known perception errors (e.g., misread numbers). Does gating attenuate steps that rely on the misread value?  
  3. **Constraint corruption curve**: Systematically flip values/relations in $C$ and measure downstream Best-of-8 Δ. Does performance degrade monotonically?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: Can we replace the instance-level visual reliability score with step- or claim-specific reliability, and does this improve step verification accuracy?  
  *Basis*: Conclusion lists "step-/claim-conditioned reliability (rather than a single global signal)" as a future direction.
- **Question 2**: How can constraint extraction and matching be made uncertainty-aware, and does this lead to more robust process rewards?  
  *Basis*: Conclusion includes "uncertainty-aware constraint extraction and matching" in future work.
- **Question 3**: What are the benefits of training the PRM (or policy) with premise-aware rewards instead of using them solely at inference time?  
  *Basis*: Conclusion proposes "integrating premise-aware rewards into training-time process optimization" as future work.

## Limitations
- Matching function specifics (entity alignment, numeric tolerance) are underspecified, risking reproduction variance.
- Relies on policy's `visualdependency` claims; if vague or missing, reliability score $r$ becomes noisy.
- Constraint schema may not cover all visual facts (e.g., qualitative attributes, dynamics).
- Dependency on proprietary qwen3‑vl‑235b‑a22b‑instruct for gold constraints; alternative teachers may degrade extractor.
- Policy sampling settings not fully documented, affecting solution diversity in Best-of-N.

## Confidence
- *Decoupling perception & reasoning*: **Medium**. Strong Best‑of‑8 gains and gating ablations, but brittle to checklist quality.
- *Structured constraints necessary*: **High**. Large drops in ablations (‑5‑8 Macro‑F1) confirm necessity.
- *DPO extractor fidelity drives gains*: **Medium**. Corruption curve is compelling, but gold‑constraint dependency limits reproducibility.

## Next Checks
1. **Extractor fidelity**: Evaluate on held‑out gold constraints; report precision/recall and correlate with Macro‑F1.
2. **Checklist quality ablation**: Replace policy checklists with gold premises; measure gated reward change.
3. **Schema stress test**: Run on items with unsupported attributes; check if $r$ discrimination collapses.
