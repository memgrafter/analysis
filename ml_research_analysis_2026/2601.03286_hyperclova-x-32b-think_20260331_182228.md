# HyperCLOVA X 32B Think

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.03286
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03286
- **Reference count**: 27
- **Primary result**: A 32B Korean-optimized VLM achieving SOTA on Korean benchmarks (e.g., KMMLU 71.3%) and agent tasks, outperforming larger models.

## Executive Summary
HyperCLOVA X 32B Think is a 32‑billion parameter decoder‑only vision‑language model built for Korean‑language reasoning and agentic tasks. It uses a Korean‑adapted tokenizer, a four‑stage pre‑training curriculum emphasizing Korean and synthetic reasoning data, and post‑training via supervised fine‑tuning and specialized reinforcement learning (MRLVR, Agent‑RL, MRLHF). The model attains leading results on Korean benchmarks (KMMLU, CLIcK, HAERAE, etc.) and agent evaluations (Tau2, Terminal Bench), and is released open‑source.

## Method Summary
The model follows a decoder‑only Transformer architecture (72 layers, hidden dim 5120) with a Qwen2.5‑VL vision encoder and a linear projector. Pre‑training proceeds in four stages: initial broad knowledge (4K context), then upsampling high‑quality and instruction data (8K), extended reasoning with 32K context, and finally curated math/code/instruction data. Synthetic data (reformulation, PII rewriting) and Fill‑in‑the‑Middle are used. Post‑training includes two‑stage SFT (text‑first, then multimodal) and three RL phases: MRLVR (verifiable rewards for reasoning), Agent‑RL (GRPO for tool use), and MRLHF (PPO for alignment).

## Key Results
- **Korean benchmarks**: KMMLU 71.3%, CLIcK 75.2%, HAERAE 87.4%, KoNET 75.1%, K‑MMBench 88.1%, K‑DTCBench 93.3%.
- **Agent benchmarks**: Tau2 58.0–71.6%, Terminal Bench 21.8%, outperforming models up to 235B parameters.
- **Efficiency**: Reduced vision resolution (1920×1080) and token cap (≤3K/image) cut compute by ~53% vs. Qwen2.5‑VL.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Korean‑Specific Tokenizer Adaptation
- **Claim**: Pruning non‑Korean BPE merge rules and substituting morpheme‑aware rules improves compression and downstream Korean performance.
- **Mechanism**: Removes low‑utility rules for Korean scripts, inserts Korean morpheme rules into vacated slots, and applies StoChasTok. Achieves up to 2.15 characters/token on Korean while preserving English/STEM compression.
- **Evidence**: [section 2.2] details the two‑stage process and reports compression rates; [abstract] notes curriculum emphasizing Korean data.
- **Break condition**: If reverting to an English‑centric tokenizer on the same Korean data mix does not degrade Korean benchmark scores.

### Mechanism 2: Four‑Stage Curriculum with Reasoning Data Annealing
- **Claim**: Progressive increase in context length and reasoning‑oriented data mix enables long‑context reasoning and agent capabilities.
- **Mechanism**: Stages shift from 4K general knowledge → 8K with upsampled CoT → 32K with scaled RoPE and stricter filtering → final stage with >32% math/code/instruction data. Synthetic reasoning data is introduced early.
- **Evidence**: [section 3.2] defines stage‑wise data proportions and context lengths; [abstract] highlights curriculum emphasis on reasoning.
- **Break condition**: If training on the final data mix from the start yields equal or better reasoning scores with less compute.

### Mechanism 3: Reinforcement Learning for Multimodal & Agent Alignment
- **Claim**: Sequential RL (MRLVR, Agent‑RL, MRLHF) aligns multimodal reasoning and tool‑use behaviors with human preferences and task objectives.
- **Mechanism**: MRLVR uses verifiable rewards (code execution, math) via GRPO; Agent‑RL uses GRPO with context‑window tuning (128K for SWE); MRLHF uses PPO with a reward model for format/language consistency.
- **Evidence**: [section 4.2] describes each RL phase, algorithms (GRPO/PPO), and reward design; [abstract] mentions alignment with human preferences.
- **Break condition**: If ablating any RL phase does not reduce agent success rates or alignment metrics.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:
Input (text/image) → Text tokenizer / Vision encoder → Linear projection → Interleaved embeddings → Transformer backbone (72 layers, 5120 hidden, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE 500K, GQA 8) → Output sampling → Post‑processing (templates, tool calls)

**Critical path**: Raw input is tokenized (text) or patchified (image), projected into a shared space, concatenated into a sequence, processed by the decoder, and decoded with template‑aware formatting.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Sequential multimodal training (text‑first) speeds vision adaptation but risks catastrophic forgetting of text reasoning.
- Vision token cap (≤3K/image) prioritizes efficiency but may limit fine‑grained OCR or detail.
- Korean‑centric optimization yields a gap on English benchmarks vs. global models like Qwen3‑VL.

**Failure signatures**:
- Systematically lower English text/image benchmark scores relative to Korean.
- Degradation on pure text benchmarks (e.g., KMMLU) after multimodal SFT/RL.
- Agent instability if rollout context length mismatches task horizon (e.g., short context for SWE tasks).
- Poor Korean tokenization compression (<2.0 chars/token) or English STEM degradation.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Tokenizer compression benchmark**: Measure tokens‑per‑character on Korean/English/STEM corpora for baseline vs. adapted tokenizer; expect ≥2.15 for Korean with <1% English drop.
2. **Curriculum ablation**: Train small variants with/without Stage 3/4 reasoning data; evaluate on KMMLU/CLIcK to isolate reasoning data impact.
3. **Vision token resolution sweep**: Process K‑DTCBench with varying max pixels (e.g., 1920×1080 vs. 2560×1440) to map token budget vs. accuracy tradeoff.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does the vocabulary adaptation method's trade‑off between Korean compression efficiency and performance on non‑Korean/STEM domains scale with increasingly aggressive pruning and substitution rules?
- **Basis**: The paper describes calibration but does not quantify the degradation curve across languages [section 3.1, Table 1].
- **Why unresolved**: No systematic exploration of the trade‑off frontier; limits extension to other low‑resource languages.
- **Resolution evidence**: Controlled experiments varying the proportion of Korean‑specific rules, measuring compression and downstream task performance across languages.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What are the precise mechanisms and quantitative extent of catastrophic forgetting of text‑based reasoning when sequentially adding multimodal training?
- **Basis**: The conclusion notes gradual degradation but provides no measurement or analysis.
- **Why unresolved**: No staged performance tracking or representation analysis to isolate cause.
- **Resolution evidence**: Ablation tracking text‑only benchmarks at each post‑training stage, combined with probing/CCA between checkpoints.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Which components of the four‑stage curriculum contribute most to Korean reasoning performance, and could a more efficient curriculum be devised?
- **Basis**: The paper states synthetic pre‑training data helps reasoning but isolates no stage or data type [section 3.2].
- **Why unresolved**: No ablation studies; relative importance of domain vs. instruction data is unknown.
- **Resolution evidence**: Ablations removing or modifying stages (e.g., skip Stage 2, vary synthetic ratio) and evaluating on Korean reasoning benchmarks.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: What is the relative contribution of the three RL phases (MRLVR, Agent‑RL, MRLHF) to agentic capabilities, and are there synergistic or antagonistic interactions?
- **Basis**: RL stages are applied sequentially; combined results are reported but not disentangled [section 4.2].
- **Why unresolved**: Interdependence prevents knowing if all stages are necessary or could be reordered.
- **Resolution evidence**: Training models with subsets of RL stages (e.g., only MRLVR, MRLVR+Agent‑RL) and detailed task‑level evaluation on Tau2/Terminal Bench.

## Limitations
- **Dataset opacity**: Exact pre‑training corpus composition and SFT mixture proportions are undisclosed, hindering replication.
- **RL hyperparameter gaps**: Critical details (KL penalties, reward weights, GRPO dynamics) are omitted, making reward tuning guesswork.
- **Tokenizer specifics**: Precise rule‑pruning/substitution algorithm and StoChasTok schedule are not fully specified.
