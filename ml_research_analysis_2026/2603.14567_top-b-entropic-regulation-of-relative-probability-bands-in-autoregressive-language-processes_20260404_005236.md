# Top-b: Entropic Regulation of Relative Probability Bands in Autoregressive Language Processes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14567
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14567
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Introduces Top-b, an entropy-adaptive decoding method that reduces average generation entropy and inter-decoding variance on reasoning benchmarks while maintaining competitive accuracy compared to static methods like Top-p.

## Executive Summary
Standard decoding strategies (Top-k, Top-p) rely on static thresholds that fail to adapt to the dynamic uncertainty inherent in language generation, leading to suboptimal trade-offs between creativity and reasoning accuracy. This paper proposes Top-b, a novel decoding method that dynamically sets a relative probability band around the mode of the model’s distribution, with bandwidth scaled linearly by the normalized Shannon entropy. Evaluated on reasoning benchmarks (GPQA, GSM8K), Top-b achieves accuracy on par with or exceeding baselines while significantly lowering average generation entropy (e.g., 0.1579 vs. 0.1764 for Top-p on GPQA) and drastically reducing inter-decoding variability—often by an order of magnitude. Token-level analysis confirms that Top-b aggressively prunes low-probability distractor tokens in low-entropy states, stabilizing the generation trajectory and improving reliability for reasoning tasks.

## Method Summary
Top-b modifies the standard autoregressive decoding step by adaptively determining the set of candidate tokens based on the instantaneous entropy of the model’s probability distribution. At each generation step \(t\), it computes the Shannon entropy \(H(p_t)\) of the token probabilities and normalizes it by \(H_{\text{max}} = \log(|V|)\), where \(|V|\) is the vocabulary size. A fixed hyperparameter \(\text{base\_bandwidth}\) scales this normalized value to produce an adaptive bandwidth \(\text{bandwidth}_t = \text{base\_bandwidth} \times (1 + H(p_t)/H_{\text{max}})\). The sampling support \(S_{b,t}\) then includes all tokens \(y\) where \(p_t(y) \geq (1 - \text{bandwidth}_t) \times p_{\text{max}}\), with \(p_{\text{max}} = \max(p_t)\). Higher entropy (greater uncertainty) expands the band (lowering the threshold), permitting more tokens; lower entropy contracts the band (raising the threshold), restricting selection to tokens very close to the mode. This dynamic adjustment contrasts with static methods: Top-k fixes the support size, Top-p fixes cumulative probability, and Min-p fixes a relative threshold independent of entropy. Top-b requires no model training and introduces only one tunable parameter (\(\text{base\_bandwidth}\)), typically around 0.3 for high-temperature settings.

## Key Results
- **Lower average entropy:** Top-b reduces average per-token entropy compared to Top-p (e.g., 0.1579 vs. 0.1764 on GPQA), indicating more confident, less stochastic continuations.
- **Drastically reduced variance:** Inter-decoding variance is lowered by an order of magnitude (e.g., \(1.136 \times 10^{-4}\) vs. \(1.1207 \times 10^{-3}\) for Top-p on GSM8K), demonstrating stable, deterministic generation.
- **Competitive reasoning accuracy:** Top-b achieves the second-highest accuracy on GPQA (0.2285) while matching or exceeding baselines on GSM8K, confirming that entropy reduction does not sacrifice correctness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Dynamic entropy-scaled band.** Top-b’s core mechanism ties the width of the relative probability band directly to the normalized Shannon entropy. As shown in the abstract and formalized in Section 4.2, the bandwidth scales linearly with \(H(p_t)/H_{\text{max}}\). This assumes that the model’s local predictive uncertainty—captured by entropy—is a reliable indicator of how broad the sampling manifold should be. In high-entropy states (e.g., creative or ambiguous contexts), a wider band preserves necessary diversity; in low-entropy states (e.g., confident reasoning steps), a narrow band aggressively prunes the long tail of low-probability tokens. The evidence lies in the explicit definition and the empirical observation that this scaling yields lower average entropy without harming accuracy.

**Variance reduction via tail pruning.** In low-entropy states, the distribution is sharply peaked. Static methods like Top-p often admit a long tail of “distractor” tokens with minuscule probabilities; sampling from this tail introduces stochastic noise that can derail reasoning chains. Top-b’s contracted band (due to low \(H\)) minimizes the effective support size \(|S_{b,t}|\) in these states, eliminating such distractors. Section 2.4 posits that this acts as an online variance-minimizing operator, reducing the “branching factor” of errors. Section 6.1 and Table 2 confirm Top-b’s variance is orders of magnitude lower than Top-p’s, and token-level analysis (Appendix D) shows drastic pruning in deterministic prompts like “2+2=”.

**Maintained accuracy with lower entropy.** The systemic effect of the two mechanisms above is a generation path where per-token entropy is consistently lower. This indicates the model traverses a sharper, more decisive probability manifold. The paper’s results (Table 1, Figure 3) show that this entropy reduction co-occurs with competitive or improved accuracy on reasoning benchmarks. The underlying assumption is that lower average sequence entropy reflects reduced confusion and more reliable reasoning—provided the task values correctness over creative diversity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Shannon Entropy**  
  **Why needed:** The entire adaptive mechanism hinges on computing \(H(p_t) = -\sum p_i \log p_i\) as a real-time measure of predictive uncertainty. Entropy is low for peaked distributions (high confidence) and high for flat ones (high uncertainty).  
  **Quick check:** For probabilities \([0.9, 0.05, 0.05]\), \(H \approx 0.469\) bits (log base 2). This low value implies the model is highly confident in the top token.

- **Autoregressive Decoding & Sampling Strategies**  
  **Why needed:** Top-b modifies the fundamental “choose next token” step. Understanding the baseline trade-off—fidelity (sticking to high-probability tokens) vs. diversity (exploring)—is essential to see why static thresholds (Top-k, Top-p) are problematic for reasoning.  
  **Quick check:** Greedy decoding always picks \(\argmax\), which can cause repetition. Top-p samples from tokens above a cumulative probability threshold \(p\), allowing diversity but with a static cutoff that may not suit all uncertainty levels.

- **Probability Support / Truncation**  
  **Why needed:** Top-b redefines the support set \(S_t\)—the subset of tokens eligible for sampling. Comparing it to Top-k (fixed \(|S|\)), Top-p (fixed cumulative mass), and Min-p (fixed relative threshold \(\alpha \cdot p_{\text{max}}\)) clarifies its novelty: dynamic relative threshold scaled by entropy.  
  **Quick check:** For a distribution with \(p_{\text{max}}=0.6\) and other tokens at \(0.04\) each, Top-p (\(p=0.9\)) admits ~9 tokens (cumulative 0.6 + 8×0.04 = 0.92), while Min-p (\(\alpha=0.1\)) admits only the top token (threshold \(0.06\)). Thus, Min-p’s support size depends strongly on \(p_{\text{max}}\).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Probability Distributor → Entropy Calculator → Bandwidth Scaler → Relative Threshold Engine → Support Filter → Renormalizer & Sampler

**Critical path:**  
1. LLM forward pass produces token probability vector \(p_t\).  
2. Compute \(p_{\text{max}} = \max(p_t)\) and Shannon entropy \(H(p_t)\).  
3. Calculate \(\text{bandwidth}_t = \text{base\_bandwidth} \times (1 + H(p_t)/H_{\text{max}})\).  
4. Set threshold \(\tau = (1 - \text{bandwidth}_t) \times p_{\text{max}}\) (if \(\text{bandwidth}_t \geq 1\), \(\tau = 0\)).  
5. Filter tokens to support set \(S_{b,t} = \{ y \mid p_t(y) \geq \tau \}\).  
6. Renormalize probabilities over \(S_{b,t}\) to sum to 1.  
7. Sample next token \(y_t\) from the renormalized distribution.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Computation vs. Stability:** Entropy calculation and filtering add \(O(|V|)\) overhead per step (negligible vs. attention), but yield substantial variance reduction.  
- **base_bandwidth hyperparameter:** The sole main knob. Too low (\(\sim 0.1\)) causes mode collapse even in moderately uncertain states; too high (\(\sim 0.5\)) approaches Top-p behavior, losing stabilization. The paper suggests \(\approx 0.3\) for high-temperature settings.  
- **Entropy as proxy:** Assumes Shannon entropy captures the “information density” relevant for logical vs. creative generation; this may not hold universally.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Mode collapse:** Outputs become overly repetitive and deterministic across contexts (base_bandwidth too low or entropy calculation buggy).  
- **No pruning effect:** Average entropy and variance remain similar to Top-p (base_bandwidth too high or bandwidth scaling misimplemented).  
- **Accuracy drop on reasoning:** Entropy decreases but reasoning accuracy falls below baselines (over-pruning cuts valid but lower-probability reasoning paths).  
- **Numerical instability:** \(H(p_t)\) returns NaN or extreme values due to zeros in \(p_t\); requires adding a small \(\epsilon\) inside the logarithm.

**First experiments:**  
1. **Replicate entropy-accuracy trade-off:** Run Top-b with \(\text{base\_bandwidth}=0.3\) on Gemma-3-4B-it across GPQA and GSM8K using standard chain-of-thought prompts. Verify that average entropy is lower than Top-p’s while accuracy remains competitive. Plot per-token entropy trajectories to confirm monotonic decline.  
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Vary \(\text{base\_bandwidth}\) from \(0.05\) to \(0.5\) at fixed temperature. Measure accuracy and inter-decoding variance. Expect a “sweet spot” near \(0.3\) that minimizes variance without accuracy loss.  
3. **Token-level pruning validation:** For deterministic prompts (e.g., “2+2=”), log support sizes for Top-b vs. Top-p. Confirm Top-b’s support shrinks to 1–3 tokens in low-entropy states, while Top-p admits dozens. For a high-entropy creative prompt (“Once upon a time”), support sizes should be comparable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization across architectures:** Does Top-b’s performance and entropy reduction hold for models like LLaMA and Qwen, not just Gemma? The future work explicitly proposes testing these families, but current validation is limited to a single model.  
2. **Scaling with model size:** How does Top-b’s stability and variance reduction scale from 2B to 405B parameters? The paper suggests a scaling laws analysis, but results are confined to a 4B model.  
3. **Multilingual and complex math:** Does the entropy-adaptive mechanism remain effective in multilingual reasoning (e.g., MGSM) or ultra-complex mathematical tasks (e.g., MATH, Olympiad)? The current benchmarks are English-focused and may not capture diverse entropy dynamics.  
4. **Optimal functional form:** Is the linear scaling \(\text{bandwidth} \propto 1 + H/H_{\text{max}}\) optimal? Non-linear mappings (e.g., quadratic, sigmoid) might better balance fidelity and stability, but the paper provides no comparison.  
5. **Impact on creative generation:** Does Top-b’s aggressive pruning in low-entropy states harm expressiveness in open-ended tasks like story writing? The paper focuses on reasoning; creative tasks may require preserved diversity.

## Limitations
- **Key hyperparameters unspecified:** The exact \(\text{base\_bandwidth}\) used for main results is not given (only \(\approx 0.30\) for high temperature), and the temperature for reported experiments is unclear.
- **Prompt templates missing:** The precise formatting for GSM8K and GPQA is not provided, hindering exact replication; minor variations can affect accuracy.
- **Implementation ambiguities:** Details like whether renormalization occurs after truncation are implied but not formalized, and vocabulary size for \(H_{\text{max}}\) may vary across tokenizers.
