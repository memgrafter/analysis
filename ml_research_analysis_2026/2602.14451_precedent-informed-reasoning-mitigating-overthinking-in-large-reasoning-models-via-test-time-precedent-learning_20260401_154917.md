# Precedent-Informed Reasoning: Mitigating Overthinking in Large Reasoning Models via Test-Time Precedent Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.14451
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14451
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: PIR consistently shortens reasoning tokens while matching or improving accuracy across math, science, and code benchmarks; e.g., on DeepSeek-R1-32B, LiveCodeBench accuracy ↑ from 41.02% to 57.11% and tokens ↓ from 5224.52 to 4023.37.

## Executive Summary
Large reasoning models (LRMs) often exhibit overthinking—generating redundant chain-of-thought tokens—which increases computational cost and can degrade performance. This paper introduces Precedent-Informed Reasoning (PIR), a test-time framework that shifts from self-exploration to guided learning from past solved examples (precedents). PIR comprises two components: Adaptive Precedent Selection (APS), which retrieves compact, model-aware precedents using semantic similarity and perplexity, and Test-time Experience Internalization (TEI), which internalizes solution patterns via lightweight LoRA adaptation. Evaluated on five benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, AMC AIME, GPQA Diamond, LiveCodeBench), PIR reduces reasoning token counts while maintaining or improving accuracy across model scales. For DeepSeek-R1-32B, LiveCodeBench accuracy rises from 41.02% to 57.11% with tokens dropping from 5224.52 to 4023.37; on AMC AIME, accuracy improves from 59.92% to 67.40% with tokens reducing from 3661.52 to 2935.83.

## Method Summary
PIR operates in two test-time stages. First, Adaptive Precedent Selection (APS) identifies the most informative precedents for a query. For each candidate precedent from a static pool, APS computes a Reference Score combining semantic similarity (cosine in embedding space) and normalized model perplexity. It then adaptively determines the optimal number of precedents \( m^* \) by maximizing the perplexity reduction of the instruction formed by concatenating the query and precedents. Second, Test-time Experience Internalization (TEI) uses the selected precedents to construct an instruction, initializes lightweight LoRA adapters (rank 8 on query/value projections), and performs a single gradient step to minimize perplexity on that instruction. The adapted model then generates reasoning for the query, producing shorter chains with improved accuracy.

## Key Results
- On DeepSeek-R1-32B, LiveCodeBench accuracy ↑ 16.09 points (41.02% → 57.11%) while tokens ↓ 23% (5224.52 → 4023.37).
- On AMC AIME, accuracy ↑ 7.48 points (59.92% → 67.40%) with tokens ↓ 20% (3661.52 → 2935.83).
- PIR delivers consistent accuracy-token trade-off improvements across GSM8K, MATH500, GPQA Diamond, and multiple model sizes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual-Criteria Precedent Ranking
**Claim**: Precedents that are both semantically similar to the target question and have low perplexity under the target model are most informative for guiding reasoning.
**Mechanism**: For a query, compute semantic similarity (embedding cosine) and model perplexity for each precedent. Combine into a Reference Score: \( \text{RS} = \text{similarity} + \text{normalized(perplexity)} \). Select top-k by RS.
**Break condition**: If semantic similarity fails to capture task-relevant reasoning patterns, or if low perplexity precedents are not useful for solving, selection may introduce noise.

### Mechanism 2: Adaptive Budget via Perplexity Reduction
**Claim**: The optimal number of precedents varies by question and model, determined by maximizing perplexity reduction of the precedent-informed instruction.
**Mechanism**: Incrementally add top-ranked precedents to form instructions \( I_m \). Compute relative perplexity reduction \( \Delta P(I_m) = P(I_0) - P(I_m) \). Choose \( m^* \) maximizing \( \Delta P \).
**Break condition**: If perplexity reduction does not correlate with downstream accuracy or efficiency gains, the adaptive budget may select an inappropriate \( m^* \).

### Mechanism 3: Test-Time Experience Internalization via LoRA
**Claim**: Updating lightweight LoRA adapters on the precedent-informed instruction at test time internalizes solution patterns, reducing redundant exploration.
**Mechanism**: Initialize LoRA parameters, perform one backward pass minimizing perplexity \( \min_{\Delta\Theta} P(I_{m^*}; \Theta + \Delta\Theta) \). The adapted model \( M(\Theta+\Delta\Theta) \) then generates reasoning with this prior.
**Break condition**: Overfitting to precedents or distortion of general reasoning abilities may hurt performance; irrelevant precedents may misguide.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input (question + precedent pool) -> APS (ranking & selection) -> TEI (LoRA adaptation) -> Reasoning Engine (generation)

**Critical Path**:
1. Given query \( q \) and precedent set \( E_s \), APS selects \( E^* = \text{top-}m^* \) precedents.
2. TEI constructs instruction \( I^* \) from \( q \) and \( E^* \), computes \( \Delta\Theta \) via one gradient step on \( I^* \).
3. Generate reasoning with \( M(\Theta+\Delta\Theta) \) on \( q \), output answer \( y \).

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- LoRA vs full fine-tuning: LoRA reduces memory/time but may limit adaptation capacity.
- Adaptive vs fixed precedent count: Adaptive avoids arbitrary budget but adds minor computation.
- Model-aware (perplexity) vs semantic-only selection: Perplexity captures model-specific informativeness but requires forward passes.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Ablation w/o APS: Degraded accuracy and increased token length.
- Ablation w/o TEI: Token length increases significantly (e.g., MATH500: 3725 vs 1485 tokens).
- Irrelevant precedents: Likely harms performance if selection fails or pool lacks diversity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Dynamic precedent curation**: How can PIR maintain high-quality, evolving precedent sets as new problems emerge? *Basis*: Authors note static pool limitations; future direction is dynamic maintenance. *Why unresolved*: Current implementation uses fixed training data.
- **System-level optimizations**: What caching/batching can reduce PIR's test-time overhead? *Basis*: Paper admits runtime overhead; suggests caching/batched retrieval. *Why unresolved*: Overhead may hinder real-time deployment.
- **Generalization to ambiguous tasks**: Does PIR work for non-deductive tasks (e.g., creative writing) with ambiguous solutions? *Basis*: Evaluated only on structured benchmarks; assumption of reusable patterns may not hold for divergent tasks. *Why unresolved*: No evidence beyond math/science/code.
- **Adaptation intensity**: How do gradient steps, learning rate, adapter rank affect accuracy-efficiency trade-off? *Basis*: Paper uses single step with fixed hyperparameters; no ablation. *Why unresolved*: Optimal settings may vary with model, task, precedent quality.

## Limitations
- Adaptive budget relies on perplexity reduction as a proxy for quality; theoretical link to accuracy/token reduction is weak.
- Compute overhead from APS (embedding queries) and TEI (gradient step) is not fully reflected in reported token counts.
- Performance depends on a diverse, high-quality precedent pool; may degrade on out-of-distribution tasks or with scarce precedents.

## Confidence
- **PIR overall** (accuracy & token reduction): **High** (consistent gains across 5 datasets and multiple model sizes).
- **APS selection** (semantic + perplexity ranking): **Medium** (ablation confirms contribution, but selection can fail if similarity/perplexity mismatch).
- **Adaptive budget via perplexity reduction**: **Medium** (empirically outperforms fixed budgets, but correlation with final accuracy not established).
- **TEI** (single-step LoRA update): **Medium** (ablation shows necessity, but single-step optimality and overfitting risks unclear).

## Next Checks
- **Perplexity-accuracy correlation analysis**: On a validation set, compute per-question perplexity reduction and accuracy change; expect positive correlation to justify adaptive criterion.
- **End-to-end compute cost measurement**: Benchmark wall-clock time and monetary cost (including embedding and adaptation) vs. standard CoT to confirm real-world efficiency.
- **Cross-domain generality test**: Apply PIR to a non-specialized model (e.g., Llama-3-8B) and a commonsense QA dataset (e.g., CommonsenseQA) to verify robustness beyond math/code tasks.
