# Multi-Station WiFi CSI Sensing Framework Robust to Station-wise Feature Missingness and Limited Labeled Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11858
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11858
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Framework achieves 0.0924 RMSE on office dataset (10% labeled data, 1 station), a 25% improvement over best baseline (0.1233 RMSE).

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a critical real-world challenge in multi-station WiFi Channel State Information (CSI) sensing: maintaining accuracy when some stations are unavailable and when labeled training data is scarce. The proposed two-stage solution first uses cross-modal self-supervised learning (CroSSL) on abundant unlabeled data to learn representations that are invariant to which stations are present. Second, during supervised downstream training with limited labels, it employs Station-wise Masking Augmentation (SMA) to simulate station unavailability, aligning the training distribution with the inference scenario. Evaluated on two real-world datasets for position estimation and image generation, the method consistently outperforms baselines, especially under severe missingness and label scarcity. Ablations confirm that both CroSSL and SMA are essential; neither alone suffices.

## Method Summary
The framework operates in two stages. **Pre-training (CroSSL)** leverages unlabeled multi-station CSI. For each sample, station-specific encoders produce embeddings, which are randomly masked (simulating missing stations). Two masked views are aggregated into global embeddings, and a VICReg loss enforces consistency between them while preventing collapse, thereby learning representations robust to station missingness. **Downstream training (with SMA)** freezes the pre-trained encoders and aggregator. With limited labeled data, entire station inputs are randomly masked before processing, exposing the downstream model to realistic incomplete inputs. The downstream task head (e.g., regressor or CNN) is then trained on the resulting global embeddings. This combination ensures the feature extractor provides station-invariant features and the downstream head learns to utilize them under actual missingness conditions.

## Key Results
- On the office position estimation dataset with only 10% labeled data and inference using 1 station, the proposed method achieves 0.0924 RMSE, beating the best baseline (OutputEnsemble) by 25% (0.1233 RMSE).
- On the factory dataset with 30% labeled data and 1 station available, it achieves 0.1310 RMSE, a 16% improvement over the best baseline (RE, 0.1552 RMSE).
- Ablation studies show that using CroSSL without SMA or SMA without CroSSL leads to significant performance drops under combined missingness and label scarcity, confirming their synergistic necessity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** CroSSL pre-training learns global representations invariant to which stations are available by enforcing consistency across artificially masked station subsets.
**Mechanism:** During pre-training on unlabeled multi-station CSI, random subsets of station-specific embeddings are masked. Two masked views of the same sample are created, and a self-supervised loss (VICReg) aligns their global aggregations while preventing collapse. This forces the encoder and aggregator to capture cross-station correlations rather than station-specific features.
**Core assumption:** Task-relevant information can be inferred from any sufficiently large subset of stations, and different station subsets should yield similar embeddings for the same underlying scene.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states CroSSL learns representations invariant to missing stations; Section III.C details masking and VICReg loss; ablation shows CroSSL alone degrades under missingness (especially factory dataset).
**Break condition:** If certain stations provide unique, non-redundant information that cannot be reconstructed from others (e.g., extreme spatial separation), the invariance constraint may discard critical information.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Station-wise Masking Augmentation (SMA) aligns the downstream model's training distribution with the inference distribution where stations are missing.
**Mechanism:** During supervised training with limited labels, entire station inputs are randomly masked (zero-padded) before processing by the pre-trained feature extractor. This exposes the downstream model to realistic incomplete inputs, forcing it to rely on the station-invariant embeddings.
**Core assumption:** The distribution of station unavailability during training (simulated by SMA) matches the distribution encountered at inference time.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes SMA exposing model to realistic missingness; Section III.D details input masking; Section IV.H & V.G show SMA improves other SSL methods, validating its role.
**Break condition:** If real-world missingness is structured (e.g., correlated failures) rather than independent per station, random SMA may not simulate the correct pattern, leading to residual train-test mismatch.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The combination of CroSSL pre-training and SMA is synergistic; CroSSL provides robust features, while SMA ensures the downstream head can utilize them under missingness.
**Mechanism:** CroSSL encodes station-missingness invariance into the feature extractor, but if the downstream model is trained only on fully observed inputs, it may overfit to having all stations. SMA bridges this gap by making downstream training experience the same missingness conditions.
**Core assumption:** The pre-trained extractor is sufficiently expressive and the downstream model sufficiently flexible to benefit from station-invariant features when trained with SMA.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states neither alone suffices; Section IV.F & V.F show combined robustness; Section IV.H & V.G ablation shows CroSSL+SMA often beats other combinations.
**Break condition:** If the pre-trained extractor fails to produce truly invariant embeddings, SMA cannot salvage performance. Conversely, if the downstream model is too rigid, it may not adapt even with SMA.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Self-Supervised Learning via Cross-Modal Consistency**  
  **Why needed:** Labeled CSI data is scarce and environment-specific. SSL leverages abundant unlabeled CSI to learn general representations that transfer to downstream tasks with few labels.  
  **Quick check:** How does the SSL pretext task (masking stations and aligning views) relate to the downstream goal of robustness to missing stations?

- **Concept: Data Augmentation by Structured Masking**  
  **Why needed:** Standard augmentations (time warp, noise) do not simulate the specific challenge of entire stations being unavailable. SMA explicitly creates the missingness pattern the model must handle at inference.  
  **Quick check:** Why is masking entire stations more effective for this problem than masking subcarriers or time steps (like Random Erasing)?

- **Concept: Multi-Station Feature Aggregation**  
  **Why needed:** Multiple stations provide spatial diversity and redundancy. A fixed aggregation (e.g., concatenation) fails if some stations drop. The framework must learn to aggregate variably available features.  
  **Quick check:** What architectural choice in the aggregator (e.g., permutation invariance) enables it to handle different station subsets?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Station-specific encoder E^(d) for each station d -> Shared aggregator A -> Downstream model D.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Preprocessing: CSI window → scaled subcarrier vector per station.  
  2. Pre-training: Input X_i^(d) → E^(d) → embedding Q_i^(d) → random station masking → A → global Z_i, Z'_i → VICReg loss.  
  3. Downstream: Input X_i^(d) → SMA masking → E^(d) → A → Z_i → D → supervised loss.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Masking rate p_mask: Too low under-exposes to missingness; too high loses information. Paper uses p_mask=0.5.  
  - Encoder capacity: Identity encoder (office) vs. small MLP (factory). Underfitting limits representation quality; overfitting risks memorizing station identities.  
  - Aggregator design: Must be permutation-invariant. Simple pooling may lose power; learned MLP aggregator is used.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Pre-training failure: Representation collapse (all embeddings identical), monitored by VICReg variance term.  
  - Downstream failure: RMSE increases sharply as available stations decrease, especially under label scarcity.  
  - SMA mismatch: If SMA masking (independent stations) differs from real missingness (e.g., correlated failures), performance degrades under unseen patterns.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Robustness to varying available stations: Train with 100% labels, test with 1,4,8 (office) or 1,4,9 (factory) stations. Expect proposed method to show smallest RMSE increase as stations drop.  
  2. Robustness to label scarcity: Fix inference condition (e.g., 1 station), vary downstream training label ratio (0.1% to 100%). Expect SSL-based methods to degrade least.  
  3. Ablation of CroSSL vs. SMA: Train with all labels but disable either CroSSL (random init) or SMA (no augmentation). Expect significant drop under missing stations, confirming necessity of both.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How well does the proposed framework generalize to other downstream tasks in WiFi CSI sensing, such as human activity recognition or gesture tracking, under station-wise feature missingness and limited labeled data?  
- How do different neural network architectures for the feature extractor and downstream model affect the framework's robustness to station-wise feature missingness and label scarcity?  
- How can stations be automatically selected or weighted to exclude non-contributory ones (e.g., mobile or noisy stations) in multi-station CSI deployments?

## Limitations
- Optimal masking probability p_mask and its sensitivity are dataset-dependent; the paper reports 0.5 but lacks systematic sweep.
- Key hyperparameters (encoder/aggregator architectures, learning rates, dropout) are only partially disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- SMA assumes independent station dropout; real-world missingness may be correlated, potentially degrading robustness.

## Confidence
- CroSSL learns station-invariant representations: **Medium** (motivated by theory and ablation, but no direct invariance measurement).
- SMA aligns training with inference missingness: **Medium** (supported by ablation, but relies on independence assumption).
- Synergy of CroSSL + SMA is essential: **High** (results are strong and consistent across datasets and baselines).

## Next Checks
1. **Correlated missingness test:** Simulate structured dropout patterns (spatial clusters, temporal bursts) to evaluate SMA’s robustness under realistic mismatches.
2. **Scalability stress test:** Vary station count from 2 to 32 (via subsampling or synthetic expansion) and measure performance, training stability, and embedding dimensionality effects.
3. **Cross-dataset transferability:** Pre-train CroSSL on the office dataset (unlabeled) and directly train downstream heads on the factory dataset with limited labels, without further pre-training, to assess generalization.
