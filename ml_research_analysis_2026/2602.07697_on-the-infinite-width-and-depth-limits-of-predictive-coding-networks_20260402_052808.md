# On the Infinite Width and Depth Limits of Predictive Coding Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07697
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07697
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: The µP parameterization guarantees stable feature learning for PCNs; when width ≫ depth, equilibrated PC energy converges to BP loss, aligning gradients.

## Executive Summary
Predictive coding networks (PCNs) offer a biologically plausible alternative to backpropagation but face stability issues when scaled to deeper and wider architectures. This paper theoretically analyzes the infinite width and depth limits of linear PCNs, extending the mean-field (µP) parameterization known to stabilize backpropagation. The core result proves that the exact same set of width- and depth-stable parameterizations ensures feature learning for PCNs. Moreover, under any such parameterization and when network width *N* is much larger than depth *L*, the equilibrated PC energy converges to the backpropagation loss, causing PC weight gradients to match BP's. These theoretical findings are empirically validated on both linear and nonlinear networks trained on CIFAR-10 and Fashion-MNIST, showing near-identical gradients once activity equilibrium is reached. The work unifies prior scaling theory and offers principled guidelines for scaling PCNs efficiently.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct a theoretical analysis of linear predictive coding networks, assuming activities have fully equilibrated (∂*F*/∂*z* = 0) so learning operates on the closed-form equilibrated energy *F** . They extend the µP parameterization framework—which scales weight initialization, learning rates, and output layer factors by powers of width *N* and depth *L*—to derive necessary and sufficient conditions for stable pre-activations, stable predictions, and stable feature updates. Solving these constraints yields the same one-dimensional families of scaling exponents that guarantee non-lazy (feature-learning) training in backpropagation. Under these parameterizations, they prove that for residual networks when *N* ≫ *L*, the factor relating *F** to the standard loss *L* scales as 1 + *O*(*L*/*N*), vanishing in the limit. Consequently, PC weight gradients converge to BP gradients. Empirically, they measure cosine similarity between PC and BP gradients on linear and nonlinear architectures (including ResNets on CIFAR-10 and Fashion-MNIST) across widths and depths, confirming the theoretical alignment when activity equilibrium is achieved.

## Key Results
- µP parameterization ensures stable feature learning for PCNs, mirroring the conditions for backpropagation.
- When width *N* ≫ depth *L*, the equilibrated PC energy *F** converges to the backpropagation loss *L*, leading to identical weight updates.
- Gradient alignment holds empirically for nonlinear networks (Tanh/ReLU) on image classification, provided activity inference converges.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Same stability constraints as BP**: By formalizing three desiderata—stable pre-activations at initialization, stable predictions during training, and stable feature updates—the authors derive constraints on scaling exponents (*a*ℓ, *b*ℓ, *c*, *d*) for a general µP parameterization. For PC with equilibrated activities, solving these constraints yields exactly the µP families known from BP (e.g., *a*ℓ=1/2, *b*ℓ=0, *c*=0, *d*=1/2). This relies on linear networks and exact equilibrium; otherwise the correspondence may break.
2. **Convergence to BP loss under *N*≫*L***: The forward-pass stability condition combined with output scaling *d*=1/2 causes the rescaling factor *s*(θ) = 1 + *O*(*L*/*N*) to vanish when *N* ≫ *L*. Hence *F** → *L* pointwise, and since PC updates use ∂*F**/∂θ while BP uses ∂*L*/∂θ, the gradients coincide. This fails if *L* scales with or exceeds *N*, as seen in deeper MLPs.
3. **Nonlinear extension via equilibrium**: Although theory assumes linearity, experiments show that if inference reaches equilibrium (using sufficient iterations and appropriate step size β), the same µP scaling yields gradient alignment in nonlinear networks. Ill-conditioning worsens with depth, requiring larger β to maintain convergence.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input layer (x) -> Latent layers (zℓ, adjusted via inference) -> Weight matrices (Wℓ, updated after equilibrium) -> Output layer (f(x) scaled by γ=γ₀ *N*ᵈ). Parameterization control via exponents *a*ℓ (preactivation scaling), *b*ℓ (init variance), *c* (learning-rate), *d* (output scaling), and α (residual branch scaling).
- **Critical path**: 1) Choose µP-compatible exponents (e.g., *a*ℓ=1/2, *b*ℓ=0, *c*=0, *d*=1/2). 2) Initialize weights with variance *N*^{-*b*ℓ}. 3) For each batch: clamp input x and target y; perform iterative inference (z ← z - β∇ₓF) until ∂F/∂z ≈ 0; then compute weight gradients on *F** and update with η = η₀ γ² *N*^{-*c*}.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Width vs depth: Convergence to BP requires *N* ≫ *L*; deeper models need wider layers.
  - Inference step size β: Too small slows equilibrium; too large causes divergence. Depth increases Hessian ill-conditioning, often requiring larger β.
  - Nonlinear activations: While theory assumes linearity, Tanh/ReLU work if equilibrium is reached; some activations may add instability.
- **Failure signatures**: 
  - No vertical: Training loss stagnant, low PC/BP gradient cosine even for large *N* → likely inadequate width or failed equilibrium.
  - Gradient explosion/vanishing: Pre-activations or updates become NaN/zero → check µP exponents.
  - Energy-loss mismatch: Ratio *F**/*L* not approaching 1 → forward-pass scaling error or insufficient equilibrium.
  - Depth-dependent instability: Alignment degrades when *L* ≥ *N*, especially in MLPs; use residual connections with α=1/2.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Width scaling toy task**: Linear MLP, *L*=5, vary *N*. Verify *F**/*L* → 1 and gradient cosine → 1 as *N* increases (Figure 2).
  2. **Depth scaling on residual nets**: Linear, vary *L* with *N* fixed, α=1/2. Confirm *s*(θ)-1 scales as *L*/*N* (Figure 3) and cosine remains high for *N*≫*L* (Figure 1).
  3. **Nonlinear CIFAR-10**: Residual network with Tanh/ReLU, mean-field, *N*=2048, *L*=2,16, vary β. With sufficiently large β, PC/BP gradient cosine near 1; small β causes degradation with depth (Figure 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to nonlinear PCNs**: How to rigorously analyze infinite-width/depth limits for nonlinear networks? The equilibrated energy lacks closed form; dynamical mean-field theory approximations might help.
2. **Alternative stable PC parameterizations**: Could there be µP-like constraints that yield stable feature learning but PC gradients that do not converge to BP? Such parameterizations would require different stability desiderata.
3. **Stable inference for deep PCNs**: What parameterizations or inference algorithms ensure equilibrium convergence in very deep networks? Depth increases Hessian ill-conditioning; joint scaling of learning and inference parameters is needed.
4. **Preserving PC’s computational advantages**: The fast saddle-to-saddle dynamics of shallow PCNs (deeper-than-wider) become unstable under µP. Can such speed-ups survive in depth-stable parameterizations? Empirical comparison on deep ResNets (*L* > 100) is required.

## Limitations
- Theoretical results assume linear architectures and exact activity equilibrium; nonlinear extension is empirical and may fail for *L* ~ *N*.
- The *N* ≫ *L* condition is restrictive; for depth-balanced regimes, PC energy deviates from BP loss and gradients misalign.
- Inference hyperparameters (β, iterations) require manual tuning; no theoretical guarantees on convergence speed.

## Confidence
- **High**: µP scaling guarantees stable feature learning and *F**→*L* for linear PCNs with *N*≫*L*.
- **Medium**: Nonlinear PCNs align with BP gradients when equilibrium is reached, validated on moderate depths and standard activations.
- **Low**: Behavior in very deep (*L* ~ *N*) or non- µP regimes remains unvalidated.
