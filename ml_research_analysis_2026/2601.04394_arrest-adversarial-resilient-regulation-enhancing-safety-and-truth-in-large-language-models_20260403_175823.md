# ARREST: Adversarial Resilient Regulation Enhancing Safety and Truth in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04394
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04394
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** ARREST improves factuality by 6.5%–39.1% on QA benchmarks and reduces safety attack success rate by 17%–41% while increasing soft refusal rates by 10%–65%, without fine-tuning the base model.

## Executive Summary
ARREST is a framework that addresses dual failures in large language models—factual hallucination and unsafe outputs—by identifying and correcting a "representational misalignment" in the model's latent activation space. It operates without fine-tuning the base model, instead using a lightweight external adversarial network to steer internal states at a single, critically misaligned layer. The method first locates this layer via probe networks that measure distributional drift between a base model and an aligned reference. Then, an adversarial regulator is trained to transform misaligned activations toward aligned distributions. Evaluations across multiple benchmarks show substantial gains in truthfulness and safety, including a notable increase in context-aware soft refusals that balance harm prevention with helpfulness.

## Method Summary
**Assumption:** A single layer exists where representational misalignment is maximal. ARREST follows a two-stage pipeline. First, it identifies the layer ℓ* where the base model's internal representations most diverge from an aligned reference (an RLHF-tuned model for safety, or an answer-prompted base model for factuality). This identification is performed by training independent probe classifiers on activations from each layer and selecting the layer with maximal probe accuracy difference. **Unknown:** Exact probe architectures (linear vs. MLP), training details, and dataset size are unspecified (see Limitations). Second, a small external generator network Gθ is trained adversarially—via a min-max game with a discriminator Dϕ—to map misaligned activations from the base model at ℓ* to the aligned distribution, optionally using a contrastive triplet loss for safety. **Assumption:** The adversarial training converges to an effective mapping, and the generator capacity (2-layer FFN) suffices (details inferred from Architecture Onboarding). At inference, Gθ intervenes by replacing the base model's activation at ℓ* before the layer's feed-forward operation, steering outputs in real time. **Unknown:** Whether intervention occurs only at the first token or throughout generation (see Limitations).

## Key Results
- **Factuality:** ARREST-HB boosts accuracy by 6.5%–39.1% across TRUTHFULQA, TRIVIAQA, COQA, and TYDIQA.
- **Safety:** Reduces Attack Success Rate (ASR) by 17%–41% and increases Soft Refusal Rate (SRR) by 10%–65% on MALICIOUS-INSTRUCT, JAILBREAKBENCH, ADVBENCH, and TRUSTLLM.
- **Soft refusals:** Outperforms RLHF-aligned models in generating context-aware, explanatory denials rather than rigid refusals, with PCA validating shifted hidden-state distributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** Both factual and safety failures originate from a measurable representational misalignment—a distributional shift—in the latent activation space of the base LLM at specific layers. An external probe network, trained as a classifier on activations from both a base model and an aligned reference, identifies the layer ℓ* where this misalignment is maximal. The internal state at ℓ* contains a sufficiently disentangled signal of the misalignment, allowing a small external network to steer it and correct downstream outputs without altering the base model. Evidence includes layer-wise probe accuracy peaks and alignment with prior work on representation-level safety.

**Mechanism 2:** A lightweight external adversarial network (generator Gθ) can learn to transform misaligned hidden states into aligned ones by minimizing a distributional divergence. Trained via an adversarial min-max game with a discriminator Dϕ, Gθ maps the base model's misaligned activation distribution (from harmful/unknown prompts) toward the aligned reference distribution (from RLHF or answer-prompted models). The combined loss (adversarial + mean squared error reconstruction) balances content preservation with correction, enabling effective inference-time intervention without full model fine-tuning.

**Mechanism 3:** The contrastive adversarial variant (ARREST-SC) encourages soft refusals by using triplet loss with anchors (misaligned states), positives (hard refusal states), and negatives (jailbroken states). This steers Gθ to move representations away from the unsafe cluster toward the safe boundary, avoiding exact replication of hard refusals and preserving semantic content. The latent geometry allows interpolation that retains helpfulness while neutralizing harm, as shown by increased soft refusal rates and maintained utility.

- **Concept: Inference-Time Intervention via External Network**: Modifying hidden states during generation using a separately trained small network, avoiding costly fine-tuning. *Why needed*: Core innovation of ARREST; separation of base model generation and steering. *Quick check*: What trade-off does the λ coefficient control between adversarial loss and reconstruction loss?
- **Concept: Probe-Based Layer Selection & PCA for Validation**: Using probes to find ℓ* and PCA to visualize intervention effects. *Why needed*: Critical design choice; intervening at the maximal-drift layer is key to efficacy. *Quick check*: Why might intervening at multiple layers degrade performance, according to the paper's ablation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
1. Probe Networks (P_ℓ): Independent linear classifiers (or small MLPs), one per layer ℓ. Trained on frozen activations from base and aligned models to classify alignment status. Outputs probe accuracy used to find ℓ*.
2. Generator (Gθ): Small 2-layer feed-forward network with GELU. Input: base model's activation ht_ℓ*. Output: steered activation ht_ℓ*'. Trained via adversarial loss to map misaligned to aligned distributions.
3. Discriminator (Dϕ): Single-layer MLP. Trained to distinguish Gθ(ht_ℓ*) from true aligned activations ˆht_ℓ*. Used only during training.
4. Intervention Module: At inference, applies Gθ to activations at layer ℓ* before the layer's feed-forward/output projection. Requires internal model access.
5. Training Data Pipeline: For safety: pairs base model activations on harmful prompts with RLHF model activations on same/refusal prompts. For factuality: pairs base model activations on questions with base model activations conditioned on correct answers.

**Critical path**:
1. **Layer Identification Phase:** For each task, train all P_ℓ on paired activation datasets. Compute layer-wise drift D(ℓ) (probe accuracy difference). Select ℓ* = argmax D(ℓ).
2. **Adversarial Training Phase:** Freeze base and reference models. Train Gθ and Dϕ on activations extracted only at ℓ* from base (misaligned) and reference (aligned). Use combined loss LG = Ladv + λ·LMSE (or triplet loss for contrastive safety).
3. **Inference Phase:** Load trained Gθ. For input, run base model up to ℓ*, capture ht_ℓ*, compute ht_ℓ*' = Gθ(ht_ℓ*), and continue generation from ht_ℓ*'.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Single vs. multi-layer intervention:** Ablation (Fig. 7) shows single-layer (ℓ*) optimal; multi-layer correction disrupts internal structure, hurting fluency/utility.
- **λ (reconstruction loss weight):** High λ over-constrains toward reference (e.g., hard refusal), losing content/soft refusals. Low λ may insufficiently correct. Optimal λ (e.g., 1e-7) balances adversarial steering and content preservation.
- **Base adversarial (ARREST-HB/SB) vs. contrastive (ARREST-HC/SC):** Contrastive uses triplet loss with explicit positive/negative prompts. It shows stronger soft refusal gains but requires careful negative prompt engineering for jailbreak triggers.
- **Target distribution source:** Factuality uses answer-prompted base states as "aligned" reference, avoiding a separate tuned model. Safety uses RLHF model states as gold standard.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Probe phase:** All P_ℓ have similar, near-random accuracy (e.g., ~50%) → representational misalignment hypothesis may not hold for this model/dataset.
- **Training phase:** Discriminator accuracy stays near 50%, or generator loss does not decrease → Gθ too small or feature distributions not separable at ℓ*.
- **Inference phase:** Utility collapse (outputs nonsensical/repetitive) or safety regression (ASR increases) → overshooting or inadequate correction. Check: BLEURT/factual accuracy on benign QA.
- **Soft refusal failure:** SRR does not increase despite ASR decrease → Gθ generating hard refusals or failing to preserve content. Check λ value and contrastive loss setup.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Reproduce Probe Layer Selection:** On LLaMA-2-7B with 100 samples from MALICIOUS-INSTRUCT, extract activations from every layer (post-attention, pre-projection). Train linear probe for each layer (e.g., 100 epochs, Adam, lr=1e-3). Plot probe accuracy vs. layer. Verify a distinct peak in mid-late layers.
2. **Train & Validate Single-Layer Mitigator (ARREST-SB):** Using identified ℓ*, extract paired activations (base on harmful prompts, Chat on same). Train Gθ (2-layer FFN, hidden dim=512, GELU) and Dϕ (1-layer MLP) with Ladv + 1e-7·LMSE for 150 epochs, batch size 16, AdamW. Evaluate: (a) discriminator accuracy should drop to ~50%, (b) visualize PCA of ht_ℓ*, ˆht_ℓ*, and Gθ(ht_ℓ*) to see shift toward aligned cluster.
3. **End-to-End Safety Evaluation on 10 Prompts:** Load trained Gθ. Run 5 harmful and 5 benign prompts through full pipeline (intervene at ℓ*). For harmful prompts: manually check refusals and classify as soft (explanatory) or hard. For benign prompts: assess answer coherence and factuality (e.g., BLEURT). Expected: ASR↓, SRR↑, no utility degradation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Do multi-head attention mechanisms and internal representations govern both safety and hallucination behaviors?
2. Can black-box prompting alone address safety and hallucination challenges, or do they require modeling latent decision boundaries in LLM feature spaces?
3. How does the adversarial training objective in ARREST explicitly shape the distribution of internal representations to steer outputs toward safety and truthfulness?
4. Under what conditions (e.g., prompt type, model layer, λ hyperparameter) do soft refusals emerge in ARREST, and how do humans perceive their helpfulness compared to hard refusals?
5. How transferable is ARREST's representational intervention to other alignment objectives beyond safety and factuality (e.g., fairness, privacy, robustness)?

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter specifications omitted:** Exact probe architectures, Gθ/Dϕ dimensions, learning rates, λ schedule, and adversarial loss type not provided, hindering precise replication.
- **Inference mechanics unclear:** Not specified whether activation replacement occurs only at first token or throughout generation; interaction with KV caching may affect coherence and metrics.
- **Dependence on internal access:** Requires extracting hidden states, limiting applicability to closed-source APIs.
- **Single-layer assumption:** While ablations show optimality, potential complementary signals across layers remain unexplored.
- **Weak corpus support:** Neighboring literature does not directly validate the specific probe-to-ℓ* identification or adversarial training setup, raising external validity concerns.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Representational misalignment detectable at a specific layer):** Medium. Paper reports clear probe accuracy peaks, but lack of probe training details prevents verification of robustness.
- **Mechanism 2 (External adversarial network steers activations):** Medium. Substantial performance gains shown, yet missing hyperparameters cast doubt on training stability and generalizability.
- **Mechanism 3 (Contrastive loss enables soft refusals):** Medium. Soft Refusal Rate improvements demonstrated, but sensitivity to λ and negative prompt engineering indicates fragility; theoretical gradient analysis lacks direct empirical validation.
