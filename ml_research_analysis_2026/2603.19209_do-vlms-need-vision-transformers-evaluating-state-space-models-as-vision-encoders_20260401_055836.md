# Do VLMs Need Vision Transformers? Evaluating State Space Models as Vision Encoders

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19209  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19209  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** VMamba (SSM) outperforms transformer-based vision encoders in frozen VLMs, dominating localization benchmarks while staying competitive on VQA; dense-task pretraining and interface stabilization are critical for robust performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether state space model (SSM) vision backbones can replace transformers in vision‑language models (VLMs). Using a controlled LLaVA‑style setup with frozen vision encoders, the authors compare VMamba (SSM) against ViT, MaxViT, and MambaVision under matched ImageNet‑1K pretraining and with detection/segmentation checkpoints. They find that VMamba achieves the strongest overall performance, particularly on grounding tasks, while maintaining competitive VQA scores. Dense‑task pretraining benefits all families, but detection‑pretrained backbones often suffer a “localization collapse” that can be fixed by strengthening the vision‑language connector and using square inputs. ImageNet accuracy and model size poorly predict VLM outcomes, highlighting the effectiveness and efficiency of SSMs for VLMs.

## Method Summary
The evaluation builds a LLaVA‑style VLM: a frozen vision encoder, a randomly initialized MLP connector, and a frozen Vicuna‑7B LLM. Images are resized (letterbox) to a target resolution—224×224 for ImageNet‑1K experiments, 512×512 for stabilization tests—and processed by the backbone. Visual tokens are extracted from a specific stage to yield a fixed number (e.g., 196 tokens). The connector maps token dimensions to the LLM’s embedding space; these tokens are concatenated with text embeddings and fed to the LLM for autoregressive generation. The model is instruction‑tuned for one epoch on the 665K LLaVA‑v1.5 dataset using AdamW (lr=2e‑5, BF16, FSDP). Performance is measured on VQA (VQA‑v2, GQA, VizWiz, TextVQA, POPE, TallyQA) and grounding (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, OCID‑Ref) benchmarks, reporting weighted averages. For dense‑pretrained backbones, COCO detection or ADE20K segmentation checkpoints are used, often with non‑square inputs before stabilization interventions.

## Key Results
- Under matched IN1K/224, VMamba achieves the highest overall performance, excelling in localization (e.g., weighted localization 39% for VMamba‑T vs 20% for ViT‑S and 31% for MambaVision‑S) while remaining VQA‑competitive (~62%).
- Dense‑task pretraining (detection/segmentation) consistently improves VQA and localization across transformer and SSM backbones.
- Detection‑pretrained backbones (e.g., ViTDet‑L, VMamba‑B) exhibit severe localization collapse (e.g., 13% weighted localization) with default connector and non‑square inputs; increasing connector capacity (3‑layer MLP) and using 512×512 square inputs recovers strong performance (e.g., ViTDet‑L up to 44%).
- ImageNet accuracy and model size show poor correlation with VLM performance, underscoring VMamba’s size efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: SSM's Directional Scans Encode Spatial Relationships
- **Claim:** VMamba’s 2D‑selective scan preserves fine‑grained spatial structure through multi‑directional state updates, enabling superior grounding without explicit spatial supervision.  
- **Mechanism:** SS4D layers propagate information across the 2D token grid via horizontal and vertical scans in both directions, repeated across stages. This architectural bias embeds spatial interactions into features, unlike ViT’s permutation‑invariant self‑attention that relies on positional encodings which may attenuate.  
- **Core Assumption:** Spatial fidelity in visual features is critical for VLM grounding tasks and transfers from classification pretraining if the backbone has strong inductive bias.  
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract claim of VMamba’s effectiveness; Section 4.2 description of SS4D scans; corpus shows SSMs can outperform transformers in VLMs at larger scales, but this study isolates the effect under matched settings.  
- **Break Condition:** If the vision–language interface (connector or input geometry) is unstable, spatial benefits may not reach the LLM, as seen in localization collapse cases.

### Mechanism 2: Dense‑Task Objectives Inject Spatial Supervision
- **Claim:** Detection or segmentation pretraining directly supervises spatial layouts, enhancing feature discriminability for grounding and VQA across backbone families.  
- **Mechanism:** Dense tasks require pixel or region‑level predictions, forcing the backbone to learn spatially precise representations. This transfers to VLMs, improving both localization and VQA when the interface is stable.  
- **Core Assumption:** Spatial fidelity gained from dense objectives complements or surpasses what classification pretraining provides, especially for backbones without inherent spatial bias.  
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract notes dense‑task tuning generally improves performance; Section 4.3 states that spatial supervision often translates into gains on both localization and VQA; corpus contains limited direct evidence, with other works exploring multi‑task pretraining but not controlled VLM swaps.  
- **Break Condition:** Some detection‑adapted checkpoints (e.g., ViTDet‑L/H) still show localization collapse without interface stabilization, indicating pretraining alone is insufficient.

### Mechanism 3: Interface Stability Mediates Feature Transfer
- **Claim:** The vision–language connector capacity and input geometry determine whether spatial features from the backbone are faithfully utilized by the LLM.  
- **Mechanism:** A weak connector (e.g., shallow MLP) may fail to map high‑resolution spatial tokens without distortion. Non‑square inputs (e.g., 1333×800) introduce aspect ratio biases that confuse LLM positional understanding. Stabilizations like deeper connectors and square inputs (512×512) mitigate these issues.  
- **Core Assumption:** Even optimal vision features can be lost during transfer if the interface design is mismatched, causing localization collapse.  
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract mentions instability and proposed stabilization strategies; Section 4.4 attributes collapse to vision–language transfer failure; Section 4.5 shows performance recovery (e.g., ViTDet‑L localization from 13% to 44%) with stronger connector and square inputs.  
- **Break Condition:** High‑resolution non‑square inputs combined with default connectors lead to collapse; stabilizations address both transmission (connector) and utilization (geometry) bottlenecks.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Image -> Letterbox Resize -> Vision Encoder (Backbone) -> Feature Map -> Token Extraction -> Visual Tokens -> MLP Connector -> LLM Input Sequence -> LLM -> Generated Text

**Critical path:**  
1. Image resized to target geometry (e.g., 224×224 or 512×512, square or non‑square).  
2. Vision encoder outputs a feature map; tokens are extracted from a specific stage (e.g., stage‑3 for hierarchical models) to yield a fixed number (e.g., L=196).  
3. Connector (MLP) transforms token dimension to match LLM embedding size.  
4. Visual tokens are concatenated with text embeddings and fed to the frozen LLM for autoregressive generation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Resolution vs. memory: Higher resolution increases tokens, raising LLM compute and GPU usage; non‑square geometries may save memory but risk instability.  
- Connector capacity: Deeper MLPs better preserve spatial structure but add parameters and latency.  
- Backbone scale: Smaller SSMs (e.g., VMamba‑T, 30M) offer strong efficiency; larger models may overfit classification objectives and degrade spatial performance.  
- Pretraining objective: Classification is scalable but may lose spatial details; dense tasks improve spatial fidelity but require careful interface design.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Localization collapse: Dramatic drop in grounding scores (e.g., ViTDet‑L weighted localization from 44% to 13% under default settings).  
- Scaling inconsistency: Larger model size or higher ImageNet accuracy correlates weakly or negatively with VLM performance.  
- Geometry sensitivity: Non‑square inputs (e.g., 1333×800) with detection‑pretrained backbones cause collapse; square inputs often stabilize.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Matched IN1K/224 backbone swaps: Compare VMamba, ViT, MaxViT, MambaVision at same resolution (224×224) and token count (L=196) to isolate architectural effects.  
2. Dense‑objective evaluations: Test detection (IN1K→COCO) and segmentation (IN1K→ADE20K) pretrained checkpoints to assess objective impact on VLM performance.  
3. Stabilization trials: Apply stronger connector (3‑layer MLP) and square input geometry (512×512) to collapse cases (e.g., ViTDet‑L, VMamba‑B) to recover localization and overall scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1  
**Question:** Why does using square input geometry (e.g., 512×512) stabilize localization in detection‑pretrained VLMs that otherwise exhibit collapse with non‑square inputs?  
**Basis in paper:** Explicitly stated in Section 4.5.3 (“We leave a deeper mechanistic analysis…”) and empirical recovery results.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates the effect but does not identify whether it arises from token distribution, positional encoding, connector capacity, or LLM utilization patterns.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Ablations controlling for aspect ratio, token spatial arrangement, connector size, and analyses of LLM attention over visual tokens under different geometries.

### Open Question 2  
**Question:** How can contrastive or self‑supervised pretraining be effectively integrated into SSM‑based vision backbones, and will this yield consistent improvements in VLM performance?  
**Basis in paper:** Conclusion suggests bringing contrastive/self‑supervised objectives to VMamba‑style backbones as a promising direction; Appendix F notes the lack of such pretrained SSM checkpoints.  
**Why unresolved:** Current SSM vision models are predominantly supervised; the potential of contrastive (CLIP‑like) or self‑supervised (DINOv2‑like) pretraining for VLMs remains unexplored due to unavailable checkpoints.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Pre‑train VMamba or similar SSMs with contrastive or self‑supervised objectives, then plug them into the same VLM framework to measure changes in VQA and localization relative to supervised pretraining.

### Open Question 3  
**Question:** Why do standard vision metrics (ImageNet accuracy, linear probing scores) poorly predict downstream VLM performance, and what alternative metrics could better guide vision backbone selection for VLMs?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred from Section 4.4.1 observations that higher ImageNet accuracy or larger backbones do not reliably translate into better VLM performance, and probing results do not fully explain VLM rankings.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper hypothesizes over‑specialization to classification objectives but lacks a diagnostic that directly measures spatial representation quality relevant to grounding and VQA.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Develop probing tasks that explicitly test spatial fidelity (e.g., fine‑grained localization, part‑whole reasoning) and correlate these with VLM outcomes across a diverse set of backbones.

## Limitations
- The exact training codebase, hyperparameters (connector dimensions, initialization), and full 665K instruction‑tuning mixture are unreleased, hindering exact replication.
- Random seed and detailed dataset preprocessing thresholds are not disclosed; minor variations could affect results.
- Dense‑pretrained checkpoint adaptation pipelines and feature extraction stages lack full standardization, complicating faithful reproduction of detection/segmentation experiments.

## Confidence
- VMamba outperforms transformers under matched IN1K/224: **High**  
- Dense‑task pretraining universally improves VLM performance: **Medium**  
- Interface stabilization (connector depth, square inputs) resolves collapse: **High**  
- SSM’s directional scans inherently preserve spatial structure for grounding: **Medium**  
- ImageNet accuracy poorly predicts VLM performance: **High**
