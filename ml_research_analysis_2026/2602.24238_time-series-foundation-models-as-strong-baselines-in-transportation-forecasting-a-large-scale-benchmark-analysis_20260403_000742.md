# Time Series Foundation Models as Strong Baselines in Transportation Forecasting: A Large-Scale Benchmark Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.24238
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24238
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: Chronos-2, a general-purpose time-series foundation model, achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive deterministic accuracy and well-calibrated probabilistic forecasts across ten diverse real-world transportation datasets without any dataset-specific training or architectural changes.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines whether a pre-trained time-series foundation model (TS-FM) can serve as an effective zero-shot baseline for transportation forecasting. The authors evaluate Chronos-2 on ten datasets covering traffic speed/volume, bike-sharing, and EV charging. The model yields state-of-the-art or highly competitive deterministic accuracy (MAE, RMSE, MAPE) and provides well-calibrated prediction intervals with sharp uncertainty bounds, all without fine-tuning or dataset-specific modifications. The results advocate for adopting TS-FMs as default, efficient baselines in transportation forecasting research, reducing the need for custom deep learning architectures.

## Method Summary
The study conducts a zero-shot evaluation of Chronos-2 across ten transportation datasets. Inputs consist of a historical context window (fixed to one week, computed as 7 days × 24 hours × 60 minutes divided by the dataset’s granularity) and, for multivariate tasks, an alternate variable as a known covariate (e.g., bike pickups when predicting dropoffs). Forecast horizons vary per dataset (1–12 steps). Deterministic metrics (MAE, RMSE, MAPE) are derived from the median (0.5 quantile) of the model’s output; probabilistic metrics (80% interval coverage, interquantile range) use the 0.1 and 0.9 quantiles. Inference uses the frozen Chronos-2 model (120M parameters, T5-based encoder-only transformer) via the chronos-forecasting library, with cross-series attention (group attention) enabled. A minimal reproduction plan involves cloning the provided repository, ensuring proper context lengths and splits, running inference, and computing metrics. Unknowns include the exact grouping strategy for cross-series attention and the aggregation method for multi-horizon errors. Common failure modes are incorrect context length or quantile extraction.

## Key Results
- Chronos-2 achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive deterministic accuracy on most datasets, often outperforming classical and specialized deep learning baselines, particularly at longer forecast horizons.
- It provides well-calibrated uncertainty intervals (empirical coverage near 80%) with sharp predictive distributions for probabilistic forecasting, without any dataset-specific adjustments.
- Performance degrades on METR-LA, a dataset with complex spatial interactions, suggesting limitations of the implicit spatial modeling approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: General-Purpose Pre-training Enables Zero-Shot Transfer
- **Claim**: Chronos-2 leverages broad statistical patterns learned during large-scale pre-training on heterogeneous time series to produce accurate forecasts on new transportation tasks without fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism**: The model maps the input context window to a latent representation that captures temporal dynamics (periodicity, trends) and decodes forecasts. Cross-series attention within a batch shares information across series, exploiting spatial correlations without predefined adjacency matrices.
- **Core assumption**: The pre-training data distribution overlaps with the latent structure of transportation time series (e.g., daily/weekly seasonality, autocorrelation).
- **Evidence**: The abstract and Section II describe the universal training objective and group attention. Competitive results against specialized baselines across ten datasets support the claim.
- **Break condition**: Performance degrades if transportation dynamics are absent from pre-training (e.g., novel infrastructure patterns) or when spatial interactions are too complex, as on METR-LA.

### Mechanism 2: Native Probabilistic Forecasting via Quantile Regression
- **Claim**: Chronos-2 natively outputs well-calibrated uncertainty intervals and sharp predictive distributions.
- **Mechanism**: The model is pre-trained to predict a fixed set of 21 quantiles (0.01 to 0.99) in a single forward pass. This quantile regression objective induces a representation of the full predictive distribution. Generated quantiles directly form intervals and point forecasts.
- **Core assumption**: The quantile-based training generalizes to out-of-domain tasks, maintaining calibration and sharpness.
- **Evidence**: The abstract mentions useful uncertainty quantification; Section II describes quantile prediction; Section III shows probabilistic evaluation with coverage near 80% and sharp IQR compared to baselines.
- **Break condition**: Calibration failures occur on some datasets (e.g., SZ-taxi coverage 63.42%), indicating overconfidence due to distribution shift in noise or volatility.

### Mechanism 3: Implicit Spatial Modeling via Group Attention
- **Claim**: The group attention mechanism implicitly models spatial dependencies among sensors/stations without requiring an adjacency matrix, allowing Chronos-2 to compete with GNNs.
- **Mechanism**: The transformer encoder alternates between temporal attention (within each series) and cross-series attention (among series in a batch). This allows information flow between related series during encoding, implicitly learning spatial correlations from the batch structure.
- **Core assumption**: Natural grouping of series in a batch (e.g., all sensors from one dataset) suffices to discover meaningful spatial relationships.
- **Evidence**: Section II explains cross-learning without adjacency matrices. Performance tables show Chronos-2 often outperforms GNNs but underperforms on METR-LA, which the authors attribute to complex spatial interactions.
- **Break condition**: Datasets with complex, non-local spatial dependencies may require explicit graph structures; performance gap on METR-LA suggests the mechanism fails in such cases.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component Map**:
  Input Preprocessor -> TS-FM Core (Chronos-2) -> Quantile Extractor -> Evaluation Module

- **Critical Path**: Context Definition → Inference → Quantile Extraction → Metric Calculation.

- **Design Tradeoffs**:
  - Context length vs. compute/performance: longer contexts capture more seasonality but increase compute and may exceed token limits; paper used 1 week as default.
  - Point vs. probabilistic output: median loses uncertainty; full probabilistic pipeline needed for coverage/IQR.
  - Cross-learning (batch size): group attention operates across series in a batch; too small a batch may limit spatial information sharing.

- **Failure Signatures**:
  - Systematically high errors across all datasets vs. simple baseline → likely incorrect context formatting, wrong horizon, or granularity mismatch.
  - Very low coverage (e.g., <70% for 80% interval) → overconfident model; possible distribution shift or quantile extraction bug.
  - Poor performance only on longer horizons → limited inductive bias for long-range consistency; may need fine-tuning.

## Limitations
- Uncertainty regarding the grouping strategy for Chronos-2’s cross-series attention, which may affect performance on datasets with complex spatial dependencies (e.g., METR-LA).
- Ambiguity in how multi-horizon metrics are aggregated (e.g., NYC Citi Bike) hampers direct comparability.
- Probabilistic calibration failures on some datasets (e.g., SZ-taxi coverage 63.42%) indicate dataset-specific limitations.
