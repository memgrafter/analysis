# Residual Connections and the Causal Shift: Uncovering a Structural Misalignment in Transformers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14760  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14760  
- **Reference count:** 24  
- **Primary result:** Residual connections propagate current-token information while supervision targets the next token, creating a representational misalignment; proposed residual attenuation (fixed cut or learnable gating) reduces this misalignment and improves validation loss and benchmark performance in trained models.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a structural misalignment in autoregressive Transformers: residual connections preserve information about the current input token in the residual stream, while the training objective predicts the next token. This creates a tension where internal representations must gradually shift from input-aligned to output-aligned. Through analysis of pretrained LLMs, the authors localize this transition to deep layers and propose residual attenuation—scaling or removing residual connections selectively—to mitigate the issue. Experiments on 150M-parameter models show that a learnable gating mechanism consistently reduces validation loss and outperforms both baseline and fixed-cut methods across multiple language modeling benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The authors combine empirical analysis and intervention. For analysis, they use a "logit lens" approach: projecting intermediate hidden states (from tied embedding spaces) onto the vocabulary to track alignment with the current token versus the next token across layers. This reveals a depth-dependent shift from input- to output-alignment. For mitigation, they modify the residual branch: either zeroing it out at a single fixed layer (fixed cut) or learning a per-layer scaling factor α (learned gating). Models are trained from scratch (150M GPT-2-style) on 10B tokens from Fineweb-Edu, with validation on heldout data and zero-shot evaluation on standard benchmarks (Wikitext, LAMBADA, OpenWebText).

## Key Results
- Residual attenuation via learnable gating reduces validation loss compared to baseline and fixed-cut methods (e.g., Wikitext perplexity: baseline 28.75 → gated 28.46; LAMBADA accuracy: baseline 33.52 → gated 32.99; OpenWebText perplexity: baseline 35.50 → gated 34.74).
- The input-to-output alignment shift occurs deep within the network (e.g., ~layer 17/32 in Gemma-2-2B), and learned gating concentrates attenuation on the final layers.
- Fixed-layer cuts are sensitive; only cuts placed at or after the transition zone yield gains, while early cuts harm performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Residual Path as a Conservative Bias
The residual stream at position *i* starts as the embedding of token *t<sub>i</sub>*. During training, the model must transform this representation to predict *t<sub>i+1</sub>*. Residual connections (adding *x<sup>l</sup>* back after each sublayer) act as a conservative bias that propagates *t<sub>i</sub>-aligned* information forward. Optimization pushes the representation toward *t<sub>i+1</sub>-aligned* features, but the residual branch persistently injects input-anchored components, causing a misalignment that is particularly problematic in deeper layers where the prediction should be primarily output-oriented.

### Mechanism 2: Deep Transition of Internal Alignment
Using tied embeddings, the authors decode each layer’s hidden state into a token distribution ("logit lens"). They observe that early layers' decoded tokens strongly match the current input token, while late layers match the next token. The transition between these regimes occurs deep within the network and varies across models. This indicates that the network gradually overcomes the residual-borne input bias through successive transformations, but the shift is not complete until late layers.

### Mechanism 3: Attenuation Reduces Leakage
By scaling the residual branch (α < 1, or α = 0 at specific layers), the model suppresses the flow of persistent *t<sub>i</sub>-aligned* information into deeper layers. This forces the forward pass to rely more on the layer’s own transformation (F<sup>l</sup>) to build prediction-oriented features. The learnable gating mechanism automatically discovers a sparse attenuation pattern—typically cutting only the final layer—which aligns with the empirically observed late transition point and yields consistent improvements.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Tied Embeddings (Weight Sharing)
- **Why needed here:** Enables the "logit lens" technique: any hidden state can be projected directly into vocabulary space using the same matrix used for token embedding, allowing decoding of a token proxy for alignment.
- **Quick check:** If an intermediate state decodes to the same token as the input, it suggests strong input-alignment. If it decodes to the next token, it suggests output-alignment.

### Concept: Logit Lens / Decoding Trajectories
- **Why needed here:** Primary tool for tracking alignment shift. By repeatedly applying the final unembedding matrix to hidden states across layers, one can trace how a representation evolves from input-like to output-like.
- **Quick check:** Two limitations: (1) In very large models (>70B), the logit lens may become less interpretable due to nonlinearities or different training regimes. (2) It assumes the unembedding space is a meaningful semantic space for all layers, which may not hold for early layers.

### Concept: Learnable Gating Mechanism (Mixture-of-Depth Inspired)
- **Why needed here:** Provides an adaptive way to decide where to attenuate residuals, overcoming the sensitivity of a fixed cut. The gating distribution is optimized to minimize validation loss.
- **Quick check:** In the update *x<sup>l+1</sup> = α x<sup>l</sup> + F<sup>l</sup>(LN(x<sup>l</sup>))*, setting α exactly to 0 would break the gradient shortcut and destabilize training; hence α is kept >0 (or learned as a probability from a distribution).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input token → Embedding → Residual Stream (x⁰) → for l=0 to L-1: [LayerNorm → Attention → Add(α·xˡ) → LayerNorm → MLP → Add(α·xˡ)] → Final LayerNorm → LM Head (xᴸ · W<sub>V</sub><sup>T</sup>) → logits.
- **Critical path:** The residual stream carries identity information forward. Each block's sublayer (Attention + MLP) computes an update, which is added to the scaled residual stream. The final LM head projects to vocabulary.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Stability vs. Misalignment: Residuals stabilize training but propagate input bias.  
  - Fixed vs. Learnable Cut: Fixed cut is simple but sensitive to layer choice; learnable gating is adaptive but adds minimal parameters and requires a selection schedule.  
  - Attenuation Strength: α must be <1 but >0 to preserve gradient flow while reducing leakage.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. Increased validation loss after attenuation (cut too early or too strong).  
  2. Learned gating collapses to no attenuation or early-layer attenuation (indicating no benefit or mis-specified objective).  
  3. Alignment metrics show no reduction in input-output gap in deeper layers.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate shift localization: On a small GPT-2, compute per-layer top-5 match to input/shifted tokens; confirm transition occurs after ~50% depth.  
  2. Fixed-cut sweep: Train models with α=0 at a single layer across all layers; plot validation loss to find optimal late cut point.  
  3. Implement & train gating model: Add learnable α per layer (initialized uniformly); monitor gate distribution (should concentrate on late layers) and compare validation loss to baseline and best fixed cut.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **At precisely which layer does the input-to-output representation alignment shift occur across different model architectures and scales?** The shift depth varies across models (e.g., ~layer 17 in Gemma-2-2B) and is unclear for >70B parameter models.
2. **What is the optimal, adaptive mechanism for residual attenuation, and how does it interact with other architectural components?** The proposed gating is simple; more granular or conditional attenuation (per head, per token) and interactions with layer norm or attention patterns are unexplored.
3. **To what extent does the misalignment and benefit of attenuation scale with model size, training data, and task diversity?** Evidence is limited to ≤7B models and 150M models trained on 10B tokens; generalizability to trillion-token, 70B+ pre-training is unknown.
4. **What are the underlying causes of token-specific variability in transition depth?** The shift is not uniform across tokens; factors like token frequency, part-of-speech, or position might drive this but are not investigated.
5. **Are the proposed similarity-based alignment metrics sufficient and directly predictive of performance?** They are proxies; it is unclear if they capture all forms of representational conflict or if alternative metrics (gradient alignment, information flow) would correlate better with loss.

## Limitations
- Analysis relies on tied embeddings and logit lens; may not generalize to models without weight sharing or at extreme scales.
- Efficacy of residual attenuation demonstrated only on 150M models trained on a specific dataset; scaling to state-of-the-art pre-training is unproven.
- Fixed-cut performance is highly sensitive; the method’s success depends on correctly identifying the transition zone, which varies across architectures.

## Confidence
- **Claim Cluster 1 (Existence of Input-Output Shift): High** – Strong empirical evidence from multiple pretrained LLMs using consistent methodology.
- **Claim Cluster 2 (Efficacy of Residual Attenuation): Medium** – Consistent improvements in specific training setup, but generalizability untested.
- **Claim Cluster 3 (General Structural Misalignment): Low-to-Medium** – Compelling explanation but not definitively isolated as the sole cause; correlational evidence from intervention.

## Next Checks
1. **Test shift detection in non-tied models:** Replicate logit lens analysis on models without tied embeddings (e.g., GPT-3, PaLM) using alternative alignment metrics (e.g., projection onto separate input/output embeddings, CCA).
2. **Fixed-cut robustness sweep:** For the 150M model, sweep cut layer (all layers) and attenuation strength (α ∈ [0.0, 1.0]) to produce a 2D heatmap of validation loss; confirm optimal region is late and narrow.
3. **Analyze learned gate distribution dynamics:** During gating model training, monitor per-layer α values over time; verify convergence to a sparse pattern (e.g., only last layers near 0) and correlate with validation loss improvement.
