# What does RL improve for Visual Reasoning? A Frankenstein-Style Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.12395
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12395
- **Reference count**: 24
- **Primary result**: RL produces targeted refinements in mid-to-late transformer layers that enhance vision-to-reasoning alignment via increased cross-modal attention, with these changes being both necessary for performance gains and transferable via model merging; vision and reasoning metrics do not improve monotonically across training stages.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the ambiguity around what reinforcement learning (RL) actually improves in visual reasoning models. Through a Frankenstein-style causal analysis—combining functional localization, parameter update characterization, and transferability tests—the authors find that RL’s benefits are not due to a uniform enhancement of visual perception. Instead, RL consistently induces focused refinements in the mid-to-late layers of a transformer-based vision-language model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B). These refinements increase attention from reasoning tokens to vision tokens, improving how visual evidence is integrated into reasoning chains. The changes are shown to be necessary (freezing them ablates gains) and modular (merging them into supervised models transfers benefits). Parameter updates during RL are concentrated in magnitude and directionally sparse compared to supervised fine-tuning. The study reveals that vision and reasoning abilities, measured in isolation, do not improve monotonically from base to supervised to RL models, challenging the assumption that RL broadly upgrades perceptual skills.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze Qwen2.5-VL-7B models across three stages: base, after supervised fine-tuning (IN), and after RL fine-tuning (GRPO on visual reasoning). They partition the 28-layer transformer into Early (0–9), Mid (10–18), and Late (19–27) regions. Key interventions include: (1) Causal probing via vision token swapping between paired images to localize layers sensitive to visual features; (2) Layer-wise analysis of parameter update norms and singular value spectra to compare IN vs. RL optimization geometry; (3) Region-wise model merging (copying layers between IN and RL checkpoints) to test transferability; (4) Region-wise freezing during RL training to establish necessity. This multi-pronged approach isolates where and how RL modifies the model, moving beyond aggregate benchmark scores.

## Key Results
- RL’s improvements are localized to mid-to-late layers, not distributed uniformly; these layers show increased sensitivity to visual information and greater impact on reasoning performance.
- RL models exhibit stronger aggregated attention from reasoning tokens to vision tokens specifically in mid-to-late layers, enhancing vision-to-reasoning alignment.
- Mid-to-late refinements are both necessary (freezing them during RL eliminates gains) and transferable (merging them into IN models recovers most RL benefits).
- Parameter updates from RL concentrate in mid-layers and display steeper singular value decay in mid-to-late layers versus supervised fine-tuning, indicating sparse, directional adjustments.
- Standalone vision (Mvis) and reasoning (Mrea) metrics do not improve monotonically across training stages; RL produces a distinct profile that decouples these abilities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Cross-modal attention enhancement
**Claim**: RL improves visual reasoning by increasing attention from reasoning tokens to visual tokens in mid-to-late layers, thereby enhancing vision-to-reasoning alignment.  
**Mechanism**: During RL training, the model learns to selectively allocate attention to relevant visual evidence when generating reasoning chains. This is reflected in higher aggregated attention mass from reasoning tokens to vision tokens in mid-to-late layers, where cross-modal integration likely occurs.  
**Core assumption**: Attention allocation serves as a proxy for functional utilization; more cross-modal attention indicates better incorporation of visual information into the reasoning process.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- [abstract] "RL consistently induces a shift in mid-to-late transformer layers... primarily improving vision-to-reasoning alignment."  
- [section 3.3] "Compared to IN models, RL models exhibit stronger attention from reasoning tokens to vision tokens. The effect is concentrated in later layers."  
**Break condition**: If artificially increasing reasoning→vision attention in mid-late layers of an IN model (without RL) does not improve reasoning metrics, or if attention patterns are uncorrelated with accuracy gains, the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2: Focused parameter update geometry
**Claim**: RL concentrates parameter updates in mid-to-late layers with steeper spectral decay, indicating refinements along a few dominant directions that tune reasoning-relevant computations.  
**Mechanism**: RL training yields larger Frobenius norms in mid-layers and a steeper decay in the singular value spectrum of per-layer update matrices in mid-to-late layers, compared to supervised fine-tuning. This geometric pattern suggests RL makes sparse, targeted adjustments rather than diffuse changes.  
**Core assumption**: A steeper singular value spectrum implies optimization energy is allocated to fewer principal directions, likely corresponding to task-specific refinements.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- [section 3.2.2] "RL updates display a markedly steeper spectral decay in the Mid-Late layers, indicating optimization energy is concentrated in a smaller number of dominant directions."  
- [section 3.2.1] "Both IN and RL concentrate the majority of their update energy in the Mid layers... RL exhibits a distinct redistribution."  
**Break condition**: If update spectrum steepness does not predict RL-specific gains, or if manipulating the spectrum (e.g., projecting updates) does not affect performance, the mechanism is invalid.

### Mechanism 3: Modularity and necessity of mid-late refinements
**Claim**: Mid-to-late layer refinements from RL encode a functional module that is both transferable via merging and necessary for RL gains.  
**Mechanism**: Model merging shows that transplanting RL-refined mid-late layers into IN models consistently improves vision-to-reasoning alignment and reasoning. Conversely, freezing mid-late layers during RL training ablates these gains. This demonstrates that RL’s reliable contribution is localized in these layers and represents a distinct functional module.  
**Core assumption**: Successful transfer via layer swapping indicates the learned functionality is self-contained and not entangled with other layers.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- [abstract] "These mid-to-late refinements are both transferable (via merging) and necessary (via freezing) for RL gains."  
- [section 3.3] "Hybrid models that preserve RL-refined layers in the Mid-Late Layers consistently retain improvements... alternative combinations do not."  
- [section 4] "Freezing the Late layers during RL training results in substantial degradation... freezing the Early yields better performance."  
**Break condition**: If merging RL mid-late layers into IN fails to yield consistent improvements across diverse recipes and benchmarks, or if freezing these layers does not impair RL training, the mechanism does not hold.

## Foundational Learning
- **Functional Localization via Causal Probing**: Determines which transformer layers process visual information by swapping vision tokens between paired images that differ in one attribute. If the output changes, the layer uses that visual feature. *Why needed*: To map functional regions (vision vs. reasoning) for interpreting RL effects. *Quick check*: How would you identify a vision-processing layer? Answer: Swap vision tokens at that layer between such paired images; output change indicates vision sensitivity.
- **Parameter Update Geometry (Norm & Singular Spectrum)**: Analyzes per-layer Frobenius norm and singular value decay of the difference between checkpoints (e.g., ∆W = W_RL – W_IN). *Why needed*: To characterize whether RL updates are diffuse or focused, and whether they differ from supervised fine-tuning. *Quick check*: What does a steeper decay in the singular value spectrum imply? Answer: Updates concentrate in fewer dominant directions, suggesting targeted, low-dimensional refinement.
- **Model Merging as Transferability Test**: Copies layers (or subspaces) from one model into another to test whether learned functionality is modular. *Why needed*: To isolate RL’s contribution regionally and check if benefits transplant to differently initialized models. *Quick check*: If merging RL mid-late layers into IN improves over IN but not to RL’s level, what does that mean? Answer: Transferred layers encode beneficial functionality, but full RL performance requires complementary changes elsewhere.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) comprising a vision encoder and a 28-layer transformer decoder. Training pipeline: Base → IN (supervised fine-tuning on instruction data) → RL (GRPO on reasoning rewards). Functional regions: Early (layers 0–9), Mid (10–18), Late (19–27). Interventions: (1) Vision token swapping at a given layer; (2) Layer skipping (zeroing out layer parameters); (3) Region-wise parameter freezing during RL; (4) Region-wise model merging (copying parameters from IN/RL checkpoints).

**Critical path**: 1) Perform causal probing on the base model to localize vision and reasoning functions across layers. 2) Compute per-layer update norms and singular spectra for IN vs. RL to characterize optimization geometry. 3) Conduct region-wise model merging to test transferability of RL refinements. 4) Run region-wise freezing during RL to establish necessity of each region for final performance.

**Design tradeoffs**: Using coarse 1/3 partitions improves interpretability but may mask finer-grained variations. Token swapping assumes vision tokens are separable; deeper integrated layers may violate this. Layer skipping disrupts residual connections and may not isolate functional roles cleanly. Merging full layers (not subspaces) could transfer irrelevant parameters. Freezing early layers might hinder gradient flow to later layers, confounding necessity assessments.

**Failure signatures**: (1) Vision token swapping yields no layer-wise sensitivity → functional localization fails. (2) Update norms/spectra show no consistent RL vs. IN difference → RL effects not concentrated. (3) Merging RL mid-late into IN does not yield consistent improvements → refinements not transferable. (4) Freezing mid-late during RL does not degrade performance → refinements not necessary.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Vision token swapping**: For OCR/counting/grounding/recognition paired images differing in one visual attribute, swap vision tokens at each layer and compute change rate in outputs to map vision-sensitive layers.
2. **Layer skipping ablation**: For each layer, zero its trainable parameters and evaluate on GSM8K/MATH500; measure error rate increase to localize reasoning-critical layers.
3. **Update geometry analysis**: Compute per-layer Frobenius norm and singular value spectrum for ∆W_IN and ∆W_RL; compare distributions across layers and training recipes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Does RL from base (without supervised init) still concentrate refinements in mid-to-late layers?** The study only examined the dominant IN+RL paradigm; direct RL could yield different layer-wise effects.
2. **Are the effects consistent across VLM architectures beyond Qwen?** All experiments used Qwen series models; other architectures may have different functional partitions or update behaviors.
3. **Does RL consistently improve other multimodal tasks (captioning, detection, video QA) via the same mid-late refinements?** Analysis was limited to visual reasoning; tasks with different objectives might engage distinct layers.

## Limitations
- Coarse regional partitioning (1/3 split) may obscure finer-grained layer-specific shifts or transitions.
- Token-swapping assumes a clean separation of vision tokens, which may not hold in deeper layers where modalities are highly integrated.
- Findings are restricted to Qwen2.5-VL-7B; generalizability to other VLM architectures (e.g., different fusion schemes, smaller models) remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Mid-late shift & cross-modal attention**: High confidence. Strong, consistent evidence from causal probing, attention analysis, and model merging across multiple training recipes.
- **Parameter update geometry**: Medium confidence. Norm and spectral analysis are robust quantitative descriptors, but the interpretive link between “steeper spectral decay” and “task-specific refinement” lacks direct correlation with fine-grained performance gains.
- **Modularity & necessity**: Medium confidence. Merging and freezing provide causal regional evidence, but freezing may disrupt gradient flow non-specifically and merging full layers could include irrelevant changes.

## Next Checks
1. **Subspace-level merging**: Identify key subspaces via PCA on ∆W_RL and transplant only these into an IN model to confirm modularity with higher precision and rule out irrelevant parameter transfer.
2. **Attention intervention causal test**: In an IN model, manually re-weight attention matrices in mid-late layers to match RL’s cross-modal pattern (e.g., increase reasoning→vision mass). If reasoning metrics improve without RL training, it causally validates the attention mechanism.
3. **Finer-grained layer ablation**: Replicate freezing/merging experiments with sliding 2–3 layer windows instead of 1/3 regions to pinpoint exact layer boundaries where RL effects are strongest and most transferable.
