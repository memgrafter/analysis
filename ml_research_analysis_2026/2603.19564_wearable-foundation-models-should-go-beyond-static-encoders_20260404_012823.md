# Wearable Foundation Models Should Go Beyond Static Encoders
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19564  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19564  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Existing wearable foundation models (WFMs) rely on short-term static encoders, limiting their utility for chronic condition management; the paper argues for three foundational shifts—open longitudinal data ecosystems, hierarchical temporal modeling, and agentic inference—to enable continuous, anticipatory health support.

## Executive Summary
This position paper argues that current wearable foundation models are structurally biased toward retrospective, short-horizon tasks (e.g., activity recognition) due to their reliance on static encoders processing fixed-length sensor windows. This design fails to capture the longitudinal, evolving nature of health states essential for managing chronic conditions. The authors propose a research agenda centered on three shifts: (1) building open, interoperable data ecosystems that integrate multimodal, long-term personal trajectories with context; (2) developing longitudinal-aware architectures with hierarchical temporal abstraction and personalization; and (3) adopting agentic inference systems that move beyond static prediction to support planning and decision-making under uncertainty. The paper does not present new experiments but reframes WFMs from passive signal interpreters to continuous, human-aligned health partners.

## Method Summary
The paper synthesizes a critique of the dominant WFM paradigm—mapping short temporal windows to predefined labels—and outlines three interdependent research directions. First, data ecosystems must overcome fragmentation by harmonizing heterogeneous multimodal streams (e.g., ECG, PPG, IMU) with contextual metadata across long durations. Second, modeling architectures must natively support long-context inference, multi-scale temporal abstraction (e.g., compressing high-frequency signals into daily/weekly trends), and personalization via persistent memory. Third, inference systems should treat WFMs as modular “bio-tools” within an agentic pipeline that iteratively gathers evidence, updates beliefs, and proposes context-aware actions. No specific architectural blueprints or evaluation metrics are provided; the work is a conceptual agenda.

## Key Results
- Static encoder paradigms structurally limit WFMs to cross-sectional, population-level analysis, excluding long-term personal trajectories and context critical for chronic disease management.
- Longitudinal-aware modeling requires hierarchical temporal representations and retrieval-augmented personal memory to handle modality sparsity and multi-scale health dynamics.
- Agentic systems can transform WFMs from passive predictors into active planning components, but require deep integration with physiological representations to avoid shallow heuristics.
- The paper provides no new experimental results or metrics, focusing instead on a research roadmap.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The dominance of static encoder paradigms structurally biases wearable foundation models (WFMs) toward short-term pattern recognition, limiting their utility for longitudinal health reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** By framing wearable health modeling as mapping fixed-length sensor windows to predefined labels, the entire pipeline—from dataset construction to inference—reinforces cross-sectional, population-level analysis. This excludes integration of long-term personal trajectories and contextual grounding necessary for chronic condition management.
- **Core assumption:** Health states evolve continuously; meaningful clinical insights (e.g., disease progression, treatment response) emerge from patterns across weeks or months, not isolated moments.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "most existing WFMs primarily map short temporal windows to predefined labels via static encoders, emphasizing retrospective prediction rather than reasoning over evolving personal history, context, and future risk trajectories."  
  [section 3.1] "most wearable datasets are organized around short temporal windows... richer contextual information... is often sparse, inconsistently integrated, or entirely unavailable."  
  [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers (e.g., "Learning Longitudinal Health Representations from EHR and Wearable Data") echo the separation of EHR and wearable data, supporting the claim about fragmentation.
- **Break condition:** If open, interoperable datasets integrating multimodal long-term trajectories and context do not emerge, WFMs will remain confined to retrospective, short-horizon tasks.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Longitudinal-aware multimodal modeling requires architectures that natively support long-context inference, temporal abstraction, and personalization, moving beyond fixed-modality, short-sequence encoders.
- **Mechanism:** Hierarchical temporal representations compress high-frequency sensor streams into multi-scale tokens (e.g., heartbeats → daily patterns → weekly trends), enabling models to capture both fine-grained events and slow health drifts. Personalization is achieved via retrieval-augmented memory that persists despite modality sparsity.
- **Core assumption:** Physiological signals exhibit multi-scale temporal dynamics; effective reasoning demands representations that align with these dynamics and individual baselines.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.2] "future WFMs must shift from static, cross-sectional windows toward longitudinally-aware architectures... adopt hierarchical temporal representations that compress high-frequency streams into coarser, multi-scale tokens."  
  [section 3.2] "To handle the inherent sparsity... longitudinal modeling should be reframed as selective evidence retrieval... maintain personal memory as a persistent, queryable health history."  
  [abstract] "Longitudinal-aware multimodal modeling, which prioritizes long-context inference, temporal abstraction, and personalization over cross-sectional or population-level prediction."
- **Break condition:** If modeling research remains focused on scaling static window lengths without hierarchical abstraction or personalization, WFMs will not achieve efficient or robust longitudinal reasoning.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Agentic inference systems are needed to transform WFMs from passive discriminative predictors into active, planning-oriented components that support decision-making under uncertainty.
- **Mechanism:** By treating WFMs as modular "bio-toolsets" rather than end-to-end solutions, a central agent can orchestrate specialized components (e.g., signal denoiser, event detector, longitudinal retriever) to iteratively gather evidence, update beliefs, and propose context-aware actions. This closes the loop between sensing and intervention.
- **Core assumption:** Real-world health support requires more than detection—it needs adaptive strategies that consider evolving evidence, user goals, and clinical constraints.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.3] "WFMs as a Bio-Toolset: a collection of modular components that can be readily integrated into agentic pipelines... not treated as end-to-end health decision-makers, but as high-fidelity perceptual and analytical components."  
  [section 3.3] "agentic inference can alleviate the mounting pressure on national healthcare systems by providing patients with more accessible, flexible tools for proactive health management."  
  [abstract] "Agentic inference systems, which move beyond static prediction to support planning, decision-making, and clinically grounded intervention under uncertainty."
- **Break condition:** If agentic frameworks fail to deeply integrate high-fidelity physiological representations (relying instead on shallow heuristics), the transition from passive monitoring to active health support will not occur.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Data Harmonization & Interoperability**  
  *Why needed here:* Overcomes dataset fragmentation by enabling joint training across heterogeneous sources (different devices, modalities, sampling rates) without forcing raw signal alignment.  
  *Quick check:* Can your preprocessing pipeline accept a new dataset with different sensor types and temporal resolutions without redesigning the model architecture?
- **Concept: Hierarchical Temporal Abstraction**  
  *Why needed here:* Allows models to operate on multiple time scales (seconds to months), essential for distinguishing acute events from chronic trends.  
  *Quick check:* Does your model's tokenization strategy allow for meaningful aggregation of minute-level data into daily or weekly summaries without losing critical information?
- **Concept: Tool Orchestration & Memory**  
  *Why needed here:* Supports agentic workflows where diverse WFM components (specialists) are called on-demand, and a persistent memory stores long-term individual baselines.  
  *Quick check:* Can your system dynamically select and chain different WFM experts based on current query and historical context, rather than running a single fixed forward pass?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Data Ingestion Layer -> Multimodal Encoder Backbone -> Temporal Compressor Module -> Personal Memory Store -> Agentic Interface
- **Critical path:** Raw sensor data is ingested as modality-tagged tokens, processed by a shared encoder, compressed into multi-scale trajectory embeddings, stored in personal memory, and accessed by an agentic interface that synthesizes actionable outputs (e.g., plans, alerts). Key dependencies: tokenization must preserve cross-modal alignment; memory retrieval must be fast and relevant.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Sequence length vs. compute:* Longer contexts improve longitudinal reasoning but increase memory and latency; use hierarchical compression to manage effective length.  
  *Modality coverage vs. sparsity:* More sensors improve robustness but increase missing-data likelihood; train with random modality dropout to simulate real-world sparsity.  
  *Agent autonomy vs. safety:* More proactive planning increases utility but raises accountability risks; constrain agent to "suggest" actions with confidence scores and require human confirmation for clinical interventions.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  *Temporal drift:* Performance degrades as user physiology changes because memory is not updated or re-weighted.  
  *Modality collapse:* Model over-relies on one high-signal modality (e.g., PPG) when others are missing, failing to detect events only visible in sparse modalities.  
  *Static output:* System only produces labels or scores, never initiates queries, requests additional sensing, or generates multi-step plans.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Long-context consistency test:** Feed synthetic trajectories with embedded slow trends (e.g., gradual sleep deterioration over 30 days). Measure if model's multi-scale representations capture trend direction and magnitude better than sliding-window baselines.  
  2. **Missing-modality robustness:** Evaluate multimodal encoder on held-out modalities during inference. Success criterion: performance drop <10% when any single modality is missing, indicating learned cross-modal robustness.  
  3. **Agentic tool-calling benchmark:** Use a simulated environment where an agent must decide (a) when to trigger a high-frequency sensing burst, (b) which specialist to consult (e.g., "stress detector" vs. "arrhythmia checker"), and (c) how to summarize findings for a clinician. Measure success rate vs. static-prediction-only baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can open, interoperable data ecosystems be engineered to unify heterogeneous multimodal wearable datasets with longitudinal trajectories and contextual metadata?  
  *Basis in paper:* Section 3.1 highlights that population-scale datasets are proprietary, open datasets are fragmented and short-term, and contextual information is sparse or absent, structurally reinforcing static modeling.  
  *Why unresolved:* No existing frameworks enable seamless cross-dataset training with consistent temporal and contextual grounding; community lacks standards for schema harmonization, metadata integration, and long-term continuity.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* A deployed open ecosystem with standardized data formats, preprocessing tools, and benchmark datasets that integrate signals, context, and multi-year trajectories, demonstrated by improved WFM generalization across studies.
- **Open Question 2:** What architectural paradigms and training objectives enable wearable foundation models to perform longitudinal health trajectory modeling over irregular, multi-year data with personalization?  
  *Basis in paper:* Section 3.2 critiques short-horizon static encoders and calls for hierarchical temporal representations, long-context inference, and personal memory akin to retrieval-augmented generation.  
  *Why unresolved:* Current WFMs process isolated windows, failing to model within-subject evolution, handle modality sparsity, or compress long sequences into multi-scale abstractions; personalization remains ad hoc.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* A WFM architecture that forecasts individualized health trajectories (e.g., disease progression) over months/years on holdout data, outperforming window-based baselines with ablations showing benefits of hierarchical modeling and memory.
- **Open Question 3:** How can wearable foundation models be designed as modular "Bio-Tools" to deeply integrate with agentic systems for adaptive, decision-oriented health support?  
  *Basis in paper:* Section 3.3 proposes the Bio-Toolset concept, arguing existing agentic frameworks use shallow abstractions and lack deep coupling with WFMs' physiological representations.  
  *Why unresolved:* Current agentic health systems rely on static feature extractors or code-based heuristics; WFMs are typically end-to-end predictors, not composable tools for iterative reasoning under uncertainty.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* An agentic pipeline where a WFM module provides high-fidelity signal interpretation (e.g., event detection, baseline comparison) to an LLM-based agent that performs multi-step planning (e.g., adaptive monitoring schedules), validated via human studies.
- **Open Question 4:** What evaluation frameworks and metrics can capture longitudinal, anticipatory health reasoning capabilities beyond static prediction accuracy?  
  *Basis in paper:* The abstract and Section 5 state the paper outlines a research agenda with "no new experimental metrics," focusing on retrospective tasks.  
  *Why unresolved:* Standard benchmarks use short-window classification/regression; no tests for trajectory forecasting, personalization, or decision quality over time, limiting progress toward anticipatory systems.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Community-endorsed benchmark suites with longitudinal tasks (e.g., predicting disease onset from years of data), metrics for personalization and uncertainty-aware planning, and leaderboards tracking real-world utility.

## Limitations
- **Feasibility of "Structurally Rich" Data Ecosystems:** Proposed open, interoperable ecosystems integrating multimodal, long-term trajectories with context face practical barriers (consent, device heterogeneity, storage/annotation costs) with no scalability roadmap.
- **Architectural Blueprint for Longitudinal Modeling:** Advocates "hierarchical temporal representations" and "personal memory" but provides no specific mechanisms (e.g., tokenization for irregular streams, memory update policies), leaving a vast, undefined design space.
- **Definition & Evaluation of "Agentic" Health Support:** The shift to planning lacks formal objectives, safety constraints, or metrics for "successful" anticipatory guidance; operationalizing this concept remains speculative and unproven.

## Confidence
- **Static encoders are insufficient for longitudinal reasoning:** **High Confidence.** Well-supported by mismatch between dataset structures (short windows, isolated tasks) and chronic condition needs (continuous trends).
- **New modeling architectures are required:** **Medium Confidence.** Need for long-context and personalization is logical, but specific architectural solutions are speculative; existing long-sequence models (Transformers, Mamba) may be adaptable but unproven for multimodal health trajectories.
- **Agentic systems are the necessary paradigm:** **Low Confidence.** Most speculative shift; assumes prior shifts are solved and introduces safety/trust complexities; no evidence of superiority over enhanced predictive models.

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset Audit:** Systematically audit 5–10 major public multimodal wearable datasets against "structurally rich" criteria. Quantify the fraction of data with: (a) >7-day continuous recording, (b) >2 synchronized modalities, (c) any contextual metadata (e.g., medication, symptoms).
2. **Long-Context Ablation:** For a long-sequence health task (e.g., predicting 30-day hypertension progression from daily PPG/activity), compare a standard sliding-window model against a model using the full available context. Measure the performance delta; a negligible gap would falsify the premise that long-context modeling provides unique value.
3. **Agentic Capability Benchmark:** Design a simulation environment for a chronic condition (e.g., type 2 diabetes). Define agentic queries requiring temporal synthesis and planning (e.g., "Given last month's activity slumps and glucose variability, propose a 3-step weekly adjustment to medication timing and evening walks"). Evaluate a baseline static predictor versus an agentic pipeline with memory on success rate and safety (avoidance of harmful suggestions).
